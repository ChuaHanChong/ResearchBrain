# HAF: Adapting Generalist VLAs to Humanoid Whole-Body Loco-manipulation via Hierarchical Action Flow and Spectral Latent RL

> [!summary] Summary
> HAF adapts frozen generalist flow-matching VLA models to humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation via two parts: HAF-VLA, which generates locomotion, torso, and arm actions in three kinematically ordered stages with cross-stage KV caches, and HAF-Steer, which runs offline-to-online SAC in a DCT-compressed flow-noise latent space. On seven real-world humanoid tasks HAF-VLA averages 70.5% normalized score versus 53.3% for vanilla pi-0.5, and HAF-Steer further raises success rates without touching the VLA backbone.

## Problem

- Single-stage flow-matching VLAs generate all body-part actions at once without modeling dependencies among locomotion, torso posture, and bimanual manipulation, causing incoherent whole-body motion.
- Offline behavior cloning alone leaves humanoid policies suboptimal at deployment, but real-world exploration is needed to close the gap.
- Directly fine-tuning large VLA backbones with online reinforcement learning demands excessive compute and risks unsafe real-robot exploration.

## Method

- **HAF-VLA** splits full-body action denoising into three sequential stages (locomotion and head, torso, then dual-arm manipulation), using ==stage embeddings== and ==cross-stage KV caches== to preserve kinematic dependencies across stages.
- **HAF-Steer** numerically inverts the frozen ==flow-matching field== to recover initial noise for demonstrated action chunks, then applies ==discrete cosine transform== along the temporal axis, keeping only the top 8 coefficients.
- A regularized ==SAC== policy is trained ==offline-to-online== purely in this compact ==DCT noise subspace==, refining the frozen VLA's outputs without updating any backbone weights.

## Results

- HAF-VLA reaches **70.5%** average normalized task score across seven long-horizon tasks versus **53.3%** for pi-0.5, **38.1%** for GR00T N1.7, **27.6%** for Cosmos Policy, and **25.2%** for ACT.
- Under visual distraction and positional perturbation, HAF-VLA scores **40.0%** and **43.3%** versus pi-0.5's **26.7%** and **36.7%**, showing stronger generalization beyond the demonstration layout.
- Applying HAF-Steer to HAF-VLA lifts Toy Storage in-distribution success from **4/10** to **10/10** and Basket Transfer out-of-distribution from **4/10** to **8/10**, while DSRL's repeated-noise baseline caused unsafe motions and training failure.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ordering action generation by kinematic dependency (base stabilization before upper-body control) is a transferable principle for adapting single-stage generalist policies to high-DOF embodiments.
> - Restricting online RL to a low-dimensional spectral subspace of a frozen generative policy's noise avoids costly and risky backbone fine-tuning while still enabling real-world adaptation.
> - Removing temporal variation from the exploration noise (as in repeated-noise parameterizations) can destabilize real-robot RL; retaining low-frequency temporal modes is safer for physical exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is progressing towards developing general-purpose agents, particularly humanoid robots, capable of operating in human-centric environments. A key enabler for this vision is the advancement of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models, which aim to translate visual observations and language instructions into robotic actions.

Existing VLA models, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and π$_{0.5}$, have demonstrated generalization capabilities on conventional robotic platforms, typically focusing on tabletop tasks or mobile manipulators. However, adapting these generalist VLAs to humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation presents distinct challenges. Humanoid robots require the synchronized coordination of locomotion, torso posture, and bimanual manipulation, which introduces high dimensionality and interdependence in their action spaces. Conventional single-stage VLA architectures often struggle to effectively manage these dependencies, leading to issues like unstable base movements inducing erratic upper-body compensatory motions, thereby degrading balance and manipulation accuracy. Some prior work on humanoid VLAs, like GR00T N1.7, WholeBodyVLA, and Ψ$_{0}$, addresses these challenges through humanoid-specific pretraining, large embodiment-specific datasets, latent skill learning, cross-embodiment transfer, or learning from human videos. These approaches, however, often involve significant computational costs or reliance on specific data types.

Another challenge in the broader landscape concerns the deployment of policies trained through offline behavior cloning. Such policies can exhibit suboptimal performance when encountering distribution shifts in real-world environments. While online reinforcement learning (RL) offers a mechanism for policy refinement, directly tuning the large VLA backbones is computationally intensive and poses safety risks during real-robot exploration, especially in high-dimensional humanoid action spaces. Existing latent-noise RL methods, such as DSRL and UniSteer, attempt to mitigate backbone updates by optimizing in a latent space, but they often face trade-offs between efficiency (e.g., repeating a single noise vector, sacrificing temporal expressiveness) and exploration space (e.g., optimizing full high-dimensional temporal noise).

This work positions itself to bridge the gap between generalist VLA foundation models and their effective application to complex humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation tasks. It aims to achieve this without requiring extensive humanoid-specific pretraining or direct fine-tuning of large VLA backbones, addressing the limitations of existing VLA adaptation and latent RL methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that effectively adapts off-the-shelf generalist VLA foundation models for humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges identified in the current research landscape:

1. **High Dimensionality and Interdependence of Humanoid Motions:** Humanoid robots necessitate synchronized control of locomotion, torso posture, and dual-arm manipulation. The high-dimensional nature of these combined actions and their kinematic interdependencies make it difficult for standard single-stage VLA architectures to generate coherent and stable whole-body motions. For instance, an unstable base can lead to compensatory movements in the upper body, compromising overall task performance and balance. The paper aims to develop a method that explicitly accounts for these dependencies.

2. **Limitations of Conventional Single-Stage VLA Architectures:** Existing generalist VLAs typically generate all body-part actions in a single step, without explicitly modeling the hierarchical dependencies between different body segments (e.g., locomotion preceding waist and arm movements). This can lead to incoherent actions and unstable robot behavior, especially in complex, long-horizon tasks. The motivation is to introduce a structured action generation process that aligns with humanoid kinematics.

3. **High Cost of Humanoid-Specific Pretraining:** Adapting generalist VLAs to humanoids often involves embodiment-specific pretraining or the collection of large humanoid-specific datasets. This significantly increases the computational and data collection costs, hindering the rapid deployment and generalization of VLAs to new humanoid platforms. The research is motivated by the need for a more efficient adaptation strategy that reuses pretrained generalist models.

4. **Suboptimality of Offline Behavior-Cloned Policies in Real-World Deployment:** Policies trained solely through offline behavior cloning often encounter performance degradation when deployed in real-world environments due to distribution shifts. The need for real-world policy refinement is evident.

5. **Computational Expense and Safety Risks of Direct VLA Fine-tuning:** While online reinforcement learning can refine policies, directly fine-tuning large VLA backbones for high-dimensional humanoid action spaces is computationally expensive. Furthermore, uncontrolled exploration in high-dimensional action spaces can lead to unsafe motions and damage to physical robots. This motivates the development of a lightweight and safe online adaptation mechanism that avoids modifying the core VLA backbone.

To address these motivations, the paper introduces the Humanoid Adaptation Framework (HAF), which includes HAF-VLA for structured action generation and HAF-Steer for efficient real-world policy refinement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Humanoid Adaptation Framework (HAF) is a two-part framework designed to transfer off-the-shelf generalist VLA foundation models to humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation tasks. It consists of HAF-VLA, a hierarchical action-flow generator, and HAF-Steer, a latent offline-to-online reinforcement learning (RL) pipeline.

#### 3.1. HAF-VLA: Hierarchical Action Flow for Humanoid Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation

HAF-VLA repurposes a pretrained generalist flow-matching VLA (specifically, π$_{0.5}$) by restructuring its action generation process to align with humanoid kinematic dependencies.
Instead of generating full-body actions in a single step, HAF-VLA orchestrates a progressive, kinematics-aware generation process through three sequential stages:

1. **Hierarchical Whole-Body Action Generation:**
 - **Action Decomposition:** Each instantaneous action `a_t` is explicitly decomposed into four kinematic sub-components: `a_move` (locomotion and skill-mode commands), `a_head` (head orientation control), `a_waist` (waist posture adjustment), and `a_manip` (bimanual manipulation targets).
 - **Nested Cumulative Action Sets:** The full D-dimensional action space is partitioned into four mutually disjoint subsets corresponding to these components. Three nested cumulative action index sets are then defined:
 - `A_1` = `A_move` ∪ `A_head` (Stage 1: Locomotion and Head)
 - `A_2` = `A_1` ∪ `A_waist` (Stage 2: Locomotion, Head, and Waist)
 - `A_3` = `A_2` ∪ `A_manip` (Stage 3: Full-body action, including Manipulation)
 - This hierarchy ensures that base stabilization (locomotion and head control) is prioritized before torso adjustment and fine manipulation, mitigating unstable compensatory movements.
 - **Cross-Stage KV-Cache Conditioning:** To ensure global coherence across stages, action features from earlier stages are re-encoded into cross-stage KV caches. These caches condition subsequent stages, providing coarse prior motion context. For example, Stage 2 is conditioned on the output of Stage 1, and Stage 3 is conditioned on outputs from both Stage 1 and Stage 2.
 - The active action sets are cumulative, meaning later stages can refine and update action dimensions predicted in earlier stages. Only the final full-body action chunk from Stage 3 is executed.

2. **Training Strategy and Deployment:**
 - All three hierarchical stages share identical weights for the action-flow expert network. A shared vision-language prefix KV cache is computed once and reused across all stages.
 - Each stage samples an independent Gaussian noise vector and a flow time `τ`. Stage-specific behavior is modulated via a trainable stage embedding `e_s`.
 - During training, a flow-matching objective is used, summing the losses from all three stages with equal weights. Teacher forcing is employed to compute cross-stage caches from masked ground-truth actions, preventing error accumulation while maintaining the hierarchical structure.
 - At inference, the three stages run sequentially with independent Gaussian noise samples. The denoised outputs of earlier stages form the caches for later ones. The robot executes the first 40 steps of the final Stage 3 output chunk using a receding-horizon control scheme, with each complete three-stage inference taking approximately 0.12 seconds on an RTX 5090 GPU.

#### 3.2. HAF-Steer: Spectral Latent RL for Flow-Matching VLAs

HAF-Steer is a lightweight reinforcement-learning method that refines a *frozen* flow-matching VLA backbone (either vanilla π$_{0.5}$ or HAF-VLA) by optimizing its initial flow noise in a compact latent space.

1. **Spectral Latent Space via Flow Reversal:**
 - **Flow Reversal:** Given a demonstrated action chunk `A*`, its corresponding initial flow noise `ϵ*` is recovered by numerically reversing the frozen flow field `Fθ` of the VLA.
 - **Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) Compression:** To reduce the dimensionality of the noise, the DCT is applied along the temporal dimension of `ϵ*`. Only the first `K` coefficients (empirically `K=8`) are retained, significantly reducing the exploration space from `H × D` to `K × D` (where H is chunk length, D is action dimension). This preserves smoothly varying temporal modes and enforces temporal smoothness, avoiding unstable high-frequency exploratory movements.
 - **Normalization:** The `K` DCT coefficients `c*` are then normalized to form a "spectral action" `z*`, which is stored in an offline spectral replay buffer along with sparse task rewards and termination flags.

2. **Offline-to-Online Spectral Policy Learning:**
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC) Initialization:** A stochastic spectral actor `πψ(z | x)` is first initialized by regressing its mean output `μψ(x)` towards the normalized expert spectral actions `z*` from the offline buffer. Only the actor is optimized during this stage.
 - **Mixed Offline–Online Reinforcement Learning:** After BC initialization, the policy interacts with the real robot. Newly collected transitions are stored in an online replay buffer `D_on`.
 - Training uses mixed mini-batches from both `D_off` and `D_on`.
 - A Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm is employed for policy updates, with an additional behavior cloning regularization term applied *only* to the offline expert data. This term helps preserve demonstration quality.
 - The critics are updated using standard entropy-regularized SAC Bellman loss.
 - The VLA backbone remains frozen throughout this process; only the spectral actor, critics, target critics, and entropy temperature are updated. The offline sampling ratio is gradually decayed.
 - **Inference Pipeline:** At test time, the trained spectral actor samples a normalized spectral action `z_t`. This `z_t` is denormalized, converted back to full temporal noise `ϵ_t` using inverse DCT (IDCT, zero-padding for truncated coefficients), and then fed into the *frozen* VLA flow model `Fθ` to produce the final refined whole-body action chunk `A_t`.

3. **Instantiation on Flow-Matching VLA Backbones:** HAF-Steer's spectral parameterization and flow-reversal procedure are applicable to both vanilla π$_{0.5}$ and HAF-VLA. When used with HAF-VLA, HAF-Steer replaces only the initial noise of HAF-VLA's final generation Stage 3, while Stages 1 and 2 operate as usual, providing their clean-action caches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The HAF framework was evaluated on two physical humanoid robots (TienKung 2.0 and TienKung 3.0) across seven real-world household loco-manipulation tasks: Laundry Loading, Clothes Retrieval, Table Tidy, Basket Transfer, Toy Storage, Ball Tossing, and Box Transfer. A normalized task score, based on predefined milestone sub-goals, was used as the primary evaluation metric over 10 independent rollouts per method. Baselines included ACT, π$_{0.5}$, GR00T N1.7, and Cosmos Policy.

#### 4.1. Q1: HAF-VLA Enables Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation Beyond Existing SOTA Methods

- **Overall Performance:** HAF-VLA achieved the best or tied-best average normalized score on all seven tasks. It improved the overall average performance from 53.3% (with π$_{0.5}$) to 70.5%.
- **Specific Task Gains:** Performance gains were notable in tasks combining locomotion with fine manipulation, such as Box Transfer (93.3% for HAF-VLA vs. 60.0% for π$_{0.5}$), Ball Tossing (56.7% vs. 33.3%), and Laundry Loading (66.7% vs. 53.3%).
- **Baseline Observations:** π$_{0.5}$ and GR00T N1.7 often exhibited locomotion drift, while Cosmos Policy was affected by higher online inference latency.
- These results suggest the effectiveness of HAF-VLA's hierarchical action generation for high-dimensional humanoid action spaces.

#### 4.2. Q2: HAF-VLA’s Hierarchical Action-Flow Design Improves Imitation-Learning Performance

An ablation study on the Laundry Loading task investigated the impact of HAF-VLA’s hierarchical action-flow design:

- **HAF-VLA (Locomotion/Head → + Waist → + Manip.):** Achieved a normalized score of 66.7%.
- **All-Joint Denoising (every stage denoises all dimensions):** Scored 53.3%, indicating that simultaneously denoising all joints is less effective than the hierarchical approach.
- **Arm-First Hierarchy (Manip. → + Waist → + Locomotion/Head):** Scored 50.0%, demonstrating the importance of prioritizing base stabilization over manipulation.
- **Vanilla π$_{0.5}$ (30 denoising steps):** Scored 20.0%, lower than its 10-step baseline (53.3%) due to increased latency amplifying locomotion errors.
- The results indicate that the hierarchical design, particularly the coarse-to-fine generation order, contributes to improved imitation-learning performance by ensuring action coherence and suppressing unstable movements.

#### 4.3. Q3: HAF-VLA Generalizes to Unseen Visual and Positional Disturbances

HAF-VLA was tested for out-of-distribution robustness:

- **Object Distraction (Laundry Loading with unseen chair):** HAF-VLA scored 40.0% compared to π$_{0.5}$'s 26.7%.
- **Position Disturbance (Clothes Retrieval with 20cm backward shift):** HAF-VLA scored 43.3% compared to π$_{0.5}$'s 36.7%.
- HAF-VLA maintained higher task performance under both visual distraction and positional perturbation, suggesting improved generalization beyond exact demonstration layouts.

#### 4.4. Q4: HAF-Steer Improves Real-World Deployment Performance Through Offline-to-Online Adaptation

HAF-Steer was evaluated on Toy Storage and Basket Transfer, both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) settings (with 30cm goal shifts), using both π$_{0.5}$ and HAF-VLA as backbones.

- **HAF-Steer's Impact:** HAF-Steer consistently improved success rates across all ID and OOD settings for both π$_{0.5}$ and HAF-VLA backbones.
 - For Toy Storage (ID), HAF-Steer on HAF-VLA achieved 10/10 successes, up from 7/10 for HAF-VLA without steering (Base Model).
 - For Basket Transfer (OOD), HAF-Steer on HAF-VLA achieved 8/10 successes, up from 4/10.
- **Behavior Cloning (Noise BC):** The BC-initialized spectral actor already showed performance improvements in several settings (e.g., 7/10 for Toy Storage ID with HAF-VLA backbone). Subsequent mixed offline-online SAC training further increased success rates, indicating the benefit of online refinement.
- **Comparison with DSRL:** DSRL, which repeats a single noise vector, failed to complete training due to unsafe exploratory motions in real-robot rollouts, underscoring the benefits of HAF-Steer's multi-mode spectral parameterization for stable latent-space exploration.
- **Complementary Nature:** Applying HAF-Steer to HAF-VLA (e.g., Toy Storage OOD: 10/10 vs. 7/10 without HAF-Steer; Basket Transfer ID: 9/10 vs. 6/10) yielded the best or tied-best performance in three out of four combined settings. This demonstrates that HAF-VLA provides a structured policy, which HAF-Steer then adaptively refines for deployment-time distribution shifts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HAF framework contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by offering an approach for adapting generalist VLA models to complex whole-body loco-manipulation tasks. Its significance stems from several key aspects:

1. **Efficient Adaptation of Generalist VLAs:** HAF provides a method to repurpose pretrained generalist VLA models for humanoid applications without requiring extensive humanoid-specific pretraining or the collection of large embodiment-specific datasets. This reduces computational costs and accelerates the deployment of advanced VLA capabilities to humanoid platforms, making it more feasible to leverage existing foundational models.

2. **Improved Whole-Body Coordination and Stability:** HAF-VLA’s hierarchical action-flow generation, which prioritizes locomotion and body posture before fine manipulation and uses cross-stage conditioning, directly addresses the challenge of coordinating high-dimensional, interdependent humanoid movements. This structured approach helps suppress unstable compensatory motions, leading to more coherent and stable whole-body actions and improved task performance in long-horizon scenarios.

3. **Safe and Efficient Real-World Policy Refinement:** HAF-Steer enables robust online adaptation without the need to fine-tune the large VLA backbone directly. By performing reinforcement learning in a low-dimensional, DCT-compressed spectral noise subspace, it significantly reduces optimization complexity and promotes temporally smooth, physically plausible exploratory movements. This enhances sample efficiency, mitigates safety risks associated with direct high-dimensional action space exploration, and allows policies to adapt to real-world distribution shifts efficiently.

4. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The empirical results demonstrate that HAF-VLA exhibits stronger generalization capabilities against visual distractions and positional perturbations compared to baseline VLA models. When combined with HAF-Steer, the framework further improves robustness, enabling higher success rates in both in-distribution and challenging out-of-distribution real-world scenarios.

The potential impact of HAF lies in advancing the capabilities of humanoid robots to operate as general-purpose agents in human-centered environments. By improving the integration of vision, language, and action for whole-body control, HAF could enable humanoids to perform a wider array of complex household and industrial tasks, enhancing their utility and autonomy. The framework's modularity, with distinct components for structured generation and online refinement, provides a blueprint for future research in hierarchical control and latent-space reinforcement learning for embodied AI.

**Limitations:** The paper acknowledges that the hierarchical pipeline of HAF-VLA increases denoising computation, which can introduce deployment latency, though it remains within real-time limits for the reported experiments. Additionally, the latent RL module (HAF-Steer) is inherently constrained by the priors of the base VLA, meaning it might not be able to fully correct erroneous motions in extreme, unforeseen scenarios. Future work is suggested to explore more efficient denoising schemes and lightweight RL implementations to further enhance real-time performance and task robustness.
