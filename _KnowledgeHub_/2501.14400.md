# SKIL: Semantic Keypoint Imitation Learning for Generalizable Data-efficient Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SKIL introduces an imitation learning framework that leverages sparse, semantic keypoint representations derived from vision foundation models to achieve data-efficient and generalizable robotic manipulation. The framework demonstrated a mean success rate of 72.8% on unseen objects across six real-world tasks, significantly outperforming baselines, and enabled complex long-horizon tasks like hanging clothes with only 30 demonstrations.

## Problem

- Existing imitation learning approaches require high sample complexity, needing an unfeasibly large number of expert demonstrations for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- Current policies struggle to generalize to novel objects and unseen environments, often overfitting to specific training data.
- It is challenging for robots to perform complex, multi-stage, and precise manipulations efficiently with current end-to-end imitation learning methods.

## Method

- **SKIL** utilizes a ==Semantic Keypoints Description Module== that leverages Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) like DiFT and SAM to extract sparse, task-relevant 3D semantic keypoints from RGB-D inputs.
- A Policy Module, consisting of a Transformer encoder and a ==Diffusion Action Head==, processes these keypoint descriptors and the robot's state to predict continuous robot actions.
- An extended version, **SKIL-H**, enables ==cross-embodiment learning== by training a trajectory prediction module on human videos, then mapping these predicted keypoint trajectories to robot actions via a robot-specific module.

## Results

- SKIL achieved a mean success rate of **72.8%** on unseen objects across six real-world manipulation tasks, more than double the highest baseline (**30%**).
- It enabled complex long-horizon tasks like 'Hang Towel' and 'Hang Cloth' with only **30 demonstrations**, achieving **72%** and **47%** success rates, respectively, where baselines failed or showed very low performance.
- SKIL demonstrated superior data efficiency, often outperforming baselines with half the number of demonstrations, and exhibited remarkable robustness to unseen objects, spatial variations, and environmental changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Successful manipulation tasks often depend on sparse, semantic features of objects (e.g., a cup's handle, a towel's corner) rather than full, dense visual understanding.
> - Vision foundation models (VFMs) can effectively distill rich visual information into robust, embodiment-agnostic semantic keypoints, which are crucial for data-efficient and generalizable robotic control.
> - Leveraging these sparse semantic representations simplifies the observation space, leading to significantly improved data efficiency and strong generalization across object variations and environmental changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has made significant strides, particularly with the advent of imitation learning (IL), which allows robots to acquire skills by observing expert demonstrations. End-to-end policy learning, mapping visual inputs directly to actions, has gained traction due to its ability to handle complex, high-dimensional observations. However, a critical bottleneck for deploying IL in real-world scenarios, especially for complex, long-horizon tasks like garment manipulation or table rearrangement, remains the **high sample complexity** and **limited generalization capabilities**.

Existing approaches in imitation learning typically fall into two categories:
1. **Advancing Data Collection Methods:** Efforts have been made to create large, diverse datasets (e.g., Droid dataset, ALOHA datasets) or improve data collection efficiency through teleoperation or simulation. While these large datasets can improve generalization, achieving zero-shot generalization to truly novel objects and environments still often requires an exorbitant amount of task-specific demonstrations. For instance, some state-of-the-art methods might need tens of thousands of demonstrations for a single complex skill.
2. **Developing Advanced Representation Techniques:** Researchers have explored various forms of visual representations, including pre-trained vision foundation models (e.g., R3M, DINOv2), 3D visual representations (e.g., Act3D, DP3, RISE), object-centric representations, and semantic-geometric features (e.g., GenDP). These representations aim to improve sample efficiency and spatial generalization. However, a common limitation is their tendency to overfit to the specific objects and scenes encountered during training, leading to poor performance when faced with unseen variations. Dense representations, while rich in information, can also introduce redundancy that hinders learning efficiency.

**Keypoint-based imitation learning** has emerged as a promising direction due to its inherent sparsity and potential for data efficiency. Earlier works in this area often relied on supervised training for keypoint extraction or off-the-shelf trackers, which struggled with generalization to novel objects or maintaining accuracy over long horizons. More recent developments leverage advanced vision models (e.g., DINOv2, SAM, LLMs) to extract semantic correspondences or generate task-relevant keypoints, leading to frameworks like ReKep, KALM, and KAT. However, these methods often face challenges with precision, latency, or applicability to continuous, long-horizon motion planning, sometimes resorting to keyframe prediction rather than continuous action outputs.

SKIL positions itself directly to address these persistent challenges. It seeks to bridge the gap between the power of vision foundation models for robust semantic understanding and the need for data-efficient, generalizable, and long-horizon robotic manipulation. By distilling rich visual information into a sparse, semantic keypoint representation, SKIL aims to provide the "critical task-relevant information" while discarding irrelevant details that cause overfitting, thereby improving sample efficiency and generalization across object variations and environmental changes, and enabling complex, multi-stage tasks previously requiring immense datasets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of SKIL are to overcome the significant limitations of current imitation learning approaches, specifically:

1. **Reduce Sample Complexity:** Complex real-world manipulation tasks, especially long-horizon ones like hanging clothes, traditionally demand an unfeasibly large number of expert demonstrations. Collecting such vast datasets is time-consuming, costly, and often impractical. SKIL aims to significantly lower the data requirement, enabling robots to learn intricate skills from a very limited number of demonstrations (e.g., as few as 30 for long-horizon tasks).
2. **Enhance Generalization Capabilities:** Existing imitation learning policies often struggle to generalize to novel objects (e.g., different types of cups or towels) and unseen environments (e.g., new backgrounds, distractors) that deviate from their training data. The motivation is to develop a policy that is inherently robust to these variations, moving beyond mere spatial generalization to encompass object and environmental generalization.
3. **Enable Long-Horizon and Precise Manipulation:** Many practical tasks involve multiple sequential steps and require high precision, which are difficult for current end-to-end policies to learn efficiently. SKIL aims to enable robots to perform such complex, multi-stage, and precise actions reliably.
4. **Leverage Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) for Robotic Perception:** Recognize the remarkable success of recent VFMs in semantic correspondence and object segmentation. The motivation is to effectively integrate these powerful models into a robot learning framework to extract task-relevant semantic information, rather than relying on dense, raw pixel data.
5. **Facilitate Cross-Embodiment Learning:** Acknowledge that human demonstrations can provide valuable insights but are challenging to transfer directly to robots due to embodiment differences. SKIL aims to leverage its semantic keypoint abstraction to create a common representational space that allows for effective learning from diverse data sources, including unlabeled human videos, thereby further boosting data efficiency.

The core insight driving SKIL is that practical manipulation tasks often depend on sparse, semantic features (e.g., the handle of a cup, the collar of a shirt) rather than highly detailed visual properties (color, texture). By focusing on these consistent semantic keypoints, the problem's dimensionality is reduced, leading to more efficient learning and better generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SKIL (Semantic Keypoint Imitation Learning) is a novel framework designed to achieve data-efficient and generalizable manipulation by leveraging sparse, semantic keypoint representations. The framework consists of two primary modules: the Semantic Keypoints Description Module and the Policy Module. An extended version, SKIL-H, also supports cross-embodiment learning.

**Key Insight:**
The central idea is that successful manipulation tasks depend more on the semantic location of critical interaction points (e.g., a cup's handle, a towel's corner) rather than a full, dense visual understanding of an object's appearance. By focusing on these sparse semantic keypoints, SKIL simplifies the observation space, making the learning process more data-efficient and robust to visual variations. Vision foundation models are crucial for extracting these semantically consistent keypoints.

#### 3.1. Semantic Keypoints Description Module

This module is responsible for generating and describing the task-relevant semantic keypoints from the input vision data (RGB-D).

- **One-time Reference Features Generation:**
 - **Purpose:** To define the semantic features that will be tracked in subsequent frames.
 - **Process:** Given a single RGB reference image of the task scene, a vision foundation model (VFM) like DiFT (or alternatives like DINOv2, RADIO) extracts patch-wise features. Simultaneously, the Segment Anything Model (SAM) generates a mask for all relevant objects. The VFM's features are masked to focus on the object, and K-means clustering is applied to these masked features to identify `N` cluster centers. These cluster centers define the "reference keypoints" and their corresponding features form the "reference features" set (`F_r`). This step is performed only once per task.
- **Cosine-similarity Map Generation:**
 - **Purpose:** To find corresponding semantic keypoints in a new input image.
 - **Process:** For any subsequent input image (`I_t`) during training or inference, the same VFM extracts its feature map (`F_t`). A cosine-similarity map (`M_t`) is then computed between `F_t` and the pre-defined `F_r` (the reference features). This map indicates the semantic correspondence between pixels in the current image and the reference keypoints.
- **Keypoints Descriptor Calculation:**
 - **Purpose:** To create a rich, compact descriptor for each matched keypoint.
 - **Process:** For each of the `N` reference keypoints, its corresponding pixel location (`h_it`, `w_it`) in the current image `I_t` is found by identifying the pixel with the maximum cosine similarity in the `M_t` map. A descriptor (`χ_it`) for each matched keypoint is then constructed, comprising:
 - **Similarity Vector (`s_it`):** An `N`-dimensional vector representing the cosine similarities between the matched keypoint's features and *all* `N` reference keypoint features. This vector uniquely identifies the matched keypoint and indicates the confidence of the match.
 - **3D Coordinate Vector (`p_it`):** The 3D position (x, y, z) of the matched keypoint, derived from the depth image and camera intrinsics.
 - These `N` descriptors (`χ_1t`..., `χ_Nt`) are then fed into the policy module.

#### 3.2. Policy Module

This module takes the keypoint descriptors and robot state to predict robot actions.

- **Transformer Encoder:**
 - **Purpose:** To fuse information from all semantic keypoint descriptors.
 - **Process:** Each keypoint descriptor (`χ_it`) is tokenized and embedded into a latent space with positional encoding. A transformer encoder processes these tokens, and their mean is computed to obtain a single, fused embedding (`W_t`) representing the overall semantic state of the relevant objects.
- **Diffusion Action Head:**
 - **Purpose:** To output continuous robot actions based on the fused semantic embedding and robot state.
 - **Process:** The fused embedding `W_t` is concatenated with the current robot state `S_t` (e.g., joint positions, end-effector pose, gripper state) and passed through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to form a compact representation (`U_t`). This `U_t` then conditions a diffusion model (specifically, a CNN-based U-Net acting as a noise prediction network, similar to Diffusion Policy). The diffusion model predicts a chunk of future actions (`a_t:t+Ha`) in a single step, ensuring temporal consistency. DDIM is used for faster inference.
- **Action Ensemble:**
 - **Purpose:** To mitigate motion jitter caused by occasional keypoint mismatches from VFMs.
 - **Process:** During training, 20% of semantic keypoint tokens are randomly dropped out before being fed to the transformer encoder. During testing, the action inference process (with random dropout) is repeated 20 times, and the median of all resulting action sequences is chosen as the final executed action. This parallel ensemble adds negligible latency.

#### 3.3. Cross-embodiment Learning (SKIL-H)

This extended version of SKIL enables learning from human videos by leveraging the embodiment-agnostic nature of semantic keypoints.

- **Motivation:** Semantic keypoints describe task-relevant features independently of the specific robot or human embodiment.
- **Architecture:** SKIL-H consists of two modules:
 1. **Trajectory Prediction Module:** Takes the fused embedding `W_t` (from SKIL's Semantic Keypoints Description Module) as input and predicts future 3D keypoint trajectories (`τ_hat`) for a specified horizon. This module is implemented using a diffusion model and trained on a dataset containing both robot and human demonstrations. For human videos, keypoint trajectories are obtained using an off-the-shelf tracking model (e.g., CoTracker) and projected to 3D.
 2. **Trajectory-to-Action Module:** Maps these predicted keypoint trajectories (`τ_hat`) and the current robot state (`S_t`) to robot actions (`a_t:t+Ha`). This module also uses a transformer encoder followed by a diffusion action head, similar to the main SKIL policy module, but it is trained *only* on robot demonstrations.
This two-stage approach allows the trajectory prediction to benefit from abundant human video data, while the robot-specific action mapping focuses solely on robot demonstrations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive real-world and simulation experiments to evaluate SKIL's performance, focusing on its data efficiency, generalization capabilities, and the benefits of cross-embodiment learning.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware:** Franka robot arm with a Robotiq gripper, observed by a fixed Zed2 stereo camera (RGB-D observations).
- **Tasks:** Six real-world tasks were chosen:
 - **Short-horizon:** Pick Mouse, Grasp Handle of Cup, Grasp Wall of Cup, Fold Towel.
 - **Long-horizon:** Hang Towel, Hang Cloth (multi-step, complex manipulation).
 - Objects were randomly initialized in position and orientation.
- **Data Collection:** Human teleoperation for expert demonstrations (20 for short-horizon, 30 for long-horizon).
- **Objects for Training/Evaluation:** 2 training objects, 10 testing objects for short-horizon tasks; 3-5 testing objects for long-horizon tasks (testing objects were unseen).
- **Baselines:** State-of-the-art imitation learning algorithms: Diffusion Policy (DP), DP3 (3D representation), RISE (3D point clouds), GenDP-S (single-view 3D semantic fields).
- **Metrics:** Average success rate on unseen testing objects.

#### 4.2. Performance & Comparison

- **Overall Superiority:** SKIL significantly outperformed all baselines across all real-world tasks (Table I). It achieved a mean success rate of **72.8%** on unseen objects, which is more than double the highest baseline (30%).
- **Dramatic Improvement on Long-Horizon Tasks:** For the highly challenging long-horizon tasks, Hang Towel and Hang Cloth, SKIL achieved **72%** and **47%** success rates respectively, with only 30 demonstrations. In stark contrast, all baselines *failed completely* on Hang Towel (0% success) and showed very low performance on Hang Cloth. This highlights SKIL's exceptional ability to handle complex, multi-stage tasks with limited data.
- **Data Efficiency (Table III):** SKIL demonstrated superior data efficiency. With just 10 demonstrations, SKIL's performance often exceeded that of baselines using 20 demonstrations across various tasks. For instance, on "Pick Mouse," SKIL achieved 50% success with 10 demos, while DP and GenDP-S only reached 40% with 20 demos. This validates the claim that sparse semantic keypoints reduce the need for extensive data.

#### 4.3. Generalization Capabilities

- **Object Generalization:** SKIL maintained high performance on *unseen* objects within the same category (Table I). This is attributed to its semantic keypoint descriptors focusing on structural features (e.g., cup handles, towel corners) rather than superficial appearance details (color, texture), which are irrelevant for the task. Baselines, particularly those relying on dense visual features, showed significant performance drops on unseen objects.
- **Spatial Generalization:** SKIL effectively handled objects placed at the edges or corners of the workspace (e.g., Pick Mouse, Fold Towel), where baselines often failed. The accurate pose understanding from semantic keypoints facilitated more precise grasping.
- **Environment Generalization (Table II):** SKIL exhibited remarkable robustness to environmental changes (distractors, background shifts, and their combination). It maintained consistently high success rates (e.g., 80-90% on Grasp Handle of Cup in challenging situations) while baselines suffered substantial performance degradation, especially with combined changes. This demonstrates that SKIL's semantic representation is less susceptible to environmental noise.

#### 4.4. Cross-embodiment Performance (SKIL-H)

- Adding unlabeled human demonstration videos significantly improved SKIL-H's performance. For "Pick Mouse," 20 human demos led to a **40% increase** in success rate compared to training solely on 10 robot demos (Figure 8). This validates the effectiveness of semantic keypoint abstraction in enabling knowledge transfer from human to robot and further boosting data efficiency by leveraging diverse data sources.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Vision Foundation Models (VFMs):** DiFT (used in SKIL) generally outperformed DINOv2 and RADIO in terms of success rates (Figure 9, Table XI). DINOv2 struggled with keypoint mismatches and occlusions. RADIO models offered a good balance of performance and lower latency, providing flexibility for deployment. This confirms that SKIL's performance is sensitive to the quality of keypoints from the VFM.
- **Keypoint Numbers:** SKIL's performance was largely insensitive to the number of keypoints (`N`) between 10 and 30 (Table IV). This suggests that a relatively small, consistent set of semantic keypoints is sufficient for robust policy learning.
- **Keypoint Proposal Strategies:** The K-means clustering strategy for reference keypoint generation performed best among the tested methods (K-means, random selection, manual selection) (Table V). K-means provides a data-driven way to identify relevant and robust keypoints without human bias.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "SKIL: Semantic Keypoint Imitation Learning for Generalizable Data-efficient Manipulation" marks a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly for real-world manipulation tasks.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Addressing Core Challenges:** SKIL directly tackles the persistent bottlenecks of high sample complexity and limited generalization in imitation learning. By demonstrating robust performance across unseen objects, scenes, and complex, long-horizon tasks with remarkably few demonstrations, it pushes the boundaries of what is achievable in data-efficient robot learning.
- **Leveraging Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) Effectively:** The paper showcases an intelligent and practical integration of powerful, pre-trained VFMs (like DiFT, SAM) into a robot control pipeline. Instead of using dense, high-dimensional features that can lead to overfitting, SKIL distills critical semantic information into sparse keypoints, showcasing a more effective paradigm for VFM utilization in robotics.
- **Enabling Complex Real-World Tasks:** The success on long-horizon tasks like "Hang Towel" with minimal demonstrations is a major breakthrough. These tasks were previously either impossible for IL with limited data or required massive datasets (e.g., 10,000+ demos). SKIL's ability to reliably perform multi-stage, precise actions opens up new possibilities for automation in unstructured environments.
- **Advancing Cross-Embodiment Learning:** The SKIL-H variant demonstrates a viable path for leveraging human video data without explicit action labels. By abstracting task-relevant information into embodiment-agnostic semantic keypoints, the framework unlocks a vast, inexpensive source of demonstration data, accelerating robot skill acquisition. This is crucial for scaling up robot deployment.
- **Robustness to Real-World Variability:** The strong performance in the presence of distractors and background changes highlights SKIL's robustness, a critical requirement for robots operating outside controlled lab environments.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Accelerated Robot Deployment:** By drastically reducing the need for expensive and time-consuming data collection, SKIL could significantly lower the barrier to entry for deploying robots in various industries, from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and domestic assistance. Tasks that were once too costly or complex to automate may now become feasible.
- **More General-Purpose Robots:** The enhanced generalization capabilities enable robots to perform tasks on a wider variety of objects and in diverse environments without extensive retraining. This moves towards the vision of truly general-purpose robots that can adapt to novel situations, rather than being confined to highly specific, pre-programmed tasks.
- **New Research Directions:** SKIL's success with sparse semantic observations may inspire further research into alternative compact representations for robot learning, pushing beyond dense perception models. The cross-embodiment learning aspect also encourages exploring more sophisticated methods for leveraging diverse multimodal data for robot skill acquisition.
