# AdaWorld: Learning Adaptable World Models with Latent Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> AdaWorld introduces a novel approach to world model learning that extracts context-invariant latent actions from unlabeled videos, enabling efficient adaptation to new environments. This method achieves superior action transfer (e.g., 70.5% human success rate on LIBERO vs. 20% for baseline) and faster world model adaptation with limited data, improving visual planning success rates in game and robotic tasks.

## Problem

- World models typically require large amounts of action-labeled data for training and costly retraining for adaptation.
- Adapting existing world models to new environments with heterogeneous actions or limited interactions is computationally intensive.
- Current approaches are either action-agnostic (lack control, require extensive fine-tuning) or action-specific (poor generalization).

## Method

- A ==Latent Action Autoencoder (LAE)== extracts compact, context-invariant "latent actions" from unlabeled videos in a self-supervised manner.
- An autoregressive world model, initialized from ==Stable Video Diffusion==, is pretrained to predict future frames conditioned on these self-supervised latent actions.
- The LAE's ==information bottleneck== design, optimized with a ==beta-VAE loss==, forces the model to capture only critical action-related variations.

## Results

- Achieves superior action transfer to unseen contexts (e.g., **FVD of 767.0** vs. **1545.2** for action-agnostic baseline on LIBERO) and higher human success rates (**70.5%** vs. **20%**).
- Demonstrates more efficient world model adaptation, attaining higher PSNR and lower LPIPS with limited finetuning steps across diverse environments (Habitat, Minecraft, DMLab, nuScenes).
- Improves visual planning success rates in game environments (Procgen) and robot manipulation tasks (VP2 benchmark), often outperforming finetuned baselines without finetuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-like adaptability in new environments may stem from transferrable action representations learned from observations.
> - Disentangling action information from complex contextual details in "in-the-wild" videos is crucial for learning context-invariant action representations.
> - Incorporating action-awareness during large-scale pretraining, even without explicit action labels, leads to more adaptable and data-efficient world models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "AdaWorld" positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of **world models** for intelligent agents. World models are computational systems designed to learn action-controlled future prediction, enabling agents to simulate environments and make decisions purely in imagination. This capability is fundamental for developing versatile and efficient AI, particularly in areas like reinforcement learning, robotics, and autonomous systems.

**Current State and Limitations:**
Traditionally, world models, while promising, face significant hurdles. A major limitation is their reliance on **substantial action-labeled data** for training and **costly retraining** when adapting to novel environments. This is particularly challenging when new environments introduce "heterogeneous actions" or require adaptation through "limited interactions." Many existing world models are either:
- **Action-agnostic:** Pretrained on large video datasets to learn general visual dynamics (e.g., models based on pre-trained video models like those referenced from Xiang et al., Agarwal et al., He et al.). While these offer improved generalization, they lack direct action controllability and require extensive, expensive fine-tuning with specific action labels.
- **Action-specific:** Designed for particular environments with well-defined action spaces. Adapting these to new action formats or environments is computationally intensive and often requires re-collecting massive amounts of action-labeled data. Even approaches using pseudo-labels (e.g., Baker et al., Zhang et al.) struggle with defining a unified action format across diverse settings.

**AdaWorld's Contribution to the Landscape:**
AdaWorld directly addresses these critical limitations by proposing a novel paradigm: **action-aware pretraining without explicit action labels**. It leverages the observation that humans can infer action effects from limited observations and generalize them across contexts. AdaWorld aims to mimic this human-like adaptability by:
- **Self-supervised Latent Action Extraction:** Instead of relying on manual action annotations, AdaWorld learns "latent actions" directly from unlabeled videos. This moves beyond prior work that uses discrete latent actions for behavior cloning (e.g., Bruce et al., Chen et al., Ye et al.), by focusing on continuous latent actions for adaptable world modeling.
- **Integration with Powerful Generative Models:** By initializing its world model with Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), AdaWorld integrates state-of-the-art generative capabilities, allowing for high-fidelity future prediction. This contrasts with earlier world models that might struggle with visual realism or long-term coherence.
- **Bridging the Gap:** AdaWorld bridges the gap between scalable, action-agnostic video pretraining and the need for action-controlled, adaptable world models. It provides a means to inject action-awareness into large-scale pretraining in a data-efficient manner, leading to faster adaptation and better performance in unseen environments. This approach is highly relevant for the development of "generalist agents" capable of operating across diverse domains with minimal specialized training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an **innovative world model learning approach, named AdaWorld, that enables efficient adaptation** to novel environments. The core problem it seeks to overcome is the prevalent issue where existing world models demand substantial action-labeled data and costly training to adapt to new environments, especially those featuring heterogeneous actions or allowing only limited interactions. This limitation severely constrains their applicability across broader domains.

The motivation for AdaWorld stems from an observation of human intelligence:
- **Human-like Adaptability:** Humans can readily estimate the effects of various actions even with limited experiences. This capability is believed to originate from internal representations of actions learned from extensive observations, which can be flexibly reused and associated with specific action spaces across different contexts. This allows humans to easily transfer observed actions and imagine transitions in new environments with minimal exposure.
- **The Guiding Question:** Inspired by these human capabilities, the authors ponder: "can we achieve human-like adaptability in world modeling by learning transferrable action representations from observations?"

To achieve this overarching goal, AdaWorld focuses on two specific manifestations of adaptability:
1. **Efficient Action Transfer:** To enable the world model to readily transfer a demonstrated action from one context to another without requiring further training. This means that given a single example of an action, the model should be able to apply that action in various novel scenes.
2. **Efficient World Model Adaptation:** To allow the world model to be efficiently adapted into specialized models that can take raw action inputs (both discrete and continuous) with only minor interactions and minimal finetuning. This would significantly reduce the cost and data requirements for deploying world models in new, controlled environments.

The main technical challenge identified is the difficulty of disentangling and effectively recognizing action information from complex, "in-the-wild" videos where actions are often entangled with contextual details (e.g., colors, textures). AdaWorld aims to overcome this by designing a mechanism to extract the most critical action-related variations from video frames.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AdaWorld's methodology is built upon two core, interlinked components: a latent action autoencoder and an autoregressive world model, trained through an "action-aware pretraining" paradigm.

#### 3.1. Latent Action Autoencoder (LAE)

The LAE is the foundational component for extracting action information from unlabeled videos in a self-supervised manner.
- **Purpose:** To learn a compact, context-invariant representation of actions, termed "latent actions," which encapsulate the essential transitions between video frames. This replaces the need for explicit, manually curated action labels.
- **Architecture:** It is based on the Transformer architecture.
 - **Encoder:** Takes two consecutive frames, $f_t$ and $f_{t+1}$, as input. It divides them into image patches, projects them to embeddings, and concatenates them with learnable tokens. A spatio-temporal Transformer processes these tokens, using interleaved spatial and temporal attention. The temporal attention incorporates rotary embeddings to capture causal relationships. Critically, after processing, only one learnable token ($a_{t+1}$) is projected to estimate the posterior of the latent action ($\mu_{\tilde{a}}, \sigma_{\tilde{a}}$), following a VAE (Variational Autoencoder) structure. The latent action $\tilde{a}$ is then sampled from this posterior.
 - **Decoder:** Receives the sampled latent action $\tilde{a}$ and the former frame $f_t$. It is a spatial Transformer that attempts to predict the subsequent frame $f_{t+1}$ in pixel space.
- **Information Bottleneck Design:** This is the key innovation for disentanglement. The latent action $\tilde{a}$ is designed to be extremely compact. By forcing the decoder to predict the *entire* subsequent frame $f_{t+1}$ using only the minimal information in $\tilde{a}$ and $f_t$, the autoencoder is compelled to compress only the *most critical variations* (i.e., the action) from $f_t$ to $f_{t+1}$, while largely ignoring contextual details. This encourages context-invariant action representations.
- **$\beta$-VAE Objective:** The LAE is optimized using a $\beta$-VAE loss. The hyperparameter $\beta$ provides flexible control over the information contained in the latent actions. A carefully chosen $\beta$ ensures a good trade-off between the latent action's expressiveness (ability to represent diverse transitions) and its context-disentangling ability (ensuring it primarily captures action, not scene details). The latent actions are compressed into a continuous latent space, enhancing expressiveness and enabling flexible composition, unlike discrete latent action approaches.

#### 3.2. Autoregressive World Model (Action-Aware Pretraining)

Once the LAE is trained, its encoder is used to automatically extract latent actions from large volumes of unlabeled videos. These extracted latent actions then serve as conditions for pretraining the main world model.
- **Purpose:** To learn to predict future frames conditioned on these self-supervisedly extracted latent actions, enabling fine-grained, frame-level control.
- **Architecture:** The world model is built upon **Stable Video Diffusion (SVD)**, a latent diffusion model, initialized from its pretrained weights. This leverages the powerful generative capabilities of SVD.
- **Conditioning:** The latent action $\tilde{a}$ is deeply integrated into the diffusion process by concatenating it with both the timestep embedding and the CLIP image embedding (from the original SVD architecture). The last frame in the model's "short-term memory" (up to $K=6$ historical frames) serves as the condition image for SVD. Historical frames are encoded using the SVD image encoder and concatenated with the noise latent map. Noise augmentation is also applied to historical frames during training to mitigate long-term drift.
- **Autoregressive Rollout:** At inference, the model can generate a sequence of future frames by repeatedly predicting the next frame and appending it to its short-term memory, thereby enabling dynamic simulation.
- **Training Data:** AdaWorld is pretrained on a massive dataset of approximately 2000 million frames, collected from a diverse range of interactive scenarios. This includes publicly available datasets (Open X-Embodiment, Ego4D, Something-Something v2, MiraData) and automatically generated gameplay videos from 1000+ environments in Gym Retro and Procgen Benchmark. A "biased action sampling strategy" is used during data generation to encourage broader exploration and scene diversity.

#### 3.3. Highly Adaptable World Models (Applications)

The unique pretraining paradigm enables AdaWorld to be highly adaptable for various applications:
- **Efficient Action Transfer:** Given a demonstration video, its latent action encoder extracts a sequence of latent actions. These context-invariant latent actions can then be directly reused as conditions to generate a new video in a completely different target context using a new initial frame, *without any additional training*.
- **Efficient World Model Adaptation:** When a new environment has a specific, limited set of action labels (e.g., from a few interactions):
 - For **discrete actions**: The latent actions corresponding to each action label are extracted and averaged. These averaged latent actions are then used to initialize the control interface of the world model. The entire model is then finetuned for a minimal number of steps.
 - For **continuous actions**: A lightweight MLP is trained to map the raw continuous action inputs to the continuous latent action space. This MLP is finetuned with minimal action-latent action pairs.
- **Action Composition and Creation:** The continuity of the latent action space allows for novel applications like composing new actions by interpolating (e.g., averaging) existing latent actions (e.g., combining "jump" and "right" to get "jump right"). Additionally, by clustering collected latent actions, flexible and controllable sets of new actions can be identified and utilized.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AdaWorld demonstrates superior performance across various tasks, highlighting its adaptability and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Action Transfer

- **Qualitative (Figure 4, Figure 10):** AdaWorld effectively disentangles demonstrated actions from their context and accurately emulates them in diverse target scenes (e.g., changing colors, object arrangements, or entire environments) without further training. Baselines (action-agnostic, optical flow, discrete latent actions) often fail to maintain action fidelity or generalize across contexts.
- **Quantitative (Table 1):** Tested on unseen LIBERO (robot manipulation) and Something-Something v2 (human activities) datasets.
 - AdaWorld significantly outperforms all baselines in Fréchet Video Distance (FVD, lower is better), Embedding Cosine Similarity (ECS, higher is better), and Human Evaluation (higher success rate). For example, on LIBERO, AdaWorld achieved FVD of 767.0 compared to 1545.2 for action-agnostic baseline, and a human success rate of 70.5% versus 20%.
 - This confirms that the continuous latent action space, combined with action-aware pretraining, enables more nuanced and generalizable action representation compared to alternatives.

#### 4.2. World Model Adaptation (Simulation Quality)

- **Performance (Table 2, Figure 11):** Evaluated on four unseen environments (Habitat, Minecraft, DMLab for discrete actions; nuScenes for continuous actions) after limited finetuning (100 samples per action/trajectory, 800 steps).
 - AdaWorld consistently achieves the highest PSNR (higher fidelity) and lowest LPIPS (better perceptual quality) across all environments.
 - All action-aware variants (AdaWorld, Flow cond., Discrete cond.) significantly outperform the action-agnostic baseline, underscoring the importance of incorporating action information during pretraining.
- **Efficiency (Figure 6):** PSNR curves show that AdaWorld adapts much more rapidly and achieves significantly better performance from the outset, even with very limited samples and finetuning steps (e.g., 200 steps), compared to conventional action-agnostic pretraining. This suggests a superior initialization for adaptation.

#### 4.3. Visual Planning in Games

- **Performance (Table 3):** Evaluated on goal-reaching tasks in four Procgen environments (Heist, Jumper, Maze, CaveFlyer) using sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC).
 - AdaWorld significantly increases success rates across all environments compared to random planning and the action-agnostic baseline.
 - Notably, AdaWorld *without finetuning* (only using averaged latent actions) still outperforms the *finetuned* action-agnostic baseline, highlighting its strong zero-shot transfer ability.
 - AdaWorld also significantly dominates Q-learning, demonstrating its ability to make more effective use of limited interactions for planning.

#### 4.4. Visual Planning in Robot Tasks

- **Performance (Table 4):** Evaluated on the VP2 benchmark (Robosuite and RoboDesk tasks) using Model-Predictive Path Integral (MPPI).
 - AdaWorld shows a clear margin of improvement in success rates over the action-agnostic baseline, even with limited finetuning steps (1K steps). For instance, in 'Robosuite push', AdaWorld achieved 63.50% success compared to 17.50% for the baseline.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Interface Initialization (Figure 6):** Initializing AdaWorld's control interface with averaged latent actions (from limited samples) is superior to random initialization, but even with random initialization, AdaWorld quickly surpasses the action-agnostic baseline, validating its adaptable control interface learned during pretraining.
- **Data Diversity (Table 5):** Increasing the diversity of pretraining data (e.g., adding real-world robot videos from Open X-Embodiment to game data) enhances the generalization ability of latent actions, even to unseen 2D virtual games.
- **Method Generality (Table 6):** Applying the action-aware pretraining concept to `iVideoGPT` (a state-of-the-art autoregressive Transformer world model) significantly improved its adaptability, demonstrating that AdaWorld's core idea is broadly applicable to different world model architectures.
- **Hyperparameter $\beta$ (Figure 7):** UMAP visualization confirms that the $\beta$-VAE effectively disentangles action from context, clustering similar actions from different environments together. The parameter $\beta$ allows tuning between expressiveness and disentanglement.
- **Action Composition/Creation (Figure 5, Table 9):** The continuous latent action space allows for semantic interpolation of actions (e.g., averaging "jump" and "right" to get "jump right"). Furthermore, clustering latent actions can generate a customizable number of distinct and controllable actions, demonstrating flexibility not available with fixed discrete action spaces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AdaWorld introduces a significant advancement in the field of world models and embodied AI, potentially ushering in a new paradigm for their pretraining and adaptation.

**Key Significance:**
- **Overcoming Data Bottlenecks:** The most profound impact of AdaWorld lies in its ability to circumvent the traditional reliance on vast amounts of *action-labeled* data and costly retraining. By extracting action information in a self-supervised manner through latent actions from *unlabeled* videos, it drastically reduces the data collection and annotation burden, making world models more practical and scalable for diverse applications.
- **Enhanced Adaptability and Generalization:** AdaWorld delivers genuinely adaptable world models capable of:
 - **Zero-shot action transfer:** The ability to take a single demonstration and apply that action in completely new, unseen contexts without any further training is a powerful step towards truly generalist agents.
 - **Sample-efficient adaptation:** The rapid and superior performance with limited interaction data and finetuning steps signifies a major leap in the efficiency of deploying world models in new environments, from games to robotics.
 - **Flexible action representation:** The use of a continuous latent action space, rather than discrete, fixed actions, opens up possibilities for action composition, interpolation, and the creation of novel, fine-grained control options. This allows for more nuanced and customizable agent behaviors.
- **New Pretraining Paradigm:** AdaWorld demonstrates that integrating action-awareness directly into the pretraining phase, even without explicit labels, is crucial for building robust and adaptable world models. This challenges the conventional action-agnostic video pretraining approach by showing the benefits of self-supervised action signal integration.
- **Broad Applicability:** The method's generality, as shown by its successful integration with and improvement of other state-of-the-art world models like `iVideoGPT`, suggests that the core principles of AdaWorld could be widely adopted across various world model architectures.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerating Embodied AI Research:** By lowering the barrier to entry for training and adapting world models, AdaWorld can significantly accelerate research in robotics, virtual agents, and other embodied AI domains. Researchers can focus more on agent intelligence and less on data collection and model retraining.
- **More Capable Generalist Agents:** The ability to transfer and rapidly learn new actions is fundamental for developing generalist AI agents that can operate across a multitude of tasks and environments, mimicking human-like flexibility. This moves towards agents that are not just task-specific but broadly competent.
- **Foundation for Advanced Simulation:** World models are essentially simulators. AdaWorld's improvements in simulation quality and planning open doors for creating more realistic and controllable virtual environments that can be used for synthetic data generation, policy learning, and even interactive content creation.
- **Real-world Applications:** In areas like autonomous driving, complex robotic manipulation, or even interactive gaming, where dynamic environments and varied actions are the norm, AdaWorld's adaptable nature could lead to more robust and deployable AI systems.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, which are common challenges in the field:
- **Real-time operation:** AdaWorld currently does not operate at real-time frequency, an important factor for interactive and robotic applications. Future work may explore distillation or sampling techniques.
- **Novel content generation:** Like many generative models, AdaWorld struggles to create genuinely novel content when rollouts extend beyond the initial scene. Scaling models and training data might alleviate this.
- **Long-term rollouts:** The model's ability for extremely long-term rollouts is limited, an area for future research focusing on strategies to maintain coherence over extended predictions.
- **Physics and Dynamics:** AdaWorld is not perfect in simulating real-world physics and complex dynamics, particularly with dramatic view shifts.

Despite these limitations, AdaWorld represents a significant step forward in making world models more practical, efficient, and broadly applicable, paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable AI systems.
