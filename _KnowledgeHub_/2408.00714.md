# SAM 2: Segment Anything in Images and Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta FAIR developed SAM 2, a foundation model extending 'segment anything' capabilities to both images and videos. This model demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across promptable image and video segmentation benchmarks, achieved with 3x fewer user interactions for video and a 6x faster image encoder than its predecessor, leveraging the newly collected, 53x larger Segment Anything Video (SA-V) dataset.

## Problem

- Existing segmentation models were primarily designed for static images, lacking generalizability to the dynamic and complex nature of video content.
- Previous video segmentation approaches were specialized (e.g., for specific object categories or semi-supervised tracking) and lacked a unified capability for segmenting 'anything' in an interactive, open-world setting.
- A significant barrier was the absence of large-scale, diverse, and open-world datasets specifically tailored for general video segmentation, hindering the training of foundation models.

## Method

- **SAM 2** introduces a unified model architecture that generalizes ==promptable segmentation== from images to videos, incorporating a fast ==Hiera image encoder== and a novel ==Memory Attention== component for temporal coherence.
- A key component is the iterative, ==human-in-the-loop data engine== that efficiently collected the large-scale and diverse Segment Anything Video (SA-V) dataset.
- The model is trained jointly on a mix of image and video datasets, including SA-1B and SA-V, using a ==simulated interactive prompting== strategy.

## Results

- The iterative data engine achieved an **8.4x** speed-up in video annotation, contributing to the creation of the Segment Anything Video (SA-V) dataset, which is **53x** larger than prior video segmentation datasets.
- SAM 2 achieved superior segmentation accuracy in interactive video settings using over **3x** fewer user interactions compared to leading baselines (SAM+XMem++ and SAM+Cutie).
- The model demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in semi-supervised Video Object Segmentation (VOS) across **17** diverse datasets and also improved image segmentation accuracy (**58.9%** 1-click mIoU vs. SAM's **58.1%**) while being **6x** faster.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single, unified foundation model can effectively handle promptable segmentation across both static images and dynamic video content.
> - Developing a large-scale, diverse dataset through an efficient, iterative human-in-the-loop data engine is essential for training robust general-purpose video segmentation models.
> - Integrating temporal memory and interactive refinement mechanisms into the model architecture significantly improves efficiency and accuracy for video segmentation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

SAM 2 represents a significant advancement in the field of computer vision, specifically in visual segmentation. It extends the concept of "foundation models" – large, pre-trained models capable of performing a wide range of downstream tasks – from static images to the more complex domain of video.

**Building on Foundation Models:** The original Segment Anything Model (SAM) established a new paradigm for image segmentation by demonstrating that a single, large model, trained on a vast and diverse dataset (SA-1B), could perform "zero-shot" or "promptable" segmentation of *any* object in an image. SAM 2 directly inherits and expands this vision into the temporal dimension. By treating an image as a single-frame video, SAM 2 provides a unified framework for both image and video segmentation, addressing a critical need for a universal visual perception system.

**Advancing Video Segmentation:** Prior to SAM 2, video segmentation tasks were often tackled by specialized models with distinct limitations:
- **Interactive Video Object Segmentation (iVOS):** These methods aimed to efficiently obtain object segmentations with user guidance (clicks, scribbles). However, many adopted modular designs (e.g., converting user inputs to masks, then propagating) and lacked a mechanism for truly interactive refinement of model mistakes across frames with memory of past interactions. SAM 2 addresses this by building a strong unified model that directly takes prompts for interactive video segmentation and uses streaming memory.
- **Semi-supervised Video Object Segmentation (VOS):** This traditional task involves tracking an object throughout a video given an initial mask in the first frame. While many VOS models have achieved high accuracy, they are often designed for specific object categories or struggle with the "segment anything" capability for diverse objects, parts, and complex scenarios like occlusions. SAM 2 shows superior performance in VOS as a special case of its broader "Promptable Visual Segmentation" (PVS) task, validating its robustness across different prompting styles (clicks, boxes, masks).

**Data-Centric AI:** A key bottleneck for developing general video segmentation models has been the lack of large, diverse, and open-world video segmentation datasets. Existing datasets are often limited in size, object diversity, or focus on whole objects rather than parts. SAM 2's success is heavily reliant on its **data engine**, which collected the unprecedentedly large and diverse "Segment Anything Video (SA-V)" dataset. This data-centric approach, where model and data are iteratively improved via human interaction, mirrors the strategy used for SA-1B and underscores the growing importance of high-quality, large-scale data in training robust foundation models. This positions SAM 2 at the forefront of a trend where data collection becomes an integral part of the research contribution itself.

In essence, SAM 2 unifies fragmented segmentation tasks under a single, powerful foundation model, addressing limitations of prior approaches in both model generality and data availability, thereby setting a new standard for promptable visual segmentation in dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop **Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM 2)**, positioned as a **foundation model** capable of solving **promptable visual segmentation (PVS)** in both images and videos. This ambitious goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the Static-Dynamic Gap:** While the original SAM successfully addressed segmentation in static images, the real world is dynamic. Visual segments exhibit complex motion, deformation, and occlusion. The rapid growth of multimedia content, predominantly video, necessitates a segmentation system that can handle this temporal dimension.
2. **Addressing Application Needs:** Many critical real-world applications in domains such as Augmented/Virtual Reality (AR/VR), robotics, autonomous vehicles, and professional video editing demand accurate spatio-temporal localization of objects beyond static image segmentation. A universal visual segmentation system applicable to both images and videos would be transformative for these areas.
3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Video Segmentation:** Existing video segmentation models and datasets fall short of providing a "segment anything in videos" capability. They often struggle with:
 - **Appearance Changes:** Objects in video undergo significant changes due to motion, deformation, occlusion, and lighting.
 - **Video Quality:** Videos can have lower quality (blur, motion, resolution) compared to carefully captured images.
 - **Efficiency:** Processing a large number of frames in real-time is a significant challenge.
 - **Dataset Scope:** Current video datasets typically lack sufficient coverage for "any object," focusing on specific categories or whole objects rather than parts, and are limited in scale.
 - **Interactive Refinement:** Previous interactive video object segmentation (iVOS) methods often lack robust mechanisms for interactively refining model mistakes across time with memory.

To achieve its objective, SAM 2 set out to deliver three core components:
- **A New Task Definition:** Generalize image segmentation to the video domain by defining the Promptable Visual Segmentation (PVS) task, which accepts points, boxes, or masks on *any* frame to define and refine spatio-temporal masks (masklets).
- **A Unified Model Architecture:** Develop a single model that processes both images (as single-frame videos) and multi-frame videos efficiently, leveraging temporal memory for coherence and interactive refinement.
- **A Large-Scale, Diverse Dataset:** Create the largest video segmentation dataset to date, "Segment Anything Video (SA-V)," through an innovative data engine, which is not restricted to specific object categories but covers any valid boundary, including parts and subparts.

Ultimately, the motivation is to create a foundational perception capability that can seamlessly and interactively segment any visual entity across both static and dynamic visual content, thereby enabling a new generation of AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology behind SAM 2 is multifaceted, integrating innovations in model architecture, a novel data collection paradigm, and a comprehensive training strategy.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture: Generalizing SAM to Video

SAM 2 is designed as a natural generalization of the original SAM, extending its promptable segmentation capabilities to the video domain while maintaining efficiency. The core architecture incorporates a streaming approach for real-time video processing.

- **Image Encoder:** A critical component is the MAE-pre-trained Hiera (Hierarchical Vision Transformer) image encoder. This encoder processes each video frame once, providing unconditioned feature embeddings. Its hierarchical nature allows for multiscale features, crucial for detailed mask decoding. Importantly, SAM 2's encoder is optimized for speed, being 6x faster than SAM's while maintaining accuracy.
- **Memory Attention:** This novel component is central to SAM 2's video processing capabilities. It consists of stacked transformer blocks that condition the current frame's features on information from past frames and previous interactions. This is achieved through cross-attention to a "memory bank" which stores representations of previously observed frames and target object "pointers."
- **Prompt Encoder and Mask Decoder:** The prompt encoder is identical to SAM's, accepting point, box, or mask prompts. The mask decoder largely follows SAM's design but includes key adaptations for video:
 - It utilizes skip connections from the hierarchical image encoder to integrate high-resolution embeddings, improving mask detail.
 - A novel "occlusion prediction head" is added, allowing the model to predict whether the object of interest is present and visible in the current frame. This is crucial for handling objects that disappear and reappear in videos.
 - While predicting multiple masks for ambiguous prompts (like SAM), SAM 2 also propagates the highest-predicted IoU mask across video frames if ambiguity persists.
- **Memory Encoder and Memory Bank:** The memory encoder generates "memories" by fusing the predicted mask (downsampled) with the unconditioned image encoder features. These memories are stored in a "memory bank," a FIFO queue that retains information from a fixed number of recent frames and also from prompted frames. Additionally, lightweight "object pointer" vectors, derived from the mask decoder's output tokens, capture high-level semantic information about the target object and are cross-attended to by the memory attention module. Temporal positional encodings are embedded into memories of recent frames to model short-term motion.

#### 3.2. Data Engine: Building the SA-V Dataset

Recognizing that a general "segment anything in videos" capability requires a significantly different and larger dataset than existing ones, the authors developed an iterative human-in-the-loop "data engine" to collect the Segment Anything Video (SA-V) dataset.

- **Iterative Refinement Phases:** The data engine evolved through three phases, progressively increasing model assistance and efficiency:
 1. **Phase 1 (SAM per frame):** Initial annotation using image-based SAM frame-by-frame. Slow (37.8 s/frame) but high-quality spatial masks.
 2. **Phase 2 (SAM + SAM 2 Mask):** Introduced SAM 2 (mask-only prompt version) for temporal propagation. Annotators still needed to re-annotate from scratch if the model erred. Achieved 5.1x speedup (7.4 s/frame).
 3. **Phase 3 (SAM 2):** Full SAM 2 with point/mask prompts and memory context, allowing annotators to provide occasional refinement clicks instead of full re-annotations. Achieved 8.4x speedup over Phase 1 (4.5 s/frame) while maintaining comparable quality.
- **Quality Verification:** A dedicated verification step ensured high annotation standards, with unsatisfactory masklets sent back for refinement.
- **Automated Masklet Generation:** To increase diversity and identify model failure modes, SAM 2 was prompted with a regular grid of points to generate candidate masklets. Satisfactory "auto" masklets were added to SA-V, while unsatisfactory ones were prioritized for human refinement, creating a virtuous cycle for data collection.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

SAM 2 is trained jointly on image and video data using a simulated interactive prompting strategy.
- **Data Mix:** The model is pre-trained on the large SA-1B image dataset, then fine-tuned on a comprehensive mix including the SA-V dataset (manual annotations only), an internal licensed video dataset, and existing open-source VOS datasets (DAVIS, MOSE, YouTubeVOS).
- **Alternating Training:** An alternating strategy samples batches from either image or video datasets based on their proportional sizes, ensuring balanced exposure.
- **Simulated Interaction:** Training involves simulating interactive scenarios, sampling 8-frame sequences (and 16-frame for fine-tuning), and providing probabilistic corrective clicks based on ground truth and model predictions.
- **Loss Functions:** A combination of focal and dice losses for mask prediction, MAE loss for IoU prediction, and cross-entropy for the new occlusion prediction head.

This integrated approach, leveraging advanced model architecture, an efficient data collection pipeline, and a tailored training methodology, allows SAM 2 to achieve its goal of universal promptable visual segmentation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence for SAM 2's effectiveness across multiple segmentation tasks, supported by the novel SA-V dataset and efficient data engine.

#### 4.1. Data Engine Efficiency and Dataset Impact

- **Annotation Speed:** The iterative data engine, particularly Phase 3 with SAM 2 in the loop, dramatically improved annotation efficiency. It achieved an average annotation time of **4.5 seconds per frame**, an **8.4x speed-up** compared to the initial per-frame manual annotation (Phase 1). This efficiency gain was accompanied by comparable or improved mask alignment scores.
- **SA-V Dataset Scale and Diversity:** The resulting Segment Anything Video (SA-V) dataset is the largest video segmentation dataset to date, comprising **50.9K videos with 642.6K masklets**, translating to **35.5 million masks**. This is a **53x increase** in masks compared to the largest existing VOS dataset (15x without automatically generated masks). SA-V is designed to be challenging, featuring small objects, parts, and objects undergoing occlusions and re-appearances. Its videos are geographically diverse and exhibit minimal performance discrepancy based on perceived gender or age in fairness evaluations.
- **Data Quality Impact:** Ablation studies demonstrated a consistent **power-law relationship** between the quantity of SA-V training data and video segmentation accuracy. Furthermore, training on the full mix of VOS, SA-1B, and the newly collected SA-V/Internal data consistently yielded the best performance across diverse benchmarks.

#### 4.2. Promptable Video Segmentation (Interactive Setting)

- **Superior Accuracy and Efficiency:** In simulated interactive settings (both offline and online evaluation), SAM 2 significantly **outperformed strong baselines (SAM+XMem++ and SAM+Cutie)** across 9 densely annotated zero-shot video datasets. SAM 2 achieved **better segmentation accuracy using over 3x fewer interactions** (clicks) than prior approaches, demonstrating a substantial improvement in user experience for interactive video annotation.

#### 4.3. Semi-supervised Video Object Segmentation (VOS)

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** When evaluated in the conventional semi-supervised VOS setting (prompted only on the first frame with clicks, bounding boxes, or ground-truth masks), SAM 2 delivered **state-of-the-art accuracy** across 17 diverse video datasets. It consistently **outperformed specialized VOS methods** like XMem++ and Cutie, even with click or box inputs (which SAM 2 handles directly, unlike the baselines which require SAM as a pre-processor). This highlights SAM 2's robustness and generality beyond its primary interactive use case.
- **Strong on SA-V Benchmarks:** SAM 2 showed particularly significant gains on the newly introduced SA-V validation and test sets (e.g., 76.8 J&F on SA-V val for Hiera-B+ with ground-truth mask, compared to 61.3 for Cutie-base+), reinforcing its "segment anything" capability on open-world, challenging segments.

#### 4.4. Image Segmentation

- **Faster and More Accurate than SAM:** SAM 2 showed improved performance on the original Segment Anything (SA) task. When trained on the SA-1B dataset alone, SAM 2 (Hiera-B+) achieved **higher 1-click mIoU (58.9%) than the original SAM (58.1%)** on the 23 SA evaluation datasets, while being **6x faster**. This efficiency gain is attributed to its optimized Hiera image encoder.
- **Enhanced with Mixed Data:** Training SAM 2 on the full mix of image and video data further boosted its image segmentation accuracy to **61.9% mIoU** on the 23 SA datasets, and showed exceptional gains on video datasets evaluated as images.

#### 4.5. Speed and Efficiency

- SAM 2 operates at **real-time speeds** in video segmentation, achieving **43.8 FPS** (Hiera-B+) and **30.2 FPS** (Hiera-L) on a single A100 GPU, demonstrating its practicality for real-world applications.

Overall, the findings strongly support the claim that SAM 2 is a unified, highly accurate, and efficient foundation model for promptable visual segmentation across both images and videos, largely due to its innovative architecture and the comprehensive SA-V dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SAM 2 represents a **significant milestone** in visual perception, extending the powerful paradigm of foundation models into the dynamic domain of video. Its significance and potential impact are profound across several dimensions:

1. **Unified Visual Segmentation:** The most critical impact is the unification of promptable image segmentation and video object segmentation into a single, cohesive foundation model. This simplifies the development of downstream applications by providing a consistent interface for diverse visual content. Instead of maintaining separate models for static and dynamic segmentation, developers can leverage SAM 2 for a wide array of tasks.

2. **Revolutionizing Interactive Video Annotation:** The model's demonstrated ability to achieve higher accuracy with significantly fewer user interactions (3x fewer clicks than prior approaches) is a game-changer for interactive video object segmentation. This dramatically reduces the human effort and time required for high-quality video annotation, accelerating data labeling for other AI tasks, and making advanced video editing tools more accessible and efficient. The release of the interactive online demo underscores its practical applicability.

3. **Open-World Video Understanding:** By moving beyond category-specific segmentation and excelling at segmenting "anything" with a valid boundary, including parts and subparts, SAM 2 pushes the boundaries of open-world video understanding. This capability is crucial for applications that encounter novel objects or require fine-grained segmentation in unconstrained environments.

4. **Driving Future Research with SA-V Dataset:** The release of the Segment Anything Video (SA-V) dataset, being 53 times larger than any existing video segmentation dataset and specifically designed for open-world, promptable segmentation, is a monumental contribution to the research community. It provides an unprecedented resource for training and benchmarking future video perception models, enabling the development of more general, robust, and fair algorithms. The iterative data engine methodology itself offers a blueprint for how large-scale, high-quality datasets can be efficiently constructed for foundation models.

5. **Enabling New Applications:** The capabilities of SAM 2 have direct and transformative potential across numerous industries:
 - **AR/VR:** Enhanced spatial awareness and realistic content manipulation.
 - **Robotics:** Improved object manipulation, navigation, and human-robot interaction in dynamic environments.
 - **Autonomous Vehicles:** More robust object detection and tracking for safer navigation and decision-making.
 - **Content Creation:** Streamlined video editing, rotoscoping, and visual effects workflows.
 - **Medical Imaging:** More precise spatio-temporal segmentation of anatomical structures or biological processes in medical videos.
 - **Surveillance and Security:** Advanced capabilities for tracking objects or individuals in complex video feeds.

6. **Fostering Open Science and Innovation:** Meta FAIR's commitment to releasing the SAM 2 model checkpoints, the SA-V dataset (under CC BY 4.0), and the training/demo code (under Apache 2.0) is pivotal. This open approach encourages widespread adoption, experimentation, and further innovation within the academic and industrial research communities, democratizing access to cutting-edge AI capabilities.

In summary, SAM 2 not only achieves state-of-the-art performance by extending a foundational AI model to video but also sets a new standard for data collection and model unification, paving the way for a more comprehensive and interactive understanding of the dynamic visual world.
