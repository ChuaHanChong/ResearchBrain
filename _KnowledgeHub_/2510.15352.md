# GaussGym: An open-source real-to-sim framework for learning locomotion from pixels

> [!summary] Summary
> GAUSSGYM is an open-source real-to-sim framework that integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting with vectorized physics simulators to enable high-throughput, photorealistic simulation for robot locomotion. It allows policies to learn directly from RGB pixel data and transfer effectively to the real world, addressing the visual sim-to-real gap.

## Problem

- Robot learning policies often rely on geometric sensors (depth, LiDAR), leading to a 'visual sim-to-real gap' as they miss crucial semantic information from RGB images.
- Current simulation environments struggle to provide both photorealistic visual fidelity and the high throughput required for efficient reinforcement learning.
- Generating diverse and realistic training environments from various sources (real-world scans, generative models) efficiently remains a challenge.

## Method

- GAUSSGYM integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for photorealistic, differentiable rendering with vectorized physics simulators like IsaacGym, achieving high fidelity and high throughput simultaneously.
- The framework processes diverse 3D environment data, including smartphone scans and outputs from generative video models, into aligned collision meshes and 3D Gaussian Splats.
- It employs an asymmetric actor-critic reinforcement learning pipeline with a recurrent encoder that fuses visual (DinoV2-extracted features) and proprioceptive streams, along with an auxiliary voxel prediction head.

## Results

- Policies trained on RGB pixel inputs within GAUSSGYM successfully learned complex locomotion behaviors, such as stair climbing, and demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a real Unitree A1 robot.
- An RGB-trained policy successfully avoided a visually distinct 'penalty region' in a navigation task, while a depth-only policy failed to detect and avoid it.
- Ablation studies confirmed that training on a large number of diverse scenes and incorporating the auxiliary voxel prediction head significantly improved learning speed and performance across different robot types and terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combination of 3DGS and vectorized physics allows for an unprecedented throughput exceeding 100,000 simulator steps per second across thousands of photorealistic environments.
> - Integrating outputs from generative video models like Veo enables the creation of diverse and 'beyond reality' training environments, significantly expanding the available scene library.
> - Policies trained with direct RGB inputs can learn semantic reasoning, such as identifying and avoiding visually marked regions, which is not possible with depth-only sensing.
