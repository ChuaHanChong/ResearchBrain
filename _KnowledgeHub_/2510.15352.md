# GaussGym: An open-source real-to-sim framework for learning locomotion from pixels

> [!summary] Summary
> GAUSSGYM is an open-source real-to-sim framework that integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting with vectorized physics simulators to enable high-throughput, photorealistic simulation for robot locomotion. It allows policies to learn directly from RGB pixel data and transfer effectively to the real world, addressing the visual sim-to-real gap.

## Problem

- Robot learning policies often rely on geometric sensors (depth, LiDAR), leading to a 'visual sim-to-real gap' as they miss crucial semantic information from RGB images.
- Current simulation environments struggle to provide both photorealistic visual fidelity and the high throughput required for efficient reinforcement learning.
- Generating diverse and realistic training environments from various sources (real-world scans, generative models) efficiently remains a challenge.

## Method

- GAUSSGYM integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for photorealistic, differentiable rendering with vectorized physics simulators like IsaacGym, achieving high fidelity and high throughput simultaneously.
- The framework processes diverse 3D environment data, including smartphone scans and outputs from generative video models, into aligned collision meshes and 3D Gaussian Splats.
- It employs an asymmetric actor-critic reinforcement learning pipeline with a recurrent encoder that fuses visual (DinoV2-extracted features) and proprioceptive streams, along with an auxiliary voxel prediction head.

## Results

- Policies trained on RGB pixel inputs within GAUSSGYM successfully learned complex locomotion behaviors, such as stair climbing, and demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a real Unitree A1 robot.
- An RGB-trained policy successfully avoided a visually distinct 'penalty region' in a navigation task, while a depth-only policy failed to detect and avoid it.
- Ablation studies confirmed that training on a large number of diverse scenes and incorporating the auxiliary voxel prediction head significantly improved learning speed and performance across different robot types and terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combination of 3DGS and vectorized physics allows for an unprecedented throughput exceeding 100,000 simulator steps per second across thousands of photorealistic environments.
> - Integrating outputs from generative video models like Veo enables the creation of diverse and 'beyond reality' training environments, significantly expanding the available scene library.
> - Policies trained with direct RGB inputs can learn semantic reasoning, such as identifying and avoiding visually marked regions, which is not possible with depth-only sensing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic locomotion and navigation in unstructured environments is rapidly advancing, largely driven by reinforcement learning (RL) and sim-to-real transfer techniques. However, a significant challenge remains in bridging the gap between simulated and real-world visual perception.

#### 1.1. Introduction

The paper "GAUSSGYM: AN OPEN-SOURCE REAL-TO-SIM FRAMEWORK FOR LEARNING LOCOMOTION FROM PIXELS" introduces a novel simulation framework designed to accelerate the development of visually intelligent robotic locomotion and navigation policies. By integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with vectorized physics simulators like IsaacGym, GaussGym achieves an unprecedented combination of high visual fidelity and high-throughput simulation. This allows robots to be trained directly from photorealistic RGB pixel data, addressing a critical bottleneck in current sim-to-real reinforcement learning paradigms. The framework supports diverse environment creation, including real-world scans, existing datasets, and even scenes generated by cutting-edge generative video models, providing a scalable and accessible platform for advancing robot learning.

#### 1.2. Challenges in Sim-to-Real Locomotion

Historically, RL for legged locomotion has relied heavily on physics simulators (e.g., MuJoCo, PyBullet) to train policies. The advent of GPU-accelerated simulators like NVIDIA's Isaac Gym has democratized this process, enabling massively parallel training of thousands of robots simultaneously. This has led to robust geometric locomotion policies, often leveraging depth maps, elevation maps, or LiDAR inputs.

However, these approaches primarily focus on geometric understanding, neglecting the rich semantic information available in RGB images. For a robot to truly operate intelligently in complex human environments, it needs to perceive visual cues like crosswalks, puddles, or warning signs—information that is often invisible to depth-only sensors. The "visual sim-to-real gap" arises from the difficulty of generating photorealistic, diverse, and high-throughput visual data within simulations that accurately reflect the real world, and subsequently, training policies that can effectively utilize this visual information and transfer to real hardware. Existing simulators often fall short in either visual fidelity, generation speed, or the diversity of scenes they can create, forcing a reliance on less informative geometric sensors.

#### 1.3. Evolution of Simulation and Scene Generation

Traditionally, simulation environments for robot learning have been created using heuristic rules, procedural generation, or handcrafted assets. While effective for physics-based training, these methods often result in visually simplistic or unrealistic scenes. Efforts to import realistic assets from libraries (e.g., ReplicaCAD, AI2-THOR, Habitat) or 3D scanners improve visual richness but often rely on textured-mesh rendering pipelines, which can still lack photorealism and are computationally expensive for vectorized, high-throughput simulation.

Recent advancements in implicit neural representations, particularly Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), have shown tremendous promise for high-fidelity scene reconstruction. However, standard NeRFs suffer from slow training and rendering speeds, making them unsuitable for the demanding throughput required by RL. The emergence of **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** has been a game-changer, offering NeRF-level visual quality with real-time, differentiable rasterization capabilities.

The landscape is also being reshaped by **generative AI models**, especially large-scale video generation models (e.g., Veo, Genie). These models can synthesize photorealistic, multi-view consistent video from text prompts, opening up possibilities for creating entirely novel and imaginative training environments that would be impossible to capture in the real world. Integrating these generative capabilities with simulation frameworks is a cutting-edge direction.

#### 1.4. Role of Radiance Fields in Robotics

Radiance fields, especially 3DGS, are increasingly being adopted in various robotics applications. They are used for high-quality visual reconstruction for manipulation tasks (e.g., grasping transparent objects), language-guided manipulation, and creating persistent object representations. In navigation, NeRFs have been explored for vision-only navigation or as differentiable collision representations. GaussGym builds on this trend but makes a crucial integration: combining the photorealistic rendering power of 3DGS with the high-throughput contact physics simulation of platforms like IsaacGym. This distinguishes it from prior work like NeRF2Real (slow, no vectorization) or LucidSim (less scalable scene creation, manual alignment, no vectorized rendering), positioning GaussGym as a comprehensive solution for addressing the visual sim-to-real gap at scale.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind GaussGym are multifaceted, aiming to address the current limitations in robot learning for locomotion and navigation:

1. **Bridge the Visual Sim-to-Real Gap:** The core motivation is to enable control policies to learn directly from rich visual (RGB) observations in simulation and transfer effectively to the real world without significant adaptation. Existing methods often rely on depth/LiDAR, which limits robots from perceiving semantic cues crucial for complex environments.
2. **Achieve Photorealistic Simulation with High Throughput:** To facilitate efficient RL training, the framework must provide both high visual fidelity (for realism) and high simulation speed (to gather enough data). GaussGym aims for "unprecedented speed—exceeding 100,000 steps per second"—while maintaining photorealistic rendering quality.
3. **Support Diverse Environment Generation:** The project seeks to democratize the creation of realistic training worlds by accepting a wide array of data sources, including casual smartphone scans, existing 3D datasets (e.g., ARKitScenes, GrandTour), and especially the outputs from advanced generative video models (e.g., Veo). This enables the creation of virtually limitless and "beyond reality" environments.
4. **Enable Semantic Reasoning from Pixels:** By providing rich visual inputs, GaussGym aims to allow policies to learn to infer geometry and semantic meaning directly from RGB pixels, leading to more intelligent decision-making (e.g., avoiding undesirable regions marked by visual cues, rather than just geometric obstacles).
5. **Provide an Open-Source, Scalable Framework:** The authors intend to open-source all code and data, offering a foundational platform for the research community to build upon, thereby accelerating future advancements in visual locomotion and navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GaussGym's methodology revolves around a novel integration of 3D Gaussian Splatting with vectorized physics simulators and a robust data processing pipeline.

#### 3.1. GaussGym Pipeline Overview

The overall pipeline (illustrated in Figure 2) is designed for flexibility and efficiency. It takes various forms of 3D environment data, processes them into a standardized format, reconstructs both a collision mesh and a 3D Gaussian Splat representation, and then uses these assets within a high-throughput, photorealistic simulation environment. The collision mesh handles physical interactions, while the Gaussian splats provide visual rendering.

#### 3.2. Data Collection and Processing

- **Diverse Data Ingestion:** GaussGym is highly flexible, ingesting data from:
 - **Posed Datasets:** Such as ARKitScenes and GrandTour.
 - **Smartphone Captures:** Casual scans with intrinsic calibration.
 - **Unposed RGB Sequences from Video Models:** Outputs from generative models like Google DeepMind's Veo.
- **Standardization and Reconstruction:** All input data are formatted into a common gravity-aligned reference frame. The **Visually Grounded Geometry Transformer (VGGT)** (Wang et al., 2025) is employed to:
 - Estimate camera intrinsics and extrinsics.
 - Generate dense point clouds and surface normals.
- **Asset Generation:** From VGGT outputs:
 - A **Neural Kernel Surface Reconstruction (NKSR)** (Huang et al., 2023) module produces high-quality **collision meshes** for physics simulation.
 - **3D Gaussian Splats** are initialized directly from VGGT point clouds. This point-cloud initialization significantly improves geometric fidelity and accelerates the convergence of the splats.
- **Alignment:** The resulting meshes and Gaussian splats are automatically aligned in a shared global frame, ensuring consistency between physics and rendering.

#### 3.3. 3D Gaussian Splatting as a Drop-in Renderer

- **Photorealistic and Vectorized Rendering:** Once reconstructed, 3D Gaussian Splats are used as a "drop-in renderer" within a vectorized physics engine (like IsaacGym). Unlike traditional raytracing or rasterization, 3DGS offers photorealistic visuals with minimal overhead and is highly amenable to parallel execution on GPUs.
- **Efficient GPU Utilization:** The system batch-renders splats across numerous environments using multi-threaded PyTorch kernels, ensuring efficient utilization of GPU resources.

#### 3.4. Optimizations for Throughput and Realism

- **Decoupled Rendering and Control Rates:** To maximize efficiency, rendering is decoupled from the robot's proprioceptive control rate and the overall simulation frequency. Images are rendered at the camera's true frame rate (e.g., 10Hz), which is typically slower than the control frequency (e.g., 50Hz), yielding speed-ups while maintaining relevant visual input.
- **Simulated Motion Blur:** To further reduce the sim-to-real gap, GaussGym introduces a novel method for simulating motion blur. It renders a small set of frames slightly offset along the camera's velocity direction and alpha-blends them into a single image. This generates realistic blur artifacts, particularly noticeable during rapid movements (e.g., stair climbing), improving visual fidelity and policy robustness.
- **Massively Parallel Scalability:** On a single RTX 4090 GPU, GaussGym can render up to 4,096 environments across 128 unique scenes at a remarkable 100,000 simulator steps per second. This throughput is near-linearly scalable across multiple GPUs, allowing for distributed training on thousands of diverse, photorealistic scenes simultaneously.

#### 3.5. Policy Learning Architecture

- **Asymmetric Actor-Critic RL:** Policies are trained end-to-end using an asymmetric actor-critic framework, avoiding multi-stage training pipelines or student-teacher distillation.
- **Recurrent Encoder for Visual-Proprioceptive Fusion:** The core of the policy is a recurrent encoder (LSTM) that fuses visual and proprioceptive streams. At each timestep, proprioceptive measurements are concatenated with features extracted from the raw RGB frame using a pre-trained **DinoV2** (Oquab et al., 2023) encoder. This produces a compact latent representation capturing temporal dynamics and visual semantics.
- **Task-Specific Heads:**
 - **Voxel Prediction Head:** The latent vector is reshaped into a coarse 3D grid and processed by a 3D transposed convolutional network. This upscales the grid into a dense volumetric prediction of occupancy and terrain heights, forcing the shared latent representation to capture scene geometry as an auxiliary task.
 - **Policy Head:** A second LSTM consumes the latent representation and its recurrent hidden state to output parameters for a Gaussian distribution over joint position offset actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GaussGym demonstrates significant advancements in photorealistic simulation for robot learning, leading to several key findings:

#### 4.1. Scalable and Diverse Environment Generation

- **Worlds Beyond Reality:** GaussGym effectively integrates data from smartphone scans, open-source datasets (e.g., GrandTour), and critically, outputs from generative video models like Veo. This enables the creation of physically impossible or difficult-to-capture real-world environments, such as caves, disaster zones, or extraterrestrial terrains (Figure 4). The robustness of VGGT for camera estimation and point cloud generation, combined with the multi-view consistency of Veo, is crucial for this capability.
- **Large-Scale Scene Library:** The framework provides access to 2,500 diverse scenes, vastly expanding the training data available for robot learning.

#### 4.2. Enhanced Visual Locomotion

- **Learning from Pixels:** Policies trained directly from RGB pixel inputs in GaussGym successfully learn complex locomotion behaviors. For instance, a policy trained on the Unitree A1 quadruped learns to precisely place its feet on stairs and adapt its gait to avoid collisions with risers (Figure 6a, 11).
- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** As a proof of concept, the stair-climbing policy trained in simulation successfully transferred to a real Unitree A1 robot without any additional fine-tuning (Figure 6b). While precise foot placement declined somewhat in the real world compared to simulation, this marks a significant step towards closing the visual sim-to-real gap.
- **Auxiliary Task Improvement:** The incorporation of an auxiliary reconstruction loss, guided by ground-truth mesh data (voxel prediction head), significantly improves learning speed and performance, particularly for tasks like stair climbing.

#### 4.3. Semantic Reasoning in Navigation

- **Beyond Geometric Cues:** In a sparse goal-tracking task with an obstacle field, GaussGym highlights the importance of RGB input over depth-only sensing. An RGB-trained policy successfully avoided a "penalty region" (a yellow floor patch) that incurred negative rewards, demonstrating its ability to perceive and reason about semantic cues beyond mere geometry (Figure 8). In contrast, a depth-only policy failed to detect the patch and walked straight through it.

#### 4.4. Sim-to-Real Transfer and Ablations

- **Impact of Design Choices:** A large-scale ablation study on humanoid (A1) and quadrupedal (T1) robots across various terrains (flat, steep, short/tall stairs) confirmed the importance of several design parameters (Table 2):
 - Not regressing on the voxel grid or not using a pre-trained DINO encoder significantly reduced performance.
 - Training on a large number of diverse scenes provided substantial performance improvements compared to using smaller subsets (10% or 50% of scenes), underscoring the value of GaussGym's scalable scene infrastructure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GaussGym represents a substantial contribution to the fields of robotics, reinforcement learning, and computer vision, with far-reaching significance and potential impact:

#### 5.1. Advancing Sim-to-Real Robotics

By enabling high-throughput, photorealistic simulation directly from RGB pixels, GaussGym directly addresses the long-standing "visual sim-to-real gap." This is crucial for developing robots that can operate reliably and intelligently in complex, visually rich real-world environments. The ability to learn from pixels allows policies to leverage semantic information, leading to more robust and adaptable behaviors than those trained solely on geometric inputs. The demonstrated zero-shot transfer, albeit initial, is a strong indicator of its potential to accelerate the deployment of learned policies on real hardware.

#### 5.2. Democratizing Photorealistic Simulation

The open-source nature of GaussGym, combined with its flexible ingestion of various data sources (including readily available smartphone scans and generative video model outputs), significantly democratizes access to high-fidelity simulation environments. Researchers and practitioners can now rapidly create diverse and realistic training worlds without needing specialized 3D modeling skills or expensive capture equipment. This lowers the barrier to entry for developing advanced visual robot learning algorithms and fosters collaborative research.

#### 5.3. Enabling Semantic and Generalizable Robot Learning

The findings regarding semantic reasoning (e.g., avoiding a yellow penalty patch) highlight GaussGym's capability to train policies that understand and react to the visual meaning of their surroundings, not just their physical geometry. This paves the way for robots that can adhere to social norms, understand warning signs, or navigate based on human-interpretable visual cues. The ability to train across thousands of diverse scenes, including "beyond reality" environments, also improves the generalization capabilities of learned policies, making them more resilient to variations in unseen real-world scenarios.

#### 5.4. Future Directions and Current Limitations

While highly impactful, the authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to exciting future research directions:

- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Despite initial success, precise foot placement in the real world still lags behind simulation. Future work needs to address factors like physical delays (e.g., image latency) and the inherent challenges of egocentric observations for visual policies.
- **Automated Reward Generation:** The current framework relies on hand-crafted cost/reward functions. Integrating foundational language models could enable more sophisticated, automatically defined reward structures based on high-level semantic goals.
- **Realistic Physical Parameters:** GaussGym currently uses uniform physical parameters. Future work could simulate diverse surface properties (ice, mud, sand) to better connect "how something looks and how it feels," improving physical realism and policy robustness.
- **Dynamic Scenes and Deformable Assets:** The current generation of generative video models (like Veo) can be inconsistent and lack fine-grained camera control. Integrating more advanced, controllable, and temporally consistent world models (e.g., Genie 3) is a clear path forward. Furthermore, the framework's current rigid-body physics limits its ability to simulate dynamic scenes, fluids, or deformable objects, which are common in real-world environments.

In conclusion, GaussGym provides a powerful, scalable, and open-source platform that is set to significantly accelerate progress in vision-based locomotion and navigation. By bridging the critical gap between high-throughput physics simulation and high-fidelity visual rendering, it opens new avenues for developing truly intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
