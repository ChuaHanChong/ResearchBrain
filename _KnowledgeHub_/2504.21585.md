# Multi-Goal Dexterous Hand Manipulation using Probabilistic Model-based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents Goal-Conditioned Probabilistic Model Predictive Control (GC-PMPC), a model-based reinforcement learning framework for multi-goal dexterous manipulation. The method combines probabilistic neural network ensembles with an asynchronous model predictive control policy, achieving sample-efficient learning and robust performance in both simulation and on a real DexHand 021, learning new goals while retaining old ones.

## Problem

- Dexterous robotic hands face challenges due to high-dimensional state and action spaces (20+ degrees of freedom).
- Complex dynamics, particularly hand-object interactions involving contact mechanics and friction, are difficult to model analytically.
- Learning-based approaches struggle with sparse rewards and require high sample efficiency for training on physical hardware.
- Generalizing to multiple manipulation goals, where objects need to be moved to various positions and orientations, is difficult.

## Method

- Introduces Goal-Conditioned Probabilistic ==Model Predictive Control== (GC-PMPC), a model-based reinforcement learning framework for multi-goal dexterous manipulation.
- Employs Goal-Conditioned Neural Network Ensembles to learn probabilistic dynamics models, accounting for uncertainty and incorporating goal information.
- Implements an Asynchronous MPC Policy with State Smoothing, where planning runs in a separate thread for real-time control, and a smoothing term minimizes large state changes for stable movements.

## Results

- GC-PMPC significantly outperformed baseline methods (SAC, TD7, DPETS, TDMPC) in simulation, achieving higher success rates across all four complex dexterous manipulation tasks with fewer samples.
- On a real DexHand 021, GC-PMPC learned three different die manipulation goals within approximately 28 minutes per goal (84 minutes total), consistently achieving higher rewards than DPETS and TD7.
- The framework demonstrated knowledge retention in real-world settings, maintaining performance on previously learned goals while acquiring new ones, and producing smooth, deliberate manipulation trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning the dynamics model on the desired goal enables efficient learning of multiple manipulation objectives without forgetting previously acquired skills.
> - Utilizing probabilistic neural network ensembles quantifies uncertainty in dynamics predictions, enhancing robustness to noise and modeling errors.
> - An asynchronous Model Predictive Control (MPC) policy allows for real-time, high-frequency control by decoupling planning from execution, crucial for physical robotic systems.
> - Including a state smoothing term in the MPC objective leads to smoother and more stable robot movements, improving overall performance and reducing wear on hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Dexterous robotic hands have the potential to revolutionize automation by enabling machines to perform human-like manipulation tasks. However, controlling these high-dimensional systems remains a significant challenge due to their complex dynamics and the difficulty of modeling hand-object interactions. Traditional control methods often struggle with these systems, leading researchers to explore learning-based approaches.

This paper introduces Goal-Conditioned Probabilistic Model Predictive Control (GC-PMPC), a novel model-based reinforcement learning framework specifically designed for multi-goal dexterous manipulation tasks. The approach combines probabilistic neural network ensembles with an asynchronous model predictive control policy to enable efficient learning and robust performance in both simulated and real-world environments.

### 2. Challenges in Dexterous Manipulation

Dexterous hand manipulation presents several key challenges that have limited progress in this field:

1. **High-dimensional state and action spaces**: Dexterous hands typically have 20+ degrees of freedom, making control and learning difficult.

2. **Complex dynamics**: The interactions between fingers and objects are difficult to model analytically due to complex contact mechanics and friction.

3. **Sparse rewards**: In many manipulation tasks, meaningful feedback only occurs when the task is successfully completed, making it difficult for learning algorithms to progress incrementally.

4. **Sample efficiency**: Training directly on physical hardware requires methods that can learn from limited interactions.

5. **Multi-goal generalization**: Real-world applications require hands to manipulate objects into various positions and orientations.

Most existing approaches fall into two categories: model-free reinforcement learning methods that require large amounts of data but can learn complex policies, and model-based methods that are more sample-efficient but often struggle with high-dimensional systems and complex dynamics.

### 3. Goal-Conditioned Probabilistic Model Predictive Control

The GC-PMPC framework addresses these challenges through a combination of probabilistic modeling and efficient control. The system architecture consists of two main components:

1. **Goal-Conditioned Neural Network Ensembles**: These networks learn to predict the system dynamics while accounting for uncertainty.

2. **Asynchronous MPC Policy with State Smoothing**: This component generates control actions by planning over the learned dynamics model.

### 4. Probabilistic Neural Network Ensembles

The dynamics model is implemented as an ensemble of probabilistic neural networks that predict the next state given the current state, action, and goal. This approach offers several advantages:

1. **Uncertainty quantification**: By modeling the distribution of possible outcomes rather than just point estimates, the system can account for uncertainty in its predictions.

2. **Ensemble diversity**: Using multiple networks trained with different initializations helps capture different modes of the dynamics and improves robustness.

3. **Goal conditioning**: By explicitly conditioning the dynamics model on the desired goal, the system can learn goal-specific dynamics that improve performance on multi-goal tasks.

The dynamics model is trained to minimize a loss function that includes both prediction errors and a penalty on excessive prediction variance:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}, g) \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \|s_{t+1} - \hat{s}_{t+1}\|^2 + \alpha \cdot \text{Var}[\hat{s}_{t+1}] \right]$$

where $s_t$ is the current state, $a_t$ is the action, $s_{t+1}$ is the next state, $g$ is the goal, $\hat{s}_{t+1}$ is the predicted next state, and $\alpha$ is a hyperparameter that controls the trade-off between prediction accuracy and variance.

### 5. Asynchronous MPC with State Smoothing

The control policy uses Model Predictive Control (MPC) to generate actions by optimizing over the learned dynamics model. However, traditional MPC can be computationally expensive, making it challenging to meet the real-time control requirements of dexterous manipulation tasks.

To address this, GC-PMPC implements an asynchronous MPC approach where the planning runs in a separate thread from the control execution. This allows the system to maintain a high control frequency while still benefiting from the planning capabilities of MPC.

Additionally, the MPC objective includes a state smoothing term that penalizes large changes in state:

$$[a_t^*, a_{t+1}^*..., a_{t+H-1}^*] = \arg\max \sum_{h=0}^{H-1} \left( \mathcal{R}(s_{t+h}, a_{t+h}) - \lambda_{\text{smooth}} \cdot |s_{t+h+1} - s_{t+h}| \right)$$

where $H$ is the planning horizon, $\mathcal{R}$ is the reward function, and $\lambda_{\text{smooth}}$ is a hyperparameter that controls the strength of the smoothing penalty.

This smoothing term helps reduce oscillations and improves the stability of the control policy, which is particularly important for real-world implementation where sudden movements can cause damage or unpredictable behavior.

### 6. Experimental Setup

The effectiveness of GC-PMPC was evaluated in both simulation and real-world settings:

1. **Simulation Environment**: Four Shadow Hand manipulation tasks of varying complexity were used for simulation testing:
 - HandReachDense: Control fingers to reach specified positions
 - HandManipulateEggDense: Move an egg object to target positions
 - HandManipulateEggRotateDense: Rotate an egg to target orientations
 - HandManipulateBlockRotateXYZDense: Rotate a block to target orientations in 3D

2. **Real-world Setup**: A DexHand 021 with 12 active degrees of freedom, equipped with fingertip tactile sensors, was used to manipulate a cubic die. An Intel RealSense D435 depth camera provided visual feedback for object pose estimation.

The tasks were designed to test the system's ability to handle multi-goal manipulation, where the robot hand needs to move objects to different target positions and orientations.

### 7. Simulation Results

The simulation results demonstrate the effectiveness of GC-PMPC compared to several baseline methods, including model-free approaches (SAC, TD7) and model-based approaches (DPETS, TDMPC).

In terms of success rates, GC-PMPC significantly outperformed all baselines across all four tasks:

Key observations from the simulation results include:

1. **Sample Efficiency**: GC-PMPC achieved higher success rates with fewer samples compared to both model-free and model-based baselines.

2. **Stability**: The reward curves for GC-PMPC showed less variance compared to other methods, indicating more stable learning and performance.

3. **Task Complexity**: While all methods struggled with more complex tasks like block rotation, GC-PMPC still maintained a significant performance advantage.

4. **Multi-Goal Performance**: The visual results in Figure 1 clearly demonstrate that GC-PMPC successfully achieved all three manipulation goals, while baseline methods failed at these tasks.

### 8. Real-World Implementation

The real-world experiments tested GC-PMPC's ability to learn and perform a multi-goal die manipulation task on the DexHand 021 platform. The system was tasked with learning to manipulate the die to show three different faces upward (numbered 2, 4, and 5).

The real-world results confirmed the simulation findings:

1. **Learning Efficiency**: GC-PMPC learned each goal within approximately 28 minutes (approximately 100 episodes) of real-world interaction, for a total learning time of about 84 minutes.

2. **Performance Advantage**: GC-PMPC consistently achieved higher rewards than the DPETS and TD7 baselines across all three goals.

4. **Motion Quality**: The execution trajectories showed smooth and deliberate movements, with appropriate finger coordination to achieve the desired die orientations.

### 9. Discussion and Implications

The results demonstrate several key advantages of the GC-PMPC approach:

1. **Efficient Multi-Goal Learning**: By conditioning the dynamics model on the goal, GC-PMPC can efficiently learn and switch between multiple manipulation objectives without forgetting previously learned skills.

2. **Robust Uncertainty Handling**: The probabilistic nature of the dynamics model allows the system to account for uncertainty in its predictions, improving robustness to noise and modeling errors.

3. **Real-Time Control**: The asynchronous MPC approach enables high-frequency control while still allowing for complex planning, addressing a key challenge in deploying MPC on real robotic systems.

4. **Smooth Motion**: The state smoothing term in the MPC objective results in more natural and stable movements, reducing wear on hardware and improving task performance.

These advantages have significant implications for robotic manipulation in practical settings, where robots need to perform a variety of tasks, adapt to changing goals, and operate robustly in noisy environments.

### 10. Conclusion

Goal-Conditioned Probabilistic Model Predictive Control (GC-PMPC) presents a promising approach to multi-goal dexterous hand manipulation. By combining probabilistic neural network ensembles with asynchronous MPC and state smoothing, the method achieves sample-efficient learning and robust performance in both simulation and real-world environments.

The success of GC-PMPC on complex manipulation tasks demonstrates the potential of model-based reinforcement learning for dexterous manipulation. The ability to learn multiple goals without forgetting previous skills is particularly valuable for practical robotic applications where versatility is essential.

Future work could explore scaling the approach to even more complex manipulation tasks, improving the sample efficiency further, and investigating the transfer of learned skills between different objects and hand morphologies. Additionally, incorporating more sophisticated perception systems could enable manipulation in less structured environments, bringing dexterous robotic manipulation closer to real-world deployment.
