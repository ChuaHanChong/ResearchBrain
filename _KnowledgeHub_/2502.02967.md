# Demonstrating a Control Framework for Physical Human-Robot Interaction Toward Industrial Applications

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the CNRS-AIST Joint Robotics Laboratory and The University of Tokyo developed a torque-based control framework for physical human-robot interaction (pHRI) in industrial settings. The framework achieves motion-tracking precision comparable to or exceeding high-gain position control, reducing peak position error by 43% and stabilization time by 39% in static disturbance tests, while ensuring robust safety through integrated constraints.

## Problem

- Existing pHRI systems often fall short of industrial performance requirements, such as precision and repeatability, despite advancements in safety features.
- Traditional high-gain position control, while precise, makes industrial robots stiff and potentially hazardous during close human-robot collaboration.
- Torque control, which enables compliant behaviors crucial for human safety, frequently compromises the consistency and repeatability required for industrial tasks.

## Method

- A comprehensive **control framework** based on a ==second-order Quadratic Programming (QP) formulation==, optimally computing desired joint accelerations while strictly enforcing kinematic and self-collision constraints.
- Integration of three dynamically switchable ==torque-based compliant control modes== (Null-Space Compliance, Full-Body Compliance, and Dual Compliance), tailored for various interaction scenarios.
- Development of a novel, robust ==low-level torque controller== for the Kinova Gen3 robot, which incorporates inverse dynamics, rotor inertia, friction compensation, and a filtered feedback loop to enhance torque tracking performance.

## Results

- The custom low-level torque controller significantly reduced torque tracking error compared to Kinova's default controller in both static and dynamic scenarios.
- In static disturbance tests, the proposed torque control demonstrated a **43%** lower mean peak position error (**3.216 mm** vs **5.632 mm**) and a **39%** shorter stabilization time (**0.512 s** vs **0.842 s**) compared to high-gain position control.
- The dual compliance mode effectively exhibited dynamic, force-dependent mode switching between stiff and compliant end-effector behaviors, providing a 'lightweight feel' during human interaction while maintaining task precision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robustly implemented torque-based control, particularly with a custom low-level controller, can achieve and even surpass the precision and stiffness of traditional high-gain position control in industrial contexts.
> - Integrating strict safety constraints directly into a QP optimization framework enables robust and 'stiff' reactive behaviors that actively counteract forces pushing towards safety limit violations, without compromising primary task performance.
> - The dual compliance strategy, facilitated by the QP framework and enhanced low-level torque control, provides an intuitive and precise human-robot interaction experience where compliance is dynamically activated based on interaction forces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is positioned within the evolving landscape of robotics, particularly at the intersection of Physical Human-Robot Interaction (pHRI) and industrial automation. The emergence of Industry 5.0 emphasizes human-centric approaches and closer physical collaboration between humans and robots. This necessitates robotic systems that can guarantee both human safety and the precision and repeatability required for industrial applications.

Prior research in pHRI has focused on aspects such as collision detection and reaction mechanisms, risk metrics integration, and advanced compliance through admittance control or soft skins. While these advancements have improved robot adaptability for close-contact interactions, the authors note that the performance of many existing pHRI systems often falls short of industrial requirements, which are defined by standards such as ISO 9283 (performance criteria) and ISO/TS 15066 (safety thresholds for collaborative robotics).

Traditional high-gain position control, prevalent in industry for its reliability and precision, renders robots stiff and potentially hazardous in human-robot collaboration. Conversely, torque control, while enabling compliant behaviors crucial for safety and human guidance, can introduce inconsistencies and reduce task repeatability due to increased sensitivity to environmental factors.

This paper addresses the gap between these two paradigms by developing a control framework that aims to achieve industrial-grade performance—characterized by high precision, repeatability, and safety—within a torque-controlled pHRI context. It specifically aims to move beyond viewing safety as a limiting factor, instead integrating it as a constraint within performance optimization, as suggested by previous research. By demonstrating that its torque control strategy can achieve motion-tracking precision comparable to or exceeding traditional position control, the work directly contributes to making advanced pHRI systems viable for industrial integration, fulfilling the dual requirements of safety and productivity in Industry 5.0.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and demonstrate a versatile control framework for Physical Human-Robot Interaction (pHRI) that is suitable for industrial applications. This overarching goal is motivated by several key challenges inherent in integrating robots into human-centric industrial environments, as envisioned by Industry 5.0.

The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Bridging the Performance Gap in pHRI:** The authors identify a gap between existing pHRI research and the practical requirements for industrial-grade performance. While pHRI is crucial for Industry 5.0, few studies practically align pHRI capabilities with industrial performance metrics such as precision and repeatability. The framework aims to bridge this gap by offering robust and precise torque-based control.
- **Ensuring Human Safety:** A paramount motivation is to guarantee human safety during close physical collaboration. Traditional high-gain position control, while precise, makes robots stiff and potentially dangerous upon human interference. The framework seeks to enable compliant behaviors through torque control, allowing workers to interact with and guide the robot safely without compromising safety through robust control mechanisms and integrated safety constraints.
- **Achieving Industrial-Grade Productivity and Precision:** While compliance is essential for safety, it often compromises the precision and repeatability required for industrial tasks. A key objective is to develop control strategies that maintain high accuracy and efficiency without sacrificing flexibility to human intervention. This includes demonstrating that torque control can achieve precision comparable to or superior to conventional position control.
- **Fostering Trust in Automation:** The authors acknowledge that trust in automation is crucial for broader pHRI adoption. While not the primary focus, the framework’s emphasis on predictable, task-specific behaviors, safety features, and reliable interactions is intended to contribute to building this trust among human operators.
- **Developing a Versatile Control Framework:** The research aims to create a flexible framework incorporating various torque-based control modes (compliance control, null-space compliance, dual compliance) suitable for both static and dynamic scenarios. This versatility allows the robot's behavior to be tailored to specific collaborative tasks, such as cooperative manipulation or kinesthetic teaching.
- **Integrating Strict Safety and Kinematic Constraints:** To meet industrial safety regulations and prevent self-collision or excessive movement, the framework seeks to integrate strict kinematic and collision constraints directly into the control formulation. This is achieved through a second-order Quadratic Programming (QP) formulation that ensures singularity-robust task tracking through a weighted hierarchy.
- **Improving Low-Level Torque Control for Specific Robots:** Recognizing limitations in default robot controllers, particularly for the Kinova Gen3, a specific objective is to introduce a novel, custom-developed low-level torque controller. This controller aims to enhance torque tracking performance, thereby enabling more precise and reliable high-level compliant control.
- **Promoting Reproducibility and Deployment:** By building the framework on the open-source robotic control software `mc_rtc`, a key objective is to ensure reproducibility for both research and industrial deployment, facilitating its adoption and further development in various robotic platforms and industrial settings.

In essence, the motivation stems from the need for robots that can safely, precisely, and repeatably operate alongside humans in industrial environments, overcoming the limitations of current control strategies to meet the holistic demands of Industry 5.0.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The presented work introduces a comprehensive control framework for pHRI, integrating multiple compliant control modes and robust safety features, implemented on a collaborative robot. The methodology is structured around a second-order Quadratic Programming (QP) formulation, supported by a custom low-level torque controller and an open-source robotic control software.

#### 3.1. Control Framework Architecture

The core of the control framework relies on a second-order Quadratic Programming (QP) formulation. This QP optimally computes desired joint accelerations ($\ddot{q}_d$) while enforcing strict kinematic and self-collision constraints. A weighted hierarchy within the QP guarantees singularity-robust task tracking performance. The QP minimizes a sum of weighted task accelerations (task-specific and posture/null-space) adjusted by a compliance parameter ($\gamma_k$) and the estimated effect of external forces ($\hat{\tau}_e$).
$$ \min_{\ddot{q}_r} \left( \sum_k w_k \left\| \ddot{e}_r^k - \ddot{e}_*^k - \gamma_k J_k M^{-1} \hat{\tau}_e \right\|^2 + w_0 \left\| \ddot{q}_r - \ddot{q}_* - \gamma_0 M^{-1} \hat{\tau}_e \right\|^2 \right) $$
$$ \text{s.t. } A_c \ddot{q}_r \leq b_c $$
Here, $\ddot{q}_r$ is the decision variable, $\ddot{e}_r^k$ and $\ddot{e}_*^k$ are the optimization variable and reference for task $k$, $J_k$ is its Jacobian, $M^{-1} \hat{\tau}_e$ represents estimated external force effects, $w_k$ and $w_0$ are task weights, and $\gamma_k, \gamma_0$ are compliance parameters for task $k$ and null-space respectively. $A_c \ddot{q}_r \leq b_c$ represents strict safety constraints.

#### 3.2. Control Modes

The framework incorporates three torque-based compliant control modes, dynamically switchable in real-time:

- **Null-Space Compliance:** The main task (e.g., end-effector pose) is kept stiff ($\gamma_k=0$), while compliance is applied only to the null-space of the robot ($\gamma_0=1$). This allows the robot to adapt its posture to external forces or human intervention without disturbing the primary task, maintaining precision at the end-effector.
- **Full-Body Compliance:** Both the main task ($\gamma_k=1$) and the null-space ($\gamma_0=1$) are compliant. The entire robot responds to external forces, making it suitable for scenarios where human operators need to freely interrupt or modify a task.
- **Dual Compliance:** This mode combines aspects of null-space and full-body compliance. The main task (end-effector) becomes compliant only when physically interacting with the relevant part of the body involved in the kinematic task, remaining stiff otherwise. The robot is always compliant in its null-space. The implementation uses the QP-based compliant end-effector task with zero stiffness and low damping. A Schmitt trigger mechanism, based on the end-effector wrench norm from a Force/Torque (F/T) sensor, handles transitions between stiff and compliant end-effector behavior, preventing spurious mode switching.

#### 3.3. Safety Constraints

Safety constraints are integrated directly into the QP formulation as strict inequality constraints. These include:

- **Torque Limits:** Enforcing adherence to industrial safety regulations.
- **Joint Velocity and Position Limits:** Preventing self-collision and excessive movement.
- **Second-Order Velocity Damper:** An extension of first-order approaches, this dynamically adjusts robot motion based on distance constraints. The constraint limits acceleration ($\ddot{e}_p$) based on the error between current velocity ($\dot{e}$) and a desired velocity ($\dot{e}_d$) derived from distance to safety limits, with a damping gain $\lambda$. This gain is tuned to ensure an overdamped system response, preventing oscillations and guaranteeing smooth transitions.
 $$ \ddot{e}_p = -\frac{\lambda}{2}(e - d_s) - \lambda \dot{e} $$
 $$ \lambda = \frac{4M^2 \xi}{(d_i - d_s)} $$
 where $e$ is current distance, $d_s$ is safety distance, $d_i$ is influence distance, $\xi$ is convergence speed coefficient, and $M$ is the amortization margin (tuned $>1$).
- **Velocity Limit Enforcement:** Bounding acceleration to keep velocities within predefined limits ($\dot{e}_{lim}$), using a similar damping principle.
 $$ \ddot{e}_v = -\lambda (\dot{e} - \dot{e}_{lim}) $$
- **Self-Collision Avoidance:** Continuously monitoring minimum distances between robot body pairs and generating damping behaviors as distances approach safety thresholds, based on velocity damper principles.

These safety features are designed to be "stiff," actively negating external forces and torques that push towards constraint violations, provided the robot's torque limits are not reached.

#### 3.4. Low-Level Torque Control (Kinova Gen3 Specific)

Given the limitations of Kinova Gen3's default torque control modes, the authors developed a novel robust low-level torque controller. This controller leverages the robot's current control capabilities and consists of five components:

- **Joint Torque-to-Current Conversion:** Converts desired joint torque into motor current using motor torque constant ($K_T$) and gear ratio ($G_r$).
- **Inverse Dynamics:** Computes desired torques based on desired accelerations.
- **Rotor Inertia Compensation:** Explicitly accounts for rotor inertia, especially critical for smaller joints, to produce appropriate torques.
- **Friction Compensation Model:** Pre-compensates static and dynamic friction between motor and torque sensor (e.g., in the harmonic drive). The model includes Coulomb and viscous friction, with a quasi-static state where friction direction depends on desired acceleration, allowing precompensation even when manipulated by a human.
- **Feedback Loop:** Improves torque tracking by processing torque error using:
 - **Kinova's Transfer Function:** A 3rd-order Z-domain filter (parameters obtained via Kinova's Kortex API) is used to filter torque error, hypothesized to account for joint torque sensor response.
 - **Leaky Integrator:** A slow-acting feedback component ($H_{leak}(s) = K_I/(s+\theta)$) further improves convergence and addresses persistent errors due to modeling inaccuracies or unmodeled friction.

#### 3.5. Hardware Setup

The framework is demonstrated on a Kinova Gen3 7-DoF collaborative robot equipped with joint torque sensors. A Bota SensONE Force/Torque (F/T) sensor is attached to the end-effector for precise external force measurement. A Next Unit of Computing (NUC) acts as an EtherCAT master for the F/T sensor, publishing data via ROS. A control PC runs the framework, connected to the NUC and robot via Ethernet. A DualShock 4 game controller, mounted on the end-effector, serves as a user interface for real-time mode switching and parameter adjustments. The framework explicitly requires robots that support joint torque control with good response and accurate tracking.

#### 3.6. Software Framework (`mc_rtc`)

The entire framework is built upon `mc_rtc`, an open-source software framework for model-based control strategies. Key `mc_rtc` features utilized include:

- **Second-Order QP Solver:** Enforces strict constraints and enables task prioritization through a weighted hierarchy.
- **Pre-defined Tasks:** Provides a range of tasks (end-effector positioning, posture control, etc.) that can be configured for compliance.
- **Robot-Agnostic Design:** Allows deployment across various robots and simulation environments (e.g., MuJoCo) using URDF models.
- **Finite State Machine (FSM):** Implemented for managing real-time mode switching (compliance modes, low-level control modes, operational scenarios) via the DS4 controller.
- **Monitoring and Logging:** Provides live plotting and an advanced logging system for continuous performance tracking.

The `mc_rtc` framework operates at 1kHz, with ROS for communication, facilitating modularity, scalability, and reproducibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides experimental results validating the proposed control framework across several key aspects, demonstrating its performance and capabilities for industrial applications.

#### 4.1. Friction Parameters Identification

The authors identified friction model parameters for the Kinova Gen3 robot based on experimental data. By operating the robot in a zero-torque stance and applying constant currents to individual joints, they measured torque error against joint velocity. The friction model derived includes Coulomb and viscous friction components, with specific parameters identified for "large" and "small" actuators (the first four and last three joints, respectively). The static friction was found to be close to Coulomb friction, simplifying the model. This identified model is crucial for the proposed low-level torque controller.

#### 4.2. Torque Tracking

A comparison of the proposed low-level torque control with Kinova’s default "high-velocity" torque control demonstrated improved performance.
- **Static Scenario:** In a static configuration with a weight attached to the end-effector (compensated by the F/T sensor), the proposed controller exhibited significantly lower torque error.
- **Dynamic Scenario:** During a 6D-Cartesian task involving sinusoidal and triangular motions, the proposed controller also showed substantially reduced torque tracking error compared to Kinova’s default.
The torque error axis was presented on a logarithmic scale to highlight these differences. These results indicate that the custom low-level torque controller provides more precise and reliable torque tracking, which is fundamental for achieving high-level compliant behaviors.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Position Control (Industrial Standard)

The framework’s performance was evaluated against high-gain position control, which represents the industrial standard for precision and stiffness.
- **Stiffness in Static Configurations:** Experiments involved dropping a 1.25kg weight from a height of 25cm onto the end-effector of the robot, which was holding a static 6D pose. Both position control and the proposed torque control (with stiff end-effector task and null-space compliance) were tested over 30 drops. Statistical analysis (Mann-Whitney U test, due to non-normal data distribution for some metrics) revealed significant differences:
 - **Peak Error:** The mean peak position error for torque control (3.216 mm) was significantly lower than for position control (5.632 mm) (p < 1.028e⁻⁶, large effect size).
 - **Stabilization Time:** Torque control exhibited a shorter mean stabilization time (0.512 s) compared to position control (0.842 s) (p = 9.513e⁻⁴, moderate effect size).
 - **Residual Error:** Torque control resulted in a much lower mean residual (static) error (0.102 mm) than position control (0.353 mm) (p < 0.001, very large effect size).
 These findings suggest that the proposed torque control approach can achieve higher precision and stiffness in static scenarios, performing at least as well as, and often better than, the manufacturer's high-gain position controller, while offering compliance in the null-space.

- **Dynamic Trajectory Tracking:** In a dynamic scenario where the robot tracked a 6D-Cartesian trajectory with sinusoidal and triangular motions, the proposed torque control demonstrated comparable tracking precision to position control for both position and velocity errors along the moving axes. As expected, it significantly outperformed Kinova’s default torque control in terms of precision. This indicates that the framework can maintain high accuracy even during complex dynamic movements, a critical requirement for industrial applications.

#### 4.4. Dual Compliance

The dual compliance strategy was demonstrated, showcasing its ability to provide intuitive and adaptive human-robot interaction.
- **Interaction and Mode Switching:** The experiments involved moving the end-effector and modifying the null-space configuration. The robot's behavior dynamically adjusted based on the end-effector wrench norm measured by the Bota F/T sensor.
- **Force-Dependent Compliance:** When the external force exceeded a higher threshold, the system switched to a compliant end-effector mode (full-body compliance). When the force dropped below a lower threshold, it reverted to a stiff end-effector with only null-space compliance. This hysteresis-based switching ensured smooth transitions.
- **Lightweight Feel and Precision:** The QP-based implementation of dual compliance, without relying on an admittance task, provided a "lightweight feel" at the end-effector while maintaining precision, attributed to the enhanced low-level torque control.
The results, visualized through time-series plots of end-effector force, joint torques, and velocities, confirmed effective mode switching and responsive robot motion based on human interaction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant step toward making advanced Physical Human-Robot Interaction (pHRI) viable and robust for industrial environments, aligning with the principles of Industry 5.0.

- **Enabling Industrial-Grade pHRI:** The most direct impact is demonstrating that torque-based control can achieve performance levels (precision, repeatability, stiffness) comparable to or exceeding traditional industrial position control, while inherently offering crucial compliance for safety. This directly addresses a critical barrier to widespread pHRI adoption in manufacturing, where performance standards are stringent.
- **Enhanced Safety in Human-Robot Collaboration:** By integrating strict kinematic, collision, and torque constraints into a QP formulation, the framework provides robust safety guarantees. The "stiff" nature of these constraints ensures that the robot actively counters forces pushing towards safety limit violations, contributing to a safer working environment for human operators.
- **Improved Trust and Usability:** The framework’s ability to offer predictable, precise, yet compliant behaviors (e.g., through dual compliance) can foster greater trust and acceptance among human workers. Features like kinesthetic teaching become more intuitive and natural, improving the overall usability of robotic systems in collaborative tasks.
- **Advancement in Low-Level Robot Control:** The development of a novel, robust low-level torque controller for the Kinova Gen3, specifically addressing the limitations of proprietary hardware through inverse dynamics, friction compensation, and refined feedback loops, has implications for optimizing the performance of other torque-controlled robots. This contributes to the foundational capabilities required for sophisticated pHRI.
- **Reproducibility and Accessibility through Open-Source Software:** Building the framework on `mc_rtc`, an open-source platform, ensures reproducibility for academic research and facilitates adaptation and deployment in diverse industrial settings. This approach lowers the barrier for industries to evaluate and integrate advanced pHRI capabilities into their existing robotic systems.
- **Contribution to Industry 5.0 Goals:** By focusing on human safety, enhanced productivity through robust performance, and facilitating intuitive human-robot interaction, the framework directly contributes to the human-centric values and goals of Industry 5.0. It moves beyond automation efficiency to emphasize human well-being and collaborative intelligence.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a robust foundation for further research in several areas, including adaptive control strategies (though acknowledging challenges in pHRI contexts), human-centric evaluation metrics, and integration of environmental sustainability considerations into pHRI system design.

In conclusion, this research provides a practical and experimentally validated control framework that addresses core technical and operational challenges of pHRI, positioning it as a significant enabler for the next generation of industrial collaborative robotics.
