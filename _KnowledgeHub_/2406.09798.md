# Sim-to-Real Transfer via 3D Feature Fields for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A sim-to-real framework giving monocular-camera VLN robots near-panoramic perception. A semantic traversable map, built from ground-projected RGB-D occupancy and semantic maps refined by UNets, predicts agent-centric navigable waypoints; a 3D feature field with volume rendering decodes CLIP-aligned novel-view features for the 11 unseen views. This transfers panoramic VLN models like ETPNav to monocular robots, reaching 44.9% success on R2R-CE val unseen.

## Problem

- Monocular VLN agents have a field of view under 90 degrees, so they miss targets and score below 39% success on R2R-CE val unseen.
- High-performing panoramic VLN models need 360-degree RGB-D rigs that are costly and bulky, so they cannot deploy on most physical robots.
- Prior sim-to-real waypoint predictors use 2D laser scanners on a fixed-height plane, missing varied-height obstacles and traversable landmarks such as stairs and doors.

## Method

- ==Semantic Traversable Map==: monocular RGB-D is ground-projected into global occupancy and 27-class semantic maps, cropped agent-centric, denoised by ==UNets==, then a third UNet outputs a traversability probability map.
- ==Waypoint selection== splits the traversable map into twelve 30-degree sectors, takes each sector maximum, rejects occupied cells, and keeps Top-K candidates, trained with ==MSE== against mixture-of-Gaussian targets.
- ==3D Feature Fields== via the ==HNR== model lift ==CLIP-ViT-B/16== features into 3D with depth, then ==volume-render== 8x8 subregion features that a transformer decoder aggregates into CLIP-aligned novel-view vectors.

## Results

- On R2R-CE val unseen, ETPNav with feature fields reaches **44.9%** SR versus **38.9%** for WS-MGMap and **37.4%** for NaVid, over **7%** gain, with SPL lower at **30.4** due to extra exploration.
- In a **100**-sample real-world lab with a LoCoBot and a **69-degree** HFOV RealSense D435, the method hits **42.0%** SR and **54.0%** OSR versus **15.0%** SR for WS-MGMap.
- Ablations: removing feature fields drops SR to **32.9%**; removing the semantic map drops it to **40.1%**; removing occupancy gives **42.7%**; cross-episode feature memory lifts SR to **45.8%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Panoramic perception, not panoramic hardware, drives VLN performance: synthesizing out-of-view features from past observations recovers most of the panoramic advantage on cheap monocular robots.
> - Semantic context matters more than geometry for traversability, since dropping the semantic map hurts more than dropping occupancy by losing doors and stairs as passable landmarks.
> - Reconstruction-based perception buys success rate at the cost of path efficiency, since the agent must explore to populate its fields, so SPL-sensitive applications need better unobserved-area prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a field within embodied artificial intelligence that focuses on enabling robotic agents to navigate complex 3D environments by interpreting natural language instructions. Early VLN research primarily utilized discrete environments, such as those built upon the Matterport3D dataset, where agents navigated predefined connectivity graphs. This setup simplified action spaces and facilitated efficient training. However, these discrete environments presented limitations for real-world application due to their structured nature.

The introduction of continuous environment settings (VLN-CE), often leveraging simulators like Habitat, allowed agents to traverse any unobstructed location using low-level, atomic actions (e.g., moving forward a small distance, turning by a few degrees). While offering greater realism, navigating with atomic actions in continuous environments proved computationally demanding and often resulted in suboptimal performance. To mitigate this, many VLN-CE approaches adopted waypoint predictors, generating a set of navigable waypoints to simplify the action space and improve efficiency, making continuous navigation more analogous to discrete graph traversal.

A significant challenge in VLN, particularly for real-world deployment, revolves around the choice of sensory input. Many high-performing VLN models rely on panoramic RGB-D observations, providing a 360-degree view of the environment. This broad field of view enables agents to perceive distant objects, assess traversability, and understand spatial relationships effectively, leading to improved navigation success rates (e.g., over 57% in simulators). However, physical robots rarely come equipped with panoramic RGB-D cameras due to their high cost and large size, making the direct deployment of these high-performance panoramic models impractical.

Conversely, VLN agents equipped with monocular RGB-D cameras are more compatible with common robotic platforms but suffer from a limited field of view (typically around 60-90 degrees horizontal). This restriction significantly curtails their environmental perception, often leading to navigation failures, with reported success rates below 39% in simulation benchmarks. The disparity between simulation performance (often achieved with panoramic views) and real-world applicability (limited by monocular sensing) highlights a critical "sim-to-real transfer" gap.

Prior attempts at sim-to-real transfer for VLN have explored various strategies. Some works, like Anderson et al. (2021), used 2D laser scanners for subgoal prediction but found significant deviations from predefined navigation graphs in unseen environments. More recent methods have attempted to leverage large language or multimodal models for zero-shot real-world VLN, but their navigation performance remains limited. Approaches like NaVid (2024) have fine-tuned large models using video-based data for monocular real-world VLN but incur substantial computational costs.

This current work positions itself as an effort to bridge this sim-to-real gap by enabling monocular robots to achieve near-panoramic perception. It aims to develop a practical and high-performance solution that allows VLN models trained with panoramic observations to be effectively deployed on robots equipped only with a monocular camera, thereby combining the performance benefits of panoramic perception with the practical advantages of monocular sensing. This involves addressing the core challenges of perceiving traversable areas and representing semantic and spatial relationships across a wide field of view using limited visual input. The use of 3D Feature Fields builds upon recent advancements in neural radiance fields (NeRF) and generalizable 3D representations, such as HNR, which can encode visual observations into dynamic 3D features and synthesize novel views.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a vision-and-language navigation (VLN) system that enables robots equipped with only a monocular camera to achieve near-panoramic perception, thereby facilitating the practical deployment of high-performance VLN models that typically rely on panoramic observations.

This objective is motivated by several identified limitations and challenges within the current VLN research landscape:

1. **Limited Performance of Monocular VLN Agents:** Existing VLN agents that use only a monocular camera exhibit restricted environmental perception due to their narrow field of view (e.g., typically 69° horizontal field of view (HFOV) and 42° vertical field of view (VFOV) in the real-world setup). This limitation frequently results in navigation failures, such as agents failing to detect targets or obstacles outside their immediate forward-facing view, leading to significantly lower navigation success rates (below 39% in benchmark simulations).

2. **Deployment Challenges of Panoramic VLN Models:** While most high-performing VLN models in simulation achieve superior navigation success rates (over 57%) by utilizing panoramic RGB-D cameras, this approach presents a significant barrier to real-world deployment. Panoramic RGB-D cameras are typically expensive and large, making them impractical for integration onto common robotic platforms. Consequently, the advanced capabilities demonstrated by these models in simulators cannot be easily transferred to physical robots.

3. **The Sim-to-Real Transfer Gap:** There is a clear discrepancy between the training and evaluation environments (simulators with often idealized sensory input, like panoramic cameras) and real-world application scenarios (where robots often have more limited and practical sensor configurations). Effective sim-to-real transfer for VLN remains a considerable challenge, particularly when bridging the gap between panoramic-trained models and monocular-equipped robots.

To address these motivations, the research identifies two specific technical challenges that must be overcome:

1. **Identifying Traversable Areas:** A monocular robot needs a robust method to identify and map traversable areas in its vicinity, as well as predict navigable waypoints, to enable effective path planning and obstacle avoidance. Existing methods relying on 2D laser scanners have limitations, such as detecting obstacles only on a fixed-height plane and failing to identify traversable obstacles like stairs or doors.

2. **Representing Semantic and Spatial Relationships within the Panorama:** The limited field of view of a monocular camera restricts the agent's understanding of the broader environment. To compensate, a mechanism is required to generate comprehensive semantic and spatial representations of the entire panorama from restricted monocular observations, enabling the agent to "see" beyond its immediate view and reason about the surrounding space.

By tackling these challenges, the authors aim to provide a practical and high-performance solution for real-world VLN, allowing monocular robots to leverage the advanced navigation capabilities of models originally designed for panoramic inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology focuses on endowing monocular robots with panoramic perception capabilities to facilitate the sim-to-real transfer of high-performance panoramic VLN models. This is achieved through two main components: a Semantic Traversable Map for waypoint prediction and 3D Feature Fields for panoramic semantic understanding.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Sim-to-Real Transfer Framework

The framework builds upon existing VLN-CE models, which typically observe panoramic RGB-D images, predict navigable waypoints, encode visual and location information into a topological map, and then use a Cross-Modal Graph Transformer to select the optimal waypoint. The proposed framework modifies this pipeline to accommodate monocular input:
1. A new waypoint predictor, based on the **Semantic Traversable Map**, is designed to predict waypoints from monocular RGB-D camera input, aiming for nearly 360° coverage.
2. The **3D Feature Fields** model is employed to predict visual representations for these candidate waypoints, including those outside the monocular camera's immediate field of view.
These augmented perceptions (waypoint predictions and novel view representations) are then fed into existing panoramic VLN models, such as ETPNav and GridMM, enabling their use on monocular robots.

#### 3.2. Semantic Traversable Map for Waypoint Prediction

This component is responsible for identifying traversable areas and navigable waypoints from monocular RGB-D observations.

- **Ground-projecting the Global Map:**
 - During navigation, monocular RGB-D observations are continuously ground-projected to update two global maps: an occupancy map ($O_t \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times 3}$) and a semantic map ($S_t \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times 27}$).
 - The occupancy map classifies regions into `void`, `free`, and `occupancy`, with dimensions $h=w=512$ (each pixel representing a 5cm x 5cm area).
 - The semantic map categorizes environmental elements into 27 classes.
 - A pre-trained UNet model is utilized to extract semantic segmentation from the RGB-D images before projection. These global maps are accumulated over time.

- **Generating the Agent-Centric Subregion Map:**
 - To focus on the robot's immediate surroundings, agent-centric subregions ($O'_t$ and $S'_t$) of size 192x192 pixels are cropped from the global occupancy and semantic maps.
 - Two learnable UNet models refine these subregion maps. They take the cropped maps concatenated with learnable positional embeddings ($P \in \mathbb{R}^{192 \times 192 \times 16}$) as input.
 - These UNets predict refined semantic ($S''_t \in \mathbb{R}^{192 \times 192 \times 27}$) and occupancy ($O''_t \in \mathbb{R}^{192 \times 192 \times 3}$) maps.
 - The training objective for these UNets is pixel-wise cross-entropy loss, comparing predicted maps with ground-truth maps ($S^{gt}_t$, $O^{gt}_t$) derived from the Matterport3D dataset's 3D semantic information.

- **Predicting the Traversable Map:**
 - The refined semantic map ($S''_t$), occupancy map ($O''_t$), and positional embeddings are concatenated and fed into another UNet model.
 - This UNet outputs a probability distribution map of traversable areas ($T_t \in \mathbb{R}^{192 \times 192}$).
 - The ground-truth traversable map ($T^{gt}_t$) is constructed from waypoints in the R2R dataset's navigation graph, represented as a mixture of 2D Gaussian distributions centered at navigable points.
 - The prediction of the traversable map is optimized using Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss.

- **Predicting Candidate Waypoints:**
 - The predicted traversable map is segmented into 12 sections, each spanning 30 degrees from the robot's center.
 - Within each section, the waypoint with the highest traversability probability is selected, provided it is not in an occupied region of the refined occupancy map ($O''_t$).
 - Ultimately, the Top-K waypoints with the highest probabilities across all sections are chosen as candidate navigation goals.

#### 3.3. 3D Feature Fields for Panoramic Perception

This component generates visual representations for candidate waypoints, especially for novel views outside the monocular camera's field of view.

- **Encoding 3D Feature Fields:**
 - Monocular RGB images are processed using a pre-trained CLIP-ViT-B/16 model to extract fine-grained visual features.
 - These 2D visual features are then projected into 3D world positions using the corresponding depth map and camera parameters, forming a 3D feature field. The Hierarchical Neural Radiance Representation (HNR) model is utilized for this purpose.

- **Volume Rendering for Novel View Representation:**
 - To decode novel view representations, the HNR model employs volume rendering.
 - For each desired novel view (e.g., a subregion in the panorama), rays are cast from the virtual camera position.
 - N points are uniformly sampled along each ray. For each sampled point, its k-nearest features are identified from the constructed 3D feature field.
 - An MLP network predicts the volume density ($\sigma_n$) and a latent vector ($r_n$) for each sampled point.
 - These latent vectors are composited using volume rendering to generate a subregion feature ($R_{(u,v)}$), forming an $8 \times 8$ feature map ($R \in \mathbb{R}^{8 \times 8 \times 512}$) for the novel view.

- **Generating Panoramic View Features:**
 - A transformer-based decoder aggregates the $8 \times 8$ feature map ($R$) into a single 512-dimensional novel view representation ($V \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 512}$). This representation is aligned with CLIP embeddings of the corresponding novel view.
 - At each navigation step, the forward-facing view's feature is directly extracted from the monocular RGB image using CLIP. The other 11 novel view features (to complete a 360-degree panorama of 12 views) are predicted using the 3D Feature Fields and HNR model.
 - These 12 view representations are then input into the baseline VLN models (e.g., ETPNav, GridMM) for navigation decision-making.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed approach was evaluated across simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating its effectiveness in enabling monocular robots to leverage panoramic perception for VLN.

#### 4.1. Comparison on Simulated Environments

Experiments were conducted on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE datasets within the Habitat simulator.

- **R2R-CE Dataset (Table 1):**
 - The approach, specifically "ETPNav w/ Feature Fields," achieved improved performance compared to previous monocular VLN methods.
 - On the Val Unseen split, it obtained a Navigation Error (NE) of 5.95, an Oracle Success Rate (OSR) of 55.8%, and a Success Rate (SR) of 44.9%. This represents an improvement of over 7% in SR compared to WS-MGMap (38.9%) and NaVid (37.4%), and approximately 10% over CM² (34.3%).
 - Similar trends were observed on the Test Unseen split, with SR of 43.7%.
 - The "GridMM w/ Feature Fields" variant also showed improvements over other monocular methods.
 - The Success Rate weighted by normalized inverse Path Length (SPL) metric, however, did not show a significant advantage. This is attributed to the increased exploration required by the agent to construct sufficient environmental observations for the 3D Feature Fields, leading to longer navigation paths.

- **RxR-CE Dataset (Table 2):**
 - For the more challenging RxR-CE dataset, "ETPNav w/ Feature Fields" demonstrated a SR of 25.5% on the Val Unseen split, outperforming previous monocular methods like NaVid (23.8%), A²Nav (16.8%), WS-MGMap (15.0%), and CM² (14.4%).
 - The OSR of 36.7% also indicated an improvement compared to prior monocular works.

#### 4.2. Comparison on Real-world Environments

Real-world experiments were conducted using an Interbotix LoCoBot WX250 equipped with an Intel RealSense D435 camera (69° HFOV, 42° VFOV) in an unstructured lab environment. 100 navigation samples across five house layouts were used.

- **Overall Performance (Table 3):**
 - The methods utilizing 3D Feature Fields demonstrated more substantial performance gains in real-world settings compared to simulated environments.
 - "ETPNav w/ Feature Fields" achieved an overall OSR of 54.0% and an SR of 42.0%. This significantly outperformed CM² (OSR 17.0%, SR 10.0%) and WS-MGMap (OSR 23.0%, SR 15.0%).
 - "GridMM w/ Feature Fields" also showed strong performance, with an overall OSR of 47.0% and SR of 36.0%.
 - The heightened performance in real-world scenarios is attributed to the 3D Feature Fields' ability to compensate for the significantly smaller field of view of the robot's camera (69° HFOV) compared to the 90° HFOV used in simulators for previous monocular methods.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **3D Feature Fields (Table 4):**
 - Directly transferring a panoramic VLN model to a monocular camera setting without 3D Feature Fields ("w/o Feature Fields") resulted in a substantial performance drop (e.g., over 20% reduction in OSR, SR, and SPL) compared to the panoramic baseline ("Panorama w/o Depth feature"). This indicates the unfeasibility of direct transfer.
 - The inclusion of 3D Feature Fields ("w/ Feature Fields") led to a 12% improvement in SR compared to the "w/o Feature Fields" setting, confirming their role in enhancing perception from predicted novel views.
 - Further incorporating a "Memory" component (preserving feature fields from previous navigation examples within the same scene) yielded additional performance gains ("w/ Feature Fields + Memory"), indicating the adaptability of 3D Feature Fields to new environments and the benefit of lifelong learning strategies.

- **Semantic Traversable Map (Table 5):**
 - The study confirmed the critical roles of both the semantic map and the occupancy map in predicting traversable areas. Removing the semantic map ("w/o Semantic map") led to a reduction in SR (40.1% vs. 44.9% for "Full pipeline"), highlighting the importance of identifiable landmarks like doors and stairs for navigation. Similarly, removing the occupancy map ("w/o Occupancy map") also resulted in a performance decrease (SR 42.7%).
 - The learnable positional embeddings were also found to be important for the UNet model's spatial understanding, with their removal ("w/o Positional embeddings") reducing navigation performance (SR 42.3%).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) that addresses a key challenge in the field: the sim-to-real transfer of high-performance models to practical robotic platforms. By endowing monocular robots with a mechanism for panoramic perception through a Semantic Traversable Map and 3D Feature Fields, the work effectively bridges the gap between sophisticated VLN models trained in simulated environments with panoramic sensors and the hardware limitations of real-world monocular robots.

The primary significance lies in its contribution of a practical and high-performance solution for real-world VLN. Historically, robots equipped only with monocular cameras have exhibited limited navigation capabilities in VLN tasks due to their restricted field of view. Conversely, high-performing VLN models typically rely on panoramic inputs, which are not feasible for most physical robots due to cost and size constraints. This work directly confronts this dilemma by demonstrating that the capabilities of panoramic VLN models can be effectively transferred and utilized by monocular robots. The improved success rates observed in both simulated and, more notably, real-world environments indicate that this approach can enhance the reliability and effectiveness of language-guided navigation for physical agents.

The methodology's potential impact extends beyond VLN. The concept of augmenting a monocular robot's perception with synthesized panoramic views and an agent-centric traversable map could be beneficial for a broader range of embodied AI tasks. This includes, but is not limited to, object goal navigation, active perception, and general robotic exploration, where a comprehensive understanding of the surrounding environment from limited sensor data is crucial. The ability to dynamically construct and update 3D feature fields from monocular observations, and then decode novel view representations, offers a pathway for robots to build richer internal representations of their environment, thereby improving their decision-making in complex and unstructured settings.

Furthermore, the work contributes to the development of more generalizable and adaptable embodied AI systems. By demonstrating the efficacy of semantic traversable maps for waypoint prediction and 3D feature fields for dynamic perception, the research offers modular components that could be integrated into various robotic architectures.

A recognized limitation of the current approach is the increased path length and lower SPL metric in some cases, primarily due to the agent's need for more extensive exploration to gather sufficient observations for constructing the semantic traversable map and 3D feature fields. Addressing this limitation by improving the predictive capabilities for unobserved areas represents a future direction for enhancing the efficiency of the system. Nevertheless, the research provides a step towards enabling more capable and practical autonomous agents in diverse real-world applications.
