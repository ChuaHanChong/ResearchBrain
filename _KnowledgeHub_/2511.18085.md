# Continually Evolving Skill Knowledge in Vision Language Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University, University of Cambridge, and AgiBot developed Stellar VLA, a knowledge-driven continual learning framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, achieving over 50% average success rate improvement on LIBERO benchmarks. The framework autonomously evolves task and skill knowledge, demonstrating robust performance and effective knowledge retention in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models heavily rely on task-specific fine-tuning, making them inefficient for continuous, lifelong learning in dynamic environments.
- Current VLA models suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where learning new tasks degrades performance on previously acquired skills.
- Scaling traditional continual imitation learning methods to the complexity and parameter size of VLA models leads to prohibitive computational costs and extensive manual annotation requirements.

## Method

- **Stellar VLA** employs ==Dirichlet Process==-based non-parametric models (==DPMM== for T-Stellar, ==HDP== for TS-Stellar) to create an adaptively expanding knowledge space for task-centric or hierarchical task-skill knowledge.
- A self-supervised cycle, utilizing a ==Variational Autoencoder (VAE)== and ==variational inference==, allows latent task representations and the knowledge space to co-evolve autonomously.
- Learned knowledge embeddings dynamically guide a ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== action head, enabling adaptive expert routing for specialized policy execution and balanced parameter sharing without adding new network parameters.

## Results

- Stellar VLA variants demonstrated over **50%** average improvement in final success rates compared to baselines on LIBERO benchmarks, alongside better overall stability (AUC) and reduced catastrophic forgetting (NBT).
- In real-world dual-arm manipulation tasks, TS-Stellar achieved the highest AUC (**82.2%**) and Final SR (**83.3%**), while significantly reducing Negative Backward Transfer to **21.7%** compared to other methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the DP-based knowledge space and the knowledge-guided expert routing mechanism are critical components for the robust continual learning performance of Stellar VLA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Non-parametric Bayesian models like Dirichlet Processes provide an effective mechanism for adaptively managing an unbounded and evolving knowledge space, essential for open-ended continual learning.
> - A self-evolving feedback loop between perception-based latent representations and the knowledge space facilitates autonomous knowledge acquisition and retention, reducing reliance on manual supervision.
> - Integrating dynamically learned knowledge directly into the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing mechanism is crucial for preventing catastrophic forgetting and enabling efficient skill specialization and sharing within VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and Continual Learning (CL) in robotics, specifically Continual Imitation Learning (CIL).

Recent advancements in VLA models, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, π0.5, and UniVLA, have demonstrated capabilities in accumulating task experience from large-scale robotic datasets. These models exhibit strong task learning and cross-task generalization abilities by jointly modeling perception, language, and control. However, adapting these large-scale VLA models to continual learning scenarios presents significant challenges. Conventional CIL methods, which typically address catastrophic forgetting through strategies like skill expansion, parameter-efficient adaptation, or experience replay, often incur substantial training and storage overhead when applied to VLA models, which can comprise over a billion parameters. This overhead arises from the common reliance of CIL methods on additional adapters or task-specific modules that increase model complexity and data processing requirements.

Some recent work has indicated that pretrained VLA models can achieve reasonable continual learning performance with relatively simple experience replay strategies, questioning the necessity of increasingly complex CIL algorithmic designs. This paper aims to investigate whether VLA models can reduce the reliance on both extensive pretraining data and high data replay rates during continual learning, thereby achieving lower training costs and storage overhead, which are critical for scalable lifelong learning.

The authors propose that effective continual learning in VLA models requires capturing and modeling task and skill relationships from data. This perspective aligns with existing task-centric representation learning approaches, which have shown that explicitly modeling task knowledge can improve policy adaptation in multi-task settings. Furthermore, Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, known for their ability to enable dynamic routing across specialized experts, offer a mechanism to mitigate task interference. This research integrates these insights by proposing a framework that allows VLA models to learn latent task knowledge for policy evolution through adaptive specialization, building on the concept of a non-parametric knowledge space for structured skill modeling introduced in prior literature.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and scalable framework for Continual Imitation Learning (CIL) in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically addressing the challenges of catastrophic forgetting and the computational overhead associated with adapting large VLA models to sequential task learning.

The core motivations and specific objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Catastrophic Forgetting and Adaptation Efficiency in VLAs:** Existing VLA models demonstrate promising knowledge accumulation during pretraining but struggle with efficient continual adaptation. Traditional CIL methods, when applied to large VLA models, often introduce significant training and storage overhead due to their reliance on additional parameters, adapters, or external modules. The motivation is to overcome this limitation by developing a CIL method that is efficient and scalable for large VLA models without increasing their parameter count.
- **Reducing Data and Training Overhead:** The paper identifies the challenge of VLA models' dependence on large-scale pretraining data and the considerable data replay often required during continual learning. A key motivation is to reduce both the training cost and storage overhead associated with these requirements, moving towards more scalable lifelong learning.
- **Enabling Task and Skill Relationship Capture:** The authors hypothesize that effective continual learning necessitates the model's ability to capture and leverage task and skill relationships inherent in the data. This motivates the design of a system that can explicitly learn and organize this knowledge.
- **Investigating Adaptive Specialization through Latent Task Knowledge:** A central question posed is whether VLA models can learn latent task knowledge to facilitate continual policy evolution through adaptive specialization. This involves exploring mechanisms that allow the model to dynamically specialize for new tasks while retaining previously learned knowledge.
- **Developing a Self-Evolving Knowledge Space:** To address the need for capturing task and skill relationships, the research aims to design a knowledge space that can evolve autonomously. This includes modeling task-relevant knowledge without predefined component numbers, and supporting both task-centric and hierarchical task-skill structures.
- **Guiding Policy Adaptation without Parameter Increase:** A significant objective is to integrate the learned knowledge structure into the policy execution mechanism to enable task specialization and mitigate interference. This integration should be achieved without increasing the size of the underlying VLA model, which is crucial for scalability. This motivates the development of a knowledge-guided expert routing mechanism within a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture.

In summary, the motivation is to advance the field of lifelong robot learning by enabling VLA models to continually acquire new skills and adapt to new tasks efficiently, robustly, and without incurring prohibitive computational or storage costs, thereby addressing a critical bottleneck in deploying intelligent agents in dynamic real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces **Stellar VLA**, an end-to-end framework for Continual Imitation Learning (CIL) in vision-language-based manipulation. The framework aims to continually evolve task-relevant knowledge, allowing the model to acquire new skills while mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

The approach is structured around three main components: a Dirichlet-Process-based knowledge space, a self-evolving learning scheme, and knowledge-guided expert routing.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is defined within a CIL setting where an agent learns sequentially from a stream of tasks {Tj}∞j=1. Each task *j* provides a set of expert demonstrations {τnj}Nn=1, comprising language instructions (l), observations (o), and actions (a). Unlike multi-task learning, CIL restricts access to past data to a small replay buffer (B), containing *q*% of past demonstrations, to simulate realistic lifelong learning demands.

#### 3.2. Dirichlet-Process-based Knowledge Space

To capture structured and interpretable task knowledge, latent features are organized into a dynamically evolving knowledge space using a Dirichlet Process (DP) prior, denoted as G ∼ DP(α, G0). This non-parametric approach allows for an unbounded number of components (clusters), enabling new knowledge components to emerge as tasks evolve. Two variants are designed:

- **Task-Centric Knowledge Modeling (T-Stellar):** A Dirichlet Process Mixture Model (DPMM) is instantiated to model task-level structure. Task latents *zj* for task *j* are sampled from a task-specific distribution Ftask(θj), where θj is drawn from G. Assuming Gaussian components, this induces dynamic clustering of task representations.
- **Hierarchical Task-Skill Knowledge Modeling (TS-Stellar):** A Hierarchical Dirichlet Process (HDP) is adopted to model shared subskills across tasks. Each task *j* is modeled as a distribution over skills. Skill-level latent variables *zji* are generated via a nested DP structure: *zji* ∼ Fskill(θji), θji ∼ Gj, Gj ∼ DP(γ, G), and G ∼ DP(α, G0). The overall task distribution parameter θtaskj is obtained by aggregating its skill components, allowing for skill sharing across tasks and forming a hierarchical task-skill knowledge space.

#### 3.3. Knowledge Space Learning via Self-Evolution

This unified learning framework enables task-centric representations and the Dirichlet-Process-based knowledge space to co-evolve. A hierarchical variational inference approach is employed:

- **Task-Centric Representation Learning:** Task-centric representations *zj* are learned from vision-language abstractions using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE). The VAE is trained with a reconstruction loss (Lrecon) to ensure semantic consistency and a Dirichlet-Process-aware KL divergence term (LKL) to regularize the latent space, aligning it with current knowledge clusters. For TS-Stellar, a hierarchical VAE jointly models task- and skill-level latents, decoding language goals (l) and visual observations (o) respectively, with corresponding hierarchical KL regularization.
- **Knowledge Space Distribution Learning:** To update the knowledge distribution (Θ), latent representations {zj} are treated as observations of G. Variational inference, specifically Memoized Variational Bayes (memoVB), is used to approximate the posterior *p*(Θ, G | {zj}). MemoVB facilitates efficient incremental updates and enables global statistic sharing across tasks. This process creates a self-evolving cycle: task-centric representation learning provides features for updating the knowledge distribution, while the knowledge space, via the KL loss, constrains *zj* to conform to inferred task or skill hypotheses.

#### 3.4. Action-Knowledge-guided Expert Routing

After learning the knowledge space and task-centric latents, a knowledge-routed Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module is introduced to allocate task-specific action prediction parameters, aiming to minimize interference between unrelated tasks while enhancing sharing among related ones. This leverages a diffusion-based MoE action head, similar to MoDE, but with knowledge-based routing.

- **Knowledge Embedding Formats:** Since DPMM and HDP produce variable numbers of clusters, two knowledge embedding formats are designed:
 - **Knowledge Relation Embedding (fR):** Measures the distance between a latent *z* and cluster centers μk, weighted by the membership probability *pk* (posterior assignment probability of *z* to cluster *k*).
 - **Top-K Semantic Embedding (fS):** Represents the discrete skill semantic affiliation of *z* by aggregating learnable embeddings of the top-K assigned clusters.
- **Expert Routing Mechanism:** The combined knowledge embedding *fknow* = [*z* ∥ *fR* ∥ *fS*] is introduced as an additional guidance token (*eknow*). This token, along with a noise embedding (*enoise*), is concatenated and fed into the MoE router. This enables task-aware expert allocation, allowing for balanced parameter sharing and isolation without increasing the overall model size.

The overall architecture integrates a pretrained vision-language encoder (CLIP, FiLM-conditioned ResNet) for input processing, a VAE for latent representation learning, and a diffusion-based transformer action head with the knowledge-prior-routed MoE. The training objective combines VAE losses (reconstruction and KL divergence), a score-matching loss for the denoising module, and a load-balancing loss to prevent expert collapse in the MoE. The training process involves continuous updates of VAE and action head parameters, with periodic updates to the Dirichlet-Process-based knowledge space using a dedicated knowledge buffer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several key findings across simulation and real-world experiments, as well as through ablation studies and visualizations.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation on LIBERO Benchmark

Experiments were conducted on LIBERO-goal, LIBERO-long, and LIBERO-30* (a 30-task subset of LIBERO-90), comparing Stellar VLA variants (T-Stellar and TS-Stellar) against advanced VLA models (MoDE, UniVLA, π0, π0.5) and robotic CIL methods (Experience Replay (ER), SeqLoRA, LoTUS, IsCiL). Both scratch and pretrained settings (cross-embodiment or LIBERO-90) were evaluated with 1% data replay.

- **Overall Performance:** T-Stellar and TS-Stellar demonstrated superior performance, achieving the highest AUC (area under the success rate curve) and final success rates across all evaluated scenarios.
- **Scratch Setting:** In the scratch setting, Stellar VLAs achieved over 50% average improvement in AUC and Final SR compared to all VLA and CIL baselines.
- **Pretrained Setting:** Under heterogeneous pretraining, Stellar VLAs outperformed state-of-the-art large VLA baselines, despite having significantly fewer parameters (~1B compared to ~3-7B for π series and UniVLA). Pretraining on LIBERO-90 yielded even more stable performance due to better domain alignment.
- **Data Replay Efficiency:** The models achieved strong continual learning performance with only 1% data replay, indicating high data efficiency.
- **TS-Stellar for Long-Horizon Tasks:** TS-Stellar specifically excelled on long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-long), suggesting the efficacy of its hierarchical task-skill modeling for complex sequences.

#### 4.2. Real-World Evaluation on Dual-Arm Manipulation

Real-world experiments involved seven dual-arm manipulation tasks, including single-arm and bimanual operations, to validate performance on a new embodiment and with compositional manipulation. Models were trained with a 5% replay rate.

- **Superior CIL Ability:** Stellar VLAs outperformed all VLA and ER baselines in AUC and Final SR, while maintaining strong NBT (negative backward transfer) and FWT (forward transfer).
- **TS-Stellar for Hierarchical Actions:** TS-Stellar surpassed T-Stellar across all metrics, indicating that task-skill modeling is particularly effective for complex hierarchical actions and long-horizon skill sequences inherent in dual-arm manipulation.
- **Forgetting Mitigation:** Visualizations demonstrated that Stellar VLAs more effectively mitigated catastrophic forgetting in dual-arm tasks, maintaining accurate grasping and coordinated handover behaviors, whereas baselines like UniVLA and π0.5 exhibited failures at different subtask stages.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies investigated the contributions of the Dirichlet-Process-based knowledge space and knowledge-guided expert routing.

- **Knowledge Space Ablation:** Replacing the Dirichlet-Process-based knowledge space with a standard Gaussian (w/o KS) or removing latent task-centric representation learning (w/o VAE) led to degraded performance. The knowledge space improved CIL by providing global task distribution information via LKL supervision, reducing feature overlap and mitigating interference between tasks.
- **Knowledge Guidance for Expert Routing:** Both the Knowledge Relation Embedding (fR) and Top-K Semantic Embedding (fS) were found to contribute positively to performance. Full knowledge guidance (fR + fS) resulted in higher AUC and Final SR, with fR capturing relevance to global knowledge space for new expert combinations, and fS encoding latent task assignments for activating task-relevant experts.

#### 4.4. Visualization for Continual Learning Process

t-SNE plots of latent representations and knowledge space components revealed the self-evolving nature of Stellar VLA.

- **Knowledge Space Evolution:** T-Stellar formed distinct clusters for new tasks, while TS-Stellar captured inter-task skill relations, creating an overlapping task-skill knowledge space. This demonstrated skill acquisition while retaining prior knowledge, with related subgoals causing overlaps in skill clusters for TS-Stellar.
- **Expert Usage Analysis:** Expert activation patterns showed task-dependent clustering, indicating that knowledge-guided expert routing induced task specialization.

#### 4.5. Computational and Engineering Analysis

- **Efficiency:** The introduction of knowledge space modeling in Stellar VLA incurred negligible overhead in terms of parameter count and inference latency compared to MoDE. Stellar VLA achieved strong performance with approximately one-third to one-seventh the parameters of the π series and UniVLA, respectively, highlighting a parameter-efficient design.
- **Training Time:** The training time for Stellar VLA on a single H200 GPU was comparable to or less than that of larger VLA models requiring multiple GPUs, with knowledge space updates adding approximately 2% overhead.
- **Storage:** A 1% replay ratio for continual learning resulted in a modest storage requirement (e.g., less than 500MB for 30 tasks), supporting the claim of efficient storage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Stellar VLA framework presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic continual learning, particularly for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with several notable implications:

- **Addressing Scalability Challenges in Lifelong Robot Learning:** The primary significance lies in offering an efficient and scalable solution for continual learning in VLA models without the conventional overhead of increasing network parameters. This addresses a critical bottleneck that has hindered the deployment of lifelong learning agents in complex, dynamic environments. By demonstrating robust performance with only 1% data replay, the framework also significantly reduces storage and computational costs associated with experience replay, making lifelong learning more practical.
- **Novelty in Knowledge Representation and Utilization:** The introduction of a Dirichlet-Process-based, self-evolving knowledge space for modeling task-centric or hierarchical task-skill relations is a novel approach. This non-parametric design allows the model to dynamically adapt the complexity of its knowledge structure, identifying new components as tasks emerge without prior specification. This explicit modeling of task knowledge provides a structured way for VLA models to understand and retain relationships between skills and tasks.
- **Effective Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting:** The research demonstrates consistent and robust improvements in mitigating catastrophic forgetting compared to both state-of-the-art VLA and CIL baselines. This is achieved through the joint optimization of task representations and the knowledge distribution, coupled with knowledge-guided expert routing that enables balanced parameter sharing and specialization. This enhanced ability to retain prior knowledge while acquiring new skills is crucial for any lifelong learning system.
- **Validation in Diverse Settings:** The strong performance observed across a range of tasks in both simulation (LIBERO benchmark) and real-world dual-arm manipulation settings validates the framework's robustness and generalizability. The success in real-world scenarios, particularly with complex bimanual operations, underscores its potential for practical robotic applications.
- **Insights into Task Knowledge Understanding:** The visualization studies provide valuable insights into how VLA models can learn, organize, and utilize task and skill knowledge. The distinct clustering for tasks and overlapping skill components offer an interpretable view of the model's internal representation, which can guide future research in designing more transparent and controllable robotic learning systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** Stellar VLA provides a foundation for several future research directions. These include exploring larger and more complex skill spaces, developing dynamic hyperparameter adaptation mechanisms for the knowledge space, and scaling the framework to even larger VLA models. Furthermore, the interpretability offered by the knowledge space could be leveraged to achieve more robust few-shot or zero-shot generalization and task-adaptive knowledge updates, moving towards truly autonomous and adaptable robots.

In essence, this work advances the feasibility of creating continuously learning robotic agents by providing a more efficient, robust, and interpretable framework for knowledge management within powerful Vision-Language-Action models.
