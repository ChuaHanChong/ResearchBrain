# Continually Evolving Skill Knowledge in Vision Language Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University, University of Cambridge, and AgiBot developed Stellar VLA, a knowledge-driven continual learning framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, achieving over 50% average success rate improvement on LIBERO benchmarks. The framework autonomously evolves task and skill knowledge, demonstrating robust performance and effective knowledge retention in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models heavily rely on task-specific fine-tuning, making them inefficient for continuous, lifelong learning in dynamic environments.
- Current VLA models suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where learning new tasks degrades performance on previously acquired skills.
- Scaling traditional continual imitation learning methods to the complexity and parameter size of VLA models leads to prohibitive computational costs and extensive manual annotation requirements.

## Method

- **Stellar VLA** employs ==Dirichlet Process==-based non-parametric models (==DPMM== for T-Stellar, ==HDP== for TS-Stellar) to create an adaptively expanding knowledge space for task-centric or hierarchical task-skill knowledge.
- A self-supervised cycle, utilizing a ==Variational Autoencoder (VAE)== and ==variational inference==, allows latent task representations and the knowledge space to co-evolve autonomously.
- Learned knowledge embeddings dynamically guide a ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== action head, enabling adaptive expert routing for specialized policy execution and balanced parameter sharing without adding new network parameters.

## Results

- Stellar VLA variants demonstrated over **50%** average improvement in final success rates compared to baselines on LIBERO benchmarks, alongside better overall stability (AUC) and reduced catastrophic forgetting (NBT).
- In real-world dual-arm manipulation tasks, TS-Stellar achieved the highest AUC (**82.2%**) and Final SR (**83.3%**), while significantly reducing Negative Backward Transfer to **21.7%** compared to other methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the DP-based knowledge space and the knowledge-guided expert routing mechanism are critical components for the robust continual learning performance of Stellar VLA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Non-parametric Bayesian models like Dirichlet Processes provide an effective mechanism for adaptively managing an unbounded and evolving knowledge space, essential for open-ended continual learning.
> - A self-evolving feedback loop between perception-based latent representations and the knowledge space facilitates autonomous knowledge acquisition and retention, reducing reliance on manual supervision.
> - Integrating dynamically learned knowledge directly into the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing mechanism is crucial for preventing catastrophic forgetting and enabling efficient skill specialization and sharing within VLA models.
