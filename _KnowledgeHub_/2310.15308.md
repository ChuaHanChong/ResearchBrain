# SAM-CLIP: Merging Vision Foundation Models towards Semantic and Spatial Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> SAM-CLIP unifies the vision encoders of SAM and CLIP into a single model, enabling state-of-the-art zero-shot semantic segmentation by combining semantic and spatial understanding. This merged architecture largely retains the individual capabilities of its constituent models while significantly reducing inference costs for edge device deployment.

## Problem

- Deploying separate Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) like CLIP and SAM is inefficient, leading to high memory and computational resource demands for inference, particularly on edge devices.
- Specialized VFMs (e.g., CLIP for semantics, SAM for spatial segmentation) lack a unified representation for tasks requiring both types of understanding.
- Training multi-task VFMs from scratch is prohibitively expensive in terms of data and computation, and simple model merging often results in catastrophic forgetting of original capabilities.

## Method

- A two-stage ==rehearsal-based multi-task distillation== process merges SAM's vision encoder with a lightweight CLIP head into a single backbone, keeping other VFM components frozen.
- Uses a combined loss function (==CLIP teacher distillation== and ==SAM self-distillation==) with differentiated learning rates for different model parts to balance knowledge transfer and prevent catastrophic forgetting.
- Leverages limited subsets of the original pre-training datasets for efficient ==memory replay== during the merging process.

## Results

- Achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot semantic segmentation, outperforming previous models by **+6.8%** mean IoU on Pascal-VOC (**60.6%**) and **+5.9%** on COCO-Stuff (**31.5%**).
- Largely retains the zero-shot classification capabilities of CLIP (e.g., **72.4%** accuracy on ImageNet) and the instance segmentation performance of SAM (e.g., **40.9 mAP** on COCO) with minimal forgetting.
- Reduces the computational cost and data requirements for merging to less than **10%** of the original pre-training, making it efficient for deployment on edge devices.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intelligently merging specialized VFMs can create a unified model with richer, more holistic visual representations that combine high-frequency localization and low-frequency semantic information.
> - Emergent capabilities, such as state-of-the-art zero-shot semantic segmentation, can arise from the synergistic combination of distinct VFM expertise within a single architecture.
> - Efficient continual learning and knowledge distillation techniques are effective for transferring knowledge and preserving original model strengths for large VFMs, without requiring full multi-task pre-training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the burgeoning field of **Vision Foundation Models (VFMs)**, a landscape rapidly populated by powerful, pre-trained models capable of performing a wide array of visual tasks. These models, exemplified by **CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training)** and **SAM (Segment Anything Model)**, are characterized by their "open-form" or "zero-shot" capabilities, meaning they can generalize to novel tasks or categories without explicit fine-tuning on domain-specific data.

However, a critical challenge arises from the distinct pre-training objectives of these VFMs, which endow them with specialized, often complementary, capabilities:
- **CLIP** excels in **semantic understanding**, trained on vast image-text pairs to align visual and textual embeddings. This allows it to perform zero-shot classification and image-text retrieval with remarkable accuracy. Its features tend to capture low-frequency signals and global semantic content.
- **SAM** specializes in **spatial understanding** and segmentation, pre-trained on an enormous dataset of 1.1 billion masks. It can generate high-quality segmentation masks from various geometric prompts (points, boxes, masks) but has limited intrinsic semantic understanding. Its features leverage high-frequency signals and local localization knowledge.

The traditional approach of deploying these VFMs independently presents significant inefficiencies:
1. **High Memory Footprint and Runtime**: Running separate models for different tasks (e.g., one for semantic classification, another for segmentation) demands substantial memory and computational resources, making them less suitable for resource-constrained environments like edge devices.
2. **Lack of Cross-Model Learning**: Keeping models separate misses opportunities for synergistic learning, where combined capabilities could unlock new functionalities.

**Multi-task learning** is an existing paradigm that aims to address these issues by training a single model on multiple objectives simultaneously. However, the paper highlights its challenges in the context of VFMs:
- **Costly Training**: Training foundation models from scratch with multiple objectives requires even more substantial computational resources and massive datasets, often exceeding the already demanding requirements of single-objective VFM pre-training.
- **Data Access and Mixing**: Accessing and effectively mixing proprietary pre-training datasets for different VFMs is challenging.
- **Gradient Interference**: Multi-task training can suffer from interfering gradients and instabilities, complicating optimization.

This paper positions itself as a solution that bridges the gap between inefficient separate deployments and challenging multi-task training from scratch, as well as the limitations of existing "training-free" model merging techniques. While many merging techniques focus on simply combining model weights without additional training (e.g., weight interpolation, task arithmetic), these often lead to "catastrophic forgetting" or decreased performance when models are trained with fundamentally different objectives.

"SAM-CLIP" introduces a novel, data-dependent merging approach that leverages **continual learning** and **knowledge distillation**. It aims to efficiently consolidate the expertise of distinct VFMs into a single unified architecture, enabling multi-task capabilities, reducing inference costs, and fostering synergistic functionalities without requiring full-scale multi-task pre-training or suffering from catastrophic forgetting. This work contributes to the broader trend of creating more generalist, efficient, and robust AI models, particularly relevant for on-device deployment and advanced multimodal understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the SAM-CLIP research can be summarized as follows:

1. **Overcome Inefficiencies of Separate VFM Deployment**: The core motivation stems from the practical inefficiencies of deploying and managing multiple specialized vision foundation models (VFMs) like CLIP and SAM independently. Each model has a large memory footprint and requires its own computational resources for inference, making deployment on edge devices (e.g., mobile phones, smart cameras) challenging. The goal is to create a single, unified model that reduces this overhead.
2. **Harmonize Complementary Capabilities**: CLIP excels at semantic understanding, recognizing "what" is in an image, while SAM is proficient in spatial understanding, precisely segmenting "where" objects are. The paper aims to merge these distinct yet complementary capabilities into a single model, creating a richer visual representation that combines both semantic and spatial knowledge.
3. **Enable Synergistic Functionalities**: Beyond simply combining existing tasks, a key objective is to demonstrate that merging these models can lead to emergent, synergistic capabilities for new tasks. Specifically, the paper targets **zero-shot semantic segmentation**—a task requiring both semantic understanding from text prompts and precise segmentation—as a prime example of such an emergent ability. This task cannot be effectively performed by either CLIP or SAM alone in a single, efficient inference pass.
4. **Efficient Knowledge Transfer and Forgetting Mitigation**: The research aims to develop an efficient merging recipe that avoids the prohibitive computational and data costs of training a multi-task VFM from scratch. Simultaneously, it seeks to prevent "catastrophic forgetting" of the original VFMs' capabilities during the merging process, ensuring that the unified model retains its foundational strengths.
5. **Practical Application for Edge Devices**: A significant practical motivation is to create a merged VFM that is well-suited for edge device applications. This implies a focus on reducing storage and computational costs during inference while maintaining high performance.

In essence, SAM-CLIP seeks to create a more compact, versatile, and intelligent VFM by intelligently combining the best attributes of existing specialized models, thereby paving the way for more advanced and efficient vision AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SAM-CLIP methodology proposes a "simple recipe" to efficiently merge Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) by integrating techniques from multi-task learning, continual learning, and knowledge distillation. The approach focuses on combining the vision encoders of two distinct VFMs into a single backbone while preserving their unique capabilities and enabling new synergistic tasks.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

- **Base VFM Selection**: The Segment Anything Model (SAM) is chosen as the base VFM, specifically its ViT-B/16 (Vision Transformer-Base with 16x16 patch size) architecture comprising 12 transformer layers. This choice is driven by SAM's efficient handling of high-resolution images, a crucial aspect for segmentation tasks.
- **Auxiliary VFM**: CLIP (ViT-B/16) serves as the auxiliary VFM, providing semantic understanding.
- **Unified Backbone (Enc_SAM-CLIP)**: The image encoder of SAM (Enc_SAM) is adopted and initialized as the shared backbone, Enc_SAM-CLIP.
- **Multi-Head Architecture**:
 - **Head_SAM**: Retains SAM's prompt encoder (PromptEnc_SAM) and mask decoder (MaskDec_SAM), initialized from the original SAM model, responsible for instance segmentation.
 - **Head_CLIP**: A lightweight CLIP head, consisting of 3 additional transformer layers, is appended to the shared backbone. Its patch token outputs are processed by a max-pooling layer to produce an image-level embedding, mimicking CLIP's original image encoder output. This head is initialized with random weights or weights from SAM's last transformer layer. The text encoder of CLIP (TextEnc_CLIP) remains frozen and is used to generate text embeddings for alignment.
 - Crucially, other modality encoders (PromptEnc_SAM and TextEnc_CLIP) remain **frozen** throughout the merging process, simplifying training and focusing on the vision backbone.

#### 3.2. Dataset Preparation

- **Limited Data Access**: The method assumes access to only *small subsets* of the original pre-training datasets, functioning as "memory replay" data in a continual learning context.
- **D_CLIP**: Merged from CC3M, CC12M, YFCC-15M, and ImageNet-21k, totaling 40.6 million unlabeled images.
- **D_SAM**: A 5.7% subset of the SA-1B dataset, containing 11 million images and 1.1 billion masks.
- **Merged-41M**: The combined training dataset of 40.8 million images (D_CLIP ∪ D_SAM).

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Process (Rehearsal-Based Multi-task Distillation)

The training is structured into two main phases to optimize convergence and mitigate catastrophic forgetting:

**Stage I: Head Probing (CLIP Head Training)**
- **Objective**: To learn reasonable initial parameters for the randomly initialized Head_CLIP.
- **Process**: The image backbone (Enc_SAM-CLIP) is **frozen**. Only Head_CLIP is trained.
- **Loss**: Cosine distillation loss (L_CLIP) is applied, aligning the pooled output of Head_CLIP (from Enc_SAM-CLIP) with the embeddings from the frozen, original CLIP encoder (Enc_CLIP) acting as a teacher.
- **Data**: Training is performed solely on D_CLIP.
- **Duration**: 20 epochs.

**Stage II: Multi-task Distillation (Joint Fine-tuning)**
- **Objective**: To allow all heads and the shared backbone (Enc_SAM-CLIP) to learn jointly, transferring knowledge from both teachers while preserving original capabilities.
- **Process**: Enc_SAM-CLIP, Head_SAM, and Head_CLIP are all **learnable**.
- **Loss**: A combined multi-task loss (L_CLIP + λL_SAM) is used.
 - **L_CLIP**: Continues from Stage I, distilling knowledge from the frozen CLIP teacher.
 - **L_SAM**: Self-distillation from the original frozen SAM teacher. It uses a linear combination of Focal and Dice losses to align the Head_SAM output (from Enc_SAM-CLIP) with the segmentation mask scores produced by the original SAM model (MaskDec_SAM(Enc_SAM(x))).
 - **Loss Ratio**: λ is set to 10 (L_CLIP : L_SAM = 1:10) to balance learning new semantic knowledge with preserving segmentation abilities.
- **Data**: Training is performed on D_SAM ∪ D_CLIP. Batches are sampled from both datasets, with D_CLIP samples contributing to L_CLIP and D_SAM samples to L_SAM.
- **Learning Rates**: Crucially, a **10x smaller learning rate** is applied to Enc_SAM-CLIP and Head_SAM parameters compared to Head_CLIP to reduce forgetting of SAM's original knowledge.
- **Mixed Input Resolution Strategy**: SAM distillation uses 1024px, while CLIP distillation uses variable resolutions (224/448px), reflecting their original training conditions.
- **Duration**: 16 epochs (with early stopping).

#### 3.4. Resolution Adaptation (Optional)

- **Objective**: To enable both CLIP and SAM heads to operate efficiently on a single, high-resolution input (1024px) without passing the image twice.
- **Process**: After the two main stages, Enc_SAM-CLIP is frozen, and Head_CLIP is fine-tuned for 3 epochs with L_CLIP, using variable resolutions including 1024px.

This comprehensive approach allows SAM-CLIP to absorb semantic knowledge from CLIP while maintaining SAM's spatial expertise, all within an efficient training framework designed to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SAM-CLIP research yields several significant findings across various evaluation metrics, demonstrating its efficacy in merging Vision Foundation Models.

#### 4.1. Efficiency and Resource Reduction

- **Computational Cost**: The proposed merging method demands significantly less computational cost and data compared to training multi-task foundation models from scratch. It requires less than 10% of the data and computational cost of their original pre-training.
- **Inference Efficiency**: SAM-CLIP achieves a unified vision backbone, reducing storage and compute costs for inference compared to deploying SAM and CLIP independently. This makes it particularly well-suited for edge device applications, as only one model needs to be loaded and a single forward pass of the backbone is required per image.

#### 4.2. Retention of Foundational Capabilities

- **Zero-Shot Classification (CLIP)**: SAM-CLIP largely retains CLIP's zero-shot classification capabilities. On benchmarks like ImageNet, ImageNet-v2, and Places-365, it achieves accuracies comparable to state-of-the-art CLIP ViT-B models (e.g., 72.4% on ImageNet, 63.2% on ImageNet-v2, 43.6% on Places-365). It even outperforms some baseline CLIP models on image retrieval tasks (e.g., 79.2% R@1 on Flickr30K, 49.3% R@1 on COCO), validating the effective transfer of CLIP's knowledge.
- **Zero-Shot Instance Segmentation (SAM)**: The merged model demonstrates minimal catastrophic forgetting of SAM's original instance segmentation abilities. It achieves performance very close to the original SAM ViT-B model on COCO (40.9 mAP) and LVIS (35.0 mAP) datasets, confirming that the self-distillation and learning rate strategies effectively preserve SAM's expertise (Table 1).

#### 4.3. Emergent Synergistic Functionality: Zero-Shot Semantic Segmentation

- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: SAM-CLIP establishes new state-of-the-art results on zero-shot semantic segmentation across 5 benchmarks (Pascal VOC, Pascal-Context, ADE20k, COCO-Stuff, COCO-Panoptic). It significantly outperforms previous models specifically designed for this task, achieving substantial gains such as +6.8% mean IoU on Pascal-VOC (60.6% mIoU for SAM-CLIP vs. 53.8% for OVS) and +5.9% on COCO-Stuff (31.5% mIoU for SAM-CLIP vs. 26.5% for SegCLIP) (Table 2).
- **Mechanism**: This emergent capability is attributed to the successful combination of CLIP's semantic understanding (via the CLIP-head and text encoder) and SAM's localization knowledge. The CLIP-head can predict semantic segmentation masks directly from text prompts.

#### 4.4. Richer Visual Representations

- **Head-Probing Evaluations**: Comparative head-probing experiments on semantic segmentation datasets (Pascal VOC, ADE20k) show that SAM-CLIP learns richer visual features than individual SAM or CLIP. When a task-specific head is trained on a frozen backbone, SAM-CLIP consistently outperforms both parent models across different head structures (Linear, DeepLabv3, PSPNet). For instance, on Pascal VOC, SAM-CLIP achieves an average of 78.8% with head probing, compared to 62.6% for SAM and 76.4% for CLIP (Table 3, Figure 4). This confirms the acquisition of both high-frequency localization and low-frequency semantic information.
- **Linear Probing for Classification**: Linear probing on image classification tasks (ImageNet, Places365) also shows SAM-CLIP achieving comparable performance to CLIP models (e.g., 80.5% on ImageNet), indicating that its image-level representation is well-learned (Table 4).

#### 4.5. Composable Heads for Enhanced Performance

- **CLIP + SAM Head Composition**: The paper demonstrates that by composing both the CLIP and SAM heads, further improvements in zero-shot semantic segmentation can be achieved. By using the CLIP-head to generate initial low-resolution semantic masks, then converting these into point prompts for the SAM-head, a more fine-grained segmentation can be produced. This pipeline (requiring only one forward pass of the backbone at 1024px) boosts performance on Pascal VOC to 66.0% mIoU, a significant gain over using only the CLIP-head at 448px (60.6%) (Table 5). This highlights the true synergy enabled by the merged architecture.

In summary, SAM-CLIP successfully merges two distinct VFMs into a single, efficient model. It not only preserves their individual strengths but also unlocks new, state-of-the-art capabilities like zero-shot semantic segmentation through a synergistic combination of semantic and spatial understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "SAM-CLIP: Merging Vision Foundation Models towards Semantic and Spatial Understanding" paper presents a significant advancement in the field of Vision Foundation Models, with profound implications for the development and deployment of future AI systems.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Model Development and Deployment

- **Unified, Efficient AI Systems**: The most immediate impact is the demonstration of creating unified, multi-task models from existing specialized VFMs without the exorbitant costs of training from scratch. This offers a pragmatic pathway for developing more comprehensive AI systems that can handle diverse vision tasks with a single backbone.
- **Democratization of Advanced AI**: By drastically reducing computational and data requirements for merging (less than 10% of original pre-training), SAM-CLIP makes advanced multi-task VFM development more accessible to researchers and organizations with limited resources, compared to the few entities capable of training massive foundation models.
- **Edge AI Enablement**: The reduced storage footprint and single-pass inference capability of SAM-CLIP make it exceptionally well-suited for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices (e.g., smartphones, drones, IoT devices). This could unlock a new generation of intelligent applications directly on devices, enhancing user experience with real-time, context-aware vision capabilities while potentially improving privacy by reducing cloud reliance.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Vision Understanding and Synergistic Capabilities

- **Holistic Visual Representations**: The merged model's ability to combine semantic understanding from CLIP and spatial localization from SAM creates a richer, more holistic visual representation. This is crucial for tasks requiring both "what" an object is and "where" it is located with precision.
- **Emergence of New Capabilities**: The state-of-the-art performance in zero-shot semantic segmentation is a prime example of synergistic functionality that arises from this merger. This task cannot be efficiently addressed by either original VFM alone. This suggests a powerful avenue for future research: identifying and unlocking other latent capabilities through intelligent VFM merging.
- **Foundation for Multimodal AI**: By merging a vision-language model (CLIP) with a vision-only model (SAM), SAM-CLIP hints at broader possibilities for constructing more sophisticated multimodal foundation models that seamlessly integrate information from various modalities (vision, language, geometry, audio, etc.).

#### 5.3. Advancements in Continual Learning and Distillation

- **Practical Continual Learning**: The paper provides a successful, practical demonstration of continual learning principles (memory rehearsal, multi-task distillation) to prevent catastrophic forgetting when adding new knowledge to a pre-trained VFM. This validates these techniques for large-scale, high-impact models.
- **Multi-Teacher Distillation**: The use of two distinct teachers (CLIP and SAM) to impart different forms of knowledge into a single student backbone is a notable extension of traditional knowledge distillation, showcasing its flexibility in complex scenarios.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

- **Optimal Merging Strategies**: The "simple recipe" might inspire more complex or automated merging strategies, perhaps exploring different backbone architectures, distillation techniques, or dynamic λ coefficients.
- **Generalizing to More VFMs**: The success with SAM and CLIP could be extended to merging other VFMs (e.g., DINOv2 for self-supervised features, MAE for reconstruction) to create even more versatile generalist vision models.
- **Addressing Limitations**: While the paper acknowledges limitations (inherited biases, dependency on base VFM architecture), its success opens avenues for research into robust merging that mitigates these factors and explores adaptive architectures.

In conclusion, SAM-CLIP is not just an incremental improvement but a significant step towards the creation of truly versatile, efficient, and intelligent foundation models capable of complex, open-world understanding. Its impact will likely be felt across diverse applications, from enhancing user experiences on consumer devices to enabling more sophisticated robotic vision and AI-driven content creation.
