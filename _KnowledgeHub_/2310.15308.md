# SAM-CLIP: Merging Vision Foundation Models towards Semantic and Spatial Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> SAM-CLIP unifies the vision encoders of SAM and CLIP into a single model, enabling state-of-the-art zero-shot semantic segmentation by combining semantic and spatial understanding. This merged architecture largely retains the individual capabilities of its constituent models while significantly reducing inference costs for edge device deployment.

## Problem

- Deploying separate Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) like CLIP and SAM is inefficient, leading to high memory and computational resource demands for inference, particularly on edge devices.
- Specialized VFMs (e.g., CLIP for semantics, SAM for spatial segmentation) lack a unified representation for tasks requiring both types of understanding.
- Training multi-task VFMs from scratch is prohibitively expensive in terms of data and computation, and simple model merging often results in catastrophic forgetting of original capabilities.

## Method

- A two-stage ==rehearsal-based multi-task distillation== process merges SAM's vision encoder with a lightweight CLIP head into a single backbone, keeping other VFM components frozen.
- Uses a combined loss function (==CLIP teacher distillation== and ==SAM self-distillation==) with differentiated learning rates for different model parts to balance knowledge transfer and prevent catastrophic forgetting.
- Leverages limited subsets of the original pre-training datasets for efficient ==memory replay== during the merging process.

## Results

- Achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot semantic segmentation, outperforming previous models by **+6.8%** mean IoU on Pascal-VOC (**60.6%**) and **+5.9%** on COCO-Stuff (**31.5%**).
- Largely retains the zero-shot classification capabilities of CLIP (e.g., **72.4%** accuracy on ImageNet) and the instance segmentation performance of SAM (e.g., **40.9 mAP** on COCO) with minimal forgetting.
- Reduces the computational cost and data requirements for merging to less than **10%** of the original pre-training, making it efficient for deployment on edge devices.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intelligently merging specialized VFMs can create a unified model with richer, more holistic visual representations that combine high-frequency localization and low-frequency semantic information.
> - Emergent capabilities, such as state-of-the-art zero-shot semantic segmentation, can arise from the synergistic combination of distinct VFM expertise within a single architecture.
> - Efficient continual learning and knowledge distillation techniques are effective for transferring knowledge and preserving original model strengths for large VFMs, without requiring full multi-task pre-training.
