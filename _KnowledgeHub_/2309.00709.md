# Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback for Realistic Traffic Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TrafficRLHF, a model-agnostic framework, applies Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) to enhance the realism of traffic simulations. It fine-tunes existing traffic models using human preferences, quantitatively reducing unrealistic behaviors like collisions and road departures while improving alignment with human perceptions of realism across diverse traffic generators.

## Problem

- Existing traffic simulation models, both rule-based and learning-based, struggle to consistently generate diverse and realistic traffic scenarios that align with human perceptions.
- The high costs and inherent safety risks of real-world autonomous vehicle (AV) testing necessitate more reliable and perceptually realistic simulation environments.
- It is challenging to capture and integrate nuanced, subjective human preferences for 'realistic' driving behaviors into simulation models using explicit rules or objective functions.

## Method

- A three-stage ==Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF)== framework, named **TrafficRLHF**, is introduced to incorporate human preferences for realism into traffic models.
- The framework collects human feedback by presenting annotators with paired or grouped generated scenarios and asking them to select the most realistic one, forming a dataset for realism alignment.
- A ==reward model== is trained on these collected human preferences to quantify scenario realism, which is then used to fine-tune existing, diverse traffic generation models (CTG, BITS, TrafficGen) in a model-agnostic manner.

## Results

- TrafficRLHF significantly reduced failure rates (collisions or road departures) by up to approximately **80%** (e.g., CTG's failure rate decreased from **0.271** to **0.054**) and improved realism deviation in generated scenarios.
- The framework enhanced realism, stability, and human preference scores across all evaluated traffic models (CTG, BITS, TrafficGen), demonstrating its broad applicability.
- The research contributes the first dataset specifically designed for realism alignment in traffic modeling, enabling human-aligned model training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RLHF, a technique successful in large language models, can effectively capture and align models with subjective human preferences for 'realism' in complex multi-agent traffic simulations.
> - Fine-tuning the entire traffic model (both encoder and decoder components) with the human feedback reward model yields the most substantial improvements in both realism and stability.
> - The learned reward model demonstrates strong generalizability, successfully improving the realism of multiple distinct traffic models (CTG, BITS, TrafficGen) even when initially trained on feedback generated from a single base model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous vehicles (AVs) necessitates extensive testing to ensure system reliability and safety. Given the substantial expenses and inherent risks associated with real-world road testing, simulation environments have become a critical tool in the AV development pipeline. The efficacy of these simulators is highly dependent on the realism of the traffic models they incorporate, particularly their alignment with human driving behaviors and perceptions.

Current approaches to traffic simulation can be broadly categorized into rule-based and learning-based methods. Rule-based models, such as those employing cellular automata or intelligent driver models, rely on predefined rules and analytical formulations. While providing a structured approach, these methods often exhibit inflexibility, struggling to capture the dynamic variety and nuanced interactions characteristic of real-world driving. They typically set fixed routes and separate longitudinal and lateral vehicle movements, which restricts their ability to mimic complex human behaviors.

Learning-based approaches, conversely, leverage deep generative models trained on large trajectory datasets to imitate observed driving behaviors. These models, including recent advancements like Conditional Traffic Generation (CTG), Bi-Level Imitation Learning System (BITS), and TrafficGen, have shown progress in generating diverse scenarios. Some of these models have even focused on generating adversarial or safety-critical scenarios to test AV robustness. However, a persistent challenge for learning-based models is their difficulty in consistently generating traffic scenarios that align with human perceptions of realism. They often lack the flexibility for users to specify customized attributes of generated traffic behaviors, leading to a gap between simulated and human-judged realistic traffic. The balance between diversity in generated scenarios and their perceived realism by human observers remains a significant hurdle.

This research integrates into the broader landscape by addressing this realism gap through the application of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). RLHF is a paradigm within human-in-the-loop learning that has gained prominence, particularly in the domain of large language models (LLMs) such as InstructGPT and ChatGPT. It addresses scenarios where explicit reward engineering for reinforcement learning is complex or costly, by instead leveraging human preference data to train a reward model. The historical development of RLHF spans over a decade, with contributions in preference-based policy learning and deep reinforcement learning from human preferences. The success of RLHF in fine-tuning LLMs to better align with human instructions and preferences has demonstrated its scalability and potential for tuning large models efficiently.

This work positions itself at the intersection of traffic simulation and RLHF, aiming to transfer the success of RLHF in capturing subjective human preferences to the challenging domain of realistic traffic modeling. By developing a model-agnostic framework that can enhance the realism of existing traffic models based on human feedback, it seeks to improve the reliability and applicability of simulation testing for autonomous vehicles, thereby making simulation insights more directly transferable to real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that utilizes reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF) to enhance the realism of existing traffic models, thereby improving the effectiveness of simulation-based testing for autonomous vehicle (AV) development. This objective is motivated by several factors and addresses specific challenges within the field.

**Motivation:**

1. **High Costs and Risks of Real-World Testing:** Conducting extensive real-world tests for AVs is prohibitively expensive and carries significant safety risks. Consequently, AV developers rely heavily on simulation to iterate, test, and validate their systems. The utility of these simulations, however, hinges on their ability to accurately reflect real-world conditions.
2. **Lack of Realism in Current Traffic Models:** A critical limitation of existing traffic simulators is their struggle to generate traffic behaviors that are both diverse and realistic in a manner consistent with human perception.
 - **Rule-based models** are often inflexible and cannot capture the complex, dynamic, and varied behaviors observed in human driving.
 - **Learning-based generative models**, while capable of producing diverse trajectories, frequently fail to align with human judgments of realism. They typically lack mechanisms for users to directly influence the "realism" attribute during generation.
3. **Bridging the Simulation-to-Reality Gap:** For insights gained from simulation testing to be applicable in real-world scenarios, the simulated traffic must be sufficiently realistic. A disparity in realism can lead to AV systems that perform well in simulation but encounter unexpected failures or inefficiencies when deployed in the real world.
4. **Leveraging Advances in RLHF:** Recent successes of RLHF in domains like large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated its effectiveness in capturing and aligning models with nuanced human preferences, even when these preferences are subjective and difficult to formalize with explicit rules or metrics. This success suggests a promising avenue for addressing the subjective nature of "realistic" traffic.

**Specific Challenges Addressed:**

The authors identify two main challenges that their framework aims to tackle:

1. **Limited Expressiveness in Capturing Human Preferences for Realism:** Traditional approaches to traffic simulation often rely on predefined rules or statistical models that are inadequate for encompassing the wide spectrum of human preferences and subjective perceptions regarding realistic traffic scenarios. Human understanding of realism is complex and context-dependent, making it difficult to encode into explicit loss functions or criteria. This work proposes using human feedback as a direct alignment tool to overcome this limitation.
2. **Unification of Diverse Traffic Simulation Models:** The field of traffic simulation comprises a variety of models, each possessing distinct architectures, underlying assumptions, data requirements, strengths, and limitations. Integrating human preferences seamlessly across such diverse models is challenging. The research aims to develop a model-agnostic approach where the RLHF framework can be applied to enhance various existing traffic models without requiring fundamental architectural changes to each model.

**Research Contributions:**

To address these motivations and challenges, the research makes three specific contributions:

1. **Introduction of the first dataset** specifically designed for realism alignment in traffic modeling, enabling the training of models sensitive to human perceptions of realism.
2. **Formulation of a reward model** that quantifies realism based on collected human preferences, providing a scalable mechanism for evaluating and guiding traffic generation.
3. **Proposal of a model-agnostic framework**, named TrafficRLHF, which leverages RLHF to effectively enhance the realism of a broad range of existing traffic models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **TrafficRLHF**, is a three-stage methodology designed to enhance the realism of existing traffic models by incorporating human preferences. The approach is model-agnostic, aiming to improve various traffic simulation models.

#### 3.1. Traffic Simulation Formulation

The problem of traffic simulation is formulated based on existing literature. For a scenario involving *M* vehicles, the state *s*<sub>*t*</sub> at time *t* is defined as *s*<sub>*t*</sub> = [*s*<sub>1</sub><sup>*t*</sup> ... *s*<sub>*M*</sub><sup>*t*</sup>], where each vehicle's state *s*<sub>*i*</sub><sup>*t*</sup> includes its *x, y* position, speed (*v*), and yaw (*θ*). The action (control) *a*<sub>*t*</sub> is similarly defined as *a*<sub>*t*</sub> = [*a*<sub>1</sub><sup>*t*</sup> ... *a*<sub>*M*</sub><sup>*t*</sup>], where *a*<sub>*i*</sub><sup>*t*</sup> consists of acceleration (˙*v*) and yaw rate (˙*θ*).

A decision-relevant context *c* is established, comprising local semantic maps for all agents (*I* = *I*<sub>1</sub>..., *I*<sub>*M*</sub>) and their historical states from *t* - *T*<sub>hist</sub> to *t* + 1 (*s*<sub>*t*-*T*<sub>hist</sub>∶*t*+1</sub>). The state *s*<sub>*t*+1</sub> at time *t*+1 is determined by a transition function *f*(*s*<sub>*t*</sub>, *a*<sub>*t*</sub>) following unicycle dynamics.

#### 3.2. Human Feedback Collection (Stage I)

This stage focuses on collecting human preferences regarding the realism of generated traffic scenarios.
- **Scenario Generation:** Pairs of traffic scenarios (*S*<sub>1</sub>, *S*<sub>2</sub>) are generated under identical initial conditions from the same context *c*. Each scenario *S*<sub>*i*</sub> represents a stacked state sequence of length *T*<sub>fut</sub>.
- **Annotation Process:** Human annotators are presented with videos of these paired scenarios, along with maps and labeled vehicles. Their task is to specify which scenario appears more realistic.
- **Addressing Low Realism:** Since initial traffic models may produce less realistic data, the framework generates *N* traffic scenarios (*S*<sub>1</sub>..., *S*<sub>*N*</sub>) per context. Annotators select the single most realistic scenario from this set. This results in *N*-1 preference pairs (*S*<sub>*i*</sub> ≻ *S*<sub>*j*</sub>), where *S*<sub>*i*</sub> is the chosen scenario and *S*<sub>*j*</sub> is any other.
- **Ground Truth Inclusion:** If annotators determine that none of the generated scenarios are realistic, the ground truth scenario (*S*<sub>gt</sub>) is used as the preferred scenario, forming pairs (*S*<sub>gt</sub> ≻ *S*<sub>*j*</sub>).
- **Efficiency:** The process is designed for sample efficiency. All trajectories are collected in simulation using existing traffic models, allowing for iterative generation and enhancement. Labeling requires minimal human effort and does not necessitate specialized expertise, making it accessible to individuals with driving experience.
- **Dataset:** This process contributes to the first dataset explicitly designed for realism alignment in traffic modeling.

#### 3.3. Reward Modeling (Stage II)

A reward model *r*<sub>HF</sub>(⋅; *ϕ*) is trained to quantify the realism of a traffic scenario based on the human preferences collected in Stage I.
- **Training Methodology:** Inspired by Abramson et al., the reward model is trained using a pairwise comparison loss:
 *L*<sub>RM</sub> = *E*<sub>*S*<sub>1</sub>,*S*<sub>2</sub>∼*D*</sub> [− log *σ*(*r*<sub>HF</sub>(*S*<sub>1</sub>; *ϕ*) − *r*<sub>HF</sub>(*S*<sub>2</sub>; *ϕ*))]
 where *S*<sub>1</sub> ≻ *S*<sub>2</sub> indicates human preference. *D* is the training dataset compiled from human preferences over scenarios generated by existing traffic models.
- **Model Architecture:** To maximize applicability across diverse traffic models, an iterative model is chosen for *r*<sub>HF</sub> that can accept arbitrary sequence lengths. Specifically, the reward model is parameterized using the encoder from the state-of-the-art Conditional Traffic Generation (CTG) model, augmented with fully-connected neural networks for the output layer. This choice is based on CTG's assumed expressiveness for diverse traffic scenes and the encoder's relative simplicity. The reward is calculated as the average reward over a sequence.

#### 3.4. Traffic Model Fine-tuning (Stage III)

In the final stage, existing traffic models are fine-tuned using the learned reward model to generate more realistic scenarios.
- **Objective Function:** The original loss function (*r*) for training the traffic model is augmented with the human feedback reward:
 ˜*r* = *r* + *α* ⋅ *r*<sub>HF</sub>(*S*)
 where *α* is a scaling term used to balance the original task performance with the alignment to human preferences, preventing overfitting to the reward score.
- **Fine-tuning Strategy:** Unlike RLHF for large language models where most parameters might be frozen, this work shows that fine-tuning the entire motion prediction model can yield higher performance improvements, given that traffic models are generally smaller than LLMs.
- **Model-Agnosticism:** The RLHF-based method achieves unification across diverse traffic models by only requiring a universal interface for reward model input. An autoregressive backbone model, CTG, is employed with a specified roll-out length to optimize near-future simulation.
- **Experimental Setup Details:**
 - **Dataset:** The nuScenes dataset is used, with a subset of 500 scenes from the training split for data collection, reward model training, and fine-tuning. Evaluation is conducted on 100 randomly sampled scenes from the validation split.
 - **Metrics:**
 - **Stability:** Measured by **failure rate (fail)**, the average percentage of agents experiencing a collision or road departure.
 - **Realism:** Quantified by **realism deviation (real)**, calculated as the average Wasserstein distance between normalized histograms of generated and ground truth driving profiles (longitudinal acceleration magnitude, lateral acceleration magnitude, and jerk).
 - **Human Preference Score:** Measured by **reward cost**.
 - **Traffic Model Benchmarks:** Conditional Traffic Generation (CTG), Bi-Level Imitation Learning System (BITS), and TrafficGen are used. Their original parameters (for CTG and BITS) or reproduced performance (for TrafficGen) are used as baselines.
 - **Data Generation:** CTG is used to generate five unique scenarios per scene from the 500 nuScenes training scenes, under a "no collision" guidance principle. Human labelers selected the most realistic or indicated ground truth as preferred.
 - **Reward Model Training:** The reward model uses the CTG encoder with fully-connected networks (512-512-512-128-32 units, Tanh activation). Trained with 200 samples to optimize performance and mitigate potential overfitting observed with larger sample sizes.
 - **Fine-tuning:** An amended PPO algorithm from ColossalAI is employed for 70 epochs. Specific *α* values (0.1 for CTG, 0.5 for BITS, 0.3 for TrafficGen) are used. For BITS and TrafficGen, fine-tuning is restricted to motion forecasting components due to their complex designs or for fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted to evaluate the realism of generated traffic behaviors using TrafficRLHF and the generalizability of its reward model across different traffic models.

#### 4.1. Reward Model Validation

The reward model (RM) was assessed against the collected human preference data. The evaluation, performed on a split of 400 training and 100 validation units, indicated that the RM's accuracy converges quickly as the sample size increases. However, the variance of the accuracy tended to broaden with further increments, which was interpreted as a potential indicator of overfitting on the relatively small NuScenes dataset. Consequently, the RM trained with 200 samples was selected for subsequent fine-tuning processes to optimize performance.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Traffic Model Fine-tuning with Reward Model (CTG+RM)

- **Qualitative Results:** Fine-tuning the Conditional Traffic Generation (CTG) model with the learned Reward Model (RM) demonstrably enhanced realism. Qualitative examples illustrated a reduction in unrealistic behaviors such as collisions and off-road driving, which were highlighted in the generated scenarios (Figure 3).
- **Quantitative Results:** When evaluated with varying guidance conditions (Table 1), CTG+RM showed improvements in stability and realism.
 - A significant decrease in failure rates (collisions or road departures) was observed across different guidance types. For instance, under "no off-road" guidance, the failure rate for CTG dropped from 0.271 to 0.054 (an approximate 80% reduction) with CTG+RM, while realism deviation improved from 0.569 to 0.376.
 - However, in scenarios where guidance was related to goal positions or speeds, the realism metrics occasionally deteriorated. This was hypothesized to occur because the strong guidance during the diffusion process in CTG could overpower the penalties provided by the RM loss.

#### 4.3. Generality of Learned Reward Model (BITS+RM, TrafficGen+RM)

To demonstrate the versatility of the trained RM, it was applied to fine-tune two other contemporary traffic models: Bi-Level Imitation Learning System (BITS) and TrafficGen.
- **Qualitative Results:** Similar to CTG, fine-tuning BITS and TrafficGen with the RM led to a reduction in unrealistic collisions and off-road conditions (Figure 4), visually confirming improved realism.
- **Quantitative Results:** When fine-tuned with the RM (which was originally trained on scenarios generated by CTG), both BITS and TrafficGen showed measurable improvements across all three evaluation metrics: realism deviation (Real ↓), failure rate (Fail ↓), and reward cost (Reward Cost ↓) (Table 2).
 - For BITS, realism deviation improved from 1.220 to 0.83, failure rate from 0.314 to 0.23, and reward cost from 12.4 to 9.5.
 - For TrafficGen, realism deviation improved from 1.683 to 1.173, failure rate from 0.39 to 0.22, and reward cost from 15.2 to 11.3.
 - While the degree of enhancement for BITS and TrafficGen was not as substantial as for CTG, these results underscored the broad applicability and generalizability of the proposed method and the learned RM.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the CTG model explored different fine-tuning strategies and the impact of various components.

- **Fine-tuning Components (Table 3):**
 - **CTG+RM (encoder):** Fine-tuning only the encoder portion of CTG with the RM resulted in substantial enhancements across all performance measures (Real ↓ 0.432, Fail ↓ 0.176, Reward Cost ↓ 9.1), indicating the role of human feedback.
 - **CTG+RM (decoder):** Fine-tuning only the decoder portion of CTG with the RM outperformed the encoder-only variant (Real ↓ 0.391, Fail ↓ 0.102, Reward Cost ↓ 5.2). This suggested that the diffusing model (decoder) plays a more integral role in generating realistic traffic scenarios.
 - **CTG+RM (full):** Fine-tuning both the encoder and decoder of CTG concurrently with the RM yielded the best overall performance (Real ↓ 0.376, Fail ↓ 0.054, Reward Cost ↓ 3.7). This configuration highlighted the importance of simultaneous fine-tuning of all model components alongside the incorporation of human feedback. The decoder was observed to be more responsive to modifications.

- **Human Feedback (Table 4):**
 - Training reward models using human feedback collected from scenarios generated by TrafficGen and BITS, rather than just CTG, resulted in a slight improvement in the realism of the fine-tuned models. For instance, when TrafficGen was fine-tuned with an RM trained on TrafficGen-generated feedback, its realism deviation improved from 1.173 (with CTG-feedback RM) to 0.924.

- **Reward Model Architecture (Table 4):**
 - Experiments using the TrafficGen encoder as the backbone for the reward model (instead of CTG encoder) yielded more significant improvements in realism for the fine-tuned traffic models, compared to variations in human feedback sources.
 - This finding led to a hypothesis that a closer similarity in latent representations between the traffic model and the reward model might be beneficial, suggesting that RLHF's primary benefit may not be in learning fundamentally better representations, but rather in aligning existing ones.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses critical challenges in the development of reliable autonomous vehicles, primarily by enhancing the realism of traffic simulations. The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Autonomous Vehicle Testing:** The core contribution is the improvement of traffic simulation realism, which is directly applicable to the testing and validation of AV systems. By generating scenarios that are more closely aligned with human driving behaviors and perceptions, the framework can help bridge the "sim-to-real" gap. This means that AVs trained and tested in these enhanced simulations are more likely to perform predictably and safely in real-world conditions.
2. **Cost and Risk Reduction:** By providing more realistic simulation environments, the framework reduces the necessity for extensive, costly, and potentially hazardous real-world testing. This translates to significant savings in resources and time during the AV development lifecycle.
3. **Addressing Key Challenges in Traffic Modeling:** The work successfully tackles two long-standing challenges:
 - **Capturing Nuanced Human Preferences:** The integration of Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) provides a mechanism to incorporate subjective human judgments of realism, which are often difficult to formalize into explicit objective functions. This allows for a more comprehensive understanding and generation of realistic traffic.
 - **Unifying Diverse Traffic Models:** The proposed model-agnostic framework demonstrates its ability to enhance the realism of a variety of existing traffic models (CTG, BITS, TrafficGen), proving its versatility and broad applicability without requiring extensive modifications to each model's core architecture.
4. **Novel Dataset Contribution:** The introduction of the first dataset specifically designed for realism alignment in traffic modeling serves as a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset can facilitate further research and development in human-aligned traffic simulation.
5. **Leveraging RLHF for New Domains:** This research effectively demonstrates the transferability and utility of RLHF, a technique that has seen significant success in large language models, to the domain of complex multi-agent simulations like traffic generation. This opens avenues for applying RLHF to other challenging simulation environments where subjective quality or realism is a key factor.
6. **Improved Simulation Reliability:** The quantitative and qualitative results indicate a significant reduction in unrealistic failures, such as collisions and off-road driving, leading to more stable and dependable simulated traffic flows. This directly contributes to building more robust AV systems.

While the work identifies limitations, such as not yet fully exploring advanced fine-tuning strategies for diffusion models or iteratively improving the traffic model with updated human preference datasets, these also represent clear opportunities for future research. The robust foundation laid by TrafficRLHF, including its dataset and model-agnostic approach, positions it to significantly advance the state of the art in realistic traffic simulation and, by extension, the safety and reliability of autonomous driving technology.
