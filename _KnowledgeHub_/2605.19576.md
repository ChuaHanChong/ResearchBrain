# Library Drift: Diagnosing and Fixing a Silent Failure Mode in Self-Evolving LLM Skill Libraries

> [!summary] Summary
> Ratchet diagnoses and fixes library drift, the silent degradation of self-evolving LLM skill libraries that accumulate skills without lifecycle management. It combines outcome-driven retirement, a bounded active-skill cap, and a meta-skill authoring prior, plus trace-level diagnostics (per-skill contribution scores, attribution verdicts, router engagement). On MBPP+ hard-100 over 100 rounds, held-out pass@1 rises from a 0.258 baseline to a 0.584 late-window mean.

## Problem

- Self-evolving skill libraries grow unboundedly without quality gates, so retrieval precision decays, stale skills get injected, and agent performance stagnates or falls below no-skill baselines.
- Existing systems extract insights, compile manuals, or add versioning, but none retire skills based on measured per-task contribution or bound library size, leaving degradation unconstrained.
- End-task metrics conflate task difficulty, model stochasticity, and library quality, so drift stays invisible until scores decline, with no early per-skill signal for intervention.

## Method

- ==Outcome-driven retirement==: the ==Curator== retires a skill once trials exceed an evidence floor of 100 and empirical contribution falls below a threshold of 0.10.
- ==Bounded active-cap== of 50 skills, evicting the lowest-contribution skill at synthesis time; paired with retirement it yields a ==non-divergence bound== on expected pass@1 below the no-skill floor.
- ==Meta-skill authoring prior== constraining the ==Synthesizer==, plus an append-only evidence log of ==Critic== verdicts (helped/hurt/neutral/inapplicable) feeding ==cluster-based synthesis== at three or more shared-pattern failures.

## Results

- Full governance lifts held-out pass@1 by **+0.328** (peak **0.658**) versus **+0.002** with injection disabled, more than doubling performance on filtered hard tasks.
- Harsh retirement (evidence floor **20**, threshold **0.0**) scores **-0.019**, below the no-skill floor; the bank collapses to **2** active skills and router engagement drops to **19%**.
- Removing the meta-skill costs **43%** of the gain (**+0.187**); disabling explicit deduplication slightly exceeds the default (**+0.374**, **+0.363**), and doubling the cap keeps the mean but triples variance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Governance is not monotonically good: acting on insufficient evidence retires useful skills and harms performance more than having no lifecycle management at all.
> - A strong authoring prior can subsume explicit deduplication filters, whose false positives discard more useful artifacts than the duplicates they block.
> - Per-artifact contribution scores and engagement metrics expose drift rounds before aggregate scores move, and transfer to any system persisting LLM-authored artifacts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a specific challenge within the rapidly developing field of Large Language Model (LLM) agents, particularly those designed to accumulate and utilize reusable procedural knowledge through self-evolving skill libraries.

**Self-Evolving Skill Libraries:** The paper positions itself in the lineage of systems like Voyager (Wang et al., 2023), ExpeL (Zhao et al., 2024), AutoManual (Chen et al., 2024), CASCADE (Huang et al., 2025), and AutoSkill (Yang et al., 2026). These systems aim to enable LLM agents to continuously learn and improve by generating and storing skills from solved tasks, thereby accelerating future task completion. The paper highlights a critical observation from recent work, specifically SkillsBench (Li et al., 2026) and a survey by Zhang et al. (2026a), indicating that while many systems focus on skill creation, crucial aspects like lifecycle management (versioning, conflict detection, deprecation) are largely neglected. This neglect leads to a discrepancy where LLM-authored skills yield minimal performance gains compared to human-curated ones, suggesting a systemic issue despite the promise of compounding knowledge. This research directly investigates and proposes solutions for this identified gap in lifecycle management.

**Agent Failure Modes:** The paper introduces "library drift" as a distinct failure mode specific to persistent, cross-task memory systems in LLM agents. It differentiates library drift from other documented failure modes, such as errors in tool use (Schick et al., 2023), planning (Yao et al., 2023), and self-correction (Shinn et al., 2023; Madaan et al., 2023). Unlike catastrophic forgetting, which involves overwriting learned representations in weight-update systems (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017), library drift occurs in frozen-weight LLMs where persistent skill artifacts, rather than neural weights, become the substrate for degradation. This work identifies a novel mechanism by which accumulating information can degrade a system's performance if not actively managed.

**Diagnostic Signals:** The research extends the concept of LLM-as-judge (Zheng et al., 2023), which assesses LLM outputs, to develop fine-grained, per-skill attribution verdicts. This approach aims to provide early-warning diagnostic signals at the trace level, distinguishing it from reliance on aggregate, post-hoc evaluation metrics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed underperformance of LLM-authored skills in self-evolving agents, despite the theoretical advantage of cumulative knowledge. Existing literature indicates that while self-evolving agents can accumulate skills, these skills often fail to translate into tangible performance improvements, a phenomenon termed "library drift." This failure mode is characterized as "silent" because end-task metrics decline gradually without explicit error signals, making diagnosis challenging.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Define and Isolate Library Drift:** To provide a formal and operational definition of library drift and demonstrate its occurrence through reproducible experimental triggers. This involves showing how the failure mode can be bracketed by conditions representing a no-skill baseline and actively harmful governance.
2. **Develop Trace-Level Diagnostics:** To move beyond aggregate end-task metrics by proposing and implementing granular diagnostic signals that can expose library drift at a per-skill level, enabling detection before significant performance degradation occurs.
3. **Propose and Verify a Governance Mechanism:** To design and experimentally validate a minimal set of governance mechanisms for skill libraries, aimed at preventing and mitigating library drift. This involves decomposing the proposed solution into its individual components through ablations to identify which elements are essential for its effectiveness.

The overarching goal is to enable self-evolving LLM agents to realize their full potential for compounding knowledge by addressing the fundamental issue of skill library quality and relevance over time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a system named **Ratchet**, designed as a single-agent loop for frozen LLMs to author, retrieve, curate, and retire natural-language skills. The system operates through a five-phase cycle per round:

1. **Eval:** Active skills are routed on held-out tasks to assess performance.
2. **Train:** The same pipeline is run on a training split to generate failure data.
3. **Critic:** A separate LLM performs an attribution verdict for each failure, determining if an injected skill HELPED, HURT, was NEUTRAL, or INAPPLICABLE. It also assigns a pattern label.
4. **Synthesizer:** This component clusters failures by canonical pattern. New skills are authored from clusters comprising three or more members.
5. **Curator:** This component computes per-skill contribution scores, retires under-performing skills, and enforces a bounded active-cap on the skill bank.

The **Router** component is responsible for selecting whether to inject a skill for a given task, and if so, which one. It employs a two-stage retrieval process (tf-idf and embedding similarities, considering the top 10 from each) followed by an LLM gate that ultimately decides whether to select one of the retrieved skills or NONE.

### 4. Key Governance Mechanisms (The "Ratchet Recipe")

The paper identifies three primary governance mechanisms within Ratchet that jointly prevent library drift:

1. **Outcome-driven retirement:** A skill `s` is retired if it has accumulated at least `N_min` trials (set to 100 in Default) and its empirical contribution score `c(s)` falls below a negative threshold `τ` (set to -0.10 in Default). This mechanism directly addresses the issue of skills accumulating without a quality signal, ensuring that underperforming or harmful skills are eventually removed.
2. **Bounded active-cap:** A hard limit `C` (set to 50 active skills in Default) is imposed on the number of skills in the active skill bank. If the Synthesizer attempts to add a new skill when the bank is at capacity, the Curator evicts the lowest-contribution skill. This prevents unbounded growth of the library, mitigating retrieval degradation and offering a formal non-divergence guarantee (Proposition 1).
3. **Meta-skill authoring prior:** The Synthesizer is guided by a meta-skill document that dictates the stylistic and structural consistency of newly authored skills. This mechanism aims to prevent the generation of harmful or redundant skills at their source, thereby reducing the load on downstream retirement processes.

### 5. Trace-Level Diagnostics

To overcome the limitations of aggregate end-task metrics, the research introduces three diagnostic signals:

1. **Per-skill contribution score:** For each active skill `s`, an empirical contribution score `c(s) = (successes(s) - failures(s)) / trials(s)` is tracked. A declining average `c` across the skill bank indicates potential drift.
2. **Attribution verdicts:** The Critic LLM provides structured verdicts (HELPED, HURT, NEUTRAL, INAPPLICABLE) for every task failure where a skill was injected. A rising proportion of "HURT" verdicts serves as an early indicator of drift. These verdicts also serve as the substrate for skill synthesis.
3. **Router engagement metrics:** The percentage of tasks for which the router chooses to inject a skill (rather than NONE) is monitored. A significant drop in engagement can signal issues like an empty or ineffective skill bank.

### 6. Experimental Protocol

- **Benchmark:** Evaluation was conducted on MBPP+ hard-100, a subset of 100 tasks from the MBPP+ test split (Liu et al., 2023). Tasks that Claude Opus 4.7 solved reliably were excluded to focus on those where a skill library could genuinely offer assistance. The dataset was split into 60 training and 40 evaluation tasks.
- **LLM and Embeddings:** All LLM calls (Solver, Critic, Router, Synthesizer) utilized Claude Opus 4.7, and embeddings were generated using Cohere embed-v4, both accessed via Amazon Bedrock.
- **Solver Design:** The Solver was designed as a single, direct LLM call without execution feedback, self-refinement, or external tool use. This setup was chosen to isolate and measure the specific contribution of the skill library.
- **Evaluation:** Each experiment ran for 100 rounds. Results are reported as mean ± standard deviation over three seeds.
- **Ablation Studies:** Eight different ablation conditions (A1-A8) were designed to systematically remove or modify specific governance mechanisms or system components, allowing for an empirical decomposition of their individual contributions to preventing library drift.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation on MBPP+ hard-100 demonstrated the occurrence of library drift and the effectiveness of the Ratchet governance recipe.

### 8. Overall Performance

- The baseline pass@1 for the tasks without any active skills was 0.258.
- The **Default (full Ratchet governance)** system achieved a late-window mean pass@1 of 0.584 (with a peak of 0.658) over 100 rounds, representing a rolling gain of +0.328. This performance more than doubled the baseline on tasks identified as genuinely difficult for the LLM.

### 9. Reproducible Triggers for Library Drift

Two specific ablation conditions bracketed the failure mode:
- **A1 (no skill injection):** The Router was forced to always select NONE, meaning no skills were ever injected into the solver prompt, even if synthesized. This condition yielded a negligible gain of +0.002, establishing the "no-skill floor" and demonstrating that skill creation alone, without active use, does not improve performance.
- **A4 (harsh retirement):** This condition aggressively retired skills by reducing the minimum evidence floor (`N_min`) from 100 to 20 trials and tightening the contribution threshold (`τ`) to 0.0. A4 resulted in a performance *loss* of −0.019, falling below the no-skill baseline. This confirmed that overly aggressive or under-evidenced governance can actively harm performance, causing the skill bank to collapse (to 2 active skills in this case) and leading to "erosion" of useful knowledge.

### 10. Decomposing the Fix: Ablation Analysis

Eight ablations were conducted to understand the contribution of individual Ratchet components:

1. **Necessity of Components (A1-A3):**
 - **A1 (no skill injection):** As noted, confirmed that skill injection is critical for any performance gain.
 - **A2 (retrieval-only routing):** Bypassed the LLM gate, directly injecting the top retrieved skill. It achieved a gain of +0.077 (24% of Default's gain), indicating that the LLM gate's ability to decline skill injection contributes significantly to performance beyond simple retrieval.
 - **A3 (no meta-skill):** Removed the meta-skill authoring prior. This resulted in a gain of +0.187 (57% of Default's gain), indicating that the meta-skill is the single most impactful component of the Ratchet recipe.

2. **Harsh Retirement (A4):** Reconfirmed its active harm, falling below the no-skill baseline. This highlights that governance is not universally beneficial and requires sufficient evidence to avoid counterproductive outcomes.

3. **Explicit Deduplication (A5-A6):**
 - **A5 (no canonicalization):** Disabled pattern deduplication.
 - **A6 (no cover-guard):** Disabled duplicate-cluster skipping.
 Both A5 (+0.374 gain) and A6 (+0.363 gain) slightly *exceeded* the Default's performance. This finding suggests that at the tested scale, the meta-skill's authoring guidance provides sufficient stylistic consistency, making explicit deduplication filters potentially counterproductive by discarding more useful skills than they prevent duplicates.

4. **Relaxed Cap and Meta-Skill Refresh (A7-A8):**
 - **A7 (bank cap=100):** Doubled the active skill cap. It achieved a comparable mean gain of +0.317 but with substantially higher variance (±0.110 vs. ±0.018 for Default). This indicates that the active cap primarily controls performance stability and variance rather than the mean gain at this scale.
 - **A8 (meta-synth refresh):** Regenerated the meta-skill every 10 rounds. It yielded a gain of +0.372 and the highest peak pass@1 (0.725), but at a 55% increase in wall time (10.1 hours vs. 6.5 hours for Default). The marginal performance improvement was deemed not to justify the significant computational overhead at this scale.

### 11. Operational Cost

The Default configuration incurred approximately 14,500 LLM calls over 100 rounds (10k solver, 4.3k critic, 152 synthesis calls), representing a 43% increase compared to the no-skill baseline (A1, 10k calls). The total wall time was 6.5 hours, 2.8 times longer than A1. Solver calls constituted the majority of the cost (63-99%).

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and development of robust LLM agentic systems, particularly those relying on persistent memory.

**Generalization of Library Drift:** The paper proposes library drift as a fundamental failure mode not exclusive to skill libraries but applicable to any system that accumulates and persists LLM-authored artifacts across tasks or episodes, including rule-based systems, workflow memories, and episodic stores. Recognizing this as a "first-class failure category" alongside tool-use and planning errors highlights its systemic nature and broad relevance.

**Importance of Trace-Level Diagnostics:** A significant impact is the emphasis on and demonstration of trace-level diagnostics (per-skill contribution scores, attribution verdicts, router engagement metrics). These diagnostics were shown to detect drift proactively, before aggregate end-task metrics decline significantly. This early-warning capability is crucial for enabling timely and targeted interventions, moving beyond reactive post-mortem analysis. The diagnostic recipe is transferable and applicable to various LLM agent architectures.

**Actionable Governance Playbook:** The verified "Ratchet recipe"—comprising outcome-driven retirement, a bounded active-cap, and a meta-skill authoring prior—provides a concrete and effective governance framework. The extensive ablation study offers practical insights, indicating which mechanisms are load-bearing (e.g., meta-skill, outcome-driven retirement with sufficient evidence) and which may be safely omitted or deemphasized at certain scales (e.g., explicit deduplication). The unexpected findings, such as the active harm of harsh retirement (A4) and the meta-skill subsuming explicit deduplication (A5/A6), underscore that governance is not always uniformly beneficial and must be carefully implemented and monitored.

**Implications for Practitioners:** The work offers a "concrete playbook" for diagnosing library drift in self-evolving agents. It suggests starting with a safe governance configuration (like the Default Ratchet), then iteratively relaxing or adjusting knobs while guided by the trace-level diagnostic signals. This approach promotes safer and more effective development of agentic systems.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors acknowledge several limitations, including evaluation on a single benchmark (MBPP+ hard-100), reliance on a single LLM (Claude Opus 4.7), empirically chosen diagnostic thresholds, and the need for empirical validation of the diagnostic signals' sensitivity in multi-step agents. Addressing these limitations in future work will further generalize and strengthen the findings.

In conclusion, this research reframes the challenge of self-evolving LLM agents from an "authoring" problem to a "librarian" problem. It demonstrates that effective lifecycle management is not merely an optimization but a prerequisite for preventing systemic performance degradation and realizing the compounding potential of LLM skill libraries.
