# Agent-Centric Visual Reinforcement Learning under Dynamic Perturbations

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the Agent-Centric Observations with Mixture-of-Experts (ACO-MoE) framework, a plug-and-play preprocessor that restores and extracts agent-centric foregrounds to robustify visual reinforcement learning agents. Evaluated on the new Visual Degraded Control Suite (VDCS) benchmark, ACO-MoE achieved 95.3% recovery of clean performance and state-of-the-art results on the DMC-GB benchmark.

## Problem

- Visual reinforcement learning agents suffer significant performance degradation when deployed in real-world settings with dynamic and heterogeneous visual perturbations (e.g., weather, sensor noise).
- Existing robustness methods, such as data augmentation or perturbation-invariant representation learning, often process corrupted pixel data, failing to recover information irrecoverably lost or distorted at the pixel level.
- Current benchmarks do not adequately address the impact of physical image degradations affecting foreground objects or evaluate scenarios involving non-stationary transitions between different perturbation types.

## Method

- The ==Visual Degraded Control Suite (VDCS)== is introduced, a new benchmark simulating dynamic, ==Markov-switching physical degradations== (e.g., rain, haze, noise) to reflect real-world complexities.
- **ACO-MoE** (==Agent-Centric Observations with Mixture-of-Experts==) is proposed as a frozen, plug-and-play preprocessing module, inserted before the RL encoder, compatible with both model-free and model-based RL algorithms.
- ACO-MoE utilizes a ==shared encoder==, a ==perturbation router==, and specialized ==dual-stream expert decoders== for RGB restoration and foreground mask prediction to construct clean, agent-centric observations on a uniform black background.

## Results

- ACO-MoE achieved an average score of **736.0** on the VDCS benchmark, outperforming the best baseline (SimGRL) by **78.5%** and recovering **95.3%** of DreamerV3's clean performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed the benefit of the Mixture-of-Experts (**10.9%** improvement) and the critical roles of both RGB restoration and foreground masking, with the router demonstrating over **99%** accuracy.
- The framework achieved state-of-the-art average performance on the DMC-GB `video-hard` (**809**, outperforming FTR by **6.2%**) and `color-hard` (**818**, outperforming SMG by **17.4%**) benchmarks, demonstrating broad generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reconstruction-based world models inevitably suffer from "representation contamination," where latent states encode nuisance corruption features alongside task-relevant information due to the reconstruction objective.
> - An ideal clean foreground observation acts as an approximate Information Bottleneck anchor, theoretically justified to achieve nuisance invariance and approximate task sufficiency by decoupling task-relevant information.
> - Using ACO-MoE as a frozen, pretrained preprocessor is crucial for maintaining stable input distributions to the downstream RL agent, preventing observation drift and ensuring training stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual reinforcement learning (RL) enables agents to learn control policies directly from visual observations, having demonstrated success in various simulated environments and real-world applications such as robotic manipulation and autonomous navigation. However, a significant challenge in deploying these agents in real-world settings is their vulnerability to visual perturbations. These perturbations, which include diverse phenomena like adverse weather conditions (rain, snow, haze), sensor noise (Gaussian noise, low-light), and data compression artifacts (JPEG), can dynamically alter the agent's visual input, leading to performance degradation or catastrophic failure.

Existing approaches to enhance robustness in visual RL typically fall into two categories:
1. **Data Augmentation and Domain Randomization:** These methods aim to improve generalization by exposing the agent to a wider variety of visual inputs during training. While they enhance tolerance to distributional shifts, the underlying issue persists: the agent still processes corrupted pixel data, which can obscure or distort task-relevant information.
2. **Perturbation-Invariant Representation Learning:** This category focuses on learning latent representations that are robust to corruption-specific features, often through contrastive or regularization objectives. However, these methods operate on the corrupted input pixels and cannot recover information that is irrecoverably lost or distorted at the pixel level due to physical occlusion or alteration of the foreground. Neither of these categories explicitly restores the corrupted observation *before* it is processed by the RL agent.

Current benchmarks, such as the DMControl Generalization Benchmark (DMC-GB), primarily evaluate robustness to background distractions (e.g., natural video backgrounds, random color shifts). These benchmarks do not adequately address the impact of physical image degradations that directly affect foreground objects or evaluate scenarios involving non-stationary transitions between different perturbation types within a single episode. Real-world environments often exhibit such dynamic and heterogeneous perturbations, posing a greater challenge than static or episode-level disturbances.

This research positions itself by addressing these identified limitations. It introduces a new benchmark designed to simulate dynamic, Markov-switching physical degradations, thereby better reflecting real-world complexities. Furthermore, it proposes a novel preprocessing framework that explicitly restores and filters visual observations before they reach the RL agent, aiming to decouple task-relevant perception from perturbation artifacts. This approach seeks to provide clean, agent-centric inputs, an aspect largely unaddressed by prior work, and is evaluated for its compatibility with both model-free and model-based RL architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to robustify visual reinforcement learning agents against dynamic and heterogeneous visual perturbations encountered in real-world deployment scenarios. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives, driven by the identified shortcomings of existing methods and benchmarks:

1. **Develop a Systematic Benchmark for Dynamic Physical Degradations:** The motivation stems from the insufficiency of existing benchmarks like DMC-GB, which primarily focus on background distractions or static perturbations. Real-world environments present dynamic, temporally switching physical degradations (e.g., changing weather, fluctuating sensor noise). The objective is to introduce the **Visual Degraded Control Suite (VDCS)** to simulate these non-stationary visual corruptions, enabling systematic evaluation of agent robustness under more realistic conditions.

2. **Theoretically Analyze and Address Representation Contamination:** A key motivation is the observed performance degradation in existing RL methods. For model-based approaches (e.g., DreamerV3), reconstruction-based objectives inadvertently force latent representations to encode perturbation-specific features, leading to "contamination" of the learned world model. For model-free methods, corruptions directly confuse task-relevant state with artifacts. The objective is to formally prove via information-theoretic analysis that this contamination occurs and to theoretically justify a strategy for mitigation, specifically by foreground extraction as an information-bottleneck surrogate.

3. **Propose a Robust Framework for Handling Heterogeneous Corruptions:** Existing image restoration models are often specialized for individual corruption types, and unified restorers struggle with heterogeneous corruptions having incompatible inverse mappings. This poses a challenge for a single preprocessing module in dynamic environments where corruption types switch unpredictably. The objective is to develop a single module, **Agent-Centric Observations with Mixture-of-Experts (ACO-MoE)**, capable of handling multiple, diverse corruptions without *a priori* knowledge of the active perturbation type at inference time.

4. **Ensure Stable Integration with Policy Learning:** Introducing a preprocessing module can potentially cause instability during RL training due to continuously shifting input distributions. The objective is to design ACO-MoE as a frozen, plug-and-play preprocessor, ensuring that it provides stable, de-perturbed observations to the RL agent without requiring modifications to the core RL algorithm or joint training that could lead to instability.

5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalizability:** The ultimate objective is to demonstrate that the proposed ACO-MoE framework significantly improves the robustness of visual RL agents. This involves outperforming strong baselines on the newly introduced VDCS benchmark, as well as demonstrating high generalizability and state-of-the-art results on existing benchmarks like DMC-GB (Color/Video Hard), and validating compatibility with various RL algorithms (model-free and model-based).

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between lab-tested visual RL performance and real-world robustness by systematically addressing the challenges of dynamic, heterogeneous visual perturbations through a new benchmark, theoretical insights, and a practical, stable preprocessing solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a new benchmark, Visual Degraded Control Suite (VDCS), and proposes a novel framework, Agent-Centric Observations with Mixture-of-Experts (ACO-MoE), to address the challenges of robust visual RL under dynamic perturbations.

#### 3.1. Visual Degraded Control Suite (VDCS)

VDCS is an extension of the DeepMind Control Suite designed to simulate non-stationary visual perturbations characteristic of real-world scenarios.
- **Dynamic Perturbation Generation:** VDCS corrupts the rendered RGB stream by applying degradation operators whose mode ($k_t$) evolves according to a sticky Markov chain. This ensures that perturbation types switch dynamically but persist for a period, reflecting real-world phenomena like weather changes or intermittent sensor noise. The severity ($\iota_t$) of each degradation is also temporally correlated within persistent segments.
- **Degradation Types:** Seven physical degradations are included: rain, haze, snow, motion blur, Gaussian noise, low-light, and JPEG compression. These operators physically alter foreground pixels and scene textures, distinguishing them from background distractions.
- **Data Generation:** VDCS is used to generate a paired offline dataset of clean/corrupted frames with pseudo-foreground masks for pretraining ACO-MoE. It is also used online during RL evaluation to apply dynamic corruptions.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides an information-theoretic analysis to formalize the problem and justify the proposed solution:
- **Representation Contamination (Proposition 5.1):** The analysis demonstrates that reconstruction-based world models (e.g., DreamerV3), which aim to minimize the difference between observed and reconstructed pixels, inevitably force their latent representations ($Z_t$) to encode information about the active corruption mode ($K_t$), even conditioned on the true state ($S_t$). This means that accurate reconstruction leads to entanglement of task-relevant and nuisance corruption information in the latent space. The proof shows that the mutual information $I(Z_t; K_t | S_t)$ remains positive if reconstruction error is sufficiently small.
- **Information-Bottleneck (IB) Objective and Foreground Extraction (Corollary 5.2):** To mitigate this, the paper connects the problem to the Information Bottleneck principle, which seeks to compress input ($X_t$) while preserving task-relevant information ($Y_t$, e.g., optimal action). It is shown that an ideal clean foreground observation ($F_t$) acts as an approximate IB anchor. Specifically, setting $Z_t = F_t$ achieves exact nuisance invariance ($I(Z_t; K_t | Y_t) = 0$) and approximate task sufficiency ($I(Z_t; Y_t) = H(Y_t) - \eta$, where $\eta$ is typically small). This theoretical finding justifies the design principle of extracting and restoring the agent-centric foreground.

#### 3.3. Agent-Centric Observations with Mixture-of-Experts (ACO-MoE)

ACO-MoE is implemented as a frozen, plug-and-play preprocessing module inserted before the RL encoder.
- **Architecture:**
 - **Shared Encoder:** Extracts multi-scale features from the corrupted RGB input ($x_t$).
 - **Perturbation Router:** A lightweight network that takes the encoder's bottleneck feature, applies global average pooling, and then an MLP to produce routing logits. These logits determine which specialized expert decoder should be activated. During inference, hard top-1 selection is used.
 - **Mixture-of-Experts Group:** Composed of $|K|$ agent-centric restoration experts, where $|K|$ corresponds to the number of degradation types in VDCS (e.g., 7). Each expert is a dual-stream decoder.
 - **RGB Restoration Stream:** Predicts an RGB residual ($\Delta_t$) which is added to the corrupted input ($x_t$) to recover a restored image ($\hat{o}_t$).
 - **Mask Prediction Stream:** Predicts a binary foreground mask logits ($\ell_{mask_t}$) to identify task-relevant pixels.
- **Agent-Centric Observation Construction:** The final agent-centric observation ($\tilde{x}_t$) is created by compositing the restored RGB foreground onto a uniform black background. This is achieved by $\tilde{x}_t = \hat{o}_t \odot m_t + b \odot (1 - m_t)$, where $m_t$ is the predicted foreground probability map and $b$ is the black background pixel value. This step aims to decouple task-relevant information from background distractors and remaining perturbation artifacts.
- **Offline Pretraining:** ACO-MoE is pretrained offline on a paired dataset generated from VDCS (or DMC-GB).
 - **Supervision:** The training objective includes four loss terms:
 - $L_{rgb}$: L1 loss between the restored image ($\hat{o}_t$) and the clean ground truth ($o_t$).
 - $L_{mask}$: Cross-entropy loss between predicted mask logits ($\ell_{mask_t}$) and pseudo ground-truth foreground masks ($m_{ps_t}$), obtained via color-threshold segmentation.
 - $L_{router}$: Cross-entropy loss for router's prediction of the corruption type ($k_t$), using teacher forcing.
 - $L_{final}$: L1 loss between the constructed agent-centric observation ($\tilde{x}_t$) and a ground-truth agent-only observation (clean foreground on black background).
 - **Stability:** Crucially, after pretraining, the ACO-MoE module is *frozen* and its weights are fixed. It acts as a static preprocessor, providing consistent input distributions to the downstream RL agent, preventing observation drift and maintaining training stability.
- **Plug-and-Play Compatibility:** The framework is designed to be compatible with both model-free (e.g., DrQ-v2) and model-based (e.g., DreamerV3, TD-MPC2) RL algorithms without requiring architectural or training procedure modifications to the RL backbones.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on VDCS and DMC-GB benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of the ACO-MoE framework.

#### 4.1. Performance on VDCS Markov-Temporal Perturbations

- **Overall Superiority:** ACO-MoE achieved an average score of 736.0 across 8 DMControl tasks, outperforming the best baseline, SimGRL (412.3), by a relative margin of 78.5%.
- **High Recovery Rate:** The framework recovered 95.3% of DreamerV3's clean performance on average, indicating a high level of robustness to dynamically switching degradations.
- **Significant Gains:** Particularly strong performance improvements were observed on tasks sensitive to low-level visual cues disrupted by physical corruptions, such as `finger_turn_hard` (+207.3%), `cheetah_run` (+126.5%), and `hopper_hop` (+105.5%).
- **Robust Imagination:** Qualitative analysis (Figure 4) showed that ACO-MoE enabled DreamerV3 to maintain consistent future predictions under motion blur corruption, contrasting with state collapse observed when DreamerV3 was exposed to raw degraded input.
- **Backbone Agnosticism:** While DreamerV3 exhibited a lower score on `finger_spin` even in clean environments (543), ACO-MoE still recovered 90.6% of this baseline. When paired with DrQ-v2, ACO-MoE allowed DrQ-v2 to score 872 on `finger_spin` under VDCS (95.6% recovery of DrQ-v2's clean performance), validating its plug-and-play nature and demonstrating that performance limitations on certain tasks can be attributed to the RL backbone rather than the preprocessing.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Mixture-of-Experts vs. Single Expert:** Replacing the MoE with a single U-Net of equivalent capacity resulted in a 10.9% average performance degradation. This indicates that corruption-specialized experts are beneficial for handling heterogeneous, switching corruptions, rather than merely increasing model capacity.
- **Dual-stream vs. Single-stream:**
 - `Mask-Only` (disabling RGB restoration) led to a 10.6% average performance drop, suggesting that foreground extraction alone is not sufficient if foreground pixels remain corrupted.
 - `RGB-Only` (disabling masking) caused a more severe average degradation of 57.1%, emphasizing the critical role of foreground isolation in suppressing background distractors.
 - `w/o Restoration` (masking without the RGB residual) generally underperformed the full model, confirming the necessity of restoration alongside masking.
- **Frozen vs. Joint Training:** Ablations revealed that unfreezing ACO-MoE during RL training consistently harmed robustness, often reducing performance to 50-60% of the full model. This supports the design choice of using ACO-MoE as a frozen preprocessor to ensure stable input distributions for the RL agent.

#### 4.3. Router Behavior Analysis

- **High Accuracy:** The router achieved over 99% routing accuracy in identifying the active corruption type under Markov-switching conditions.
- **Semantic Specialization:** Residual confusions primarily occurred between visually similar corruption pairs (e.g., rain and snow, Gaussian noise and JPEG), indicating that the router learned semantically meaningful distinctions for expert selection.

#### 4.4. Generalization to RoboSuite

- ACO-MoE demonstrated strong generalization to contact-rich manipulation tasks on RoboSuite (Door, Lift) using TD-MPC2 as the backbone.
- It achieved significant gains: +344% on `Door` and +167% on `Lift` compared to Q$^2$ under VDCS Markov-switching. Under the `Video Hard` setting, it surpassed FTR by +34% and +75% respectively. This further validated its plug-and-play compatibility and architecture-agnostic nature.

#### 4.5. Generalization to DMC-GB

- ACO-MoE achieved state-of-the-art average performance on the DMC-GB `video-hard` (809, outperforming FTR by 6.2%) and `color-hard` (818, outperforming SMG by 17.4%) benchmarks.
- This indicates that the benefit of composing an agent-centric foreground onto a uniform black background extends beyond physical degradations to background distractions, effectively eliminating nuisance variation regardless of its origin.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of visual reinforcement learning by systematically addressing the challenge of dynamic and heterogeneous visual perturbations, a significant barrier to the real-world deployment of autonomous agents.

1. **Advancement in Robust Visual RL:** The work introduces a comprehensive framework that robustifies visual RL agents against a range of non-stationary and heterogeneous visual corruptions. This directly addresses a critical gap in existing research, where focus has largely been on static degradations or background distractions.

2. **Novel Benchmark for Realistic Evaluation:** The introduction of the Visual Degraded Control Suite (VDCS) is impactful. By simulating Markov-switching physical degradations, VDCS provides a more realistic and challenging benchmark than prior work, fostering future research into robust perception and control under complex, real-world environmental dynamics.

3. **Theoretical Foundation for Robust Representations:** The information-theoretic analysis, demonstrating representation contamination in reconstruction-based world models and justifying foreground extraction as an Information Bottleneck surrogate, provides a principled understanding of the underlying failure modes and offers a theoretical basis for designing robust visual representations. This can guide the development of future algorithms.

4. **Practical and Flexible Solution:** ACO-MoE offers a plug-and-play preprocessing module that is compatible with diverse RL algorithms (model-free and model-based) without requiring modifications to their architectures or training procedures. Its frozen nature ensures stability during RL training, simplifying integration and reducing the complexity of deployment.

5. **Substantial Performance Gains:** The achieved state-of-the-art performance on both the novel VDCS and existing DMC-GB benchmarks, with significant recovery rates of clean performance, demonstrates the practical efficacy of the approach. This increased robustness can lead to more reliable and deployable RL agents in various applications.

6. **Dual-Stream Agent-Centric Observation:** The validation of the dual-stream approach (RGB restoration combined with foreground masking) underscores the complementary benefits of these two processes in disentangling task-relevant information from both physical degradations and background distractions. Compositing onto a black background further enhances the agent-centric nature of the observations.

7. **Potential for Real-World Applications:** By enhancing the robustness of visual RL to common real-world disturbances like weather, sensor noise, and varying lighting, this research has direct implications for improving the safety and reliability of autonomous systems in robotics, autonomous driving, and other domains where agents operate in dynamic and unpredictable visual environments.

The limitations acknowledged, such as potential adaptability issues to out-of-distribution corruptions and performance degradation on fine-grained tasks under severe pixel-level corruptions, indicate clear directions for future work, including extending to open-set corruption spaces and validation on physical robotic platforms. Overall, this work represents a significant step towards developing more robust and deployable visual reinforcement learning agents.
