# MMDuet2: Enhancing Proactive Interaction of Video MLLMs with Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> MMDUET2, a Video MLLM developed by Peking University and Meituan, improves proactive interaction by using multi-turn reinforcement learning and a text-to-text chat template to autonomously determine when and what to respond during streaming video. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on proactive interaction benchmarks, with a PAUC of 53.3 on the WEB dataset and significantly reduced reply duplication, while maintaining general video understanding capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing proactive Video MLLMs rely on manually tuned response thresholds, which are sensitive and lead to inconsistent or redundant replies.
- Training proactive interaction models requires precise reply timestamp annotations, which are difficult, costly, and prone to inaccuracies.
- Prior RL-enhanced Video MLLMs had not explored real-time, multi-turn dialogue, limiting their application in truly interactive scenarios.

## Method

- MMDUET2 formulates ==proactive interaction== as a text-to-text generation task, where the model explicitly outputs a textual response or "NO REPLY," eliminating manual thresholds.
- A multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) framework is introduced, employing a specialized reward system (PAUC, replication, in-span, prefix rewards) to encourage timely and accurate responses without precise timestamp annotations.
- A large-scale, high-quality proactive dialogue dataset was constructed from web and ego-centric videos to facilitate robust training and diverse interaction scenarios.

## Results

- MMDUET2 (RL-trained) achieved state-of-the-art performance on the ProactiveVideoQA benchmark, reaching a PAUC of 53.3 and a **4.2%** duplicate reply proportion on the [WEB] dataset, outperforming MMDuet's 38.9 PAUC and **81.3%** duplication.
- The model maintained performance nearly identical to its base Qwen2.5-VL **3B** model on standard offline video understanding benchmarks (Video-MME, MVBench, LongVideoBench), demonstrating no catastrophic forgetting.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of each reward component (replication, in-span, prefix rewards) in preventing issues such as redundant responses, out-of-context replies, and verbose output.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing proactive interaction as a text-to-text task allows the model to autonomously decide on response timing and content, removing the need for manual threshold tuning.
> - Reinforcement learning with a custom, multi-faceted reward system is effective for teaching timely and relevant proactive responses, overcoming the challenge of precise temporal annotation in training data.
> - Maintaining a lower frame sampling rate during supervised fine-tuning prevents model collapse, while a higher rate during inference significantly enhances proactive response timeliness and quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on MMDUET2 is situated at the cutting edge of multimodal AI, specifically within the rapidly evolving field of Video Multimodal Large Language Models (Video MLLMs). These models have dramatically improved video understanding and multi-modal interaction capabilities.

Historically, most Video MLLMs have operated in a "turn-based" manner, where the model only responds after an explicit user query. However, the broader research landscape is increasingly shifting towards "proactive interaction," a more advanced paradigm where models autonomously decide when to respond during continuous video playback. This proactive capability is crucial for real-time applications such as live-stream analysis, intelligent surveillance, egocentric assistance agents, and socially interactive AI, where timely and contextually relevant interventions are paramount.

Existing efforts in proactive video MLLMs, such as VideoLLM-Online (Chen et al., 2024a), MMDuet (Wang et al., 2024), Dispider (Qian et al., 2025), and TimeChat-Online (Yao et al., 2025), have explored various methods for enabling models to predict response timing, often relying on probability scores and manually tuned thresholds. However, these methods face challenges in achieving precise timing and avoiding redundant responses, partly due to the difficulty of acquiring exact reply timestamp annotations for supervised training. The ProactiveVideoQA benchmark (Wang et al., 2025) has also emerged to evaluate these capabilities, highlighting the community's focus on this problem.

Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) has gained traction as a powerful post-training strategy for enhancing Video MLLMs, moving beyond purely supervised approaches. Works like VLM-RLAIF (Ahn et al., 2024), Video-R1 (Feng et al., 2025), VideoChat-R1 (Li et al., 2025), LongVILA-R1-7B (Chen et al., 2025b), and R1-Omni (Zhao et al., 2025) have demonstrated RL's effectiveness in improving video grounding, temporal dynamics capture, and multimodal perception. However, a significant gap identified by the authors is that "existing RL-enhanced VideoMLLMs have not explored real-time interaction or multi-turn dialogue," which limits their applicability in truly interactive scenarios.

MMDUET2 directly addresses this gap by being one of the first to apply multi-turn reinforcement learning to enhance proactive, real-time video MLLM interaction. It pushes the boundaries by proposing a novel, entirely text-based approach to response timing and content generation, overcoming the limitations of manual thresholds and the impracticality of precise timestamp annotations. By integrating RL with a carefully designed reward system, MMDUET2 aims to achieve state-of-the-art performance in both response timing and quality, thus significantly advancing the state of proactive video MLLMs towards more natural and effective real-time human-AI interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this work is to develop a Video MLLM capable of **proactive interaction** that autonomously decides *when* and *what* to respond during streaming video playback, moving beyond conventional turn-based dialogue systems. The authors identify and are motivated by two critical limitations in previous proactive interaction methods:

1. **Dependency on Manual Response Thresholds**: Existing proactive Video MLLMs often determine response timing by predicting a probability score against a pre-defined threshold. This threshold requires manual tuning during inference, which is highly sensitive. An improperly set threshold can lead to undesirable behaviors, such as the model never replying or frequently producing duplicated content, hindering user experience. The authors aim to eliminate this manual tuning by adopting an entirely text-based approach where the model explicitly outputs "NO REPLY" or a textual response, making the decision-making process an intrinsic part of language generation.

2. **Difficulty of Annotating Precise Reply Timestamps**: Training proactive interaction models typically requires supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with exact reply timestamps. However, annotating precise timestamps for specific events within long video segments is computationally intensive, challenging, and prone to inaccuracies due to the coarse-grained nature of typical scene segmentation and captioning. This often leads to responses that are not timely, appearing several seconds after the relevant visual information. The motivation here is to overcome this data annotation bottleneck by designing a training method that encourages timely and accurate responses *without* requiring precise response time annotations in the training data.

To address these motivations, the key objectives are:
- To propose a novel text-to-text approach that allows the model to autonomously determine whether to respond or remain silent at each turn, based on dialogue history and visual context.
- To introduce a multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) based training method that encourages timely and accurate responses by leveraging a niche reward design, thereby circumventing the need for precise reply timestamp annotations.
- To construct a large-scale, high-quality proactive dialogue dataset from diverse video sources (web and ego-centric videos) to facilitate robust training.
- To develop MMDuet2, a state-of-the-art proactive Video MLLM, and demonstrate its superior performance in response timing and quality compared to existing baselines on relevant benchmarks, while maintaining strong offline video understanding capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MMDUET2 proposes a comprehensive methodology that integrates a novel text-to-text formulation of proactive interaction with a sophisticated multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) framework.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction

The authors curated a dataset of approximately 52,000 videos, primarily from YouTube (Live-WhisperX) and ego-centric datasets (Ego-Exo4D, EgoExoLearn), ensuring diversity. The construction process involves:
1. **Scene Segmentation and Captioning**: Videos are divided into scenes with relatively independent and clear content. For web videos, sentence boundaries in subtitles are used for scene segmentation, and MLLMs generate detailed captions. For ego-centric videos, existing segment-level annotations are leveraged.
2. **QA Generation**: An LLM is used to generate 2-4 questions per video, each with a list of answers corresponding to different scenes. If a scene's information cannot answer a question, "NO REPLY" is assigned.
3. **Proactive Dialogue Construction**: Two types of dialogues are generated:
 - **One Question, Multiple Answers (1QnA)**: A single question is asked at the beginning, and the model must provide answers within the timespan of corresponding video scenes.
 - **Multiple Questions, Multiple Answers (nQnA)**: Users can ask questions at any time. The model first provides an "immediate answer" summarizing past relevant scenes, then continues to answer future scenes proactively until the next question is raised.

#### 3.2. Formulating Proactive Dialogue with a Chat Template

A key innovation is the representation of the entire proactive interaction as a text-to-text chat, compatible with standard LLM frameworks.
- **System Message**: A customized system message initializes the dialogue, defining the assistant's role (answer questions based on continuous frames) and rules (include information since last reply, output "NO REPLY" if no relevant info). This also helps prevent catastrophic forgetting of offline tasks.
- **User Turns**: The user provides frames (1-2 frames per turn, sampled every 2 seconds) and/or text input (the question or subsequent implicit turn).
- **Assistant Turns**: The model explicitly generates either a textual response or "NO REPLY." This text-based decision-making eliminates the need for manual thresholds.
This approach allows the model to predict both *when* to respond (by not generating "NO REPLY") and *what* to say, seamlessly integrating the timing decision into the generative process.

#### 3.3. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

MMDUET2 uses Qwen2.5-VL 3B as its base model.
- **Dataset**: The majority of the constructed proactive dialogue dataset is used for SFT. Model answers are intentionally placed at the *end* of their respective reply timespans during SFT to ensure that all relevant events have occurred, thereby preventing hallucinations.
- **Auxiliary Data**: To preserve general offline video understanding capabilities, 25,000 examples from LLaVA-Video (offline QA) and 25,000 from tarsier2 (video captioning) are included, formatted with Qwen2.5-VL's default template.
- **Training Details**: Frames are sampled at 2-second intervals, using 128 tokens per frame, with 2 frames per user turn. This phase is conducted on 16 H800 GPUs for about 8 hours.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Training

The RL phase is critical for enhancing response timeliness and quality without precise timestamp annotations.
- **Motivation for RL**: SFT alone yields low-frequency, high-latency responses and struggles to learn optimal timing. Precise ground-truth reply times are difficult to annotate. RL, guided by a reward system that values correctness and timeliness, allows the model to learn these behaviors autonomously.
- **Reward Modeling**: The reward system is designed to encourage early and accurate responses while penalizing undesirable behaviors.
 1. **PAUC Reward (r_PAUC)**: Inspired by the Proactive Area Under Curve (PAUC) metric (Wang et al., 2025). PAUC quantifies response quality by considering both correctness (text similarity to ground truth) and timeliness (earlier responses for the same correctness are better). The authors modify PAUC by using a max score S=4 (instead of 2) and calculating similarity only for the current turn's reply. An initial score of 0.5 is given at `t_start` to penalize entirely incorrect responses more heavily.
 2. **Replication Reward (r_rep)**: Penalizes generating redundant or duplicated replies. It uses an LLM to judge if new information is already covered, encouraging focus on new visual cues.
 3. **In-span Reward (r_in_span)**: Penalizes replies outside any ground-truth reply span, ensuring contextual relevance.
 4. **Prefix Reward (r_pfx)**: Penalizes verbose replies that repeat previous content before adding new information, promoting conciseness.
 The overall reward `r` is a weighted sum of these components, with `ω_PAUC = 3, ω_rep = 2, ω_in_span = 0.5, ω_pfx = 2`, balancing informativeness and simplicity.
- **Training Details**: A subset of videos is reserved for RL. To mitigate sparse rewards and the temporal credit assignment problem in long videos, RL rollouts are performed on short video spans (20-60 seconds). Ground-truth replies for turns before the selected span are provided. GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) is used with 4 rollouts, leveraging SGLang and verl frameworks. This phase takes about 20 hours on 8 H800 GPUs.
- **Frame Sampling Density**: For SFT, a 2-second frame interval is used to avoid bias towards "NO REPLY". For RL, different intervals are explored, but the paper suggests that inference benefits significantly from a higher frame rate (1 second) to allow earlier perception and decision-making.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of MMDUET2 in enhancing proactive interaction and maintaining general video understanding capabilities.

- **State-of-the-Art Proactive Performance**:
 - **ProactiveVideoQA Benchmark**: MMDUET2 (RL-trained) significantly outperforms existing baselines (VideoLLM-Online, MMDuet) in both PAUC (Proactive Area Under Curve) and reduction of reply duplicate proportion across various video categories ([WEB], [EGO], [TV], [VAD]). For instance, on the [WEB] dataset, MMDuet2 achieved a PAUC of 53.3 and a duplicate proportion of 4.2, compared to MMDuet's 38.9/81.3. This highlights improved timeliness and reduced redundancy.
 - **StreamingBench Proactive Output (PO) Task**: MMDuet2 (RL-trained) achieves the highest accuracy (34.69) compared to Dispider (25.34) and MMDuet (29.44), further confirming its superior proactive capabilities.
 - The model showed poorer performance on the [VAD] surveillance video dataset, indicating an ongoing challenge for current models in this domain.

- **Inference Speed**: Despite the text-to-text approach requiring the generation of "NO REPLY" tokens, MMDuet2's inference wall time (2m52s) for the [WEB] task is comparable to MMDuet (2m27s), suggesting that the approach is practical for real-time applications.

- **Maintenance of Offline Video Understanding**:
 - Evaluations on standard offline benchmarks (Video-MME, MVBench, LongVideoBench) show that MMDuet2 (both SFT and RL versions) maintains performance almost identical to the base Qwen2.5-VL 3B model. This indicates that the proactive interaction training does not lead to catastrophic forgetting of general video understanding abilities.

- **Ablation Studies on Reward Items**:
 - The ablation studies confirmed the critical role of each reward component in the RL phase.
 - Removing `r_rep` (replication reward) led to a significant increase in duplicate responses and an unreasonably high PAUC.
 - Removing `r_in_span` (in-span reward) caused the model to generate replies even in unrelated video spans, drastically increasing response density and making evaluation unfeasible for long videos.
 - Removing `r_pfx` (prefix reward) resulted in verbose prefix repetitions and performance degradation, especially on challenging ego-centric videos. These results underscore the necessity of the multi-faceted reward design for robust proactive interaction.

- **Impact of Frame Sampling Density**:
 - **SFT Phase**: A 1-second frame interval during SFT caused model collapse (always "NO REPLY"), indicating that a relatively lower frequency (2 seconds) is better to avoid over-biasing the model during supervised training.
 - **RL Phase**: Frame interval had no significant impact on performance during RL.
 - **Inference Phase**: Reducing the frame interval from 2 seconds to 1 second during inference significantly improved performance (e.g., PAUC on [WEB] from 44.2 to 53.3). This suggests that a higher decision rate allows the model to perceive appropriate response timings earlier, enhancing user experience and PAUC scores. The model's robustness to varying frame sampling strategies is demonstrated, recommending low-frequency training for cost savings and high-frequency inference for better interaction.

- **RL Training Dynamics**:
 - The training process showed three distinct stages:
 1. **Transition Period (Steps 0-180)**: Initial decline in response count and performance as the model shifts from SFT's response patterns to RL's objectives.
 2. **Growth Period (Steps 180-450)**: Rapid increase in response frequency and PAUC performance, with controlled repetition, as the model learns to generate earlier and more accurate responses.
 3. **Plateau Period (Steps 450-489)**: Performance stabilizes on the easier [WEB] task, but a slight increase in repetition is observed on the more challenging [EGO] task, pointing to potential generalization issues with long, complex videos that could be addressed with more diverse data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in MMDUET2 marks a significant advancement in the field of video multimodal large language models, particularly in enabling more natural and effective human-AI interaction in real-time streaming video scenarios.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Novel RL-based Training for Proactive Interaction**: The most impactful contribution is the introduction of a multi-turn reinforcement learning method that enables models to generate timely and accurate proactive responses *without* requiring precise timestamp annotations in the training data. This overcomes a major bottleneck in data annotation and model development, making the creation of sophisticated proactive systems more feasible.
2. **Text-to-Text Proactive Decision-Making**: By reformulating the response timing decision into a text generation task (generating "NO REPLY" or a full response), the paper offers a highly compatible and generalizable approach. This eliminates the need for manual threshold tuning and extensive architectural modifications, allowing integration with most existing LLM training and inference frameworks.
3. **Large-scale Proactive Dialogue Dataset**: The construction of a high-quality dataset of 52,000 videos with diverse 1QnA and nQnA dialogue structures provides a valuable resource for future research and development in proactive video MLLMs.
4. **State-of-the-Art Proactive Video MLLM (MMDuet2)**: The resulting model, MMDuet2, demonstrates superior performance on established proactive interaction benchmarks (ProactiveVideoQA, StreamingBench), outperforming previous baselines in both response timeliness (PAUC) and quality (reduced duplication and repetition). Crucially, this is achieved without compromising its general offline video understanding capabilities.

**Potential Impact:**

The implications of MMDUET2 are far-reaching, especially for real-time AI applications:
- **Enhanced Real-time Assistance**: MMDuet2 could power more intelligent and responsive AI assistants in live-stream analysis, offering real-time commentary, summarization, or answering audience questions proactively.
- **Improved Surveillance and Monitoring**: In surveillance systems, the model could proactively highlight salient events or anomalies, providing timely alerts without requiring constant human monitoring.
- **Egocentric AI Agents**: For wearable cameras and egocentric assistance, the model could offer contextual help or information as events unfold, making AI more deeply integrated into human experiences.
- **Socially Interactive AI**: The ability to decide when to interject or remain silent is fundamental to natural human conversation. MMDuet2's advancements move AI agents closer to more intuitive and socially aware interactions.
- **Democratization of Proactive AI**: By simplifying the training paradigm (no precise timestamp annotation) and enhancing framework compatibility, this work lowers the barrier for researchers and developers to build and experiment with proactive multimodal systems.

**Future Directions**:
The authors acknowledge several promising avenues for future research, including expanding the dataset to cover more diverse tasks beyond QA (e.g., teaching and learning), integrating computational efficiency techniques like visual token compression, and extending proactive capabilities to include other modalities like speech understanding and generation, paving the way for truly multimodal, real-time interactive AI.
