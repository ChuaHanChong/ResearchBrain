# CRAFT: Video Diffusion for Bimanual Robot Data Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A video diffusion-based framework, CRAFT, generates diverse, action-consistent bimanual robot manipulation data to improve policy robustness and generalization. It achieves significantly higher success rates (e.g., up to 89.3% in cross-embodiment simulation and 95% in real-world camera viewpoint generalization) across various visual conditions and robot embodiments by synthesizing photorealistic demonstrations.

## Problem

- Robot learning, particularly for bimanual manipulation, is constrained by the scarcity, high cost, and limited visual diversity of real-world data.
- Policies trained on limited real-world data often lack robustness and generalization capabilities across varying environmental conditions, object configurations, camera viewpoints, and different robot embodiments.
- Existing data augmentation methods typically address only a few aspects of visual variability, and a unified framework for generating diverse data across multiple visual axes for bimanual tasks has been largely absent.

## Method

- **CRAFT** (Canny-guided Robot Data Generation using Video Diffusion Transformers) is introduced as a ==video diffusion==-based framework for scalable bimanual demonstration generation.
- The framework first expands a small set of real-world demonstrations into diverse simulation trajectories by replaying and varying object poses, then extracts ==Canny-edge control videos== from these simulations.
- A pre-trained video diffusion model is conditioned on these Canny-edge videos, real-world reference images, and language instructions to synthesize photorealistic target videos across ==seven distinct augmentation techniques== (object pose, lighting, object color, background, camera viewpoint, multi-view, and cross-embodiment transfer).

## Results

- In simulation, CRAFT achieved up to **89.3%** success rates for cross-embodiment tasks (e.g., UR5 to Franka Panda), significantly surpassing baselines (**2.0-6.0%**) and policies trained on directly collected target robot data (**55.0-69.0%**).
- Real-world experiments demonstrated that CRAFT-trained policies consistently outperformed policies trained solely on real-world data and other augmentation baselines, achieving high success rates (e.g., **17-19/20** successes) across diverse lighting, background, camera viewpoint, object color, and cross-embodiment generalization scenarios.
- An ablation study confirmed that Canny-edge conditioning nearly doubled performance (**21.6%** success rate vs. **10.3%** without) in a simulated Stack Bowls task, highlighting its effectiveness in providing clear structural guidance critical for manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Canny-edge conditioning provides optimal structural guidance for video diffusion models in robot data generation, preserving essential robot and object motion while allowing flexibility for synthesizing diverse visual appearances.
> - Video diffusion models can effectively synthesize photorealistic, temporally coherent, and action-consistent robot demonstrations with corresponding action labels, addressing the need for structured temporal data in robot learning.
> - A unified pipeline that systematically generates diverse data across multiple visual axes significantly enhances policy robustness and generalization, offering a scalable alternative to expensive real-world data collection, even for cross-embodiment transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "CRAFT" operates within the domain of robot learning, specifically addressing challenges in imitation learning for bimanual manipulation tasks. Imitation learning, which trains robotic policies from human or expert demonstrations, has facilitated the development of increasingly capable robot systems. However, its effectiveness is often constrained by the availability of large, diverse datasets. Collecting real-world robot data, particularly for complex bimanual tasks, is resource-intensive and often yields limited visual diversity, hindering the generalization and robustness of learned policies across varying environmental conditions, object configurations, camera viewpoints, and robot embodiments.

Existing research in data augmentation for robot learning has explored various strategies, but these often focus on specific subsets of augmentations. For instance, some methods augment only third-person or wrist-camera views, while others concentrate solely on cross-embodiment transfer for single-arm robots. A unified framework capable of simultaneously generating diverse data across multiple visual axes (e.g., lighting, background, object appearance, camera views, and cross-embodiment transfer) for bimanual manipulation has been largely absent. The Sim2Real gap, where policies trained in simulation struggle to transfer to the real world, further complicates data scaling. Traditional approaches to bridge this gap often involve replaying simulated demonstrations on physical robots or constructing complex predictive world models.

The emergence of powerful diffusion-based video generative models has opened new avenues for synthesizing high-fidelity visual data. While these models have been used for tasks like video prediction or reconstructing world models, their potential for systematically synthesizing action-labeled demonstrations for imitation learning, particularly for coordinated bimanual tasks and across multiple axes of visual variation, has not been fully leveraged. CRAFT aims to fill this gap by integrating these advanced video generation capabilities with structured guidance to produce highly diverse and physically plausible robot demonstrations, thereby advancing the field's ability to scale robot learning without prohibitive real-world data collection costs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the fundamental limitations imposed by the cost and narrow visual diversity of real-world data in bimanual robot learning from demonstrations. The authors observe that policies trained on such limited data often lack robustness when deployed in environments with different viewpoints, object configurations, lighting, backgrounds, object colors, or when transferred to different robot embodiments.

The specific motivations and objectives driving CRAFT are:

- **Address Data Scarcity and Cost:** Real-world data collection for bimanual manipulation is expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale, leading to insufficient data volumes for training robust policies. CRAFT aims to provide a scalable method for generating a large volume of synthetic demonstrations.
- **Enhance Visual Diversity:** Policies need to generalize across a wide array of visual conditions to be robust. Existing augmentation methods typically address only a few aspects of visual variability. CRAFT seeks to provide a unified pipeline that spans seven distinct axes of visual variation: object pose, lighting conditions, object color, background environment, camera viewpoint, combined wrist and third-person perspectives, and cross-embodiment transfer.
- **Improve Policy Robustness and Generalization:** By exposing policies to a broader and more diverse range of visual inputs during training, the objective is to significantly improve their success rates and generalization capabilities when faced with unseen scenarios in both simulated and real-world deployments.
- **Facilitate Cross-Embodiment Transfer for Bimanual Robots:** Transferring learned skills from one robot platform to another (cross-embodiment learning) is crucial for wider applicability but challenging, especially for bimanual tasks. CRAFT aims to enable photorealistic cross-embodiment transfer by synthesizing data for target robots based on source robot demonstrations.
- **Generate Temporally Coherent and Action-Consistent Data:** Simply augmenting individual frames is insufficient for robot learning, which requires understanding motion and temporal sequences. A key objective is to generate synthetic videos that are not only photorealistic but also temporally coherent and accurately paired with corresponding action labels, ensuring physical plausibility of the synthesized robot motions.
- **Leverage Video Diffusion Models Effectively:** The authors intend to demonstrate that modern video diffusion models can be effectively conditioned to generate robot data. Their specific insight is that Canny-edge detection can extract essential structural cues (robot arms, objects) from simulated trajectories while abstracting away irrelevant low-level details. This abstraction allows the diffusion model greater flexibility to synthesize diverse visual appearances while preserving the underlying physically plausible robot motion structure.
- **Bypass Sim2Real Replay:** Rather than requiring simulated demonstrations to be replayed on a real robot to bridge the Sim2Real gap, CRAFT aims to directly synthesize photorealistic images from simulator-generated trajectories, thereby eliminating the need for this costly physical step.

In essence, CRAFT is motivated by the need for robust, generalizable bimanual robot manipulation policies and aims to achieve this by developing a scalable, unified, and high-fidelity synthetic data generation framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CRAFT (Canny-guided Robot Data Generation using Video Diffusion Transformers) is a video diffusion-based framework designed for scalable bimanual demonstration generation. It synthesizes temporally coherent manipulation videos while simultaneously producing action labels. The methodology comprises three main stages: Trajectory Expansion, Video Generation, and Augmented Dataset Construction.

**A. Trajectory Expansion**
The process begins with a small set of real-world teleoperation demonstrations ($D_{real}$).
1. **Digital Twin Creation:** A digital twin pipeline is used to transfer objects and robots from the real-world setup into a simulation environment. This is achieved using tools like AprilTags for object localization and known object meshes, as found in benchmarks like RoboTwin.
2. **Simulation Replay:** Each real-world trajectory ($\tau_{real}$) is replayed in the simulation to generate a corresponding source video ($V_s$) and simulation trajectory ($\tau_{sim}$). This initial step results in a simulation dataset ($D_{sim}$) equivalent in size to $D_{real}$.
3. **Data Scaling and Variation:** To expand $D_{sim}$, the authors adopt an approach inspired by DexMimicGen. Each $\tau_{real}$ is decomposed into object-centric subtasks. A transformation operator applies random translations and rotations to the target object's pose, sampled from a uniform distribution within the robot's physically feasible workspace.
4. **Validation:** Each new candidate trajectory, generated with a novel scene configuration, is executed in simulation and validated for task success. Only successful trajectories are retained to further expand $D_{sim}$.
5. **Canny-Edge Extraction:** The validated simulation trajectories are rendered into source videos ($V_s$). From these $V_s$, Canny-edge control videos ($V_c$) are extracted. These edge representations are then post-processed using techniques like edge thickening and edge connectivity to create cleaner and more informative structural control signals, bridging disconnected edges and strengthening salient features.

**B. Video Generation**
This stage leverages a pre-trained video diffusion model to convert the structured information from simulation into photorealistic videos.
1. **Model Backbone:** The Wan2.1-Fun-Control model is utilized, as it supports conditioning on various modalities, including Canny edges, depth, and skeleton pose.
2. **Canny-Edge Conditioning Rationale:** Canny-edge conditioning is selected over depth or skeleton pose because it offers a balance. Skeleton pose only captures robot arm structure without object information, while depth conditioning retains too much fine-grained scene detail, which limits the model's flexibility to synthesize diverse visual appearances. Canny edges preserve the salient structural features of both robot arms and objects while abstracting away low-level pixel details, providing clear guidance for motion while allowing visual appearance variations.
3. **Conditional Generation:** The diffusion model learns to approximate the conditional distribution $p_\phi(V_d | I_f, V_c, \ell)$, where:
 - $V_d$: The synthesized photorealistic target video.
 - $I_f$: A real-world reference image, which can be modified or omitted depending on the desired augmentation.
 - $V_c$: The Canny-edge control video extracted in the previous stage, providing structural and motion guidance.
 - $\ell$: A language instruction, providing semantic guidance for visual characteristics.
4. **Appearance Variation:** By selectively varying $I_f$ and $\ell$ while keeping $V_c$ fixed, the model synthesizes diverse target videos ($V_d$) that preserve the underlying robot motion structure but exhibit varied visual appearances. Large Language Models (LLMs) are used to automatically generate semantic variants for $\ell$.

**C. Augmented Dataset Construction**
This final stage constructs the augmented dataset ($D_{gen}$) by pairing the diffusion-synthesized videos ($V_d$) with the corresponding action labels from the simulation trajectories. CRAFT supports a modular application of seven distinct augmentation techniques:

1. **Object Pose:** Variations are introduced during trajectory expansion by randomly translating and rotating object poses in simulation. A reference image showing gripper-object contact is used to enhance fidelity.
2. **Lighting:** The reference image ($I_f$) is augmented using an image generation model (Veo3) to synthesize variants under different ambient illuminations (e.g., blue, green light). This preserves complex scene properties like shadows.
3. **Object Color:** An empty table image is used as $I_f$ to avoid anchoring to a specific object color. The Canny-edge video ($V_c$) provides object contours, and the language instruction ($\ell$) specifies the desired object color. LLMs generate lists of color prompts.
4. **Background:** The reference image ($I_f$) is omitted to allow maximum background variation. The language instruction ($\ell$) describes the desired background, with LLMs generating diverse background descriptions.
5. **Cross-Embodiment:** Demonstrations from a source robot are retargeted to a target robot using forward kinematics (FK) and inverse kinematics (IK) in simulation. These retargeted demonstrations generate the $V_c$ for the target robot, and the diffusion model synthesizes videos of the new embodiment.
6. **Camera Viewpoint:** Multiple cameras are placed in the simulator, and their views are tiled into a single image ($I_{s,tile}$). This tiled image is used to extract $V_c$, and a similarly tiled $I_f$ is provided. The diffusion model generates multi-view videos while maintaining the tiled structure.
7. **Wrist + Third-Person View:** Similar to camera viewpoint, this method tiles views from the left wrist camera, right wrist camera, and a third-person (external) camera into a single image. This tiled input guides the diffusion model to synthesize multi-view demonstrations.

Through these stages, CRAFT repeatedly applies this procedure to generate a large, visually diverse, and action-consistent dataset ($D_{gen}$) for downstream policy training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates CRAFT across both simulated and real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, primarily using the Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) policy for training and evaluation.

**A. Simulation Experiments**
Simulation experiments were conducted using the RoboTwin benchmark, focusing on cross-embodiment transfer from a bimanual UR5 (source robot) to a bimanual Franka Panda (target robot) on three tasks: Lift Pot (LP), Place Cans in Plasticbox (PC), and Stack Bowls (SB).

- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:**
 - **Shadow** (a data editing baseline) showed low success rates (2.0% - 6.0%), struggling due to segmentation mask occlusion of critical gripper-object contact points.
 - **CRAFT (Target)**, which applies the video diffusion model to collected source-to-target transfer demonstrations without additional generation, achieved moderate improvements (11.3% - 21.6%).
 - **CRAFT (Ours)**, the full pipeline generating 1000 demonstrations with object pose, lighting, and background augmentation, achieved significantly higher success rates (82.6% for LP, 89.3% for PC, 86.0% for SB). These results notably surpassed the "Collected Target" baseline (55.0% - 69.0%), which involves collecting 100 demonstrations directly on the target robot. This indicates that CRAFT's diverse data generation can effectively substitute for costly direct data collection on target robots.

- **Ablation Study (Canny-edge Conditioning):**
 - An ablation was performed on the Stack Bowls task comparing video generation with and without Canny-edge control input, isolating its effect on synthesized image quality for ACT performance.
 - **CRAFT w/o Canny** (using raw simulation images as input) achieved a 10.3% success rate.
 - **CRAFT w/ Canny** (using processed Canny edges) achieved a 21.6% success rate, nearly doubling the performance. This suggests that Canny edges provide clearer structural guidance, leading to higher-fidelity synthesized images, particularly for gripper-object contact, which is crucial for manipulation.

**B. Real-World Experiments**
Real-world experiments were conducted on a bimanual Franka Research 3, evaluating CRAFT across seven augmentation techniques on three tasks: Lift Roller (LR), Place Cans in Plasticbox (PC), and Stack Bowls (SB). Policies were compared against policies trained on real-world data only (ACT w/o Aug), CRAFT with only object pose augmentation (CRAFT Pose-Only), and augmentation-specific baselines.

- **Lighting Generalization:** Tested under blue, green, red, and yellow ambient illumination. CRAFT (Ours) achieved 17/20 (LR), 14/20 (PC), and 12/20 (SB) successes, significantly outperforming ACT w/o Aug (3/20, 1/20, 0/20) and Color Jitter baseline (13/20, 9/20, 8/20). This indicates that photorealistic diffusion-based lighting augmentation is more effective than 2D color manipulation.
- **Background Generalization:** Tested across three distinct background scenarios. CRAFT (Ours) achieved 18/20 (LR), 15/20 (PC), and 10/20 (SB) successes, outperforming ACT w/o Aug (4/20, 0/20, 0/20) and RoboEngine (4/20, 5/20, 6/20), which suffered from segmentation inconsistencies.
- **Camera View Generalization:** Tested across four camera perspectives. CRAFT (Ours) achieved 19/20 (LR), 18/20 (PC), and 18/20 (SB) successes, substantially outperforming Fine-Tuned VISTA (14/20, 8/20, 6/20), demonstrating the advantage of video diffusion for multi-view generation.
- **Object Color Generalization:** Trained on red objects, evaluated on gray objects. CRAFT (Ours) achieved 18/20 (LR), 18/20 (PC), and 17/20 (SB) successes, substantially outperforming SAM3 (15/20, 9/20, 11/20), which had segmentation issues.
- **Wrist + 3rd Person View Generation:** CRAFT Pose-Only with 100 generated demonstrations approached the performance of ACT w/o Aug trained on 100 real wrist-camera demonstrations. CRAFT (Ours) with 1000 generated demonstrations achieved perfect success rates (20/20) on Lift Roller and Stack Bowls, and 19/20 on Place Cans, demonstrating high effectiveness and scalability for multi-view camera generation.
- **Cross-Embodiment (Real-World):** Transfer from a bimanual xArm7 (source) to a bimanual Franka Panda (target). CRAFT (Ours) achieved 17/20 (LR), 15/20 (PC), and 16/20 (SB) successes, outperforming Shadow (2/20, 1/20, 1/20) and even the "Collected Target" baseline (5/20, 2/20, 3/20), confirming its capability to substitute real target robot data collection.

**General Findings:** CRAFT consistently demonstrated that policies trained on its generated, visually diverse datasets significantly outperform baselines and policies trained solely on limited real-world data. The method effectively generates photorealistic and temporally coherent videos with action labels, leading to improved robustness and generalization in bimanual manipulation tasks across both simulation and real-world scenarios. The results highlight the substantial benefit of diffusion-based video generation for expanding demonstration diversity in robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CRAFT framework presents several significant contributions and has the potential to impact the field of robot learning in multiple ways:

- **Addresses Data Scarcity in Robotics:** CRAFT offers a scalable and cost-effective solution to the pervasive problem of data scarcity in robot learning. By synthesizing large volumes of photorealistic and action-consistent demonstrations, it reduces the reliance on expensive and time-consuming real-world data collection, particularly for complex bimanual tasks. This could accelerate the development and deployment of robotic systems.
- **Enhances Policy Robustness and Generalization:** The ability of CRAFT to generate data across seven distinct axes of visual variation—including object pose, lighting, background, object color, camera viewpoint, multi-view perspectives, and cross-embodiment transfer—directly leads to more robust and generalizable robot policies. Policies trained on CRAFT-generated data consistently outperform those trained on limited real data or simpler augmentation strategies, demonstrating improved performance in diverse and previously unseen environments.
- **Unified Data Augmentation Framework:** Unlike prior work that often focuses on isolated augmentation techniques, CRAFT provides a comprehensive and unified pipeline. This modularity allows researchers and practitioners to apply any subset of augmentations, offering flexibility and efficiency in dataset creation tailored to specific task requirements.
- **Leverages Advanced Generative Models for Robotics:** The research effectively demonstrates the power of video diffusion models, specifically conditioned by Canny-edge structural cues, for robot data generation. The finding that Canny-edge conditioning provides optimal guidance—preserving structure while allowing appearance variability—is an important methodological contribution for future applications of generative models in robotics.
- **Facilitates Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** CRAFT significantly advances cross-embodiment learning for bimanual manipulation. Its capability to retarget demonstrations and synthesize photorealistic videos for different robot platforms without requiring any real-world data collection on the target robot is a crucial step towards creating more adaptable and versatile robotic systems.
- **Bypasses Traditional Sim2Real Constraints:** By directly synthesizing photorealistic data from simulated trajectories, CRAFT eliminates the need for physically replaying simulated demonstrations on real robots, thereby streamlining the Sim2Real transfer process and making it more data-efficient.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The success of CRAFT is likely to inspire further research in leveraging advanced video generation techniques for various aspects of robot learning, including exploring different conditioning modalities, improving fidelity for highly dynamic or deformable object manipulations (as noted in limitations), and integrating with more sophisticated scene understanding and reasoning capabilities.

In summary, CRAFT provides a robust and scalable method for generating diverse synthetic data, fundamentally improving the training of bimanual robot manipulation policies. Its findings offer a clear path towards developing more generalizable and practical robotic systems by mitigating one of the most significant bottlenecks in the field: the cost and limitations of real-world data.
