# AMPLIFY: Actionless Motion Priors for Robot Learning from Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Tech developed AMPLIFY, a modular framework that enables robots to learn generalizable policies by decoupling visual motion prediction from action inference. The system converts visual dynamics into compact, discrete motion tokens, allowing it to leverage large-scale action-free video data for learning motion priors. This approach significantly improves data efficiency, achieving a 1.94x improvement over prior methods in few-shot settings and enabling zero-shot generalization with an average 60.5% success rate on unseen tasks.

## Problem

- The scarcity and high cost of collecting action-labeled robot data severely limit the scalability and generalization of learned robot policies.
- Traditional Behavior Cloning struggles to generalize to out-of-distribution tasks and cannot effectively utilize abundant action-free video data.
- Prior approaches to visual dynamics modeling for robot control are often computationally intensive (pixel-space prediction) or lack generalizability (e.g., relying on object masks).

## Method

- A three-stage modular framework that first preprocesses keypoint tracks from videos, then tokenizes them into compact, discrete latent motion codes using ==Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ)==.
- Trains a forward dynamics model to predict autoregressive sequences of these latent motion tokens from observations and task goals, utilizing abundant action-free video data.
- Develops an inverse dynamics model that translates the predicted latent motion tokens (along with visual and proprioceptive state) into robot action chunks, trained on limited action-labeled robot data.

## Results

- AMPLIFY achieved over **2x** better pixel-wise accuracy and **3.7x** better MSE compared to ATM on the LIBERO dataset for forward dynamics prediction.
- In few-shot learning settings, AMPLIFY showed an average **1.94x** improvement over ATM with only 2 demonstrations per task on LIBERO, demonstrating superior data efficiency.
- The framework achieved an average **60.5%** success rate in zero-shot generalization to unseen LIBERO tasks (**27x** improvement over best BC baseline), representing the first reported non-trivial success without in-distribution action data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling policy learning into forward (motion prediction) and inverse (action inference) dynamics allows independent scaling and training on different data modalities.
> - Compressing visual dynamics into compact, discrete motion tokens derived from keypoint trajectories provides an efficient and generalizable intermediate representation for learning and prediction.
> - Learning a local-window categorical distribution for instantaneous velocities in the decoder helps capture fine-grained and multimodal motion dynamics more effectively than direct regression.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions AMPLIFY within the rapidly evolving landscape of robot learning, which seeks to replicate the success of large-scale foundation models in other AI domains (e.g., image and language) by training "generalist models" for robotics. A central challenge in this pursuit is the scarcity and high cost of collecting action-labeled robot data, especially when compared to the vast amounts of readily available action-free video data on the internet.

Traditional **Behavior Cloning (BC)**, the prevailing approach for learning policies from expert demonstrations, fundamentally relies on large volumes of action-labeled data. The authors highlight its limitations: poor scalability to action-free videos and inability to generalize to out-of-distribution tasks due to its monolithic nature and lack of mechanisms to leverage heterogeneous data.

Prior efforts to incorporate large-scale video data into robot learning have explored several avenues:
- **Representation Learning:** Methods like reward/value prediction or time-contrastive learning extract useful features from videos but typically focus on static observations and do not explicitly model sequential dynamics.
- **Model-Based Approaches:** Training full video prediction models to capture visual dynamics offers a promising direction. However, pixel-space prediction is computationally intensive, limiting real-time control and often leading to compromises like open-loop control.
- **Latent Action Representations:** Learning compressed, latent features predictive of future states avoids high inference costs. Yet, these representations are often trained on image reconstruction objectives, capturing details not always relevant to policy learning.
- **Keypoint and Optical Flow Methods:** Recognizing the importance of motion over appearance, these approaches abstract visual dynamics into keypoint trajectories or optical flow fields. While effective, existing methods (e.g., ATM, Track2Act) often rely on expensive video prediction models, object-centric masks, calibrated cameras, or inefficient online planning, hindering their generality and efficiency. Many also do not learn a compact latent space for keypoints, leading to high computational costs and coarse dynamics predictions.

AMPLIFY addresses these limitations by proposing a novel, modular framework that **decouples policy learning into forward and inverse dynamics**, with a crucial intermediate step of encoding visual dynamics into **compact, discrete motion tokens derived from keypoint trajectories**. This allows the forward dynamics model to be trained on abundant action-free video data, while the inverse dynamics model can be trained on more limited, action-labeled interaction data. This decomposition is a direct response to the "data scarcity" problem and the need for "generalizable policies," enabling the system to harness diverse data modalities that traditional BC cannot. By focusing on *latent keypoint motion*, AMPLIFY aims to capture the essential dynamics relevant for control without the overhead of pixel-level prediction, positioning it as a highly efficient and generalizable approach in the continuum of robot learning from large-scale data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of AMPLIFY is to enable robots to learn generalizable and data-efficient policies by effectively leveraging the vast amounts of readily available action-free video data, which remains largely untapped by conventional robot learning methods.

The core motivations driving this research are:
1. **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** Action-labeled data for robotics is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to collect, severely limiting the generalization capabilities of learned policies. In contrast, action-free video data is abundant. The framework seeks to bridge this gap by finding a way to extract actionable knowledge from this passive data.
2. **Improving Generalization:** Traditional Behavior Cloning (BC) struggles to generalize across tasks, object categories, environments, and conditions because it directly maps observations to actions from limited expert demonstrations. AMPLIFY aims to foster better generalization by learning more robust and transferable priors from diverse video sources.
3. **Decoupling Task Understanding from Execution:** The authors observe that learning "what motion defines a task" (task understanding) and "how robots can perform it" (task execution) are distinct challenges. By decoupling these two aspects into a forward dynamics model and an inverse dynamics model, AMPLIFY allows for independent scaling and training on different data modalities.
4. **Efficiency through Latent Motion Representation:** Prior approaches that model visual dynamics in pixel space (e.g., full video prediction) are computationally intensive and slow. The motivation behind using "compact, discrete motion tokens derived from keypoint trajectories" is to create an efficient and generalizable intermediate representation that captures essential motion information without the redundant details of pixel-level predictions.
5. **Learning from Heterogeneous Data:** The framework is designed to integrate various data types, including action-free human videos, undirected robot interaction data, and expert robot demonstrations, enabling a more holistic approach to data utilization.

In essence, AMPLIFY's key objectives are to develop a modular and efficient framework that can leverage the richness of large-scale video data to build powerful, generalizable motion priors, ultimately leading to more data-efficient and robust robot policies capable of solving novel tasks, even with minimal or zero in-distribution action-labeled data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AMPLIFY (Actionless Motion Priors for Learning Inverse and Forward Dynamics) is a three-stage modular framework designed to decouple visual motion prediction from action inference.

**Problem Setup:**
The framework assumes access to two primary datasets:
- **Video dataset (V):** Consisting of observations and goal specifications `{(o_t, g)}`. This data is action-free and can include human videos.
- **Robot interaction dataset (R):** Consisting of observations, proprioceptive states, and actions `{(o_t, q_t, a_t)}`. This data is action-labeled and specific to the robot.
The goal is to learn a visual control policy `π` that can be decomposed into a forward dynamics model `f: O × G → Z` (learning a motion prior in a latent space `Z`) and an inverse dynamics model `f_inv: O × Q × Z → A` (mapping latent motion to actions). This decomposition is crucial for independent scaling of `f` and `f_inv` using `V` and `R` respectively.

**Stages of AMPLIFY:**

1. **Preprocessing Keypoint Tracks (Section 2.1):**
 - An off-the-shelf point tracking model, CoTracker, is used to augment the video dataset `V`.
 - For each timestep `t`, a `20x20` uniform grid of `N=400` points is initialized in the image `o_t`.
 - These points are then tracked through the next `T=16` frames (`o_{t:t+T}`), yielding 2D pixel coordinates of keypoint tracks `κ_t ∈ R^(T×N×2)`.
 - The choice of a uniform grid is for simplicity and generality, and re-initializing the grid in each frame ensures consistent coverage and handles camera movements and occlusions.

2. **Motion Tokenization (Section 2.2):**
 - The core idea here is to learn a compact, discrete latent space for modeling motion, contrasting with prior pixel-space prediction methods.
 - Single-step velocities `u_t ∈ R^((T-1)×N×2)` are computed from the pre-processed keypoint trajectories `κ_t`.
 - A keypoint encoder `E_θ` (causally-masked transformer encoder) maps `u_t` to a sequence of continuous latent vectors `~z_t`.
 - These latent vectors are then quantized into discrete codes (tokens) `z_t ∈ Z^(b×d)` using **Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ)**. FSQ is chosen for its implicit codebook and single reconstruction loss, avoiding common VQ-VAE issues like representation collapse.
 - A keypoint decoder `D_θ` (unmasked transformer decoder) reconstructs the velocities from the discrete codes.
 - Crucially, the decoder outputs a **categorical distribution over a local `W×W` window** (e.g., `15x15`) for each point, representing fine-grained instantaneous velocities. This inductive bias favors local motion and can capture multimodal distributions better than direct regression.
 - The encoder and decoder are jointly trained on `V` using a cross-entropy loss `L_AE`.

3. **Forward Dynamics (Actionless Motion Prior) (Section 2.3):**
 - After the motion tokenizer is trained and frozen, an autoregressive transformer `f(o_t, g)` is trained to predict the tokenized motion sequence `z_t` for the next `T` timesteps.
 - Inputs to `f` include encoded current image observations (flattened feature map from a pre-trained ResNet-18) and a T5 text embedding of the task description. These are concatenated with a "start of sequence" (SOS) token.
 - A block-causal attention mask is applied (non-causal for conditioning tokens, causal for motion tokens).
 - The model is trained with a cross-entropy loss `L_forward` on the predicted latent codes `z_t`, *without* decoding them back to pixel space. Gradients only flow to the dynamics model, not the frozen tokenizer.

4. **Inverse Dynamics (Section 2.4):**
 - Finally, an inverse dynamics model `f_inv(o_t, q_t, z_t)` is trained to decode the latent motion tokens into a distribution over action chunks `a_t = a_{t:t+T}`.
 - This module is designed as a general reference follower and is *not* conditioned on the goal (`g`). It is trained on the action-labeled robot interaction data `R`.
 - The model uses a transformer decoder that cross-attends to image tokens, a linear projection of proprioceptive state, and the latent motion codes from the forward dynamics model.
 - An action head outputs a distribution over length-`T` action chunks (e.g., isotropic Gaussian prior).
 - It is trained with a negative log-likelihood (NLL) loss `L_inv` with a temporal discount `γ` to reduce the impact of less accurate long-horizon predictions.
 - Crucially, `f_inv` can be trained on ground-truth tokens `z_t` from the tokenizer or fine-tuned on the predicted outputs `ˆz_t` from the forward dynamics model, with both `E_θ` and `f` frozen.

**Inference (Section 2.5):**
During deployment, the forward dynamics model `f` autoregressively predicts a sequence of latent motion tokens `ˆz_t` given the current observation and task. The inverse dynamics model `f_inv` then decodes these predicted tokens (along with visual and proprioceptive state) into an action chunk, which is executed. Temporal ensembling is used to aggregate action predictions from previously predicted chunks for robust control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AMPLIFY's effectiveness is evaluated across two main axes: the quality of its dynamics predictions and the utility of these predictions for downstream tasks, including robotic policy learning and conditional video generation.

#### 4.1. Quality of Forward Dynamics Prediction (Section 3.1)

The quality of AMPLIFY's forward dynamics model, which predicts latent keypoint trajectories, was assessed on three diverse video datasets: LIBERO (simulated robot), BridgeData v2 (real-world robot), and Something-Something v2 (human videos).
- **Superior Accuracy:** AMPLIFY consistently demonstrated more accurate predictions compared to state-of-the-art baselines like ATM and Track2Act.
 - On the LIBERO dataset, AMPLIFY achieved over **2x better pixel-wise accuracy** and **3.7x better MSE** compared to ATM.
 - It outperformed Track2Act (which uses a goal image, unlike AMPLIFY) on the `∆_AUC` metric across BridgeData v2 and Something-Something v2 (4-6% improvement).
 - It also surpassed a baseline of predicting videos with Seer followed by CoTracker.
- **Design Choice Validation:** This superior performance is attributed to key design choices: compressing motion into a compact latent space, predicting discrete tokens with autoregressive transformers, and using local-window pixel space classification in the decoder to model multimodal motion distributions and capture fine-grained dynamics.

#### 4.2. Utility of Predicted Latent Motions for Policy Learning (Section 3.2)

The true measure of AMPLIFY's utility lies in its ability to enhance robotic policy learning, evaluated across four critical dimensions:

- **In-Distribution Performance:** In a standard behavior cloning (BC) setup where both forward and inverse dynamics models are trained only on demonstration data, AMPLIFY proved competitive with state-of-the-art BC methods. It also outperformed other video pre-training methods (ATM, UniPi) in this setting. This suggests that even without external data, AMPLIFY's architecture can learn effective policies.
- **Few-Shot Learning:** In low-data regimes, where the inverse dynamics model is trained on only 4%, 10%, or 20% of available demonstrations, AMPLIFY showed significant advantages.
 - Both AMPLIFY and ATM consistently outperformed a no-pre-training baseline, highlighting the strong prior provided by video pre-training on keypoint dynamics.
 - AMPLIFY achieved stronger performance than ATM across almost every LIBERO subset, particularly in extreme low-data settings. With only 2 demonstrations per task, AMPLIFY demonstrated an average **1.94x improvement over ATM**. This validates the higher utility of AMPLIFY's latent motion representation for action prediction compared to pixel-space track predictions.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** By training the forward dynamics model on *both* human and robot video data, while the inverse model uses only robot action data, AMPLIFY successfully leveraged human demonstrations.
 - On real-world manipulation tasks, AMPLIFY demonstrated average improvements of **1.32x, 1.4x, and 1.5x** over a Diffusion Policy baseline, with the gap widening as task complexity increased. This highlights AMPLIFY's ability to learn common dynamics across embodiments and effectively harness action-free human data.
- **Generalization (Zero-Shot):** In a highly challenging setting where no action data was available for target tasks (training on LIBERO 90, evaluating on unseen LIBERO Long, Object, Spatial, Goal suites), traditional BC methods completely failed (near-zero success rates).
 - In stark contrast, AMPLIFY achieved an impressive **average 60.5% success rate** on these target tasks. This represents the first reported non-trivial success on LIBERO without using any in-distribution action data and a **27x average improvement over the best BC baseline**. This remarkable generalization capacity underscores the value of latent dynamics prediction as a versatile interface for learning general priors from action-free videos and suggests the benefit of a reference-following inverse dynamics model over pure imitation learning.

#### 4.3. Utility of Predicted Latent Motions for Conditional Video Generation (Section 3.3)

Beyond robot control, AMPLIFY's predicted latent motion tokens also proved valuable for conditional video generation.
- When used to condition an existing video prediction model (AVDC), AMPLIFY's motion tokens led to improved generated video quality across standard metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM). This indicates that the latent motion representation captures rich, structured dynamics relevant not only for control but also for generating high-fidelity visual content.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AMPLIFY represents a significant advancement in robot learning, offering a novel paradigm for leveraging the vast and diverse landscape of action-free video data to overcome the inherent limitations of data scarcity in robotics. Its core contribution lies in the elegant decomposition of policy learning into distinct forward and inverse dynamics models, mediated by a compact, discrete latent representation of keypoint motion.

The key significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Bridging the Data Modality Gap:** By effectively decoupling "what motion defines a task" from "how robots perform it," AMPLIFY provides a flexible framework to utilize heterogeneous data sources. This means that readily available action-free human or robot videos can inform the forward dynamics (motion prior), while limited action-labeled robot data trains the inverse dynamics (action inference). This drastically expands the pool of usable data for robot learning.
2. **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** The impressive results in few-shot learning demonstrate that AMPLIFY can learn effective policies with significantly fewer action-labeled demonstrations. This is crucial for real-world robotics where collecting thousands of expert trajectories is often impractical. This data efficiency directly translates to faster iteration cycles and reduced development costs for new robotic capabilities.
3. **Unprecedented Generalization Capabilities:** The most impactful result is AMPLIFY's ability to achieve non-trivial success in zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks, a feat where traditional Behavior Cloning methods completely fail. This suggests that learning general motion priors allows robots to infer and execute actions for tasks they have never seen performed by a robot with action labels, paving the way for truly generalist robotic agents.
4. **Versatile Latent World Model:** The demonstration of AMPLIFY's latent motion tokens improving conditional video generation highlights its versatility beyond just robot control. This implies that the learned motion representation captures fundamental visual dynamics that are useful for a broader range of vision-based tasks, establishing it as a powerful latent world model.
5. **Scalability and Efficiency:** By abstracting motion into a compact latent space rather than relying on computationally intensive pixel-level predictions, AMPLIFY offers a more efficient and scalable approach to dynamics modeling, enabling faster inference and potentially real-time control in complex environments.

**Potential Impacts:**
- **Accelerated Robot Deployment:** Easier policy learning with less data can accelerate the deployment of robots in diverse real-world applications, from manufacturing to service industries.
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** The ability to learn from action-free human videos opens doors for more intuitive human-robot collaboration, where robots can observe human demonstrations and adapt their actions.
- **Foundation Models for Robotics:** AMPLIFY contributes to the broader vision of creating foundation models for robotics, where large-scale pre-training on diverse data can lead to highly capable and adaptable general-purpose robots.
- **Future Research Directions:** The limitations discussed, such as extending to 3D motion prediction, handling stochastic environments, learning from online exploration data, and integrating with larger Vision-Language Models (VLMs), provide clear and promising avenues for future research to further enhance robot capabilities.

In conclusion, AMPLIFY presents a robust and innovative framework that effectively addresses the data bottleneck in robot learning, paving the way for more generalizable, data-efficient, and scalable robotic systems that can intelligently interact with the world by leveraging the abundance of uncurated visual information.
