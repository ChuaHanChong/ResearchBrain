# How Far Are Vision-Language Models from Constructing the Real World? A Benchmark for Physical Generative Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Salesforce AI Research introduced DreamHouse, a new benchmark designed to evaluate the physical generative reasoning of Vision-Language Models by challenging them to synthesize residential timber-frame structures compliant with codified engineering standards. The evaluation revealed that even frontier VLMs achieve low joint pass rates, topping out at 7.1% for simultaneously meeting structural validity and visual fidelity, underscoring a significant gap in their understanding of real-world physical constraints.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) benchmarks primarily focus on perceptual realism, assessing visual plausibility rather than adherence to physical laws or constructability.
- Current physical reasoning evaluations for VLMs are largely comprehension-based, failing to test the models' ability to generate physically realizable artifacts from scratch.
- A lack of objective, verifiable criteria makes it difficult to assess the physical constructibility of VLM-generated 3D designs, as most existing methods prioritize visual metrics over structural integrity.

## Method

- The **DreamHouse** benchmark was developed, focusing on residential timber-frame construction due to its fully codified engineering standards (International Residential Code) and objective verification criteria.
- A procedural generation pipeline creates over 26,000 structurally verified 3D timber-frame models across 13 architectural styles, complete with multi-view renderings and a structured scene graph for diverse evaluation scenarios.
- A deterministic, simulation-free suite of 10 physics-based tests directly scores generated structures against IRC compliance, structural physics, and geometric integrity, providing objective and interpretable feedback to the VLM agent.

## Results

- State-of-the-art VLMs achieved consistently low joint pass rates (highest 7.1% for Claude on Planner-Atomic in 'Frame' condition) for simultaneously satisfying structural validity and visual fidelity.
- VLM performance varied significantly across agentic protocols: GPT-5 excelled in holistic planning (79.2% structural pass in Planner-Atomic), while Gemini improved substantially with phase decomposition and feedback (from 45.4% in Planner-Atomic to 78.5% in Planner-Managed).
- Some models (Claude, Gemini) showed structural improvements in occluded 'Facade' views, suggesting an ability to infer hidden structures from appearance, whereas GPT-5's performance declined significantly (from 79.2% to 63.7%) under occlusion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual plausibility and structural validity are orthogonal capabilities in VLMs; models excelling in visual fidelity do not necessarily produce physically sound structures, and vice-versa.
> - The design of agentic protocols and scaffolding significantly impacts a VLM's performance in physically grounded generative tasks, sometimes more than the choice of the underlying VLM itself.
> - Frontier VLMs demonstrate substantial limitations in physical generative reasoning, with consistently low joint pass rates, indicating a critical gap in their ability to synthesize physically coherent and buildable structures.
