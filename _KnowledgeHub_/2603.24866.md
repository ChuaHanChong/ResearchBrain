# How Far Are Vision-Language Models from Constructing the Real World? A Benchmark for Physical Generative Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Salesforce AI Research introduced DreamHouse, a new benchmark designed to evaluate the physical generative reasoning of Vision-Language Models by challenging them to synthesize residential timber-frame structures compliant with codified engineering standards. The evaluation revealed that even frontier VLMs achieve low joint pass rates, topping out at 7.1% for simultaneously meeting structural validity and visual fidelity, underscoring a significant gap in their understanding of real-world physical constraints.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) benchmarks primarily focus on perceptual realism, assessing visual plausibility rather than adherence to physical laws or constructability.
- Current physical reasoning evaluations for VLMs are largely comprehension-based, failing to test the models' ability to generate physically realizable artifacts from scratch.
- A lack of objective, verifiable criteria makes it difficult to assess the physical constructibility of VLM-generated 3D designs, as most existing methods prioritize visual metrics over structural integrity.

## Method

- The **DreamHouse** benchmark was developed, focusing on residential timber-frame construction due to its fully codified engineering standards (International Residential Code) and objective verification criteria.
- A procedural generation pipeline creates over 26,000 structurally verified 3D timber-frame models across 13 architectural styles, complete with multi-view renderings and a structured scene graph for diverse evaluation scenarios.
- A deterministic, simulation-free suite of 10 physics-based tests directly scores generated structures against IRC compliance, structural physics, and geometric integrity, providing objective and interpretable feedback to the VLM agent.

## Results

- State-of-the-art VLMs achieved consistently low joint pass rates (highest 7.1% for Claude on Planner-Atomic in 'Frame' condition) for simultaneously satisfying structural validity and visual fidelity.
- VLM performance varied significantly across agentic protocols: GPT-5 excelled in holistic planning (79.2% structural pass in Planner-Atomic), while Gemini improved substantially with phase decomposition and feedback (from 45.4% in Planner-Atomic to 78.5% in Planner-Managed).
- Some models (Claude, Gemini) showed structural improvements in occluded 'Facade' views, suggesting an ability to infer hidden structures from appearance, whereas GPT-5's performance declined significantly (from 79.2% to 63.7%) under occlusion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual plausibility and structural validity are orthogonal capabilities in VLMs; models excelling in visual fidelity do not necessarily produce physically sound structures, and vice-versa.
> - The design of agentic protocols and scaffolding significantly impacts a VLM's performance in physically grounded generative tasks, sometimes more than the choice of the underlying VLM itself.
> - Frontier VLMs demonstrate substantial limitations in physical generative reasoning, with consistently low joint pass rates, indicating a critical gap in their ability to synthesize physically coherent and buildable structures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a recognized gap in the evaluation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), specifically concerning their capacity for physical generative reasoning. Current VLM benchmarks primarily focus on perceptual realism, assessing a model's ability to generate visually plausible 3D layouts, shapes, and appearances. While VLMs have demonstrated advancements in perceptual capabilities, their understanding of physical laws and constraints, particularly in generative tasks, remains largely unexplored.

Existing benchmarks, such as PhysBench and VSI-Bench, primarily evaluate physical reasoning and spatial recall through comprehension tasks, where models observe and answer questions about physical scenarios. Similarly, much of the research in 3D scene generation prioritizes photometric or geometric metrics against reference renderings, without explicitly measuring structural validity. Models like TRELLIS and SpatialGen produce visually impressive 3D structures but may not ensure their physical constructibility or adherence to engineering standards.

This paper positions itself as a response to the need for benchmarks that evaluate a VLM's ability to "build" rather than merely "perceive" or "comprehend" the physical world. It extends the concept of code-driven structured generation, where VLMs generate executable programs verified by domain-specific oracles, by replacing visual oracles with deterministic engineering compliance tests. While works like VIGA use photometric feedback for iterative refinement and MCP-Universe includes 3D design, they often lack domain-specific physical validation.

Furthermore, this research relates to physically-grounded assembly tasks, exemplified by projects like BrickGPT, which focuses on generating physically stable LEGO structures. DreamHouse, however, tackles a significantly more complex domain: residential timber-frame construction. This domain involves orthotropic materials, heterogeneous connection mechanics, and multi-condition verification based on codified engineering standards, presenting a more rigorous challenge than isotropic LEGO assemblies. By introducing DreamHouse, the authors aim to establish physical validity as a distinct and underdeveloped axis for evaluating multimodal intelligence, orthogonal to visual realism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and validate DreamHouse, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate physical generative reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This capability is defined as the synthesis of artifacts that concurrently satisfy geometric, structural, constructability, and code-compliance constraints. The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations in the current VLM evaluation paradigm:

- **Dominance of Perceptual Realism:** Existing VLM evaluation heavily emphasizes visual plausibility. Models are typically assessed on whether their generated 3D outputs "look correct," with little attention paid to whether these outputs could actually be built or physically stand. This focus on appearance neglects the underlying physical realities and rigorous constraints that govern the real world.
- **Gap in Physical Understanding for Generative Tasks:** While some benchmarks explore physical reasoning and spatial understanding (e.g., PhysBench, VSI-Bench), these are primarily comprehension tasks. They evaluate a model's ability to interpret existing physical scenarios but do not test its capacity to generate physically realizable artifacts from scratch, without visible ground truth for structural elements. This gap is critical for applications that require models to move beyond passive observation to active construction.
- **Need for Automation in Design-to-Construction:** The automation of design-to-construction pipelines necessitates models that can not only design visually appealing structures but also ensure their structural integrity and adherence to building codes. Current VLMs, lacking robust physical generative reasoning, are insufficient for such applications, highlighting a practical need for this capability.
- **Lack of Objective and Verifiable Correctness Criteria:** Evaluating physical constructibility requires objective, verifiable criteria. The paper identifies residential timber-frame construction as an ideal domain because its engineering standards are fully codified, and the correctness of its discrete components can be objectively verified without subjective human annotation. This contrasts with more abstract or visually-driven 3D generation tasks where objective structural assessment is often absent.
- **Demand for Iterative and Agentic Evaluation:** Standard benchmarks often assess only final outputs, providing limited insight into a model's planning, structural reasoning, or self-correction abilities. The motivation includes developing an evaluation framework that supports iterative, agentic interaction, allowing models to observe intermediate build states, generate construction actions, and receive structured environmental feedback to refine their outputs. This allows for a more fine-grained assessment of the generative process.

By addressing these motivations, DreamHouse aims to provide a robust framework for benchmarking VLMs on their capacity to synthesize physically valid structures, thereby pushing the frontier of multimodal intelligence towards real-world applicability in domains like architecture, engineering, and construction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DreamHouse benchmark is constructed upon a foundation of residential timber-frame construction, chosen for its codified engineering standards and objectively verifiable correctness. The methodology encompasses benchmark construction, task formalization, distinct evaluation protocols, and a comprehensive suite of metrics.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Construction

- **Testbed Choice:** Residential timber framing was selected as the domain. This choice is motivated by the domain's fully codified correctness criteria (load-path integrity, member sizing, connection geometry, assembly order), allowing for objective verification without human annotation. It is also visually rich and stylistically diverse, ensuring that perceptual plausibility alone is insufficient for success. This domain was preferred over an initial bridge-generation testbed, which allowed models to analytically invert visual cues into structural parameters, bypassing true structural understanding.
- **Parametric Generation and Dataset Scale:** Structures are generated procedurally using a Blender Python script, starting from JSON configurations defining footprint, story count, and roof pitch. This process instantiates detailed 3D timber-frame models, including individual members (sills, joists, studs, rafters) with correct positions, orientations, and IRC cross-sections, preserving identities and parent-child relationships in a structured scene graph. The dataset comprises over 26,000 structurally verified models across 13 distinct architectural styles (e.g., A-Frame, Barn, Colonial, Split-Level), ranging from 133 to 1,548 members. All structures meet Level of Detail 350 (LoD 350) standards, indicating fabrication-grade detail, and are provided with multi-view renderings from five canonical viewpoints.
- **Structural Validation Suite:** A deterministic, simulation-free suite of 10 physics-based tests operates directly on the scene graph to score generated structures against engineering and code-compliance standards, specifically the International Residential Code (IRC). These tests are grouped into three pillars:
 - **IRC Compliance:** Load Path (T1), Span Limits (T2), On-Centre Spacing (T3), Stability Score (T9).
 - **Structure Physics:** Deflection (T5), Cantilever Limits (T8).
 - **Geometric Integrity:** Standard Lumber Dimensions (T4), Roof Coverage (T6), Gap Detection (T7), Dual-End Connection (T10).
 The suite's design ensures objective, interpretable feedback, with no physics simulation required.

#### 3.2. Task Formalization

Evaluation is formalized as an agentic generation task involving a VLM agent (A), a Blender environment executor (E), and the structural validator (V). In each turn, the agent receives an observation (multi-view task input and previous structured validation feedback) and generates a Blender Python action. The executor applies the action, updating the scene graph, and the validator produces feedback (pass/fail status, violation counts). This feedback closes the loop, allowing for iterative refinement. The agent produces a hierarchical JSON plan before writing code, specifying member categories, phase ordering, and assembly dependencies. Each task has a per-step retry budget and a global budget.

#### 3.3. Agentic Protocols

Three protocols are defined to vary external phase management and scaffold design:

- **Planner-Atomic (T_S):** The agent generates a complete construction script in a single code block. Failure triggers full script regeneration. This tests holistic, single-pass structural synthesis without intermediate phase-level feedback.
- **Planner-Reactive (T_Q):** The agent generates a single script covering all construction phases. After each phase is materialized, a subset of validation tests (load path, stability) is applied. Failure results in full script regeneration from conversation history, invalidating subsequent phases. This demands holistic planning and sequential commitment without external phase guidance.
- **Planner-Managed (T_W):** The agent generates scripts one phase at a time under external phase management. Each phase must pass a subset of validation tests before the next is unlocked, with per-phase retry budgets. Previous scene states are preserved on failure, isolating each phase as an independent sub-task and providing the strongest external scaffolding.

#### 3.4. Iterative Refinement with Visual Feedback (Exploratory Task)

This task explores a model's ability to refine visual alignment without compromising structural validity. Starting from a structurally valid structure with sub-optimal visual fidelity, the agent iteratively refines the structure. Feedback includes a rendered side-by-side comparison, and structural validation is enforced at every iteration; any action causing structural regression is rejected.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Metrics

- **Structural Validity:** A binary metric where a structure must satisfy all 10 tests in the validation suite to pass. No partial credit is awarded, reflecting the discontinuous nature of physical constraints.
- **Visual Fidelity (S):** Measures pixel-level agreement between multi-view renders of generated and target structures using alpha-weighted Mean Squared Error (MSE). A union alpha mask penalizes missing geometry. The score ranges from 0 to 1, with a passing threshold (τ) of 0.6. This is computed only for structurally passed structures.
- **Topological Fidelity (T):** Measures structural correspondence directly on the scene graph, independent of rendering, using a composite score based on:
 - **Census accuracy (C):** Correct number of members per category.
 - **Hungarian match rate (M):** Spatial placement accuracy of individual members using Hungarian algorithm for optimal assignment.
 - **Voxel IoU (V):** Volumetric overlap of the assembled structure.
 A weighted sum combines these, with positional match (M) having the highest weight. This is also computed only for structurally passed structures.

#### 3.6. Experiments

Experiments were conducted with three frontier VLMs: GPT-5, Gemini 3 Flash, and Claude Opus 4.5, using identical prompting without fine-tuning. The evaluation subset consisted of 1,200 structures per model-protocol cell, uniformly sampled across all 13 styles, totaling 21,600 independent agentic tasks and approximately 200,000 API calls. Two input conditions were tested: "Frame" (bare timber framing visible) and "Facade" (framing occluded by cladding).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation across state-of-the-art VLMs revealed several patterns concerning physical generative reasoning capabilities:

- **O1: Structural Validity and Visual Fidelity are Orthogonal:** Models frequently achieve high structural pass rates by generating generic, physically safe assemblies that may not visually match the target. For example, GPT-5 led structurally in Planner-Atomic (79.2%) but scored lowest visually (0.312), while Claude achieved the highest visual score (0.406) but not the highest structural pass rate. The joint pass rate, requiring simultaneous structural validity and visual fidelity (S ≥ 0.6), was consistently low across all models and protocols, with the highest being 7.1% (Claude, Planner-Atomic, Frame condition). This indicates that visual imitation does not guarantee physical validity, and vice versa.
- **O2: Structural Reasoning is Not a Monolithic Capability:** Models exhibited distinct performance profiles depending on the generation protocol, suggesting different underlying reasoning modes. GPT-5 performed strongly in Planner-Atomic (79.2% structural pass rate) but significantly declined under Planner-Managed (33.3%). Conversely, Gemini's structural pass rate improved substantially from Planner-Atomic (45.4%) to Planner-Managed (78.5%). Claude showed relative stability across protocols. This suggests that some models excel at holistic planning (e.g., GPT-5 single-pass synthesis), while others benefit from phase decomposition and intermediate feedback (e.g., Gemini). Planner-Reactive, which demands self-decomposition without external guidance, proved to be more challenging.
- **O3: Some Models Can Infer Structure from Occluded Views, Others Cannot:** When the framing was occluded by finished cladding (Facade condition), Claude and Gemini showed structural improvements (Claude: 71.6% to 77.9%; Gemini: 45.4% to 53.9%). Gemini was the only model to also improve visually in this condition (0.376 to 0.394), suggesting it might leverage architectural priors to infer hidden structures from appearance. In contrast, GPT-5's structural performance declined sharply (79.2% to 63.7%) under the Facade condition, indicating reliance on direct structural mimicry that is disrupted by occlusion.
- **O4: Protocol Choice Dominates Model Performance:** The choice of agentic protocol was found to have a greater impact on performance than the selection of the model itself. For instance, Gemini's structural pass rate increased by 33 percentage points simply by shifting from Planner-Atomic to Planner-Managed, surpassing GPT-5 which had previously outperformed it significantly. This highlights that the design of the agentic scaffolding is as consequential as the underlying VLM's capabilities for physically grounded generative tasks.
- **Other Observations - Discontinuity of Physical Constraints:** Topological fidelity, a measure of structural resemblance, did not reliably predict structural validity. Models often produced structures with high topological scores that still failed multiple critical physical tests due to discontinuous physical constraints (e.g., a single over-spanned joist invalidates the entire structure). This further underscores that standard perceptual and topological benchmarks are insufficient proxies for evaluating true physical generative reasoning.
- **Iterative Refinement with Visual Feedback:** In an exploratory task, models were given visual feedback to refine their outputs while maintaining structural validity. All models showed moderate but consistent improvements in visual scores, with Claude gaining the most (+0.033). Structural retention rates ranged from 68.5% (Claude) to 78.1% (GPT-5), indicating that visual refinement did not catastrophically destabilize structural integrity.
- **Open-Source Model Evaluation:** A pilot evaluation of open-source models (Qwen3-VL-8B/30B-A3B-Instruct and Kimi K2.5) revealed a 0% structural pass rate. These models struggled with generating syntactically valid Blender Python code or produced assemblies that failed all structural tests. Even Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, a larger MoE model, achieved only a 20.6% structural pass rate on renderable outputs, with significantly lower visual similarity than frontier models. This suggests that reliable code generation and spatial reasoning for these tasks are currently frontier-only capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DreamHouse benchmark introduces physical generative reasoning as a critical new evaluation axis for Vision-Language Models, distinct from existing metrics focused on perception, comprehension, or simulation. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Redefining VLM Evaluation:** The research demonstrates that visual plausibility and structural validity are orthogonal attributes. Models that excel on conventional perceptual or general reasoning benchmarks often perform poorly on physically grounded tasks. This finding necessitates a re-evaluation of how multimodal intelligence is assessed, moving beyond superficial realism to functional constructibility.
- **Exposing Capability Gaps in Frontier Models:** The consistently low joint pass rates (e.g., 7.1% for the best-performing model-protocol combination) for state-of-the-art VLMs highlight substantial and previously undiagnosed limitations in their ability to generate physically coherent structures. This exposes a crucial frontier for research and development in multimodal AI.
- **Guidance for Agentic System Design:** The discovery that agentic protocol design significantly impacts model performance (often more than the choice of model itself) provides valuable guidance for developing AI agents. It underscores the importance of scaffolding, phase management, and structured feedback in complex generative tasks, suggesting that iterative, managed approaches can unlock capabilities that are otherwise inaccessible in single-shot or reactive modes.
- **Advancing Automation in AEC:** By focusing on residential timber-frame construction and adhering to LoD 350 standards, DreamHouse directly targets challenges in the Architecture, Engineering, and Construction (AEC) sector. The benchmark offers a verifiable target for tracking progress in automating design-to-construction pipelines, where models need to generate structures that are not only aesthetically pleasing but also buildable and code-compliant.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** DreamHouse provides a concrete, executable benchmark and a detailed validation suite, offering a standardized platform for researchers to develop and test models with enhanced physical understanding. Future work can extend this by incorporating more complex material assemblies (concrete, steel), exploring different visual representations, and devising training objectives that enable models to internalize structural grammar more proactively. The high computational cost associated with current evaluations also suggests a need for more efficient methods or focused model architectures.
- **Bridging the Physical-Digital Divide:** The benchmark contributes to bridging the gap between digital models and real-world physical constraints. By requiring models to reason about load paths, span limits, and connection integrity, it pushes AI towards a deeper understanding of how virtual designs translate into tangible, stable physical artifacts.
