# Optimizing Chain-of-Thought Reasoners via Gradient Variance Minimization in Rejection Sampling and RL

> [!summary] Summary
> A dynamic sampling framework improves the training efficiency of Chain-of-Thought reasoners by adaptively allocating computational resources based on prompt difficulty and gradient norms, achieving 2-4x faster convergence compared to standard RAFT while maintaining or improving final accuracy on mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Chain-of-Thought training methods like RAFT use static/uniform sampling that inefficiently allocates equal resources to all prompts
- High variance in gradient estimation leads to slow convergence and suboptimal performance
- The E-step of EM-based CoT training relies on inefficient Monte Carlo estimation of the ELBO gradient, creating the primary computational bottleneck

## Method

- Formalize CoT reasoning as a ==latent variable problem== and develop **GVM-RAFT** with dynamic inference budget scheduling
- Optimize sample allocation by minimizing gradient variance under computational constraints
- Extend approach to other RL algorithms like GRPO

## Results

- 2-**4x** faster convergence compared to vanilla RAFT on mathematical reasoning tasks, while also improving final test accuracy under the same computational budget
- Theoretical guarantees for convergence under standard smoothness conditions
- Successful integration with other RL algorithms like GRPO showing similar improvements

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prompt-specific difficulty and convergence behavior should guide computational resource allocation
> - Dynamic sampling based on acceptance rates and gradient norms leads to more efficient training
> - Method generalizes beyond RAFT to other RL-based training approaches

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a key challenge in the field of LLMs: **improving the reasoning capabilities of these models, specifically in the context of chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning.** CoT reasoning is a technique where LLMs generate intermediate reasoning steps to solve complex problems. This research builds upon existing techniques like iterative reward-ranked fine-tuning (RAFT) but proposes a novel optimization strategy to address their limitations.

**Broader Research Landscape:**

1. **Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** CoT has become a prominent area of research in LLMs, aiming to improve their problem-solving abilities. Researchers are exploring different methods for eliciting and refining CoT reasoning, including prompting strategies, fine-tuning techniques, and reinforcement learning approaches.

2. **Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) has been instrumental in aligning LLMs with human preferences. This work contributes to a growing body of research that leverages RL to optimize LLMs for specific tasks, particularly those requiring reasoning and problem-solving. The paper engages with algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and REINFORCE-style algorithms, including GRPO.

3. **Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (RAFT):** RAFT and its variants are a family of techniques used to fine-tune LLMs by iteratively generating responses, evaluating them based on a reward signal, and fine-tuning the model on the best-performing responses. This work identifies the shortcomings of traditional RAFT approaches, such as uniform sampling, and proposes a more efficient and theoretically grounded method for allocating computational resources.

4. **Variance Reduction Techniques:** Variance reduction is a core concept in machine learning and optimization. The paper contributes to this area by applying variance reduction strategies to the specific problem of training CoT reasoners. The connection of RAFT to the EM algorithm is covered, and the paper builds on the theoretical understanding of variance reduction to allocate the inference budget to prompts with varying levels of difficulty.

5. **Verifier-Based RL:** Recent projects such as OpenAI's O1 and DeepSeek's R1 have highlighted the potential of using verifier scores (which evaluate the correctness of final answers) for building reasoning models. This research aligns with this trend, focusing on improving reasoning models using verifier-based rewards.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives of the paper are:

1. **To identify and address the limitations of existing CoT training methods, particularly RAFT-style algorithms, regarding inefficient stochastic gradient estimation due to static sampling strategies.** The authors argue that uniform sampling, which assigns equal computational resources to all prompts, fails to account for prompt-specific difficulty and convergence behavior.

2. **To develop a dynamic sample allocation strategy that minimizes the variance of stochastic gradients under a computational budget constraint.** This involves adaptively allocating computational resources across prompts based on their difficulty and gradient norms.

3. **To provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed dynamic sampling strategy, demonstrating its accelerated convergence guarantees.** The paper aims to establish a principled framework for efficient on-policy sampling under computational budget constraints.

4. **To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method through real-world experiments on mathematical reasoning tasks, showing improved convergence speed and accuracy compared to existing approaches.** The paper aims to outperform existing deep RL methods in terms of convergence rate and final test accuracy.

**Motivation:**

The authors are motivated by the desire to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of training LLMs for reasoning tasks. They believe that by addressing the limitations of existing methods, they can develop algorithms that converge faster, achieve higher accuracy, and make better use of limited computational resources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology and approach can be summarized as follows:

1. **Formalization of CoT Reasoning as a Latent Variable Problem:** The authors formalize CoT reasoning as a latent variable problem, where the intermediate reasoning steps are treated as hidden variables. This framework allows them to apply techniques from expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm and rejection sampling.

2. **Identification of the Bottleneck:** They identify the inefficient Monte Carlo estimation of the ELBO (Evidence Lower Bound) gradient during the E-step of the EM algorithm as the primary bottleneck in CoT training.

3. **Development of GVM-RAFT:** The core of the paper lies in the development of GVM-RAFT (Gradient Variance Minimization RAFT), a refined RAFT variant that incorporates a dynamic inference budget scheduling strategy. The strategy dynamically allocates computational resources based on prompt hardness and gradient norms. This involves:
 - Estimating the acceptance rates of rejection sampling for each prompt.
 - Estimating the Lipschitz bounds which measure the contribution of the samples by the gradients
 - Formulating an optimization problem to minimize the variance of the gradient estimator, subject to a computational budget constraint.
 - Deriving a closed-form solution for the optimal sample allocation.

4. **Theoretical Analysis:** The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed dynamic sampling strategy, demonstrating its accelerated convergence guarantees under smoothness and convexity conditions.

5. **Extension to Other RL Algorithms:** They extend their dynamic inference budget scheduling strategy to other RL algorithms, specifically GRPO (Gradient Ratio Policy Optimization), to demonstrate the generalizability of their approach.

6. **Experimental Validation:** The authors conduct experiments on mathematical reasoning tasks using Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B models. They compare the performance of GVM-RAFT and GVM-GRPO against baseline algorithms like vanilla RAFT and GRPO, evaluating the convergence speed and final test accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the paper are:

1. **GVM-RAFT achieves a 2-4x speedup in convergence rate compared to vanilla RAFT.** This indicates that the dynamic sample allocation strategy significantly improves the efficiency of training.

2. **GVM-RAFT considerably improves the final test accuracy compared to vanilla RAFT.** This demonstrates that the proposed method not only converges faster but also achieves better performance on unseen prompts.

3. **GVM-RAFT sometimes outperforms state-of-the-art deep RL methods such as GRPO.** This suggests that the proposed approach is highly competitive with existing techniques for training LLMs for reasoning tasks.

4. **The dynamic sampling strategy can be successfully integrated into other RL algorithms, such as GRPO, leading to similar improvements in convergence and test accuracy.** This highlights the generalizability of the proposed method and its potential for application beyond RAFT-style algorithms.

5. **The paper has theoretical guarantees of convergence under standard smoothness conditions.** Theorems prove that the loss function decreases at each iteration and also provide faster decrease rates when the loss function is convex.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this research are:

1. **Improved Efficiency of LLM Training:** The proposed GVM-RAFT algorithm offers a more efficient way to train LLMs for reasoning tasks, reducing the computational resources required to achieve a given level of performance. This is particularly important for resource-constrained environments or when training large models.

2. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** By improving the training process, GVM-RAFT can lead to LLMs with enhanced reasoning capabilities, allowing them to solve more complex problems and generate more accurate and reliable responses.

3. **Generalizability to Other RL Algorithms:** The dynamic sampling strategy is not limited to RAFT-style algorithms and can be integrated into other RL frameworks, potentially benefiting a wider range of applications in LLM training.

4. **Theoretical Foundation for Efficient Sampling:** The theoretical analysis provides a solid foundation for understanding the benefits of dynamic sampling and can guide the development of future algorithms in this area.

5. **Practical Applications in Industry:** The collaboration with Salesforce AI Research suggests that the proposed techniques have practical applications in industry, potentially leading to improved LLMs for various real-world tasks, such as customer service, content creation, and data analysis.

**Potential Impact:**

The work is likely to influence future research in the field of LLMs by:

- Encouraging the development of more sophisticated sampling strategies that take into account prompt-specific characteristics.
- Promoting the integration of variance reduction techniques into RL-based training methods for LLMs.
- Inspiring further theoretical analysis of the convergence properties of these techniques.
- Contributing to the development of more efficient and effective LLMs for reasoning and problem-solving.
