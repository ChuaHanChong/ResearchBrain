# From Motion to Behavior: Hierarchical Modeling of Humanoid Generative Behavior Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce Generative Behavior Control (GBC), a task for creating long-horizon humanoid behaviors, and PHYLOMAN, a hierarchical framework that uses Large Language Models for high-level planning and physics-based control for low-level motion generation. This approach synthesizes complex, physically plausible, and semantically coherent action sequences up to ten times longer than previous methods, supported by the new GBC-100K multimodal dataset.

## Problem

- Existing human motion generative models often focus only on low-level motions or high-level action planning, failing to integrate hierarchical, goal-oriented structures.
- Current methods struggle to maintain temporal coherence and physical plausibility over extended durations for complex action sequences.
- There is a persistent semantic gap between abstract high-level behavioral goals and the granular, low-level actions required to execute them.

## Method

- ==The Generative Behavior Control (GBC)== task is introduced, formally defining behavior via a `BehaviorScript` that includes high-level descriptions, `PoseScripts`, and `MotionScripts`.
- The PHYLOMAN framework is proposed, which employs Large Language Models for high-level task planning and physics-based controllers for low-level continuous motion planning.
- A large-scale multimodal dataset, GBC-100K, was created with approximately 100,000 video-SMPL pairs, featuring hierarchical, fine-grained, goal-oriented textual annotations.

## Results

- PHYLOMAN increased the `Success Rate` by **133%** and reduced `Physical Error` by **91%** for long-sequence generation (1024 frames) compared to methods without the Task and Motion Planning pipeline.
- The LLM planner within PHYLOMAN achieved an **82.1%** `Success Rate` and a `Diversity` score of **109.7**, outperforming heuristic planning significantly.
- The inclusion of a physics-based controller reduced `Physical Error` by **60.5%**, demonstrating its effectiveness in maintaining motion quality and realism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human motions can be effectively controlled by a hierarchical task and motion planning approach, with LLMs guiding high-level plans to enhance motion diversity and physical fidelity.
> - Fine-tuning LLMs with structured behavioral data like GBC-100K significantly improves their capability for purposeful and varied high-level behavior planning.
> - Integrating physics-based controllers into low-level motion planning is crucial for ensuring continuous physical plausibility and biomechanical accuracy in generated long-horizon sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the field of human motion generative modeling and synthesis. Existing research in this domain often concentrates on either the generation of low-level, short-period motions or high-level action planning. However, a gap exists in adequately addressing the hierarchical and goal-oriented nature of human activities. Current methods frequently encounter difficulties in maintaining temporal coherence and physical plausibility over extended durations for complex action sequences. Furthermore, they struggle to bridge the semantic divide between abstract high-level goals and concrete low-level actions.

Previous advancements in deep learning have enabled progress in 3D pose estimation, tracking, and realistic human movement synthesis. Physics-informed approaches have contributed to ensuring physical plausibility in generated motions. Additionally, large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated potential in high-level action planning and understanding. In robotics, Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) frameworks attempt to integrate task planning with motor control, though they exhibit limitations in capturing the full complexity of human-like motion.

This paper proposes a shift from focusing solely on motion generation to explicitly modeling human behavior, drawing inspiration from cognitive science. By emphasizing the goal-oriented semantics, intentions, and planning inherent in human actions, this research aims to produce extended motion sequences that are both physically feasible and semantically coherent, thereby advancing the state of the art in human behavior generation and control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance human motion generation towards comprehensive human behavior modeling. This involves characterizing complex, diverse human motions in daily activities, driven by various high-level intentions. The authors identify a specific need to align these motions with hierarchical behavior plans, which existing methods have not fully addressed.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in the current research landscape:
- **Lack of Hierarchical Understanding:** Current human motion generative models predominantly focus on either low-level, short-duration motions or abstract high-level action planning, without integrating the hierarchical, goal-oriented structure inherent in human activities. This leads to a disconnect between high-level intentions and their low-level physical manifestations.
- **Limited Temporal Coherence and Physical Plausibility:** While short-term motion synthesis has progressed, existing methods often fail to maintain temporal coherence and physical realism over longer durations, resulting in unrealistic or inconsistent motion sequences. Physics-informed approaches have improved stability but typically do not bridge the gap between low-level actions and high-level goals effectively.
- **Semantic Gap between Goals and Actions:** There is a persistent challenge in semantically aligning high-level behavioral goals with the granular, low-level actions required to execute them. Traditional robotics Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) approaches, while integrating task planning with motor control, often lack the capability to capture the full complexity and nuanced intentions of human motion.

To address these motivations, the research introduces a novel task named Generative Behavior Control (GBC) with two main objectives:
1. **Generation of Complex, Long-Horizon Action Sequences:** To enable the creation of extended, intricate action sequences that current motion generation methods struggle to model effectively. The target is to generate sequences approximately ten times longer than those produced by existing state-of-the-art methods.
2. **Unified Framework for Planning, Generation, and Intention Understanding:** To develop a cohesive framework that integrates action planning, motion generation, and intention understanding, thereby producing physically consistent behaviors that embody both semantic meaning and purposeful planning over prolonged sequences.

Furthermore, a significant objective is the creation of a new, large-scale dataset, GBC-100K. This dataset is designed to overcome limitations of existing benchmarks by providing detailed, semantically aligned action sequences with hierarchical textual annotations, facilitating behavioral planning with LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the proposed Generative Behavior Control (GBC) task and its implementation through a hierarchical framework called PHYLOMAN (PHYsics-informed generative modeling of LOng-horizon planning for hierarchical huMANoid behaviour). The core insight is that human motions can be jointly controlled by task and motion planning, guided by LLMs to enhance motion diversity and physical fidelity.

#### 3.1. Generative Behavior Control (GBC) Formulation

GBC aims to synthesize long-term humanoid behaviors that adhere to both physical constraints and high-level semantic objectives. A `BehaviorScript B = {D, P, A}` formally defines behavior:
- `D`: A high-level description (e.g., "a person energetically dancing in circles at a party") categorized by semantic elements like Subject, Emotion/State/Style, Action, Direction/Goal, and Environment/Background.
- `P = {p0..., pn}`: A sequence of `PoseScripts`, each describing a single atomic action or static body configuration (e.g., "raise right arm").
- `A = {a0..., an-1}`: A sequence of `MotionScripts`, each describing a transition between consecutive `PoseScripts` (`pi` and `pi+1`).

#### 3.2. PHYLOMAN Framework Architecture

PHYLOMAN operates as a purely inference framework, with individual components trained separately. It decomposes behavior generation into two main stages: Task Planning and Motion Planning (Figure 2).

##### 3.2.1. Task Planning (High-level)

- **LLM as Behavior Planner:** An LLM is utilized for its reasoning and context understanding capabilities. Given a natural language behavior description (e.g., "conduct an orchestra on stage"), the LLM generates a hierarchical `Composed Script`, which is a sequence of `PoseScripts {pi}` and `MotionScripts {ai}`. This process uses a Chain-of-Thoughts framework to enrich sequences with kinematic attributes.
- **Parameterizing Text to Poses:** To bridge the semantic gap between linguistic annotations and discrete postures, a pre-trained Variational Autoencoder (VAE) from PoseScript is adopted. This VAE's text encoder and pose decoder transform each `PoseScript pi` into its SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear) representation `xi`, ensuring semantic alignment.
- **High-level Constraints (CT):** A soft constraint `CT(xi, ai, xi+1) = 0` is enforced at this level. `CT` combines joint-based constraints (range and biomechanical limits) and semantic consistency, ensuring transitions respect joint limits, maintain biomechanical feasibility, and align with the intended behavior.

##### 3.2.2. Motion Planning (Low-level)

- **Motion Diffusion Prior:** A Diffusion Model (e.g., CondMDI) is used to generate an initial discrete trajectory `M` (a sequence of SMPL parameters) connecting the consecutive SMPL representations `xi`. This model captures the complex distribution of human motion by iteratively refining samples.
- **Continuous Motion Planning:** Using `M` as a reference, a physics-based humanoid controller (e.g., PHC, HOVER) generates control signals. These signals guide a simulated avatar (within a simulator like MuJoCo) to follow the discrete poses, producing a smooth and biomechanically accurate continuous trajectory `τi`.
- **Low-level Constraints (CM):** A constraint `CM(τi(λ)) ≤ 0` is maintained throughout the continuous motion. `CM` aggregates joint limits, collision avoidance (preventing violations of minimum allowable distances between body parts), and dynamic feasibility (ensuring the motion adheres to the physical dynamics of the SMPL model, governed by equations of motion in the simulator). A sliding window approach is employed for real-time performance while preserving stability and global task coherence.

#### 3.3. GBC-100K Dataset

To support and evaluate this framework, the authors introduce GBC-100K, a large-scale multimodal dataset with approximately 100,000 annotated video-SMPL pairs, totaling 250 hours of video.
- **Data Collection:** Raw videos were sampled from YouTube-8M and curated from datasets such as Kinetics, UCF101, HMDB, HM3.6M, ActivityNet, Motion-X, and FLAG3D.
- **Filtering:** Videos were filtered using YOLOv8-Pose to retain clips with discernible single human activities, and TransNetv2 was used for scene segmentation.
- **Motion Estimation:** TRAM, an advanced 3D Human Pose Estimation model, extracted SMPL representations, which were then converted into `PoseScript` representations and filtered for quality.
- **Motion Description:** A state-of-the-art multimodal large language model generated hierarchical textual annotations: high-level `BehaviorScripts` for full videos, and corresponding `MotionScripts` for individual clips.
The dataset features hierarchical, fine-grained, goal-oriented textual descriptions, supporting open-vocabulary motion synthesis, and offering greater length and diversity in annotations compared to existing benchmarks (Table 1).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted to validate the efficacy of PHYLOMAN in generating extended, coherent human motion sequences that align with high-level directives and maintain physical realism, utilizing the GBC-100K dataset.

#### 4.1. GBC-100K Dataset Validation

- **Multimodality:** Comparative benchmarking (Table 2) showed that existing state-of-the-art motion generation methods (MotionLCM, MDM, MotionCLR) trained on GBC-100K achieved higher `MultiModality` scores compared to when trained on HumanML3D. This finding indicates the effectiveness of GBC-100K's fine-grained textual annotations in fostering diverse motion generation conditioned on text, and suggests that GBC-100K is a more challenging and comprehensive benchmark.
- **FID and Diversity:** While some FID metrics showed gaps, this was attributed to the diverse range of motions, varied video quality, and potentially less precise text alignment inherent in the internet-sourced videos used for GBC-100K, implying a more realistic and varied distribution compared to existing datasets.

#### 4.2. Long-Sequence Behavior Generation

- **Performance Metrics:** Ablation studies on long-sequence (1024 frames) motion generation (Table 3) demonstrated PHYLOMAN's performance. Compared to methods without the Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) pipeline (MoMask and T2M-GPT), PHYLOMAN achieved a 133% increase in `Success Rate` (from 0.3 to 0.7) and a 91% reduction in `Physical Error` (from 1.224 to 0.105). This indicates that the integrated TAMP pipeline significantly contributes to task completion, physical plausibility, and motion naturalness.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Planning and Physical Constraints

- **Hierarchical Planning (LLM Planner):** The LLM planner within PHYLOMAN (using Chain-of-Thought) significantly outperformed a heuristic planning method. It achieved a `Success Rate` of 0.821 compared to 0.118 and a `Diversity` score of 109.7 compared to 19.32 (Table 3). This confirms the importance of LLM-driven hierarchical planning for generating purposeful and varied behaviors.
- **Text-to-Pose Mapping:** PHYLOMAN's text-to-pose mapping showed a marginal decrease in `Physical Error` (0.093 vs. 0.101) but substantial improvements in `Success Rate` (0.821 vs. 0.452) and `Diversity` (109.7 vs. 97.73) compared to heuristic approaches. This suggests improved semantic alignment and practical task completion.
- **Physics Simulation (Controller):** Incorporating a physics-based controller (HOVER) reduced `Physical Error` by 60.5% (from 0.235 to 0.093) while maintaining similar `Success Rates` (0.821) and slightly increasing `Diversity` (by 8.0%). This highlights the role of physical constraints in preserving motion quality and realism.
- **Fine-tuning LLMs:** Fine-tuning LLMs on the GBC-100K dataset's hierarchical annotations resulted in improved behavior planning performance, even slightly outperforming closed-source LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-sonnet) in certain metrics when compared to zero-shot usage (Table 4). This indicates the value of structured, real-world data in enhancing LLM capabilities for motion planning.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples (Figures 4, 5, 6) illustrated PHYLOMAN's ability to generate long-horizon behaviors that maintained semantic alignment and physical plausibility, such as martial arts techniques, gym training, and dancing. A failure case in swimming demonstrated challenges in maintaining physical balance and temporal consistency for highly dynamic and fluid motions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework and a dataset that address a recognized gap in the generative modeling of human motion. The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Advancement in Human Behavior Modeling:** By shifting focus from low-level motion generation to hierarchical, goal-oriented behavior modeling, this work provides a framework for understanding and synthesizing human activities in a more cognitively aligned manner. This represents a step towards generating human movements that are not just plausible but also embody specific intentions and plans.
- **Long-Horizon, Coherent Behavior Generation:** The PHYLOMAN framework demonstrates the ability to generate physically consistent and semantically coherent humanoid behaviors that are significantly longer (10x) than those produced by prior methods. This capability is critical for applications requiring continuous, complex, and purposeful actions.
- **Novel Dataset for Research:** The introduction of GBC-100K provides a large-scale, multimodal benchmark dataset. Its hierarchical, fine-grained textual annotations, goal-oriented nature, and inclusion of real-world internet video data make it a more comprehensive and challenging resource than previous datasets. This dataset can facilitate future research in areas such as multimodal learning, language-driven motion synthesis, and behavior understanding.
- **Unified Framework:** PHYLOMAN integrates LLM-driven high-level planning with physics-based low-level motion control, bridging the gap between abstract linguistic instructions and physically executable humanoid poses. This unified approach offers a robust methodology for complex behavior synthesis, combining semantic understanding with physical realism.
- **Interpretable Control:** The hierarchical planning structure within PHYLOMAN allows for more interpretable control over generated behaviors, as high-level instructions are systematically decomposed into executable motion sequences.
- **Foundation for Future Research and Applications:** The work lays a foundation for future research in behavioral modeling of human motions. Its direct implications extend to practical applications in embodied intelligence (e.g., controlling humanoid robots), creating realistic digital avatars for virtual reality and gaming, and advancing human-computer interaction by enabling more natural and expressive virtual agents. The public availability of the dataset and source code is intended to foster further community engagement and development.
