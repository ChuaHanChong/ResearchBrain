# From Motion to Behavior: Hierarchical Modeling of Humanoid Generative Behavior Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce Generative Behavior Control (GBC), a task for creating long-horizon humanoid behaviors, and PHYLOMAN, a hierarchical framework that uses Large Language Models for high-level planning and physics-based control for low-level motion generation. This approach synthesizes complex, physically plausible, and semantically coherent action sequences up to ten times longer than previous methods, supported by the new GBC-100K multimodal dataset.

## Problem

- Existing human motion generative models often focus only on low-level motions or high-level action planning, failing to integrate hierarchical, goal-oriented structures.
- Current methods struggle to maintain temporal coherence and physical plausibility over extended durations for complex action sequences.
- There is a persistent semantic gap between abstract high-level behavioral goals and the granular, low-level actions required to execute them.

## Method

- ==The Generative Behavior Control (GBC)== task is introduced, formally defining behavior via a `BehaviorScript` that includes high-level descriptions, `PoseScripts`, and `MotionScripts`.
- The PHYLOMAN framework is proposed, which employs Large Language Models for high-level task planning and physics-based controllers for low-level continuous motion planning.
- A large-scale multimodal dataset, GBC-100K, was created with approximately 100,000 video-SMPL pairs, featuring hierarchical, fine-grained, goal-oriented textual annotations.

## Results

- PHYLOMAN increased the `Success Rate` by **133%** and reduced `Physical Error` by **91%** for long-sequence generation (1024 frames) compared to methods without the Task and Motion Planning pipeline.
- The LLM planner within PHYLOMAN achieved an **82.1%** `Success Rate` and a `Diversity` score of **109.7**, outperforming heuristic planning significantly.
- The inclusion of a physics-based controller reduced `Physical Error` by **60.5%**, demonstrating its effectiveness in maintaining motion quality and realism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human motions can be effectively controlled by a hierarchical task and motion planning approach, with LLMs guiding high-level plans to enhance motion diversity and physical fidelity.
> - Fine-tuning LLMs with structured behavioral data like GBC-100K significantly improves their capability for purposeful and varied high-level behavior planning.
> - Integrating physics-based controllers into low-level motion planning is crucial for ensuring continuous physical plausibility and biomechanical accuracy in generated long-horizon sequences.
