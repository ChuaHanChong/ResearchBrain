# Accelerating Reinforcement Learning with Learned Skill Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> The SPiRL method from USC's CLVRai lab enables accelerated reinforcement learning by jointly learning continuous skill embeddings and a state-conditioned prior over these skills from unstructured data. This approach effectively guides exploration in high-dimensional skill spaces, leading to more efficient transfer to new, complex tasks and demonstrating improved performance over baselines on robot manipulation and navigation.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning is often data-inefficient, requiring extensive online interaction to learn new tasks from scratch.
- Existing methods struggle to effectively leverage large amounts of unstructured, offline experience data due to lack of task-specific rewards or the need for structured task distributions.
- Exploring the high-dimensional, continuous skill embedding space resulting from skill extraction poses a significant challenge for downstream learning, potentially hindering efficiency.

## Method

- **SPiRL** learns a ==continuous skill embedding space== and a ==state-conditioned skill prior== offline from unstructured data using a ==stochastic latent variable model==, minimizing ==KL divergence== between inferred posteriors and the learned prior.
- A ==hierarchical policy== for downstream tasks uses the pre-trained skill decoder to execute skills, while a high-level policy predicts skill embeddings.
- ==Maximum-entropy RL== (==Soft Actor-Critic==) is modified to explicitly regularize the high-level policy towards the learned state-conditioned skill prior, guiding exploration in the skill space.

## Results

- **SPiRL** consistently outperforms flat model-free RL and skill-based methods without prior guidance across Maze Navigation, Block Stacking, and Kitchen environments, reliably solving complex tasks.
- Ablation studies confirm that the learned state-conditioned skill prior is critical for efficient exploration and successful downstream learning.
- The method demonstrates robustness, learning effective skills and priors even from sub-optimal or mixed-quality training data, and learned priors can be reused for multiple downstream tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a state-conditioned prior over skills is crucial for effectively guiding exploration in high-dimensional continuous skill spaces.
> - Without such a learned prior, exploration in rich skill embedding spaces can be prohibitively difficult, often negating the benefits of skill transfer.
> - Temporal abstraction provided by skills is essential for solving complex, long-horizon tasks efficiently, while single-step action priors are insufficient.
