# Distilling Reinforcement Learning Policies for Interpretable Robot Locomotion: Gradient Boosting Machines and Symbolic Regression

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at University College London developed a framework to distill opaque neural network Reinforcement Learning policies for robot locomotion into intrinsically interpretable "glass-box" models. Gradient Boosting Machines (GBMs) and Explainable Boosting Machines (EBMs) successfully replicated expert gaits with high accuracy (up to 0.99 R² score) and, in some cases, outperformed the original neural experts in simulated task performance while requiring significantly less simulated experience for distillation.

## Problem

- Neural network-based policies for robot locomotion are typically "black-box" models, hindering interpretability and explainability.
- The lack of transparency impedes the broader acceptance, deployment, and certification of autonomous robots in safety-critical or consumer-facing applications.
- Intrinsically interpretable models are generally not suitable for direct gradient-based optimization used in Reinforcement Learning.

## Method

- Train opaque neural network-based ==Reinforcement Learning== (RL) expert policies using PPO for various robot locomotion gaits on a Unitree A1 quadruped robot in IsaacGym.
- Employ a modified Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) algorithm, incorporating an episode-dependent policy alternation curriculum, to efficiently distill the expert behavior.
- Utilize Gradient Boosting Machines (GBMs), Explainable Boosting Machines (EBMs), and Symbolic Regression as target interpretable "glass-box" policy representations, trained via supervised learning on the aggregated dataset.

## Results

- EBMs consistently achieved the highest R² scores in replicating expert actions across all gaits (e.g., **0.9906** for trotting), closely followed by GBMs, while symbolic policies showed significantly lower fidelity.
- GBM and EBM policies demonstrated robust task performance during simulated rollouts, with some configurations outperforming the neural RL expert by over **10%** (e.g., GBM walk policy) and operating stably in isolation.
- GBMs enabled global explanations via feature importance and partial dependence plots, and EBMs provided direct global and local explanations through visualized shape functions, revealing key observations like "Previous Action" and "RM State" as highly influential for specific joint actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy distillation, particularly with a modified DAgger algorithm, effectively addresses the distribution shift problem in imitation learning, allowing interpretable models to learn complex robot locomotion behaviors from neural experts.
> - Gradient Boosting Machines and Explainable Boosting Machines can achieve both high fidelity to complex neural policies and robust task performance, sometimes even exceeding the original expert's performance, without sacrificing interpretability.
> - Different intrinsically interpretable models (e.g., EBMs vs. GBMs) can highlight distinct observation importance rankings, offering varied insights into the underlying decision-making processes of the learned policies.
