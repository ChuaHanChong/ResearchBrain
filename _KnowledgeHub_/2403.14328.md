# Distilling Reinforcement Learning Policies for Interpretable Robot Locomotion: Gradient Boosting Machines and Symbolic Regression

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at University College London developed a framework to distill opaque neural network Reinforcement Learning policies for robot locomotion into intrinsically interpretable "glass-box" models. Gradient Boosting Machines (GBMs) and Explainable Boosting Machines (EBMs) successfully replicated expert gaits with high accuracy (up to 0.99 R² score) and, in some cases, outperformed the original neural experts in simulated task performance while requiring significantly less simulated experience for distillation.

## Problem

- Neural network-based policies for robot locomotion are typically "black-box" models, hindering interpretability and explainability.
- The lack of transparency impedes the broader acceptance, deployment, and certification of autonomous robots in safety-critical or consumer-facing applications.
- Intrinsically interpretable models are generally not suitable for direct gradient-based optimization used in Reinforcement Learning.

## Method

- Train opaque neural network-based ==Reinforcement Learning== (RL) expert policies using PPO for various robot locomotion gaits on a Unitree A1 quadruped robot in IsaacGym.
- Employ a modified Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) algorithm, incorporating an episode-dependent policy alternation curriculum, to efficiently distill the expert behavior.
- Utilize Gradient Boosting Machines (GBMs), Explainable Boosting Machines (EBMs), and Symbolic Regression as target interpretable "glass-box" policy representations, trained via supervised learning on the aggregated dataset.

## Results

- EBMs consistently achieved the highest R² scores in replicating expert actions across all gaits (e.g., **0.9906** for trotting), closely followed by GBMs, while symbolic policies showed significantly lower fidelity.
- GBM and EBM policies demonstrated robust task performance during simulated rollouts, with some configurations outperforming the neural RL expert by over **10%** (e.g., GBM walk policy) and operating stably in isolation.
- GBMs enabled global explanations via feature importance and partial dependence plots, and EBMs provided direct global and local explanations through visualized shape functions, revealing key observations like "Previous Action" and "RM State" as highly influential for specific joint actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy distillation, particularly with a modified DAgger algorithm, effectively addresses the distribution shift problem in imitation learning, allowing interpretable models to learn complex robot locomotion behaviors from neural experts.
> - Gradient Boosting Machines and Explainable Boosting Machines can achieve both high fidelity to complex neural policies and robust task performance, sometimes even exceeding the original expert's performance, without sacrificing interpretability.
> - Different intrinsically interpretable models (e.g., EBMs vs. GBMs) can highlight distinct observation importance rankings, offering varied insights into the underlying decision-making processes of the learned policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) have significantly propelled robot locomotion capabilities, enabling robots to perform complex movements over challenging terrains and through animal motion imitation. Neural networks are widely adopted for representing RL policies and value functions due to their function approximation capabilities and suitability for gradient-based optimization. However, this prevalence introduces a critical challenge: the "black-box" nature of neural network policies. This lack of transparency impedes interpretability and explainability, which are increasingly vital for the broader acceptance and deployment of robots in safety-critical, industrial, or consumer-facing applications. Such applications often necessitate rigorous behavior and system certification.

Existing research in robot locomotion has explored modular or hierarchical neural architectures to enhance policy performance or learning efficiency, but these typically do not inherently provide deep interpretability insights, with some exceptions in manipulation. While observation importance has been studied in some neural locomotion policies, it often relies on black-box saliency methods. Concurrently, the field of Explainable AI (XAI) and Explainable Reinforcement Learning (XRL) has been developing. Policy distillation or extraction is an emerging technique within XRL, where complex neural policies are translated into more interpretable models. Prior work has demonstrated distillation into decision trees, state machines, and list processing programs across various domains, including simple games, power system control, aircraft separation assurance, and sensor-based robot navigation.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the specific gap of distilling neural RL policies for *robot locomotion* into intrinsically interpretable "glass-box" models, which has not been extensively explored using methods like Gradient Boosting Machines (GBMs), Explainable Boosting Machines (EBMs), and Symbolic Regression. It aims to leverage the inherent transparency of these models to make robot locomotion policies understandable, thereby fostering trust and facilitating real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a novel framework for distilling opaque, neural network-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies for robot locomotion into more interpretable, "glass-box" policy representations.

The specific objectives underpinning this aim include:
- To propose and implement a modified Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) algorithm incorporating an episode-dependent policy alternation curriculum. This modification is designed to efficiently distill feedback control policies, specifically addressing the distribution shift problem inherent in traditional behavioral cloning.
- To train and evaluate the effectiveness of three distinct interpretable model types—Gradient Boosting Machines (GBMs), Explainable Boosting Machines (EBMs), and Symbolic Regression—as distilled policies for various robot locomotion gaits, namely walking, trotting, pacing, and bounding.
- To thoroughly analyze and unveil the observation-action mappings embedded within these distilled policies. This includes providing both global and local explanations of the learned robot behaviors and evaluating the importance of different observations in determining joint actions.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical needs:
- **Addressing the "Black-Box" Problem:** While neural networks have enabled advanced robot locomotion, their inherent opacity hinders understanding of how decisions are made. This lack of transparency is a significant barrier to the widespread adoption of autonomous robots, particularly in applications where safety, reliability, and accountability are paramount.
- **Facilitating Certification and Trust:** For robots to operate in industrial, domestic, or safety-critical environments, their behavior often requires certification. Interpretable policies allow for verification, debugging, and clear explanations of actions, which is essential for building trust and meeting regulatory requirements.
- **Overcoming Training Limitations of Interpretable Models:** Intrinsically interpretable models, such as decision trees or symbolic expressions, are typically not amenable to the gradient-based optimization methods commonly used in RL. Policy distillation offers an indirect yet effective approach to imbue these models with learned RL behavior.
- **Improving Efficiency in Policy Development:** The authors note that while training expert neural policies can be computationally intensive (e.g., 205 hours of simulated experience), the distillation process itself can be significantly more sample-efficient (aiming for 10 minutes of simulated interaction per gait).
- **Leveraging Existing Neural Policy Structure:** The observation that many neural networks used in previous locomotion research are relatively shallow (2 to 5 hidden layers) suggests that the complex observation-action mappings they learn might be representable in simpler, interpretable forms, thereby motivating the distillation approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this work comprises two primary stages: (i) the training of neural network-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) expert policies, and (ii) the subsequent distillation of these expert policies into interpretable policy forms. This sequential approach is necessitated by the fact that the chosen interpretable models are not directly suitable for gradient-based optimization, which is a fundamental requirement for most policy gradient RL methods.

**A. Training Reinforcement Learning Expert Policies**
1. **RL Algorithm:** The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm is utilized for training the expert policies. PPO is a widely adopted policy gradient method known for its balance of performance and sample efficiency.
2. **Simulation Environment:** The training is conducted within IsaacGym, a high-performance simulation platform that supports parallel environment execution, enabling efficient data collection.
3. **Robot Platform:** The Unitree A1 quadruped robot serves as the experimental platform for all locomotion tasks.
4. **Locomotion Gaits:** Expert policies are developed for four distinct locomotion gaits: walking, trotting, pacing, and bounding.
5. **Reward Function Design:** The reward functions are structured using "reward machines," as detailed in prior work. This approach extends the MDP state with reward machine states, aiming to enhance learning efficiency and policy robustness by incorporating logical rules.
6. **Observations and Actions:**
 - **Observations:** The state space typically includes joint states, joint velocities, base orientation, base linear and angular velocities, feet contact states, feet height, and target velocity/distance. A learned state estimator is employed for base velocity and feet contact forces.
 - **Actions:** Policies output joint position targets, which are then executed by low-level joint PD controllers (P=20, D=0.5) at a control frequency of 50 Hz.
7. **Training Protocol:** Each expert policy is trained for 1,500 PPO updates using 1024 parallel environments. This cumulative training effort amounts to approximately 205 hours of simulated experience per gait. During training, the robot operates with randomized forward velocity commands (in the range of [-1, 1] m/s) and yaw rate commands (in [-1, 1] rad/s) to promote generalization.

**B. Distilling Interpretable Locomotion Policies**
The distillation process is framed as an imitation learning problem, wherein the goal is for the interpretable policies to mimic the behavior of the pre-trained neural RL experts. To address the inherent distribution shift challenges of standard behavioral cloning, a modified DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) algorithm is employed.

1. **Modified DAgger Algorithm with Curriculum:**
 - **Episode-Dependent Policy Alternation (Algorithm 1):** The core modification involves an episode-dependent alternation of actions between the expert and the currently distilled policy during data collection. A frequency parameter `n` dictates how often the expert's actions are utilized (specifically, every 1/n steps). This `n` parameter increases progressively throughout the distillation process, implementing a curriculum. The lowest alternation ratio observed in the distillation datasets is 1/8.
 - **Dataset Aggregation:** For each step in an episode, an action is chosen either from the expert or the current distilled policy based on the `n` parameter. However, the state-action pair `(s_t, π_expert(s_t))` is *always* added to the dataset D, ensuring that the distilled policy learns from optimal expert actions.
 - **Distillation Efficiency:** The data aggregation phase for each gait uses `t = 1000` steps, translating to only 10 minutes of simulated interaction. The process runs for `max episodes = 30`, with `n` updating after cycling through four linear velocity commands (`n_f = 4`).
2. **Interpretable Target Models:** Once the dataset D is aggregated, three types of interpretable policies are trained via supervised learning, reserving 20% of D for testing:
 - **Gradient Boosting Machines (GBMs):** These are ensemble models that sequentially build weak learners, typically shallow decision trees, to iteratively correct the errors of previous models. GBMs inherently allow for feature importance analysis and visualization of individual decision trees (leveraging).
 - **Explainable Boosting Machines (EBMs):** EBMs combine the additive structure of Generalized Additive Models (GAMs) with gradient boosting. They model the response as a sum of shape functions (`f_i`) for individual features and optionally pairwise interaction terms (`f_i,j`). EBMs provide direct global explanations through these visualized shape functions (using).
 - **Symbolic Regression:** This method employs genetic algorithms to evolve mathematical expressions that best describe the input-output data without prespecifying the model structure. The authors use unary operators `[sin(·), tanh(·), ·², ·³]` and binary operators `[·+·, ·−·, ·×·]`, with complexity constraints (max operator complexity 4, overall complexity 90), run for 20 iterations per distillation (using).
3. **Hyperparameter Settings:** Default hyperparameters, optimized for robust performance, are used for GBMs and EBMs. Specific parameters are set for the symbolic regression's genetic algorithm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Performance of Distilled Policies**
The performance of the distilled policies was evaluated across two dimensions: regression accuracy in imitating the expert and task performance during simulated rollouts.

1. **Regression Performance (R² Scores on Test Sets - Table II):**
 - **EBMs** consistently achieved the highest R² scores across all gaits (e.g., Walk: 0.9787, Trot: 0.9906, Pace: 0.9819, Bound: 0.9637), indicating a strong ability to accurately replicate the expert neural policy's actions.
 - **GBMs** also demonstrated high R² scores, very similar to EBMs, performing effectively in imitating the expert (e.g., Walk: 0.9705, Trot: 0.9863, Pace: 0.9752, Bound: 0.9537).
 - **Symbolic policies** exhibited significantly lower R² scores (e.g., Walk: 0.6811, Trot: 0.7334, Pace: 0.7331, Bound: 0.6564), suggesting less fidelity to the expert's behavior. The authors note that increasing genetic algorithm iterations could improve this, but with higher computational cost.

2. **Task Performance (Average Episodic Rewards - Figure 2):**
 - **GBM and EBM policies** generally maintained robust performance regardless of the alternation ratio used during testing, including when operating entirely in isolation (alternation ratio 0), a scenario not present in the distillation training data.
 - A notable finding was that for all gaits, at least one configuration of distilled policies (i.e., when alternated with the RL expert at various ratios) outperformed the neural RL expert itself (alternation ratio 1).
 - When operating strictly by themselves (alternation ratio 0), GBM and EBM policies exhibited strong performance: the GBM walk policy exceeded the neural expert by over 10%, EBM and GBM trot policies by 3%, pace policies by 2%, and bound policies by nearly 7%. This was often attributed to superior linear and angular velocity reward performance.
 - **Symbolic policies** showed a rapid decay in performance as the reliance on the neural expert decreased. While they sometimes matched or slightly outperformed alternatives when heavily alternated with experts (e.g., 12% for pace and 10% for bound with 1/2 alternation ratio), their effectiveness sharply declined for lower alternation ratios (below 1/6 for walk and trot, below 1/4 for pace and bound), rendering them unstable and unusable when run in isolation.

3. **Visual Gait Sequences (Figures 3, 4, 5):**
 - When tested in isolation, **GBM and EBM policies** produced visually similar and stable gait sequences for all tasks, sustaining full test episode durations.
 - **Symbolic policies**, conversely, generated visibly degraded gaits and were unable to maintain stable locomotion for more than a few gait cycles in isolation, aligning with their lower quantitative performance metrics.

**B. Interpretability of Distilled Policies**
The study rigorously analyzed the interpretability aspects of the distilled policies.

1. **Gradient Boosting Machines (GBMs):**
 - **Observation Importance:** Interpretability was derived using feature importance and permutation importance methods (Table I, Figure 11). These methods quantified the relevance of observations for each joint action across different gaits.
 - **Key Observations:** "Previous Action" and "RM State" (Reward Machine State) consistently emerged as highly important features across various joint types (hip, thigh, calf) and gaits. The two importance methods largely agreed on the top features, with minor variations in the second or third most relevant observations.
 - **Global Explanations:** GBMs permit global explanations through the inspection of individual decision trees (Figure 6) that comprise the ensemble, and via partial dependence plots (Figure 7). These visualizations facilitate counterfactual analysis, enabling insights into how specific observation values influence action outputs, which is valuable for certification.

2. **Explainable Boosting Machines (EBMs):**
 - **Intrinsic Interpretability:** EBMs, as modified Generalized Additive Models (GAMs), inherently provide direct global importance metrics for single and pairwise feature terms (Equation 4, Figure 10). This does not require additional models or computations.
 - **Visual Global Explanations:** The contribution of each term to the final prediction can be visualized through plots of the learned shape functions (`f_i` or `f_i,j`). Figure 8 illustrates this with an example for the Front Left Hip joint in the trot policy, showing how foot height influences actions and a pairwise interaction between previous action and Hind Right Thigh joint position resembles a signed "exclusive or" operation.
 - **Local Explanations:** EBMs also facilitate local explanations, illustrating the specific contribution of each feature to a given action for a particular input observation (Figure 9). This capability is presented as particularly useful for safety certification and malfunction diagnosis.
 - **Divergent Feature Importance:** It was noted that the top important features identified by EBMs differed from those found by GBMs (Table I vs. Figure 10), indicating that observation importance can vary based on the specific model architecture used for distillation.

3. **Symbolic Regression:**
 - **Analytical Transparency:** Symbolic policies are intrinsically interpretable as they consist of explicit analytical mathematical expressions.
 - **Limitations:** Despite this inherent transparency, a detailed analysis of their interpretability (e.g., via partial derivatives) was omitted due to their comparatively inferior task performance and the potentially high complexity (up to 90 terms per policy), which could make granular interpretation challenging.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes a novel framework for enhancing the interpretability of reinforcement learning policies in robot locomotion, a domain traditionally dominated by "black-box" neural networks.

The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:
- **Bridging the Interpretability Gap:** The primary impact lies in effectively transforming complex, opaque neural network RL policies into interpretable "glass-box" models (GBMs and EBMs). This directly addresses a critical barrier to the wider adoption of autonomous robots in real-world scenarios.
- **Enabling Trust and Acceptance:** By providing clear global and local explanations of robot behaviors, the research fosters greater trust in autonomous systems. This transparency is crucial for regulatory bodies, engineers, and end-users to understand, predict, and ultimately accept robotic operations, especially in safety-critical applications where accountability is paramount.
- **Facilitating Safety Certification and Debugging:** The ability to explain *why* a robot takes a particular action, through mechanisms like feature importance in GBMs or shape function visualizations in EBMs, is invaluable for safety certification processes. It also allows engineers to debug learned policies more effectively, diagnose malfunctions, and refine control strategies.
- **Demonstrating Performance with Interpretability:** The findings indicate that high levels of interpretability can be achieved without compromising task performance. GBM and EBM policies not only accurately imitate expert behavior but, in several cases, even outperform the original neural RL experts when operating autonomously. This demonstrates that interpretability does not necessarily come at the cost of efficacy.
- **Efficiency in Policy Generation:** The proposed methodology significantly reduces the computational burden of developing robust, interpretable policies. While expert neural policies required hundreds of hours of simulation, the distillation process achieves effective interpretable policies with only 10 minutes of simulated interaction per gait, making the deployment of interpretable models more practical.
- **Novelty in Robot Locomotion:** The work is presented as the first to demonstrate the use of GBMs, EBMs, and symbolic policies as functional policies for robot locomotion through distillation, thus extending the repertoire of explainable RL techniques applicable to complex robotic systems.
- **Future Research Foundation:** This work lays a foundation for future research avenues, including scaling the approach to more complex exteroceptive policies (those relying on external sensory information), incorporating uncertainty estimates into interpretable policies, and extending the methodology to robot manipulation tasks, further broadening the scope of interpretable robotics.

Overall, this research is a step towards enabling widespread and trustworthy adoption of autonomous robots by providing the means to understand their learned locomotion behaviors.
