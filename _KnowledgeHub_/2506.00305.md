# Learning Aerodynamics for the Control of Flying Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a control framework for jet-powered humanoid robots, like iRonCub-Mk1, that enables stable flight in windy environments by integrating data-driven aerodynamic models derived from CFD simulations and wind tunnel experiments into a whole-body controller.

## Problem

- Controlling complex, non-aerodynamic humanoid robots in flight, especially with external winds, due to highly turbulent and separated flows.
- Lack of explicit, accurate aerodynamic models for articulated, bluff-body humanoid robots.
- Limitations of single-mode locomotion for humanoids in diverse and challenging environments.

## Method

- Designed and built iRonCub-Mk1, a 43.3 kg jet-powered humanoid robot with custom jetpack and reinforced forearms for thrust and attitude control.
- Developed a comprehensive methodology combining wind tunnel experiments and extensive CFD simulations to generate an aerodynamic dataset for the robot's complex shape.
- ==Trained Deep Neural Network== and simpler Axisymmetric models for real-time aerodynamic force prediction and integrated them into an aerodynamic-aware whole-body controller.

## Results

- The mechanical design of iRonCub-Mk1 was successfully validated through FEM analysis, confirming structural integrity for jet engine integration.
- Aerodynamic models (DNN and Axisymmetric) were accurately derived from the CFD dataset, with the DNN achieving a maximum Relative Mean Error of 3x10^-6 on validation data, and CFD models validated against wind tunnel data with an average error below **10%**.
- The aerodynamic-aware controller enabled the robot to complete all designed flight maneuvers in simulation with external wind, while a baseline controller failed to stabilize, demonstrating the necessity of aerodynamic compensation. Ground experiments also showed appropriate compensatory responses on the physical hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Changes in robot joint configurations significantly impact aerodynamic forces (up to 20% variation) more than airspeed, suggesting Reynolds-independence for modeling in certain operating ranges.
> - Combining CFD simulations and wind tunnel data with machine learning effectively models complex aerodynamic forces on highly non-aerodynamic, articulated robot bodies.
> - Explicitly accounting for aerodynamic forces via an aerodynamic-aware controller is crucial for stable flight of humanoid robots in external wind conditions, unlike baseline controllers that neglect these effects.
