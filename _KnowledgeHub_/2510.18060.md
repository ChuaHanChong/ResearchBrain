# SPACeR: Self-Play Anchoring with Centralized Reference Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Applied Intuition, UC Berkeley, NYU, and Stanford developed SPACER, a framework that integrates a pretrained imitation learning model as a centralized reference to guide decentralized self-play policies, producing autonomous vehicle simulation agents with human-like, reactive behaviors. This approach yields policies 50 times smaller and 10 times faster than state-of-the-art imitation learning models, while improving realism for vehicle and vulnerable road user interactions.

## Problem

- Autonomous vehicle (AV) simulation agents must exhibit realistic, human-like, and reactive traffic behaviors for robust closed-loop testing.
- Existing imitation learning (IL) models provide realism but are computationally expensive, slow for inference, and struggle with reactivity in dynamic scenarios.
- Self-play reinforcement learning (RL) agents are reactive and scalable but often diverge from human driving norms, producing unrealistic behaviors that require extensive heuristic shaping.

## Method

- The SPACER framework combines a pretrained ==tokenized autoregressive motion== model as a centralized reference policy with decentralized self-play policies.
- A combined objective function incorporates task performance, a human-likeness reward based on the reference model's likelihood, and a KL divergence term to align the self-play policy's action distribution with the reference.
- The approach uses a tokenized trajectory action space, common to both self-play and reference models, enabling direct computation of KL divergence and providing fine-grained, per-agent, per-timestep feedback.

## Results

- SPACER achieved a composite realism score of **0.741** for vehicle agents, outperforming baseline PPO (**0.710**) and Human-Regularized PPO (**0.716**), and was competitive with state-of-the-art imitation learning models like CAT-K (**0.766**).
- Policies are significantly more efficient, being approximately 50 times smaller in parameter size and 10 times faster in inference throughput (211.8 scenarios/sec) compared to large generative imitation models.
- SPACER agents demonstrated improved realism for vulnerable road users (**0.729** vs. PPO **0.648**) and provided more reliable closed-loop planner evaluations by exposing suboptimal planner behaviors more effectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a pretrained, centralized imitation learning model as a soft prior effectively anchors decentralized self-play policies to human driving distributions, overcoming the realism limitations of pure RL.
> - A combined objective, leveraging both likelihood rewards and KL divergence from the reference model, guides self-play to achieve human-like behaviors without sacrificing reactivity or requiring heavy reward shaping.
> - The framework yields computationally efficient policies that are robust to the quality of the reference model, suggesting self-play agents can refine behavior beyond the reference's limitations through closed-loop interaction.
