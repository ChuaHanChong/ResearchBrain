# SPACeR: Self-Play Anchoring with Centralized Reference Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Applied Intuition, UC Berkeley, NYU, and Stanford developed SPACER, a framework that integrates a pretrained imitation learning model as a centralized reference to guide decentralized self-play policies, producing autonomous vehicle simulation agents with human-like, reactive behaviors. This approach yields policies 50 times smaller and 10 times faster than state-of-the-art imitation learning models, while improving realism for vehicle and vulnerable road user interactions.

## Problem

- Autonomous vehicle (AV) simulation agents must exhibit realistic, human-like, and reactive traffic behaviors for robust closed-loop testing.
- Existing imitation learning (IL) models provide realism but are computationally expensive, slow for inference, and struggle with reactivity in dynamic scenarios.
- Self-play reinforcement learning (RL) agents are reactive and scalable but often diverge from human driving norms, producing unrealistic behaviors that require extensive heuristic shaping.

## Method

- The SPACER framework combines a pretrained ==tokenized autoregressive motion== model as a centralized reference policy with decentralized self-play policies.
- A combined objective function incorporates task performance, a human-likeness reward based on the reference model's likelihood, and a KL divergence term to align the self-play policy's action distribution with the reference.
- The approach uses a tokenized trajectory action space, common to both self-play and reference models, enabling direct computation of KL divergence and providing fine-grained, per-agent, per-timestep feedback.

## Results

- SPACER achieved a composite realism score of **0.741** for vehicle agents, outperforming baseline PPO (**0.710**) and Human-Regularized PPO (**0.716**), and was competitive with state-of-the-art imitation learning models like CAT-K (**0.766**).
- Policies are significantly more efficient, being approximately 50 times smaller in parameter size and 10 times faster in inference throughput (211.8 scenarios/sec) compared to large generative imitation models.
- SPACER agents demonstrated improved realism for vulnerable road users (**0.729** vs. PPO **0.648**) and provided more reliable closed-loop planner evaluations by exposing suboptimal planner behaviors more effectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a pretrained, centralized imitation learning model as a soft prior effectively anchors decentralized self-play policies to human driving distributions, overcoming the realism limitations of pure RL.
> - A combined objective, leveraging both likelihood rewards and KL divergence from the reference model, guides self-play to achieve human-like behaviors without sacrificing reactivity or requiring heavy reward shaping.
> - The framework yields computationally efficient policies that are robust to the quality of the reference model, suggesting self-play agents can refine behavior beyond the reference's limitations through closed-loop interaction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous vehicles (AVs) necessitates simulation environments capable of producing realistic, human-like, and reactive traffic behaviors for robust closed-loop testing. Current approaches broadly fall into two categories: imitation learning (IL) and self-play reinforcement learning (RL).

Imitation learning, particularly with large generative models like tokenized autoregressive models (e.g., SMART, CAT-K) and diffusion models, has demonstrated an ability to capture complex, realistic human driving behaviors directly from expert demonstrations. These models excel at realism by learning the human data distribution. However, they often incur high computational costs during inference, leading to slow simulation speeds, and can struggle to adapt reactively in dynamic, closed-loop interactive scenarios where they might simply replay historical data.

Conversely, self-play reinforcement learning trains agents through repeated interactions within a simulated environment, naturally fostering reactivity and scalability in multi-agent settings. Prominent works like GIGAFlow have shown its capacity for producing robust driving policies. The primary challenge with self-play RL, however, is that policies optimized solely for task-based rewards (e.g., reaching goals, avoiding collisions) can diverge significantly from human driving norms. This divergence often results in unrealistic or unpredictable behaviors, necessitating extensive heuristic design and reward shaping to guide learning toward human-likeness. While methods like Human-Regularized PPO attempt to bias policies toward human-likeness with limited demonstration data, a scalable and computationally efficient approach that consistently anchors self-play policies to realistic human distributions while preserving RL's benefits remains a research gap.

This work, SPACER, positions itself as a hybrid framework designed to bridge these gaps. It leverages the realism capabilities of pretrained imitation-learned models to guide self-play RL, aiming to achieve both human-like behavior and the reactivity and scalability inherent to self-play, thereby addressing the limitations of both paradigms in autonomous driving simulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop autonomous vehicle (AV) simulation agent policies that are simultaneously human-like, fast, and scalable in multi-agent settings. This is motivated by the critical need for realistic and reactive simulation policies to rigorously test AV planning systems before real-world deployment.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Realism and Reactivity Gap:** AV simulation policies must be both realistic (exhibiting human-like, predictable, and socially aware behaviors) and reactive (capable of meaningful responses to other agents in complex, interactive scenarios). Existing imitation learning models produce realistic behaviors but are computationally expensive, slow during inference, and may not adapt well in closed-loop, reactive settings. Self-play RL, while reactive and scalable, often produces policies that deviate from human norms, leading to unrealistic behaviors despite maximizing task-based rewards. The key objective is to combine the strengths of both paradigms.
- **Improving Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** Large diffusion-based or tokenized imitation models, while achieving high realism, are computationally intensive. They typically require substantial parameter sizes (e.g., Transformer-based architectures) and sequential inference steps, limiting their scalability for large-scale multi-agent simulations. A core objective is to achieve human-like behaviors with computationally lightweight policies that enable high-throughput, real-time multi-agent simulation.
- **Guiding Self-Play towards Human Norms:** Self-play RL policies often require extensive heuristics and reward shaping to prevent divergence from human driving behaviors. The research aims to introduce a more principled and automated mechanism to anchor self-play learning to the human driving distribution without sacrificing the benefits of RL. This involves leveraging a pretrained model to provide a "human-likeness" signal.
- **Enabling Reliable Closed-Loop Planner Evaluation:** A significant application of realistic sim agents is the evaluation of ego-vehicle motion planners. Current imitation-based simulators may suffer from "false-positive collisions" or unrealistic interactions, leading to unreliable estimates of planner quality. The objective is to develop sim agents that provide more realistic and reliable closed-loop evaluation, accurately reflecting how a planner would perform when interacting with human-like traffic.
- **Developing a Novel Framework for Policy Learning:** The research proposes "human-like self-play" as a novel framework. This involves integrating a pretrained tokenized autoregressive motion model as a *centralized reference policy* to guide decentralized self-play policies. This reference model is intended to provide specific signals (likelihood rewards and KL divergence) that align the self-play policies with the human driving distribution while retaining the scalability of self-play RL.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing simulation paradigms by synergistically combining their strengths, leading to AV simulation agents that are robust, realistic, reactive, and efficient for scalable testing and development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SPACER framework proposes "human-like self-play," which leverages a pretrained tokenized autoregressive motion model as a centralized reference policy ($\pi_{\text{ref}}$) to guide the learning of decentralized self-play policies ($\pi_{\theta}$). The core idea is to anchor the self-play policies to the human driving distribution while maintaining the scalability and reactivity of reinforcement learning.

**Problem Formulation:**
The problem is formulated as a partially observable multi-sequential decision-making problem. Each agent $i$ receives a partial observation $o_i_t$ and selects an action $a_i_t$ according to its policy $\pi_{\theta}(a_i_t | o_i_t)$. The environment transitions based on the joint actions of all agents. The challenge is that standard task-based rewards (e.g., goal-reaching, collision avoidance) do not guarantee human-like behavior.

To address this, SPACER introduces a combined reward and objective function:
$r_t = r_{\text{task}_t} + \alpha r_{\text{humanlike}}(s_t, a_t)$ (Equation 1)
$L(\theta) = L_{\text{PPO}}(\theta; A[r]) - \beta D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot | o_t) \parallel \pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot | s_t))$ (Equation 2)

This formulation combines three components:
1. **Task Performance:** $L_{\text{PPO}}(\theta)$ represents the standard Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) objective, which encourages agents to achieve task goals, avoid collisions, and stay on the road.
2. **Human-likeness Reward:** This term, $r_{\text{humanlike}}(s_t, a_t) = \log \pi_{\text{ref}}(a_t | s_t)$, provides a dense, per-timestep feedback by scoring each executed action under the reference distribution. This reward encourages driving behaviors that maximize the likelihood of human-like actions.
3. **Distributional Alignment (KL Divergence):** The KL divergence term $D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot | o_t) \parallel \pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot | s_t))$ serves as a direct signal at every timestep to align the self-play policy's action distribution with the human driving distribution captured by the reference model. This term is computed in closed form due to the aligned action spaces.

**Pretrained Reference Tokenized Model ($\pi_{\text{ref}}$):**
A pretrained tokenized model (e.g., SMART, CAT-K) is used as $\pi_{\text{ref}}$. This model is trained on real-world human driving trajectories and serves as a proxy for the human driving distribution. Key characteristics and advantages of using this type of reference model include:
- **Centralized Context:** Unlike the decentralized self-play policy that observes only a local view ($o_t$), the reference model is centralized, observing the full scene state ($s_t$) of all agents. This provides privileged information, akin to a teacher-student framework, allowing it to provide richer, more globally informed guidance.
- **Tractable Training:** The approach requires only a single forward pass of the reference model per rollout batch to obtain per-agent, per-timestep action distributions. This avoids the computational cost of autoregressive sampling during training.
- **Aligned Action Space:** Both the self-play policy ($\pi_{\theta}$) and the reference policy ($\pi_{\text{ref}}$) operate over the same discrete, tokenized trajectory action space (K-disk algorithm for clustering trajectories into K discrete tokens). This alignment eliminates the need for online tokenization and enables direct, closed-form computation of the KL divergence.
- **Fine-grained Feedback:** The reference model provides a distinct distribution for each agent's action at every timestep, addressing the credit assignment problem in multi-agent RL by offering per-agent, per-timestep feedback rather than sparse, trajectory-level rewards.
- **Generalization Beyond Log Replay:** Unlike relying on logged trajectories for evaluation, the reference model generalizes beyond recorded data, providing guidance in novel self-play states.

**Practical Considerations:**
- **Goal-reaching Reward and Post-Goal Behavior:** To prevent unnatural acceleration and unrealistic stops, a "goal-dropout" strategy is adopted where agents are trained with and without goal conditioning. When anchored to a reference model, the explicit goal reward can potentially be removed without performance degradation, further enhancing realism.
- **Tokenized Action Space and Policy Frequency:** The action space is aligned with the reference model using a tokenized trajectory action space with K-disk clustering. This allows the policy to operate at a different frequency (e.g., 5 Hz) than the simulator (e.g., 10 Hz), selecting a trajectory token that is then executed by the simulator.
- **Reward Formulation (Task Rewards):** The task reward $r_{\text{task}}$ includes penalties for collisions, off-road driving, and a bonus for goal achievement. Weights for these terms ($\alpha, \beta, w_{\text{collided}}, w_{\text{offroad}}, w_{\text{goal}}, w_{\text{humanlike}}$) are varied in ablations.

**Implementation Details:**
Experiments are conducted in GPUDrive, a GPU-accelerated simulator built on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD). Agents receive local observations including nearby vehicles, lane geometry, and road features within a 50m radius. A late-fusion feedforward MLP model serves as the policy/value network. Training uses PPO on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU for 1 billion environment steps, controlling up to 64 agents with a shared decentralized policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SPACER against baseline self-play RL methods and state-of-the-art imitation learning models across WOSAC metrics and closed-loop planner evaluation tasks.

#### 4.1. Human-like Self-Play Evaluation on WOSAC (Vehicle Agents)

Table 1 demonstrates SPACER's performance on the Waymo Open Sim Agents Challenge (WOSAC) validation set, focusing on vehicle agents. WOSAC metrics evaluate distributional realism across kinematics, inter-agent interactions, and adherence to map constraints, aggregated into a composite realism score.

- **Realism Improvement:** SPACER achieves a composite realism score of 0.741, outperforming baseline PPO (0.710) and Human-Regularized PPO (HR-PPO) (0.716). It shows marked improvements in Kinematic (0.411 vs. 0.327 for PPO) and Interactive (0.779 vs. 0.751 for PPO) realism. This indicates that anchoring self-play with a pretrained reference model effectively guides policies toward human-like behavior.
- **Competitive with Imitation Learning Baselines:** SPACER's composite realism (0.741) surpasses SMART (0.720) and is competitive with CAT-K (0.766), a leading imitation learning model. Notably, SPACER achieves lower collision (0.036) and off-road (0.056) rates compared to SMART (0.17 and 0.13) and CAT-K (0.06 and 0.09).
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** SPACER policies are significantly more efficient. The decentralized MLP has approximately 65,000 parameters, which is about 50 times smaller than CAT-K (around 3.2 million parameters). In terms of throughput, SPACER achieves 211.8 ± 5.6 scenarios/sec, roughly 10 times faster than SMART (22.5 ± 0.0 scenarios/sec) and CAT-K (22.5 ± 0.0 scenarios/sec) on a single A100 GPU.

#### 4.2. Reference Model Quality Effect

Figure 2 illustrates that SPACER's realism is robust to the quality of the underlying reference model. Even when anchored to a smaller, lower-performing reference model (0.3M parameters, 0.64 realism), SPACER policies consistently achieve a high composite realism score (approximately 0.73). Similarly, varying the token vocabulary size of the reference model (100, 200, 400) results in SPACER policies clustering around 0.73 realism. This suggests the reference model acts as a soft prior, guiding behavior rather than requiring perfect imitation, and SPACER agents can refine behavior beyond the reference model's limitations through closed-loop interaction.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on Pedestrian and Cyclist Behavior (VRUs)

Table 2 shows SPACER's performance on VRUs (pedestrians and cyclists). SPACER significantly improves VRU realism, achieving a composite score of 0.729 compared to PPO (0.648) and HR-PPO (0.668). Substantial gains are observed across all realism metrics (Kinematic, Interactive, Map) and minADE, indicating the anchoring mechanism is effective even for the higher stochasticity of VRU motion.

#### 4.4. Effect of Anchoring Parameters

Ablation studies on the likelihood weight ($\alpha$) and KL alignment weight ($\beta$) (Figure 3, Table A2) reveal:
- **KL Alignment is Key:** The KL alignment term (KL + $r_{\text{inf}}$) contributes most significantly to realism, leading to a composite score of 0.74. It helps maintain policy diversity (entropy) while increasing log-likelihood.
- **Likelihood Reward (LLH) Impact:** Likelihood-only optimization (Goal + LLH) showed modest improvements in top-1 composite realism but negatively affected top-5 performance and reduced policy entropy.
- **Goal Reward Removal:** Once anchored, the explicit goal-reaching reward can be removed without performance loss, further improving realism.

#### 4.5. SPACER Agents for Fast & Accurate Closed-Loop Planner Evaluation

- **Enhanced Closed-Loop Realism:** For ego-vehicle planner evaluation, SPACER agents demonstrate more reactive and realistic interactions. Compared to open-loop log-replay evaluation and closed-loop simulation with CAT-K, SPACER leads to consistently lower correlations with ground-truth log replays in PDM scores (Figure 4). This suggests SPACER agents more effectively penalize unsafe planner behaviors, particularly in collision scenarios, providing a more reliable assessment of planner quality.
- **High Throughput:** SPACER maintains its efficiency advantage during planner evaluation, achieving a throughput of 211.8 ± 5.64 scenarios/sec, approximately 10 times faster than SMART (22.5 ± 0.01 scenarios/sec).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SPACER framework represents a development in autonomous driving simulation by combining the advantages of imitation learning and self-play reinforcement learning, offering a new paradigm for testing and potentially training AV planning systems.

The key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:

- **Realistic and Reactive Simulation at Scale:** SPACER policies demonstrate human-like behaviors while being significantly more reactive and computationally efficient than existing imitation learning models. This combination addresses a critical need for AV testing, allowing for large-scale, high-throughput simulation of complex multi-agent scenarios that accurately reflect real-world human interactions.
- **Improved AV Planner Evaluation:** The ability of SPACER agents to provide realistic closed-loop interactions, including appropriate responses to ego-vehicle planner actions, is important for robust planner evaluation. By reducing "false-positive collisions" and unrealistic behaviors, SPACER can lead to more reliable and accurate assessments of AV planner quality, accelerating the iteration cycles for planner development. The finding that SPACER yields lower correlations with log replays for PDM scores suggests it can more effectively expose and penalize suboptimal planner behaviors.
- **Computational Efficiency for Development and Deployment:** The resulting policies are remarkably lightweight (approximately 50 times smaller in parameter size and 10 times faster at inference than large generative models). This efficiency has implications for resource-constrained development environments and potentially for on-board edge deployment if such policies were to control other vehicles in a connected environment for collective decision-making.
- **Principled Human-likeness Guidance:** The framework offers a structured method for anchoring self-play RL to human driving distributions using a pretrained reference model. This reduces the reliance on manual reward shaping and heuristics, simplifying the development of human-like policies in multi-agent RL. The robustness to the quality of the reference model also suggests a flexible and adaptable approach.
- **Advancing Multi-Agent RL for Autonomous Systems:** SPACER contributes to the broader field of multi-agent reinforcement learning by demonstrating an effective strategy for integrating knowledge from large-scale behavior data into efficient, reactive agents. This can inform the development of policies for other complex interactive autonomous systems.
- **New Avenues for VRU Simulation:** The significant improvements in VRU (pedestrian and cyclist) realism achieved by SPACER indicate the potential for developing more sophisticated and context-aware simulations for vulnerable road users, which is a complex but crucial aspect of AV safety.

Overall, SPACER facilitates the scalable, real-time, and realistic closed-loop evaluation of AV planners, and lays groundwork for potentially training them within diverse, human-like traffic scenarios, thereby supporting the safe and effective deployment of autonomous driving technology.
