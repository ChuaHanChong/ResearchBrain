# Agentic Self-Evolutionary Replanning for Embodied Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Agentic Self-Evolutionary Replanning (SERP) framework enables embodied agents to dynamically adapt their action models and perform cross-level replanning by integrating local self-evolution and global graph-based reasoning. This approach achieves over 10% higher success rates on complex planning tasks and 20-40% greater token efficiency compared to existing methods in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Embodied navigation systems frequently encounter failures due to environmental uncertainties, perception errors, and limitations within the robot's action models.
- Current replanning (RP) methods maintain static robot action models, which prevents agents from learning from execution failures and improving their intrinsic capabilities.
- Iterative LLM-based global replanning is computationally inefficient, leading to high token usage and latency when processing large, comprehensive semantic scene graphs.

## Method

- Introduced Agentic Self-Evolutionary Replanning (SERP), a cross-level framework that enables dynamic model evolution through runtime learning for enhanced resilience.
- Developed Local Agentic Self-Evolution (ASE), which employs In-context Learning with Auto-Differentiation (ILAD) to adapt the robot's action model parameters and loss function weights based on execution feedback.
- Proposed Global Graph ==Chain-of-Thought== (GCOT), a mechanism that dynamically distills large scene graphs into smaller, task-relevant subsets for token-efficient LLM-based global replanning.

## Results

- SERP achieved over **10%** higher success rates on complex planning tasks and demonstrated superior robustness in adapting to suboptimal initial parameter settings compared to baselines.
- The GCOT component yielded substantial token efficiency, attaining over **20%** higher Reduced Graph Token Ratio (RGTR) in searching tasks and over **40%** higher in planning tasks compared to the SayPlan baseline.
- Real-world experiments validated ASE's ability to adapt control parameters for navigating narrow passages and GCOT's capacity to identify and redirect to alternative viable goals in dynamic environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of gradient-based optimization (Auto-Differentiation) with LLM-driven global parameter resets (In-context Learning) within ILAD allows robots to escape local optima and achieve robust self-evolution.
> - Decoupling the search for relevant scene elements (using a smaller CLIP model) from high-level reasoning (using an LLM) significantly reduces token expenditure and computational overhead during global replanning.
> - Enabling robots to evolve their internal action models from experience shifts the paradigm from merely circumventing failures to actively enhancing fundamental execution capabilities, leading to more resilient agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation represents a critical and rapidly advancing frontier in robotics, focusing on enabling robots to understand natural language instructions and translate them into executable actions within physical environments. This field has traditionally relied on vision-language navigation (VLN) methods for detailed instructions, but the rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) has marked a significant shift towards addressing ambiguous, high-level task instructions by leveraging their commonsense reasoning capabilities for multi-task strategies and plan formulation.

However, even with LLM-based planning, real-world embodied navigation is inherently prone to failures due to environmental uncertainties, perception errors, and limitations of the robot's action models. To address this, **replanning (RP)** has emerged as a crucial mechanism, allowing robots to adjust their plans in response to execution failures. Existing RP approaches typically fall into two categories: global-level replanning, which focuses on semantic failures (e.g., a planned room being inaccessible), and local-level replanning, which deals with physical failures (e.g., avoiding dynamic obstacles).

The current work, "Agentic Self-Evolutionary Replanning (SERP)," critically identifies and addresses two significant limitations of the existing replanning landscape:

1. **Frozen Action Models:** Most current RP methods treat the robot's ego action model as static, focusing solely on adjusting the external plan to circumvent failures. This prevents the robot from learning from its mistakes and improving its intrinsic capabilities, potentially leading to repeated errors or inefficient detours. Previous attempts at model evolution often get stuck in local optima due to static loss functions and gradient-only updates.
2. **Inefficient LLM Utilization:** Iterative replanning, especially at the global level involving large semantic scene graphs, often requires repeated LLM inferences. This leads to excessive token usage, high computational latency, and potential "hallucinations" if the input context is too large or irrelevant. While some works like SayPlan have tried to reduce graph size, they may introduce additional token costs during graph distillation or operate under assumptions of perfect robot execution.

SERP positions itself as a paradigm shift by advocating for **evolving models** rather than frozen ones. It proposes a novel framework that not only enables cross-level replanning (jointly addressing semantic and physical failures) but also empowers the robot to **self-evolve its action model** by learning from runtime experiences. This pushes the field towards more resilient and adaptive autonomous agents that can "conquer rather than circumvent" failures. Furthermore, it tackles the LLM efficiency problem by introducing a token-efficient graph distillation mechanism, making iterative LLM-based replanning more practical. This research contributes to advancing the core capabilities of embodied AI by making agents more robust, adaptive, and computationally efficient in dynamic, uncertain environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the resilience and robustness of embodied navigation systems in complex, uncertain environments. The authors recognize that failures are an inherent part of autonomous navigation, and while replanning offers a solution, existing methods are fundamentally limited.

The core motivation stems from two critical shortcomings of current replanning (RP) approaches:

1. **Stagnant Robot Capabilities:** Existing RP methods primarily focus on modifying the external plan (e.g., finding an alternative path or target) while **freezing the robot's internal action model**. This means the robot's fundamental capabilities and parameters for execution remain unchanged, preventing it from truly *learning* from its failures. If an execution failure is due to a weakness in the robot's own model (e.g., poor obstacle avoidance parameters), simply finding a detour doesn't address the root cause, leading to repetitive mistakes and inefficient navigation. The motivation is to enable the robot to **upgrade itself** by adapting its action model at runtime, moving beyond merely circumventing failures to actively conquering them.
2. **Inefficient LLM Usage in Iterative Replanning:** LLMs are powerful for high-level reasoning and task planning but can be computationally expensive when required to perform repeated inferences over large semantic spaces (like comprehensive scene graphs). This iterative querying leads to excessive token usage and increased latency, making real-time, adaptive replanning challenging. The motivation here is to develop a **token-efficient** method for leveraging LLMs in replanning, minimizing computational overhead without compromising reasoning quality.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth the following key objectives:

- **Introduce Self-Evolutionary RePlanning (SERP):** To establish a novel framework that allows for dynamic model evolution through runtime learning, shifting from static to adaptable robot models.
- **Develop Agentic Self-Evolution (ASE):** To create a local-level mechanism that enables fine-grained, run-time updates of the robot's action model parameters and even its loss function weights. This mechanism aims to overcome the limitations of existing gradient-based optimization methods by incorporating "bad optima escape" capabilities.
- **Propose Global Graph Chain-of-Thought (GCOT):** To design a global-level replanning mechanism that efficiently distills large scene graphs into smaller, semantically relevant subsets. This aims to reduce LLM token expenditure and computational latency during iterative replanning, while maintaining rich contextual information.
- **Demonstrate Cross-Level Replanning:** To show that SERP can seamlessly integrate local (physical) and global (semantic) replanning, allowing the robot to recover from a wider range of failures.
- **Validate Superior Robustness and Efficiency:** Through extensive simulation and real-world experiments, to prove that SERP achieves higher success rates with lower token expenditure compared to existing methods across diverse environments and tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SERP's methodology is built upon a novel cross-level replanning framework that integrates two synergistic components: **Local Agentic Self-Evolution (ASE)** and **Global Graph Chain-of-Thought (GCOT)**. This dual-layered approach allows SERP to address both physical execution failures and semantic planning shortcomings.

**A. Problem Formulation and Cross-level Replanning**
The environment is represented by a hierarchical multilevel 3D scene graph G=(N, E), encoding semantic and geometric information. Given a natural-language task instruction I, the objective is to generate executable actions A using a planner π parameterized by P, while adhering to physical constraints C. When execution failures occur (due to discrepancies in G or suboptimal P), replanning becomes necessary to update G and P. SERP decomposes this into:
- **Global replanning (φG):** Iteratively updates the relevant subset of the scene graph Ĝ ⊆ G to fulfill the task (handles semantic failures).
- **Local replanning (φL):** Iteratively updates the robot's parameters P through a differentiable motion planner to ensure trajectory feasibility (handles physical failures).

**B. Local Agentic Self-Evolution (ASE)**
ASE is designed to evolve the robot's action model parameters (P = {qs, pv, η}, which weigh path tracking, speed tracking, and obstacle distance in the motion cost function F) and even the loss function weights ({α, β, γ, ω}) based on execution feedback.
- **Motivation:** Faulty actions often result from an improper parameter set P. Traditional auto-differentiation (AD) methods can get stuck in local optima due of the non-convex landscape of actions over parameters.
- **ILAD (In-context Learning with Auto-Differentiation):** This is the core of ASE, combining the precision of gradient-based optimization with the global reasoning capabilities of LLMs:
 - **Auto-Differentiation (AD):** At most epochs, parameters P are updated via gradient descent on a closed-loop self-evolution loss function LφL. This loss function includes terms for path adherence, speed adherence, obstacle distance, and distance to goal.
 - **In-context Learning (IL):** Periodically (at predefined epochs), a Vision-Language Model (VLM) is invoked. The VLM analyzes the failure context (previous execution data, parameters) and globally resets the parameters, allowing the system to "escape" local optima and explore new optimization directions. This process highlights the "mutual assistance" between IL (for global resets and adaptive loss adjustment) and AD (for fine-tuned local optimization).
- **Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) for Initialization:** To provide a robust starting point for parameter optimization, SERP employs RAG. The robot's past navigation experiences (images, SLAM data, captions, parameters) are stored in a vector database. At runtime, the current scene's context is used to query this database, retrieving a coarse initial parameter setting P(0) that broadly fits the situation. This improves the efficiency of subsequent ILAD optimization.

**C. Global Graph Chain-of-Thought (GCOT)**
GCOT addresses the inefficiency of repeatedly querying LLMs over large scene graphs during global replanning.
- **Insight:** For a given task, only a small, semantically relevant subset (distilled graph Ĝ) of the full scene graph G is typically required for effective planning.
- **Dynamic Graph Distillation:** GCOT iteratively extracts this distilled graph Ĝ from the long-term memory G:
 - **Task Decomposition:** The LLM first decomposes the natural language task instruction I into atomic sub-tasks.
 - **Candidate Element Retrieval (CLIP):** A small Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) model, more efficient for searching, retrieves a predetermined number of candidate elements from the full scene graph G based on latent feature similarity with the sub-tasks and failure feedback. This leverages CLIP's efficiency for searching and frees the LLM for reasoning.
 - **Contextual Selection and Graph Formation (LLM):** The LLM then selects the most contextually relevant candidates from the CLIP-identified pool and constructs a compact, task-specific distilled graph Ĝ.
 - **Iterative Planning:** The LLM reasons over this smaller Ĝ to generate plans for individual sub-tasks, summarizing them into a unified task plan. If the task is unplannable, the LLM refines the context and samples Ĝ again. This dual-memory architecture (long-term G, short-term Ĝ) allows for efficient reasoning.
- **Decoupling Reasoning and Searching:** By using CLIP for efficient element searching and LLM for high-level reasoning and contextual selection, GCOT significantly reduces the number of tokens exposed to the LLM, enhancing efficiency and robustness against hallucinations.

The overall SERP process (Algorithm 1) outlines this hierarchical execution: an initial plan is generated; if failure occurs, local ASE is attempted first. If local recovery fails, global GCOT is activated to generate a new path plan, which then feeds back into local execution. This iterative loop continues until success or timeout.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted in Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D) simulations across six scenarios and in real-world settings, demonstrate SERP's superior robustness and efficiency compared to baselines like SayPlan, AD, and VLM-only approaches.

**A. Qualitative Evaluations of SERP:**

- **Local Replanning (ASE) Effectiveness:**
 - **Robustness to Initialization:** When initialized with "proper" parameters via RAG, AD (Auto-Differentiation) successfully evolved the robot's parameters to bypass an obstacle. However, with "poor" RAG initialization, AD failed, getting stuck in a local optimum. In contrast, SERP (using ILAD) successfully navigated the challenge even with poor initialization. The IL (In-context Learning) intervention at specific epochs globally reset parameters, demonstrating its ability to "rethink from a whole new replanning direction" and escape local optima, leading to long-term safe navigation.
 - **Necessity of IL and AD Integration:** An ablation study showed that IL alone led to "value explosion" in parameters and task deviation, indicating that VLM provides intuitive understanding but lacks precise parameter adjustment. This highlights the critical importance of integrating both IL (for global exploration) and AD (for fine-grained local optimization).
- **Global Replanning (GCOT) Effectiveness:**
 - **Physical Failure Handling:** When a path was completely blocked by an obstacle, GCOT successfully generated an alternative path through a different room, demonstrating its ability to reformulate global plans.
 - **Semantic Failure Handling:** When the scene graph contained inaccurate information (a sink cabinet falsely included in a kitchen where it was removed), GCOT identified the semantic error. It then distilled the graph and revised the task plan to target a correct sink object, showcasing its capacity to adapt to environmental discrepancies and semantic inconsistencies.

**B. Quantitative Evaluations of SERP:**

- **Simulation Performance (vs. SayPlan):**
 - **Higher Success Rate (SR) and SPL:** SERP significantly outperformed SayPlan, achieving over 10% higher SR on complex **planning tasks**. This was attributed to SERP's cross-level replanning, which can handle failures (physical and semantic) that require both local recovery and global re-planning, unlike SayPlan's global-only iterative approach.
 - **Superior Token Efficiency (RGTR):** SERP achieved substantially higher Reduced Graph Token Ratio (RGTR) – over 20% higher in searching tasks and over 40% higher in planning tasks. This confirms the efficiency of GCOT's element-level graph distillation approach, which focuses on task-relevant elements rather than larger subgraphs, thus reducing LLM token expenditure.
 - **LLM Impact:** An ablation study indicated that stronger LLMs (GPT-4o vs. Qwen3-Max) further enhance task success rates, demonstrating the importance of advanced reasoning capabilities.
- **Self-Evolution Capabilities (vs. AD and VLM):**
 - SERP achieved significantly higher SR and lower Mean Adaptation Epoch Count (MAEC) compared to AD and VLM (GPT-4o) alone. This advantage was more pronounced with larger initial parameter deviations (e.g., over 40% higher SR than AD for 60%-90% deviation). This confirms SERP's robustness in adapting to suboptimal initial parameter settings.

**C. Real-World Experiment:**

- **Adaptive Navigation:** A robot successfully navigated through an "almost-closed" narrow door, a scenario where the robot initially got stuck. SERP's local ASE adapted the control parameters (e.g., reducing focus on path tracking, increasing obstacle distance regularization), allowing the robot to successfully traverse the constraint. This demonstrated the necessity and effectiveness of ASE for adapting internal parameters to dynamic physical constraints.
- **Global Real-World Adaptation:** SERP enabled the robot to identify and redirect to alternative viable goals when a path was blocked or a target object was missing, validating its global replanning capabilities in real-world dynamic scenarios.
- **Ablation Study:** The real-world ablation study confirmed that for challenging scenarios like an "almost closed door," ASE was essential for success. Without ASE, the robot consistently failed to pass through the narrow gap, whereas with ASE, it completed the task, albeit with longer navigation times.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Agentic Self-Evolutionary Replanning (SERP) represents a significant advancement in embodied navigation, with far-reaching implications for robotics and artificial intelligence.

**1. Paradigm Shift towards Evolving Models:** SERP fundamentally shifts the paradigm from using static, frozen robot action models to dynamic, self-evolving ones. This move from "circumventing" to "conquering" failures is critical for building truly autonomous and resilient robots. Instead of merely planning around inherent weaknesses, robots can now learn from experience to improve their fundamental execution capabilities, making them inherently more robust and adaptable.

**2. Enhanced Resilience and Robustness in Complex Environments:** By seamlessly integrating local (physical) and global (semantic) replanning with a self-evolutionary mechanism, SERP drastically improves a robot's ability to recover from diverse and complex failures. This is crucial for real-world applications where environments are often dynamic, uncertain, and unpredictable, and where unforeseen obstacles or semantic ambiguities are common. This enhanced resilience translates directly into more reliable and trustworthy autonomous systems.

**3. Efficient and Practical Integration of LLMs in Robotics:** SERP's Global Graph Chain-of-Thought (GCOT) addresses a major bottleneck in LLM-based robotics: the high computational cost and latency of iterative inference over large semantic spaces. By intelligently distilling scene graphs into minimal, task-relevant subsets, SERP achieves superior token efficiency without compromising reasoning quality. This makes LLM integration more practical for real-time robotic applications, broadening the scope for complex task planning and instruction following.

**4. Novel Approach to Model Adaptation (ILAD):** The Agentic Self-Evolution (ASE) component introduces a novel "in-context learning with auto-differentiation (ILAD)" mechanism. This innovative blend of gradient-based optimization (AD) with symbolic reasoning and global parameter resets (IL) provides a powerful method for escaping local optima—a common challenge in reinforcement learning and control. This could inspire new approaches to self-supervised learning and continuous adaptation in other AI domains.

**5. Broader Impact on Autonomous Systems:** The principles introduced by SERP could extend beyond embodied navigation to other autonomous systems that operate in dynamic environments. The ability for a system to not only replan its actions but also to adapt its own operational parameters based on failures has implications for:
 - **Industrial Robotics:** Robots that can automatically fine-tune their manipulation or assembly parameters in response to production errors.
 - **Autonomous Vehicles:** Vehicles that can adjust their driving policies to novel road conditions or unexpected vehicle behaviors.
 - **Search and Rescue:** Robots that can adapt their locomotion or sensing strategies in damaged or unfamiliar terrains.
 - **Personalized Robotics:** Robots that can learn and adapt to individual user preferences and specific home environments over time.

In summary, SERP contributes a foundational framework for intelligent agents that are not only capable of executing complex tasks but also possess the intrinsic ability to learn, adapt, and evolve their own operational models. This pushes the boundaries of autonomous systems towards a future where robots are more intelligent, resilient, and truly self-sufficient.
