# DR-SAC: Distributionally Robust Soft Actor-Critic for Reinforcement Learning under Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> DR-SAC introduces the first actor-critic based distributionally robust reinforcement learning algorithm for offline continuous control, significantly enhancing policy performance under environmental uncertainties through KL-divergence-constrained ambiguity sets. It consistently achieves higher rewards than non-robust baselines and existing robust methods while demonstrating substantial computational efficiency improvements.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies are highly sensitive to environmental uncertainties, leading to performance degradation when deployed in environments that deviate even slightly from training conditions.
- Existing Distributionally Robust RL (DR-RL) methods are primarily value-based or restricted to tabular settings, struggling with continuous action spaces and computationally expensive KL-divergence uncertainty sets.
- Offline DRL with KL-divergence uncertainty faces the 'double-sampling' issue when nominal transition distributions are unknown, making traditional empirical estimation methods intractable.

## Method

- Developed ==Distributionally Robust== Soft Actor-Critic (**DR-SAC**), an actor-critic based framework for offline learning in continuous action spaces under KL-divergence transition uncertainty.
- Introduced a scalable functional inner optimization that reformulates per-state-action scalar dual problems into a single functional optimization, drastically reducing computational cost.
- Integrated generative models (Variational Autoencoders) to learn nominal transition distributions from offline datasets, thereby resolving the 'double-sampling' issue.

## Results

- DR-SAC consistently outperformed SAC and other non-robust baselines, achieving up to 9.8 times higher average reward in LunarLander under engine parameter changes and over **75%** improvement in Cartpole with observation noise.
- The functional approximation significantly improved training efficiency, requiring less than **2%** of the training time of its accurate variant (e.g., 4.7 vs 260 minutes in Pendulum) and up to 23.2 times less time than RFQI.
- VAE-based generative modeling offered a robust and efficient solution for transition distribution estimation, with robustness largely insensitive to latent dimension and outperforming diffusion models in training time efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extending actor-critic methods to a distributionally robust framework is viable and effective for continuous control, offering better scalability and performance than value-based robust approaches.
> - Functional approximation of the dual variable is critical for making KL-divergence based robust optimization computationally tractable and efficient in continuous action spaces.
> - Generative modeling provides an effective way to estimate unknown transition distributions in offline settings, enabling robust learning without requiring environment interaction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has demonstrated considerable success across various domains, including game playing and robotic control. A specific area of growth is offline DRL, which trains policies from static datasets without requiring real-time environment interactions, thereby enhancing safety and data efficiency. Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) is a notable algorithm within this paradigm, characterized by its entropy-regularized framework, which offers theoretical foundations and practical effectiveness in stochastic control.

Despite these advancements, a significant obstacle to deploying DRL in real-world systems is its sensitivity to environmental uncertainties. Policies optimized in a nominal environment often experience performance degradation when confronted with minor shifts or perturbations in the deployment environment. These discrepancies can arise from various sources, such as uncertain transition and reward functions, observation and actuator noise, or parameter variations.

Distributionally Robust Reinforcement Learning (DR-RL) addresses this vulnerability by framing the problem within a Robust Markov Decision Process (RMDP). Instead of assuming a single, fixed environment model, DR-RL optimizes policies against a worst-case scenario within an ambiguity set of possible environment models.

While DR-RL algorithms have been proposed, much of the existing work has focused on theoretical guarantees and sample complexity, primarily in tabular settings or using value-based methods. For instance, Robust Fitted Q-Iteration (RFQI) is one of the few offline DR-RL algorithms applicable to continuous action spaces, but it is limited to uncertainty sets defined by Total Variation (TV) distance. TV distance offers analytical convenience but lacks generalizability to other divergence measures. Furthermore, RFQI’s underlying Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI) is a value-based method known for limitations such as deterministic policies, reduced applicability to high-dimensional action spaces, and sensitivity to errors in state-action value estimation.

Actor-critic algorithms, which combine the benefits of low-variance value estimation and scalable policy optimization, are widely preferred in continuous control benchmarks and practical applications. However, a distributionally robust counterpart for actor-critic methods, particularly for offline learning in continuous action spaces with more general uncertainty measures like Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, has not been developed. This identified gap forms the core motivation for the presented work.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to introduce Distributionally Robust Soft Actor-Critic (DR-SAC), positioning it as the first actor-critic based DR-RL algorithm specifically designed for offline learning in continuous action spaces. The motivation for this development stems from several identified limitations in the existing DR-RL landscape:

1. **Addressing Vulnerability to Environmental Uncertainty:** Standard DRL policies often perform poorly when deployed in environments that deviate even slightly from their training conditions. This vulnerability necessitates robust learning approaches that can maintain performance under varying environmental dynamics, which DR-SAC aims to provide by optimizing against worst-case transition models.

2. **Bridging the Gap in DR-RL Methodologies:** Current DR-RL algorithms are predominantly confined to value-based methods or tabular settings. Value-based methods, such as FQI or RFQI, have inherent limitations including generating deterministic policies and struggling in high-dimensional continuous action spaces. Actor-critic methods, exemplified by SAC, have demonstrated empirical success and scalability in continuous control tasks. The motivation is to extend the advantages of actor-critic methods to the DR-RL framework, providing a more versatile and effective approach for real-world continuous control.

3. **Handling KL-Divergence Uncertainty Sets:** Many existing robust methods, like RFQI, use Total Variation (TV) distance to define uncertainty sets due to its analytical convenience. However, KL-divergence offers a more general and flexible way to model distributional uncertainty, reflecting a broader class of environmental shifts. The motivation is to develop a robust algorithm capable of optimizing policies against worst-case transition models within KL-divergence-constrained ambiguity sets, which presents significant computational challenges not addressed by prior work.

4. **Enabling Offline Learning in Continuous Spaces:** The research specifically targets the offline RL setting, where agents learn from a fixed, pre-collected dataset without further interaction with the environment. This setting is crucial for safety and data efficiency in many real-world applications. A key challenge in offline DR-RL with KL-divergence uncertainty is that nominal transition distributions are unknown, and direct empirical estimation suffers from the "double-sampling" issue, rendering traditional Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) approaches inapplicable. A core motivation is to devise a method to accurately estimate these distributions and enable robust learning in this constrained setting.

Based on these motivations, the work outlines three main contributions:
- **Formulation of a Distributionally Robust Maximum Entropy Framework:** Developing a theoretical framework for maximum entropy learning under transition uncertainties modeled by KL-divergence-constrained ambiguity sets, including derivation of distributionally robust soft policy iteration with convergence guarantees.
- **Scalable Reformulation via Functional Inner Optimization:** Exploiting an interchange property to reformulate per-state-action scalar inner optimization problems into a shared functional optimization. This aims to reduce computational cost and enable application to continuous action spaces.
- **Integration of Generative Modeling for KL-Based Robustness:** Introducing generative models (specifically VAEs) to estimate unknown nominal transition distributions and construct empirical measures, thereby resolving the double-sampling issue and facilitating robust soft policy learning in offline, continuous control tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of DR-SAC involves extending the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) framework to handle distributionally robust optimization under KL-divergence uncertainty in an offline, continuous action space setting.

**A. Formulation:**
The work begins by defining a standard Markov Decision Process (MDP) and the associated soft value (V) and soft state-action value (Q) functions, which incorporate an entropy regularization term. The standard soft Bellman operator and policy improvement rule for SAC are presented.

The core extension involves introducing the Robust Markov Decision Process (RMDP) framework, denoted as M_δ = (S, A, R, P(δ), γ). Here, uncertainty is modeled through KL-divergence balls centered at a nominal transition distribution p_0(s'|s,a). The objective is to find a policy that maximizes the soft value function under the worst-case transition model within this KL-divergence-constrained ambiguity set P(δ). This leads to the definition of a distributionally robust soft value function and distributionally robust soft Q-function, both involving an `inf` operation over the ambiguity set P(δ).

**B. Algorithm Development: Distributionally Robust Soft Actor-Critic (DR-SAC)**

1. **Distributionally Robust Soft Policy Iteration:**
 - **DR Soft Policy Evaluation:** For a fixed policy π, the DR soft Q-function is estimated by iteratively applying a distributionally robust soft Bellman operator, T^π_δ Q(s,a). This operator incorporates the worst-case expectation over future states within P(δ).
 - **Dual Formulation:** To address the intractability of optimizing over an infinite-dimensional set of transition distributions, the authors apply strong duality for worst-case expectations over a KL-divergence ball. This reformulates the `inf` operation into a `sup` over a scalar dual variable β, making the problem tractable and dependent only on the nominal transition distribution p_0(s'|s,a).
 - **Convergence Guarantees:** Proposition 3.4 establishes that the DR soft Bellman operator is a γ-contraction mapping, guaranteeing the convergence of iterative policy evaluation to the unique DR soft Q-value.
 - **DR Soft Policy Improvement:** The policy update rule is similar to SAC, where the new policy minimizes the KL divergence to the exponential of the DR soft Q-function. Proposition 3.5 demonstrates that this update ensures a non-decreasing value of the policy.
 - **Overall Convergence:** Theorem 3.6 states that the alternating process of DR soft policy evaluation and improvement converges to the optimal DR policy.

2. **Addressing Challenges for Continuous Action Spaces and Offline Settings:**

 - **Scalable Reformulation via Functional Inner Optimization:** A key computational challenge is that the dual problem's scalar optimization over β must be solved independently for every (s,a) pair. To enhance efficiency in continuous spaces, the authors reformulate this into a single functional optimization problem. Proposition 3.7, based on the interchange of minimization and expectation in decomposable spaces, allows replacing the per-(s,a) `sup_β` with a `sup_g` over a function g(s,a), which approximates the optimal β for all (s,a) pairs jointly. This results in a functional DR soft Bellman operator T^π_(δ,g) Q(s,a).

 - **Generative Modeling for Nominal Distributions:** In the offline setting, the nominal transition distributions p_0(s'|s,a) are unknown, and no simulator is available. Direct empirical estimation of the non-linear expectations in the KL-based dual formulation suffers from a "double-sampling" issue. To overcome this, the method incorporates a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to learn the conditional transition distribution p_0(s'|s,a) from the offline dataset. The trained VAE then generates synthetic next-state samples, forming an empirical measure ˜p_0(s'|s,a) used in the empirical DR soft Bellman operator ẽT^π_(δ,g) Q(s,a).

3. **Distributionally Robust Soft Actor-Critic (Algorithm 1):**
 - The complete algorithm uses neural networks to parameterize the value function V_ψ, Q-functions Q_θ (with multiple critics Q_θ_i to mitigate overestimation), the stochastic policy π_ϕ, and a neural network G_η to approximate the functional g(s,a).
 - Training involves alternating updates:
 - VAE (φ) for transition model estimation.
 - Optimization of G_η to find the optimal functional ẽg* using backpropagation.
 - Updates for the V-network (ψ) and Q-networks (θ) using the empirical DR soft Bellman operator.
 - Policy network (ϕ) and entropy temperature (α) updates, similar to standard SAC.
 - Soft updates for target networks ¯ψ and ¯θ.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to demonstrate DR-SAC's robustness under environmental uncertainties and its computational efficiency in offline RL tasks.

**A. Robustness Performance:**
DR-SAC was tested on five continuous RL environments (Pendulum, Cartpole, LunarLander, Reacher, HalfCheetah) against non-robust baselines (SAC, FQI, DDPG, CQL) and the existing robust offline algorithm RFQI. Perturbations included environmental parameter changes, random noise added to observed states, and random actuator noise.

- **Consistent Outperformance:** DR-SAC consistently achieved higher average rewards than the SAC baseline and other non-robust algorithms across various perturbation types and intensities.
- **Quantitative Improvements:**
 - In LunarLander, DR-SAC attained up to 9.8 times higher average reward than SAC when engine power parameters were reduced by 30%.
 - In Pendulum, DR-SAC outperformed SAC by 35% when the length parameter changed by 20%, and over 40% when mass changed by 120%.
 - In Cartpole, DR-SAC showed over 75% performance improvement compared to SAC and DDPG under certain observation noise levels.
 - In HalfCheetah, DR-SAC maintained stable performance (over 6300 reward) while SAC's reward decreased significantly when `back_damping` varied by 50%.
 - In Reacher, DR-SAC exhibited the best performance across all tested observation noise levels and `joint_damping` perturbations.
- **RFQI Performance:** While RFQI achieved comparable robustness to DR-SAC in some specific scenarios (e.g., Cartpole), it often performed poorly or unacceptably in others (e.g., Pendulum, HalfCheetah, LunarLander). This was attributed to RFQI's sensitivity to dataset distributions, particularly when datasets do not provide broad coverage over the action space, a common issue for BCQ-based algorithms.

**B. Ablation Studies - Training Efficiency:**

- **Functional Approximation vs. Accurate Bellman Operator:** A variant, DR-SAC-Accurate (without functional approximation), which directly solves the per-(s,a) scalar optimization, was implemented. DR-SAC-Functional (with functional approximation) achieved comparable or superior robustness while requiring less than 2% of the training time of DR-SAC-Accurate (e.g., 4.7 minutes vs. 260 minutes in Pendulum). This validates the significant computational efficiency gained through functional approximation.
- **Comparison with RFQI:** DR-SAC demonstrated substantial improvements in training efficiency over RFQI. DR-SAC required up to 23.2 times less training time than RFQI (e.g., 36 minutes vs. 238 minutes in LunarLander). The training time overhead compared to their non-robust counterparts was also notably lower for DR-SAC (at most 2.6 times SAC) than for RFQI (at least 11.3 times FQI). This efficiency gap was partly attributed to RFQI's need for a high number of gradient descent steps (e.g., 1000) for its functional optimization, whereas DR-SAC achieved comparable performance with only 5 steps.

**C. Ablation Studies - Generative Model Selection:**

- **VAE Robustness to Latent Dimension:** DR-SAC's robustness was largely insensitive to the latent dimension of the VAE model (e.g., between 5 and 20 in Pendulum), indicating stable performance despite inherent estimation errors from the generative model.
- **Comparison with Other Generative Models:** Diffusion models and Normalizing Flows were explored as alternatives to VAEs. Flow-based models exhibited unstable performance. Diffusion-based models achieved comparable robustness but required at least 4.5 times more training time than the VAE-based approach. This supported the choice of VAE as a balanced option for robustness and efficiency.
- **V-Network Usage:** An ablation study on the V-network in SAC demonstrated that retaining the V-network improved the algorithm's stability and reduced its sensitivity to variations in the behavior policy underlying the offline dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of DR-SAC marks a methodological advancement in the field of deep reinforcement learning. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Robust DRL in Real-World Continuous Control:** DR-SAC addresses a critical limitation of DRL: its vulnerability to environmental uncertainties. By providing an actor-critic based, distributionally robust framework for continuous action spaces, the algorithm facilitates the deployment of DRL agents in practical, real-world scenarios where environmental conditions are rarely perfectly known or stable. This capability is particularly relevant for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and industrial control.

2. **Bridging Theoretical Gaps in DR-RL:** The work extends distributionally robust theory to the maximum entropy actor-critic framework. It establishes distributionally robust soft policy iteration with convergence guarantees, providing a more rigorous foundation for robust policy optimization in continuous state-action spaces.

3. **Algorithmic Innovations for Scalability and Practicality:**
 - The **functional inner optimization** significantly enhances computational efficiency by reformulating per-state-action optimizations into a single, shared functional optimization. This reduction in training time (over 80% compared to RFQI) makes DR-SAC applicable to large-scale, complex problems where previous methods were prohibitively slow.
 - The integration of **generative modeling (VAEs)** resolves the "double-sampling" issue inherent in KL-divergence-based dual formulations when nominal transition distributions are unknown in offline settings. This approach makes robust learning feasible without requiring online interaction or simulators, which are often unavailable in real-world offline learning contexts.

4. **Superior Performance and Efficiency:** The experimental results demonstrate DR-SAC's ability to achieve significantly higher average rewards under various perturbations compared to non-robust baselines, while also outperforming or matching the robustness of existing robust methods like RFQI at a fraction of the computational cost. This combination of robustness and efficiency represents a practical step forward for offline DR-RL.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** As the first actor-critic based DR-RL algorithm for offline continuous control under KL-divergence uncertainty, DR-SAC opens avenues for further research. This includes exploring different generative models, investigating broader classes of uncertainty sets, and applying the framework to more complex real-world control tasks. The publicly available code also facilitates reproducibility and encourages community engagement.

In summary, DR-SAC represents a robust and efficient solution for learning policies that can generalize and perform reliably under environmental shifts, thereby contributing to the practical applicability of deep reinforcement learning in dynamic and uncertain real-world environments.
