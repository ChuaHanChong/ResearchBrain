# Accelerating Visual Policy Learning with Sampling-Based Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> SGPS (Sampling-Guided Policy Search) trains visual legged and humanoid policies by coupling sampling-based MPC with decoupled first-order policy gradients through differentiable simulation. MPC supplies a fixed tracking reference and behavior-cloning initialization; training alternates recurring MPC action-target refinement with short-horizon gradient updates, learning from depth without a privileged teacher. It needs 196x fewer environment steps than PPO on trot, and a distilled policy transfers zero-shot to a real Go2.

## Problem

- RL for perceptive locomotion needs massive parallel simulation and GPU memory for visual observations, and typically relies on a privileged state teacher distilled into a visual student.
- First-order policy gradients through differentiable simulation (SHAC, D.Va) cut training cost but, as local optimizers, converge to unintended contact patterns when rewards do not distinguish gaits.
- Sampling-based MPC such as DIAL-MPC produces dynamically feasible contact-rich behaviors, but deployment requires online optimization and state estimation, e.g., motion capture, rather than a reactive visual policy.

## Method

- **Annealed sampling-MPC reference generation**: ==DIAL-MPC==-style ==spline-node search== with a non-differentiable task reward yields a success-gated fixed tracking reference plus noise-augmented ==behavior-cloning== data for policy initialization.
- **Recurring sampling-based action-target refinement**: each training block re-searches from current states, accepts segments beating the seed plan's tracking score by a margin, and adds a decaying ==target-cloning loss== to ==FoPG== tracking.
- **Decoupled visual FoPG plus DAgger skill distillation**: depth is rendered from ==stop-gradient states== so physics gradients train the encoder without renderer derivatives; trot, crawl, and hurdle specialists merge into one reference-free policy.

## Results

- State-based Go2 trot: PPO needs **196x** more environment interactions than SGPS; the SGPS gait reproduces the reference's diagonal contact pattern (correlation **+0.78** vs **+0.86**), versus **+0.21** for gait-blind FoPG from scratch.
- Refinement ablation: visual Go2 trot reaches about **16,000** return near **4.1M** interactions versus **11,000** without refinement; G1 visual carrying reaches about **26,500** versus **20,500** near **6M**; state trot gains modestly.
- Visual G1 carrying nears the DAgger state expert's final return by about **4.1M** interactions, later exceeding it, while the DAgger student stays below its teacher; distilled Go2 policy transfers zero-shot with **2.7 ms** inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sampling-based search and first-order gradients are complementary: search picks contact modes that local gradients tend to miss, while gradients cheaply robustify the policy under perturbed states and randomized dynamics.
> - Much of the sample-efficiency gain comes from a good reference plus behavior-cloning initialization; recurring refinement pays off mainly for visual policies, which plateau markedly lower without it.
> - Excluding rendering from gradient graphs lets differentiable physics train visual encoders, skipping privileged teachers; FoPG equals fitting gradient-step action targets, so derivative-free search targets plug into the same update.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A real Unitree Go2 crawls beneath a beam, resumes trotting, then climbs onto and traverses a box before landing. It performs this sequence autonomously, and it was trained without hardware fine-tuning. It uses onboard observations and a velocity command, not a sampled reference or online MPC. The method behind it, SGPS, keeps differentiable policy learning but repeatedly gives it new action targets from sampling-based search. The result is a learned policy rather than a controller that must keep planning online.

Top row: simulation. Bottom row: real Go2 hardware. Read right to left: the robot crawls beneath a beam, trots toward a box, then climbs, traverses, and lands. Translucent poses indicate intermediate motion. The courses are analogous; the simulation course uses a wider box. The paper reports zero-shot transfer and autonomous transitions with velocity command as the only user input.

### 2. From sampled plans to a policy

Training policies through contact-rich tasks can require substantial simulation and compute, and local gradient updates may settle on the wrong contact pattern. Sampling-based model-predictive control (MPC) offers another route. It tests candidate action sequences in a simulator, executes the selected action, and replans. [[2409.15610|DIAL-MPC]] already uses annealed sampling to plan locomotion, but it is an online controller rather than a learned visual policy.

SGPS uses sampling during training. First, MPC generates a task-successful trajectory, and behavior cloning (BC) initializes a policy from its states and actions. Training then repeats a cycle:

- Search from selected training states for better action sequences.
- Accept a segment only if its tracking score on the nominal training model clears a pre-set minimum and improves on the seed plan by a margin.
- Use the accepted actions as fixed cloning targets for the next block of short-horizon first-order policy-gradient (FoPG) updates.

FoPG uses differentiable simulation to update policy parameters, and these updates use perturbed resets and randomized dynamics. Throughout, the tracking reference stays fixed while the action targets change. Visual specialists take detached depth images as inputs, but rendering is excluded from the gradient computation. This builds on [D.Va](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2505.10646), which already decouples rendering from the policy-gradient computation. Acceptance on the nominal model does not guarantee improved policy return.

A: task objective → sampling MPC → fixed reference plus sampled actions → BC policy initialization. B: accepted action targets feed a reference-tracking loss plus a λ-weighted target-cloning loss, followed by decoupled FoPG updates under perturbed resets and randomized dynamics. C: SGPS specialists are distilled with DAgger into one visual policy that takes depth input and requires neither reference inputs nor online MPC.

### 3. What the refinement comparison establishes

The key comparison uses a simulated Go2 with a visual policy, performing a flat-terrain forward trot at 0.8 m/s. Three training seeds were used. Return is the paper's task reward, evaluated deterministically on the nominal model. At about 4.1 million environment interactions, full SGPS reaches approximately 16,000 return, versus approximately 11,000 for SGPS without recurring refinement.

Both variants retain BC initialization and reference tracking. The ablation removes recurring search and accepted-target cloning together, so it tests the pair rather than either component alone. Environment-step counts include search rollouts, and elapsed time includes refinement search. The result therefore does not show fewer interactions or a guaranteed wall-clock speedup. It also does not establish that the gain persists under randomized-model evaluation.

### 4. From specialists to hardware

Separate Go2 specialists for trot, crawl, and hurdle are combined into one visual policy through a DAgger distillation stage. This is an additional step, not part of SGPS's recurrent refinement. The unified policy was deployed zero-shot on the real Go2, with autonomous transitions using onboard observations and a velocity command, and with no online MPC or reference input.

SGPS contributes a way to combine repeated search guidance with local policy optimization while keeping the tracking reference fixed. It also has clear limits. It relies on differentiable dynamics, a successful sampled reference, and task-specific objectives. The humanoid pushing and carrying results are simulated, with simplified object motion and hand–object interactions. Complex manipulation and humanoid hardware deployment remain untested.
