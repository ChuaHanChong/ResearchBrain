# Prompt to Transfer: Sim-to-Real Transfer for Traffic Signal Control with Prompt Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> PromptGAT extends Grounded Action Transformation for sim-to-real traffic signal control by querying GPT-4 with cloze-style prompts about weather, road type, and lane vehicle counts. The inferred dynamics (acceleration, deceleration, emergency deceleration, startup delay) are fused into the forward model's input, yielding better next-state prediction, better-grounded actions, and smaller CityFlow-to-SUMO performance gaps for a DQN policy.

## Problem

- RL traffic signal policies trained in simulators degrade on deployment because simulator transition dynamics differ from real road conditions like weather, road type, and vehicle load.
- Conventional grounded action transformation fits its forward model purely on real trajectories, needing data covering the whole state distribution to predict next states accurately.
- Such forward models mispredict unobserved states from rare events or extreme weather, whereas human knowledge readily infers slower acceleration and longer startup delays there.

## Method

- ==Prompt-based dynamics modeling==: a ==cloze template== of Task, Context (weather, road type, lane vehicle count), and Output Restriction elicits four numeric dynamics indicators from GPT-4 per lane.
- ==Fusion module== concatenates lane state, LLM-inferred dynamics, and domain knowledge, passes them through a linear ReLU layer, and feeds the per-lane features as an extra forward-model input.
- **PromptGAT** keeps ==Grounded Action Transformation (GAT)=='s structure: the augmented forward model trains on real data with MSE, the inverse model trains on simulated data with cross-entropy to output grounded discrete phases.

## Results

- Across four SUMO settings, PromptGAT cut the travel-time gap versus direct transfer, for example snowy V4 from **221.25** to **199.06** seconds and rainy V3 from **94.63** to **87.25**.
- PromptGAT beat Vanilla-GAT on most metrics, including V3 travel-time gap **87.25** versus **103.06** and V2 queue gap **30.53** versus **33.82**, with lower variance.
- Forward-model prediction loss was consistently lower than Vanilla-GAT, and accuracy improvement correlated significantly with mitigated travel-time, throughput, and queue gaps (**p ≤ 0.001**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM priors can substitute for scarce real-world coverage, supplying plausible physical parameters for rare conditions where purely data-driven dynamics models extrapolate badly.
> - Gains flow through an explicit causal chain: better forward-model accuracy yields better-grounded actions, a correlation worth measuring rather than assuming in grounding pipelines.
> - Evidence is simulator-to-simulator with hand-set target parameters, so LLM estimates match a scripted ground truth; genuine physical deployment remains unvalidated.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traffic Signal Control (TSC) is a critical area of research in intelligent transportation systems, aiming to optimize traffic flow and mitigate congestion in urban environments. Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods have demonstrated capabilities in addressing TSC challenges through policies trained in simulation environments. These simulated environments allow for extensive trial-and-error learning without the risks or costs associated with real-world deployments.

However, a well-documented challenge in deploying RL policies from simulation to reality, known as the "sim-to-real" gap, often results in performance degradation. This gap primarily arises from discrepancies in system dynamics between the idealized simulation environments and complex, unpredictable real-world conditions. Existing approaches to bridge this gap generally fall into three categories: domain randomization, which trains policies to be robust to environmental variations; domain adaptation, which aligns features between source (simulation) and target (real-world) domains; and grounding methods, which correct simulator bias to match real-world dynamics. Grounded Action Transformation (GAT) is one such grounding framework that adjusts simulated dynamics to resemble real-world conditions.

Traditional GAT methods learn a forward model (which predicts the next state) solely from real-world data. This approach can be data-intensive and may struggle with unobserved or rare real-world states, such as those occurring under extreme weather conditions. The current work positions itself at the intersection of sim-to-real transfer, traffic signal control, and the emerging field of Large Language Models (LLMs). LLMs, having been trained on vast amounts of text, exhibit inference capabilities and possess implicit human knowledge. This research introduces LLMs into the GAT framework to enhance the understanding and profiling of system dynamics, particularly for conditions where real-world data might be scarce or novel. By leveraging LLMs, the paper aims to provide a more general and accurate understanding of how environmental factors (e.g., weather, road type, traffic state) influence traffic dynamics, thereby improving the transferability of RL policies for TSC. This represents a novel application of LLMs to address the sim-to-real transfer problem in the context of traffic signal control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to mitigate the performance gap that arises when Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies, trained in simulation environments for Traffic Signal Control (TSC), are deployed in real-world settings. This phenomenon is commonly referred to as the "sim-to-real" transfer problem.

The motivation for addressing this problem stems from several factors:
- **Prevalence of the Sim-to-Real Gap**: While RL methods show promise in simulators for TSC by learning optimal policies through trial and error, the inherent differences in system dynamics between simulated and real-world traffic environments lead to a notable drop in performance upon real-world deployment. This discrepancy undermines the practical applicability of simulator-trained policies.
- **Limitations of Traditional Grounded Action Transformation (GAT)**: The GAT framework is a method designed to bridge the sim-to-real gap by making simulator dynamics resemble real-world dynamics. A crucial component of GAT is the forward model, which predicts the next real-world state given a current state and action. Conventionally, this forward model is learned exclusively from real-world data. However, this approach has limitations:
 - **Data Scarcity**: Learning an accurate forward model across the entire state distribution requires a substantial amount of real-world data, which can be expensive and time-consuming to collect.
 - **Handling Unobserved States**: Traditional GAT struggles when the policy encounters states not previously observed in the real-world data. This is particularly problematic for rare events or unusual conditions, such as extreme weather, where the learned forward model may produce significant prediction errors.
- **Leveraging Implicit Human Knowledge**: Humans possess an intuitive understanding of how various factors, such as weather conditions (e.g., snow, rain), road types, and traffic states, influence vehicle behavior and overall traffic dynamics. For instance, humans know that vehicles move slower with reduced acceleration and deceleration in snowy conditions, impacting green light throughput. This implicit knowledge is difficult to encode directly into traditional learning models.
- **Opportunity with Large Language Models (LLMs)**: Recent advancements have demonstrated that pre-trained LLMs, with their extensive training on diverse data, are equipped with sophisticated inference abilities. These models can interpret natural language descriptions and generate rational responses based on embedded knowledge. The authors hypothesize that LLMs can be leveraged to understand and profile system dynamics by processing contextual information (e.g., weather, road type, traffic state) and inferring their impact on traffic indicators.

Therefore, the key objectives are:
1. To develop a novel method, PromptGAT, that integrates LLMs into the GAT framework to address the sim-to-real transfer problem in TSC.
2. To utilize LLMs to understand how specific environmental and traffic conditions (weather, road types, traffic states) influence real-world traffic dynamics, thereby enriching the learning of the GAT's forward model.
3. To enhance the accuracy and generalization capability of the forward model by incorporating LLM-inferred dynamic knowledge, especially for conditions underrepresented in real-world data.
4. To enable the RL agent to learn more realistic and transferable policies by grounding actions based on a more accurate and comprehensive understanding of real-world dynamics, ultimately mitigating the performance gap when deployed from simulation to reality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, PromptGAT, extends the Grounded Action Transformation (GAT) framework by incorporating Large Language Models (LLMs) to enhance the modeling of real-world system dynamics for sim-to-real transfer in Traffic Signal Control (TSC).

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: RL-based TSC and GAT

- **RL-based TSC**: The TSC problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) where a policy ($\pi_{\theta}$) is learned to choose actions (traffic signal phases) to optimize a reward function (e.g., minimize queue length, delay). Deep Q-Network (DQN) is employed as the RL algorithm to learn an optimal policy in a simulated environment ($E_{sim}$).
- **Grounded Action Transformation (GAT)**: GAT aims to reduce the discrepancy between simulator ($P_{\phi}$) and real-world ($P^{*}$) transition dynamics. It introduces an action transformation function, $g_{\phi}(s_t, a_t) = h_{\phi}^{-}(\hat{s}_{t+1}, s_t)$, which comprises two key components:
 - **Forward Model ($f_{\phi}^{+}$)**: Trained on real-world data, it predicts the next possible real-world state ($\hat{s}_{t+1}$) given the current state ($s_t$) and action ($a_t$): $\hat{s}_{t+1} = f_{\phi}^{+}(s_t, a_t)$. This model helps understand real-world transitions.
 - **Inverse Model ($h_{\phi}^{-}$)**: Trained on simulation data, it predicts a "grounded" action ($\hat{a}_t$) that, when executed in the simulator, would lead to the state $\hat{s}_{t+1}$ from $s_t$: $\hat{a}_t = h_{\phi}^{-}(\hat{s}_{t+1}, s_t)$. The RL policy then learns in the simulator by executing these grounded actions.

#### 3.2. Prompt-based GAT: Integrating LLMs

The core innovation of PromptGAT is the integration of LLMs to enhance the forward model's ability to profile real-world dynamics.

- **In-context Learning for Dynamics Knowledge**:
 - LLMs are leveraged for their in-context, zero-shot/few-shot inference capabilities.
 - A natural language "Context" is constructed, comprising information about the environment: `⟨Weather⟩⟨Road Type⟩⟨Traffic State⟩`. `Traffic State` refers to the number of vehicles on a specific lane ($N_v^l$).
 - A "Prompt" template is designed: `⟨Task⟩⟨[Context]⟩⟨Output Restriction⟩`.
 - `⟨Task⟩`: Explains the intention, e.g., to estimate average acceleration, deceleration, emergency deceleration, and startup delay (key dynamic indicators).
 - `⟨Output Restriction⟩`: Specifies the desired output format (e.g., `[average acceleration: {value}]`).
 - By providing this prompt to an LLM (specifically GPT-4.0), the model infers and outputs quantitative values for the dynamic indicators, representing $D_l^r$, the concrete real-world system dynamics for lane $l$ at time $t$. This process allows the LLM to provide insights into dynamic changes based on domain knowledge.

- **Prompt-based Dynamics Modeling Module**:
 - Traditional forward models only consider $(s_t, a_t)$. PromptGAT recognizes that real-world state transitions are also influenced by domain knowledge ($K_d$), such as weather and road conditions.
 - The LLM-inferred dynamics ($D_l^r$) are incorporated into the forward model's input. For each lane $l$, the current lane state ($s_l^t$), the LLM-derived dynamics knowledge ($D_l^r$), and the perceptible domain knowledge ($K_l^d = \text{(weather, road)}$) are fused.
 - A fusion module, implemented as a concatenation operation (`Concatenate(s_l^t, D_l^r, K_l^d)`), creates an intermediate representation ($\dot{x}_l^t$). This is then passed through a ReLU-activated linear layer to produce a feature space ($x_l^t$) for each lane.
 - The integrated information from all lanes ($X_t = (x_1^t, x_2^t, \dots, x_n^t)$) is then used as an additional input to the forward model. The updated forward model is expressed as: $\hat{s}_{t+1} = f_{\phi}^{+}(s_t, a_t, X_t)$.
 - The forward model ($f_{\phi}^{+}$) is approximated by a neural network and optimized by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between its predictions ($\hat{s}_{i,t+1}$) and actual real-world next states ($s_{i,t+1}$), sampled from trajectories in $D_{real}$.
 - The inverse model ($h_{\phi}^{-}$) remains similar to the vanilla GAT, trained on simulation data using Categorical Cross-Entropy (CE) loss, as its learning cost is lower and LLM integration was not deemed necessary for its function in this work.

#### 3.3. Overall Algorithm

1. **Pre-training**: An initial policy ($\pi_{\theta}$) is trained for a set number of iterations in the simulation environment ($E_{sim}$).
2. **Iterative Learning Loop**: For each iteration:
 - The current policy ($\pi_{\theta}$) is rolled out in both $E_{sim}$ and a designated $E_{real}$ (SUMO with specific configurations), collecting trajectories for $D_{sim}$ and $D_{real}$.
 - For each lane and time step in the real-world trajectories, the LLM is queried with a prompt consisting of the corresponding domain information ($K_l^d$: weather, road condition) and traffic state ($N_l^v$), to acquire the dynamic indicators ($D_l^r$).
 - **Forward Model Update**: The extracted dynamics ($D_l^r$) are fused with the state information ($s_l^t, K_l^d$) to create $X_t$. The forward model ($f_{\phi}^{+}$) is then updated using $D_{real}$ to minimize MSE loss, predicting $\hat{s}_{t+1}$ based on $(s_t, a_t, X_t)$.
 - **Inverse Model Update**: The inverse model ($h_{\phi}^{-}$) is updated using $D_{sim}$ to minimize CE loss, predicting $\hat{a}_t$ from $(\hat{s}_{t+1}, s_t)$.
 - **Policy Training**: The policy ($\pi_{\theta}$) is refined using RL (DQN) in $E_{sim}$, utilizing the grounded actions provided by the GAT mechanism.

#### 3.4. Experiment Settings

- **Simulation-to-Simulation Setup**: Cityflow serves as $E_{sim}$, and SUMO serves as $E_{real}$. This setup allows for reproducible experiments that mimic real-world complexity.
- **Real-world Configurations**: Four distinct configurations (V1, V2, V3, V4) are modeled in SUMO to represent diverse real-world scenarios:
 - V1 & V2: Heavy industry roads with lighter-loaded (V1) or heavier-loaded (V2) vehicles, varying in acceleration, deceleration, and startup delay.
 - V3 & V4: Special weather conditions (rainy for V3, snowy for V4), altering vehicle dynamics like acceleration, deceleration, emergency deceleration, and startup delay.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: The performance gap ($\psi_{\Delta} = \psi_{real} - \psi_{sim}$) is evaluated using: Average Travel Time (ATT), Throughput (TP), Reward, Average Queue Length (Queue), and Average Delay (Delay). Lower $\psi_{\Delta}$ for ATT, Queue, Delay, and higher $\psi_{\Delta}$ for TP, Reward indicate better performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate PromptGAT's ability to mitigate the sim-to-real gap, assess the rationality of LLM inferences, and compare its performance against baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Prompt Intention Analysis

The study first evaluated whether the LLM (GPT-4.0) could provide rational inferences about traffic dynamics based on contextual prompts. By presenting the LLM with scenarios combining vehicle numbers, road types, and weather conditions (e.g., sunny/light industry, sunny/heavy industry, rainy/normal, snowy/normal), the LLM inferred values for average acceleration, deceleration, emergency deceleration, and startup delay. A comparison between these LLM inferences and predefined "real-world" settings (V1-V4) showed that the LLM's outputs reflected similar tendencies and realistic variations. For instance, snowy conditions (V4) consistently resulted in lower acceleration and deceleration rates and higher startup delays compared to rainy (V3) or sunny conditions, aligning with practical laws of physics and human understanding of driving in adverse weather. This analysis confirmed the LLM's capability to provide realistic and consistent dynamic inferences based on given contextual information.

#### 4.2. Ability to Mitigate the Performance Gap

The research demonstrated that directly transferring policies trained in a simulated environment ($E_{sim}$) to varied real-world configurations ($E_{real}$, modeled in SUMO) resulted in substantial performance degradation across all evaluated metrics (Average Travel Time, Throughput, Reward, Average Queue Length, Average Delay). This confirmed the existence of a significant sim-to-real gap. PromptGAT was then applied, and its performance was compared. The results indicated that PromptGAT consistently and effectively reduced this performance gap across all four distinct real-world settings (V1-V4). The purple area in Figure 5 visually represented the significantly smaller gap achieved by PromptGAT compared to the orange area representing direct transfer.

#### 4.3. Comparison to Baseline Models

A quantitative comparison was conducted against two baselines: "Direct-Transfer" (RL policy trained in $E_{sim}$ and applied directly to $E_{real}$ without adaptation) and "Vanilla-GAT" (the standard Grounded Action Transformation without LLM integration). PromptGAT consistently outperformed both baselines across most metrics and all experimental settings (V1-V4).
- For metrics where smaller values for the gap ($\psi_{\Delta}$) are desired (Average Travel Time, Average Queue Length, Average Delay), PromptGAT generally achieved the lowest values.
- For metrics where larger values for the gap ($\psi_{\Delta}$) are desired (Throughput, Reward), PromptGAT typically yielded the highest values (or the smallest negative gaps, indicating less performance degradation).
For example, in setting V4 (snowy weather, the most severe condition), PromptGAT reduced the Average Travel Time gap to 199.06 (compared to 221.25 for Direct-Transfer and 207.47 for Vanilla-GAT), and improved the Reward gap to -74.11 (compared to -87.23 for Direct-Transfer and -75.57 for Vanilla-GAT). These results indicate PromptGAT's superior ability to transfer policies and maintain performance under diverse and challenging real-world conditions.

#### 4.4. Further Study and Correlation Analysis

A further investigation into the internal mechanisms of PromptGAT revealed its contribution to the forward model's accuracy. PromptGAT demonstrated a lower prediction error for the forward model compared to Vanilla-GAT (Figure 6, left), confirming its ability to more accurately approximate real-world system dynamics.
A correlation analysis was performed to establish the relationship between the improvement in forward model accuracy and the mitigation of the performance gap in $E_{real}$. The study found a strong positive correlation between the improvement in accuracy ($\Delta_{Accuracy\uparrow}$) and the improvement in mitigating the gap for various metrics (Average Travel Time, Throughput, and Waiting Queue Length). This means that a more accurate dynamics profiling by PromptGAT directly translated into a greater reduction of the sim-to-real performance gap. Statistical significance was reported for these correlations, with p-values ≤ 0.001, indicating a robust relationship (Figure 7). This finding substantiates the hypothesis that leveraging LLMs to profile realistic system dynamics improves the forward model, which in turn leads to more effectively grounded actions and ultimately better policy transferability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Traffic Signal Control (TSC) and the broader domain of sim-to-real transfer.

**Novelty and Methodological Contribution**: The paper introduces PromptGAT, which is identified as the first method to leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) for mitigating the sim-to-real performance gap in TSC. By integrating LLM inference capabilities into the Grounded Action Transformation (GAT) framework, PromptGAT addresses a critical limitation of traditional GAT: its reliance on extensive real-world data and its struggle with unobserved or rare conditions. The novel use of prompt-based dynamics modeling allows for the incorporation of implicit human knowledge about how environmental factors (like weather and road types) and traffic states influence vehicle dynamics, thereby enriching the forward model's understanding of the real world.

**Improved Real-World Deployability of TSC Policies**: The consistent reduction in the performance gap observed in experiments across diverse "real-world" scenarios (simulated heavy industry roads and various weather conditions) indicates that PromptGAT can enable RL-trained TSC policies to perform more robustly and effectively when deployed in actual urban environments. This has direct implications for enhancing transportation efficiency, reducing congestion, and improving urban mobility. The ability to better predict and adapt to dynamic changes, especially under challenging or infrequent conditions (e.g., snowy weather), is crucial for reliable traffic management systems.

**Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer Research**: Beyond TSC, this work offers a generalizable approach for incorporating external, human-like knowledge into dynamics modeling for sim-to-real problems. By demonstrating that LLMs can provide rational and realistic inferences for physical system dynamics, the research suggests a pathway to reduce the dependency on vast amounts of real-world data for learning accurate forward models. This could accelerate the development and deployment of RL solutions in other complex control tasks where real-world data collection is expensive or hazardous, such as robotics or autonomous driving.

**Foundation for Future Research**: The successful integration of LLMs opens new avenues for research at the intersection of natural language processing, artificial intelligence, and physical control systems. Future work could explore more sophisticated prompting strategies, fine-tuning LLMs for specific domain knowledge, or integrating other forms of human knowledge (e.g., expert rules, common sense reasoning) through LLMs to further enhance sim-to-real transfer capabilities in dynamic and unpredictable environments. The findings establish a hopeful precedent for leveraging the advanced inference abilities of LLMs to solve practical challenges in real-world deployment.
