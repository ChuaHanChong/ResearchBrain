# ST-VLM: Kinematic Instruction Tuning for Spatio-Temporal Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ST-VLM, a Vision-Language Model, is developed using a kinematic instruction tuning framework to enable precise spatio-temporal reasoning about moving objects in dynamic videos. The model, trained on the new STKit dataset and evaluated on STKit-Bench, demonstrates superior performance in quantifying object kinematics compared to existing VLMs, including proprietary models.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle to perform precise, quantitative kinematic analysis of moving objects in dynamic videos (e.g., calculating distance, speed, or direction).
- A significant data scarcity exists for large-scale, video-based instruction tuning datasets focused explicitly on kinematic understanding.
- VLMs lack the ability to integrate visual kinematic perception with linguistic knowledge, logical inference, and arithmetic for complex multi-step reasoning in dynamic contexts.

## Method

- A novel ==kinematic instruction tuning framework== is introduced, defining ==seven fundamental spatio-temporal tasks== for quantifying object motion.
- A new dataset, ==STKit==, is constructed from 3D-annotated videos and expanded using a scalable ==4D reconstruction-based pseudo-labeling pipeline== for unlabeled videos.
- ST-VLM is developed by fine-tuning LLaVA-OneVision on the blended STKit and other VLM datasets to endow it with specialized kinematic understanding, and ==STKit-Bench== is created for evaluation.

## Results

- ST-VLM achieved an average accuracy of **59.8%** on the STKit-Bench, significantly outperforming GPT-4V (**28.5%**) and other leading VLMs by more than **30 percentage points** in kinematic reasoning tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both 3D-annotated video data and pseudo-labeled data from the 4D reconstruction pipeline are essential, contributing to an average accuracy improvement from **27.9%** to **51.1%** and then to **59.8%**, respectively.
- The model also demonstrated improved performance on various general video understanding and physical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., PerceptionTest, MVBench), indicating a broader enhancement of video comprehension.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dedicated kinematic instruction tuning, particularly with precise 3D-annotated video data, significantly enhances a VLM's ability to perform quantitative spatio-temporal reasoning.
> - A scalable pseudo-labeling pipeline leveraging 4D reconstruction effectively addresses data scarcity by generating high-quality kinematic labels from unlabeled videos.
> - Acquiring robust kinematic understanding not only improves specialized tasks but also generalizes to enhance overall video comprehension and physical reasoning in VLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress, enabling AI systems to process and understand information from both visual (images, videos) and textual modalities. Early VLMs primarily focused on image understanding, demonstrating capabilities in tasks like image captioning, visual question answering (VQA), and object recognition. More recently, the advent of large language models (LLMs) has propelled VLMs to new heights, leading to models like GPT-4V, LLaVA, and Qwen-VL that exhibit impressive general-purpose reasoning over static images.

A subsequent wave of research has extended VLMs to video, aiming for "video understanding." These video-based VLMs (e.g., VideoLLaMA, InternVideo, LLaVA-Video) have shown promise in temporal understanding tasks such as action recognition, event localization, and answering questions about video content. Datasets like TVQA+, VidSTG, MVBench, and PerceptionTest have emerged to evaluate these capabilities.

However, a critical gap remains: the ability to perform robust *spatio-temporal reasoning*, especially concerning *kinematic elements* of moving objects within dynamic videos. While recent works like SpatialVLM and SpatialCoT have improved *spatial reasoning* in image-based VLMs by incorporating 3D information, these models are inherently limited to static scenes or frame-by-frame analysis and do not effectively capture the *temporal dynamics* of movement. Existing video VLMs, despite their advancements in general video understanding, often fall short when asked to quantify motion—tasks like calculating traveled distance, average speed, or precise movement direction. As demonstrated by the paper, even advanced models like GPT-4V struggle with these specific kinematic queries, often resorting to speculation or declaring the need for specialized software.

This paper directly addresses this significant limitation. It positions itself at the intersection of VLM development and spatio-temporal understanding, specifically targeting the nuanced comprehension of *kinematics*. By introducing a novel dataset (STKit) and benchmark (STKit-Bench) focused on kinematic instruction tuning, the authors aim to instill a deeper physical understanding of object motion into VLMs. This differentiates their work from prior efforts in general video VQA or spatial grounding by emphasizing quantitative and precise kinematic analysis rather than qualitative descriptions or broader semantic understanding. The focus on real-world scales and complex trajectories, alongside a method for scaling data generation, makes this work particularly relevant for high-stakes applications such as autonomous driving, sports analytics, and embodied AI, where accurate kinematic reasoning is paramount. In essence, ST-VLM seeks to evolve VLMs from merely "seeing" motion to truly "understanding" and quantifying it.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **endow Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with robust spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities, specifically focusing on the kinematic understanding of moving objects in dynamic videos**. This aims to move beyond generic video comprehension and enable VLMs to perform precise, quantitative analysis of motion.

The key motivations driving this research stem from several critical limitations of existing VLM paradigms:

1. **Inability to Quantify Kinematic Elements:** Despite significant advancements in VLMs, current models exhibit a profound struggle in analyzing fundamental kinematic elements of moving objects. This includes estimating traveled distance, calculating traveling speed, determining movement direction, or comparing these quantities between multiple objects. As highlighted in Figure 1, a state-of-the-art model like GPT-4V fails to provide accurate answers to such questions, either giving highly speculative responses or indicating a lack of capability. This deficiency severely limits their utility in applications requiring precise physical understanding.

2. **Lack of Kinematic-Specific Training Data:** The success of recent VLMs often hinges on large-scale, well-annotated datasets. While numerous datasets exist for general video understanding or image-based spatial reasoning, there is a distinct absence of large-scale, video-based instruction tuning datasets explicitly designed for kinematic understanding. This data scarcity is a primary bottleneck for training VLMs to acquire these specific spatio-temporal reasoning skills.

3. **Need for Multi-step Reasoning:** Real-world applications often demand complex, multi-step reasoning that integrates various forms of intelligence. For VLMs, this means combining visual perception (e.g., estimating object speed from video) with linguistic knowledge (e.g., knowing the distance between cities or the speed of an airplane), logical inference (e.g., time = distance/speed), and arithmetic computation. Without a foundational understanding of kinematics, VLMs cannot effectively engage in such integrated reasoning for dynamic scenarios.

4. **Critical for Real-World Applications:** Spatio-temporal reasoning, particularly kinematic analysis, is indispensable across a wide array of vital applications. These include:
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Predicting the movement of other vehicles and pedestrians, understanding road dynamics, and making safe navigation decisions.
 - **Sports Analytics:** Quantifying player movements, speeds, and trajectories for performance evaluation and tactical analysis.
 - **Augmented/Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** Creating interactive and physically consistent virtual environments.
 - **Embodied AI/Robotics:** Enabling robots to perceive and interact intelligently with dynamic environments, understanding how objects move and change.

The authors hypothesize that, similar to how large-scale 3D annotated data improved image-based spatial reasoning, a dedicated video dataset focusing on dynamic object motion annotated with precise kinematic information can significantly enhance the spatio-temporal reasoning abilities of VLMs. This motivation leads directly to the creation of STKit and STKit-Bench, and the development of ST-VLM, aiming to bridge the gap between general video understanding and accurate kinematic comprehension.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers around a novel kinematic instruction tuning framework designed to infuse VLMs with spatio-temporal reasoning abilities. The core components include the creation of a specialized dataset (STKit), an evaluation benchmark (STKit-Bench), a scalable pseudo-labeling pipeline, and the development of a fine-tuned VLM (ST-VLM).

#### 3.1. Kinematic Instructions and STKit Dataset Construction

The foundation of the approach is the definition of **seven fundamental kinematic tasks** categorized under "Single Object" and "Multiple Objects" to cover various aspects of spatio-temporal reasoning (Table 1). These tasks include:
- **Single Object:** Traveled Distance, Traveling Speed, Movement Direction, Direction Timestamp.
- **Multiple Objects:** Traveled Distance Comparison, Traveling Speed Comparison, Movement Direction Comparison.
These tasks require the model to infer both absolute and relative kinematic quantities.

To generate the training data (STKit), the authors combine two main strategies:

- **Spatio-Temporal Grounding of Dynamic Videos with 3D Annotations:**
 - They leverage existing datasets that provide precise 3D object coordinates in real-world scale for each timestamp. This includes autonomous driving datasets (Argoverse2, NuScenes) with LiDAR-based point clouds and sports videos (Ego-Exo4D) with SLAM/VIO-based estimated point clouds from AR devices.
 - For each object, its 3D center coordinates are used to construct trajectories by sampling at 0.5-second intervals.
 - **Traveled Distance and Speed:** Calculated as the cumulative sum of Euclidean distances between consecutive 3D object centers, and then divided by duration for speed.
 - **Movement Direction:** Defined relative to an object's initial movement direction (12 o'clock reference) and then converted into intuitive discretized clockwise directions (e.g., 3 o'clock, 9 o'clock) to be human-readable and suitable for LLM consumption. Due to the high complexity of 3D trajectories in sports, direction tasks are excluded from that domain.
 - Template-based prompts and questions are created for each task, along with visual prompts (bounding boxes) highlighting objects of interest.

- **Pseudo-labeling for Unlabeled Dynamic Videos:**
 - To address the scarcity of 3D-annotated videos and extend STKit to diverse domains, a novel **automatic pseudo-labeling pipeline based on 4D reconstruction** is proposed (Figure 4).
 - **Geometric Reconstruction Branch:** Utilizes MonST3R for 4D scene reconstruction (estimating depth, camera intrinsics/extrinsics) in dynamic videos. To overcome the scale ambiguity inherent in monocular reconstruction, Metric3Dv2 is integrated to obtain absolute metric depth at a real-world scale, canonicalizing the 4D scene.
 - **Semantic Understanding Branch:** Employs Grounded-SAM2, an open-vocabulary video semantic understanding model, to extract 2D bounding boxes, segmentation masks, and trajectories of moving objects (cars, humans). Confidence scores and bounding box sizes are used for filtering to ensure pseudo-label reliability.
 - **Spatio-temporal Grounding in Canonicalized 4D Scene:** The 2D segmentation masks are lifted into 3D point clouds within the canonicalized 4D reconstructed scene. Kinematic quantities (distance, speed, direction) are computed by tracking the barycenter of these 3D object coordinates across frames. Empirical filtering and smoothing strategies, validated against labeled datasets, are applied to improve the quality of pseudo-labels.
 - This pipeline enables scaling up STKit by generating 89K QA pairs from 15.5K unlabeled videos from BDD100K, LLaVA-Video, and MultiSports datasets, complementing the 27K QA pairs from 3D-annotated videos.

#### 3.2. STKit-Bench

A new benchmark, **STKit-Bench**, is introduced to evaluate VLMs' spatio-temporal reasoning abilities specifically for object kinematics. It comprises 1,400 QA pairs (200 for each of the seven tasks) from four diverse datasets: NuPlan, NuScenes, Argoverse2 (autonomous driving), and Ego-Exo4D (sports). Crucially, the benchmark ensures a balanced label distribution for each task to prevent biased evaluation. Evaluation metrics include Accuracy (with a tolerance for distance/speed), MAE, and IoU (for direction timestamp).

#### 3.3. Kinematic Instruction Tuning (ST-VLM Development)

- **Base Model:** LLaVA-OneVision 7B, a VLM capable of processing various visual inputs (single images, multiple images, videos), is chosen as the base architecture.
- **Fine-tuning:** ST-VLM is developed by fine-tuning LLaVA-OneVision on the constructed STKit dataset.
- **Data Blending Strategy:** To prevent overfitting to STKit and ensure generalization, the fine-tuning data is blended with:
 - A subset of general supervised fine-tuning (SFT) datasets (LLaVA-Video-178K).
 - OpenSpatialDataset, to enhance the model's foundational spatial reasoning ability, which is advantageous for spatio-temporal tasks.
- This blending strategy helps ST-VLM maintain performance on generic benchmarks while acquiring specialized kinematic understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence that ST-VLM significantly advances the state-of-the-art in spatio-temporal reasoning, particularly for kinematic understanding in dynamic videos.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance on STKit-Bench

- **Significant Outperformance:** ST-VLM dramatically surpasses all baseline models, including powerful proprietary models like GPT-4V and GPT-4o, as well as leading open-source video-based VLMs (e.g., VideoLLaMA3, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OneVision) and even autonomous driving-specific models (Dolphins, ELM) on the STKit-Bench.
- **Quantitative Improvements:** For instance, ST-VLM achieves an average accuracy of 59.8%, which is a 31.3% improvement over GPT-4V (28.5%) and a 32.4% improvement over LLaVA-OneVision (27.4%). In specific tasks, ST-VLM's accuracy is 49.5% for Traveled Distance (TD) compared to GPT-4V's 4.5%, and 42.0% for Traveling Speed (TS) compared to GPT-4V's 4.5%. This highlights the baselines' severe struggle with precise kinematic quantification.
- **Autonomous Driving Models' Limitations:** Dolphins and ELM, despite being trained on road scenes, show poor generalization to the specific kinematic instructions of STKit-Bench, achieving average accuracies of only 17.4% and 27.6%, respectively, further emphasizing the novelty of ST-VLM's kinematic understanding.

#### 4.2. Robustness Across Diverse Settings

- **Resistance to Enhanced Baselines:** When GPT-4V is augmented with contextual support (few-shot examples and additional geometric inputs like camera extrinsics and depth maps), its performance improves, but it still remains significantly inferior to ST-VLM. This indicates that ST-VLM's superior performance is due to its intrinsic learned kinematic understanding rather than merely better prompt engineering or explicit external data provision.
- **Robustness to Question Variations:** ST-VLM maintains robust performance even when questions in STKit-Bench are paraphrased using GPT-4. This demonstrates that the model has acquired a generalized understanding of kinematics, rather than overfitting to specific question templates or phrasing.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of STKit Data Composition

- **Impact of Labeled Data:** Ablation studies confirm the critical role of the STKit dataset. Incorporating data from 3D-annotated videos alone significantly boosts performance (from a baseline average accuracy of 27.9% to 51.1%).
- **Value of Pseudo-labeled Data:** The pseudo-labeling pipeline, leveraging 4D reconstruction, further enhances performance by an additional 8.7% (reaching 59.8% average accuracy). This validates the effectiveness of the proposed pipeline in mitigating data scarcity and improving model capabilities.

#### 4.4. Generalized Spatio-Temporal Understanding

- **Improved General Video Understanding:** ST-VLM, despite being fine-tuned for kinematics, outperforms its base model, LLaVA-OneVision, and even larger models (e.g., LLaVA-N-Video-32B) on general video understanding benchmarks (e.g., PerceptionTest, MVBench, VideoMME, MLVU, NExT-QA). This suggests that kinematic instruction tuning imparts a more fundamental and transferable understanding of video dynamics.
- **Enhanced Physical Reasoning:** A notable 7.4% improvement is observed on PerceptionTest, a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate the comprehension of fundamental physical properties in videos.
- **Stronger Spatio-Temporal Grounding:** ST-VLM shows improved performance across various other spatio-temporal reasoning tasks, including temporal grounding (ActivityNet, Charades-STA) and spatial-aware VideoQA (DramaQA, TVQA+), demonstrating its generalized capability beyond just kinematic quantities.

#### 4.5. Emerging Multi-step Reasoning Capabilities

- **Integration of Knowledge:** Qualitatively, ST-VLM demonstrates an emergent capability to perform complex multi-step reasoning. It can effectively integrate its newly acquired kinematic understanding from videos with the LLM's existing abilities, such as knowledge retrieval (e.g., speed of a normal airplane), logical reasoning, and arithmetic computation.
- **Practical Examples:** The paper provides examples where ST-VLM correctly answers complex questions like "How much slower is the object's speed than a normal airplane?" or identifying which team a faster player belongs to, tasks that LLaVA-OneVision struggles with. This showcases ST-VLM's ability to combine object kinematics with commonsense knowledge and calculations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ST-VLM and its accompanying resources, STKit and STKit-Bench, represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models and spatio-temporal reasoning. The work holds substantial significance and potential impact across several dimensions:

#### 5.1. Bridging a Critical Gap in VLM Capabilities

The most immediate significance is ST-VLM's ability to fill a crucial void in VLM capabilities. By providing models with explicit kinematic understanding, the research moves VLMs beyond descriptive or associative video comprehension towards precise, quantitative, and physically grounded reasoning about object motion. This shifts the paradigm from merely recognizing events to truly understanding the "how" and "how much" of dynamic interactions.

#### 5.2. Novel and Essential Resources for Research

The introduction of **STKit** as a kinematic instruction tuning dataset and **STKit-Bench** as a dedicated benchmark is a major contribution. These resources provide the necessary foundation for future research in spatio-temporal reasoning, enabling standardized development and evaluation of models tailored for understanding kinematics. The comprehensive nature of STKit, covering various kinematic tasks and dynamic scenarios, ensures a holistic approach to training.

#### 5.3. Scalable Data Generation for 3D/4D Understanding

The proposed pseudo-labeling pipeline, leveraging 4D reconstruction, addresses a fundamental challenge in 3D/4D vision: the scarcity of expertly annotated data. By demonstrating an effective method to generate high-quality kinematic labels from unlabeled videos, the research offers a practical and scalable solution for expanding training datasets. This approach can significantly accelerate progress in areas requiring detailed 3D/4D understanding by reducing reliance on costly and labor-intensive manual annotation.

#### 5.4. Enhanced AI for Real-World Applications

The improved spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities of ST-VLM have profound implications for numerous real-world applications:

- **Autonomous Driving:** More accurate perception of other road users' speed, distance, and direction will lead to safer and more reliable self-driving systems, capable of making better predictions and decisions in complex traffic scenarios.
- **Sports Analytics:** ST-VLM can revolutionize sports analysis by providing granular data on player movements, speeds, and trajectories, facilitating advanced tactical insights, performance optimization, and objective commentary.
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Robots equipped with ST-VLM's kinematic understanding can better navigate dynamic environments, predict object movements, and interact more intelligently with the physical world, leading to more robust and versatile robotic systems.
- **Augmented/Virtual Reality:** A deeper understanding of real-world kinematics can enhance the realism and interactivity of AR/VR applications, allowing virtual objects to behave more consistently with physical laws and human movements.

#### 5.5. Facilitating Complex Multi-step Reasoning

The demonstration of ST-VLM's emergent ability to integrate kinematic understanding with high-level cognitive processes (knowledge retrieval, logical reasoning, arithmetic) is particularly significant. It showcases a path towards more intelligent AI systems that can tackle open-ended, multi-faceted problems, moving beyond isolated task performance to comprehensive reasoning. This capability is crucial for developing truly general-purpose AI assistants.

#### 5.6. Advancing Foundational Video Understanding

The fact that ST-VLM generalizes well to other generic video understanding benchmarks (even outperforming larger models in some cases) suggests that kinematic instruction tuning contributes to a more fundamental and robust comprehension of video content. This indicates that understanding "how things move" is not merely a specialized skill but a core component of overall video intelligence, potentially influencing future architectural designs and training strategies for VLMs.

In conclusion, ST-VLM marks a crucial step forward in advancing Vision-Language Models. By systematically addressing the challenge of kinematic understanding in dynamic videos through novel data, benchmarks, and a scalable methodology, this research paves the way for more capable, intelligent, and real-world applicable AI systems.
