# Safe Continual Reinforcement Learning in Non-stationary Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Vanderbilt University investigated safe continual reinforcement learning by formally defining the problem and introducing robotic benchmarks tailored for non-stationary, safety-critical scenarios. The study revealed a fundamental tension between maintaining safety constraints and preventing catastrophic forgetting, demonstrating that existing methods generally struggle to achieve both simultaneously.

## Problem

- Most conventional reinforcement learning methods assume stationary environments, which fails in real-world systems with evolving dynamics or operating conditions.
- Safety-critical applications require adherence to strict safety constraints not just during deployment but throughout the entire learning process, a challenge compounded by non-stationarity.
- Continual learning agents often experience catastrophic forgetting, where new knowledge overwrites or degrades performance and safety protocols from previously learned tasks.

## Method

- The problem of safe continual reinforcement learning was formally defined as a ==Non-Stationary Constrained Markov Decision Process (NSCMDP)==.
- Three robotic benchmark environments (Damaged HalfCheetah Velocity, Damaged Ant Velocity, **Safe Continual World**) were introduced or modified to evaluate safe continual adaptation challenges.
- Two ==regularization-based== algorithms, **Safe EWC** (==Safe Elastic Weight Consolidation==) and **CF-EWC** (==Cost-Fisher Elastic Weight Consolidation==), were proposed as initial solutions to mitigate identified trade-offs.

## Results

- **Safe EWC** consistently achieved low total costs and competitive normalized forgetting across benchmarks, often demonstrating the most favorable balance between safety and continual learning, though sometimes with reduced task reward or success rate.
- Standard RL algorithms (e.g., **PPO**) attained high rewards but incurred the highest safety costs, while Safe RL methods (e.g., **CPO**) kept costs low but often exhibited higher normalized forgetting.
- Catastrophic forgetting of safety adherence was observed in some Safe RL algorithms, with significant spikes in safety violations upon revisiting tasks, even when reward forgetting was mitigated.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fundamental tension exists between simultaneously satisfying safety constraints and preventing catastrophic forgetting in dynamic, non-stationary environments.
> - Existing standalone Safe Reinforcement Learning or Continual Reinforcement Learning methods are generally inadequate for addressing the combined demands of safe continual learning.
> - Remembering task rewards does not automatically prevent the catastrophic forgetting of critical safety constraints when learning across changing tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated efficacy in generating controllers for complex systems in the absence of precise physical models, with successful applications in domains such as robotic control, autonomous driving, and drone operation. However, the deployment of RL agents in real-world environments presents two significant challenges: managing non-stationarity and ensuring safety constraints.

Most conventional control-oriented RL methods assume stationary environments, where system dynamics and operating conditions remain constant. This assumption frequently breaks down in real-world scenarios, where unforeseen changes can occur over time. The field of **Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL)** has emerged to address non-stationarity, focusing on how agents can learn sequentially across changing tasks or environments while preventing catastrophic forgetting—the loss of previously acquired knowledge when learning new information.

Concurrently, the field of **Safe Reinforcement Learning (Safe RL)** concentrates on developing methods to ensure that RL agents satisfy specified safety constraints throughout both their learning and execution phases. This is crucial for physical systems where transient violations during adaptation are unacceptable. Safe RL employs various techniques, including constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs), Lyapunov functions, control barrier functions, and model predictive control.

Traditional **Adaptive Control** methods also address control in non-stationary dynamics by adjusting controller parameters online. While effective for systems with known model structures or linear parameterizations of non-stationarity, they often require relearning previously seen dynamics without a system model. Model-Free Adaptive Control approaches mitigate the need for precise models but typically lack mechanisms for retaining knowledge across revisited dynamics, a gap CRL aims to fill.

Despite substantial advancements in both CRL and Safe RL independently, their intersection—**Safe Continual Reinforcement Learning**—remains comparatively underexplored. Existing work in this specific area is limited, with studies often focusing on simplified tasks or lacking comprehensive evaluation metrics for both safety and continual learning performance. This research aims to systematically investigate this gap, providing benchmarks and evaluating the performance of various RL approaches when faced with simultaneous requirements for continual adaptation and safety adherence in non-stationary environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate safe continual reinforcement learning (RL) by addressing the challenges of developing RL algorithms that can adapt over a system's lifetime while consistently preserving safety, even in environments with unexpectedly changing dynamics.

The motivation for this work stems from critical limitations of current RL approaches when applied to real-world, long-duration deployments:
1. **Non-stationary Environments:** Many real-world systems operate in environments where dynamics, operating conditions, or even system parameters (e.g., due to wear and tear, damage) are not constant but evolve over time. Standard RL methods, which often assume stationarity, are ill-equipped to handle such changes without significant performance degradation. An agent must rapidly adapt to new conditions without forgetting how to operate effectively in previously encountered states.
2. **Safety-Critical Operations:** In applications like robotic control, autonomous vehicles, or industrial processes, safety is paramount. Controllers must satisfy strict safety constraints not just during deployment, but throughout the entire learning process, as transient violations can lead to catastrophic failures or damage. Existing Safe RL methods typically assume fixed environments, which does not account for the additional complexity introduced by non-stationarity.
3. **Catastrophic Forgetting:** When an agent continually learns in a sequence of tasks or environments, it often suffers from "catastrophic forgetting," where newly acquired knowledge overwrites or degrades performance on previously learned tasks. In a safety-critical context, this could mean an agent forgets crucial safety protocols after adapting to new environmental conditions.
4. **Lack of Integrated Solutions:** While Continual RL (CRL) addresses non-stationarity and Safe RL addresses safety, their combined requirements pose a unique challenge. It is unclear whether a CRL agent will remember safety constraints after adaptation, or if a Safe RL method will itself succumb to catastrophic forgetting under non-stationarity. The intersection of these two fields lacks systematic study and dedicated algorithms.

To address these motivations, the research sets forth several specific objectives:
- **Formalize the Problem:** To provide a clear understanding, the paper formally defines Safe Continual Reinforcement Learning as a Non-Stationary Constrained Markov Decision Process (NSCMDP), integrating concepts from Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs) and Non-Stationary Markov Decision Processes (NSMDPs).
- **Develop Benchmark Environments:** To enable systematic investigation and comparison, the study introduces three benchmark environments that capture critical aspects of safety-critical continual adaptation in robotics.
- **Evaluate Existing Approaches:** The research evaluates representative algorithms from Safe RL, Continual RL, and their combinations to characterize their strengths and weaknesses in the context of safe continual learning.
- **Propose Candidate Algorithms:** Two new regularization-based strategies, Safe Elastic Weight Consolidation (Safe EWC) and Cost-Fisher Elastic Weight Consolidation (CF-EWC), are developed and assessed as initial solutions to mitigate the identified trade-offs.
- **Identify Open Challenges:** Through empirical analysis, the study aims to highlight the fundamental tension between maintaining safety constraints and preventing catastrophic forgetting, thereby identifying key open challenges and outlining future research directions for developing resilient learning-based controllers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology adopted in this research involves defining the problem of Safe Continual Reinforcement Learning, developing specific benchmark environments, proposing and evaluating novel algorithms, and benchmarking existing RL, Safe RL, and Continual RL methods against these new environments.

#### 3.1. Formal Problem Definition

The problem is formally defined as a Non-Stationary Constrained Markov Decision Process (NSCMDP). This integrates the concepts of a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) with a Non-Stationary Markov Decision Process (NSMDP). An NSCMDP is defined by the tuple $\langle S, A, \{p^{(\tau)}\}_{\tau \in T}, \{r^{(\tau)}\}_{\tau \in T}, \{C^{(\tau)}\}_{\tau \in T}, \{d^{(\tau)}\}_{\tau \in T} \rangle$, where $S$ is the state space, $A$ is the action space, $\{p^{(\tau)}\}_{\tau \in T}$ are task-dependent transition probabilities, $\{r^{(\tau)}\}_{\tau \in T}$ are task-dependent reward functions, $\{C^{(\tau)}\}_{\tau \in T}$ are task-dependent cost functions, and $\{d^{(\tau)}\}_{\tau \in T}$ are task-dependent cost limits. The objective is to find a policy that maximizes reward for the current task ($\tau$) while satisfying both forgetting constraints for previous tasks ($\kappa \in T_{\prec}$) and safety constraints for both previous and current tasks.

#### 3.2. Assumptions

The study operates under four key assumptions:
- **Discrete, Dramatic Task Changes:** Environmental changes are assumed to occur as distinct, abrupt tasks rather than gradual shifts.
- **Known Task Boundaries:** The agent is provided with information about when task changes occur.
- **Soft Safety Constraints:** Safety is enforced in expectation or through penalties, allowing for a trade-off between safety and adaptation, rather than strict, inviolable requirements.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting as Primary Challenge:** The focus for continual learning is on preventing performance degradation on earlier tasks after learning new ones.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Environments

Three robotic benchmark environments are introduced or modified to evaluate safe continual RL methods:
- **Damaged HalfCheetah Velocity:** An extension of the HalfCheetah Velocity safe RL benchmark. The agent aims to run below a maximum velocity. Non-stationarity is introduced by simulating physical damage (removing the front or back leg) and repair, cycling through tasks like {nominal, back, nominal, front, back, nominal, front, nominal} every 1 million timesteps. The cost is an indicator function for exceeding the velocity constraint (3.2096 m/s).
- **Damaged Ant Velocity:** Similar to the HalfCheetah, this modifies the Ant Velocity safe RL benchmark. The ant robot experiences removal and repair of its front or back pairs of legs. The task sequence and change frequency are identical to HalfCheetah. The cost is an indicator for exceeding a velocity constraint (2.6222 m/s). Both Damaged HalfCheetah and Ant present a direct trade-off between maximizing reward (speed) and satisfying the velocity safety constraint.
- **Safe Continual World:** This environment builds on Continual World, which itself extends Meta World. A robotic arm performs a sequence of everyday tasks (e.g., faucet-close, button-push, drawer-close) in a non-stationary setting. Safety is introduced by placing a coffee mug between the robot arm and the task goal, with a penalty based on the mug's tilt angle (in degrees) if it's tipped. Task changes occur every 2 million timesteps, and the sequence {faucet-close, button-push, drawer-close} is repeated once.

#### 3.4. Proposed Safe Continual Reinforcement Learning Algorithms

The proposed algorithms extend the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) framework, chosen for its simplicity and common use in continual learning.
- **Safe Elastic Weight Consolidation (Safe EWC):** This algorithm modifies the reward function in PPO+EWC by subtracting a weighted cost term: $r_{\text{Safe EWC}}(s, a) = r(s, a) - \beta C(s, a)$, where $\beta$ is a hyperparameter and $C(s,a)$ is the current task cost. This implicitly encourages safety by penalizing violations directly within the reward signal, thereby influencing policy optimization.
- **Cost-Fisher Elastic Weight Consolidation (CF-EWC):** This approach modifies the computation of the Fisher information matrix in EWC. Instead of simply measuring parameter importance for past reward maximization, it reweights the importance inversely to the cost incurred. The Fisher information $F_i$ is approximated as $\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \frac{1}{(c_n + 1)} \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_i} \log p(y_n|x_n; \theta^*_A)\right)^2$. The intuition is to allow greater plasticity for parameters associated with unsafe behaviors while preserving those related to safe, high-reward actions.

#### 3.5. Baseline Algorithms

Eight algorithms are benchmarked, covering standard RL, Safe RL, Continual RL, and the proposed Safe Continual RL methods. All selected methods are trust-region, on-policy policy-gradient algorithms (PPO or TRPO) to ensure a consistent underlying RL mechanism:
- **RL Algorithms:** PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization).
- **Safe RL Algorithms:** CPO (Constrained Policy Optimization), PPO-Lag (Lagrangian PPO), CPPO-PID (Constraint-Controlled PPO with PID).
- **Continual RL Algorithms:** PPO+EWC (PPO with Elastic Weight Consolidation), Experience Replay (inspired by CLEAR, mixes past and present experiences).
- **Safe Continual RL Algorithms (Proposed):** Safe EWC, CF-EWC.

#### 3.6. Hyperparameter Optimization

Hyperparameters for each method are optimized separately for each benchmark environment using the Tree-Structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) algorithm in Optuna. Proxy tasks are used for tuning:
- PPO: Tuned to maximize return on the nominal first task.
- Safe RL: Tuned to maximize return while accounting for costs on nominal environments.
- Continual RL: Tuned to maximize return on the nominal dynamics and the first new task, measuring forgetting on nominal dynamics and performance on the new task.

#### 3.7. Evaluation Metrics

Performance is assessed using five metrics:
- **Final Task Reward:** Average undiscounted cumulative reward over the final 20 episodes of each task.
- **Total Cost:** Average total cost per task.
- **Forgetting:** The difference in undiscounted cumulative reward when revisiting a task compared to its last encounter. A negative value indicates positive forward transfer.
- **Normalized Forgetting:** Mean forgetting normalized by the magnitude of the reward range.
- **Average Success Rate:** Running average task completion rate for Safe Continual World tasks (used instead of reward for this environment).

#### 3.8. Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted using five random seeds per algorithm. Training durations range from 4-6 hours for Damaged HalfCheetah Velocity to 12-14 hours for Safe Continual World and Damaged Ant Velocity on an AMD Ryzen Threadripper 3960X 24-Core Processor with 256 GB RAM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical results reveal a fundamental tension between the objectives of maintaining safety constraints and preventing catastrophic forgetting under non-stationary dynamics. Existing methods generally struggle to achieve both objectives simultaneously.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Across Environments

- **Damaged HalfCheetah Velocity (Table 1):**
 - PPO achieved the highest reward but also the highest total cost, indicating it prioritized reward over safety.
 - Safe RL methods (CPO, PPO-Lag, CPPO-PID) reduced costs significantly compared to PPO but exhibited higher normalized forgetting, demonstrating limited continual learning capabilities.
 - Continual RL methods (PPO+EWC, Experience Replay) showed reduced normalized forgetting, but incurred high total costs, compromising safety. Experience Replay also suffered from low final reward and sample inefficiency.
 - **Safe EWC** achieved the second-lowest normalized forgetting and the lowest total cost, though with a reduced reward, suggesting a favorable trade-off between safety and continual learning.
 - CF-EWC did not effectively prevent safety violations, performing similarly to non-safe methods in terms of cost.
- **Damaged Ant Velocity (Table 2):**
 - Trends mirrored HalfCheetah: PPO+EWC and CF-EWC achieved the highest rewards but also the highest costs. Safe RL methods kept costs low but sometimes at the expense of higher forgetting (CPO).
 - Many methods exhibited positive forward transfer (low or negative forgetting), suggesting that learning on the intact ant could benefit learning on damaged variants.
 - **Safe EWC** provided the most favorable trade-off, achieving the lowest total costs while maintaining a competitive reward, though with slightly higher normalized forgetting than some (PPO, PPO+EWC, CF-EWC).
 - Experience Replay could not be reliably trained due to out-of-memory errors with the larger state space.
- **Safe Continual World (Table 3):**
 - PPO, PPO+EWC, and CF-EWC achieved the highest average success rates but also the highest total costs, as they do not explicitly penalize safety violations. PPO+EWC had the lowest forgetting among them.
 - Safe RL methods (CPO, PPO-Lag, CPPO-PID) and Safe EWC generally achieved substantially lower success rates, reflecting a stronger emphasis on safety.
 - CPPO-PID had the lowest total cost but near-zero task success.
 - **Safe EWC** achieved a low total cost and relatively low normalized forgetting but at the expense of task success, highlighting the challenging trade-off in complex environments.

#### 4.2. Visualizing the Tradeoff Between Metrics (Figure 3)

The visualizations confirmed the trade-offs. Safe EWC generally positioned favorably, balancing cost and forgetting, while its reward or success rate was often constrained by its safety adherence. CF-EWC consistently aligned with PPO and PPO+EWC, improving reward and forgetting but not safety.

#### 4.3. Behavior During Lifelong Learning (Figure 4)

- **Reward Dynamics:** All methods experienced reward drops after task changes, indicating an adaptation period. Safe EWC and CPPO-PID showed better knowledge retention than CPO in HalfCheetah, leading to comparable reward trajectories late in training. In Damaged Ant, Safe EWC often achieved higher reward on task revisits (forward transfer), while CPO converged faster but forgot more.
- **Cost Dynamics:** Safe EWC consistently maintained a lower average cost throughout training, particularly at task onset, compared to PPO+EWC and CPO. Safety violations generally decreased for Safe EWC over training. All methods exhibited increased safety violations immediately after task changes, even when safety constraints remained consistent.

#### 4.4. Remembering Safety Constraints (Figure 5)

The study found that simply avoiding catastrophic forgetting of rewards does not automatically prevent forgetting of safety constraints. CPO, a safe RL algorithm, showed significant spikes in safety violations upon task shifts in the Safe Continual World, even when revisiting tasks. This suggests catastrophic forgetting of safety adherence when the reward signal changes. Safe EWC, in contrast, maintained safety constraints but with a much lower average task success rate, indicating a prioritization of safety over performance in this complex environment.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Tables 4-5)

- **Safe EWC Penalty Location:** Moving the cost penalty from reward shaping to direct loss regularization in Safe EWC led to increased safety violations. This suggests that reward shaping is more effective for embedding safety into the optimization objective, likely due to the relative strength of the cost term compared to PPO's reward maximization and EWC's parameter-change penalty.
- **Component Ablation:** Adding EWC to PPO generally reduced catastrophic forgetting. CF-EWC, by incorporating the Cost-Fisher modification, further reduced forgetting in most cases and improved reward, but did not reliably improve safety. This indicates that merely allowing "unsafe" parameters to be forgotten is insufficient to ensure future safe behavior. Safe EWC, by adding a cost-based reward penalty to PPO+EWC, substantially improved safety satisfaction while maintaining low forgetting, confirming its effectiveness as a safe continual RL approach. Its performance-cost trade-off in Safe Continual World underscores the difficulty of balancing objectives in complex settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the development and deployment of intelligent autonomous systems:

**Formalization and Clarification of a Critical Problem:** By formally defining Safe Continual Reinforcement Learning as a Non-Stationary Constrained Markov Decision Process (NSCMDP), the paper provides a clear conceptual framework for an under-explored and critical research area. This formalization integrates non-stationarity and safety, highlighting their intertwined nature in real-world applications and establishing a foundation for future theoretical and algorithmic development.

**Development of Novel Benchmarks:** The introduction of three new or modified robotic benchmark environments (Damaged HalfCheetah Velocity, Damaged Ant Velocity, and Safe Continual World) is a key contribution. These benchmarks are designed to capture safety-critical continual adaptation challenges, offering standardized and discriminative platforms for evaluating and comparing future Safe Continual RL algorithms. Their complexity and realism are intended to push the boundaries of current methods and accelerate progress in the field.

**Systematic Empirical Characterization:** The comprehensive empirical evaluation of a range of standard RL, Safe RL, Continual RL, and proposed Safe Continual RL methods provides crucial insights. The findings clearly illustrate the inherent tension between achieving high task performance, maintaining safety, and preventing catastrophic forgetting. This characterization demonstrates that existing methods are generally inadequate for addressing the combined demands of safe continual learning, underscoring the necessity for specialized solutions.

**Introduction of Initial Candidate Solutions:** The proposed Safe EWC and Cost-Fisher EWC algorithms serve as important initial steps toward addressing the challenges. Safe EWC demonstrates a competitive balance between mitigating forgetting and ensuring safety, even if it sometimes entails a reduction in task performance due to its reward shaping mechanism. CF-EWC, while not consistently improving safety, provides valuable insights into how modifying Fisher information for cost-awareness can influence learning dynamics and plasticity, informing future designs. These methods offer baselines and directions for subsequent algorithmic research.

**Identification of Key Open Challenges and Future Research Directions:** Beyond presenting solutions, the paper effectively outlines critical open questions and research avenues. These include the need for:
- **Explicit Retention of Safety Constraints:** Developing mechanisms that explicitly preserve safety knowledge across task changes, rather than relying on implicit learning from reward signals.
- **Resolution of Objective Tensions:** Exploring methods to better balance task performance, continual learning, and safety, potentially through direct optimization of multi-objective formulations or integration of diverse techniques.
- **Hard Safety Constraints:** Addressing scenarios requiring strict, inviolable safety guarantees, which necessitates fundamentally different approaches than soft constraints.
- **Imperfect Task Detection:** Investigating the impact of ambiguous or undetected task changes on safe continual learning, reflecting real-world complexities.
- **Appropriate Benchmarks:** Continued development of diverse benchmarks that offer a nuanced spectrum of difficulty and realism.

**High Relevance to Real-World Autonomous Systems:** The problem addressed is central to the reliable deployment of autonomous systems in dynamic, uncertain, and safety-critical real-world environments. The insights and methods developed contribute directly to creating more robust, resilient, and trustworthy learning-based controllers for applications such as autonomous robotics, space exploration, and critical infrastructure management, where human intervention may be limited or impossible.
