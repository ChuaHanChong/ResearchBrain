# Safe Continual Reinforcement Learning in Non-stationary Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Vanderbilt University investigated safe continual reinforcement learning by formally defining the problem and introducing robotic benchmarks tailored for non-stationary, safety-critical scenarios. The study revealed a fundamental tension between maintaining safety constraints and preventing catastrophic forgetting, demonstrating that existing methods generally struggle to achieve both simultaneously.

## Problem

- Most conventional reinforcement learning methods assume stationary environments, which fails in real-world systems with evolving dynamics or operating conditions.
- Safety-critical applications require adherence to strict safety constraints not just during deployment but throughout the entire learning process, a challenge compounded by non-stationarity.
- Continual learning agents often experience catastrophic forgetting, where new knowledge overwrites or degrades performance and safety protocols from previously learned tasks.

## Method

- The problem of safe continual reinforcement learning was formally defined as a ==Non-Stationary Constrained Markov Decision Process (NSCMDP)==.
- Three robotic benchmark environments (Damaged HalfCheetah Velocity, Damaged Ant Velocity, **Safe Continual World**) were introduced or modified to evaluate safe continual adaptation challenges.
- Two ==regularization-based== algorithms, **Safe EWC** (==Safe Elastic Weight Consolidation==) and **CF-EWC** (==Cost-Fisher Elastic Weight Consolidation==), were proposed as initial solutions to mitigate identified trade-offs.

## Results

- **Safe EWC** consistently achieved low total costs and competitive normalized forgetting across benchmarks, often demonstrating the most favorable balance between safety and continual learning, though sometimes with reduced task reward or success rate.
- Standard RL algorithms (e.g., **PPO**) attained high rewards but incurred the highest safety costs, while Safe RL methods (e.g., **CPO**) kept costs low but often exhibited higher normalized forgetting.
- Catastrophic forgetting of safety adherence was observed in some Safe RL algorithms, with significant spikes in safety violations upon revisiting tasks, even when reward forgetting was mitigated.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fundamental tension exists between simultaneously satisfying safety constraints and preventing catastrophic forgetting in dynamic, non-stationary environments.
> - Existing standalone Safe Reinforcement Learning or Continual Reinforcement Learning methods are generally inadequate for addressing the combined demands of safe continual learning.
> - Remembering task rewards does not automatically prevent the catastrophic forgetting of critical safety constraints when learning across changing tasks.
