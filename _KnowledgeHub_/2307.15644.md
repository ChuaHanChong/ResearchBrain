# Scaling Data Generation in Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A scalable paradigm for generating large-scale, high-quality training data for Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is proposed, utilizing 1200+ photo-realistic 3D environments, robust navigation graph construction, and a Co-Modulated GAN for image recovery. This approach generates 4.9 million instruction-trajectory pairs, improving R2R test-unseen success rate to 80% and significantly reducing the generalization gap to less than 1% across various VLN benchmarks.

## Problem

- Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) suffers from a scarcity of diverse, high-quality training data, which limits agents' ability to generalize to unseen environments.
- Existing VLN datasets are relatively small, and prior automated data generation methods often struggled with issues like poor navigation graph quality, low visual fidelity, or reliance on non-public models.
- Collecting human demonstrations for VLN is inherently expensive and time-consuming, hindering the scalability of data acquisition.

## Method

- **ScaleVLN** leverages 1200+ photo-realistic 3D environments from HM3D and Gibson to generate 4.9 million instruction-trajectory pairs, vastly increasing data scale and diversity.
- A novel heuristic for ==navigation graph construction== ensures dense, fully traversable, and collision-free graphs, improving the quality of sampled trajectories.
- A ==Co-Modulated GAN (Co-Mod GAN)== is trained to recover photo-realistic images from faulty 3D renders, addressing visual fidelity issues in source environments and enhancing data quality.

## Results

- Achieved an R2R test-unseen single-run success rate of **80%** with CLIP ViT-H/14, an **8%** absolute improvement over previous state-of-the-art methods.
- Reduced the generalization gap between seen and unseen R2R environments to **less than 1%**, compared to an **8%** gap in prior best methods.
- Established new state-of-the-art performance across various VLN benchmarks, including REVERIE, CVDN (improving Goal Progress by **25.26%** relative), and R2R-CE (boosting continuous environment SR by **5%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, fully traversable navigation graphs are crucial for VLN agents, with noisy graphs or obstacles significantly degrading performance and generalization.
> - Generating data from a greater number of diverse environments has a stronger positive impact on agent generalization than merely increasing sample quantity within a limited set of environments.
> - Recovering photo-realistic images from faulty 3D renders using a Co-Mod GAN substantially boosts agent performance, particularly in environments with lower initial visual quality, while low-quality data can even be detrimental.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a challenging task in embodied AI where an agent must navigate a realistic environment following natural language instructions. The field demands robust understanding of instructions, accurate visual perception of the environment, and effective interaction with the physical space. A persistent bottleneck in VLN research has been the **scarcity of diverse, high-quality training data**. Existing datasets, like R2R, are relatively small and often lack the variability needed for agents to generalize to unseen environments.

Prior research has attempted to address this data scarcity in several ways:
- **Observation Augmentation:** Techniques like augmenting an agent's visual observations (e.g., EnvEdit) or employing large vision-linguistic models pre-trained on vast image-text data from the web (e.g., AirBERT, VLN-BERT, LM-Nav). While these improve generalization, they often do not provide in-domain visual-language data or physical interaction experience.
- **Human Data Collection:** Efforts to collect more human demonstrations have been crucial but are inherently expensive and time-consuming, limiting scalability.
- **Synthetic Environment Generation:** Creating new simulated environments (e.g., ProcTHOR) offers diversity but still requires subsequent annotation or instruction generation.
- **Automated Data Generation (Synthetic Instructions):** Recent large-scale efforts like AutoVLN and MARVAL have aimed to automatically generate instruction-trajectory pairs from existing 3D environments. These works demonstrated the promise of scaling up data, but often faced limitations related to graph quality, visual fidelity, or reliance on non-public models. For example, MARVAL used a non-public language model (Marky) and visual encoder (MURAL), as well as a GAN for novel viewpoint synthesis, limiting its reproducibility.

This paper, "Scaling Data Generation in Vision-and-Language Navigation" (ScaleVLN), directly builds upon and significantly advances this last category by proposing a **fully reproducible, effective, and automated paradigm for large-scale data generation.** It distinguishes itself by:
1. **Leveraging more environments:** Utilizing 1200+ photo-realistic environments from HM3D and Gibson, which are substantially larger and more diverse than the Matterport3D scenes used by many prior works.
2. **Focusing on data quality:** Explicitly addressing issues like navigation graph quality (dense, fully traversable, collision-free) and visual fidelity (recovering faulty rendered images using a Co-Modulated GAN), which were identified as weaknesses in previous synthetic data generation pipelines (e.g., AutoVLN's graphs with obstacles, or low-quality Gibson renders).
3. **Reproducibility:** Emphasizing the use of fully accessible resources and a straightforward execution pipeline, contrasting with methods that rely on proprietary components.
4. **Comprehensive analysis:** Beyond just generating data, the paper rigorously investigates the influence of each component in the data generation and training pipeline, providing valuable guidelines for future research.

By tackling these challenges, ScaleVLN aims to push the boundaries of VLN agent performance, particularly in terms of generalization to unseen environments, and significantly reduce the gap between agent and human performance. It aligns with the broader trend in AI of "data-centric AI," where improving the quantity and quality of data rather than just model architecture leads to significant breakthroughs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations of this research are:

1. **Overcome Data Scarcity in VLN:** The primary motivation stems from the recognition that current VLN datasets are limited in size and diversity, hindering the training of generalizable agents. The authors aim to tackle this "common data scarcity issue" head-on.
2. **Propose an Effective and Scalable Data Generation Paradigm:** The central objective is to develop a method, termed "ScaleVLN," that can automatically synthesize a vast quantity of high-quality instruction-trajectory pairs for learning VLN. This paradigm should be efficient and applicable to diverse environments.
3. **Utilize a Broader Range of Environments:** Specifically, the goal is to leverage thousands of photo-realistic environments from datasets like HM3D and Gibson, which offer significantly more navigable space and visual diversity than traditionally used datasets like Matterport3D.
4. **Improve Data Quality through Robust Pipeline Components:** The authors motivate the need to address specific data quality issues, such as constructing highly traversable navigation graphs that avoid obstacles and recovering photo-realistic images from potentially faulty 3D renders. This ensures the synthetic data is genuinely useful rather than noisy.
5. **Investigate the Influence of Pipeline Components:** A crucial objective is to quantitatively evaluate how different components of the data generation paradigm (e.g., graph quality, image quality, instruction generation method, data quantity vs. environment diversity) impact the agent's performance. This provides empirical insights and actionable guidelines.
6. **Develop Optimal Strategies for Data Utilization:** The paper aims to study how the large-scale augmented data can be most effectively applied in the pre-training and fine-tuning stages of VLN agents to maximize performance and generalization. This includes exploring the role of various pre-training tasks.
7. **Achieve New State-of-the-Art Performance:** Ultimately, the motivation is to demonstrate the power of their large-scale, high-quality data by significantly improving the performance of existing VLN agents on various benchmark tasks, including reducing the generalization gap between seen and unseen environments.
8. **Ensure Reproducibility and Accessibility:** The authors explicitly state their goal to create a "fully reproducible and straightforward to execute" large-scale training regime, making their advancements accessible to the broader research community.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the belief that high-quality, abundant in-domain data, coupled with a systematic understanding of its generation and utilization, is key to pushing VLN agents towards human-level navigation capabilities and generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ScaleVLN paradigm involves a systematic, multi-stage approach to generate large-scale, high-quality vision-and-language navigation data and effectively utilize it for agent training. The pipeline covers environment acquisition, graph construction, visual data enhancement, trajectory sampling, instruction generation, and a tailored training strategy.

#### 3.1. Data Generation Paradigm (ScaleVLN)

1. **Collecting Environments:**
 - The method utilizes 1200+ photo-realistic indoor 3D scans from the **HM3D** (800 training scenes) and **Gibson** (491 training and validation scenes) datasets. These provide over 150,000 m² of navigable area, roughly 7.5 times larger than the standard Matterport3D (MP3D) training scenes.

2. **Constructing Navigation Graphs:**
 - Recognizing the criticality of high-quality graphs, ScaleVLN employs a novel heuristic:
 - **Excessive Viewpoint Sampling:** The Habitat simulator is used to sample a large number of navigable viewpoints, ensuring comprehensive coverage of open spaces while maintaining a minimum geodesic distance of 0.4m between them.
 - **Agglomerative Clustering:** Adjacent viewpoints are grouped using Agglomerative Clustering with a 1.0m distance threshold. This effectively positions nodes near the center of open spaces.
 - **Graph Refinement:** A rough graph is initially created by connecting viewpoints within a 5.0m radius, capping connections at five edges per viewpoint. This is then refined using the approach from to ensure fully-connected and fully-traversable graphs with no edges passing through obstacles. The resulting graphs have an average edge length of 1.41m and an average node degree of 4.55.

3. **Recovering Faulty Rendered Images:**
 - Addressing the issue of lower image quality from 3D meshes (especially in Gibson), the authors frame this as an image-to-image translation problem.
 - A **Co-Modulated GAN (Co-Mod GAN)** is trained on rendered-and-camera-image pairs from Matterport3D.
 - This trained GAN is then used to recover photo-realistic images from the faulty rendered images in HM3D and Gibson, mitigating noise from broken, distorted, or missing regions.

4. **Sampling Trajectories:**
 - For navigation tasks requiring detailed instructions, the method follows PREVALENT by collecting all possible shortest paths between any two viewpoints connected by three to five intermediate nodes on the newly constructed graphs.
 - This yields 2,890,267 paths from HM3D and 2,051,443 paths from Gibson. For REVERIE, a different sampling strategy focusing on target objects is employed (details in Appendix A.2).

5. **Generating Navigational Instructions:**
 - The off-the-shelf **EnvDrop Speaker**, a simple LSTM-based language generation model pre-trained on R2R instruction-path pairs, is used to produce corresponding instructions for each sampled trajectory. The authors note that while less diverse than GPT-2, EnvDrop Speaker yields similar performance improvements for R2R.

This entire process results in a total of **4.9 million instruction-trajectory pairs** (ScaleVLN dataset), an unprecedented scale in VLN data.

#### 3.2. Agent Training Strategy

1. **Baseline VLN Models:**
 - The research employs two state-of-the-art VLN agents:
 - **Dual-Scale Graph Transformer (DUET):** Known for building an on-the-fly topological map to extend action space and facilitate planning/error correction. Used for R2R, CVDN, and REVERIE.
 - **History Aware Multimodal Transformer (HAMT):** Explicitly stores observations at each step to improve vision-instruction alignment. Used for R2R-CE.

2. **Two-Stage Training (Pre-training and Fine-tuning):**
 - **Pre-training:** The augmented ScaleVLN data (along with R2R and PREVALENT) is used for pre-training. Common proxy tasks are employed:
 - **Masked Language Modeling (MLM):** Predicts masked words based on textual context and trajectory.
 - **Single-Action Prediction (SAP):** Predicts the next action given instruction and path history.
 - **Masked Region Modeling (MRM):** Predicts labels for masked regions in historical observations (found to be less effective with large data).
 - **Fine-tuning:** Models are then fine-tuned on the specific downstream task datasets (R2R, CVDN, REVERIE, R2R-CE) using **Imitation Learning (IL)** via DAGGER. This method allows learning from diverse paths and reduces exposure bias.
 - **Optimal Data Split:** The study investigates the best combination of augmented data (ScaleVLN, PREVALENT) and original R2R data for both pre-training and fine-tuning.

3. **Visual Encoder:**
 - CLIP ViT-B/16 is primarily used as the visual encoder, a powerful model pre-trained on millions of image-text pairs. CLIP ViT-H/14 is also explored for further performance gains.

This comprehensive methodology ensures not only the generation of a massive dataset but also its effective integration into a robust training pipeline, leading to significant performance improvements and deep insights into the factors influencing VLN agent generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several key findings and quantitative results that highlight the effectiveness of the ScaleVLN paradigm:

#### 4.1. Impact of Data Generation Components

- **Navigation Graph Quality is Crucial:** Experiments (Table 1) show that high-quality, fully traversable graphs with high viewpoint density and no obstacles are essential. Our method's graphs (1.16 nodes/m², 0% collision) significantly outperform previous approaches like AutoVLN (0.36 nodes/m², 29.35% collision), leading to better generalization to unseen environments (+3.09% SR on R2R Val-Unseen over AutoVLN, and +3.09% SR over baseline without augmented HM3D data). Noisy graphs with obstacles degrade performance.
- **Environment Diversity Outweighs Sample Quantity:** While more samples generally help, generating data from a greater *number of diverse environments* has a stronger positive impact on an agent's generalization ability than simply increasing the sampling density within a limited set of environments (Table 2). This confirms the importance of visual diversity.
- **Image Quality Recovery is Highly Beneficial:** Using the Co-Mod GAN to recover photo-realistic images from faulty renders significantly boosts agent performance (Table 4). This improvement is particularly pronounced for Gibson environments, which often have lower-quality 3D meshes. Without recovery, low-quality data can even harm performance, even with more environments (e.g., HM-E(R)+Gib-E(F) performs worse than HM-E(R) alone).
- **Augmented Instruction Quality Matters:** While the EnvDrop Speaker, a simple LSTM-based model, provides reasonable instructions (Bleu-4 27.66), the study acknowledges that better instruction generation (higher Bleu-4) could further improve navigation performance (Table 5).

#### 4.2. Optimal Data Utilization Strategies

- **Complementary Pre-training Data:** Combining augmented data from both Matterport3D (PREVALENT) and additional environments (HM3D, Gibson) during pre-training is more effective than using either alone (Table 3, Table 6). Learning from diverse environments improves generalization.
- **Retaining Augmented Data in Fine-tuning is Key for Generalization:** Keeping the augmented data from new environments (HM-E) during fine-tuning (alongside original R2R data) is highly beneficial. This reduces overfitting to the small R2R dataset and significantly improves generalization to unseen environments (+2.51% SR in Val-Unseen, Method #4 vs. #6 in Table 6). Crucially, this strategy nearly **eliminates the generalization gap** between seen and unseen environments to less than 1% SR (80.02% vs. 79.10%). However, including PREVALENT in fine-tuning can harm performance due to overfitting to the limited MP3D scenes.
- **Effective Pre-training Tasks:** Masked Language Modeling (MLM) and Single-Action Prediction (SAP) are very effective and complementary pre-training tasks, significantly enhancing agent performance (Table 7). Masked Region Modeling (MRM) was found to slightly degenerate results when combined with other tasks, possibly due to noise introduced by predicting object categories for masked images when rich semantic representations are already learned from large data.

#### 4.3. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **R2R Dataset (Single-Run Success Rate):**
 - ScaleVLN pushes the R2R test-unseen single-run success rate to an unprecedented **77% SR** with DUET and CLIP ViT-B/16, an 8% absolute improvement over previous SOTA (BEVBert at 73%) (Table 9).
 - With a more powerful visual encoder (CLIP ViT-H/14) and image augmentation (EnvEdit), the success rate reaches a remarkable **80% SR** on R2R test-unseen.
 - The generalization gap between seen and unseen environments is reduced to **less than 1%** (versus 8% in the previous best method), significantly improving robustness.
 - The gap to human performance (86% SR) is narrowed to 6%, similar to the agent's OSR/SR difference, suggesting future work could focus on policy networks for stopping problems.
- **REVERIE (Remote Embodied Visual Referring Expression):** ScaleVLN achieves new state-of-the-art results across all metrics on the REVERIE task, surpassing AutoVLN even with fewer high-quality HM3D scenes (Table 8).
- **CVDN (Vision-and-Dialog Navigation):** The method establishes new state-of-the-art performance on the CVDN test-unseen split, improving Goal Progress (GP) by 1.41 meters (a 25.26% relative gain) (Table 10). This indicates strong generalization of R2R-style augmented data to tasks with different instruction types.
- **R2R-CE (Continuous Environments):** Even though ScaleVLN generates discrete data, it significantly boosts VLN performance in continuous environments by 5% SR, showcasing the data's utility in more realistic scenarios (Table 11). This is achieved by supporting high-level decision-making with a candidate waypoint predictor.
- **Generalizability Across Models:** ScaleVLN data consistently benefits various VLN agents (Seq2Seq, EnvDrop, HAMT), showing substantial absolute SR increases (12.2%, 3.8%, 5.5% respectively) compared to using only PREVALENT (Table 15).
- **Long-Horizon VLN (R4R):** The dataset also improves performance on long-horizon tasks like R4R, leading to consistent gains in SR, nDTW, and SDTW, indicating better path following (Table 16).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ScaleVLN paradigm represents a significant step forward in Vision-and-Language Navigation, offering both immediate practical benefits and profound implications for future research in embodied AI.

1. **Breakthrough in VLN Performance:** Achieving an 80% single-run success rate on the challenging R2R test split and nearly eliminating the generalization gap between seen and unseen environments sets a new benchmark for VLN. This brings agents closer to human-level performance and demonstrates the immense power of scaled, high-quality data.
2. **Fully Reproducible and Accessible Data Generation:** Unlike some previous large-scale data generation efforts that relied on proprietary models or non-public resources, ScaleVLN emphasizes reproducibility by using fully accessible tools and publicly available environments. This allows the broader research community to leverage and build upon this work, accelerating progress in the field.
3. **Actionable Guidelines for Data-Centric AI:** The comprehensive analysis of each component in the data generation and utilization pipeline provides invaluable empirical insights. Researchers now have clear guidelines on:
 - The critical importance of high-quality navigation graph construction (dense, traversable, collision-free).
 - The necessity of visual fidelity (image recovery) to counteract issues in 3D renders.
 - The relative importance of environment diversity over sheer sample quantity.
 - Optimal strategies for combining augmented and original data during pre-training and fine-tuning.
 - The most effective proxy tasks for pre-training.
 These guidelines can inform the design of future VLN datasets and training methodologies.
4. **Enhanced Generalization Across Diverse VLN Tasks:** The demonstrated state-of-the-art results across a range of VLN benchmarks (R2R, REVERIE, CVDN, R2R-CE), which involve different instruction styles (detailed, high-level, dialog history) and action spaces (discrete, continuous), highlight the broad utility and generalization potential of the ScaleVLN data. This suggests that the generated data captures fundamental aspects of visual-language grounding and spatial reasoning relevant to many navigation problems.
5. **Foundation for Future Embodied AI Research:** The ScaleVLN paradigm can serve as a powerful tool for data augmentation in VLN and other visual navigation problems, fostering more robust and capable embodied AI agents. The findings regarding the remaining gap to human performance (e.g., in stopping problems) also point to clear directions for future policy learning research. The ability of discrete data to boost performance in continuous environments further broadens its applicability to more realistic robotic scenarios.

In summary, ScaleVLN not only provides a massive, high-quality dataset but also a meticulously analyzed methodology, fundamentally advancing the state-of-the-art in vision-and-language navigation and providing a robust framework for future data-driven research in embodied intelligence.
