# Efficient RL Training for LLMs with Experience Replay

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from FAIR at Meta integrates experience replay into Large Language Model post-training, demonstrating that reusing past trajectories can reduce computational costs by up to 40% while maintaining or improving model accuracy and training stability. The work challenges the conventional reliance on strictly on-policy data, advocating for a more compute-efficient training paradigm.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training for Large Language Models (LLMs) incurs prohibitive computational costs, with inference for data generation consuming over 80% of the GPU budget.
- Current state-of-the-art RL methods for LLMs predominantly use an "on-policy" paradigm, discarding generated training trajectories after a single gradient update, leading to extreme sample inefficiency.
- A prevailing assumption in LLM RL is that the performance degradation from using off-policy (stale) data outweighs any computational benefits of data reuse.

## Method

- The study introduces and systematically investigates the application of ==experience replay==, a classical RL technique, for LLM post-training using an ==asynchronous RL pipeline==.
- A theoretical framework is developed to quantify the trade-off between compute efficiency, data off-policiness, sample diversity, and the cost of trajectory generation, deriving optimal buffer design principles.
- Extensive empirical experiments fine-tune Qwen and Llama models on reasoning and coding tasks, evaluating the impact of replay buffer size, worker-to-trainer ratios, and sampling strategies on efficiency and performance.

## Results

- Implementation of a well-configured replay buffer reduced the computational budget (GPU hours) by up to **40**% while achieving comparable or superior final accuracy relative to on-policy baselines.
- Achieved superior accuracy for any given compute budget compared to strictly on-policy training and improved pass@k metrics for k > 1, indicating better preservation of model output diversity.
- Theoretical analysis formalized the trade-off, showing that replay buffers are optimal when rollout generation is expensive or off-policy effects are negligible, aligning with empirical observations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strictly on-policy training is not universally optimal for LLMs; compute efficiency is maximized by an optimal balance of data freshness, diversity, and the high cost of trajectory generation.
> - Experience replay acts as a regularizer, increasing the diversity of the training distribution, which stabilizes training, prevents premature performance crashes, and can lead to higher peak accuracy.
> - The focus for LLM RL fine-tuning should shift from maximizing performance per gradient step to maximizing performance per unit of compute, where experience replay offers a practical method for achieving high efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become a crucial component in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of modern Large Language Models (LLMs), facilitating advancements in complex tasks such as mathematics and coding. However, a significant challenge in LLM post-training using RL is the prohibitive computational cost associated with continuous data generation. Unlike pre-training, which uses static datasets, RL requires the policy to generate new training trajectories iteratively. Current state-of-the-art pipelines, such as those employing Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or Generalized Reinforce Policy Optimization (GRPO), typically operate in an "on-policy" manner, where generated rollouts are used for a single gradient update and then discarded. This "generate-then-discard" paradigm contributes to substantial inference costs, frequently exceeding 80% of the post-training GPU budget.

In the broader context of classical Deep Reinforcement Learning, Experience Replay (ER)—the practice of storing and reusing past trajectories in a buffer—is a foundational technique for improving sample efficiency and stabilizing training, exemplified by algorithms like DQN, SAC, and DDPG. Despite its established utility in other RL domains, ER has been largely overlooked in LLM training. The prevailing assumption in the LLM community has been that the performance degradation resulting from off-policy data, often referred to as "staleness," outweighs the computational benefits of data reuse.

This work directly challenges this prevailing assumption. It positions itself as a systematic investigation into the efficacy of replay buffers for LLM post-training, specifically focusing on compute efficiency. While a few very recent works have begun to reintroduce replay buffers to LLM training, their motivations have generally differed. Some approaches utilize buffers primarily for enhancing exploration and final model performance, often necessitating specialized loss functions or complex filtering strategies. Others propose dynamic sampling or multi-phase training to maximize data quality. In contrast, this research focuses on demonstrating that simple experience replay can substantially reduce the compute budget in standard asynchronous pipelines while maintaining or improving accuracy, without introducing new training paradigms aimed at state-of-the-art accuracy alone. It bridges a gap between established classical RL practices and the emerging field of LLM RL, suggesting that the "on-policy as possible" dogma in LLM RL may be suboptimal.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to systematically investigate the utility of experience replay in the context of LLM post-training and to challenge the widely held belief that strict on-policy data is essential for achieving high performance. The authors aim to demonstrate that discarding training trajectories after a single use is computationally inefficient.

The primary motivation stems from the significant computational cost associated with current LLM reinforcement learning pipelines. Continuous generation of new training trajectories, a requirement for RL, leads to inference costs that can consume over 80% of the GPU budget during post-training. Standard on-policy methods exacerbate this issue by being extremely sample inefficient, using generated rollouts only once before discarding them. This inefficiency presents a substantial barrier to scaling LLM training and making it more accessible.

Specifically, the key objectives include:
- **Formalizing the Trade-off:** To develop a mathematical framework that quantifies the inherent trade-off between compute efficiency, the degree of data off-policiness (staleness), sample diversity, and the high computational cost of trajectory generation. This involves analyzing the bias-variance decomposition in stochastic gradient descent within the context of experience replay.
- **Deriving Optimal Design Principles:** To theoretically derive optimal buffer sizes and replay ratios, demonstrating that compute efficiency is maximized not by strict on-policiness, but by an optimal balance of data freshness, diversity, and generation cost.
- **Empirical Validation of Efficiency Gains:** To conduct extensive experiments demonstrating that a well-designed replay buffer can drastically reduce inference compute—quantified as GPU hours—without degrading the final model performance. In some cases, the aim is to show that performance can even improve, concurrently preserving policy entropy and stabilizing training.
- **Shifting Training Paradigm:** To advocate for a shift in focus from maximizing performance per gradient step to maximizing performance per unit of compute, presenting experience replay as a straightforward and implementable approach for achieving high-efficiency RL fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a dual approach, combining theoretical analysis with extensive empirical experimentation to evaluate the effectiveness of experience replay in LLM training.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Analysis

The theoretical framework investigates the impact of replay buffer design on learning efficiency within a simplified synchronous RL setting. This setting models alternating phases of trajectory generation and gradient descent steps.
- **Model Setup:** A first-in, first-out (FIFO) replay buffer of capacity N is considered. At each training step, R new rollouts are generated and added to the buffer (evicting the oldest), and a minibatch of size B is sampled uniformly from the buffer for a gradient update. The compute cost of an update is defined as `c = B + μR`, where `μ` is the compute cost ratio between a forward-backward pass and one rollout generation.
- **Assumptions:**
 - **Target Smoothness (Assumption 4.1):** The objective function `F` is L-smooth.
 - **Bias (Assumption 4.2):** A constant `κ ≥ 0` exists such that the bias of the gradient estimate `E[ε_t,i | F_t]` is bounded by `κ ||θ_t - θ_t_i||`, where `θ_t_i` is the policy parameter when the `i`-th sample was generated. This assumption accounts for the bias introduced by reusing off-policy data, linking it to the parameter drift.
 - **Variance (Assumption 4.3):** A non-decreasing function `σ` and a coefficient `ρ ∈` exist. The per-sample variance `E[||ε_t,i||^2]` is bounded by `σ^2(t - t_i)` (increasing with staleness). Additionally, correlation between different samples `corr(ε_t,i, ε_t,j)` is bounded by `ρ |t_i - t_j| / N`, reflecting dependencies induced by shared update histories.
- **Convergence Theorem (Theorem 4.4):** Under these assumptions, a convergence bound for the expected squared norm of the gradient `||∇F(θ_t)||^2` is derived. This bound highlights the interplay between learning rate, buffer parameters, bias, and variance.
- **Optimal Design (Theorem 4.5):** By optimizing the convergence bound for an asymptotically large compute budget, the theoretical analysis characterizes optimal ratios for the staleness horizon (`x* = N/R`) and replay ratio (`y* = B/R`). This theorem demonstrates that optimal design balances staleness-induced noise growth (`σ^2`), coupling between replayed samples and parameter iterates (`ρ`), and the rollout-vs-training compute imbalance (`μ`). A closed-form expression for `x*` is also provided under a power-law assumption for `σ`.

#### 3.2. Empirical Analysis

Empirical experiments are conducted in an asynchronous RL setting, which partitions GPUs into `W` inference workers for rollout generation and `T` trainers for optimization.
- **Models and Datasets:** Experiments primarily fine-tune Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen2.5-7B models (Qwen et al., 2025) using GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) on the OpenR1-Math-220k reasoning dataset (OpenR1 et al., 2025). Evaluation is performed on OpenR1-Math-220k or MATH (Hendrycks et al., 2021). Additional experiments include Qwen3-8B on Lean coding tasks and Llama 3.2 3B on OpenR1-Math-220k.
- **Training Setup:**
 - **Asynchronous Pipeline:** Inference workers continuously generate trajectories and add them to a replay buffer, while trainers continuously sample from this buffer for optimization.
 - **Optimization:** Adam optimizer with constant learning rates. Batch size of 60 (or 63 for 7 trainers).
 - **GRPO Loss:** The GRPO loss utilizes an advantage `A` computed at the time of rollout generation, normalized by group statistics, and does not include a KL regularization term.
- **Hyperparameter Exploration:**
 - **Buffer Size (N):** Varied across a wide range to study the effect on off-policiness, training speed, and stability.
 - **Worker-to-Trainer Ratio (W/T):** Explored different ratios (e.g., (6,2), (5,3), (4,4)) to observe the impact on compute efficiency, replay ratio, and sample diversity.
 - **Learning Rate:** Ablations performed to ensure optimal learning rates for both baseline and buffer configurations.
- **Sampling Strategies:**
 - **Uniform Sampling:** The default strategy where trainers sample uniformly at random from the buffer.
 - **Positive-Bias Sampling:** A refined strategy that prioritizes sampling of "correct" rollouts (δN freshest correct rollouts + (1-δ)N freshest general rollouts).
 - **Uniform without Replacement:** Explored to increase local diversity.
- **Alternative Loss Function:** The AsymRE loss (Arnal et al., 2025), which does not feature importance ratio correction, is also tested in conjunction with replay buffers.
- **Metrics:** Accuracy (pass@1) and pass@k are measured against compute spent (estimated using the compute ratio `γ = (1 + W/T) / (1 + μ)`) and wall-time. Training stability and policy entropy are also monitored. Experiments are run with at least 4 random seeds, reporting median and interquartile range.
- **Compute Estimation:** The compute ratio `μ` (cost of generation vs. training a batch) is empirically estimated by tracking the number of unique rollouts generated (`K_inference`) and non-unique rollouts processed (`K_training`) relative to the number of inference and trainer GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the integration of experience replay into LLM RL training.

#### 4.1. Compute Efficiency and Performance Gains

- **Significant Compute Savings:** A primary finding is that a well-configured replay buffer can reduce the computational budget by up to 40% while achieving comparable or superior final accuracy relative to on-policy baselines. This contradicts the established paradigm that strict on-policy training is optimal for LLMs.
- **Superior Accuracy per Compute:** For any given compute budget, the accuracy achievable with experience replay was observed to be superior to that from strictly on-policy training.
- **Training Stabilization:** The use of a replay buffer demonstrated an additional benefit of stabilizing the training process, preventing premature performance crashes and in some instances enabling the attainment of higher peak accuracy. This effect was more pronounced in smaller models.

#### 4.2. Impact of Buffer Hyperparameters

- **Buffer Size and Off-Policiness:** Increasing the buffer size, which subsequently increases the average off-policiness of samples, was found to have two effects: it slowed down the training process (requiring more steps to reach peak accuracy) but also stabilized it, potentially leading to a higher maximum achievable accuracy. This suggests that moderate off-policiness can act as a regularizer, increasing training distribution diversity and potentially preventing overfitting.
- **Replay Ratio (W/T):** As the ratio of inference workers to trainers (W/T) decreased, the compute cost per gradient update dropped, while the average replay ratio (how many times a sample is reused) increased. Moderate replay ratios did not adversely affect maximal accuracy, but aggressive replay could degrade performance, possibly due to reduced local sample diversity. Nevertheless, even extreme configurations could remain attractive due to their high compute efficiency.

#### 4.3. Output Diversity and Generalization

- **Improved Pass@k:** Training with experience replay was found to improve pass@k metrics for k > 1. This improvement was observed not only in absolute terms but also proportionally, suggesting that experience replay helps preserve the model’s output diversity, which is a concern in RL for LLMs. This effect is attributed to the increased diversity of the training distribution resulting from the reuse of older samples.

#### 4.4. Wall-time Speed

- Gains in wall-time speed from using a buffer in the asynchronous setup were found to match or exceed the gains in compute efficiency. This is attributed to the buffer's ability to decouple data production from consumption, reducing stalls caused by variable latencies or suboptimal worker-to-trainer ratios, thereby smoothing the asynchronous pipeline.

#### 4.5. Optimality Curves and Refinements

- **Pareto Frontier:** After extensive hyperparameter sweeps across learning rates and buffer configurations, the best buffer configurations consistently outperformed the best non-buffer configurations, establishing a clear Pareto frontier for efficiency.
- **Advanced Sampling and Losses:** Further refinements, such as positive-bias sampling (prioritizing correct rollouts) and the AsymRE loss function (which does not feature importance ratio correction), demonstrated additional performance improvements over basic uniform sampling with GRPO. Conversely, sampling without replacement did not yield conclusive benefits in preliminary tests.

#### 4.6. Theoretical Validation

- The theoretical analysis formalized the trade-off and characterized optimal replay buffer designs. It indicated that when rollout generation is expensive (high `μ`) or off-policy effects (noise `σ` and correlation `ρ`) are negligible, a replay buffer becomes optimal, characterized by a larger staleness horizon and a higher replay count. Conversely, for cheap rollouts or high off-policy variance/correlation, an on-policy approach remains more effective. These theoretical insights generally aligned with the empirical observations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant challenge to the conventional wisdom in Large Language Model (LLM) reinforcement learning, which has largely favored strictly on-policy training data. By demonstrating the efficacy of experience replay, a foundational technique in classical RL, in the context of LLM post-training, the work offers a pathway to more computationally efficient training pipelines.

The key significance and potential impact areas include:
- **Challenging Prevailing Assumptions:** The work directly refutes the belief that fresh, on-policy data is indispensable for high performance in LLM RL, opening up new architectural and algorithmic possibilities for LLM fine-tuning.
- **Substantial Compute Cost Reduction:** The empirical demonstration of up to 40% compute savings while maintaining or improving accuracy provides a tangible benefit. This reduction in GPU hours can translate into lower operational costs, faster experimentation cycles, and increased accessibility for researchers and practitioners, particularly for resource-intensive LLM development.
- **Improved Training Stability:** The observed regularization effect of replay buffers, leading to more stable training and prevention of crashes, is valuable for robust and reliable LLM development, especially with larger, more complex models.
- **Shift in Optimization Objectives:** The research advocates for optimizing "performance per unit of compute" rather than "performance per gradient step." This reframing is crucial for practical LLM deployment and aligns RL objectives with real-world resource constraints.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The theoretical framework provides a principled understanding of the trade-offs involved in experience replay for LLMs. This can guide future research into more sophisticated buffer management strategies, such as adaptive sampling (e.g., prioritized replay), advanced off-policy correction mechanisms, and novel loss functions specifically designed to leverage stale data more effectively. The preliminary success with positive-bias sampling and AsymRE suggests promising avenues for further optimization.
- **Practical Applicability:** The proposed experience replay approach is described as straightforward and easy to implement, requiring only modest deviations from current state-of-the-art pipelines. This facilitates its adoption in existing production environments and research workflows.

While the results are consistent for the evaluated small and mid-size models, further validation on larger "frontier" models is necessary to confirm the scalability of these efficiency gains. Nevertheless, this work lays a foundational step towards making LLM reinforcement learning more sustainable and efficient.
