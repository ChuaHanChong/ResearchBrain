# Diffusion Reinforcement Learning Based Online 3D Bin Packing Spatial Strategy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Han et al. introduce a Diffusion Reinforcement Learning framework for the Online 3D Bin Packing Problem, which improves decision quality, learning efficiency, and adaptability by integrating Diffusion Probabilistic Models into the Actor network. This approach achieved enhanced space utilization and faster convergence compared to traditional DRL and heuristic methods on certain datasets, while modeling complex action distributions effectively.

## Problem

- Online 3D Bin Packing (Online 3D-BPP) requires real-time decisions for sequentially arriving items with incomplete information, posing challenges due to high-dimensional action spaces and strict time constraints.
- Traditional heuristic algorithms for BPP are often suboptimal, lack intelligence, and have limited generalization across diverse problem instances due to reliance on fixed, manually designed rules.
- Current Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) approaches for Online 3D-BPP suffer from low sample efficiency, unstable policy optimization, limited generalization, and an inability to effectively model complex multimodal action distributions.

## Method

- The Online 3D-BPP is formalized as a Markov Decision Process, with state representation enhanced by a height map and item dimensions.
- A ==Diffusion== Probabilistic Model is integrated into the Actor network of an Actor-Critic framework to generate packing actions, allowing for the modeling of complex multimodal action probability distributions.
- A feasibility mask prediction module is incorporated to constrain the action distribution, ensuring generated actions adhere to physical packing constraints and reducing exploration waste.

## Results

- The diffusion model-based approach demonstrated a rapid reduction in action loss during early training and maintained consistently lower action loss throughout, indicating effective capture of action space distribution.
- The method achieved improved space utilization (**57.9%**) and items packed (13.9) on the RS dataset, and **64.1%** space utilization with 16.7 items on the CUT-1 dataset, outperforming a baseline BPP method.
- Visual comparisons showed the proposed method produced more structured and compact packing arrangements, characterized by placing larger items first and heavier items at the bottom, contributing to better space utilization and stacking stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Diffusion Probabilistic Models within a DRL Actor network can effectively capture complex, multimodal action distributions, leading to more diverse and efficient packing strategies.
> - Modulating generated actions with a feasibility mask, derived from an independent prediction network, significantly improves the rationality of decisions by ensuring adherence to physical packing constraints.
> - A step-by-step reward mechanism, based on incremental occupied volume, provides more frequent feedback, which guides the agent towards efficient packing paths and accelerates model convergence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the Online 3D Bin Packing Problem (Online 3D-BPP), a combinatorial optimization challenge with direct applications in logistics, warehousing, and intelligent manufacturing. The objective of 3D-BPP is to efficiently place items into containers to maximize space utilization. Traditional 3D-BPP assumes complete knowledge of all item information beforehand, allowing for global optimization. However, real-world scenarios, such as dynamic project management or online order sorting, involve items arriving sequentially with information revealed gradually. This defines the Online 3D-BPP, which demands real-time decision-making, high-quality solutions, and system robustness, posing more complex challenges than its offline counterpart.

The Online 3D-BPP is characterized by three main difficulties:
1. **Incomplete Information**: Item sizes, shapes, and sequences are unknown in advance, requiring decisions based on real-time arrivals.
2. **High Dimensional Action Space**: Multiple choices for placement position and orientation for each item lead to a vast action space, challenging efficient exploration without increased computational complexity.
3. **Real-time Requirements**: Decisions must be made immediately upon item arrival, necessitating high responsiveness from algorithms.

Historically, solutions for BPP have involved traditional heuristic algorithms (e.g., tabu search, first-fit, greedy algorithms). While intuitive and capable of providing quick feasible solutions, these methods often rely on manually designed rules, rarely achieve optimal solutions, and struggle to maintain consistent performance across diverse problem scenarios.

In recent years, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has emerged as an approach for addressing the Online 3D-BPP due to its capability in dynamic environments. However, DRL applications in this domain face several persistent challenges:
- **Low Sample Efficiency**: Requiring large amounts of interaction data to learn effective policies.
- **High Dimensional State-Action Spaces**: Difficulty in efficiently exploring and optimizing policies in complex, vast decision spaces.
- **Unstable Policy Optimization**: Policies can be prone to instability during training, affecting convergence and performance.
- **Limited Generalization and Adaptability**: Trained models often do not transfer well to scenarios with diverse item types or sizes, often being restricted to item dimensions similar to those used during training.
- **Difficulty in Modeling Complex Action Distributions**: Traditional DRL policies, often based on unimodal Gaussian distributions, struggle to represent the multi-modal and complex nature of optimal packing actions.

Previous DRL-based works have explored various techniques, including data-driven tree search (DDTS), brain-inspired experiential reinforcement models, Transformer networks, integration with Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS), constrained DRL frameworks utilizing feasibility masks, Packing Configuration Trees (PCT), heuristic-guided DRL models, and dual-task learning approaches. While these methods have improved performance and robustness, the core challenges of sample efficiency, high dimensionality, and generalization persist.

This paper aims to advance the state-of-the-art by integrating Diffusion Probabilistic Models into a DRL framework. This integration is proposed as a method to address the limitations of existing DRL approaches, specifically targeting the challenges of modeling complex action distributions, improving policy quality, enhancing learning efficiency, and boosting generalization in high-dimensional online 3D bin packing environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an advanced online 3D bin packing algorithm that overcomes the limitations observed in existing deep reinforcement learning (DRL) approaches. This involves improving decision-making quality, learning efficiency, and adaptability in complex, dynamic packing environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges within the Online 3D Bin Packing Problem:
1. **Inefficiencies of Traditional Methods**: Traditional heuristic algorithms, while providing approximate solutions, often rely on fixed, hand-engineered rules derived from human experience. These methods are frequently suboptimal, lack intelligence, and exhibit limited generalization across different problem instances. The need for more flexible and efficient strategies that can adapt to varying conditions is therefore significant.
2. **Limitations of Current DRL Approaches**: Despite the progress made by DRL in combinatorial optimization, its application to online 3D-BPP still faces substantial hurdles. These include:
 - **Low Sample Efficiency**: DRL algorithms typically require extensive interaction with the environment to learn effective policies, making training computationally intensive and time-consuming.
 - **High Dimensional State-Action Spaces**: The myriad possibilities for item placement positions and orientations create a vast action space, complicating policy exploration and optimization.
 - **Unstable Policy Optimization**: The learning process in DRL can be unstable, leading to fluctuating performance and difficulties in converging to robust solutions.
 - **Poor Generalization and Adaptability**: Existing DRL models often struggle to transfer learned policies to scenarios with different item sizes or types than those encountered during training. This limits their practical applicability in diverse real-world settings where item characteristics vary.
 - **Inability to Model Complex Action Distributions**: Many DRL policies, particularly those based on unimodal distributions (e.g., Gaussian policies), are inherently limited in their capacity to represent the complex, multi-modal probability distributions of optimal packing actions. This restricts the diversity and quality of generated actions.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the proposed research are:
- To formalize the online 3D-BPP as a Markov decision process (MDP) and enhance environmental perception through a height map-based state representation, thereby providing richer structural information to the learning agent.
- To introduce Diffusion Probabilistic Models within the Actor-Critic (AC) framework, specifically as the actor network, to effectively model complex multimodal action probability distributions. This aims to improve policy quality, accelerate learning efficiency, and enable the generation of more diverse and reasonable packing decisions.
- To integrate a feasibility mask prediction module that constrains the action distribution to ensure generated actions adhere to physical packing constraints (e.g., support, boundaries, non-overlapping), thereby reducing exploration waste and improving the rationality of decisions.
- To implement a step-by-step reward mechanism that provides more frequent feedback to the agent, guiding it towards efficient packing paths and faster model convergence.
- To experimentally evaluate the proposed algorithm on public datasets, demonstrating its performance in terms of space utilization, number of packed items, and convergence speed compared to state-of-the-art DRL and heuristic-based methods, particularly its adaptability in uncertain online scenarios.
- To contribute a novel technical approach that enhances the applicability of diffusion reinforcement learning for combinatorial optimization, offering a robust framework for high-dimensional, complex decision-making in online 3D bin packing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates Diffusion Probabilistic Models into a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) framework to optimize spatial strategies for the Online 3D Bin Packing Problem. The algorithm is structured as a closed-loop system encompassing state perception, policy generation, action filtering, and environment interaction.

**Problem Formalization and State Representation:**
The Online 3D-BPP is formalized as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The state representation for the agent incorporates a height map of the current container and the geometric information of the item to be packed.
- **Height Map (H)**: A 2D grid representing the current filled height at each (x, y) coordinate within the container.
- **Item Dimensions (D_n)**: The length (`l_n`), width (`w_n`), and height (`h_n`) of the next item to be placed. To facilitate processing by a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), `D_n` is expanded into a three-channel tensor, where each channel is an `L x W` matrix with all elements being `l_n`, `w_n`, or `h_n`, respectively.
- The combined state `(H, D_n)` forms a `4 x L x W` array as input to the state encoding module. A CNN is designed to encode this raw state vector into learnable feature representations, which are then fed into the policy and value networks.

**Action Space Management and Constraints:**
To manage the high-dimensional action space, a placement generation module first identifies a fixed-length set of free subspaces as potential placement candidates. This module helps in maintaining a consistent size for the packing action space, irrespective of the item's dimensions. Each item can be placed in six predefined orientations.
The algorithm adheres to seven specific constraints to ensure stable stacking and efficient space utilization:
1. **Support Constraint**: Items must be fully or partially supported, with their center of mass vertically above the support area.
2. **Orientation Constraint**: Items must conform to six predefined placement orientations.
3. **Boundary Constraint**: Items must remain within the container's boundaries.
4. **Parallel Constraint**: Each packed layer must be parallel to the container walls.
5. **Non-overlap Constraint**: Items must not overlap within the container.
6. **Time Constraint**: Decisions must be made within 0.01 seconds, reflecting the online nature of the problem.
7. **Loading Order Constraint**: Larger and heavier items should be placed at the bottom, and smaller, lighter items on top.

**Reinforcement Learning Framework (Actor-Critic):**
The learning architecture is based on the Actor-Critic (AC) framework, designed to address challenges of high dimensionality, multiple constraints, and sparse feedback.
- **Actor Network (Policy Network)**: This network is responsible for generating action probability distributions. In this work, the Actor network integrates a Diffusion Probabilistic Model to model complex action distributions.
- **Critic Network (Value Network)**: This network evaluates the state-value functions of actions chosen by the Actor, providing guidance for policy optimization through backpropagation.
- The objective of the AC algorithm is to maximize the expected return `J(π) = E_τ~π [Σ γ^t * R(s_t, a_t)]`, where `γ` is the discount factor, and `R(s_t, a_t)` is the immediate reward.

**Diffusion Model-Based Policy Generation:**
A core aspect of the proposed method is the integration of Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DPMs) into the Actor network. DPMs are introduced to enhance strategy modeling and action generation diversity by effectively fitting complex multimodal action distributions, which traditional unimodal policies struggle with.
- **DPM Mechanism**: DPMs define a gradual forward diffusion process that adds Gaussian noise to data (`x_0` to `x_T`), progressively destroying its structure. The inverse process is a denoising process that gradually restores the original data from pure noise. This inverse process is parameterized as a Gaussian distribution.
- **Role as Guiding Policy**: In packing tasks, DPMs generate reasonable packing action distributions by gradually removing noise. They capture the complex multi-modal distribution of the action space and dynamically adjust the action generation strategy based on the current environmental state. The problem is framed as a conditional sampling problem where optimal packing actions are sampled from optimal trajectories by conditioning on `y=1` (indicating an optimal action). This is achieved by approximating the reverse diffusion process using classifier-guided sampling, where a separate model predicts the cumulative reward of trajectory samples, and its gradients are used to adjust the mean of the reverse process towards optimal actions.

**Feasibility Mask Prediction:**
To prevent wasted exploration on invalid actions, an independent Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) network is introduced. This MLP takes the CNN-encoded state features as input and outputs a feasibility mask for all candidate positions of the current object. During training, this mask learns from actual action feasibility. During policy execution, it modulates the action distribution generated by the Actor network, ensuring that only actions satisfying physical constraints (support, boundaries, non-overlapping) are selected.

**Reward Function Design:**
A step-by-step reward mechanism is adopted. The immediate reward is based on the incremental volume occupied by the successful placement of the current object. If an action is invalid, a zero reward is given, and the current episode is terminated. This design aims to increase the frequency of reward feedback, effectively guiding the agent to learn efficient packing paths, and accelerating model convergence compared to terminal reward-based approaches.

**Training Process:**
The overall algorithm involves two main stages: deep integrated model learning and actual bin packing.
1. **Cargo Stacking**: The agent obtains items online and loads them sequentially. During training, each loading experience is stored in an experience replay pool.
2. **Model Training**: Random samples are drawn from the experience replay pool to train the deep neural network. The diffusion model, policy network (Actor), and value network (Critic) are synchronously optimized through a joint training mechanism. The training loop involves sampling transition batches, iteratively applying the reverse diffusion process, guiding with return gradients, executing actions, and updating the Actor and Critic network parameters.

**Parameter Settings:**
Key parameters include: discount factor `γ = 0.99`, immediate reward based on volume increase, 0 penalty for invalid actions, Actor learning rate `1e-4`, Critic learning rate `5e-4` (Adam optimizer), batch size `64`, replay buffer capacity `10000`, diffusion steps `t = 100`, training epochs `1000`, and maximum steps per epoch `100`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed diffusion reinforcement learning-based algorithm was evaluated through experiments conducted on a 64-bit Ubuntu 18.04 system, using Python and the Pytorch library, with an Intel Xeon E5-2660 v3 CPU and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU. The container dimensions were set to L=W=H=10.

**Datasets:**
The model was trained and tested on three public datasets:
- **RS (Random Sampling)**: Generated with random item sequences to assess robustness and efficiency in stochastic scenarios.
- **CUT-1**: Created by dividing boxes into items and sorting them by Z-coordinate, suitable for structured data.
- **CUT-2**: Developed by considering stacking dependencies among items, representing more realistic packing scenarios with complex dependencies.

**Action Loss Evolution:**
A comparison of action loss evolution during training (Figure 7) demonstrated that the proposed diffusion model-based approach achieved a rapid reduction in action loss during the early training stages. This method maintained a consistently lower action loss throughout the training process compared to a traditional BPP method. This observation suggests that the diffusion model effectively captures the complex distribution of the action space, which contributes to the generation of more efficient action policies.

**Quantitative Performance Comparison (Table III):**
The performance of the proposed method ("Ours") was compared against several baseline algorithms, including Boundary Rule (Online), BPH (Online), LBP (Offline), and BPP (Online), based on the average number of packed items and space utilization percentage.

- **RS Dataset**: The proposed method achieved 13.9 packed items with 57.9% space utilization. This represents an improvement over the BPP method, which achieved 11.9 items and 49.7% space utilization. The paper notes that while an improvement, it is "relatively modest" due to the high randomness of the dataset, which can restrict action exploration.
- **CUT-1 Dataset**: The proposed method packed 16.7 items with 64.1% space utilization. This exceeded the BPP method's performance of 15.1 items and 60.1% space utilization.
- **CUT-2 Dataset**: On this dataset, the proposed method achieved 16.5 packed items and 63.2% space utilization. In contrast, the BPP method recorded 17.6 items and 69.6% space utilization. For this specific dataset, the proposed method showed lower performance compared to the BPP method in both metrics.

Overall, the diffusion model-based approach generally improved both adaptability and convergence speed by providing structured guidance during the action generation process, particularly on the RS and CUT-1 datasets.

**Visual Packing Results (Figure 8):**
Visual comparisons of packing results across the datasets indicated that the proposed method resulted in more structured and compact packing arrangements. The visual evidence suggested a strategy of placing larger items first and heavier items at the bottom, which is considered beneficial for both space utilization and stacking stability. Compared to other methods that exhibited noticeable gaps, especially in corners and upper areas, the proposed approach achieved a tighter packing density. These visual observations largely aligned with the quantitative results, supporting the effectiveness of the diffusion model in complex packing scenarios.

**Dynamic Sequence of Packing (Figure 9):**
A dynamic sequence of packing steps on the CUT-2 dataset demonstrated the progressive generation of packing actions over 100 denoising steps. The process, guided by a feasibility mask, produced a compact and orderly packing structure. The final depicted state showed 63.2% space utilization, consistent with the quantitative results for CUT-2. The authors noted minor height differences in the final packed structure, suggesting potential for further refinements, such as incorporating stability-related objectives or adjusting diffusion steps, to enhance policy precision and structural uniformity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented by Han et al. introduces a Diffusion Reinforcement Learning (DRL) framework for the Online 3D Bin Packing Problem, contributing to both the theoretical understanding of DRL in combinatorial optimization and its practical application.

### 6. Significance in Research

- **Enhanced Action Distribution Modeling**: The primary significance lies in demonstrating that Diffusion Probabilistic Models can effectively model complex multimodal action distributions within a reinforcement learning framework. This addresses a limitation of traditional DRL policies, which often struggle with the diverse and high-dimensional action spaces inherent in online 3D bin packing.
- **Improved Policy Quality and Learning Efficiency**: By using a step-by-step denoising generation process, the method enhances policy diversity and robustness. The experimental results, particularly the faster reduction in action loss and improved space utilization on certain datasets, indicate that the diffusion model contributes to better policy convergence speed and the generation of high-quality packing actions, even with limited training data.
- **Robust Framework for Complex Decision-Making**: The proposed approach provides a robust framework for addressing high-dimensional, complex decision-making problems under uncertainty and stringent constraints. Formalizing the problem as an MDP, combined with a height map-based state representation and feasibility mask prediction, enhances the agent's environmental perception and ensures physically valid actions.
- **Advances in Diffusion Reinforcement Learning**: This work contributes to the growing field of diffusion reinforcement learning by showcasing the effective collaboration between diffusion strategies and RL, thereby expanding the applicability of diffusion models beyond generative tasks to sequential decision-making.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Logistics and Warehousing Optimization**: The direct application of this research is in improving space utilization and operational efficiency in logistics, intelligent warehousing, and manufacturing. By achieving more compact and stable packing arrangements, companies could potentially reduce shipping costs, increase storage capacity, and optimize automated robotic packing systems.
- **Real-time Decision Support**: The online nature of the algorithm and its demonstrated adaptability in uncertain scenarios (like the RS dataset) suggest its potential for real-time decision support systems where item information arrives dynamically. This could be particularly valuable in e-commerce fulfillment centers or dynamic transportation scheduling.
- **Foundation for Future DRL Applications**: The methodology offers a technical approach for integrating advanced generative models with reinforcement learning to tackle combinatorial optimization problems. This could inspire further research into leveraging diffusion models or other generative AI techniques for complex planning and scheduling tasks in various industrial and scientific domains.
- **Development of Robust AI Systems**: By addressing issues such as sample efficiency, high dimensionality, and policy stability, the framework provides practical guidance for developing more robust and generalizable AI systems capable of adapting to diverse and changing environments.

While the performance on the CUT-2 dataset indicated an area for further refinement, the overall improvements in space utilization and convergence efficiency on other challenging datasets, coupled with the method's strong adaptability in uncertain online scenarios, underscore its potential for practical application and its contribution to the advancement of combinatorial optimization.
