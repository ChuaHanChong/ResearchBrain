# VTouch++: A Multimodal Dataset with Vision-Based Tactile Enhancement for Bimanual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VTOUCH presents a large-scale multimodal dataset for bimanual robot manipulation, integrating synchronized vision, tactile, and proprioceptive data from diverse real-world robot platforms. The dataset supports learning robust representations for contact-intensive tasks and facilitates systematic research through a skill-axis task design, demonstrated by its effectiveness in cross-modal retrieval and policy validation.

## Problem

- Existing datasets for bimanual manipulation often lack comprehensive, high-fidelity physical interaction signals (e.g., tactile feedback), hindering contact-rich task learning.
- Many datasets suffer from limited scale, insufficient task diversity, or the sim-to-real gap, making policies difficult to transfer to physical robots.
- Current task organization in datasets often limits systematic decomposition, skill transfer, and generalization analysis for complex bimanual operations.

## Method

- Developed **VTOUCH**, a large-scale multimodal dataset derived from real-world bimanual robot demonstrations, synchronously capturing ==robot proprioception==, ==multi-view RGB-D==, and explicit ==fingertip tactile signals==.
- Implemented a ==matrix-style skill-axis task design==, structuring demonstrations along orthogonal dimensions like bimanual coordination patterns, atomic manipulation actions, and contact/tactile modes.
- Utilized an automated data collection pipeline on ==heterogeneous robot embodiments== (fixed dual-arm, wheeled-arm, UMI-style mobile manipulators) to ensure scalability and real-world relevance.

## Results

- The VTOUCH dataset comprises over **120,000 episodes** and **1,000+ hours** of multimodal data, including approximately **36 million image frames**, for over **380 bimanual tasks**.
- Cross-modal retrieval experiments showed the proposed contrastive learning method significantly outperformed baselines, achieving a Visual-to-Pose **R@1 = 2.16%** and **mAP = 7.69%**, compared to the best baseline's R@1 of 0.2897% and mAP of 1.4807%.
- Real-robot validation demonstrated that a diffusion-based policy achieved the lowest **MAE = 0.022** and highest **Expert Similarity = 0.848** in action reconstruction, indicating the dataset's utility for training capable policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity physical interaction signals, particularly from vision-based tactile sensors, are crucial for robust bimanual manipulation in contact-intensive scenarios.
> - A systematic, multi-dimensional skill-axis framework for task design enables fine-grained skill transfer, generalization analysis, and better understanding of complex bimanual operations.
> - Effective cross-modal alignment between visual, tactile, and proprioceptive data, facilitated by contrastive learning, yields superior representations for multimodal perception in bimanual tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied intelligence in robotics has seen advancements, yet bimanual manipulation, particularly in tasks involving frequent physical contact, remains a challenge. This challenge is partly attributed to a scarcity of datasets that comprehensively capture rich physical interaction signals, offer systematic task organization, and provide sufficient scale.

Existing datasets in robotic manipulation exhibit specific limitations:
- **Human Bimanual Interaction Datasets (e.g., Ego4D, Epic-Kitchens, GRAB, ARCTIC)**: These datasets provide extensive coverage of human activities and bimanual interactions, some with high-fidelity kinematic trajectories and RGB-D data. However, they typically lack robot-specific proprioception, precise contact force information, or the embodiment constraints relevant for robotic systems. They are primarily suited for high-level task understanding rather than learning contact-intensive control policies.
- **Robotic Manipulation Datasets (Simulation-based, e.g., DexGraspNet, BiDexHands)**: These offer virtually unlimited scale and precise ground truth annotations (states, forces). However, they are susceptible to the "sim-to-real gap," where discrepancies in dynamics, sensing, and rendering hinder the direct transfer of policies to physical robots.
- **Real-Robot Manipulation Datasets (e.g., RoboNet, AgiBot-World)**: While physically realistic, these datasets are generally limited in scale, sensing modalities, or task diversity, particularly in bimanual scenarios. Many such datasets predominantly rely on visual observations and infrequently integrate high-bandwidth tactile sensing. Attempts to overcome collection costs, such as robot-free paradigms (e.g., FreeTacMan), improve efficiency but still require policy transfer to real robots with potentially different morphologies.
- **Multimodal Perception Datasets (e.g., ContactDB, ContactPose, TacQuad, FeelSight, V-HOP)**: These works explore inferring contact information or integrating tactile sensors (e.g., GelSight, DIGIT) with other modalities. While some provide high-resolution contact geometry and force cues, they are often limited in scale, task-specific, or lack integration with continuous bimanual manipulation tasks and physical dynamics. The challenge lies in synchronizing tactile signals with vision and proprioception within large-scale, general-purpose manipulation datasets.

The VTOUCH dataset is introduced to address these limitations. It aims to bridge the gap by providing a large-scale, multimodal dataset derived from real-world bimanual robot manipulation demonstrations. It synchronously captures robot proprioception, multi-view RGB-D observations, and explicit fingertip tactile signals, grounded in actual hardware execution. This work positions itself as a resource for facilitating research into contact-intensive bimanual manipulation by providing physically verified, scalable, and systematically organized data for multimodal representation learning and policy development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observed limitations in existing datasets for bimanual robotic manipulation, particularly concerning contact-rich tasks. Progress in embodied intelligence for such complex scenarios is hindered by the lack of datasets that offer rich physical interaction signals, systematic task organization, and sufficient scale.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **To provide high-fidelity physical interaction signals:** A core limitation of many existing datasets is the absence or low fidelity of physical interaction data, especially tactile feedback. The paper aims to address this by leveraging vision-based tactile sensing (specifically, Li and Adelson tactile sensors). These sensors are intended to capture high-resolution contact geometry and force cues, which are critical for tasks involving delicate physical interaction and force modulation. The motivation is that robust bimanual manipulation in real-world environments necessitates granular awareness of contact dynamics, which cannot be fully derived from visual or proprioceptive data alone.

- **To enable systematic learning through structured task design:** Traditional datasets often organize tasks around fixed, discrete labels, which can limit the systematic decomposition and generalization of learned skills. The motivation is to move beyond this by adopting a "matrix-style" or "skill-axis" task design. This approach aims to structure demonstrations along fundamental, orthogonal axes such as bimanual coordination patterns, atomic manipulation actions, and contact/tactile modes. This systematic organization is intended to facilitate the recomposition and analysis of tasks, enabling fine-grained skill transfer, generalization analysis, and a clearer understanding of the underlying capabilities required for complex bimanual operations.

- **To ensure scalability through automated data collection:** Creating large-scale datasets for real-robot manipulation is resource-intensive. The paper aims to achieve scalability by employing automated data collection pipelines. These pipelines are designed to cover real-world, demand-driven scenarios. The motivation is to overcome the prohibitive cost and slowness of manual data collection for high degree-of-freedom dual-arm systems, thereby providing a sufficiently large volume of diverse data necessary for training robust learning-based policies.

- **To offer a foundational resource for contact-intensive manipulation research:** By integrating the above objectives, the work aims to create a comprehensive dataset. The motivation is to provide a reliable and physically grounded resource that avoids "sim-to-real" discrepancies, allowing researchers to develop and evaluate learning-based methods, including imitation learning and diffusion-based policies, directly on data reflecting real-world physics. This resource is intended to accelerate advancements in robust and generalizable bimanual manipulation in unstructured environments.

- **To bridge theoretical modeling, real-robot data acquisition, and reproducible policy learning:** The dataset is positioned as an interface between different stages of robotic research. The motivation is to facilitate the transition from theoretical algorithmic development to practical, real-world robot deployment. By providing a common benchmark and data standard, the dataset aims to support the reproducible training and evaluation of manipulation policies, fostering a more direct comparison and iterative improvement of methods.

- **To benchmark representative learning methods and demonstrate the necessity of multimodal perception:** As part of validating the dataset's effectiveness, the paper aims to conduct quantitative experiments. The motivation is to demonstrate empirically that multimodal perception (specifically, visual-tactile fusion) and explicit bimanual coordination are critical for achieving competence in contact-intensive bimanual manipulation tasks. This also serves to validate the utility of the dataset for training and evaluating such policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VTOUCH dataset addresses limitations in existing bimanual manipulation datasets by integrating vision-based tactile sensing, a matrix-style task design, and scalable data collection pipelines. The methodology encompasses the design of a robust data acquisition system, a structured task classification framework, comprehensive data preprocessing, and an evaluation of cross-modal retrieval.

#### 3.1. Data Acquisition System Design

The system is structured into hardware, software, and a human-robot interface.
- **Hardware Configuration**: A multi-configuration physical dual-arm robot platform is used, including fixed dual-arm systems, wheeled-arm systems, and UMI-style mobile manipulators. A unified hardware abstraction interface semantically aligns different robot configurations. Integrated sensors include head-mounted and wrist-mounted RGB-D cameras and fingertip tactile sensors (GelSight-style, based on Li and Adelson). High-precision cross-modal synchronization is achieved via a unified hardware triggering module for proprioceptive data, cameras, and tactile sensors.
- **Software Architecture**: A ROS2-based multimodal data-flow pipeline is implemented. Hardware triggering and timestamp mechanisms ensure temporal alignment of visual, tactile, and proprioceptive data. Different sampling rates are handled through interpolation and alignment strategies. Consistency verification and anomaly detection mechanisms are applied during acquisition and post-processing to maintain data integrity.
- **Human-Machine Interaction Interface**: The system supports teleoperation teaching, action recording, and playback on real robots. Demonstrators receive real-time visual and state feedback during teaching and can review/filter trajectories via playback, ensuring demonstration quality. Recorded trajectories are reproducible on physical platforms for policy learning and evaluation.

#### 3.2. Task Design

A multi-dimensional skill-axis framework defines tasks as "minimally executable instances" by selecting specific values along orthogonal skill axes.
- **Core Axes**:
 - **Bimanual Coordination Structure**: Models structural and temporal interdependencies (e.g., Symmetric, Asymmetric, Master–Slave, Sequential; Synchronous, Staggered, Alternating).
 - **Atomic Action Type**: Decomposes tasks into minimal, interpretable operational units (e.g., grasp, hold, pull, push, rotate, align, insert, release) to enhance semantic clarity.
 - **Contact & Tactile Mode**: Explicitly models interactive processes at the tactile level (e.g., Fingertip Contact State, Force Regulation Mode, Contact Asymmetry).
- **Supplementary Axes**:
 - **Object & Geometry**: Covers object count, constraint type (free motion, kinematic constraint, insertion-like), and object geometry categories.
 - **Perception Modality**: Evaluates Visual Availability and Tactile Dependency for multimodal necessity analysis.
 - **Task Composition Hierarchy**: Characterizes task horizon via Atomic Task Count.
This framework facilitates the generation and categorization of diverse, well-structured task instances.

#### 3.3. Data Preprocessing and Quality Control

- **Data Review and Initial Labeling**: Sliding-window statistics on sensor channels detect distribution shifts and abrupt deviations. Heuristic rules generate event-level weak labels (e.g., contact onset, anomalous interactions, failures). Temporal consistency across modalities aids in cross-validating anomalies.
- **Automatic Anomaly Detection**: A channel-wise statistical approach flags samples exceeding an n-sigma threshold based on sliding window mean and variance.
- **Temporal Alignment and Resampling**: Raw sensor data from heterogeneous sampling rates are aligned to a unified frequency (e.g., 30Hz for vision/tactile, 100Hz for proprioception) using the robot control loop as the reference. Visual observations are frame-aligned; proprioceptive and tactile signals are resampled via linear interpolation or zero-order hold.
- **Demonstration Filtering and Segmentation**: Trajectories with excessive missing data or severe temporal jitter are filtered. Aligned trajectories are segmented into task-relevant episodes.

#### 3.4. Dataset Construction

The dataset is collected on the OpenLoong bimanual manipulation platform.
- **Platform Details**: Dual 7-DOF arms (14 joints), three RGB-D cameras (left, right, head), four GelSight-style visual-tactile sensors on end effectors, and joint/end-effector/gripper state feedback.
- **Observation Modalities**: RGB (3x HxW), Visual-Tactile (3x HxW), Joint State (14 positions + 14 velocities), End-Effector (7x2 pose), Gripper (2 width).
- **Action Space**: Joint Control (14D position commands), End-Effector Pose Control (7-DOF pose per arm), Gripper Control (binary open/close). Actions can be absolute or relative deltas.
- **Data Processing Pipeline**: Raw data is processed at 30Hz, including temporal alignment, frame stacking (n_obs_steps consecutive frames), per-modality normalization, and quality filtering.
- **Dataset Statistics**: Over 120,000 episodes, 10-60s duration per episode, 30Hz sampling rate, totaling over 36 million frames and 1,000+ hours of multimodal data. It is stored in RoboMimic or LeRobot format.

#### 3.5. Cross-Modal Alignment

A CLIP-style contrastive learning framework aligns visual (V), tactile (T), and pose (P) modalities into a shared d-dimensional normalized latent space.
- **Contrastive Learning Framework**: Symmetric InfoNCE loss optimizes alignment, mapping semantically paired samples close together in the latent space. Embeddings are L2-normalized. A learnable temperature parameter controls softmax sharpness.
- **Modality Encoder Architectures**:
 - **Visual Encoder**: Frozen pretrained DINOv2 (ViT-B/14) backbone (768D features) with a trainable linear projection layer to the shared space. Temporal aggregation uses mean pooling or learnable attention pooling.
 - **Tactile Encoder**: A lightweight five-stage convolutional network (TactileCNNEncoder) (channels 3→32→64→128→256→d) with global average pooling, or frozen pretrained tactile foundation models (AnyTouch2, Sparsh) with a trainable linear projection head. Temporal aggregation mirrors visual encoder.
 - **Pose Encoder**: A four-layer MLP processes 14-dimensional robot state (12 joint angles, 2 gripper openings). It includes Batch Normalization, GELU activation, and Dropout, followed by a linear projection. Input keypoints are normalized.
- **Multi-Modal Fusion Encoder**: For dual-modality joint queries (e.g., VP→T), embeddings are concatenated and projected through a trainable linear fusion layer, then L2-normalized.
- **Training Configuration**: AdamW optimizer, initial learning rate 10^-4, weight decay 0.01, cosine annealing with linear warm-up (first 5% steps), mixed-precision, and gradient accumulation.
- **Retrieval Evaluation Metrics**: Recall@k (R@k) and Mean Average Precision (mAP) are used to quantify retrieval performance on a held-out test set.
- **Retrieval Experiments**: Evaluates bimodal mutual retrieval (V↔T, V↔P, T↔P) and trimodal retrieval (two-to-one, one-to-two queries). Comparisons are made against CCA and PLSCA baselines, both with randomly initialized CNNs and pretrained Sparsh backbones.

#### 3.6. Real-Robot Validation and Inference

- **Motivation**: Ensures policies accurately reproduce training actions and identifies issues (action mismatches, instability) prior to real-world deployment.
- **Overall Scoring System**: A four-layer progressive validation strategy assesses policy behavior:
 - **Layer 1 (Action Reconstruction)**: Measures MAE, MSE, and Expert Similarity between predicted and ground-truth expert actions on training data.
 - **Layer 2 (Single-Step Closed-Loop)**: Verifies physical reasonableness and smoothness through action statistics, jerk analysis, and physical validity checks.
 - **Layer 3 (Short-Horizon Rollout)**: Tests temporal consistency by running multi-step predictions from initial states, isolating policy behavior from observation noise (measures error trajectory, final error, error growth).
 - **Layer 4 (Consistency Evaluation)**: Measures output variance for stochastic policies to ensure reproducible behavior.
 An overall weighted score combines these component scores.
- **Experimental Validation**: Three policy implementations (ACT with single-frame observation, ACT with temporal context, and a diffusion-based policy) are validated on the VTOUCH dataset.
- **Real Robot Inference Pipeline**: Based on a ROS2 architecture, it integrates sensors, policies, and robot hardware.
 - **Observation Acquisition**: Multimodal observations (RGB, tactile, proprioception) from various sensors.
 - **Temporal Synchronization**: A buffering mechanism maintains a sliding window of recent observations, providing the latest synchronized tuple for inference.
 - **Action Space and Control Modes**: Supports Joint Space Control, End-Effector Pose Control, and Delta (incremental) Control.
 - **Action Processing and Safety**: Raw policy outputs undergo processing, including joint-level safety limits (clipping), velocity constraints, and optional temporal interpolation.
 - **Topic Configuration and Compatibility**: A configurable topic mapping scheme decouples policy code from ROS topic names, enabling compatibility across multiple robot setups and supporting checkpoints from RoboMimic and LeRobot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VTOUCH dataset and its associated evaluations present several key findings regarding large-scale multimodal data for bimanual manipulation.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics and Utility

- **Scale and Richness**: The VTOUCH dataset provides over 120,000 bimanual manipulation episodes, accumulating more than 1,000 hours of multimodal data. This includes approximately 36 million image frames and hundreds of millions of state records. It integrates synchronized joint-level proprioception, multi-view RGB-D observations, and high-resolution fingertip tactile signals.
- **Heterogeneous Embodiments**: Data collection spanned multiple robot embodiments, specifically fixed dual-arm platforms, wheeled-arm systems, and UMI-style mobile manipulators. This heterogeneity aims to support the development of generalizable policies across diverse robotic platforms.
- **Skill-Axis Task Design**: The proposed skill-axis framework allowed for the structured generation and categorization of over 380 bimanual tasks, composed of more than 100 atomic actions. This systematic organization is intended to facilitate skill transfer and generalization studies, moving beyond discrete task labels by semantically describing tasks along dimensions like coordination patterns, action types, and contact modes.

#### 4.2. Cross-Modal Retrieval Performance

The evaluation of cross-modal retrieval, using a CLIP-style contrastive learning framework, demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed approach in aligning visual, tactile, and proprioceptive (pose) modalities.
- **Superiority over Baselines**: The "Ours" method, employing learned encoders and a temperature-scaled InfoNCE objective, consistently outperformed all tested baselines (CCA and PLSCA, both with Random-CNN and Sparsh backbones) across all bimodal and trimodal retrieval configurations.
- **Bimodal Retrieval**: For example, in Visual-to-Tactile (V→T) retrieval, the "Ours" method achieved an R@1 of 0.24% and mAP of 1.23%, substantially higher than the best baseline (CCA with Sparsh) which achieved R@1 of 0.0837% and mAP of 0.5781%. Similar improvements were observed for other bimodal directions (T→V, T→P, P→T, V→P, P→V). The largest relative improvement was seen in Visual-to-Pose (V→P) retrieval, where "Ours" achieved an R@1 of 2.16% and mAP of 7.69%, significantly outperforming the best baseline's R@1 of 0.2897% and mAP of 1.4807%.
- **Trimodal Retrieval**: Even greater performance gains were observed in trimodal retrieval tasks, which involve fusing two modalities as a query or target. For instance, in Visual-Pose-to-Tactile (VP→T) retrieval, "Ours" achieved an R@10 of 2.64% and mAP of 1.39%, compared to the best baseline (CCA with Sparsh) at R@10 of 0.8304% and mAP of 0.5959%. The substantial gains in these complex retrieval tasks support the benefit of end-to-end training with learnable encoders and temperature parameters, indicating that the learned visual-tactile-pose representations capture meaningful cross-modal correspondences essential for contact-intensive bimanual manipulation.

#### 4.3. Real-Robot Validation and Policy Benchmarking

In-distribution validation on real robots using the VTOUCH dataset was performed with different policy implementations.
- **Policy Performance**: The diffusion-based policy achieved the best overall performance with the lowest Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.022 and the highest Expert Similarity of 0.848 in action reconstruction. The temporal Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) model demonstrated stable short-horizon behavior with a negative error growth of -0.010.
- **Reconstruction Quality**: The validation results indicate that the policies achieved moderate performance in action reconstruction, suggesting they can effectively fit expert demonstrations within the observed data regimes. This serves as an auxiliary validation of model capacity.
- **Validation Framework Utility**: The progressive four-layer validation strategy provides diagnostic information, allowing for targeted debugging beyond simple pass/fail metrics, which is beneficial for preparing policies for real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VTOUCH dataset and its accompanying research contributions hold several significances and potential impacts on the field of embodied intelligence and robotic manipulation.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical Data Gap

The primary significance of this work is its direct response to a recognized limitation in the current research landscape: the scarcity of large-scale, multimodal datasets for bimanual robot manipulation that integrate real-world physical interaction signals (vision, tactile, and proprioception) within a bimanual context. By providing such a dataset, VTOUCH offers a foundational resource that researchers can use to develop and evaluate advanced robotic manipulation policies. This avoids the inherent limitations of purely human-centric datasets (lack of robot-specific data) and simulation-based datasets (sim-to-real gap), offering physically grounded and verified interaction data.

#### 5.2. Enabling Advanced Multimodal Representation Learning

The dataset’s synchronized capture of visual, tactile, and proprioceptive data is crucial for advancing multimodal representation learning. The demonstrated effectiveness of the contrastive learning framework in cross-modal retrieval tasks indicates that the VTOUCH dataset facilitates the learning of robust, semantically meaningful representations that integrate these diverse sensory streams. This is particularly impactful for contact-intensive tasks where tactile feedback is critical but often overlooked or poorly integrated in existing approaches. The results suggest a potential shift from predominantly vision-driven learning pipelines towards truly multimodal, interaction-centric representations.

#### 5.3. Facilitating Systematic Research and Generalization

The skill-axis classification framework is a notable methodological contribution. By structuring demonstrations along orthogonal dimensions (e.g., coordination patterns, atomic actions, contact modes), the framework moves beyond monolithic task labels. This systematic organization enables fine-grained analysis of bimanual skills, controlled studies on the role of different sensory modalities or coordination complexities, and facilitates research into skill transfer and generalization across varying task compositions. This could lead to more modular and generalizable bimanual manipulation policies that can adapt to novel situations by recomposing atomic actions and coordination patterns.

#### 5.4. Advancing Real-World Robot Deployment

The collection of data from heterogeneous real-world robot platforms (fixed dual-arm, wheeled-arm, UMI-style manipulators) on the OpenLoong platform is significant for developing policies that are adaptable to diverse robot embodiments. The inclusion of a detailed real-robot validation and inference pipeline, incorporating a progressive multi-layer strategy, provides a practical framework for transitioning policies from training to real-world deployment. This systematic validation helps identify and diagnose issues that simulation might miss, enhancing the robustness and reliability of learned behaviors in physical systems.

#### 5.5. Broader Impact on Embodied AI Applications

The capabilities enabled by a dataset like VTOUCH are critical for the advancement of embodied intelligence in domains requiring complex physical interaction. This includes applications in household service (e.g., handling delicate objects, kitchen tasks), retail assistance (e.g., stocking shelves, packaging), and industrial assembly (e.g., precise part manipulation, collaborative tasks). The ability to perform contact-rich bimanual tasks robustly and adaptably is a key step towards more capable and autonomous robots operating in human-centric environments.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The work also lays the groundwork for several future research avenues. These include extending the dataset to more complex mobile humanoid embodiments, focusing on full task-level policy learning beyond short-horizon skill retrieval, exploring self-supervised or weakly supervised learning with unpaired multimodal data for greater scalability, and investigating physically consistent force annotation through inverse dynamics or sim-to-real transfer techniques.
