# Endowing GPT-4 with a Humanoid Body: Building the Bridge Between Off-the-Shelf VLMs and the Physical World

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University developed BiBo, a framework that enables off-the-shelf Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like GPT-4 to control humanoid agents by translating high-level user instructions into low-level physical commands. This system achieved an average success rate of 90.2% for single interaction tasks and 41.0% for composite tasks, outperforming baseline methods by 12.5% and 29.1% respectively, while generating physically plausible and precise motions at real-time speeds.

## Problem

- Humanoid agents often struggle with flexible and diverse interactions in open-ended physical environments due to their complex structure and the variability of the real world.
- Developing highly capable humanoid models traditionally relies on collecting large-scale, costly human-scene interaction datasets, which are difficult to generalize and prohibitively expensive.
- A significant gap exists between the high-level reasoning and open-world generalization abilities of powerful general-purpose VLMs and the precise low-level motor control required for humanoid agents to perform physical actions.

## Method

- ==The BiBo== framework introduces an "embodied instruction compiler" that leverages an off-the-shelf VLM (GPT-4o) to translate high-level user instructions into structured, low-level executor commands.
- The compiler employs a three-stage Visual Question Answering (VQA) pipeline (Basic Attribute Analysis, Agent Pose Reasoning, Key Joint Generation) with multi-round conversation, reflection, and voting mechanisms to generate precise action parameters.
- A "diffusion-based motion executor," built upon a Latent Diffusion Model (LDM), translates these structured commands into full-body humanoid motions by jointly decoding previous generated and actual executed motions, ensuring continuity and adapting to real-time physical feedback.

## Results

- BiBo achieved an average task success rate of **90.2%** for single interaction tasks and **41.0%** for composite tasks in randomly generated scenes, demonstrating a **12.5%** and **29.1%** improvement over state-of-the-art baselines, respectively.
- The system demonstrated high motion quality and efficiency, with an FID of **0.076**, an average inference time of **0.047** seconds per sequence (supporting >20Hz control), and superior control precision (e.g., MAE for Hand position: 0.0571m).
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the compiler's voting mechanism and image label utilization, and the executor's LDM design, causal attention, and integration of actual executed motion for adaptability and continuity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-the-shelf VLMs can effectively bridge the gap between high-level reasoning and low-level physical control for humanoids, reducing the need for extensive, task-specific interaction data collection.
> - A structured, coarse-to-fine command representation, including motion captions, 2D locations, facing directions, and key joint targets, is crucial for VLMs to effectively guide complex humanoid actions.
> - Integrating both previously generated motion and actual executed motion (which incorporates environmental feedback) into a diffusion-based motion generation model enables continuous, physically adaptive, and real-time humanoid control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid agents is advancing, with research focusing on scene perception and interaction. Current humanoid agents demonstrate capabilities in text-guided motion generation and executing predefined plans for interactive tasks. However, a significant challenge remains in enabling these agents to flexibly handle diverse user-intended scene interactions within open and dynamic physical environments.

Traditional approaches to developing highly capable humanoid models often rely on collecting large-scale human-scene interaction datasets. This data-centric scaling has been successful for less complex platforms like robotic arms or wheeled robots. However, due to the inherent structural complexity of humanoid bodies and the vast variability of the physical world, collecting sufficiently diverse and comprehensive data for humanoids becomes prohibitively expensive and presents difficulties in generalization. This limitation restricts the adaptability and versatility of humanoid agents in real-world scenarios.

In parallel, general-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) such as GPT-4, Gemini, and Qwen have demonstrated robust open-world reasoning and adaptability across a wide range of tasks, often without requiring task-specific fine-tuning. This capability presents an alternative paradigm for humanoid control, suggesting that direct leverage of these pre-trained, powerful VLMs could circumvent the costly and challenging data collection process. This research positions itself at the intersection of these two fields, aiming to bridge the gap between the high-level reasoning abilities of off-the-shelf VLMs and the low-level motor control requirements of humanoid agents in physical environments. The work proposes a framework to enable VLMs to control humanoids, thereby promoting more versatile interaction without extensive specialized data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable off-the-shelf Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to control humanoid agents, thereby leveraging the VLMs' strong open-world generalization capabilities to reduce the reliance on extensive, costly data collection for diverse physical interactions.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and challenges:
- **Limitations of Traditional Humanoid Control:** Humanoid agents frequently struggle with flexible and diverse interactions in open-ended environments. Existing solutions often necessitate the collection of massive datasets to train specialized models. This approach is costly, time-consuming, and presents generalization difficulties due to the high degrees of freedom in humanoid bodies and the vast complexity of real-world interactions.
- **Emergence of Powerful VLMs:** Off-the-shelf general-purpose VLMs have demonstrated remarkable reasoning and adaptability across a wide array of tasks without specific fine-tuning. This suggests a potential pathway to empower humanoids with advanced intelligence without the need for bespoke, large-scale interaction datasets.
- **Bridging High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Control:** A critical gap exists between the abstract, high-level understanding and planning capabilities of VLMs and the precise, low-level motor control required for a humanoid to execute physical actions. The research is motivated to construct a functional bridge between these two distinct layers of intelligence and control.

To address these, the specific objectives are:
1. **Develop a mechanism for VLMs to perceive and interpret physical environments:** The system needs to allow a VLM to understand the context of a user's instruction within a given scene.
2. **Translate high-level instructions into executable primitive commands:** The VLM must be capable of breaking down complex, natural language user instructions (e.g., "have a rest") into structured, low-level commands that include specific control parameters (e.g., "sit casually, location: (1, 2), facing: 90°").
3. **Generate human-like motions from these primitive commands:** The system needs a robust motion generation component that can translate structured commands into fluid, physically plausible humanoid movements.
4. **Incorporate dynamic adaptation to physical feedback:** The motion generation process must be able to account for real-time physical interactions, such as collisions or external forces, and adapt the generated motion sequences accordingly to maintain stability and task coherence.
5. **Achieve high success rates for diverse interaction tasks:** The integrated framework should demonstrate a notable improvement in performing both basic and complex interaction tasks in varied, open environments.
6. **Improve precision in text-guided motion execution:** The generated motions should accurately reflect the user's intent and command specifications.

The work conceptualizes its framework, BiBo, as analogous to a computer system, where an "embodied instruction compiler" translates high-level user instructions into low-level commands, and a "diffusion-based motion executor" interprets these commands to operate the humanoid "hardware." This analogy underpins the design philosophy of modularity and clear separation of concerns between reasoning and execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, BiBo (Building humanoId agent By Off-the-shelf VLMs), comprises two primary components: an embodied instruction compiler and a diffusion-based motion executor.

#### 3.1. Embodied Instruction Compiler

The embodied instruction compiler enables an off-the-shelf VLM (specifically GPT-4o) to translate high-level user instructions into low-level, structured executor commands based on environmental observations. This process is structured as a three-stage visual question-answering (VQA) pipeline, designed to progressively refine the action command.

1. **Basic Attribute Analysis:**
 - **Input:** User instruction, multi-view scene images with object labels, scene descriptions (object IDs, coordinates, parent-child relationships), and the agent's current status (pose, held objects, previous actions, ongoing action status).
 - **Process:** The compiler prompts the VLM to refine the user instruction into a sequence of clear, executable instructions. It then asks the VLM to determine the "next motion" to be executed, generate a brief motion caption, and identify basic attributes. These attributes include:
 - **Related Objects:** Categorized as "target" (intended interaction target), "at" (anchor for agent localization), and "by" (tool used).
 - **Key Joints:** Identification of important joints involved (e.g., pelvis, hands, feet, head).
 - **Interaction Type:** Classification into contact, non-contact, distal, or manipulate.
 - **Inverse Kinematics (IK) usage:** A boolean flag indicating whether precise IK control is required.
 - **Refinement:** To enhance accuracy, the final result for basic attributes is selected through majority voting across five parallel VLM instances.
 - **Multi-round conversation and rule-based reflection:** The VLM maintains a conversation history. A reflection mechanism detects errors (e.g., format violations, conflicting actions, implausible spatial relationships) and triggers a rollback and, in logical inconsistency cases, inserts the reflection result into the conversation history to guide future reasoning.

2. **Agent Pose Reasoning:**
 - **Objective:** To infer the agent's location and facing direction relative to the anchor object.
 - **Process:** Instead of direct coordinate output, which VLMs struggle with, this stage transforms pose prediction into a visual identification task.
 - **Relative Direction:** Multi-view images of the anchor and target objects are captured. Labels are uniformly placed around the anchor object at specific intervals, each corresponding to a relative direction. The VLM, provided with these labeled images and textual descriptions, selects one label as the agent's relative direction. Orient Anything is used to predict object orientation and confidence scores. For non-distal interactions, labels represent standing locations.
 - **Distance from Anchor:** A simplified bird's-eye view (BEV) map of the scene (showing walkable areas, obstacles, agent, and object positions) is presented to the VLM. Along the predicted direction, distance labels are placed, and the VLM selects a standing location. This step is skipped for non-distal interactions, fixing the distance at 0.4m.
 - **Facing Direction:** Using the BEV map, candidate facing directions (arrows with angles and target objects) are displayed around the standing location. The VLM selects the final facing direction.

3. **Key Joint Generation:**
 - **Objective:** To specify target positions for key joints relative to object surfaces.
 - **Process:** For each key joint, an 8x8 grid of labels is overlaid on a scene image of the anchor object from the relevant viewpoint. The VLM selects a label as an anchor point on the object surface and then infers the joint's direction and distance relative to this point (e.g., "up," "down," "0.2m"). For smaller objects or when force exertion is needed, simplified strategies are applied (e.g., direct object center placement).
 - **Structured Command Output:** The compiler outputs a structured command C = {c, l, f, J}, including motion caption (c), 2D location (l), facing direction (f), and a set of key joint targets (J). This command simplifies motion complexity while retaining critical information.

#### 3.2. Motion Diffusion Executor

The motion diffusion executor translates the structured command from the compiler into full-body humanoid motions. It is a Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) composed of a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) and a Diffusion module.

1. **VAE Design:**
 - **Architecture:** A shared 9-layer Skip-Transformer architecture for both encoder and decoder. Each layer uses 4-head multi-head attention.
 - **Encoding:** The VAE encodes two motion sequences: the previously generated motion (M_g) and the actual executed motion (M_a) (which incorporates physical feedback from the environment) into latent representations (S_g and S_a).
 - **Decoding:** The VAE jointly decodes the latents of the previous (S_g) and newly generated future motion (S_f) into future joint trajectories (M_f). This joint decoding is crucial for ensuring smooth transitions between motion segments.
 - **Causal Self-Cross Attention:** A causal mask is applied within self-attention layers, and a growing memory window is used for cross-attention. This design ensures temporal causality, meaning each motion frame or latent token can only attend to its preceding tokens or frames, thus preventing future information leakage and enforcing continuity.

2. **Diffusion Module:**
 - **Architecture:** A 9-layer Transformer Decoder with skip connections as the diffusion backbone.
 - **Denoising Process:** The Diffusion module extends future motion latents (S_f) from Gaussian noise, guided by the structured command (C) and the latent representation of the actual executed motion (S_a).
 - **Conditioning:** Motion captions are encoded using a pre-trained text encoder (BERT), and other control parameters (location, facing, joint targets) are encoded via an MLP. These tokens, along with S_a, condition the denoising process through cross-attention.
 - **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** Employed to modulate the strength of the control conditions during denoising.
 - **Environmental Awareness and Continuity:** By conditioning the diffusion on S_a, the future motion M_f inherently accounts for physical feedback from the environment. The joint decoding by the VAE ensures that M_f is continuous with M_g, even when S_a (actual executed motion) deviates from M_g due to environmental interactions.

3. **Post-Optimization and Tracking:**
 - **Inverse Kinematics (IK) Post-Optimization:** After motion generation, an IK step (custom FABRIK algorithm for upper limbs) refines the posture to improve control accuracy, especially for grasp targets.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Tracking Policy:** A pre-trained PHC tracking policy drives the humanoid joints to follow the generated trajectories in the physical simulation environment (IsaacGym).

#### 3.3. Online Control Loop

These components form an online control loop: user instruction triggers the compiler, which generates a command. This command is processed by a state machine (navigation or interaction states). The state machine feeds the command to the executor, which generates motion. The humanoid executes the motion in the physical environment, and physical feedback influences subsequent generations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate BiBo's capabilities in task completion and motion quality, comparing it against several state-of-the-art methods and conducting ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Task Completion

- **Task Setting:** Six types of single interaction tasks (Reach, Watch, Sit, Sleep, Touch, Lift) and three levels of composite tasks (Simple, Medium, Hard) were defined with specific success criteria (e.g., distance, angular error, force application).
- **Dataset:** 100 randomly generated scenes using InfiniGen, resulting in 1,365 single tasks and 162 composite tasks. Tasks were constructed semi-automatically, with LLMs generating tasks conditioned on examples and scene information.
- **Comparison Methods:** UniHSI, HumanVLA, TokenHSI, and CLoSD. These methods often lacked an integrated planner, so they were provided with programmatically generated ground-truth action plans. BiBo was evaluated with both online planning and ground-truth plans.
- **Results:**
 - **Overall Performance:** BiBo achieved an average success rate of 90.2% for single interactions and 41.0% for composite tasks with online planning.
 - **Comparison with Baselines:** BiBo demonstrated an average improvement of 12.5% in single interaction tasks and 29.1% in composite tasks compared to other methods. It outperformed all baselines across most task categories. For instance, in "Sit" tasks, BiBo (95.84%) significantly surpassed UniHSI (81.03%), TokenHSI (72.95%), and CLoSD (76.99%). In "Lift" tasks, BiBo (65.42%) showed substantial improvement over HumanVLA (44.90%), TokenHSI (48.19%), and CLoSD (7.71%). For composite tasks, BiBo (Simple: 58.82%, Medium: 36.54%, Hard: 27.78%) substantially outcompeted CLoSD (Simple: 26.47%, Medium: 7.05%, Hard: 2.38%).
 - **Online vs. Ground-Truth Planning:** BiBo's online planning performance was close to that achieved with ground-truth plans (within 4.38%), indicating the effectiveness of the VLM-driven compiler.
- **Ablation Study (Compiler & Executor Components):**
 - **Compiler:** The voting mechanism improved the task success rate by 4.1%, and using image labels for visual reasoning improved it by 22.9%.
 - **Executor:** The IK post-optimization improved joint control accuracy. Incorporating actual executed motion enhanced physical adaptability, while using previous generated motion inputs contributed to precise interaction. Visual information and reflection further improved planning quality, and moving speed control enhanced locomotion.

#### 4.2. Motion Quality

- **Setting:** Evaluated quantitatively using Average Inference Time per Sequence (AITS), Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), R-Precision (R.P.@1, R.P.@2, R.P.@3), Diversity, Physical Plausibility (Penetration, Float, Skate), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for control precision. Qualitative evaluation included user studies and visual inspections.
- **Dataset:** HumanML3D dataset, with motion sequences between 40 and 200 frames.
- **Comparison Methods:** MDM, MotionLCM, MotionStreamer, MoGenTS, MoConVQ, DiP, and CLoSD.
- **Quantitative Results:**
 - **Efficiency:** BiBo achieved a fast AITS of 0.047 seconds, supporting on-the-fly control (>20Hz), comparable to MotionLCM (0.042s).
 - **Text Alignment & Realism:** BiBo showed strong text alignment (R.P.@1: 0.542, R.P.@2: 0.738, R.P.@3: 0.829) and improved motion realism (FID: 0.076) compared to other real-time arbitrary-length generation methods like MotionStreamer (FID: 0.084) and DiP (FID: 0.210).
 - **Physical Plausibility:** While primarily a non-physical approach, BiBo (with physical tracking) demonstrated comparable physical plausibility metrics (Penetration: 0.19, Float: 20.6, Skate: 0.01) to dedicated physical methods like CLoSD (Penetration: 0.30, Float: 20.2, Skate: 0.01).
 - **Control Precision:** BiBo achieved the highest control precision (MAE: Head 0.0310m, Hand 0.0571m, Foot 0.0335m), significantly outperforming DiP and MotionLCM.
- **Ablation Study (Executor Motion Generation):**
 - LDM and causal attention designs in the VAE improved motion quality and continuity.
 - Incorporating previously generated motion effectively reduced discontinuity during motion transitions.
 - Incorporating actual executed motion, while potentially affecting raw generation quality, significantly enhanced adaptability to environmental interactions (as shown in task success rates).
 - Visualization of collision handling demonstrated that BiBo’s approach (integrating both actual executed and previously generated motion) preserved motion continuity while adapting to physical collisions, unlike methods relying solely on one or the other, which led to falls or jitters.
- **Qualitative Results and Case Studies:**
 - **User Study:** BiBo's generated motions and scene interactions were preferred by participants over those from MotionLCM and DiP(CLoSD).
 - **Visual Evaluation:** Compared to UniHSI, BiBo generated more natural motions. Against HumanVLA, BiBo successfully handled object transportation even when the initial agent pose was not perfectly aligned. MoConVQ showed limited motion activity, while CLoSD struggled with precise control, areas where BiBo demonstrated superior performance.
 - **VLM Variants:** Experiments with different VLM sizes (Claude Sonnet 3.5, Qwen2.5-VL, GPT-4o mini, GPT-4o) showed that BiBo's prompt design generalized well, and task success rates generally improved with larger VLM models, confirming the scaling ability.
 - **Accuracy of VQA Stages:** Voting mechanism improved basic attribute analysis accuracy by 10.3%. Orient Anything and textual label descriptions improved pose reasoning accuracy by 31.9%. Label grids for key joint generation improved localization by 55.9%.

#### 4.3. Limitations and Failure Cases

- **Executor Generalization:** The motion executor's generalization capability is limited by the size of its training dataset (HumanML3D).
- **Environmental Modeling:** While incorporating physical feedback, the model does not explicitly model environmental geometry (e.g., height maps, point clouds), which could further enhance proactive obstacle avoidance and fine-grained action decomposition.
- **Interaction Modes:** The current framework focuses on human-scene interactions, with potential for expansion to hand-object and human-human interactions.
- **VLM Limitations:** Failure cases included the VLM misinterpreting scene layout, mismatches between textual labels and objects, or failing to handle multiple spatial relations correctly. Lack of explicit scene geometry modeling in the executor also contributed to issues in fine-grained action decomposition and proactive obstacle avoidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents BiBo, a novel framework that endows off-the-shelf Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with the capability to control humanoid agents. Its significance lies in several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the VLM-to-Physical-World Gap:** BiBo successfully establishes a bridge between the high-level reasoning and generalization abilities of VLMs and the low-level physical execution requirements of humanoid robots. This represents a substantial step towards leveraging general-purpose AI models for complex embodied tasks, potentially reducing the need for extensive, domain-specific data collection for humanoid control.
2. **Novel Structured Action Representation:** The introduction of an embodied instruction compiler that translates natural language into a structured humanoid action command (including motion caption, location, facing, and key joint targets) is a notable advancement. This structured representation facilitates coarse-to-fine embodied reasoning, improving humanoid behavior planning and modeling by providing a clear, actionable intermediate representation for the VLM.
3. **Advanced Motion Generation with Environmental Feedback:** The development of a diffusion-based motion executor, particularly its novel application of the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) with causal attention and joint decoding of both generated and actual executed motions, is significant. This approach effectively incorporates real-time environmental feedback while maintaining motion continuity, leading to state-of-the-art performance in arbitrary-length, real-time motion synthesis with enhanced physical plausibility and control precision. This LDM application also offers insights for its use in broader domains requiring dynamic adaptation.
4. **Enhanced Task Success and Generalization:** The experimental results demonstrate BiBo's high interaction task success rates in varied, randomly generated environments. This capability, achieved primarily through leveraging VLM reasoning rather than extensive task-specific data, suggests a pathway towards more generalized and flexible humanoid agents capable of operating in diverse, open-world settings.
5. **Reduced Data Dependence:** By utilizing off-the-shelf VLMs, BiBo offers an alternative to the prohibitively expensive and challenging data-centric scaling approaches common in robotics. This could accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent humanoid agents by lowering the barrier to entry for complex interaction capabilities.

The potential impact of this work extends to various applications where humanoids can interact naturally and adaptably with their surroundings, such as assistive robotics, intelligent manufacturing, interactive entertainment, and simulation environments. As VLMs continue to evolve and 3D encoding technologies improve, BiBo's framework lays a foundation for future advancements in more robust, context-aware, and physically intelligent humanoid agents.
