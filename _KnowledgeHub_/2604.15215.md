# A Hierarchical Spatiotemporal Action Tokenizer for In-Context Imitation Learning in Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> Retrocausal, Inc. researchers developed HiST-AT, a Hierarchical Spatiotemporal Action Tokenizer, to improve in-context imitation learning by using hierarchical clustering and spatiotemporal reconstruction for robot actions. This framework achieved a 59% average success rate on RoboCasa, a 6% increase over previous methods, and a 67.0% average success rate on ManiSkill, demonstrating enhanced generalization and transferability.

## Problem

- Existing action tokenizers in in-context imitation learning (ICIL) struggle to capture comprehensive action structures, often relying on 'flat' clustering methods.
- Current action representation methods insufficiently exploit temporal cues, leading to a lack of temporal smoothness critical for stable and continuous robot control.
- Robotic policies learned via ICIL often exhibit limited generalization, particularly when adapting to novel tasks or sparse demonstration data.

## Method

- The **HiST-AT** (Hierarchical Spatiotemporal Action Tokenizer) framework integrates ==hierarchical vector quantization== using two learned codebooks for ==subactions== and ==actions==.
- A novel ==spatiotemporal reconstruction module== jointly reconstructs original robot actions (spatial cues) and their corresponding ==timestamps== (temporal cues) for ==self-supervised representation learning==.
- ==Lipschitz regularization== is applied to maintain smoothness in latent action representations across the hierarchical quantization levels, ensuring ==temporal consistency==.

## Results

- HiST-AT achieved an average success rate of **59%** on the RoboCasa benchmark, outperforming the previous best action tokenizer, ICRT+LipVQ-VAE, by **6%**.
- On ManiSkill, HiST-AT attained an average success rate of **67.0%**, surpassing ACT+LipVQ-VAE by **5.3%** across tasks like Pick Cube, Push Cube, and Stack Cube.
- The model demonstrated superior cross-dataset transferability (**62.5%** success rate on Human dataset vs. **52.5%** for LipVQ-VAE) and improved zero-shot generalization (**11.4%** vs. **5.2%** for LipVQ-VAE) on RoboCasa.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling actions hierarchically, from fine-grained sub-actions to higher-level actions, significantly enhances the quality and structure of action representations for ICIL.
> - Explicitly leveraging both spatial and temporal information through joint reconstruction enables the learning of more robust and temporally coherent action tokens.
> - A moderate weighting for temporal reconstruction (e.g., λ_temp = 0.02) is crucial for optimal performance, as excessive emphasis on timestamp prediction can hinder the learning of effective action semantics.
