# A Hierarchical Spatiotemporal Action Tokenizer for In-Context Imitation Learning in Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> Retrocausal, Inc. researchers developed HiST-AT, a Hierarchical Spatiotemporal Action Tokenizer, to improve in-context imitation learning by using hierarchical clustering and spatiotemporal reconstruction for robot actions. This framework achieved a 59% average success rate on RoboCasa, a 6% increase over previous methods, and a 67.0% average success rate on ManiSkill, demonstrating enhanced generalization and transferability.

## Problem

- Existing action tokenizers in in-context imitation learning (ICIL) struggle to capture comprehensive action structures, often relying on 'flat' clustering methods.
- Current action representation methods insufficiently exploit temporal cues, leading to a lack of temporal smoothness critical for stable and continuous robot control.
- Robotic policies learned via ICIL often exhibit limited generalization, particularly when adapting to novel tasks or sparse demonstration data.

## Method

- The **HiST-AT** (Hierarchical Spatiotemporal Action Tokenizer) framework integrates ==hierarchical vector quantization== using two learned codebooks for ==subactions== and ==actions==.
- A novel ==spatiotemporal reconstruction module== jointly reconstructs original robot actions (spatial cues) and their corresponding ==timestamps== (temporal cues) for ==self-supervised representation learning==.
- ==Lipschitz regularization== is applied to maintain smoothness in latent action representations across the hierarchical quantization levels, ensuring ==temporal consistency==.

## Results

- HiST-AT achieved an average success rate of **59%** on the RoboCasa benchmark, outperforming the previous best action tokenizer, ICRT+LipVQ-VAE, by **6%**.
- On ManiSkill, HiST-AT attained an average success rate of **67.0%**, surpassing ACT+LipVQ-VAE by **5.3%** across tasks like Pick Cube, Push Cube, and Stack Cube.
- The model demonstrated superior cross-dataset transferability (**62.5%** success rate on Human dataset vs. **52.5%** for LipVQ-VAE) and improved zero-shot generalization (**11.4%** vs. **5.2%** for LipVQ-VAE) on RoboCasa.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling actions hierarchically, from fine-grained sub-actions to higher-level actions, significantly enhances the quality and structure of action representations for ICIL.
> - Explicitly leveraging both spatial and temporal information through joint reconstruction enables the learning of more robust and temporally coherent action tokens.
> - A moderate weighting for temporal reconstruction (e.g., λ_temp = 0.02) is crucial for optimal performance, as excessive emphasis on timestamp prediction can hinder the learning of effective action semantics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically addressing challenges in imitation learning (IL). Imitation learning aims to enable robots to acquire skills from expert demonstrations. A known limitation of traditional IL is its struggle with generalization, often necessitating the collection of extensive, high-quality, and task-specific demonstrations for effective learning and adaptation to new tasks.

In recent years, the paradigm of in-context imitation learning (ICIL) has emerged as a promising alternative, inspired by the in-context learning capabilities observed in large language models (LLMs). ICIL allows robotic policies to infer and execute new tasks by conditioning on demonstrations provided at inference time, without requiring model retraining. This approach offers increased flexibility and efficiency for real-world robotic deployment.

Despite the advantages of ICIL, a significant challenge remains in effectively learning contextualized action representations from demonstrations. Existing action tokenization methods, which discretize and encode robot actions, have explored various strategies, including discretizing action dimensions, parameterizing actions with neural networks, and learning low-dimensional embeddings using variational autoencoders (VAEs). While some works integrate action tokenization into vision-language-action (VLA) models, a recurring issue across many of these approaches is a lack of temporal smoothness in tokenized action trajectories, which can negatively impact stable and successful robotic manipulation. Efforts to enhance smoothness have included techniques like Lipschitz regularization, Gaussian process priors, and action aggregation.

A direct predecessor to this work is LipVQ-VAE, an action tokenizer based on a vector-quantized autoencoder (VQ-VAE). LipVQ-VAE employs flat clustering of input actions and primarily relies on spatial cues by reconstructing actions, with temporal smoothness enforced through Lipschitz regularization.

The presented research builds upon these prior efforts by drawing inspiration from advances in other domains, particularly temporal action segmentation. The authors note the success of spatiotemporal reconstruction and hierarchical vector quantization in this area. By integrating these concepts, the current work seeks to overcome the limitations of existing action tokenizers in ICIL, specifically regarding their inability to capture hierarchical action structures and explicit spatiotemporal dependencies. The paper aims to develop more effective and transferable action representations that inherently possess temporal smoothness and structural coherence, thereby enhancing the generalization and performance of ICIL systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and generalization capabilities of in-context imitation learning in robotics by developing a more effective action tokenizer. The authors identify a critical bottleneck in current ICIL systems: their struggle to learn robust contextualized action representations from demonstrations.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing action tokenizers:
1. **Limited Temporal Modeling:** Many existing action tokenizers, including those that enforce smoothness (e.g., LipVQ-VAE), primarily focus on spatial information by reconstructing individual actions. They often fail to explicitly model or preserve temporal smoothness across action trajectories, leading to potential discontinuities and noise in tokenized actions. Temporal smoothness is crucial for stable and continuous robot control.
2. **Lack of Hierarchical Structure:** Previous methods typically employ a "flat" clustering approach for discretizing actions. This means they treat actions as individual units without explicitly capturing inherent hierarchical structures, where short-term sub-action primitives combine to form longer-term, coherent actions. Understanding such hierarchies could provide richer, more abstract representations.
3. **Ineffective Transferability:** The aforementioned limitations can lead to action representations that are less effective and less transferable to novel tasks or environments, hindering the generalization capabilities that ICIL aims to provide.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a novel hierarchical spatiotemporal action tokenizer, termed HiST-AT. The key objectives articulated in the paper are:
- To develop a hierarchical vector quantization framework for action tokenization in ICIL. This involves a multi-level clustering approach designed to discover both fine-grained sub-action primitives and higher-level coherent action clusters. The initial step is to demonstrate that this hierarchical approach, even when primarily exploiting spatial information via action reconstruction, can outperform non-hierarchical baselines.
- To further integrate temporal information into the action tokenizer. This involves explicitly modeling spatiotemporal dependencies by jointly reconstructing both input actions (spatial cues) and their associated timestamps (temporal cues) during the tokenization process.
- To empirically evaluate the proposed HiST-AT on diverse simulation and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks, demonstrating its superior performance and generalization capabilities compared to existing state-of-the-art methods in in-context imitation learning. The goal is to show that HiST-AT can extract more effective and transferable action representations by capturing both hierarchical structures and spatiotemporal correlations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach introduces a Hierarchical Spatiotemporal Action Tokenizer (HiST-AT), which is integrated into an In-Context Imitation Learning (ICIL) framework based on ICRT. The ICIL framework models robotic manipulation as a next-token prediction problem, where a transformer auto-regressively processes tokenized prompt observations and actions to predict query actions. The HiST-AT is responsible for generating the action tokens.

The HiST-AT architecture comprises two primary modules: hierarchical clustering and spatiotemporal reconstruction.

**Latent Representations of Robot Actions:**
1. **Encoder:** An encoder network, $f_{\theta}$, maps each input robot action $x$ (consisting of relative position and gripper angle) from a sequence $X$ into a dense latent representation $v$.
2. **Lipschitz Regularization:** The latent representations $V$ are then processed by a Lipschitz-conditioned network $f_{\psi}$, which applies Lipschitz regularization to enforce smoothness. This yields Lipschitz-regularized latent representations $V'$. The Lipschitz conditioning in each layer is achieved by scaling layer weights based on a trainable Lipschitz bound, promoting continuity in the latent space.

**Hierarchical Clustering:**
This module performs a two-level vector quantization process:
1. **First Quantization Level (Subclusters):** Each Lipschitz-regularized latent vector $v'_{k} \in V'$ is mapped to its closest prototype $z_{j^*}$ within a subaction codebook $Z = \{z_j\}^{\alpha K}_{j=1}$. The codebook $Z$ contains $\alpha K$ subaction prototypes, where $K$ is the number of main action clusters and $\alpha$ is a ratio parameter. This mapping uses an $L_2$ distance criterion. The resulting quantized vectors form $Q_Z$.
2. **Intermediate Lipschitz Regularization:** $Q_Z$ is subsequently passed through another Lipschitz-conditioned network $f_{\omega}$, which further refines and smooths the subaction prototypes, yielding $Q_Z'$.
3. **Second Quantization Level (Clusters):** Each Lipschitz-regularized subaction prototype $q_{Z',k} \in Q_Z'$ is then mapped to its closest prototype $a_{i^*}$ within a higher-level action codebook $A = \{a_i\}^K_{i=1}$. The codebook $A$ contains $K$ action prototypes. This process generates the final quantized representations $Q_A$.

**Spatiotemporal Reconstruction:**
This module utilizes the learned representations for reconstruction, exploiting both spatial and temporal cues:
1. **Spatial Reconstruction:** The final quantized representation $Q_A$ is fed into a spatial decoder. This decoder's architecture mirrors that of the encoder and aims to reconstruct the original input robot actions, producing $\hat{X}$. The objective is to ensure that the compressed action tokens retain sufficient information to accurately represent the original spatial action.
2. **Temporal Reconstruction:** The intermediate Lipschitz-regularized subaction prototypes $Q_Z'$ are passed to a temporal decoder. This decoder, implemented as a simple MLP network, predicts the timestamps associated with the actions, yielding $\hat{T}$. This explicit timestamp prediction is designed to capture and preserve temporal dependencies and smoothness within the action sequences.

**Training Losses:**
The HiST-AT model, including the encoder, regularizers, codebooks, and decoders, is trained using a composite loss function:
1. **Hierarchical Clustering Losses:**
 - **Commitment Losses ($L_{commit,Z}$, $L_{commit,A}$):** These losses encourage the latent vectors ($v'_k$) and intermediate prototypes ($q_{Z',k}$) to remain close to their assigned codebook entries ($q_{Z,k}$ and $q_{A,k}$, respectively). A stop-gradient operator is applied to the codebook entries.
 - **Codebook Losses ($L_{codebook,Z}$, $L_{codebook,A}$):** These losses update the codebook entries ($q_{Z,k}$ and $q_{A,k}$) to move closer to the latent vectors ($v'_k$) and intermediate prototypes ($q_{Z',k}$), respectively. A stop-gradient operator is applied to the latent vectors/prototypes.
 - These are combined into $L_{vq,Z} = L_{commit,Z} + L_{codebook,Z}$ and $L_{vq,A} = L_{commit,A} + L_{codebook,A}$.
2. **Spatiotemporal Reconstruction Losses:**
 - **Spatial Reconstruction Loss ($L_{spat}$):** This is the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the reconstructed actions $\hat{X}$ and the original input actions $X$.
 - **Temporal Reconstruction Loss ($L_{temp}$):** This is the MSE between the predicted timestamps $\hat{T}$ and the ground truth timestamps $T$.
3. **Final Loss:** The overall training loss is a weighted sum: $L = \lambda_{vq}(L_{vq,Z} + L_{vq,A}) + \lambda_{spat}L_{spat} + \lambda_{temp}L_{temp} + \lambda_{reg}(L_{reg,Z} + L_{reg,A})$.
 - $\lambda_{vq}$, $\lambda_{spat}$, $\lambda_{temp}$, and $\lambda_{reg}$ are hyperparameters that control the weights of the hierarchical clustering, spatial reconstruction, temporal reconstruction, and Lipschitz regularization terms, respectively. The $L_{reg,Z}$ and $L_{reg,A}$ refer to Lipschitz regularization losses associated with the codebooks, which ensure smoothness of the codebook embeddings.

This comprehensive approach allows HiST-AT to learn action representations that capture both fine-grained and high-level structural information, while simultaneously ensuring temporal consistency and smoothness through joint spatiotemporal reconstruction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive evaluations on both simulation and real robotic manipulation benchmarks, comparing HiST-AT against several established methods and alternative action tokenizers.

**Simulation Robotic Manipulation Results:**
- **RoboCasa Dataset:** Experiments on seven tasks in the RoboCasa environment (MimicGen dataset) showed that HiST-AT achieved an average success rate of 59.0%. This represents a notable improvement over the previous best-performing method, ICRT+LipVQ-VAE, which achieved 53.0%. The performance gap between HiST-AT and the lowest performing MLP-based tokenizer was 14.8%. HiST-AT demonstrated superior performance across multiple individual tasks.
- **ManiSkill Dataset:** Evaluation on three tasks in ManiSkill, where HiST-AT was integrated into a modified ACT framework, yielded an average success rate of 67.0%. This surpassed ICRT+LipVQ-VAE by 5.3%, further indicating the effectiveness of HiST-AT in learning robust action representations.

**Ablation Results:**
- **Impacts of Model Components (RoboCasa):** An ablation study analyzed the individual contributions of hierarchical clustering and spatiotemporal reconstruction:
 - Starting from the LipVQ-VAE baseline (53.0% average success rate).
 - Adding only spatiotemporal reconstruction improved performance to 55.2%.
 - Adding only hierarchical clustering improved performance to 57.3%.
 - Incorporating both components (HiST-AT) resulted in the highest average success rate of 59.0%, demonstrating that these modules provide complementary gains.
- **Impacts of Codebook Sizes (ManiSkill):** Investigation into the sizes of the subaction ($\alpha K$) and action ($K$) codebooks showed that a configuration of (64, 16) achieved optimal overall performance. Increasing the number of subaction clusters from 32 to 64 (i.e., (32,16) to (64,16)) improved performance, suggesting benefits from capturing finer-grained dynamics. However, further increasing the number of action clusters from 16 to 32 (i.e., (64,16) to (64,32)) did not provide additional gains, indicating potential redundancy in larger codebook sizes.
- **Impacts of $\lambda_{temp}$ (ManiSkill):** Analysis of the temporal reconstruction weight $\lambda_{temp}$ revealed that a moderate value of 0.02 was most effective. Both very low and very high values of $\lambda_{temp}$ led to decreased performance, suggesting that a balanced emphasis on temporal reconstruction is crucial to avoid hindering the learning of action representations.

**Generalization Capabilities:**
- **Cross-Dataset Results (RoboCasa MimicGen $\rightarrow$ Human):** When transferring from the MimicGen dataset to the Human dataset (which features sparser and less structured object arrangements), HiST-AT demonstrated improved robustness. It outperformed ICRT+LipVQ-VAE by an average of 5% (57.5% vs. 52.5%), suggesting that its hierarchical and spatiotemporal representations are more transferable.
- **Zero-Shot Results (RoboCasa):** In zero-shot experiments (training on a subset of tasks and testing on unseen tasks), HiST-AT achieved an average success rate of 9.0%, outperforming ICRT+LipVQ-VAE (5.2%) by 3.8%. This indicates stronger generalization to unseen action sequences.

**Real-World Robotic Manipulation Results (Sim-to-Real):**
- Evaluations on a UR5e arm for Pick Cube and Stack Cube tasks, using a dataset combining synthetic and real demonstrations, showed that HiST-AT achieved higher success rates (Pick Cube: 0.23 vs. 0.19; Stack Cube: 0.14 vs. 0.12) compared to ICRT+LipVQ-VAE. The authors also noted that HiST-AT produced smoother motions in real-world settings, despite the inherent challenges of sim-to-real transfer.

In summary, the main findings consistently demonstrate that HiST-AT establishes new performance benchmarks in ICIL across diverse simulation and real-world scenarios. The ablation studies confirm that both hierarchical clustering and spatiotemporal reconstruction are integral components contributing to these performance gains, fostering more robust and generalizable action representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces a hierarchical spatiotemporal action tokenizer (HiST-AT) that addresses critical limitations in learning effective action representations for in-context imitation learning (ICIL) in robotics. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several aspects:

1. **Advancement in Action Representation Learning:** The work significantly advances the field of action representation learning by explicitly modeling both hierarchical structures and spatiotemporal dependencies within robot actions. Unlike previous methods that often relied on flat clustering and primarily spatial reconstruction, HiST-AT provides a more comprehensive and nuanced representation.
2. **Improved Performance in ICIL:** The consistent outperformance of HiST-AT over prior state-of-the-art methods across multiple simulation environments (RoboCasa, ManiSkill) and in real-world robotic manipulation tasks demonstrates its effectiveness. This leads to higher success rates in complex manipulation tasks, which is a direct measure of policy quality.
3. **Enhanced Generalization and Transferability:** The results from cross-dataset and zero-shot experiments are particularly impactful. By showing improved performance in these challenging scenarios, the research indicates that HiST-AT learns action representations that are more robust and transferable to unseen tasks and environments. This is crucial for reducing the data burden associated with deploying robots in diverse real-world settings, where collecting exhaustive demonstrations for every new task is impractical.
4. **Foundation for Scalable Robot Learning:** Better generalization in ICIL contributes to the broader goal of scalable robot learning. The ability for robots to adapt to new tasks from a few demonstrations at inference time, without requiring costly retraining, is a key enabler for widespread robotic autonomy. HiST-AT's contributions move closer to achieving this flexibility.
5. **Deeper Understanding of Action Structure:** The hierarchical clustering component of HiST-AT allows for the discovery of sub-action primitives that compose into higher-level actions. This provides a structured understanding of robot behaviors, which could potentially be leveraged for more interpretable and controllable robot policies.
6. **Improved Smoothness and Stability in Robot Control:** By explicitly incorporating temporal reconstruction and maintaining Lipschitz smoothness, HiST-AT contributes to generating smoother and more continuous action trajectories. This characteristic is vital for stable and reliable physical robot operation, minimizing jerky movements and potential wear or failures.
7. **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper identifies future work, such as exploring more advanced temporal objectives and decoders. This suggests avenues for further optimizing the capture of temporal dynamics, potentially leading to even more robust and performant action tokenizers.

In conclusion, this work represents a step towards more capable and adaptable robotic systems. By providing a method for learning effective, generalizable, and temporally coherent action representations, it contributes to the development of robots that can more flexibly and efficiently learn and execute tasks from demonstrations in complex, dynamic environments.
