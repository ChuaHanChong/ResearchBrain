# Precise Aggressive Aerial Maneuvers with Sensorimotor Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Zhejiang University developed sensorimotor policies enabling quadrotors to perform precise aggressive aerial maneuvers through narrow openings using only onboard vision and proprioception. The framework achieves high success rates for single, dynamic, and consecutive gap traversals, demonstrating robust reactive control and adaptability to diverse gap geometries.

## Problem

- Autonomous quadrotors face challenges in performing precise aggressive maneuvers through narrow openings using only onboard sensing, often relying on external localization infrastructure.
- Traditional modular architectures, which separate state estimation, trajectory planning, and control, suffer from information loss, error accumulation, and inflexibility in complex, constrained environments.
- Direct application of model-free reinforcement learning is difficult due to high-dimensional, partially observable inputs and extremely sparse rewards within the narrow feasible solution space of tight SE(3) constraints.

## Method

- A two-stage policy distillation framework trains a 'teacher' policy using online ==reinforcement learning== with low-dimensional surrogate observations, then distills its behavior into a 'student' policy operating directly on high-dimensional visual and proprioceptive inputs.
- An 'Informed Reset' strategy initializes the quadrotor to states derived from model-based optimal trajectories, significantly accelerating RL exploration in sparse reward environments.
- A comprehensive suite of sim-to-real transfer techniques, including perturbation forces, response randomization, and perceptual latency modeling, enhances robustness for zero-shot deployment on physical hardware.

## Results

- Quadrotors successfully navigated 20 cm x 60 cm rectangular gaps with a 5 cm clearance, achieving **90%** success for gap roll angles up to 90° and **73.3%** for pitch angles up to 60°.
- The policies demonstrated reactive control by traversing dynamic, handheld moving gaps without explicit dynamic training, and adapted to diverse gap geometries (e.g., triangular, elliptical) without requiring manually defined traversal states.
- Ablation studies confirmed that 'Informed Reset' significantly improved RL exploration efficiency (**96%** success with 1G samples vs. **70%** without) and that sim-to-real techniques were crucial for maintaining performance at extreme tilt angles, with learned policies exhibiting greater robustness to tightening SE(3) constraints than model-based baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sensorimotor policies can successfully enable precise aggressive quadrotor maneuvers under strict SE(3) constraints, matching or exceeding privileged-state baselines despite using only onboard vision.
> - The combination of 'Informed Reset' and robust domain randomization is critical for efficient learning and reliable sim-to-real transfer in tasks characterized by sparse rewards and narrow solution spaces.
> - An end-to-end sensorimotor mapping offers a robust alternative to traditional modular architectures by reducing information loss, mitigating cascading errors, and overcoming limitations of handcrafted features or predefined traversal poses.
