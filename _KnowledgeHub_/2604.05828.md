# Precise Aggressive Aerial Maneuvers with Sensorimotor Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Zhejiang University developed sensorimotor policies enabling quadrotors to perform precise aggressive aerial maneuvers through narrow openings using only onboard vision and proprioception. The framework achieves high success rates for single, dynamic, and consecutive gap traversals, demonstrating robust reactive control and adaptability to diverse gap geometries.

## Problem

- Autonomous quadrotors face challenges in performing precise aggressive maneuvers through narrow openings using only onboard sensing, often relying on external localization infrastructure.
- Traditional modular architectures, which separate state estimation, trajectory planning, and control, suffer from information loss, error accumulation, and inflexibility in complex, constrained environments.
- Direct application of model-free reinforcement learning is difficult due to high-dimensional, partially observable inputs and extremely sparse rewards within the narrow feasible solution space of tight SE(3) constraints.

## Method

- A two-stage policy distillation framework trains a 'teacher' policy using online ==reinforcement learning== with low-dimensional surrogate observations, then distills its behavior into a 'student' policy operating directly on high-dimensional visual and proprioceptive inputs.
- An 'Informed Reset' strategy initializes the quadrotor to states derived from model-based optimal trajectories, significantly accelerating RL exploration in sparse reward environments.
- A comprehensive suite of sim-to-real transfer techniques, including perturbation forces, response randomization, and perceptual latency modeling, enhances robustness for zero-shot deployment on physical hardware.

## Results

- Quadrotors successfully navigated 20 cm x 60 cm rectangular gaps with a 5 cm clearance, achieving **90%** success for gap roll angles up to 90° and **73.3%** for pitch angles up to 60°.
- The policies demonstrated reactive control by traversing dynamic, handheld moving gaps without explicit dynamic training, and adapted to diverse gap geometries (e.g., triangular, elliptical) without requiring manually defined traversal states.
- Ablation studies confirmed that 'Informed Reset' significantly improved RL exploration efficiency (**96%** success with 1G samples vs. **70%** without) and that sim-to-real techniques were crucial for maintaining performance at extreme tilt angles, with learned policies exhibiting greater robustness to tightening SE(3) constraints than model-based baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sensorimotor policies can successfully enable precise aggressive quadrotor maneuvers under strict SE(3) constraints, matching or exceeding privileged-state baselines despite using only onboard vision.
> - The combination of 'Informed Reset' and robust domain randomization is critical for efficient learning and reliable sim-to-real transfer in tasks characterized by sparse rewards and narrow solution spaces.
> - An end-to-end sensorimotor mapping offers a robust alternative to traditional modular architectures by reducing information loss, mitigating cascading errors, and overcoming limitations of handcrafted features or predefined traversal poses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous quadrotors have demonstrated capabilities in aggressive flight maneuvers, which are essential for navigating complex and constrained environments. Achieving precise execution of these maneuvers, particularly through narrow openings, is critical for expanding the operational envelopes of robotic systems. Historically, such precision has often relied on external localization infrastructure, providing accurate and direct state information (e.g., position, velocity) to the control system. However, for quadrotors operating solely with onboard sensory data, acquiring comparable state feedback accuracy and reliability presents a significant challenge.

Prior research in narrow gap traversal has approached the problem through modular architectures. Some methods utilize visual-inertial odometry for state estimation, followed by pre-specified or rule-based trajectory planning. While these approaches have achieved notable demonstrations, they typically involve limitations. Pre-planned trajectories may lack real-time adaptability to perception uncertainties or tracking deviations. Rule-based planners often rely on handcrafted visual features and algorithmic designs that can be overfitted to specific setups, making them inconvenient or suboptimal for diverse scenarios like consecutive gap traversal or varied geometries. Furthermore, the partitioned modules (e.g., state estimation, trajectory optimization) introduce interfaces that can lead to information loss and cascading errors, which are particularly detrimental in aggressive flight maneuvers with low fault tolerance.

This work draws inspiration from biological systems, such as birds, which navigate complex environments aggressively using direct sensorimotor capabilities without explicit state estimation or trajectory planning. This observation motivates an investigation into computational approaches that develop direct data-driven sensing-acting mappings. While sensorimotor/visuomotor learning has shown promise in robotics tasks like object manipulation, legged locomotion, and high-speed drone racing, these applications either involve quasi-static conditions, benefit from high degrees of freedom for error recovery, or operate within more forgiving solution spaces. The specific challenge of aggressive traversal through narrow gaps with Special Euclidean group (SE(3)) constraints, which requires the quadrotor to exploit its asymmetric airframe geometry and momentary tilted pose, introduces strict, non-convex constraints. This differentiates it from tasks primarily focused on time-optimality under less stringent positional constraints, such as drone racing. The research presented here addresses whether a sensorimotor policy learning approach can succeed under these stringent constraints where control tolerance is limited.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable autonomous quadrotors to perform diverse, precise, and aggressive aerial maneuvers, specifically through narrow openings, using only onboard vision and proprioceptive sensing. This encompasses tasks such as single gap traversal, traversal through dynamic gaps, consecutive gap traversal, and navigation through geometrically varied apertures.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several key challenges and limitations in the existing robotics landscape:
- **Exploiting Maneuverability:** A fundamental bottleneck in maximizing the maneuverability of drones is the capability to execute precise aggressive maneuvers with lightweight onboard sensors. Such maneuvers are critical for expanding a system's accessible operational area, for instance, by navigating through constricted spaces like windows, tree gaps, or cave entrances.
- **Addressing SE(3) Constraints:** Aggressive traversal through narrow gaps often necessitates the quadrotor to adopt a tilted attitude, leveraging the asymmetry of its airframe. This introduces tight, non-convex SE(3) constraints on the system's pose relative to the environment, requiring precise control over both translation and rotation.
- **Limitations of Modular Architectures:** Traditional modular approaches, which separate functions like state estimation, trajectory planning, and control, face inherent issues. These include potential information loss at module interfaces, accumulation of errors, and the need for extensive manual tuning and optimization of each component. Such designs can also be inflexible and suboptimal when extended to new scenarios or gap geometries, as they often rely on predefined traversal poses or handcrafted visual features.
- **Inspiration from Biology:** The ability of biological entities, such as birds, to perform aggressive navigation through confined spaces without explicit reliance on intermediate representations like gap pose estimation or trajectory planning, suggests an alternative, more integrated approach for robotic control.
- **Overcoming Reinforcement Learning Challenges:** Direct application of model-free reinforcement learning (RL) to such problems is difficult due to high-dimensional and recurrent observations, partial observability, and especially the extremely narrow feasible solution space, leading to inefficient exploration and sparse rewards.
- **Robustness and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Developing policies in simulation that can be reliably deployed on physical hardware in a zero-shot manner requires robust sim-to-real transfer techniques, accounting for unmodeled dynamics, noisy actuation, and perceptual discrepancies.

By addressing these points, the research aims to develop a framework that allows quadrotors to autonomously determine optimal traversal strategies from experience, directly mapping sensory inputs to low-level control commands, thereby mirroring the sensorimotor integration observed in biological systems and enabling new capabilities for autonomous flight.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employs a sim-to-real online policy learning paradigm, training neural network policies in a simulated environment for subsequent zero-shot deployment on physical quadrotors. This framework is designed to overcome challenges associated with high-dimensional inputs, partial observability, and constrained solution spaces typical of aggressive aerial maneuvers.

The approach utilizes a two-stage teacher-student training method, or policy distillation, to decouple the original partially observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) into a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and a supervised learning (SL) problem.

**Stage 1: Online Reinforcement Learning from Surrogate Observations (Teacher Policy)**
- **Problem Formulation:** An oracle MDP is constructed with a low-dimensional surrogate observation space to facilitate efficient RL training.
- **Observation Space:** The RL policy receives as input:
 - `n_g` (typically 32) 3D points uniformly sampled along the edge of the immediate gap to be traversed. This point representation allows for generalizable learning across different gap geometries.
 - Roll and pitch angles (`φ_t`, `θ_t`) from the onboard flight controller.
 - Privileged information: Body-frame linear velocity (`v_t`).
 - Previous action (`a_{t-1}`).
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs low-level control commands: collective thrust (`T_t`) and 3-axis body rates (`ω_x_t`, `ω_y_t`, `ω_z_t`).
- **Reward Signal:** A composite reward function is designed to shape behavior:
 - **Traversing reward:** Provided when the quadrotor's collider is near or traversing the gap without collision, with a special scaling factor for pitched gaps to encourage aggressive pitching.
 - **Shaping reward:** Encourages movement towards the geometric center of the next passable region.
 - **Smoothness penalty:** Punishes large action magnitudes and changes between control steps to promote natural and smooth motion.
 - **Speed constraint:** A soft penalty for exceeding a maximum speed (4 m/s).
 - **Distillation regularization:** Encourages maintaining the gap within the camera's field of view (FoV) during approach.
 - **Approaching and traversing regularization:** Promotes traversal with velocity perpendicular to the gap plane and the quadrotor's nose oriented towards the plane.
- **Policy Representation:** The actor and critic networks are represented by neural networks. A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with a global max-pooling layer serves as a point encoder for gap points, followed by a feedforward MLP that fuses these features with other observations.
- **Informed Reset (IR):** To address the sparse rewards and narrow solution space, the quadrotor is strategically initialized to "informative states" along trajectories generated by a model-based trajectory optimizer (quotient space-based method for SE(3) constrained trajectories). This guides RL exploration by increasing the likelihood of encountering high-return states. A probability of 0.5 determines if the reset is from the informative state dataset or a random state within the task space.
- **Optimization Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for policy optimization, leveraging efficient data generation from the lightweight surrogate observations.

**Stage 2: Observation Space Distillation via Supervised Learning (Student Policy)**
- **Problem Formulation:** The behavior of the RL teacher policy is distilled into a policy that operates directly on high-dimensional visual inputs and proprioception.
- **Observation Space:** The student policy receives:
 - A masked binary image (320x256 resolution) representing the gap, simplifying visual processing and aiding sim-to-real transfer. For consecutive gaps, only the immediate next gap's mask is provided.
 - Roll and pitch angles from the flight controller.
 - Previous action.
- **Action Space:** Outputs the same 4-dimensional control commands (collective thrust, body rates) as the teacher, plus an additional dimension for a "traversal prediction" signal, used to trigger a recovery program in real-world deployment.
- **Policy Representation:** A convolutional neural network (CNN) encodes the masked image. The visual features are concatenated with attitude measurements and the previous action, then fed into a single-layer Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) to maintain a belief state from historical observations, addressing partial observability. A final MLP generates actions.
- **Optimization Algorithm:** The Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) algorithm, an online imitation learning method, is employed to train the SL policy, effectively mitigating the covariate shift problem.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer Techniques:**
A suite of domain randomization and system identification techniques is implemented to enhance robustness and repeatability in real-world deployment:
- **Perturbation Force:** Random, transient forces are applied to the simulated quadrotor to mimic unmodeled dynamics and prevent overfitting to specific simulator characteristics, encouraging reliance on visual inputs.
- **Response Simulation for Flight Controller:** A calibrated, parameterized model simulates the response of collective thrust and body rates from historical policy commands, accounting for actuation delay and low-pass filtering. This includes a precise throttle-to-thrust mapping calibrated using real-world data.
- **Response Randomization:** Random factors are applied to the simulated flight controller responses and their underlying parameters (e.g., actuation delays) to model real-world variability.
- **Perceptual Latency Simulation:** Camera image generation time and neural network inference time are modeled in the simulator.
- **Mask Observation Randomization:** Camera intrinsic parameters are randomized, and noise (via max/min pooling) is introduced to the edges of the generated masks to account for imperfections in real-world segmentation.

**Real-World Deployment:**
- **Hardware:** A custom quadrotor platform (38cm x 10cm, 820g) equipped with a monocular camera, PX4 Autopilot, and an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX for onboard computation.
- **Control Flow:** The deployed policy controls the quadrotor through the gap. Upon the policy's predicted successful traversal (via a dedicated output head), a recovery program is triggered. This program uses a PD controller for attitude stabilization and an optical flow-based module for deceleration and hovering.
- **Landmark Perception:** Initially, color-threshold segmentation on illuminated frames is used. For more realistic scenarios, a lightweight segmentation model (MobileNetv3 encoder + ASPP module) is trained and deployed, providing noisy mask inputs to the policy.
- **Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL):** For some experiments, an HIL testbed is used, where synthetic image inputs are generated from motion capture data to control a physical quadrotor, allowing evaluation without physical gap instances.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings demonstrating the capabilities and robustness of the proposed sensorimotor policy framework for aggressive quadrotor maneuvers.

**Single Rectangular Narrow Gap Traversal:**
- The method enabled a quadrotor to navigate a 20 cm x 60 cm rectangular gap with a 5 cm clearance. This was achieved for gaps with unknown positions and orientations, including extreme roll angles up to 90° and pitch angles up to 60°.
- Repeatability was high: for gap roll angles ≤ 60°, the success rate was 29 out of 30 trials. For roll angles > 60°, it was 27 out of 30 trials (90%). For pitch angles, success rates were 100% for 30°, 80% for 45°, and 73.3% for 60°.
- The policy autonomously aligned the quadrotor's longitudinal axis parallel to the gap's edge during traversal, without explicit gap pose detection or predefined traversal poses.
- Control analysis showed the policy driving body rates to their limits (e.g., 6 rad/s for 90° roll) to execute aggressive maneuvers.
- The recurrent neural network (RNN) in the policy architecture enabled successful navigation even when the gap temporarily exited the camera's field of view.

**Reactive Dynamic Gap Traversal:**
- Policies trained exclusively on static gaps demonstrated the ability to traverse handheld moving gaps with unpredictable translational or rotational motions. This indicates a robust reactive control capability.
- Domain randomization during training was identified as a key factor in enabling this reactive behavior, by exposing the policy to a diverse range of relative motion patterns between the quadrotor and the target.
- While reactive, the policy's limitations include an inability to anticipate future gap movements, leading to potential collisions if motion patterns change abruptly very close to the gap, or if gap speeds exceed the quadrotor's fundamental control authority limits at aggressive orientations.

**Consecutive Narrow Gaps Traversal:**
- The research demonstrated, for the first time with onboard sensing, precise traversal through consecutive closely placed narrow gaps. Tracks consisting of two or three gaps were successfully navigated.
- The policies learned complex behaviors, such as rapid attitude changes (e.g., 75° roll difference between gaps) and coordinated thrust modulation to regain altitude between gaps.
- Hardware-in-the-loop experiments showed that simulated and real-world trajectories were similar, with errors remaining bounded, validating the closed-loop control robustness.

**Traversal through Narrow Passable Regions with Various Geometries:**
- The framework allowed for the development of policies capable of navigating through geometrically diverse gaps (e.g., triangular, parallelogram, elliptical, diamond-shaped, arch-shaped) without requiring manually defined traversal states or handcrafted visual features.
- Observed traversal orientations were consistent for triangular gaps (aligning with the longest edge), multimodal for parallelograms (aligning with long edge or diagonal), and more dispersed for arch-shaped gaps, reflecting the varying geometric constraints.

**Validation under Learned Segmentation as Noisier Landmark Observations:**
- The policies exhibited tolerance to noisy and imperfect inputs generated by a lightweight learned segmentation model, operating on non-illuminated gaps in diverse visual backgrounds.
- Success rates were maintained at closer ranges (2-4 meters, ≥ 60° orientation), though performance degraded at longer ranges or skewed perspectives due to decreasing segmentation quality.

**Key Ingredients for Policy Learning and Sim-to-Real Transfer (Ablation Studies):**
- **Informed Reset (IR):** This strategy significantly improved RL exploration efficiency and success rates. For single gap traversal, IR-enabled learning achieved 96% success within a 1G sample budget, compared to 70% without IR, requiring three times fewer samples. It was crucial for learning to traverse consecutive gaps, where standard RL without IR failed to find feasible solutions.
- **Sim-to-Real Techniques:**
 - **Response Randomization (RR) and Perturbation Force (PF):** These two components were critical for the performance of the vision-based distillation policies, especially at extreme tilt angles (60° and 80°). Their removal led to significant performance degradation, often resulting in lateral collisions or altitude loss. They expand the state space and prevent overfitting to simulated dynamics.
 - **Response Parameter Randomization (RPR):** This had a less significant impact, attributed to the low-latency nature of the collective thrust and bodyrate control interface.

**Performance Evaluation with Reference Baseline Systems:**
- **Comparison to Privileged-State Planners:** The proposed distilled sensorimotor policy achieved success rates comparable to or sometimes exceeding model-based trajectory optimization methods (Wang et al., Falanga et al. with privileged states), despite operating with indirect visual observations and no external localization. The learned policy generated qualitatively distinct trajectories compared to model-based planners, which sometimes produced distorted or infeasible paths under certain conditions.
- **Comparison to Vision-Based Baseline:** Against Falanga et al.'s vision-based system, the proposed method demonstrated superior robustness and success rates, particularly at longer initial distances. Falanga's system struggled with maintaining continuous corner observations and replanning limitations near the gap, leading to accumulated errors.
- **SE(3) Constraint Tightness:** The RL policies exhibited greater robustness to tightening SE(3) constraints (smaller gaps, larger tilt angles) compared to model-based planners. The threshold for "too tight" constraints differed substantially, with RL policies maintaining performance in regimes where baselines failed.
- **Influence of Vision vs. Constraints:** As geometric constraints tightened, the vision-based aspect of the task became the primary performance bottleneck, eventually leading to failure for both vision-based and privileged-state approaches when constraints became extremely stringent. Onboard sensing impacted traditional modular planners more severely than the learned policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of autonomous aerial robotics, particularly in the domain of aggressive flight maneuvers under stringent environmental constraints.

**Advancement in Autonomous Flight Control:** The primary contribution is the successful realization of sensorimotor precise aggressive maneuvers for underactuated multirotors under strict SE(3) constraints, utilizing end-to-end policies derived from onboard sensing. This achievement pushes the boundaries beyond previous work by demonstrating robust performance in challenging scenarios, including high-tilt single gaps, dynamic gaps, and consecutive gap sequences.

**Paradigm Shift from Modular to End-to-End Control:** The work provides evidence for an alternative to traditional modular control architectures widely used in robotics. By developing a direct sensorimotor mapping, the method can potentially eliminate the need for laborious manual tuning of individual modules (e.g., state estimation, trajectory planning) and their interfaces. This can mitigate issues such as information loss, cascading errors, and sub-optimal human bias introduced by manual design, leading to more integrated and robust systems.

**Enhanced Deployment Readiness and Robustness:** The method's ability to learn from experience, combined with end-to-end optimization and comprehensive simulation randomization, enables the development of policies that extract robust, task-relevant representations from observations. This translates to higher repeatability and resilience during real-world deployment, allowing the system to tolerate unmodeled dynamics, noisy sensing, and environmental variability without explicit fine-tuning.

**Generalizability and Adaptability:** The framework demonstrates flexibility in handling diverse gap geometries without requiring manually defined traversal poses or handcrafted visual features. Furthermore, the learned policies exhibit remarkable reactive control capabilities, allowing them to adapt to dynamic environments (e.g., moving gaps) even when such scenarios were not explicitly part of the training data.

**Potential Impact and Future Directions:**
- **Expanding Autonomous Mission Space:** By enabling quadrotors to precisely navigate complex, cluttered, and constrained environments, the research contributes to expanding the range of missions autonomous drones can undertake, from industrial inspection and search-and-rescue to environmental monitoring.
- **Robotics Research Methodology:** The successful integration of reinforcement learning with policy distillation and informed reset strategies provides a blueprint for addressing complex control problems with sparse rewards and narrow solution spaces in other robotic domains.
- **Transition to Unstructured Environments:** A primary long-term goal is to extend these capabilities beyond simplified perception structures (artificial landmarks, masked images) to general unstructured environments. This would involve leveraging robust, general-purpose visual foundation models or direct learning from raw sensory modalities like depth images or LiDAR, while maintaining high-frequency, low-latency performance.
- **Addressing the Precision-Generalization Dilemma:** The research acknowledges the fundamental challenge in robot learning of balancing context-specific excellence with broad versatility. Further work is needed to develop frameworks for generalized aggressive whole-body maneuvers that can break this dilemma.
- **Mitigating Sim-to-Real Discrepancies:** While current sim-to-real techniques significantly improve performance, residual discrepancies can still limit ultimate precision. Future research could explore interactive learning on physical platforms or advanced hardware-in-the-loop setups that incorporate real-world dynamics more extensively, to further bridge the simulation-reality gap.
