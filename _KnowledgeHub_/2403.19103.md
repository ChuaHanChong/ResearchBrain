# Automated Black-box Prompt Engineering for Personalized Text-to-Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> PRISM introduces an iterative VLM-driven algorithm that automatically generates human-interpretable and transferable text prompts for black-box text-to-image (T2I) models. The system achieves superior generalizability and visual accuracy across various T2I generators, including DALL-E and Midjourney, while producing prompts that are easily understandable and editable by users.

## Problem

- Controlling text-to-image models requires labor-intensive manual prompt engineering, which is sensitive to phrasing and demands significant human expertise.
- Existing automated prompt generation methods often lack transferability across different T2I models or necessitate white-box access to proprietary models.
- Many automated approaches produce non-interpretable 'soft tokens' or embeddings, hindering human understanding, refinement, or creative editing of the generated prompts.

## Method

- ==PRISM== employs an iterative, VLM-driven approach, using a =='Prompt Engineer Assistant' (GPT-4V)== to propose and refine textual prompts by analyzing feedback.
- A VLM-based =='Judge Model' (GPT-4V)== evaluates visual similarity between generated and reference images with nuanced, flexible criteria, guiding the prompt refinement process without needing white-box access.
- The algorithm refines a ==distribution of candidate prompts in parallel streams==, leveraging the VLM's in-context learning and reasoning abilities to improve prompt quality and interpretability over iterations.

## Results

- PRISM demonstrated superior performance and generalizability across closed-source T2I models (DALL-E 2, DALL-E 3, Midjourney) compared to baselines that often require white-box access.
- The method produced fully human-readable and editable prompts, achieving low mean negative log-likelihood scores and enabling intuitive user modifications for creative applications.
- PRISM maintained high visual accuracy (CLIP-I and DINO scores) in generated images for personalized T2I and image inversion tasks, while also significantly reducing unsafe prompt generation (**0.1-6.9%** failure rate on DALL-E safeguards).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models can effectively function as iterative optimizers and reasoning engines for complex, black-box creative tasks like prompt engineering, mimicking human problem-solving strategies.
> - An iterative feedback loop between a T2I generator, a VLM-based prompt refiner, and a VLM-based judge enables the generation of highly transferable and human-interpretable prompts for black-box models.
> - Refining a distribution of candidate prompts over multiple parallel streams allows for broader exploration of the solution space and enhanced robustness compared to optimizing a single prompt.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of generative AI, specifically focusing on text-to-image (T2I) models. The broader landscape is dominated by the pursuit of controllable generation—the ability to steer powerful generative models to produce specific, desired outputs.

Historically, achieving controllable generation involved labor-intensive methods such as manual data collection, fine-tuning, or retraining models from scratch. While effective for specific tasks, these approaches were often costly, did not generalize well across models, and lacked efficiency. The advent of T2I models like Stable Diffusion, DALL-E, and Midjourney revolutionized this by allowing users to guide generation through natural language prompts.

However, controlling T2I models effectively introduced a new bottleneck: **prompt engineering**. Manually crafting prompts is a skill-intensive, iterative, trial-and-error process that demands significant human effort and domain expertise. Prompts are notoriously sensitive to phrasing, making the process inefficient and difficult for non-experts.

This difficulty motivated the development of **automated prompt engineering** or **personalized/subject-driven T2I generation**. Existing automated methods, while promising, suffer from several limitations that PRISM aims to overcome:
- **Lack of Transferability:** Many methods require pre-collected, architecture-specific keywords or are heavily optimized for particular T2I models, failing to generalize to others.
- **White-box Access Requirement:** Approaches like Textual Inversion (Gal et al., 2023) or PEZ (Wen et al., 2023) often necessitate access to the underlying model's parameters or embeddings for gradient-based optimization. This limitation precludes their use with powerful, proprietary black-box models like DALL-E or Midjourney.
- **Non-Interpretable Prompts:** Some methods generate "soft tokens" or optimized embeddings that are effective for the model but incomprehensible to humans, hindering further refinement or creative editing.

PRISM directly addresses these shortcomings by proposing an algorithm that can generate **human-interpretable and transferable prompts** with only **black-box access** to T2I models. This aligns with a growing trend in AI research that leverages large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) as "optimizers" or "reasoning engines" to solve complex problems without direct model interaction. The paper explicitly draws inspiration from "LLM jailbreaking" techniques (Chao et al., 2023) and LLMs as optimizers (Pryzant et al., 2023), positioning itself at the intersection of generative AI, prompt engineering, and the emergent capabilities of large foundation models. By doing so, PRISM carves out a unique niche that bridges the gap between sophisticated T2I models and user-friendly, controllable generation, especially for closed-source platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind PRISM stems from the acknowledged limitations of current methods for controlling text-to-image generative models. While manual prompt engineering is powerful, its labor-intensive nature, sensitivity to phrasing, and requirement for expert knowledge make it a significant barrier to widespread and efficient use. Existing automated prompt generation techniques, on the other hand, frequently fall short due to their lack of transferability across different T2I models, their need for white-box access to the target model, or their tendency to produce opaque, non-interpretable prompts.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of PRISM are clearly defined:

1. **Automate Prompt Engineering:** Develop an algorithm capable of automatically generating effective text prompts for T2I models, thereby reducing human effort and expertise requirements.
2. **Ensure Human-Interpretability and Editability:** Produce prompts that are easily understood and modifiable by human users, fostering creative control and intuitive interaction with T2I models. This is a crucial differentiator from methods that generate non-linguistic "soft tokens."
3. **Achieve Transferability Across T2I Models:** Create prompts that are robust and effective not just for a single model, but for a wide range of T2I generators, including both open-source models (like Stable Diffusion) and proprietary, black-box platforms (like DALL-E and Midjourney). This "black-box compatibility" is a major objective, addressing a critical gap in existing methods.
4. **Effectively Capture Desired Concepts:** Enable the generation of accurate prompts for various visual concepts, encompassing specific objects, artistic styles, and the overall content of an image (image inversion).
5. **Leverage LLM/VLM Capabilities for Iterative Refinement:** Design a system that mimics the human prompt engineering process of iterative refinement by exploiting the in-context learning and reasoning abilities of large language and vision-language models. This moves beyond single-shot or gradient-based optimization methods.

In essence, PRISM seeks to create a versatile, robust, and user-friendly framework for controllable T2I generation that can dynamically adapt to various models and tasks, much like a human expert, but with the efficiency of automation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRISM, or **Prompt Refinement and Iterative Sampling Mechanism**, is an iterative, VLM-driven approach designed to automatically generate human-interpretable and transferable prompts for black-box text-to-image (T2I) models. Its core idea is to mimic the human prompt engineering process by iteratively refining a distribution of candidate prompts based on feedback from a T2I generator and a VLM-based judge.

The algorithm comprises three main components:

1. **Prompt Engineer Assistant (F):** This component is implemented using a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-4V in the main experiments. Its role is to propose and refine textual prompts. It receives a reference image, a previously generated image, and an evaluation score. Leveraging its in-context learning abilities, F analyzes this feedback and generates an "improvement" narrative along with a new, refined candidate prompt. The system prompts for F are carefully designed to instruct the VLM to act as a prompt engineer, focusing on detailed object/style description, avoiding irrelevant elements, and incorporating previous successes and failures. Crucially, F updates its *sampling distribution* of prompts, rather than optimizing a single prompt, allowing for greater diversity and escape from local optima.
2. **Text-to-Image (T2I) Generator (G):** This is the black-box model that PRISM aims to control. In the experiments, this includes models like SDXL-Turbo, Stable Diffusion 2.1, DALL-E 2, DALL-E 3, and Midjourney. G takes the prompt generated by F and produces an image.
3. **Judge Model (D):** Also implemented using a VLM (GPT-4V), D evaluates the visual similarity between the image generated by G and the original reference image. Unlike fixed metrics (e.g., CLIP similarity) that might struggle with nuanced or task-specific criteria, D uses system prompts to define flexible evaluation criteria (e.g., "how similar are the main objects," "how similar are the styles"). This VLM-based judge provides fine-grained, human-understandable feedback scores (0-10) to the Prompt Engineer Assistant F.

**The Iterative Process (Algorithm 1):**

The PRISM algorithm operates in parallel streams (`N`) over multiple iterations (`K`), for a total compute budget of `N * K`:

1. **Initialization:** For each stream, a reference image (`x_k,n`) is randomly selected from a given collection.
2. **Prompt Generation:** The Prompt Engineer Assistant (F) samples a candidate prompt (`y_k,n`) based on the current context (including chat history and previous feedback).
3. **Image Generation:** The T2I Generator (G) uses `y_k,n` to produce a generated image (`x̂_k,n`).
4. **Evaluation:** The Judge Model (D) calculates an "in-iteration score" (`s'(x_k,n, y_k,n)`) indicating the visual similarity between the generated image (`x̂_k,n`) and the reference image (`x_k,n`) according to the task-specific criteria.
5. **Refinement:** The Prompt Engineer Assistant (F) updates its internal "belief" or prompt sampling distribution. This update is informed by `x_k,n`, `x̂_k,n`, `y_k,n`, the score `s'`, and the chat history of that particular stream. This is where the VLM's in-context learning is critical, enabling it to "reason" about why a prompt worked or failed and how to improve.
6. **Repeat:** Steps 1-5 are repeated for `K` iterations within each of the `N` parallel streams.
7. **Final Selection:** After all iterations, a subset of `C` prompts with the best in-iteration scores is collected. These `C` prompts are then re-evaluated against *all* reference images to calculate a "total score." The prompt with the highest total score (and highest log-likelihood in case of a tie) is returned as the best-performing prompt (`y*`).

This methodology, particularly the use of VLMs for both prompt generation/refinement and nuanced evaluation, allows PRISM to effectively perform "iterative reasoning" within the image generation loop. It avoids the need for differentiable loss functions or model parameter access, making it highly compatible with black-box T2I models and robust to the stochasticity inherent in generative processes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate PRISM's superior performance across several crucial dimensions, especially its generalizability, interpretability, and black-box compatibility.

**1. Superior Generalizability and Transferability:**
- PRISM consistently achieves the best performance across almost all metrics when evaluated with **closed-source T2I models** such as DALL-E 2, DALL-E 3, and Midjourney. This is a significant advantage over baselines like Textual Inversion (TI) and PEZ, which often require white-box access or architecture-specific keywords and thus struggle with transferability.
- While some baselines like CLIP-Interrogator might show competitive performance on open-source models like Stable Diffusion (due to their design space alignment), PRISM's performance holds up and often excels across a broader range of T2I models, proving its robustness.

**2. High Human-Interpretability and Editability:**
- A standout finding is PRISM's ability to produce **fully human-readable and interpretable prompts**. Quantitatively, PRISM achieves the lowest mean negative log-likelihood (NLL) scores, indicating that its generated prompts are semantically aligned with a learned distribution of English text.
- Qualitatively, PRISM's prompts are descriptive and coherent, unlike PEZ's indecipherable soft tokens or Textual Inversion's uninterpretable embeddings. This interpretability allows for **easy prompt editing** by users, unlocking new creative possibilities (demonstrated in applications like changing attributes while maintaining core concepts).

**3. Strong Visual Accuracy:**
- PRISM maintains high visual accuracy in generated images, as measured by CLIP image similarity scores (CLIP-I) and DINO V2 embedding similarity (DINO). It effectively captures fine-grained details, colors, shapes, and overall visual fidelity, outperforming most baselines in both personalized T2I generation and direct image inversion tasks.
- For complex objects or styles (e.g., red monster toy, specific artistic genres), PRISM is shown to be the only method capable of generating remotely similar or accurate representations.

**4. Reduced Unsafe Prompt Generation:**
- When interacting with DALL-E safeguards, PRISM generates significantly fewer unsafe prompts (failing only 0.1-6.9% of the time) compared to baselines like CLIP-Interrogator and PEZ (up to 16.7% failure rate). This suggests a safer, more aligned generation process, partly due to the VLM's reasoning capabilities being guided by ethical system prompts.

**5. Versatility Across Tasks:**
- **Personalized T2I Generation:** PRISM excels at generating prompts for specific objects (DreamBooth dataset) and artistic styles (Wikiart dataset). It accurately identifies genres, eras, and artists, providing fine-grained details like brushstroke style and color palettes.
- **Direct Image Inversion:** Inverting an image back into an accurate descriptive prompt, PRISM generates prompts that are both semantically coherent and visually effective in regenerating the original image, even for highly unique features.

**6. Ablation Studies Confirm Component Effectiveness:**
- **VLM Advantage:** PRISM significantly outperforms zero-shot GPT-4V, highlighting that the iterative refinement mechanism adds substantial value beyond the VLM's inherent capabilities.
- **N and K Trade-off:** The number of parallel streams (N) and iterations (K) impacts performance, with optimal configurations being task-dependent. This provides guidance for practitioners to tune the system for specific needs.
- **VLM Judge Superiority:** The VLM-based Judge (GPT-4V) is shown to be superior to a traditional CLIP-based judge. The VLM judge can perform fine-grained analysis and adhere to nuanced, task-specific criteria, avoiding irrelevant details that a less flexible metric might capture.
- **Importance of Components:** The ablation studies confirm that the Judge (D), re-evaluation, and the iterative budget all contribute positively to prompt accuracy and quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PRISM algorithm represents a significant advancement in controllable text-to-image generation, with far-reaching implications for both research and practical applications. Its key significance lies in addressing fundamental limitations of existing automated prompt engineering methods.

**1. Democratizing Access to Advanced T2I Models:**
By enabling automated, black-box prompt engineering, PRISM makes powerful proprietary T2I models like DALL-E and Midjourney more accessible and controllable for a broader audience. Users no longer need deep domain expertise in prompt crafting or white-box access to the models, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for high-quality, personalized image generation. This could accelerate creative workflows in various industries, from advertising and design to entertainment and education.

**2. Bridging the Gap Between Human and Machine Creativity:**
The generation of human-interpretable and editable prompts is a crucial feature. It allows human users to easily understand, modify, and build upon the prompts suggested by the AI, fostering a more intuitive and collaborative creative process. This departs from "black-box" prompt optimization that yields incomprehensible tokens, allowing prompt engineering to become a more iterative human-AI partnership rather than a mysterious black art. This interpretability also facilitates prompt distillation and multi-concept generation, expanding creative possibilities.

**3. Unlocking the Full Potential of VLMs in Optimization:**
PRISM effectively demonstrates the power of Vision-Language Models (like GPT-4V) as general-purpose optimizers and reasoning engines for complex, iterative tasks in the vision-language domain. By leveraging in-context learning and iterative feedback, PRISM shows how VLMs can mimic human problem-solving strategies, even in non-LLM-centric fields. This could inspire new research directions for applying powerful foundation models to other challenging inverse problems or control tasks where direct gradient-based optimization is infeasible.

**4. Enhanced Robustness and Transferability:**
The algorithm's ability to generate prompts that transfer across diverse T2I architectures is a testament to its robust design. This means that a prompt generated for one model can potentially be reused or slightly adapted for another, saving computational resources and making the generated prompts more valuable assets. This is particularly relevant in a landscape where multiple powerful T2I models coexist.

**5. Safer and More Aligned AI Generation:**
The finding that PRISM generates fewer unsafe prompts when interacting with models like DALL-E indicates a step towards more aligned and responsible AI. By leveraging the reasoning capabilities of VLMs, PRISM can be guided by ethical considerations embedded in its system prompts, contributing to the development of generative tools that are less prone to misuse or accidental generation of harmful content.

**Broader Impact and Limitations (as acknowledged by authors):**
The authors responsibly acknowledge potential broader impacts, including the risk of malicious actors attempting to "jailbreak" or adversarially manipulate the system, similar to LLM vulnerabilities. They commit to implementing safeguards upon public release and continually improving the safety of their method. While the current implementation might incur financial costs due to reliance on high-performance commercial VLMs, the authors highlight the increasing affordability of such models and the flexibility to use cheaper or open-source alternatives, ensuring the method's long-term viability and accessibility. The cost flexibility and ability to reuse prompts for multiple generations further mitigate this concern.

In conclusion, PRISM offers an elegant and powerful solution to a critical problem in generative AI, pushing the boundaries of automated control and interpretability. Its success underscores the transformative potential of combining advanced generative models with the reasoning capabilities of large language and vision models, promising a future where controllable AI-powered creativity is more accessible and intuitive.
