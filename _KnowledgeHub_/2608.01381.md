# DreamTrajectory: Trajectory-Guided Action Generation with World Model Alignment for Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamTrajectory introduces a framework for mobile manipulation that explicitly guides whole-body action generation with a planned end-effector trajectory and refines actions using a lightweight world model. This approach yielded a 54.8% average success rate on the MS-HAB benchmark, outperforming existing end-to-end VLA policies, and improved real-world task success rates to 90.0%.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for mobile manipulation often suffer from imprecise coordinated base-arm predictions because the intended motion is implicitly encoded in a high-dimensional action space.
- These policies typically execute action chunks in an open-loop manner, leading to an accumulation of control errors and unmodeled contacts that cause deviations between planned and executed motions.
- The absence of a mechanism to verify if predicted actions will achieve their intended motion results in failures only being revealed post-execution, hindering robustness in dynamic environments.

## Method

- **DreamTrajectory** jointly generates a planned end-effector trajectory and whole-body action chunk, where the explicit trajectory guides action generation within a shared Transformer using ==group-causal attention==.
- A lightweight, action-conditioned ==trajectory world model== predicts the actual induced end-effector trajectory of a candidate action chunk, capturing physical deviations from ideal kinematics.
- A test-time ==search-predict-score== refinement procedure samples candidate actions, uses the world model to predict their outcomes, and selects the action that best aligns with the planned trajectory and maintains smoothness before execution.

## Results

- DreamTrajectory achieved an average success rate of **54.8%** across six MS-HAB subtasks, outperforming baselines like ACT (**28.0%**) and π_0.5 (**32.3%**).
- Ablation studies showed that trajectory guidance improved success by **15.2 percentage points** (to **47.5%**) over the action-only baseline, and world-model refinement provided an additional **7.3 percentage point** gain (to **54.8%**).
- Real-world experiments on an ARX LIFT mobile manipulator demonstrated an increase in average success rate from **63.3%** (baseline) to **90.0%** using the complete DreamTrajectory framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly guiding action generation with an end-effector trajectory, rather than relying on implicit representations, improves the precision and coordination of mobile manipulator movements.
> - A compact task-space trajectory world model can efficiently predict the real-world execution outcomes of actions, allowing for effective pre-execution refinement without high computational overhead.
> - The two components—trajectory guidance and world-model refinement—are complementary, with trajectory guidance providing a strong initial policy and refinement correcting residual errors across tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Mobile manipulation tasks, which involve robots coordinating base and arm movements in dynamic and unstructured environments, present significant challenges compared to fixed-base manipulation. The larger effective action space due to coupled base and arm motions, combined with continuously changing viewpoints and contact conditions, increases the complexity of action prediction and sensitivity to execution errors.

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies represent a prominent approach for robot control. Early VLA policies like RT-1, RT-2, and PaLM-E established foundations for scalable vision-language robot learning, with subsequent work (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, Octo, RoboFlamingo, OpenVLA, π family, GR00T N1) focusing on cross-task and cross-embodiment transfer, adaptation efficiency, and spatial grounding. However, these methods primarily predict action sequences directly.

A limitation identified in these direct action prediction approaches, particularly for mobile manipulation, is the absence of an explicit task-space motion plan. This can lead to imprecise coordinated base-arm predictions because the intended motion remains implicit. Furthermore, these policies often execute predicted action chunks open-loop, without mechanisms to verify if the actions will achieve the intended motion, leading to the accumulation of control errors and unmodeled contacts.

The research area of "trajectory-aware policies" seeks to introduce intermediate motion representations beyond direct action prediction. Examples include RT-Trajectory (using trajectory sketches), 3D Diffuser Actor (predicting 3D pose trajectories), and TraceVLA (employing visual traces or image-plane paths). These methods use trajectories as external guides or inputs for separate optimization processes.

Concurrently, "action-conditioned world models" focus on predicting the consequences of candidate actions. Models like DINO-WM and V-JEPA learn to predict future visual features or latent video representations conditioned on actions, which can be used for planning. DreamZero, MotuBrain, and GigaWorld-Policy integrate future prediction with robot control, often operating in image or video space.

DreamTrajectory integrates aspects from both trajectory-aware policies and action-conditioned world models to address the limitations in mobile manipulation. It distinguishes itself by:
1. **Jointly generating a planned end-effector trajectory and a whole-body action chunk** within a single action expert, where the trajectory explicitly guides action generation. This differs from methods that use trajectories as external inputs.
2. **Introducing a lightweight trajectory world model** that predicts the end-effector trajectory induced by a *candidate* action chunk. This model operates in compact task-space trajectory prediction rather than high-dimensional image or video space, enabling efficient test-time refinement.
3. **Aligning the planned trajectory with the predicted induced trajectory** through a search-predict-score procedure before execution, providing a mechanism for real-time plan-execution consistency.

The work specifically targets the ManiSkill-HAB (MS-HAB) benchmark, which focuses on contact-rich tasks requiring concurrent base and arm motion, making it a relevant platform for evaluating mobile manipulation capabilities that go beyond sequential navigation and fixed-base manipulation. By introducing an explicit intermediate representation and a verification mechanism, DreamTrajectory aims to enhance the precision and robustness of mobile manipulation compared to existing end-to-end VLA policies.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The research addresses two limitations observed in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies when applied to mobile manipulation:

1. **Imprecise Coordinated Base-Arm Prediction:** Existing VLA policies often map observations directly to whole-body action chunks. This approach necessitates searching a high-dimensional action space without an explicit task-space motion plan. Consequently, the specific motion that an action chunk is intended to realize remains implicit. This implicitness can hinder precise coordination between the mobile base and the manipulator arm, especially in tasks requiring complex, coupled movements under continuously changing viewpoints and whole-body constraints. The motivation here is to introduce an explicit, physically meaningful intermediate representation—an end-effector trajectory—that can guide action generation in this large, kinodynamically constrained action space. The hypothesis is that specifying *where* the gripper should move (the trajectory) before determining *how* the base and arm should realize that motion (the actions) can improve precision.

2. **Accumulation of Plan-Execution Deviations due to Open-Loop Execution:** Current VLA policies typically execute predicted action chunks in an open-loop manner. There is often no built-in mechanism to verify whether the commanded actions will actually produce the intended motion. As a result, control errors, unexpected collisions, and out-of-distribution contacts encountered during execution are revealed only *after* a failure has occurred. These errors can accumulate, creating a significant gap between the planned motion and the realized motion, thereby compromising task success. The motivation is to integrate a mechanism for real-time consistency checking. This involves predicting the actual motion that a candidate action chunk would induce and comparing it against the intended motion plan *before* execution, allowing for refinement and selection of a more robust action. This pre-execution verification aims to mitigate the accumulation of errors and improve robustness in dynamic, contact-rich environments.

In summary, the core objectives are to:
- Develop a framework that explicitly guides whole-body action generation with an intention-level end-effector trajectory.
- Implement a mechanism to verify and refine predicted action chunks at test time by assessing their predicted execution outcome against the planned trajectory.
- Demonstrate that these two components, when integrated, lead to improved success rates in complex mobile manipulation tasks, particularly those involving contact-rich interactions and coordinated base-arm movements in both simulated and real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamTrajectory (DT) is a trajectory-guided framework designed for language-conditioned mobile manipulation, addressing the identified limitations through two main components: a trajectory-guided VLA for joint generation and a lightweight action-conditioned trajectory world model for test-time action refinement.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem involves learning a policy for a mobile manipulator that, at each replanning step `t`, receives egocentric visual inputs (`o_t`), proprioceptive state (`s_t`), and a language instruction (`ℓ`). The objective is to predict a future action chunk `a_t:t+H-1` over a horizon `H`, where each action contains low-level control commands for both the mobile base and the manipulator. A standard VLA models the action distribution `π_θ(a_t:t+H-1 | o_t, s_t, ℓ)`.

#### 3.2. Trajectory-Guided Action Generation

DT augments this standard VLA formulation by jointly generating a future end-effector motion trajectory `τ_t:t+H-1` alongside the whole-body action chunk.
- **Trajectory Representation:** The end-effector trajectory `τ_t:t+H-1` is represented as a sequence of `H` 7D poses, where each pose (`p_B_t^t+h`, `q_B_t^t+h`) includes a 3D position and a 4D quaternion orientation. These poses are expressed in a chunk-local frame `B_t`, which is anchored at the robot's base pose at the current replanning step `t` and remains fixed throughout the prediction horizon. This representation captures the combined effect of future base and arm motions.
- **Joint Generation:** The planned trajectory and action chunks share the same horizon `H` and are jointly generated by a dual-stream action expert: `(ˆτ_t:t+H-1, ˆa_t:t+H-1) ∼ π_θ(τ_t:t+H-1, a_t:t+H-1 | o_t, s_t, ℓ)`.
- **Conditional Flow Matching:** DT models this joint generation process using conditional flow matching. During training, a training trajectory chunk `τ` and action chunk `a` are independently sampled with Gaussian noise (`ϵ_τ`, `ϵ_a`) and a shared flow time `σ`. The interpolated variables (`τ_σ`, `a_σ`) are created, allowing the trajectory and action denoising processes to evolve synchronously.
- **Shared Transformer and Group-Causal Attention:** A shared Transformer architecture predicts the trajectory and action velocity fields (`v_τ`, `v_a`). A joint flow-matching objective `L_VLA` is used for training. A key architectural detail is the **group-causal attention mask**. Trajectory tokens attend to the multimodal prefix (observations, state, language instruction) and previous trajectory tokens. Action tokens, however, attend to the complete trajectory stream *in addition* to the multimodal prefix and previous action tokens. This asymmetric dependency ensures that the planned trajectory explicitly guides the action denoising process, preventing information from action tokens from influencing trajectory generation.
- **Inference:** At inference time, DT begins with independently sampled trajectory and action noise. It then jointly integrates the learned velocity fields from `σ = 1` to `σ = 0` using Euler steps to produce the planned trajectory `ˆτ` and the initial whole-body action chunk `ˆa`.

#### 3.3. Action Refinement with a Trajectory World Model

To mitigate plan-to-execution deviations, DT incorporates a trajectory world model for refining the VLA-predicted action chunk before execution.
- **Action-Conditioned Trajectory World Model:** This recurrent world model, denoted `W_ϕ`, takes the dual-view observation (`o_t`), proprioceptive state (`s_t`), and a *candidate* action chunk `a_t:t+H-1` as input. It predicts the *induced* end-effector trajectory `˜τ(a)` that this candidate action chunk would produce in the real environment. This model is trained on interaction data to capture deviations arising from contacts, self-collisions, and controller tracking errors, which are not typically captured by analytical kinematics. The language instruction is omitted for the world model as it predicts task-agnostic execution outcomes; task intent is implicitly passed through the VLA-planned trajectory.
- **Test-Time Search-Predict-Score Refinement:** At each replanning step, the initial VLA-predicted action chunk `ˆa` is refined through a three-step procedure:
 1. **Gaussian Sampling:** A set of `N` candidate action chunks `C` is constructed. This set includes the original VLA-predicted chunk `ˆa` and `N-1` perturbation chunks. These perturbations (`ϵ_i`) are generated by adding Gaussian noise, where each action dimension follows an independent AR(1) Gaussian process. For `N=30`, candidates are sampled around `ˆa`.
 2. **Dream Trajectory Predict:** The world model `W_ϕ` evaluates each candidate action chunk in `C` in parallel, predicting its induced trajectory `˜τ(a)` (the expected physical execution outcome).
 3. **Align & Refine:** Each candidate action `a ∈ C` is scored based on two criteria:
 - **Trajectory Agreement (`S_traj(a)`):** This measures how well the world model's predicted induced trajectory `˜τ(a)` aligns with the VLA-planned trajectory `ˆτ`.
 - **Action Smoothness (`S_smooth(a)`):** This penalizes abrupt changes in the candidate action chunk to promote physically executable motions.
 The candidate `˜a` that maximizes a weighted combination of these scores (`λS_traj(a) + (1 − λ)ηS_smooth(a)`) is selected for execution.

#### 3.4. Training

DT's training is conducted in two separate stages:
- **Stage I: VLA Training:** The VLA backbone is initialized with pretrained weights from `π_0.5` and fine-tuned on expert demonstrations. Each training sample includes observations, state, language instruction, the ground-truth future end-effector trajectory (`τ_t:t+H-1`), and the ground-truth whole-body action chunk (`a_t:t+H-1`). Trajectory labels are 7D end-effector poses in the current robot base frame, capturing combined base and arm motion.
- **Stage II: World Model Training:** The trajectory world model is trained on a separate dataset of robot-environment interactions, encompassing both successful and failed trials. Training samples consist of dual-view observations, proprioceptive state, the recorded action chunk (`a_t:t+H-1`), and the *physically executed* trajectory (`τ_exec_t:t+H-1`) induced by those actions. `τ_exec` captures the actual outcomes, including deviations from nominal kinematics. This model is optimized using a supervised trajectory prediction objective.

This modular, two-stage training approach allows the VLA to learn expressive, guided policies, while the world model learns to accurately predict physical execution outcomes, which are then used for online refinement without requiring VLA retraining.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DreamTrajectory (DT) was conducted on both simulated (MS-HAB benchmark) and real-world mobile manipulation tasks, assessing its performance against baselines and analyzing the contributions of its individual components.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiment Results (MS-HAB)

- **Overall Performance:** DreamTrajectory achieved an average success rate of **54.8%** across six MS-HAB subtasks. This represents the highest average success rate among the evaluated end-to-end baselines, including ACT (28.0%), Diffusion Policy (27.7%), RDT-1B (8.7%), GR00T N1 (21.2%), and the `π_0.5` baseline (32.3%).
- **Task-Specific Gains:** DT demonstrated substantial improvements in contact-rich articulated-object tasks. For instance, on "Open Fridge," DT achieved 51.0% success compared to `π_0.5`'s 5.0%. On "Close Counter," DT reached 80.0% success, up from `π_0.5`'s 38.0%. On "Pick Bowl," DT reached 35.0% compared to `π_0.5`'s 19.0%.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study (MS-HAB)

The ablation study isolated the contributions of trajectory guidance and world-model refinement:
- **Trajectory Guidance:** Adding explicit trajectory guidance to the action-only `π_0.5` baseline (referred to as "DT w/o refiner") increased the average success rate from 32.3% to **47.5%**. This represents a 15.2 percentage point gain. Significant improvements were observed in contact-rich tasks: "Open Fridge" increased from 5.0% to 44.0%, and "Close Counter" from 38.0% to 72.0%. Modest decreases were noted on "Pick Apple" and "Open Counter," suggesting the primary benefit is in tasks requiring precise whole-body contact coordination.
- **World-Model Refinement:** Further incorporating the world-model-based test-time refinement ("DT") on top of trajectory guidance resulted in an additional improvement, raising the average success rate from 47.5% to **54.8%**. This 7.3 percentage point gain was observed across all six subtasks, indicating that refinement corrects residual errors even when the initial policy is robust. For example, "Close Counter" improved from 72.0% to 80.0%.
- **Complementary Components:** The overall 22.5-point gain over the action-only baseline was attributed to 15.2 points from trajectory guidance and 7.3 points from refinement, demonstrating that these two components are complementary in enhancing performance.

#### 4.3. World-Model Architecture Ablation

An evaluation of various world model architectures revealed:
- **Superiority of Learned Models:** All learned models (Diffusion, One-shot Transformer, Cross Attention, GRU) substantially outperformed an analytical open-loop forward kinematics (FK) model. FK exhibited an xyz ADE of 0.241m and angular ADE of 27.0°, while learned models achieved significantly lower errors. This highlights the importance of learning to capture non-kinematic factors like contacts and controller errors.
- **GRU Performance:** The GRU-based recurrent world model achieved the best overall accuracy for predicting induced 7D end-effector trajectories over an H=16 horizon. It recorded an xyz Absolute Distance Error (ADE) of 0.028m, xyz Final Distance Error (FDE) of 0.035m, and an Angular ADE of 6.2°.

#### 4.4. Computational Efficiency

- **Parameter Overhead:** The trajectory head added only 0.017M parameters, which is less than 0.01% relative to the 3.5B-parameter `π_0.5` VLA. The trajectory world model added 49.02M parameters, representing 1.40% of the VLA's parameters. The complete DT framework introduced a total of 49.04M additional parameters, or 1.40% of the baseline VLA.
- **Inference Latency:** The additional latency per replanning step was measured at 3.65 ms for the trajectory head and 8.10 ms for the trajectory world model. The complete DT framework incurred an additional latency of 11.75 ms per step. These figures suggest that the computational overhead is limited relative to the performance gains.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiments

Experiments on an ARX LIFT mobile manipulator for three tasks (fruit pick-and-place, drawer opening, and drawer closing) demonstrated consistent improvements:
- **Overall Performance:** The average success rate increased from 63.3% (`π_0.5` baseline) to **81.7%** with trajectory guidance (`DT w/o refiner`), and further to **90.0%** with the complete DT framework.
- **Task-Specific Gains:** "Fruit Pick-and-Place" improved from 45.0% to 80.0% with DT. "Drawer Opening" increased from 60.0% to 90.0%. "Drawer Closing" achieved 100.0% success with trajectory guidance alone. These results indicate that the benefits observed in simulation extend to physical mobile manipulators.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DreamTrajectory framework introduces an explicit end-effector trajectory as an intermediate representation and integrates a world model for real-time plan-execution alignment, offering several points of significance and potential impact for mobile manipulation:

- **Enhanced Precision and Coordination:** By explicitly guiding whole-body action generation with a planned end-effector trajectory, DreamTrajectory addresses the challenge of imprecise base-arm coordination in high-dimensional action spaces. This can lead to more accurate and reliable execution of complex mobile manipulation tasks, particularly those requiring tight coupling between locomotion and dexterous interaction. The substantial gains observed in contact-rich articulated-object tasks on MS-HAB support this claim.

- **Increased Robustness to Execution Errors:** The introduction of a lightweight action-conditioned trajectory world model, combined with a search-predict-score refinement procedure, provides a mechanism for online verification of planned actions. By predicting the induced trajectory of candidate actions and comparing them against the intended plan *before* execution, the system can identify and correct potential deviations arising from control errors, unexpected contacts, or environmental uncertainties. This capability can enhance the robustness of mobile manipulators, reducing the accumulation of errors that often lead to task failures in unstructured environments.

- **Bridging High-Level Intent and Low-Level Control:** DreamTrajectory effectively bridges the gap between high-level task intent (expressed through language and represented by the planned end-effector trajectory) and low-level whole-body control commands. The trajectory serves as a physically meaningful task-space reference, enabling more interpretable and guided action generation compared to purely end-to-end direct action prediction.

- **Computational Efficiency:** The design of the trajectory world model to operate in a compact task-space (trajectory space) rather than high-dimensional observation space (pixel or latent video space) contributes to its computational efficiency. The low parameter overhead and minimal additional inference latency make the refinement process practical for real-time robotic applications, suggesting scalability to various mobile manipulation platforms.

- **Advancement in Generalist Robot Policies:** The framework contributes to the ongoing development of generalist robot policies by enabling more robust and versatile mobile manipulation capabilities. By explicitly addressing challenges specific to mobile platforms—like continuous viewpoint changes and dynamic contact conditions—DreamTrajectory supports the broader goal of deploying robots in diverse, unstructured real-world settings beyond controlled laboratory environments.

- **Implications for Real-World Deployment:** The consistent performance improvements demonstrated on physical mobile manipulators across various tasks (pick-and-place, drawer manipulation) indicate the practical applicability of DreamTrajectory. This suggests a potential for more capable autonomous systems in logistics, healthcare, personal assistance, and other domains where mobile manipulation is critical.

In summary, DreamTrajectory provides a method for more precise and robust mobile manipulation by integrating explicit trajectory guidance and pre-execution plan-execution alignment. Its impact lies in enhancing the reliability and autonomy of mobile robots in complex, dynamic environments.
