# A Whole-Body Motion Imitation Framework from Human Data for Full-Size Humanoid Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework enables full-size humanoid robots to perform diverse and stable human motions by integrating contact-aware motion retargeting with nonlinear centroidal Model Predictive Control. It achieves a 100% success rate and lower tracking errors compared to baselines in challenging motions, demonstrating real-time performance on physical hardware.

## Problem

- Endowing full-size humanoid robots with diverse, stable, and anthropomorphic motions remains a challenge due to kinematic and dynamic discrepancies between humans and robots.
- Traditional model-based robot control methods often produce rigid motions, while data-driven approaches struggle with real-time stability and transfer from simulation to complex physical hardware.
- Existing motion retargeting techniques often fail to produce kinematically feasible or dynamically stable trajectories, and real-time computation of complex multi-rigid body dynamics is frequently unfeasible.

## Method

- A three-stage framework integrates contact-aware whole-body motion retargeting, a nonlinear centroidal ==Model Predictive Control (NMPC)==, and an optimization-based Whole-Body Controller (WBC).
- Motion retargeting uses a Graph Neural Network (GNN) with a comprehensive objective function to generate kinematically feasible and anthropomorphic reference trajectories, explicitly considering foot contacts.
- NMPC ensures real-time dynamic balance, accurate trajectory tracking, and robustness against disturbances by optimizing contact forces and joint velocities within physical and dynamic constraints.

## Results

- Achieved a **100%** success rate across challenging "Wave," "Yoga," and "Stride" motions, demonstrating superior robustness compared to linear MPC and WBC-only baselines.
- Exhibited consistently lower tracking errors, with CoM RMSE for "Yoga" motion at 0.0170m, significantly outperforming baseline methods of 0.0400m (LMPC+WBC) and 0.0747m (WBC).
- Successfully validated on a full-size physical humanoid robot, reliably executing diverse tasks such as upper-body movements, one-legged standing, and simple walking over multiple real-world trials.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of contact-aware, data-driven motion retargeting with model-based nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) is crucial for achieving both anthropomorphic motion and real-time dynamic stability.
> - Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) can effectively handle morphological differences and generate kinematically feasible robot reference trajectories by incorporating contact awareness and specific loss functions.
> - Centroidal dynamics-based NMPC, utilizing a Real-Time Iteration scheme for efficiency, effectively maintains dynamic balance and robustness for complex whole-body motions on a full-size humanoid robot.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of performing anthropomorphic motions that are both diverse and stable remains a critical challenge in robotics. This paper directly addresses this challenge, fitting into the broader landscape of human-robot interaction, teleoperation, and autonomous robotics.

Historically, approaches to endowing humanoid robots with human-like motion capabilities have followed several paths. Traditional model-based methods, focusing on kinematic and dynamic analysis, have been effective in ensuring stability through techniques like Zero Moment Point (ZMP) control. However, these methods often prioritize balance over the anthropomorphism and naturalness of motions, resulting in rigid or unnatural robot behaviors.

More recently, data-driven approaches, particularly those leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) combined with adversarial training, have shown promise in synthesizing graceful and life-like behaviors in simulation. While visually impressive, transferring these learned policies from simulation to complex real-world humanoid robots, with their inherent physical constraints and uncertainties, remains a significant hurdle.

The third significant paradigm, and the one most directly relevant to this paper, involves motion retargeting combined with high-performance controllers. This approach aims to directly translate human motion data onto a robot, offering the highest potential for achieving human-like similarity. However, this method faces two primary challenges:
1. **Kinematic and Dynamic Mismatch:** Humans and humanoid robots possess different morphologies, joint limits, and dynamic properties. Accurately mapping human motion to a robot without violating its physical constraints is complex. Prior attempts, such as inverse kinematics based on morphing parameters or optimization-based strategies, often suffer from accuracy issues, computational expense, or susceptibility to local minima. Data-driven retargeting, while beneficial, can perform inadequately with unfamiliar actions.
2. **Maintaining Dynamic Balance:** Ensuring the robot remains stable in real-time while executing complex, anthropomorphic motions, especially in the presence of disturbances, is another major hurdle. Simplification of dynamic models for real-time computation can lead to instability if predetermined assumptions are violated. Conversely, highly accurate multi-rigid body dynamics models are computationally intensive, making real-time control unfeasible. Model Predictive Control (MPC) has emerged as a compelling solution by balancing these trade-offs, offering the ability to anticipate and react to events while enhancing motion expressiveness.

This paper positions itself by proposing a novel framework that organically integrates contact-aware whole-body motion retargeting with non-linear centroidal Model Predictive Control (NMPC). By doing so, it directly tackles the aforementioned challenges. It builds upon previous work in motion retargeting, notably extending a dual-arm retargeting method to whole-body motion with contact awareness, and leverages advancements in NMPC for legged robots to ensure real-time stability and accuracy. The work distinguishes itself by providing a unified, robust solution demonstrated on a full-size physical humanoid robot, moving beyond simulation or simplified systems. This positions the research at the forefront of efforts to enable more capable, robust, and interactive humanoid robots for diverse real-world applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable a full-size humanoid robot to accurately, robustly, and in real-time imitate a diverse range of complex and expressive human motions from human motion data. This goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Enhancing Humanoid Robot Capabilities:** Anthropomorphic motion is fundamental for humanoid robots to effectively learn human-specific tasks, improve performance in diverse settings, and interact more naturally with humans and environments. The current limitations in motion imitation hinder these capabilities.
2. **Addressing Kinematic and Dynamic Differences:** The significant morphological and dynamic discrepancies between humans and robots present a major challenge. Existing motion retargeting methods often produce motions that are kinematically infeasible or dynamically unstable for robots, motivating the need for a solution that explicitly considers these physical constraints.
3. **Ensuring Real-Time Stability and Robustness:** While imitating human-like motion, maintaining dynamic balance and robustness against external disturbances in real-time is crucial for safe and reliable robot operation. Simplified control models often compromise accuracy, and complex models sacrifice real-time performance. There is a need for a control strategy that can balance these requirements.
4. **Bridging the Gap between Data-Driven and Model-Based Approaches:** The paper seeks to combine the strengths of data-driven motion retargeting (for anthropomorphism) with model-based control (for stability and robustness). The motivation is to create a synergy that overcomes the individual limitations of each approach.

Based on these motivations, the authors outline their specific contributions and objectives:

- **Propose a Unified Framework:** To develop a comprehensive system that seamlessly integrates motion retargeting and model-based control, specifically tailored for full-size humanoid robots, to achieve anthropomorphism and robustness simultaneously.
- **Introduce Contact-Aware Whole-Body Motion Retargeting:** To design a novel retargeting method that can mimic diverse human motions, explicitly considering foot contact with the environment. This aims to generate high-quality, physically plausible reference trajectories that serve as excellent initial conditions for the subsequent controller.
- **Extend Non-linear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) to Whole-Body Humanoid Robots:** To adapt and apply NMPC, which is based on centroidal dynamics, to whole-body humanoid control. The objective is to ensure real-time motion tracking accuracy, maintain balance robustly, and prevent the robot from violating any nonlinear dynamic constraints, even in the presence of external disturbances.
- **Validate on a Real-World Full-Size Humanoid Robot:** To experimentally demonstrate the practical effectiveness and versatility of the proposed method on physical hardware, showcasing its ability to perform a wide range of whole-body motions (e.g., upper-body movements, one-legged standing, quasi-static motions) with high tracking accuracy and stability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for whole-body motion imitation operates as a three-stage pipeline: (1) Whole-Body Motion Retargeting, (2) Nonlinear Centroidal Model Predictive Control (NMPC), and (3) Whole-Body Controller (WBC). This modular design allows for a systematic approach to transforming human motion data into robust robot actions.

#### 3.1. A. Whole-Body Motion Retargeting

The initial stage focuses on transforming human motion data into a kinematically feasible reference trajectory for the humanoid robot. This process is "contact-aware" and designed to address the inherent morphological differences and physical constraints between humans and robots.

1. **Input Data:** The system takes human motion trajectories (joint positions) and extracts binary foot contact signals by detecting near-zero foot height and velocity. The robot's root state is scaled based on the human pelvis joint and corresponding height.
2. **Graph Neural Network (GNN) Architecture:** The method extends a previously developed dual-arm retargeting technique using a GNN.
 - A **graph encoder** ($f_\theta$) processes the human motion, representing the human skeleton as a spatial-temporal graph where nodes are joint positions and edges are joint offsets. This encodes the motion into latent vectors ($Z$).
 - A **graph decoder** ($f_\phi$) then translates these latent vectors into target robot joint angles ($Q_{robot}$), using the robot's structural information (initial offsets and rotations as edge features).
 - **Forward Kinematics** converts the robot joint angles into joint positions ($P_{robot}$).
3. **Objective Function:** To ensure high-quality, anthropomorphic, and physically plausible retargeted motions, a comprehensive objective function ($L$) is minimized, comprising four weighted terms:
 - **Vector Similarity Loss ($L_{vec}$):** Aligns normalized vectors between key human and robot joints (e.g., hips-knees, shoulders-elbows) to maintain motion similarity.
 - **Foot Contact Loss ($L_{foot}$):** Enforces consistency in foot contact with the ground by minimizing the height and velocity of robot feet identified as being in contact based on human data.
 - **Smoothness Loss ($L_{smo}$):** Promotes temporal consistency by penalizing large deviations in joint positions between consecutive time steps.
 - **Self-Collision Loss ($L_{col}$):** Prevents the robot's links from colliding with each other by penalizing close proximity between modeled capsule links.

The output of this stage is either an online reference trajectory for the next moment or a whole offline reference trajectory, complete with a contact schedule.

#### 3.2. B. Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC)

The NMPC component is crucial for dynamic stability, real-time trajectory tracking, and robustness against disturbances. It assumes a given contact schedule, derived from the motion retargeting, and uses the retargeted trajectory as an initial guess.

1. **Optimization Formulation:** The continuous optimal control problem is discretized into a finite-dimensional Nonlinear Program (NLP) using a direct multi-shooting method. To achieve real-time performance, a Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) approach is employed, performing only one SQP step per control instance (Real-Time Iteration scheme).
2. **State and Control Input:**
 - **State vector ($x$):** Includes the Center of Mass (CoM) position and velocity ($h_{com}$), base rotation and orientation ($r_B$, $\theta_B$), and joint angles ($q_j$).
 - **Control input vector ($u$):** Consists of contact forces and torques for each foot ($f_{c_1}$, $\tau_{c_1}$, $f_{c_2}$, $\tau_{c_2}$) and joint velocities ($\dot{q}_j$).
3. **Cost Function:** A least-squares cost function ($l(x(t), u(t), t)$) is defined to minimize deviations from the reference state ($x_{ref}$) and control input ($u_{ref}$) trajectories, weighted by matrices $Q$ and $R$ respectively. A terminal cost ($\Phi(x(T))$) is also included.
4. **Constraints:**
 - **Equality Constraints:** These enforce physical laws and desired behaviors. For contact points ($c_i$):
 - Stance feet: Velocity must be zero ($v_{c_i} = 0$), implying no slip.
 - Swing feet: Must track a reference trajectory ($v_{c_i} \cdot \hat{n} = v^*(t)$), and contact forces/torques must be zero ($f_{c_i} = 0, \tau_{c_i} = 0$).
 - **Inequality Constraints:** These define physical limits and environmental interactions:
 - **Surface Friction Cone:** Ensures contact forces remain within the friction limits for stability.
 - **Joint Position and Torque Limits:** Prevents the robot from exceeding its mechanical operational boundaries.

The NMPC continuously computes optimal robot states and control inputs to track the reference trajectory while maintaining dynamic balance and resisting disturbances.

#### 3.3. C. Whole-Body Controller (WBC)

The final stage is an optimization-based Whole-Body Controller that translates the NMPC's optimized state and control input trajectories into actual joint torques for the robot.

1. **Optimization Problem:** Instead of a hierarchical optimization, the WBC formulates a single Quadratic Program (QP). High-priority tasks (e.g., maintaining balance, avoiding joint limits) are treated as hard constraints, while lower-priority tasks (e.g., precise tracking of specific joint positions) are embedded in the cost function.
2. **Dynamics Integration:** This controller explicitly considers the full nonlinear rigid body dynamics of the robot, ensuring that the computed torques are physically consistent and lead to the desired motion.

The WBC computes the desired joint torques ($\tau^*$) that are then executed by the real full-size humanoid robot, completing the motion imitation pipeline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research team conducted extensive experiments both in simulation and on a real-world full-size humanoid robot to validate their proposed framework.

#### 4.1. A. Experimental Setup

The experiments utilized a full-size humanoid robot (1.5m height, 52kg weight, 27 degrees of freedom). The simulation environment was built using Raisim. The NMPC ran at an average update rate of 30Hz with a time horizon $T_d = 1s$. The OCS2 library was used for solving the NMPC problem, and qpOASES was employed for the WBC's QP problem. For motion retargeting, the AMASS human motion dataset was used for training (8:2 training/test split) with an Adam optimizer, achieving robust performance after 20 epochs on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 D GPU and Intel i9-14900KF CPU.

#### 4.2. B. Case Studies and Performance Evaluation

1. **Offline Motion Retargeting:** The method successfully retargeted complex human motions like "high-level yoga balance" and "high-dynamic stride walking" (Figure 3). Crucially, the retargeting process accurately considered and preserved the foot contact sequences between the human and the robot, providing high-quality reference trajectories.
2. **Online Motion Retargeting:** The framework demonstrated real-time online mapping at 30Hz (Figure 4). A computational time evaluation (Table I) showed that the motion retargeting module averaged 11.34 ms, the Nonlinear MPC 23.72 ms, and the Whole-Body Control a mere 0.31 ms. The combined time of approximately 35 ms allows for real-time operation at the desired 30Hz frequency.
3. **Push Recovery:** In simulation, the humanoid robot effectively resisted external disturbances while performing both standing and walking motions (Figure 5). For instance, the robot successfully recovered from a 140 Ns disturbance during stance and a 150 Ns disturbance during walking. The NMPC controller allowed the robot to actively swing its arms and adjust its base position and momentum to maintain stability, validating its robustness.
4. **Generalization to Another Humanoid Robot:** The authors also demonstrated the method's transferability by testing it on a different humanoid robot, as detailed in the accompanying videos.

#### 4.3. C. Comparative Study

A quantitative comparison was conducted against two baseline methods: (1) a linear MPC approach combined with a Whole-Body Controller (LMPC+WBC) and (2) an ablation study using only the Whole-Body Controller with direct retargeting results (WBC). The evaluation focused on success rate and tracking accuracy (RMSE for CoM, leg, and arm joints) for "Wave," "Yoga," and "Stride" motions (Table II).

- **Success Rate:** The proposed method achieved a perfect 10/10 success rate across all three challenging motions (Wave, Yoga, Stride). In contrast, LMPC+WBC achieved 10/10 for Wave, but only 5/10 for Yoga and 7/10 for Stride. The WBC-only approach performed even worse, with 8/10 for Wave, 2/10 for Yoga, and complete failure for Stride. This highlights the superior robustness and capability of the integrated NMPC.
- **Tracking Error (RMSE):** The proposed method consistently demonstrated lower average RMSE across CoM, leg joints, and arm joints for all motions compared to both baselines. For example, for "Yoga" motion, our method achieved CoM RMSE of 0.0170m compared to 0.0400m (LMPC+WBC) and 0.0747m (WBC).
- **Insights:** The NMPC module's ability to consider a more precise dynamic model and actively synthesize motion stability over the prediction horizon significantly improved both accuracy and robustness, especially for highly dynamic motions. Interestingly, the arm joint tracking performance was generally better than the leg joints. This is attributed to the NMPC prioritizing the robot's centroidal dynamics and balance, allowing leg joints to deviate slightly from the retargeting results to ensure overall stability, a necessary trade-off for robust locomotion.

#### 4.4. D. Real-World Experiment

The most compelling findings involved the successful deployment and testing on physical humanoid robot hardware (Figure 6). The robot, equipped with the proposed framework, successfully performed:

- Upper-body motions while maintaining stance balance.
- A hand-raising motion.
- A challenging one-legged standing task.
- Simple walking tasks (as shown in videos).

Each real-world motion was tested five times without failure, demonstrating the practical reliability and robustness of the system. The use of a protective rope did not exert any force, ensuring the robot's performance was genuine.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in the domain of real-time human motion imitation. The proposed whole-body motion imitation framework offers a robust and effective solution to a long-standing challenge: enabling full-size humanoid robots to perform diverse, anthropomorphic, and dynamically stable motions derived from human data.

#### 5.1. Key Significance

1. **Unified and Robust Framework:** The core strength lies in the seamless and effective integration of contact-aware motion retargeting with real-time non-linear centroidal Model Predictive Control (NMPC) and a Whole-Body Controller. This unified approach successfully bridges the gap between generating human-like kinematic references and ensuring dynamic stability and robustness on complex robotic platforms.
2. **Enhanced Real-Time Performance and Robustness:** The use of NMPC, tailored for whole-body humanoid control, is crucial. It allows the robot to track complex trajectories accurately, maintain balance robustly against external disturbances, and adhere to non-linear dynamic constraints in real-time. This is a substantial improvement over methods that sacrifice either precision or computational efficiency. The push recovery experiments clearly demonstrate this robustness.
3. **High-Quality Anthropomorphic Motion:** The contact-aware graph neural network-based motion retargeting component effectively handles morphological differences between humans and robots, producing kinematically feasible and high-quality reference trajectories that preserve the expressive nature of human motion, including crucial foot contact information.
4. **Validation on Full-Size Hardware:** Demonstrating the framework's capabilities on a real-world, full-size humanoid robot, performing diverse and challenging tasks like one-legged standing and walking, is a critical achievement. It validates the practical applicability and reliability of the theoretical and simulated results, moving beyond simplified models or simulation-only validation.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

1. **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction:** More natural and expressive robot motions can significantly enhance human-robot interaction. Robots that move like humans are more intuitive, predictable, and engaging to interact with, potentially leading to safer and more effective collaboration in various settings, from homes to workplaces.
2. **Expanding Robot Application Domains:** The ability to accurately imitate diverse human motions unlocks new possibilities for humanoid robots in fields requiring complex, human-like dexterity and balance. This includes assistive robotics (e.g., helping the elderly or disabled), industrial tasks (e.g., performing complex assembly or manipulation), exploration in unstructured environments, and entertainment.
3. **Foundation for Advanced Locomotion and Manipulation:** This framework provides a solid foundation for future research into more complex robot behaviors. It lays the groundwork for developing humanoid robots capable of more dynamic locomotion (e.g., running, jumping) and intricate manipulation tasks by extending the principles to multi-contact scenarios and dexterous hands.
4. **Contribution to Robot Learning and Control:** The work contributes valuable insights into how to effectively combine data-driven learning with model-based control for complex, high-dimensional robotic systems, offering a blueprint for future developments in robust and adaptive robot control.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge current limitations, which also serve as clear directions for future research:
- **Lack of Dexterous Hand Control:** The current framework focuses on whole-body motion but does not encompass the control of dexterous hands, which are critical for fine manipulation.
- **Limited Multi-Contact Maneuvers:** The method primarily focuses on single or dual-foot contact scenarios and currently lacks consideration for more complex multi-contact maneuvers (e.g., using hands to push off surfaces, leaning against walls for support).

Future work aims to address these limitations by extending the framework to incorporate dexterous hand control and enable the robot to perform multi-contact tasks. Overcoming these challenges would further enhance the versatility and utility of humanoid robots, bringing them closer to realizing their full potential as highly capable anthropomorphic assistants.
