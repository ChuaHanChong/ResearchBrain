# SlotPi: Physics-informed Object-centric Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SlotPi presents an object-centric reasoning model that integrates universal physical laws derived from Hamiltonian mechanics into its predictive framework. This approach enables improved accuracy and adaptability across diverse dynamic scenarios, including complex rigid body interactions, fluid dynamics, and coupled fluid-object systems.

## Problem

- Existing object-centric dynamic simulation methods often lack explicit integration of fundamental physical laws, leading to physically inconsistent predictions.
- Current models frequently lack rigorous validation across diverse and complex real-world scenarios, particularly those involving intricate fluid dynamics and coupled fluid-object interactions.

## Method

- **SlotPi** introduces a modular architecture combining a Physical Module that leverages Hamiltonian principles via attention to compute generalized momentum and coordinates, ensuring physical consistency.
- A Spatiotemporal Reasoning Module captures non-conservative forces and learned patterns, with outputs from both modules integrated via a weighted sum for comprehensive dynamic updates.
- The model utilizes upstream object-centric models (e.g., STATM-SAVi) to extract initial slot representations from input video frames, which are then processed for future state prediction.

## Results

- SlotPi achieved state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets like CLEVRER (e.g., 1.6 to 3.02 point advantage in FG-ARI over baselines) and for predictive Visual Question Answering on CLEVRER and Physion.
- The model significantly outperformed specialized fluid prediction models (FNO, U-Net) on Navier-Stokes datasets and accurately simulated complex fluid-object interactions in a new real-world dataset, demonstrating robust predictive capabilities.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of the physics module (best performance when fully weighted, 𝜆=1) and the superiority of attention-based Hamiltonian embedding over MLP-based approaches for dynamic reasoning efficiency and accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding Hamiltonian mechanics through an attention-based network provides a generalizable and effective method for integrating universal physical laws into data-driven models for robust dynamic prediction.
> - The combination of a physics-informed module with a spatiotemporal reasoning module is crucial for accurately capturing both conservative and non-conservative forces in complex real-world dynamics.
> - The proposed architecture successfully generalizes its predictive capabilities to heterogeneous physical systems, allowing for unified modeling of rigid bodies, fluid dynamics, and their coupled interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence and computer vision, primarily **object-centric learning, intuitive physics, and world models**.

- **Object-Centric Learning:** The paper explicitly builds upon recent advancements in slot-based object-centric learning methods (e.g., SlotFormer, STATM, SAVi). These methods aim to decompose complex visual scenes into individual object representations, enabling models to reason about distinct entities rather than processing the entire scene holistically. SlotPi utilizes these upstream object-centric models for slot extraction, signifying its reliance on and contribution to this paradigm.
- **Intuitive Physics and World Models:** A core inspiration for SlotPi comes from human "intuitive physics," the innate ability to understand and predict physical interactions. This area is critical for building AI systems that can reason about the world like humans. World models, which aim to learn a compact representation of the environment and its dynamics, are a key component of this. SlotPi's goal is to simulate human intuitive physical behavior, enhancing object-centric models with a deeper understanding of physical laws to build more adaptive world models.
- **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs):** While traditional PINNs typically embed partial differential equations (PDEs) or boundary conditions directly, SlotPi takes a unique approach by embedding "universal physical laws" inspired by Hamiltonian mechanics. This is a novel way of integrating physics knowledge into a data-driven model, differing from methods that rely on pre-known specific fluid equations. This positions SlotPi as a physics-informed model but with a more generalizable physical foundation.
- **Dynamic Simulation and Prediction:** The paper addresses the challenge of predicting complex dynamics, including rigid body interactions, fluid dynamics, and coupled fluid-object interactions. Current object-centric models often struggle with these complexities, particularly the lack of explicit physical knowledge and limited validation on diverse, real-world scenarios. SlotPi aims to fill these gaps by making predictions that are more consistent with physical laws, thus extending the capabilities of existing predictive world models.
- **Bridging Data-Driven and Physics-Based Approaches:** The work represents an effort to combine the strengths of data-driven deep learning models (for learning complex patterns from raw visual data) with the robustness and generalization of physics-based models (for adhering to fundamental physical principles). This hybrid approach is a significant trend in AI, addressing limitations of purely black-box neural networks.

In summary, SlotPi pushes the boundaries of object-centric learning by embedding a generalized physics-informed module, validated across a wider range of physical phenomena, including challenging fluid and fluid-object interactions, which is a notable step towards more robust and interpretable AI for dynamic scene understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The authors clearly articulate two critical limitations in existing object-centric dynamic simulation methods, which directly motivate the development of SlotPi:

1. **Lack of Physical Knowledge Integration:** Current models, despite progress in scene decomposition and interaction computation, often overlook the fundamental physical laws governing the world. Humans develop "common-sense" physical insights through observation (e.g., objects don't stop abruptly without external forces) and apply this knowledge to reason about dynamics. Existing AI models typically do not explicitly incorporate such physical principles, limiting their ability to produce physically consistent predictions, especially in novel or complex scenarios.
2. **Insufficient Validation Across Diverse and Real-World Scenarios:** Current object-centric models often lack rigorous validation on a broad spectrum of dynamic scenarios, particularly those involving intricate interactions like fluid dynamics and coupled fluid-object interactions. Real-world dynamics are highly complex, with fluid motion influencing objects and vice-versa. Models need to capture these characteristics accurately to be truly adaptive.

To address these gaps, the **key objectives** of SlotPi are:

- To propose a novel, generalizable latent Hamiltonian network module that explicitly models temporal dynamics and latent physical interactions for high-level physical reasoning. This module is intended to support unified modeling of heterogeneous physical systems (rigid bodies, fluids, and their interactions) while preserving decoupled representations of internal dynamics.
- To introduce SlotPi, an unsupervised object-centric reasoning model that transcends the limitations of conventional rigid-body and particle-based systems by effectively modeling complex dynamical systems. This aims to demonstrate strong adaptability and advanced physical reasoning capabilities.
- To construct and validate the model on a new real-world dataset featuring scenarios with higher complexity, such as fluid-object interactions, to prove its robustness and applicability in diverse, challenging environments.

The overarching motivation is to simulate human intuitive physical behavior, constructing a more adaptable object-centric reasoning system that integrates universal physical laws, thereby laying a foundation for developing more advanced world models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SlotPi is a slot-based physics-informed object-centric reasoning model designed to integrate physical laws into dynamic predictions. Its architecture is modular, comprising two primary components: a Physical Module and a Spatiotemporal Reasoning Module, which work synergistically.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The model first leverages an upstream object-centric model (e.g., SAVi, STATM-SAVi, STEVE) to extract slot representations {𝑆₀..., 𝑆𝑡} from input video frames {𝑥₀..., 𝑥𝑡}. Each 𝑆𝑡 represents N slots, each a D-dimensional vector capturing information about an object or background entity at time t. SlotPi then processes these slots to predict future states.

#### 3.2. Physical Module

This module is explicitly designed based on **Hamiltonian principles** to impose physical constraints on the model's predictions.
- **Generalized Momentum and Coordinates:** Using the input slots 𝑆𝑡, a cross-attention mechanism computes the generalized momentum 𝑃𝑡, drawing information from historical corresponding slots ({𝑠^(i,0)..., 𝑠^(i,𝑡-1)}). Simultaneously, a self-attention mechanism computes the generalized coordinates 𝑄𝑡 from the current slots 𝑆𝑡. The use of attention allows the model to dynamically determine relevance for these physical quantities.
- **Total Energy (Hamiltonian):** The total energy 𝐻𝑡 of the system is computed by applying self-attention to both 𝑄𝑡 and 𝑃𝑡. A linear layer with a Softplus activation ensures that the energy remains positive.
- **Hamilton's Equations and Euler's Method:** Based on 𝐻𝑡, the rates of change for 𝑃𝑡 and 𝑄𝑡 are derived using partial derivatives (Hamilton's canonical equations: 𝜕𝐻𝑡/𝜕𝑃𝑡 and -𝜕𝐻𝑡/𝜕𝑄𝑡). Euler’s method is then applied to numerically integrate these rates, computing the predicted momentum 𝑏𝑃𝑡+1 and coordinates 𝑏𝑄𝑡+1 for the next time step. A small Δ𝑡 is used to prevent excessive energy fluctuations.

#### 3.3. Spatiotemporal Reasoning Module

Recognizing that real-world dynamics often involve non-conservative forces (like friction or external influences) and energy dissipation that a pure Hamiltonian system cannot fully capture, this module is introduced.
- **Temporal and Spatial Reasoning:** Similar to prior work like STATM, this module employs a cross-attention mechanism for temporal dynamic reasoning (considering historical slots {𝑆₀..., 𝑆𝑡}) and a self-attention mechanism for computing spatial interactions between different slots within 𝑆𝑡.
- **Combined Output:** The outputs from temporal and spatial attention mechanisms are summed to produce 𝑐𝑆𝑇𝑡+1, representing the dynamic updates not explicitly captured by the physical module.

#### 3.4. Prediction Integration

The final predicted slot representation 𝑏𝑆𝑡+1 is obtained by a weighted sum of the output from the Physical Module (𝑏𝑄𝑡+1, which represents the physically constrained state) and the Spatiotemporal Reasoning Module (𝑐𝑆𝑇𝑡+1, which captures non-conservative dynamics and other learned patterns): 𝑏𝑆𝑡+1 = 𝜆 𝑏𝑄𝑡+1 + 𝑐𝑆𝑇𝑡+1. The weighting factor 𝜆 is typically set to 1, but its impact is also investigated in ablation studies.

#### 3.5. Training

- **Loss Function:** The model is trained to minimize the ℓ2 loss between the predicted slots and ground-truth slots (L𝑆), simulating an autoregressive generation process. When an object-centric model also handles image reconstruction, an image reconstruction loss (L𝐼, also ℓ2) is added, leading to a total loss L = L𝑆 + L𝐼.
- **Optimization:** Adam (or AdamW for fluid experiments) optimizer is used with specific learning rates and warm-up/decay schedules.
- **Data Handling:** The paper details specific configurations for different datasets, including encoder choices (CNN, ResNet, patch embedding), number of slots, slot dimensions, and training hyperparameters to optimize performance for each scenario.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SlotPi underwent extensive evaluation across various datasets and tasks, demonstrating robust performance and adaptability.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Dataset Experiments (OBJ3D, CLEVRER)

- **Visual Quality and Object Dynamics:** On OBJ3D, SlotPi achieved the best LPIPS score and performed well on PSNR and SSIM. On the more complex CLEVRER dataset, SlotPi significantly outperformed baselines (SlotFormer, STATM) across all visual quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and object dynamics metrics (FG-ARI, FG-mIoU, ARI). Notably, SlotPi showed a 1.6 to 3.02 point advantage in FG-ARI over STATM and SlotFormer on CLEVRER, indicating more accurate dynamic simulations.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons (Figure 2, Figures S1, S2) revealed that SlotPi maintained consistent object dynamics and image quality over long sequences, while baselines exhibited issues like incorrect dynamics, blurriness, inaccurate colors, and object deformation.
- **Visual Question Answering (VQA):** For predictive VQA tasks on CLEVRER, SlotPi surpassed previous state-of-the-art (STATM), achieving first place on the public leaderboard for "per option" accuracy and second for "per question." On the challenging Physion dataset, SlotPi outperformed all baselines, including scene-centric diffusion models (VDT), suggesting that its predictions are more physically consistent. (Figure S3 shows qualitative results for Physion, noting that pixel quality is limited by the upstream decoder, not SlotPi itself).

#### 4.2. Fluid Dataset Experiments (Navier-Stokes)

- **Quantitative Performance:** On a fluid dataset based on Navier-Stokes equations, SlotPi significantly outperformed specialized fluid prediction models like FNO and U-Net across RMSE, MAE, and HCT metrics, indicating superior accuracy in predicting complex spatiotemporal fluid patterns.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations (Figure 3) showed that SlotPi's fluid dynamics predictions closely aligned with ground truth.
- **Adaptability:** This demonstrates SlotPi's capacity to model fluid dynamics effectively, extending its applicability beyond rigid body interactions.

#### 4.3. Real-world Dataset Experiments (Fluid-Object Interactions)

- **Scene Decomposition:** The upstream STATM-SAVi, with a ResNet encoder tailored for this dataset, effectively decomposed scenes into individual object features (Table S2, Figure 5, S4), maintaining slot consistency over time even with reflections or reduced fluid transparency.
- **Prediction Performance:** SlotPi significantly outperformed SlotFormer and STATM in both image quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and dynamic simulation (FG-ARI, FG-mIoU, ARI) on this challenging dataset.
- **Qualitative Results:** Figure 4 illustrates SlotPi's ability to accurately simulate complex interactions, such as objects rotating on water surfaces and non-trivial fluid-object dynamics (e.g., a yellow object's upward motion being influenced by a red object and fluid, leading to rotation and altered trajectory).
- **Robustness:** This part of the evaluation strongly validates SlotPi's robust predictive capabilities and adaptability across a composite scenario involving both fluid and object dynamics.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Physical Module (Effect of 𝜆):** Experiments varying the weight 𝜆 for the physical module showed that setting 𝜆=1 (full contribution from the physical module) yielded the best performance on CLEVRER. When 𝜆 was learnable, it gradually increased towards 1 during training, reinforcing the importance of the physical module. Removing the physical module (𝜆=0) led to inferior performance, highlighting its crucial contribution.
- **Hamiltonian Embedding Method:** Comparing MLP-based embedding with attention-based embedding for the Hamiltonian equations, the attention mechanism significantly outperformed MLPs in FG-ARI, FG-mIoU, and ARI on CLEVRER (Table 8, S4). Attention was also more efficient, requiring only one block compared to multiple MLP layers, thus avoiding a sharp increase in model parameters.
- **Number of Training Frames:** On the real-world dataset, using more burn-in and rollout frames (10 burn-in, 15 rollout) performed better than fewer (6 burn-in, 10 rollout). This was particularly important for slow movements influenced by fluids, where insufficient frames could lead the model to mistakenly predict objects as stationary (Figure 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SlotPi model represents a significant step forward in the field of object-centric reasoning and intuitive physics for artificial intelligence, with several notable implications:

- **Enhanced Physical Consistency and Generalization:** By embedding universal physical laws derived from Hamiltonian mechanics, SlotPi consistently produces predictions that are more physically plausible and stable over longer sequences. This intrinsic understanding of physics allows the model to generalize better to novel scenarios and interactions, which is a key challenge for purely data-driven models.
- **Broadened Scope of Dynamic Modeling:** SlotPi successfully models a diverse range of physical phenomena, including rigid body interactions, complex fluid dynamics, and crucially, the coupled interactions between fluids and objects. This goes beyond the capabilities of many existing object-centric models that are typically limited to rigid body systems. This expanded scope makes it more applicable to real-world environments where such mixed interactions are common.
- **Improved Performance in Downstream Tasks:** The superior dynamic predictions translate directly into better performance on downstream tasks like Visual Question Answering (VQA), particularly for predictive questions. This suggests that a deeper understanding of physical dynamics is vital for higher-level cognitive reasoning in AI.
- **Contribution to World Model Development:** The paper explicitly states that SlotPi lays a foundation for developing more advanced world models. A robust world model needs to accurately simulate future states of its environment. By integrating physics, SlotPi moves closer to building AI systems that can mentally "simulate" complex realities in a human-like fashion.
- **Novel Physics-Informed Approach:** SlotPi’s method of embedding Hamiltonian theory via attention mechanisms, without relying on pre-known specific PDEs or integrating them into network structures (like some PINNs), offers a more generalizable way to infuse physical knowledge into neural networks. This could inspire new directions in physics-informed machine learning.
- **Real-world Applicability:** The creation and successful validation on a new, challenging real-world dataset featuring fluid-object interactions underscore the practical relevance and potential for deployment of such models in applications like robotics, autonomous systems, and advanced simulation.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge certain limitations, which also point to avenues for future research:
- **Per-Dataset Retraining and Configuration:** The model currently requires retraining and specific configuration adjustments for each new dataset, hindering true "general-purpose" applicability.
- **Two-Stage Training:** The reliance on an upstream object-centric model for slot extraction means SlotPi operates in a two-stage process. A unified, end-to-end architecture that jointly trains the object-centric module and SlotPi on diverse datasets would be a significant advancement.

Overall, SlotPi makes a substantial contribution to building more intelligent and adaptive AI systems by effectively merging the strengths of data-driven object-centric learning with the fundamental principles of physics. Its demonstrated performance across a broad spectrum of dynamic scenarios marks a promising step towards AI with more intuitive physical reasoning capabilities.
