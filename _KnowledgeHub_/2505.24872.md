# ProxyThinker: Test-Time Guidance through Small Visual Reasoners

> [!summary] Summary
> PROXYTHINKER introduces a training-free inference-time method that guides large untuned vision-language models to exhibit "slow-thinking" visual reasoning, leveraging smaller RFT-trained experts. This approach enables large models to achieve performance competitive with or surpassing full RFT-trained counterparts while significantly reducing computational overhead.

## Problem

- Training "slow-thinking" reasoning capabilities in large vision-language models (LVLMs) using Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) is computationally intensive and resource-demanding.
- The high costs of RFT limit its application to smaller LVLMs, typically under 7 billion parameters, creating a bottleneck for scaling advanced reasoning to more capable base models.
- Existing RFT paradigms require maintaining multiple model copies, leading to high memory usage and extensive training times.

## Method

- Guides a large, untuned base Vision-Language Model (VLM) during inference by dynamically modifying its ==output distribution== using a logit shift derived from a small RFT-trained "Expert" model and an untrained "Amateur" model.
- The logit shift, `α · (z_ψ1 - z_ψ0)`, isolates and transfers the token-level biases introduced by RFT in the small expert to the large base model's logits.
- Optimized for efficiency using the vLLM framework with asynchronous tensor parallelism, achieving significant speedups in inference latency compared to prior multi-model decoding methods.

## Results

- Consistently improved visual reasoning accuracy across MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, MMMU-Pro, and R1-OneVisionBench, with untuned base models often competing with or outperforming full RFT-trained counterparts.
- Achieved a **38x** speedup in inference over naive multi-model decoding, with an optimized runtime that closely matches running a **32B** RFT expert directly, demonstrating practical efficiency.
- Enabled the emergence of sophisticated reasoning behaviors in large models, including a **137.4%** increase in backtracking and a **43.9%** increase in verification behaviors on MathVision, inherited from the RFT expert.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Fine-Tuning primarily serves to elicit and amplify existing reasoning behaviors within a base model's sampling distribution, rather than imparting fundamentally new knowledge.
> - Sophisticated "slow-thinking" reasoning patterns, such as self-verification and self-correction, can be effectively transferred from small, specialized RFT models to larger generalist models at inference time.
> - Strategic, training-free logit manipulation during decoding offers a computationally efficient alternative to expensive full model retraining for imparting complex behaviors to large language models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of large vision-language models (LVLMs), advanced reasoning capabilities ("slow-thinking" models), and efficient inference techniques. The broader landscape of AI research has seen a significant surge in the development of models capable of multi-step reasoning and deliberation, often dubbed "slow-thinking" models. These models, exemplified by architectures like OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1, and QwQ, distinguish themselves from "fast-thinking" counterparts by engaging in iterative self-reflection and generating detailed intermediate reasoning steps, mimicking human problem-solving processes. Such models have demonstrated remarkable success in complex domains, particularly in mathematical and scientific reasoning, and have recently been extended to multimodal tasks, enhancing LVLMs' performance in scenarios requiring structured understanding across modalities.

A common and highly effective paradigm for developing these "slow-thinking" capabilities, especially in LVLMs, is Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), often implemented through Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT). While powerful, RFT is notoriously computationally intensive and resource-demanding. It typically involves maintaining multiple model copies, leading to high memory usage, and alternates between computationally expensive rollout and optimization phases, resulting in extensive training times. These high costs have significantly hindered the application of RFT to very large LVLMs (e.g., those with more than 7 billion parameters), creating a bottleneck for scaling advanced reasoning to larger, more capable base models.

PROXYTHINKER directly addresses this critical computational challenge. It builds upon recent observations suggesting that RFT primarily "elicits and amplifies reasoning behaviors that are already included in the sampling distributions of the base model," rather than teaching entirely new knowledge. This insight motivates the development of inference-time techniques that can achieve similar reasoning enhancements without the exorbitant training costs. The paper explicitly draws parallels to a line of work on "decoding-steering" of language models (e.g., DExperts, Contrastive Decoding), which modify model output distributions at inference time to improve generation quality or control specific attributes. PROXYTHINKER carves out a unique niche by focusing specifically on transferring *reasoning abilities* from small, RFT-trained visual reasoners to large, untuned base models, distinguishing its motivation and contribution from prior decoding-time methods that target other aspects like toxicity or genericness. By providing a training-free solution to a costly problem, PROXYTHINKER contributes to making advanced visual reasoning capabilities more accessible and scalable across the spectrum of LVLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PROXYTHINKER stems from the high computational and resource costs associated with training "slow-thinking" capabilities in large vision-language models (LVLMs) using Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT). While RFT has proven highly effective in imparting sophisticated visual reasoning, self-verification, and self-correction behaviors, its intensive nature, requiring significant GPU memory and extensive training hours, severely limits its application to models beyond a certain size, typically constraining it to models with fewer than 7 billion parameters. This creates a significant barrier to scaling advanced reasoning to the most capable large base models.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a training-free method for reasoning transfer:** To enable large, untuned LVLMs to acquire "slow-thinking" visual reasoning capabilities without incurring any additional training costs for the large base model.
2. **Elicit sophisticated reasoning behaviors:** To modify the decoding dynamics of large models such that they exhibit emergent behaviors similar to RFT-trained models, such as self-verification and self-correction, crucial for complex problem-solving.
3. **Achieve competitive performance:** To enable untuned large base models, when guided by PROXYTHINKER, to achieve performance on par with or even surpass their full-scale RFT-trained counterparts on challenging visual reasoning benchmarks.
4. **Ensure practical efficiency:** To implement the proposed method with optimized parallelism techniques, significantly reducing inference-time overhead compared to previous multi-model decoding methods, thereby paving the way for practical deployment.

In essence, PROXYTHINKER aims to democratize access to advanced visual reasoning by decoupling the acquisition of reasoning patterns from the computationally expensive RFT process, allowing larger and more powerful base models to leverage the reasoning expertise distilled into smaller, more affordably trained models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PROXYTHINKER introduces an inference-time technique that guides a large Vision-Language Model (VLM) to adopt "slow-thinking" reasoning patterns without any parameter updates or fine-tuning of the large model itself. The core idea is to leverage the subtle differences in token probabilities between an RFT-trained "Expert" model and its non-RFT "Amateur" counterpart to steer the decoding process of a larger "Base" model.

The approach involves three distinct models during inference:
1. **Large Base Model (Ψ):** This is the target model (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-32B or 72B-Instruct) whose reasoning capabilities are to be enhanced. It remains untuned.
2. **Small Reasoning Expert Model (ψ₁):** A smaller VLM (e.g., OpenVLThinker-7B, VL-Rethinker-7B) that has undergone Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) and exhibits desirable "slow-thinking" reasoning behaviors.
3. **Small Amateur Model (ψ₀):** A small base VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) of the same architecture as the Expert model, but *without* RFT.

At each decoding step *t*, all three models (Ψ, ψ₁, ψ₀) process the current input images *I* and the text prefix *x*<*t* to produce their respective logit vectors: *z*Ψ, *z*ψ₁, and *z*ψ₀. PROXYTHINKER then modifies the output distribution of the large Base model Ψ using a logit shift calculated from the difference between the Expert and Amateur models' logits:

`p(x_t | x_<t, I) = softmax [z_Ψ (x_t | x_<t, I) + α · (z_ψ1 (x_t | x_<t, I) − z_ψ0 (x_t | x_<t, I))]`

Here, `α` is a positive hyperparameter that controls the influence of this logit difference. The rationale is that the term `(z_ψ1 - z_ψ0)` isolates the token-level biases and shifts in probability mass introduced by the RFT process in the small Expert model. By adding this delta, scaled by `α`, to the logits of the large Base model, PROXYTHINKER effectively "transfers" these RFT-induced reasoning patterns. This steers the large model towards generating token sequences that reflect structured, reflective, and self-correcting reasoning strategies, such as assigning higher probabilities to tokens indicating self-verification ("Wait", "However") at critical points in the reasoning process, as exemplified in Figure 2. A token *x*t is then sampled from this adjusted distribution and appended to the input sequence, and the process continues autoregressively.

**Implementation Details:**
To address the computational overhead of running multiple models concurrently, PROXYTHINKER incorporates significant optimizations:
- **vLLM Framework:** The system is implemented within the vLLM framework, leveraging its advanced inference features like KV cache, tensor parallelism, and continuous batching. This departs from naive coarse-grained pipeline parallelism used in earlier decoding-time methods, which led to substantial GPU idle time.
- **Optimized Tensor Parallelism:** Tensor parallelism is applied to the large Base model. Crucially, the small Expert and Amateur models are run *asynchronously* in separate tensor-parallel process groups. Logits from all models are then synchronized via collective communication just before the final sampling step. This optimized scheduling minimizes GPU idle time, significantly reducing inference latency.

This methodology allows for efficient migration of reasoning behaviors learned by smaller, RFT-trained models to larger, more capable base models without the prohibitive costs of direct RFT training, making advanced reasoning more practical for large-scale LVLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PROXYTHINKER demonstrates a consistent and substantial improvement in the visual reasoning capabilities of large, untuned vision-language models across various challenging benchmarks. The key findings and results are:

1. **Consistent Performance Boost:** PROXYTHINKER consistently boosts performance across nearly all evaluated mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision) and multi-disciplinary reasoning benchmarks (MMMU-Pro, R1-OneVisionBench). For instance, using OpenVLThinker-7B as an expert improved Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct's MathVision accuracy from 38.4% to 40.8%.
2. **Competitiveness with Full-Scale RFT:** Remarkably, PROXYTHINKER enables untuned base models to compete with and, in some cases, even surpass the performance of their full-scale RFT counterparts. The example above shows PROXYTHINKER outperforming the directly RFT-trained VL-Rethinker-32B (40.5%) on MathVision, despite the small expert's poor accuracy (25.3%). This suggests that the improvement is not from knowledge transfer but from efficient activation of latent reasoning abilities in the large model.
3. **Scalability Across Model Sizes:** The method remains effective for larger base models. At the 32B scale, PROXYTHINKER achieves similar average relative improvements to full RFT models. For 72B models, it still yields consistent, albeit smaller, gains, demonstrating its robustness as model size increases.
4. **Importance of RFT Expert Quality:** The degree of improvement is generally correlated with the quality of the RFT expert. Stronger RFT experts, like VL-Rethinker-7B, which provide more structured reasoning paths, lead to more effective enhancements. Conversely, underperforming or undersized experts can misguide the base model and introduce negative transfer effects, highlighting the critical role of a well-aligned expert.
5. **Robustness to Hyperparameter `α`:** The single hyperparameter `α` (controlling the logit difference influence) shows high robustness. Values between 0.1 and 1.0 consistently yield noticeable improvements over the base model, indicating that the method is not overly sensitive to fine-tuning this parameter. The results also show PROXYTHINKER significantly outperforms a modified contrastive decoding baseline, emphasizing the crucial role of the Amateur model in calibrating the logit distribution.
6. **Reasoning Capacity Boundary (Pass@k):** PROXYTHINKER demonstrates a balanced approach to reasoning capacity. While it achieves higher Pass@1 accuracy, its Pass@k curve for k ≥ 2 lies between that of the base model and the small RFT expert. This suggests it transfers the expert's efficient sampling behavior while largely retaining the base model's diverse exploration capability, striking a valuable trade-off.
7. **Emergence of Sophisticated Reasoning Behaviors:**
 - **Qualitative:** Case studies reveal that PROXYTHINKER effectively combines the explicit "thinking process" tags from the small expert with the large model's deeper, step-by-step reasoning style, often providing more interpretable and visualized solutions.
 - **Quantitative:** Analysis of reasoning patterns on MathVision shows PROXYTHINKER significantly increases backtracking and verification behaviors (e.g., +137.4% for backtracking, +43.9% for verification over the base model), strengths inherited from the RFT expert. Importantly, it also retains the subgoal planning ability that tends to diminish in small RFT models.
8. **Significant Computational Efficiency:** The optimized implementation, built on vLLM with asynchronous tensor-parallel process groups, delivers a 38x speedup over previous naive decoding-time steering methods. This optimized runtime closely matches that of directly running a 32B RFT expert, making PROXYTHINKER a practical and efficient solution in terms of inference latency and compute, with only a modest, relatively decreasing memory overhead.
9. **Domain-Agnostic Transferability:** Experiments on the R1-OneVision-Bench, spanning diverse academic disciplines (Math, Physics, Biology, Chemistry), confirm that PROXYTHINKER's transfer ability is not restricted to specific subjects. It consistently improves performance across all domains, providing strong evidence for the activation of a general visual reasoning prior.

In summary, PROXYTHINKER successfully achieves its objectives, demonstrating a highly effective and computationally efficient method for enhancing the visual reasoning capabilities of large LVLMs by leveraging small, RFT-trained experts at inference time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PROXYTHINKER represents a significant step forward in making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible and scalable for large vision-language models (LVLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Democratizing Advanced Reasoning for Large Models:** The most profound impact of PROXYTHINKER is its ability to bypass the prohibitive computational and resource costs of directly applying Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) to large LVLMs. By enabling large, untuned models to inherit "slow-thinking" reasoning capabilities from smaller, more affordably trained RFT experts at inference time, PROXYTHINKER democratizes access to these sophisticated problem-solving strategies. This means that researchers and practitioners without access to massive compute clusters can still imbue their large foundation models with advanced reasoning, accelerating the development of more intelligent multimodal AI systems.

2. **Enhanced Practical Deployment:** The meticulous optimization of PROXYTHINKER's implementation, achieving a 38x speedup over previous naive decoding-time methods and matching the inference latency of a fully trained RFT expert, underscores its practical viability. This efficiency makes PROXYTHINKER suitable for real-world deployment scenarios where latency and throughput are critical, bridging the gap between cutting-edge research and practical application.

3. **New Insights into RFT Mechanisms:** PROXYTHINKER’s success provides strong empirical evidence supporting the hypothesis that RFT primarily serves to *elicit and amplify existing reasoning behaviors* within a base model, rather than imparting fundamentally new knowledge. By effectively transferring these behaviors via logit manipulation, the work offers valuable insights into how RFT influences model behavior and opens new avenues for understanding and engineering model reasoning.

4. **Catalyst for Future Decoding-Time Algorithms:** This work establishes a promising direction for future research in decoding-time algorithms. It demonstrates that strategic manipulation of model outputs during inference can yield significant functional improvements, particularly for complex behaviors like multi-step reasoning. This could inspire novel techniques for controlling other desirable model attributes or mitigating undesirable ones without the need for expensive retraining.

5. **Accelerating Multimodal AI Development:** By providing an efficient means to enhance visual reasoning, PROXYTHINKER can accelerate the development of more capable and versatile multimodal AI systems. Such systems are crucial for applications requiring deep understanding of visual contexts combined with complex problem-solving, across fields such as scientific discovery, engineering, education, and autonomous systems. The ability to activate domain-agnostic reasoning further broadens its applicability.

6. **Cost-Benefit Trade-off for Model Development:** From an economic and systems perspective, PROXYTHINKER offers an attractive trade-off: a modest, fixed inference-time overhead in exchange for avoiding the exponentially larger upfront training costs of scaling RFT to large models. This makes high reasoning quality achievable with substantially lower upfront compute, making advanced AI research and development more accessible.

In conclusion, PROXYTHINKER is not just an incremental improvement but a paradigm shift in how advanced reasoning capabilities can be instilled in large vision-language models, offering a path to higher intelligence that is both effective and economically feasible.
