# ProxyThinker: Test-Time Guidance through Small Visual Reasoners

> [!summary] Summary
> PROXYTHINKER introduces a training-free inference-time method that guides large untuned vision-language models to exhibit "slow-thinking" visual reasoning, leveraging smaller RFT-trained experts. This approach enables large models to achieve performance competitive with or surpassing full RFT-trained counterparts while significantly reducing computational overhead.

## Problem

- Training "slow-thinking" reasoning capabilities in large vision-language models (LVLMs) using Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) is computationally intensive and resource-demanding.
- The high costs of RFT limit its application to smaller LVLMs, typically under 7 billion parameters, creating a bottleneck for scaling advanced reasoning to more capable base models.
- Existing RFT paradigms require maintaining multiple model copies, leading to high memory usage and extensive training times.

## Method

- Guides a large, untuned base Vision-Language Model (VLM) during inference by dynamically modifying its ==output distribution== using a logit shift derived from a small RFT-trained "Expert" model and an untrained "Amateur" model.
- The logit shift, `α · (z_ψ1 - z_ψ0)`, isolates and transfers the token-level biases introduced by RFT in the small expert to the large base model's logits.
- Optimized for efficiency using the vLLM framework with asynchronous tensor parallelism, achieving significant speedups in inference latency compared to prior multi-model decoding methods.

## Results

- Consistently improved visual reasoning accuracy across MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, MMMU-Pro, and R1-OneVisionBench, with untuned base models often competing with or outperforming full RFT-trained counterparts.
- Achieved a **38x** speedup in inference over naive multi-model decoding, with an optimized runtime that closely matches running a **32B** RFT expert directly, demonstrating practical efficiency.
- Enabled the emergence of sophisticated reasoning behaviors in large models, including a **137.4%** increase in backtracking and a **43.9%** increase in verification behaviors on MathVision, inherited from the RFT expert.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Fine-Tuning primarily serves to elicit and amplify existing reasoning behaviors within a base model's sampling distribution, rather than imparting fundamentally new knowledge.
> - Sophisticated "slow-thinking" reasoning patterns, such as self-verification and self-correction, can be effectively transferred from small, specialized RFT models to larger generalist models at inference time.
> - Strategic, training-free logit manipulation during decoding offers a computationally efficient alternative to expensive full model retraining for imparting complex behaviors to large language models.
