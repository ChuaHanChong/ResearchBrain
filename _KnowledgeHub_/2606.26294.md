# The Red Queen Gödel Machine: Co-Evolving Agents and Their Evaluators

> [!summary] Summary
> The Red Queen Gödel Machine (RQGM) is a recursive self-improvement framework where learned evaluators co-evolve with the agents they score. Its core mechanism, controlled utility evolution, splits search into epochs with a frozen evaluator inside each epoch and evaluator replacement only at boundaries, so per-epoch guarantees survive a changing objective. Co-evolved coders, writers and graders beat fixed-evaluator baselines, often at lower token cost.

## Problem

- Prior self-improving agents such as the Darwin and Huxley Gödel Machines and HyperAgents assume a stationary evaluation criterion, a fixed verifier or benchmark held outside the improvement loop.
- Open-ended targets like paper writing and Olympiad proof writing have no direct benchmark, so existing archive searches cannot supply an improvement signal for them at all.
- Static criteria saturate, invite reward hacking, and inherit judge biases, while updating a benchmark mid-run is costly and forces the search to restart.

## Method

- **Controlled utility evolution** runs search in fixed-evaluator epochs and changes the objective only at boundaries, applying ==selective erasure== of records that depended on the displaced evaluator.
- Each archive node becomes a shared ==multi-agent workspace== of task-agent and evaluator roles under one meta-agent, selected by ==Thompson sampling== over ==clade metaproductivity== with a three-level role/task scheduler.
- Challenger evaluators are promoted only when they raise an ==epsilon-best-belief== score on a held-out ground-truth anchor, with exponentially spaced checkpoints bounding re-evaluation cost linearly in budget.

## Results

- On Polyglot coding, RQGM reaches **71.7%** held-out pass rate against the prior state of the art's **69.9%**, using **1.35x-1.72x** fewer blended search tokens.
- Co-evolved writers lift mean reviewer-panel acceptance from **21.8%** to **38.8%** at matched compute (**1.78x**) and **40.5%** overall (**1.86x**); the specialist prover scores **4.33** versus **4.07**.
- The co-evolved grader tops IMO-GradingBench accuracy at **3x** lower search cost, roughly **9** points above baselines, and the no-erasure control stays pinned at Spearman rho above **0.90**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Freezing an objective within epochs and erasing dependent records at boundaries is a general recipe for keeping convergence guarantees under non-stationary or adversarial utilities.
> - Erasure is load-bearing, not bookkeeping: retaining stale scores leaves the archive ranked by the displaced criterion, so a stronger evaluator cannot re-rank anything.
> - A learned evaluator helps even where ground truth exists, and a co-evolved judge is capped by its anchor, which bounds how far bootstrapped evaluation can drift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a limitation in the field of recursive self-improving agents, which have demonstrated proficiency in tasks such as agentic coding and general domains. Prior work, including systems like the Darwin and Huxley Gödel Machines (DGM and HGM) and HyperAgents, typically relies on a *stationary evaluation criterion*. This implies a fixed verifier, benchmark, or labeled dataset that remains constant as the agent improves.

The authors note that this approach diverges from biological evolution, where species adapt within dynamic, co-evolving environments. Current self-improvement methods face challenges when:
- A target task lacks a direct benchmark (e.g., scientific paper writing or proof generation).
- Evaluation processes are slow or provide limited information.
- Static benchmarks become saturated or susceptible to "reward hacking" as agents advance.

The Red Queen Gödel Machine (RQGM) seeks to extend the capabilities of self-improving agents by integrating the evaluation process into the improvement loop. This aligns with a broader push toward open-ended search and automated scientific discovery, where the objective itself might evolve. The work draws inspiration from classical multi-agent co-evolutionary concepts and the use of large language models (LLMs) as judges, but differentiates itself by allowing the evaluators themselves to improve over time, rather than remaining fixed. By introducing non-stationary utilities, the RQGM aims to overcome the constraints of fixed evaluation, opening avenues for self-improvement in complex, open-ended scenarios that lack predefined, static success metrics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of recursive self-improvement by incorporating principles of co-evolution, similar to those observed in natural biological systems. This involves moving beyond the prevalent assumption of stationary evaluation criteria in self-improving agents.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Introduce Non-Stationary Utilities:** The core motivation is to make evaluation a dynamic component of the self-improvement loop. This allows for search guided by evolving evaluators, adversarial objectives, and dynamic utility functions that are not bound by static benchmarks. The paper aims to formalize this concept within an evolutionary framework.
2. **Develop a Framework for Controlled Utility Evolution:** To achieve non-stationary utilities while maintaining the theoretical underpinnings of existing self-improvement guarantees, the authors aimed to design a mechanism called "controlled utility evolution." This involves organizing the search into distinct "epochs," where the evaluation criterion remains fixed within each epoch, but can be updated at epoch boundaries. This approach seeks to ensure that self-improvement guarantees, typically established for stationary objectives, hold on a per-epoch basis, even as the overall objective evolves across epochs.
3. **Instantiate Co-Evolution through Learned Evaluators:** A concrete objective was to implement this controlled utility evolution by co-evolving learned evaluators. These evaluators are designed to improve alongside the task-performing agents they are meant to guide, thereby dynamically shaping the search utility.
4. **Address Limitations of Stationary Evaluation:** The research is motivated by several practical challenges inherent in fixed evaluation systems:
 - **Lack of Direct Benchmarks:** Many complex, open-ended tasks, such as scientific paper writing or mathematical proof generation, lack objective, ground-truth benchmarks for direct artifact evaluation. Learned evaluators can provide the necessary search signal in such scenarios.
 - **Inefficient or Uninformative Evaluation:** When existing evaluation methods are slow, expensive, or provide weak signals, dynamic evaluators could offer a cheaper or more informative proxy.
 - **Benchmark Saturation and Reward Hacking:** As agents improve, static benchmarks can become saturated or vulnerable to "reward hacking," where agents learn to exploit the evaluation metric without achieving true competence. Evolving evaluators, especially through adversarial objectives, can introduce stricter and more robust criteria, mitigating these issues.
5. **Demonstrate Practical Benefits Across Diverse Domains:** A key objective was to empirically investigate the RQGM across different domains to validate its practical advantages:
 - Show that co-evolved learned evaluation can improve search efficiency even in domains where ground-truth verification exists but is costly (e.g., coding).
 - Demonstrate that learned evaluation can enable and improve agent performance in domains lacking objective evaluation criteria (e.g., paper writing, proof writing).
 - Illustrate how evaluator replacements can impose a curriculum-like effect on task agents, guiding the search toward better solutions over time.
 - Confirm that co-evolution can also lead to improvements in the evaluators themselves, such as enhanced accuracy or correction of biases (e.g., debiasing LLM judges against AI-generated text).

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to facilitate the extension of self-improvement capabilities to more open-ended and complex settings, potentially leading to more capable and autonomous AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Red Queen Gödel Machine (RQGM) is presented as an evolutionary framework that extends recursive self-improvement algorithms like the Huxley-Gödel Machine (HGM) and HyperAgents. The core innovation lies in integrating the evaluation process into the search loop, allowing for co-evolution of agents and their evaluators.

#### 3.1. Foundational Search Algorithm

The RQGM builds upon the tree search formulation of HGM. The system maintains an archive of agent nodes (a tree `T_t`), starting with a seed node. At each step, the search either:
- **Modifies a node:** An existing node `a` is edited, and the modified copy is added as a child of `a`. This process is guided by a meta-agent within each node's workspace.
- **Evaluates a node:** A node `a` is tested on a task, yielding a binary outcome (success/failure). These outcomes are aggregated as `S_a` and `F_a` for each node.

**Search Utility:** Instead of solely relying on a node's individual performance, RQGM adopts Clade Metaproductivity (CMP), which measures the success rate pooled across a node's entire clade (the subtree rooted at `a`). Thompson sampling over these clade-level outcomes is used to select nodes for expansion or evaluation, balancing exploration and exploitation. The final agent is chosen based on its `epsilon`-best-belief score (`BB_epsilon(a)`), a conservative lower bound of its utility estimated from a Beta posterior.

#### 3.2. Multi-Agent Architecture and Search Space

RQGM redefines an "archive node" not as a single agent but as a shared, evolvable *multi-agent workspace*. Each workspace contains `K` agentic roles, which can include both task agents (e.g., a coder, writer, prover) and learned evaluators (e.g., a code reviewer, paper reviewer, proof grader).
- **Meta-Agent:** Each node includes a meta-agent responsible for overseeing the workspace and modifying its code and coordination during the search.
- **Role-Specific Tasks:** Each role `r` has an associated task pool `D_r` on which it is scored.
- **Learned Evaluators:** For "evaluator-dependent" roles (those without a fixed external benchmark), evaluation is performed by a learned evaluator located in a dedicated "evaluator slot." These slots can be replaced during search. An "epoch vector" (`j`) tracks the state of all evaluator slots.
- **Utility Definition:** Utility is calculated through a two-stage uniform averaging: first over a role's tasks (`p_r,j(a)`) and then over all roles (`U_j(a)`). This ensures balanced optimization across all roles.
- **Data Isolation:** To prevent memorization and enhance generalization, the system separates feedback for node creation from evidence for node selection. Training results and ancestral information are visible to the meta-agent for self-modification, but search utility is derived exclusively from evaluations on a held-out validation split. Final performance is reported on a separate held-out test split.

#### 3.3. Three-Level Sampling Hierarchy

A scheduler manages the interleaved expansion and evaluation, guided by a UCB-Air gate. When evaluating, the scheduler samples:
1. A node (`a*`) based on Thompson sampling over CMP.
2. A role (`r*`) within `a*` that is eligible and has been least-evaluated.
3. A task (`d*`) within `r*` that is eligible and has been least-evaluated.
This balanced sampling across roles and tasks ensures that the pooled Beta accumulator's posterior mean converges to the role-task balanced utility (`U_j(a)`) asymptotically (Proposition 1).

#### 3.4. Controlled Utility Evolution Mechanism

This is the core mechanism enabling non-stationary objectives.
- **Epoch-Local Stationarity (Assumption 2):** Within each epoch, all learned evaluators and their associated artifact-generation protocols and scoring rules are frozen. This ensures that each epoch constitutes a fixed-criterion search problem, allowing HGM's theoretical guarantees to apply directly (Proposition 3).
- **Utility Transitions at Epoch Boundaries (Definition F.2):** The utility function can only change at predefined epoch boundaries (checkpoints). When an evaluator slot is updated, a "utility transition" occurs.
- **Ground-Truth Best-Belief Evaluator Replacement:** At an epoch boundary, candidate evaluators (including the incumbent and challengers discovered during the epoch) are compared on a *fixed, evaluator-independent ground-truth anchor dataset*. The candidate evaluator with the highest `epsilon`-best-belief score (`BB_epsilon(e)`) on this anchor is promoted, with ties favoring the incumbent. This ensures that the promoted evaluator is statistically likely to outperform the incumbent on the ground truth (Proposition 4).
- **Selective Erasure:** Upon an evaluator replacement, all utility records that depended on the *displaced* evaluator are erased from the archive. This ensures criterion-consistency under the new epoch's utility (Proposition 2) and prevents mixing evidence from different utility functions. Non-dependent records (e.g., from evaluator-independent roles or the ground-truth anchor) are retained.
- **Amortized Transition Cost:** To manage the computational cost of re-evaluating nodes after erasure, RQGM re-scores old nodes only when they are revisited by the search. Exponentially spaced checkpoints ensure that the cumulative number of records exposed to erasure or re-evaluation grows linearly with the total search budget (`O(B)`) rather than quadratically (`O(B^2)`) (Proposition 6).

#### 3.5. Experimental Design

The RQGM was empirically investigated across three domains:
- **Coding (Polyglot):** Generator (coder) co-evolves with a learned code reviewer. Anchor for coder: executable Polyglot tests. Anchor for reviewer: CRAVE dataset (accepted/rejected pull requests).
- **Paper Writing and Reviewing:** Generator (writer) co-evolves with a learned paper reviewer. Anchor for reviewer: APReS accept/reject decisions.
- **Proof Writing and Grading (Olympiad-level):** Generator (prover) co-evolves with a learned proof grader. Anchor for grader: IMO-GradingBench human grades.

Experiments compared RQGM (generalist and specialist agents) against HGM-H (HGM search with frozen evaluators) and other prior baselines. A principled search intervention involved introducing an adversarial objective at epoch boundaries in paper reviewing to correct LLM self-preference bias, rewarding reviewers for rejecting AI-generated papers. GPT-5.5 (low) was used for main experiments, with a cost-reduction ablation exploring Nemotron 3 Ultra for search-time task-agent calls. Costs were reported in "blended tokens" (input + output, with output weighted at 5x).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical investigation of the Red Queen Gödel Machine (RQGM) yielded several findings across diverse domains, indicating its effectiveness and outlining mechanisms for improvement.

#### 4.1. Enhanced Search Even with Existing Ground Truth (RQ1)

- **Domain:** Polyglot coding tasks.
- **Finding:** The RQGM coder achieved a held-out test pass rate of 71.7%, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art HGM-H's 69.9%.
- **Efficiency:** The RQGM accomplished this while utilizing 1.35× to 1.72× fewer tokens than HGM-H. This efficiency gain is attributed to the integration of an agent-as-a-judge code reviewer, which provides a complementary and computationally cheaper signal (queried once compared to multi-turn evaluations by standard coding agents).
- **Mechanism:** Analysis indicated that 90% of accepted patches in the Polyglot run modified shared task-agent functionality or infrastructure, benefiting both the coder and the reviewer. This suggests that co-evolution allowed for synergistic improvements across roles without splitting effort into competing objectives.

#### 4.2. Improved Agents in Domains Lacking Objective Evaluation (RQ2)

- **Domain: Scientific Paper Writing:**
 - **Finding:** Co-evolved writers using RQGM achieved a 1.78× higher acceptance rate from a diverse panel of reviewers at matched search compute, and a 1.86× higher rate overall (from 21.8% to 40.5% for HGM-H to RQGM specialist, respectively).
 - **Mechanism:** This improvement was linked to the co-evolving reviewer, whose objective was regularized to be more stringent on AI-generated text, providing a robust signal for the writer.
- **Domain: Olympiad-level Proof Writing:**
 - **Finding:** The RQGM specialist prover attained the best panel-mean score and Pass@6 rate (fraction of proofs scoring at least 6 out of 7 points). While performing similarly to HGM-H at matched compute, RQGM demonstrated divergence at longer horizons where HGM-H stagnated. Compared to the IMO25 baseline, the RQGM specialist achieved a higher panel-mean score and Pass@6 rate, though slightly lower on the stricter Pass@7 metric.
 - **Mechanism:** The performance was attributed to a co-evolved grader that outperformed static baselines and HGM-H, guiding the search toward better proof solutions.

#### 4.3. Evaluator Replacements Act as a Curriculum (RQ3)

- **Finding:** Evaluator replacements permanently re-ranked the archive of agents. Spearman's 𝜌 correlation between post- and pre-replacement rankings settled well below 1 and did not recover, indicating substantial reordering. In contrast, a control run without selective erasure maintained a high correlation (𝜌 ≥ 0.90), underscoring the necessity of erasure for guiding search.
- **Mechanism:** Each new evaluator enforced a stricter criterion, pulling previously lower-ranked lineages into contention. However, the most successful lineage (backbone) consistently remained resilient across transitions, suggesting that the curriculum raised the overall population's capabilities around a stable, improving core rather than restarting the search.

#### 4.4. Co-Evolution Improves the Evaluators Themselves (RQ4)

- **Domain: Proof Grading:**
 - **Finding:** The co-evolved RQGM grader emerged as the strongest judge, outperforming static baselines and the HGM-H grader. It achieved this with 3× lower search cost than HGM-H.
 - **Mechanism:** This task is constrained by a reference solution, making it less susceptible to self-preference bias. Co-evolution alone was sufficient to improve the grader's accuracy, which in turn supported the prover's improvements.
- **Domain: Paper Reviewing:**
 - **Problem:** LLM judges are known to exhibit self-preference bias, often over-accepting AI-generated text. The HGM-H reviewer showed this bias, accepting AI-generated papers at up to 1.91× the rate of human papers.
 - **Solution:** The RQGM framework allowed for an adversarial objective to be introduced at an epoch boundary. Papers accepted by a displaced (biased) reviewer were used to form an adversarial pool, and subsequent epochs rewarded new evaluators for rejecting these AI-generated samples.
 - **Finding:** This intervention resulted in an RQGM reviewer that accepted AI-generated and human-written papers at similar, calibrated rates while maintaining 80% ground-truth accuracy on the APReS anchor. This provided the paper writer with a more challenging and less exploitable signal.

#### 4.5. Reduced Search Costs via Hybrid-Model Approaches

- **Finding:** Using a lower-cost model (Nemotron 3 Ultra) for search-time task-agent calls, while retaining GPT-5.5 (low) for the meta-agent, reduced search-token costs by approximately 13.0×. This achieved performance comparable to the GPT-5.5-only run on final evaluation.
- **Mechanism:** Since evaluation calls dominate search costs (65–69%), leveraging a more efficient foundation model for these calls significantly reduced overall computational expenditure without substantial loss in final performance for the paper review domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Red Queen Gödel Machine (RQGM) represents a methodological advancement in the development of self-improving artificial intelligence, particularly by addressing the limitations of stationary evaluation criteria. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging Self-Improvement and Co-Evolutionary Dynamics:** The RQGM framework introduces a mechanism for recursive self-improvement under non-stationary utilities, directly mirroring co-evolutionary principles observed in natural systems. This allows AI agents to adapt to dynamically changing evaluation landscapes, a key feature missing in many existing self-improvement paradigms.
2. **Enabling Self-Improvement in Hard-to-Verify Domains:** A major impact is the ability to extend self-improvement to domains where objective, ground-truth benchmarks are absent or insufficient. By co-evolving learned evaluators alongside task agents (e.g., in scientific paper writing or mathematical proof generation), the RQGM provides a viable path for continuous improvement in tasks that lack clear, static success metrics. This could accelerate automated scientific discovery and creative processes in AI.
3. **Enhanced Efficiency and Robustness of AI Systems:** The empirical results demonstrate that co-evolved systems can match or exceed the performance of fixed-evaluator baselines, often with improved token efficiency. This is attributed to factors such as shared expansion costs across roles, the introduction of more heterogeneous utility signals, and a curriculum-like effect where increasingly stringent evaluators harden the agent population over time. This suggests a pathway to more robust and resource-efficient AI development.
4. **Principled Management of Non-Stationary Objectives:** The concept of "controlled utility evolution," through fixed-evaluator epochs and statistically guided evaluator replacements, provides a theoretical and practical framework for managing dynamic objectives. This enables sophisticated interventions, such as the introduction of adversarial objectives mid-search to correct for biases (e.g., LLM self-preference bias), which would be incompatible with static evaluation criteria.
5. **Towards Autonomous AI Development:** The RQGM points toward a class of self-improving co-evolving systems that could recursively bootstrap their own capabilities without continuous human intervention. This vision suggests a future where AI systems can autonomously refine their objectives and evaluation methods, potentially leading to the discovery of novel solutions and capabilities beyond what can be specified or overseen by human designers.

**Future Research Directions:**
The authors note that the current investigation is preliminary, conducted over relatively short search horizons. Future work intends to explore longer co-evolutionary runs, hypothesizing that these will further compound the curriculum effect and lead to more discerning evaluators and capable agents. The framework also highlights the critical role of ground-truth anchors in guiding evaluator improvement while allowing more complex objectives to be layered on top. This includes exploring richer objectives that enable evaluators to discover decision boundaries not explicitly specified by benchmarks, as exemplified by the adversarial reviewer's role in identifying human-machine distinctions. The limitations of the current framework, such as reliance on anchor quality and the epoch-local nature of theoretical guarantees, are acknowledged as areas for ongoing research.
