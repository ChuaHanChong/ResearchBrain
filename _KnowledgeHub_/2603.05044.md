# WebFactory: Automated Compression of Foundational Language Intelligence into Grounded Web Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> WebFactory introduces an automated, closed-loop reinforcement learning pipeline to distill large language model intelligence into grounded web agent behaviors using high-fidelity offline environments. This approach enables scalable and reproducible training of GUI agents, achieving superior performance on offline and online benchmarks compared to models trained on human data and zero-shot foundation models.

## Problem

- LLMs possess extensive internet-scale knowledge but struggle to reliably translate abstract intent into precise, actionable GUI interactions due to a "semantic-to-action gap."
- Existing agent training paradigms face a dilemma between scalability and control, with human-crafted data being expensive and biased, and live web training introducing non-determinism, safety risks, and noise.
- Inefficient intelligence compression methods often focus on raw data volume rather than effectively leveraging an LLM's latent knowledge into actionable behaviors.

## Method

- Developed a high-fidelity, fully controllable ==offline web environment== that automatically generates realistic websites with LLM assistance, ensuring reproducibility and full observability for data generation.
- Implemented a knowledge-driven dual-track task generation system (operation and information-retrieval) with validators, coupled with a strong executor and filtering pipeline for scalable collection of high-quality interaction trajectories.
- Utilized a reinforcement learning framework with a unified action space and a per-step decomposed reward function (format and accuracy) for policy optimization from the generated trajectories.

## Results

- The knowledge-driven approach significantly improved task executability to **86.3%** and validity to **92.6%**, while trajectory generation success nearly doubled to **84.3%**, with a **38%** reduction in average steps.
- WebFactory-**3B**, trained on synthetic data, outperformed GUI-R1-**3B** (trained on human data) on internal offline operational tasks (**71.8%** TCR vs. **68.2%**) and showed a **162%** improvement over QwenVL2.5-**3B** on offline-to-online transfer (**53.4%** average TCR).
- The agent demonstrated strong generalization, achieving the highest success rate (**84.2%**) on GUI-Act-Web and substantially higher Type accuracy (**66.0%**) on the challenging GUI-Odyssey benchmark compared to other foundation models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can act as architects for their own embodiment, driving the creation of training environments and tasks to efficiently compress their latent intelligence into grounded behaviors.
> - The efficiency of intelligence compression is a critical factor in agent development, potentially more impactful than raw data volume, as demonstrated by agents trained on synthetic data matching or exceeding those trained on human data.
> - Agents trained exclusively in controlled offline environments can achieve robust generalization, effectively transferring to complex, unseen real-world online platforms and diverse public GUI benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent agents capable of interacting with Graphical User Interfaces (GUIs), particularly web interfaces, is a prominent area within artificial intelligence. Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated advanced reasoning and world knowledge derived from vast internet corpora, often referred to as "internet-scale intelligence." However, this intelligence is largely descriptive and lacks the grounding necessary for reliable, actionable interaction within complex and dynamic GUI environments. This discrepancy constitutes a "semantic-to-action gap" that current research endeavors aim to bridge.

Existing paradigms for training GUI agents face a fundamental dilemma between scalability and control. One approach relies on human-crafted data and environments, which are expensive, scarce, prone to bias, and time-consuming to produce. Manual synthesis of high-fidelity environments and annotation of interaction trajectories require significant expert effort. Conversely, training on the live web offers potential for scale but sacrifices control, introducing issues such as non-determinism, safety risks, noise, privacy concerns, and anti-automation defenses (e.g., CAPTCHAs). These limitations impede reproducible research and sustainable agent development.

Previous work in web-agent environments has evolved from simplified DOM-centric tasks (e.g., MiniWoB, MiniWoB++) to more realistic, multi-domain benchmarks like VisualWebArena and WebArena, which offer multimodal grounding and self-hostable environments. WebChoreArena further emphasized long-horizon reasoning and reproducibility. Beyond browser-centric setups, environments like ALFWorld and BabyAI have explored language-grounded control. Frameworks such as BrowserGym aim to standardize APIs, while datasets like Mind2Web provide extensive human-annotated tasks. Research has also extended to OS- and GUI-level control (e.g., Windows Agent Arena, OSWorld, Android-in-the-wild, GUI-Odyssey).

In terms of training paradigms, methods have included supervised sequence modeling (e.g., Decision Transformer), conservative offline Reinforcement Learning (RL), and preference- or feedback-driven optimization. Web-specific innovations involve curricula derived from agent failures, outcome-supervised reward modeling, reusable skill abstractions, and hierarchical formulations for complex workflows. Approaches for reasoning, exploration, and data collection have integrated scaffolds like ReAct, Voyager, Reflexion, and Tree-of-Thoughts to enhance planning and self-correction. Automated task and trajectory generation systems, such as Synatra, Harnessing Webpage UIs, PAE, NNetNav, InSTA, WebShop, AgentTrek, and Explorer, have explored converting indirect knowledge into executable demonstrations or leveraging unsupervised interaction with live websites.

WebFactory positions itself within this landscape by proposing a fully automated, closed-loop RL pipeline that addresses the limitations of both human data and live web training. It argues for leveraging LLMs as architects of their own embodiment, moving beyond their role as mere components for fine-tuning. The work focuses on "intelligence compression" – efficiently distilling LLM knowledge into grounded, actionable behavior within a high-fidelity, controllable offline environment. This approach prioritizes safety, reproducibility, and cost-effectiveness, offering a complementary alternative to live-web pipelines by providing a deterministic and verifiable substrate for agent training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, fully automated closed-loop reinforcement learning pipeline, named WebFactory, for training GUI agents. This pipeline is designed to systematically compress the latent internet intelligence encoded within large language models (LLMs) into efficient, grounded, and actionable agent behaviors.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current GUI agent training paradigms:

1. **Bridging the Semantic-to-Action Gap:** LLMs possess extensive "internet-scale intelligence" – a rich world model and reasoning capabilities derived from vast internet corpora. However, this intelligence is primarily descriptive. Converting abstract intent (from an LLM's knowledge) into tangible environmental changes (a specific GUI action like a click or keystroke) represents a significant "semantic-to-action gap." LLMs "know about" GUI interactions but often lack the precise grounding to perform them reliably in complex and dynamic GUI environments. The research aims to close this gap by transforming passive knowledge into active, grounded intelligence.

2. **Addressing Limitations of Human-Crafted Data:** Traditional approaches that rely on human labor for data annotation and environment synthesis face severe limitations. These include:
 - **Immense Cost and Scarcity:** Generating thousands of high-quality, human-annotated trajectories is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
 - **Inherent Biases:** Human-annotated data can introduce biases, limiting the generality of trained agents.
 - **Manual Environment Synthesis:** Painstaking, manual creation of high-fidelity environments can take weeks of expert effort, creating a bottleneck for scaling.

3. **Mitigating Risks and Non-Determinism of Live Web Training:** Training agents on the live web, while offering scale, introduces substantial challenges that hinder reproducible and safe research:
 - **Chaotic Environment:** The live web is dynamic and unpredictable, making consistent experimentation difficult.
 - **Non-Determinism:** Websites frequently change layouts, content, and functionality, leading to non-reproducible agent behaviors.
 - **Safety Risks:** Agents interacting with live systems can lead to unintended consequences, including data breaches or service disruptions.
 - **Noise and Anti-Automation:** CAPTCHAs, bot detection, and other defenses create formidable barriers, masking true agent capabilities and adding noise to data.
 - **Privacy Concerns:** Interactions with live user data raise significant privacy issues.

4. **Improving Intelligence Compression Efficiency:** The authors argue that the focus on raw data volume in agent training overlooks a more critical factor: the efficiency with which an LLM's latent knowledge can be compressed into actionable agent behavior. The motivation is to shift the paradigm from simply acquiring more data to effectively leveraging existing LLM intelligence through an efficient compression mechanism.

By addressing these motivations, WebFactory seeks to establish a scalable, safe, and cost-effective approach for developing general-purpose interactive agents, leveraging LLMs not just as components but as architects for their own embodiment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WebFactory introduces a fully automated, closed-loop reinforcement learning pipeline designed to compress LLM-encoded internet intelligence into grounded web agent behaviors. The methodology integrates several key components: a high-fidelity offline environment, knowledge-aware task generation, scalable trajectory collection, and a decomposed-reward RL training framework.

#### 3.1. High-Fidelity, Fully Controllable Web Environment

To ensure scalable data generation and automated RL training, the authors developed a fully controllable offline environment. This environment replicates production websites while guaranteeing reproducibility and full observability. Key features of its design include:
- **Cost-Effective Synthesis:** An LLM-assisted synthesis pipeline automatically generates realistic websites, including layouts, workflows, and content, enabling rapid expansion of training domains without manual engineering.
- **Safety and Reproducibility:** Sites boot into pre-authenticated sessions with seeded profiles, bypassing login/MFA. Anti-automation defenses (CAPTCHA, bot detection) are disabled. All content is versioned in static datasets for exact reproducibility, ensuring stable benchmarks and safe experimentation.
- **Full Access and Observability:** Full access to frontend code, databases, and interaction logic facilitates rapid iteration and instrumentation. Ground-truth data and site knowledge are exposed for task synthesis, trajectory correctness enforcement, and automatic reward computation.
- **Curated Site Families:** Ten site families, including e-commerce, information search, and travel planning, were curated. These sites feature diverse UI patterns (forms, drag-and-drop, hover menus) to provide comprehensive coverage of web interaction paradigms.
- **Open-Source and Flexible:** The entire codebase is open-source, allowing researchers to extend the site collection or implement custom tasks. Task difficulty is adjustable across data complexity, UI complexity, and workflow depth.

#### 3.2. Knowledge Preservation and Task Generation

Leveraging the full observability of the offline environment, WebFactory employs a knowledge-driven approach to guarantee task validity and answerability.
- **Knowledge Specification:** For each site, a machine-readable knowledge specification is extracted, detailing the navigation graph, page-level semantics, affordances, and canonical interaction flows. This complete observability prevents the generation of tasks referencing non-existent pages or infeasible actions.
- **Dual-Track Task Generation:**
 - **Operation Tasks:** Evaluate long-horizon interaction competence (e.g., "Add iPhone 17 to cart"). These are synthesized by traversing the navigation graph to ensure executability.
 - **Information-Retrieval Tasks:** Pose queries with guaranteed answers drawn directly from the observable data layer (e.g., "What are Cafe A’s weekend hours?"). Answer availability and required navigation paths are verified prior to generation, providing unambiguous ground-truth answers.
- **Validators:** Generated tasks undergo validation for schema conformance, visibility of referenced elements, path feasibility (via dry-run along the navigation path), and answerability for retrieval tasks.

#### 3.3. Batch Data Generation at Scale

A strong executor, specifically OpenAI's `computer-use-preview`, is utilized within the controlled offline environment to execute the predefined task set and collect trajectory data.
- **High-Quality Trajectories:** The executor, assisted by site-exposed auxiliary knowledge, generates interaction trajectories.
- **Filtering Pipeline:** Collected traces undergo a filtering process to remove low-quality data and ensure reproducibility and correctness. This includes:
 - **State-Replay Checks:** Re-running trajectories with the same seed to verify consistent state hashes.
 - **Key-Node Coverage:** Ensuring that required nodes along the optimal path are visited.
 - **Answer Validation:** Computing normalized F1-scores against canonical answers for retrieval tasks.
- **Behavioral Intent Alignment Feedback:** This mechanism further enhances information retrieval tasks, though specific details are not elaborated upon in the provided abstract/introduction. The resulting high-quality trajectories populate a replay buffer.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning from Generated Trajectories

The RL training framework builds upon the GUI-R1 framework and extends it to accommodate information retrieval tasks.
- **Unified Action Space:** The agent operates within a structured action space where each action `a_t` is a tuple `{a_act_t, a_point_t, a_text_t}`. `a_act_t` defines the action type (e.g., `click`, `type`, `scroll`, `get_final_answer`), `a_point_t` specifies coordinates, and `a_text_t` provides input text or directional parameters.
- **Decomposed Reward Function:** A per-step reward `R_t = αR_f + βR_accuracy` is designed:
 - **Format Reward (`R_f`):** Validates the structural integrity of the agent's output, including proper JSON formatting, valid action types, appropriate parameter types, and conditional requirements (e.g., text for `type` actions).
 - **Accuracy Reward (`R_accuracy`):** Employs hierarchical validation. Action type matching is prioritized, followed by action-specific parameter evaluation:
 - For `click` actions, it checks if the click point is within the ground-truth bounding box.
 - For `type` or `scroll` actions, it uses an F1-score threshold (τ = 0.5) for text comparison, with normalization for case/punctuation invariance.
 - For `get_final_answer`, it uses the maximum F1-score against a set of equivalent answers.
 - For `drag` actions, it checks the Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth drag coordinates.
- **Training Algorithm:** GRPO (Group-Normalized Reinforcement Learning) and related algorithms are used for policy optimization.

#### 3.5. Closed-Loop Pipeline

The entire process, from knowledge materialization and task synthesis to trajectory generation, RL training, and systematic evaluation, is integrated into a fully scriptable, automated pipeline operating with minimal human oversight. This closed-loop design enables continuous improvement and adaptation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted validate the effectiveness of the WebFactory pipeline across various dimensions, demonstrating superior performance, data efficiency, and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of Knowledge and Data-Driven Approach

- **Task Generation Quality:** The integration of knowledge and data-driven methods substantially enhanced task generation. Tasks generated with "Knowledge + Data" achieved an 86.3% executability rate on actual websites, significantly higher than the 31.3% for "No Knowledge/Data." Task validity also increased from 42.3% to 92.6%. The approach leveraged website structure to generate more diverse and complex multi-step interaction tasks, increasing the proportion of complex tasks by 4.4 times compared to the baseline.
- **Trajectory Data Quality:** Knowledge-driven methods notably improved the quality of generated trajectories. The success rate of trajectory generation nearly doubled from 42.6% to 84.3%. Concurrently, the average number of steps required for task execution decreased by 38% (from 15.7 to 9.8), indicating increased efficiency. The proportion of valid data also improved from 58.3% to 89.6%, indicating enhanced reliability of training trajectories.

#### 4.2. Performance on Different Benchmarks

- **Internal Offline Website Benchmark:**
 - General-purpose vision-language models (QwenVL2.5-3B, GPT-4o) exhibited limited capabilities, with Task Completion Rates (TCR) below 30%.
 - Reinforcement learning-based agents demonstrated stronger performance. WebFactory-3B, trained solely on synthetic data, achieved performance comparable to GUI-R1-3B (trained on large-scale human-annotated data). For operational tasks, WebFactory-3B achieved a TCR of 71.8% and accuracy of 87.6%, slightly surpassing GUI-R1-3B (68.2% TCR, 85.3% accuracy). Similar competitive results were observed for information retrieval tasks.
- **Offline-to-Online Transfer:**
 - WebFactory-3B demonstrated robust generalization to real-world online platforms (Amazon, Airbnb, Booking). It achieved an average TCR of 53.4%, which represents a 162% improvement over QwenVL2.5-3B (20.4%) and a 44% gain over GUI-R1-3B (37.0%).
 - The model consistently achieved the highest accuracy across all three online platforms (79.3% on Amazon, 75.6% on Airbnb, and 77.4% on Booking), indicating effective transfer from synthetic offline training to unseen online environments. Statistical analysis showed non-overlapping 95% confidence intervals, confirming the significance of the improvement.
- **Public GUI Agent Benchmarks:**
 - WebFactory-3B exhibited strong generalization across diverse public GUI benchmarks. On GUI-Act-Web, it achieved the highest success rate (SR) of 84.2%, outperforming GPT-4o (41.8%) and QwenVL2.5-3B (55.6%).
 - On OmniAct-Desktop, WebFactory-3B achieved 85.3% Type accuracy and 73.9% SR, closely matching GUI-R1-3B.
 - Notably, on the challenging GUI-Odyssey benchmark, WebFactory-3B reached 66.0% Type accuracy, substantially higher than GUI-R1-3B (54.8%) and the zero-shot foundation models. These results indicate cross-domain transfer capability despite training on synthetic data only.

#### 4.3. Pipeline Performance with Different Foundation Models

- An evaluation of different foundation models (GPT-5, Claude Opus 4.1, Claude Sonnet 4) as architects for the entire data generation process revealed varying "embodiment potential." GPT-5 achieved the strongest overall performance, particularly in Type accuracy, and maintained robust performance across diverse GUI environments. Claude Opus 4.1 performed competitively but slightly lower, while Claude Sonnet 4 showed greater variability in generalization. This finding suggests that a model's inherent reasoning and world knowledge directly influence the potential of the final agent.

#### 4.4. Action Distribution and Transition Dynamics

Analysis of the ground-truth action distribution in the dataset showed a dominance of `click` (47.8%), `wait` (24.1%), and `scroll` (20.9%) actions, collectively comprising over 90% of interactions. Action transition dynamics revealed strong self-loops (e.g., `click` to `click`, `scroll` to `scroll`) and predictable sequential patterns (e.g., `click` frequently followed by `wait` or `scroll`).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WebFactory research presents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and large language models, offering a scalable and cost-effective paradigm for developing general-purpose interactive agents.

**Scalable and Cost-Effective Agent Development:** The most immediate impact of WebFactory is its ability to transform LLM's descriptive intelligence into actionable GUI agent behaviors without the traditional reliance on expensive, scarce, and biased human-annotated data or the inherent risks and non-determinism of live web interactions. This provides a sustainable and economical path for training robust web agents at scale.

**Enhanced Reproducibility and Safety in Research:** By utilizing high-fidelity, fully controllable offline environments, WebFactory eliminates the non-determinism, safety concerns, and noise associated with live web interactions. This creates an ideal setting for systematic and reproducible reinforcement learning experimentation, accelerating research in GUI agents by providing stable, versioned benchmarks and verifiable outcomes.

**Empirical Validation of Intelligence Compression:** The study provides empirical evidence for the "intelligence compression" thesis. The agent, trained on synthetic data from only 10 websites within WebFactory, achieved performance comparable to or surpassing agents trained on equivalent volumes of human-annotated data from much larger sets of environments. This suggests that the efficiency of distilling LLM knowledge is a critical factor, potentially more so than raw data volume alone.

**Refined Scaling Laws for Embodied Agents:** The findings motivate a refinement of scaling laws for embodied agents. The research suggests that an agent's asymptotic performance may not solely be governed by data volume but also by the foundation model's intelligence compression efficiency and its inherent "embodiment potential." This introduces a new axis for evaluating and selecting foundation models beyond conventional metrics.

**Foundation for General-Purpose, Self-Evolving Agents:** WebFactory represents a critical step towards creating agents that can rapidly adapt and self-evolve. The pipeline's programmability and generative infrastructure allow for the systematic identification of agent weaknesses and the on-demand synthesis of dedicated training environments and curricula to address these deficits. This closed-loop, self-correcting mechanism lays a foundation for truly autonomous and robust agent intelligence.

**Broader Applicability:** While validated in GUI settings, the paradigm of transforming latent knowledge into grounded action holds promise for more complex physical embodied environments. This suggests that the principles demonstrated by WebFactory could be extended to a wider range of robotic and interactive AI applications.

**Open-Sourced Toolchain:** The public release of the code and resources, including environments, task generators, the training pipeline, and evaluation tools, fosters community engagement and facilitates scalable and reproducible research across diverse web domains. This open-source contribution is likely to accelerate advancements in the field.

In summary, WebFactory offers a transformative approach to developing web agents, emphasizing efficiency, reproducibility, and safety, while also providing fundamental insights into the relationship between LLM intelligence and grounded agent behavior.
