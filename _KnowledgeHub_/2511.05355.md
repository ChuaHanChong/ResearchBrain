# SAD-Flower: Flow Matching for Safe, Admissible, and Dynamically Consistent Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SAD-Flower framework applies a control-augmented approach to Flow Matching, using Control Barrier Functions and Control Lyapunov Functions, to generate trajectories that formally satisfy safety, admissibility, and dynamic consistency constraints. This method consistently achieves perfect constraint satisfaction across various robotic tasks during test-time inference, without retraining, while maintaining competitive planning performance.

## Problem

- Data-driven generative trajectory planners, while capable of complex behavior, inherently lack formal guarantees for state (safety), action (admissibility), and dynamic consistency.
- Existing constraint-aware methods for generative models often rely on soft guidance, primarily addressing state constraints, and frequently neglect action constraints and dynamic consistency, leading to residual violations.
- Current approaches struggle to provide rigorous theoretical guarantees for simultaneously satisfying all critical constraints, particularly for new or unseen requirements at test time without retraining.

## Method

- Augment the continuous ==Flow Matching== process with a virtual control input, transforming trajectory generation into a controlled dynamical system, formulating state (safety) and action (admissibility) constraints via Control Barrier Functions and dynamic consistency via a Control Lyapunov Function.
- Determine the control input via a Quadratic Program at each step, minimizing deviation from the learned flow while enforcing all constraint conditions as hard constraints with prescribed-time scheduling.
- Utilize a two-phase integration, allowing the generative model to initially evolve trajectories freely before activating control for constraint satisfaction, balancing diversity and guarantees.

## Results

- Demonstrated perfect satisfaction of state and action constraints across navigation (Maze2d), locomotion (Hopper, Walker2d), and manipulation (Kuka Block-Stacking) tasks.
- Achieved competitive task rewards while enforcing constraints, outperforming baselines in overall constraint adherence and preventing dynamically inconsistent "local traps."
- Exhibited robustness to varied integration steps, tighter test-time constraints, and moderate learned dynamics model inaccuracies, successfully scaling to high-dimensional manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A control-augmented Flow Matching framework can formally guarantee simultaneous satisfaction of state, action, and dynamic consistency constraints through CBF/CLF-based interventions during inference.
> - Dynamic consistency, enforced via a Control Lyapunov Function, is critical for generating executable trajectories and preventing local trap issues, distinguishing it from methods focused only on state/action projections.
> - A two-phase control activation, with delayed constraint enforcement, effectively balances preserving the generative model's learned distribution with ensuring strict adherence to constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative models, particularly diffusion models and flow matching (FM), have recently emerged as a significant area in data-driven trajectory planning. These models have demonstrated an ability to learn and generate complex, long-horizon behaviors by directly inferring patterns from large datasets. This approach contrasts with traditional data-driven planners that often combine learned dynamics with separate optimization routines, which can lead to model exploitation—where trajectories perform well under the learned model but fail in real-world scenarios due to approximation errors. Generative planners offer advantages such as capturing multimodal and high-dimensional behaviors, mitigating compounding errors, and supporting task compositionality, making them attractive for various real-world planning and control applications.

Despite these advantages, a critical limitation of existing generative model planners is their inherent lack of formal guarantees regarding constraint satisfaction. Specifically, they often fail to ensure adherence to state constraints (critical for safety, e.g., collision avoidance), action constraints (necessary for admissibility, e.g., respecting torque or speed limits), and dynamic consistency (ensuring that planned states evolve according to the system's true dynamics, making trajectories executable). Satisfying these properties is fundamental for the reliable and safe deployment of autonomous systems, especially in domains like robotics.

Various training-free methods have been proposed to address parts of this problem, including guidance-based sampling techniques (e.g., Classifier Guidance, Decision Diffuser) and constraint-aware diffusion models (e.g., SafeDiffuser). However, these approaches typically focus primarily on state constraints, frequently neglect action constraints and dynamic consistency, and largely rely on soft guidance mechanisms. Such soft guidance often lacks formal theoretical guarantees and can result in residual constraint violations, particularly when encountering constraints that were not present during the model's training phase. This highlights a gap in the current research for methods that can provide rigorous theoretical guarantees for the simultaneous satisfaction of state, action, and dynamic consistency constraints, especially for unseen requirements at test time. The SAD-Flower framework aims to address this identified gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind developing SAD-Flower stems from the critical shortcomings of current data-driven generative planners in meeting fundamental requirements for real-world deployment. While Flow Matching (FM) has shown promise in generating diverse and complex trajectories from data, its inherent lack of formal guarantees for safety, admissibility, and dynamic consistency renders the generated paths potentially unsafe, infeasible, or inexecutable.

The key objectives of this research are therefore:

- **To ensure safety:** Guarantee that generated trajectories do not violate state constraints, such as avoiding collisions or operating within designated safe zones.
- **To ensure admissibility:** Guarantee that generated trajectories respect action constraints, such as staying within torque, velocity, or power limits of actuators.
- **To ensure dynamic consistency:** Guarantee that the sequence of states and actions in a generated trajectory is physically realizable, meaning subsequent states logically follow from preceding states and applied actions according to the system's dynamics.
- **To provide formal (theoretical) guarantees:** Develop a framework grounded in control theory that offers rigorous proof of constraint satisfaction. This moves beyond soft guidance approaches that only encourage adherence.
- **To operate without retraining:** Enable the satisfaction of new or unseen constraints during the inference (test-time) phase, without requiring the generative model to be retrained on new data or specific constraint sets. This enhances the adaptability and practical utility of the planner.
- **To balance generative flexibility with guarantees:** Integrate constraint enforcement in a manner that preserves the underlying data distribution learned by the flow matching model while ensuring hard constraints are met.

By addressing these objectives, the researchers aim to develop a robust and reliable trajectory planning framework suitable for safety-critical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SAD-Flower framework proposes a control-augmented approach to flow matching (FM) for generating constrained trajectories. The core methodology involves transforming the trajectory generation process into a controlled dynamical system, enabling the application of techniques from nonlinear control theory to enforce constraints.

**Flow Matching Foundation:**
The work builds upon the Continuous Flow Matching (CFM) paradigm, where an unknown data distribution of expert trajectories `p(τ)` is represented as the terminal point of a probability path `p_θ_t(τ)`. This path is characterized by a time-dependent vector field `v_θ_t(τ_t)` that acts on samples `τ_0` drawn from a simple prior distribution `p_0(τ)`. The generative process involves numerically solving the ordinary differential equation (ODE) `dτ_t/dt = v_θ_t(τ_t)` to evolve `τ_0` to `τ_1`.

**Control Augmentation:**
SAD-Flower augments this standard FM process by introducing a virtual control input `u_t` into the ODE during trajectory generation: `dτ_t/dt = v_θ_t(τ_t) + u_t`. This augmentation allows the framework to actively steer trajectories towards constraint satisfaction while minimizing deviation from the learned generative distribution.

**Constraint Formulation with Control Theory:**
The three types of constraints (safety, admissibility, and dynamic consistency) are reformulated as conditions from control theory:

- **State and Action Constraints (SC, AC):** These are addressed using **Control Barrier Functions (CBFs)**. For each time step `k` in a trajectory, state CBFs (`h_s_k(τ_t)`) and action CBFs (`h_a_k(τ_t)`) are defined as signed distance functions to the boundaries of the safe and admissible sets, respectively. A positive value of these functions indicates that the state or action is within the permissible set. The control `u_t` is designed to ensure that the time derivatives of these CBFs satisfy `d_h/dt ≥ -φ(t)h`, which guarantees that if the trajectory starts within the safe/admissible set (or is driven into it), it will remain there.
- **Dynamic Consistency (DC):** This is addressed using a **Control Lyapunov Function (CLF)**. A CLF `V(τ_t)` is defined as the sum of squared errors between the predicted next state (using a dynamics model `f`) and the actual next state in the trajectory (`0.5 * Σ ||s(k) - f(s(k-1), a(k-1))||^2`). The control `u_t` is designed to ensure that the time derivative of `V(τ_t)` satisfies `dV/dt ≤ -φ(t)V`. This condition ensures that the dynamic consistency error decays to zero, making the trajectory physically executable.
- **Prescribed-Time Scheduling:** To ensure that constraints are strictly satisfied at the terminal time `t=1` of the flow, a scheduling function `φ(t) = c / (1-t)^2` (with `c > 0`) is introduced. This function makes the CBF and CLF conditions increasingly restrictive as `t` approaches `1`, guaranteeing satisfaction within the finite time horizon of the flow matching process.

**Control Input Design via Quadratic Programming (QP):**
At each step of the numerical integration, the control input `u_t` is determined by solving a constrained Quadratic Program (QP). This QP minimizes the norm of the control input `||u||^2`, thus minimizing the perturbation to the learned flow field, subject to all CBF and CLF derivative conditions being satisfied as hard constraints. This QP formulation allows for efficient computation, making the approach tractable.

**Two-Phase Trajectory Integration:**
The trajectory generation process is divided into two phases:
1. **Uncontrolled Phase (0 ≤ t < T_0):** In this initial phase, `u_t = 0`. The trajectory evolves solely under the learned FM vector field `v_θ_t`. This phase aims to preserve the diversity and characteristics of the learned data distribution, as early interventions could prematurely distort unstructured samples.
2. **Controlled Phase (T_0 ≤ t ≤ 1):** Once `t` reaches `T_0`, the QP-based control `u_t` is activated. This phase ensures that the trajectory converges to satisfy all specified constraints by the final time `t=1`. The activation time `T_0` can be tuned to balance generative flexibility and the need for formal guarantees.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
The framework provides theoretical guarantees based on the established properties of CBFs and CLFs. Theorem 5.1 asserts that if the QP is feasible for all `t` in the controlled phase, the resulting trajectory `τ_1` will satisfy the safety, admissibility, and dynamic consistency constraints at `t=1`. The authors discuss that, in practice, QP feasibility is typically maintained due to the structure of flow matching dynamics and the nature of the CBF/CLF constraints. For cases with unknown true dynamics, a learned forward model is used for the CLF, and robustness mechanisms like robust CBFs or constraint tightening can be incorporated to account for model approximation errors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SAD-Flower was conducted across various tasks, including navigation (Maze2d-Large, Maze2d-Umaze), locomotion (Hopper, Walker2d), and manipulation (Kuka Block-Stacking), using D4RL and other benchmarks. The method was compared against several generative model-based baselines, including Diffuser, Truncation, Classifier Guidance (CG), standard Flow Matching (FM), SafeDiffuser, and Decision Diffuser.

**Consistent Constraint Satisfaction:**
SAD-Flower consistently demonstrated perfect satisfaction of state (safety) and action (admissibility) constraints across all tested environments. Dynamic consistency violations were reported as minor, primarily attributed to numerical integration precision rather than fundamental issues in the control design. This contrasts with all other baselines, which exhibited varying degrees of violations in one or more constraint categories. For instance, SafeDiffuser could enforce state constraints but failed on admissibility, while Decision Diffuser often struggled with dynamic consistency despite addressing admissibility. Reject-sampling methods were found to be ineffective for unseen constraints, as the underlying generative model did not inherently produce valid samples.

**Competitive Planning Performance:**
Despite enforcing strict constraints, SAD-Flower maintained competitive planning performance (measured by task rewards) relative to the baselines. In Maze2d tasks, it achieved high rewards with full constraint satisfaction. In locomotion tasks, while rewards were sometimes slightly lower than unconstrained baselines, this was often due to inherent conflicts between the constraints (e.g., a low torso height constraint conflicting with forward jumping/walking objectives) and the task goals. SAD-Flower still achieved comparable or superior performance to other constraint-aware methods in these scenarios. In Kuka Block-Stacking, it guaranteed safety while achieving rewards comparable to other safety-enforcing baselines.

**Effectiveness Drivers of SAD-Flower:**

- **Dynamic Consistency Prevents Local Traps:** The enforcement of dynamic consistency through the CLF was shown to be crucial. Methods relying solely on state and action constraint projections, such as SafeDiffuser, could lead to "local trap" problems characterized by discontinuities and unexecutable trajectories. SAD-Flower's CLF-based coupling of states and actions prevented such issues, ensuring coherent and smooth trajectory evolution.
- **Delayed Activation Avoids Premature Interventions:** The two-phase integration process, where control is only activated after a prescribed flow time `T_0`, was found to be beneficial. Early intervention on unstructured samples could degrade performance by distorting the learned distribution. Delaying control activation allowed the generative model to produce meaningful structures first, leading to higher rewards with only minor increases in dynamic consistency violations, while still guaranteeing safety and admissibility.
- **Efficiency of QP vs. Non-Convex Optimization:** A comparison with DPCC, an optimization-based generative planning method using full-system non-convex optimization, highlighted the efficiency of SAD-Flower's QP-based approach. DPCC incurred significantly higher computational costs (up to 70 times slower) and yielded lower rewards, suggesting that early, aggressive non-convex optimization can be detrimental. SAD-Flower's lightweight QP formulation and delayed activation demonstrated superior computational tractability and performance preservation.

**Reliability Analysis:**

- **Integration Accuracy:** The method's reliability was assessed by varying the number of ODE discretization steps. While increasing steps improved dynamic consistency (as the controller had more opportunities to intervene), even with fewer steps (e.g., 25), inconsistencies were minor. Safety and admissibility were maintained across the full range of steps, indicating a configurable trade-off between computational complexity and consistency.
- **Stricter Test-Time Constraints:** SAD-Flower demonstrated robustness to increasingly restrictive constraints (e.g., tightening the allowable torso height in Hopper). It maintained perfect safety and admissibility, with rewards decreasing naturally due to the task-constraint conflict, but generally performing at least on par with other methods under such conditions.
- **Scalability:** The framework successfully scaled to a high-dimensional dexterous manipulation task (D4RL Adroit Relocate, 39D state, 30D action), where it was the only method to simultaneously satisfy state and action constraints while maintaining low dynamic consistency violation.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The performance and constraint satisfaction of SAD-Flower remained stable across a range of values for the constraint strength parameter `c`, indicating low sensitivity and practical reliability.
- **Robustness to Imperfect Dynamics:** Even when the learned forward dynamics model was trained on significantly reduced datasets (e.g., 1% of original data), SAD-Flower maintained full constraint satisfaction and stable dynamic consistency. Degradation in safety occurred only with severe model inaccuracies (e.g., 0.1% dataset or high injected noise), demonstrating tolerance to moderate model errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SAD-Flower represents a significant advancement in data-driven trajectory planning by bridging the gap between the generative capabilities of flow matching models and the critical need for formal guarantees in safety-critical applications. Its main contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Safety:** By formally guaranteeing state safety, action admissibility, and dynamic consistency, SAD-Flower substantially increases the reliability of generative planners. This is paramount for deployment in real-world systems, especially in domains like robotics, autonomous vehicles, and industrial automation where safety failures can have severe consequences.
- **Test-Time Adaptability:** The ability to enforce new or unseen constraints at test time without requiring retraining is a key practical advantage. This flexibility allows for rapid adaptation to changing operational environments, mission requirements, or unexpected obstacles, making the planning system more versatile and robust.
- **Principled Constraint Enforcement:** The integration of Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) and Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) with prescribed-time control principles provides a theoretically grounded framework, moving beyond heuristic or soft guidance methods. This leads to higher confidence in the system's behavior under various conditions.
- **Mitigation of Common Planning Issues:** The methodology effectively prevents problems such as "local traps" and the generation of dynamically inconsistent trajectories, which are common pitfalls in other generative or projection-based planning approaches. This results in more robust and executable plans.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The use of Quadratic Programming (QP) for computing the control input, combined with a two-phase activation strategy, ensures computational tractability. This makes the constraint enforcement process feasible within the iterative sampling loop of generative models, providing a balance between computational cost and performance.
- **Scalability to Complex Systems:** The demonstrated effectiveness on high-dimensional robotic manipulation tasks (e.g., Adroit Relocate) indicates the framework's scalability to complex, multi-joint systems, expanding the range of potential applications.

**Potential Impacts and Future Directions:**

The work has the potential to positively impact the development of autonomous systems by enabling a new generation of planners that are both intelligent (learning from data) and trustworthy (providing formal guarantees). This could accelerate the adoption of advanced AI in safety-critical domains currently reliant on more rigid, hand-crafted control strategies.

Future research directions suggested by this work include:
- **Extension to Stochastic Dynamics:** Adapting the framework to handle systems with inherent uncertainties or noise in their dynamics would further broaden its applicability.
- **Complex Constraint Geometries:** Exploring methods to efficiently incorporate and enforce more intricate and non-convex constraint geometries.
- **Online Replanning and Real-Time Control:** While SAD-Flower is designed for planning, further optimizing its inference speed and integrating it with receding-horizon control strategies could enable its use in real-time, online replanning scenarios.
- **Robustness to Model Mismatch and Perception Errors:** Investigating robust dynamics learning techniques (e.g., uncertainty-aware models) and integrating with perception systems to derive constraints from high-dimensional sensor data are important practical considerations for real-world deployment.

The authors acknowledge that while the method improves constraint satisfaction, it still inherits limitations of data-driven approaches, such as potential biases from training data. They recommend rigorous validation, system-level testing, and appropriate human oversight for deployment in safety-critical systems, highlighting the ongoing need for responsible AI development in robotics.
