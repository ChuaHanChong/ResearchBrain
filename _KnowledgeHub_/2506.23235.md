# Generalist Reward Models: Found Inside Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanjing University reveal that a powerful, generalist reward model is latently present within any LLM trained via standard next-token prediction, which they term the 'endogenous reward.' They provide theoretical proof that this endogenous reward can be directly extracted from the LLM's logits and empirically demonstrate that using it for reinforcement learning fine-tuning yields a +5.8% average performance gain on math reasoning benchmarks while outperforming explicitly trained reward models.

## Problem

- Aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human values and instructions is costly and slow, primarily due to the expense and scalability issues of collecting human preference data for Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF).
- Alternative methods like Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) or 'LLM-as-a-judge' are often heuristic, lack robust theoretical foundations, and can inherit biases from the judging LLM.
- Existing LLM alignment techniques heavily rely on external feedback or new preference datasets, indicating a bottleneck in achieving efficient and scalable alignment.

## Method

- The paper proposes that a generalist reward model, termed the ==endogenous reward==, is latently present within any Large Language Model (LLM) trained via standard next-token prediction.
- A theoretical framework is established that proves the LLM's logits are a solution to an ==offline Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) objective==, enabling direct extraction of a principled reward signal without further training or external data.
- The extracted endogenous reward can then be used to fine-tune the LLM through reinforcement learning, aiming to mitigate ==compounding errors== and enhance performance in a more efficient and scalable manner.

## Results

- EndoRM significantly outperformed all other training-free baselines and four top-performing, explicitly trained reward models on RM-Bench and Multifaceted-Bench, demonstrating superior accuracy and robustness in evaluating diverse preference pairs.
- Experiments on the Domain-Specific Preference dataset confirmed that EndoRM acts as a dynamic and promptable judge, adapting its evaluation criteria based on natural language system prompts, achieving highest accuracy (e.g., **76.89%** on academic data for EndoRM-Academy) in corresponding domains.
- Reinforcement learning fine-tuning with the endogenous reward improved the base model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B) by an average of **+5.8%** across five math reasoning benchmarks (e.g., **+10.7%** on Minerva, **+7.1%** on AMC), empirically validating the theoretical gains against compounding errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The logits of an LLM trained via next-token prediction are theoretically equivalent to the optimal Q-function derived from an offline Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) objective, meaning the LLM implicitly learns a reward function.
> - The 'endogenous reward' can be directly recovered from the LLM's logits without additional training, and subsequent reinforcement learning (RL) using this reward leads to a policy with a provably superior linear error bound (O(H*ε_π)) compared to the quadratic error (O(H^2*ε_π)) of the base model from imitation learning.
> - The endogenous reward is dynamic and can be controlled at inference time by providing natural language instructions, enabling a promptable and generalist reward model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a central and costly challenge in the development of Large Language Models (LLMs): **alignment**. Alignment refers to the process of ensuring that LLMs produce outputs that are helpful, harmless, and adhere to human values and instructions. The dominant paradigm for achieving this is Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which involves training a separate reward model (RM) on extensive human preference data, followed by fine-tuning the LLM using this RM.

The research critically positions itself against the backdrop of this established RLHF paradigm and its inherent limitations:
- **Cost and Scalability of RLHF:** The authors highlight that collecting and curating high-quality human preference datasets for reward model training is "notoriously slow, expensive, and difficult to scale." This is a widely acknowledged bottleneck in the industry and academia.

To address these limitations, several alternative approaches have emerged:
- **Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) / LLM-as-a-judge:** These methods aim to bypass human labeling by using a powerful, often proprietary, LLM (a "teacher" model) to generate reward signals or preference labels. While cost-effective, the authors point out their heuristic nature, lack of rigorous theoretical foundation, and susceptibility to inheriting biases or stylistic quirks from the judge model.
- **Generative Reward Models (GenRMs):** A more recent approach that unifies the policy and reward model architectures, framing reward modeling as a next-token prediction task (e.g., predicting "Yes" for a correct answer). The paper positions existing GenRMs as special cases within its own broader theoretical framework, suggesting that their success, previously heuristic, can now be theoretically justified by the proposed "endogenous reward" concept.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and its variants (IPO, KTO):** These methods bypass the explicit reward modeling stage by directly optimizing the language model on preference pairs. While effective, they still rely on the availability of preference datasets (human or AI generated) to drive the optimization process. The paper notes that DPO implicitly learns a reward function, but this learning is still contingent on external preference data.

**The Novelty and Departure of This Work:**
This paper fundamentally distinguishes itself by proposing a radical departure from these existing paradigms. Instead of *external* feedback (human or AI) or *implicit learning* from *new* preference data, the authors argue that a powerful, generalist reward model is **already latently present within any LLM** trained via standard next-token prediction. They term this the "endogenous reward."

This work shifts the focus from *creating* or *externally sourcing* a reward signal to *uncovering* and *eliciting* an intrinsic reward signal that the LLM has already learned during its pre-training or supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase. By providing a rigorous theoretical proof of its equivalence to offline inverse reinforcement learning and its effectiveness in subsequent RL, the paper offers a principled, training-free, and potentially more scalable approach to LLM alignment. It is the first to provide a theoretical proof of the effectiveness of reinforcement learning for LLMs in this context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the significant practical and theoretical challenges associated with aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human values and intentions. The conventional method, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), is bottlenecked by its reliance on massive, costly, and difficult-to-scale human preference datasets. While alternative methods like RLAIF (Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback) or "LLM-as-a-judge" have emerged to mitigate the cost, they often operate on heuristic principles, lacking a robust theoretical foundation and potentially inheriting biases from the judging LLM.

Against this backdrop, the authors pose a fundamental question: **Must a high-quality reward signal always be sourced externally?** Their key objectives are to challenge this assumption and demonstrate a novel, intrinsic source for such a signal:

1. **Discovery of Endogenous Reward:** To discover and conceptualize that a powerful, generalist reward model is not something external to be built, but rather an "endogenous reward" latently present within any LLM trained via standard next-token prediction.
2. **Rigorous Theoretical Foundation:** To provide a strong theoretical basis for this discovery. Specifically, to prove that this endogenous reward is not a mere heuristic, but is theoretically equivalent to a reward function learned through offline Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL). This connection aims to elevate the approach beyond empirical observation to a principled method.
3. **Direct Elicitation without Further Training:** To establish a method to directly elicit this high-quality reward signal from a base (pre-trained or supervised fine-tuned) LLM without requiring any further training of a separate reward model or additional preference data collection.
4. **Provable Improvement through RL:** To theoretically prove that subsequent reinforcement learning using this endogenous reward leads to a policy with a provably superior error bound compared to the base model. This is a critical objective, as it provides a formal guarantee for the effectiveness of RL in improving LLMs, a claim that the authors state is the first of its kind for LLMs.
5. **Empirical Validation and Superiority:** To empirically validate these theoretical claims, demonstrating that the proposed method (EndoRM) not only outperforms existing heuristic LLM-as-a-judge approaches but can also surpass explicitly trained reward models that rely on expensive human-labeled data.
6. **Paving the Way for a New Alignment Paradigm:** To propose that these findings can replace the traditional reward modeling stage, heralding a more efficient, powerful, and scalable paradigm for LLM alignment, and potentially extending to multi-modal models.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to streamline and improve LLM alignment by revealing and leveraging an inherent capability of the models themselves, thereby reducing external dependencies and offering stronger theoretical guarantees.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this paper hinges on establishing a profound theoretical connection between the standard training objective of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the principles of Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL).

#### 3.1. MDP Formulation of LLM Generation

The authors begin by formalizing the language generation task of an LLM as a **Markov Decision Process (MDP)**. In this framework:
- **State Space (S):** The set of all finite-length strings (contexts).
- **Action Space (V):** The vocabulary set (individual tokens).
- **Policy (π):** The LLM itself, which selects the next token given a context.
- **Deterministic Transitions:** Concatenation of the selected token to the current state.
- **Reward (r):** An unknown function indicating the quality of the generated token sequence.
- **Horizon (H):** The maximum length of the generated sequence.

#### 3.2. Next-Token Prediction as Behavior Cloning

Standard LLM training, whether pre-training or supervised fine-tuning (SFT), uses a **next-token prediction** objective. This objective maximizes the log-likelihood of predicting the next token given preceding tokens from a dataset of expert demonstrations (Eq. 1). From an imitation learning (IL) perspective, this is a form of **behavior cloning**, where the LLM learns to mimic the "expert" behavior present in the training data.

#### 3.3. Connection to Offline Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL)

The pivotal step is connecting next-token prediction to IRL. IRL aims to recover an unknown reward function from expert demonstrations, assuming these demonstrations are optimal.
- **Offline Inverse Soft Q-Learning:** The authors adopt an offline IRL formulation based on inverse soft Q-learning (Eq. 6). This objective seeks an optimal Q-function, Q*, that best explains the expert data in a static dataset.
- **Proposition 1: Logits as the IRL Solution:** The paper's central theoretical contribution is proving that the **logits (or raw output scores) of an LLM trained via next-token prediction (`b_f`) are precisely a solution to this principled offline IRL objective.**
 - By re-expressing the IRL objective (Eq. 6) as a maximum likelihood problem (Eq. 8), the authors show that it essentially seeks a Q-function whose corresponding softmax policy (`π_Q`) maximizes the likelihood of the expert demonstrations.
 - Since a language model's training via next-token prediction *already* finds a policy (`b_π`) that maximizes this exact log-likelihood, and this policy is defined as a softmax over its logits (`b_f`), it logically follows that the LLM's logits (`b_f`) themselves constitute the optimal Q-function (`b_Q`) from the IRL perspective. This is a profound insight: the generative LLM already contains an implicitly learned evaluation function.

#### 3.4. Eliciting the Endogenous Reward

Once `b_Q` (the logits) is identified as the optimal Q-function, the **endogenous reward (`b_r`)** is directly recovered using the inverse soft Bellman operator (Eq. 10).
- The derived formula (Eq. 11) reveals that the endogenous reward can be interpreted as a **shaped reward** of the log-probability of generating a token (`log(b_π(a_h|s_h))`), with a potential function based on the LLM's value function (`V_bQ`).
- The total outcome reward for a complete response (Eq. 12) is shown to be proportional to the log-probability of the entire response under the LLM (`log(b_π(τ|s_1))`), plus a term dependent only on the initial prompt. This intuitively means that responses more likely generated by the LLM (i.e., those frequently appearing in its training data) inherently receive higher endogenous rewards.
- The authors also demonstrate how existing Generative Reward Models (GenRMs) can be seen as specific instances of this general formulation, providing a theoretical justification for their empirical success.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Justifications for Endogenous Reward

The paper provides two key theorems to support the use of the endogenous reward:
- **Theorem 1 (Reward Error):** This theorem analyzes the difference in preference distributions induced by the true reward vs. the endogenous reward. It shows that the total variation distance between these distributions is bounded by `O(H * ε_π)`, where `H` is the horizon and `ε_π` is the maximum log-probability difference between the expert policy and the LLM's policy. This implies that if the LLM is a good imitation of the expert data, its endogenous reward will accurately reflect true preferences.
- **Theorem 2 (Policy Error from RL Fine-tuning):** This is arguably the most impactful theoretical result. It compares the sub-optimality of two approaches:
 1. Direct next-token prediction (behavior cloning), yielding `b_π`.
 2. Learning a new policy `π_RL` by applying reinforcement learning using the extracted endogenous reward `b_r`.
 The theorem proves that `b_π` suffers from a **quadratic dependence** on the response length (`O(H^2 * ε_π)`) in its sub-optimality, due to compounding errors inherent in imitation learning. In contrast, `π_RL` (trained with the endogenous reward) achieves a **superior linear dependence** (`O(H * ε_π)`). This directly supports the effectiveness of RL with the endogenous reward in mitigating compounding errors and improving the policy.
- **Ineffectiveness of Iterative Improvement:** The authors also clarify that this self-improvement process cannot be infinitely iterated. Once `π_RL` is derived, it is optimal for `b_r`, and extracting a new reward from `π_RL` would yield the same `b_r`, leading to convergence.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

To empirically validate their theoretical claims, the authors conducted experiments addressing three main research questions:
- **Q1 (RM performance):** Used RM-Bench to evaluate the classification accuracy of EndoRM against training-free baselines (Generative Verifier, GenRM-Pairwise, GenRM-Pointwise) and state-of-the-art explicitly trained RMs. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct was used as the base model for fair comparison.
- **Q2 (Instruction-following):** Utilized Multifaceted-Bench and Domain-Specific Preference (DSP) dataset to assess EndoRM's ability to act as a generalist, promptable reward model by providing domain-specific system prompts.
- **Q3 (Self-improvement via RL):** Employed MATH-lighteval as a training dataset and Qwen2.5-Math-7B as the base model. RL fine-tuning was performed using the endogenous reward, and performance was evaluated on five math reasoning benchmarks (AIME 2024, AMC, Minerva, OlympiadBench, MATH-500).

The methodology comprehensively combines theoretical derivations, including novel proofs, with extensive empirical validation on established benchmarks, providing strong support for their central hypothesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results robustly validate the theoretical claims made by the authors, demonstrating the efficacy and superiority of the endogenous reward model (EndoRM).

#### 4.1. Reward Accuracy on Diverse Preference Pairs (Q1)

- **Superiority over Training-Free Baselines:** On the RM-Bench, EndoRM significantly outperformed all other training-free baselines (Generative Verifier, GenRM-Pairwise, GenRM-Pointwise) that utilized the same Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct base model. This highlights the inherent quality of the reward signal directly extracted from the LLM's logits, without any specific training for reward modeling.
- **Outperformance of Explicitly Trained RMs:** Crucially, EndoRM also achieved a higher average score than four top-performing, explicitly trained reward models (e.g., Skywork/Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B, NVIDIA/Nemotron-340B-Reward) that require expensive human-labeled data curation and dedicated training. This is a powerful finding, suggesting that a "free" reward model can be more effective than heavily resourced alternatives.
- **Scalable Robustness:** On the Multifaceted-Bench, EndoRM consistently outperformed all baselines across five diverse domains (Nectar, OpenHermes, UltraFeedback, Chatbot Arena Conversations, Domain-Specific Preference). This demonstrates that the endogenous reward is robust and performs well even with increasing task complexity and preference diversity.

#### 4.2. Verifying the Instruction-Following Ability (Q2)

- **Dynamic and Promptable Judge:** Experiments on the Domain-Specific Preference (DSP) dataset confirmed that EndoRM acts as a dynamic and promptable judge, inheriting the instruction-following capabilities of the base LLM.
- **Domain-Specific Performance:** By simply providing domain-specific system prompts (e.g., "EndoRM-Academy"), the EndoRM adapted its evaluation criteria. The results (Table 2) showed a clear diagonal pattern: each domain-specific EndoRM achieved its highest accuracy when evaluated on data from its corresponding domain (e.g., EndoRM-Academy scored 76.89% on academic data, significantly higher than its performance on other domains). This validates the claim that the endogenous reward is not static but can be molded by natural language instructions at inference time.

#### 4.3. Self-Improvement via Reinforcement Learning (Q3)

- **Empirical Validation of Theoretical Claims:** The most critical empirical finding validates Theorem 2. Reinforcement learning fine-tuning (RLFT) using the extracted endogenous reward consistently improved the base model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B) across all five math reasoning benchmarks (AIME 2024, AMC, MATH-500, Minerva, OlympiadBench).
- **Significant Performance Gains:** The RLFT model achieved an average performance gain of +5.8% compared to the base model (from 33.0% to 38.8% average). Notably, there were substantial improvements on specific benchmarks, such as +10.7% on Minerva and +7.1% on AMC.
- **Qualitative Improvement:** The example provided in Appendix E vividly illustrates the self-improvement. The base model produced an incorrect, convoluted, and even nonsensical output (including Python code snippets) for a math problem, while the RL-tuned model provided a clear, concise, and correct solution. This qualitatively demonstrates the mitigation of "compounding errors" as predicted by the theory.

In summary, the experimental results provide strong empirical evidence for the existence of a high-quality, generalist, and promptable reward signal within LLMs, and confirm that leveraging this signal for RL fine-tuning leads to provable and measurable improvements in model performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The discovery and theoretical grounding of the "endogenous reward" proposed in this paper carries profound implications for the future of Large Language Model (LLM) alignment, development, and application.

#### 5.1. A Paradigm Shift in Model Alignment

- **Elimination of Dedicated Reward Modeling:** The most immediate and significant impact is the potential to render the explicit reward modeling stage (and the associated costly human preference data collection) obsolete. This collapses the traditional three-stage RLHF pipeline into a more streamlined process.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** By directly eliciting the reward signal from a pre-trained or SFT model, the method drastically reduces the engineering complexity, computational cost, and time-to-deployment for aligned LLMs. It suggests that data typically collected for preference modeling can instead be integrated directly into pre-training or SFT, implicitly strengthening the endogenous reward mechanism. This aligns with the idea of "reinforcement pre-training" but provides a more principled basis.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Personalization and Controllability

- **Dynamic, Promptable Evaluation:** Unlike static external reward models, the endogenous reward is dynamic and controllable at inference time through natural language meta-questions. This allows for unprecedented flexibility, enabling users or enterprises to guide the model's RL training towards their specific values or domain-specific criteria by simply altering the input instruction.
- **Interactive Alignment:** This transforms alignment from a static, post-hoc process into a potentially dynamic, interactive dialogue with the model, paving the way for truly personalized AI behavior.

#### 5.3. Principled Reinforcement Model Distillation

- **Teacher-Student Learning:** The framework offers a novel and more principled approach to knowledge distillation. Instead of merely mimicking a large teacher model's outputs or using its judgments to train an intermediate reward model, this method allows the extraction of the teacher model's token-level endogenous reward.
- **Learning Underlying Principles:** This endogenous reward can then be used to fine-tune a smaller student model via RL, enabling the student to learn and optimize the teacher's underlying principles and judgment, rather than just surface-level behaviors. This represents a more powerful form of knowledge transfer, potentially allowing smaller models to capture the nuanced capabilities of their larger counterparts more effectively.

#### 5.4. Extending Reinforcement Learning Beyond Text

- **Multi-modal AI Alignment:** The RLHF paradigm is heavily reliant on human preference data, which is exponentially more difficult and expensive to collect for multi-modal domains (images, video, audio) compared to text. The endogenous reward mechanism, being applicable to any autoregressive model, transcends this limitation.
- **New Frontiers in Generative AI:** As long as a generative model can process a (potentially multi-modal) prompt and generate a sequence of tokens, it can be prompted to evaluate its own output. This significantly widens the scope for applying powerful RL techniques to align and enhance generative models in domains where RLHF has been impractical, promising scalable paths to improving quality and controllability of multi-modal AI.

#### 5.5. Limitations and Future Work

The authors candidly discuss critical limitations and avenues for future research:
- **Self-Reinforcing Biases:** A significant concern is the potential for the endogenous reward system to amplify undesirable biases present in the base model's training data. If the model's "internal worldview" is flawed, it might inadvertently reward outputs that are factually incorrect or socially harmful.
- **Mitigation Strategies:** Future work should explore hybrid approaches, such as using sparse but high-quality human or rule-based feedback to provide periodic corrections, steering the model away from reinforcing its own flaws.
- **Prompt Engineering for Reward:** Research into "prompt engineering" specific meta-questions will be crucial to robustly and safely elicit the desired reward signals, ensuring the endogenous reward aligns with complex human values.

In conclusion, this research marks a significant conceptual and practical advancement in LLM alignment. By demonstrating that the capacity for self-evaluation and a principled reward signal are inherent properties of generative models, it offers a vision for a more efficient, scalable, and autonomous future for aligning LLMs and potentially other complex AI models, moving closer to the goal of genuinely self-improving AI.
