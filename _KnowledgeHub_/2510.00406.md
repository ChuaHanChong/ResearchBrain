# VLA-RFT: Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Fine-tuning with Verified Rewards in World Simulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Westlake and Zhejiang Universities introduced VLA-RFT, a framework for fine-tuning Vision-Language-Action policies by interacting with a learned world model to generate verified rewards. This method enhanced robustness to environmental perturbations and improved average task success rates by 4.5 percentage points on the LIBERO benchmark with significantly fewer training iterations.

## Problem

- Imitation learning (IL) for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffers from compounding errors and poor robustness to out-of-distribution states.
- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) for robotics is hindered by high real-world interaction costs, the sim-to-real gap, and limitations of offline RL.
- Existing VLA training paradigms lack sample efficiency and robustness when facing environmental perturbations.

## Method

- A data-driven ==world model==, trained on offline datasets, serves as a high-fidelity, interactive simulator to predict future visual observations conditioned on robot actions.
- Pre-trained VLA policies (using ==flow-matching==) are fine-tuned via a ==Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO)== framework, extended with an ==SDE-policy== for improved exploration.
- A ==verified reward== mechanism compares policy-generated trajectories against goal-achieving references within the world model, providing dense, trajectory-level feedback for optimization.

## Results

- VLA-RFT achieved a **4.5 percentage point** increase in average success rate (from **86.6%** to **91.1%**) across LIBERO standard suites with only **400** fine-tuning iterations.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced robustness to various out-of-distribution conditions, showing improvements such as **+6.7 points** for major goal position perturbations and **+6.5 points** for minor combined perturbations.
- The world model accurately approximated environmental dynamics (MSE **0.0039**, PSNR **25.23 dB**, SSIM **0.906**, LPIPS **0.059**) and produced temporally consistent, action-conditioned visual frames.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learned world model can effectively bypass the sim-to-real gap and high interaction costs of traditional RL, serving as a controllable simulator for efficient policy fine-tuning.
> - Comparing full predicted trajectories within the world model yields superior and more stable reinforcement signals than action-level or single-frame comparisons.
> - Integrating stochasticity into a flow-matching policy via an SDE formulation enhances exploration capabilities crucial for reinforcement learning without sacrificing stable action generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The VLA-RFT paper addresses a critical juncture in the evolution of embodied AI, specifically the training of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic control. The broader research landscape for VLAs is characterized by two dominant paradigms: imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL).

**Current Landscape:**
- **Imitation Learning (IL) for VLAs:** Many contemporary VLA models leverage large, pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and are primarily trained through imitation learning on extensive datasets of expert demonstrations. This approach benefits from the powerful perceptual generalization of VLMs, enabling robots to operate in diverse visual environments. However, IL-based policies suffer from inherent limitations, notably "compounding errors" or "distribution shift." Minor deviations from the demonstrated trajectories can lead the policy into unfamiliar states, where it has no expert guidance, resulting in brittle performance and poor robustness, especially in novel or perturbed scenarios.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for VLAs:** RL offers a promising alternative by allowing agents to learn from trial and error, optimizing behaviors beyond expert demonstrations and encouraging exploration. This can enhance generalization and long-horizon task performance. However, traditional RL for robotics faces its own set of significant hurdles:
 - **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Simulation-based RL often requires millions of interactions and struggles with transferring policies learned in simplified simulations to the complexities of the real world.
 - **Cost and Safety:** Direct real-world RL training is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and can pose safety risks.
 - **Offline RL Limitations:** While offline RL avoids real-world interaction, it is still vulnerable to distribution shift and cannot learn from the consequences of its own actions, restricting its ability to discover novel, superior behaviors.

**VLA-RFT's Position:**
VLA-RFT strategically positions itself to bridge these gaps. It attempts to combine the strengths of both IL and RL while mitigating their respective weaknesses. Instead of relying on conventional, handcrafted simulators prone to the sim-to-real gap, VLA-RFT proposes using a **data-driven world model** as a *controllable simulator*. This world model, trained on real robot interaction data, effectively learns the dynamics of the environment. By operating within this learned simulator, the framework can:
1. **Reduce Interaction Costs:** Policies can be fine-tuned via RL without costly and risky real-world interactions.
2. **Mitigate Sim-to-Real:** Since the world model is trained on real data, it inherently captures more realistic dynamics than a purely synthetic simulator, thus lessening the sim-to-real problem.
3. **Overcome Offline RL Limitations:** By interacting with the *learned* environment, the policy can learn from its own actions and explore beyond the initial demonstration data, unlike pure offline RL.
4. **Enhance Robustness:** The RL fine-tuning explicitly optimizes for robust performance, moving beyond the mere imitation of expert trajectories.

In essence, VLA-RFT contributes to the growing body of work that leverages generative models and learned world models to create more efficient and robust learning paradigms for embodied agents. It follows recent trends in using world models for planning and control, but distinctively focuses on reinforcement fine-tuning of pre-trained VLA models using *verified rewards* derived from these learned dynamics, offering a practical pathway to scalable VLA training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of VLA-RFT are directly aimed at overcoming the significant limitations inherent in current VLA training paradigms:

1. **Address Compounding Errors and Poor Robustness in Imitation Learning:** Existing VLA models, heavily reliant on imitation learning (IL), are prone to accumulating errors when encountering states even slightly outside the distribution of expert demonstrations. This leads to brittle performance and a lack of robustness under novel or perturbed environmental conditions. The authors are motivated to find a way to enhance the generalization and robustness of VLA models beyond what pure IL can offer.

2. **Overcome the Challenges of Traditional Reinforcement Learning:** While reinforcement learning (RL) has the potential to mitigate IL's shortcomings by enabling exploration and optimization beyond demonstrated behaviors, its practical application to VLA models is hampered by:
 - **High Real-World Interaction Costs:** Training RL agents directly in the physical world is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and raises safety concerns.
 - **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Relying on handcrafted simulators for RL introduces a significant discrepancy between simulated and real-world physics, making learned policies difficult to transfer.
 - **Limitations of Offline RL:** Offline RL, though cost-effective, cannot learn from novel interactions, making it vulnerable to distribution shifts and unable to discover truly novel, optimal strategies.
 The core motivation is to develop an RL-based fine-tuning approach that avoids these pitfalls.

3. **Improve Sample Efficiency in VLA Training:** Traditional RL often demands millions of interactions, making it highly sample-inefficient. A key objective for VLA-RFT is to drastically reduce the sample requirements for reinforcement fine-tuning, making the process more practical and scalable.

4. **Develop a Controllable and Data-Driven Simulator:** To achieve the above objectives, the authors aim to leverage a "world model" as a novel, data-driven simulator. This simulator should be capable of predicting future visual observations conditioned on action sequences, thereby enabling policy rollouts in a realistic, yet simulated, environment. The motivation here is to avoid the limitations of handcrafted simulators and the costs of real-world interaction.

5. **Design an Efficient and Action-Aligned Learning Signal:** A critical part of the motivation is to create a robust and continuous reward mechanism that is both task-grounded and "verified." By comparing predicted trajectories against goal-achieving references, the system can generate dense, trajectory-level rewards that provide a strong, actionable learning signal, further reducing sample complexity and enhancing training stability.

In summary, the key objective is to establish a **practical and efficient post-training paradigm** for VLA models that significantly enhances their generalization and robustness, especially under distribution shift, by seamlessly integrating the benefits of reinforcement learning through a data-driven world model without incurring the high costs and complexities of real-world or traditional simulation-based RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-RFT proposes a two-stage training paradigm that leverages a learned world model for efficient reinforcement fine-tuning of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. The framework combines supervised pre-training with a novel reinforcement fine-tuning strategy, incorporating verified rewards within a Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework.

**Formal Problem Definition:**
The training process is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), defined by $M := (O, S, A, L)$, where $O$ are observations (real images), $S$ are robot proprioceptive states, $A$ is the action space, and $L$ refers to natural language instructions. The VLA policy $\pi_\theta$ generates a sequence of actions conditioned on an initial observation, language instruction, and robot state. The world model $g_\phi$ acts as an interactive simulator, predicting future visual observations based on actions, enabling the computation of verified rewards.

**Stage I: WM and Policy Pre-training**
This stage aims to provide stable initializations for both the world model and the VLA policy to mitigate instability during subsequent reinforcement learning.

1. **World Model (WM) Training:**
 - **Objective:** To capture environment dynamics and serve as a high-fidelity simulator.
 - **Approach:** An interactive video prediction model, inspired by models like iVideoGPT, is trained. It consists of a pre-trained tokenizer and an autoregressive Transformer backbone.
 - **Optimization:** The WM is optimized via maximum likelihood estimation ($L_{WM}^{MLE}(\phi)$) on offline datasets, predicting future observations conditioned on past observations and actions. This allows it to learn visual appearance and action-conditioned dynamics. The model takes a 7-dimensional action input consistent with the VLA's action output.

2. **VLA Pre-training:**
 - **Objective:** To ensure the VLA policy produces stable action chunks, providing a reliable starting point.
 - **Approach:** A dual-system VLA policy is used, consisting of an upstream VLM encoder (e.g., VLA-Adapter) and a flow-matching action head ($\pi_\theta^{fm}$). Flow matching is chosen for its stable training properties for continuous actions.
 - **Optimization:** The VLM encoder and the flow-matching head are pre-trained on an expert demonstration dataset $D$ using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss ($L_{VLA}^{MSE}(\theta)$). This loss minimizes the difference between the predicted flow and the target flow field derived from noise-perturbed action chunks and standard Gaussian noise.

**Stage II: VLA Optimization through WM Interaction**
This stage focuses on reinforcement fine-tuning the VLA policy using interactions within the pre-trained world model.

1. **SDE-Policy: Policy Parameterization via Flow and Sigma:**
 - **Problem:** Traditional flow matching is a deterministic ODE process, limiting exploration during RL.
 - **Approach:** The deterministic flow-matching formulation is extended into a stochastic differential equation (SDE) process. A "Sigma Net" (mirroring the flow-matching head architecture) is introduced to output a variance vector ($\sigma_k^\psi$) for each integration step, parameterizing the stochasticity of the policy.
 - **Action Generation:** At inference, actions are generated by discretizing the integration into steps. For each step, the flow-matching head predicts the mean ($\mu_k$), and the Sigma Net predicts the variance ($\Sigma_k = (\sigma_k^\psi)^2$), defining a Gaussian distribution from which the next action chunk is sampled.
 - **Log-Likelihood:** The average of step-wise log-likelihoods across these denoising steps is computed as the log-probability of a rollout ($\bar{\ell}_{\theta,\psi}$). This enables calculation of the policy ratio $r = \exp(\bar{\ell}_{\theta,\psi} - \bar{\ell}_{old})$ for GRPO.

2. **Interactive WM Simulation and Verified Reward:**
 - **Role of WM:** The pre-trained world model serves as an interactive simulator. Given an initial frame and a policy-generated action chunk, the WM predicts a visual trajectory (sequence of future frames).
 - **Reward Generation:** A "verified reward" is computed by comparing this *predicted* visual trajectory against the *ground-truth* visual trajectory from a goal-achieving reference (obtained from the offline dataset).
 - **Reward Function:** The reward $R$ is defined as the negative weighted sum of per-frame reconstruction loss ($L_1$) and perceptual similarity loss (LPIPS) between the generated and ground-truth frames. This dense, trajectory-level reward is designed to be task-grounded and reliable.
 - **Variance Reduction:** To reduce variance, $N$ rollouts from the same starting state are sampled, and a group average reward ($\bar{R}_{group}$) is computed as a baseline. The advantage for each rollout is then $Adv_n = R_n - \bar{R}_{group}$.

3. **GRPO Optimization:**
 - **Objective:** To fine-tune the VLA policy using the generated rewards.
 - **Framework:** Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed, offering reliable gradient estimates.
 - **Loss Function:** The final objective ($L_{VLA}^{GRPO}(\theta, \psi)$) combines:
 - A clipped advantage term (similar to PPO) to optimize the policy ratio $r$.
 - An auxiliary flow-matching MSE loss ($L_{VLA}^{MSE}(\theta)$) with a small weight ($\lambda_{mse}$) for training stability.
 - An entropy regularization term ($-\alpha H(\pi_{\theta,\psi})$) to encourage exploration.
 - **Training Loop:** For each task instance, $N$ rollouts are generated by the VLA and simulated by the WM. Verified rewards and advantages are computed, and then the policy $\pi_\theta$ and Sigma Net $\psi$ are updated using the GRPO objective.

**Experimental Setup:**
The framework is evaluated on the LIBERO benchmark. A lightweight variant of VLA-Adapter (Wang et al., 2025) is used as the base policy. The world model is a compact 138M-parameter autoregressive model based on LLaMA, pre-trained on the LIBERO dataset. Training uses a distributed RL framework (VERL) on A800 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate VLA-RFT yielded several significant findings across world model capabilities, VLA performance improvements in standard and perturbed settings, and the crucial role of its core components.

#### 4.1. World Model Capabilities

- **High Fidelity and Temporal Consistency:** The pre-trained world model demonstrated strong capabilities in approximating environmental dynamics and generating future visual observations. Quantitative metrics showed low reconstruction error (MSE 0.0039), high signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR 25.23 dB), good structural similarity (SSIM 0.906), and low perceptual distance (LPIPS 0.059) when comparing generated frames with ground-truth images.
- **Action-Conditioned Dynamics:** Qualitative results confirmed that the world model produces sharp, temporally consistent frames that accurately capture both static backgrounds and changes driven by robot actions. This indicates that the world model effectively learns complex visual appearance and action-conditioned dynamics necessary for a reliable simulator.

#### 4.2. Performance Improvements for VLA

**LIBERO Standard Suites:**
- **Significant Performance Boost with Fewer Iterations:** VLA-RFT achieved a notable improvement in average success rate (SR) across the LIBERO standard suites. With only **400 fine-tuning iterations**, VLA-RFT increased the average SR from 86.6% (Base VLA-Adapter after 150K SFT iterations) to **91.1%**, a gain of **+4.5 percentage points**.
- **Consistent Gains Across Tasks:** Improvements were observed across all four suites: Spatial (+6.0 points to 94.4%), Object (+6.4 points to 94.4%), Goal (+2.6 points to 95.4%), and Long (+3.0 points to 80.2%).
- **Superior Efficiency:** The results highlight VLA-RFT's remarkable efficiency, achieving superior performance with drastically fewer optimization steps (0.4K iterations) compared to the 150K iterations required for the strong supervised fine-tuning (SFT) baseline.

**LIBERO Perturbation Suites (Out-of-Distribution Robustness):**
- **Enhanced Robustness to Distributional Shifts:** VLA-RFT consistently improved the robustness of the VLA policy under various perturbed conditions designed to simulate out-of-distribution scenarios.
 - **Object Position Perturbation:** For minor shifts (±2.5 cm), SR improved from 69.3% to 73.5% (+4.2 points); for major shifts (±5 cm), SR improved from 48.0% to 52.5% (+4.5 points).
 - **Goal Position Perturbation:** Minor shifts saw an increase from 74.5% to 79.0% (+4.5 points); major shifts, which are more challenging, showed a significant gain from 44.8% to 51.5% (+6.7 points).
 - **Robot State Perturbation:** Minor (±20 units) and major (±50 units) perturbations led to improvements of +2.5 points (to 76.5%) and +3.5 points (to 67.0%), respectively.
 - **Combined Perturbations:** In the most challenging combined perturbation settings, VLA-RFT delivered a substantial +6.5 points improvement for minor perturbations (from 63.5% to 70.0%) and +3.0 points for major perturbations (from 34.0% to 37.0%).
- **Broader Action Distribution:** Analysis of action distributions (e.g., X and Z dimensions) revealed that VLA-RFT yields broader coverage across action dimensions compared to the narrowly concentrated actions of the SFT-only policy. This broader exploration likely contributes to its enhanced adaptability and generalization under perturbations.

#### 4.3. Key Factors for VLA-RFT (Reward Design Comparison)

- **Crucial Role of World Model in Verified Rewards:** The study compared three reward designs to isolate the impact of the world model and the "verified reward" mechanism:
 - **Reward Type 1 (Action-level supervision without WM):** Relied solely on the negative L1 distance between policy and dataset actions. This yielded minimal gains (+1.1 points average SR), underscoring the insufficiency of imitation learning signals alone.
 - **Reward Type 2 (WM-generated images vs. real images):** Used the world model to generate images from policy actions but compared them against real dataset images (negative MAE and LPIPS). This achieved moderate improvements (+0.5 points).
 - **Reward Type 3 (Our VLA-RFT - WM-based trajectory comparison):** Rendered trajectories from *both* policy and dataset actions within the *same* world model, comparing them using negative MAE and LPIPS across time. This approach, which maximizes the world model's utility by comparing within a consistent generative space, led to the most significant improvements, achieving **91.1% average SR (+4.5 points over base policy)**.
- **Conclusion on Reward Design:** The results unequivocally demonstrated that the world model, especially when used to generate and compare full trajectories for reward computation (Reward Type 3), is a *key component*. It provides a reliable, high-quality optimization signal that substantially enhances both policy performance and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLA-RFT presents a highly significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation, with several potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Imitation Learning and Reinforcement Learning:** The framework offers a practical solution to combine the strengths of both paradigms. It leverages the perceptual generalization of pre-trained VLA models (from imitation learning) and enhances their robustness and generalization through efficient reinforcement fine-tuning. This hybrid approach allows for robust skill acquisition without discarding the valuable knowledge embedded in large VLM-based policies.

2. **Addressing the Challenges of Real-World RL:** By utilizing a data-driven world model as a controllable simulator, VLA-RFT effectively bypasses the major obstacles of traditional real-world RL:
 - **Reduced Interaction Costs:** It eliminates the need for expensive, time-consuming, and potentially unsafe real-world interactions for policy optimization.
 - **Mitigating Sim-to-Real Gap:** Since the world model is learned from real interaction data, it naturally captures more realistic environment dynamics, reducing the notorious sim-to-real gap that plagues many simulation-based RL approaches.

3. **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** The ability to achieve significant performance improvements with remarkably few fine-tuning steps (0.4K iterations compared to 150K for SFT baselines) is a major breakthrough. This extreme sample efficiency makes VLA-RFT highly scalable and practical for real-world deployment, where data collection and computational resources are often limited.

4. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The demonstrated ability of VLA-RFT to improve performance in standard settings and, more importantly, maintain stable task execution under various perturbed conditions, is crucial. This enhanced robustness to distributional shifts means robots can operate more reliably in unpredictable real-world environments. The broader action distribution observed in VLA-RFT policies suggests a more adaptable and less brittle control strategy.

5. **New Paradigm for Post-Training VLAs:** The paper establishes world-model-based reinforcement fine-tuning as a promising "post-training paradigm" for VLAs. This suggests a future where initial VLA policies are trained efficiently via imitation, and then incrementally improved and made more robust through interaction with a learned environment model, rather than relying solely on ever-larger demonstration datasets.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its advantages, the authors openly acknowledge several limitations that also point to important avenues for future research:
- **Dependence on Expert Data Quality:** The verified reward mechanism, while effective, still relies on the similarity between generated trajectories and *expert demonstrations*. This fundamentally limits the policy's ability to discover strategies that *surpass* expert performance. Future work could explore learned reward models (e.g., VLAC) that provide more intrinsic, task-relevant feedback.
- **World Model Representational Capacity:** The authors highlight that the capacity of the current world model is a bottleneck. Scaling to larger models trained on more diverse and extensive data could significantly improve out-of-distribution generalization.
- **Lack of Explicit WM Planning:** The current framework does not explicitly integrate the world model into planning, which could further enhance long-horizon reasoning and complex task execution.
- **Policy Architecture Focus:** The study concentrates on flow-matching-based policies. Extending the framework to a broader class of policy architectures would be valuable.

**Overall Impact:**
VLA-RFT represents a significant step towards more autonomous and capable robotic systems. By offering an efficient, safe, and robust method for fine-tuning VLA models, it accelerates the path toward practical real-world deployment of generalist robots. The framework's ability to learn from internal simulations generated by a data-driven world model is a powerful concept that is likely to influence future research in embodied AI, machine learning, and robotics.
