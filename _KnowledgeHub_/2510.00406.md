# VLA-RFT: Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Fine-tuning with Verified Rewards in World Simulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Westlake and Zhejiang Universities introduced VLA-RFT, a framework for fine-tuning Vision-Language-Action policies by interacting with a learned world model to generate verified rewards. This method enhanced robustness to environmental perturbations and improved average task success rates by 4.5 percentage points on the LIBERO benchmark with significantly fewer training iterations.

## Problem

- Imitation learning (IL) for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffers from compounding errors and poor robustness to out-of-distribution states.
- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) for robotics is hindered by high real-world interaction costs, the sim-to-real gap, and limitations of offline RL.
- Existing VLA training paradigms lack sample efficiency and robustness when facing environmental perturbations.

## Method

- A data-driven ==world model==, trained on offline datasets, serves as a high-fidelity, interactive simulator to predict future visual observations conditioned on robot actions.
- Pre-trained VLA policies (using ==flow-matching==) are fine-tuned via a ==Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO)== framework, extended with an ==SDE-policy== for improved exploration.
- A ==verified reward== mechanism compares policy-generated trajectories against goal-achieving references within the world model, providing dense, trajectory-level feedback for optimization.

## Results

- VLA-RFT achieved a **4.5 percentage point** increase in average success rate (from **86.6%** to **91.1%**) across LIBERO standard suites with only **400** fine-tuning iterations.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced robustness to various out-of-distribution conditions, showing improvements such as **+6.7 points** for major goal position perturbations and **+6.5 points** for minor combined perturbations.
- The world model accurately approximated environmental dynamics (MSE **0.0039**, PSNR **25.23 dB**, SSIM **0.906**, LPIPS **0.059**) and produced temporally consistent, action-conditioned visual frames.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learned world model can effectively bypass the sim-to-real gap and high interaction costs of traditional RL, serving as a controllable simulator for efficient policy fine-tuning.
> - Comparing full predicted trajectories within the world model yields superior and more stable reinforcement signals than action-level or single-frame comparisons.
> - Integrating stochasticity into a flow-matching policy via an SDE formulation enhances exploration capabilities crucial for reinforcement learning without sacrificing stable action generation.
