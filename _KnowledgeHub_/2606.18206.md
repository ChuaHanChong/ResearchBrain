# Fixed-Point Reasoners: Stable and Adaptive Deep Looped Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Fixed-Point Reasoners (FPRM), developed by researchers at ELLIS Institute Tübingen and ETH Zurich, present a looped transformer architecture designed for stable and adaptive reasoning. The model incorporates a fixed-point convergence mechanism for intrinsic halting and utilizes pre-normalization with residual scaling to manage signal propagation in deep effective layers, achieving competitive or superior performance on various puzzle tasks while adaptively allocating computation based on task complexity.

## Problem

- Existing looped architectures lacked robust, end-to-end adaptive halting mechanisms, often relying on fixed loops or unreliable Adaptive Computation Time (ACT) networks that did not consistently scale compute with input difficulty.
- Deep effective layers in looped transformers faced signal propagation issues, particularly with standard post-normalization, limiting their ability to fully leverage increased computational depth.
- Pre-normalization, while generally preferred for signal propagation in deep non-looped networks, caused unbounded activation magnitudes and instability when applied in recurrent (looped) settings.

## Method

- Introduced a ==fixed-point convergence mechanism== where iterations halt when the relative L

 norm of the hidden state's residual falls below a predefined tolerance, enabling end-to-end adaptive computation without external modules.
- Implemented pre-normalization combined with learnable layer-wise and iteration-wise residual scaling, theoretically guaranteeing activation boundedness and improved signal propagation for stable training at deep effective layers.
- Employed an adaptive damping factor during inference, adjusted using a patience mechanism, to stabilize iterations and ensure reliable convergence to fixed-points, particularly when oscillations might occur.
- Utilized truncated backpropagation through time (BPTT) with deep supervision for training and integrated depth-wise convolutions for enhanced performance on specific tasks.

## Results

- Achieved **94.2%** accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme, outperforming all other 7M parameter models and even larger 27M parameter models (e.g., Attractor Model at **91.4%**) and TRM (**74.7%**).
- Demonstrated adaptive compute scaling, using more effective layers for harder problems, and maintained high accuracy (e.g., **98.1%** on State-Tracking A

 at length 128) across varying task difficulties, contrasting with ACT's unreliability.
- Showed that pre-norm with residual scaling successfully keeps activation norms bounded, enabling trainability at depths where naive pre-norm diverges and improving expressivity compared to post-norm models (e.g., FPRM's performance saturated at almost twice the compute compared to its post-norm variant on Sudoku-Extreme).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The fixed-point convergence criterion provides an intrinsically adaptive halting mechanism that naturally scales compute with task difficulty, outperforming external ACT modules like those used in TRM in terms of both accuracy and efficiency.
> - Combining pre-normalization with learnable residual scaling effectively addresses signal propagation issues in deep recurrent transformers, enabling stable training and superior utilization of deeper effective layers where naive pre-norm or post-norm fail or underperform.
> - FPRM achieves competitive or superior performance on several reasoning benchmarks without requiring complex hierarchical structures, suggesting that improved signal propagation and stability can simplify reasoning model design.
> - The proposed architectural modifications and fixed-point optimization strategies provide theoretical guarantees for activation boundedness and convergence, offering a robust foundation for building adaptive latent reasoning systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges in the development of neural network models capable of compositional and adaptive reasoning. The broader research landscape for reasoning in neural networks often involves two key aspects: flexibility in computation and adaptivity to task difficulty.

One prominent approach, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mechanisms in large language models (LLMs), allows models to scale computation through verbalization and halt based on predicted tokens. However, CoT typically requires specialized training regimes and curated reasoning traces, which can make end-to-end training complex.

An alternative emerging approach is the use of *looped architectures*. These models decouple effective computational depth from parameter count by applying a single neural network block recurrently. This inherent looping structure offers flexibility, allowing computation to scale along the depth dimension at test time. Previous work with looped architectures, such as Hierarchical Reasoning Models (HRM) and Tiny Reasoning Models (TRM), have shown success in puzzle tasks like Sudoku and ARC-AGI by incorporating hierarchical looping mechanisms. However, existing looped models often face two main limitations:
1. **Halting Decision:** Many approaches either fix the number of loops or rely on external Adaptive Computation Time (ACT) networks. ACT networks can introduce optimization challenges due to their discrete objectives and have been observed to not always scale inference compute effectively with input difficulty.
2. **Signal Propagation:** As the number of loops increases, looped architectures effectively become very deep networks, inheriting signal propagation issues common in deep non-looped architectures. Standard transformer designs, particularly those using post-normalization (post-norm), tend to suffer from these issues at greater depths, while pre-normalization (pre-norm) can lead to unbounded activation magnitudes.

This paper positions itself by proposing a novel Fixed-Point Reasoning Model (FPRM) that aims to improve upon existing looped architectures by:
- Introducing a fixed-point convergence mechanism for end-to-end adaptive halting, eliminating the need for external ACT modules.
- Addressing signal propagation issues in deep looped transformers by modifying the architectural design, specifically by employing pre-norm layers combined with residual scaling.
- Demonstrating that these improvements enable a simpler, non-hierarchical looped transformer to achieve competitive or superior performance while exhibiting adaptive compute.

The work connects to Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQ) which also utilize fixed-points, but typically find them via quasi-Newton methods rather than direct fixed-point iterations. It also distinguishes itself from score-based and energy-based models, which rely on energy landscape descent for iterative computation. By focusing on trainable depth and stable fixed-point convergence, this research contributes to the development of more robust, efficient, and interpretable reasoning capabilities in neural networks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to enhance the stability, adaptivity, and computational efficiency of deep looped transformer models for reasoning tasks. The authors identify two main problems that motivate their work:

1. **The Need for Adaptive and End-to-End Halting Mechanisms in Looped Architectures:** Reasoning tasks often require variable amounts of computation depending on their complexity. Looped architectures offer the flexibility to provide variable test-time compute by iterating a single computational block multiple times. However, current methods for deciding *when to halt* this computation are problematic.
 - Many models use a fixed or randomly sampled number of loops, which eliminates adaptivity.
 - Adaptive Computation Time (ACT) networks, used in models like HRM and TRM, involve separate modules trained to predict halting decisions. These modules introduce optimization difficulties due to continuous relaxation of discrete objectives and have been observed to not reliably scale compute with task difficulty. The authors specifically show that ACT can fail to provide true adaptivity, leading to either insufficient or excessive computation.
 - The motivation here is to develop an adaptive halting mechanism that is end-to-end trainable, does not require an external module, and naturally scales computation based on the input's inherent difficulty, leading to more efficient resource allocation. The proposed solution is to halt computation when the model's hidden state converges to a fixed-point.

2. **Addressing Signal Propagation Issues in Deep Looped Transformers:** As looped models perform more iterations, they effectively create a very deep neural network. Deep networks, whether looped or non-looped, are susceptible to signal propagation problems, which can hinder training stability and the effective utilization of deeper layers.
 - Specifically, a common design choice in existing looped models (e.g., Universal Transformers, HRM, TRM) is the use of *post-normalization* (post-norm). While post-norm helps keep activation magnitudes bounded, which is crucial for stability during repeated iterations, it can also lead to signal propagation issues that limit the effective depth the model can leverage.
 - *Pre-normalization* (pre-norm) is generally preferred in deep non-looped transformers for its better signal propagation properties, but it tends to cause activation magnitudes to grow exponentially, leading to instability in recurrent settings.
 - The motivation is to overcome this trade-off: to enable the use of pre-norm in looped architectures to improve signal propagation and depth utilization, while simultaneously ensuring activation boundedness to maintain training stability. This is crucial for enabling models to effectively leverage the "deep effective layers" that harder tasks demand.

By addressing these two motivations, the authors aim to develop a Transformer-based Fixed-Point Reasoning Model (FPRM) that is both stable under deep unrolling, adaptive in its compute allocation, and effective on compositional reasoning benchmarks, without relying on complex hierarchical structures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Fixed-Point Reasoner Model (FPRM) is a Transformer-based looped architecture designed to achieve stable and adaptive reasoning by addressing signal propagation issues and incorporating a fixed-point convergence halting mechanism. The methodology centers on architectural modifications and a specific training/inference strategy.

#### 3.1. Architectural Modifications for Improved Signal Propagation

The core of FPRM's architectural improvements lies in enabling stable training at deeper effective layers by switching from post-normalization to pre-normalization, while simultaneously controlling activation magnitudes.

- **Switching to Pre-Norm:** Traditional post-norm (where normalization is applied after residual addition) in looped models is hypothesized to cause signal propagation issues, limiting effective depth. Pre-norm (where normalization is applied to the input of each sub-layer before the residual addition) typically offers better signal propagation in deep networks. However, in looped settings, naive pre-norm can lead to unbounded activation magnitudes over many iterations, causing training instability.
- **Recovering Boundedness via Residual Scaling:** To leverage the benefits of pre-norm while maintaining stability, FPRM introduces scaling parameters:
 - **Layer-wise residual scaling (α₁ and β₁):** Within a single application of the recurrent function `f_θ(z; x)`, the residual stream (`z_ℓ-1`) and the sub-layer output (`f_ℓ_θ_ℓ(Norm_pre(z_ℓ-1))`) are weighted by tied scalars `α₁` and `β₁` (Equation 2). This controls the contribution of the previous layer's state versus the current sub-layer's output.
 - **Iteration-wise input mixing (α₂ and β₂):** Between consecutive applications of the recurrent function (`z_0_i+1 = f_θ(z_2L_i; x)`), the previous hidden state (`z_2L_i`) and the original input (`x`) are mixed with tied scalars `α₂` and `β₂` (Equation 3). This helps re-inject the original problem information and stabilize the overall recurrence.
 - **Theoretical Guarantee of Boundedness (Theorem 1):** The paper provides a theorem demonstrating that with specific coupling between `α₁`, `β₁`, `α₂`, and `β₂` (e.g., `β₂ = 1 - α₂α_1^(2L)`), the fixed-point iterates remain bounded, preventing activation divergence. This enables pre-norm to be used stably.
 - **Theoretical Guarantee of Convergence (Theorem 2):** Another theorem establishes that if `α₂` is sufficiently small, the looped step becomes a contraction mapping, ensuring convergence to a unique fixed-point at a linear rate. The parameters `α₁` and `α₂` are made learnable, allowing the model to balance contractivity and expressivity.

#### 3.2. Fixed-Point Halting Mechanism

FPRM utilizes the convergence of its hidden state to a fixed-point as an intrinsic halting mechanism, eliminating external ACT networks.
- **Halting Criterion:** The iteration stops when the relative L∞ norm of the residual (`r_i = ||z_i - f_θ(z_i; x)||∞ / (||f_θ(z_i; x)||∞ + ϵ)`) falls below a predefined tolerance `τ` (set to 0.1 in experiments).
- **Mitigating Oscillation through Damping (Theorem 3):** To address cases where iterations might oscillate around a fixed-point rather than converging, a damping factor `η` is introduced. The damped iteration `g_η,θ(z; x) = η f_θ(z; x) + (1 - η) z` can locally stabilize oscillations while preserving the fixed-points of `f_θ(z; x)`.
- **Adaptive Damping (Algorithm 1 - FPOPT):** During inference, `η` is adaptively adjusted using a patience mechanism. If the residual `r` stops improving for `P` consecutive iterations, `η` is decayed geometrically (`η ← γ η`). This allows the model to find fixed-points more reliably and adaptively control the compute-accuracy trade-off.

#### 3.3. Optimization of Fixed-Point Models

FPRM uses truncated backpropagation through time (BPTT) for training, a common approach for recurrent networks.
- **Implicit Gradient Computation:** At a fixed-point `z* = f_θ(z*; x)`, the gradient with respect to parameters `θ` can be expressed using the implicit function theorem as `dz*/dθ = (I - J)^(-1) P`, where `J` is the Jacobian of `f_θ` with respect to `z` and `P` is the Jacobian with respect to `θ`.
- **Truncated Neumann Series:** Since `f_θ` is designed to be contractive, `(I - J)^(-1)` can be approximated by a truncated Neumann series `sum_(j=0)^(k-1) J^j`. This allows for a fixed memory footprint during training by performing `k` backward passes.
- **Exponential Decay of Error (Proposition 1):** The paper provides a proposition showing that the error from this truncation decays exponentially with `k`, bounding the approximation error.

#### 3.4. Overall FPRM Framework (Algorithm 2)

- **Non-Hierarchical Structure:** In contrast to HRM and TRM, FPRM does not incorporate a hierarchical looping mechanism, opting for a classic looped architecture. This simplification is enabled by the improved signal propagation.
- **Deep Supervision:** During training, FPRM employs deep supervision. The model iterates in windows of `k` steps (truncated-BPTT depth). After each window, an intermediate loss is computed, and `k` backward passes are performed. The state `z` is then detached from the graph for the next window, continuing until the fixed-point is reached or maximum iterations are met.
- **Depth-wise Convolutions:** FPRM integrates depth-wise convolutions on the latent representations at the beginning of each loop, which is found to enhance performance on tasks like Sudoku and ARC (2D convolution) or state-tracking (1D convolution).

This methodology combines theoretical grounding for stability and convergence with practical architectural and optimization strategies, aiming for a robust, adaptive, and computationally efficient reasoning model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate FPRM's performance on puzzle tasks, its adaptivity to task difficulty, and the effectiveness of its architectural modifications in addressing signal propagation.

#### 4.1. Puzzle Tasks Performance

- **Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Hard, ARC-AGI-1, ARC-AGI-2 (Table 1):** FPRM, with 7M parameters, demonstrates competitive performance on these benchmarks.
 - **Sudoku-Extreme:** FPRM achieves 94.2% accuracy, outperforming all other 7M parameter models and even larger 27M parameter models like Attractor Model (91.4%) and HRM (55.0%), and 14M URM (77.6%). It also surpasses TRM (74.7%).
 - **Maze-Hard:** FPRM achieves 87.0% accuracy, outperforming the 7M TRM (85.3%) and 7M Attractor Model (46.7%), though it is lower than the 27M Attractor Model (93.1%).
 - **ARC-AGI-1:** FPRM achieves 47.5% pass@2 accuracy, outperforming TRM (44.6%) and HRM (40.3%) at comparable parameter counts.
 - **ARC-AGI-2:** FPRM achieves 6.2% pass@2 accuracy, comparable to the publicly available TRM checkpoint (6.2%) but lower than the reported TRM result (7.8%) and URM (≥16.0%). The authors attribute this to ARC being more sensitive to parameter count.
- **Hierarchy vs. Signal Propagation:** The results suggest that FPRM's architectural improvements might offer similar benefits to the hierarchical structure used in HRM/TRM, as FPRM achieves better performance without hierarchy. Figure 6 shows FPRM maintaining higher accuracy than TRM across varied effective layers, with the gap widening at higher compute budgets.
- **Impact on TRM:** Attempts to introduce FPRM's architectural modifications (pre-norm, residual scaling) to TRM show a detrimental impact on TRM's performance (Table 3), suggesting a need for careful redesign beyond just swapping components.

#### 4.2. Adaptivity to Task Difficulty

- **State-Tracking (A₅ and S₅) (Figure 4):**
 - Plain TRM and TRM with ACT fail to extrapolate effectively on sequence lengths beyond training (length 32).
 - Adding a causal 1D convolution to TRM (TRM+conv) significantly improves length generalization, reaching high accuracy.
 - However, even with convolution, TRM+conv+ACT performs worse on A₅ (65.3%) compared to TRM+conv without ACT (91.4%) and uses substantially more compute on S₅. This indicates ACT's unreliability in scaling compute.
 - FPRM scales compute smoothly with sequence length while maintaining high accuracy, reaching 98.1% on A₅ and 98.8% on S₅ at length 128. This demonstrates FPRM's adaptive nature.
- **Sudoku-Extreme (Figure 5):**
 - FPRM adapts its inference compute (number of effective layers) to the difficulty of Sudoku puzzles (measured by empty cells), using more layers for harder problems. It achieves higher accuracy and greater efficiency compared to TRM with ACT enabled.
 - TRM's default behavior (without ACT) does not adapt compute, exhausting the maximum budget for all difficulties.
- **Compute-Accuracy Trade-off (Figure 8):** The damping parameters (decay rate `γ` and patience `P`) in FPRM's fixed-point optimizer provide a direct mechanism to control the compute-accuracy trade-off.
 - Higher decay rates (`γ` closer to 1) lead to more effective layers and higher accuracy, as the state evolves longer before halting.
 - Patience `P` has a minor impact, especially for larger `γ`. This demonstrates that practitioners can tune these parameters to select a desired operating point on the Pareto front.

#### 4.3. Depth-Induced Signal Propagation Issues

- **Boundedness of Activation Norms and Trainability (Figure 2a, 2b):**
 - Pre-norm models without residual scaling show exponential growth in activation norms at initialization, leading to divergence and inability to train at deep effective layers (Figure 2b).
 - Both post-norm and pre-norm with residual scaling successfully keep activations bounded, enabling trainability at larger depths (Figure 2a). This confirms residual scaling's role in stabilizing pre-norm.
- **Higher Depth Utilization (Figure 2a, 7a, 6):**
 - **State-tracking:** FPRM (pre-norm with residual scaling) demonstrates improved depth utilization, where increased effective layers directly translate to higher expressivity (solving longer sequences). In contrast, post-norm models show limited expressivity gains with increasing depth (Figure 2a).
 - **Sudoku-Extreme:** FPRM's performance saturates at almost twice the compute compared to its post-norm variant when scaling inference-time compute (Figure 7a), indicating better signal propagation and ability to leverage deeper effective layers. FPRM also outperforms TRM across the entire range of effective layer depths, with the gap widening at higher budgets (Figure 6).
- **Training Dynamics of Residual Scales (Table 2, Figure 9):**
 - **Initialization Sensitivity:** A coarse ablation shows that high `α₁` (weighting residual stream) and low `α₂` (initial contractiveness) lead to better initial performance on Sudoku-Extreme (Table 2).
 - **Learned Distributions:** After training, the median `α₁` and `α₂` values remain near their initial points, but the distributions widen. `α₂` values tend to concentrate at smaller values, suggesting the model learns to be more contractive during training (Figure 9).

In summary, the results indicate that FPRM's architectural modifications effectively mitigate signal propagation issues, enabling stable training and superior utilization of deep effective layers. The fixed-point halting mechanism allows FPRM to adapt its computation to task difficulty, leading to more efficient and accurate reasoning compared to models relying on ACT.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on Fixed-Point Reasoners (FPRM) offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the development of deep learning models for reasoning:

- **Demonstrated Adaptive Computation:** FPRM establishes an effective mechanism for neural networks to adapt their computational expenditure to the difficulty of an input. By leveraging fixed-point convergence as an intrinsic halting criterion, it eliminates the need for separate, often hard-to-optimize, adaptive computation time (ACT) modules. This direct coupling of computation and problem complexity can lead to more efficient and resource-aware models, particularly beneficial for complex reasoning tasks where computational demands vary widely. This capability is shown to outperform TRM's ACT in terms of both accuracy and efficiency.

- **Improved Signal Propagation in Looped Architectures:** The architectural modifications, specifically the combination of pre-normalization with residual scaling, address a fundamental challenge in deep looped networks: signal propagation. By enabling the stable use of pre-norm, FPRM allows for better gradient and representation flow through many effective layers. This enhancement in "depth utilization" means models can genuinely leverage increased computational depth for harder problems, rather than being bottlenecked by architectural limitations. This contribution is relevant not only for looped transformers but potentially for other very deep recurrent neural network architectures.

- **Questioning the Role of Hierarchy:** FPRM achieves competitive or superior performance on several reasoning benchmarks without employing the hierarchical looping structure present in models like HRM and TRM. This suggests that the benefits previously attributed to hierarchy in these models might, in part, be a consequence of alleviating signal propagation issues. If this hypothesis holds, it could simplify the design of future reasoning models, potentially leading to more straightforward and easier-to-understand architectures.

- **Theoretical Grounding for Stability and Convergence:** The paper provides theoretical proofs for the boundedness of activations and the convergence of fixed-point iterations under specific conditions of residual scaling and damping. This theoretical foundation strengthens the understanding of why these architectural choices are effective and provides guidance for future design. The adaptive damping mechanism is a practical solution for common issues like oscillation around fixed-points.

- **Foundation for Future Research in Latent Reasoning:** FPRM's success in end-to-end, adaptive latent reasoning on algorithmic tasks (Sudoku, Maze, State-Tracking, ARC-AGI) pushes the boundaries of what can be achieved with compact, specialized models. It opens avenues for investigating whether these compositional reasoning behaviors translate to more complex domains like natural language processing. The identified "open challenge" of achieving logarithmic complexity for state-tracking problems with Turing-complete latent reasoning architectures highlights a clear direction for further algorithmic and architectural innovations.

- **Practical Implications for Model Design:** The findings regarding the compute-accuracy trade-off controlled by damping parameters (decay rate `γ` and patience `P`) offer practical guidance for practitioners. These parameters provide a clear mechanism to tune model behavior at inference time, allowing for explicit control over the balance between computational cost and prediction accuracy based on application requirements.

In summary, FPRM represents a step towards more stable, efficient, and truly adaptive reasoning systems in deep learning, with implications for both the theoretical understanding and practical design of recurrent neural networks.
