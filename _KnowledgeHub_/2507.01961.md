# AC-DiT: Adaptive Coordination Diffusion Transformer for Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University and BAAI researchers developed AC-DiT, an Adaptive Coordination Diffusion Transformer that enables end-to-end mobile manipulation through novel mechanisms for base-manipulator coordination and adaptive multimodal perception. The framework achieves a 55.6% mean success rate on the Maniskill-Hab benchmark in simulation, surpassing existing methods, and shows robust performance on complex real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Existing mobile manipulation methods often fail to explicitly model the dynamic and kinematic relationship between the mobile base and the manipulator, leading to error accumulation.
- Current approaches typically rely on a single visual observation modality throughout a task, ignoring distinct perceptual requirements at different stages (e.g., 2D for localization, 3D for interaction).
- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have largely focused on tabletop manipulation, with limited application to the more complex domain of mobile manipulation involving navigation and precise interaction.

## Method

- AC-DiT proposes an end-to-end Adaptive Coordination Diffusion Transformer framework for unified control of mobile base and manipulator.
- It introduces "Mobility-to-Body Conditioning" which pretrains a lightweight head to extract latent mobility features, explicitly conditioning the main policy to anticipate and account for mobile base movements.
- It implements "Perception-Aware Multimodal Adaptation" that dynamically weights 2D image and 3D point cloud features based on their relevance to the current language instruction, adapting perception to task-specific needs.

## Results

- Achieved a mean success rate of 55.6% on the Maniskill-Hab simulation benchmark, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like RDT (42.9%) for long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks.
- Demonstrated superior performance in real-world experiments on the Agilex Cobot Magic platform, for instance achieving 43.8% success for "Cucumber in Basket" where baselines like ACT had 0.0%.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both Mobility-to-Body Conditioning and Perception-Aware Multimodal Adaptation contribute to performance gains, increasing mean success rates from 44.8% (multimodal) to 47.0% (with MBC) and 49.0% (with MBC and PMA) respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling the mobile base's influence on the manipulator significantly mitigates error accumulation and enhances whole-body coordination in high-degree-of-freedom mobile manipulation systems.
> - Dynamically adapting the reliance on different visual modalities (2D for semantic understanding, 3D for geometric precision) based on task context improves overall performance and robustness across varied task stages.
> - Diffusion Transformer architectures can be effectively extended for complex, end-to-end mobile manipulation by incorporating novel conditioning mechanisms that address inter-robot part coordination and adaptive multimodal perception.
