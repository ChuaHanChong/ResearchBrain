# AC-DiT: Adaptive Coordination Diffusion Transformer for Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University and BAAI researchers developed AC-DiT, an Adaptive Coordination Diffusion Transformer that enables end-to-end mobile manipulation through novel mechanisms for base-manipulator coordination and adaptive multimodal perception. The framework achieves a 55.6% mean success rate on the Maniskill-Hab benchmark in simulation, surpassing existing methods, and shows robust performance on complex real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Existing mobile manipulation methods often fail to explicitly model the dynamic and kinematic relationship between the mobile base and the manipulator, leading to error accumulation.
- Current approaches typically rely on a single visual observation modality throughout a task, ignoring distinct perceptual requirements at different stages (e.g., 2D for localization, 3D for interaction).
- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have largely focused on tabletop manipulation, with limited application to the more complex domain of mobile manipulation involving navigation and precise interaction.

## Method

![[2507.01961-method.png]]
*Overview of AC-DiT: mobile-to-body conditioning (upcoming base motion conditions arm control) plus perception-aware multimodal adaptation.*

- AC-DiT proposes an end-to-end Adaptive Coordination Diffusion Transformer framework for unified control of mobile base and manipulator.
- It introduces "Mobility-to-Body Conditioning" which pretrains a lightweight head to extract latent mobility features, explicitly conditioning the main policy to anticipate and account for mobile base movements.
- It implements "Perception-Aware Multimodal Adaptation" that dynamically weights 2D image and 3D point cloud features based on their relevance to the current language instruction, adapting perception to task-specific needs.

## Results

- Achieved a mean success rate of 55.6% on the Maniskill-Hab simulation benchmark, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like RDT (42.9%) for long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks.
- Demonstrated superior performance in real-world experiments on the Agilex Cobot Magic platform, for instance achieving 43.8% success for "Cucumber in Basket" where baselines like ACT had 0.0%.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both Mobility-to-Body Conditioning and Perception-Aware Multimodal Adaptation contribute to performance gains, increasing mean success rates from 44.8% (multimodal) to 47.0% (with MBC) and 49.0% (with MBC and PMA) respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling the mobile base's influence on the manipulator significantly mitigates error accumulation and enhances whole-body coordination in high-degree-of-freedom mobile manipulation systems.
> - Dynamically adapting the reliance on different visual modalities (2D for semantic understanding, 3D for geometric precision) based on task context improves overall performance and robustness across varied task stages.
> - Diffusion Transformer architectures can be effectively extended for complex, end-to-end mobile manipulation by incorporating novel conditioning mechanisms that address inter-robot part coordination and adaptive multimodal perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

AC-DiT operates at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields within robotics and artificial intelligence: mobile manipulation, language-conditioned robotic control, imitation learning, and diffusion-based generative models (specifically Diffusion Transformers, DiTs).

Historically, mobile manipulation has been approached in two main ways:
- **Two-stage approaches:** These decompose long-horizon tasks into sub-tasks using high-level planners (e.g., Vision-Language Models or Large Language Models) which are then executed sequentially by atomic skill modules (e.g., through imitation learning or reinforcement learning). While modular, these methods can suffer from compounding errors between stages and may lack seamless coordination.
- **End-to-end methods:** These frameworks aim to jointly optimize the control of both the mobile base and the manipulator within a unified action space, often using imitation learning or reinforcement learning. This allows for holistic optimization and better handling of concurrent or rapidly alternating locomotion and manipulation.

Existing end-to-end approaches, however, face two critical limitations that AC-DiT directly addresses:
1. **Lack of explicit coordination modeling:** Current methods often fail to explicitly account for the intricate kinematic and dynamic interdependencies between the mobile base and the manipulator. Small errors in base movement can significantly impact manipulator performance, leading to error accumulation in high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) systems.
2. **Suboptimal multimodal perception:** Most existing methods utilize a single visual observation modality (either 2D images or 3D point clouds) throughout the entire mobile manipulation process. This overlooks the distinct perceptual requirements at different task stages (e.g., 2D for semantic understanding during localization, 3D for geometric precision during interaction).

Furthermore, the broader landscape of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**, which enable large-scale models to interpret multimodal inputs and generate robot actions, has seen significant progress. While pioneering works like OpenVLA, RT-2, and ManipLLM have advanced general robot control, they have primarily focused on tabletop manipulation tasks. Extending these powerful VLA paradigms to the more complex domain of mobile manipulation, which involves navigating dynamic environments while simultaneously performing precise manipulation, remains a significant challenge.

AC-DiT contributes to this landscape by:
- **Advancing end-to-end mobile manipulation:** It proposes a novel end-to-end framework built upon the Diffusion Transformer architecture, specifically designed for mobile manipulation, thereby moving beyond the limitations of two-stage or less coordinated end-to-end systems.
- **Introducing explicit coordination mechanisms:** The "Mobility-to-Body Conditioning" mechanism is a direct response to the coordination challenge, allowing the manipulator's actions to be informed by the mobile base's anticipated movements, thus reducing error accumulation.
- **Pioneering adaptive multimodal perception:** The "Perception-Aware Multimodal Adaptation" mechanism is a significant step towards addressing the varied perceptual needs of different mobile manipulation stages, dynamically adjusting reliance on 2D and 3D sensory inputs.
- **Extending VLA capabilities:** By adopting a diffusion-based action generation paradigm and explicitly addressing the unique challenges of mobile manipulation, AC-DiT expands the applicability and robustness of VLA models beyond typical tabletop scenarios.

In essence, AC-DiT aims to bridge critical gaps in the field, offering a more robust, adaptable, and coordinated solution for complex mobile manipulation tasks by leveraging state-of-the-art generative models and novel conditioning strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind AC-DiT stems directly from the identified shortcomings of existing mobile manipulation approaches. The authors pinpoint two critical limitations:
1. **Implicit Coordination:** Current methods often fail to explicitly model the dynamic and kinematic relationship between the mobile base and the manipulator. This oversight leads to a critical problem: even minor inaccuracies in the mobile base's motion can propagate and accumulate, severely impacting the manipulator's ability to perform precise actions, especially in high-degree-of-freedom systems. The challenge is to achieve seamless "whole-body control" where the base's movement optimally supports the manipulator's task.
2. **Fixed Perception Modality:** Existing approaches typically rely on a single visual observation modality (either 2D images or 3D point clouds) throughout the entire mobile manipulation process. This ignores the fact that different stages of a task have distinct perceptual requirements. For instance, semantically rich 2D images are crucial for object localization and scene understanding, while geometrically precise 3D point clouds are indispensable for fine-grained interaction and grasping. Treating all stages with the same modality leads to suboptimal performance.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the AC-DiT framework are:

1. **To develop an end-to-end mobile manipulation framework:** The goal is to create a unified system that can process observations and language commands to directly predict coordinated actions for both the mobile base and the manipulator, moving away from complex, multi-stage pipelines.
2. **To enhance mobile base and manipulator coordination:** This involves explicitly modeling the influence of the mobile base on manipulator control. The objective is to guide the policy to anticipate and account for base motion, thereby reducing error accumulation and improving overall whole-body control.
3. **To adapt to distinct multimodal perception requirements:** The aim is to dynamically adjust the robot's reliance on different visual modalities (2D images and 3D point clouds) based on the specific perceptual needs of the current stage within a mobile manipulation task. This includes adapting to semantic requirements for navigation and precise geometric requirements for interaction.
4. **To achieve superior empirical performance:** The final objective is to demonstrate that the proposed AC-DiT, with its novel coordination and perception mechanisms, outperforms existing state-of-the-art baselines in both simulated and real-world mobile manipulation tasks, validating its effectiveness and robustness.

In essence, the authors aim to build a more intelligent and adaptable mobile manipulation system that understands the interplay between locomotion and manipulation and leverages the strengths of different sensory inputs dynamically throughout complex tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AC-DiT (Adaptive Coordination Diffusion Transformer) is designed as an end-to-end diffusion transformer model for mobile manipulation, integrating two core mechanisms to address the identified challenges: Mobility-to-Body Conditioning and Perception-Aware Multimodal Adaptation.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The framework builds upon a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, which is known for its ability to capture complex data distributions and generate continuous actions. AC-DiT comprises:
- **Encoders:**
 - **Image Encoder:** Uses SigLIP as the backbone to extract 2D visual features from three camera views (exterior, left-wrist, right-wrist).
 - **3D Encoder:** Leverages Lift3D, which transforms raw point clouds into token sequences compatible with 2D foundation models (SigLIP with LoRA adapter), allowing the SigLIP backbone to extract 3D geometric features.
 - **Text Encoder:** Also uses SigLIP to extract language features from natural language instructions.
 All features are projected into a shared latent space.
- **Action Heads:**
 - **Lightweight Mobility Action Head ($H_l$):** A smaller DiT-based head (170 million parameters) primarily responsible for extracting latent mobility features, capturing base motion representation.
 - **Mobile Manipulation Action Head ($H$):** The main DiT-based policy head that takes reweighted multimodal features and latent mobility features as input to predict full mobile base and manipulator actions.

#### 3.2. Mobility-to-Body Conditioning

This mechanism aims to explicitly model the influence of mobile base motion on manipulator control, thereby enhancing whole-body coordination and reducing error accumulation in high-DoF systems.
- **Pretraining Phase:** Before training the full AC-DiT model, the modality encoders and the lightweight mobility action head ($H_l$) are pretrained. During this phase, $H_l$ is supervised *only* by mobile base actions, allowing it to learn effective representations of base movement. The LoRA adapter on the 3D encoder is fine-tuned, while other encoder parts are frozen.
- **Latent Mobility Feature Extraction:** After pretraining, $H_l$ is used to extract "latent mobility features" ($F_m$). These features are formed by concatenating action tokens from the final DiT block of $H_l$ at each denoising time step, effectively encoding the mobile base's anticipated actions.
- **Full Model Training:** When training the full AC-DiT model, the mobile manipulation action head ($H$) predicts actions for both the mobile base and the manipulator. Crucially, these predictions are conditioned not only on multimodal visual and language features but also on the extracted latent mobility features ($F_m$). By integrating $F_m$ into the cross-attention layers of $H$, the manipulator's planning is explicitly informed by the expected movement of the mobile base, leading to more coordinated and robust whole-body control.

#### 3.3. Perception-Aware Multimodal Adaptation

This mechanism addresses the varied perceptual needs across different stages of mobile manipulation by dynamically weighting the importance of different visual modalities.
- **Feature Projection:** 2D visual features (from three camera views), 3D geometric features, and language features are first projected into a shared latent space using lightweight MLP projectors.
- **Dynamic Weight Calculation:** The core idea is to measure the relevance of each visual modality to the current language instruction. This is achieved by computing the cosine similarity between each projected visual feature (e.g., front-view image feature, point cloud feature) and the projected language feature. These similarity scores are then normalized to obtain importance weights (denoted as $w$).
- **Perception-Aware Feature Generation:** These importance weights are applied to the original visual tokens, producing "perception-aware visual features" ($F_v$).
- **Action Prediction:** These reweighted $F_v$, along with the language feature ($F_l$) and the latent mobility feature ($F_m$), form the conditioning input for the mobile manipulation action head ($H$). This allows the model to adaptively emphasize specific modalities based on the task context—e.g., relying more on 2D inputs for semantic object identification or 3D inputs for precise geometric interaction, and downweighting uninformative views.

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

Both the pretraining of $H_l$ and the full model training utilize a denoising Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, a standard objective for Diffusion Models, to guide the prediction of action sequences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AC-DiT demonstrates superior performance in both simulated and real-world mobile manipulation tasks, validating the effectiveness of its proposed adaptive coordination and multimodal perception mechanisms.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Mobile Manipulation (Maniskill-Hab Benchmark):**
 - AC-DiT consistently achieves the highest success rates across challenging long-horizon tasks, with a mean success rate of **55.6%**. This significantly outperforms existing baselines such as ACT (23.6%), DP (28.8%), 3DP (21.6%), RDT (42.9%), and $\pi^0$ (33.5%).
 - Specific strong performances include "go to fridge, then open the door" (90.7%) and "go to counter, then close the drawer" (97.3%), highlighting its capability for precise and coordinated mobile manipulation, even when mobile base and manipulator motions are concurrent.
 - The paper notes that 3DP struggles with large-scale scene-level point clouds for navigation, while RDT's lack of explicit coordination modeling leads to error accumulation affecting manipulator performance. AC-DiT's mechanisms directly address these weaknesses.

- **Bimanual Manipulation (RoboTwin Benchmark):**
 - The proposed mechanisms (especially an adapted "dual-arm-to-dual-arm conditioning" and perception-aware multimodal adaptation) are shown to be effectively extended to bimanual tasks.
 - AC-DiT achieves an impressive **90.1%** mean success rate, vastly outperforming DP (37.9%), and significantly surpassing 3DP (73.3%) and RDT (70.0%).
 - Tasks like "Handover" (100% success rate for AC-DiT) particularly underscore the importance of precise cross-arm coordination, which AC-DiT's adapted conditioning mechanism facilitates by providing short-term motion priors to each arm.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- Conducted on the Agilex Cobot Magic platform, involving four 6-DoF arms and a mobile base, equipped with multiple 2D and 3D cameras.
- Evaluated on four complex, long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks: "Cucumber in basket," "Store breads," "Hang towel," and "Clean table," each involving multiple sub-tasks.
- **Quantitative Results:** AC-DiT again demonstrates superior performance compared to real-world baselines like ACT and $\pi^0$. For example, in "Cucumber in Basket," AC-DiT achieved an overall task success rate of 43.8% compared to ACT's 0.0% and $\pi^0$'s 12.5%. Similar improvements are observed across sub-tasks and other complex scenarios (e.g., "Store Breads": AC-DiT 75.0% vs. $\pi^0$ 53.0%).
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations illustrate AC-DiT's ability to execute multi-step tasks, including intricate bimanual coordination (e.g., "Hang Towel" where dual arms hand over the towel while the mobile base navigates). The "Perception-Aware Multimodal Adaptation" mechanism's effectiveness is visualized, showing dynamic changes in importance weights for different visual inputs. For example, during grasping, the right-wrist camera and point cloud gain importance, while for navigation, the exterior camera becomes more crucial. This confirms AC-DiT's adaptive reliance on modalities based on the task stage.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Impact of Multimodality:** Using both 2D and 3D visual inputs (44.8% mean success rate) significantly outperforms using only 2D (37.5%), highlighting the benefit of multimodal fusion.
- **Impact of Mobility-to-Body Conditioning (MBC):** Adding MBC to multimodal inputs further boosts performance to 47.0%, confirming that explicitly modeling the mobile base's influence on the manipulator significantly improves coordination.
- **Impact of Perception-Aware Multimodal Adaptation (PMA):** The full AC-DiT model, incorporating both MBC and PMA, achieves the highest mean success rate of 49.0%. This verifies that dynamically weighting multimodal features based on task stage enhances the system's ability to handle complex, long-horizon tasks.
- **Condition Injection Method:** Concatenating all conditional features in each cross-attention injection step slightly outperforms alternating condition injection (44.8% vs. 43.7%).
- **Conditioning Relationship:** Using "Mobility-to-Body" conditioning (49.5%) is more effective than conditioning on "Up-to-Body" (manipulator joint position, 47.3%), reinforcing the argument that anticipating base motion is crucial for mitigating error accumulation in whole-body control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AC-DiT framework represents a significant step forward in the field of mobile manipulation, addressing long-standing challenges that have limited the practical deployment of robots in complex, unstructured environments like households.

#### 5.1. Significance of the Work

- **Enhanced Coordination:** The introduction of the "Mobility-to-Body Conditioning" mechanism directly tackles the critical issue of coordination between the mobile base and the manipulator. By explicitly modeling and leveraging the influence of base motion as a contextual prior for manipulator control, AC-DiT significantly reduces error accumulation in high-degree-of-freedom systems. This is crucial for precise, robust, and reliable task execution, especially in dynamic environments where slight errors can compound rapidly.
- **Adaptive Multimodal Perception:** The "Perception-Aware Multimodal Adaptation" mechanism is a novel contribution that allows the robot to intelligently process and utilize diverse sensory inputs (2D images for semantic information, 3D point clouds for geometric precision) based on the specific requirements of different task stages. This dynamic weighting of modalities makes the robot's perception system more robust, efficient, and tailored to the varying demands of complex, multi-stage tasks.
- **End-to-End Robustness:** By providing an end-to-end framework based on Diffusion Transformers, AC-DiT demonstrates a unified approach to mobile manipulation that avoids the pitfalls of multi-stage pipelines (e.g., error propagation between planning and execution modules). This holistic optimization leads to more seamless and fluid robot behavior.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The extensive empirical validation, encompassing both simulated and real-world experiments, clearly shows AC-DiT outperforming existing state-of-the-art baselines, often by a significant margin. Its success in complex, long-horizon tasks, including those requiring bimanual coordination, highlights its practical applicability and robustness.
- **Extensibility and Generalization:** The successful adaptation of the conditioning mechanism to bimanual tasks (dual-arm-to-dual-arm conditioning) indicates the framework's inherent extensibility beyond just mobile platforms, suggesting its potential for broader applications in multi-robot or multi-arm systems.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancing Robotic Autonomy:** AC-DiT contributes to making robots more autonomous and capable of performing complex household chores or industrial tasks that require both mobility and dexterous manipulation. This moves closer to the vision of general-purpose robots assisting humans in daily life.
- **Reducing Development Complexity:** An effective end-to-end solution can potentially simplify the development and deployment of mobile manipulation systems by reducing the need for complex, hand-tuned inter-module coordination or extensive task-specific engineering.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a strong foundation for future research in several directions, including exploring more advanced diffusion models, integrating larger pre-trained models, enhancing real-time adaptability, and developing more sophisticated error recovery mechanisms. The insights into dynamic multimodal fusion and explicit coordination are broadly applicable to other complex robotic tasks.
- **Safety and Efficiency:** By reducing error accumulation and enabling more precise control, AC-DiT can lead to safer and more efficient robot operations, particularly in environments where human interaction or delicate objects are involved.

#### 5.3. Limitations Acknowledged by Authors

The authors acknowledge that, like other imitation learning approaches, AC-DiT's performance is sensitive to the quantity and quality of demonstration data. It inherently inherits any suboptimal or biased behaviors present in the training demonstrations, which could limit its performance in scenarios beyond the training distribution. Overcoming this dependency would likely require integrating reinforcement learning or more sophisticated data augmentation/curation techniques.

#### 5.4. Broader Societal Impact

The authors state that their work "does not pose direct societal impacts" but rather aims to "foster research in the field of end-to-end control for mobile manipulation while promoting the healthy, controlled, and sustainable development of the entire field." While the immediate direct impact might be limited to the research community, advancements in mobile manipulation technologies have long-term societal implications. They can lead to robots assisting in elder care, performing dangerous tasks, improving logistics, or transforming home automation, with potential ethical and economic considerations that would need to be addressed as these technologies mature. The authors' focus on "healthy, controlled, and sustainable development" is a positive recognition of these broader responsibilities.
