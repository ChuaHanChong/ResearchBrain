# FA: Forced Prompt Learning of Vision-Language Models for Out-of-Distribution Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces the "Forced prompt leArning" (FA) framework, a new approach for Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP. It improves OOD detection by deeply enriching the model's understanding of in-distribution (ID) data through a novel forced prompt learning strategy, achieving state-of-the-art performance across various benchmarks, with up to a 16.09% increase in average AUROC and a 38.7% reduction in average FPR95 on challenging datasets in few-shot settings.

## Problem

- Existing OOD detection methods, particularly those based on Vision-Language Models (VLMs), frequently rely on resource-intensive external OOD datasets or struggle with generalizing OOD-related knowledge.
- Current VLM-based OOD detection approaches often focus on explicitly acquiring 'OOD-related knowledge,' which is limited in its ability to generalize to diverse, unseen OOD data.
- Many traditional and VLM-based OOD methods face challenges such as high computational costs, reliance on extensive labeled in-distribution (ID) datasets, or difficulties in effectively generalizing, especially in few-shot learning scenarios.

## Method

- Introduces =="Forced prompt leArning" (FA)==, a CLIP-based framework that utilizes both frozen "original prompts" and learnable ==forced prompts== for each in-distribution (ID) class to enhance OOD detection.
- Employs a novel ==Forced Cross-Entropy (FCE) loss== to compel the learnable forced prompts to capture richer, more diversified, and salient semantic descriptions of ID classes.
- Maintains parameter efficiency comparable to existing prompt learning methods like CoOp by freezing original prompts and sharing learnable context vectors across classes for the forced prompts.

## Results

- Achieves state-of-the-art OOD detection performance across conventional and challenging benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet-1k, OpenImage-O, NINCO), consistently outperforming baselines, particularly in few-shot settings.
- In the 1-shot setting, FA_GL reduced average FPR95 from **31.62%** to **27.81%** and improved average AUROC from **92.01%** to **93.26%** on ImageNet-1k OOD benchmarks.
- Demonstrates robustness and insensitivity to the forced coefficient
K, and maintains in-distribution classification accuracy while outperforming baselines on diverse ID datasets, including challenging low-resolution ones like EuroSAT, with improvements of up to **16.09%** in average AUROC.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Superior OOD detection can be achieved by thoroughly exploiting and enriching in-distribution (ID) knowledge, rather than explicitly attempting to acquire OOD-related knowledge.
> - Compelling Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to learn more discriminative and detailed features for ID classes implicitly enhances the separation between ID and OOD samples in the embedding space.
> - The use of a 'forced coefficient' within the FCE loss function prompts the learnable prompt to significantly surpass the generic representation provided by a stable, frozen original prompt, thereby deepening ID understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection is a fundamental and pressing challenge in the field of artificial intelligence, crucial for ensuring the safety, reliability, and trustworthiness of AI models deployed in real-world scenarios. AI systems frequently encounter data that deviates from their training distribution, and the ability to identify such OOD samples is vital to prevent erroneous or even harmful predictions.

Historically, OOD detection methods primarily focused on single-modal models, often relying on post-hoc analysis of model outputs (e.g., softmax probabilities, feature activations) or specialized training strategies to learn a decision boundary between in-distribution (ID) and OOD data. While these methods achieved some success, they often suffered from limitations such as high computational costs, reliance on extensive labeled ID datasets, or difficulties in generalizing to diverse, unseen OOD data, particularly in few-shot learning settings.

The emergence of large-scale pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP has significantly reshaped the landscape of few-shot learning and, more recently, OOD detection. VLMs, with their powerful cross-modal understanding and strong generalization capabilities, offer a promising avenue for OOD detection. Current CLIP-based OOD methods largely concentrate on explicitly acquiring "OOD-related knowledge." This typically involves:
1. **Leveraging external auxiliary OOD datasets:** Training on vast external datasets to learn what "out-of-distribution" looks like. However, this is resource-intensive and impractical given the infinite and agnostic nature of real-world OOD data.
2. **Extracting OOD features from ID data:** Learning OOD cues from "exposed outliers" within the ID training images themselves, such as background regions. The generalization of such context-specific OOD features to arbitrary OOD data is often limited.
3. **Learning negative prompts:** Designing prompts that semantically represent "non-ID" concepts. Yet, these negative prompts are often too restrictive to capture the immense diversity of actual OOD data.

This paper distinguishes itself by proposing a **paradigm shift**: instead of focusing on the intricate and often elusive OOD-related knowledge, it posits that **fully exploiting in-distribution (ID) knowledge** can lead to more effective OOD detection. This motivation is explicitly linked to recent findings in open-set recognition, which suggest that enhancing closed-set classification accuracy often correlates with improved open-set recognition capabilities. By making the model's understanding of ID classes more robust, comprehensive, and finely-grained, the intrinsic separation between ID and novel OOD samples in the embedding space is implicitly enhanced. This approach represents a novel and potentially more scalable direction in VLM-based OOD detection, circumventing the inherent challenges of explicitly modeling the vastness of OOD data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly improve Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection performance, particularly in few-shot learning scenarios, by leveraging the powerful capabilities of pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), specifically CLIP.

The core motivation for this work stems from the observed limitations of existing CLIP-based OOD detection methods. While VLMs have shown remarkable promise, most current approaches tend to focus on explicitly learning characteristics associated with "out-of-distribution" data. This typically involves:
- **High resource demands:** Many methods necessitate large external auxiliary OOD datasets for training, incurring substantial computational costs and data annotation efforts. This dependency makes them less practical for real-world applications where truly OOD data is often unknown and infinite.
- **Limited generalization of OOD knowledge:** Approaches that derive OOD-related features from specific regions within in-distribution (ID) images (e.g., background elements) or rely on a constrained set of "negative prompts" often struggle to generalize effectively to the vast and diverse spectrum of OOD samples encountered in practice. The learned OOD "signatures" are too specific and fail to encompass the broader concept of novelty.

Instead of navigating the complexities of OOD-related knowledge, the authors introduce a novel and counter-intuitive hypothesis: **that a deeper, richer, and more diversified understanding of In-Distribution (ID) classes can inherently lead to superior OOD detection capabilities.** This insight is inspired by findings in open-set recognition literature, which suggest that a classifier highly proficient at discerning ID classes is often inherently better at identifying samples outside that defined distribution.

Therefore, the key objectives of the proposed "Forced prompt leArning" (FA) framework are:
1. **Exploit ID knowledge:** To develop a CLIP-based framework that maximally utilizes the available in-distribution knowledge from few-shot ID samples.
2. **Learn richer ID descriptions:** To compel the VLM to learn a "forced prompt" that encodes more diversified and comprehensive semantic descriptions of ID classes, going beyond the simple textual semantics of class labels. This enriched representation aims to make ID samples more distinct and easier to discern.
3. **Enhance ID/OOD separation:** To force higher semantic similarity between ID images and their corresponding forced prompts, thereby creating a more pronounced decision boundary between ID and OOD samples in the multimodal embedding space.
4. **Efficiency and independence:** To achieve these improvements without relying on external auxiliary datasets and while maintaining an identical or comparable number of trainable parameters as existing efficient methods like CoOp, making the solution practical and resource-friendly.

By focusing on making ID representations exceptionally clear and distinct, the FA framework aims to implicitly improve the model's ability to confidently differentiate ID from any unseen OOD data, thereby addressing the limitations of explicit OOD modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed "Forced prompt leArning" (FA) framework builds upon the foundation of CLIP and prompt learning techniques, introducing a novel mechanism to deepen the model's understanding of in-distribution (ID) data for enhanced OOD detection.

**Core Architecture:**
FA utilizes CLIP's pre-trained image encoder $f(\cdot)$ and text encoder $g(\cdot)$. For each ID class $c$, the method introduces two types of prompts:
1. **Original Prompt:** This prompt is initialized using a standard manual template (e.g., "a photo of a [class-c]"). Critically, it is **frozen** during training. Its role is to serve as a stable reference point, preserving CLIP's powerful generalization capabilities inherent in its pre-trained state.
2. **Forced Prompt:** This prompt is also initialized identically to the original prompt, using the same manual template. However, unlike the original prompt, the context vectors within the forced prompt are **learnable**. This learnable component is shared across all classes, similar to the CoOp architecture, which is beneficial for few-shot learning scenarios due to its parameter efficiency.

**Forced Prompt Learning Strategy:**
The central idea is to "force" the learnable forced prompt to capture richer, more diversified, and more salient semantic descriptions of the ID classes than the relatively generic descriptions offered by the original prompt. This "forcing" is achieved through a novel **Forced Cross-Entropy (FCE) loss function**, denoted as $L_{FCE-K}$.

The $L_{FCE-K}$ is defined as:
$L_{FCE-K} = E_{(x,y_c) \sim D_{ID}^{train}} \left[ -\log \frac{e^{s_f^c / \tau}}{P_C_{j=1} e^{s_f^j / \tau} + K P_C_{j=1} e^{s_o^j / \tau}} \right]$
where:
- $(x, y_c)$ is an image-label pair from the ID training dataset.
- $s_f^c = \text{cos}(z, t_f^c)$ represents the cosine similarity between the image feature $z=f(x)$ and the forced prompt feature $t_f^c$ for the correct class $c$.
- $s_o^j = \text{cos}(z, t_o^j)$ represents the cosine similarity between the image feature $z=f(x)$ and the original prompt feature $t_o^j$ for any class $j$.
- $\tau$ is a temperature hyperparameter.
- $K$ is the **forced coefficient**, a hyperparameter ($K \ge 0, K \in N$) that controls the intensity of the "forcing."

**Mechanism of the FCE Loss:**
The denominator of the FCE loss is crucial. It sums the exponentiated similarities of the image feature to *all forced prompts* AND a weighted sum ($K$) of similarities to *all original prompts*. For an ID image $x$ belonging to class $y_c$, the loss minimizes the negative log probability of $y_c$. To minimize this loss, the similarity $s_f^c$ (between the image and its correct forced prompt) must become significantly higher than the sum of all other similarities in the denominator. Since the original prompt already provides a good, but generic, representation, the forced prompt is compelled to learn more discriminative and detailed features to surpass this baseline, effectively enriching the ID knowledge.

- **Initialization:** Both prompts are initialized using the manual template "a photo of a [class-c]". This ensures that both prompts begin with clear semantic information, leveraging CLIP's prior knowledge effectively, which is found to be superior to random initialization.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** By freezing the original prompt and using a shared learnable vector for the forced prompt, FA maintains a parameter count equivalent to CoOp, avoiding an increase in computational overhead.

**Model Inference for OOD Detection:**
For OOD detection, FA can be combined with existing score functions. The paper demonstrates its compatibility with:
- **MCM score ($S_{MCM}$):** This score is based on the maximum similarity between the global image feature $z_g$ and an adapted text feature $t_a^c$. The adapted text feature in FA incorporates both the forced and original prompt features.
- **GL-MCM score ($S_{GL-MCM}$):** This extends MCM by also considering local image features $z_l^i$, further enhancing the discriminative power.

In these score functions, the forced coefficient $K$ reappears, weighting the contribution of the original prompt's similarities in the denominator. This structure during inference implies that an ID image, having been "forced" to align strongly with its specific forced prompt during training, will likely yield a higher OOD score than an OOD image, which will not align as strongly with any specific forced prompt and will be more influenced by the weighted sum of generic original prompt similarities.

This methodology represents a strategic departure from explicit OOD modeling, instead focusing on making ID classes so distinct that anything else is readily recognized as OOD, all while being resource-efficient and highly compatible with existing VLM architectures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations presented in the paper consistently demonstrate that the proposed Forced prompt leArning (FA) framework achieves state-of-the-art performance in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection across various benchmarks, especially in few-shot settings.

**1. Conventional OOD Detection (ImageNet-1k as ID):**
- **Superior Performance:** On the widely used ImageNet-1k benchmark with OOD datasets like iNaturalist, SUN, Places, and Textures, FA significantly outperforms existing CLIP-based OOD detection methods, including zero-shot baselines (MCM, GL-MCM, CLIPN), CLIP-based post-hoc methods (MSP, ODIN, Energy, ReAct, MaxLogit), and other prompt learning methods (CoOp, LoCoOp, IDLike, LSN, NegPrompt, SCT).
- **Significant Improvement in Few-Shot Settings:**
 - In the **1-shot setting**, FA with GL-MCM scoring (FA_GL) reduces the average FPR95 (False Positive Rate at 95% True Positive Rate) from 31.62% (SCT_GL, the best baseline) to 27.81%, and improves the average AUROC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve) from 92.01% to 93.26%. Even FA with MCM scoring (FA_MCM) surpasses the best competing method in this setting.
 - Similar trends are observed in the **16-shot setting**, where FA_GL achieves an average FPR95 of 25.68% and AUROC of 93.82%, outperforming SCT_GL (27.27% FPR95, 93.31% AUROC).
- **Maintenance of ID Accuracy:** Crucially, FA achieves these improvements in OOD detection without sacrificing in-distribution (ID) classification accuracy, maintaining comparable or slightly better Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k compared to other prompt learning methods.
- **Dataset Specifics:** The paper acknowledges that FA performs less optimally on the SUN dataset, attributing this to the known issue of significant ID-category samples present within the SUN dataset, aligning with recent critical analyses of OOD benchmarks.

**2. Challenging OOD Detection (Cleaner OOD Datasets):**
- To address concerns about contamination in conventional OOD benchmarks, FA was evaluated on cleaner and more challenging OOD datasets: OpenImage-O, NINCO, and ImageNet-O.
- FA consistently demonstrates superior performance on these more rigorous benchmarks as well. For example, in the 16-shot setting, FA_GL achieves an average FPR95 of 53.61% and AUROC of 83.93%, outperforming SCT_GL (58.25% FPR95, 82.24% AUROC). This confirms the robustness and effectiveness of FA even when facing genuinely difficult OOD samples.

**3. Performance Across Various ID Datasets:**
- The versatility of FA is further demonstrated by experiments using eight additional diverse ID datasets (e.g., UCF101, EuroSAT, Food101, StanfordCars, Caltech101, FGVCAircraft, Flowers102, OxfordPets).
- FA generally achieves superior OOD performance compared to baselines across these datasets.
- **Notable Strong Performance:** For instance, with UCF101 as the ID dataset (16-shot), FA_MCM achieves an average AUROC of 99.42% compared to SCT_GL's 96.03%.
- **Effectiveness on Low-Resolution Data:** Even for challenging, low-resolution datasets like EuroSAT (64x64 pixels) used as ID, FA_MCM significantly outperforms the best-competing method (LoCoOp_GL), showing improvements of 16.09% in average AUROC and a remarkable 38.7% reduction in average FPR95.

**4. Ablation Studies:**
- **Influence of L_FCE-K:** The proposed Forced Cross-Entropy loss ($L_{FCE-K}$) is empirically validated to be highly effective, showing significant performance gains over a standard cross-entropy loss (e.g., for FA_GL, average FPR95 improves from 34.29% to 25.68%, and AUROC from 90.99% to 93.82%).
- **Initialization:** Manual initialization of both the forced and original prompts with clear semantic templates ("a photo of a [class-c]") is crucial for performance, outperforming random initialization.
- **Shared Learnable Vector:** The use of a shared learnable vector for the forced prompt across all classes is confirmed to be more effective in few-shot settings compared to independent vectors, consistent with prior prompt learning research.

**5. Sensitivity Study:**
- The framework exhibits **robustness and insensitivity** to the choice of the forced coefficient $K$ (tested from 1 to 6). Performance remains consistently high and generally stable across this range, indicating ease of hyperparameter tuning and reliability.

In summary, the results consistently validate the core hypothesis of the paper: by deeply enriching the model's understanding of ID knowledge through forced prompt learning, superior and more generalized OOD detection can be achieved without the need for external OOD data or complex OOD-specific architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "FA: Forced Prompt Learning of Vision-Language Models for Out-of-Distribution Detection" carries significant implications and potential impact on the fields of artificial intelligence, computer vision, and machine learning.

**1. Paradigm Shift in OOD Detection:** The most profound contribution of this work is its demonstration that a novel, ID-centric approach can outperform methods explicitly designed to learn OOD-related knowledge. By focusing on making in-distribution (ID) data representations exceptionally distinct and comprehensive, FA implicitly creates a stronger boundary against unseen out-of-distribution (OOD) data. This challenges the conventional wisdom in OOD detection and could inspire a new wave of research exploring the power of robust ID modeling for anomaly and novelty detection tasks.

**2. Practicality and Efficiency for Real-World AI Systems:**
- **No External OOD Data Required:** A major limitation of many OOD methods is their reliance on large, diverse external OOD datasets. FA successfully eliminates this dependency, making it highly attractive for real-world scenarios where true OOD data is typically unknown, uncurated, and effectively infinite. This significantly reduces data collection and annotation costs.
- **Computational Efficiency:** By maintaining the same number of trainable parameters as CoOp, a well-known efficient prompt learning method, FA avoids introducing additional computational overhead. This makes it suitable for deployment on resource-constrained platforms or in applications where model complexity is a concern.
- **Few-Shot Learning Excellence:** The method's exceptional performance in few-shot settings is critical for domains where labeled data is scarce, such as medical imaging, scientific discovery, or specialized industrial inspections.

**3. Enhanced Reliability and Trustworthiness of AI:** OOD detection is a cornerstone of building robust and trustworthy AI systems. By significantly improving the ability of models to identify novel inputs, FA directly contributes to:
- **Safety-Critical Applications:** Preventing AI systems from making overconfident predictions on unfamiliar data in high-stakes environments (e.g., autonomous driving, intelligent diagnosis).
- **Robust Deployment:** Ensuring that AI models deployed in dynamic real-world environments can gracefully handle unexpected inputs, leading to more resilient applications.

**4. Broad Generalizability:** The consistent state-of-the-art performance of FA across a diverse array of OOD benchmarks (conventional, challenging, and various ID datasets, including low-resolution ones) underscores its strong generalizability. This suggests that the underlying principle of enriching ID knowledge is robust and widely applicable, enhancing its utility across different domains and data characteristics.

**5. Advancement of Prompt Learning for VLMs:** FA offers a new, effective strategy for adapting powerful pre-trained Vision-Language Models like CLIP to downstream tasks beyond standard classification. It showcases how subtle yet targeted modifications to prompt learning can unlock significant capabilities for complex problems like OOD detection, further expanding the utility of foundation models.

**6. Catalyst for Future Research:** The authors explicitly state their expectation that this study will "bring new insight on VLMs-based OOD detection methods and inspire more future research." This work could stimulate further investigation into:
- How to further refine ID knowledge exploitation for OOD tasks.
- The interplay between ID distinctness and OOD separation.
- Applications of similar "forcing" mechanisms in other open-world recognition problems.

In conclusion, FA offers a practical, efficient, and highly effective solution for OOD detection, grounded in a novel theoretical insight. Its potential to enhance the reliability and trustworthiness of AI systems, particularly in data-scarce and dynamic environments, positions it as a significant advancement in the field.
