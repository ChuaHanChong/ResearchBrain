# Rubric-based On-policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Rubric-based On-policy Distillation (ROPD) enables effective distillation from black-box large language models by using structured, prompt-specific semantic rubrics instead of internal logits. This method consistently improves student model performance, achieving up to 10 times greater sample efficiency and robust cross-architectural generalization across complex reasoning tasks compared to logit-based approaches.

## Problem

- Traditional On-Policy Distillation (OPD) relies on accessing a teacher model's internal logits, restricting its application to "white-box" (open-source) LLMs.
- High-performing proprietary or API-governed LLMs are inaccessible as teachers for logit-based distillation dueating to their black-box nature.
- Existing black-box distillation methods often use implicit or holistic response-level signals, which lack the fine-grained, explicit guidance needed for complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- **ROPD** induces prompt-specific rubrics using a Rubricator (typically the teacher LLM) by synthesizing criteria from multiple teacher responses and student rollouts.
- A Verifier (also typically the teacher LLM) evaluates each student rollout against the induced rubric, assigning a binary pass/fail for each criterion and calculating a weighted pass-rate as the reward signal.
- The student policy is optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (==GRPO==), which leverages these rubric-derived rewards, normalized within rollout groups, to update the model.

## Results

- ROPD achieved the highest Pass@1 performance across all 14 black-box benchmark configurations, even surpassing the GPT-5.2-chat-latest teacher on some mathematical reasoning tasks.
- In white-box comparisons, ROPD closed **74.1%** of the performance gap between student and teacher models, demonstrating 1.8 times greater effectiveness than logit-based OPD (LOPD) baselines.
- ROPD showed significant efficiency gains, achieving comparable performance with 9.6 times fewer samples and a **6.3x** wall-clock speedup than LOPD, alongside superior training stability and robust cross-architecture generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured semantic rubrics provide a more robust and outcome-oriented supervisory signal for complex reasoning than dense, token-level logits, which can be misaligned with true correctness.
> - Key design principles, including multi-teacher coverage for rubric induction, cross-rollout rubric sharing for consistency, and blind verification to prevent bias, are crucial for ROPD's performance.
> - Rubric rewards offer superior discrimination between correct and superficially plausible responses compared to scalar judges and allow for fine-grained credit assignment, avoiding inter-dimensional interference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has established On-Policy Distillation (OPD) as a significant paradigm for post-training and model alignment. Traditional OPD methods typically rely on the teacher model's output logits to provide dense supervisory signals. This approach enables a student model to learn from its own generated trajectories, a mechanism that has demonstrated efficacy in transferring complex reasoning capabilities and is considered standard practice in developing advanced open-source models. However, this logit-based OPD is fundamentally tied to a "white-box" setting, requiring full access to the teacher's internal states and next-token distributions. This dependency restricts distillation efforts to open-source models, rendering high-performance proprietary models inaccessible as teachers.

This research addresses this limitation by exploring a complementary path: rubric-based OPD. While existing black-box distillation methods employ various response-level signals such as preference pairs, discriminators, discrete verbal trajectory scores, or scalar evaluator rewards, these signals often remain implicit or holistic, summarizing complex quality dimensions into a single value or hiding criteria behind learned scores.

Simultaneously, the field of rubric-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) has advanced, particularly in tasks requiring verifiable rewards. Rubrics have been introduced to decompose response quality into fine-grained dimensions for open-ended tasks, extending RL beyond binary outcomes. Prior work in this area, such as Rubrics-as-Rewards (RaR) and OpenRubrics, primarily treats rubrics as evaluation instruments to measure response quality against predefined criteria.

This work introduces Rubric-based On-Policy Distillation (ROPD) to bridge the gap between these areas. ROPD repositions rubrics not merely as evaluation tools but as a dynamic distillation interface. It aims to retain the core on-policy nature of OPD without relying on logit-based signals, thereby enabling scalable distillation using only teacher-generated responses. By inducing shared rubrics from the contrast between multiple teacher answers and on-policy student rollouts, and converting these into weighted pass-rate rewards for optimization, ROPD offers a novel approach to knowledge transfer that is compatible with black-box teacher models and provides explicit, semantic guidance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable On-Policy Distillation (OPD) in "black-box" scenarios where access to a teacher model's internal logits is unavailable, thereby making high-performance proprietary models usable as teachers. This goal is motivated by the inherent limitation of prevailing logit-based OPD methods, which mandate a "white-box" setting. The reliance on dense token-level logit signals restricts distillation to open-source models, preventing the transfer of knowledge from powerful, closed-source models.

To achieve this, the authors aim to investigate whether structured semantic rubrics can serve as a scalable and effective alternative to teacher logits. They hypothesize that high-level semantic guidance, derived from analyzing teacher-student contrasts, can provide a more robust and outcome-oriented supervisory signal for complex reasoning tasks than the fine-grained, token-level distributions of logits. The motivation stems from the observation that token-level logits, while dense, can reflect stochastic phrasing variations that may not be directly relevant to distilling core reasoning principles, and can sometimes be a misaligned proxy for correctness.

Specifically, the key objectives include:
- **Develop a foundational framework (ROPD)**: Create a simple yet effective framework for rubric-based OPD that induces prompt-specific rubrics and uses them to score student rollouts for on-policy optimization.
- **Demonstrate black-box compatibility**: Prove that this framework can achieve competitive, or even superior, performance compared to logit-based methods, despite operating solely on text-generated responses.
- **Improve efficiency**: Achieve significant gains in sample and computational efficiency compared to logit-based OPD by leveraging the information density of rubric-based rewards.
- **Enhance robustness**: Develop a distillation method that exhibits superior stability during training and robust cross-architecture generalization, even when distilling to significantly less capable student models or across models with disparate reasoning patterns.
- **Provide an interpretable alternative**: Offer a distillation approach that provides clearer semantic guidance, focusing on task-level reasoning principles rather than surface-form mimicry.

The overall motivation is to offer a flexible, black-box-compatible, and efficient alternative to logit-based OPD, thereby widening the applicability of this powerful distillation paradigm to a broader range of LLMs, including proprietary and cross-architectural settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ROPD (Rubric-based On-Policy Distillation) instantiates black-box OPD by translating textual teacher responses into structured, prompt-specific rubrics, which are then used to compute rewards for student policy optimization. The framework operates in two main stages: Rubric Induction and Rubric-based Verification, followed by policy optimization using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

#### 3.1. Problem Setup for Black-box OPD

In this setup, for a given input prompt `x`, a trainable student policy `π_θ` generates a rollout `y ~ π_θ(· | x)`. The teacher model `π_T` provides evaluative feedback on this output, serving as the supervisory signal. Unlike white-box OPD which accesses `p_T(· | x, y_<t)`, black-box OPD assumes teacher-side distributions are inaccessible, focusing on designing a reward function that distills teacher capabilities from discrete textual interactions.

#### 3.2. ROPD Framework

The ROPD pipeline involves two core components, a Rubricator and a Verifier, which are typically assumed by the teacher model itself to streamline the design.

1. **Rubric Induction**:
 - For a given prompt `x`, the system first collects a set of teacher responses `Y_T_x = {y_T_j}` (e.g., `m=4` samples) from `π_T` and student rollouts `Y_S_x = {y_S_i}` (e.g., `n=8` samples) from `π_θ`. `Y_T_x` provides high-level evidence of desirable solution strategies.
 - A `Rubricator` (e.g., GPT-5.2-chat-latest or Qwen3-30B-A3B) then processes the input prompt `x`, the teacher responses `Y_T_x`, and the student rollouts `Y_S_x`. Its function is to synthesize a set of prompt-specific rubrics `C_x = {c_k}` (where `K` is between 4 and 12, dynamically chosen based on complexity).
 - Each rubric item `c_k` comprises a textual criterion `ρ_k` and an importance weight `w_k > 0`. These criteria are designed to capture quality dimensions consistently shown by teacher responses, target systematic weaknesses in student responses, and guide the student towards higher quality.
 - A crucial aspect is that `C_x` is shared across all `n` student rollouts for the same prompt, ensuring consistency in the reward signal within the rollout group.

2. **Rubric-based Verification**:
 - With the induced rubric set `C_x`, a `Verifier` (also typically the teacher model) evaluates each student rollout `y_S_i` against every rubric item `c_k`.
 - For each rollout-criterion pair, `v_i,k` is assigned a binary value (0 or 1), indicating whether `y_S_i` satisfies `ρ_k`. The Verifier operates "blindly," meaning it scores both teacher and student rollouts together to calibrate bias from varying question difficulties, but without knowing the identity of the generator (teacher or student) to prevent identity-driven bias.
 - The response-level score `s_i` for a student rollout `y_S_i` is then calculated as the weighted pass rate:
 `s_i = (Σ_k=1^K w_k * v_i,k) / (Σ_k=1^K w_k + ϵ)`
 where `ϵ` is a small constant for numerical stability. This `s_i` serves as the reward signal for policy optimization.

#### 3.3. On-policy Optimization with GRPO

ROPD uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to optimize the student policy `π_θ` from these response-level rewards.
- For each prompt `x`, GRPO samples `n` responses from the old policy `π_θ_old` and obtains the calculated rewards `{r_i}`.
- The advantage `A_i` for each response `y_i` is normalized within its group:
 `A_i = (r_i - mean({r_j}_(j=1)^n)) / (std({r_j}_(j=1)^n) + ϵ)`
 This normalization avoids the need for a separate value model and makes updates dependent on relative quality within the group.
- The clipped GRPO objective updates `π_θ` based on these advantages, using a policy ratio `ρ_i,t(θ)` and a KL penalty against a fixed reference policy `π_ref`.

#### 3.4. Key Design Principles

The paper identifies several foundational design principles critical to ROPD's performance:
- **Multi-teacher coverage**: Employing multiple teacher responses (e.g., `m=4`) to seed the Rubricator encourages the induction of generalizable criteria rather than over-anchoring the rubric to a single solution trajectory.
- **Cross-rollout rubric sharing**: Utilizing a single shared rubric per prompt allows the system to identify systematic reasoning gaps across the student rollout distribution.
- **Blind verification**: The Verifier evaluates responses without knowing their origin (teacher or student). This prevents identity-driven bias while the inclusion of teacher responses in the blind pool helps calibrate reward distributions across varying problem difficulties.

The teacher model typically fulfills the roles of both Rubricator and Verifier. Experiments also indicate that replacing them with an auxiliary LLM has a marginal impact, demonstrating the paradigm's flexibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of ROPD was conducted across various benchmarks and model configurations, demonstrating its performance, efficiency, and robustness.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Student Models**: Qwen3-4B (primary) and Gemma3-4B-it (for cross-architecture generalization).
- **Teacher Models**: GPT-5.2-chat-latest (black-box setting, API access) and Qwen3-30B-A3B (white-box setting, open-weight).
- **Benchmarks**: Mathematical reasoning (AIME 24/25, HMMT 25), scientific reasoning (GPQA-Diamond), medical reasoning (HealthBench), and out-of-domain instruction following (IFEval).
- **Training Data**: DAPO-Math-17K for math, and RaR-Science/Medical-20K for science and medical tracks.
- **Baselines**: Compared against SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning), T-Judge, and black-box methods like OVD and GAD, as well as logit-based OPD (LOPD) and ExOPD for white-box comparisons.

#### 4.2. Performance in Black-Box Scenarios

ROPD consistently achieved the best Pass@1 performance across all 14 benchmark configurations in the black-box setting (Table 1). Notably, on AIME25 (thinking mode), ROPD scored 68.75%, surpassing the GPT-5.2-chat-latest teacher's performance of 67.08%. The most substantial gains were observed on challenging benchmarks like HMMT25 (Nov.), where ROPD increased the base model's score from 7.08% to 41.67%, representing a +34.6 absolute improvement. ROPD also showed slight improvements on IFEval, indicating preservation of broad instruction-following alignment.

#### 4.3. Performance in White-Box Scenarios

Despite being restricted to text-only information, ROPD outperformed existing white-box logit-based distillation baselines (LOPD and ExOPD) (Table 2). ROPD closed 74.1% of the performance gap between the student and teacher models, which is 1.8 times more effective than LOPD (42.1%). Furthermore, ROPD maintained a +10.6 point lead over ExOPD, suggesting that the architecture of rewards (semantic rubrics) can be more impactful than optimizing reward magnitude alone. This result indicates that for complex reasoning tasks, rubric-based signals can be a more effective form of supervision than dense, token-level logits.

#### 4.4. Efficiency and Convergence Analysis

ROPD demonstrated significant advantages in efficiency (Figure 3). It achieved comparable performance to LOPD with approximately 9.6 times fewer samples (1.6k vs. 15.4k). This sample efficiency translated into a 6.3x wall-clock speedup for ROPD to reach the same performance threshold (5.5h vs. 34.4h). Additionally, ROPD exhibited superior generalization stability, maintaining robust performance throughout training without the post-saturation degradation observed in LOPD.

#### 4.5. Cross-Architecture Generalization

ROPD demonstrated robust cross-architecture transferability (Table 3). When using a less capable student model, Gemma3-it-4B, ROPD consistently elevated performance above the base model. For example, on AIME24, performance increased from 6.67% to 10.00%, a 50% relative improvement. This suggests that ROPD's criterion-referenced rubrics provide an absolute supervisory signal that remains informative even for low-quality responses and under significant architectural shifts.

#### 4.6. Analysis: Why Rubric Rewards Transcend Teacher Logits

The analysis section provided insights into ROPD's empirical success:
- **Rubric vs. Scalar Judge**: A case study demonstrated that rubrics offer a 3.6x wider margin for discriminating between correct and superficially fluent but logically flawed responses compared to scalar judges (Table 4). This fine-grained discrimination ensures substantive reasoning is prioritized.
- **Logit Misalignment**: Analysis showed a significant inverse correlation between teacher logit and ground-truth correctness (0.35 AUC for logit vs. 0.90 AUC for rubric rewards) (Figure 4a). This indicates that logit-based methods can reward fluent but incorrect paths, while rubrics directly target correctness.
- **Mimicry for Understanding, Divergence for Transcendence**: ROPD initially shows rapid stylistic alignment (token overlap) with the teacher, but then its accuracy and rubric rewards scale synchronously while its logit distribution diverges (Figure 5a, 4b). This suggests ROPD uses the teacher as a "springboard" to transcend specific token distributions for higher-order correctness.
- **Decoupled Supervision**: ROPD's decomposition of quality into independent, verifiable criteria allows for granular credit assignment. This prevents "inter-dimensional interference" observed in LOPD, where improving one facet might degrade another (Table 5).

#### 4.7. Ablation Study

An ablation study confirmed the necessity of ROPD's core design choices (Table 6):
- **Multi-teacher coverage**: Transitioning from four to one teacher answer caused a 17.9 point drop in Pass@1, emphasizing the need for diverse teacher strategies to induce generalizable criteria.
- **Cross-rollout rubric sharing**: Utilizing a single shared rubric per prompt yielded a +3.75 point gain, highlighting its role in surfacing systematic reasoning gaps.
- **Blind scoring**: Revealing identities to the Verifier resulted in a 3.25 point performance drop. However, retaining teacher responses in the blind pool was crucial to maintaining reward calibration and discriminative power.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Rubric-based On-policy Distillation (ROPD) signifies a shift in the approach to knowledge transfer in Large Language Models. Its primary significance lies in reconciling competitive performance with enhanced accessibility and efficiency, offering several potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Black-Box Distillation**: ROPD addresses a critical limitation of traditional logit-based OPD by allowing the use of proprietary or API-governed LLMs as teachers. This expands the ecosystem of available teachers, potentially accelerating the development of smaller, specialized models by leveraging the capabilities of state-of-the-art closed-source models without requiring internal access.

2. **Superior Performance and Efficiency**: The empirical results demonstrate that ROPD not only often surpasses advanced logit-based OPD methods in performance but also achieves substantial gains in sample efficiency (up to 10x) and wall-clock speedup (6.3x). This efficiency can significantly reduce the computational resources and time required for post-training, making high-quality distillation more accessible.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Cross-Architecture Compatibility**: ROPD exhibits superior training stability and robust generalization across different model architectures and capabilities. This implies that the rubric-based supervisory signal is more universally informative, even for student models that are significantly less capable or have disparate reasoning patterns from the teacher. This broadens the applicability of distillation to a wider range of student architectures and initial policy qualities.

4. **Shift Towards Semantic Guidance**: The findings suggest that high-level semantic rubrics provide a more effective, outcome-oriented distillation signal for complex reasoning tasks than fine-grained token-level logits. This paradigm shift from "logit-matching" to "semantic guidance" highlights the value of extracting structured reasoning principles, which can be less susceptible to stochastic noise and better aligned with true correctness.

5. **Interpretability and Targeted Improvement**: Rubrics inherently offer an interpretable distillation interface, decomposing complex response quality into discrete, verifiable criteria. This allows for granular credit assignment and provides clearer, actionable feedback to the student model, potentially leading to more targeted and understandable improvements in reasoning capabilities.

6. **New Baseline for Scalable Distillation**: ROPD establishes a versatile, flexible, efficient, and robust baseline for scalable distillation. This could pave the way for future research into designing, validating, and calibrating semantic principles for knowledge transfer, particularly as frontier models become increasingly opaque and heterogeneous.

While ROPD has mainly been evaluated on formal reasoning tasks, its potential impact extends to other domains by offering a method that reconciles performance, efficiency, and accessibility in the evolving landscape of LLMs. Future work may explore its efficacy in subjective or creative tasks and further validate its reliance on meta-evaluation components across diverse model architectures.
