# Rubric-based On-policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Rubric-based On-policy Distillation (ROPD) enables effective distillation from black-box large language models by using structured, prompt-specific semantic rubrics instead of internal logits. This method consistently improves student model performance, achieving up to 10 times greater sample efficiency and robust cross-architectural generalization across complex reasoning tasks compared to logit-based approaches.

## Problem

- Traditional On-Policy Distillation (OPD) relies on accessing a teacher model's internal logits, restricting its application to "white-box" (open-source) LLMs.
- High-performing proprietary or API-governed LLMs are inaccessible as teachers for logit-based distillation dueating to their black-box nature.
- Existing black-box distillation methods often use implicit or holistic response-level signals, which lack the fine-grained, explicit guidance needed for complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- ROPD induces prompt-specific rubrics using a Rubricator (typically the teacher LLM) by synthesizing criteria from multiple teacher responses and student rollouts.
- A Verifier (also typically the teacher LLM) evaluates each student rollout against the induced rubric, assigning a binary pass/fail for each criterion and calculating a weighted pass-rate as the reward signal.
- The student policy is optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which leverages these rubric-derived rewards, normalized within rollout groups, to update the model.

## Results

- ROPD achieved the highest Pass@1 performance across all 14 black-box benchmark configurations, even surpassing the GPT-5.2-chat-latest teacher on some mathematical reasoning tasks.
- In white-box comparisons, ROPD closed 74.1% of the performance gap between student and teacher models, demonstrating 1.8 times greater effectiveness than logit-based OPD (LOPD) baselines.
- ROPD showed significant efficiency gains, achieving comparable performance with 9.6 times fewer samples and a 6.3x wall-clock speedup than LOPD, alongside superior training stability and robust cross-architecture generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured semantic rubrics provide a more robust and outcome-oriented supervisory signal for complex reasoning than dense, token-level logits, which can be misaligned with true correctness.
> - Key design principles, including multi-teacher coverage for rubric induction, cross-rollout rubric sharing for consistency, and blind verification to prevent bias, are crucial for ROPD's performance.
> - Rubric rewards offer superior discrimination between correct and superficially plausible responses compared to scalar judges and allow for fine-grained credit assignment, avoiding inter-dimensional interference.
