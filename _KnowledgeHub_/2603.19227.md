# Bridging Semantic and Kinematic Conditions with Diffusion-based Discrete Motion Tokenizer

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University and The Chinese University of Hong Kong developed MoTok, a diffusion-based discrete motion tokenizer, to unify semantic and kinematic control in human motion generation. The framework achieves high motion fidelity and precise control with significantly reduced token budgets, for instance reducing Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) to 0.025 and average error to 0.0008 cm while using one-sixth fewer tokens than prior methods.

## Problem

- Continuous diffusion models excel at kinematic control but are slow for high-level semantic inputs, operating on raw motion sequences.
- Discrete token-based generators handle semantic conditioning well but often entangle high-level semantics with low-level motion details, requiring high token rates.
- Existing methods frequently face a trade-off between effective kinematic constraint imposition and preserving motion fidelity and realism.

## Method

- A ==Perception–Planning–Control paradigm== was proposed, unifying input conditions, facilitating token-space planning, and synthesizing motion via diffusion-based decoding.
- **MoTok**, a ==diffusion-based discrete motion tokenizer==, was introduced to decouple semantic abstraction from low-level motion details, allowing compact tokens to capture semantics while a ==diffusion decoder== handles fine-grained reconstruction.
- A ==coarse-to-fine conditioning scheme== harmonizes semantic cues and kinematic constraints, applying coarse guidance during token planning and enforcing fine-grained control via ==optimization-based guidance== during diffusion denoising.

## Results

- MoTok-DDM-4 reduced Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) from **0.061 to 0.029** and trajectory error from **0.0098 cm to 0.0049 cm** in "Pelvis" trajectory control, using one-sixth of the tokens compared to MaskControl.
- For text-to-motion generation on HumanML3D, MoTok-DDM-2 achieved an **FID of 0.033**, outperforming all compared methods, while MoTok-AR-4 reduced T2M-GPT's FID nearly threefold (**0.053 vs. 0.141**).
- The framework demonstrated robustness, maintaining or improving motion fidelity even under joint text and trajectory control, contrasting with prior methods that often degraded under such constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Delegating fine-grained motion detail recovery to a diffusion decoder allows discrete tokens to efficiently capture abstract semantic information with a reduced token budget.
> - Applying low-level control signals in a dual-path manner – both as coarse guidance in token planning and as fine-grained enforcement in the diffusion decoder – prevents a trade-off between controllability and motion realism.
> - Diffusion-based decoders, particularly with explicit temporal modeling, are superior for reconstructing intricate motion details and maintaining fidelity compared to conventional convolutional decoders.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human motion generation is a research area with applications in animation, robotics, and embodied agents. Recent advancements have seen the emergence of two primary paradigms for conditional motion generation: continuous diffusion models and discrete token-based generators.

Continuous diffusion models have demonstrated proficiency in reconstructing continuous motion with smooth dynamics and fine-grained local details, making them effective for kinematic control. However, these models often operate on raw motion sequences, which can lead to slow inference and challenges in handling high-level semantic inputs.

Conversely, discrete token-based generators compress continuous motion into discrete tokens, allowing for conditional sequence modeling with scalable architectures and flexible conditioning. This approach leverages language-model-style generators and is effective for semantic conditioning, where high-level instructions (e.g., text descriptions) guide the overall motion. A limitation of existing motion tokenizers is their tendency to entangle high-level semantics with low-level motion details. This often necessitates high token rates or hierarchical codes to ensure faithful reconstruction, increasing the computational burden on downstream generators and complicating controllable generation where fine-grained kinematic signals might compete with or override semantic conditioning.

The challenge in the broader landscape is effectively integrating low-level kinematic constraints with high-level semantic intent while preserving motion quality and efficiency. Prior methods often exhibit a trade-off, where imposing stronger kinematic constraints can degrade motion fidelity. This work aims to bridge these two paradigms by proposing a framework that leverages the strengths of both, thereby addressing the existing limitations in integrating semantic and kinematic control for motion generation. It seeks to decouple semantic abstraction from fine-grained reconstruction, enabling more efficient and controllable motion synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that existing motion generation paradigms exhibit complementary strengths. Continuous diffusion models excel at reconstructing intricate motion details and achieving kinematic control, while discrete token-based generators are effective for processing high-level semantic information. However, current token-based methods often struggle to represent fine-grained motion details efficiently, requiring a high token budget or complex hierarchical structures, which can conflate semantic and kinematic information. This entanglement can lead to a compromise between controllability and motion realism, especially when attempting to impose both high-level semantic and low-level kinematic constraints.

Based on this insight, the authors propose a division of labor where discrete tokens primarily capture semantic abstraction, and diffusion models are responsible for the detailed reconstruction of continuous motion. The key objectives of this work are articulated through three main contributions:

1. **To propose a three-stage Perception–Planning–Control paradigm for controllable motion generation.** This framework aims to provide a unified pipeline that can process diverse conditions, perform token-space planning, and synthesize motion, supporting both autoregressive (AR) and discrete diffusion (DDM) planners. This decomposition is intended to separate high-level planning from low-level kinematics, enhancing generalization across generator architectures and motion generation tasks.
2. **To introduce MoTok, a diffusion-based discrete motion tokenizer.** MoTok is designed to decouple semantic abstraction from low-level motion reconstruction. By delegating the recovery of fine-grained motion details to a diffusion decoder, MoTok aims to enable the use of compact, single-layer tokens with a significantly reduced token budget. This design reduces the burden on downstream planners, allowing them to focus on semantic structure without needing to encode fine kinematic details.
3. **To develop a coarse-to-fine conditioning scheme.** This scheme is designed to harmonize semantic cues and kinematic constraints by distributing control across the planning and control stages. Kinematic conditions are applied as coarse constraints during the planning stage to guide token generation, and then enforced as fine-grained constraints during the control stage through optimization-based guidance within the diffusion denoising process. This coarse-to-fine approach is intended to prevent low-level kinematic details from interfering with token-space planning, thereby avoiding a trade-off between controllability and realism.

Collectively, these objectives aim to develop a robust and efficient framework for conditional motion generation that can simultaneously achieve high-fidelity motion, precise kinematic control, and strong semantic alignment with reduced computational overhead.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is structured around a three-stage **Perception–Planning–Control** paradigm, central to which is the **MoTok (Motion Tokenizer)**.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework: Perception–Planning–Control

- **Perception:** This initial stage involves encoding heterogeneous input conditions. Conditions are categorized into:
 - **Global Conditions (c_g):** These provide sequence-level guidance without requiring frame-wise alignment, such as text descriptions or style labels.
 - **Local Conditions (c_s_1:T):** These are aligned with the motion timeline and specify kinematic control signals, like target root trajectories, keyframes, or contact hints.
- **Planning:** In this stage, a token-space planner generates a discrete motion token sequence (z_1:N). The framework supports a unified interface for both autoregressive (AR) and discrete diffusion (DDM) generative models. Global conditions are typically embedded as a dedicated token, while local conditions are integrated by additive fusion with positional embeddings.
- **Control:** The final continuous motion (x̂_0) is synthesized during this stage via diffusion-based decoding. Fine-grained kinematic constraints are enforced directly within the diffusion denoising process through an optimization-based guidance mechanism. This allows for precise low-level control without burdening the discrete planner with high-frequency details.

#### 3.2. MoTok: Diffusion-based Discrete Motion Tokenizer

MoTok factorizes motion representation into compact discrete code sequences and relies on a diffusion decoder for fine-grained reconstruction. This design differs from conventional VQ-VAE tokenizers that directly decode continuous motion from discrete codes.

- **Convolutional Encoder (E):** Takes a motion sequence (θ_1:T) and produces a temporally downsampled latent sequence (h_1:N).
- **Vector Quantizer (Q):** Discretizes the latent sequence h_1:N into a discrete token sequence z_1:N and corresponding quantized latents q_1:N. A single-layer codebook of size K (e.g., 1024) is used.
- **Decoder with Diffusion-based Reconstruction:** Instead of direct regression, MoTok decodes q_1:N into a per-frame conditioning signal s_1:T using a convolutional decoder D(·). This signal then conditions a reverse diffusion process P_ϕ(·) parameterized by a neural denoiser f_ϕ. The denoiser predicts the clean motion x̂_0 from a noisy input x_t at timestep t, incorporating conditioning embeddings that combine timestep and s_1:T. The architecture of f_ϕ includes linear projection, residual 1D convolution modules for temporal modeling, and MLPs with AdaIN-style transformation for conditioning injection.

- **Training Objectives:** MoTok is trained end-to-end with a conditional denoising objective (L_diff, Smooth-L1 loss) and a VQ commitment loss (L_commit) to regularize codebook usage.

#### 3.3. Unified Conditional Motion Generation Details

- **Condition Encoding:** Global conditions (e.g., text) are encoded by E_g(·) into a sequence-level feature M_g. Local conditions (e.g., trajectory) are encoded by E_s(·) into a token-aligned feature sequence M_s_1:N.
- **Planning in Discrete Token Space:**
 - **Discrete Diffusion Planning:** Follows a masked-token diffusion paradigm. A token embedding sequence is constructed, with M_g at the first position and M_s_n added to motion token positions.
 - **Autoregressive Planning:** Uses the same interface, with M_g at the first position, and local conditions integrated to maintain causal temporal alignment for next-token prediction.
- **Classifier-free Guidance (CFG):** Applied to token-space planning, extended to multiple conditions (semantic and trajectory) via alternating guidance pairs. This strategy involves combining conditional and unconditional predictions to balance semantic guidance and control fidelity without additional networks.
- **Control in Diffusion Decoding:** After token generation, MoTok decodes the tokens into s_1:T. During the iterative denoising steps of the diffusion model, an auxiliary control loss L_ctrl(x̂_k, c_s_1:T) measures deviation from local conditions. The denoising update is refined by applying a gradient step based on this control loss, ensuring precise low-level control directly at the continuous motion level.

#### 3.4. Instantiation for Different Tasks

- **Text-to-Motion:** Only global text embedding (from a pre-trained CLIP text encoder) is used.
- **Text and Trajectory Control:** Combines global text embedding with a time-synchronized trajectory embedding. The trajectory is encoded by a convolutional encoder (E_traj) into token-aligned features M_s_1:N, which are injected during planning and further enforced via refinement during diffusion decoding.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** HumanML3D and KIT-ML, standard paired text-motion benchmarks.
- **Conditioning:** CLIP text encoder for global text conditions; convolutional encoder (mirroring MoTok's motion encoder) for local trajectory conditions.
- **Training:** MoTok is evaluated with two planning backbones: a MoMask-style discrete diffusion planner and a T2M-GPT-style autoregressive transformer. Training incorporates condition dropout and random token replacement. Variants include different compression ratios (MoTok-DDM-4/2, MoTok-AR-4/2, where 4/2 indicates the temporal compression ratio).
- **Inference:** Uses a spaced diffusion scheme (Fast27) for faster sampling and incorporates decoding-time refinement for trajectory control.
- **Ablation Studies:** Investigate the impact of decoder design (convolutional, DiffusionHead, DiffusionConv), latent dimension, temporal downsampling rate, kernel size in temporal diffusion, and the dual-path injection of low-level control signals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across various experimental settings and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Text and Trajectory Control

On the HumanML3D dataset, MoTok demonstrates substantial improvements in controllable motion generation:
- **Fidelity and Controllability:** Compared to MaskControl, MoTok-DDM-4 significantly reduces Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) from 0.061 to 0.029 and trajectory error from 0.0098 cm to 0.0049 cm in the "Pelvis" setting. In the "Random One" setting, MoTok-DDM-2 reduces FID from 0.083 to 0.025 and average error from 0.0072 cm to 0.0008 cm.
- **Token Efficiency:** These improvements are achieved with significantly fewer tokens; MoTok uses only one-sixth of MaskControl’s token budget in some settings.
- **Robustness to Constraints:** Unlike prior methods (e.g., MaskControl) that show degraded motion quality when trajectory constraints are introduced, MoTok-DDM and MoTok-AR maintain or improve FID under joint text-and-trajectory control compared to text-only generation. This suggests that trajectory information serves as complementary guidance rather than a competing constraint, attributed to the hierarchical conditioning and decoding-time diffusion guidance.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons indicate that MoTok generates motions that follow provided trajectories more faithfully while maintaining smoothness, whereas other methods may struggle with fine-grained trajectory alignment.

#### 4.2. Text-to-Motion Generation

On standard text-to-motion benchmarks (HumanML3D and KIT-ML), MoTok exhibits strong performance, particularly in motion realism (FID):
- **HumanML3D:** MoTok-DDM-4 achieves a lower FID (0.039) than the MoMask baseline (0.045) while using only one-sixth of the tokens. MoTok-DDM-2 further reduces FID to 0.033, outperforming all compared methods. MoTok also improves autoregressive planning, with MoTok-AR-4 reducing T2M-GPT’s FID by nearly threefold (0.053 vs. 0.141) with the same token budget.
- **KIT-ML:** MoTok achieves the lowest FID of 0.144, surpassing the strongest baseline (0.155) while maintaining competitive performance across other metrics.
- These results collectively support the effectiveness of diffusion-based decoding for generating high-quality motions with a reduced number of tokens, implying compact and semantically expressive motion representations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies reveal insights into MoTok's design choices:
- **Decoder Design:** Diffusion-based decoders consistently outperform plain convolutional decoders for reconstruction. Introducing explicit temporal modeling via residual 1D convolutions (DiffusionConv) further improves generation fidelity and controllability, especially under stochastic noise. This indicates that diffusion-based decoding effectively recovers fine-grained motion details.
- **Latent Dimension:** A latent dimension of 768 is found to provide sufficient capacity for accurate reconstruction and stable token-space planning. Smaller dimensions compromise expressiveness and degrade both generation fidelity and controllability.
- **Temporal Downsampling Rate:** Moderate downsampling rates (e.g., 2 or 4) yield the best balance between reconstruction quality and downstream planning performance. Very low downrates (denser tokens) are harder to model, while excessive compression removes essential motion structure.
- **Kernel Size in Temporal Diffusion:** A moderate temporal receptive field (kernel size of 5 or 7) generally produces optimal results, balancing temporal dependency modeling and avoiding over-smoothing of sparse tokens. The optimal kernel size is found to be sensitive to the temporal compression ratio.
- **Dual-path Low-level Conditioning:** Injecting low-level control signals in both the generator (for coarse, condition-aware planning) and the tokenizer decoder (for fine-grained enforcement during denoising) is crucial. Injecting them in only one stage results in either increased control error, degraded motion fidelity, or unstable adherence to constraints.

#### 4.4. Additional Analyses (Appendix)

- **Decoder’s Detail Recoverability (Appendix A.1):** A two-stage training protocol confirmed that MoTok’s diffusion-based decoder consistently improves both reconstruction (lower Recon FID) and downstream text-to-motion performance (lower T2M FID) compared to conventional convolutional decoders, even when sharing identical discrete token sequences. This indicates superior recovery of fine-grained details.
- **Semantic Usability of Tokens (Appendix A.2):** Motion-to-text (M2T) captioning tasks showed that tokens produced by MoTok lead to better captioning performance than those from standard VQ-VAE, suggesting MoTok tokens encode richer and more usable semantic information. This is attributed to the diffusion decoder offloading high-frequency detail recovery, allowing discrete tokens to focus on low-frequency, overall motion structure.
- **CFG Scale Ablation (Appendix C):** Performance typically follows a unimodal pattern with respect to CFG scale, where moderate guidance is optimal, and excessive guidance degrades fidelity. The optimal CFG scale shifts with the temporal compression rate, requiring larger scales for lower compression ratios (more tokens) to amplify text condition influence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a fundamental challenge in conditional human motion generation: effectively integrating high-level semantic instructions with precise low-level kinematic controls without compromising motion quality or efficiency. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Bridging Paradigms:** The proposed Perception–Planning–Control paradigm, with MoTok as its core, successfully bridges the strengths of continuous diffusion models (for kinematic control and fine-grained details) and discrete token-based generators (for semantic conditioning and scalable planning). This unified approach offers a solution to the long-standing trade-off between controllability and realism in motion synthesis.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Fidelity:** MoTok demonstrates the ability to achieve significantly higher motion fidelity and controllability with a substantially reduced token budget (e.g., one-sixth of prior methods). This efficiency gain has implications for reducing computational overhead in training and inference, making more complex motion generation tasks feasible.
- **Robust Controllability:** The coarse-to-fine conditioning scheme allows for effective enforcement of kinematic constraints. A key finding is that MoTok not only maintains but can *improve* motion fidelity even under stronger kinematic constraints, a behavior opposite to that observed in many prior methods. This robustness to detailed control signals is a significant advancement.
- **Decoupling of Semantic and Kinematic Information:** By delegating fine-grained motion recovery to a diffusion decoder, MoTok frees the discrete tokens to focus on abstract semantic information. This architectural decoupling simplifies the learning task for both the token encoder/quantizer and the downstream planners, leading to more semantically meaningful discrete representations and improved text-motion alignment.
- **Unified and Flexible Framework:** The framework's ability to support various conditioning types (global text, local trajectories) and different token-space generative architectures (discrete diffusion, autoregressive) under a unified interface highlights its flexibility and potential for broad applicability across diverse motion generation tasks, including conditional generation and motion editing.
- **Advancement in AI-driven Animation and Robotics:** The ability to generate highly realistic, controllable, and semantically consistent human motions has direct implications for advancing character animation in games and film, developing more natural and responsive embodied agents, and creating sophisticated control systems for humanoid robotics. The improved precision and efficiency can accelerate the development of applications requiring nuanced human motion.
