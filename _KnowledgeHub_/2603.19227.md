# Bridging Semantic and Kinematic Conditions with Diffusion-based Discrete Motion Tokenizer

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University and The Chinese University of Hong Kong developed MoTok, a diffusion-based discrete motion tokenizer, to unify semantic and kinematic control in human motion generation. The framework achieves high motion fidelity and precise control with significantly reduced token budgets, for instance reducing Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) to 0.025 and average error to 0.0008 cm while using one-sixth fewer tokens than prior methods.

## Problem

- Continuous diffusion models excel at kinematic control but are slow for high-level semantic inputs, operating on raw motion sequences.
- Discrete token-based generators handle semantic conditioning well but often entangle high-level semantics with low-level motion details, requiring high token rates.
- Existing methods frequently face a trade-off between effective kinematic constraint imposition and preserving motion fidelity and realism.

## Method

- A ==Perception–Planning–Control paradigm== was proposed, unifying input conditions, facilitating token-space planning, and synthesizing motion via diffusion-based decoding.
- **MoTok**, a ==diffusion-based discrete motion tokenizer==, was introduced to decouple semantic abstraction from low-level motion details, allowing compact tokens to capture semantics while a ==diffusion decoder== handles fine-grained reconstruction.
- A ==coarse-to-fine conditioning scheme== harmonizes semantic cues and kinematic constraints, applying coarse guidance during token planning and enforcing fine-grained control via ==optimization-based guidance== during diffusion denoising.

## Results

- MoTok-DDM-4 reduced Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) from **0.061 to 0.029** and trajectory error from **0.0098 cm to 0.0049 cm** in "Pelvis" trajectory control, using one-sixth of the tokens compared to MaskControl.
- For text-to-motion generation on HumanML3D, MoTok-DDM-2 achieved an **FID of 0.033**, outperforming all compared methods, while MoTok-AR-4 reduced T2M-GPT's FID nearly threefold (**0.053 vs. 0.141**).
- The framework demonstrated robustness, maintaining or improving motion fidelity even under joint text and trajectory control, contrasting with prior methods that often degraded under such constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Delegating fine-grained motion detail recovery to a diffusion decoder allows discrete tokens to efficiently capture abstract semantic information with a reduced token budget.
> - Applying low-level control signals in a dual-path manner – both as coarse guidance in token planning and as fine-grained enforcement in the diffusion decoder – prevents a trade-off between controllability and motion realism.
> - Diffusion-based decoders, particularly with explicit temporal modeling, are superior for reconstructing intricate motion details and maintaining fidelity compared to conventional convolutional decoders.
