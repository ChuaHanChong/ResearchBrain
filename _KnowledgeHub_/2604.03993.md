# Can LLMs Learn to Reason Robustly under Noisy Supervision?

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents a systematic analysis of noisy label mechanisms within the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) framework for large language models. It introduces Online Label Refinement (OLR), a novel method that dynamically corrects potentially noisy training labels using the model's own generated majority answers, leading to consistent performance improvements in mathematical and general reasoning tasks across various noise conditions.

## Problem

- Training reasoning-oriented LLMs with RLVR relies on large volumes of perfectly curated data, which is difficult to acquire in real-world scenarios.
- Existing noisy label learning methods are not directly applicable to RLVR due to its near-infinite label space, on-policy data generation, and conditional label influence.
- A lack of systematic analysis and mitigation strategies for noisy labels specifically within the RLVR paradigm.

## Method

- Defined a taxonomy for noisy labels in RLVR, distinguishing between 'inactive' (not rollout-feasible) and 'active' (rollout-feasible) noisy labels based on their impact.
- Developed Online Label Refinement (OLR), a self-correcting mechanism that identifies and replaces noisy labels with model-generated majority answers based on two criteria: a positive convergence slope of the answer's pass-rate and historical consistency.
- Integrated OLR into the Group Relative Policy Optimization (==GRPO==) training process, allowing the model to dynamically refine its training data after an initial early learning phase.

## Results

- OLR achieved average gains of **3.6%** on in-distribution mathematical benchmarks and **3.3%** on out-of-distribution tasks under inactive noisy labels, maintaining performance at **50%** noise comparable to **10%** noise.
- Under active noisy labels, OLR produced average gains of **3.9%** on mathematical benchmarks and **4.6%** on out-of-distribution tasks, preventing model collapse observed in baselines.
- The method successfully reduced the proportion of original noisy labels by approximately **30%** and introduced minimal new errors (below **5%**), demonstrating high efficacy in label refinement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Identified 'Early Correctness Coherence,' a phenomenon where accuracy on both clean and noisy samples increases similarly during early RLVR training due to positive cross-sample coupling.
> - Active noisy labels are more detrimental to RLVR training than inactive noisy labels, as they can be reinforced and skew the policy.
> - The model's emergent reasoning capabilities, specifically its ability to generate consistent majority answers from rollouts, can be leveraged for effective self-correction of noisy training labels.
