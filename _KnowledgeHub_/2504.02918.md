# Morpheus: Benchmarking Physical Reasoning of Video Generative Models with Real Physical Experiments

> [!summary] Summary
> The Morpheus benchmark quantitatively assesses the physical reasoning capabilities of video generative models against real-world physical experiments, evaluating their adherence to fundamental Newtonian mechanics. It found that current models consistently struggle to accurately simulate physical dynamics, revealing a substantial gap between visual fidelity and true physical plausibility.

## Problem

- Current video generative model (VGM) evaluations primarily focus on subjective perceptual aspects or semantic alignment, lacking rigorous quantitative assessment of physical plausibility.
- Existing physical reasoning evaluation methods are often subjective, prone to vision-language model (VLM) hallucinations, or limited by single-trajectory matching that can yield 'false negatives' due to unobserved initial conditions.
- There is a critical need for an objective, interpretable, and quantitative framework to directly measure VGMs' adherence to fundamental physical laws using real-world data, rather than simulated environments.

## Method

- Introduced **Morpheus**, a ==physics-informed benchmark== based on a curated dataset of **130** real-world videos of **9** Newtonian mechanics experiments, augmented with visually diverse yet physically consistent variants.
- Developed a robust pipeline using advanced computer vision (==SAM-2==, ==DEVA tracking==, ==Depth Anything V2==) to extract precise ==2D object trajectories== from videos.
- Proposed a ==hierarchical evaluation framework== with a ==Discard Rate==, a ==Dynamical Score== using ==Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)==, and a ==Physical Invariance Score== to quantify the preservation of conserved physical quantities.

## Results

- Real-world videos consistently yielded optimal performance, with minimal discard rates and high Dynamical (**0.98–0.99**) and Physical Invariance (**>0.93**) scores, establishing a robust upper bound for model performance.
- The best-performing generative models, like **WAN-2.1** with keyframe interpolation (avg total score **0.55**) and **COSMOS-predict2** with multi-frame conditioning (avg score **0.52**), still fall considerably short of real-world physical accuracy.
- Evaluated models frequently violated fundamental **conservation laws** (e.g., energy, momentum) and exhibited significant deviations from governing equations of motion, even when generated trajectories appeared superficially plausible.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art video generative models consistently fail to accurately encode fundamental physical principles, indicating a significant gap between visual realism and true 'world modeling' capabilities.
> - Providing more precise conditioning, such as multi-frame prompts or keyframe interpolation, generally improves physical reasoning scores and reduces discard rates, suggesting models benefit from explicit temporal context.
> - High discard rates across models (23-47%) reveal persistent issues with temporal consistency, object permanence (e.g., objects vanishing or appearing), and generating sufficient motion, which are foundational for accurate physical simulation.
