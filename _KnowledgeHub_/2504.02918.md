# Morpheus: Benchmarking Physical Reasoning of Video Generative Models with Real Physical Experiments

> [!summary] Summary
> The Morpheus benchmark quantitatively assesses the physical reasoning capabilities of video generative models against real-world physical experiments, evaluating their adherence to fundamental Newtonian mechanics. It found that current models consistently struggle to accurately simulate physical dynamics, revealing a substantial gap between visual fidelity and true physical plausibility.

## Problem

- Current video generative model (VGM) evaluations primarily focus on subjective perceptual aspects or semantic alignment, lacking rigorous quantitative assessment of physical plausibility.
- Existing physical reasoning evaluation methods are often subjective, prone to vision-language model (VLM) hallucinations, or limited by single-trajectory matching that can yield 'false negatives' due to unobserved initial conditions.
- There is a critical need for an objective, interpretable, and quantitative framework to directly measure VGMs' adherence to fundamental physical laws using real-world data, rather than simulated environments.

## Method

- Introduced **Morpheus**, a ==physics-informed benchmark== based on a curated dataset of **130** real-world videos of **9** Newtonian mechanics experiments, augmented with visually diverse yet physically consistent variants.
- Developed a robust pipeline using advanced computer vision (==SAM-2==, ==DEVA tracking==, ==Depth Anything V2==) to extract precise ==2D object trajectories== from videos.
- Proposed a ==hierarchical evaluation framework== with a ==Discard Rate==, a ==Dynamical Score== using ==Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)==, and a ==Physical Invariance Score== to quantify the preservation of conserved physical quantities.

## Results

- Real-world videos consistently yielded optimal performance, with minimal discard rates and high Dynamical (**0.98–0.99**) and Physical Invariance (**>0.93**) scores, establishing a robust upper bound for model performance.
- The best-performing generative models, like **WAN-2.1** with keyframe interpolation (avg total score **0.55**) and **COSMOS-predict2** with multi-frame conditioning (avg score **0.52**), still fall considerably short of real-world physical accuracy.
- Evaluated models frequently violated fundamental **conservation laws** (e.g., energy, momentum) and exhibited significant deviations from governing equations of motion, even when generated trajectories appeared superficially plausible.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art video generative models consistently fail to accurately encode fundamental physical principles, indicating a significant gap between visual realism and true 'world modeling' capabilities.
> - Providing more precise conditioning, such as multi-frame prompts or keyframe interpolation, generally improves physical reasoning scores and reduces discard rates, suggesting models benefit from explicit temporal context.
> - High discard rates across models (23-47%) reveal persistent issues with temporal consistency, object permanence (e.g., objects vanishing or appearing), and generating sufficient motion, which are foundational for accurate physical simulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in video generative models (VGMs), exemplified by systems such as SORA, COSMOS, and Veo3, have achieved notable improvements in visual fidelity and realism. These developments have fueled interest in the potential of VGMs to function as "world models" – systems capable of understanding and predicting the dynamics, causal interactions, and underlying mechanisms of the physical world. Such capabilities could have transformative implications for applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and scientific simulation.

However, the current landscape of evaluation methods for VGMs exhibits limitations in assessing their physical reasoning capabilities. Existing approaches typically fall into two main categories:
1. **Subjective Human Judgment or Language-Based Validation:** These methods involve evaluating plausibility through human perception or Vision-Language Models (VLMs) answering questions like "does this video look legitimate?" While useful for initial screening, these evaluations are inherently subjective and often fail to probe whether a model comprehends unobserved physical constraints, such as mass, inertia, or force coupling. Furthermore, VLMs have been observed to hallucinate or overlook subtle physical inconsistencies.
2. **Purely Visual or Geometric Verification:** This category includes methods that assess temporal consistency of appearances or trajectories. However, visual coherence alone does not guarantee physical correctness. A generated trajectory may appear smooth and plausible yet violate fundamental physical laws due to inconsistent initial conditions or incorrect coupling between hidden parameters and dynamics. Subtle, physically meaningful deviations, originating from factors such as mass distribution or air resistance, are often visually underdetermined and difficult to detect through appearance-based metrics. Additionally, trajectory matching, a common technique, typically assumes a single ground-truth trajectory, which may not accurately reflect real-world physics where multiple valid trajectories can exist due to unobservable parameters.

The paper identifies a critical gap in the existing evaluation paradigms: the failure to adequately bridge the visual generation capabilities of VGMs with the requirements of actionable world modeling. For applications in embodied AI, robotics, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems, a fundamental understanding of physical dynamics, particularly Newtonian mechanics, is essential. Current benchmarks often focus on perceptual and semantic aspects of video generation, or rely on simulated data, rather than rigorously evaluating adherence to physical laws in real-world contexts.

This work addresses this deficiency by introducing a physics-informed evaluation framework. It leverages advances in physics-informed machine learning, which traditionally model fully observable scientific data, and repurposes them to fit physical dynamics from noisy video data. The focus on Newtonian mechanics is justified by its prevalence in embodied environments and its deterministic nature, which facilitates quantitative analysis in controlled settings. By doing so, the research aims to provide a more principled and quantitative benchmark for assessing whether VGMs truly capture underlying physical dynamics or merely produce visually appealing but physically inconsistent motion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to evaluate the capacity of modern video generative models to encode and adhere to Newtonian dynamics when generating videos of physical systems. This objective is motivated by the growing interest in positioning these models as "world models" capable of understanding and predicting physical interactions, which is crucial for their deployment in critical applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and scientific simulation.

The motivations underpinning this objective are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Limitations of Current Evaluation Paradigms:** The paper highlights that existing evaluation methods for video generative models are insufficient for assessing true physical understanding. Subjective human or VLM-based judgments, while useful for initial qualitative assessments, cannot quantify adherence to unobservable physical principles (e.g., mass, inertia, force coupling) or distinguish between aesthetically pleasing but physically implausible generations. Similarly, trajectory matching, which compares generated paths to a ground truth, often incorrectly penalizes physically valid trajectories that diverge due to subtle, unobservable variations in initial conditions, implicitly assuming a single correct outcome. A more rigorous, quantitative approach is necessary to move beyond superficial visual fidelity.
- **Need for Physics-Informed Metrics:** To ascertain whether VGMs truly function as world models, it is essential to evaluate their ability to respect physical invariants and governing laws. These invariants, such as conserved energy or momentum, impose strong constraints on admissible dynamics and provide an infallible baseline for physical correctness. Quantifying adherence to such laws offers a principled, objective means to benchmark models' understanding of physical reality, rather than merely their ability to produce visually consistent motion.
- **Focus on Newtonian Mechanics:** The decision to focus on Newtonian mechanics is strategic for two primary reasons:
 1. **Fundamental to Embodied AI:** Newtonian mechanics govern the majority of physical dynamics encountered in robotic and embodied environments. A solid understanding of these principles is foundational for agents that must interact reliably with the real world, perform table-top manipulation, or operate in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems.
 2. **Quantifiability and Controllability:** In controlled settings, the underlying Newtonian mechanics are deterministic and can be largely estimated from video data. This characteristic makes them more amenable to optimization with physics-informed neural networks and facilitates clearer quantitative checks, in contrast to more complex or chaotic phenomena like fluid dynamics or electromagnetism.
- **Driving Advancement in VGMs:** By revealing the current models' shortcomings in capturing physical principles despite their visual realism, the research aims to provide actionable insights for future development. A robust evaluation framework can guide the design of new architectures, training methodologies, and conditioning strategies that explicitly encourage or learn physically consistent behaviors.

The research specifies two primary contributions aimed at achieving these objectives:
1. **Development of Morpheus:** The introduction of a physics-informed evaluation framework that provides a suite of interpretable, physics-based metrics. This framework extracts latent state variables from generated videos and systematically evaluates them against governing ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and conservation laws.
2. **Comprehensive Evaluation of State-of-the-Art VGMs:** The application of Morpheus to a range of prominent video generative models (including both closed-source and open-source models). By conditioning these models with initial frames from a curated dataset of real-world physical phenomena, the study aims to empirically demonstrate the extent to which contemporary models succeed or fail in capturing real-world physical dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development and application of Morpheus, a physics-informed evaluation framework designed to quantify the adherence of video generative models to Newtonian dynamics. The approach comprises several interconnected stages: dataset creation, video augmentation, prompting strategies, trajectory extraction, and the definition and application of physics-informed metrics.

#### 3.1. Dataset Methodology

The foundation of Morpheus is a curated dataset of 130 real-world videos capturing nine distinct Newtonian physical phenomena. These include: falling objects, projectile motion, holonomic pendulums, double pendulums, sliding objects, rolling objects, bouncing balls, collisions, and spring-mass systems.
- **Controlled Conditions:** Videos were recorded under controlled laboratory conditions, allowing for systematic variation of initial parameters (e.g., release height, launch angle, initial velocity). This control enables targeted assessment of adherence to specific physical laws, unlike evaluations based on uncontrolled, general-purpose video data.
- **Diverse Initializations:** Each experiment was repeated 10-20 times with varying initial conditions to provide robust coverage of dynamic behaviors.
- **VGM Conditioning:** The first frame(s) or short multi-frame segments from these real-world videos are used to condition the video generative models, ensuring that generated sequences originate from the same initial states as the real experiments.

#### 3.2. Visually Diverse Conditioning for Robust VGM Evaluation

To enhance the robustness of evaluations and reduce sensitivity to specific experimental setups, the initial videos are augmented with visually diverse, yet physically consistent, variants.
- **Style Transfer:** The video-to-video transfer method, specifically COSMOS-Transfer1, is employed with object masks extracted from the original videos. This allows for semantic and appearance changes of the objects while maintaining constraint by the provided object masks.
- **Augmentation Scale:** Original initializations are expanded 10-fold by generating three variations with three distinct stylization prompts per video (or fewer for some experiments).
- **Filtering:** Augmented initializations undergo manual inspection to ensure visual and physical plausibility, filtering out artifacts or inconsistencies.

#### 3.3. Prompting Methods

The research investigates how different levels of control via prompting influence the physical realism of generated videos:
- **Textual Prompts:** Generic textual descriptions of the physical scene and motion (e.g., "A soda can rolling off a slope") are used. These are further enhanced using Vision-Language Models (e.g., ChatGLM or model-specific upsamplers) to create richer, more detailed prompts that explicitly describe expected physical dynamics.
- **Single-Image Prompts:** The first frame of an initial video is provided to the VGM, fixing the initial object location.
- **Video (Multi-Frame) Prompts:** A sequence of initial frames is provided, specifying both initial positions and velocities, offering the highest degree of control. This includes "keyframe-interpolation," where both the first and last frames are provided to constrain the generation.

#### 3.4. Trajectory Extraction

To analyze adherence to physical laws, objects' trajectories and state variables are extracted from both real-world and generated videos:
- **Object Segmentation:** Segment Anything 2 (SAM-2) is utilized for reliable, zero-shot 2D mask generation of objects. Depth Anything V2/V3 is used for depth consistency checks, verifying that objects remain at a constant distance from the camera, justifying the use of 2D trajectories.
- **Centroid Extraction:** The centroid (center of mass) of the masked object(s) is extracted for each frame, forming 2D trajectories over time.
- **Velocity and Acceleration Estimation:** The central difference method is applied to the trajectory coordinates to estimate velocity and acceleration.
- **Noise Reduction:** A series of smoothing operations, including linear regression with a sliding window and Savitzky-Golay filtering, are applied to mitigate noise introduced by imperfections in the tracking pipeline.

#### 3.5. Physics-Informed Evaluation Metrics

Two primary quantitative metrics, along with a discard rate, form the core of the Morpheus evaluation:
- **Discard Rate:** This metric quantifies the proportion of generated videos that are unusable for physical analysis. Videos are discarded if:
 - Objects disappear or lack sufficient permanence.
 - The number of objects is inconsistent (e.g., duplication).
 - Motion is minimal or absent (stillness).
 Real-world videos serve as a baseline, showing zero discard rate.
- **Dynamical Score:** This score measures the adherence of generated trajectories to the governing equations of motion.
 - **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs):** PINNs are trained on the extracted 2D trajectories. Unlike standard neural networks, PINNs incorporate the known physical laws (expressed as Ordinary Differential Equations, ODEs) directly into their loss function. For example, for a falling ball, the PINN's loss would include a term enforcing `acceleration_y + g = 0`.
 - **Score Calculation:** The score is calculated as `max(1 - Normalized Mean Square Error (NMSE), 0)`, where NMSE measures the fit of the PINN-learned trajectory to the extracted data. A higher score (closer to 1) indicates better adherence to the governing dynamics. The score is scale-invariant.
- **Physical Invariance Score:** This metric quantifies the consistency of conserved physical quantities (invariants) within a generated video.
 - **Invariant Derivation:** For each physical experiment, a list of theoretically conserved quantities is identified (e.g., total energy, energy-to-mass ratio, acceleration, and horizontal momentum-to-mass ratio for falling/projectile motion; period and pendulum length for a holonomic pendulum).
 - **Score Calculation:** The standard deviation of the time series for each invariant is calculated. This standard deviation is then normalized to a score between 0 and 1, where 1 indicates perfect invariance (zero standard deviation). The best score across a 25% sliding window of the trajectory is selected to account for transient effects. A high standard deviation signifies poor modeling of the respective invariant.

#### 3.6. Evaluation and Validation

- **Benchmarked Models:** A range of state-of-the-art video generative models, including both closed-source (Veo3, Kling) and open-source (Wan2.1, COSMOS, CogVideoX, PyramidalFlow, LTX-Video) models, were evaluated.
- **Real-World Baseline:** Real-world videos consistently achieve near-perfect Dynamical and Physical Invariance Scores (typically >0.90) and zero discard rates, serving as an upper bound for evaluation.
- **Comparison with VLMs:** Morpheus scores are compared against a state-of-the-art VLM-based evaluator (VideoPHY2-AUTOEVAL) to demonstrate the limitations of subjective assessments for precise physical evaluation.
- **Robustness Analysis:** The robustness of Morpheus scores is validated against common trajectory extraction errors (Gaussian jitter, random gaps) and ablations on PINN architecture and scoring window length.
- **Human Alignment:** A human study with three judges rating videos for physical plausibility is conducted, showing a strong correlation between Morpheus scores and human judgment for motion plausibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis using the Morpheus framework revealed several key insights regarding the physical reasoning capabilities of contemporary video generative models:

- **Significant Gap Between Visual Fidelity and Physical Fidelity:** Despite generating aesthetically pleasing and visually realistic videos, even the highest-performing models consistently fall short in capturing real-world physical dynamics. Real-world videos achieved Dynamical Scores of 0.96-0.99 and Physical Invariance Scores of ≥ 0.90 with zero discards. In contrast, the best generative models struggled to exceed a combined score of 0.66, indicating a substantial deficit in their intrinsic physical understanding.

- **Impact of Temporal Conditioning:**
 - **Keyframe-Interpolation (First and Last Frames):** Models conditioned on both the first and last frames (e.g., Wan2.1 Keyframe) achieved the highest aggregate scores (0.66) and the lowest discard rates (0.16). This suggests that providing explicit boundary conditions significantly constrains the generation space, encouraging the model to interpolate a plausible physical trajectory.
 - **Multi-Frame Extrapolation (Consecutive Frames):** Providing a few consecutive frames as context (e.g., Cosmos-Predict2 Multi) improved performance over single-frame models (0.59 vs. 0.50 average score) and reduced discard rates by approximately 5%. This temporal context helps models infer initial velocities and accelerations that are ambiguous in static images.
 - **Single-Frame Extrapolation:** This was identified as the most challenging setting, resulting in high discard rates due to static or erratic motion, even for leading closed-source models like Kling-2.5 and Veo3.

- **Model-Specific Performance and Failure Modes:**
 - **Open vs. Closed Source:** Powerful open-source models, such as Wan2.1 (with keyframe conditioning) and Cosmos-Predict2, demonstrated competitive performance, often outperforming proprietary models like the Veo3 family and Kling-2.5. This suggests that physical reasoning capabilities are influenced by architectural design and training strategies, not solely model scale or proprietary data.
 - **"Stillness" Trap:** A prevalent failure mode for models like PyramidalFlow and LTX-Video was generating videos where objects remained stationary. This resulted in high discard rates (over 45%).
 - **Hallucination and Duplication/Disappearance:** Closed-source models (Veo3, Kling-Turbo) rarely produced static videos but frequently suffered from object duplication or disappearance, particularly in complex scenarios like the double pendulum. This indicates that while these models aggressively simulate motion, they may struggle with object permanence.

- **Influence of Prompt Upsamplers:** Improvements from enhanced prompts were context-dependent. Significant performance boosts were observed for Wan2.1 (+20.8% in multi-frame) and Cosmos-Predict2 (+27.9% in single-frame), likely due to explicit language cues guiding expected dynamics. Conversely, for LTX-Video and CogVideoX, enhanced prompts sometimes led to performance degradation (up to -34%), suggesting that overly complex prompts combining visual and physical instructions can be confusing for certain models.

- **Fine-Grained Physical Failures:** Analysis per experiment category revealed specific blind spots. Pendulum experiments, involving complex constrained motion, were particularly challenging. For instance, Veo3 performed poorly in pendulum generations compared to other models. Models generally performed better on rolling and sliding tasks than on collisions or pendulums, possibly due to a richer representation of these smoother, less chaotic motions in their training data.

- **Limitations of VLM-Based Evaluators:** A comparative study with VideoPHY2-AUTOEVAL, a VLM fine-tuned for physical commonsense, demonstrated that VLM-based evaluations are insufficient for precise physical assessment. The VLM assigned only moderate scores to clean real-world recordings (Physical Commonsense score of 3.71, compared to 3.53 for generated videos) and showed no significant difference in physical rule adherence between real (58.5%) and generated (51.1%) content. Critically, the VLM sometimes "hallucinated" violations in clean real physics (e.g., assigning 0% Joint Physical Consistency to real projectiles, bouncing balls, and collisions) while rewarding generated videos of the same phenomena with comparable or higher pass rates. This indicates that VLMs perceive visual plausibility but struggle with quantitative physical consistency.

- **Metric Validation:** The Morpheus framework's metrics were validated through several means:
 - **Human Alignment:** The total Morpheus score exhibited a Spearman rank correlation of 0.708 (p < 10^-3) with averaged human ratings for physical plausibility, for videos passing the discard filter. While the Dynamical Score showed strong correlation with human judgment (0.768), the Physical Invariance Score did not significantly correlate (-0.157), reinforcing that humans perceive apparent motion but struggle to detect violations of subtle physical invariants, underscoring the need for objective, physics-informed metrics.
 - **Robustness to Noise:** The scores demonstrated robustness to trajectory-extraction noise (Gaussian jitter and random gaps), with relative deviations of total scores remaining ≤ 3%. Smoothing techniques further reduced these deviations.
 - **Consistency:** The metric proved insensitive to variations in PINN architecture and scoring-window length, preserving model rankings.

In summary, the experimental results indicate that while video generation models have achieved high visual fidelity, their ability to accurately encode and adhere to fundamental Newtonian mechanics remains limited.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant implications for the development and evaluation of video generative models, particularly in the context of their proposed role as "world models" for various advanced AI applications.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Novel Physics-Informed Evaluation Framework:** The introduction of Morpheus represents a substantial advancement in the rigorous evaluation of video generative models. By moving beyond subjective human judgment, VLM-based assessments, or superficial trajectory matching, Morpheus provides a principled, quantitative, and interpretable method to assess a model's understanding of underlying physical laws and conservation principles. This addresses a critical gap in current evaluation methodologies.
- **Quantitative Benchmarking for Physical Reasoning:** Morpheus offers a robust benchmark for comparing the physical reasoning capabilities of different video generative models. Its Dynamical and Physical Invariance scores provide objective metrics that directly probe adherence to Newtonian mechanics, offering a clearer picture of a model's true physical understanding compared to merely visual plausibility.
- **Identification of Core Weaknesses:** The findings explicitly highlight that despite impressive visual realism, contemporary video generative models consistently struggle to encode fundamental Newtonian mechanics accurately. This provides concrete evidence that current models are not yet true "world models" in the sense of physically accurate simulators, challenging optimistic claims and directing research efforts.
- **Controlled, Reproducible Assessment:** By leveraging a dataset of real-world videos captured under controlled conditions, Morpheus offers a reproducible and verifiable evaluation environment. This controlled setting is crucial for isolating and precisely testing adherence to specific physical laws, enhancing the reliability of the assessment.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Guiding Future Research in Generative AI:** Morpheus can serve as a critical tool for guiding the development of next-generation video generative models. By providing a clear, quantitative measure of physical consistency, it can help researchers identify specific architectural shortcomings, refine training objectives, and develop new methods that explicitly incorporate physical priors or learning mechanisms to improve physical reasoning.
- **Advancing Embodied AI and Robotics:** For applications in embodied AI, robotics, and autonomous systems, where reliable interaction with the physical world is paramount, a deep understanding of physics is indispensable. Morpheus offers a benchmark that can drive the development of generative models capable of generating physically plausible simulations, which is crucial for robust decision-making, planning, and control in real-world environments.
- **Enabling Reliable Scientific Simulation:** The framework's ability to quantitatively assess adherence to physical laws could pave the way for more accurate and trustworthy scientific simulations generated by AI. This could impact fields requiring high-fidelity physical modeling, such as engineering, materials science, or physics research.
- **Establishing Higher Standards for "World Models":** The research contributes to establishing more rigorous and verifiable standards for what constitutes a "world model." It emphasizes that visual realism is insufficient and that true world models must demonstrate intrinsic understanding and adherence to the laws governing physical reality. This encourages a shift in focus from purely aesthetic generation to physically grounded intelligence.
- **Dataset and Framework for the Community:** The provision of the Morpheus dataset and evaluation framework (code and data availability) fosters open science and provides a valuable resource for the broader research community to further explore and improve physics-aware generative models.

In conclusion, this work significantly advances the evaluation of video generative models by introducing a robust, physics-informed framework. Its findings underscore a fundamental limitation of current models and provide a clear roadmap and tools for developing future generative AI systems that possess a more profound and accurate understanding of the physical world.
