# Generative Skill Chaining: Long-Horizon Skill Planning with Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Generative Skill Chaining (GSC) trains a diffusion model per manipulation skill over the joint distribution of precondition state, skill parameters, and effect state, then composes these unconditional models at inference into a chain-graph score for any unseen skill skeleton. Parallel reverse diffusion, blended forward/backward by a dependency factor and steered by classifier guidance, samples long-horizon plans, matching or beating CEM search and reaching 100% on constrained packing.

## Problem

- Classical task-and-motion planning needs known system state and accurate kinodynamic models, and its exhaustive symbolic-geometric search scales poorly to long, constrained manipulation skeletons.
- Options-style meta-policies trained over primitive skills are task-specific, so they generalize poorly to skeletons and goals never seen during training.
- Existing skill-chaining models are discriminative: they only score a proposed plan's feasibility, forcing costly search, and autoregressive rollouts stay myopic with cascading error.

## Method

- Per-skill ==unconditional diffusion models== with a ==transformer (DiT-style)== score network capture the joint state-action-next-state distribution, tying skill policy and transition dynamics together consistently.
- Skeleton composition converts skills into a linear probabilistic chain graph whose score is a sum of per-skill scores minus shared-state scores, blending forward and backward flow by dependency factor gamma.
- ==Classifier-based guidance== adds a constraint likelihood term to the chain score, taking gradients on denoised samples so unseen test-time goals and geometric constraints steer parallel reverse diffusion.

## Results

- Across nine unseen TAMP skeletons of length 4-8, **GSC** matches or beats policy-CEM search, notably **0.68 versus 0.40** and **0.60 versus 0.52** on rearrangement push tasks.
- On constrained packing, guidance lifts success from 0.70/0.80/0.50 without it to **0.75/1.00/1.00**, versus 0.65/0.68/0.20 for policy CEM and 0.45/0.45/0.10 random CEM.
- Ablating the dependency factor shows gamma=0 hallucinates states inconsistent with the initial state and gamma=1 degenerates to myopic policy shooting; **gamma=0.5** with 128 diffusion steps balances both.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling a skill as a joint precondition-parameter-effect distribution, not a policy plus separate dynamics, lets independently trained modules compose into plans without joint training.
> - Generative composition replaces search: sampling every skill in parallel makes planning cost largely skeleton-length agnostic, while goal information propagates backward instead of only forward.
> - Gains presuppose a given symbolically feasible skeleton, full observability, low-dimensional object poses, and a fixed expert-trained skill library; candidate filtering by a success classifier remains necessary.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the fundamental challenge of **long-horizon manipulation planning** in robotics, a problem characterized by complex inter-step dependencies and prohibitively large search spaces. This domain is critical for enabling robots to perform intricate tasks in unstructured environments, moving beyond simple pick-and-place operations.

Historically, this problem has been approached through several avenues:

- **Task and Motion Planning (TAMP):** TAMP methods aim to jointly plan high-level symbolic task sequences and low-level continuous motions. While effective, they typically rely on complete knowledge of the system state and accurate kinodynamic models of the environment, making them difficult to deploy in real-world scenarios where such models are often unavailable or imperfect. The strong assumptions underlying TAMP limit its practical applicability and scalability.
- **Learning-based Approaches (e.g., Options Framework, Meta-Policies):** Earlier learning-based methods often trained "meta-policies" that operate over a temporally-extended action space of primitive skills. However, these methods are typically *task-specific*, meaning they generalize poorly to new tasks not encountered during training. Their applicability is limited to variations of the tasks they were explicitly trained for.
- **Discriminative Skill Chaining:** More recent advancements have focused on "skill-level models" that can be composed to solve new tasks. These "skill chaining" methods learn to assess the *feasibility* of a given plan by determining if the effects of one skill satisfy the preconditions of the next. Prominent examples include search-based strategies with CEM (Cross-Entropy Method) optimization, like STAP (Task-Agnostic Policy Sequencing). The primary limitation here is their *discriminative* nature: they can only evaluate a proposed plan, requiring an *exhaustive search process* (often auto-regressive) to find a valid sequence. This leads to significant scalability issues as task length and complexity increase, often resulting in myopic planning and cascading errors.
- **Generative Models for Planning:** The rise of generative models, particularly diffusion models, has led to their application in various planning contexts, including imitation learning and offline reinforcement learning (e.g., Diffuser, Decision Diffuser, Diffusion Policy). These models excel at synthesizing sequences of states and/or actions that resemble training data and can sometimes incorporate constraints. However, a significant gap remains: most existing generative planning approaches primarily focus on capturing the distribution of solutions for *known training tasks*. They struggle to *compose* individual skills flexibly to solve entirely *unseen new tasks* with arbitrary sequences and constraints, which is precisely what long-horizon manipulation demands.

This paper, "Generative Skill Chaining (GSC)," positions itself at the intersection of these fields. It aims to bridge the gap by proposing a *generative* and *compositional* framework that leverages the strengths of diffusion models to overcome the limitations of prior skill chaining and generative planning methods. By learning individual skill-level generative models and composing them at inference time, GSC seeks to provide a scalable, generalizable, and constraint-aware solution to long-horizon manipulation that is not tied to specific training tasks. It represents a paradigm shift from search-based, discriminative feasibility checking towards direct, parallel generation of valid skill chains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Generative Skill Chaining (GSC) is to enable robots to solve complex **long-horizon manipulation tasks** that involve intricate inter-step dependencies, efficiently and reliably, even for unseen scenarios. More specifically, the authors aim to:

1. **Develop a generative framework for skill chaining:** Move beyond discriminative approaches that merely evaluate plan feasibility, towards a method that can directly *generate* valid sequences of skill parameters and states.
2. **Ensure compositionality and generalization:** Create a system where individually learned skill models can be flexibly *composed* at inference time to form plans for *unseen task skeletons* of arbitrary lengths, without requiring explicit training on those specific long-horizon tasks. This addresses the generalization limitations of task-specific learning methods.
3. **Efficiently handle action dependencies:** Reason about the long-term effects and inter-dependencies between sequential actions, ensuring that the output of one skill aligns with the precondition of the next, and ultimately leads to the global task goal.
4. **Incorporate flexible constraint handling:** Allow for the easy integration of task-specific geometric or kinematic constraints during inference, which is crucial for practical robot operation in diverse environments.
5. **Achieve scalability:** Develop a method that can scale to increasingly complex and long skill sequences, overcoming the computational bottlenecks of exhaustive search-based planning.
6. **Demonstrate real-world applicability:** Validate the proposed framework not just in simulation, but also on a physical robot, showcasing its robustness to perturbations and its ability to replan in dynamic environments.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the shortcomings of existing approaches:

- **Computational Intractability:** Long-horizon tasks involve searching in exponentially large planning spaces, making traditional TAMP and exhaustive search methods computationally prohibitive for real-world applications.
- **Lack of Generalizability:** Many learning-based methods are task-specific, failing to generalize to new combinations of skills or entirely unseen task goals. This limits their utility in dynamic environments where novel tasks frequently arise.
- **Myopic Planning:** Auto-regressive or greedy skill chaining methods often suffer from myopia, making decisions based only on immediate success without considering long-term consequences or the final goal, leading to suboptimal or invalid plans.
- **Absence of Multimodal Solutions:** Deterministic policy priors or single-trajectory optimization methods often struggle to find diverse, multimodal solutions that might exist for a given task, potentially limiting robustness.
- **Difficulty with Constraints:** Integrating arbitrary, test-time specified constraints into planning has historically been challenging for many learning-based robotic systems.
- **Bridging Generative Models and Robotics:** While diffusion models show great promise in generating complex data, their direct application to *compositional planning for novel tasks* in robotics, especially with implicit inter-skill dependencies, remained an underexplored area.

By addressing these motivations, GSC aims to significantly advance the field of long-horizon robotic manipulation, making robots more autonomous, adaptable, and capable of solving complex problems in diverse settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Generative Skill Chaining (GSC) introduces a probabilistic framework that leverages skill-centric diffusion models to generate long-horizon manipulation plans. Its core idea revolves around training individual skill-level diffusion models and then composing their learned distributions at inference time to solve unseen tasks.

The methodology can be broken down into the following key components:

1. **Problem Formulation:**
 - GSC assumes a given *symbolically feasible skeleton* $\Phi_K = \{(\pi_1, o_1)..., (\pi_K, o_K)\}$ of skills, where $\pi_i$ is a primitive skill from a predefined library and $o_i$ are the relevant objects.
 - The goal is to find optimal continuous skill parameters $a \in \mathcal{A}_\pi$ for each skill, along with intermediate states, such that the entire sequence is geometrically feasible, satisfies skill preconditions/effects, and meets a final goal condition, potentially under additional task-specific constraints.
 - The environment state is fully observable (poses and sizes of objects), simplifying the perception aspect to focus on planning.

2. **Action Primitives as Diffusion Models:**
 - Each primitive skill $\pi$ is characterized by its state-action-state transitions: (current state $s$, skill parameters $a_\pi$, next state $s'$).
 - GSC trains an *unconditional diffusion model* for each skill $\pi$ to capture the *joint distribution* $q_\pi(s_t, a_t, s'_t)$. This joint distribution implicitly represents both the skill policy $P_\pi(a_\pi|s)$ and the transition dynamics $T_\pi(s'|s, a_\pi)$, ensuring consistency between actions and their effects.
 - The diffusion model estimates the score function $\epsilon_\pi(s_t, a_t, s'_t, t)$ using a **transformer backbone** architecture (inspired by DiT), which processes noisy state-action-state distributions at different diffusion timesteps. Skill-specific object order is also encoded as input.

3. **Sequencing Skill Diffusion Models (Probabilistic Linear Chaining):**
 - Instead of myopic, auto-regressive sequencing, GSC formulates the long-horizon problem as sampling from a joint distribution over the entire sequence of states and actions.
 - It combines the score functions of individual skill diffusion models. For a sequence of two skills, $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$, the combined score function $\epsilon_\Phi$ is derived from linearly combining forward-conditional ($q_{\pi_1}(s^{(0)}, a^{(0)}, s^{(1)}) q_{\pi_2}(a^{(1)}, s^{(2)}|s^{(1)})$) and backward-conditional ($q_{\pi_1}(s^{(0)}, a^{(0)}|s^{(1)}) q_{\pi_2}(s^{(1)}, a^{(1)}, s^{(2)})$) distributions.
 - The key innovation is the **dependency factor $\gamma \in$**, which weights the influence of the current skill's effect on the subsequent skill's precondition (forward flow) versus the next skill's precondition on the current skill's effect (backward flow). A $\gamma$ of 0.5 balances these influences, implicitly enforcing skill affordability and transition feasibility across the chain.
 - This linear chaining of score functions allows for *parallel sampling* from all individual skill models during inference, making it agnostic to both the specific task skeleton and its length.

4. **Classifier-Based Guidance for Constraint Satisfaction:**
 - GSC flexibly integrates additional planning constraints $\Psi$ (e.g., maximizing object distance, specific final state conditions) during inference.
 - This is achieved by adding a *classifier guidance term* $\epsilon_\Psi$ to the overall combined score function $\epsilon_{\Phi,\Psi} = \epsilon_\Phi + \epsilon_\Psi$.
 - The guidance term is proportional to the gradient of the log-likelihood of the constraint function $h_\Psi$ with respect to the noisy samples. Although constraints are typically defined on denoised samples, the gradients are calculated w.r.t. noisy samples after first estimating the denoised version.
 - A weight factor $\alpha$ controls the strength of the constraint guidance.

5. **Sampling and Selection:**
 - The planning process involves iteratively denoising a sequence of noisy states and actions, starting from Gaussian noise, guided by the combined score function $\epsilon_{\Phi,\Psi}$ and the specified initial/goal states.
 - Since diffusion models can produce multimodal solutions, GSC samples *multiple candidate plan sequences*.
 - To select the best plan, a separate **success probability prediction module** $Q(s, s'): S \times S \to$ is trained for each skill using both successful and failure transitions. The final plan is chosen based on maximizing the product of individual skill success probabilities.

6. **Data Collection and Real-World Deployment:**
 - Skill data (state-action-state transitions) is collected in simulation using a random agent performing successful executions for each primitive skill. Around 5000 successful transitions are collected per skill.
 - For real-world deployment, the system uses AprilTag for robust pose detection, reconstructing the real scene in a simulator. Planned skill parameters are then executed on a Franka Panda robot arm.
 - Closed-loop control involves updating the scene and potentially replanning if sub-task completion is not met or perturbations occur, demonstrating robustness.

In essence, GSC represents a significant departure from traditional search-based methods by offering a probabilistic, generative, and compositional approach that enables efficient, flexible, and generalizable long-horizon planning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted a series of experiments, both in simulation and on a real robot, to validate the efficacy, generalizability, constraint-handling capabilities, and robustness of Generative Skill Chaining (GSC).

1. **Toy Domain Validation:**
 - A 2D toy domain experiment (point navigation) served as a proof-of-concept.
 - It demonstrated how two individual skill diffusion models (e.g., "pick hook" and "push block") could be merged and sampled in parallel to generate valid sequences (e.g., red square -> bottom circle -> green square).
 - The experiment also qualitatively showed GSC's ability to satisfy constraints such as a "fixed initial state," "constrained goal region," and "fixed initial and final states," highlighting the flexibility of classifier-based guidance.

2. **Long-Horizon Manipulation in Simulation:**
 - GSC was evaluated on nine challenging TAMP tasks across three domains in a PyBullet simulator:
 - **Hook Reach:** Tasks involving using a hook to pull a box before picking and placing it.
 - **Constrained Packing:** Tasks requiring placing multiple boxes on a rack without collision.
 - **Rearrangement Push:** Tasks involving placing and pushing a box under a rack using a hook.
 - These tasks varied in skeleton length (4 to 8 skills) and demanded sophisticated long-horizon action dependency reasoning.
 - **Performance Comparison (Table 1):** GSC achieved success rates comparable to or better than state-of-the-art search-based baselines like STAP (Policy CEM) and significantly outperformed DAF (Generalization) and Random CEM. For instance, in "Rearrangement Push Task 1," GSC scored 0.68 compared to STAP's 0.40, and in "Hook Reach Task 2," GSC matched STAP at 0.84. This demonstrates GSC's strong capability in generalizing to unseen, complex tasks without specific training.

3. **Imposing Additional Constraints:**
 - Beyond the standard task goals, GSC's constraint handling was specifically tested on "Constrained Packing" by imposing an objective to *maximize the distance between all "place" skill action parameters* in the skeleton (a task-agnostic secondary objective).
 - **Performance Comparison (Table 2):** GSC *with constraint guidance* showed a notable increase in success rates compared to GSC without guidance and all baselines. For example, in "Constrained Packing Task 3," GSC with guidance achieved a perfect 1.0 success rate, while GSC without guidance scored 0.50, and STAP scored 0.20. This quantitatively and qualitatively validates GSC's ability to incorporate and satisfy novel constraints at test time, resulting in more accurate and consistent state-action sequences.

4. **Generalization to Unseen Sequences and Perturbations (Real-World Robot):**
 - GSC's generalization was further tested on a highly complex "Rearrangement Push Task 4" (an 8-skill sequence) where the goal required specific placement of both the box and the hook to enable a subsequent "push" skill, demanding an even longer dependency horizon. GSC successfully generated plans for this.
 - **Real Robot Experiment:** The framework was deployed on a Franka Panda arm in a closed-loop fashion. The robot executed GSC-generated plans, and in response to perturbations or unfulfilled sub-task conditions, the system could replan from the current state. This demonstrates GSC's robustness, adaptability, and practical applicability in dynamic physical environments.

5. **Importance of Dependency Factor $\gamma$:**
 - A qualitative study highlighted the critical role of the dependency factor $\gamma$.
 - When $\gamma=0$ (weak forward signal from initial state), the model hallucinated inconsistent object positions.
 - When $\gamma=1$ (weak backward signal from goal), the model became myopic, failing to consider long-horizon dependencies.
 - Empirically, $\gamma=0.5$ provided a balanced performance, effectively integrating both forward and backward information flow.

6. **Implementation Details:**
 - The authors provided details on their skill library (pick, place, push, pull) and their parameterization relative to object origins, ensuring clarity and reproducibility.
 - They noted that the quality of results depends on the diversity of the expert dataset and the quality of trained skill diffusion models. Sampling multiple candidate solutions and filtering them using a success probability module ($Q(s, s')$) was crucial for high success rates.

Overall, the findings strongly support GSC as an effective and versatile framework for long-horizon manipulation planning. Its generative, compositional, and constraint-aware nature allows it to surpass previous methods in generalizability and robustness, particularly for unseen complex tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Generative Skill Chaining (GSC) presents a significant advancement in long-horizon robotic manipulation, offering a novel paradigm that holds substantial promise for future robotics research and applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Generative Models and Robotics Planning:** GSC successfully integrates the powerful capabilities of diffusion models, traditionally used for image or data generation, into the complex domain of robotic planning. It demonstrates how these models can be adapted not just for imitation learning, but for compositional planning where pre-trained components are flexibly combined to solve novel problems. This opens new avenues for leveraging advanced generative AI in robotics.

2. **Enhanced Generalizability to Unseen Tasks:** One of the most critical challenges in robot learning is generalization. GSC's ability to compose individually learned skill diffusion models to solve *unseen task skeletons* of arbitrary lengths without explicit training on those full tasks is a major breakthrough. This capability directly addresses the limitations of task-specific policies, enabling robots to adapt to a much wider range of real-world scenarios and user requests.

3. **Improved Scalability for Long-Horizon Planning:** By framing long-horizon planning as a parallel sampling problem from a composite distribution, GSC effectively mitigates the scalability issues inherent in traditional search-based and auto-regressive skill chaining methods. The exponential growth of search space with task length is circumvented, making complex, multi-step tasks computationally tractable.

4. **Flexible and Intuitive Constraint Handling:** The integration of classifier-based guidance for constraint satisfaction at inference time is a highly impactful feature. It allows human operators or higher-level task planners to specify arbitrary, task-agnostic constraints (e.g., geometric preferences, safety zones, style parameters) on-the-fly, without retraining the skill models. This flexibility is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic and human-centric environments.

5. **Robustness and Real-World Applicability:** The demonstration of GSC in a closed-loop system on a physical Franka Panda robot, including its ability to replan in the face of perturbations, is vital. It validates the framework's robustness and practicality beyond simulated environments, moving closer to real-world deployment for complex manipulation tasks in factories, warehouses, or even homes.

6. **Multimodal Solution Generation:** Diffusion models inherently capture multimodal distributions. This allows GSC to generate diverse, plausible solutions for a given task, offering a significant advantage over deterministic planning methods that might get stuck in local optima or miss alternative valid plans. The use of a success probability module further refines the selection of optimal multimodal solutions.

7. **Foundation for Future TAMP Systems:** While GSC currently assumes a given task skeleton, the authors acknowledge this as a limitation. However, its modularity makes it a crucial component that can be integrated with symbolic task planners or skill discovery frameworks to create a more complete, unified Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) system. This decomposition allows researchers to focus on different aspects of the TAMP problem, accelerating progress.

In summary, GSC advances the state-of-the-art by providing a robust, scalable, and generalizable generative framework for long-horizon skill chaining. Its impact will likely be felt in applications requiring adaptable and autonomous manipulation, from industrial assembly and logistics to assistive robotics, pushing the boundaries of what robots can achieve in complex, real-world environments.
