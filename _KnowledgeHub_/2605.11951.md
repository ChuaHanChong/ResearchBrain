# From Reaction to Anticipation: Proactive Failure Recovery through Agentic Task Graph for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AgentChord is an agentic robotic-manipulation system that models a task as a directed graph, pre-augments it before execution with anticipatory recovery branches for likely failure modes, then compiles nominal and recovery transitions into low-latency compiled monitors, avoiding reactive re-planning; it raises real-world success rate to 77.5% versus 72.5% for the best baseline while cutting execution time.

## Problem

- Existing failure-recovery pipelines follow a stepwise detect-reason-recover loop that reasons only after a failure occurs, causing high latency from repeated MLLM calls.
- Backtracking-based recovery resets progress and proves unreliable in long-horizon, asynchronous-synchronous bimanual manipulation with complex coordination.
- Constraint-compiling monitors reduce VQA calls but stay tied to fixed hand-crafted constraint points, and recovery still needs full re-planning or naive backtracking.

## Method

- ==Task structuring agent== (composer) parses instruction and observation into a directed task graph with semantic sub-goal nodes and constrained motion-transition edges.
- ==Recovery orchestration agent== (arranger) predicts failure modes per edge and augments the graph with forward-moving recovery nodes/edges, enforcing shortest-path progress via a distance constraint.
- ==Execution compilation agent== (conductor) compiles nominal and recovery transitions into interruptible programs via hierarchical constrained solvers and compiled monitors that trigger pre-compiled recovery without re-invoking the MLLM.

## Results

- On three simulated bimanual tasks with disturbances, **AgentChord** reaches **99.2%** average success rate versus **97.5%** for Code-as-Monitor and **92.5%** for ReKep, with lowest execution time (**41.5s** vs **78.1s**).
- On six real-world tasks, AgentChord attains **77.5%** average success rate and **92.2s** execution time, beating Code-as-Monitor (**72.5%**, 130.9s) and ReKep (**65.0%**, 107.1s).
- Fine-tuning Sim2Real-VLA with half AgentChord-generated recovery trajectories raises success from **26/50** to **39/50** on a cup-shift recovery scenario, versus RDT (**16/50**) and pi0 (**20/50**) baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anticipating failure and pre-compiling recovery before execution removes the latency penalty of reactive detect-reason-recover loops without sacrificing accuracy.
> - Constraining recovery to be forward-moving in graph distance avoids the regressive resets that make simple backtracking unreliable for long-horizon bimanual tasks.
> - Failure-recovery trajectories generated by an anticipatory planner transfer as useful training data, letting a downstream VLA policy learn recovery behavior directly.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has advanced from structured industrial applications to complex tasks in dynamic and unstructured environments. Despite this progress, achieving reliable execution remains challenging due to the inevitability of task failures. Consequently, the development of robust automatic failure detection and recovery mechanisms is considered essential for autonomous robotic systems.

Previous research in failure handling typically employs a "detect-reason-recover" pipeline. Recent studies have incorporated Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for failure detection, often framing it as a visual question answering (VQA) problem. Subsequent recovery strategies generally involve either synthesizing corrective actions from predefined skill libraries or generating language-based instructions for language-conditioned models. However, these MLLM-centric approaches often face limitations: slow inference due to computational demands and API-call latency, and imprecise grounding where language-based instructions may lack the detail required for precise robotic control (e.g., exact object positions or spatial relationships).

To mitigate the computational cost and imprecision of frequent MLLM calls, some frameworks, such as ReKep and Code-as-Monitor, utilize MLLMs to extract key constraints and compile them into executable monitoring code. This enables more efficient real-time monitoring by minimizing repeated VQA calls. Nevertheless, the generated monitoring code can be limited to a predefined set of error types. Furthermore, the underlying failure recovery strategies in these methods often involve re-executing the entire task pipeline or resorting to simple backtracking. These reactive approaches can result in high latency, increased computational cost, loss of progress, and reduced reliability, particularly in complex scenarios like bimanual manipulation.

This work draws inspiration from human cognitive capabilities, where individuals often anticipate potential failures and formulate contingency plans proactively. This human-like foresight enables rapid and targeted recovery actions before minor deviations escalate into severe task failures. Within the broader context of agentic systems, where multiple foundation models are coordinated as distinct agents for perception, planning, and execution, existing approaches have not fully integrated efficient and proactive failure anticipation and recovery. This paper introduces a system designed to address these limitations by developing a cohesive agentic framework that supports anticipatory failure handling, aiming to enhance the robustness and efficiency of robotic manipulation systems beyond current reactive paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the persistent challenge of achieving reliable robotic manipulation in dynamic and unstructured environments, where task failures are inherent. Current reactive failure recovery frameworks, which typically follow a stepwise "detect-reason-recover" pipeline, are identified as inefficient and unreliable. These existing methods often suffer from high latency and limited robustness due to the delays associated with reasoning about failures and subsequently planning recovery actions after a failure has already occurred. This reactive paradigm frequently leads to substantial setbacks, such as backtracking to previous states, re-planning entire task segments, or even complete task failure.

Inspired by the human capacity to anticipate potential failures and proactively formulate contingency plans, the core objective of this work is to develop a robotic system that integrates this anticipatory capability. The aim is to enable robots to predict likely failure modes before execution and embed specific, context-aware recovery behaviors directly into their task plan.

Specifically, the key objectives for the proposed AgentChord system are:
1. **Proactive Failure Anticipation**: To develop a mechanism that can predict probable failure modes for various stages of a manipulation task *before* execution begins.
2. **Integrated Framework**: To establish a unified system that tightly couples task structuring, failure anticipation, detection, and recovery within a single, coherent framework.
3. **Recovery-Augmented Task Representation**: To model manipulation tasks as directed graphs, where nominal task progression is augmented with anticipatory recovery branches. These branches define corrective actions for specific failure modes.
4. **Low-Latency, Targeted Recovery**: To enable immediate and precise responses to detected failures by transitioning directly to pre-compiled recovery behaviors. This approach aims to circumvent the high latency and computational cost associated with on-the-fly re-planning or full pipeline re-invocation.
5. **Forward-Moving Recovery**: To ensure that recovery actions contribute to continuous task progress, preventing regressive resets or backtracking that would move the robot further away from the final goal.
6. **Enhanced Reliability and Efficiency**: To significantly improve the success rates and execution efficiency of robotic systems, particularly in complex, long-horizon, and bimanual manipulation tasks, in both simulated and real-world environments.
7. **Effective Foundation Model Utilization**: To demonstrate a more efficient use of foundation models by leveraging them for upfront reasoning (task graph construction and recovery branch generation) rather than for repeated, real-time reactive decision-making.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to advance the autonomy and dependability of robotic systems in practical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AgentChord is structured as a choreographed agentic system, comprising three specialized agents: a Task Structuring Agent (Composer), a Recovery Orchestration Agent (Arranger), and an Execution Compilation Agent (Conductor). The system interfaces with perception modules, a library of atomic manipulation skills, and multimodal foundation models (MLLMs).

**System Interfaces and Feature Representation:**
- **Functional Tools**: The system integrates `F_perc` (perception tools like SAM3 for open-vocabulary segmentation, AnyGrasp for grasp pose generation, FoundationPose for 6D pose estimation), `F_action` (an action library of atomic skills like grasp, move, rotate), and `F_mllm` (model-call tools for MLLMs like GPT or Gemini for reasoning).
- **Perception Modules**: Given RGB-D observations `o_t`, `F_perc` generates object masks `m_k_t` and object-level point clouds `P_k_t` for target objects, and provides 6-DoF grasp poses.
- **Robot State**: The system continuously monitors the robot's joint configuration `q_t` and gripper state `g_t`.
- **Geometric Feature Extraction**: A task-dependent feature vector `z_t = Φ(ˆx_t, ξ_t)` is extracted from perception outputs `ˆx_t` and robot states `ξ_t`. This includes object centroids, boundary points, principal orientation axes (via PCA), fractional object locations, gripper opening width, and open/closed states. This feature representation is flexible and can leverage various forms of perceptual and proprioceptive signals.

**Directed Graph for Task Execution (Composer):**
- The Task Structuring Agent (Composer) interprets a high-level task instruction `I` and an initial observation `o_0` to synthesize a directed task graph `G = (V, E)` using `F_mllm`.
- **Nodes `v_i ∈ V`**: Represent semantic sub-goals. Each node is associated with sub-goal constraints `C_i_sub` that define successful completion conditions (e.g., object in a specific location).
- **Edges `ε_i→j ∈ E`**: Represent motion transitions between sub-goals. Each edge is associated with path constraints `C_i→j_path` that must remain valid during the transition (e.g., collision avoidance, object stability).
- **Constraints**: Both sub-goal and path constraints are expressed as inequality functions `c(z) ≤ 0`, enabling online evaluation using the feature vector `z_t`.

**Proactive Failure Anticipation and Recovery (Arranger):**
- The Recovery Orchestration Agent (Arranger) augments the nominal task graph `G` before execution. It invokes `F_mllm` to analyze each node and its outgoing edges, predicting a set of likely failure modes `F_i→j` for each nominal edge `ε_i→j`.
- **Graph Augmentation**: For each predicted failure mode `f_i→j_m`, the agent defines a recovery node `v_rec(i,j)_m` and specific recovery edges. These edges route execution from the failure context (e.g., during `ε_i→j`) to the recovery node and then merge back into a feasible downstream node `v_k` in the nominal task graph. The augmented graph is `G_aug = (V ∪ V_rec, E ∪ E_rec)`.
- **Forward-Moving Recovery Principle**: A crucial aspect is that recovery branches are constrained to be "forward-moving". This means that triggering a recovery action for a failure during an edge `ε_i→j` (with `v_fail = v_i` being the last completed sub-goal) must not increase the remaining graph-theoretic distance to the terminal goal `v_N`. Formally, `Dist(v_rec(i,j)_m, v_N) ≤ Dist(v_fail, v_N)`. This principle prevents regressive behavior and ensures continuous progress.

**Graph Compilation via Hierarchical Constrained Solvers (Conductor):**
- The Execution Compilation Agent (Conductor) transforms the `G_aug` into executable programs.
- **Node Synthesis (Sub-goal Realization)**: For each node `v_i`, a target end-effector pose `e_i` is computed by solving a constrained optimization problem. This problem minimizes an objective function `λ_i_sub(e; ˆx_i)` (including collision avoidance, reachability, pose regularization, bimanual coordination) subject to `C_i_sub`. This is implemented using a hybrid global-local optimization strategy.
- **Edge Synthesis (Transition Realization)**: For each edge `ε_i→j` (nominal or recovery), a constraint-aware transition strategy drives the robot from `e_i` to `e_j` while satisfying `C_i→j_path`. This is achieved using a receding-horizon refinement approach. At each control step `t`, an optimization problem is solved over a future horizon `H` to plan an end-effector pose sequence `e_t:t+H`, subject to `C_i→j_path`. Only the first `M` commands are executed before replanning, adapting to the latest `ˆx_t`. The objective `λ_i→j_path` includes progress-to-goal, smoothness, and safety margins.
- **Compiled Monitors and Triggers**: Each anticipated failure mode `f_i→j_m` is represented as an executable scalar function `f_i→j_m(z_t) ≤ 0`. During execution, AgentChord continuously evaluates these functions. A monitor `M_i→j_m(t)` triggers recovery only if `f_i→j_m(z_t') > ε` (indicating a violation) persists for a specified window `K`, preventing spurious activations from noise. Upon trigger, execution immediately switches to the pre-compiled recovery edge `ε_i→rec(i,j,m)`.

**Agentic Coordination and Execution Workflow:**
- The Composer builds the nominal graph.
- The Arranger augments it with anticipatory recovery branches and corresponding monitoring functions.
- The Conductor compiles the entire `G_aug` into executable actions and low-latency monitors.
- During online execution, AgentChord traverses `G_aug`, executing the active edge program. If a monitor triggers, the system instantly switches to the pre-compiled recovery branch; otherwise, it proceeds to the next node upon sub-goal completion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of AgentChord was conducted on diverse long-horizon bimanual manipulation tasks in both simulated and real-world environments, comparing its performance against four baseline methods: Inner Monologue (IM), DoReMi (DRM), ReKep, and Code-as-Monitor (CaM).

**Performance in Simulated Manipulation Tasks (Table I):**
- **Tasks**: Single-arm pour water, dual-arm pour water, and rearrange table were evaluated with stochastic object dropping disturbances (probabilities `p=0.05` and `p=0.10`).
- **Success Rate**: AgentChord achieved the highest average success rate of 99.2%, consistently outperforming baselines (IM: 79.2%, DRM: 92.5%, ReKep: 92.5%, CaM: 97.5%). For several tasks, AgentChord reached 100% success.
- **Execution Efficiency**: AgentChord demonstrated superior efficiency with the lowest average execution time (41.5s) and fewest average episode steps (352). In contrast, IM averaged 105.9s, DRM 54.4s, ReKep 78.1s, and CaM 78.1s, with corresponding higher step counts.
- **Baseline Analysis**: ReKep showed limitations in dual-arm tasks, with simple backtracking proving insufficient for complex coordinated actions. CaM achieved competitive step counts but incurred longer execution times due to repeated MLLM invocations for reactive planning.

**Performance in Real-world Manipulation Tasks (Table II and Fig. 4, 5):**
- **Tasks**: Six tasks were evaluated: single-arm pour water, dual-arm pour water, rearrange table, handover block, fold towel, and setup coffee tray. External disturbances were explicitly introduced (e.g., perturbing objects, forcibly removing them).
- **Success Rate**: AgentChord maintained the highest average success rate of 77.5%, exceeding baselines (IM: 59.2%, DRM: 66.7%, ReKep: 65.0%, CaM: 72.5%).
- **Execution Efficiency**: AgentChord also exhibited the shortest average execution time (92.2s), demonstrating its efficiency in real-world scenarios compared to baselines (IM: 143.5s, DRM: 107.1s, CaM: 130.9s).
- **Qualitative Observations**: Visualizations (Fig. 4) illustrate AgentChord's ability to respond to compiled failure-monitoring functions by transitioning to corresponding pre-compiled recovery plans, even under varied failure types. Stress tests (Fig. 5) confirmed the system's robustness under frequent perturbations.

**Deep Analysis of Failure Cases (Section IV-D):**
The analysis identified three primary sources of AgentChord's failures in real-world experiments:
1. **Reasoning Limitations**: In long-horizon tasks, the recovery orchestration agent, powered by MLLMs, occasionally failed to anticipate rare or compound failure modes, or incorrectly selected a recovery target node within the graph.
2. **Inverse Kinematics (IK) Limitations**: Even with a sound recovery plan, the IK solver sometimes failed to find a kinematically feasible solution, particularly when attempting to re-grasp objects in unfavorable or challenging orientations.
3. **Perception and Execution Compilation Errors**: Inaccurate object masks, noisy point clouds, or incorrect feature extraction led to imprecise pose estimates. Additionally, the compilation agent sometimes invoked inappropriate function tools for monitoring, causing mismatches between intended recovery logic and actual checks.

**Leveraging Generated Data for Policy Learning (Table III):**
- An experiment was conducted on the simulated single-arm pour water task, specifically focusing on recovering from a shifted cup position.
- **Methodology**: A baseline policy, Sim2Real-VLA, was fine-tuned with 40 successful trajectories. A variant, Sim2Real-VLA (rec), used 20 successful trajectories and 20 AgentChord-generated failure-recoverable trajectories.
- **Results**: Sim2Real-VLA (rec) achieved 39 out of 50 successes, a notable improvement over the baseline Sim2Real-VLA which achieved 26 out of 50 successes.
- **Implication**: This finding suggests that incorporating failure-recovery behaviors generated by AgentChord into training data can enhance the robustness and recovery capabilities of learned policies.

**Ablation Study on Forward-Moving Recovery Graph (Table V):**
- A comparison between AgentChord and AgentChord-BT (a backtracking variant without pre-compiled recovery nodes/edges) demonstrated the benefit of the proactive, forward-moving recovery paradigm.
- AgentChord achieved higher success rates (86.7%) and lower execution times (105.6s) than AgentChord-BT (73.3% success, 128.4s execution) and ReKep (68.3% success, 122.4s execution). This indicates that while compiled monitoring improves performance, pre-compiled forward-moving recovery branches are more efficient and effective than simple backtracking.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents AgentChord, a framework that advances robotic manipulation by introducing a paradigm shift from reactive failure recovery to proactive failure anticipation and planning. This approach holds several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Robotic Reliability and Autonomy**: By enabling robots to foresee potential failures and pre-plan corrective actions, AgentChord contributes to a substantial increase in task success rates and overall system reliability. This moves robots closer to autonomous operation in complex, dynamic, and unstructured real-world environments where failures are inevitable.
2. **Improved Efficiency**: The proactive nature of AgentChord, coupled with pre-compiled recovery branches and low-latency monitors, significantly reduces execution time. This is achieved by minimizing the need for costly, real-time re-planning or full task re-invocation, which are common drawbacks of reactive systems. This efficiency is critical for time-sensitive applications and for reducing operational costs.
3. **Human-Inspired Robotics**: The design principle of anticipating failures is inspired by human cognitive capabilities. Emulating this aspect brings robotic manipulation closer to human-level dexterity and adaptability, potentially leading to more intuitive and robust robot behaviors.
4. **Structured and Interpretable Task Representation**: The recovery-augmented task graph provides a clear, structured, and interpretable representation of a manipulation task. This graph-based approach can simplify debugging, allow for easier modification of task logic, and facilitate the integration of various task constraints and recovery strategies.
5. **Effective Utilization of Foundation Models**: AgentChord demonstrates a method for leveraging the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs more effectively. Instead of repeatedly querying MLLMs for reactive decisions during execution, the framework uses them for upfront, comprehensive reasoning during task structuring and failure anticipation, thereby optimizing their use and reducing latency.
6. **Bridging Symbolic Planning and Learned Control**: The finding that AgentChord-generated failure-recovery trajectories can improve the robustness of learned policies is significant. This opens a pathway for automatically generating valuable training data that imbues learned behaviors with failure recovery capabilities, potentially accelerating the development of more resilient robot learning systems.
7. **Generalizability Across Complex Tasks**: The evaluation on diverse single-arm and bimanual manipulation tasks, including those involving deformable objects and cluttered environments, suggests the framework's generalizability. This indicates its potential applicability to a wide range of real-world robotic challenges.
8. **Future Research Directions**: The identified limitations, such as MLLM reasoning gaps for rare failures, IK feasibility issues, and perception robustness, provide clear directions for future research. This iterative improvement process can further enhance the capabilities of anticipatory recovery systems.

In summary, AgentChord represents a step towards more intelligent, efficient, and robust robotic manipulation by integrating anticipatory planning into a structured agentic framework. Its impact could be observed in industrial automation, service robotics, and other domains requiring high-dexterity and reliable task execution in dynamic settings.
