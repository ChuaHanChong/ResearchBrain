# VLA-Pro: Cross-Task Procedural Memory Transfer for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-Pro, from Fudan University and collaborators, introduces a framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to transfer procedural memory across tasks by storing manipulation experiences as task-specific LoRA adapters and dynamically fusing them at inference. This approach leads to substantial improvements in cross-task generalization, achieving up to a 207% relative increase in success rate on simulated benchmarks and elevating real-world average success from 5.8% to 65.0%.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with cross-task generalization, often overfitting to training data and failing on unseen tasks or objects.
- Existing architectural or prompt-based approaches for VLA adaptability do not fully internalize procedural experience into dynamically retrievable model parameters.
- Current VLA models often exhibit execution biases towards frequently observed actions, leading to incorrect behavior in novel, specific contexts.

## Method

- **VLA-Pro** stores task-specific procedural memories as compact ==LoRA== adapters, trained individually on seen tasks, after an initial shared base adapter captures general manipulation knowledge.
- It abstracts execution stages into structured procedural states (action type, object property, end-effector orientation, interaction point) from multimodal inputs for enhanced memory indexing and retrieval.
- During inference, the framework dynamically retrieves the most relevant procedural memories using action-aware matching, then fuses these LoRA adapters to adapt the base VLA model for the current action chunk.

## Results

- VLA-Pro boosted average success rates on unseen tasks in the RoboTwin benchmark by up to **207%** (RDT backbone) and **47%** (π0.5 backbone) and improved zero-shot performance on RLBench by **7.1** percentage points over the π0.5 baseline.
- In real-world robotic manipulation, the framework increased the average success rate from **5.8%** to **65.0%** across six held-out tasks using the π0.5 backbone.
- Analysis showed a positive correlation between procedural state extraction accuracy and physical execution success, and identified k=2 retrieved memories as optimal for overall performance on RLBench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parameterizing procedural memory as lightweight, task-specific LoRA adapters allows for efficient storage and dynamic transfer of manipulation experiences.
> - Structured procedural states provide a robust and precise mechanism for memory retrieval, enabling VLA models to identify and apply highly relevant past experiences.
> - Dynamically fusing retrieved memories based on the current context and action type allows the base VLA policy to adapt to novel tasks and mitigate execution biases without permanent model modification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant area of advancement in embodied intelligence and robotics, aiming to integrate multimodal perception, linguistic reasoning, and motor control into unified frameworks. Models such as π0, π0.5, OpenVLA, and X-VLA have demonstrated capabilities in executing complex tasks and exhibiting robustness within their training distributions. They achieve this by encoding visual observations and natural language instructions into shared representation spaces, leveraging large-scale pre-training to map semantic cues to action outputs.

Despite these advancements, a critical challenge for VLA models is cross-task generalization. Existing models frequently exhibit overfitting to specific trajectory distributions and operational patterns encountered during training, which limits their ability to adapt to novel scenarios involving unseen tasks, objects, or unfamiliar scenes without extensive fine-tuning. This limitation prevents them from achieving the broad adaptability observed in biological systems.

The current work is situated within efforts to enhance VLA adaptability, broadly categorized into architectural restructuring and contextual prompting. Architecture-based approaches, exemplified by AtomicVLA's skill-guided Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) or MemoryVLA and Chameleon's perceptual-cognitive mechanisms, aim to encode reusable execution priors or manage temporal dependencies through predefined structural modules. Prompt-based approaches, such as X-ICM, leverage in-context demonstrations by appending textualized examples from seen tasks to aid generalization for relevant unseen tasks.

However, the authors identify a gap in these existing methods: they often rely on fixed structural priors or transient contextual conditioning and do not fully internalize procedural experience into dynamically retrievable and executable model parameters. The proposed VLA-Pro framework addresses this by seeking to imbue VLA models with a mechanism analogous to human procedural memory, enabling the implicit transfer of past manipulation experiences to novel tasks through adaptable model parameters. This approach aims to bridge the gap between high-level procedural recall and fine-grained motor adaptation by parameterizing cross-task experiences as internalizable execution weights.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the recognized limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly their struggles with cross-task generalization. While these models have shown proficiency in tasks similar to their training data, they often fail to adapt to unseen tasks, even those that share semantic or physical similarities with learned ones. This deficiency contrasts with human cognitive abilities, where procedural memory plays a role in enabling flexible adaptation to novel situations by implicitly recalling and applying past experiences. For example, humans can adapt their grip and orientation for a lying bottle by recalling experiences with other cylindrical lying objects. Furthermore, this mechanism can counteract execution biases towards frequently observed actions, ensuring correct behavior in specific novel contexts.

Inspired by these human memory mechanisms, the key objective of this work is to develop a framework, named VLA-Pro, that enhances VLA models with a similar capability for transferring procedural memory. The framework aims to improve cross-task generalization by storing task-relevant manipulation experiences during training and dynamically transferring these memories during inference.

More specifically, the objectives include:
- **Parameterizing Procedural Memory:** To represent task-specific procedural experiences in a format that can be stored and retrieved, specifically by training and storing task-specific LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) adapters. These adapters are designed to encapsulate the procedural knowledge for individual tasks.
- **Structured State Representation:** To abstract execution stages into structured procedural states from multimodal inputs (visual observations, language instructions, interaction history). This structured representation is intended to make memory more indexable and retrievable, moving beyond raw observations or free-form language descriptions.
- **Dynamic Retrieval and Fusion:** To enable the VLA model to retrieve the most relevant procedural memories from a memory bank at inference time, based on the current multimodal context. These retrieved memories (LoRA adapters) are then to be dynamically fused and applied to the base VLA backbone to generate actions appropriate for the current action chunk. This process aims to allow the policy to reuse relevant manipulation patterns while mitigating interference from unrelated task behaviors.
- **Cross-Backbone Generalization:** To demonstrate that the proposed VLA-Pro framework is a plug-and-play solution that can enhance cross-task generalization across various existing VLA backbone models.

By achieving these objectives, the researchers aim to provide an effective mechanism for transferring manipulation experience to novel tasks, thereby improving the specialization capability of general-purpose policies while preserving modularity and execution stability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-Pro is introduced as a plug-and-play framework designed to facilitate cross-task procedural memory transfer in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The core idea involves adapting relevant procedural memory based on the current execution state, enabling improved generalization to unseen tasks. The methodology is divided into three main components: Procedural Memory Storage, Cross-Task Memory Retrieval, and Memory Merging and Adaptation.

#### 3.1. Procedural Memory Storage

In VLA-Pro, each procedural memory $M_i$ for a seen task $T_i$ is defined as a tuple $(D_i, \Delta\theta_i)$. Here, $D_i = \{z^{(1)}_i, z^{(2)}_i, \dots, z^{(m_i)}_i\}$ represents a sequence of structured procedural states extracted from task $T_i$, and $\Delta\theta_i$ is the task-specific LoRA adapter associated with that task.
- **Structured Procedural State:** Instead of utilizing raw observations or free-form language, each execution stage is abstracted into a compact, structured procedural state $z = \{a, o, e, p\}$. These fields correspond to the current action type, object-related geometric property, end-effector orientation, and target interaction point, respectively. This representation aims to enhance the indexability and retrievability of procedural experience.
- **LoRA Adapter Preparation:** Initially, a shared base LoRA adapter, $\Delta\theta_{\text{base}}$, is fine-tuned jointly on all seen tasks. This base adapter captures general manipulation knowledge. Subsequently, for each individual task $T_i$, a separate task-specific adapter, $\Delta\theta_i$, is fine-tuned starting from $\Delta\theta_{\text{base}}$ using demonstrations specific to $T_i$. These $\Delta\theta_i$ adapters constitute the parameterized procedural memories.

#### 3.2. Cross-Task Memory Retrieval

The objective of this stage is to identify the most relevant procedural memories from the constructed memory bank for the current execution stage of an unseen task. Retrieval is performed over structured procedural states, which is intended to allow for more precise memory addressing.
- **Current Procedural State Extraction:** Before generating an action chunk, a procedural state extractor $E(\cdot)$ (implemented using a vision-language model like Gemini-3-Flash) processes the current visual observation ($O_t$), the full task instruction ($l$), and the interaction history ($H_{<t}$) to infer the current execution stage, yielding the query state $\hat{z}_t$. The interaction history helps capture the temporal order of subtasks. The extraction is guided by a structured schema consistent with source memories.
- **Task-Wise Memory Retrieval with Action-Aware Procedural Matching:** Each source memory is indexed by its procedural state sequence $D_i$. The relevance between the current query state $\hat{z}_t$ and a source memory $M_i$ is determined by the maximum similarity between $\hat{z}_t$ and any state $z^{(m)}_i$ within $D_i$: $s_i = \max_{m} \text{Sim}(\hat{z}_t, z^{(m)}_i)$. For efficiency, textual procedural states are pre-encoded.
 An action-aware matching mechanism is employed, where the similarity $\text{Sim}(\hat{z}_t, z^{(m)}_i)$ is computed as a weighted sum of similarities across individual fields: $\sum_{f \in \{a,o,e,p\}} w_f(\hat{a}_t) \text{Sim}_f(\hat{z}_{t,f}, z^{(m)}_{i,f})$. The weights $w_f(\hat{a}_t)$ are conditioned on the action field $\hat{a}_t$, allowing different action categories to emphasize particular procedural attributes (e.g., "pick" actions might emphasize object geometry).
 After computing similarities for all source memories, the top-k most relevant memories are retrieved, denoted by their index set $K_t$.

#### 3.3. Memory Merging and Adaptation

The retrieved top-k procedural memories are then composed to adapt the base VLA model for the current execution stage.
- **Fusion Coefficients:** The similarity scores of the retrieved tasks are normalized using a Softmax function to obtain fusion weights $\{\alpha_j\}_{j \in K_t}$. These coefficients indicate the relative contribution of each retrieved memory.
- **Fused Adapter:** The corresponding task-specific LoRA adapters are then merged through a parameter-wise weighted summation: $\Delta\theta^{\text{fused}}_t = \sum_{j \in K_t} \alpha_j \Delta\theta_j$. This results in a single adapter tailored to the current execution stage.
- **Dynamic Adaptation:** The fused adapter $\Delta\theta^{\text{fused}}_t$ is loaded onto the pretrained VLA model parameters to form a temporary policy for the current action chunk. Upon completion of the chunk, the fused adapter is unloaded, and the model reverts to its original base state. This process is repeated for subsequent execution stages with updated interaction history. This preserves modularity and avoids permanent modification of the base model.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

Experiments were conducted on three platforms:
- **Simulated Benchmarks:** RoboTwin (customized with 8 training and 9 held-out test tasks) and RLBench (using the X-ICM task split with 18 training and 23 zero-shot test tasks, 8 of which are selected as source memories).
- **Real-world:** A UR7e robot arm with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper and RealSense D405 camera, involving 6 training tasks and 6 held-out testing tasks.
A strict separation of training (seen) and testing (unseen) tasks was maintained to assess cross-task generalization. VLA-Pro was evaluated across different backbones, including X-VLA, RDT, and π0.5. Gemini-3-Flash was used for procedural state extraction, and OpenAI’s text-embedding-3-large for Action-Aware Procedural Matching. For RoboTwin, k=1 was used for retrieval, while k=2 was used for RLBench.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to address four key research questions regarding VLA-Pro's effectiveness, the roles of its components, retrieval accuracy, and the impact of the number of retrieved memories.

#### 4.1. Overall Results (RQ1: Effectiveness on unseen tasks)

- **RoboTwin Benchmark:** VLA-Pro demonstrated consistent performance improvement across all three evaluated VLA backbones.
 - With X-VLA as the backbone, the average success rate increased from 17.0% to 30.0% (a 76% relative improvement).
 - With RDT, the average success rate improved from 11.1% to 34.1% (a 207% relative improvement).
 - With π0.5, the average success rate rose from 40.4% to 59.3% (a 47% relative improvement).
 These results indicate that the procedural memory mechanism enhances cross-task generalization and is compatible with different VLA architectures. For specific tasks like `place_bell_behind`, the π0.5 baseline achieved 0.0% success, whereas VLA-Pro achieved 100.0%, suggesting its ability to mitigate execution biases towards dominant, high-frequency actions and activate correct spatial-relation memories.

- **RLBench Benchmark:** VLA-Pro, built upon the π0.5 backbone, achieved the highest average success rate of 20.9% across 9 zero-shot tasks. This represented an improvement of 10.7 percentage points over RDT, 6.2 percentage points over AtomicVLA, and 7.1 percentage points over the π0.5 baseline. This demonstrates that the framework can leverage task-specific procedural experiences to improve zero-shot performance even with a rich base LoRA adapter.

- **Real-world Experiments:** VLA-Pro substantially improved cross-task generalization in real-world manipulation scenarios. Using π0.5 as the backbone, the average success rate increased from 5.8% to 65.0% over six held-out tasks. Qualitative examples illustrated VLA-Pro's ability to correct dominant placement patterns (e.g., `place bottle box right`) and transfer object-level patterns (e.g., `tap the chips can`, `place mic on box`).

#### 4.2. Procedural Memory Parameter Integration (RQ2: Roles of parameter components)

An analysis on selected RoboTwin pick-and-place tasks investigated the contributions of VLM-backbone and action-expert components to memory transfer.
- On **seen tasks**, adapting both VLM-backbone and action-expert components together yielded the strongest performance, suggesting that joint adaptation improves task-specific behavior precision.
- On **unseen tasks**, generalization gains were task-dependent. For tasks highly similar to training patterns (e.g., `pick up upright bottle`), adapting the entire parameter set through the action-expert was more effective. However, for tasks requiring substantial zero-shot generalization (e.g., `pick up cup` and `apple`), VLM-backbone adaptation proved more effective. This implies that the VLM-backbone contributes more to high-level semantic and physical reasoning in novel scenarios.
- **Runtime Analysis:** VLA-Pro introduced relatively small runtime overhead. By caching text embeddings and reusing loaded LoRA adapters, the framework maintained efficiency, with its latency being minor compared to the total action chunk generation and execution time.

#### 4.3. Retrieval Effectiveness Analysis (RQ3: Impact of retrieval accuracy)

- **Impact of Retrievers:** Evaluation of different retrievers (Gemini-3-Flash and Qwen2.5-VL-7B) showed that Gemini-3-Flash achieved a higher Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) for procedural state extraction, which correlated with higher physical execution success rates. This indicates that more accurate retrieval leads to better task performance.
- **Impact of Task Similarity:** A positive correlation was observed between the similarity score of an unseen task to its corresponding source memories and the performance gains achieved by VLA-Pro. Tasks with higher similarity generally exhibited larger relative success improvements, indicating that more relevant source memories provide more transferable procedural experience.

#### 4.4. Effect of Top-k Memory Merging (RQ4: Influence of number of retrieved memories)

Experiments on RLBench tasks investigated the impact of the number of retrieved procedural memories (k).
- VLA-Pro with k=2 achieved the optimal average success rate of 20.9%, which was a 51.4% improvement over the π0.5 baseline (13.8%).
- Configurations with k=1 and k=3 yielded slightly lower average success rates (16.9% and 16.4% respectively). This suggests that effectively transferring knowledge often benefits from integrating insights from a proper number of related experiences, rather than relying solely on a single source or an excessively large set.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLA-Pro framework contributes to the field of embodied artificial intelligence by addressing a persistent challenge in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: generalizing to unseen tasks. The significance of this work lies in its proposal and empirical validation of a novel mechanism for cross-task procedural memory transfer.

The core impact is derived from several key aspects:
- **Enhanced Cross-Task Generalization:** VLA-Pro systematically demonstrates improved generalization capabilities on novel tasks across diverse simulated benchmarks (RoboTwin, RLBench) and real-world robotic manipulation scenarios. The reported success rate improvements, particularly the substantial gains in simulation (up to 207% relative improvement) and real-world settings (from 5.8% to 65.0% success rate), indicate a practical advancement in robotic adaptability.
- **Procedural Memory Mechanism:** The introduction of structured procedural states for memory representation, combined with the use of lightweight LoRA adapters for storing task-specific experiences, provides a modular and efficient way to parameterize and internalize manipulation knowledge. This approach moves beyond purely architectural or prompt-based methods by making "executable" memories directly retrievable and adaptable.
- **Action-Aware Retrieval and Dynamic Fusion:** The framework's ability to dynamically retrieve and fuse relevant procedural memories based on the current multimodal context and action-specific requirements allows for flexible adaptation. This mechanism helps to mitigate common issues in VLA models, such as overfitting to training trajectory distributions and execution biases toward high-frequency actions, leading to more stable and context-appropriate decision-making.
- **Backbone Agnosticism:** The consistent performance improvements observed across different VLA backbones (X-VLA, RDT, and π0.5) suggest that VLA-Pro is a plug-and-play framework, indicating broad applicability and potential for integration with future VLA model architectures.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** The use of LoRA adapters, combined with caching of text embeddings and efficient online adaptation, ensures minimal computational overhead and negligible latency. This makes the framework suitable for real-time robotic control, addressing practical deployment considerations.

The potential impact of VLA-Pro extends to developing more versatile and robust robotic systems capable of operating in dynamic and unstructured environments. By enabling robots to implicitly leverage past experiences for novel tasks, the framework moves towards more general-purpose robot manipulation policies.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the dependence on procedural state extraction quality and the restricted size of current memory banks. Future work, as outlined by the authors, intends to explore more robust state extraction, continuous memory expansion, and more efficient fusion strategies. Addressing these areas could further enhance VLA-Pro's capabilities, potentially enabling VLA models to approach human-like memory utilization for complex, long-horizon, and highly dynamic real-world manipulation tasks.
