# FLEX: Full-Body Grasping Without Full-Body Grasps

> [!summary] Summary
> FLEX generates full-body 3D humans grasping objects in cluttered scenes without any full-body grasping data. It composes a frozen hand-grasp prior (GrabNet) and a body-pose prior (VPoser) by optimizing their latent codes at inference under hand-matching, obstacle-avoidance and gaze losses plus a learned pose-ground prior, yielding far more diverse, scene-aware grasps than GOAL and SAGA.

## Problem

- Full-body grasping methods train on MoCap datasets of humans interacting with objects; such capture is tedious and cannot scale to varied scenes and object placements.
- Prior generative models like GOAL and SAGA fail to generalize when objects sit at extreme heights or inside furniture, producing unnatural stretched or ground-penetrating bodies.
- Existing benchmarks place objects in a narrow height range with no surrounding clutter, so obstacle-aware grasping is untested, and generated pose diversity stays very limited.

## Method

- **FLEX** (Full-body Latent Exploration) inverts frozen ==GrabNet== hand-grasp and ==VPoser== body-pose generators at test time, searching their latents plus approach angle, pelvis translation and global orientation by ==gradient descent==.
- A novel ==obstacle loss== sums a ==signed-distance== body-obstacle intersection term with a body-mesh connectivity term that treats the body as a graph and penalizes all non-largest connected components.
- A ==pose-ground prior== MLP trained on AMASS predicts pelvis roll and pitch from pose, removing two degrees of freedom; one shared mapping network drives all interdependent latents from a single vector.

## Results

- On ReplicaGrasp, FLEX reaches **2.20%** object contact versus **1.14%** for GOAL and **1.21%** for SAGA, with obstacle penetration **0.53%** versus **6.87%** and **7.27%**, and zero ground distance.
- Diversity dominates: **10.37 cm** sample and **69.78 cm** overall on ReplicaGrasp versus **1.10** and **43.84** for SAGA; on GRAB, **24.94** and **46.46 cm** despite never training there.
- Ablations: removing the obstacle loss raises obstacle penetration from **0.97%** to **10.57%**; freezing the pose latent cuts sample diversity to **0.27 cm**; dropping the pose-ground prior leaves humans **71.72 cm** airborne.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Composing independently trained priors with geometric constraints at inference can beat models trained on the joint task, sidestepping expensive paired data collection entirely.
> - Treating the mesh as a graph is a cheap, general trick: signed-distance penalties alone miss limbs that pass through an obstacle and resurface outside it.
> - Test-time latent search inherits the ceiling of its frozen priors; failures trace back to the generators, so quality improves for free as better priors appear.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic 3D human avatars interacting with their environments is an active area of research with applications in fields such as augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), video games, and robotics. A specific challenge within this domain is synthesizing a virtual human's full body and hands in the act of grasping 3D objects.

Existing methods for generating full-body grasps primarily rely on collecting and training on large-scale 3D datasets of humans interacting with objects, such as the GRAB dataset. While these datasets have enabled progress, the reliance on extensive data collection presents several limitations. Firstly, acquiring 3D motion capture (MoCap) data, especially when involving objects and complex scenes, is a tedious, complex, and resource-intensive process requiring specialized hardware and skilled technicians. Secondly, the physical world presents an exponentially vast number of interaction scenarios, making it practically impossible to capture all possible human-object interactions. Consequently, models trained on task-specific datasets often exhibit limited generalization capabilities. For example, models trained on GRAB may fail when object positions or orientations deviate from the training distribution and typically produce a narrow range of body poses. They also struggle in the presence of scene furniture or obstacles.

The current work positions itself as an alternative approach that addresses these limitations by fundamentally rethinking how to achieve 3D human-object interaction synthesis without requiring new, dedicated full-body grasping datasets. Instead of direct supervision on full-body grasp data, it leverages existing, separately trained generative models for body poses and hand grasps. This approach seeks to overcome the data collection bottleneck and improve generalization and pose diversity, especially in complex, realistic scenes containing obstacles. It draws on techniques from test-time optimization, where task-specific constraints are used to refine model outputs during inference, a principle applied in various 3D computer vision and graphics tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to generate realistic and diverse full-body human poses for grasping everyday objects within complex, realistic household environments, specifically in the presence of obstacles. This overarching goal is driven by several more specific motivations and aims:

- **Overcoming Data Scarcity and Generalization Limitations:** A core motivation is to move beyond the constraints imposed by data-driven methods that rely on large 3D full-body grasping datasets. Such datasets are expensive and challenging to acquire, and models trained on them often exhibit poor generalization when objects are placed in novel positions, orientations, or when obstacles are present in the scene. The paper aims to develop a method that performs robustly in these "out-of-distribution" scenarios.
- **Enhancing Pose Diversity:** Existing data-driven approaches for full-body grasping tend to produce a limited variety of human poses, often reflecting the narrow range of motions captured in their training data. A key objective is to generate a wider, more natural, and diverse set of full-body grasping poses that are appropriate for various object placements and environmental contexts.
- **Achieving Scene-Awareness:** The research seeks to enable the generation of human grasps that are aware of and appropriately interact with their surrounding environment, particularly by avoiding intersections with scene obstacles (e.g., furniture, shelves, drawers). This necessitates a mechanism for collision avoidance within the generation process.
- **Leveraging Existing Knowledge without New Data:** The central motivation for the proposed method is the insight that instead of collecting new full-body grasping data, one can leverage the successes of pre-existing, separately trained generative models: those for general human body poses and those for hand-object grasps. By composing these priors using geometrical and anatomical constraints, the goal is to synthesize plausible full-body grasps without requiring explicit full-body grasping examples. This strategy aims to reduce the burden of data collection while capitalizing on the strengths of specialized models.
- **Introducing a Challenging Benchmark:** To properly evaluate scene-aware full-body grasping, the authors aim to introduce a new dataset, ReplicaGrasp, which features objects in a wider variety of positions and within diverse furniture configurations, thus presenting a more realistic and challenging benchmark than existing datasets.

In summary, the motivation stems from the practical and generalization limitations of current data-intensive approaches, prompting the development of a data-efficient, constraint-driven framework capable of generating diverse and scene-aware full-body human grasps.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed FLEX (Full-body Latent Exploration), operates as an optimization-based framework at inference time, leveraging pre-trained generative models for human body poses and hand grasps without requiring any full-body grasping data.

#### 3.1. ReplicaGrasp Dataset

To address the limitations of existing benchmarks, the authors introduce the ReplicaGrasp dataset. This dataset comprises 50 everyday objects from the GRAB dataset placed in 48 distinct receptacles within ReplicaCAD scenes, simulated using Habitat. These receptacles include both rigid and articulated furniture (e.g., drawers, refrigerators, cupboards) with varying states (e.g., open degrees), resulting in a total of 4,800 object instances. The varied placement ensures objects appear in diverse configurations, such as deep inside a refrigerator or on a high shelf, necessitating scene-aware full-body grasps that avoid obstacles.

#### 3.2. Preliminaries: Differentiable Models and Pre-trained Priors

FLEX builds upon established differentiable 3D human and hand models, as well as pre-trained generative priors:

- **Hand Model:** The MANO model is used, which takes full-finger articulated pose (θh), wrist translation (th), and global orientation (Rh) as input to output a 3D hand mesh.
- **Human Body Model:** The SMPL-X model is utilized, a statistical 3D whole-body model (including body, face, and hands) that takes full-body pose (θb), full-finger articulated pose (θh), pelvis translation (tb), and orientation (Rb) to output a 3D human mesh.
- **Pre-trained Generative Models (Frozen):**
 - **Body Pose Prior (P):** VPoser, a variational autoencoder, is used to generate feasible full-body poses (θb) from a latent vector (v).
 - **Hand Grasp Generator (G):** GrabNet is employed, which takes a latent vector (w), an approaching angle (α), and an object representation (O) to generate MANO parameters ({θh, th, Rh}) for a hand grasp.

#### 3.3. Method: Latent Space Search with Constraints

FLEX's core mechanism is a gradient-based search procedure performed at inference time. Given an object mesh and surrounding obstacle meshes, FLEX optimizes several interdependent parameters: the latent vectors `w` (for hand grasp) and `v` (for body pose), the approaching angle `α`, and the SMPL-X pelvis translation `tb` and global orientation `Rb` (represented in yaw-pitch-roll format). The weights of the pre-trained generative models (P and G) remain frozen.

To streamline the optimization of these interdependent parameters, a mapping network (a 2-layer MLP) is introduced. This network takes a single controllable latent vector `z` as input and maps it to all the parameters optimized during the search. FLEX optimizes `z` and the weights of this mapping network. Multiple `z` vectors (N=500) are initialized and optimized concurrently, with the best-performing samples (top 16) selected at the end.

#### 3.4. Loss Functions

The optimization process minimizes a combination of loss functions:

- **Hand Matching Loss (Lhm):** This loss enforces geometric consistency between the hand generated by the MANO model (Vh) and the hand part of the SMPL-X body mesh (Vhb). It is defined as the L2 distance between corresponding vertices. This is crucial for composing the separate hand and body priors.
- **Obstacle Loss (Lo):** Designed to prevent human-obstacle penetration, this loss has two components:
 - **Body-obstacle intersection loss (Linter_o):** This term sums the absolute signed distances for all human mesh vertices that lie inside any obstacle mesh (i.e., having a negative signed distance).
 - **Body mesh connectivity loss (Lcon_o):** This novel component addresses cases where a body part penetrates an obstacle and re-emerges on the other side. By treating the human mesh as a graph, the loss penalizes vertices that are part of disconnected components other than the largest one, preventing the body from "breaking" through obstacles.
- **Gaze Loss (Lg):** This optional loss encourages the human's head to orient towards the target object by minimizing the angle between the head's forward direction vector and the vector pointing from the head to the object.

The total loss `L = λhm Lhm + λo Lo + λg Lg` is minimized using the Adam optimizer, with carefully selected weights (λ) and gradient scaling for different parameters to facilitate convergence.

#### 3.5. Pose-Ground Prior

To ensure generated human poses are physically plausible and do not "fly," a pose-ground prior is incorporated. A 2-layer MLP, trained on the AMASS dataset, predicts the roll and pitch components of the human pelvis orientation from the body pose (θb). This constrains the body's orientation with respect to the ground, leaving only the yaw component free for rotation. Additionally, the vertical component of the human body translation (tz_b) is fixed such that the lowest vertex of the generated human mesh touches the ground plane.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evaluations of FLEX on both the newly introduced ReplicaGrasp dataset and the existing GRAB dataset, comparing it against two baselines: GOAL and SAGA. Ablation studies and human evaluations further characterize FLEX's performance.

#### 4.1. Performance on ReplicaGrasp Dataset

On the challenging ReplicaGrasp dataset, which features objects in varied positions within complex scenes with obstacles, FLEX demonstrates performance improvements over the baselines:

- **Object Contact:** FLEX achieved the highest object contact percentage (2.20%), surpassing GOAL (1.14%) and SAGA (1.21%). This indicates a more consistent ability to establish contact with the target object, particularly in scenarios where baselines struggled due to extreme object placements.
- **Obstacle Penetration:** FLEX significantly reduced obstacle penetration, achieving a percentage of 0.53%, which is substantially lower than GOAL (6.87%) and SAGA (7.27%). This highlights the effectiveness of FLEX's obstacle avoidance mechanisms.
- **Ground Distance:** FLEX consistently generated humans with a ground distance of 0.00 cm, indicating stable and ground-aligned poses. In contrast, SAGA (9.40 cm) and GOAL (5.31 cm) often produced "flying" humans or humans whose feet penetrated the ground, especially for objects at extreme heights.
- **Pose Diversity:** FLEX demonstrated substantially higher diversity in generated full-body poses. Its sample diversity was 10.37 cm (compared to GOAL's 0.20 cm and SAGA's 1.10 cm), and its overall diversity was 69.78 cm (compared to GOAL's 37.89 cm and SAGA's 43.84 cm). This indicates FLEX's ability to generate a wider range of natural and varied grasping poses.

#### 4.2. Performance on GRAB Dataset

Despite not being trained on GRAB's full-body grasping data, FLEX performed comparably to baselines that were explicitly trained on it:

- **Object Contact and Penetration:** FLEX achieved competitive object contact (1.63%) and penetration (2.73%) scores, which were on par with or slightly better than GOAL (1.62% contact, 3.50% penetration) and SAGA (2.19% contact, 3.43% penetration).
- **Pose Diversity:** FLEX significantly outperformed both baselines in terms of diversity metrics on the GRAB dataset, with sample diversity of 24.94 cm (vs. GOAL's 0.11 cm and SAGA's 1.06 cm) and overall diversity of 46.46 cm (vs. GOAL's 7.87 cm and SAGA's 17.35 cm).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of FLEX's key components:

- **No Hand Grasp Optimization:** Freezing the hand-grasp latent variable `w` resulted in significantly worse object contact, indicating the necessity of searching for optimal hand grasps to facilitate full-body interaction.
- **No Body Pose Optimization:** Preventing search in the full-body latent space `v` led to increased obstacle penetration and a substantial reduction in pose diversity, demonstrating the importance of body flexibility for obstacle avoidance and varied poses.
- **No Obstacle Loss:** Removing the obstacle loss terms (Linter_o and Lcon_o) caused a significant increase in obstacle penetration, validating their role in scene-aware interaction.
- **No Pose-Ground Prior:** Eliminating the pose-ground prior resulted in "flying" humans, characterized by very large distances from the ground, confirming its role in ensuring physically plausible body placement.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons illustrate FLEX's capabilities:

- **Diversity:** FLEX generates a variety of reasonable grasping poses, including crouched, standing, squatting, and kneeling positions, adapted to object height and scene constraints (e.g., reaching into a refrigerator or on a high shelf).
- **Generalization:** Unlike baselines that often produce rigid or unnatural poses (e.g., elongated bodies for high objects, buried legs for low objects) and struggle with diverse object placements, FLEX adapts the human pose to the specific environmental context, demonstrating improved generalization.

#### 4.5. Human Evaluation

A human study conducted on Amazon Mechanical Turk evaluated the perceptual quality of FLEX's generated grasps. Participants rated 85% of the generated full-body grasps as natural and realistic, based on questions regarding human realism, grasp naturalness, and body stability. This indicates a high level of perceptual plausibility for the generated interactions.

#### 4.6. Failure Cases

The paper acknowledges failure cases, primarily attributed to limitations of the pre-trained generative models (e.g., specific hand grasp failures) or scenarios where all constraints are met but the resulting pose is still perceived as unnaturally stretched or rare.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FLEX presents a notable contribution to the field of 3D human-object interaction by introducing an approach that addresses several key limitations of existing methods.

- **Addressing Data Dependency:** The work significantly reduces the reliance on large-scale, expensive-to-collect 3D full-body grasping datasets. By demonstrating that robust and diverse full-body grasps can be generated by composing pre-existing, separately trained hand-grasping and body-pose priors, FLEX offers a more scalable and data-efficient paradigm. This is particularly impactful for research areas where comprehensive, task-specific 3D human interaction data is difficult to acquire.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Diversity:** FLEX demonstrates a substantial improvement in generalizing to novel object positions, orientations, and scenes with obstacles, a significant challenge for previous data-driven approaches. The ability to generate a wide and diverse range of realistic human poses in complex environments is a critical step towards more versatile and natural virtual human agents.
- **Scene-Aware Interaction:** The introduction of a novel obstacle loss, particularly the body mesh connectivity component, enables the generation of human poses that effectively avoid collisions with scene geometry. This capability is essential for creating truly realistic and interactive virtual environments.
- **ReplicaGrasp as a New Benchmark:** The introduction of the ReplicaGrasp dataset provides the research community with a more challenging and realistic benchmark for evaluating full-body grasping in complex, scene-aware scenarios. This can stimulate further research into robust and generalized interaction synthesis.
- **Flexibility and Future Potential:** The optimization-based framework of FLEX is model-agnostic, meaning its performance can directly benefit from future advancements in underlying differentiable hand-grasping and human body pose generative models. This inherent adaptability suggests sustained relevance as component technologies evolve.

The potential impact of this research spans several application domains:

- **AR/VR and Video Games:** More realistic and diverse virtual human avatars can significantly enhance immersion and interactivity in AR/VR experiences and video games, allowing characters to perform a wider array of natural interactions with dynamic environments.
- **Robotics:** While not directly a robotics paper, the insights into synthesizing diverse and scene-aware human grasping strategies could inform the development of more adaptable robotic manipulation systems, particularly for tasks in unstructured or cluttered human environments.
- **Virtual Prototyping and Simulation:** The ability to generate realistic human interactions in virtual environments can be valuable for virtual prototyping, ergonomic studies, and creating more realistic simulators for training or research in fields like embodied AI.

In conclusion, FLEX contributes a novel, data-efficient, and effective method for synthesizing diverse, scene-aware full-body human grasps, addressing key limitations of data-intensive approaches and opening new avenues for realistic human-computer interaction and virtual content creation.
