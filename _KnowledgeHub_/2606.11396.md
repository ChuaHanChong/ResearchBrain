# PLUME: Probabilistic Latent Unified World Modeling and Parameter Estimation for Multi-Finger Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PLUME is a flow-matching world model for multi-finger dexterous manipulation that jointly estimates a particle belief over hidden physical parameters (friction, shape) and predicts dynamics conditioned on that belief. A Wasserstein autoencoder learns a unified latent over parameters and rewards; a planner scores autoregressive rollouts by decoded reward weighted by inverse-dynamics likelihood, giving zero-shot sim-to-real screwdriver turning at 93.3% validity.

## Problem

- Domain randomization seeks parameter-agnostic policies, but precise tasks like screwdriver turning need strategies that change with friction and object shape values.
- Existing world models rely on fixed-horizon history to implicitly absorb dynamics variation, and are rarely deployed on variation-sensitive contact-rich multi-finger tasks.
- Online system-identification RL needs heavy reward engineering and models no uncertainty over estimates; learned particle filters target localization and skip downstream planning.

## Method

- **PLUME**'s ==Wasserstein autoencoder== with transformer encoder and cross-attention pooling maps heterogeneous parameters plus reward into one latent, with ==flow-matching== gradients detached from the encoder to preserve capacity.
- A single masked ==flow-matching DiT-style transformer== learns several conditional distributions at once (belief update, rollout, inverse dynamics), with a ==Mamba-2== history encoder compressing past actions and observations.
- A ==receding-horizon planner== autoregressively samples trajectories, scores them by belief-decoded reward exponentially weighted by inverse-dynamics log-likelihood, and stops execution when likelihood falls below an out-of-distribution threshold.

## Results

- Simulated screwdriver turning reaches **81.3%** validity versus **70.7%** for the Unified World Model baseline, a **10.6**-point gain with **31%** lower goal distance, significant under a McNemar test.
- Zero-shot hardware screwdriver transfer gives **93.3%** validity over **30** trials (**2** failures) versus **86.7%** for UWM, using only **2.6** actions per trial on average.
- Valve goal distance drops **54%** and disk flicking **20%** versus UWM; PLUME alone solves DexArt bucket lifting at **71.3%** success while all baselines score **5.3%** or below.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly estimating and conditioning on physical parameters beats implicit history-based or parameter-agnostic policies when small parameter shifts demand qualitatively different manipulation strategies.
> - Weighting predicted reward by transition likelihood curbs planner exploitation of world-model error; removing likelihood collapsed screwdriver validity from 81.3% to 46.7%.
> - Belief-based online adaptation needs contact-rich information gathering: gains shrank on brief-contact disk flicking, and training still requires simulator ground-truth parameter labels.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly with multi-finger hands, has seen advancements with data-driven methods for fine manipulation tasks. However, reliability and generalizability remain challenges, especially for tasks sensitive to uncertain physical parameters such as object shape, pose, and friction coefficients.

**Limitations of Current Approaches:**
- **Domain Randomization:** Standard domain randomization techniques, while useful for learning parameter-agnostic policies, may be insufficient for precise tasks where manipulation strategies need to change based on specific parameter values. For example, a screwdriver turning task might require different forces or contact points depending on the screw's resistance or the screwdriver's grip.
- **World Modeling:** Existing world modeling methods often rely on a fixed-horizon history to implicitly account for dynamics variation. These methods are typically not deployed to the type of precise multi-finger manipulation tasks considered in this paper, which are highly sensitive to explicit dynamics variations.
- **Data Collection:** Collecting high-quality data for multi-finger manipulation is challenging. While simulation allows for scalable data collection and parameter variation, teleoperation challenges persist, and data generation policies may require task-specific designs. This often results in heterogeneous datasets, which current methods may not leverage effectively.
- **System Identification:** Traditional system identification methods for estimating parameters often require complex reward engineering, are trained online, and may not model uncertainty over parameter estimates or enable planning. While some approaches adapt particle filtering, they typically do not integrate parameter estimation with downstream planning for physical parameters.
- **Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Offline RL methods focus on learning policies from pre-generated datasets to avoid costly environment interactions. However, these methods generally do not emphasize joint system identification and policy learning in the context of varying physical parameters.

**PLUME's Contribution to the Landscape:**
PLUME addresses these gaps by proposing a world model that explicitly reasons over variations in dynamics by learning to estimate a belief over latent parameters. This belief, derived from observations and actions, conditions the world model's operations. This approach allows a single policy to adapt to situations with different dynamics, enhancing the generalizability and reliability for precise, parameter-sensitive tasks. Furthermore, by learning from potentially lower-quality, heterogeneous offline datasets, PLUME aims to reduce the dependency on perfectly curated expert demonstrations. The integration of online parameter inference into the world model allows for alignment with true dynamics during deployment without requiring re-training or fine-tuning, which distinguishes it from many existing methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust and adaptable control system for dexterous multi-finger manipulation tasks that are highly sensitive to uncertain physical parameters.

The key motivations driving this research are:

- **Addressing Sensitivity to Physical Parameters:** Dexterous manipulation tasks, such as screwdriver turning, are highly sensitive to parameters like object shape, pose, and friction coefficients. These parameters, often unknown at deployment, significantly influence the true system dynamics and necessitate adaptive manipulation strategies. Existing domain randomization techniques, which aim to learn parameter-agnostic policies, may not provide the precision required for such tasks.
- **Enabling Adaptive Dynamics Modeling:** Current world modeling methods typically rely on a fixed-horizon history to implicitly account for dynamics variations. This approach is often insufficient for tasks where explicit reasoning about dynamics changes due to varying physical parameters is crucial. The motivation is to develop a world model that can explicitly estimate and adapt to these parameter-induced dynamics variations.
- **Leveraging Offline, Heterogeneous Data:** While real-world data collection for multi-finger hands is challenging, simulation allows for scalable data generation and parameter variation. However, even in simulation, generating perfectly optimal data can be difficult, leading to heterogeneous datasets that may include sub-optimal or even failed trajectories. The motivation is to learn useful models from such mixed-quality datasets, moving beyond the need for purely expert demonstrations. Sub-optimal data can still provide valuable information about system dynamics, especially when combined with reward labels to guide planning.
- **Facilitating Online Adaptation without Re-training:** A significant challenge in deploying simulation-trained policies to the real world is the need to adapt to discrepancies between simulated and real-world parameters (Sim2Real gap). The motivation is to enable a world model to align with true dynamics at deployment through online parameter inference, rather than requiring computationally expensive re-training or fine-tuning. This allows for the deployment of a single policy across varying real-world conditions.
- **Integrating Parameter Estimation and Planning:** To achieve precise manipulation, the system needs to both infer unknown physical parameters and utilize these inferences to inform future actions. The objective is to jointly learn a belief over parameter values and system dynamics, then use this joint model for robust planning by considering estimated rewards and the likelihood of planned transitions. This integration aims to create plans that are both high-reward and dynamically feasible.
- **Learning Unified Representations:** Physical parameters can be qualitatively diverse (e.g., shape descriptors, friction coefficients) and rewards themselves may depend on partially observable variables. The motivation is to learn a unified latent representation that can encode these different types of information, making it useful for conditioning world model learning and enabling richer reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Probabilistic Latent Unified World Modeling and Parameter Estimation (PLUME), is an end-to-end trained world model designed to handle uncertainty in physical parameters during multi-finger manipulation. It jointly learns to estimate a belief over latent parameters and models system dynamics conditioned on these parameters.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

PLUME consists of three main components: a parameter representation learning autoencoder, a long-horizon history encoder, and a joint flow matching model.

- **Learning Parameter Representations:**
 - A **Wasserstein Auto-Encoder (WAE)** is employed to learn a joint latent representation $z_t$ for a set of relevant system parameters $\rho_t$ (e.g., friction, shape descriptors) and the task reward $r_t$. The set of these variables is denoted as $\tilde{\rho}_t = \rho_t \cup \{r_t\}$.
 - An **encoder E**, implemented as a transformer, takes tokenized inputs of $\tilde{\rho}_t$ (each element with an additive learned modality embedding) and uses a cross-attention pooling layer to output a single latent vector $z_t$.
 - Separate **decoder MLPs D$_i$** are used to reconstruct each parameter $\rho_i$ and the reward $r_t$ from $z_t$.
 - The WAE regularizes $z_t$ to follow a standard normal prior, which facilitates initializing the parameter belief.

- **History Modeling:**
 - A **Mamba-2 encoder C** compresses the trajectory history, comprising past actions ($u_{1:t}$) and observations ($y_{0:t-1}$), into a history context vector $c_t$. This aims to capture long-range dependencies and provide useful context, mitigating challenges associated with limited particles or noisy observations in particle-filtering-based methods. The initial history context $c_0$ is a zero vector.

- **Joint Flow Matching:**
 - A **flow matching model M**, based on a transformer backbone similar to DiT, is trained to learn multiple conditional distributions concurrently. These distributions include parameter estimation ($p(Z_{t+1}|Z_t, y_t, u_{t+1}, y_{t+1}, c_t)$), rollout generation ($p(u_{t+1}, y_{t+1}, Z_{t+1}|Z_t, y_t, c_t)$), and inverse dynamics ($p(u_{t+1}|Z_t, y_t, Z_{t+1}, y_{t+1}, c_t)$).
 - The model receives as input the current observation $y_t$, action $u_{t+1}$, next observation $y_{t+1}$, history context $c_t$, current latent belief $Z_t$ (a set of particles), next latent belief $Z_{t+1}$, and a flow matching timestep $t_{flow}$.
 - A mask input $m$ is used to specify which variables are conditioned upon (mask value = 1) and which are to be generated (mask value = 0). Noisy inputs are used for generated variables, and clean values for conditioned variables, analogous to in-painting.
 - Transformer features for generated variables are passed through an MLP to predict flow matching velocities, which are used in loss calculation and ODE integration.

#### 3.2. Training Loop

The entire system—flow matching model M, history encoder C, and parameter encoder E with decoders D—is trained end-to-end on an offline trajectory dataset D.

- **Particle Filtering Emulation:** During training, an exponential moving average (EMA) version of the model, M$_{EMA}$, is used to perform filtering, simulating the inference-time behavior.
- **Masking Strategy:** For each training step, a random mask $m$ is sampled. While $y_t$ and $c_t$ are always treated as known, booleans for $Z_t, Z_{t+1}, u_{t+1}, y_{t+1}$ are independently sampled from a Bernoulli distribution (p=0.5) to determine if they are conditioned or generated. For particle sets ($Z_t, Z_{t+1}$), the boolean applies to the entire set.
- **Loss Function:** The total loss L combines three components:
 - Reconstruction loss: $|| \hat{\tilde{\rho}}_{t+1} - \tilde{\rho}_{t+1} ||_2^2$ between the decoded and true $\tilde{\rho}_{t+1}$.
 - MMD regularization: $ \beta \cdot \text{MMD}(z_t)$ to enforce the standard normal prior on the latent space.
 - Flow matching loss: $L_{FM}$ for the variables specified by the mask.
- **Gradient Flow Control:** A specific design choice is to *not* update the WAE (E and D) with gradients originating from the flow matching loss for $Z$. This prevents the flow matching objective from optimizing its own target within the latent space, aiming to preserve the representational capacity of the WAE for meaningful parameter encoding while allowing the flow matching to learn effective dynamics.

#### 3.3. Planning

PLUME employs a receding-horizon trajectory-sampling approach for action planning.

- **Trajectory Generation:** Conditioned on the current latent belief $Z_t$, observation $y_t$, and history context $c_t$, the planner autoregressively samples a batch of $B$ trajectories of length $H$ from the flow matching model's rollout distribution. The history context $c_t$ is updated within the rollout using generated actions and observations.
- **Online Adaptation:** As the task progresses, the latent belief $Z_t$ is iteratively updated by the world model using new observations and actions. This online belief update dynamically adjusts the world model's internal dynamics, allowing for adaptation to uncertainty during execution without re-training.
- **Trajectory Scoring:** Trajectories are scored based on two factors to balance reward maximization with dynamic feasibility:
 - **Estimated Rewards:** $\hat{r}_t = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K D_r(z_t^k)$, which is the average decoded reward from the particles in the belief.
 - **Estimated Log-Likelihoods:** $\ell_t$, approximated using the flow matching loss, specifically from an inverse-dynamics distribution ($p(u_{t+1}|Z_t, y_t, Z_{t+1}, y_{t+1})$). A low inverse-dynamics likelihood suggests an unrealistic or dynamically inconsistent trajectory, even if it promises high rewards.
- **Score Calculation:** Each trajectory receives a score $s = \sum_{t=1}^H \gamma^t \hat{r}_t e^{\ell_t/\lambda}$, where $\gamma$ is a discount factor and $\lambda$ is a temperature parameter. Rewards are normalized to during training.
- **Action Execution:** The first action of the trajectory with the highest $s$ score is executed. The process then repeats with an updated state and belief.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Detection:** To prevent catastrophic failures, an OOD stopping criterion is implemented. If the average trajectory log-likelihood falls below a predefined threshold $\ell_{min}$, the execution stops, indicating that the model cannot confidently plan from the current state.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PLUME was conducted across multiple multi-finger manipulation tasks in simulation and on a hardware platform, comparing its performance against several baselines.

#### 4.1. Evaluated Tasks

- **Simulated Screwdriver Turning:** Precision turning task with varied screwdriver shape, finger-object friction, screw resistance friction, initial pose, and grasp. Shape descriptors and friction coefficients constitute $\rho$.
- **Hardware Screwdriver Turning:** Zero-shot transfer of the simulation-trained model to a real-world screwdriver task.
- **Simulated Disk Flicking:** Ficking a disk to a random goal with varied friction between finger/disk and disk/table. Friction coefficients constitute $\rho$.
- **Simulated Valve Turning:** Turning a valve with varied finger-valve friction and joint friction.
- **DexArt Bucket Lifting:** Lifting a bucket from a set of 11 distinct shapes, involving a 6 DoF arm and Allegro hand. Image observations are used, processed by pre-trained features, along with proprioception. Shape descriptor constitutes $\rho$.

#### 4.2. Baselines

- **Unified World Model (UWM):** A state-of-the-art behavior cloning method that unifies policy and world model learning. Failures were filtered out for UWM training to match its original implementation.
- **Flow Q-Learning (FQL):** A state-of-the-art offline reinforcement learning method using a flow-matching-based policy with offline Q-learning and behavior cloning regularization.
- **Decision Diffuser (DD):** A diffusion-based method that learns return-conditioned policies over observations and an inverse dynamics model for action prediction.

Baselines were trained with a fixed-length history of observations for implicit parameter reasoning, as they do not perform explicit parameter estimation as PLUME does.

#### 4.4. Key Findings

- **Superior Performance Across Tasks:** PLUME consistently achieved higher validity rates and lower goal distances compared to all baselines across the simulated and hardware tasks.
 - For **simulated screwdriver turning**, PLUME showed a statistically significant improvement in validity (10.6 percentage point increase over UWM) and a 31% reduction in goal distance.
 - For **hardware screwdriver turning** (zero-shot transfer), PLUME maintained the highest validity rate (93.3%) and a lower goal distance (0.77 rad) than UWM (0.86 rad), with significantly better reliability than FQL and DD. PLUME policies stopped earlier due to OOD detection, achieving desired outcomes more efficiently.
 - On **disk flicking** and **valve turning**, PLUME significantly outperformed baselines, achieving a 54% lower distance to goal for valve turning and a 20% lower distance for disk flicking compared to UWM.
 - For the **DexArt bucket task**, PLUME was the only method to achieve consistent success (71.3%), indicating its ability to reason about partially observable state variables and complex reward functions, even with image observations.
- **Efficacy of Explicit Parameter Estimation:** The results suggest that explicitly estimating and conditioning on system parameters (as PLUME does) leads to better performance on precise and sensitive tasks than methods attempting to learn parameter-agnostic policies or implicitly model variations.
- **Benefits of Likelihood-Weighed Planning:** Ablation studies showed that removing likelihood weighting from the planner ("No Likelihood") significantly degraded performance (e.g., screwdriver validity dropped to 46.7%), underscoring the importance of considering dynamic consistency in trajectory scoring.
- **Multi-Particle Belief vs. Point Estimate:** The "Single Particle" ablation generally underperformed PLUME, particularly in complex tasks like the screwdriver, indicating the benefit of maintaining a multi-particle belief for representing uncertainty.
- **Value of End-to-End Latent Learning:** While the "Detached" ablation (which likely refers to further detaching world model gradients from latent representation learning beyond the specific Z gradient detachment in PLUME's default) showed mixed results, the overall superior performance of PLUME indicates the benefit of its integrated architecture where representation learning is informed by flow matching gradients, shaping a latent space useful for dynamics modeling.
- **Online Adaptation:** PLUME's ability to update its belief over parameters online allows it to adapt its dynamics predictions during task execution, a factor in its robust performance and zero-shot transfer capabilities.
- **Computational Performance:** Planning times per action ranged from 0.18s to 0.68s, with particle updates occurring efficiently (0.03-0.05s). Training required approximately 8 hours on 2 RTX 5090 GPUs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents PLUME, a method that addresses key challenges in dexterous multi-finger manipulation under uncertainty, demonstrating several significant advancements and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalizability:** PLUME's explicit estimation of physical parameters and subsequent conditioning of the world model enables robust performance across diverse, uncertain environmental conditions, including variations in object shape, pose, and friction coefficients. This capability is critical for deploying robotic systems to real-world scenarios where precise parameter values are often unknown or variable.
- **Effective Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot transfer of a simulation-trained policy to a hardware screwdriver turning task without any fine-tuning or hardware-specific parameter tuning highlights PLUME's ability to bridge the simulation-to-reality gap. This reduces the significant effort and data traditionally required for real-world robotic deployment.
- **Online Adaptation and Reduced Re-training:** By maintaining and updating a particle-approximated belief over latent parameters during execution, PLUME allows the world model to adapt its dynamics predictions online. This eliminates the need for costly re-training or fine-tuning when faced with new or uncertain physical parameters, leading to more efficient and flexible robotic systems.
- **Leveraging Heterogeneous Data:** PLUME's design facilitates learning from offline datasets that may contain sub-optimal or even failed trajectories. This is a practical advantage, as high-quality, perfectly curated expert data is often difficult and expensive to acquire in complex manipulation tasks. The method's ability to extract useful dynamics information from such diverse data sources broadens its applicability.
- **Unified Framework for Control:** The integration of parameter estimation, representation learning, and world modeling into a single, end-to-end trainable flow-matching framework offers a comprehensive approach to intelligent robotic control. This unified structure enables richer reasoning over dynamics variations and facilitates adaptive planning.
- **Improved Planning for Precise Tasks:** The planner's ability to score trajectories based on both estimated rewards and estimated likelihoods of transitions contributes to generating plans that are not only high-reward but also dynamically feasible. This helps prevent the generation of unrealistic or exploitative plans that could lead to failures in sensitive manipulation tasks.
- **Broader Applicability to Complex Manipulation:** Demonstrated success on challenging tasks like screwdriver turning, valve turning, disk flicking, and the DexArt bucket task, which involve contact-rich interactions, dynamic motions, and partially observable states, suggests PLUME's potential for application to a wider range of complex dexterous manipulation problems in industrial, service, and research settings.

Future work exploring fine-tuning models with real-robot data (potentially labeled by the pre-trained model) and investigating more memory-efficient architectures for the transformer components could further enhance PLUME's capabilities and address its current limitations.
