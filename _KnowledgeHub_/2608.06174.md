# Support Operation Factorization: Compositional Readout of Frozen Vision Encoders under Controlled Interventions

> [!summary] Summary
> A structured readout mechanism and evaluation framework are introduced to interpret how frozen vision encoders represent image edits compositionally, by explicitly binding spatial support to specific operations. This approach reveals robust compositional generalization in DINOv3 and SigLIP2 features on synthetic and natural images, while identifying representational boundaries in certain rendering environments and intervention types.

## Problem

- Previous analyses of self-supervised visual encoders often treat image edits as global directions in latent space, failing to explicitly link a change to its specific spatial region.
- This lack of explicit spatial binding can lead to "operation laundering," where a non-injective alignment allows multiple operations to incorrectly share the same representational slot, masking true compositional understanding failures.
- Standard probing techniques typically assess one factor at a time, missing interaction effects or the ability to generalize to unseen combinations of spatial regions and transformations.

## Method

- The research defines an ==innovation field== as the difference in patch tokens between original and edited images, then decomposes it along ground-truth "support" (spatial region) and "operation" (transform class) axes.
- A "Support×Operation Factorization" (**SO-OPF**) readout is developed, which separates "where" a change occurs (support salience) from "what" the change is (operation identity) through a factored energy formulation.
- A ==leave-one-cell-out== evaluation protocol, combined with a ==Hungarian injective assignment (INJ)== metric, is used to rigorously test compositional generalization and detect "operation laundering" by ensuring a one-to-one mapping between predicted and ground-truth factors.

## Results

- SO-OPF achieved high injective cell accuracy (INJ) on unseen combinations: **0.874 ± 0.009** on Shapes3D-Extended and **0.799 ± 0.015** on image-disjoint COCO when the factorial taxonomy was explicitly provided.
- Under a weakly supervised setting, SO-OPF recovered substantial performance, achieving **0.769 ± 0.090** INJ on Shapes3D and **0.762 ± 0.039** INJ on COCO with over **90%** factorial assignment recovery from flat labels.
- On the MuJoCo dataset, DINOv3's INJ dropped by **0.221** from **0.790** (many-to-one alignment) to **0.569** (injective alignment), while per-cell analysis revealed strong performance for pattern-related edits (**>0.99**) but poor performance for hue and inversion edits (**0.090–0.275**), indicating a specific failure boundary.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The
> - injective cell accuracy
> - (INJ) metric is crucial for revealing
> - operation laundering
> - , demonstrating that non-injective evaluations can significantly overestimate compositional understanding by masking representational collapses.
> - Frozen DINOv3 and SigLIP2 features possess sufficient intrinsic compositional information in their innovation fields to enable strong compositional generalization to unseen support-operation bindings, even with weakly supervised assignment recovery.
> - The compositional routing ability of vision encoders exhibits boundaries, particularly in environments like MuJoCo, where certain material-space operations (e.g., hue, inversion) produce less discriminable token-level signatures than pixel-space overlays, leading to failures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a limitation in the analysis of self-supervised visual encoders, such as DINOv3, I-JEPA, and V-JEPA, which are known to encode systematic image edits in their patch-token features. Previous analyses often treat these encoded changes as "factors of variation," typically representing them as global directions in latent space (e.g., principal components or linear concept probes). While disentanglement methods (e.g., β-VAE, FactorVAE) explicitly adopt this global-direction view, and predictive world models motivate similar analyses, this approach has a critical drawback: it does not explicitly bind a change class to the specific image region where that change occurred.

This lack of explicit binding can lead to scenarios where a readout cannot differentiate between distinct spatial factors (e"g., floor-hue vs. wall-pattern edits) or, more problematically, attributes both changes to the same representational slot. The paper terms this specific failure mode "operation laundering," where a non-injective alignment allows multiple operations to be credited through a single representational slot, thereby masking a failure in compositional understanding. Existing disentanglement work, as highlighted by Locatello et al. (2019), often struggles with unsupervised disentanglement without inductive bias and has been shown to fail compositional generalization (Montero et al. 2021; Schott et al. 2022). Furthermore, standard probing techniques typically assess one factor at a time, potentially missing interaction effects or compositional failures.

The current work differentiates itself by focusing on a structured readout mechanism that explicitly binds "where" (spatial support) and "what" (operation) dimensions. It extends the concept of compositional generalization, typically evaluated with unseen combinations of known primitives (Lake and Baroni 2018; Keysers et al. 2020), to spatial-visual factors within the context of analyzing frozen vision encoder features. By introducing a "support×operation" grid and a "leave-one-cell-out" protocol, it directly tests the ability of a readout to handle unseen combinations of spatial regions and transformations. This approach contrasts with causal representation learning (Schölkopf et al. 2021), which focuses on identifiability of individual coordinates from interventions, by instead addressing whether a readout can compose spatial bindings given a frozen encoder and known intervention structure, under a held-out compositional evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a structured readout mechanism capable of compositionally interpreting the innovation fields of frozen visual encoders under controlled interventions. This involves determining both *what* changed and *where* it changed, even when a specific *support-operation* pairing has not been explicitly encountered during training.

The motivations underpinning this objective are multifold:

1. **Addressing the Limitations of Global-Direction Factors:** Standard analyses often represent factors as global directions in a latent space, failing to explicitly link a change to its spatial location. This can lead to ambiguity, where, for instance, a readout cannot distinguish between an edit to the "floor hue" and an edit to the "wall pattern," or improperly attributes both to the same internal representational slot. Such a limitation is detrimental for applications like predictive world models, where independent manipulation of scene attributes is desired.
2. **Preventing Operation Laundering:** A key motivation is to overcome the problem of "operation laundering," where a non-injective alignment between ground-truth factors and internal readout slots can allow multiple distinct operations to incorrectly share the same internal slot, leading to an inflated perception of accuracy. The research seeks to introduce an evaluation metric that specifically detects and penalizes this failure mode.
3. **Evaluating Compositional Generalization for Spatial Factors:** The paper aims to test whether a readout can genuinely compose held-out *support-operation* bindings. This involves defining factors as cells within a support×operation grid and evaluating the readout under a "leave-one-cell-out" protocol, where a specific combination of support and operation is withheld during training but its individual components are present in other training examples.
4. **Separating Carrier Capacity from Assignment Recovery:** The research seeks to experimentally distinguish between two related but distinct questions:
 - **Carrier Capacity:** Given that the support–operation taxonomy (the grid structure) is known, can the proposed factored readout compose held-out cells? This tests the inherent capacity of the readout architecture.
 - **Assignment Recovery:** Can the mapping from flat, observed cell labels to the underlying factorial grid locations be estimated without explicit axis labels for supports and operations? This investigates the ability to discover latent structure from weakly supervised signals.
5. **Establishing Boundaries of Generalization:** The work also aims to identify the conditions and scenarios under which the proposed approach succeeds or fails. This includes testing performance across different renderers (MuJoCo vs. Shapes3D) and comparing against a "compressed identity" assignment control to understand if simple label order provides sufficient structural information.

In summary, the core objective is to advance the analysis of frozen vision encoders by enabling a more granular, spatially-aware, and compositionally robust interpretation of visual interventions, while rigorously evaluating its performance under conditions that expose representational deficiencies like operation laundering.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology focuses on analyzing the "innovation field" generated by frozen vision encoders under controlled image interventions and developing a structured readout, SO-OPF, to interpret these changes compositionally.

#### 3.1. Frozen Encoder and Innovation Field

The study utilizes pre-trained, frozen vision transformers, specifically DINOv3-ViT-L/16 and SigLIP2-L/16-256. These encoders map an input image to a grid of patch tokens ($z \in R^{N \times d}$). An "innovation field" ($\Delta z$) is defined as the difference between the patch tokens of an intervened image ($x_{int}$) and a source image ($x_{src}$), where $x_{int}$ results from a single visual edit on $x_{src}$. This $\Delta z$ serves as the sole input to the readout.

#### 3.2. Supports and Operations

The innovation field is decomposed along two axes:
- **Support ($s \in S$):** A ground-truth spatial region (e.g., floor, wall, object) where the visual change primarily occurs.
- **Operation ($o \in O$):** The transform class applied (e.g., hue rotation, color inversion, stripe overlay).
A predictive factor is defined as a cell $(s, o)$ in the factorial grid $S \times O$. For most experiments, a $3 \times 3$ grid is used, leading to nine cells.

#### 3.3. Compositional Metric with Injective Binding

A "leave-one-cell-out" protocol is employed for evaluation. For each cell $(s^*, o^*)$ in the grid, all other $|S||O|-1$ cells are used for training. Crucially, the support $s^*$ and operation $o^*$ still appear in other training cells, but their specific binding $(s^*, o^*)$ is withheld for testing.
- **Joint Prediction:** For a test batch, the readout produces an energy grid $E \in R^{B \times |S| \times |O|}$, and the prediction is $(\hat{s}, \hat{o}) = \text{arg max}_{s,o} E_{b,s,o}$.
- **Axis Alignment and Operation Laundering:** To account for potential permutations in the internal energy axes, ground-truth axis values are bound to energy slots using mean support- and operation-marginal energy matrices computed from training data. A critical aspect is the prevention of "operation laundering," a failure where a non-injective alignment allows multiple operations to be credited through the same slot.
- **Hungarian Injective Assignment (INJ):** To counter operation laundering, the Hungarian algorithm is used to enforce an injective (one-to-one) mapping between ground-truth axis values and energy slots for evaluation. A held-out example is considered correct only if its joint argmax aligns with the correct ground-truth binding under this injective mapping. This metric, injective cell accuracy (INJ), is averaged across held-out cells and seeds.

#### 3.4. The SO-OPF Readout

The proposed "Support×Operation Factorization" (SO-OPF) readout separates "how much change" (support salience) from "which operation" (operation identity).
1. **Support Router:** A learned softmax-based router ($M_{s,n}$) assigns patches to support slots based on a linear transformation of the source patch token ($z_{src,n}$), positional embeddings ($p_n$), and learned support queries ($q_s$). The router does not receive pixel masks during training.
2. **Factored Energy:** The energy $E_{s,o}$ for a cell is factored as $A_s \pi_{s,o}$.
 - $A_s$ (support salience) measures the innovation mass under support $s$, calculated by summing squared innovation magnitudes ($\text{sal}_n = \|\Delta z_n\|^2_2$) weighted by $M_{s,n}$.
 - $\pi_{s,o}$ (competitive operation posterior) is a softmax over operation identities, derived from scale-normalized innovation features ($h_s$). $h_s$ is a mean of layer-normalized $\Delta z_n$ weighted by $M_{s,n}$ and $\text{sal}_n$. A one-hidden-layer MLP ($f_{\theta}$) processes $h_s$, followed by softmax with an annealed temperature $\tau$.

#### 3.5. Assignment Semantics

The paper explores three ways to map flat observed-cell labels ($y$) to the factorial energy grid:
- **Factorial Q:** The assignment matrix $Q$ directly uses a one-hot vector for the true grid location $(s_y, o_y)$. This mode assumes the grid taxonomy is explicitly supplied.
- **Learned Q:** Each row of $Q$ is learned (softmax(a_y)) and optimized jointly with the readout. This tests structure recovery from flat labels without explicit axis information.
- **Compressed Identity:** Observed label $y$ maps directly to grid column $y$. This serves as a control for literal label-order interpretation, often violating axis structure.

#### 3.6. Core and Axis-Aware Objectives

- **Core Protocol ($L_{core}$):** Uses cell cross-entropy loss ($L_{cell}$) and a negative entropy term for mean support usage ($L_{mask}$) to encourage non-empty support slots. This is the weakly supervised setting for "learned Q."
- **Enhanced Protocol ($L_{enh}$):** Adds localization loss ($L_{loc}$), assignment balance ($L_{bal}$), axis balance ($L_{axis}$), and operation injectivity ($L_{opinj}$). $L_{opinj}$ uses native operation labels, making this an axis-aware supervised setting, primarily used for carrier comparisons.

#### 3.7. Dense Carrier Baseline

A dense, non-negative carrier is used for comparison, pooling squared innovation channels independently for each operation. This carrier couples operation identity to change magnitude, contrasting with SO-OPF's factored approach.

#### 3.8. Substrates

Experiments are conducted on three distinct datasets:
- **Shapes3D-Extended:** Synthetic data with three supports (floor, wall, object) and three operations (hue, invert, pattern), totaling nine cells.
- **MuJoCo (Renderer-Sensitivity Test):** A rebuilt substrate using the MuJoCo physics engine to test compositional routing transfer across renderers, featuring box geometry and different camera/lighting. Operations are material-space hue/inversion and pixel-space pattern.
- **Image-disjoint COCO:** Real-world images with edits on person, vehicle, and background regions. All source images are unique across cells, ensuring strong generalization testing.

#### 3.9. Optimization and Comparators

Adam optimizer is used for readout training. Baselines include a bilinear structured carrier, MLP and attention probes on mean-pooled or token-level $\Delta z$, and an oracle-region operation classifier. Randomly initialized ViT and raw-pixel carriers are also evaluated on COCO.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the SO-OPF readout, the impact of assignment semantics, and the boundaries of compositional generalization across different datasets and encoders.

#### 4.1. Known Structure (Factorial Q) and Learned Structure (Learned Q)

- **Shapes3D and COCO (DINOv3):** When the support-operation taxonomy is explicitly provided (Factorial Q), the SO-OPF carrier demonstrates strong compositional generalization. On Shapes3D, it achieves an INJ of 0.874 ± 0.009, and on globally image-disjoint COCO, it reaches 0.799 ± 0.015.
 - Under the weakly supervised "Learned Q" setting (flat cell labels only), SO-OPF recovers a significant portion of this performance. Shapes3D yields 0.769 ± 0.090 INJ, with 90.0% factorial assignment recovery. COCO reaches 0.762 ± 0.039 INJ, with 96.1% recovery. While demonstrating substantial recovery of structure, the lower mean INJ and higher across-seed variation compared to Factorial Q indicate that learning the assignment from flat labels is not entirely lossless.
- **COCO Replication with SigLIP2:** The findings on COCO are replicated with SigLIP2. Factorial Q achieves 0.826 ± 0.012 INJ, while Learned Q reaches 0.751 ± 0.074 INJ, with 96.5% assignment recovery. This consistency across encoders suggests the pattern of strong carrier performance and a measurable cost for assignment estimation from flat labels is robust.

#### 4.2. Injective Scoring and Operation Laundering

- The "injective cell accuracy" (INJ) metric, employing Hungarian injective assignment, is crucial for detecting "operation laundering" (multiple operations claiming the same slot).
- On MuJoCo, the effect of laundering is pronounced. Learned assignment for DINOv3 initially scores 0.790 under a many-to-one alignment but drops significantly to 0.569 after injective scoring, a reduction of 0.221. This substantial gap indicates that the aggregate many-to-one score masked significant slot collapse and reuse. A paired seed-level analysis showed that 9 out of 10 MuJoCo seeds experienced a drop greater than 0.05.
- SigLIP2 on MuJoCo exhibits a similar pattern, dropping from 0.629 (many-to-one) to 0.484 (injective). This confirms that non-injective metrics can conceal severe representational failures.

#### 4.3. Renderer Boundary and Operation-Specific Failures

- The MuJoCo substrate presents a boundary to the generalizability of compositional routing. With DINOv3, Factorial Q only achieves 0.452 ± 0.011 INJ, and Learned Q, despite improving aggregate INJ to 0.569, still suffers from 43.3% collapse and 64.3% assignment recovery.
- SigLIP2 performs similarly on MuJoCo, with Factorial Q at 0.476 ± 0.008 INJ and Learned Q at 0.484 ± 0.167 INJ, accompanied by 51.1% collapse. The failure is not encoder-specific.
- Per-cell breakdowns on MuJoCo reveal an operation-specific pattern: pattern-related cells consistently score high (e.g., >0.99 for DINOv3 factorial), while hue and inversion cells perform poorly (e.g., 0.090–0.275 for DINOv3 hue cells). This suggests that material-space operations (hue, inversion) may produce less discriminable token-level signatures than pixel-space overlays (pattern) within these encoders, leading to the observed failure boundary.

#### 4.4. Baselines and Carrier Comparison

- **Unstructured Probes:** Standard MLP and attention probes, or even an oracle-region operation classifier, performed poorly on the held-out binding task (e.g., COCO INJ scores: MLP 0.049, Attention 0.117, Oracle 0.310). This indicates that specialized readout structure is necessary beyond simple probing.
- **SO-OPF vs. Bilinear Carrier:** Under Factorial Q, SO-OPF consistently outperforms a bilinear structured carrier across all datasets (e.g., COCO: SO-OPF 0.799 vs. Bilinear 0.601).
- **Factored vs. Dense Carrier (Enhanced Supervision):** Under matched axis-aware enhanced supervision, the factored SO-OPF carrier significantly improves Learned Q INJ from 0.653 (dense carrier) to 0.841, while also eliminating the dense carrier's 0.071 laundering gap. This supports the effectiveness of SO-OPF's factored design in separating support salience from operation identity.

#### 4.5. Additional Fixed-Structure Tests

- **Larger Grid:** SO-OPF with Factorial Q achieved 0.870 ± 0.013 INJ on a larger 3×5 grid (15 cells) on Shapes3D, demonstrating scalability.
- **Simultaneous Edits:** For two simultaneous edits, a top-2 readout achieved 0.843 ± 0.006 joint accuracy (uniform chance 1/36) under Factorial Q.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research makes several contributions to the field of interpretable machine learning and representation analysis, with potential implications for building more robust and compositional AI systems.

#### 5.1. Methodological Advancements

- **Formal Definition of Support×Operation Factors and Injective Evaluation:** The introduction of a "support×operation" definition for factors, combined with a "leave-one-cell-out" metric and Hungarian injective binding, provides a rigorous framework for evaluating compositional generalization in vision encoders. This framework explicitly exposes "operation laundering," a critical failure mode that can be obscured by traditional, non-injective metrics. This methodological advancement helps in more accurately diagnosing the representational capabilities of frozen encoders.
- **Novel SO-OPF Carrier Design:** The proposed SO-OPF carrier, which factors cell energy into support salience and a competitive operation posterior, offers a principled way to separate "where" a change occurs from "what" the change is. Its performance, particularly its ability to outperform dense carriers and reduce laundering gaps under matched supervision, demonstrates its effectiveness in routing compositional information.

#### 5.2. Insights into Encoder Capabilities

- **Compositional Recovery on Synthetic and Natural Images:** The study demonstrates that frozen DINOv3 and SigLIP2 features, when combined with the SO-OPF readout, are capable of composing held-out spatial-visual bindings on controlled synthetic datasets (Shapes3D-Extended) and natural images (Image-disjoint COCO). This indicates that these encoders implicitly encode compositional information that can be effectively read out with the right architectural design.
- **Feasibility of Weakly Supervised Assignment Recovery:** The "learned Q" results show that a significant portion of the factorial grid structure can be recovered from flat observed-cell labels alone, without explicit axis-level supervision. This suggests a path towards discovering compositional structure with minimal supervision.
- **Identification of Representational Boundaries:** The detailed analysis on the MuJoCo dataset highlights a critical boundary where compositional routing fails, particularly for material-space operations (hue and inversion) compared to pixel-space overlays (pattern). This finding, consistent across DINOv3 and SigLIP2, is significant because it exposes specific limitations of current frozen representations or intervention types, rather than hiding them. Such boundaries are crucial for guiding future research in developing more robust and universally compositional visual representations.

#### 5.3. Potential Impact

- **Improved Analysis of Foundation Models:** The SO-OPF framework offers a powerful tool for researchers to probe and understand the compositional capacities and limitations of large self-supervised vision models, aiding in the development of more transparent and interpretable AI systems.
- **Advancing World Models and Embodied AI:** In applications like predictive world models or embodied AI, where agents need to independently manipulate and reason about scene attributes, the ability to compositionally understand "where" and "what" changes occur is fundamental. SO-OPF's explicit binding of spatial regions and operations could contribute to building more capable and reliable systems.
- **Guiding Encoder Development:** By systematically revealing the strengths and weaknesses of different encoders and intervention types, this research can inform the design of future self-supervised learning objectives and architectures that are explicitly optimized for compositional understanding across diverse visual domains and transformations.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Directions

The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also suggest future research avenues:
- The "learned Q" setting, while weakly supervised, still benefits from known grid dimensions and intervention construction. It does not claim unsupervised representation learning or full identifiability of underlying factors.
- The MuJoCo boundary, while identified across two encoders, does not definitively isolate the cause (e.g., intervention geometry, encoder limitations). Future work could explore encoders trained with renderer-diverse augmentation or investigate more semantic operations (e.g., object removal) and unstructured supports.
- The current operations are low-level appearance edits, and supports are predefined. Moving towards discovering spatial decompositions and handling more complex, semantic actions would further advance the field.

In conclusion, this work provides a valuable methodology and a concrete readout architecture for assessing and enhancing compositional understanding in frozen vision encoders, contributing to both the diagnostic tools for AI systems and the foundational understanding of visual representations.
