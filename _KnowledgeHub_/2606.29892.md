# Trust Your Instincts: Confidence-Driven Test-Time RL for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Fudan University researchers developed T2VLA, a framework that enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to achieve self-bootstrapping policy improvement without requiring external reward feedback. It leverages intrinsic generation confidence as an evaluative signal for autonomous policy refinement, leading to substantial performance gains across diverse robotic manipulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models heavily depend on costly and difficult-to-obtain external environmental feedback or predefined success signals.
- Bridging the 'Era of Experience' gap from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to VLA models is challenging, as continuous robotic actions lack a discrete ground truth for straightforward self-verification.
- Reliance on external rewards restricts VLA models from autonomous adaptation and continuous self-improvement in novel or changing environments where explicit reward signals are unavailable.

## Method

- **T2VLA** (Test-time VLA) is an architecture-agnostic framework that replaces external environmental rewards with ==intrinsic rewards== derived entirely from the model's ==generation confidence==.
- It employs ==confidence-driven dual expert bootstrapping==, which autonomously mines and maintains high-quality reference trajectories from exploratory rollouts using task-conditioned ==local pseudo-experts== and a ==dynamic global expert pool==.
- Guidance from these dual experts is translated into an intrinsic reward using ==DTW-based hybrid similarity==, combined with a ==KL penalty==, for robust policy optimization via ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==.

## Results

- T2VLA improved the OpenVLA-OFT baseline on the LIBERO benchmark by an average of **+6.2%** absolute success rate, reaching **97.2%**, surpassing state-of-the-art supervised fine-tuning models and external-critic-dependent methods.
- It achieved significant gains for continuous flow-matching VLAs, with a **+24.2%** absolute improvement for π₀, and boosted performance on the bimanual RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark by an average of **+21.3%** absolute success rate.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of dual expert bootstrapping, optimal expert pool capacity, DTW for trajectory alignment, and dynamic expert fusion in achieving stable and high-performance policy improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA models possess intrinsic evaluative capabilities through their generation confidence (e.g., mean log-probability for discrete actions, denoising likelihood for continuous actions), which empirically correlates strongly with task success rates.
> - A dual expert bootstrapping mechanism, combining an immediate local pseudo-expert with a stable dynamic global expert pool, is crucial for balanced exploration and stable policy optimization.
> - Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is essential for robustly aligning and comparing continuous action trajectories, effectively neutralizing temporal misalignments and accurately measuring spatial similarity for reward calculation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in embodied artificial intelligence, unifying multimodal perception, reasoning, and control by mapping visual observations and natural language instructions to physical actions. Their development follows the advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Current methods for training VLAs largely rely on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or behavioral cloning, which can be limited by the high costs of data collection and generalization bottlenecks.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as an approach to push VLA capabilities beyond static imitation learning, addressing some of the data scarcity and generalization challenges. However, existing RL approaches for VLAs typically necessitate external environmental feedback. This feedback often comes in the form of predefined success signals from simulators, human annotators, or external Large Language Models (LLMs), thereby confining the efficacy of these methods to scenarios where such explicit supervision is available.

The broader research landscape is moving towards paradigms where models can learn autonomously from their own unannotated experiences, often referred to as the "Era of Experience." This shift has seen some success in other domains, such as test-time reinforcement learning for VLMs in mathematics and coding tasks, where models can self-optimize using unlabeled trajectories and mechanisms like majority voting for self-verification.

A critical gap exists in extending this test-time learning success to VLA models. Unlike discrete problems such as math, robotic manipulation involves generating continuous action sequences and trajectories, where multiple divergent paths can lead to the same goal. This complexity eliminates a straightforward ground truth for self-reflection and verification, making direct application of VLM test-time RL challenging.

This paper addresses this gap by introducing T²VLA (Test-time VLA), an architecture-agnostic framework that enables VLA models to achieve self-bootstrapping policy improvement without relying on external reward feedback. It distinguishes itself by demonstrating that VLA models possess intrinsic evaluative capabilities through their generation confidence, which can substitute for external verification. By developing a method to leverage these internal signals, the work contributes to the advancement of autonomous and scalable VLA policy optimization, reducing dependence on labor-intensive reward engineering and specialized simulation environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to achieve autonomous self-improvement and continuous policy refinement *without* requiring external environmental feedback or predefined success signals. This aims to overcome a fundamental limitation in current reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for VLAs.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key observations and challenges:

1. **Reliance on External Rewards:** Existing RL methods for VLA models are heavily dependent on external supervision signals. These signals, such as sparse binary success flags from simulators, dense proxy rewards engineered via auxiliary models, or human annotations, are costly to obtain, require careful design, and restrict the application of RL to predefined and often limited scenarios. This reliance prevents VLA models from achieving true autonomous adaptation in novel or changing environments where explicit reward signals may be unavailable or impractical to define.

2. **Bridging the "Era of Experience" Gap:** While the concept of models learning directly from their own unannotated trajectories (the "Era of Experience") has been validated in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for tasks like mathematics and coding through test-time RL, translating this success to embodied VLA tasks is challenging. VLM test-time RL often relies on a singular, discrete correct answer for self-verification. In contrast, robotic manipulation generates continuous action trajectories, where multiple successful sequences can exist, making a clear ground truth for self-reflection difficult to establish intrinsically.

3. **Untapped Internal Evaluative Capabilities:** The authors hypothesize that VLA models might possess internal evaluative capabilities that could serve as a substitute for external verification. Empirical analysis on discrete-action VLAs revealed a strong positive correlation between internal generation confidence (specifically, the length-normalized mean log-probability of generated trajectories) and task success rates. This observation provides the foundational motivation: if higher confidence correlates with success, this intrinsic signal could be leveraged for self-bootstrapping.

Based on this, the key challenges that T²VLA aims to address are:

- **Formulating an Effective Intrinsic Reward:** How to construct a trajectory-level intrinsic reward, R_self(τ), solely from the model's own internal signals without external verification. Raw generation confidence alone, while indicative, was found to be insufficient for stable policy optimization.
- **Reliable High-Quality Reference Extraction:** How to autonomously identify, extract, and maintain high-quality reference "expert" trajectories from the model’s own exploratory rollouts, given that external success labels are absent.
- **Robust Reward Design for Continuous Actions:** How to design a robust trajectory reward signal based on these internally established references, especially considering the variability in temporal horizons and the continuous nature of robotic actions.

The work's objective is to develop a framework that systematically addresses these challenges, enabling VLA models to learn and improve continuously in a self-supervised manner, moving beyond the constraints of explicitly supervised reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

T²VLA (Test-time VLA) is presented as an architecture-agnostic framework for self-bootstrapping policy optimization in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its core methodology involves replacing external environmental rewards with intrinsic rewards derived entirely from the model’s internal signals, thereby enabling continuous policy adaptation without explicit supervision. The framework operates in four sequential phases:

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Problem Formulation and Confidence Estimation

The robotic manipulation task is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where a VLA policy π_θ generates actions based on visual observations and language instructions. The traditional RL objective maximizes expected external return E[R_env(τ)]. T²VLA reformulates this to maximize E[R_self(τ)], where R_self(τ) is an intrinsic reward.

- **Length-Normalized Confidence Estimation:** To provide a reliable internal signal for identifying high-quality trajectories, the framework computes a confidence score for each generated rollout (τ_i).
 - **Discrete-Action VLAs (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT):** Confidence (c_disc_i) is defined as the mean log-probability of actions over the effective execution horizon (T_i), excluding padding tokens. This normalization ensures comparability across trajectories of different lengths.
 - **Continuous Flow-Based VLAs (e.g., π₀, GR00T):** Confidence (c_flow_i) is derived from the mean Gaussian likelihood of transitions along the denoising process over the effective rollout length (L_i), reflecting denoising consistency.

#### 3.2. Confidence-Driven Dual Expert Bootstrapping

This mechanism autonomously mines and maintains high-quality behavioral anchors directly from the model's exploratory rollouts, leveraging the estimated intrinsic confidence scores.

- **Task-Conditioned Local Pseudo-Expert Election:** For each specific language instruction (task l) within a batch of N sampled trajectories (D_l), the trajectory with the highest confidence score (τ*_local,l = arg max_τ_i∈D_l c_i) is designated as the Local Pseudo-Expert. This expert provides an immediate, on-policy target for optimization.
- **Dynamic Global Expert Pool (P_l):** To ensure training stability and preserve historical high-quality behaviors, a task-conditioned dynamic Global Expert Pool is maintained. This pool acts as a priority-based memory buffer (with a capacity K, typically K=5). Only newly elected Local Pseudo-Experts are eligible for inclusion. The pool retains the top-K historical experts based on their confidence scores, ensuring the knowledge base remains high-quality and relevant to the evolving policy. This prevents over-reliance on potentially suboptimal individual batch rollouts.

#### 3.3. DTW-Based Hybrid Similarity Reward

This module translates the guidance from the dual experts into an informative intrinsic reward signal for arbitrary exploratory rollouts.

- **Trajectory Alignment via Dynamic Time Warping (DTW):** Robotic action sequences often exhibit temporal variations (e.g., pacing differences, phase shifts). Euclidean distance, which enforces strict point-wise matching, fails to capture true structural similarity under such variations. T²VLA employs Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) to non-linearly align trajectories based on their spatial morphology rather than strict temporal indices.
 - Action dimensions (continuous end-effector poses) are first min-max normalized to [0, 1].
 - DTW calculates a cumulative distance (D_DTW) by finding an optimal warping path.
 - A length-invariant similarity score (Sim_DTW) is then computed, normalized to the range (0, 1]. This provides a robust measure of similarity between exploratory trajectories and expert references.
- **Dynamic Hybrid Reward Design:** For each exploratory trajectory (τ_i) for a given task (l), a hybrid similarity reward (r_sim_i) is computed:
 - `r_sim_i = w * Sim_DTW(τ_i, τ*_local,l) + (1 - w) * max_τ_p∈P_l Sim_DTW(τ_i, τ_p)`
 - The interpolation weight `w ∈ [0, 1]` dynamically balances the influence of the local expert and the global pool. `w` is calculated using a Min-Max scaling of the local expert's confidence (c*_local,l) relative to the minimum (c_min,l) and maximum (c_max,l) confidence scores within the current global pool: `w = clip((c*_local,l - c_min,l) / (c_max,l - c_min,l + ε), 0, 1)`. This ensures that highly confident local discoveries contribute more, while uncertain batches leverage stable historical expertise.
- **KL Penalty:** A Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalty is added to the final proxy reward (`r_i`) to prevent excessive deviation from the initial policy (π_ref, the SFT model) and maintain a valid behavioral distribution. `r_i = r_sim_i - β * Σ D_KL(π_θ(·|s_i,t) || π_ref(·|s_i,t))`.

#### 3.4. Policy Optimization via GRPO

The VLA policy parameters (θ) are updated using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

- **Advantage Estimation:** GRPO computes a baseline-free advantage (A_i) for each trajectory by group-normalizing the batch of proxy rewards: `A_i = (r_i - μ(r)) / (σ(r) + ε)`. This eliminates the need for a separate critic network.
- **Policy Update:** The policy is updated by maximizing a clipped surrogate objective (similar to PPO), leveraging the group-normalized advantages. This iteratively aligns the policy with the self-bootstrapped expert guidance, completing the continuous self-improvement loop.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several key findings regarding the effectiveness and versatility of the T²VLA framework:

#### 4.1. Empirical Validation of Intrinsic Confidence as a Signal

- **Confidence-Success Correlation:** Empirical analysis on discrete-action VLAs (OpenVLA-OFT, StarVLA) consistently demonstrated a strong positive correlation between internal generation confidence (mean action log-probability) and task success rate. This correlation was observed across different initial policy strengths, interaction environments (simulated and world-model-generated observations), and also for continuous-action VLAs (GR00T) using denoising likelihoods. This finding is fundamental, validating the use of intrinsic model confidence as a reliable indicator for autonomous self-bootstrapping. The correlation was shown to strengthen during policy optimization, indicating its stability as a signal.

#### 4.2. Performance on the LIBERO Benchmark

- **Discrete-Action VLAs (OpenVLA-OFT):** T²VLA achieved consistent and substantial gains across all four LIBERO task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long). It improved the OpenVLA-OFT baseline by an average of +6.2% absolute success rate, reaching 97.2%. This performance surpassed state-of-the-art supervised fine-tuning (SFT) models like UniVLA (95.2%) and outperformed concurrent test-time training approaches such as EVOLVE-VLA (95.8%), which relies on an external critic. The performance gap to oracle-guided RL (SimpleVLA-RL, 99.1%) was substantially reduced, demonstrating efficacy without external rewards.
- **Continuous-Action VLAs (π₀, π₀.₅):** T²VLA demonstrated architecture-agnostic applicability by yielding significant improvements on continuous flow-matching models. It achieved a +24.2% absolute improvement in average success rate for π₀ and boosted π₀.₅ performance on the Goal suite by +7.2%.

#### 4.3. Performance on the RoboTwin 2.0 Benchmark (Bimanual Control)

- T²VLA consistently outperformed the SFT baseline on the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, which features bimanual coordination tasks with varying execution horizons. The framework achieved an average absolute improvement of +21.3% in success rate. Notably, it showed a significant leap on short-horizon tasks (e.g., Beat Hammer from 28.1% to 68.0%) and an 18.4% improvement on complex long-horizon tasks like Stack Bowls. This indicates its scalability to high-dimensional control scenarios without environmental rewards.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Dual Expert Bootstrapping:** The study confirmed the complementarity of the Local Pseudo-Expert and the Global Expert Pool. Relying solely on the local expert led to higher variance, while only using the global pool slowed adaptation. The dual-expert mechanism achieved the best performance (95.3% on LIBERO-Long), balancing aggressive exploration with stability.
- **Expert Pool Capacity:** A moderate capacity (K=5) for the global expert pool was found to be optimal. Smaller pools (K=3) offered insufficient behavioral diversity, while larger pools (K=10) retained stale trajectories, diluting the guidance signal.
- **DTW for Trajectory Alignment:** Empirical evidence demonstrated DTW's superiority over Euclidean distance for aligning continuous action trajectories. DTW effectively neutralized temporal misalignments and recovered robust spatial congruence, which rigid point-wise matching failed to achieve, thus preventing high-quality exploratory rollouts from being spuriously penalized.
- **Smooth Expert Fusion:** The dynamic Min-Max Confidence Scaling for the interpolation weight (`w`) was crucial for training stability (95.3%). Rigid or hard-gating strategies led to early policy collapse due to abrupt gradient shifts, highlighting the necessity of smooth integration.

#### 4.5. Applicability under Challenging Settings

- **Bootstrapping Threshold:** Under an extreme 1-shot data scarcity setting, T²VLA improved performance on several LIBERO suites (approx. +20% absolute gains). However, performance degraded on the complex Long-horizon suite (from 17.3% to 11.0%). This highlights a "prior threshold": an excessively weak initial policy can generate uninformative exploratory rollouts, causing similarity rewards to degrade into noise and policy optimization to collapse.
- **World-Model-Generated Interactions:** T²VLA showed applicability when using action-conditioned observations synthesized by an OpenSora world model, improving the success rate on LIBERO-Spatial from 61.2% to 63.3%. This indicates its potential beyond direct interaction with standard simulators.
- **Learning Dynamics:** Visualized learning curves (e.g., on LIBERO-Goal 1-shot) demonstrated a consistent upward trend in both training and evaluation success rates, confirming stable and progressive policy refinement driven exclusively by intrinsic rewards.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The T²VLA framework represents a significant step towards autonomous and scalable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model development, with several potential impacts on the field of embodied artificial intelligence:

1. **Breaking Free from External Rewards:** The most substantial impact is the elimination of reliance on external environmental reward signals. This addresses a core bottleneck in VLA reinforcement learning, which traditionally requires costly and labor-intensive reward engineering, pre-defined success metrics, or human annotation. By demonstrating effective policy optimization purely from intrinsic, model-generated signals, T²VLA enables VLAs to learn and improve in scenarios where external feedback is unavailable, ill-defined, or difficult to obtain.

2. **Enabling Autonomous Self-Improvement:** This work pushes VLA models further into the "Era of Experience," where agents learn continuously from their own interactions and observations without direct human supervision. This self-bootstrapping capability is crucial for developing truly autonomous robotic systems that can adapt and generalize in dynamic, open-ended environments.

3. **Broad Applicability and Scalability:** T²VLA's architecture-agnostic design, demonstrated across diverse VLA paradigms (discrete-action token generation, continuous flow-matching, denoising diffusion models) and challenging benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0, RoboCasa), suggests wide applicability. This versatility means that the benefits of intrinsic reward-driven learning can be leveraged across a broad spectrum of existing and future VLA architectures.

4. **Reduced Data Annotation Burden and Cost:** By removing the need for explicit reward labels, T²VLA offers a pathway to significantly reduce the data annotation burden associated with training advanced robotic policies. This can accelerate research and development in embodied AI by making large-scale data collection and policy optimization more feasible and economical.

5. **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** Models capable of continuous self-refinement based on internal signals are likely to be more robust to environmental variations and better equipped to adapt to novel situations. This could lead to more resilient robotic systems that can perform effectively even in deployment settings that differ from their training environments.

6. **New Research Directions:**
 - **Intrinsic Motivation Research:** T²VLA highlights the potency of intrinsic model confidence as an evaluative signal, opening avenues for further research into other forms of intrinsic motivation and self-supervision in embodied AI.
 - **Curriculum Learning and Robust Initialization:** The identification of a "bootstrapping threshold" emphasizes the importance of a sufficient initial policy competence. This motivates research into robust initialization strategies, pre-training techniques, or curriculum learning approaches that can ensure a foundational level of skill, even for complex, long-horizon tasks.
 - **Integration with World Models:** The successful application of T²VLA with world-model-generated interactions suggests potential for creating fully self-contained learning loops, where VLA policies improve in entirely synthetic environments generated by learned world models.
 - **Real-World Deployment:** While primarily demonstrated in simulation, the framework's self-bootstrapping nature is highly relevant for future physical robot deployments, where external reward signals are often challenging to implement.

In conclusion, T²VLA provides a foundational framework for intrinsic-reward-driven reinforcement learning in VLA models, representing a tangible step towards autonomous policy evolution and mitigating the resource-intensive requirements of traditional RL.
