# V-Zero: Answer-Label-Free On-Policy Distillation with Contrastive Evidence Gating for Fine-Grained Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> V-Zero introduces an answer-label-free on-policy distillation framework with contrastive evidence gating to enhance fine-grained visual reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models. This approach achieved an average performance gain of 3.1 points across four perception benchmarks and demonstrated over 5 times faster training than SFT and 10 times faster than RL methods.

## Problem

- Agentic visual reasoning methods based on Reinforcement Learning (RL) incur high computational costs due to extensive exploration and require complex, predefined reward rules.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) approaches for fine-grained visual reasoning demand large-scale, costly textual annotations and risk catastrophic forgetting of general MLLM capabilities.
- Standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD) provides only token-level correction and lacks an explicit mechanism to assess the correctness or visual grounding of an entire reasoning trajectory.

## Method

- **V-Zero** reinterprets standard OPD as an asymmetric, negative-free stop-gradient alignment objective that is then augmented.
- A ==contrastive evidence gating== mechanism is introduced, where a teacher model re-evaluates student-generated trajectories under both task-relevant (positive) and irrelevant (negative) visual evidence views.
- A 'contrastive evidence gate' is computed for each trajectory, weighting the on-policy distillation signal based on the trajectory's visual grounding, thus enabling trajectory-level discrimination without requiring human-annotated answer labels.

## Results

- V-Zero achieved an average performance gain of **3.1** points (from **75.3** to **79.2**) across VStar, HR-4K, HR-8K, and ZoomBench fine-grained perception benchmarks.
- The framework demonstrated significant training efficiency, being over 5 times faster than supervised fine-tuning methods and over 10 times faster than reinforcement learning baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the contrastive evidence gate improved performance, and increasing the rollout group size from 4 to 8 further boosted the perception-average score from **78.1** to **79.2**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard On-Policy Distillation, while providing token-level correction, lacks a mechanism to effectively evaluate the overall visual grounding and reliability of an entire reasoning trajectory.
> - Contrasting a model's log-probabilities for a given reasoning trajectory under distinct positive and negative visual evidence views provides a strong, answer-label-free signal for trajectory-level visual grounding.
> - Modulating the on-policy learning objective with a dynamically computed, stop-gradient 'contrastive evidence gate' allows for efficient training that prioritizes visually well-grounded reasoning paths.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has highlighted the need for improved fine-grained visual reasoning capabilities. Unlike general visual understanding, fine-grained reasoning requires MLLMs to inspect local details, identify specific visual evidence, and ground their reasoning in particular image regions.

Current approaches to enhance fine-grained visual reasoning in MLLMs broadly fall into two categories:
- **Agentic methods (e.g., "thinking with images")**: These methods integrate visual search and reasoning by allowing models to perform visual operations and gather evidence during inference. While promising, they often rely on Reinforcement Learning (RL), which is characterized by costly exploration and the need for predefined, verifiable reward rules. Examples include DeepEyes, Thyme, and Pixel Reasoner.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: This approach involves fine-tuning MLLMs on large-scale annotated image-text data. SFT can achieve strong results, but it necessitates extensive textual supervision and carries the risk of catastrophic forgetting, where the model loses previously acquired general capabilities. An example is Zooming without Zooming (ZwZ).

On-Policy Distillation (OPD) has emerged as an alternative, offering dense supervision on trajectories sampled from the student model itself, potentially avoiding the challenges of reward-based RL and offline SFT. However, standard OPD typically provides token-level correction without explicit mechanisms to assess the correctness of an entire reasoning trajectory, meaning it may optimize locally plausible but ultimately erroneous paths.

This work, V-Zero, aims to advance fine-grained visual reasoning by addressing the limitations of existing methods. It proposes an answer-label-free approach that leverages OPD but enhances it with a novel contrastive evidence gating mechanism. This positions V-Zero as an efficient alternative that seeks to combine the benefits of on-policy learning with a trajectory-level discriminative signal, without the need for extensive answer labels or the computational overhead of agentic tools at inference time.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method for improving fine-grained visual reasoning in MLLMs that overcomes the significant drawbacks of current paradigms. Specifically, the authors aim to answer the question: "Can visual reasoning be improved without costly RL exploration, large-scale textual answer labels, or substantially disrupting the original capabilities of MLLMs?"

This objective is motivated by several identified limitations:
- **Costly RL Exploration**: Agentic visual reasoning methods, while effective, often rely on reinforcement learning. RL typically involves extensive exploration of the action space, leading to high computational costs and requiring complex hand-designed rules to verify the correctness of actions or generated sequences. This makes such methods resource-intensive to develop and train.
- **Dependence on Large-Scale Textual Supervision for SFT**: Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods, which achieve competitive results, demand large datasets of annotated image-text pairs, including detailed reasoning traces or final answers. The creation of such datasets is resource-intensive, and models fine-tuned in this manner may suffer from catastrophic forgetting, losing their general capabilities due to specialization on the supervised task.
- **Limitations of Standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD)**: While OPD offers a promising way to learn from self-generated trajectories, the authors' analysis reinterprets it as a "negative-free stop-gradient alignment objective." This perspective reveals a core limitation: standard OPD provides effective token-level correction but lacks "trajectory-level discrimination." This means that if a student model begins to generate an erroneous reasoning path, the teacher in standard OPD can only provide local corrections based on that prefix, without evaluating the overall correctness or relevance of the full trajectory to the ultimate task. This can lead to optimizing for locally plausible continuations even if the trajectory as a whole is diverging from a correct answer.

To address these motivations, V-Zero aims to provide a solution that is:
1. **Answer-label-free**: Eliminating the need for costly human annotation of textual answers.
2. **Computationally efficient**: Faster than both RL and SFT methods.
3. **Generalizable**: Capable of improving fine-grained reasoning without significantly degrading the MLLM's existing general capabilities.
4. **Trajectory-level discriminative**: Introducing a mechanism to evaluate the reliability of entire reasoning trajectories, rather than just individual tokens.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

V-Zero proposes an answer-label-free framework that enhances On-Policy Distillation (OPD) with a contrastive evidence gating mechanism for fine-grained visual reasoning. The approach is structured around a novel interpretation of OPD and the integration of teacher-side visual evidence comparison.

#### 3.1. Revisiting OPD as Negative-Free Stop-Gradient Alignment

The paper first reinterprets standard OPD as an asymmetric alignment objective. In OPD, a student policy ($\pi_s$) samples trajectories from prompts. A teacher model ($\pi_t$) then provides dense token-level supervision by generating targets for these student-induced states. The student is optimized to match the teacher's next-token predictions. This is formalized as minimizing a reverse-KL divergence between the student's and teacher's distributions over the next token.

The authors present a view where this process is an "asymmetric alignment" because the student is the online branch being optimized, while the teacher provides a fixed, stop-gradient target. When privileged information (e.g., a specific visual crop) is available to the teacher but not the student, the teacher's view ($v_t$) is augmented. The core limitation identified is that this alignment, even with privileged information, remains "negative-free." It provides local token-level correction but lacks an explicit mechanism to score the overall correctness or visual grounding of a *full trajectory*. This means standard OPD cannot distinguish between visually well-grounded trajectories and those that might be locally coherent but ultimately deviate from task-relevant visual evidence.

#### 3.2. V-Zero Framework

V-Zero addresses this limitation by introducing a contrastive evidence gating mechanism that operates at the trajectory level. The overall process is as follows:

1. **Student Rollouts**: Given a prompt (question + full image), the student model ($\pi_s$) generates a group of $G$ "sibling rollouts" (trajectories). Each trajectory consists of a sequence of tokens.

2. **Teacher Evidence Views**: For each student-sampled trajectory, the teacher model ($\pi_t$) re-evaluates the same token sequence under two distinct visual evidence views, in addition to the original full image:
 - **Positive View ($z_+$)**: A regional crop from the original image that specifically preserves task-relevant visual evidence, as identified during dataset curation.
 - **Negative View ($z_-$)**: An equal-sized crop randomly sampled from an irrelevant region of the image, after the original image has been downsampled by a factor of 2.

 The teacher computes the log-probabilities of each token in the student's trajectory, conditioned on the original prompt, the history of generated tokens, and *each of these additional visual views*. This yields $\ell^{(g)}_{+,k}$ for the positive view and $\ell^{(g)}_{-,k}$ for the negative view for each token $k$ in trajectory $g$.

3. **Contrastive Evidence Gating**:
 - **Token-level Evidence Gap ($\Delta^{(g)}_k$)**: For each token, the difference between the positive and negative view log-probabilities is calculated: $\Delta^{(g)}_k = \ell^{(g)}_{+,k} - \ell^{(g)}_{-,k}$. A larger positive gap indicates stronger teacher support for that token when relevant visual evidence is present.
 - **Trajectory-level Evidence Score ($p^{(g)}$)**: These token-level gaps are averaged over the entire trajectory to yield a single score $p^{(g)}$, representing the overall visual evidence support for that trajectory.
 - **Normalization**: The raw evidence scores $p_x = (p^{(1)}, \ldots, p^{(G)})$ from the group of sibling rollouts for a given prompt are normalized (mean-shifted and standard-deviation-scaled) to produce an "evidence advantage" $a^{(g)}$. This quantifies whether a specific rollout is better visually grounded than its siblings for that prompt.
 - **Contrastive Evidence Gate ($w^{(g)}$)**: The evidence advantage $a^{(g)}$ is then transformed into a non-negative, stop-gradient "contrastive evidence gate" $w^{(g)}$. This gate is clipped to ensure stability during training ($w_{min} \le w^{(g)} \le w_{max}$). The gate value modulates the learning signal: rollouts with stronger visual grounding (higher $a^{(g)}$) receive a larger weight, while poorly grounded ones receive a smaller weight. The stop-gradient ensures that the gating mechanism itself does not receive gradients, preventing direct optimization of the gate value.

4. **V-Zero Objective**: After computing the trajectory-level gate, the negative visual view is discarded. The training objective remains a reverse-KL minimization, but it is now gated by $w^{(g)}$. The student is optimized to match the teacher's distribution when the teacher is conditioned on the *positive visual evidence view* ($z_+$). The surrogate loss used in practice incorporates the detached positive-view sampled log-ratio score, scaled by the contrastive evidence gate $w^{(g)}$.

This methodology allows V-Zero to construct dense on-policy supervision that is sensitive to the visual grounding of entire reasoning trajectories, all without requiring annotated textual answer labels or external reward signals. The only specialized input needed for training is the pair of positive and negative visual evidence views, which are not used at inference time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on multiple visual reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that V-Zero consistently improves fine-grained visual reasoning capabilities while maintaining generalization and significantly enhancing training efficiency.

**Performance on Fine-Grained Visual Reasoning Benchmarks:**
- V-Zero, using a Qwen3.5-4B base model, demonstrated improvements across all four fine-grained perception benchmarks compared to the Qwen3.5-4B backbone.
 - **VStar**: +4.7 points improvement.
 - **HR-4K**: +3.4 points improvement.
 - **HR-8K**: +2.0 points improvement.
 - **ZoomBench**: +5.5 points improvement.
- The average performance gain across these perception benchmarks was 3.1 points (from 75.3 to 79.2).
- V-Zero achieved top-tier performance among visually grounded reasoning systems, even considering that these systems often use different backbones. It recorded the best scores on HR-4K, HR-8K, ZoomBench, and MMStar, indicating its competitiveness with specialized training pipelines that might rely on RL or extensive SFT.
- The improvements were achieved without using annotated textual answer labels. The training signal relied solely on contrasting paired positive and negative visual evidence views. The student model performs standard full-image inference at test time, without requiring additional tool calls or visual evidence crops.

**Training Efficiency:**
- V-Zero demonstrated substantial improvements in training speed.
 - It was more than **5 times faster** than previous supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods like ZwZ.
 - It was more than **10 times faster** than reinforcement learning (RL) baselines like DeepEyes.
- V-Zero completed its main training runs in 4.8 hours on 8 NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 96G GPUs, while ZwZ (8xH100) took approximately 1 day and DeepEyes (8xH100) took approximately 2 days. The authors note that the speedups are conservative, given V-Zero used less powerful GPUs (RTX PRO 6000 vs. H100).

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effect of Contrastive Evidence Gating**: Removing the contrastive evidence gate led to a decrease in perception-average performance (from 79.2 to 78.0), and degraded scores on VStar, HR-4K, and ZoomBench. This indicates that the gate, which weights rollouts based on their group-relative visual evidence scores, is beneficial for emphasizing visually well-grounded trajectories, especially in scenarios with constrained visual information.
- **Teacher and Student Size**: Using a larger teacher model (Qwen3.5-27B) with a Qwen3.5-4B student yielded a higher perception average (79.2) compared to using a Qwen3.5-9B teacher (78.9) with the same 4B student. This suggests that a more capable teacher can provide a stronger distillation signal.
- **Rollout Group Size**: Increasing the group size of sibling rollouts from G=4 to G=8 improved the perception-average score (from 78.1 to 79.2) and gains on HR-4K, HR-8K, and ZoomBench. This supports the hypothesis that a larger group provides more informative within-prompt comparisons for the contrastive evidence gate.
- **Training Step**: The perception average improved substantially from the base model (75.3 at Step 0) after training with V-Zero, peaking at step 60 (79.2). This indicates that the proposed method effectively strengthens fine-grained visual reasoning over the course of training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The V-Zero framework offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of multimodal large language models and fine-grained visual reasoning:

- **Answer-Label-Free Training**: A key innovation is the elimination of the requirement for annotated textual answer labels. By relying solely on paired positive and negative visual evidence views for its teacher-side signal, V-Zero significantly reduces the manual annotation burden and associated costs typically involved in training MLLMs for complex reasoning tasks. This can accelerate research and development in areas where high-quality textual annotations are scarce or difficult to obtain.

- **Enhanced Training Efficiency**: V-Zero demonstrates a substantial improvement in training speed, being over 5 times faster than supervised fine-tuning methods and over 10 times faster than reinforcement learning baselines. This efficiency makes it more feasible to train and iterate on MLLMs for fine-grained tasks, potentially broadening access to advanced visual reasoning capabilities for researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources.

- **Improved Visual Grounding without Inference-Time Tools**: The method improves the MLLM's ability to ground its reasoning in specific image regions during training, yet the student model operates in a standard full-image inference setting without requiring any additional tool calls, region proposals, or visual evidence crops at test time. This ensures a streamlined and efficient inference process, avoiding the overhead often associated with agentic visual reasoning systems.

- **Trajectory-Level Discrimination for OPD**: By introducing the contrastive evidence gating mechanism, V-Zero addresses a recognized limitation of standard On-Policy Distillation—the absence of explicit trajectory-level discrimination. This mechanism allows the model to learn not just local token-level corrections but also to evaluate and prioritize entire reasoning paths based on their visual grounding, leading to more robust and accurate reasoning.

- **Generalizability**: The experimental results indicate that V-Zero preserves the general capabilities of the base MLLM while enhancing fine-grained visual reasoning. This mitigates the risk of catastrophic forgetting, which can be a concern with highly specialized fine-tuning approaches.

The V-Zero framework represents a step towards developing more autonomous and efficient methods for training MLLMs in visually intensive tasks. Its answer-label-free nature and high computational efficiency could facilitate broader adoption and application of advanced visual reasoning in various domains, including visual question answering, image understanding, and potentially even fields requiring detailed analysis of visual data, such as medical imaging or autonomous driving.
