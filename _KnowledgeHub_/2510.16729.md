# Vision-Centric 4D Occupancy Forecasting and Planning via Implicit Residual World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Implicit Residual World Model (IR-WM) improves vision-centric 4D occupancy forecasting and planning for autonomous driving by predicting only dynamic environmental changes instead of reconstructing entire scenes. This approach enhances efficiency and achieves state-of-the-art results on nuScenes, reducing planning L2 error to 0.53m and collision rate to 0.17%.

## Problem

- Existing vision-centric world models for autonomous driving inefficiently allocate model capacity by reconstructing entire future scenes, including redundant static backgrounds.
- This full-scene regeneration approach limits the model's ability to effectively encode salient, dynamic scene context from observations.
- Accumulation of prediction errors over multiple timesteps is a challenge in autoregressive environmental forecasting.

## Method

- Developed an **IR-WM** (==Implicit Residual World Model==) that models the current world state and autoregressively predicts only ==residual changes== for future states, conditioned on ego-vehicle actions.
- Incorporated a ==Feature Alignment Module== to correct semantic and dynamic misalignments, mitigating error accumulation during autoregressive rollout.
- Utilized auxiliary ==vision-centric 4D occupancy forecasting== to supervise ==Bird's-Eye-View (BEV)== representations, enhancing semantic richness and geometric fidelity for robust planning.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance in 4D occupancy forecasting on nuScenes, with an IoU_c of **40.8** and weighted ~IoU_f of **38.2** for inflated movable objects, surpassing previous methods by approximately **1 point**.
- Demonstrated superior end-to-end trajectory planning, reducing the average L2 error to **0.53m** (from **0.85m** for Drive-OccWorld) and the average collision rate to **0.17%** (from **0.29%**) on the nuScenes validation set.
- Ablation studies confirmed that future BEV features from the world model substantially improved planning accuracy, reducing the L2 average error from **0.87m** (Fully Decoupled) to **0.53m** (Semi-Coupled).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Focusing on predicting residual changes in the environment, rather than full-scene reconstruction, significantly improves model efficiency and its capacity to understand dynamic elements.
> - Temporal alignment of features using ego-motion transforms and a dedicated feature alignment module is crucial for maintaining consistency and reducing error accumulation in autoregressive forecasting.
> - Implicit BEV feature forecasting within a world model provides strong supervisory signals for trajectory planning, yielding substantial accuracy gains compared to fully decoupled approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving systems are increasingly adopting end-to-end architectures that directly map raw sensor data to driving commands. A core component of these systems is the "world model," which aims to understand the current environment and predict its future evolution under potential ego-vehicle actions. Existing vision-centric world models often cast future environment forecasting as a task of full-scene reconstruction. This typically involves regenerating the entire scene, including static backgrounds, for both current and future states. This approach is identified as inefficient because it allocates significant model capacity to redundant information (static backgrounds), potentially limiting the capacity available for encoding salient scene context from current and past observations.

This work addresses this inefficiency by proposing a shift from full-scene regeneration to modeling the current state and subsequently predicting only the "residual" changes—the dynamic elements and alterations—in the environment. This positions the proposed Implicit Residual World Model (IR-WM) as an advancement within the field of vision-centric world models for autonomous driving, specifically targeting improved efficiency and accuracy in environmental forecasting. Furthermore, the paper contributes to the understanding of how occupancy forecasting can be coupled with trajectory planning in end-to-end autonomous driving systems, exploring different coupling schemes to optimize planning outcomes. It also builds upon recent advancements in 4D occupancy prediction and perception-planning integration, providing a refined methodology for spatiotemporal scene understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed inefficiency in existing vision-centric world models for autonomous driving. Many current models expend substantial computational and representational capacity on the full reconstruction of future scenes, redundantly modeling static backgrounds. This approach can limit a model's ability to effectively encode critical, dynamic scene context from visual observations.

The key objectives of this work are:

- **Develop an Implicit Residual World Model (IR-WM):** The central objective is to design a novel world model that moves beyond full-scene reconstruction. Instead, IR-WM aims to model the current world state effectively and then predict only the residual changes or evolution of this state, conditioned on ego-vehicle actions and scene context. This design is intended to allocate model capacity more efficiently, focusing on dynamic elements and significant changes.
- **Enhance Modeling Efficiency and Dynamics Understanding:** By focusing on residuals, the model is motivated to direct its capacity towards encoding salient scene context from visual observations and predicting dynamic changes, rather than static elements. This is expected to lead to improved understanding of temporal differences and environmental dynamics.
- **Improve Temporal Coherence and Alleviate Error Accumulation:** Grounding each future prediction in the preceding state, and incorporating an alignment module to correct semantic and dynamic misalignments, aims to ensure temporal consistency and mitigate the accumulation of prediction errors over multiple timesteps.
- **Leverage 4D Occupancy Forecasting for Richer Representations:** The model aims to use auxiliary vision-centric 4D occupancy forecasting to supervise the Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) representations. This is intended to enhance the semantic richness and geometric fidelity of both current and future states, providing a robust foundation for reliable downstream planning.
- **Investigate Forecasting–Planning Coupling Schemes:** A detailed objective is to study the various ways in which occupancy forecasting and trajectory planning can be coupled. This includes analyzing the impact of different coupling levels (tightly coupled, semi-coupled, fully decoupled) on planning accuracy and latency, aiming to identify an optimal integration strategy.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** The ultimate goal is for IR-WM to demonstrate superior performance in both 4D occupancy forecasting and end-to-end trajectory planning tasks on standard autonomous driving benchmarks, validating the effectiveness of the implicit residual modeling paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Implicit Residual World Model (IR-WM) is an end-to-end framework designed for vision-centric 4D occupancy forecasting and trajectory planning. It operates by encoding the current scene, autoregressively forecasting future states as residuals, and then decoding these states into task-specific outputs.

**A. Preliminary Framework**
The model adheres to a three-stage decomposition common in end-to-end planning with world models:
1. **Scene Context Encoding:** A scene encoder (E) processes historical and current camera observations (I) and ego trajectories (τ) to generate compact state representations (S), specifically Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) features.
2. **Action-Conditioned Future State Rollout:** An autoregressive future predictor (W) uses past states (S) and future ego trajectories (τ) to predict subsequent future states.
3. **Downstream Perception and Planning:** Task decoders (D*) convert the state representations into perception outputs (e.g., occupancy maps O) or planning outputs (e.g., future trajectories τ).

**B. Implicit Residual World Model (IR-WM) Architecture**
IR-WM consists of three main parts:

1. **Scene Encoding from Visual Observation:**
 - **Encoder:** The BEVFormer architecture serves as the scene encoder (E). It takes multi-view camera images (historical and current) as input.
 - **Output:** It extracts and transforms these visual inputs into BEV features (S_bev), which represent the current state of the environment. These features are then pushed into a streaming memory for subsequent future forecasting.

2. **Future Forecasting with Streaming Memory:**
 - **Autoregressive Predictor (W):** This component is built as a stack of transformer layers. Its purpose is to model context changes over time.
 - **Input:** At each timestep `t`, the predictor takes learnable BEV queries. It performs deformable self-attention and deformable temporal cross-attention to the streaming memory (S_bev from `t-m` to `t-1` frames). Ego-trajectory embeddings (τ from `t-m` to `t`) are also used to condition the predictions.
 - **Residual Prediction:** Instead of predicting the full future BEV state, W outputs a residual (∆S_bev_t) representing the predicted changes relative to the previous BEV state.
 - **Temporal Alignment:** For efficiency, deformable sampling is used in temporal cross-attention. Reference points are computed from ego-motion transforms (δτ_t), derived from (τ_t-1:t), to pre-align features across time before aggregation, effectively compensating for ego-motion.
 - **Feature Alignment Module:**
 - After adding the predicted residual (∆S_bev_t) to the previous BEV features (S_bev_t-1) to obtain an initial S_bev_t, a feature alignment module is applied.
 - This module aims to correct semantic and dynamic misalignments and alleviate error accumulation during autoregressive rollout.
 - Inspired by AdaNorm, it modulates the BEV features by dynamically generating scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters for layer normalization.
 - These parameters are generated by separate Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) from two sources:
 - Predicted semantic occupancy maps (O_align_t) for semantic alignment.
 - Ego-motion transformations (δτ_t) for dynamic alignment.
 - A lightweight MLP head generates O_align_t from S_bev_t, which is supervised by a cross-entropy loss (L_align).
 - The aligned BEV features (Ŝ_bev_t) are then pushed into the streaming memory for the next rollout step.

3. **4D Occupancy Forecasting and Planning (Task Decoders):**
 - **Occupancy Head (D_o):** An MLP-based head takes the BEV features (S_bev_t) and applies a channel-to-height mapping to lift them into a 3D vertical volume. This produces dense semantic occupancy (O_t), where each voxel encodes geometry and class information. Rolling this out over multiple future steps (0:f-1) yields 4D occupancy.
 - **Planning Head (D_p):** This head is responsible for ego-trajectory generation.
 - A learnable plan query is concatenated with a high-level command embedding.
 - This combined query is passed to a transformer decoder.
 - The decoder performs cross-attention over the current BEV features (S_bev_t) enhanced by an adapter (stack of convolutions for feature enhancement), to capture fine-grained scene context and attend to dynamic obstacles.
 - An MLP then outputs the ego translation (δτ_t+1), which is used to update the ego pose for the next timestep (τ_t+1 = τ_t + δτ_t+1).

**C. Interaction between Forecasting and Planning (Coupling Schemes)**
The study investigates three ways forecasting can influence planning:
- **Tightly Coupled:** Planning uses predicted semantic occupancy (O_t) to filter initial candidate trajectories (optimized via a safety-oriented cost function) before the planning head (D_p) predicts the final trajectory.
- **Semi-Coupled (IR-WM Default):** Planning relies solely on the generated BEV features (S_bev_0:f-1). The occupancy head serves only as auxiliary supervision and regularization, without directly guiding trajectory filtering during inference.
- **Fully Decoupled:** Trajectory prediction is performed only on the current BEV features (S_bev_0), with future BEV features and semantic occupancy serving solely as auxiliary supervision during training. No explicit future forecasting is required during inference for planning.

**D. Training Objective**
The model is jointly optimized using a combination of perception and planning losses:
- **Semantic Occupancy Supervision (L_occ):** A combination of cross-entropy loss (voxel-wise semantic classification), Lovász loss (IoU optimization), and binary occupancy loss (free/occupied space fidelity) is applied to the predicted occupancy maps (O_t) against ground truth (Ô_t) across all future steps.
- **Planning Supervision (L_plan):** This combines:
 - L2 regression loss for predicted ego trajectories (τ_t) against ground truth (Ô_τ_t).
 - A collision loss (L_coll) that penalizes predicted waypoints overlapping with occupied voxels in ground truth occupancy (Ô_t).
- **Joint Optimization:** The final loss (L) is a weighted sum of L_align, L_occ, and L_plan, with hyperparameters (e.g., λ_plan, λ_coll) balancing their contributions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates IR-WM on the nuScenes and nuScenes-Occupancy datasets for 4D occupancy forecasting and end-to-end trajectory planning.

**A. 4D Occupancy Forecasting**

1. **Movable Objects (GMO) Forecasting:**
 - **Inflated GMO (nuScenes):** IR-WM achieved IoU_c = 40.8, IoU_f = 37.2, and weighted `~IoU_f` = 38.2. These metrics surpass the previous state-of-the-art, Drive-OccWorld, by approximately 1 point across all measures, indicating enhanced capability in predicting the current distribution and future evolution of dynamic objects.
 - **Fine-Grained GMO (nuScenes-Occupancy):** For voxel-level predictions, IR-WM attained IoU_c = 16.2 and IoU_f = 14.5, outperforming previous methods like OccProphet (+0.8 on IoU_c and +3.8 on IoU_f), demonstrating robustness in capturing fine spatial details of dynamic agents.

2. **Movable & Static Objects (GMO & GSO) Forecasting:**
 - **Fine-Grained GMO & GSO (nuScenes-Occupancy):** IR-WM achieved IoU_c = 20.3 (overall), 15.2 (GMO), and 24.5 (GSO) for future predictions. These results represent improvements over Drive-OccWorld and OccProphet, demonstrating proficient joint modeling of both dynamic agents and static structures. The weighted future score `~IoU_f` also established a new benchmark.
 - **Fine-Grained Multi-Class Objects (MMO & MSO) Forecasting:** In the multi-class setting, IR-WM achieved mIoU_c = 15.2 and mIoU_f = 14.4, with a weighted `~mIoU_f` = 14.6. This performance exceeded Drive-OccWorld across all metrics. Per-class analysis indicated broad improvements, including notable gains for challenging categories like Pedestrian (+1.4) and Traffic cone (+3.2), suggesting IR-WM's ability to capture heterogeneous motion and fine-grained static context.

**B. End-to-End Planning**

- IR-WM achieved an average L2 error of 0.53 m for trajectory planning on the nuScenes validation set, a reduction compared to baselines like Drive-OccWorld (0.85 m) and GaussianAD (0.64 m).
- The long-horizon error at 3s was 0.85 m, demonstrating temporal consistency.
- The average collision rate was 0.17%, which is lower than Drive-OccWorld (0.29%) and other occupancy-based planners. These results suggest that IR-WM generates informative implicit representations, contributing to both accurate and safe planned trajectories.

**C. Ablation Studies and Analysis**

1. **Interaction between Forecasting and Planning:**
 - Forecasting performance was largely comparable across Tightly Coupled, Semi-Coupled, and Fully Decoupled variants.
 - The Semi-Coupled approach, used as IR-WM's default, provided a balance of accuracy and efficiency.
 - Explicitly using occupancy to filter candidate trajectories (Tightly Coupled) offered only marginal planning gains over Semi-Coupled, but introduced additional latency (764 ms vs. 706 ms).
 - Future BEV features from world models substantially improved planning accuracy (Semi-Coupled L2 avg: 0.53 m vs. Fully Decoupled L2 avg: 0.87 m), despite increasing inference latency (706 ms vs. 558 ms).
 - Even the Fully Decoupled variant achieved competitive planning performance (L2 avg: 0.87 m) compared to other methods in the literature, suggesting that the world model's learning provides strong supervisory signals for trajectory learning.

2. **Impact of Feature Alignment:**
 - Removing the feature alignment module resulted in a decrease in IoU_f for typical static objects (e.g., Barrier, Construction, Sidewalk, Drivable surface) by 0.2-0.4 points. This indicates that feature alignment contributes to mitigating error accumulation over time, particularly for static scene elements.

3. **Exploration on Temporal Self-Supervision (TSS):**
 - Applying an additional L2 loss to supervise predicted BEV features with BEV features extracted from future images (TSS) provided only a marginal regularization effect on occupancy prediction. This suggests that the implicit residual world model, guided by 4D occupancy, already inherently maintains strong temporal consistency.

4. **Impact of BEV Feature Resolutions:**
 - IR-WM consistently outperformed the Drive-OccWorld baseline at the same BEV resolution (200x200).
 - Higher spatial resolution (200x200) offered a significant advantage over coarser features (128x128), indicating the importance of fine-grained BEV representation for accurate occupancy prediction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents IR-WM, an Implicit Residual World Model, which contributes to the field of autonomous driving by offering an alternative to full-scene reconstruction for environmental forecasting.

**A. Technical Significance:**
- **Efficiency in World Modeling:** The primary contribution is the shift from reconstructing entire future scenes to explicitly modeling only their changes (residuals). This approach aims to allocate model capacity more effectively, focusing computational resources on dynamic elements and scene evolution rather than static backgrounds.
- **Enhanced Dynamics Understanding:** By emphasizing temporal differences, the model is designed to better capture environmental dynamics, which is critical for safe and effective autonomous navigation.
- **Improved Temporal Consistency:** The integration of the feature alignment module and the autoregressive nature of the residual prediction contribute to maintaining temporal coherence across predicted states and mitigating error accumulation, which is a common challenge in sequential prediction tasks.
- **Robust Scene Representation:** The supervision of BEV features with 4D occupancy forecasting provides a fine-grained, semantically rich, and geometrically accurate representation of the environment, benefiting both perception and planning.

**B. Performance Impact:**
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** IR-WM achieves leading performance in both vision-centric 4D occupancy forecasting and end-to-end trajectory planning on the nuScenes benchmark. This demonstrates the practical effectiveness of the implicit residual modeling paradigm.
- **Improved Planning Accuracy and Safety:** The significant reduction in L2 planning error and collision rates suggests that the model's enhanced understanding and forecasting capabilities directly translate into more accurate and safer ego-vehicle trajectories.

**C. Research Implications:**
- **Forecasting-Planning Coupling Insights:** The investigation into different coupling schemes provides empirical insights for future research, indicating that while implicit future state generation substantially boosts planning accuracy, directly using explicit occupancy for trajectory filtering yields only marginal gains with added latency.
- **World Models as Supervisors:** The findings suggest that world models can generate strong supervisory signals for trajectory learning, potentially enabling more flexible and efficient training strategies for autonomous driving systems, including fully decoupled inference architectures under certain conditions.

Overall, IR-WM represents a step toward more efficient and robust world models for autonomous driving, offering a method for understanding and predicting complex driving environments with higher fidelity and contributing to the development of safer and more reliable autonomous systems.
