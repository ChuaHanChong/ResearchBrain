# Unsupervised Elicitation of Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents Internal Coherence Maximization (ICM), an unsupervised algorithm for fine-tuning language models by having them generate and refine their own labels based on internal consistency and mutual predictability. The research demonstrates that this method can match or surpass human supervision on various tasks, including the successful training of a production-scale Claude 3.5 Haiku assistant that performs competitively against its human-supervised counterpart.

## Problem

- Prevailing language model alignment methods (e.g., SFT, RLHF) heavily rely on human supervision.
- As language models develop superhuman capabilities, obtaining high-quality and reliable human supervision becomes difficult or impossible, creating a bottleneck.
- Human supervision can lead to models mimicking human mistakes, reward hacking, or failing to fully unlock their latent superhuman potential.

## Method

- Introduces ==Internal Coherence Maximization (ICM)==, an unsupervised algorithm that enables language models to fine-tune themselves by generating and refining their own labels.
- ICM defines a scoring function combining =="mutual predictability"== (how well the pretrained model can infer labels from other labels) and "logical consistency" to evaluate label set quality.
- An iterative search procedure, inspired by ==simulated annealing==, is employed to construct and refine the label set, including a critical =="ConsistencyFix"== step to resolve inconsistencies.

## Results

- ICM-trained models achieved performance comparable to or surpassing models trained on human supervision across various tasks, including TruthfulQA, GSM8K-verification, and Alpaca (helpfulness/harmlessness).
- The method successfully elicited superhuman capabilities, matching golden supervision and outperforming human annotators on a gender prediction task.
- Anthropic applied ICM to train a Claude 3.5 Haiku-based assistant without human supervision; its unsupervised reward model (**75.0%** accuracy) outperformed a human-supervised one (**72.2%**), and the resulting assistant policy won **60%** of head-to-head comparisons against a human-supervised policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Powerful pretrained language models inherently possess a rich understanding of complex concepts like correctness and helpfulness, which can be "elicited" rather than "taught."
> - Mutual predictability, measuring how well a model can infer labels from other labels within a dataset, is the critical component for driving internal coherence in unsupervised elicitation.
> - Unsupervised methods can match or surpass human supervision for aligning LLMs, especially on tasks where human judgment is suboptimal or limited by the models' superhuman capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models has been characterized by their immense capabilities, often refined through a post-training paradigm that heavily relies on human supervision, such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or instruction fine-tuning with human-provided demonstrations. This work directly confronts the growing limitations of this human-centric approach.

**The "Supervision Bottleneck" and Alignment Scalability:** As LLMs become more complex and achieve superhuman performance on various tasks (e.g., advanced mathematics, scientific reasoning, complex coding), obtaining high-quality human supervision becomes increasingly difficult, expensive, and sometimes impossible. Humans may struggle to accurately evaluate or demonstrate desired behaviors for tasks where the AI already surpasses human cognitive abilities. This creates a "supervision bottleneck," a critical challenge for scaling AI alignment, where human limitations hinder the potential and reliability of advanced AI systems. Prior work has shown that LMs can mimic human mistakes (Asare et al., 2023) or exploit flaws in human feedback (Wen et al., 2024b), further underscoring the unreliability of current methods for frontier models.

**Leveraging Latent Knowledge:** The paper is built on the premise that pretrained LLMs already possess rich latent knowledge about crucial human concepts like truthfulness, mathematical correctness, helpfulness, and harmlessness. The challenge is not to "teach" these concepts from scratch, but to "elicit" or "unlock" them from within the model's vast learned representations without external human labels. This aligns with a growing body of research indicating that base models harbor significant, often underutilized, capabilities (Kadavath et al., 2022; Burns et al., 2022; Yue et al., 2025).

**Advancing Unsupervised Elicitation:** This work represents a significant leap in unsupervised elicitation methods. Earlier attempts, like CCS (Burns et al., 2022), demonstrated the possibility of finding latent knowledge using logical consistency but often underperformed supervised approaches in realistic settings due to the challenge of distinguishing true knowledge from other prominent features satisfying consistency (Farquhar et al., 2023). This paper introduces "mutual predictability" as a crucial component to overcome these limitations, demonstrating for the first time that unsupervised elicitation can match or exceed human supervision on practical benchmarks and even for training general-purpose assistants.

**Relation to Weak-to-Strong Generalization:** The authors note that unsupervised elicitation can be viewed as an extreme case of "weak-to-strong generalization" (Burns et al., 2023; Hase et al., 2024). While weak-to-strong generalization aims to use weak human supervision to elicit stronger capabilities, this work pushes the boundary by attempting to achieve this *without any* human supervision, effectively ignoring even "weak" labels.

By demonstrating that powerful, general-purpose assistants can be trained without human labels, this research proposes a fundamental shift in the AI post-training paradigm, offering a pathway to overcome the human supervision bottleneck and scale AI alignment to superhuman levels.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop and validate an unsupervised algorithm capable of fine-tuning pretrained language models for downstream tasks without relying on external human supervision. This overarching goal is driven by several critical motivations:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Human Supervision:** The primary motivation stems from the inherent difficulties of current post-training paradigms (e.g., RLHF) that depend on human input. As LLMs become more advanced and acquire superhuman capabilities, humans find it increasingly challenging to provide high-quality demonstrations or reliable preference feedback. This can lead to models mimicking human errors or exploiting flaws in the feedback mechanisms, ultimately hindering the development of truly capable and aligned AI. The paper explicitly asks, "How do we train LMs to do tasks that are too difficult for humans to demonstrate or evaluate reliably?"

2. **Eliciting Latent Knowledge:** The authors hypothesize that pretrained models already possess a rich internal understanding of many important human concepts, such as mathematical correctness, truthfulness, helpfulness, and harmlessness. Rather than attempting to teach these concepts during post-training, the goal is to "elicit" or "unlock" this existing latent knowledge. This approach bypasses the need for external, human-specified labels, which often constrain model performance to human-level understanding or introduce human biases.

3. **Achieving Superhuman Alignment:** For tasks where LLMs are already superhuman, relying on human supervision can bottleneck the model's potential. The research aims to develop a method that allows models to leverage their full capabilities, even if humans cannot fully grasp or evaluate the task. This is particularly relevant for aligning future frontier models with complex, nuanced principles that humans might struggle to define or monitor perfectly.

4. **Practical Application for Frontier Models:** Beyond theoretical exploration, a key objective is to demonstrate the practical utility of unsupervised elicitation for commercial-grade frontier models. The ultimate aim is to show that this method can produce general-purpose AI assistants that are comparable to, or even surpass, those trained with extensive human supervision, thus offering a scalable and efficient alternative for AI development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is **Internal Coherence Maximization (ICM)**, an unsupervised algorithm designed to fine-tune pretrained language models by leveraging their internal understanding of concepts.

**A. Problem Statement:**
The goal is to estimate labels {y_i} for a given set of inputs {x_i} purely using the LM itself, without any external human-provided labels {y*_i}. The focus is primarily on classification tasks (e.g., reward modeling), with applications extended to open-ended generation tasks via reinforcement learning.

**B. Scoring Function (U(D)):**
ICM evaluates the quality of a model-generated label set `D = {(x_i, y_i)}` using a scoring function with two main components:

1. **Mutual Predictability (P_θ(D)):** This term measures how well the pretrained model (P_θ) can infer each label y_i when conditioned on all other labels in the dataset `D \ (x_i, y_i)`. It is calculated as the sum of log probabilities `log P_θ(y_i | x_i, D \ (x_i, y_i))` across all examples.
 - **Intuition:** A high mutual predictability score indicates that the labels collectively define a single, coherent concept according to the model. If a label for one example can be confidently inferred from the labels of other examples, it suggests internal consistency within the model's understanding of that concept.
 - **Implementation:** In the primary algorithm, this is achieved via in-context learning, where other labeled examples serve as demonstrations for predicting the current example's label.

2. **Logical Consistency (I(D)):** To prevent degenerate solutions (e.g., assigning the same label to all data points, which could inflate mutual predictability), ICM introduces a penalty for logical inconsistencies. This is measured by `I(D) = Σ c(x_i, y_i, x_j, y_j)`, where `c` is an indicator function checking for inconsistencies between pairs of labels.
 - **Examples of Logical Constraints:** For mathematical correctness or truthfulness, "two different answers cannot both be True." For preference comparisons (e.g., Alpaca), "A > B and B > A cannot both be True." Even simple, general constraints are found to be effective.

3. **Overall Scoring Function:** The two terms are combined: `U(D) = α · P_θ(D) − I(D)`, where `α` is a hyperparameter to balance their relative importance.

**C. Our Algorithm (Algorithm 1: Internal Coherence Maximization):**
Finding the optimal label set to maximize `U(D)` is computationally intractable. ICM employs an efficient approximate search algorithm inspired by simulated annealing:

1. **Initialization:** The process begins by randomly selecting and labeling `K` examples, forming an initial labeled dataset `D`. These initial labels are then put through a `consistencyfix` step (Algorithm 2) to resolve any immediate logical contradictions. A small `K` (e.g., 8) is found to be optimal, balancing context provision with noise reduction.

2. **Iterative Labeling and Refinement:** For a specified number of iterations:
 - **Temperature Update:** A "temperature" parameter `T` is gradually decreased, making the algorithm more selective over time (simulated annealing).
 - **Example Selection:** An example `x_i` is sampled to be (re)labeled. Unlabeled examples or those with existing consistency relationships are prioritized.
 - **Label Proposal:** The pretrained model `P_θ` predicts the most likely label `ˆy_i` for `x_i`, conditioned on the current labeled dataset `D \ {(x_i, y_i)}`.
 - **ConsistencyFix (Algorithm 2):** After `ˆy_i` is proposed, any new or existing logical inconsistencies involving `x_i` are actively resolved. This involves enumerating consistent label options for inconsistent pairs `(x_i, x_j)` and selecting the combination `(ˆy_i, ˆy_j)` that maximizes `U(D)`. This proactive fixing helps prevent the algorithm from rejecting potentially correct labels that initially contradict a set of consistently wrong labels.
 - **Acceptance/Rejection:**
 - If the new set of labels `ˆD` improves the overall score `U(ˆD) > U(D)`, the update is accepted.
 - If the score decreases (`U(ˆD) <= U(D)`), the update is accepted with a probability `exp(∆/T)`, where `∆` is the score difference. This stochastic acceptance allows the algorithm to escape local optima, especially in early iterations when `T` is high.

**D. Scalable ICM (Appendix G):**
For very large datasets and production-grade models, the original ICM faces two scalability challenges:
1. **Context Window Limitation:** In-context learning for mutual predictability requires all labeled examples to fit into the model's context window, which is impractical for long-context production data.
2. **Sequential Labeling:** Labeling one example at a time is inefficient for large datasets.

The scalable variant addresses these by:
1. **Fine-tuning for Mutual Predictability:** Replaces in-context learning with fine-tuning. The dataset `D` is partitioned into `F` folds. For each fold `D_f`, a model `ˆθ_f` is trained on `D \ D_f` and then used to relabel examples in `D_f`. This approximates conditioning on "all but a few" labels, allowing parallel fine-tuning and inference.
2. **Parallel Batch Labeling:** Labels are updated in parallel batches across folds, significantly improving efficiency.
3. **Simplified ConsistencyFix (Algorithm 4):** A more efficient `ConsistencyFix-MaxProb` is introduced. For each consistency group, it identifies the example with the model's most confident prediction and then adjusts other labels in that group to be consistent with it.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various benchmarks and with different LLMs yield compelling findings:

1. **Matches Golden Supervision on Core NLP Tasks:** On TruthfulQA (truthfulness) and GSM8K-verification (mathematical correctness), ICM achieves performance comparable to models trained directly on golden (true) labels. This is a crucial result, demonstrating that ICM can effectively discover the underlying correct concepts without ever seeing ground truth. This is achieved despite zero-shot performance often being no better than random guessing.

2. **Outperforms Crowdsourced Human Supervision:** On the Alpaca dataset (evaluating helpfulness and harmlessness), ICM substantially outperforms training with real-world, crowdsourced human labels. This is particularly significant because helpfulness and harmlessness are complex, subjective concepts where human judgment can be inconsistent or limited. This finding suggests ICM can extract a more coherent and reliable understanding of these concepts from the model itself than imperfect human labels can provide.

3. **Exceeds Post-Trained Chat Models:** ICM outperforms zero-shot prompting with commercial chat models (e.g., Llama 2 chat), which have undergone extensive, diverse human-supervised post-training. This indicates that ICM can elicit capabilities more effectively than traditional post-training for these specific benchmarks.

4. **Scales with Pretrained Model Capabilities:** ICM's effectiveness improves with the underlying power of the pretrained model. On TruthfulQA, while ICM moderately underperforms golden labels with Llama 8B, it achieves comparable performance with Llama 70B, demonstrating its ability to leverage more advanced latent knowledge.

5. **Elicits Superhuman Capabilities:** In an author gender prediction task (using the Blog Authorship Corpus), where pretrained LMs are strongly superhuman, ICM matches golden supervision (80% accuracy) and significantly outperforms human annotators (60% accuracy). This directly validates the method's ability to elicit capabilities beyond human performance.

6. **Trains a Frontier Assistant Chatbot Without Supervision:** Using a scalable variant of ICM, the researchers successfully trained a Claude 4 Sonnet-based assistant without any human supervision.
 - An unsupervised reward model (RM) was first trained using ICM on 5,000 pairwise preference data (task: "Output A is more helpful, harmless, and honest than Output B").
 - This unsupervised RM was then used to train an assistant via reinforcement learning.
 - The resulting unsupervised assistant **matches its human-supervised counterpart** (trained on production-grade human labels) on average when evaluated by a production-grade Claude 4 Opus RM.
 - It achieved **higher scores on chat and safety** tasks, while showing lower scores on math and coding (possibly due to higher quality of human labels in these crisp reasoning domains).
 - Remarkably, the unsupervised RM led to **faster learning during RL** (e.g., 2.5x speed on Chat and Chat-Hard tasks).

**Ablation Studies:**
- **ICM vs. Randomly Perturbed Labels:** Labels generated by ICM consistently lead to substantially better performance than randomly perturbed labels that have the *same accuracy* as ICM's labels. This confirms that ICM generates labels that are more aligned with the model's internal understanding of the task, not just any noisy labels.
- **Robustness to Initialization:** ICM proves robust even to "worst-case" initializations (e.g., entirely wrong labels), experiencing only a moderate performance drop rather than complete failure, thanks to its iterative correction mechanism.
- **Impact of Logical Consistency:** While beneficial, logical consistency's impact varies. It is crucial for tasks like Alpaca to prevent degenerate solutions (e.g., assigning the same label to all data points) but less critical for others (e.g., TruthfulQA) where mutual predictability alone often suffices. It primarily helps reduce variance and guide the search towards non-degenerate solutions.

**Limitations Acknowledged:** The authors note that ICM cannot elicit concepts or skills unless they are "salient" to the pretrained model (e.g., subjective preferences like "poems about the sun"). They also address potential data contamination concerns, providing evidence against it, such as low zero-shot performance, reformatting of tasks, and the use of distinct production data for Claude 4 Sonnet assistant training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "UNSUPERVISED ELICITATION OF LANGUAGE MODELS" marks a pivotal advancement in the field of AI, particularly for the development and alignment of frontier language models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Breaking the Human Supervision Barrier for Superhuman AI:** This work offers a crucial pathway to overcome the "supervision bottleneck" that currently limits the development of increasingly capable AI systems. By demonstrating that LLMs can be fine-tuned effectively without human labels, it opens the door to training models for tasks where AI capabilities far exceed human understanding or evaluation capacity. This is critical for future AIs that will operate in highly complex domains like scientific discovery or advanced engineering.

2. **Scalability of AI Alignment:** The ability to elicit desired behaviors (like helpfulness, harmlessness, and honesty) from models without human input significantly enhances the scalability of AI alignment efforts. Traditional RLHF methods are resource-intensive, requiring vast amounts of human labor. ICM suggests a future where alignment can keep pace with accelerating AI capabilities, potentially leading to more robust and reliable AI systems at a lower human cost. This could particularly augment methods like Constitutional AI, allowing for the autonomous generation and refinement of internal alignment principles based on constitutional directives.

3. **Unlocking Latent Knowledge in Foundation Models:** The findings reinforce the idea that large pretrained models possess a vast reservoir of latent knowledge and sophisticated conceptual understanding. ICM provides a powerful mechanism to "unlock" and operationalize this inherent understanding, transforming base models into highly aligned assistants without the distortions or limitations introduced by human-labeled data.

4. **Practical Implications for Frontier Model Development:** The successful training of a Claude 4 Sonnet-based assistant that matches human-supervised counterparts, and even learns faster, demonstrates the immediate practical utility of ICM. This could revolutionize how frontier models are refined and aligned within leading AI labs, offering a more efficient and effective path to deploying powerful, general-purpose AI assistants. The faster learning observed with unsupervised RMs also implies potential for significant gains in training efficiency.

5. **Re-evaluating the Role of Human Feedback:** While the paper does not suggest eliminating humans from the loop entirely, it redefines the nature of human involvement. Instead of providing granular, task-specific labels, humans might transition to higher-level roles, such as defining "constitutions" or abstract principles (as in Constitutional AI) and then validating whether the AI interprets and aligns with these principles as intended. This shift allows humans to focus on intent and oversight rather than being a bottleneck for task-specific supervision.

6. **Advancing AI Safety Research:** By enabling models to self-supervise for complex concepts like safety and honesty, this research contributes directly to the field of AI safety. Ensuring that powerful AIs act in accordance with human values is paramount, and ICM offers a novel tool to instill these values directly from the model's internal coherence, reducing reliance on potentially flawed external signals.

In conclusion, this research presents a significant paradigm shift, moving beyond the limitations of human supervision to harness the intrinsic capabilities of large language models. ICM offers a scalable, effective, and practically viable approach to elicit complex behaviors and values, paving the way for more capable and aligned frontier AI systems.
