# PriorVLA: Prior-Preserving Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PriorVLA introduces a framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that preserves and leverages broad pretrained priors during downstream adaptation, preventing prior forgetting. The approach integrates explicit scene and motor priors via learnable query interfaces and dual action experts, achieving improved performance, especially in out-of-distribution generalization and few-shot learning, across various simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks with reduced trainable parameters.

## Problem

- Full fine-tuning of pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for downstream tasks can lead to "prior forgetting," where generalizable knowledge from pretraining is lost.
- Existing VLA adaptation methods primarily focus on modifying or restricting components, lacking explicit mechanisms to preserve and leverage pretrained forward-pass representations (scene and motor priors) as usable sources for the adapted policy.
- Adapted policies trained via full fine-tuning on limited downstream data often generalize poorly under out-of-distribution conditions and few-shot settings, since narrow task-specific data cannot substitute for broad pretrained knowledge.

## Method

- **PriorVLA** uses ==Dual Action Experts (DAE)==: a frozen "Prior Expert" (original Action Expert) to preserve motor priors and a trainable "Adaptation Expert" for task specialization, both operating on the same noisy action trajectory.
- Introduces ==Expert Queries (EQ)== as learnable token interfaces: ==Scene Queries== capture VLM scene priors, ==Motor Queries== capture frozen Prior Expert motor priors, and ==Action Queries== integrate these priors into the Adaptation Expert.
- The framework optimizes approximately ==25% of the VLA model parameters== (Adaptation Expert, Expert Queries, VLM's vision encoder) using a standard ==flow-matching MSE objective==, keeping other components frozen.

## Results

- Achieved **77% ID** and **53% OOD** success on RoboTwin 2.0, demonstrating gains of **+10pp** and **+11pp** over the π

_{0.5} baseline in Easy (ID) and Hard (OOD) settings, respectively.
- Reached an average success rate of **99.1%** across LIBERO suites, surpassing strong VLA baselines like OpenVLA-OFT (**97.1%**) and π

_{0.5} (96.9%).
- On real-world robot tasks, PriorVLA achieved **+16pp OOD** success in standard data and **+22pp OOD** success in few-shot settings compared to π

_{0.5}, while updating 25% fewer parameters than full fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling prior preservation (via a frozen expert) from task specialization (via a trainable expert) effectively maintains broad pretrained motor knowledge during adaptation.
> - Learnable query interfaces provide a crucial mechanism for capturing and integrating relevant scene and motor priors from frozen pretrained components into the adapting policy.
> - The value of a pretrained prior source stems from its learned knowledge rather than simply providing an additional architectural branch or trainable parameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large-scale pretraining has established Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models as foundational components for developing generalist robot manipulation policies. These models leverage diverse robot data, simulation experiences, and pretrained vision-language backbones to acquire broad priors that facilitate general manipulation capabilities, including grounding language instructions in visual observations and generating actions across varied tasks and embodiments. Examples include RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and $\pi_0$.

Despite these advances, real-world manipulation's complexity often necessitates adapting pretrained VLA models to downstream tasks. A common adaptation strategy is full fine-tuning, which can improve in-distribution (ID) performance rapidly. However, full fine-tuning treats the pretrained model primarily as an initialization, updating all parameters to fit limited task-specific data. This process can lead to "prior forgetting," where the broad, generalizable priors learned during pretraining are shifted towards narrow patterns specific to the training distribution, potentially weakening out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization.

Existing adaptation methods for VLA models typically focus on refining action representations, adjusting fine-tuning objectives, reducing trainable parameters (e.g., using low-rank updates), freezing or protecting the Vision-Language Model (VLM) while placing adaptation capacity in action-side modules, or constraining/merging parameter updates. Lightweight interface designs, such as query-style tokens or bridge attention, also connect vision-language features to action policies. While these methods contribute to more efficient and stable adaptation, they primarily address *how* to fine-tune, restrict, protect, or connect pretrained components. The explicit preservation and leveraging of pretrained forward-pass representations as usable prior sources during downstream action generation has been less explored in previous VLA adaptation frameworks. This work addresses this gap by proposing a mechanism for explicit prior preservation and integration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an adaptation framework for VLA models that preserves and leverages the broad priors acquired during large-scale pretraining, rather than treating pretraining merely as initialization for subsequent fine-tuning. The motivation stems from the observation that full fine-tuning, while effective for in-distribution performance, can cause "prior forgetting." This occurs when the extensive knowledge embedded in a pretrained VLA model—encompassing general vision-language representations and reusable motor priors for action generation—is distorted or lost as the model's parameters are entirely updated to fit limited, narrow downstream task data.

The authors identify two complementary types of priors that emerge from the pretrained model's forward pass and should be preserved:
1. **Scene priors**: Encoded by the VLM, these capture task-relevant visual structure.
2. **Motor priors**: Encoded by the action expert, these represent regularities in action generation.

The core motivation is that these pretrained priors are valuable resources that can supplement limited downstream data. By explicitly preserving and making these priors usable, an adapted policy can handle novel observations and generate reliable actions, particularly in few-shot learning scenarios and under out-of-distribution conditions. The existing methods, which largely focus on modifying, restricting, or connecting pretrained components, are seen as lacking a direct mechanism for maintaining these forward-pass representations as readily usable prior sources for the adapted policy. Therefore, the work aims to introduce a framework that not only preserves these priors but also provides a learnable interface for the adapted policy to integrate them.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, PriorVLA, is designed to preserve pretrained priors and learn to leverage them during downstream adaptation. It comprises two main architectural components: Dual Action Experts (DAE) and Expert Queries (EQ).

#### 3.1. Dual Action Experts (DAE)

PriorVLA addresses the issue of motor prior shifting during fine-tuning by decoupling prior preservation from task specialization.
- **Prior Expert**: The original pretrained Action Expert (AE) is retained as a frozen component. It serves as a read-only source of motor priors, meaning its internal denoising representations provide motor-prior features, but its final action prediction is discarded. Its weights remain unchanged throughout adaptation.
- **Adaptation Expert**: A new trainable Action Expert, initialized with the same weights as the pretrained AE, is introduced. This expert is responsible for specializing to the downstream task and producing the final action chunk.
During the denoising process, both experts are executed along the same noisy action trajectory. At each denoising step, both receive the same noisy action chunk. Only the denoising output of the Adaptation Expert is used to update the trajectory. The updated chunk is then fed back to both experts for the subsequent denoising step. This design ensures that the frozen Prior Expert’s motor knowledge is preserved and accessible.

#### 3.2. Expert Queries (EQ)

To make the preserved priors usable for downstream action generation, PriorVLA introduces Expert Queries, which are learnable token interfaces attached to different functional paths within the model. These queries facilitate the capture and integration of scene and motor priors.
- **Scene Queries (SQ)**: These are learnable tokens inserted into the VLM input sequence alongside the original visual and language input tokens. They participate in the VLM's self-attention mechanism, enabling them to capture task-relevant scene priors from the VLM's representations. Their layer-wise key-value caches provide a compact interface for scene priors.
- **Motor Queries (MQ)**: These are learnable tokens appended to the frozen Prior Expert. Their purpose is to capture motor priors from the Prior Expert's internal denoising representations. To maintain the integrity of the frozen pretrained denoising path, the Prior Expert's noisy action tokens are prevented from attending to Scene or Motor Queries. Conversely, Motor Queries self-attend and read only from the Prior Expert's noisy action tokens, allowing them to focus on action-side representations.
- **Action Queries (AQ)**: These are learnable tokens inserted alongside the noisy action tokens of the trainable Adaptation Expert. They are designed to integrate the captured scene priors (from Scene Queries) and motor priors (from Motor Queries) within the Adaptation Expert, thereby guiding its action generation process. The raw noisy-action key-value caches of the Prior Expert are explicitly excluded, ensuring that motor information is routed through the Motor Query interface rather than being directly copied from frozen states. Action Queries are not decoded as actions; their role is to organize multi-source priors within the Adaptation Expert.

#### 3.3. Training

- **Trainable Parameters**: PriorVLA optimizes the full Adaptation Expert, the three groups of Expert Queries, and the VLM's vision encoder. All other VLM parameters and the Prior Expert are kept frozen. This results in updating approximately 25% of the parameters compared to a full fine-tuning approach. This strategy aims to maintain pretrained prior sources in the frozen VLM components and the Prior Expert while allowing specific parts to specialize.
- **Objective**: The framework is trained using the standard flow-matching mean-squared error (MSE) objective. This loss is applied solely to the denoising prediction of the Adaptation Expert. No auxiliary loss functions are imposed on the Expert Queries or the Prior Expert; all trainable components are optimized exclusively through the downstream action objective.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PriorVLA was evaluated across a range of simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, including RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO, and eight real-world tasks on two robot embodiments. The evaluation focused on overall performance, out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, performance under limited data (few-shot settings), and real-world transferability.

#### 4.1. Simulated Evaluation on RoboTwin 2.0

- **Standard Setting**: PriorVLA achieved 77% success in the Easy (in-distribution, ID) setting and 53% success in the Hard (OOD) setting, demonstrating gains of +10 and +11 percentage points, respectively, over the $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline. The OOD performance showed more substantial improvement compared to $\pi_0$ and other baselines.
- **Data Scale**: Across few-shot, standard, and large-data regimes, PriorVLA consistently improved Hard (OOD) performance over $\pi_{0.5}$ by +11, +11, and +6 percentage points, respectively. In the Easy (ID) setting, performance improved under few-shot and standard data, becoming comparable to $\pi_{0.5}$ in the large-data regime. These results suggest that preserved priors are particularly beneficial under limited data and remain useful for OOD generalization even as data scales.

#### 4.2. Simulated Evaluation on LIBERO

- PriorVLA achieved an average success rate of 99.1% across the four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long). This performance exceeded that of strong VLA baselines, including OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and $\pi_{0.5}$ (96.9%), indicating an improvement in standard in-distribution benchmark performance.

#### 4.3. Real-World Robot Experiments

Evaluations were conducted on a single-arm Franka robot and a dual-arm AC-One platform across eight tasks, under both standard and few-shot data conditions (10 demonstrations per task).
- **Standard Data**: PriorVLA achieved 81% ID success and 57% OOD success, surpassing $\pi_{0.5}$ by +12 and +16 percentage points, respectively.
- **Few-Shot Data**: With only 10 demonstrations per task, PriorVLA reached 48% ID success and 32% OOD success, representing gains of +24 and +22 percentage points over $\pi_{0.5}$, respectively.
- These gains were achieved while updating approximately 25% fewer parameters compared to full fine-tuning, indicating improved parameter efficiency alongside performance benefits.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablations on a subset of six RoboTwin 2.0 tasks provided insights into the contributions of PriorVLA's components:
- **Prior Expert (PE) Role**: Removing the Prior Expert (or functionally equivalent, disabling Motor Queries) reduced Hard success from 49% to 42%. Replacing the pretrained Prior Expert with a randomly initialized frozen branch yielded similar results (43% Hard success), indicating that the benefit comes from pretrained motor priors, not merely an extra branch. Making the Prior Expert trainable also degraded performance (44% Hard success), suggesting that a stable, frozen prior source is more effective.
- **Expert Queries (EQ) Role**: Removing all Expert Queries substantially reduced Hard performance to 28%, demonstrating that learnable interfaces are crucial for leveraging the preserved priors.
- **Individual Query Contributions**: Removing Scene Queries caused the largest drop in Hard performance, while removing Motor Queries or Action Queries also weakened OOD generalization.
- **Vision Encoder**: Freezing the vision encoder decreased both Easy and Hard performance, suggesting that visual adaptation remains complementary to prior preservation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces PriorVLA, a framework that proposes a "prior-preserving" perspective on downstream adaptation for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This reframes adaptation from solely fitting limited downstream data to learning to effectively utilize broad, pretrained structure.

The significance of PriorVLA is demonstrated through its empirical performance. The framework consistently achieves stronger overall performance compared to full fine-tuning and other state-of-the-art VLA baselines across diverse benchmarks, including RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO, and real-world tasks. Its gains are most pronounced in challenging scenarios such as out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation and few-shot learning settings. The method also updates approximately 25% fewer parameters than full fine-tuning, indicating increased parameter efficiency. Ablation studies further support that these performance improvements are attributable to the explicit preservation of pretrained priors and the integration facilitated by learnable query interfaces, rather than simply reduced trainable parameters or additional frozen architectural branches.

The potential impact of PriorVLA includes contributing to more data-efficient and generalizable robot manipulation systems. By reducing the reliance on extensive task-specific demonstrations, the framework could lower the barriers to developing and deploying robotic solutions. Its improved robustness to distribution shifts suggests that robots adapted with PriorVLA might perform more reliably in varied real-world conditions, extending the applicability of VLA models in practical scenarios.

However, the research also notes several limitations. The RoboTwin 2.0 evaluation was conducted on a representative subset of tasks rather than the full benchmark. In real-world evaluations, OOD factors were applied jointly, which limits the disentanglement of individual factor effects. PriorVLA also introduces additional inference computation due to the execution of the frozen Prior Expert during denoising; while manageable in the current chunked-control setting, more efficient implementations could be explored. Furthermore, while ablations validate the role of preserved priors, a finer-grained analysis of how scene and motor priors emerge, interact, and evolve across layers and denoising steps is identified as future work.
