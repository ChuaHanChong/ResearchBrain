# Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation with Pretrained Image-Editing Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces SuSIE, a system that enables zero-shot robotic manipulation by generating visual subgoals using pretrained image-editing diffusion models. The approach successfully decomposes complex tasks, allowing robots to perform manipulation with novel objects and instructions in new environments, achieving state-of-the-art results on the CALVIN benchmark and demonstrating generalization on a real robot.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to understand and execute natural language commands in diverse, unstructured environments without extensive task-specific training data.
- Traditional robotic manipulation policies often fail to generalize to novel objects, environments, or instructions.
- End-to-end learning for robotic manipulation requires prohibitively massive amounts of robot-specific training data.

## Method

- SuSIE (==Subgoal Synthesis== via Image Editing) leverages a fine-tuned image-editing diffusion model to generate visual 'subgoal' images from a current scene image and a language instruction.
- A separate low-level, goal-conditioned policy then executes actions to reach each generated visual subgoal iteratively until the task is completed.
- The image-editing diffusion model is fine-tuned on a combination of language-labeled robot trajectories (Bridge dataset) and language-labeled video data (Something-Something dataset).

## Results

- SuSIE achieved state-of-the-art results on the CALVIN benchmark, outperforming methods trained on significantly more data and generalizing to unseen test environments.
- The method demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization capabilities, successfully manipulating novel objects (e.g., crayons, bell peppers) in novel environments following novel instructions on both simulated and real robots.
- SuSIE outperformed baseline methods like RT-1-X and Oracle GCBC in various manipulation tasks, showcasing greater precision and more effective subgoal generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Image-editing diffusion models, pretrained on large internet-scale datasets, can be fine-tuned to generate realistic and achievable visual subgoals for robotic manipulation tasks.
> - Decomposing complex robotic tasks into high-level subgoal synthesis and low-level goal-reaching allows each component to specialize, improving generalization.
> - Combining robot-specific trajectory data with broad video data for fine-tuning the subgoal generation model is crucial for learning both robotic capabilities and general object interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Enabling robots to understand and execute natural language commands in diverse, unstructured environments remains a significant challenge in robotics. Current approaches typically require extensive task-specific training data, making it difficult to handle novel objects or instructions. The paper "Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation with Pretrained Image-Editing Diffusion Models" by Black, Nakamoto, et al. introduces an innovative solution to this problem by leveraging pretrained image-editing diffusion models to generate visual subgoals for robotic manipulation tasks.

This approach, called SuSIE (Subgoal Synthesis via Image Editing), bridges the gap between large-scale internet-trained vision-language models and precise robotic control systems. By decomposing complex tasks into manageable visual subgoals, SuSIE enables robots to perform manipulation tasks they've never encountered before—handling novel objects in novel environments following novel language commands.

### 2. Background and Motivation

Robotic manipulation presents unique challenges that differ from other language-guided AI tasks. Robots need to understand language commands, recognize objects in their environment, and physically interact with them with precise motor control. Traditional approaches face several limitations:

1. Task-specific policies often fail to generalize to new scenarios
2. Direct language-conditioned methods may lack the precision needed for complex manipulation
3. End-to-end learning requires massive amounts of robot training data

The motivation behind SuSIE is to leverage the semantic knowledge embedded in large pretrained vision-language models to overcome these limitations. By tapping into the rich understanding of objects, actions, and spatial relationships that these models have acquired from internet-scale training data, robots can better generalize to new scenarios.

### 3. The SuSIE Method

SuSIE decomposes robotic control into two manageable components:

1. **Subgoal Synthesis**: A fine-tuned image-editing diffusion model generates visual "subgoals" representing intermediate states that would lead to task completion.

2. **Goal-Reaching Policy**: A low-level goal-conditioned policy executes actions to reach each generated subgoal.

The key insight is that image-editing diffusion models, like InstructPix2Pix, are already trained to understand how scenes should change in response to language instructions. These models can be fine-tuned to generate realistic and achievable subgoals for robotic manipulation tasks.

During execution, SuSIE operates as follows:
1. The system receives a current image of the scene and a language instruction
2. The subgoal synthesis model generates a subgoal image showing how the scene should change
3. The goal-reaching policy executes actions to achieve this visual subgoal
4. This process repeats iteratively until the task is completed

This approach effectively separates high-level planning (what should happen) from low-level control (how to make it happen), allowing each component to focus on its specialized task.

### 4. Training Process

The training process for SuSIE involves two main phases:

1. **Fine-tuning the Subgoal Synthesis Model**:
 - Starting with a pretrained InstructPix2Pix model
 - Fine-tuning on a combination of:
 - Language-labeled robot trajectories (Bridge dataset)
 - Language-labeled video data (Something-Something dataset)
 - The model learns to predict scene changes that correspond to language instructions

2. **Training the Goal-Conditioned Policy**:
 - Using goal-conditioned behavioral cloning (GCBC)
 - Training on robot demonstration data
 - Learning to execute actions that move from the current state to a goal state

The authors emphasize the importance of training on both robotics data and non-robotics video data. The robotics data teaches the model about robot-specific capabilities, while the video data provides broader knowledge about object interactions and spatial relationships.

### 5. Experimental Setup

The researchers evaluated SuSIE in two main settings:

1. **CALVIN Benchmark**: A simulated environment with a Franka Emika robot arm performing household manipulation tasks
 - Four environments with different layouts
 - Three environments used for training, one for zero-shot testing
 - Tasks include picking, placing, pushing, and stacking objects

2. **Real Robot Experiments**: Tests on a physical WidowX robot
 - Bridge dataset with real robot trajectories and language annotations
 - Novel objects and environments not seen during training
 - Diverse manipulation tasks like picking and placing different objects

Both settings evaluated the method's ability to:
- Follow language instructions
- Generalize to novel objects and environments
- Complete complex multi-step tasks

### 6. Key Results

SuSIE demonstrated impressive performance across multiple experiments:

1. **CALVIN Benchmark Performance**:
 - Achieved state-of-the-art results
 - Outperformed methods trained on significantly more data
 - Successfully generalized to the unseen test environment

2. **Comparison with Baselines**:
 - Outperformed RT-1-X (a strong language-conditioned policy)
 - Even outperformed Oracle GCBC with privileged information in some cases
 - Showed greater precision in manipulation tasks

3. **Qualitative Performance**:
 - Generated coherent and realistic subgoals
 - Successfully manipulated objects of various shapes, sizes, and colors
 - Executed complex multi-step tasks through iterative subgoal generation

The researchers also conducted ablation studies to understand the contribution of different components:

- **Data Sources**: Models trained on both robot data and video data (Bridge + Something-Something) generated higher quality subgoals than those trained on robot data alone
- **Pretraining**: Starting from the pretrained InstructPix2Pix model was crucial for good performance, compared to training from scratch
- **Subgoal Distance**: Generating subgoals at an appropriate temporal distance improved performance compared to generating either very close or very distant subgoals

### 7. Zero-Shot Generalization

One of the most impressive aspects of SuSIE is its ability to generalize to completely new scenarios:

1. **Novel Objects**: The system successfully manipulated objects not seen during training, such as crayons, markers, and various household items.

2. **Novel Instructions**: SuSIE could follow language commands with new combinations of actions and objects.

3. **Novel Environments**: The method performed well in new environments with different layouts, lighting conditions, and backgrounds.

The paper presents several examples of zero-shot generalization:

```
Example 1: "Put the orange crayon in the wooden bowl"
Example 2: "Put the green spoon in the wooden bowl"
Example 3: "Put the yellow bell pepper in the ceramic bowl"
```

For each of these examples, SuSIE generated a sequence of subgoals that led to successful task completion, even though the specific combinations of objects and actions were not seen during training.

### 8. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its impressive performance, the authors acknowledge several limitations of the current approach:

1. **Model-Policy Gap**: The subgoal synthesis model sometimes generates subgoals that are difficult for the low-level policy to achieve. Future work could involve making the diffusion model aware of the policy's capabilities.

2. **Spatial Reasoning**: The system occasionally struggles with complex spatial reasoning tasks, such as precise object placement or manipulation in cluttered environments.

3. **Long-Horizon Planning**: While SuSIE can handle sequences of subgoals, very long-horizon tasks remain challenging.

4. **Failure Cases**: The paper honestly presents several failure cases, particularly with objects that have unusual shapes or are visually similar to other objects.

The authors suggest several promising directions for future research:

- Integrating additional sensor modalities (e.g., touch, force)
- Improving the coupling between the subgoal generator and the low-level policy
- Extending the approach to more complex, multi-step tasks
- Incorporating feedback mechanisms to recover from failures

### 9. Conclusion

SuSIE represents a significant advance in zero-shot robotic manipulation by leveraging pretrained image-editing diffusion models to generate visual subgoals. The approach effectively bridges the gap between the semantic understanding of large vision-language models and the precise control needed for robotic manipulation.

By decomposing complex tasks into manageable subgoals, SuSIE enables robots to perform tasks with novel objects in novel environments following novel language commands—all without task-specific training data. The impressive results on both simulated benchmarks and real-world robots demonstrate the potential of this approach for creating more adaptable and capable robotic systems.

The work highlights the value of transferring knowledge from large-scale pretrained models to robotics applications, opening up new possibilities for creating robots that can understand and execute diverse tasks in unstructured environments. As both diffusion models and robotic control systems continue to advance, approaches like SuSIE are likely to play an increasingly important role in the development of more capable and flexible robotic systems.
