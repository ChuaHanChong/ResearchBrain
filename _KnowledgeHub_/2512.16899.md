# Multimodal RewardBench 2: Evaluating Omni Reward Models for Interleaved Text and Image

> [!summary] Summary
> Multimodal RewardBench 2 (MMRB2) introduces the first comprehensive benchmark for evaluating reward models on multimodal understanding and interleaved text and image generation. It reveals that frontier MLLM judges like Gemini 3 Pro achieve the highest human agreement (up to 80%) but still struggle with nuanced distinctions and exhibit biases in reasoning tasks, while demonstrating a strong correlation between benchmark performance and downstream generative AI task success.

## Problem

- The complete absence of a comprehensive benchmark designed for "omni reward models" capable of evaluating interleaved text and image sequences.
- Unique complexities of omni models, such as arbitrarily interleaved content, and the difficulty of automatically verifying the quality of generated images.
- The challenge of obtaining high-quality human preference data for complex multimodal outputs to train and reliably evaluate reward models.

## Method

- MMRB2 provides a benchmark structured around four core subtasks: Text-to-Image Generation, Image Editing, Interleaved Generation, and Multimodal Reasoning, each with ==1,000 expert-annotated preference pairs==.
- Prompts are curated from 21 existing benchmarks and newly designed tasks, with responses generated by a diverse set of state-of-the-art models and ==multimodal tool-using agents==.
- High-quality human preference annotations are collected through a meticulous ==two-stage process==, including ==ensemble filtering== and rigorous quality control to ensure strong human consensus and mitigate biases.

## Results

- Gemini 3 Pro leads MLLM-as-a-judge performance with **74-**80%** accuracy** across all subtasks, establishing the current state-of-the-art for multimodal judgment on MMRB2.
- Widely used evaluators like GPT-4o achieve only **51-**65%** accuracy**, indicating they are insufficient for reliably assessing frontier multimodal models.
- Supervised reward models and open-source MLLM judges generally underperform top proprietary MLLM-as-a-judge baselines, especially in multimodal reasoning (e.g., Qwen3-VL-30BA3B trailed Gemini 3 Pro by **22 percentage points**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The benchmark successfully highlights current limitations of MLLM-as-a-judge, revealing that even frontier models like Gemini 3 Pro, while leading, do not fully match human judgment.
> - MLLM judges struggle with fine-grained differentiation between similar outputs from the same model and exhibit a bias towards image-containing responses in multimodal reasoning tasks.
> - Performance on MMRB2 shows a strong correlation (Pearson's r > 0.8) with success in various downstream generative AI tasks, affirming its practical value for model selection and optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and rapidly emerging gap in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly concerning the evaluation and training of advanced multimodal models. The broader landscape can be understood through several converging trends:

**The Rise of Reward Models (RMs) in LLM Training:** Reward models have become indispensable for training large language models (LLMs), primarily through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Christiano et al., 2017; Bai et al., 2022). They provide a scalable mechanism for aligning LLMs with human preferences, evaluating model performance, identifying systematic weaknesses (Zheng et al., 2023), and assessing data quality for synthetic data generation pipelines (Wang et al., 2022b). Existing benchmarks like RewardBench (Lambert et al., 2025; Malik et al., 2025) have focused on text-only LLMs, while Multimodal RewardBench (MMRB1) (Yasunaga et al., 2025) extended this to image-text-to-text tasks for multimodal large language models (MLLMs).

**The Emergence of "Omni Models":** A new generation of AI models, termed "omni models," is pushing the boundaries by enabling understanding, generation, and reasoning with *interleaved text and images* in arbitrary sequences (OpenAI, 2024; Chameleon Team, 2024; Google DeepMind, 2025a). These models represent a significant leap beyond previous multimodal systems, which often handled images and text separately or in more constrained, uni-directional flows (e.g., image-to-text or text-to-image). Omni models are designed for a vast array of real-world applications, from interactive storytelling and complex multi-step instructions to scientific reasoning.

**The Unexplored Territory of Omni Reward Modeling:** Despite the rapid advancements in omni models, reward modeling for these highly capable systems has remained largely unexplored. This omission is primarily due to the unique challenges they present:
- **Arbitrary Interleaved Sequences:** Omni models can produce responses with any combination and order of text and images, making evaluation far more complex than for text-only or even image-text-to-text outputs.
- **Difficulty in Automatic Image Verification:** Unlike some domains (e.g., math, coding) where correctness can be automatically verified, evaluating the quality of generated or edited images often requires human judgment, which is difficult to automate at scale (Hessel et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2023).
- **High-Quality Preference Data:** Collecting reliable human preference data for such complex multimodal outputs demands carefully designed annotation protocols (Liang et al., 2024) and robust quality control.
- **Diverse Task Coverage:** Omni models are versatile, requiring a benchmark that spans a broad range of tasks for comprehensive evaluation (Liu et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2025).

**MMRB2's Contribution to the Landscape:** "Multimodal RewardBench 2" directly addresses this void. It serves as the **first comprehensive benchmark specifically designed for reward models on multimodal understanding and (interleaved) generation.** It builds upon previous efforts like MMRB1 but critically expands the scope to true *omni models* capable of generating and reasoning with mixed text and image sequences. By providing a unified, challenging, and reliable evaluation framework, MMRB2 is positioned to become a foundational tool for accelerating research in omni reward modeling, enabling systematic progress in evaluation, post-training, and alignment of frontier multimodal AI. It allows researchers to rigorously compare different reward models, identify their strengths and weaknesses, and ultimately guide the development of more capable and human-aligned omni AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Multimodal RewardBench 2" (MMRB2) is to establish the **first comprehensive benchmark for reward models tailored to omni models** that handle interleaved image and text sequences. This objective is driven by several critical motivations, stemming from the rapid advancements in multimodal AI and the existing gaps in their evaluation:

1. **Address the Lack of Omni Reward Model Benchmarks:** The most fundamental motivation is the complete absence of a benchmark specifically designed for omni reward models. While reward models are central to LLM development and some multimodal reward benchmarks exist (e.g., MMRB1 for image-text-to-text), none adequately cover the complexities of omni models that generate and understand *arbitrarily interleaved sequences of text and images*. This omission makes it nearly impossible to systematically measure and improve the quality of reward models for these advanced systems.

2. **Overcome Unique Challenges of Omni Modality:** The very nature of omni models introduces new difficulties for reward modeling:
 - **Interleaved Generation Complexity:** Omni models can produce responses where text and images are interwoven, demanding sophisticated evaluation criteria that assess coherence, alignment, and quality across modalities simultaneously.
 - **Image Verification Difficulty:** Automatically verifying the quality and factual correctness of generated or edited images remains a hard problem.
 - **Data Annotation Complexity:** Obtaining high-quality human preference data for interleaved multimodal content is inherently more challenging than for text-only or simpler multimodal outputs, requiring meticulous annotation protocols.
 - **Diverse Application Coverage:** Omni models are intended for a broad spectrum of applications, necessitating a benchmark that covers diverse tasks in both generation and reasoning to be truly comprehensive.

3. **Facilitate Future Research and Development:** By providing a robust evaluation framework, MMRB2 aims to:
 - **Enable Systematic Comparison:** Allow researchers to systematically compare existing and future omni reward models, including MLLM-as-a-judge approaches and preference-trained models.
 - **Identify Strengths and Weaknesses:** Surface the specific capabilities and failure modes of current reward models, guiding targeted improvements.
 - **Advance Post-Training Techniques:** Provide a crucial tool for reinforcement learning from human feedback and other post-training methods for omni models, which rely heavily on high-quality reward signals.
 - **Promote Innovation:** Inspire the development of new architectures, training methodologies, and evaluation techniques for multimodal AI.

To achieve these objectives, MMRB2 is meticulously designed with three key characteristics:
- **Practical yet Challenging Prompts:** The benchmark includes diverse, real-world prompts that push the boundaries of frontier models, drawn from 21 existing benchmarks and newly created tasks.
- **State-of-the-Art Responses:** Responses are generated by a wide array of state-of-the-art multimodal models and specialized agents, ensuring the benchmark reflects current capabilities and realistic failure points.
- **High-Quality Human-Expert Preferences:** The preference pairs are curated with a rigorous ensemble filtering strategy, ensuring strong human-expert consensus (>90% agreement) while remaining challenging for automated judges.

In essence, MMRB2 seeks to establish a much-needed foundation for the nascent field of omni reward modeling, allowing the research community to systematically evaluate, improve, and align the next generation of multimodal AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MMRB2 adopts a rigorous, multi-faceted methodology to construct a comprehensive and challenging benchmark for omni reward models. The approach integrates diverse task coverage, advanced response generation, and meticulous human preference annotation with robust quality control.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Structure: Four Core Subtasks

MMRB2 is structured around four distinct, yet interconnected, multimodal tasks, each comprising 1,000 expert-annotated preference pairs:
1. **Text-to-Image Generation:** Models generate images from textual prompts, evaluating aspects like object composition, spatial relationships, attribute binding, and text rendering.
2. **Image Editing:** Models modify 1-3 input images based on textual instructions, assessing faithfulness to the edit request and preservation of irrelevant regions. This includes novel "text-heavy editing" and "multi-image editing" tasks.
3. **Interleaved Generation:** Models produce mixed sequences of text and images in response to multimodal prompts (e.g., storytelling, how-to guides). Evaluation focuses on coherence, global planning, and text-visual coordination.
4. **Multimodal Reasoning ("Thinking with Images"):** Models solve complex problems requiring visual understanding and logical inference. Responses may include interleaved text and intermediate "sketchpad" images, with evaluation emphasizing accurate perception, spatial reasoning, and clear explanation.

#### 3.2. Prompt and Response Collection

- **Prompt Curation:** Prompts are strategically sampled from 21 existing benchmarks (e.g., WISE, DreamBench, Chameleon) via stratified sampling to ensure diversity in difficulty and subtask type, using only test splits to prevent data leakage. Additionally, novel, practical tasks (e.g., multi-image editing, text-heavy editing) are designed and integrated, often generated using powerful LLMs like GPT-4o.
- **Response Generation:** A wide array of state-of-the-art models (7–11 per task) are used to generate responses, including both proprietary API models (e.g., Gemini 2.0/2.5 Flash Image, Imagen 3/4/Ultra, GPT-Image-1) and open-source systems (e.g., SD 3.5-L, FLUX).
- **Agent-Based Responses:** For challenging interleaved generation and multimodal reasoning tasks where even strong MLLMs struggle, multimodal tool-using agents (e.g., GPT-Gemini-agent, GPT-Image-agent) are developed. These agents, inspired by Visual Sketchpad (Hu et al., 2024), allow an LLM backbone (e.g., GPT-4.1, o3, GPT-5) to write Python code and call image generation/editing tools, with tool outputs (text and images) returned for further planning. This approach ensures responses reflect cutting-edge capabilities and failure modes.

#### 3.3. Human Preference Annotations

A meticulous two-stage process ensures high-quality and informative human preference data:
- **Ensemble Filtering (for Tasks 1-3: Generative Tasks):**
 - Nine multimodal MLLM judges (spanning API and open-source models) evaluate every candidate response pair twice (A vs. B, and B vs. A) to mitigate position bias.
 - "Easy" pairs (where >90% of judge evaluations show unanimous preference) are filtered out, focusing human annotation efforts on more challenging, nuanced comparisons where current AI judges disagree.
- **Human Preference Annotation (for Tasks 1-3: Generative Tasks):**
 - Professional annotators from Surge AI independently evaluate each remaining pair three times.
 - A comprehensive evaluation framework with task-specific criteria is used (e.g., faithfulness to text/image, overall image quality, cross-generation image congruence, text-image alignment, technical quality of text, text rendering correctness).
 - Annotators provide a 7-point Likert scale preference (converted to pairwise preferences), along with detailed rationales.
 - Strict quality control measures are applied: filtering out annotations with high inter-annotator disagreement (rating spread > 4 points), ambiguous average ratings (3.0-4.0), and majority vote ties. This process yields 1,000 high-agreement pairs per task.
- **Multimodal Reasoning Task Annotation (Task 4):**
 - Due to the presence of ground truth answers, human annotations are collected *per model response* rather than pairwise. Annotators assess whether the reasoning and final answer are correct.
 - Preference pairs are then *constructed*: a human-preferred sample (unanimous annotator agreement on correct reasoning and answer) is paired with a non-preferred sample (correct answer/incorrect reasoning OR incorrect answer/with reasoning). Responses can be text-only or image-plus-text, allowing for same-modality and mixed-modality comparisons.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Method for Reward Models

- **Positional Consistent Dual Evaluation:** To mitigate position bias (where judges systematically prefer the first item), each candidate reward model/judge evaluates every preference pair twice: once in the original order (A vs. B) and once with responses swapped (B vs. A). Both judgments are treated as independent data points.
- **Judge-Human Agreement Computation:** The performance of a reward model (or MLLM-as-a-judge) is measured by its binary agreement score with the established human preferences. An agreement of 1.0 is awarded if the judge's preference matches the human majority vote, and 0.0 otherwise.

This comprehensive methodology ensures MMRB2 is a robust, diverse, and high-quality benchmark capable of accurately evaluating the performance and identifying the subtle failure modes of cutting-edge omni reward models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using MMRB2 yielded several significant findings regarding the capabilities of current omni reward models, MLLM-as-a-judge approaches, and their correlation with downstream task performance.

#### 4.1. Performance of MLLM-as-a-Judge

- **MMRB2 Poses Significant Challenges:** Across all four subtasks, current MLLM-as-a-judge approaches struggle to match human-level agreement (which is >90%). This indicates that the benchmark successfully identifies areas where AI judgment still falls short.
- **Gemini 3 Pro Leads the Pack:** The latest proprietary model, Gemini 3 Pro (Google DeepMind, 2025b), demonstrated the strongest overall performance, achieving 74-80% accuracy across all subtasks. This positions it as the current state-of-the-art for multimodal judgment on these complex tasks.
- **Moderate Performance from Other Frontier Models:** GPT-5 (OpenAI, 2025b) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (Gemini Team, 2025) achieved moderate performance, with accuracies ranging from 66-75%.
- **GPT-4o is Insufficient for Frontier Evaluation:** GPT-4o (OpenAI, 2024), commonly used as an evaluator in existing benchmarks, only attained 51-65% accuracy. This suggests it is no longer suitable for reliably evaluating frontier multimodal models, particularly on reasoning-heavy tasks, where its performance was lowest.
- **Open-Source Models Lag, Especially in Reasoning:** Qwen3-VL-32B (Qwen Team, 2025) emerged as the best performing open-source model, achieving 55-69% accuracy. While showing progress, open-source models exhibited a notable performance gap compared to top API models, especially in multimodal reasoning. For example, the strongest open-source model on reasoning (Qwen3-VL-30BA3B at 57.3%) trailed Gemini 3 Pro by 22 percentage points.
- **Scaling Trends Observed:** Within families of models (e.g., Gemma 3, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL), performance generally improved with an increasing number of parameters.

#### 4.2. Performance of Supervised Reward Models

- **Task-Specific RMs Are Less Reliable on MMRB2:** Traditional task-specific metrics (e.g., CLIPScore, VQAScore) and preference-trained reward models (e.g., ImageReward, HPSv2/3, PickScore, EditReward, UnifiedReward) struggled on MMRB2's challenging prompts and frontier model outputs. For instance, ImageReward and VQAScore achieved only 54.0% and 58.3% respectively on text-to-image evaluation, significantly below MLLM-as-a-judge baselines like Qwen3-VL-32B (64.1%) and Gemini 3 Pro (74.4%).
- **Preference Training Improves Over Heuristics:** Training with human preferences *did* improve performance for these models compared to purely heuristic metrics. For example, EditReward, trained on image editing preferences, showed a +9.4% gain over a Qwen2.5-VL-7B judge.
- **Distribution Shift is a Factor:** The underperformance of many preference-trained RMs compared to stronger MLLMs is attributed partly to a distribution shift; many RMs were trained on data from earlier-generation systems, making them less effective at judging outputs from more capable, recent models. Strong MLLMs, with their broader capabilities, set a higher bar through simple prompting.

#### 4.3. Correlation with Downstream Tasks

- A key finding is the **strong correlation (Pearson's r > 0.8 for all tasks and models)** between performance on MMRB2 and success in various downstream generative AI tasks. When different reward models were used for Best-of-N sampling on benchmarks like GenAI-Bench, GEdit-Bench, ISGBench, and EMMA, higher MMRB2 scores consistently led to better downstream performance. This empirically validates MMRB2's utility as a reliable proxy for downstream effectiveness, demonstrating its practical value in model selection and optimization.

#### 4.4. Fine-Grained Analysis of Errors

- **Same-Model vs. Different-Model Pairs:** Judges consistently achieved higher agreement with human preferences on pairs comparing outputs from *different* models (different-model pairs) than on pairs comparing two outputs from the *same* model (same-model pairs). The gap ranged from 5-13 percentage points for top judges in generative tasks. This suggests that current judges find it more difficult to discern fine-grained quality differences between very similar outputs from a single model.
- **Multimodal Reasoning: Image Bias:** In multimodal reasoning tasks, judges exhibited a strong bias towards responses that included images. When humans preferred a response containing images over a text-only response in mixed-modality pairs, judges performed significantly better (e.g., GPT-5 showed a 49.3-point performance gap). Conversely, when humans preferred a text-only response over one with images, judge accuracy dropped substantially. Even Gemini 3 Pro, the best overall judge, showed a 17.9-point gap. This highlights a critical failure mode: current AI judges struggle to recognize the value of concise, accurate text-only reasoning when an image-containing alternative is present.

#### 4.5. Test-Time Scaling of Rewards

- **Modest Gains for Some Models:** Employing test-time scaling (taking a majority vote from K independent judgments) yielded only modest improvements (0.8–1.2%) for GPT and Gemini models (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro improved from 71.3% to 72.5% at K=9).
- **No Improvement for Qwen3-VL:** Qwen3-VL models showed no measurable performance gains from test-time scaling. This suggests that simply increasing inference attempts may not be an effective scaling strategy for all multimodal reward models and that alternative methods might be necessary.

In summary, MMRB2 successfully reveals the current limitations of both MLLM-as-a-judge and supervised reward models for omni AI, while also demonstrating the strong potential of frontier models like Gemini 3 Pro. The detailed analyses provide concrete insights into specific failure modes, offering clear directions for future research to improve multimodal reward modeling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Multimodal RewardBench 2 (MMRB2) represents a significant and timely contribution to the rapidly evolving field of multimodal artificial intelligence, particularly in the critical area of model evaluation and alignment. Its significance and potential impact span several key dimensions:

1. **Establishing a Foundational Benchmark for Omni Reward Models:** MMRB2 is the **first comprehensive benchmark** specifically designed for "omni reward models" capable of evaluating interleaved text and image sequences. This is a crucial step for a nascent yet rapidly advancing area of AI. By providing a standardized, challenging, and high-quality evaluation framework, MMRB2 lays the groundwork for systematic research and development, similar to how benchmarks revolutionized progress in text-only and earlier multimodal models.

2. **Advancing MLLM Evaluation and Understanding:**
 - **New State-of-the-Art:** MMRB2 identifies and quantifies the performance of frontier models like Gemini 3 Pro as highly capable judges, setting a new bar for MLLM-as-a-judge capabilities. This helps the community understand what is currently achievable in automated multimodal evaluation.
 - **Revealing Limitations of Current Evaluators:** The benchmark critically highlights that widely used evaluators, such as GPT-4o, are no longer sufficient for accurately assessing frontier multimodal models, especially on complex reasoning tasks. This forces the community to re-evaluate current practices and adopt more capable evaluation tools.
 - **Driving MLLM Research:** By identifying the specific weaknesses of MLLM judges (e.g., bias towards images in reasoning, difficulty with fine-grained distinctions), MMRB2 provides clear, data-driven directions for improving future multimodal LLMs themselves, not just their reward modeling capabilities.

3. **Guiding the Development of Future Omni Models:** Reward models are central to training high-performing, human-aligned AI systems through RLHF. A robust benchmark for omni reward models is therefore indispensable for:
 - **Improved Post-Training:** Enabling more effective fine-tuning and alignment of omni models with human preferences for complex multimodal generation and reasoning tasks.
 - **Error Analysis and Mitigation:** The fine-grained error analysis, particularly concerning the bias towards image-containing responses and difficulty with same-model comparisons, offers concrete insights for researchers to address specific failure modes in reward model design and training. This can lead to more nuanced and unbiased multimodal AI.
 - **Benchmarking Generative Capabilities:** While not its primary goal, the win rate analysis of generative models on MMRB2's tasks provides an additional, robust measure of current generation capabilities, informing the development of next-generation multimodal generative models.

4. **Practical Impact on Downstream Applications:** The demonstrated strong correlation between MMRB2 performance and success in downstream tasks (e.g., using Best-of-N sampling) underscores its practical utility. This means that improvements in MMRB2 scores directly translate to better real-world performance for applications relying on omni models, making the benchmark a valuable tool for model selection, quality control, and maximizing the effectiveness of generative AI deployments.

5. **Setting a Future Research Agenda:** The "Limitations and Future Directions" section openly discusses avenues for expanding MMRB2 to other modalities (video, audio, 3D), richer task formats (multi-turn, agentic interactions), and additional evaluation dimensions (safety, bias, multilingualism). This forward-looking perspective positions MMRB2 not just as a static benchmark but as a modular foundation for continuous evolution, inviting broader participation and innovation from the research community.

In essence, MMRB2 is more than just a dataset; it is a critical infrastructure that will accelerate the development, evaluation, and alignment of the next generation of highly capable multimodal AI systems, ensuring they are more useful, reliable, and consistent with human intent. The backing of FAIR at Meta Superintelligence Labs further ensures that this benchmark is well-positioned to become a widely adopted standard in multimodal AI research.
