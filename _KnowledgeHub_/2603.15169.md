# ForceVLA2: Unleashing Hybrid Force-Position Control with Force Awareness for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ForceVLA2 is an end-to-end framework integrating hybrid force-position control and explicit force awareness into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for contact-rich manipulation. This approach achieved an average success rate of 66% across five tasks, demonstrating improved reliability and robustness over existing VLA baselines, notably outperforming π0 by 48%.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on position control, limiting their stability, precision, and robustness in contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- Force information, when incorporated, is often treated as a passive perceptual input rather than an active control signal for adaptive, closed-loop interaction.
- Current VLAs lack effective multi-scale reasoning, struggling to integrate force perception with active force-position interaction across different task stages and subtasks.

## Method

- **ForceVLA2** introduces a ==dual-level architecture== where a ==VLM expert== uses ==force-based prompts== for long-horizon, force-aware task planning.
- An ==action expert== employs a ==Cross-Scale Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== to adaptively fuse high-level VLM guidance with real-time interaction forces for ==closed-loop hybrid force-position regulation==.
- A dedicated ForceVLA2-Dataset was created, including 1,000 trajectories across five contact-rich tasks with multi-view images, task prompts, proprioceptive states, and explicit force prompts and signals.

## Results

- ForceVLA2 achieved an average success rate of **66%** across five contact-rich manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming state-of-the-art baselines, including π0 (**18%**) and ForceVLA (**35%**).
- The framework demonstrated enhanced robustness, actively adjusting interaction forces to avoid arm overload, maintaining stable contact under dynamic perturbations, and adapting to misaligned surfaces.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of force prompts, the multimodal encoder, and the Cross-Scale MoE, with the MoE providing the most significant performance gain of **26%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit force awareness through force prompts for high-level VLM reasoning and a direct force pathway into the MoE for low-level control is crucial for robust contact-rich manipulation.
> - A Cross-Scale Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) dynamically routes information from visual, state, and force modalities, allowing the policy to prioritize relevant cues based on the current manipulation phase.
> - Probabilistic modeling of subtask transitions, combined with a flow-matching policy for force-aware action generation, enables the framework to manage complex multi-stage contact tasks effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements with large vision-language models (VLMs) demonstrating capabilities in perception and reasoning. However, these models have largely operated within virtual domains. The current research landscape is moving towards Vision-Language-Action (VLAs) models, which extend VLMs to embodied intelligence, connecting perception and reasoning with physical interaction. This integration aims to enable robots to understand and manipulate objects in the physical world, laying a foundation for enhanced sample efficiency, generalizability, and adaptive behavior in complex environments.

Despite progress in semantic grounding and language following by recent VLAs like OpenVLA and $\pi_0$, a significant limitation remains: these models generally lack the ability to reason about physical dynamics and engage in fine-grained contact interactions. While some efforts, such as ForceVLA, have incorporated force as an auxiliary perceptual input, existing VLA frameworks typically do not leverage force for active, adaptive closed-loop interaction. This contrasts with human motor control, where real-time force sensing is critical for adaptively refining both force and position during interaction, guiding subtasks, and managing contact.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing ForceVLA2, a framework that aims to unify force awareness with hybrid force-position control within the VLA paradigm. It positions itself as the first end-to-end hybrid force-position control framework with explicit force awareness for VLAs, thereby advancing the capabilities of embodied intelligence in contact-rich manipulation tasks beyond purely position-based control or passive force sensing. The work builds upon and critiques previous VLA models, particularly $\pi_0$ and ForceVLA, by emphasizing the need for active force regulation integrated at multiple levels of the control hierarchy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel vision-language-action framework, ForceVLA2, that equips robots with active hybrid force-position control and explicit force awareness to improve performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current VLA models:
- **Limited Physical Interaction in VLAs**: Existing VLAs primarily rely on position control, which restricts their stability, precision, and robustness when performing tasks that involve physical contact. They generally lack explicit mechanisms for sensing and regulating interaction forces.
- **Passive Role of Force Information**: While some VLAs incorporate force sensing, it is often treated as a passive perceptual input rather than an active control signal. This prevents robots from adaptively responding to real-time physical interactions.
- **Absence of Multi-Scale Force Reasoning**: Current VLAs do not effectively reason about spatiotemporal relationships across different task stages, nor do they integrate force perception with active force-position interaction within subtasks. This contrasts with human behavior, where force sensing and regulation are integrated hierarchically for precise manipulation.
- **Challenges in Contact-Rich Scenarios**: In tasks requiring subtle contact interactions, such as assembly or wiping, visual cues alone can be ambiguous or insufficient. Force feedback provides essential information that existing models struggle to leverage effectively.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives and contributions of ForceVLA2 are:
1. **Introduce ForceVLA2**: To design and implement an end-to-end hybrid force-position control framework for VLAs. This involves developing a dual-level architecture where a VLM expert uses force-based prompts for long-horizon, force-aware task planning, and an action expert employs a Cross-Scale Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) to adaptively fuse this knowledge with real-time interaction forces for closed-loop hybrid force-position regulation.
2. **Construct ForceVLA2-Dataset**: To create a comprehensive dataset specifically for contact-rich manipulation. This dataset includes 1,000 trajectories across five tasks (wiping, pressing, assembling) with multi-view images, task prompts, proprioceptive states, and notably, force prompts and force signals incorporated into the action space. This dataset aims to bridge the gap between embodied hybrid force-position interaction and force-aware task concepts for training and evaluation.
3. **Demonstrate Enhanced Performance**: To experimentally validate that ForceVLA2 substantially improves success rates and reliability in various contact-rich manipulation tasks. The research aims to show that the proposed framework mitigates common failure modes, such as arm overload and unstable contact, surpassing the performance of state-of-the-art VLA baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ForceVLA2 is designed as a unified vision-language-action framework that integrates multi-scale force cues for both long-horizon reasoning and short-horizon reactive manipulation, drawing inspiration from human sensorimotor control.

**Architectural Design (ForceVLA2 Framework)**:
The framework takes multi-view images, task prompts, force prompts, and proprioceptive states (end-effector (EE) pose and force signals) as input.

1. **Long-Horizon Force Awareness via Prompting**:
 - **Force Prompts**: To address the insufficiency of purely vision-language inputs in contact-rich scenarios, force prompts are introduced. These are textual cues that indicate the current subtask and encode stage-specific physical context, enabling the VLM to form force-aware task concepts. This mechanism allows the model to leverage pre-trained VLM knowledge for assessing subtask completion, transitioning between stages, and guiding force-aware manipulation.
 - **VLM Integration**: Visual observations from multiple RGB cameras, along with a global task instruction (text prompt) and the stage-specific force prompt, are jointly encoded. A SigLIP-based vision-language model (based on PaliGemma) processes these inputs. Visual tokens are processed by a visual encoder, and linguistic tokens (concatenation of text and force prompts) by a text encoder. Their outputs are then concatenated and fed into the VLM, producing fused contextual tokens that serve as high-level guidance for task sequencing and spatial-semantic reasoning.

2. **Short-Horizon Force-to-Control Loop**:
 - **Multi-modal Encoding for EE 6D Pose and Force**: The EE 6D pose ($p$) is encoded through a linear layer. Raw 6D force-torque readings ($f_{raw}$) are similarly projected into the model embedding space. These encoded pose and force tokens are concatenated to form a multi-modal state representation.
 - **Direct Force Pathway**: A key innovation is that the force embedding ($E_F$) *bypasses* the higher-level cross-modal interaction and feeds directly into the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module. This creates a short-term, reactive pathway, which is theorized to preserve gradient fidelity for rapid force feedback, reduce over-reliance on past trajectories, and support active exploration (as discussed in Appendix A).
 - **Cross-modal Interaction**: The state embeddings interact with the high-level visual-language features (from the VLM expert) through cross-attention. This process injects global task semantics into the local proprioceptive streams, allowing the model to interpret force and motion signals under the guidance of task intent.
 - **Cross-Scale Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)**: The conditioned multi-modal sequence (fused visual-language, cross-attended state, and directly injected force) is processed by a sparse MoE layer. This MoE contains three modality-specific experts (visual, state, and force), implemented as lightweight MLPs. A dynamic gating network computes token-wise routing weights to adaptively activate the most relevant expert for the current manipulation phase. This mechanism allows the network to prioritize visual reasoning during free-space motion and shift emphasis to force or position cues when contact and compliance become critical.
 - **Flow Matching Policy for Position-Force Control**: The fused representation from the MoE is then projected to the latent space of a flow-matching policy. This policy generates force-aware actions by progressively denoising a noise-initialized action, conditioned on the multi-modal context. The final action output $a_t$ comprises a desired change in end-effector pose ($\Delta p_t$) and a predicted contact force ($f_t$), enabling hybrid force-position control.
 - **Probabilistic Modeling of Subtask Transition**: The model also outputs a continuous value $s_t$ (from 0 to 1) indicating subtask progress. When $s_t$ exceeds a threshold, the controller transitions to the next subtask. The ground truth for $s_t$ during training is calculated based on observed orientational alignment, remaining distance to the goal, and instantaneous contact force using specific probability distributions (Beta, Exponential, Uniform).

**ForceVLA2-Dataset**:
- To support training and evaluation, the authors collected a new dataset specifically for contact-rich manipulation, emphasizing force-control signals and subtask annotation.
- **Hardware**: A 7-DOF Flexiv Rizon 4s robotic arm with a DH Robotics AG-95 adaptive gripper. Visual observations were captured by three RGB cameras (two static Intel RealSense D455, one wrist-mounted D435). A 6D force/torque sensor recorded interaction forces.
- **Teleoperation**: Demonstrations were collected using the force-feedback GELLO teleoperation framework, which allowed human operators to record high-quality trajectories with natural motion dynamics and synchronized force and motion data.
- **Tasks**: The dataset includes 1,000 trajectories across five contact-rich tasks: "press bottle," "clean vase," "clean board," "retrieve plate," and "assemble gears." Object poses were varied to enhance diversity.
- **Annotation**: Each task was segmented into 3–5 subtasks, with boundaries annotated offline based on visual inspection and analysis of synchronized force signals.
- **Modalities**: The dataset comprises time-aligned streams of RGB images, end-effector 6D poses, force signals, task prompts, and force prompts, providing comprehensive data for force-aware and subtask-structured policy learning.

**Training and Implementation**:
The ForceVLA2 model was trained on 8 A100 GPUs for 30,000 steps with a batch size of 32, taking approximately 10 hours. It uses an AdamW optimizer with a cosine decay learning rate schedule. The inference speed achieved was 15 Hz on a 4090 GPU. The setup is designed to be hardware-agnostic, using Jacobian-based mapping for execution, which allows compatibility with various robot types. Real-world evaluation was prioritized due to the sensitivity of force interactions to simulation fidelity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated ForceVLA2's performance on 5 contact-rich manipulation tasks from the ForceVLA2-Dataset, using success rate (%) over 20 trials as the primary metric. Comparisons were made against baselines including ACP, $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, ForceVLA, and $\pi_0$ with naive force input ($\pi_0$ w/ F).

**Overall Performance on Contact-Rich Tasks**:
- **Superior Success Rates**: ForceVLA2 achieved an average success rate of 66% across all tasks. This significantly outperformed $\pi_0$ (18.0% average), $\pi_{0.5}$ (31.0% average), ForceVLA (35.0% average), and $\pi_0$ w/ F (17.0% average).
- **Performance Gap**: ForceVLA2 surpassed $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$ by 48.0% and 35.0% respectively. On particularly force-sensitive tasks like "assemble gears," ForceVLA2 achieved a 70% success rate, outperforming the second-best method (ForceVLA) by 50 percentage points.
- **Effectiveness of Force Integration**: Models incorporating explicit force feedback (ForceVLA2, ForceVLA) generally demonstrated improved performance on force-sensitive tasks compared to those without. However, $\pi_0$ w/ F, which used a simple concatenation of force input, performed similarly to the base $\pi_0$, suggesting that structured force integration is crucial and naive input can degrade pre-trained VLA representations. ACP, leveraging admittance control, achieved only a 16.0% success rate, indicating limited generalization.

**Qualitative Performance and Robustness**:
- **Force-Intensive Tasks and Overload Avoidance**: In tasks like "clean board" and "bottle pressing," which are prone to robot arm overload due to excessive contact force, ForceVLA2 demonstrated the ability to actively adjust interaction forces, successfully completing tasks where other VLAs failed due to lack of reactive adjustment.
- **Dynamic Force-Tracking under Perturbations**: When subjected to sudden external perturbations (e.g., lowering the table during "bottle pressing"), ForceVLA2 reacted quickly to new contact configurations and completed the task, showcasing robust dynamic force-tracking. Competing VLAs often failed to maintain stable contact.
- **Robust Wiping and Re-targeting**: In the "vase wiping" task, ForceVLA2 maintained contact and adapted its motion to misaligned surfaces, successfully completing the task. In the "retrieve plate" task, it used force-guided probing to explore and autonomously retried after initial grasp failures, demonstrating robust adaptation.

**Ablation Studies**:
- **Component-wise Ablations**: A stepwise ablation study demonstrated the individual contributions of the Force Prompt (FP), Multimodal Encoder (ME), and Cross-Scale MoE (CM) modules. Starting from $\pi_0$ (18.0% average success rate):
 - Adding FP increased the average success rate by 9.0% to 27.0%.
 - Adding FP and ME further increased it by 13.0% to 40.0%.
 - The full ForceVLA2 (with FP, ME, and CM) achieved 66.0% average success rate, with the Cross-Scale MoE module providing the most significant gain of 26.0%.
- **Modality Fusion in Cross-Scale MoE**: An ablation study on the MoE module inputs (Visual Modality (VM) and Force Modality (FM)) showed that both visual-language context and force signals are essential.
 - Using only state (baseline for MoE) yielded 36.0% average success.
 - Adding VM improved it to 50.0% (a 14.0% gain).
 - Adding both VM and FM (the full ForceVLA2 MoE configuration) further improved it to 66.0% (a 16.0% gain over VM only).
 - Interestingly, for "Press Bottle" and "Clean Board," introducing an *extra* force token slightly degraded performance, hypothesized to be due to simpler force requirements in these tasks where additional degrees of freedom could destabilize the policy.
- **Force Injection in Multimodal Encoder (Appendix C)**: Experiments on where to inject the force branch indicated that injecting force into the VLM pathway significantly degraded performance (5.0% average success). Injecting force into the Multimodal Encoder yielded 58.0% success, and fusing it jointly with the state modality (EE 6D pose) further improved it to 66.0%, confirming the effectiveness of the direct, reactive pathway and multi-level fusion strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ForceVLA2 represents a significant advancement in the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action models for robotic manipulation, particularly in tasks requiring fine-grained physical interaction. Its core contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Bridging High-Level Cognition and Low-Level Physical Interaction**: The framework effectively links abstract visual-language reasoning with precise, real-time force-position control. By incorporating force prompts into the VLM expert and integrating force signals via a Cross-Scale MoE in the action expert, ForceVLA2 enables a more comprehensive understanding and execution of contact-rich tasks. This moves VLAs beyond semantic grounding to embody a more human-like physical intelligence.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Robustness**: ForceVLA2's ability to actively regulate interaction forces leads to substantially improved success rates and increased reliability in complex manipulation scenarios. It effectively mitigates common failure modes such as arm overload and unstable contact, and demonstrates robustness under dynamic perturbations and environmental misalignments. This advancement is critical for deploying robots in unstructured real-world environments where precise and compliant physical interaction is essential.
- **Novelty in Hybrid Control and Force Awareness**: The paper introduces the first end-to-end hybrid force-position control framework with explicit force awareness for VLAs. This represents a paradigm shift from passive force sensing to active force regulation, expanding the controllable subspace of robots beyond what is achievable with position-only control. The theoretical analysis supporting the direct, reactive force pathway provides foundational insights into this improved controllability.
- **Contribution of a Specialized Dataset**: The ForceVLA2-Dataset is a valuable contribution to the research community. By providing multi-modal data including unique force prompts and force-control supervision across diverse contact-rich tasks, it offers an empirical foundation for future research in force-aware and subtask-structured manipulation policy learning, addressing a current gap in existing large-scale robot learning datasets.
- **Broadening Robotic Applications**: The capabilities demonstrated by ForceVLA2 have implications for a wide range of robotic applications where precise, compliant, and robust physical interaction is necessary. This includes delicate assembly operations, surgical robotics, collaborative robots interacting with humans, and various tasks in domestic or industrial settings that involve complex contact dynamics.
- **Directions for Future Research**: This work lays the groundwork for further exploration into multi-modal and multi-scale integration within embodied AI. It suggests that moving beyond simplistic force data incorporation to a more architecturally informed, hierarchical integration of force information is a promising path towards more capable and adaptable robotic systems.
