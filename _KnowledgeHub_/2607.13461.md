# Joint On-and-Off Policy Learning for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> JOP-VLN introduces a three-stage training paradigm that integrates off-policy imitation learning with on-policy reinforcement learning for Vision-and-Language Navigation. This framework achieves a new state-of-the-art success rate of 69.9% on the R2R Val-Unseen split using single-view RGB observations and demonstrates adaptability in real-world deployments on a quadruped robot.

## Problem

- Imitation learning (IL) methods in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) suffer from covariate shift, leading to cumulative errors and poor generalization when encountering out-of-distribution states.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for VLN often rely on in-distribution data, which limits their ability to effectively address covariate shift and explore novel states for improved generalization.
- There is a lack of systematic integration between off-policy imitation learning (for error recovery) and on-policy reinforcement learning (for exploration) to leverage both paradigms' strengths in VLN.

## Method

- Developed **JOP-VLN**, a novel ==three-stage progressive training pipeline== that systematically combines ==off-policy imitation learning== with ==on-policy reinforcement learning==, built upon a ==Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct== VLM backbone.
- Introduced VLN-specific adaptations to a joint learning framework, including a ==high-entropy trajectory sampling== strategy for on-policy ==GRPO== to foster exploration.
- Devised an ==error-correction-prioritized trajectory sorting== strategy for imitation learning, enabling the model to focus on and learn more effectively from its frequent failure points.

## Results

- Achieved a new state-of-the-art success rate (SR) of **69.9%** on the R2R Val-Unseen split, surpassing previous methods by **4.8% SR** and **2.6% SPL**, while using only single-view RGB observations.
- Demonstrated strong performance on the RxR Val-Unseen split with **68.0% SR**, **59.3% SPL**, and **71.7% nDTW**, indicating effective long-horizon decision-making.
- Showcased real-world adaptability and consistent instruction following on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot in both outdoor and indoor environments with few-shot adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synergistically combining off-policy imitation learning (for error recovery from expert demonstrations) and on-policy reinforcement learning (for exploration and generalization) creates more robust VLN agents.
> - Focusing RL training on high-entropy action predictions (uncertain decisions) prevents overconfidence and enhances exploration, while prioritizing learning from oracle-corrected mistakes in IL directly improves error recovery.
> - A progressive, multi-stage training approach, starting with foundational skills, then refining error recovery, and finally integrating joint on-and-off policy learning, is effective for building comprehensive navigation capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a fundamental task within the field of embodied AI, requiring an agent to navigate a physical environment by following natural language instructions. Recent advancements have seen the adaptation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for navigation decision-making, leveraging their capabilities in language understanding and visual perception.

Within this landscape, two predominant paradigms have emerged for training VLN agents:
1. **Imitation Learning (IL):** This approach trains agents on expert demonstrations. While effective in acquiring basic skills, IL suffers from covariate shift, where cumulative errors arise when the agent encounters states not present in the training data, leading to poor generalization. The Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) algorithm has been employed to mitigate this by augmenting training sets with oracle-labeled off-policy trajectories, thereby improving error recovery. However, IL-based methods remain heavily reliant on expert demonstrations, which can limit their generalization to novel or unseen environments.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Inspired by the success of Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), some studies have applied reinforcement techniques, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), often following an initial IL phase. These methods aim to enhance reasoning and exploration capabilities. While effective in improving action prediction, the use of in-distribution data in these RL phases often fails to adequately address the covariate shift encountered during inference in new environments.

A notable gap identified by this work is the lack of systematic integration between these two distinct paradigms. Previous efforts like VLN-R1 and OctoNav incorporate GRPO training but primarily leverage existing (in-distribution) trajectories, which may not fully tackle covariate shift. The JOP-VLN framework proposes to bridge this gap by synergistically combining the strengths of off-policy imitation learning, which excels at error recovery and leveraging expert knowledge, with on-policy reinforcement learning, which facilitates exploration and improved generalization. This integration aims to create a more robust and generalizable VLN agent capable of learning from both expert demonstrations and self-exploration.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation behind the JOP-VLN research is to overcome the inherent limitations of current predominant VLN training paradigms, namely imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL), when applied in isolation or sequential, non-integrated manners.

Specifically, the authors identify the following challenges that motivated their work:
- **Covariate Shift in Imitation Learning:** IL-based methods, despite advancements, are prone to cumulative errors when agents encounter out-of-distribution states not covered in expert demonstrations. This leads to poor generalization beyond the training data. While DAgger helps in error recovery, reliance on expert demonstrations still limits robustness in unseen environments.
- **Limited Generalization in Reinforcement Learning:** Current RL approaches for VLN, even those using GRPO, often operate on in-distribution data. This limits their ability to effectively address covariate shift and explore novel states, thereby constraining generalization to new scenarios.
- **Absence of Paradigm Integration:** A significant observation is the lack of a framework that systematically and synergistically integrates the benefits of both off-policy imitation learning (for robust error recovery and leveraging expert data) and on-policy reinforcement learning (for enhanced reasoning, exploration, and generalization).

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the JOP-VLN framework are:
- To introduce a novel VLN framework that **synergistically combines off-policy imitation learning and on-policy exploration**. This aims to harness the dual benefits of error recovery from expert demonstrations and trajectory exploration driven by reward signals.
- To develop a **three-stage progressive training pipeline** that systematically builds navigation capabilities, starting from fundamental skills, moving to improved error recovery, and finally integrating joint on-and-off policy learning.
- To adapt and enhance existing techniques for the VLN context, specifically by introducing **VLM-specific modifications** to a joint learning framework like CHORD. This includes:
 - Implementing a **high-entropy trajectory sampling strategy** to enhance RL training efficiency by preventing the model from becoming overconfident on low-entropy samples and fostering exploration.
 - Devising an **error-correction-prioritized trajectory sorting strategy** for imitation learning, enabling the model to focus on and learn more effectively from its frequent failure points.
- To demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed framework through extensive experiments, achieving **state-of-the-art performance** on challenging VLN benchmarks and validating its adaptability in real-world scenarios.

Through these objectives, the authors aim to advance the development of more generalizable, robust, and capable embodied agents for vision-and-language navigation.

### 3. Methodology and approach

JOP-VLN introduces a novel three-stage progressive training paradigm that integrates off-policy imitation learning (IL) with on-policy reinforcement learning (RL) for Vision-and-Language Navigation. The model architecture is built upon Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, a large vision-language model, and adopts a multi-turn dialogue paradigm. At each step, the model processes a natural language instruction, a long-term memory of sampled past frames, and a short-term memory of recent interleaved observations and actions. This design facilitates efficient KV cache reuse for sequential action chunk prediction.

The three progressive training stages are detailed as follows:

**Stage 1: Multi-task Training**
In the initial stage, the model is co-trained on two distinct tasks to acquire foundational navigation skills and enhance its visual understanding capabilities:
- **Action Prediction:** The model is trained to predict action chunks within the multi-turn dialogue paradigm, which involves generating sequences of up to four discrete actions (forward, left, right, stop).
- **Trajectory Summarization:** The model learns to synthesize textual instructions conditioned on sampled frames from navigation videos. This task aims to improve the model's ability to ground visual information with linguistic descriptions.
For this stage, a large dataset of 793.4k samples for action prediction from R2R, RxR, EnvDrop, and ScaleVLN, and 30.8k video-instruction pairs from R2R and RxR for trajectory summarization are aggregated.

**Stage 2: Imitation Learning with DAgger Trajectories**
This stage focuses on improving the agent's error-recovery capabilities and addressing covariate shift. The Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) algorithm is employed to generate off-policy trajectories:
- A heuristic strategy is used where the learned policy guides navigation, but an oracle intervenes to provide corrected actions when the agent's deviation from the ground-truth trajectory exceeds a predefined threshold.
- Crucially, to mitigate the issue of erroneous actions in StreamVLN's DAgger, JOP-VLN replays all visited states to obtain corresponding oracle action chunks, which are then used for imitation learning. This ensures that the agent learns from accurate expert guidance.
Approximately 11.3k DAgger trajectories are synthesized and combined with existing R2R, RxR, and ScaleVLN trajectories, resulting in 453.3k training samples for this stage.

**Stage 3: Joint On-and-Off Policy Learning with High-entropy and Error-correction-prioritized Trajectories**
The final stage integrates on-policy reinforcement learning (GRPO) with off-policy imitation learning, drawing inspiration from the CHORD framework, but with VLN-specific adaptations.
- **On-policy Exploration with GRPO on High-entropy Trajectories:**
 - The policy generates multiple (G=8) action chunk responses for each input. Each response is evaluated using four reward functions:
 - **Format reward ($R_{format}$):** Binary reward (1 or 0) for adhering to the specified action chunk format.
 - **Exact match reward ($R_{em}$):** Binary reward for an exact match with the oracle action chunk.
 - **Position reward ($R_{pos}$):** Cosine similarity between the predicted and oracle changes in robot pose ($\triangle x, \triangle y, \triangle yaw$).
 - **Distance reward ($R_{dis}$):** Normalized Euclidean distance between predicted and oracle displacements.
 - Advantages ($\hat{A}_i$) are calculated from these rewards. The policy is then optimized using the GRPO objective, incorporating a token-level policy gradient loss for enhanced training stability.
 - **High-entropy Trajectory Sampling:** To foster exploration and prevent "zero-gradient" states, only the top 20% of samples with the highest action token entropies (recorded during DAgger collection) are used for GRPO training. This focuses learning on uncertain predictions.
- **Off-policy Imitation Learning:**
 - The model continues to perform imitation learning by minimizing a sentence-level cross-entropy loss on a corpus of expert demonstrations collected via DAgger.
- **Joint On-and-Off Policy Learning (CHORD Framework Adaptation):**
 - Both GRPO and IL objectives are combined into a single loss function: $L_{CHORD}(\theta) = (1 - \mu)L_{GRPO}(\theta) + \mu L_{IL}(\theta)$.
 - A **dynamic decay schedule for $\mu$** is implemented, starting at 0.5 (equal weighting) and gradually decreasing to 0.05 (RL-dominated) over the first 200 training steps. This stabilizes early RL updates while progressively shifting emphasis towards exploration.
 - **Error-correction-prioritized Trajectory Sorting:** For the IL component, DAgger trajectories are sorted based on their "learning value," defined by the proportion of actions within a trajectory that were corrected by the oracle. This prioritization ensures the model more frequently learns from trajectories where it previously made mistakes, directly enhancing error recovery.
In this stage, an additional 7.4k DAgger trajectories are collected. From these, 33.3k high-entropy samples are used for GRPO, and 30.0k samples are used for IL.

**Training Details:** The Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone is optimized using AdamW. Learning rates for the language model, projector, and vision encoder are progressively adjusted across stages. GRPO parameters, KL coefficient $\beta=0.1$ and clipping parameter $\epsilon=0.2$, are set.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments conducted both in simulation and real-world environments demonstrate the efficacy and robustness of the JOP-VLN framework.

**Simulation Benchmarks (VLN-CE R2R and RxR Val-Unseen splits):**
- **State-of-the-Art Performance on R2R:** JOP-VLN achieves a success rate (SR) of 69.9% on the R2R Val-Unseen split. This represents a new state-of-the-art for methods using single-view RGB observations, surpassing the previous SOTA, CorrectNav, by 4.8% in SR, 8.9% in Oracle Success rate (OS), and 2.6% in Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL). The model also achieved a navigation error (NE) of 3.64. Notably, JOP-VLN also outperforms some methods that utilize panoramic views (e.g., OmniNav, NavFoM).
- **Strong Performance on RxR:** On the RxR Val-Unseen split, JOP-VLN obtains a 68.0% SR and 59.3% SPL, with a normalized dynamic time warping (nDTW) of 71.7%. These results indicate its capability for effective long-horizon decision-making.

**Real-World Qualitative Results:**
- **Adaptability and Robustness:** JOP-VLN was evaluated on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot in challenging physical environments, including an outdoor garden and an indoor office. Using a few-shot adaptation strategy (10 navigation trajectories for fine-tuning per environment), the model demonstrated consistent and accurate instruction following across both settings.
- **Identified Failure Modes:** The primary failure instances in real-world deployment were attributed to ambiguous referents in long instructions, particularly when multiple visually similar landmarks (e.g., several doors in a corridor) co-exist, occasionally leading the agent to enter an incorrect path.

**Ablation Studies (on R2R Val-Unseen split):**
- **Effectiveness of Trajectory Summarization (TS):** Integrating trajectory summarization in Stage 1 improved performance (SR from 49.5% to 49.9%), indicating that enhanced visual understanding contributes to better navigation.
- **Effectiveness of Imitation Learning on DAgger Trajectories:**
 - Finetuning with DAgger trajectories in Stage 2 resulted in a substantial increase in SR from 49.9% to 64.4%, highlighting its role in enhancing error-recovery capabilities.
 - Further integration of newly collected DAgger trajectories in Stage 3 continued to boost SR to 68.1%, underscoring the importance of off-policy DAgger data in mitigating covariate shift.
- **Effectiveness of CHORD with High-entropy Sampling and Error-correction-prioritized Trajectory Sorting (CHORD-ec):**
 - Applying GRPO on high-entropy samples (pure RL in Stage 3) yielded better SPL and NE metrics compared to pure imitation learning, demonstrating the benefits of on-policy exploration.
 - The CHORD framework, which jointly applies GRPO and IL, outperformed both pure IL and pure RL methods in Stage 3. This indicates that combining both paradigms is more effective than either alone.
 - The inclusion of the error-correction-prioritized trajectory sorting strategy further enhanced performance (SR increased from 68.7% to 69.9% in the full model), showing that prioritizing learning from past mistakes is beneficial for model improvement.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The JOP-VLN framework represents a methodological advancement in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) by effectively integrating two previously distinct training paradigms: off-policy imitation learning (IL) and on-policy reinforcement learning (RL). This work addresses the identified gap in the research landscape, where existing methods either suffered from covariate shift and limited generalization (IL) or inadequately handled out-of-distribution states (RL).

The key contributions and their potential impact are:

- **Novel Integrated Training Paradigm:** The introduction of JOP-VLN's three-stage progressive training pipeline provides a structured approach to building robust VLN agents. By synergistically combining IL's error recovery capabilities from expert demonstrations and RL's exploration driven by reward signals, the framework leverages the strengths of both, leading to more capable and generalizable agents.
- **Enhanced Exploration and Error Recovery:** The VLN-specific techniques introduced, namely high-entropy trajectory sampling for GRPO and error-correction-prioritized trajectory sorting for IL, are significant. High-entropy sampling promotes efficient exploration by focusing RL training on uncertain decisions, thereby preventing the model from becoming complacent or stuck in local optima. Error-correction-prioritized sorting ensures that the model actively learns from its past mistakes, directly improving its ability to recover from navigation errors. These techniques contribute to more effective and stable training.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving a new state-of-the-art success rate on the R2R Val-Unseen benchmark, and competitive performance on RxR, demonstrates the practical superiority of the JOP-VLN framework. This performance, achieved with single-view RGB observations, surpasses methods using more extensive sensory inputs like panoramic views, highlighting the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed learning approach.
- **Improved Generalization and Real-World Adaptability:** The significant performance gains on Val-Unseen splits indicate improved generalization to novel environments, a crucial requirement for embodied AI. Furthermore, the qualitative validation in real-world outdoor and indoor scenarios, even with few-shot adaptation, underscores the framework's robustness and potential for practical deployment in complex physical environments. This real-world applicability is a critical step towards developing truly autonomous navigation systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** JOP-VLN sets a new benchmark and provides a strong foundation for future research in VLN and embodied AI. It encourages further exploration into the synergistic integration of diverse learning paradigms, as well as the development of more sophisticated mechanisms for exploration, error correction, and robust generalization in dynamic, real-world settings. The identified failure modes related to ambiguous referents also highlight areas for future work in language grounding and disambiguation.

In summary, JOP-VLN advances the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation by providing a robust, efficient, and highly performing framework that effectively combines the benefits of imitation learning and reinforcement learning, with a clear potential for real-world application.
