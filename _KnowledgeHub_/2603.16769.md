# GDPO-SR: Group Direct Preference Optimization for One-Step Generative Image Super-Resolution

> [!summary] Summary
> GDPO-SR presents a one-step generative image super-resolution framework that incorporates Group Direct Preference Optimization, enhancing generative capacity and visual quality while maintaining efficient inference. The method achieved leading performance, including the highest PSNR (28.18dB), SSIM (0.7839), MUSIQ (65.63), and CLIPIQA (0.7020) on the DRealSR dataset.

## Problem

- One-step generative image super-resolution (ISR) models, while efficient, often suffer from limited generative capacity and deterministic output, hindering diverse detail reconstruction.
- Existing preference optimization algorithms (DPO, GRPO) are not directly applicable or fully effective for one-step ISR due to the lack of stochasticity and limitations in capturing local image details.
- Achieving a balanced trade-off between reconstruction fidelity and perceptual quality in generative ISR remains a challenge for current methods.

## Method

- Developed a ==Noise-Aware One-Step Diffusion== (NAOSD) model with controllable noise injection and an unequal-timestep strategy to enable the generation of diverse ISR outputs from a single low-resolution input.
- Introduced Group Direct Preference Optimization (GDPO), an online preference optimization strategy that generates groups of diverse ISR candidates and calculates group-relative advantages.
- Designed an Attribute-Aware Reward Function (ARF) that adaptively combines full-reference (PSNR) and no-reference (MANIQA, MUSIQ) metrics based on image region characteristics (smooth versus detailed) for robust reward calculation.

## Results

- GDPO-SR consistently improved its NAOSD base model across all metrics in its Attribute-Aware Reward Function (PSNR, MANIQA, MUSIQ) and generalized to other no-reference metrics, validating the GDPO strategy's effectiveness.
- The method achieved leading quantitative performance on several full-reference (PSNR, LPIPS, DISTS) and no-reference (MUSIQ, CLIPIQA) metrics across DRealSR, RealSR, and DIV2K-val datasets, outperforming state-of-the-art diffusion models.
- Qualitative comparisons demonstrated that GDPO-SR produced clearer structures, richer fine details, and more natural textures without introducing significant additional computational overhead during inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting controlled stochasticity into one-step diffusion models is crucial for enabling preference-based reinforcement learning, allowing for diverse sample generation from deterministic inputs.
> - An adaptive reward function, which dynamically weighs fidelity and perceptual quality based on image content characteristics, provides more effective guidance for generative ISR than static or single-metric rewards.
> - Group-based online preference optimization, by computing advantages relative to other generated samples, can effectively enhance both local detail reconstruction and overall visual quality in one-step ISR.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Image Super-Resolution (ISR) is a field focused on reconstructing high-resolution (HR) images from low-resolution (LR) inputs. This work specifically targets Real-World Image Super-Resolution (Real-ISR), which addresses the challenges posed by complex and unknown degradations in real-world LR images, making it a more ill-posed problem than classical ISR. The primary goal in Real-ISR is to synthesize realistic details without introducing visual artifacts.

Historically, generative adversarial networks (GANs) have been a prominent approach for Real-ISR, aiming to enhance perceptual realism. However, GAN-based methods often contend with training instability and the generation of artifacts. More recently, diffusion models have emerged as a powerful alternative, demonstrating enhanced capabilities in synthesizing natural ISR images with richer details.

A significant development within diffusion-based Real-ISR has been the adaptation of pre-trained large-scale text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models, such as Stable Diffusion, as foundational backbones. Early methods leveraging these models, like StableSR, PASD, and SeeSR, typically employ multi-step denoising processes, starting from noise and using the LR image as a control signal. While these multi-step approaches can produce high-quality, detailed outputs, they incur substantial computational costs and are susceptible to generating hallucinated details.

To address the efficiency limitations, one-step diffusion-based Real-ISR methods have been proposed. These methods accelerate inference by directly mapping the LR image to its HR counterpart, often through techniques like distillation, thereby eliminating the random noise initialization and multi-step denoising. While significantly faster, these one-step models generally suffer from limited generative capacity, which can result in a sacrifice of fine details in the reconstructed images.

Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, particularly preference optimization algorithms like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), have shown success in aligning model outputs with human preferences across various generative tasks, including large language models and image generation. The pioneering work of DP$^2$OSR applied Diffusion-DPO to multi-step generative Real-ISR to mitigate hallucinated details and improve visual quality.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of one-step diffusion models and preference-based reinforcement learning. It seeks to bridge the gap in one-step generative ISR by leveraging RL to enhance generative capacity and detail reconstruction, while maintaining the efficiency of a single inference step. The authors identify specific limitations in applying existing DPO and GRPO directly to one-step ISR, which their proposed method aims to overcome.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose Group Direct Preference Optimization (GDPO), a novel approach to integrate reinforcement learning into the training of one-step generative image super-resolution (ISR) models. The overarching goal is to enhance the generative capacity and visual quality of one-step ISR models without compromising their inherent speed advantage or introducing undesired artifacts.

This objective is driven by several identified challenges and limitations in the existing landscape of generative ISR:

1. **Limited Stochasticity in One-Step Generative ISR Models:** Most current one-step diffusion models for Real-ISR operate deterministically, directly mapping a low-resolution (LR) input to a single high-resolution (HR) output. This deterministic nature fundamentally hinders the application of preference optimization algorithms like DPO and GRPO, which require the ability to generate diverse outputs for a given input to learn preferences effectively. The model needs to be able to produce a range of potential outputs, some preferred and some not, for the optimization to take place.

2. **Limitations of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for Real-ISR:** While DPO has proven effective in other domains, its application to Real-ISR faces challenges. DPO typically performs preference optimization using a single pair of positive and negative samples, which are often generated offline. This offline, limited pairing restricts the diversity of training data, potentially limiting the model's ability to learn robust preferences and improve performance comprehensively across varied image content.

3. **Limitations of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for Real-ISR:** GRPO addresses the data diversity issue by generating multiple samples online and computing a group-relative advantage for each. This improves sample efficiency compared to DPO's offline pairs. However, existing GRPO implementations, such as those used in image generation, often calculate the likelihood of the entire image. For ISR tasks, this global likelihood approach overlooks crucial local details that are vital for high-quality reconstruction. Ignoring these local details can lead to degraded visual quality in the reconstructed images, as fine textures and structures are not adequately prioritized.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire to combine the efficiency of one-step ISR with the quality benefits of preference learning. The authors aim to overcome the deterministic nature of existing one-step models and address the specific shortcomings of DPO (limited data diversity) and GRPO (neglect of local details) when applied to the complex task of Real-ISR. This would enable a one-step model to generate more diverse, higher-quality, and perceptually pleasing super-resolved images.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Group Direct Preference Optimization (GDPO) framework for one-step generative ISR consists of two main components: a noise-aware one-step diffusion (NAOSD) model and a preference optimization strategy.

#### 3.1. Noise-Aware One-Step Diffusion (NAOSD) Model

The NAOSD model serves as the base policy model, designed to inject stochasticity into the deterministic nature of existing one-step Real-ISR models, enabling the generation of diverse outputs from a single LR input.

1. **Latent Feature Extraction and Semantic Guidance:** Given an LR input image ($I_{LR}$), a VAE encoder ($E$) extracts its latent feature ($z_{LR}$). Concurrently, a composite prompt extraction module, integrating DAPE and a CLIP text encoder, derives a text embedding ($c_t$) to provide semantic guidance.
2. **Controllable Noise Injection:** To introduce stochasticity, controllable Gaussian noise ($\epsilon \sim N(0, I)$) is injected into the latent feature $z_{LR}$ at a specific noise addition timestep $t_{add}$. This process generates a perturbed latent representation ($\tilde{z}$) as defined by:
 $\tilde{z} = \sqrt{\alpha_{t_{add}}} z_{LR} + \sqrt{\beta_{t_{add}}} \epsilon$, where $\alpha_{t_{add}} + \beta_{t_{add}} = 1$. The value of $t_{add}$ controls the intensity of the injected noise.
3. **One-Step Denoising:** The perturbed latent $\tilde{z}$ is then processed by a UNet, which performs denoising at a diffusion timestep $t_{diff}$, conditioned on $c_t$ and $t_{diff}$. This yields the restored latent $z_{SR}$:
 $z_{SR} = \frac{(\tilde{z} - \sqrt{\beta_{t_{diff}}} \text{UNet}(\tilde{z}, c_t, t_{diff}))}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t_{diff}}}}$, where $\alpha_{t_{diff}} + \beta_{t_{diff}} = 1$.
4. **Image Reconstruction:** Finally, a VAE decoder ($D$) maps the restored latent $z_{SR}$ back to the super-resolved image $I_{SR} = D(z_{SR})$.
5. **Unequal-Timestep Strategy:** To balance the trade-off between generative diversity and reconstruction fidelity, an unequal-timestep strategy is introduced. A larger $t_{add}$ is set to expand the sampling space and increase output diversity, while a more conservative (smaller) $t_{diff}$ is used to stabilize fidelity and prevent performance degradation from excessive noise.
6. **Training Loss:** The NAOSD model is pre-trained using a combination of L1 loss, LPIPS loss, and Variational Score Distillation (VSD) loss. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is applied to fine-tune both the VAE encoder and the UNet.

#### 3.2. Group Direct Preference Optimization (GDPO)

GDPO builds upon the NAOSD model to perform online preference optimization. Both the reference ISR model ($\pi_{ref}$) and the policy ISR model ($\pi_{\theta}$) are initialized with the pre-trained NAOSD model.

1. **Advantage Calculation Stage:**
 - **Sample Group Generation:** For each LR input image, the policy model generates a group of $G$ diverse ISR candidates ($S = \{I_i^{SR}\}_{i=1}^G$) by injecting different random noise samples ($\{\epsilon_i\}_{i=1}^G$) into the NAOSD model.
 - **Attribute-Aware Reward Function (ARF):** Each generated sample $I_i^{SR}$ is evaluated using a specially designed ARF. This function adaptively combines full-reference (FR) metrics (specifically PSNR, measuring fidelity to the ground truth HR image) and no-reference (NR) metrics (MANIQA and MUSIQ, evaluating perceptual quality).
 - The adaptive weighting mechanism calculates the proportion of smooth regions ($\rho_s$) and detailed regions ($\rho_d$) in the image. This is achieved by converting the image to grayscale, partitioning it into patches, computing Sobel gradient magnitude histograms, and then Shannon entropy to categorize patches as smooth or detailed.
 - The reward for each sample ($R_i$) is calculated as:
 $R_i = \rho_s \sum_{f \in G_{FR}} \frac{s_{f_i}}{|G_{FR}|} + \rho_d \sum_{f \in G_{NR}} \frac{s_{f_i}}{|G_{NR}|}$, where $s_{f_i}$ is the min-max normalized score for metric $f$.
 - **Group-Relative Advantage Calculation:** The absolute reward scores ($R_i$) are converted into group-relative advantages ($A_i$) using a normalization approach, similar to GRPO:
 $A_i = \frac{R_i - \text{mean}(\{R_j\}_{j=1}^G)}{\text{std}(\{R_j\}_{j=1}^G)}$. This formulation informs the model about the relative quality of samples within the generated group.

2. **Policy Optimization Stage:**
 - **GDPO Loss Formulation:** The policy model's parameters are updated by minimizing a modified Diffusion-DPO loss that incorporates the calculated group-relative advantages. The GDPO loss is formulated as:
 $L_{GDPO} = -\mathbb{E}_{x_0 \sim D, x_t \sim q(x_t|x_0)} \log \sigma \left(-\omega \left(\sum_{i=1}^G A_i \left(\| \epsilon - \pi_{\theta}(x_t, t) \|_2^2 - \| \epsilon - \pi_{ref}(x_t, t) \|_2^2 \right) \right) \right)$,
 where $x_t$ is the noisy latent of ISR candidates, $\omega$ is a hyperparameter, and $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid function.
 - **Optimization Mechanism:** This loss function drives the policy model to prioritize samples with higher advantages. A larger $A_i$ for a sample increases its weight in the sum, making the argument to the sigmoid function more negative, which in turn increases $\log \sigma(\cdot)$ and guides the gradient to favor these high-reward samples. This approach combines the precision of DPO's pixel-level constraints (inheriting Diffusion-DPO's implicit likelihood computation) with the efficiency and group-wise learning of GRPO, enabling effective learning of local details.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of GDPO-SR was evaluated through extensive experiments on both real-world (DRealSR, RealSR) and synthetic (DIV2K-val) datasets, comparing it against its base model, state-of-the-art SD-based methods, and other RL-based approaches.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Base Model (NAOSD)

- GDPO-SR demonstrated consistent improvements across all metrics incorporated into its Attribute-Aware Reward Function (ARF), including PSNR, MANIQA, and MUSIQ. For instance, on the RealSR dataset, PSNR improved from 25.25dB to 25.48dB, MANIQA from 0.6459 to 0.6615, and MUSIQ from 69.06 to 69.42.
- The model also showed enhanced performance on metrics not included in ARF, particularly no-reference (NR) metrics like CLIPIQA and AFINE, signifying broader quality improvements.
- These results validate that the GDPO strategy effectively enhances the performance of the NAOSD base model, particularly highlighting its generalization capability under realistic degradations on real-world datasets.

#### 4.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

- **Quantitative Performance:** GDPO-SR achieved leading performance among all compared methods on several full-reference (FR) metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, and DISTS) across all three datasets. It also showed strong performance in SSIM and FID, indicating its ability to reproduce fidelity and structure. For NR metrics, GDPO-SR maintained overall leading performance, suggesting that it produces visually more natural and pleasing results in addition to restoring high-fidelity details.
 - On the DRealSR dataset, GDPO-SR recorded the highest PSNR (28.18dB), SSIM (0.7839), MUSIQ (65.63), and CLIPIQA (0.7020), alongside the lowest LPIPS (0.2851) and DISTS (0.2112), surpassing both multi-step (e.g., PASD, SeeSR) and one-step (e.g., OSEDiff, InvSR) approaches.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Visual comparisons demonstrated that GDPO-SR produces clearer structures and richer fine details compared to its base model NAOSD and other SD-based methods. Examples showed GDPO-SR reconstructing accurate fine texture patterns and legible characters, where competing methods produced oversaturated, over-smoothed, or inaccurately shaped details.

#### 4.3. Comparison with RL-based Methods

- When compared to DP$^2$OSR, a multi-step RL-based Real-ISR method, DP$^2$OSR showed better NR metrics but significantly poorer FR metrics, suggesting a trade-off where generative capability comes at the cost of fidelity.
- In contrast, GDPO-SR, as a one-step diffusion model, achieved a more balanced performance, improving fidelity while maintaining competitive perceptual quality. Qualitative results confirmed that GDPO-SR produced more faithful reconstructions, whereas DP$^2$OSR generated richer but sometimes inconsistent details.

#### 4.4. Complexity Analysis

- GDPO-SR exhibited similar inference time and FLOPs as OSEDiff, a one-step diffusion approach, indicating that the integration of noise injection and GDPO did not introduce additional computational overhead during inference.
- While having slightly more parameters than InvSR, GDPO-SR demonstrated higher inference efficiency and smaller FLOPs, suggesting an optimized balance between model size and computational cost.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of GDPO:** GDPO outperformed Diffusion-DPO and DanceGRPO, highlighting its advantage in leveraging inter-group online samples for balanced optimization of both FR and NR metrics. DanceGRPO improved NR metrics but degraded FR metrics, suggesting its global likelihood estimation was insufficient for capturing local details.
- **Effectiveness of ARF:** Experiments on the ARF components showed that relying solely on either FR or NR metrics for rewards led to trade-offs (e.g., FR-only improved fidelity but degraded perception). The adaptive weighting in ARF, which dynamically adjusted weights based on smooth and detailed regions, consistently yielded the best performance across all metrics, confirming its role in reliable image quality evaluation.
- **Effect of Group Size (G):** Increasing the group size G (from 4 to 8) generally improved the model's generative capability, indicated by higher NR metrics. G=6 was identified as a favorable balance point, where larger diversity led to more effective preference learning.
- **Timestep in NAOSD ($t_{add}, t_{diff}$):** Increasing $t_{add}$ and $t_{diff}$ together enhanced generative ability and sample diversity (larger fluctuation range) but compromised fidelity. The unequal-timestep strategy ($t_{add}$=250, $t_{diff}$=100) successfully balanced fidelity and generative capacity by allowing greater noise injection for diversity while maintaining controlled denoising for fidelity.

#### 4.6. Comparisons with GAN-based Methods (Supplementary)

- GDPO-SR outperformed three representative GAN-based methods (RealESRGAN, BSRGAN, LDL) on most NR metrics across all test datasets, while delivering competitive results on FR metrics. This indicates GDPO-SR's superior balance of fidelity and perceptual quality compared to GAN-based counterparts.

In summary, the findings indicate that GDPO-SR effectively addresses the limitations of prior one-step and RL-based ISR methods. It consistently produces super-resolved images with enhanced visual quality and structural fidelity, demonstrating superior performance and generalization across diverse datasets and degradation types.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Group Direct Preference Optimization (GDPO) as a novel framework for one-step generative Image Super-Resolution (ISR), addressing several challenges in the field. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in One-Step Generative ISR:** GDPO-SR directly tackles the limitation of limited generative capacity and stochasticity in existing one-step diffusion-based ISR models. By introducing a noise-aware one-step diffusion (NAOSD) model with an unequal-timestep strategy, the work enables the generation of diverse outputs, a prerequisite for effective preference learning. This advances the utility of one-step models, making them capable of generating higher-quality and more varied results without sacrificing their inherent speed advantage.

2. **Novel Integration of Reinforcement Learning for ISR:** The paper proposes a sophisticated integration of preference optimization into the ISR pipeline. It transcends the limitations of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which is constrained by offline-generated, limited sample pairs, and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which tends to overlook local image details. GDPO combines the benefits of online sample generation with pixel-level constraints and group-relative advantage formulation, leading to more robust and precise learning of human preferences in image quality.

3. **Enhanced Perceptual Quality and Fidelity Balance:** A key contribution is the Attribute-Aware Reward Function (ARF), which dynamically balances fidelity-related and perception-related metrics based on the varying content of image regions (smooth versus detailed). This adaptive evaluation system is crucial for generative ISR, where a trade-off between fidelity (e.g., PSNR) and perceptual quality (e.g., realism) is often observed. GDPO-SR demonstrates a more favorable balance, delivering both clearer structures and richer fine details, which is a substantial improvement over methods that prioritize one over the other.

4. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The experimental results show that GDPO-SR achieves superior performance across multiple real-world and synthetic datasets compared to numerous state-of-the-art multi-step and one-step diffusion models, as well as other RL-based and GAN-based approaches. This indicates enhanced robustness to various real-world degradations and better generalization capabilities, making it more applicable in practical scenarios.

5. **Practical Efficiency:** By operating in a one-step inference manner, GDPO-SR maintains high computational efficiency. The complexity analysis confirms that the GDPO framework does not add significant computational overhead during inference, making it suitable for applications requiring real-time or near real-time super-resolution, such as in consumer electronics or professional imaging workflows.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as the increased training overhead due to generating multiple outputs and the heuristic nature of the current reward function, provide clear directions for future work. This includes exploring more efficient sample generation strategies during training and developing reward functions that align more closely with complex human visual perception, potentially through learned reward models.

Overall, this work makes a notable contribution to generative image super-resolution by establishing a new, effective paradigm that leverages reinforcement learning to overcome existing limitations of one-step diffusion models. It provides a foundation for developing highly efficient and perceptually superior image enhancement systems.
