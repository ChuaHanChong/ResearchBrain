# Sparsh: Self-supervised touch representations for vision-based tactile sensing

> [!summary] Summary
> Sparsh introduces a family of self-supervised touch representations for vision-based tactile sensors, trained on over 460,000 unlabeled tactile images to reduce reliance on costly labeled data. The work also establishes TacBench, a standardized benchmark for evaluating these representations, demonstrating Sparsh's consistent outperformance of end-to-end models by an average of 95.1% across diverse manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-based tactile sensing in robotics often requires developing custom perception models for each specific manipulation task or sensor type.
- Training these task- and sensor-specific models necessitates large amounts of expensive and difficult-to-obtain labeled real-world tactile data.
- A lack of standardized benchmarks and broadly applicable pre-trained models hinders reproducible research and consistent progress in tactile sensing.

## Method

- Develops Sparsh, a family of self-supervised learning models utilizing Vision Transformers, adapting Masked Autoencoders (MAE), DINO/DINOv2, and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) for tactile image data.
- Curates a large dataset of over 460,000 unlabeled tactile images by combining newly collected 'Touch-Slide' data with existing datasets to pre-train Sparsh models.
- Establishes TacBench, a standardized benchmark comprising six diverse, touch-centric tasks (e.g., force estimation, slip detection, pose estimation, grasp stability, textile recognition, manipulation planning) to evaluate tactile representations.

## Results

- Sparsh models consistently outperformed end-to-end trained baselines, achieving an average 95.1% improvement, especially when labeled data was limited.
- Latent-space SSL methods (DINO, JEPA) generally performed better than pixel-reconstruction methods (MAE), with Sparsh (VJEPA) excelling in slip detection and Sparsh (DINO) robustly estimating forces and visualizing force fields.
- Policies trained with Sparsh representations for a bead maze task achieved approximately 16% lower trajectory errors in simulation and traversed 20-53% greater distances on a real robot compared to end-to-end policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised learning on large datasets of unlabeled tactile images produces robust, general-purpose representations that generalize across tasks and sensor types.
> - Learning tactile representations in latent space (e.g., via DINO or JEPA) is more effective than pixel-level reconstruction (MAE), improving robustness to noise and variations in lighting or sensor markings.
> - Pre-trained tactile representations significantly reduce the need for large, labeled datasets in downstream tasks, enabling efficient adaptation to new manipulation challenges and facilitating cross-sensor transfer.
