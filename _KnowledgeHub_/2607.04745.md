# Trajectory-Anchor Optimization for Overconfident Thermal Visual Place Recognition: Zero-Leakage OOD Auditing and Kidnapped-Robot Recovery

> [!summary] Summary
> Trajectory-Anchor Optimization (TAO) introduces a computationally bounded, real-time backend that addresses the 'overconfident forced-matching' issue of thermal Visual Place Recognition (VPR) models and the high overhead of Multi-Hypothesis Tracking. It effectively suppresses false positives in unmapped environments by 2-5x and enables rapid kidnapped-robot recovery in 4-5 frames, achieving deterministic operation suitable for embedded robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Modern thermal VPR frontends exhibit 'overconfident forced-matching,' generating highly plausible yet false loop candidates in unmapped (Out-of-Distribution) environments, leading to catastrophic SLAM failures.
- Classical Multi-Hypothesis Tracking (MHT) for spatial ambiguity resolution suffers from exponential computational complexity, making it impractical for real-time robotic deployment.
- There is a need for a computationally deterministic and bounded backend to rigorously audit VPR outputs and provide reliable fail-safe filtering, especially in safety-critical autonomous navigation.

## Method

- **Trajectory-Anchor Optimization (TAO)** frames multi-view temporal verification as independent, closed-form ==SE(2) Procrustes alignment== problems, circumventing MHT's combinatorial explosion.
- The system uses a regularized optimization objective that integrates a weighted ==SE(2) Procrustes alignment==, a ==first-order velocity dynamics constraint== to penalize unstructured hallucinations, and the frontend similarity score.
- Computational determinism is ensured by vectorizing K parallel hypotheses into a single tensor framework, solved efficiently via ==batched Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)== with ==O(KN) complexity==, executing in under 1.2 ms per frame.

## Results

- TAO significantly suppressed False Acceptance Rates (FPRs) of erroneous loop closures by **2–5x** in true map-out scenarios compared to single-view baselines, without scenario-specific parameter tuning.
- The system achieved complete global recovery in kidnapped-robot scenarios within **4 to 5 frames** (**22–27m** physical travel), demonstrating a **100%** coarse global recovery triggering rate within a **10m** tolerance.
- TAO's operations maintain a strict computational bound, executing in less than **1.2 ms** per frame for **K=100** parallel hypotheses and **N=20** temporal window size on standard embedded platforms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Even high-performing deep thermal VPR models are prone to overconfidence and require robust, independent geometric backend verification to ensure SLAM integrity.
> - Passive rigid Procrustes verification has an information-theoretic breakdown point at micro-scales (e.g., 5m operational boundary) where structured false anchor sequences can locally mimic true trajectories.
> - A computationally bounded, deterministic backend like TAO can function as an effective macro-scale fail-safe filter for true map-out environments and a rapid initialization engine for kidnapped-robot recovery, without requiring complex iterative solvers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical challenge in the field of autonomous navigation, specifically concerning Thermal Infrared (TIR) Visual Place Recognition (VPR) and its integration with Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems. TIR cameras are favored for long-term navigation due to their robustness against severe diurnal illumination changes. Recent advancements in deep learning, particularly with foundation models such as AnyThermal-SALAD, have significantly improved the closed-set retrieval accuracy of TIR-VPR frontends.

However, the paper identifies an architectural paradox: improved VPR frontend performance does not automatically guarantee a safer SLAM backend. Deep models, especially when processing low-texture thermal imagery, exhibit an "overconfident forced-matching" failure mode. This phenomenon leads to the generation of highly plausible, locally consistent, yet false loop candidates, even in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) or unmapped environments, without a noticeable drop in similarity scores. Such false positives can corrupt the SLAM graph, leading to catastrophic localization failures.

Classical approaches to mitigate spatial ambiguities, like Multi-Hypothesis Tracking (MHT), maintain divergent trajectory beliefs. However, MHT suffers from exponential hypothesis management overhead, which introduces prohibitive computational latency and violates the real-time constraints essential for robotic operations. This computational burden becomes particularly severe during extended periods in unmapped territory.

This work positions itself at the intersection of robust thermal perception, real-time SLAM, and safety-critical system design. It aims to bridge the gap between advanced but overconfident VPR frontends and the need for computationally bounded, reliable backend verification. By proposing Trajectory-Anchor Optimization (TAO), the authors seek to provide an efficient and deterministic alternative to MHT for OOD auditing and kidnapped-robot recovery, thereby enhancing the safety and operational integrity of autonomous thermal navigation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed vulnerability of modern thermal visual place recognition (TIR-VPR) frontends. Despite their high closed-set retrieval performance, particularly with foundation models, these systems are prone to an "overconfident forced-matching failure mode." In out-of-distribution (OOD) or unmapped environments, they generate highly plausible yet false loop candidates, backed by deceptively high similarity scores. This structural vulnerability can lead to critical errors in a SLAM backend, such as accepting topologically inconsistent loop closures, which can corrupt the global map and cause system failure.

A secondary, but equally important, motivation is to overcome the severe computational limitations associated with classical Multi-Hypothesis Tracking (MHT) backends. While MHT can theoretically mitigate spatial ambiguities by managing multiple localization beliefs, its exponential growth in hypothesis complexity during extended unmapped periods renders it impractical for real-time robotic deployment due to prohibitive computational latency and non-deterministic overheads.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of the Trajectory-Anchor Optimization (TAO) work are detailed as follows:

1. **Develop a Computationally Bounded Backend:** To design a VPR backend that prioritizes strict computational determinism and bounded time-complexity over the combinatorial complexity of MHT. This involves creating a framework that can efficiently evaluate a large pool of parallel hypotheses without dynamic tree expansion or volatile memory allocations, ensuring real-time performance.
2. **Conduct Rigorous OOD Auditing:** To establish a stringent "zero-leakage" evaluation protocol to honestly assess the capabilities and limitations of a lightweight, passive geometric backend in detecting OOD conditions. This includes explicitly mapping the breakdown points of passive verification at different scales (e.g., micro-scale vs. macro-scale).
3. **Provide a Robust Fail-Safe Filter:** To demonstrate that the proposed backend can function as an efficient fail-safe mechanism, capable of isolating catastrophic topological breaks and suppressing critical false acceptances generated by overconfident VPR frontends, especially in true map-out scenarios.
4. **Enable Efficient Kidnapped-Robot Recovery:** To facilitate fast and reliable global relocalization after a robot is "kidnapped" or loses track, by leveraging the deterministic multi-view alignment capabilities of the proposed system.
5. **Bridge Deep VPR and Global Recovery:** To offer an interpretable backend evidence signal that explicitly connects the capabilities of deep thermal VPR, OOD auditing, and global recovery within a unified, computationally minimalist optimization model, without claiming to be a flawless solution for all frontend hallucinations.

The authors explicitly state that the goal is not to present TAO as a universal solution for all frontend hallucinations, but rather to conduct a rigorous analysis of what a lightweight, passive geometric backend can and cannot safeguard, defining its operational boundaries.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces Trajectory-Anchor Optimization (TAO) as a computationally bounded multi-view backend designed to address the overconfidence of TIR-VPR frontends and the computational inefficiencies of Multi-Hypothesis Tracking (MHT).

**From MHT to TAO:**
The core idea behind TAO is to circumvent the combinatorial explosion of MHT by framing the multi-view temporal verification of all candidate hypotheses as independent, closed-form SE(2) Procrustes alignment problems. Unlike MHT, which suffers from non-deterministic overheads due to tree pruning and memory allocation, TAO enforces complete architectural determinism. It evaluates up to K=100 parallel hypotheses concurrently.

**Mathematical Formulation:**
At time *t*, TAO considers a short-term local trajectory $P_t = \{p_{t-N+1}, \dots, p_t\}$ (where $p_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$) integrated from noisy relative odometry within a sliding window of size *N*. For each *j*-th VPR retrieval hypothesis branch, $q_c^j(i)$ denotes the corresponding map-side anchor positions. The system solves the following joint optimization problem for each branch:

$\min_{R,t} \sum_{i=t-N+1}^{t} w_i \rho_\delta \left( \|q_c^j(i) - (Rp_i + t)\|^2 \right) + \lambda \sum_{i=t-N+2}^{t} \|\Delta q_c^j(i) - R\Delta p_i\|_2^2 + \gamma \sum_{i=t-N+1}^{t} w_i (1 - s_{i,c_j(i)})$

Here:
- The first term represents a weighted SE(2) Procrustes alignment, minimizing the distance between the transformed local trajectory points and the map anchors. $\rho_\delta$ is a robust Huber penalty function.
- The second term incorporates a first-order velocity dynamics constraint. It penalizes mismatches between the relative displacement of map anchors ($\Delta q_c^j(i) = q_c^j(i) - q_c^j(i-1)$) and the rotated relative displacement from odometry ($\Delta p_i = p_i - p_{i-1}$). This constraint prevents the frontend from fitting arbitrary or erratic translations (unstructured hallucinations) by penalizing unphysical velocity profiles.
- The third term integrates the frontend similarity score ($s_{i,c_j(i)}$) directly into the optimization cost, penalizing low similarities.
- $\lambda$ and $\gamma$ are weighting parameters for the velocity dynamics and similarity terms, respectively.
- $w_i$ are weights.

**Computational Implementation:**
For fixed branch correspondences, this regularized SE(2) Procrustes alignment problem is solved deterministically using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD). To ensure computational determinism and efficiency, the K parallel hypothesis branches are fully vectorized into a single three-dimensional tensor framework. Cross-covariance matrices for all branches are constructed simultaneously via Einstein summation, yielding a unified tensor. The K separate 2x2 Procrustes optimizations are then executed in a single batched SVD invocation, offloading algebraic computation to optimized C-based libraries. This approach avoids iterative non-linear solvers. Dynamic rejection of erroneous anchors is handled by an analytical residual masking layer using O(1) matrix broadcasting. The computational complexity per query frame is strictly bounded at O(KN), where K is the number of VPR branches and N is the temporal sliding window size, decoupled from the total sequence length. The authors report execution times of less than 1.2 ms per frame for K=100 and N=20 on standard embedded platforms.

**SVD Uncertainty Diagnostics:**
Intermediate singular values from the SVD decomposition are exposed as interpretable backend uncertainty diagnostics:
- **Geometric Capture ($C_{geom} = \sigma_1 \sigma_2$):** Quantifies the 2D spatial support of the trajectory sequence.
- **Anisotropy Ratio ($A_{aniso} = \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2} + \epsilon}$):** Detects degeneracies like aperture effects or straight-line motions where cross-trajectory confidence is unobservable.
- **Proxy Entropy ($H_{proxy} = -\log(C_{geom} + \epsilon)$):** A measure of uncertainty derived from geometric capture.

The total minimized optimization cost ($C_{TAO}$) from Eq. (1) serves as the unified multi-view OOD auditing signal.

**Zero-Leakage Auditing Protocol:**
To ensure an honest and rigorous evaluation, the authors enforced strict "zero-leakage" rules for all experiments:
1. **No Query Ground-Truth Filtering:** Candidate retrieval cannot be pruned using ground-truth spatial proximity at runtime.
2. **No Oracle Resetting:** Localization state cannot be re-initialized with ground truth upon failure.
3. **No Runtime Position Gating:** Query-position thresholds are forbidden during online OOD detection.
4. **Isolated Calibration:** All hyperparameters and validation thresholds are tuned exclusively on a separated calibration split.
5. **A Posteriori Evaluation:** Held-out test metrics are computed strictly offline after online scoring.
Answerability labels are appended only after all runtime-accessible confidence metrics are calculated. For map-out benchmarks, retained maps are built by deleting contiguous segments, and answerability is determined offline based on a 10m radius.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several key findings based on experiments conducted primarily on the STheReO-KAIST thermal dataset and validated on IRSLAM/NUFR KRI.

**Closed-Set Retrieval vs. Metric Answerability:**
Using AnyThermal-SALAD as the frontend on the STheReO-KAIST dataset (morning map, evening query), the system demonstrated high closed-set retrieval performance. At a coarse macroscopic threshold, the un-gated R@100 (<10m) reached 0.999. However, when strict metric boundaries were applied (2.5m and 5m), the percentage of strictly answerable queries and the top-1 recall rates decreased significantly. This indicated that while deep foundation models are effective at generating initial candidate proposals, their raw similarity scores are insufficient for reliable fine-grained localization, necessitating an independent OOD auditing backend.

**Micro-Scale Auditing: Failure Analysis at the 5m Operational Boundary:**
At a strict 5m micro-scale operational boundary for OOD detection, single-frame image-only confidence measures (top-1 score gate) performed poorly, yielding an OOD detection AUC of 0.543. The incorporation of raw SVD geometric confidence metrics only marginally improved this to 0.567. This performance indicated a fundamental limitation: within a 5m radius, overconfident foundation models could generate structured false anchor sequences that were locally parallel to the true trajectory manifold. In such instances, the relative displacements (∆q ≈ R∆p) could still satisfy the first-order velocity term in the optimization, leading to a low cost despite the absolute metric error. This exposed an information-theoretic boundary, suggesting that passive rigid Procrustes verification has limited observability for fine metric resolution at the micro-scale.

**Macro-Scale Auditing: True Map-Out Environments as a Fail-Safe Filter:**
To assess the backend's utility at a macroscopic deployment scale, experiments were conducted in "true map-out" scenarios where the robot traversed physically unmapped territory (contiguous segments were removed from the map database). In these conditions, conventional single-view baselines (e.1., top-1 score gate) exhibited high False Acceptance Rates (FPRs) for false loop closures, reaching up to 32.6% in severe unmapped conditions. In contrast, the minimized TAO optimization cost, reinforced by the velocity dynamics constraint, significantly suppressed these catastrophic FPRs. TAO reduced FPRs to 4.9% in one high-ambiguity segment (h3) and 11.9% in another (h4), representing a 2–5x improvement without scenario-specific parameter tuning. This demonstrated that TAO functions as a robust, computationally efficient macro-scale fail-safe filter, effectively isolating major topological breaks and preventing catastrophic graph corruption in unmapped deployment zones.

**Cross-Environmental Generalizability: Preliminary IRSLAM-KRI Transfer Check:**
A zero-transfer evaluation on the public IRSLAM/NUFR KRI dataset (without re-calibration) was performed to check generalization. Due to the absence of absolute pose ground truth, the evaluation was reformulated as a topological route-progress proxy check. TAO's regularized cost outperformed single-image top-1 score gates in both full and held-out AUC, and it successfully reduced false acceptances. This suggested that TAO's regularized cost captures fundamental structural breaks rather than environment-specific heuristic correlations, indicating a degree of cross-environmental generalizability.

**Active Deployment Integrity: Kidnapped-Robot Global Recovery:**
TAO was benchmarked for kidnapped-robot global recovery under various odometry-drift regimes. It consistently isolated the true global hypothesis branch, achieving complete convergence within a macroscopic 10m tolerance envelope in just 4 to 5 frames, requiring minimal physical travel distances of 22–27m. The system demonstrated a 100% coarse global recovery triggering rate within this practical tolerance. This highlighted TAO's capability as a deterministic, constant-time initialization engine for coarse global recovery, effectively pulling the belief state from a completely kidnapped state back into a region suitable for local fine-trackers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents significant contributions to the fields of autonomous navigation, robotics, and SLAM, particularly within challenging thermal environments.

**Addressing the Frontend Overconfidence Paradox:** The work rigorously demonstrates a critical limitation of modern, high-performing thermal VPR foundation models: their overconfident generation of false loop candidates in OOD or unmapped regions. This highlights that raw frontend similarity scores are insufficient for ensuring autonomous system safety and underscores the necessity of robust backend verification.

**Enabling Real-time SLAM Safety:** By introducing Trajectory-Anchor Optimization (TAO), the authors provide a computationally minimalist and deterministic backend alternative to the prohibitively expensive Multi-Hypothesis Tracking (MHT). TAO's O(KN) bounded complexity and sub-2ms execution time per frame make it suitable for real-time deployment on embedded robotic platforms, thereby addressing a major bottleneck in practical SLAM systems operating in dynamic or unknown environments.

**Robust Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Auditing:** TAO offers a reliable and interpretable multi-view evidence signal for detecting macro-scale topological breaks. By significantly reducing the False Acceptance Rate (FPR) of erroneous loop closures in true map-out scenarios, TAO enhances the integrity of the SLAM graph, preventing catastrophic localization failures and improving the overall safety and trustworthiness of autonomous systems.

**Efficient Kidnapped-Robot Recovery:** The demonstrated ability of TAO to rapidly and reliably recover from kidnapped-robot scenarios within a macroscopic tolerance (e.g., 10m in 4-5 frames) is crucial for real-world robustness. This capability allows autonomous robots to regain localization after significant disorientation, facilitating a seamless transition to fine-grained tracking.

**Transparent Operational Boundaries:** The explicit identification of a "micro-scale breakdown point" at the 5m operational boundary is a significant aspect of this research. By clearly delineating where passive geometric verification struggles due to structured frontend hallucinations, the work sets realistic expectations and prevents overclaiming. This transparency guides future research towards either more active exploration strategies or specialized fine-grained verifiers to achieve higher metric precision.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** TAO can contribute to the deployment of more reliable and safer autonomous robots, especially those operating in demanding thermal environments or conditions involving long-term navigation and exposure to unmapped areas.
- **Foundation for Future SLAM Systems:** The methodology and insights provided, particularly regarding the need for computationally bounded and robust backend verification, can serve as a foundation for developing next-generation SLAM systems that integrate powerful deep learning frontends with safety-critical integrity checks.
- **Guidance for Deep Learning in Robotics:** The work provides a case study illustrating the gap between perceived performance of deep learning models (e.g., high retrieval recall) and their actual robustness in safety-critical applications, prompting a re-evaluation of evaluation protocols and the integration of geometric consistency constraints.
- **Research Trajectories:** The identified limitations and future trajectories, such as integrating variational probabilistic calibration and expanding to multi-route, meter-level evaluations in real-world deployments, suggest a clear path for further development towards industry-grade SLAM solutions.

In summary, this research advances the state-of-the-art in thermal VPR and SLAM by providing a practical, efficient, and robust solution for managing frontend overconfidence and ensuring navigational safety in autonomous systems.
