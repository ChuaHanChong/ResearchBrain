# The Role of Domain Randomization in Training Diffusion Policies for Whole-Body Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at TU Darmstadt and DFKI systematically investigated how dataset diversity, achieved through domain randomization (DR), and dataset size influence the performance of Diffusion Policies (DPs) for whole-body humanoid locomotion. The study found that DPs require millions of transitions and effective DR, especially perturbations and terrain variations, to achieve stable walking comparable to state-of-the-art RL policies.

## Problem

- Controlling high-degree-of-freedom, intrinsically unstable humanoid robots for complex whole-body motions like locomotion remains a significant challenge.
- The effectiveness and data requirements of Diffusion Policies (DPs) for whole-body humanoid control, particularly locomotion, were largely unexplored despite their success in manipulation tasks.
- There was an unclear understanding of whether DPs for locomotion would share the 'data efficiency' seen in manipulation or the 'data-hungry' nature of robust reinforcement learning (RL) policies.

## Method

- A two-stage framework was developed: first, robust offline datasets were generated using an ==Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP)== reinforcement learning policy under varied Domain Randomization (DR); second, Diffusion Policies (DPs) were trained on these datasets.
- Twenty-four distinct datasets were collected using an AMP policy on a Unitree H1 humanoid in IsaacGym, systematically varying DR types (dynamics, perturbations, terrain, initial state, humanoid scale) and sizes (500K, 2M, 8M transitions).
- Diffusion Policies, based on an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture, were trained and evaluated using receding horizon control across non-randomized and randomized target environments to assess generalization.

## Results

- Diffusion Policies (DPs) trained with 500K transitions consistently showed poor performance; success rates only became notable with 2M samples and improved further with 8M samples.
- The best-performing DPs, trained with sufficiently large and diversified datasets using domain randomization (DR), achieved stable walking behaviors comparable to the state-of-the-art source RL policy.
- Perturbation and terrain randomization proved most effective, leading to near-perfect success rates (**0.99**-**1.0**) in both non-randomized and randomized evaluation environments with 8M samples, outperforming dynamics, scale, and initial state randomization in terms of generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion Policies (DPs) for complex whole-body humanoid control exhibit 'data hunger,' requiring significantly larger datasets (millions of transitions) for stable locomotion, unlike their perceived data efficiency in manipulation tasks.
> - Domain Randomization (DR) is essential for training successful DPs in humanoid control, with datasets collected without DR almost universally failing regardless of size.
> - Perturbation and terrain randomization are particularly critical DR strategies, consistently yielding strong positive impacts on DP performance and robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated at the nexus of several rapidly evolving fields: **humanoid robotics, imitation learning, reinforcement learning (RL), and diffusion models for policy learning.**

Humanoid robots represent a significant frontier in robotics due to their potential to operate in human-designed environments. However, controlling these high-degree-of-freedom, intrinsically unstable systems, especially for complex whole-body motions like locomotion and manipulation, remains a grand challenge. Traditional control methods often struggle with robustness and adaptability to varied conditions.

**Imitation Learning (IL)** and **Reinforcement Learning (RL)** have emerged as powerful paradigms for learning complex behaviors for robots. IL allows robots to acquire skills from human demonstrations (e.g., motion capture data), while RL enables learning through trial and error in simulated environments. Techniques like Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP), extensively cited and used in this paper, leverage the strengths of both, allowing robots to learn natural and stylized motions while pursuing task-specific goals. The ability to transfer policies learned in simulation to the real world, known as **sim-to-real transfer**, is crucial for practical robot deployment. **Domain Randomization (DR)** is a widely adopted strategy to achieve this by exposing the RL agent to a variety of environmental parameters during training, thereby improving generalization.

Recently, **Diffusion Models**, initially popular in generative AI for image synthesis, have made significant inroads into robot policy learning, giving rise to "Diffusion Policies (DPs)." DPs have shown impressive results in robot *manipulation* tasks, often demonstrating strong performance even with relatively small datasets. This success has sparked interest in their applicability to more complex control problems.

This paper addresses a critical gap: while DPs have excelled in manipulation, their effectiveness and data requirements for *whole-body humanoid control*, especially locomotion, are largely underexplored. Existing DP work for robot control often relies on small, sparse real-world datasets, or focuses primarily on the diffusion framework itself without a deep dive into the underlying data characteristics. In contrast, robust RL policies for locomotion heavily leverage large, diverse datasets generated under extensive DR in simulation.

This research explicitly investigates whether the lessons learned from DR in RL for locomotion apply to DPs, and whether DPs for complex whole-body control share the "data efficiency" observed in manipulation tasks. By systematically ablating different DR techniques and dataset sizes, the authors aim to provide foundational insights into how to effectively train DPs for humanoids, thus contributing to the broader goal of developing robust, generalizable, and commercially viable humanoid robots. It builds upon previous works like DiffuseLoco, which demonstrated DPs' ability to integrate multiple skills, but extends the focus to the crucial role of the source dataset's diversity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **systematically investigate the role of dataset diversity (achieved through Domain Randomization) and dataset size in training Diffusion Policies (DPs) for whole-body humanoid control.** Specifically, the authors aim to understand:

1. **How different types of Domain Randomization (DR) applied during data collection affect the performance and generalization capabilities of DPs for humanoid locomotion.** This includes commonly used DR techniques (dynamics, perturbations, terrain, initial state) and a novel approach (humanoid scale randomization).
2. **The impact of varying dataset sizes on the successful training of DPs for complex, dynamic humanoid tasks.** This explores whether the data efficiency often observed in DP for manipulation tasks extends to whole-body control.
3. **Whether DPs can achieve stable and robust walking behaviors for humanoids, matching the performance of state-of-the-art RL policies, and what dataset characteristics are necessary for this.**

The motivation for these objectives stems from several key observations and challenges:

- **Underexplored DPs in Humanoid Locomotion:** While DPs have shown impressive results in robotic manipulation, their application to the more complex and dynamic domain of humanoid whole-body control (especially locomotion) is largely unexplored.
- **Data Requirements Discrepancy:** The success of DPs in manipulation often comes with a narrative of data efficiency, sometimes requiring only a few expert trajectories. However, robust RL policies for locomotion typically demand significantly larger and more diverse datasets, often generated with extensive DR. The authors want to clarify whether DPs for locomotion align with the "few-shot" success in manipulation or the "data-hungry" nature of RL for locomotion.
- **Importance of Domain Randomization:** DR is well-established as a critical technique for robust RL and sim-to-real transfer. However, its specific impact on the data used to train DPs, particularly for humanoids, remains an open question. Understanding which DR strategies are most effective can guide future data collection efforts.
- **Growing Demand for Humanoid Policies:** As humanoid robots become more prevalent, there is an increasing demand for general and robust whole-body motion policies. This research contributes to developing methodologies for training such policies efficiently and effectively.

In essence, the paper seeks to provide empirical evidence and insights into the specific data characteristics—both in terms of quantity and quality (diversity)—that are essential for unlocking the full potential of Diffusion Policies in the challenging domain of whole-body humanoid control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a two-stage framework: first, generating robust offline datasets using a powerful Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy under extensive Domain Randomization (DR); second, training Diffusion Policies (DPs) on these varied datasets and evaluating their performance.

#### 3.1. Offline Dataset Generation via Reinforcement Learning

- **RL Algorithm:** The foundation of data generation is the **Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP)** algorithm, which combines goal-conditioned RL with Imitation Learning (IL). AMP learns a discriminator that provides a "style reward," encouraging the robot to mimic reference motions, complemented by handcrafted task-specific rewards (e.g., velocity tracking).
- **Environment and Robot:** The experiments are conducted in a simulated **IsaacGym** environment using a **Unitree H1 humanoid robot model**. IsaacGym's parallel simulation capabilities allow for efficient training and data collection.
- **Motion Prior (Style Data):** Ten motion capture sequences from the AMASS dataset are used. These sequences cover various walking patterns (forward, backward, sideways, rotations, figure-8 walking), providing rich "style" information for the AMP discriminator. Inverse kinematics is used to convert human motion keypoints into humanoid joint configurations.
- **Action Space:** Actions are 19-dimensional vectors representing desired positional changes for each actuated joint, processed by a Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller.
- **Observation Space:** The policy receives a reduced observation vector, while the critic receives privileged information (e.g., base linear/angular velocities, gravity vector, joint positions/velocities, target command, previous actions).
- **Reward Function:** A task reward function `r_g` encourages tracking desired linear and angular velocities, combined with regularization rewards (Table 1) for stability and smoothness (e.g., penalizing joint limits, high velocities/accelerations, non-flat base orientation) and the style reward from the AMP discriminator.
- **Goal-Conditioning:** The policy is trained to track goal velocity commands (linear x, y, and yaw rate), sampled uniformly from predefined ranges.
- **Domain Randomization for Data Collection:** The core of the methodology lies in generating datasets with different DR strategies. While the initial AMP policy is trained with *extensive* DR to ensure robustness, the *data collection* process for DPs systematically varies the DR applied. This allows for an ablation study of DR effects. The DR categories are:
 - **Dynamics Randomization:** Varying simulator parameters like body friction, added base mass, link mass multipliers, PD gains multipliers, center of mass (COM) displacement, joint friction, and damping coefficients (Table 2).
 - **Perturbation Randomization:** Applying random force perturbations to the humanoid's torso at regular intervals to challenge stability.
 - **Terrain Randomization:** Using varied terrains supported by IsaacGym (obstacles, bumpy surfaces) instead of just flat ground.
 - **Initial State Randomization:** Sampling initial states from the expert dataset and optionally adding random joint position/velocity noise.
 - **Humanoid Scale Randomization:** Collecting data from humanoids of different scales (0.8, 1.0, 1.2), where mass, inertia, PD gains, and torque limits are scaled accordingly. The AMP policy used for data collection was found to be robust across these scales.
- **Dataset Generation:** The robust AMP policy is used to collect 24 distinct datasets: 8 environment setups (No rand, Dynamics, Perturbations, Terrain, 3 Scales, Init. State rand, Terrain + Perturb., All) * 3 dataset sizes (500K, 2M, 8M transitions).

#### 3.2. Diffusion Policy Training and Evaluation

- **Diffusion Policy Architecture:** The DPs adopt an encoder-decoder architecture, similar to DiffuseLoco. It consists of:
 - Two 2-layer MLP encoders for previous state-action transitions and goal information.
 - Six Transformer decoder layers with 8-head cross-attention.
 - The MLP embeddings, along with a diffusion timestep embedding, serve as conditional inputs.
 - Noisy ground-truth actions are passed through the Transformer decoder.
 - The model minimizes the mean squared error loss between the predicted noise and the sampled noise.
- **Control Strategy:** Receding horizon control is used, where the DP predicts `n` future steps, but only the first predicted action is executed.
- **Evaluation Environments:** Two distinct evaluation setups are used to assess generalization:
 - **Non-randomized Target Environment:** A flat surface without any DR.
 - **Randomized Target Environment:** A complex, uneven terrain with milder dynamics randomization (Table 4) to provide a fair challenge for all configurations.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** For each 10-second evaluation episode (500 simulation steps) with a constant forward velocity command (1 m/s), three metrics are recorded over 3 random seeds:
 - **Success Rate:** Percentage of episodes that do not terminate prematurely.
 - **Tracking Performance:** Euclidean distance between current and commanded velocities (lower is better).
 - **Smoothness:** Sum of squared L2 norms between consecutive actions (lower is better).
- **Baseline Comparison:** The performance of the best DPs is compared against the source AMP RL policy in both evaluation environments.

This comprehensive methodology allows for a detailed investigation into the interplay of data quantity and quality for training DPs in challenging humanoid locomotion tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The results section provides a detailed quantitative analysis of the Diffusion Policies' performance across various dataset sizes and Domain Randomization (DR) configurations in two distinct evaluation environments.

#### 4.1. General Observations Across Evaluation Environments

- **Data Hunger for Humanoid Control:** Unlike some manipulation tasks where DPs show data efficiency, training DPs for stable humanoid locomotion requires significantly larger datasets. A dataset size of 500K transitions consistently yielded poor results, with almost no configurations achieving stable walking in either evaluation setting. Success rates only started to become notable with 2M samples and improved further with 8M samples.
- **Crucial Role of Domain Randomization:** DR is essential for successful DP training, even when evaluating in a non-randomized target environment. Datasets collected "No rand." (without any DR) almost universally failed to produce successful policies, regardless of dataset size. This highlights that simply having a large amount of data without diversity is insufficient for DPs to learn robust humanoid control.
- **Matching Source RL Policy Performance:** The best-performing Diffusion Policies were able to match the success rate, tracking performance, and smoothness of the original source AMP RL policy in both non-randomized and randomized target environments, especially with large, diverse datasets.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Non-Randomized Target Environment (Figure 2)

- **500K Samples:** No configuration achieved stable walking (0% success rate), with high tracking errors and variable smoothness.
- **2M Samples:**
 - Configurations with `Dynamics rand.`, `Perturbations`, and `Terrain` showed strong improvement, achieving success rates near 1.0.
 - `3 Scales` and `Init. State rand.` performed poorly, with very low success rates (0.01% and 0.16% respectively).
 - `Terrain + Perturb.` and `All` also achieved a 1.0 success rate, demonstrating the benefits of combining effective DR.
 - Notably, `Perturbations` and `Terrain + Perturbations` yielded the best tracking performance and smoothness.
- **8M Samples:**
 - Most DR configurations (except `No rand.`) achieved very high success rates (near 1.0). Even `3 Scales` and `Init. State rand.` significantly improved to a 1.0 success rate.
 - `Dynamics rand.` was an exception, only reaching 95% success.
 - Tracking performance and smoothness generally improved across the board, with `Perturbations`, `Terrain`, `3 Scales`, `Init. State rand.`, `Terrain + Perturb.`, and `All` showing excellent results, closely matching the source RL policy (Table 3).

#### 4.3. Evaluation on Randomized Target Environment (Figure 3)

- **500K Samples:** Similar to the non-randomized environment, no configuration achieved stable walking (0% success rate), reinforcing the need for larger datasets.
- **2M Samples:**
 - `Perturbations` and `Terrain + Perturb.` emerged as strong performers, achieving high success rates (0.94 and 0.96 respectively) and good tracking/smoothness.
 - `All` also showed strong performance (0.99 success rate).
 - `Dynamics rand.` and `Terrain` showed some success (0.43 and 0.57 respectively) but were less robust.
 - `No rand.`, `3 Scales`, and `Init. State rand.` continued to perform poorly.
- **8M Samples:**
 - `Perturbations`, `Terrain`, `Terrain + Perturb.`, and `All` achieved almost perfect success rates (0.99 to 1.0) with excellent tracking and smoothness, closely matching the source RL policy (Table 5).
 - `3 Scales` and `Init. State rand.` showed significant improvement compared to 2M samples (0.82 and 0.79 success rates, respectively).
 - `Dynamics rand.` surprisingly performed worse in this randomized environment compared to the non-randomized one, achieving only a 50% success rate despite the larger dataset, suggesting that this specific randomization might not generalize well to *milder* or different types of randomization in the target.

#### 4.4. Specific Impact of Randomization Types

- **Perturbation and Terrain Randomization:** These consistently showed the strongest positive impact on DP performance, leading to high success rates and robust control even with 2M samples, especially in randomized target environments. Combining them (`Terrain + Perturb.`) was also highly effective.
- **Dynamics Randomization:** While effective in the non-randomized target, its performance degraded significantly in the randomized target environment with 8M samples, implying that the specific range of dynamics randomization during training might not align well with the evaluation randomization, or that it's less impactful for generalization than perturbations or terrain.
- **Humanoid Scale and Initial State Randomization:** These randomizations were less critical for robust locomotion and only showed substantial improvement with the largest dataset size (8M), and even then, their performance in the randomized target environment was not as strong as perturbation or terrain randomization. The authors note that their base RL policy was already robust to scale variations.

In summary, the key findings underscore that for complex whole-body humanoid control using Diffusion Policies, both **sufficiently large datasets (millions of transitions)** and **judiciously applied Domain Randomization (especially perturbations and varied terrains)** are indispensable. The data-efficient narrative of DPs from manipulation tasks does not directly translate to the more dynamic and challenging domain of humanoid locomotion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the fields of robotics, machine learning, and humanoid control:

1. **Challenging the "Data-Efficiency" Narrative for DPs in Complex Tasks:** The paper provides crucial empirical evidence that, unlike their performance in simpler manipulation tasks, Diffusion Policies for complex, dynamic whole-body humanoid control require significantly larger and more diverse datasets. This finding is vital for researchers and practitioners, setting realistic expectations and guiding data collection strategies for applying DPs to challenging locomotion and full-body tasks. It suggests that the complexity of the task profoundly influences the data requirements of DPs.

2. **Highlighting the Indispensable Role of Domain Randomization for DPs:** The study conclusively demonstrates that Domain Randomization (DR) is not merely beneficial but *essential* for training successful DPs in humanoid control, even for evaluation in non-randomized environments. This underscores that DPs, much like traditional RL policies, benefit immensely from diversified training data to achieve robustness and generalization. This finding bridges the knowledge from RL for sim-to-real transfer to the emerging field of DPs.

3. **Providing Guidance on Effective DR Strategies:** The ablation study of various DR techniques offers practical insights into which types of randomization are most impactful. The findings suggest that **perturbation and terrain randomization are particularly critical** for learning robust humanoid locomotion policies with DPs. This direct guidance can inform future experimental designs, allowing researchers to prioritize specific DR techniques during dataset generation, potentially leading to more efficient and effective training processes.

4. **Advancing Robust Humanoid Control:** By demonstrating that DPs, when trained with adequately large and diverse datasets (generated via DR), can achieve stable, commanded, and smooth walking behaviors comparable to state-of-the-art RL policies, the paper contributes to the broader goal of making humanoid robots more capable and deployable. Robust locomotion is a foundational skill for humanoids, and this work provides a clearer path for DPs to achieve it.

5. **Benchmarking and Future Research Directions:** The systematic approach to dataset generation, DP training, and evaluation provides a valuable benchmark for future research. It opens avenues for exploring how to further reduce data requirements while maintaining robustness, perhaps through more advanced DR techniques, novel DP architectures tailored for whole-body control, or sophisticated data augmentation strategies. It also prompts deeper investigation into why certain DR types (e.g., dynamics randomization in some scenarios) perform less effectively than others.

In conclusion, this paper clarifies the often-ambiguous data requirements of Diffusion Policies when applied to complex, dynamic robotic systems like humanoids. It establishes the critical importance of Domain Randomization and large datasets for success, thereby providing foundational knowledge that will significantly influence the design and training of robust, generalizable policies for the next generation of humanoid robots.
