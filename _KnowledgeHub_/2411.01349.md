# The Role of Domain Randomization in Training Diffusion Policies for Whole-Body Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at TU Darmstadt and DFKI systematically investigated how dataset diversity, achieved through domain randomization (DR), and dataset size influence the performance of Diffusion Policies (DPs) for whole-body humanoid locomotion. The study found that DPs require millions of transitions and effective DR, especially perturbations and terrain variations, to achieve stable walking comparable to state-of-the-art RL policies.

## Problem

- Controlling high-degree-of-freedom, intrinsically unstable humanoid robots for complex whole-body motions like locomotion remains a significant challenge.
- The effectiveness and data requirements of Diffusion Policies (DPs) for whole-body humanoid control, particularly locomotion, were largely unexplored despite their success in manipulation tasks.
- There was an unclear understanding of whether DPs for locomotion would share the 'data efficiency' seen in manipulation or the 'data-hungry' nature of robust reinforcement learning (RL) policies.

## Method

- A two-stage framework was developed: first, robust offline datasets were generated using an ==Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP)== reinforcement learning policy under varied Domain Randomization (DR); second, Diffusion Policies (DPs) were trained on these datasets.
- Twenty-four distinct datasets were collected using an AMP policy on a Unitree H1 humanoid in IsaacGym, systematically varying DR types (dynamics, perturbations, terrain, initial state, humanoid scale) and sizes (500K, 2M, 8M transitions).
- Diffusion Policies, based on an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture, were trained and evaluated using receding horizon control across non-randomized and randomized target environments to assess generalization.

## Results

- Diffusion Policies (DPs) trained with 500K transitions consistently showed poor performance; success rates only became notable with 2M samples and improved further with 8M samples.
- The best-performing DPs, trained with sufficiently large and diversified datasets using domain randomization (DR), achieved stable walking behaviors comparable to the state-of-the-art source RL policy.
- Perturbation and terrain randomization proved most effective, leading to near-perfect success rates (**0.99**-**1.0**) in both non-randomized and randomized evaluation environments with 8M samples, outperforming dynamics, scale, and initial state randomization in terms of generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion Policies (DPs) for complex whole-body humanoid control exhibit 'data hunger,' requiring significantly larger datasets (millions of transitions) for stable locomotion, unlike their perceived data efficiency in manipulation tasks.
> - Domain Randomization (DR) is essential for training successful DPs in humanoid control, with datasets collected without DR almost universally failing regardless of size.
> - Perturbation and terrain randomization are particularly critical DR strategies, consistently yielding strong positive impacts on DP performance and robustness.
