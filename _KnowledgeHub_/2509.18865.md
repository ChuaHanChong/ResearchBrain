# Bi-VLA: Bilateral Control-Based Imitation Learning via Vision-Language Fusion for Action Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Bi-VLA framework extends bilateral control-based imitation learning by integrating vision and natural language, allowing a single robot policy to execute multiple manipulation tasks. It achieved up to 90% success in language-disambiguated tasks and 75% in novel environments with distractors, demonstrating versatility and robustness through its CVAE-Transformer architecture with SigLIP language encoding.

## Problem

- Traditional bilateral control-based imitation learning is often limited to single-task settings, requiring separate models for each skill and hindering versatility.
- Unilateral control systems lack force feedback, compromising robot robustness in contact-rich tasks that require precise force modulation.
- Existing Vision-Language Models in robotics frequently neglect robust force feedback, limiting their effectiveness in physically interactive scenarios.

## Method

- **Bi-VLA** integrates a bilateral control system with vision-language fusion using a ==Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE)==-based Transformer architecture.
- A four-channel bilateral control setup with disturbance and reaction force observers is used for data collection, providing rich force feedback without dedicated sensors.
- Natural language instructions are encoded by SigLIP and visual features by EfficientNet, fused via FiLM modulation, then processed by the CVAE-Transformer to generate leader robot trajectories.

## Results

- Bi-VLA (SigLIP) achieved **90%** overall success in the language-disambiguable Two-Target task, outperforming a vision-only baseline (**50%**) and a DistilBERT variant (**60%**).
- The framework successfully generalized across both language-disambiguable and vision-disambiguable tasks with reduced data (Bi-VLA SigLIP-Mix), showing **70%** in Two-Target and **90%** in Two-Source tasks.
- Bi-VLA (SigLIP) and Bi-VLA (SigLIP-Mix) maintained **75%** overall success in an unlearned 3-ball environment with distractors, significantly outperforming the biased vision-only baseline (**50%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Natural language input is crucial for disambiguating tasks where visual information alone is insufficient, enabling a single policy to perform context-dependent actions.
> - The choice of language encoder impacts performance, with SigLIP demonstrating superior language grounding accuracy and execution reliability compared to DistilBERT.
> - Integrating vision-language fusion into a force-aware bilateral control framework allows for multitask generalization and robustness in novel environments with distractors without degrading performance on vision-resolvable tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly evolving, driven by the increasing demand for robots capable of performing complex tasks in dynamic, human-centric environments such as homes, hospitals, and service industries. Unlike traditional industrial robots, these service robots require high adaptability, flexibility, and the ability to interact with diverse objects and respond to human commands. Imitation learning (IL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for transferring human manipulation skills to robots, alleviating the challenges of manual programming.

Historically, data collection for IL often relies on teleoperation. **Unilateral control** teleoperation systems, such as ALOHA and Mobile ALOHA, excel at collecting large datasets for kinematics-driven tasks. However, a significant limitation of unilateral control is the absence of force feedback to the human operator. This lack of haptic information restricts the robot's ability to perform robustly in contact-rich tasks, where understanding and modulating forces are crucial for successful and compliant interaction with the environment and objects.

To address this, **bilateral control-based imitation learning** has gained prominence. Bilateral control allows the human operator to not only command the robot's position but also to *feel* the forces and torques experienced by the robot in real-time. This haptic feedback enriches the demonstrations, enabling the robot to learn more nuanced and robust force-dependent skills, improving generalization across objects with varying properties like hardness and weight. Early works in this area utilized recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) to reproduce skills requiring precise force control. More recently, the field has seen the integration of modern, powerful architectures like **Transformers**, as exemplified by Bi-ACT, which combines bilateral control with visual observations and action chunking.

In parallel, the broader robotics and AI community has witnessed the rise of **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, such as CLIP, DistilBERT, and EfficientNet. These models are revolutionizing how robots interpret and act upon human instructions by fusing visual perception with natural language understanding. Systems like CLIPort and PerAct use pre-trained VLM features for tasks like instruction-following pick-and-place, while others combine language encoders and visual backbones with fusion mechanisms like FiLM to enable flexible instruction grounding. While these VLM-driven approaches offer unparalleled versatility in interpreting commands, many still predominantly rely on unilateral teleoperation or primarily kinematic control, thereby inheriting the limitation of lacking robust force feedback for contact-rich interactions.

This paper, "Bi-VLA," strategically positions itself at the intersection of these two critical research streams: **force-sensitive bilateral control** and **versatile vision-language models**. Prior bilateral control methods, including the authors' own Bi-ACT, were largely restricted to single-task settings, requiring separate models for each skill. While Bi-LAT introduced language into bilateral control, its primary focus was on modulating applied force, not enabling a single model to adapt to multiple task contexts. Bi-VLA directly addresses this fundamental limitation. By integrating natural language instructions and visual features into a bilateral control framework, it aims to create a single, unified policy that can adapt to multiple tasks based on multimodal input, thereby significantly enhancing the generality and practical applicability of bilateral imitation learning. It leverages and refines existing VLM components, such as replacing DistilBERT with SigLIP as a language encoder, to push the boundaries of multimodal integration in force-aware robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a novel framework that extends bilateral control-based imitation learning beyond its conventional single-task limitation**, enabling a single robot model to perform multiple diverse manipulation tasks.

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Demand for Adaptable Service Robots:** As highlighted in the introduction, there is a growing need for robots in human-centered applications (e.g., cooking, eldercare) that can adapt to dynamic, unstructured environments and interact with objects of varying properties. Traditional methods, including existing bilateral control approaches, often require task-specific models, which limits their versatility and scalability in such complex settings.
2. **Limitations of Unilateral Control:** While imitation learning is a promising approach, widely used unilateral control methods (e.g., ALOHA) do not provide force feedback. This omission compromises robustness, especially in tasks requiring precise contact, force modulation, and compliant interaction.
3. **Untapped Potential of Bilateral Control:** Bilateral control effectively addresses the force feedback gap, offering richer demonstrations and better generalization for contact-rich tasks. However, its widespread adoption has been hindered by the requirement for separate, task-specific policies, limiting its practical utility in multi-task scenarios.
4. **Bridging the Gap with Multimodal AI:** Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated significant success in grounding language instructions in visual scenes for robotic manipulation. However, many VLM-based robotics systems often neglect force feedback, making them less robust for certain physical interactions. The motivation is to combine the strengths of VLM-driven versatility with the robustness of force-aware bilateral control.
5. **Enhancing Generalization and Flexibility:** The authors specifically aim to create a system where a single policy can flexibly switch between different tasks based on natural language instructions and visual cues, without requiring retraining or manual model selection for each new task. This would represent a significant step towards more autonomous and versatile robots.
6. **Improving Language Grounding Fidelity:** The motivation also includes investigating the impact of different language encoders (e.g., SigLIP vs. DistilBERT) on the accuracy and reliability of language grounding within this multimodal, force-aware framework, aiming to identify optimal components for improved performance.

In essence, Bi-VLA seeks to create a more intelligent and adaptable robotic system by synergistically combining the physical awareness of bilateral control with the cognitive flexibility afforded by vision-language understanding, moving away from rigid, single-skill robot policies towards more general-purpose manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Bi-VLA (Bilateral Control-Based Imitation Learning via Vision-Language Fusion for Action Generation) is a novel framework designed to enable a single robot policy to perform multiple tasks by integrating robot state data, visual observations, and natural language instructions.

**A. Data Collection**
The core of Bi-VLA relies on a four-channel bilateral control system, which facilitates leader-follower teleoperation. In this setup:
- The human operator manipulates a **leader robot**, which provides commands to a **follower robot**.
- **Position and torque information** are continuously exchanged between the leader and follower robots, allowing the operator to feel the contact forces experienced by the follower robot. The control law ensures synchronized joint angles (θ_l - θ_f = 0) and balanced torques (τ_l + τ_f = 0).
- Crucially, external torques are estimated using a **disturbance observer (DOB)** and a **reaction force observer (RFOB)**, eliminating the need for dedicated force/torque sensors and potentially reducing hardware cost and complexity.
- **Multimodal data** is recorded during demonstrations:
 - **Robot joint data:** Angle, velocity, and torque from the follower robot.
 - **Visual inputs:** RGB images from two cameras (overhead and gripper-mounted, 640x360 resolution, 100 Hz).
 - **Natural language instructions:** Operator-provided commands describing the intended action, temporally aligned with robot states and visual frames.
- For experiments, 6 demonstrations (3 for "Up" and 3 for "Down") were collected per task type. For the multitask Bi-VLA (SigLIP-Mix) variant, only 4 raw demonstrations (one per condition) were used to test low-data generalization.
- The **DABI data augmentation technique** was applied. It synchronizes high-frequency robot signals (1000 Hz) with lower-frequency image data (100 Hz) by downsampling the robot signals, effectively expanding the dataset tenfold (e.g., 6 demonstrations to 60).

**B. Learning Model**
Bi-VLA's learning model is built upon a **Transformer-driven Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE)**, an architecture well-suited for sequence generation and learning latent representations.
- **Input Modalities:** The model simultaneously takes in follower joint states (angle, velocity, torque), RGB images from both cameras, and natural language instructions.
- **Language Encoding:** Natural language commands (e.g., "put ball upward") are converted into fixed-length **embeddings using SigLIP**. SigLIP (Sigmoid Loss for Language-Image Pre-Training) is a contrastive language-image pre-training model known for its robust performance. In contrast, a baseline model used DistilBERT for comparison.
- **Visual Feature Extraction:** Visual features from the RGB images are extracted using **EfficientNet**, a convolutional neural network architecture optimized for efficiency and accuracy.
- **Vision-Language Fusion:** The SigLIP-generated text embeddings are fused with the EfficientNet-extracted visual features through **FiLM-based modulation** (Feature-wise Linear Modulation). This method allows the language input to conditionally modulate the visual features, aligning them to form a shared, semantically rich representation.
- **Unified Latent Space:** The fused vision-language features are then combined with the robot joint angle, velocity, and torque data. This comprehensive input forms a unified latent representation that captures all relevant multimodal information.
- **Action Generation:** This unified latent representation is processed by the CVAE-based Transformer encoder-decoder. The model learns to generate action chunks, specifically predicting the **leader robot's joint trajectories** (angle, velocity, and torque sequences). Training is performed by minimizing the reconstruction error between the generated actions and the ground-truth leader trajectories from the demonstrations.

**C. Inference**
During real-time inference:
- The Bi-VLA model receives the current follower joint states, synchronized camera images, and a natural language instruction from the user.
- It processes these multimodal inputs through its SigLIP, EfficientNet, and FiLM-fusion components to generate a unified latent representation.
- The CVAE-Transformer then predicts the next action chunk, which comprises the leader robot's desired joint angles, velocities, and torques.
- These predicted outputs are fed into the bilateral control system, which converts them into real-time commands applied to the follower robot. This closed-loop system allows the robot to execute tasks dynamically, conditioned on live sensory input and linguistic commands.

**D. Experimental Setup**
- **Hardware:** ROBOTIS OpenManipulator-X arms were used as leader and follower robots, chosen for their cost-effectiveness. ELP USB cameras provided visual input.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Two-Target Task (Language-Disambiguable):** The robot picks a ball from a fixed source and places it at one of two visually identical targets ("Up" or "Down"), with the choice specified by language (e.g., "put ball upward"). This tests the necessity of language grounding.
 - **Two-Source Task (Vision-Disambiguable):** The robot picks a ball from one of two distinct source locations ("Up" or "Down") and places it at a fixed target. Vision alone is sufficient for disambiguation here. This task also included a generalization test with an "unlearned 3-ball environment" (distractor).
- **Evaluated Models:** Four variants were compared:
 1. **Bi-ACT:** A vision-action baseline without language input.
 2. **Bi-VLA (DistilBERT):** Trained only on the Two-Target task, using DistilBERT for language encoding.
 3. **Bi-VLA (SigLIP):** Trained on task-specific data, using SigLIP for language encoding.
 4. **Bi-VLA (SigLIP-Mix):** Multitask training on both Two-Target and Two-Source tasks, using SigLIP and a reduced data budget (4 raw demonstrations).
- **Metrics:** Success rates for Pick, Move, and Place phases, and an overall success rate, across 10 independent trials per condition.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments validated Bi-VLA's effectiveness in real-world robotic tasks, particularly highlighting the crucial role of vision-language fusion and the superior performance of SigLIP as a language encoder.

**A. Two-Target Task (Language-Disambiguable)**
- **Bi-ACT (Vision-only baseline):** Achieved only 50% overall success. It consistently succeeded in the "Up" case (100%) but completely failed in the "Down" case (0%), demonstrating a biased "Up-only" policy when language cues were absent and vision alone could not distinguish the target. This unequivocally showed that language input is indispensable for this task.
- **Bi-VLA (DistilBERT):** Improved to 60% overall success. While it achieved 100% success for "Up" and correctly identified the "Down" target during pick/move, it struggled with reliable placement in the "Down" case (20% success), indicating limitations in the language grounding or action generation with this encoder.
- **Bi-VLA (SigLIP):** Demonstrated robust performance with 90% overall success. It achieved 80% in the "Up" case and a perfect 100% in the "Down" case across all phases, showcasing its ability to accurately ground linguistic instructions and execute precise, force-aware actions for both commands. This highlighted SigLIP's effectiveness.
- **Bi-VLA (SigLIP-Mix):** Achieved 70% overall success, with a balanced 70% for both "Up" and "Down" conditions. Despite being trained on significantly less data and across multiple tasks, it successfully incorporated linguistic cues to disambiguate the task, outperforming Bi-ACT and demonstrating multitask generalization without complete task-specific training.

**B. Two-Source Task (Vision-Disambiguable)**
- **Bi-ACT:** Achieved 95% overall success (90% "Up", 100% "Down"). As expected, when vision alone provided sufficient cues, the vision-only baseline performed very well.
- **Bi-VLA (SigLIP):** Maintained a comparable 90% overall success (100% "Up", 80% "Down"). This result is crucial as it demonstrates that integrating language grounding does not negatively interfere with performance when the task can be resolved solely by vision.
- **Bi-VLA (SigLIP-Mix):** Also achieved 90% overall success (100% "Up", 80% "Down"). This indicates that multitask training does not hinder vision-based disambiguation, further supporting the framework's versatility.

**C. Two-Source Task (Unlearned 3-Ball Environment - Generalization Test)**
This more challenging test introduced a distractor in an unlearned environment.
- **Bi-ACT:** Again collapsed to a biased policy, achieving only 50% overall success (100% "Up", 0% "Down"). The additional distractor exacerbated its inability to adapt without explicit language.
- **Bi-VLA (SigLIP):** Maintained robust performance with 75% overall success (100% "Up", 50% "Down").
- **Bi-VLA (SigLIP-Mix):** Also achieved 75% overall success (90% "Up", 60% "Down"), demonstrating a more balanced performance between "Up" and "Down" compared to the task-specific SigLIP model.

These results indicate that incorporating language grounding significantly enhances a policy's ability to generalize to novel environments and handle distractors, especially when visual information alone might be ambiguous or insufficient.

**Summary of Findings:**
1. **Language is essential:** For tasks where vision is ambiguous, natural language input is critical for disambiguation and successful task completion. Vision-only policies fail without it.
2. **SigLIP outperforms DistilBERT:** The choice of language encoder matters significantly, with SigLIP demonstrating superior grounding accuracy and execution reliability compared to DistilBERT.
3. **Multitask Generalization:** Bi-VLA (SigLIP-Mix) successfully performs across both language-disambiguable and vision-disambiguable tasks with reduced data, without negative interference. This validates its core contribution of enabling a single model to handle multiple heterogeneous tasks.
4. **No Performance Hindrance:** When vision alone is sufficient, language integration does not degrade performance, confirming that the multimodal fusion is robust and adaptable.
5. **Improved Generalization to Novel Environments:** Language grounding enhances robustness and generalization in unseen environments with distractors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Bi-VLA framework represents a significant step forward in robotic manipulation, offering solutions to several long-standing challenges in the field. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Overcoming the Single-Task Limitation in Bilateral Control:** This is the most direct and crucial contribution. By enabling a single policy to handle multiple tasks, Bi-VLA transforms bilateral control-based imitation learning from a highly specialized, task-specific approach into a more general and practical framework. This reduces the substantial effort and resources traditionally required for training separate models for every new skill.
2. **Enhanced Robot Versatility and Adaptability:** Bi-VLA allows robots to operate more flexibly in dynamic, unstructured environments. The ability to interpret natural language commands and adapt to varying visual cues within a single model makes robots more versatile for human-centered applications like assistive robotics, service industry tasks, and even complex manufacturing where tasks can change frequently.
3. **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** The integration of natural language instructions makes robots more accessible and intuitive for non-expert users. Humans can simply tell the robot what to do using everyday language, rather than relying on complex programming interfaces or precise kinematic control. This paves the way for more seamless and natural human-robot collaboration.
4. **Robustness in Contact-Rich Tasks with Multimodal Intelligence:** Bi-VLA uniquely combines the force-feedback robustness of bilateral control with the semantic understanding of vision-language models. This synergy means robots can perform delicate, contact-rich manipulations (e.g., handling fragile objects, precise assembly) while simultaneously understanding high-level natural language goals, addressing a critical gap in many existing VLM-based manipulation systems.
5. **Data Efficiency and Low-Resource Generalization:** The successful performance of Bi-VLA (SigLIP-Mix) with a significantly reduced number of demonstrations highlights its potential for data efficiency and low-data generalization. This is vital for real-world robotics, where collecting vast, diverse datasets can be time-consuming and expensive.
6. **Advancement in Multimodal Fusion Techniques:** The empirical validation of SigLIP and FiLM-based fusion within a force-aware robotic context provides valuable insights into effective multimodal integration strategies, informing future research on how best to combine diverse sensory inputs for robust robotic control.
7. **Foundation for Future Complex Manipulations:** Bi-VLA lays the groundwork for more sophisticated multi-step manipulation tasks, complex object interactions, and adaptive responses to unforeseen environmental changes. The authors acknowledge these as future research directions, indicating the potential for this framework to scale to even more intricate robotic behaviors.

In conclusion, Bi-VLA's successful integration of vision-language understanding into a bilateral control framework marks a significant advance towards building more intelligent, versatile, and human-friendly robotic systems. By empowering a single robot policy to understand and execute diverse tasks through natural language, while maintaining physical robustness in contact, Bi-VLA holds substantial promise for broadening the real-world applicability of robots across a wide array of service and industrial domains.
