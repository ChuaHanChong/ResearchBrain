# Epona: Autoregressive Diffusion World Model for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Epona is an autoregressive diffusion world model for autonomous driving that decouples temporal dynamics, handled by a causal transformer over compressed latents, from spatial rendering and trajectory planning, handled by twin diffusion transformers sharing a flow-matching objective, trained with a chain-of-forward strategy to curb autoregressive drift. It generates consistent minute-long high-resolution driving video with state-of-the-art FVD and outperforms strong end-to-end planners on NAVSIM.

## Problem

- Diffusion world models such as Vista model joint distributions over fixed-length video clips, blocking variable-length long-horizon prediction and multimodal trajectory planning.
- GPT-style autoregressive world models like GAIA-1 rely on frame tokenization and quantization that degrades visual quality, and their causal structure restricts prediction to only the next action, not long-horizon trajectories.
- Diffusion and autoregressive paradigms have complementary shortcomings: diffusion lacks temporal decomposition, autoregressive sacrifices visual precision, leaving no framework reconciling both for joint scene-and-trajectory world modeling.

## Method

- Decoupled spatiotemporal factorization: a ==causal GPT-style transformer== models temporal dynamics in compressed latent space while twin ==diffusion transformers== handle spatial frame rendering and trajectory generation separately.
- Asynchronous multi-modal generation: two diffusion transformers asynchronously denoise a 3-second trajectory and the next frame from a shared temporal latent under a common ==flow-matching objective==, enabling real-time planning-only inference.
- **Chain-of-forward training**: periodically runs extra forward passes on self-predicted frames during training to close the teacher-forcing versus inference gap, paired with a ==temporal-aware DCAE decoder== for flicker-free video.

## Results

- On NuScenes video generation, Epona reaches FVD **82.8**, **7.4%** better than Vista's **89.4**, while extending max generation duration to **120s/600 frames** versus **40s** for the next-best baseline.
- On the NAVSIM planning benchmark Epona attains PDMS **86.2**, beating camera-lidar baselines like DRAMA at **85.5** and VADv2 at **80.9** using only a single camera and no auxiliary supervision.
- Ablations confirm design choices: removing joint video-trajectory training drops PDMS from **86.2** to **78.1**, and the temporal-aware DCAE decoder lowers FVD10 from **52.95** to **50.77** versus the vanilla decoder.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling causal temporal reasoning from continuous spatial generation lets one model inherit autoregressive flexibility without the quality loss that discrete tokenization usually causes.
> - Sharing one latent across video and trajectory heads is what buys planning quality: removing joint training costs 8 PDMS points, more than any single architectural tweak.
> - Naive teacher-forced training silently breaks at inference; injecting self-predicted frames mid-training is a cheap general fix for autoregressive drift beyond driving world models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of generative AI, world modeling, and autonomous driving. The broader research landscape for autonomous driving has seen a paradigm shift from traditional modular pipelines (perception -> prediction -> planning) towards more integrated, end-to-end systems that mimic human-like driving understanding. Generative world models, in particular, are emerging as a powerful approach to enable autonomous agents to learn inherent world knowledge, predict future dynamics, and facilitate decision-making in a self-supervised manner.

Currently, generative driving world models largely fall into two categories:
1. **Diffusion-based approaches (e.g., Vista, SVD):** These excel at generating visually impressive, high-fidelity images and short videos by modeling the global joint distribution of fixed-length frame sequences. However, their limitations include:
 - Inability to generate variable-length, long-horizon predictions.
 - Lack of explicit causal temporal structures, leading to difficulties in modeling progressive dynamics.
 - Poor integration with flexible trajectory planning due to their global, fixed-length nature.
2. **GPT-style autoregressive methods (e.g., GAIA-1, DrivingGPT, Driving-World):** These models handle temporal flexibility through sequential next-token prediction, allowing for variable-length generation. Their shortcomings are:
 - Significant degradation in visual quality and planning precision due to the discretization and tokenization of continuous visual and action spaces.
 - Causal transformers often predict only the *next* action, limiting their ability for long-horizon trajectory *planning*.

"Epona" aims to reconcile these complementary shortcomings. It introduces an **autoregressive diffusion world model** that seeks to combine the best of both worlds: the high visual fidelity and continuous representation of diffusion models with the temporal flexibility and long-horizon capabilities of autoregressive approaches.

Specifically, Epona differentiates itself by:
- **Addressing fixed-length limitations:** Unlike traditional video diffusion that struggles with long, variable-length outputs, Epona's autoregressive nature allows for minutes-long video generation.
- **Maintaining continuous representation:** In contrast to GPT-style models that tokenize images and actions, Epona operates in continuous latent spaces for both visual and trajectory predictions, preserving detail and precision.
- **Integrating planning:** It provides a mechanism for multi-step, continuous trajectory planning, a capability often lacking or limited in prior diffusion models and more rigid in GPT-style models (which focus on discrete next-token actions).
- **Combating autoregressive drift:** A common challenge in autoregressive models is error accumulation over long sequences. Epona introduces a novel "chain-of-forward" training strategy to mitigate this.

The work also relates to concurrent efforts like MagicDriveDiT and InfinityDrive, which focus on video generation for autonomous driving but are primarily scene synthesis models lacking causal dynamics and real-time planning capabilities. Epona distinguishes itself by its emphasis on a complete "world model" that supports both visual generation and actionable planning for decision-making.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Epona stems from the critical limitations of existing world models in autonomous driving, which hinder their practical deployment and efficacy in complex, dynamic environments:

1. **Lack of Flexible-Length, Long-Horizon Prediction:** Autonomous driving requires understanding and predicting future events over extended periods (e.g., minutes, not just seconds) and adapting to dynamic situations. Current diffusion models are often confined to fixed, short video lengths, making them unsuitable for continuous world simulation and long-term planning. GPT-style models offer temporal flexibility but suffer from visual degradation.
2. **Inadequate Integration of Trajectory Planning:** A truly effective world model for autonomous driving must seamlessly integrate visual scene generation with precise vehicle motion planning. Existing diffusion models typically lack robust planning modules, while GPT-style models often rely on discretized actions, compromising precision and limiting long-horizon planning capabilities.
3. **Compromised Visual Fidelity and Precision:** While diffusion models excel in visual quality, their inability to model per-timestep local distributions leads to difficulties in progressive dynamics. Conversely, GPT-style models, despite their temporal flexibility, sacrifice high-frequency visual details and planning accuracy due to their tokenization process.
4. **Error Accumulation in Autoregressive Loops:** Autoregressive generation, while enabling long sequences, is prone to error accumulation and content drift, where small errors compound over time, leading to inconsistent or unrealistic outputs.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the Epona project are:

- **Develop an Autoregressive Diffusion World Model:** To create a novel framework that synergistically combines the strengths of diffusion models (high visual quality, continuous representation) and autoregressive models (flexible-length, long-horizon generation).
- **Achieve High-Resolution, Long-Duration Video Generation:** To enable the creation of minutes-long, consistent, and visually realistic future driving scenes at high resolution, significantly surpassing the temporal limits of prior models.
- **Enable Accurate and Real-time Trajectory Planning:** To integrate a robust motion planning module that can predict continuous, multi-step vehicle trajectories in real-time, bridging the gap between generative world models and practical end-to-end planners.
- **Address Autoregressive Drift:** To devise an effective training strategy that mitigates error accumulation and content drift, ensuring the consistency and quality of long-term predictions.
- **Implicitly Learn Traffic World Knowledge:** To demonstrate that the world model, through self-supervised future prediction tasks, can implicitly acquire an understanding of real-world driving dynamics and traffic rules.

Ultimately, Epona aims to move towards human-like, end-to-end autonomous driving systems by providing a foundation model capable of both understanding the physical world and making informed decisions within it.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Epona is an autoregressive diffusion world model characterized by its novel architectural designs and training strategy, which enable high-resolution, long-horizon video generation and accurate trajectory planning. Its core methodology revolves around localized spatiotemporal distribution modeling, achieved through three key innovations:

1. **Decoupled Spatiotemporal Factorization:**
 - **Insight:** Traditional video diffusion models model a joint global distribution, lacking explicit causal temporal constraints, leading to error accumulation in long sequences.
 - **Approach:** Epona disentangles spacetime processing. A **GPT-style transformer with causal attention** (Multimodal Spatiotemporal Transformer, MST) handles temporal dynamics in a compressed latent space. Simultaneously, **twin diffusion transformers** (Trajectory Planning DiT and Next-frame Prediction DiT) are responsible for fine-grained spatial rendering and trajectory generation, respectively. This separation allows the model to capture causality in the temporal domain while leveraging diffusion for high-fidelity spatial details.

2. **Asynchronous Multi-modal Generation:**
 - **Insight:** Seamlessly integrating motion planning with visual modeling is crucial for autonomous driving.
 - **Approach:** Epona decouples trajectory planning from visual generation via parallel denoising processes. Two specialized Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) are employed:
 - **Trajectory Planning Diffusion Transformer (TrajDiT):** A compact DiT that models the policy for predicting an entire N-step (e.g., 3-second) future vehicle trajectory in a single pass.
 - **Next-frame Prediction Diffusion Transformer (VisDiT):** Another DiT dedicated to generating the single next future camera observation.
 - Both TrajDiT and VisDiT share **flow-matching objectives** and are conditioned on the same compact temporal latent representation (output of MST), ensuring alignment while allowing for modality-specific optimizations. This modularity enables flexible usage, such as real-time planning by running only MST and TrajDiT.

3. **Chain-of-Forward Training Strategy:**
 - **Insight:** Autoregressive models suffer from "autoregressive drift" – a domain gap between training (teacher-forcing with ground truth history) and inference (using self-predicted history), leading to error accumulation and quality degradation.
 - **Approach:** Periodically during training, Epona performs multiple forward passes using **self-predicted frames**. Instead of sampling from pure noise, it leverages the model-predicted velocity to estimate denoised latents in a single step (`x̂(0) = x(t) + t*vΘ(x(t), t)`). These estimated latents are then used as conditioned history for subsequent autoregressive predictions. This process simulates inference-time errors, enhancing the model's robustness to deviations and improving long-term consistency.

**Architectural Components:**

- **Multimodal Spatiotemporal Transformer (MST):** Encodes historical driving context (encoded visual latents {Zt} and action sequences {at-1→t}) into a compact latent representation (F). It uses interleaved multimodal spatial and causal temporal attention layers, supporting variable historical context lengths and reducing memory consumption.
- **Diffusion Transformers (TrajDiT & VisDiT):** Both are based on DiT frameworks, utilizing a Dual-Single-Stream architecture for processing condition information and noise. VisDiT includes an additional modulation branch for action control. They are optimized using the rectified flow loss for continuous latent and trajectory prediction.
- **Temporal-aware Deep Compression Autoencoder (DCAE) Decoder:** To improve image decoding efficiency and temporal consistency, Epona adopts a DCAE for encoding/decoding, which significantly compresses latents. To address flickering common with image-based decoders in video generation, spatiotemporal self-attention layers are introduced *before* the DCAE decoder during fine-tuning, enhancing inter-frame consistency.

**Training Details:**
The entire Epona model (MST, TrajDiT, VisDiT) is trained end-to-end using a joint loss, which is a sum of the rectified flow losses for trajectory prediction (`L_traj`) and visual prediction (`L_vis`). Training is conducted from scratch on NuPlan and NuScenes datasets, requiring significant computational resources (48 NVIDIA A100 GPUs for two weeks).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate Epona's significant advancements in both video generation and trajectory planning for autonomous driving.

**Video Generation:**
- **State-of-the-Art Visual Quality:** Epona achieves a state-of-the-art Frechet Video Distance (FVD) score of **82.8** on the NuScenes benchmark, surpassing the previous best (Vista) by 7.4% (89.4). This indicates smoother and more realistic video generation quality. FID scores are also competitive (7.5 vs. Vista's 6.9).
- **Extended Long-Horizon Generation:** A crucial achievement is the model's ability to generate **minutes-long videos (over 2 minutes, 600 frames)**, a substantial improvement over prior works like Vista (15 seconds / 150 frames) or DrivingWorld (40 seconds / 400 frames). This extended duration is critical for simulating dynamic real-world driving scenarios.
- **High-Fidelity and Consistency:** Qualitative comparisons (Figure 5, 7, 10) show that Epona generates consistent, high-fidelity driving scenes with detailed structures and vehicles over long durations, without noticeable drift or degradation.
- **Trajectory-Controlled Generation:** The model successfully generates controllable videos based on predefined pose trajectories (Figure 6), which is essential for simulating specific driving maneuvers and extreme scenarios.

**Trajectory Planning:**
- **Competitive Performance on NuScenes:** On the NuScenes benchmark, Epona achieves competitive L2 error (1.25m avg.) and notably the **lowest 1-second collision rate (0.01%)** compared to other end-to-end planners. This is achieved without any auxiliary supervision (e.g., 3D boxes, lanes) and using only front-camera input, whereas many competitors rely on richer inputs or additional supervision. This highlights its implicit understanding of basic traffic rules.
- **State-of-the-Art on NAVSIM:** On the more challenging NAVSIM benchmark, Epona achieves a state-of-the-art overall **Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 86.2**, outperforming strong end-to-end planners like UniAD, PARA-Drive, and LAW. This demonstrates its robust motion planning capability in a perception-free setting.
- **Real-time Planning:** The modular design allows the MST and TrajDiT components to function as a real-time motion planner, achieving speeds of up to **20 Hz** on a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU.

**Traffic World Knowledge Understanding:**
- Epona implicitly learns essential traffic world knowledge (e.g., stopping at red lights) purely from self-supervised future prediction tasks (Figure 1C). This emergent capability underscores the potential of world models to acquire deep understanding of driving dynamics without explicit rule-based programming.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effect of Shared Latent:** Jointly training video prediction with trajectory prediction using a shared latent representation significantly boosts planning performance (PDMS improved from 78.1 to 86.2 on NAVSIM), confirming that visual signals aid in capturing complex driving dynamics.
- **Effect of Chain-of-Forward Training:** This strategy is crucial for long-term generation, preventing rapid degradation of visual quality and FID scores over extended autoregressive sequences (Figure 7, 8).
- **Effect of Temporal-aware DCAE Decoder:** The temporal interaction module in the decoder effectively mitigates flickering artifacts and enhances the smoothness and consistency of generated videos (Table 6).
- **Effect of Context Length:** Conditioning on more historical frames (up to 10) generally improves video quality (FVD), demonstrating the model's ability to leverage richer context (Table 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Epona represents a significant stride in the development of world models for autonomous driving, with profound implications for the future of the field:

1. **Unified and Robust World Modeling:** By successfully merging the strengths of diffusion models (high-fidelity continuous generation) and autoregressive models (temporal flexibility, long-horizon prediction), Epona offers a more holistic and robust world model. This unification addresses a long-standing challenge in generative AI, demonstrating that it's possible to achieve both high visual quality and extensive temporal consistency.

2. **Advancing End-to-End Autonomous Driving:** Epona's capacity to jointly predict high-fidelity future trajectories and driving scenes within an end-to-end framework moves autonomous driving closer to a human-like, self-supervised paradigm. This reduces the reliance on extensive manual annotations and explicit supervision, simplifying development and potentially improving generalization. The ability to implicitly learn traffic rules from raw data is particularly impactful for creating more adaptable and intelligent driving systems.

3. **Real-time Decision Making and Planning:** The modular design of Epona, particularly the ability to perform real-time trajectory planning (MST + TrajDiT at 20 Hz) by decoupling it from visual generation, is critical for practical autonomous vehicle deployment. This speed, combined with accurate planning, can enable quicker and safer decision-making in dynamic driving environments.

4. **Enhanced Simulation and Validation:** The ability to generate minutes-long, consistent driving scenarios with controllable trajectories offers an invaluable tool for autonomous driving simulation, testing, and validation. This can accelerate the development cycle, allow for the exploration of rare or dangerous edge cases, and provide richer synthetic data for training other modules.

5. **Robustness to Long-Term Prediction Challenges:** The "chain-of-forward" training strategy directly tackles the pervasive problem of error accumulation in autoregressive models. This innovation is broadly applicable to any sequential generative task, making long-term, high-quality predictions more feasible and reliable.

6. **Paving the Way for Foundation Models in Robotics:** Epona serves as an example of a "foundation model" for physical AI, specifically for autonomous driving. Its generalizable approach to understanding world dynamics and predicting multimodal futures suggests a pathway towards more capable and versatile AI agents in various robotic and embodied AI domains.

7. **Open Science and Reproducibility:** The stated intention to make the code publicly available (on GitHub) will significantly impact the research community, fostering further development, benchmarking, and innovation based on Epona's architecture and strategies.

In conclusion, Epona significantly advances the state of the art in driving world models by elegantly addressing critical limitations of existing paradigms. Its ability to generate long, consistent, high-fidelity future scenes while enabling real-time, accurate trajectory planning makes it a highly promising direction for next-generation autonomous driving systems and beyond.
