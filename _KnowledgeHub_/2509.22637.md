# Variational Reasoning for Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A variational reasoning framework is introduced for large language models, treating thinking traces as latent variables within a probabilistic objective. This approach consistently improves accuracy over supervised finetuning and reinforcement learning methods across various reasoning benchmarks, while also demonstrating enhanced training stability and generalization.

## Problem

- Supervised Finetuning (SFT) for LLM reasoning demands expensive, meticulously curated thinking traces, leading to generalization issues and catastrophic forgetting.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for reasoning often exhibit training instability, reduced output diversity, and a bias towards easier questions, resulting in suboptimal performance on complex tasks.
- Existing methods lack a principled, robust training objective that can effectively scale and generalize reasoning capabilities across diverse and challenging problem types.

## Method

- A ==variational inference framework== treats thinking traces as latent variables to maximize the marginal log-likelihood of correct answers for a given input.
- An ==Importance-Weighted Autoencoder (IWAE)-style multi-trace Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)== objective is employed to provide a tighter lower bound and improve gradient estimation, utilizing an accuracy-based estimator for answer probability.
- The variational posterior is stabilized by optimizing it with a ==forward-KL divergence==, which prevents mode collapse and leverages answer hints to guide the generation of high-quality thinking traces.

## Results

- The method consistently achieved over **8.5%** higher average accuracy than strong SFT baselines and over **14%** higher than RL baselines across various reasoning benchmarks, including competitive math, coding, and general knowledge.
- Training dynamics showed improved stability, characterized by lower average training loss and reduced gradient norm spikes compared to baseline methods.
- Significant generalization improvements were observed on out-of-distribution tasks like GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro, despite training data focusing on math and code, indicating robust transferability of reasoning skills.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The variational framework unifies existing methods like Rejection Sampling Finetuning and binary-reward RL, showing they can be re-expressed as local forward KL optimizations implicitly weighted by model accuracy.
> - Common RL-style methods exhibit an unnoticed systematic bias towards easier questions, as their optimization inherently prioritizes problems where the model already performs well.
> - An accuracy-based estimator for the probability of a correct answer given a thought process is theoretically and empirically superior, offering lower variance and more robust training compared to likelihood-based methods.
