# Variational Reasoning for Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A variational reasoning framework is introduced for large language models, treating thinking traces as latent variables within a probabilistic objective. This approach consistently improves accuracy over supervised finetuning and reinforcement learning methods across various reasoning benchmarks, while also demonstrating enhanced training stability and generalization.

## Problem

- Supervised Finetuning (SFT) for LLM reasoning demands expensive, meticulously curated thinking traces, leading to generalization issues and catastrophic forgetting.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for reasoning often exhibit training instability, reduced output diversity, and a bias towards easier questions, resulting in suboptimal performance on complex tasks.
- Existing methods lack a principled, robust training objective that can effectively scale and generalize reasoning capabilities across diverse and challenging problem types.

## Method

- A ==variational inference framework== treats thinking traces as latent variables to maximize the marginal log-likelihood of correct answers for a given input.
- An ==Importance-Weighted Autoencoder (IWAE)-style multi-trace Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)== objective is employed to provide a tighter lower bound and improve gradient estimation, utilizing an accuracy-based estimator for answer probability.
- The variational posterior is stabilized by optimizing it with a ==forward-KL divergence==, which prevents mode collapse and leverages answer hints to guide the generation of high-quality thinking traces.

## Results

- The method consistently achieved over **8.5%** higher average accuracy than strong SFT baselines and over **14%** higher than RL baselines across various reasoning benchmarks, including competitive math, coding, and general knowledge.
- Training dynamics showed improved stability, characterized by lower average training loss and reduced gradient norm spikes compared to baseline methods.
- Significant generalization improvements were observed on out-of-distribution tasks like GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro, despite training data focusing on math and code, indicating robust transferability of reasoning skills.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The variational framework unifies existing methods like Rejection Sampling Finetuning and binary-reward RL, showing they can be re-expressed as local forward KL optimizations implicitly weighted by model accuracy.
> - Common RL-style methods exhibit an unnoticed systematic bias towards easier questions, as their optimization inherently prioritizes problems where the model already performs well.
> - An accuracy-based estimator for the probability of a correct answer given a thought process is theoretically and empirically superior, offering lower variance and more robust training compared to likelihood-based methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of sophisticated reasoning abilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) has become a paramount focus in contemporary AI research, driving advancements across diverse complex tasks such as mathematics, coding, and scientific problem-solving. This paper positions itself squarely within this landscape, addressing critical limitations of the two dominant paradigms currently employed for training such reasoning capabilities: Supervised Finetuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

SFT, while empirically successful, often requires meticulously curated "long-thinking traces," which are exceptionally costly and time-consuming to collect. Moreover, as an offline method, SFT frequently struggles with generalization to novel problem types and can lead to catastrophic forgetting of previously learned knowledge. RL-based methods, particularly those leveraging verifiable rewards, have shown promise in mitigating reward hacking. However, they are plagued by issues of training instability, potential collapse of output diversity, and a tendency to underperform on harder questions, sometimes even yielding lower Pass@K accuracy than their base models. These challenges underscore a fundamental need for more principled and robust training objectives.

"Variational Reasoning for Language Models" directly addresses these systemic issues by introducing a novel probabilistic framework. By re-conceptualizing thinking traces as latent variables within a variational inference paradigm, the authors propose a method that not only offers a principled objective for maximizing the likelihood of correct answers but also provides a unifying lens through which existing SFT and RL approaches can be better understood. The paper explicitly identifies and analyzes an unnoticed bias in popular RL-style methods (like Rejection-Sampling Finetuning, binary-reward RL, and Group Relative Policy Optimization - GRPO) towards easier questions. By offering a stable and theoretically grounded alternative, this work aims to overcome the inherent limitations of current state-of-the-art methods, paving the way for LLMs that can reason more reliably, generalize more effectively, and learn more stably, especially on challenging, complex tasks. This represents a significant step towards developing more robust and truly intelligent reasoning agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations observed in current state-of-the-art methods for training reasoning capabilities in large language models. The authors identify two primary approaches—Supervised Finetuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL)—and detail their respective shortcomings. SFT, while effective, suffers from the high cost of collecting long, detailed thinking traces and may exhibit poor generalization or catastrophic forgetting. RL methods, despite their success with verifiable rewards, are often plagued by training instability, collapse of output diversity, and a bias towards easier questions, leading to suboptimal performance on more challenging tasks. These persistent issues highlight a critical gap and the urgent need for a more principled and robust training objective for reasoning models.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several key objectives:
1. **Develop a Principled Probabilistic Framework:** The primary objective is to introduce a novel variational reasoning framework that treats thinking traces as latent variables. This framework aims to optimize the log-likelihood of producing correct answers by leveraging variational inference, thereby offering a theoretically sound alternative to existing heuristic-driven approaches.
2. **Ensure Tractability and Tightness of the Objective:** To make the optimization feasible, the authors aim to extend the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) to an Importance-Weighted Autoencoder (IWAE)-style multi-trace objective. This extension is designed to provide a strictly tighter lower bound on the true log-likelihood, leading to more effective learning.
3. **Stabilize Variational Posterior Training:** A specific objective is to address the potential instability in training the variational posterior. The authors propose a forward-KL divergence formulation to optimize the variational posterior, which is intended to prevent mode collapse and enable more effective utilization of answer hints, guiding the generation of high-quality thinking traces.
4. **Provide a Unifying Theoretical Interpretation:** Beyond proposing a new method, a significant objective is to analyze and reinterpret existing dominant approaches, such as rejection-sampling finetuning (RFT) and binary-reward RL (including GRPO), within the proposed variational framework. This analysis aims to shed light on their underlying mechanisms and, crucially, to uncover previously unnoticed biases, particularly towards easier questions.
5. **Empirically Validate Superior Performance and Stability:** The framework's practical efficacy is a key objective, to be demonstrated through comprehensive empirical validation. This involves evaluating the method across a wide range of reasoning tasks and LLM families (Qwen 2.5 and Qwen 3), showing consistent improvements over strong baselines in terms of accuracy and demonstrating enhanced training stability.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors seek to advance the fundamental understanding and practical capabilities of reasoning in LLMs, providing a more reliable and theoretically grounded path forward for developing advanced AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this paper revolves around introducing a variational reasoning framework that views thinking traces as latent variables, optimized through variational inference to enhance LLM reasoning capabilities.

#### 3.1. Probabilistic Formulation and ELBO Derivation

The authors begin by defining a reasoning model $\pi_{\theta}(z, y|x)$, which generates a thinking process $z$ and a final answer $y$ for a given input question $x$. This joint probability is decomposed into $\pi_{\theta}(y|x, z) \cdot \pi_{\theta}(z|x)$. The ultimate goal is to maximize the marginal log-likelihood of producing a correct answer, $\log P_{\theta}(Y_x|x)$, where $Y_x$ is the oracle set of correct answers. This marginalization over all possible thinking traces $z$ is intractable.
To circumvent this intractability, the paper applies variational inference, introducing a variational posterior $q_{\phi}(z|x, y')$ that approximates the true posterior $P_{\theta}(z|x, Y_x)$. This variational posterior is conditioned on an auxiliary answer hint $y'$ (typically an oracle answer from $Y_x$), encouraging it to generate thinking traces more likely to lead to correct answers. The evidence lower bound (ELBO) is derived:
$L_{ELBO}(x, Y_x, y'; \pi_{\theta}, q_{\phi}) = E_{q_{\phi}(z|x,y')}[\log \pi_{\theta}(Y_x|x, z)] - D_{KL}(q_{\phi}(z|x, y')||\pi_{\theta}(z|x))$
Maximizing this ELBO with respect to $q_{\phi}$ is shown to be equivalent to minimizing the reverse KL divergence $D_{KL}(q_{\phi}(z|x, y')||P_{\theta}(z|x, Y_x))$.

#### 3.2. IWAE-Style Multi-Trace Extension

To further tighten the lower bound and improve gradient estimation, the ELBO is extended to an Importance-Weighted Autoencoder (IWAE)-style formulation, leveraging multiple $K$ sampled thinking traces. This $L^K_{ELBO}$ bound is strictly tighter as $K$ increases.
The gradient of $L^K_{ELBO}$ with respect to the model parameters $\theta$ is derived using importance sampling, where each trace $z_k$ (sampled from $q_{\phi}$) is weighted by $\rho_k = \frac{\pi_{\theta}(z_k, Y_x|x)}{q_{\phi}(z_k|x, y')}$.
A key challenge is the high variance associated with computing the likelihood ratio $\frac{\pi_{\theta}(z_k|x)}{q_{\phi}(z_k|x, y')}$ for long reasoning traces. To mitigate this, the authors propose using the geometric mean of token-level likelihood ratios, effectively normalizing the ratio per token.
For estimating $\pi_{\theta}(Y_x|x, z_k)$ (the probability of a correct answer given $x$ and $z_k$), two unbiased estimators are considered: likelihood-based and accuracy-based. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) demonstrates that the accuracy-based estimator (sampling multiple answers $y$ from $\pi_{\theta}(y|x, z_k)$ and checking correctness) has lower worst-case variance, especially when the number of possible correct answers $|Y_x|$ is large. This accuracy-based estimator is thus adopted in experiments.

#### 3.3. Optimizing the Variational Posterior via Forward KL Divergence

While the ELBO (minimizing reverse KL) is used for optimizing the main model $\pi_{\theta}$, the authors find that optimizing $q_{\phi}$ via reverse KL can lead to instability, especially when $\pi_{\theta}$ is already strong from pre-training. To address this, they propose optimizing $q_{\phi}$ using the *forward KL divergence*, $D_{KL}(P_{\theta}(z|x, Y_x)||q_{\phi}(z|x, y'))$. This objective, approximated via sampling from $\pi_{\theta}(z|x)$ and weighting by answer correctness, essentially acts as a weighted supervised finetuning (SFT) for $q_{\phi}$, enabling it to effectively learn from correct answer hints without collapsing.

#### 3.4. Overall Training Pipeline (Algorithm 1)

The method integrates these components into an iterative training pipeline:
1. **Initialize:** A reasoning model $\pi_{\theta_0}$ and a variational posterior $q_{\phi_0}$ (initialized from $\pi_{\theta_0}$).
2. **Update $q_{\phi}$:** In each round, $q_{\phi}$ is updated by minimizing the forward KL divergence. This involves sampling thinking traces from $\pi_{\theta}$ and weighting them by their accuracy in producing correct answers to guide $q_{\phi}$.
3. **Update $\pi_{\theta}$:** Subsequently, $\pi_{\theta}$ is updated by maximizing the IWAE-style ELBO. This involves sampling $K$ thinking traces from the *updated* $q_{\phi}$, computing importance weights (using geometric mean for likelihood ratio and accuracy-based estimator for answer probability), and applying weighted gradients.

#### 3.5. Connection to Other Methods

A significant part of the methodology involves theoretically connecting the variational framework to existing methods like Rejection Sampling Finetuning (RFT) and binary-reward RL (including GRPO). The authors show that these methods can be re-expressed as local forward KL optimizations, implicitly weighted by the model's accuracy on a given instruction. This analysis reveals a previously unnoticed systematic bias in these methods towards easier questions. In contrast, the variational reasoning framework aims to balance attention across questions, regardless of their initial difficulty.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

The method is empirically validated on Qwen 2.5 and Qwen 3 model families across diverse reasoning benchmarks (math, coding, general knowledge). Training uses a curated dataset (Bespoke-Stratos-17k), and comparisons are made against strong SFT and RL baselines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical validation of the variational reasoning framework on the Qwen 2.5 and Qwen 3 model families yields several significant findings:

1. **Consistent Performance Superiority:** The variational reasoning method consistently outperforms strong baselines, including SFT (Bespoke-Stratos) and RL-based approaches (General-Reasoner, RLT), across a wide array of reasoning benchmarks. These benchmarks span diverse domains such as competitive mathematics (MATH500, AIME24&25, AMC23, OlympiadBench), coding (LiveCodeBench Easy, Medium, Hard), and general knowledge (GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro). For instance, on the Qwen3-4B-Base, variational reasoning achieves over 8.5% higher average accuracy than Bespoke-Stratos-4B† and over 14% higher than General-Reasoner-4B in other domains. This demonstrates the robustness and effectiveness of the proposed framework across different model scales (4B, 7B, 8B, 32B).

2. **Strong Generalization to Out-of-Distribution Tasks:** Notably, the method shows significant improvements on out-of-distribution benchmarks like GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro, even though the training data (Bespoke-Stratos-17k) primarily covers math and code problems. This indicates that the reasoning abilities learned through the variational framework generalize effectively beyond the specific domains of the training data, a crucial aspect for truly capable LLMs.

3. **Improved Training Stability:** The analysis of training dynamics reveals that variational reasoning leads to lower average training loss and fewer gradient norm spikes compared to baseline methods. This enhanced stability is attributed to the adaptive weighting mechanism (e$\rho_k$) within the framework, which prioritizes high-quality reasoning traces aligned with the model's policy, facilitating more consistent and effective learning.

4. **Effectiveness of Accuracy-Based Estimators:** Ablation studies confirm the theoretical finding that the accuracy-based estimator for $\pi_{\theta}(Y_x|x, z_k)$ (the probability of generating a correct answer given a thought process) significantly outperforms a naive likelihood-based estimator. The geometric mean of token likelihood also performs well, suggesting that robust estimation of this component is critical for the framework's success, particularly when the space of correct answers is large.

5. **Benefits of Multiple Thinking Traces and Answer Hints:** The results show that increasing the number of sampled thinking traces (K in Algorithm 1) from the variational posterior generally enhances model performance, highlighting a practical trade-off between computational cost and reasoning accuracy. Furthermore, conditioning the variational posterior on an answer hint ($y'$) is empirically demonstrated to be crucial, significantly improving performance compared to variants without such conditioning.

6. **Robustness to Design Choices:** The method exhibits robustness to different prompt templates used for the variational posterior and to the specific data usage strategies (e.g., using a single-best trace vs. weighted multiple traces). While using weighted multiple traces without mixing with original data achieves optimal performance, even simplified data usage schemes yield strong results. The method also shows robust performance regardless of data overlap between variational posterior training and reasoning model training, indicating good generalization of the learned posterior.

These findings collectively underscore that variational reasoning provides a principled, stable, and highly effective approach for improving the reasoning capabilities of large language models, addressing key limitations of prior SFT and RL methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Variational Reasoning for Language Models" paper introduces a highly significant contribution to the field of large language models, with profound potential impact across both theoretical understanding and practical application.

#### 5.1. A Principled and Unifying Probabilistic Framework

The most significant contribution is the introduction of a principled probabilistic framework based on variational inference. By treating thinking traces as latent variables, the paper offers a theoretically sound approach to training reasoning LLMs, moving beyond ad-hoc heuristics often found in SFT and RL. This framework unifies disparate methods by demonstrating that techniques like rejection sampling finetuning and binary-reward RL can be interpreted as specific instantiations of local forward-KL objectives. This unification provides a clearer theoretical lens through which to analyze, compare, and potentially improve a wide range of existing methods.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Performance and Training Stability

Empirically, the method demonstrates consistent and substantial improvements over strong baselines across a diverse set of challenging reasoning benchmarks, including math, coding, and general knowledge tasks. This suggests a significant leap in the ability of LLMs to generate accurate and robust reasoning processes. Crucially, the framework also exhibits greater training stability, characterized by lower loss and fewer gradient spikes. This stability is vital for scaling up LLM training and for ensuring reliable performance in real-world applications, where instability can be a major impediment.

#### 5.3. Unveiling Hidden Biases in Existing Methods

The theoretical analysis reveals a previously unnoticed implicit bias in common RL-style methods (RFT, binary-reward RL, GRPO) towards easier questions. By showing that these methods essentially optimize a forward-KL divergence weighted by model accuracy, the paper exposes why they might struggle with harder problems. This insight is critical, as it informs the design of future reasoning objectives that can more evenly address the spectrum of problem difficulty. The variational framework, by contrast, offers a pathway to mitigate this bias, ensuring that difficult cases receive appropriate attention during training.

#### 5.4. Practical Implications for Advanced LLMs

The ability to consistently outperform baselines and generalize well to out-of-distribution tasks signifies a practical breakthrough. This means LLMs trained with variational reasoning could be more reliable in real-world scenarios requiring deep logical thought, such as automated problem-solving, code generation, scientific discovery, and complex decision-making systems. The robustness demonstrated across different model scales (4B to 32B) and prompt templates further underscores its practical utility.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The paper opens up several promising avenues for future research. Explicitly, the authors suggest exploring multi-round training (beyond the single round used in experiments) and investigating richer designs for the variational posterior, particularly concerning the answer hint $y'$. These directions could further enhance the model's reasoning capabilities and potentially lead to even tighter theoretical bounds or more efficient training regimes. The detailed derivations and ablation studies also provide a strong foundation for future theoretical and empirical explorations into the mechanics of LLM reasoning.

In essence, "Variational Reasoning for Language Models" does not just offer another incremental improvement; it proposes a foundational shift in how LLM reasoning can be conceived and optimized, promising more capable, stable, and theoretically grounded AI systems.
