# Scaling Agentic Reinforcement Learning for Tool-Integrated Reasoning in VLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> VISTA-Gym provides a scalable, tool-integrated environment to train Vision-Language Models (VLMs) using agentic reinforcement learning for visual reasoning tasks. The VISTA-R1 agent, trained in this environment, outperforms comparable baselines by 9.51%–18.72% on diverse benchmarks and generalizes effectively to unseen tasks, showcasing enhanced tool utilization and core reasoning abilities.

## Problem

- VLMs struggle with multi-step visual interactions and capturing fine-grained visual details, limiting their ability to truly "think with images."
- Existing tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) efforts are narrow, and current open-source VLMs often fail to effectively select, invoke, and coordinate visual tools, sometimes degrading performance.
- A lack of scalable and unified agentic training environments specifically tailored for general-purpose, tool-integrated visual reasoning in VLMs exists.

## Method

- **VISTA-Gym**, a scalable, visual-centric, ==tool-integrated== agentic training environment, unifies 7 reasoning-intensive VQA tasks from 13 datasets and provides 26 visual tools via a standardized API.
- VISTA-Gym features an executable interactive environment with a Gymnasium-style API for agent actions and observations, supported by a scalable infrastructure utilizing microservices and asynchronous training for efficiency.
- VISTA-R1, a VLM-based agent, is trained in a two-stage framework: initially fine-tuned with imitation learning on expert trajectories and then refined with online reinforcement learning using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and a sparse, format-aware reward design.

## Results

- VISTA-R1-**8B** significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines of comparable size by **9.51%**–**18.72%** with tools and **2.03%**-**11.24%** without tools, across 11 public reasoning-intensive VQA benchmarks.
- The trained VISTA-R1 agent demonstrates strong generalization to out-of-distribution tasks, achieving accuracy comparable to substantially larger proprietary models (e.g., GPT-o3, Claude-4.5-Sonnet).
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of both the SFT warmup and online RL stages, with GRPO yielding the most robust performance among RL algorithms, and the sparse, format-aware reward design improving adherence to the desired reasoning protocol.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning is critical for robust tool-integrated reasoning in VLMs, as merely exposing tools without explicit reasoning supervision can sharply decrease accuracy.
> - Optimal problem-solving arises from an interleaved loop where reasoning guides tool invocation, rather than relying on either capability (reasoning or tool use) in isolation.
> - Multi-task and multi-tool training within a diverse environment like VISTA-Gym are essential for achieving strong generalization capabilities and preventing over-specialization in VLM agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress, demonstrating strong capabilities in tasks like visual question answering, multimodal reasoning, and grounded mathematical problem solving. However, the authors identify a critical gap: while VLMs excel at *understanding* static images and leverage text-based reasoning paradigms (like Chain-of-Thought and reinforcement learning for refining reasoning quality), their ability to "think with images"—that is, to reason through multi-step visual interactions and capture fine-grained visual structures or spatial relationships—remains limited. Existing approaches often rely on static visual embeddings and shallow cross-modal alignment, which fall short for real-world scenarios demanding dynamic visual engagement.

This limitation has spurred the introduction of "tool-integrated reasoning" (TIR), where VLMs are equipped with external tools (e.g., grounding, zoom-in, search) to facilitate finer-grained perception and object interaction reasoning. Prior efforts in TIR have primarily been narrow in scope, often confined to specific tasks (e.g., image search for knowledge, zoom-in for object grounding) or relying on single specialized tools. Current open-source VLMs, as highlighted by the authors' exploratory experiments, still struggle significantly with the effective selection, invocation, and coordination of these visual tools, often showing degraded performance when tools are naively introduced.

In parallel, reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training agents in interactive environments, with recent efforts creating unified platforms for VLM agents in domains like gaming and robotics. These environments offer controllable dynamics, grounded feedback, and structured task spaces, which are highly beneficial for agent learning. However, the simulation of tool-integrated thinking in VLMs, particularly for *open-domain visual reasoning*, has remained largely underexplored. Existing VLM-specific RL environments typically focus on compositional visual games or embodiment tasks requiring intermediate state information, rather than general-purpose tool-integrated visual reasoning.

The present work directly addresses this void by introducing VISTA-Gym, a scalable, agentic training environment specifically designed to foster robust, generalizable tool-integrated reasoning in VLMs across diverse, real-world multimodal reasoning tasks. By unifying a broad range of tasks, standardizing a comprehensive tool interface, and providing a scalable interactive infrastructure, this research pushes the frontier of VLM capabilities beyond passive image understanding towards active, interactive, and intelligent visual problem-solving, thereby making a significant contribution to advancing the "thinking with images" paradigm. It seeks to unlock the full potential of tool use for VLMs by providing the necessary training ground and a principled RL-based training strategy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in complex, multi-step visual reasoning tasks. While VLMs have demonstrated strong capabilities in various visual question answering (VQA) and multimodal reasoning benchmarks, their performance often relies on static visual embeddings and text-only reasoning, failing to adequately capture fine-grained visual details, spatial relationships, and quantitative dependencies. This deficiency prevents them from truly "thinking with images" – an essential capability for real-world problem-solving.

Specifically, the authors identify the following key problems:
1. **Limited "Thinking with Images":** Existing VLMs struggle with interleaved visual interaction and reasoning, making them less effective in tasks requiring dynamic visual exploration or detailed analysis.
2. **Inadequate Tool Integration:** Despite the introduction of tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) to augment VLMs with external visual tools (e.g., grounding, zoom-in), current open-source models (and even some proprietary ones) are largely inept at effectively selecting, invoking, and coordinating these tools. Exploratory experiments vividly demonstrate that simply exposing tools to base VLMs often *degrades* performance, indicating that models lack the intrinsic capability to leverage them meaningfully.
3. **Lack of Scalable Training Environments:** There is a critical absence of scalable and unified agentic training environments specifically tailored for general-purpose, tool-integrated visual reasoning in VLMs. Current RL environments for agents are often text-only or narrowly focused on specific VLM applications (e.g., games, robotics embodiment), offering limited support for multimodal grounding and diverse visual reasoning tasks.

Motivated by these challenges, the paper sets forth two primary objectives:

1. **To introduce VISTA-Gym:** A scalable, visual-centric, tool-integrated agentic training environment. VISTA-Gym aims to systematically enhance the reasoning and decision-making capabilities of VLM agents by providing:
 - **Diverse multi-modal reasoning tasks:** A comprehensive suite of VQA tasks spanning various reasoning types (perception, symbolic manipulation, chart/document interpretation) across multiple public datasets.
 - **Unified, extensible tool interface:** A standardized API for a rich set of pre-defined visual tools (e.g., detection, OCR, chart parsing, math solvers) that allow agents to offload sub-problems and ground high-level reasoning in structured intermediate results.
 - **Scalable interactive infrastructure:** An efficient training facility leveraging techniques like multithreading, parallel execution, and asynchronous rollouts to enable large-scale trajectory collection and automated evaluation compatible with diverse agent architectures.
2. **To develop VISTA-R1:** A VLM-based agent trained within VISTA-Gym for robust, tool-augmented reasoning. The objective for VISTA-R1 is to demonstrate the effectiveness of VISTA-Gym as a training ground by enabling a VLM to interleave tool-use with agentic reasoning via a multi-turn trajectory sampling and end-to-end reinforcement learning framework. The ultimate goal is for VISTA-R1 to significantly outperform state-of-the-art baselines in reasoning-intensive VQA benchmarks, showcasing strong generalization and tool-integration skills.

In essence, the research is driven by the need to transform VLMs from passive visual observers into active, intelligent agents capable of complex, interactive visual problem-solving by providing them with the necessary tools and a dedicated environment to learn their effective utilization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around two main components: **VISTA-Gym**, the scalable training environment, and **VISTA-R1**, the VLM-based agent trained within it using a two-stage reinforcement learning framework.

#### 3.1. VISTA-Gym: Scalable Tool-Integrated Agentic Training Environment

VISTA-Gym is designed as a comprehensive platform to incentivize and train VLMs in tool-integrated visual reasoning.

- **Diverse Task and Tool Collection:**
 - **Reasoning-Intensive VQA Tasks:** VISTA-Gym unifies training and evaluation across 7 distinct reasoning tasks derived from 13 public datasets. These tasks span diverse domains, including Chart Understanding (e.g., FigureQA, ChartQA), Geometric Reasoning (e.g., Geometry3K, UniGeo), Geospatial Reasoning (e.g., MapQA), Scientific Reasoning (e.g., ScienceQA), Document Understanding (e.g., DocVQA), and Spatial/Compositional Reasoning (e.g., CLEVR). This breadth ensures agents learn strong generalization and tool-integration skills.
 - **Visual-Centric Tool Sets:** To compensate for the fine-grained perception and domain-specific routines often lacking in smaller open-source VLMs, VISTA-Gym provides a standardized and extensible API to 26 pre-defined tools. These tools are categorized into four families:
 1. **Perception:** Includes tools like GroundingDINO (open-set object detection), SAM (segmentation), and EasyOCR (text extraction).
 2. **Chart Understanding:** Incorporates specialized tools such as ChartMoE for converting charts to tables or SVG, captioning, and QA analysis.
 3. **Diagram Formalization:** Tools like CDL (for geometric diagrams) and Inter-GPS (for symbolic parsing) convert visual diagrams into formal, symbolic structures.
 4. **Math Solvers:** Features multimodal solvers like G-LLaVA and MultiMath for complex mathematical reasoning grounded in visual inputs.

- **Executable Interactive Environment:**
 - **Interface:** Adopts a Gymnasium-style API with `reset` and `step` functions, defining a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). `reset` initializes a task with an initial observation (question + image), and `step` processes an agent's action.
 - **Action Space:** Actions are formalized as typed tuples consisting of a tool's unique identifier and its arguments. The action space is strictly constrained by the available toolset.
 - **Observation Space:** After each tool execution, the environment provides an observation `o_t`, which includes either successful execution results or runtime error messages, serving as crucial feedback for the agent's subsequent reasoning.

- **Scalable Training Facility:**
 - **VLMs as Tools:** Compute-intensive VLMs (e.g., G-LLaVA, ChartMoE) are integrated as highly concurrent microservices via a three-layer architecture (FastAPI, Tool layer, Ray Actor layer) to ensure efficiency and minimize latency associated with model loading.
 - **Asynchronous Training:** Ray is utilized to orchestrate asynchronous concurrency during RL rollouts, managing batched HTTP requests to tool servers and load balancing, optimizing GPU utilization.
 - **Extensible Tool Set:** A generic `BaseTool` interface facilitates plug-and-play integration of new tools, and Ray's automated failure recovery ensures operational robustness.

#### 3.2. VISTA-R1: Bootstrapping Tool-Integrated Reasoning via RL for VLMs

VISTA-R1 employs a two-stage training framework to learn robust tool-augmented reasoning.

- **Stage I: Warmup with Imitation Learning (Behavioral Cloning - BC/SFT):**
 - The agent's policy is initialized by fine-tuning on synthesized expert trajectories.
 - **Candidate Generation and Filtering:** Proprietary models (GPT-5) are used to generate tool-executing trajectories, which are then filtered to retain only those matching ground truth answers.
 - **Rationale Densification:** Concise rationales from initial generations are replaced with extended, more detailed reasoning traces produced by an open-weights expert (Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking) to strengthen reasoning supervision.
 - **Objective:** The BC objective maximizes the likelihood of these interleaved thought-action tokens, providing a strong prior for tool syntax and selection.

- **Stage II: Online Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - After SFT, the agent is trained in the VISTA-Gym environment using multi-turn rollouts. Each step involves the agent emitting a reasoning segment (`<think>`) followed by a tool call (`<tool_call>`), which VISTA-Gym executes and provides feedback for.
 - **Policy Improvement:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is adopted. GRPO computes group-normalized advantages over `G` rollouts, where `r_i,k(θ)` is the token-level importance ratio and `b_A_i,k` is the normalized advantage across all tokens. This approach aims for low-variance, difficulty-adaptive credit assignment.

- **Reward Design:**
 - A multi-round interaction protocol is enforced: intermediate turns must generate reasoning with function calls, and the final turn must generate reasoning with a final answer.
 - **Repetition Penalty (R_rep):** A high-priority negative reward is assigned for contiguous token/phrase/character repeats, acting as a dominant negative signal.
 - **Format Reward (R_format):** Conditional on no repetition, a reward is given for structural well-formedness (e.g., correct XML tags, order, non-nested closure) at every turn.
 - **Correctness Reward (R_correct):** The final prediction is extracted, and a binary reward is given if it matches the ground truth, but only for repetition-free, well-formed outputs.
 - **Final Reward:** The total rollout-level reward is the sum `R(U) = R_rep(U) + R_format(U) + R_correct(U)`. This sparse, format-aware design encourages adherence to the `think→tool_call→answer` protocol and prevents exploitation of intermediate heuristics.

The overall approach combines the structured learning benefits of imitation learning with the interactive refinement power of reinforcement learning, all within a custom-built, scalable environment tailored for complex visual reasoning tasks requiring diverse tool use.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on 11 public reasoning-intensive VQA benchmarks (5 in-distribution and 6 out-of-distribution) yielded significant findings:

- **Superior Performance of VISTA-R1:** VISTA-R1 demonstrates strong performance across all benchmarks. Notably, VISTA-R1-8B, an 8-billion parameter model, significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines of comparable sizes by **9.51%–18.72%** (with tools) and **2.03%-11.24%** (even without tools). This highlights that the agentic training not only improves tool use but also enhances the base reasoning capabilities of the VLM.
- **Critical Role of Reinforcement Learning for TIR:** A key observation is that merely augmenting VLMs with tools *without explicit reasoning supervision* leads to a sharp accuracy drop. In contrast, the RL training within VISTA-Gym delivers substantial gains. This underscores that base VLM checkpoints inherently lack robust tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) abilities, and RL is crucial for unlocking these capabilities in visual reasoning contexts.
- **Strong Generalization Capabilities:** VISTA-R1 exhibits remarkable generalization to out-of-distribution tasks not used during training. For instance, VISTA-R1-8B achieves accuracy on these unseen benchmarks that is comparable to substantially larger proprietary models (e.g., GPT-o3 and Claude-4.5-Sonnet). This indicates that the VISTA-Gym training paradigm fosters transferable TIR policies.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** VISTA-R1 demonstrates impressive parameter efficiency. VISTA-R1-2B (a 2-billion parameter model) achieves competitive or even superior performance compared to 8B baselines. Similarly, VISTA-R1-8B performs comparably to baselines with 38B parameters, suggesting that the training framework can extract high performance from smaller models.

#### 4.1. Ablation Studies of VISTA-R1

The paper further dissects the contributions of different components through ablation studies:

- **Effect of Tool-Integrated Reasoning:** The full VISTA-R1, which synergistically couples reasoning with tool execution, significantly outperforms variants that either enable reasoning but disable tools (w/o Tools, 63.66% accuracy for InternVL3-8B) or expose tools without reinforcing reasoning (w/o Reasoning, 48.40%). This confirms that optimal problem-solving arises from an *interleaved loop* where reasoning guides tool invocation, rather than relying on either capability in isolation.
- **Effect of Training Stage:** Both the SFT warmup (+3.46% accuracy) and the subsequent online RL (+10.19% over SFT) stages are critical. SFT establishes behavioral priors for tool interaction, while RL enables deeper, interleaved reasoning for complex tasks.
- **Effect of RL Algorithm:** GRPO delivers the most robust performance compared to other preference-optimization algorithms like PPO and DAPO. GRPO's group-normalized advantages provide low-variance, difficulty-adaptive credit assignment, leading to more stable gains.
- **Effect of Reward Design:** The proposed sparse, format-aware reward design, which combines repetition penalty, format reward, and correctness reward, yields superior accuracy by encouraging adherence to the desired `think→tool_call→answer` protocol.
- **Effect of Tool and Reasoning in Vanilla VLMs (without RL training):** Directly augmenting vanilla VLMs with tools severely degrades accuracy, and intrinsic chain-of-thought offers only modest gains. Even with tool-selection priors, commercial VLMs show task-dependent gains, while smaller open-source models struggle, underscoring the necessity of dedicated agentic training.

#### 4.2. Additional Studies of VISTA-Gym

- **Data and Tool Diversity:** Multi-task and multi-tool training are crucial for generalization. Single-task training exhibits weak cross-task transfer, indicating that exposure to diverse tasks and heterogeneous tool schemas mitigates over-specialization and broadens the learned action policy.
- **Reasoning Trajectory Quality:** Longer and higher-quality expert trajectories used during SFT lead to better subsequent RL performance, highlighting the importance of rich initial behavioral priors.
- **Tail-Patch Curriculum:** Employing an easy-to-hard curriculum by focusing RL on "hard-but-learnable" instances (those with rollout pass rates in [0.125, 0.375]) further boosts performance, refining long-horizon reasoning at the frontier of the model's competence.

#### 4.3. Error Analysis and Case Studies

Error analysis showed that agentic training within VISTA-Gym markedly resolves the majority of tool-call and reasoning failures observed in base models. Human studies also confirm a significant improvement in the quality of generated interleaved tool-use and reasoning steps post-training. Case studies illustrate VISTA-R1's ability to perform schema-correct tool invocation, ground answers in tool outputs, generalize across tools (e.g., using a chart tool for geometry), and show robustness to imperfect tool outputs by identifying and exploiting partial signals.

In summary, the findings robustly confirm that VISTA-Gym is an effective and scalable training ground, enabling VLMs to acquire strong, generalizable tool-integrated reasoning capabilities that significantly surpass existing baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VISTA-Gym and the training of VISTA-R1 represent a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models, with broad implications and potential impact:

1. **Unlocking "Thinking with Images":** The most direct significance is the breakthrough in enabling VLMs to perform true "thinking with images." By providing a structured environment and a principled RL framework, this work moves VLMs beyond passive image understanding to active, interactive, and multi-step visual reasoning. This capability is crucial for solving complex, real-world problems that demand dynamic engagement with visual information.
2. **Democratizing Advanced Multimodal AI:** VISTA-R1 demonstrates that smaller open-source VLMs (e.g., 8B parameters) can achieve performance comparable to, or even exceeding, much larger proprietary models (e.g., 38B, or even some versions of GPT and Claude) on challenging reasoning tasks. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, making advanced tool-integrated multimodal AI more accessible and fostering innovation within the open-source community.
3. **Scalable and Generalizable Tool Integration:** VISTA-Gym provides a robust, scalable, and extensible infrastructure for training VLMs to effectively use a diverse set of external tools. This addresses a critical limitation of previous work, which often focused on narrow tasks or single tools. The ability to generalize tool-use across different tasks and tool types is a major step towards truly agentic VLMs that can adapt to novel situations.
4. **Robust Agentic Reinforcement Learning Framework:** The two-stage training (SFT then online RL with GRPO) and the carefully designed sparse, format-aware reward function provide a highly effective methodology for developing agentic VLMs. The ablation studies confirm the necessity of each component, offering valuable insights for future research in training complex AI agents. The discovery of GRPO's robustness and the benefits of a tail-patch curriculum further refine the RL training paradigm for VLMs.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** VISTA-Gym is provided as an open-source platform (code and data), serving as a crucial foundation for subsequent research. It enables reproducible experiments, fosters the development of new tool ecosystems, and allows for exploration of richer stepwise semantics and longer-horizon TIR. The detailed error analysis and case studies also provide valuable diagnostic tools for understanding VLM failures and guiding improvements.
6. **Broad Applicability Across Domains:** The diverse set of tasks integrated into VISTA-Gym (chart understanding, geometric reasoning, geospatial, scientific, document understanding) demonstrates the wide applicability of tool-integrated VLMs.
 - **Scientific and Medical Research:** The involvement of UT Southwestern Medical Center authors highlights the potential for this work in medical imaging analysis, scientific data interpretation, and diagnostic support, where precise visual reasoning and tool use (e.g., for detailed measurements, pattern recognition) are paramount.
 - **Data Analysis:** Enhanced chart and document understanding can revolutionize automated data analysis, business intelligence, and financial reporting.
 - **Robotics and Embodied AI:** While not the primary focus, the agentic nature and visual grounding could extend to robotics, enabling more intelligent decision-making based on complex visual inputs and tool interactions.
 - **Education:** Solving geometry and math problems with visual aids could improve educational tools.

In essence, this research propels VLMs into a new era of interactive and intelligent visual problem-solving, equipping them with the crucial ability to "think with images" in a scalable and generalizable manner, promising significant impact across various scientific and industrial applications.
