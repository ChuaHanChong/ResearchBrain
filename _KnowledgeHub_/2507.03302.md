# Leveraging Out-of-Distribution Unlabeled Images: Semi-Supervised Semantic Segmentation with an Open-Vocabulary Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, SemiOVS, allows semi-supervised semantic segmentation models to utilize abundant out-of-distribution unlabeled images by employing an open-vocabulary model for robust pseudo-label generation. The approach achieved state-of-the-art performance on Pascal VOC, with up to a 12.6% mIoU improvement over FixMatch in low-label settings, and consistently enhanced results on Pascal Context.

## Problem

- Semantic segmentation requires extensive pixel-level annotations, which are costly and time-consuming to acquire.
- Current semi-supervised semantic segmentation (SSSS) approaches mainly utilize unlabeled data assumed to be in-distribution, limiting their applicability to real-world scenarios with diverse data sources.
- Naively incorporating abundant out-of-distribution (OOD) unlabeled images can lead to noisy pseudo-labels, misguiding model training and degrading performance due to distribution shifts.

## Method

- A framework called ==SemiOVS== is introduced, integrating a standard semi-supervised segmentation model with a pretrained ==open-vocabulary segmentation (OVS) model==.
- The OVS model is strategically deployed to generate ==pseudo-labels== for unlabeled out-of-distribution (OOD) images, leveraging its strong generalization capabilities.
- OVS pseudo-label generation is optimized with an extended text prompt set (including potential OOD classes) and a label space refinement process.

## Results

- SemiOVS established new state-of-the-art performance on Pascal VOC, demonstrating a **12.6%** mIoU improvement over FixMatch and a **3.0** mIoU gain over SemiVL in low-label settings (e.g., 92 labeled images).
- The framework consistently delivered substantial performance enhancements on the more complex Pascal Context dataset, showing gains of **6.**6%** to 10.2%** in the 1/32 protocol, and achieved SOTA on Pascal VOC Blended and Priority protocols.
- Ablation studies confirmed that leveraging an OVS model for OOD pseudo-labeling consistently improved performance across all label settings, contrasting with the detrimental effects of directly using standard semi-supervised models for OOD data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abundant out-of-distribution (OOD) unlabeled images, often neglected, can significantly improve semi-supervised semantic segmentation when effectively utilized.
> - Open-vocabulary segmentation (OVS) models possess the generalization capacity to generate robust and accurate pseudo-labels for OOD images, overcoming limitations of standard segmentation models.
> - Optimizing OVS pseudo-label generation through extended text prompts and label space refinement is critical for successfully incorporating OOD data into semi-supervised learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the rapidly evolving field of **semi-supervised semantic segmentation (SSSS)**, a crucial area of computer vision aimed at reducing the prohibitive cost and time associated with obtaining dense pixel-level annotations for training segmentation models. Traditional supervised semantic segmentation models require vast amounts of meticulously labeled data, which is a significant bottleneck for real-world applications. SSSS mitigates this by leveraging a small set of labeled images alongside a larger pool of unlabeled images.

The existing SSSS landscape has seen significant progress, with prominent paradigms like **consistency regularization** (e.g., Mean Teacher, FixMatch, UniMatch) and **self-training/pseudo-labeling** techniques. These methods have demonstrated impressive results in academic settings, typically by using carefully controlled splits of benchmark datasets where the unlabeled images are assumed to be "in-distribution" (i.e., sharing similar statistical properties with the labeled data).

However, this paper identifies a critical, yet largely unexplored, gap: the effective utilization of **out-of-distribution (OOD) unlabeled images**. In real-world scenarios, abundant unlabeled data can be readily acquired from diverse sources like web scraping or large-scale public datasets (e.g., COCO). While vast, this data often exhibits distribution shifts compared to the target labeled dataset due to variations in image quality, resolution, object poses, lighting, and background complexity. Naively applying existing SSSS methods to such OOD data can be detrimental, as inaccurate pseudo-labels generated from OOD samples can misguide network training and degrade performance, a phenomenon illustrated effectively in Figure 1 of the paper (e.g., misclassifying a giraffe as a horse).

Concurrently, the field of **open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVS)** has emerged, driven by the success of large-scale vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP. OVS models possess strong generalization capabilities, enabling them to segment objects using arbitrary text queries, often beyond predefined categories. Recent SSSS methods, such as SemiVL, have begun to explore the integration of VLMs to enhance segmentation performance by providing richer semantic guidance.

This work specifically *bridges the gap* between traditional SSSS, which struggles with OOD data, and the generalization power of OVS models. It proposes a novel SSSS framework (SemiOVS) that leverages the open-vocabulary capabilities of OVS models *specifically for pseudo-labeling OOD unlabeled images*. Unlike prior SSSS studies that either focus solely on in-distribution unlabeled data or integrate VLMs more broadly into the SSSS process, SemiOVS strategically deploys an OVS model to address the unique challenges posed by OOD data. This targeted approach represents a significant advancement by making a vast, previously underutilized, resource (OOD unlabeled images) accessible and beneficial for SSSS, thereby pushing the boundaries towards more robust and practical segmentation solutions for real-world applications. The paper clearly states its distinct contribution by noting that previous research has not "addressed leveraging additional unlabeled images," particularly OOD ones.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from a fundamental challenge in computer vision: the high cost and difficulty of acquiring pixel-level ground-truth annotations for semantic segmentation. While supervised learning has achieved remarkable success, its reliance on extensive labeled datasets limits its applicability in many real-world settings where annotation is prohibitively expensive or time-consuming. Semi-supervised learning offers a viable alternative by leveraging abundant unlabeled data.

However, the authors identify a critical limitation in existing semi-supervised semantic segmentation (SSSS) research:
1. **Limited Scope of Unlabeled Data:** Most SSSS studies have focused on controlled academic settings using unlabeled images drawn from the *same distribution* as the labeled data. This fails to reflect real-world scenarios where unlabeled images are often abundant but originate from *diverse, out-of-distribution (OOD) sources* (e.g., web-scraped images, large public datasets like COCO).
2. **Challenges with OOD Data:** Directly using OOD unlabeled images in standard SSSS frameworks can be detrimental. Distribution shifts can lead to inaccurate pseudo-labels, misguiding the training process, and potentially degrading model performance. The paper graphically illustrates this issue, showing a standard segmentation model misclassifying a giraffe as a horse or a light rail as a bus due to its unfamiliarity with OOD visual concepts.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of this study are:
1. **To Investigate the Efficacy of Large-Scale Unlabeled OOD Images:** The primary objective is to test the hypothesis that incorporating a significantly larger set of unlabeled images, even if OOD, can improve the performance of semi-supervised learners, offering a more practical solution for real-world applications.
2. **To Propose a Novel Framework (SemiOVS) for OOD Data Utilization:** Develop a new semi-supervised semantic segmentation framework that can effectively leverage unlabeled OOD images by mitigating the challenges of distribution shift.
3. **To Integrate Open-Vocabulary Segmentation (OVS) Models:** Specifically, to utilize the strong generalization capabilities of pretrained OVS models to generate reliable pseudo-labels for OOD images, thereby providing accurate supervision signals to the standard segmentation model.
4. **To Enhance Pseudo-labeling for OOD Data:** Introduce specific enhancements to the OVS process—an extended text prompt set including potential OOD classes and a refinement process to align OVS predictions with the target label space—to maximize the accuracy and utility of OOD pseudo-labels.
5. **To Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** Demonstrate that the proposed SemiOVS framework, when integrated with various SSSS baselines, can achieve superior performance across different evaluation protocols and datasets, especially in low-label settings, without incurring additional computational costs during inference.
6. **To Offer a Practical and Compatible Solution:** Provide a flexible framework that is compatible with existing SSSS methods, enabling easy integration and practical deployment in diverse applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **SemiOVS (Semi-Supervised Semantic Segmentation with an Open-Vocabulary Model)**, a novel framework designed to effectively utilize unlabeled out-of-distribution (OOD) images in semi-supervised semantic segmentation. The core idea is to combine a standard semi-supervised segmentation model (referred to as *f*) with a pretrained open-vocabulary segmentation (OVS) model (referred to as *g*).

The overall objective function is structured into a supervised loss (L_s) and an unsupervised loss (L_u), where L_u is further decomposed into an in-distribution flow (L_u(in)) and an OOD flow (L_u(out)), weighted by a hyperparameter λ.

1. **Supervised Flow (L_s):** This component is standard, applying a pixel-level cross-entropy loss between the predictions of the segmentation model *f* on labeled images (x_l) and their corresponding ground-truth annotations (y_l).

2. **Unsupervised In-Distribution Flow (L_u(in)):** For unlabeled images (x_u_in) that are presumed to be in-distribution, the framework adopts the widely used weak-to-strong consistency regularization paradigm (inspired by FixMatch). The segmentation model *f* generates pseudo-labels from weakly augmented versions of the images (x_w_in). These pseudo-labels then supervise the predictions of strongly augmented versions (x_s_in) of the same images. A confidence threshold (τ) is used to filter out unreliable pseudo-labels.

3. **Unsupervised Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Flow (L_u(out)):** This is the central contribution of SemiOVS. This flow specifically targets unlabeled OOD images (x_u_out), sourced from web scraping or large datasets like COCO.
 - **Dual-Model Pseudo-labeling:** Instead of relying on the standard segmentation model *f* (which struggles with OOD data) for pseudo-labeling, the weakly augmented OOD images (x_w_out) are processed by the *pretrained OVS model g*.
 - **OVS Model (g) Details:** The OVS model consists of an image encoder (g_v) and a text encoder (g_t).
 - The image encoder (g_v) extracts visual embeddings from x_w_out.
 - The text encoder (g_t) processes a *text prompt set T* to generate text embeddings. This prompt set is crucial for the open-vocabulary nature of the model.
 - Pixel-level similarity is computed between image and text embeddings, forming a cost volume.
 - A decoder (g_d) then refines this into high-resolution segmentation predictions (p_w_out), from which initial pseudo-labels (y_w_out,init) are derived using argmax.
 - **Key Enhancements for OOD Pseudo-labeling:** The authors introduce two critical strategies to optimize OVS-generated pseudo-labels for the semi-supervised task:
 1. **Extended Text Prompt Set:** The text prompt set *T* is not limited to the N_in predefined classes of the target dataset (e.g., Pascal VOC's 20 classes + background). Instead, it is *extended* to include additional categories (N > N_in) that are likely to appear in the OOD images (e.g., all 171 classes from COCO-Stuff). This prevents OOD objects (like a "giraffe" not in Pascal VOC) from being forced into a similar in-distribution category (like "horse") by the OVS model.
 2. **Refinement Process:** After the initial pseudo-labels are generated, any pixel assigned to a class *not* part of the target N_in classes is re-labeled as "background." This aligns the OVS's broad predictions with the specific label space required for the semi-supervised segmentation task.
 - **Supervision:** The pseudo-labels (y_w_out) from the OVS model then supervise the predictions (p_s_out) of the standard segmentation model *f* on the strongly augmented OOD images (x_s_out). A confidence threshold (τ) is also applied here.

**Implementation Details:**
- The **SED (Simple Encoder-Decoder)** framework, known for its efficiency and performance, is employed for the OVS model.
- **Multiple prompt templates** (e.g., "a photo of a {class}", "a bright photo of a {class}") are used to enrich text representations.
- A **class concept ensemble strategy** (inspired by SemiVL) averages embeddings from multiple semantic descriptions for each category (e.g., "dining table", "table for eating at") to provide richer class information.
- **Offline pseudo-label generation** for OOD images is utilized to improve training efficiency.
- **Datasets:** Pascal VOC 2012 (Original, Blended, Priority protocols) and Pascal Context are used as target datasets. OOD images are sourced from web-scraped data (approx. 32k images) and COCO-Stuff (approx. 95k-118k images, in strict and filtered settings).
- **Baselines:** FixMatch, UniMatch, PrevMatch (using ResNet-50/101 with DeepLabV3+), and SemiVL (using ViT-B/16).
- **Evaluation Metric:** Mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU).

This methodology elegantly addresses the challenges of OOD data by leveraging the generalization power of OVS models in a targeted and refined manner, effectively making a vast external data source valuable for semi-supervised learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation of SemiOVS across Pascal VOC and Pascal Context datasets consistently demonstrates its effectiveness and superiority.

1. **Efficacy of Leveraging Additional Unlabeled Images:** The initial findings (Table 1) confirm the hypothesis that using additional unlabeled images, even OOD ones, can significantly improve the performance of semi-supervised learners, particularly in low-label settings. For instance, UniMatch with ResNet-50 shows improvements of +6.3 mIoU and +6.2 mIoU in the 92 and 183 labeled image settings, respectively, when augmented with COCO-Stuff-Filtered images. This highlights the potential of abundant real-world data if handled appropriately.

2. **Superiority of OVS for OOD Pseudo-labeling:** A crucial ablation study (Table 6) compares the proposed SemiOVS (using an OVS model for OOD pseudo-labeling) against a baseline where the *standard semi-supervised segmentation model* (Semi-Seg Model) attempts to pseudo-label OOD images.
 - The Semi-Seg Model with OOD data shows initial gains in low-label settings (+2.8 mIoU for 92 labels) but *degrades* performance in higher-label settings (-0.7 and -2.0 mIoU for 732 and 1464 labels, respectively). This confirms that naive use of OOD data can be detrimental due to distribution shifts and noisy pseudo-labels when the core model is not equipped to handle them.
 - In contrast, SemiOVS consistently delivers *substantial performance gains across all label settings*, including higher-label scenarios (e.g., +5.2 mIoU for 92 labels, +0.6 mIoU for 1464 labels). This decisively proves that the OVS model effectively mitigates the problems of OOD data, generating more accurate and reliable pseudo-labels that consistently benefit training.

3. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** SemiOVS consistently achieves state-of-the-art results across various evaluation protocols and baselines:
 - **Pascal VOC (Original Protocol):** SemiOVS outperforms existing SOTA methods by a large margin. For example, SemiOVS (w/ PrevMatch, R-101) achieves +3.5 mIoU over PrevMatch in the 92-label setting, and SemiOVS (w/ SemiVL, ViT-B/16) achieves +3.0 mIoU over SemiVL in the same setting, establishing new SOTA. When integrated with FixMatch (R-50), SemiOVS shows a remarkable 12.6% improvement for 92 labels (Table 2).
 - **Pascal VOC (Blended and Priority Protocols):** The proposed method also significantly improves performance and achieves SOTA results in these protocols (Tables 3 and 4).
 - **Pascal Context Dataset:** On this more complex dataset with many categories, SemiOVS yields substantial improvements over baselines, with gains ranging from 6.6% to 10.2% in the 1/32 protocol (Table 5).

4. **Impact of OOD Data Sources:** Among the tested OOD datasets, COCO-Stuff-Filtered generally provides the best overall performance gains, suggesting that a degree of relevance or filtering of OOD data can be beneficial. Web-scraped images also improve performance but to a lesser extent, possibly due to higher noise and diversity (Table 1).

5. **Ablation Studies Confirm Component Effectiveness:**
 - **Class Concept Ensemble:** Using multiple semantic concepts for each category (e.g., "dining table", "table for eating at") for the OVS text encoder further enhances performance compared to single class names (Table 7).
 - **Quantity of OOD Images:** More unlabeled OOD images generally lead to better performance, although significant gains are observed even with relatively smaller amounts (Table 8).
 - **OVS Model Efficiency:** SemiOVS achieves higher performance with significantly fewer parameters compared to the large OVS model used for pseudo-label generation itself, demonstrating efficient knowledge transfer (Table 9).
 - **Confidence Threshold (τ):** A wide range of thresholds (0.0 to 0.5) for OOD pseudo-labels yields good performance, outperforming the baseline. Higher thresholds can reduce performance by overly discarding diverse OOD information (Table 10).
 - **Loss Weight (λ):** Moderate values (0.5 to 2.0) for the OOD flow loss weight provide a robust balance, with extreme values leading to diminished returns (Table 11).
 - **Text Prompt Set Construction:** The *extended text prompt set with refinement* is crucial. Using only in-distribution classes for OVS text prompts leads to performance degradation, while a comprehensive prompt set significantly boosts results, preventing misclassification of true OOD objects (Table 12 and Fig. 5).

6. **Dataset Characteristics and Distribution Analysis:** UMAP visualizations and pixel frequency distributions (Figs. 6 and 7) illustrate both the overlap and distinct differences between Pascal VOC, COCO, and web-scraped images. This analysis underscores why traditional pixel-level pseudo-labeling struggles with OOD data and highlights the necessity of the OVS model to bridge these distributional gaps accurately.

In summary, the main findings unequivocally establish that OOD unlabeled images are a valuable resource for semi-supervised semantic segmentation, provided they are effectively processed by an OVS model with appropriate prompt engineering and pseudo-label refinement. SemiOVS not only outperforms prior SOTA methods but also offers a robust and practical solution for real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of "Leveraging Out-of-Distribution Unlabeled Images: Semi-Supervised Semantic Segmentation with an Open-Vocabulary Model" are substantial, addressing critical bottlenecks in real-world computer vision applications.

1. **Bridging the Reality Gap in Semi-Supervised Learning:** The most profound impact of this work is its direct assault on the disparity between academic semi-supervised learning (SSL) settings and real-world data availability. By demonstrating how to effectively utilize readily available, albeit out-of-distribution (OOD), unlabeled data (like web-scraped images or large public datasets), the paper liberates SSL from the restrictive assumption of strictly in-distribution unlabeled data. This makes semi-supervised semantic segmentation a far more practical and deployable solution in scenarios where obtaining new, perfectly matched labeled data is infeasible or too costly.

2. **Unlocking Vast Data Resources:** The approach effectively unlocks the immense potential of online and large-scale public image repositories as valuable training resources. Instead of discarding OOD data or relying on expensive domain adaptation techniques, SemiOVS provides a method to robustly integrate this data, fundamentally expanding the pool of usable information for model training.

3. **Advancing State-of-the-Art in SSSS:** By achieving new state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmark datasets and protocols, particularly in low-label settings, SemiOVS pushes the technical boundaries of semi-supervised semantic segmentation. The significant gains (e.g., +3.0 to +12.6 mIoU over baselines) indicate a major leap forward in model accuracy and robustness.

4. **Intelligent Integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The paper showcases an intelligent and efficient way to leverage the powerful generalization capabilities of pretrained open-vocabulary segmentation (OVS) models. Rather than replacing existing SSSS architectures, SemiOVS uses the OVS model as a "smart pseudo-labeler" specifically for OOD data. This strategic deployment allows the benefits of large VLMs to be reaped without incurring their high computational costs during inference for the final segmentation task. This is a crucial distinction and a practical advantage for deployment.

5. **Enhanced Model Generalizability:** By exposing the segmentation model to a wider diversity of visual concepts and contexts through OOD images, the framework likely improves the model's generalizability beyond the strict confines of the labeled dataset. This makes the trained models more robust to variations and unexpected scenarios in deployment.

6. **Compatibility and Modularity:** The framework's compatibility with existing semi-supervised segmentation methods (FixMatch, UniMatch, PrevMatch, SemiVL) is a significant advantage. This modularity means researchers and practitioners can readily integrate SemiOVS components into their preferred SSSS pipelines, extending its potential impact across a broad range of applications without requiring a complete overhaul of existing systems.

7. **Inspiration for Future Research:** The work opens several promising avenues for future exploration:
 - **Domain-Specific Applications:** Extending the framework to diverse real-world domains like industrial defect segmentation, medical imaging, or specialized content analysis, where labeled data is extremely scarce and domain-specific pretrained models are emerging.
 - **Smarter OOD Sample Selection:** Developing advanced strategies to filter or select the most beneficial OOD images, potentially removing irrelevant or overly noisy samples to maximize gains.
 - **Dynamic OOD Handling:** Investigating adaptive weighting or confidence thresholding mechanisms for OOD pseudo-labels during training.
 - **Deeper VLM Integration:** Exploring more complex ways to integrate VLM capabilities beyond just pseudo-labeling, perhaps for feature distillation or cross-modal consistency.

In conclusion, SemiOVS represents a significant step towards practical and robust semantic segmentation models, addressing a core limitation of previous semi-supervised approaches. Its ability to effectively leverage abundant, yet challenging, OOD unlabeled data, combined with its state-of-the-art performance and compatibility, positions it to have a lasting impact on real-world applications of computer vision.
