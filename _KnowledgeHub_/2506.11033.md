# Adaptive Shielding for Safe Reinforcement Learning under Hidden-Parameter Dynamics Shifts

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Virginia and The University of Texas at Austin developed an Adaptive Shielding framework that enables safe reinforcement learning in environments with varying, unobserved dynamics by integrating proactive safety-regularized policy optimization with an online adaptive shield. This approach demonstrated consistent reductions in safety violations and strong generalization to out-of-distribution dynamics while maintaining task performance and incurring modest computational overhead.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents face safety risks and generalization challenges when environmental dynamics shift due to unobserved parameters (e.g., friction, mass).
- Existing adaptive RL methods primarily focus on generalization without explicit safety mechanisms during adaptation.
- Traditional safe RL approaches often assume stationary dynamics, making them unsuitable for online adaptation to dynamic changes.

## Method

- The framework combines ==Safety-Regularized Optimization (SRO)== for proactive, training-time safety with an ==adaptive shielding== mechanism for reactive, execution-time safety.
- A ==function encoder== infers a low-dimensional representation of hidden dynamics online from observed transitions, updating both the policy and the shield.
- The adaptive shield uses uncertainty-aware ==Adaptive Conformal Prediction (ACP)== to compute dynamic safety margins and filter unsafe actions.

## Results

- The combined SRO + Shield method achieved lower cost rates with competitive returns across various tasks, outperforming baseline safe RL algorithms.
- It demonstrated robust generalization, maintaining a favorable balance of high return and low cost rates in out-of-distribution environments with unseen dynamics.
- The framework introduced only a modest increase in execution time (e.g., **3.94s** for RCPO vs. **5.05s** for Ours in Car-Goal) with moderate shielding trigger rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proactive safety during training (SRO) and reactive shielding during execution are complementary, leading to more robust safety and performance.
> - Online inference of hidden dynamics using function encoders provides efficient adaptation to unseen environmental changes without full policy retraining.
> - Incorporating uncertainty quantification via Adaptive Conformal Prediction is crucial for providing probabilistic safety guarantees in dynamically changing environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous systems operating in real-world environments face challenges from unseen shifts in dynamics, such as changes in friction, mass, or gravity, which can impact safety and generalization. This work addresses a gap at the intersection of two established research directions: adaptive reinforcement learning (RL) and safe RL.

Adaptive methods, including hypernetworks, contextual policies, and mixtures-of-experts, have enabled RL agents to adjust to varying environmental dynamics by inferring latent contexts. These methods primarily focus on enhancing generalization capabilities without explicitly providing mechanisms for enforcing safety during the adaptation process.

Conversely, safe RL frameworks, often utilizing constrained Markov decision processes (CMDPs), aim to ensure safety by imposing limits on cumulative costs. Techniques like state augmentation and shielding are used to enhance safety in various RL algorithms. However, a common limitation of these safe RL approaches is the assumption of stationary dynamics, meaning they are not typically designed for online adaptation under shifts in hidden parameters.

This research integrates adaptive mechanisms with safety guarantees, specifically by adapting a shielding framework to online variations in hidden dynamics. It aims to bridge the gap where adaptive RL lacks explicit safety mechanisms and traditional safe RL assumes stationary dynamics, by proposing a solution that maintains safety during online adaptation to unseen dynamic shifts. The use of function encoders for dynamics inference and conformal prediction for uncertainty-aware safety margins positions this work within advanced model-based and uncertainty-quantification techniques in safe and adaptive RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework for safe reinforcement learning in environments where underlying dynamics vary due to unobserved, hidden parameters (e.g., mass, friction, gravity). These variations, which can change across episodes, introduce safety risks and challenge the generalization capabilities of conventional RL systems.

The specific objectives are:
1. **Maximize Expected Cumulative Discounted Reward:** The agent seeks to learn a policy that achieves high task performance, measured by the expected sum of discounted rewards.
2. **Satisfy Safety Constraints on Average Cost Rate:** Concurrently, the policy must adhere to safety constraints, which are formalized as minimizing the average cost rate. This means keeping the long-term frequency of safety violations below a predefined threshold (δ).
3. **Adapt Online to Hidden Parameter Shifts:** The framework must enable the RL agent to infer changes in environmental dynamics online from observed transition data, without requiring prior knowledge of these hidden parameters. This adaptation should apply to both the policy and the safety mechanism.
4. **Provide Uncertainty-Aware Safety Guarantees:** The safety mechanism should incorporate uncertainty quantification to ensure conservative behavior, especially under prediction errors or distribution shifts in dynamics.

The motivation for this work stems from the critical need for robust and safe autonomous systems in real-world applications such as robotics and autonomous driving. In these domains, unforeseen changes in operating conditions can lead to severe consequences. Existing adaptive RL methods often overlook safety during adaptation, while safe RL methods typically assume static environments. The researchers aim to address this limitation by creating a unified framework that allows RL agents to continuously learn and adapt to dynamic changes safely, thereby enhancing the reliability and applicability of RL in safety-critical settings. The choice of function encoders for dynamics inference is motivated by their capacity for compact, expressive, and efficient online representation of varying functions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Adaptive Shielding framework integrates two complementary safety mechanisms that operate during both training and execution, along with a method for online dynamics inference.

#### 3.1. Safety-Regularized Optimization (SRO)

During policy training, a proactive mechanism called Safety-Regularized Optimization (SRO) is introduced. This method modifies the standard reward maximization objective to encourage the policy to learn behavior that leads to low long-term costs.
- **Cost-Sensitive Value Estimate:** The core of SRO is a safety measure, $Q^\pi_{\text{safe}}(s, a, b_\phi)$, which is designed to provide a local cost-sensitivity score for an action. This score is defined as the negative ratio of the expected cumulative cost in a local neighborhood of action $a$ (weighted by the policy's local action density) to the state-value function for cost, $V^\pi_C(s, b_\phi)$, plus a small constant $\epsilon$. Higher values of $Q^\pi_{\text{safe}}$ (closer to 0) indicate actions associated with lower long-term costs. The design bounds $Q^\pi_{\text{safe}}$ in $(-1, 0]$.
- **Augmented Objective:** This safety measure is integrated into the policy optimization by defining an augmented action-value function: $Q^\pi_{\text{aug}}(s, a, b_\phi) = Q^\pi_R(s, a, b_\phi) + \alpha Q^\pi_{\text{safe}}(s, a, b_\phi)$. Here, $Q^\pi_R$ is the reward action-value function, and $\alpha \ge 0$ is a hyperparameter balancing reward and safety. The policy is updated by maximizing the expected value of $Q^\pi_{\text{aug}}$.
- **Theoretical Implication:** Proposition 1 states that within the set of zero-violation policies (where $J_C(\pi)=0$), maximizing the augmented objective $J_{\text{aug}}(\pi)$ is equivalent to maximizing the standard reward objective $J_R(\pi)$. This indicates that SRO does not degrade performance if a policy is already safe. Furthermore, SRO is shown to preserve the monotonic improvement and worst-case constraint bounds of algorithms like TRPO and CPO.

#### 3.2. Online Hidden-Parameter Adaptation

To enable adaptation to unseen dynamics, the framework uses a function encoder (FE) to infer a low-dimensional representation of the underlying dynamics ($T_\phi$) online.
- **Function Encoder Role:** A function encoder, $\hat{f}_{\text{FE}}$, approximates environment dynamics by projecting them onto a set of pre-trained neural basis functions, $g_i(s_t, a_t)$. The prediction of the next state $\hat{s}_{t+1}$ is given by $\sum_{i=1}^k b_i \cdot g_i(s_t, a_t)$, where $b_i$ are coefficients.
- **Online Coefficient Inference:** These coefficients ($b_1, \ldots, b_k$), which serve as a low-dimensional representation $b_\phi$ of the dynamics $T_\phi$, are derived from observed transition samples through a least-squares optimization problem. As the agent interacts with the environment and collects new transitions, these coefficients are refined online, providing an updated representation of the dynamics without retraining the policy. This representation $b_\phi$ is concatenated with the state to serve as input to the policy.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Shielding Mechanism

At execution time, a reactive adaptive shielding mechanism filters unsafe actions based on predictions and quantified uncertainties. This mechanism operates in five steps:
1. **Pre-Safety Check:** Before full safety verification, a pre-safety indicator $\nu(e(s_t), E_t)$ is evaluated. If $\nu(e(s_t), E_t) > L_\nu \Delta_{\max}$ (where $\Delta_{\max}$ is a bound on per-step feature change and $L_\nu$ is a Lipschitz constant), the policy's action is executed directly to improve computational efficiency. Otherwise, full safety verification is triggered.
2. **Action Generation:** The policy $\pi$ generates $N$ candidate actions by sampling from its distribution $\pi(\cdot | s_t, b_\phi)$.
3. **Transition Prediction:** For each candidate action, the function encoder $\hat{f}_{\text{FE}}$ predicts the next state $\hat{s}^{(i)}_{t+1}$.
4. **Safety Verification (Uncertainty-Aware):** Adaptive Conformal Prediction (ACP) is used to compute uncertainty-aware safety margins. A SafetyScore for each action is calculated as $\nu(e(\hat{s}^{(i)}_{t+1}), \hat{E}_{t+1}) - 2L_\nu \Gamma_t$, where $\Gamma_t$ is the adaptive conformal prediction bound for next-state prediction error, calibrated for a user-specified failure probability $\delta$. Positive scores indicate safety compliance.
5. **Action Selection:** A safe action set $\hat{A}_{\text{safe}}(s_t)$ is defined as actions with positive SafetyScores. If this set is non-empty, an action is chosen uniformly from the top $k$ safe actions ranked by their scores. If the set is empty, the action with the highest (but still negative) safety score is chosen.
- **Theoretical Guarantee:** Theorem 1 characterizes the connection between prediction errors in the learned dynamics model and a bound on the average cost rate. It states that for a policy augmented with the adaptive shield, the average cost rate $\xi^\pi(s, \phi)$ is bounded by $\delta + \bar{\epsilon}(1-\delta)$, where $\delta$ is the ACP failure probability and $\bar{\epsilon}$ is a term related to the probability of the safe action set being empty. If safe actions always exist, $\bar{\epsilon}=0$, leading to $\xi^\pi(s, \phi) \leq \delta$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation was conducted on the Safe-Gym benchmark using Point and Car robots across four tasks (Goal, Button, Push, Circle). The approach was compared against six safe RL baselines, and three variants of the proposed method were analyzed: Safety-Regularized Optimization (SRO) only, Adaptive Shield only, and their combination (SRO + Shield).

**RQ1: Trade-offs Between Safety and Task Performance During Training**
- **Baseline Performance:** Traditional safe RL baselines exhibited varied performance. PPO-Lag often achieved high returns but incurred higher cost rates. CPO, PPO-Saute, and RCPO-Saute enforced strict safety but frequently sacrificed reward. USL underperformed across tasks, and CUP maintained task performance but violated strict cost limits. RCPO showed a reasonable balance but struggled with very low cost thresholds.
- **Proposed Method Performance:** The combined SRO + Shield method consistently achieved lower cost rates while maintaining competitive returns. Both SRO-only and Shield-only variants also reduced cost violations compared to baselines but were less effective than the combined approach. This suggests a complementary effect between proactive training-time safety (SRO) and reactive execution-time safety (Shield).

**RQ2: Generalization to Out-of-Distribution Environments**
- **OOD Setup:** Evaluation was performed in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios with unseen hidden parameters (gravity, damping, mass, inertia, friction sampled from disjoint ranges) and increased obstacle numbers.
- **Proposed Method Generalization:** The combined SRO + Shield method consistently achieved a favorable balance of high return and low cost rate across all tasks in OOD environments. This indicated robust adaptation to previously unseen dynamics. SRO-only and Shield-only variants also performed well, but the combined method demonstrated greater stability and a more optimal trade-off. The shield's logic, filtering unsafe actions based on predicted states, contributed to its stability in OOD conditions.
- **Baseline Generalization:** Baselines like CPO and Saute maintained low cost rates but at the expense of significantly reduced returns. Other algorithms displayed high variance and sensitivity to dynamics shifts, failing to maintain consistent safety-performance trade-offs in OOD settings. The paper also noted that even oracle-informed baselines (provided with ground-truth hidden parameters) showed performance degradation under varying parameters, highlighting the challenge of dynamic environments.

**RQ3: Execution-Time Overhead**
- **Overhead Measurement:** The average runtime per episode was compared between the baseline RCPO and the combined SRO + Shield method.
- **Results:** The proposed method introduced a modest increase in execution time relative to the base policy (e.g., Car-Goal: 3.94s for RCPO vs. 5.05s for Ours; Point-Goal: 3.81s for RCPO vs. 4.32s for Ours). The shielding trigger rate remained moderate (e.g., Car-Goal: 18.10%, Point-Button: 29.07%), indicating selective intervention rather than constant overhead.

**Ablation Studies and Additional Findings (Summarized from Appendix):**
- **Compatibility:** The adaptive shielding mechanism demonstrated improved safety when combined with other base RL algorithms like PPO-Lag, indicating its general applicability.
- **Dynamics Representation:** Function Encoders provided stable dynamics representations and achieved next-state prediction accuracy comparable to oracle-informed predictors under distribution shifts, outperforming a naive Transformer-based alternative in terms of stability and effectiveness when integrated with the shield.
- **Higher-Dimensional Control:** The approach showed promising performance in the HalfCheetah-Velocity task with multiple hidden parameters and tightened safety constraints, suggesting scalability to more complex robot dynamics.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The safety bonus $\alpha$ in SRO influenced the reward-safety trade-off, with higher values generally improving safety at a controlled impact on reward, consistent with theoretical bounds. Sampling size for the shield showed no consistent pattern, implying that prediction errors and inherent randomness are key challenges.
- **Q_safe vs. Q_C:** The normalized Q_safe term was preferred over direct use of Q_C due to its bounded nature, which simplifies hyperparameter tuning and leads to more stable optimization compared to Lagrangian methods sensitive to learning rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a framework that directly addresses a critical challenge in reinforcement learning: maintaining safety in autonomous systems when environment dynamics are unknown and shifting. The proposed Adaptive Shielding framework, by integrating Safety-Regularized Optimization with an online adaptive shield, offers a notable contribution to the field of safe and generalizable RL.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Integrated Safety and Adaptability:** The framework successfully combines proactive training-time safety measures with reactive execution-time shielding, enabling safe operation in dynamic environments where previous methods often struggled due to assumptions of stationary dynamics or lack of explicit safety mechanisms during adaptation.
- **Online Adaptation with Function Encoders:** The utilization of function encoders for online inference of hidden dynamics represents an efficient and effective approach to continuously update the agent's understanding of its environment. This allows the policy and shield to adapt rapidly without extensive retraining, crucial for real-world deployment.
- **Uncertainty-Aware Safety Guarantees:** The incorporation of Adaptive Conformal Prediction (ACP) into the shielding mechanism provides rigorous, uncertainty-aware safety margins. This probabilistic guarantee is particularly important for safety-critical applications, as it allows for conservative action filtering based on model prediction confidence.
- **Theoretical Grounding:** The theoretical analysis connecting dynamics prediction errors to bounds on the average cost rate provides a formal basis for the safety guarantees, offering insights into the reliability of the shielded policy.
- **Empirical Validation:** Extensive evaluation on standard benchmarks, including out-of-distribution scenarios, demonstrates that the approach consistently reduces safety violations while maintaining competitive task performance and incurring only modest computational overhead. This empirical evidence supports the practical viability of the framework.

**Potential Impact:**
The proposed Adaptive Shielding framework has the potential to enhance the reliability and trustworthiness of AI-powered autonomous systems in domains such as robotics, autonomous driving, and industrial automation. By ensuring safety under varying and unforeseen environmental dynamics, it could facilitate the broader adoption of advanced RL techniques in safety-critical applications, moving towards more robust and generalizable intelligent agents.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations. The safety guarantees rely on specific assumptions about the cost function's structure. The method's dependence on an offline dataset for pre-training the function encoder may limit its applicability in scenarios without prior data. Furthermore, the evaluation has been confined to simulated environments. Future work will aim to relax these modeling assumptions, reduce reliance on offline data, and extend evaluations to physical robotic platforms to assess scalability and real-world applicability. These steps are crucial for translating the theoretical and simulated benefits into practical, deployable solutions.
