# MotionCtrl: A Unified and Flexible Motion Controller for Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> MotionCtrl introduces a framework that achieves unified and flexible control over video generation by independently managing camera and object motion. The system integrates specialized modules into latent video diffusion models, yielding more natural videos with precise motion control and enhanced text-to-video alignment.

## Problem

- Existing controllable video generation methods often fail to disentangle camera motion from object motion, limiting fine-grained control.
- Many prior approaches require retraining or fine-tuning for each new motion template, hindering flexibility and generalizability.
- Some motion conditions used in previous methods are appearance-dependent, leading to unnatural object appearances in generated videos.

## Method

- **MotionCtrl** extends Latent Video Diffusion Models with a Camera Motion Control Module (**CMCM**) and an Object Motion Control Module (OMCM) for disentangled control.
- CMCM uses camera poses (rotation and translation matrices) and integrates with the LVDM's temporal transformers for global camera motion control.
- OMCM uses 2D displacement vectors for object trajectories and integrates with the LVDM's convolutional layers for local object motion control, employing a multi-step training strategy with augmented datasets.

## Results

- MotionCtrl achieves superior control efficacy, with a CamMC score of 0.0289 for basic camera poses (versus AnimateDiff's 0.0548) and an ObjMC score of 28.877 for object motion (versus VideoComposer's 36.8351).
- The framework maintains high video quality and text alignment, recording better FID (124.09), FVD (852.15), and CLIPSIM (0.2319) scores compared to baseline models.
- Ablation studies confirm that integrating CMCM with temporal transformers and training OMCM with a dense-then-sparse trajectory strategy are optimal, validating the architectural and training design choices.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly disentangling camera and object motion via dedicated modules within a unified framework enables superior and fine-grained control over video generation.
> - Integrating global camera motion control with temporal transformers and local object motion control with spatial convolutional layers provides an effective architectural design for distinct motion types.
> - A multi-step training strategy, leveraging synthesized captions and trajectories, effectively addresses data scarcity by first learning camera motion and then object motion using a dense-to-sparse trajectory approach.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "MotionCtrl" is positioned within the rapidly evolving field of generative AI, specifically focusing on **controllable video generation**. This area has witnessed a significant shift from earlier generative models like Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) towards **diffusion models**, which have demonstrated remarkable success in generating high-quality and diverse images and, more recently, videos.

The broader landscape of video generation research includes:
- **Text-to-Video (T2V) Generation:** Models like LVDM, Imagen Video, Make-A-Video, and VideoCrafter aim to synthesize videos from textual prompts, emphasizing content generation and temporal consistency. MotionCtrl builds upon these foundations, using LVDM (VideoCrafter1) as its underlying video generation model.
- **Controllable Video Synthesis:** A more recent and challenging direction is to exert fine-grained control over specific aspects of the generated video, beyond just textual prompts. This includes controlling styles, content, and crucially, motion.

MotionCtrl addresses a critical limitation in existing controllable video generation methods, which often fall into one of the following categories:
1. **Template-Based Motion Control:** Many approaches, such as AnimateDiff, Tune-a-video, LAMP, and MotionDirector, learn motion by referencing specific template videos or a series of them. While effective for replicating *known* motions, these methods typically require retraining or fine-tuning a new model for each different motion template. This limits their flexibility and generalizability.
2. **Undifferentiated Motion Control:** Other methods, like VideoComposer and DragNUWA, offer more generalized motion control using conditions like motion vectors or trajectories. However, they largely treat all motion uniformly, failing to **disentangle camera motion from object motion**. This lack of clear distinction prevents fine-grained and independent control over these two fundamental types of movement within a video. For example, VideoComposer uses dense motion vectors which can inadvertently capture object shapes, leading to unnatural results when the desired motion is global camera movement. DragNUWA, while using trajectories, also conflates these motions as it relies on optical flow, which doesn't inherently separate foreground and background movement.

MotionCtrl directly addresses these gaps by proposing a unified framework that *explicitly distinguishes and independently controls* camera and object motion. This allows for:
- **Fine-grained Control:** Users can specify a camera pan while simultaneously dictating the trajectory of an object within the scene, something prior models struggled with.
- **Enhanced Flexibility:** It enables novel combinations of camera and object movements that were previously difficult to achieve with a single model.
- **Improved Generalizability:** By using appearance-free conditions (camera poses and trajectories) and a unified model, it aims to adapt to a wide array of motions without per-template fine-tuning, thus being more practical and scalable.

In essence, MotionCtrl pushes the boundary of controllable video generation by moving towards more intuitive, disentangled, and flexible control mechanisms, bridging the gap between general video synthesis and precise motion direction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **MotionCtrl**, a unified and flexible motion controller for video generation capable of effectively and independently controlling both camera motion and object motion within a single framework.

This objective is driven by several key motivations and the recognition of significant challenges in the existing landscape of controllable video generation:

- **Lack of Disentangled Motion Control:** Videos primarily consist of two types of motion: global motion caused by camera movement (camera motion) and local motion resulting from object movement (object motion). Previous works either primarily focused on one type of motion (e.g., AnimateDiff for camera zooms/pans) or failed to clearly distinguish and control these two independently (e.g., VideoComposer and DragNUWA using generic motion vectors or trajectories). This limitation restricts the diversity and fine-grained control achievable in generated videos. The authors aim to overcome this by enabling separate, yet combinable, control over these motion types.
- **Limited Control Capabilities and Diversity:** The inability to independently control camera and object motion means that users cannot easily combine different types of movements or achieve precise, nuanced control. For instance, creating a video with a zooming camera while a specific object follows a defined path was challenging. MotionCtrl seeks to unlock this flexibility.
- **Impact of Motion Conditions on Appearance:** Some existing methods use motion conditions that are "appearance-dependent" (e.g., dense motion vectors in VideoComposer). These can inadvertently encode structural or appearance information from a reference video, leading to unnatural or distorted objects in the generated output. A motivation for MotionCtrl is to use "appearance-free" motion conditions (camera poses and trajectories) to preserve the natural appearance and shape of objects.
- **Need for Generalizable Models:** Many prior approaches require training a new model or LoRA for each specific camera motion (e.g., AnimateDiff's 8 separate LoRAs for basic camera movements) or template video. This is resource-intensive and impractical for diverse applications. MotionCtrl aims to be a relatively generalizable model that can adapt to a wide array of camera poses and trajectories once trained, without continuous fine-tuning.
- **Data Scarcity for Comprehensive Training:** A significant practical challenge is the non-existence of a large-scale dataset containing video clips with complete annotations for captions, camera poses, *and* object movement trajectories. Creating such a dataset from scratch would require immense effort and resources. This motivates the development of a clever training strategy that leverages existing, albeit imperfect, datasets and augmentation techniques.
- **Architectural Differences between Motion Types:** Camera and object motions have fundamentally different properties – camera motion is a global transformation, while object motion is local. This necessitates a carefully designed architecture that respects these inherent differences for effective control.

In summary, MotionCtrl is motivated by the desire to provide users with more powerful, intuitive, and flexible control over video generation by disentangling and independently managing camera and object movements within a single, generalizable model, while overcoming the practical constraints of data availability and architectural design.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MotionCtrl is designed as an extension to existing Latent Video Diffusion Models (LVDM) like VideoCrafter1, aiming to provide unified and flexible control over camera and object motion. Its methodology is characterized by a delicately designed architecture and a multi-step training strategy tailored to overcome data limitations.

MotionCtrl is built upon LVDM, which uses a denoising U-Net (𝜖

#### 3.2. MotionCtrl Architecture

To achieve disentangled control, MotionCtrl introduces two main modules that act as adapter-like components integrated into the LVDM:
- **Camera Motion Control Module (CMCM):**
 - **Purpose:** To control global camera movements.
 - **Input:** A sequence of camera poses $RT = \{RT_0, \ldots, RT_{L-1}\}$, where each pose is represented by its 3x3 rotation matrix and 3x1 translation matrix, resulting in a $L \times 12$ tensor.
 - **Integration:** CMCM is a lightweight module consisting of fully connected layers. It integrates with LVDM's *temporal transformers*, specifically the second self-attention module. The camera pose sequence is first expanded spatially to match the latent features ($H \times W \times L \times 12$), then concatenated with the output of the first self-attention module, and finally projected back to the original channel size via a fully connected layer before being fed into the second self-attention module. This design leverages the temporal transformers' role in fusing information across frames, aligning with the global nature of camera motion.
- **Object Motion Control Module (OMCM):**
 - **Purpose:** To control local object movements.
 - **Input:** Object trajectories (𝑇𝑟𝑎𝑗𝑠), represented as a sequence of 2D displacement vectors $\{(u_{(x_1,y_1)}, v_{(x_1,y_1)}), \ldots\}$, indicating the movement of an object from frame to frame. This results in a $L \times \hat{H} \times \hat{W} \times 2$ tensor, where $\hat{H}, \hat{W}$ are latent spatial dimensions. The first frame and non-trajectory points are zero-padded.
 - **Integration:** OMCM consists of multiple convolutional layers and downsampling operations. It extracts multi-scale features from the 𝑇𝑟𝑎𝑗𝑠 and adds these features spatially to the input of LVDM's *convolutional layers* within the encoder. This approach, inspired by T2I-Adapter, focuses trajectory information on the spatial aspects of the latent representation, suitable for local object motion.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy and Data Construction

Recognizing the absence of a single dataset containing captions, camera poses, *and* object movement trajectories, MotionCtrl employs a multi-step training strategy and leverages augmented datasets:

1. **Learning the Camera Motion Control Module (CMCM):**
 - **Dataset:** `augmented-Realestate10K`. Realestate10K provides videos with camera pose annotations but lacks captions.
 - **Augmentation:** Captions for each video are *synthesized* using Blip2 (an image captioning algorithm) by extracting keyframes and concatenating their descriptions.
 - **Strategy:** Only the CMCM's new MLP layers and the temporal transformers of the LVDM are trainable. The majority of LVDM's parameters are frozen. This adapter-like approach preserves the LVDM's pre-trained generative quality while allowing CMCM to learn camera motion, mitigating issues from the limited scene diversity of Realestate10K.

2. **Learning the Object Motion Control Module (OMCM):**
 - **Dataset:** `augmented-WebVid`. WebVid provides videos with captions but lacks object trajectories.
 - **Augmentation:** Object movement trajectories are *synthesized* using the motion segmentation module of ParticleSfM for about 243,000 WebVid videos.
 - **Trajectory Sparsity:** To enable user-friendly sparse trajectory input at inference, OMCM is trained using sparse trajectories sampled from the synthesized dense trajectories. To address the issue of scattered sparse trajectories, a Gaussian filter is applied for refinement.
 - **Training Schedule:** OMCM undergoes an initial training phase with *dense trajectories* (for rich information) followed by fine-tuning with *sparse trajectories* (to adapt to inference conditions).
 - **Strategy:** During this phase, both LVDM and the already-trained CMCM are frozen. Only OMCM is trained, ensuring it adds object motion control capabilities with minimal impact on the established components.

#### 3.4. Evaluation

- **Datasets:** Custom evaluation datasets were constructed for camera motion control (basic and complex poses from Realestate10K, WebVid, HD-VILA) and object motion control (diverse handcrafted trajectories).
- **Metrics:**
 - **Quality:** Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), CLIP Similarity (CLIPSIM).
 - **Control Efficacy:** CamMC (Euclidean distance between predicted and ground truth camera poses) and ObjMC (Euclidean distance between predicted and ground truth object trajectories), with poses/trajectories extracted via ParticleSfM.
 - **Subjective:** User study evaluating video quality, text similarity, motion similarity, and overall preference.

This comprehensive methodology, combining architectural innovations with a pragmatic multi-step training approach, allows MotionCtrl to achieve disentangled and flexible motion control despite inherent data challenges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate MotionCtrl's significant advancements in controllable video generation across both qualitative and quantitative metrics.

#### 4.1. Superiority in Motion Control

- **Camera Motion Control:** MotionCtrl consistently outperforms AnimateDiff and VideoComposer.
 - For **basic camera poses** (e.g., zooms, pans), MotionCtrl achieves a CamMC score of 0.0289, significantly lower than AnimateDiff's 0.0548. Furthermore, MotionCtrl can adjust camera movement speeds, a capability AnimateDiff lacks.
 - For **complex camera poses**, MotionCtrl achieves a CamMC score of 0.0735, better than VideoComposer's 0.0950. Qualitatively, MotionCtrl generates more natural-looking videos by using appearance-free camera poses (rotation and translation matrices), avoiding the issue where VideoComposer's dense motion vectors inadvertently capture object shapes from reference videos, leading to unnatural artifacts (e.g., the Eiffel Tower example in Fig. 4).
- **Object Motion Control:** MotionCtrl also demonstrates superior object motion control compared to VideoComposer. It achieves an ObjMC score of 28.877, lower than VideoComposer's 36.8351. Qualitatively, objects generated by MotionCtrl adhere more precisely to the given trajectories across frames (Fig. 5).
- **Combined Control:** MotionCtrl successfully demonstrates its ability to independently and simultaneously control both camera and object motion within the same video, enabling flexible and diverse combinations (Fig. 1).

#### 4.2. Video Quality and Text Alignment

Beyond motion control, MotionCtrl maintains or improves overall video quality and semantic alignment with text prompts:
- **CLIPSIM:** MotionCtrl achieves a higher CLIP Similarity (0.2319) than AnimateDiff (0.2144) and VideoComposer (0.2214), indicating better semantic alignment with text prompts.
- **FID and FVD:** MotionCtrl records lower (better) FID (124.09) and FVD (852.15) scores compared to AnimateDiff (FID 157.73, FVD 1815.88) and VideoComposer (FID 130.97, FVD 1004.99). This signifies higher visual quality and temporal coherence of the generated videos.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies Confirm Design Choices

- **Integrated Position of CMCM:** The ablation study confirms that integrating CMCM with LVDM's *temporal transformers* is crucial for effective camera motion control, yielding a CamMC of 0.0289. Other integration points (time embedding, spatial cross-attention, spatial self-attention) show significantly worse performance (CamMC scores around 0.08-0.09), reinforcing that camera motion, as a global temporal transformation, is best handled through temporal processing units.
- **Dense vs. Sparse Trajectories for OMCM:** The hybrid training strategy for OMCM (initial training on dense trajectories followed by fine-tuning on sparse trajectories) is shown to be the most effective, achieving an ObjMC of 25.1198. Training solely on dense (54.4114) or sparse (34.6937) trajectories yields inferior results, highlighting the benefit of leveraging rich dense information initially and then adapting to the sparsity expected during inference.
- **Training Strategy Order:** Training CMCM before OMCM is confirmed as the optimal multi-step strategy. Reversing the order degrades object motion control performance, as subsequent CMCM training would modify shared components, disrupting OMCM's learned adaptations.

#### 4.4. Generalizability and Extensibility

- MotionCtrl demonstrates generalizability by effectively controlling a wide range of camera movements and trajectories without requiring fine-tuning for each individual motion.
- The framework is also shown to be extensible, successfully deployed on AnimateDiff, demonstrating its adaptability to other video generation models.

In summary, MotionCtrl not only achieves superior, fine-grained, and disentangled control over camera and object motion but also maintains high video quality and text alignment. The comprehensive ablation studies validate its architectural design and multi-step training strategy as effective solutions to complex challenges in controllable video generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MotionCtrl represents a significant advancement in the field of controllable video generation, offering capabilities that address critical limitations of previous approaches. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

- **Enhanced Control and Creativity for Content Creators:** By enabling independent and flexible control over both camera and object motion within a unified model, MotionCtrl empowers artists, designers, and content creators with unprecedented precision. This allows for more sophisticated storytelling, complex scene choreography, and highly customized visual narratives. Users can now dictate complex camera movements (e.g., tracking shots, dynamic pans) while simultaneously animating specific characters or elements along custom paths, leading to richer and more expressive video content.
- **Foundation for More Intuitive Generative AI Interfaces:** The use of appearance-free conditions like camera poses (rotation/translation matrices) and object trajectories makes the control mechanism more intuitive and less prone to unintended visual artifacts compared to methods relying on dense motion vectors. This shift towards more semantic and abstract motion controls could pave the way for user interfaces where motion can be described or drawn directly, without needing to reference existing video segments for motion cues.
- **Advancement in Disentangled Representation Learning:** The successful disentanglement of camera and object motion is a notable achievement in representation learning for generative models. This approach, which assigns specific modules and training strategies to distinct motion types, could inspire future research in breaking down other complex generative factors (e.g., lighting, texture, pose) into independently controllable components.
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity Challenges:** The innovative multi-step training strategy, combined with intelligent data augmentation (synthesizing captions and trajectories), provides a practical blueprint for developing advanced generative models in scenarios where perfectly comprehensive, multi-modal datasets are scarce. This methodology is highly significant for pushing the boundaries of AI research where meticulously curated datasets are often a bottleneck.
- **Efficiency and Scalability for Production:** The "unified and flexible" nature of MotionCtrl means a single trained model can handle a wide variety of motions, obviating the need for separate models or extensive fine-tuning for each specific motion (as seen in some LoRA-based approaches). This reduces computational overhead, storage requirements, and development time, making it more practical for large-scale deployment in industry applications, especially in areas like marketing, entertainment, and virtual reality. The involvement of Tencent's research labs underscores this potential for real-world impact and integration into commercial products.
- **Improved Realism and Quality:** By ensuring that motion control does not negatively impact the appearance or shape of objects, MotionCtrl contributes to generating more natural and high-fidelity videos. This enhances the overall realism and aesthetic quality, which is crucial for broad adoption in content creation.

In conclusion, MotionCtrl significantly advances the state-of-the-art in controllable video generation by providing a robust, flexible, and unified framework for disentangled camera and object motion control. Its methodological innovations and demonstrated capabilities lay a strong foundation for future research and have substantial potential to revolutionize how video content is created and manipulated using AI.
