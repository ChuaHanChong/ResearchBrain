# Evaluating Gemini Robotics Policies in a Veo World Simulator

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics Team developed a novel Veo World Simulator, built on frontier video generation models, to evaluate generalist robot policies. The simulator accurately predicts policy performance and out-of-distribution generalization, and identifies safety-critical behaviors that are replicable in real-world hardware.

## Problem

- Evaluating generalist robot policies for scalability, comprehensiveness, and safety poses a significant challenge.
- Hardware evaluations are prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and unsafe for probing diverse scenarios and edge cases.
- Traditional physics-based simulators struggle with realistic asset generation, complex object behaviors, and the visual sim-to-real gap.

## Method

- A generative evaluation system, Veo (Robotics), was developed by fine-tuning the Veo2 text-to-video model for robot action conditioning and multi-view consistency.
- Generative image-editing techniques (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image) and multi-view completion are used to synthesize diverse out-of-distribution (OOD) and safety-critical scene variations.
- The system evaluates Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, specifically Gemini Robotics On-Device (GROD) models, by generating future image sequences conditioned on initial observations and robot poses.

## Results

- The Veo (Robotics) simulator accurately predicts the relative performance of different policies in nominal conditions, achieving a Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) of 0.03 and a Pearson correlation of 0.88 with real-world success rates.
- It effectively predicts OOD generalization, showing strong correlations (e.g., Pearson 0.91 for background changes) in forecasting the relative impact of various generalization axes on policy performance.
- The generative evaluation system successfully identified potentially unsafe behaviors by "red teaming" policies in simulation, and these predicted unsafe behaviors were replicated and observed in real-world hardware experiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frontier video generation models can serve as effective evaluators across the full spectrum of robot policy assessment, including nominal performance, OOD generalization, and safety probing.
> - Combining generative scene editing with action-conditioned video models enables systematic exploration of policy vulnerabilities in complex, interactive scenarios.
> - The system's predictive fidelity for policy performance and safety is validated through strong correlations with real-world hardware evaluations, offering a scalable in-silico evaluation paradigm.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and long-standing challenge in robotics: the scalable, comprehensive, and safe evaluation of robot policies. As robot policies become increasingly "generalist"—capable of performing diverse tasks in varied environments—their evaluation becomes exponentially complex. Traditional evaluation methods face significant limitations:

- **Hardware Evaluation:** Conducting real-world hardware evaluations for generalist policies is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and often unsafe, especially when probing edge cases or safety-critical scenarios. The "long tail" of potential scenarios makes exhaustive hardware testing impractical.
- **Physics-Based Simulators:** While physics-based simulators offer a controlled environment, they present their own set of difficulties. These include the laborious curation and accurate simulation of a wide range of realistic assets (especially non-rigid objects), and the notorious "sim-to-real gap," where visual discrepancies between simulation and reality can lead to policies that perform poorly in the real world. Bridging this gap often requires extensive manual tuning and expertise.

The paper positions **video generation models** as a promising alternative, capable of overcoming many of these limitations. Prior work in this nascent area has shown how fine-tuned video generation models can produce robot policy rollouts (Du et al., 2023) or act as action-conditioned world models for coarse language (NVIDIA, 2025; Russell et al., 2025; 1XW, 2025; Bruce et al., 2024) or even in-distribution policy evaluations (Guo et al., 2025; Quevedo et al., 2025).

This work significantly advances the state-of-the-art by demonstrating that these video models can be employed for the **full spectrum of policy evaluation use cases**, moving beyond mere in-distribution performance assessment. Specifically, it extends their utility to:
1. **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) generalization:** Systematically evaluating how policies perform in novel environments, with unseen objects, backgrounds, or distractors.
2. **Red teaming for physical and semantic safety:** Probing for unsafe behaviors in scenarios that are difficult or dangerous to replicate in the real world.

Crucially, the paper distinguishes itself from related work in safety evaluation. While there is extensive research on physical safety in autonomous systems (Favaro et al., 2023, 2025; Gao et al., 2025b) and emerging work on semantic safety for generalist robots using non-interactive benchmarks (Bianchi et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023, 2024; Sermanet et al., 2025; Jindal et al., 2025), this paper focuses on **closed-loop, interactive safety evaluation**. This means assessing safety where the robot's actions dynamically influence the outcome, a more complex and realistic scenario than static assessments based on text, images, or videos alone. The methodology, by combining state-of-the-art generative models with robot-specific fine-tuning and systematic OOD scene generation, establishes a novel paradigm for robot policy evaluation that could become standard in the development of future generalist embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties in evaluating generalist robot policies. As highlighted in the introduction, the very appeal of these policies—their ability to perform diverse tasks across varied environments—creates a fundamental technical hurdle for assessing their reliability, generalization capabilities, and safety. Traditional evaluation methods are insufficient: hardware evaluations are impractical for broad coverage and too risky for safety-critical edge cases, while physics-based simulators struggle with realistic asset generation, complex object behaviors (e.g., non-rigid objects, liquids), and the persistent visual domain gap.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of this paper are:

1. **Demonstrate the Comprehensive Utility of Video Models:** To show that frontier video models can serve as effective evaluators for the *entire spectrum* of robot policy assessment. This encompasses not only nominal, in-distribution performance but also out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization and the crucial aspect of probing for physical and semantic safety violations.
2. **Introduce a Generative Evaluation System:** To develop and present a novel generative evaluation system. This system is built upon a frontier video foundation model (Veo) and is specifically optimized for robot action conditioning and multi-view consistency. It further integrates generative image-editing techniques and multi-view completion to synthesize realistic and diverse variations of real-world scenes along multiple axes of generalization.
3. **Validate Predictive Fidelity:** To rigorously validate that the proposed system accurately preserves the base capabilities of the underlying video model. This validation aims to demonstrate that the system can faithfully simulate scenes that have been edited to include novel interaction objects, visual backgrounds, and distractor objects.
4. **Enable Advanced Policy Insights:** Through its fidelity, the system aims to enable several advanced evaluation capabilities:
 - Accurately predicting the relative performance of different policies under both nominal and OOD conditions.
 - Determining the relative impact of various generalization axes on a policy's performance.
 - Facilitating "red teaming" of policies to expose behaviors that violate physical or semantic safety constraints without endangering physical assets or humans.

In essence, the overarching goal is to pioneer a scalable, general, and safe in-silico evaluation paradigm that can keep pace with the rapid advancements in generalist robot policy development, thereby accelerating their deployment in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is a novel generative evaluation system built upon **Veo (Robotics)**, a fine-tuned version of Google DeepMind's state-of-the-art text-to-video model. The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. Model Architecture and Training

- **Base Model:** The system utilizes the **Veo2 text-to-video model** (van den Oord and Roman, 2024, 2025), which employs a latent diffusion architecture. This architecture first compresses spatio-temporal data into efficient latent representations using autoencoders, then uses a transformer-based denoising network to iteratively refine noisy latent inputs into high-fidelity video outputs.
- **Pretraining Data:** Veo2 is pretrained on a vast dataset of videos, images, and corresponding multi-level text annotations, rigorously filtered for quality, safety, and removal of personally identifiable information. Semantic deduplication prevents overfitting.
- **Robotics Fine-tuning (Veo (Robotics)):** The pretrained Veo2 model is then fine-tuned on a large-scale robotics dataset. This dataset comprises diverse manipulation tasks performed by robots across numerous scenes.
 - **Action Conditioning:** The fine-tuned model is capable of generating future image sequences conditioned on an initial image observation of the scene and a *sequence of future robot poses*. This allows the simulation of a robot's closed-loop interaction with the environment.
 - **Multi-View Generation:** To support modern policy architectures that often rely on multiple camera views, Veo2 is fine-tuned to generate multi-view consistent video frames. This is achieved by tiling observations from four cameras (top-down, side, left, and right wrist views) and training the model to predict these tiled future frames given the initial frame and future robot poses.

#### 3.2. Policy Under Evaluation

- The research evaluates end-to-end **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies** based on the **Gemini Robotics On-Device (GROD)** model. These policies are trained on a large-scale dataset of real-world expert robot demonstrations collected over 12 months from a fleet of ALOHA 2 robots. GROD policies predict a 1-second action chunk at 50 Hz.

#### 3.3. Scene Generation for Policy Evaluation

- **Nominal Scenarios:** For in-distribution evaluations, the fine-tuned Veo (Robotics) model is used directly with scenes and instructions similar to its training data.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Scenarios:** To evaluate generalization, novel scenes are generated:
 - An initial RGB observation from the robot's overhead camera is programmatically edited using **Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (NanoBanana)**. Language descriptions are used to specify changes (e.g., adding a new object, altering the visual background, introducing a distractor).
 - **Multi-View Synthesis:** The single-view edited overhead observation is then passed to a specially fine-tuned Veo2 model to synthesize consistent observations for the robot's other three camera views, ensuring a complete and coherent multi-view initial state.
 - The task instruction for the robot is adjusted to match the edited scene (e.g., "put pink brush in bowl" instead of "put banana in bowl").
- **Safety Red Teaming Scenarios:** These scenarios are also created through generative editing, but with specific criteria:
 - They are filtered by **Gemini 2.5 Pro (as a critic)** to ensure they are non-trivial and contain hazards, require multimodal reasoning, and feature ambiguity (object, destination, trajectory/affordance, human interaction). This systematic generation ensures challenging safety probes.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol and Metrics

- **Tasks:** Five bimanual manipulation tasks on the ALOHA 2 platform are used. Task variations include initial object positions, distractor identity/location, visual backdrops, and instruction generalization (rephrasing, typos, language, specificity), leading to 80 scene-instruction combinations.
- **Rollouts and Scoring:** Policies are rolled out in the Veo (Robotics) simulator for 8-second episodes. These generated videos are then scored by human evaluators using a binary success metric.
- **Real-World Validation:** A critical aspect of the methodology is the extensive real-world validation. Over 1600 hardware evaluations are conducted with eight GROD policy checkpoints across the five tasks, carefully replicating the edited OOD and safety scenarios in the physical world to compare against the simulator's predictions.
- **Quantitative Metrics:**
 - **Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV):** Measures the consistency of policy rankings between predicted and real-world success rates (lower is better, range).
 - **Pearson Correlation Coefficient:** Quantifies the linear relationship between predicted and real success rates.

This comprehensive methodology allows for both large-scale in-silico experimentation and rigorous empirical validation, establishing the credibility of the Veo (Robotics) world simulator for advanced policy evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence for the effectiveness of the Veo (Robotics) world simulator across nominal, out-of-distribution (OOD), and safety-critical evaluation scenarios.

#### 4.1. Nominal Scenario Evaluation

- **Accurate Policy Ranking:** In nominal (in-distribution) conditions, the Veo (Robotics) model demonstrates strong predictive capabilities for robot policy performance. It accurately ranks different policy checkpoints by their success rates, achieving a Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) of 0.03.
- **Strong Correlation with Reality:** A high linear correlation (Pearson coefficient of 0.88) is observed between the predicted success rates from the simulator and the actual success rates measured in real-world hardware evaluations. This indicates that the simulator's predictions are highly aligned with real-world outcomes.
- **Absolute Performance Discrepancy:** While relative rankings and correlations are strong, the absolute predicted success rates in the simulator are consistently lower than their real-world counterparts. This is acknowledged as an area for future improvement but does not undermine its utility for comparative analysis.

#### 4.2. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Scenario Evaluation

- **Comparing Axes of Generalization for a Single Policy:**
 - The simulator accurately predicts the relative impact of different axes of generalization on a policy's performance. For a strong policy (Policy A), it correctly identifies that changes in small/large distractors have the least impact, followed by visual background changes, and then novel objects to be manipulated as having the largest detrimental effect. This ranking consistency is high (MMRV = 0.06), and the linear correlation in performance degradation across these axes is strong (Pearson = 0.86).
 - Qualitative insights into failure modes, such as policies incorrectly targeting familiar objects when instructed to manipulate novel ones, are also generated by the simulator, providing valuable diagnostic information for policy improvement.
- **Comparing Policies Across Generalization Axes:**
 - The Veo (Robotics) model effectively compares the relative performance of different policies under various OOD conditions (background, small/large distractors, novel objects).
 - Strong correlations between predicted and real success rates are observed for background (Pearson = 0.91, MMRV = 0.0), small distractors (Pearson = 0.86, MMRV = 0.10), and large distractors (Pearson = 0.77, MMRV = 0.14).
 - For novel object generalization, while the correlation is lower (Pearson = 0.56, MMRV = 0.15), this is attributed to the universally low success rates of policies in these particularly challenging scenarios, making differentiation inherently harder.

#### 4.3. Red Teaming for Safety

- **Discovery of Unsafe Behaviors:** The generative evaluation system successfully identifies potentially unsafe behaviors by "red teaming" policies in synthetically edited scenes containing safety-critical elements.
- **Real-World Validation of Safety Findings:** Crucially, the unsafe behaviors predicted by the Veo (Robotics) simulator were successfully **replicated and observed in real-world experiments**. Examples include the robot making contact with a human hand when instructed to grab an object, and attempting to close a laptop lid without clearing scissors, risking screen damage. This real-world validation confirms the simulator's predictive power for safety.
- **Power of Generative Methods for Safety:** This finding highlights the immense potential of generative models for safety evaluation, enabling the discovery of vulnerabilities that would be extremely challenging, costly, or dangerous to probe using physical hardware alone.

In summary, the results demonstrate that the Veo (Robotics) simulator provides a highly predictive and versatile platform for evaluating generalist robot policies, extending significantly beyond conventional simulation capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Evaluating Gemini Robotics Policies in a Veo World Simulator" paper presents a significant advancement in the field of robotics and AI, with profound potential impacts on the development and deployment of generalist robot agents.

#### 5.1. Enabling Scalable and Comprehensive Robot Evaluation

The most immediate and impactful contribution is providing a viable path towards **scalable and comprehensive evaluation** for generalist robot policies. By demonstrating that action-conditioned video models can effectively simulate nominal, out-of-distribution (OOD), and safety-critical scenarios, this work overcomes key limitations of traditional hardware-based and physics-based simulation methods. This capability is essential for the future of robotics, where policies must reliably operate in complex, varied, and unpredictable real-world environments.

#### 5.2. Accelerating Generalist Robot Development

The ability to generate effectively "infinite scene variations" through generative image editing, coupled with the high fidelity and predictive power of the Veo (Robotics) simulator, means that researchers can rapidly and safely test policies against an unprecedented diversity of conditions. This will significantly **accelerate the iteration cycle** for developing generalist robot policies, allowing for quicker identification of failure modes, systematic evaluation of generalization capabilities, and efficient comparison of different policy architectures or training methodologies.

#### 5.3. Enhancing Robot Safety

Perhaps the most impactful aspect is the potential for **predictive red teaming for safety**. By enabling the discovery of potentially unsafe behaviors in a simulated, controlled environment, this system allows developers to identify and mitigate risks *before* deploying robots in the real world. This is particularly crucial for "semantic safety," where robots need to understand and respect commonsense constraints to operate safely around humans and valuable assets. The validation that simulated unsafe behaviors manifest in reality underscores the system's practical value in building safer AI systems. This capability is paramount for public trust and broader adoption of intelligent robots.

#### 5.4. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap with Generative AI

The paper effectively showcases the power of **frontier generative AI models** (Veo, Gemini Flash Image) to create realistic and diverse visual environments for robotics. By leveraging web-scale video datasets and expressive architectures, these models can generate photorealistic and physically plausible interactions, effectively bridging the visual sim-to-real gap that has plagued traditional physics simulators. This approach offers a unified and scalable recipe for simulating complex objects and behaviors that are challenging for traditional methods.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Robotics Research

While acknowledging limitations such as simulating contact-rich interactions, long-horizon generation, and the need for automated scoring, this work lays a robust foundation for future research. It clearly outlines the "massive potential impact of video models in robotics," setting a research agenda for improving fidelity, temporal consistency, and automation. As these areas mature, the proposed paradigm could become the standard infrastructure for developing robust, capable, and ethically sound embodied AI agents.

In conclusion, this research marks a pivotal step towards developing truly generalist robots by providing a crucial missing piece: a scalable, comprehensive, and safe evaluation framework powered by cutting-edge generative AI. Its impact will likely be felt across research and industry, accelerating the progress of robotics towards real-world deployment.
