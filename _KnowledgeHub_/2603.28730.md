# SOLE-R1: Video-Language Reasoning as the Sole Reward for On-Robot Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SOLE-R1, a specialized video-language reasoning model from MIT and RAI Institute, reliably serves as the sole reward signal for on-robot reinforcement learning. It enables zero-shot learning of unseen manipulation tasks across diverse environments and robots, achieving at least 50% success on 24 tasks while significantly reducing reward hacking compared to general-purpose VLMs.

## Problem

- General-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently fail as robust reward functions for robot reinforcement learning due to their lack of robustness to partial observability and distribution shifts in dynamic environments.
- Robot policies trained with existing VLM rewards often learn to exploit perceptual or reasoning errors in the reward model, leading to 'reward hacking' (achieving high predicted reward without actual task completion).
- Current VLM reward learning approaches typically lack explicit intermediate reasoning and sufficient training data covering diverse non-expert behaviors, limiting their generalization and ability to support online RL from scratch.

## Method

- Developed **SOLE-R1**, a video-language model that generates per-timestep ==spatiotemporal Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== explanations and ==dense scalar task progress estimates== directly from robot video observations and natural language goals.
- Created a comprehensive ==training data synthesis pipeline== that includes diverse non-expert trajectories (generated via ==action deviations== in simulation and ==temporal perturbations== in real-world demonstrations) and annotates them with ground-truth CoT and progress estimates.
- Employed a two-stage hybrid training framework: ==Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)== to develop high-quality CoT reasoning, followed by ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)== to refine the accuracy and calibration of progress prediction for use as an online RL reward.

## Results

- **SOLE-R1** enabled zero-shot online RL from random initialization, achieving at least **50%** success on **24** previously unseen manipulation tasks across four simulation environments and a real-robot setup (50 tasks in total).
- Demonstrated superior robustness to reward hacking, with **SOLE-R1**'s failures predominantly categorized as 'signal-limited' rather than policies exploiting perceptual errors, outperforming general-purpose VLMs which largely failed via reward hacking.
- Generalization was observed across unseen task families, scene layouts, camera viewpoints, and robot embodiments, and offline evaluations showed higher Value-Order-Correlation (VOC) on real-world expert demonstrations and improved performance on general vision reasoning benchmarks compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit, per-timestep spatiotemporal Chain-of-Thought reasoning directly from raw video observations is crucial for building robust reward signals that are less susceptible to reward hacking and provide interpretable explanations for robot behavior.
> - Training data must comprehensively cover diverse non-expert, negative, and recovery trajectories, in addition to expert demonstrations, to enable the reward model to accurately distinguish between actual task progress and failure states.
> - A hybrid training strategy, combining supervised learning for reasoning and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards for progress estimation, is effective in producing a well-calibrated and accurate reward function suitable for zero-shot online RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) into robotics has gained increasing attention due to their advanced reasoning and perception capabilities. The broader research landscape aims to leverage these foundation models to automate or simplify the supervision of robot learning, ideally reducing the need for manual reward engineering and human data annotation. The long-term goal is to enable robots to acquire new skills autonomously through interaction with their environment, guided primarily by these pretrained models.

However, current state-of-the-art VLMs, such as GPT-5 and Gemini-3-Pro, exhibit limitations when directly employed as reward functions or evaluators in reinforcement learning (RL) for robotics. These limitations include a lack of robustness to partial observability and distribution shift. Robotic policies trained with rewards derived from these models have been observed to exploit perceptual or reasoning errors in the VLMs, leading to behaviors that elicit high predicted rewards without genuinely achieving the task goal. This phenomenon, referred to as "reward hacking," poses a significant challenge to the practical deployment of VLM-based reward systems for online robot learning.

Existing specialized VLM reward models, such as RoboReward, Robometer, and LRM, have demonstrated progress in predicting task progress from videos. However, these models often rely on expert demonstrations for training, may lack explicit intermediate reasoning capabilities, and are frequently evaluated on narrower task sets, rather than in a zero-shot online RL setting from random initialization across diverse environments.

SOLE-R1 addresses these identified limitations by proposing a video-language reasoning model explicitly designed to provide a robust, dense, and interpretable reward signal for online RL. It seeks to overcome reward hacking by integrating spatiotemporal chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning and training on diverse non-expert trajectories, thereby enhancing the model's ability to distinguish genuine progress from exploitable visual cues. This work contributes to the development of more general and reusable reward models, moving closer to the paradigm of self-supervised robot skill acquisition guided solely by natural language goals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust vision-language reasoning model, SOLE-R1, capable of serving as the *sole* reward signal for online reinforcement learning in robotic manipulation tasks. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges and aspirations:

1. **Addressing Reward Hacking:** A central motivation stems from the observed fragility of current state-of-the-art VLMs when used as reward functions in RL. Policies tend to exploit perceptual limitations and reasoning failures of these models, achieving high perceived rewards without true task success. The objective is to mitigate this vulnerability by designing a reward model that provides more reliable and less exploitable progress estimates.

2. **Enabling Zero-Shot Online RL from Scratch:** The research aims to move towards an idealized paradigm where robots can learn complex manipulation skills entirely from random initialization, solely guided by a pretrained foundation model and a natural language goal. This eliminates the need for ground-truth rewards, success indicators, expert demonstrations, or task-specific tuning, which are labor-intensive to provide.

3. **Integrating Spatiotemporal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** The authors hypothesize that explicit, per-timestep CoT reasoning, which describes visual changes, progress/regression, and next subgoals, can enhance the robustness and interpretability of the reward signal. By forcing the model to articulate its reasoning, it is encouraged to focus on causal changes rather than superficial correlations, thereby providing a more grounded and less exploitable reward.

4. **Generating Dense and Continuous Progress Estimates:** For effective online RL, especially from scratch, a dense and continuous reward signal is often more beneficial than sparse, binary success indicators. The objective is to produce per-timestep scalar progress estimates that can directly serve as dense rewards, guiding efficient exploration and learning.

5. **Building Robust Training Data:** Recognizing that the quality of reward models depends heavily on their training data, a key motivation is to develop a scalable pipeline for synthesizing large-scale, high-quality video trajectories with aligned CoT reasoning and continuous progress supervision. This data needs to cover not only successful trajectories but also diverse non-expert and failure scenarios to prevent optimistic extrapolation and improve robustness.

6. **Enhancing Generalization:** The research aims to develop a reward model that generalizes to a wide array of unseen tasks, novel environments, different robot embodiments, and varying camera viewpoints. This requires fostering video-native reasoning that integrates both spatial and temporal understanding effectively.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between the impressive capabilities of VLMs in perception and reasoning and their practical application as robust, general-purpose reward functions for autonomous robot learning, thereby accelerating the development of more capable and adaptable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for developing SOLE-R1 involves a three-pronged approach: designing a video-language reasoning framework, synthesizing specialized training data, and implementing a hybrid training framework.

#### 3.1. SOLE-R1 Video-Language Reasoning Framework

SOLE-R1 is a vision-language model built upon the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone. It is designed for video-native temporal reasoning to assess task progress for natural language goal-conditioned tasks.

- **Input:** At each timestep `t`, the model receives a natural language goal `g`, a sliding temporal context window of `K` frames `o_{t-K+1:t}` (or all available frames if `t < K`), the initial frame `o_0`, and its own previous progress prediction `p_{t-1}`. The use of a multi-frame window is intended to encourage reasoning about motion, contact events, and state transitions, rather than static single-frame captions. Sparse use of `K=0` and dropout of `p_{t-1}` during training are employed to foster global video reasoning and prevent over-reliance on previous predictions.
- **Output:** The model autoregressively generates a structured response `y_t = [<think> m_t </think>, <answer> p_t </answer>]`.
 - `m_t` is a free-form natural language Chain-of-Thought (CoT) explanation, designed to describe salient visual changes since `t-1`, whether these changes advance or regress the goal, and the next implied subgoal.
 - `p_t` is a dense scalar progress estimate in the range `[-100, 100]`, directly used as a reward signal `r_t = ψ * clip(p_t, -c, c)` for online RL. Inference can occur at a lower frequency than control, in which case rewards are linearly interpolated.

#### 3.2. SOLE-R1 Training Data Synthesis

The training data for SOLE-R1 is constructed in two stages to elicit robust spatiotemporal reasoning:

- **Stage 1: Foundational Spatiotemporal Reasoning:** This stage focuses on general grounding in spatial and multi-frame temporal reasoning.
 - **Reasoning over space:** Supervised with explicit 3D relations (e.g., relative position, distance, containment, occlusion) using datasets like SSR-CoT, SpaceOm, SpaceThinker (via VQASynth), and robotics-relevant rationales from Embodied CoT and Robo2VLM-Reasoning. This emphasizes explanations grounded in visual evidence, including depth cues.
 - **Reasoning over time (video/multi-image):** Includes multi-image "spot-the-difference" data and embodied video QA/reasoning datasets (RoboVQA, From Seeing to Doing) that require explicit comparison across moments and attention to action-relevant temporal evidence. All data are normalized into SOLE-R1's multi-frame prompting and structured output format.

- **Stage 2: Video-based Spatiotemporal Progress Reasoning:** This stage specializes the model for dense progress estimation using robot videos.
 - **Non-expert Trajectory Generation:**
 - **Simulation (RoboCasa):** Non-expert trajectories are generated by injecting random deviations into expert demonstrations. Deviation start times `q` are selected, and `w` uniformly sampled actions are executed. The trajectory then either terminates or recovers by interpolating to a downstream expert state. This process creates trajectories exhibiting varying levels of expertise and failure modes.
 - **Real-world (Open X-Embodiment - OXE):** Since simulator state is unavailable, non-expert trajectories are constructed by temporally perturbing expert videos, primarily through "reversal points." Segments are reversed (e.g., `o_{q:q+w-1} = reverse(o_{q-w+1:q})`), producing trajectories that visually "undo" recent progress and induce clear regression events. Diversity is increased by varying the number and length of reversal windows.
 - **Ground-truth CoT Reasoning and Progress Estimates:**
 - **Simulation:** Progress `y_t` is computed from continuous geometric distances (end-effector-object contact, object-goal distance), normalized to `v_t ∈`. Dense CoT traces `m_t` are generated using templated language based on these distance changes, then refined using foundation VLMs.
 - **Real-world:** Temporal order serves as a progress proxy for unperturbed expert videos (`v_t = (t-1)/(T-1)`). For temporally perturbed segments, supervision is inherited from the original expert timestep, explicitly labeling reversed segments as negative progress. A foundation VLM conditioned on the goal, temporal context, and progress target generates CoT traces that describe visual changes and label them as advancement or regression.
 - **Dataset Mixture and Balancing:** The full dataset comprises 1.2M spatiotemporal CoT traces from 41K videos, combined with 10M images/video frames and 4M CoT traces from foundational reasoning data. During training, batches are balanced across general reasoning (40%), embodied reasoning/planning (30%), and progress prediction (30%).

#### 3.3. SOLE-R1 Hybrid Training Framework

A two-stage hybrid recipe is employed:

- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The model is fine-tuned on the combined spatiotemporal reasoning data (Section 3). The objective is to maximize the likelihood of the structured output `y = [m_t; p_t]`. SFT develops high-quality CoT reasoning and general spatiotemporal grounding. It runs for one epoch over all data.
- **Stage 2: RL with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** After SFT, the model is refined using a GRPO-based RL method, specifically focusing on the robot video-based progress prediction dataset. This stage addresses the limitation of SFT where the scalar progress value `p_t` is a small fraction of the output tokens, receiving weak emphasis.
 - **Verifiable Reward:** A reward function `r(o) = r_format(o) + r_acc(o)` is defined. `r_format` enforces parseable output structure, and `r_acc` measures progress accuracy as `α * exp(-|p_hat_t - p_t|/τ)`, penalizing absolute prediction error. This explicitly optimizes for the correctness and calibration of the predicted progress value.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive experimental results validating SOLE-R1's performance and robustness across various settings.

- **Zero-Shot Online RL Performance:** SOLE-R1 enabled zero-shot online RL from random initialization, achieving at least 50% success on 24 previously unseen manipulation tasks. These tasks spanned four simulation environments (RoboSuite, ManiSkill, Meta-World, LIBERO) and a real-robot setting, covering diverse manipulations such as pick-and-place, articulated object interaction, and button/lever/knob operations.
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** SOLE-R1 substantially outperformed general-purpose reasoning models (GPT-5, Gemini-3-Pro, Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5) and specialized reward models (Robometer, RoboReward, ReWiND, TOPReward, LRM, VLAC, LIV). GPT-5 and Gemini variants achieved 50% success on 7 and 5 tasks, respectively, while non-reasoning models largely showed near-zero success, with minor exceptions in Meta-World.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** The model demonstrated generalization to unseen task families, scene layouts, camera viewpoints, and robot embodiments (e.g., Sawyer, WidowX AI, Fetch Mobile Manipulator, different Franka gripper fingers/wrist camera angles). This suggests that SOLE-R1 learns reusable spatiotemporal progress primitives rather than task-specific heuristics.
- **Robustness to Reward Hacking:** Analysis of "perceived success" (based on predicted rewards) versus "true success" (ground-truth environment signals) indicated that SOLE-R1's failures were primarily "signal-limited" (low perceived, low true success). This means the model usually recognized non-success but provided rewards that were too flat or noisy to drive effective learning. In contrast, general-purpose VLM rewarders mainly failed due to "reward hacking" (high perceived, low true success), where policies exploited VLM perceptual errors to achieve high predicted rewards without task completion.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **No-CoT progress training:** Removing the ` <think> ` channel degraded learning and shifted failures towards signal-limited types, indicating that explicit CoT reasoning is crucial for clear, granular progress signals.
 - **Expert-only video progress supervision:** Training solely on expert trajectories (without authentic non-expert data) significantly increased reward-hacking failures, highlighting the importance of exposing the model to realistic failure states.
 - **No foundational spatial & multi-frame reasoning data:** Removing this foundational data reduced overall success and also increased reward-hacking failures, suggesting that broad spatiotemporal grounding is essential for generalization and robustness.
- **Zero-Shot Scaling:** The research observed a positive scaling law: SOLE-R1's performance (number of solved downstream tasks) increased with the diversity of task types included in its training data synthesis for progress supervision.
- **VLA RL Steering:** SOLE-R1 rewards successfully steered a pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy (SmolVLA) toward specific goals, improving success in complex, long-horizon, cluttered settings where sparse rewards failed.
- **Offline Evaluations:** SOLE-R1 achieved higher Value-Order-Correlation (VOC) than Generative Value Learning (GVL) on 1,000 real-world videos from 50 Open X-Embodiment datasets, particularly for high-quality, human-teleoperated datasets. It also showed consistent performance gains on general spatial and vision-language reasoning benchmarks (SpatialBench, SSRBench, CV-Bench) compared to its backbone and the SSR model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SOLE-R1 represents a advancement in leveraging vision-language models for robot learning, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enabling Zero-Shot Online Reinforcement Learning:** The core impact is the demonstration that a video-language reasoning model can serve as the *sole* reward signal for learning unseen manipulation tasks from scratch through online RL. This capability significantly reduces the need for laborious and task-specific reward engineering, human demonstrations, or manual success labels, which are major bottlenecks in deploying robots to new tasks. This could accelerate the development and deployment of more versatile robotic systems.
- **Increased Robustness to Reward Hacking:** By integrating per-timestep Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and training on a diverse set of authentic non-expert trajectories, SOLE-R1 exhibits greater robustness to reward hacking compared to existing general-purpose and specialized VLM reward models. This addresses a critical limitation that has hindered the practical application of VLM-based rewards, making the learned policies more reliable and less prone to exploiting perceptual errors.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The model's ability to generalize across various unseen task families, environments, robot embodiments, and camera viewpoints suggests that it learns fundamental spatiotemporal progress primitives. These reusable primitives could form the basis for more general-purpose robot learning systems, reducing the domain-specific expertise required for new applications.
- **A Foundation for General, Reusable Reward Models:** The findings underscore the potential of grounded spatiotemporal reasoning as a pathway toward creating more general and reusable reward models. Such models could act as a universal supervision layer, abstracting away task-specific details and providing high-level guidance for a wide range of robotic skills.
- **Transparency and Interpretability:** The explicit CoT reasoning generated by SOLE-R1 offers a degree of transparency into the model's decision-making process. This interpretability can be valuable for debugging learning failures and understanding why a robot behaves in a particular manner, which is crucial for safety and development in complex robotic systems.
- **Contribution to the Research Community:** The release of models, data, and code promotes reproducibility and provides a valuable resource for further research in VLM-based reward learning, spatiotemporal reasoning, and zero-shot online RL.

**Limitations and Broader Impact:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations. SOLE-R1's performance is still constrained by the quality and coverage of its video-based progress supervision. Its rewards can exhibit flatness, noise, or miscalibration when visual evidence is brief, occluded, or ambiguous. Furthermore, while it reduces reward hacking, SOLE-R1 is not a substitute for robot safety mechanisms. Policies can still exploit residual reward errors, necessitating external constraints, monitoring, and validation for deployment on physical robots.

From a broader impact perspective, while SOLE-R1 advances robot autonomy by reducing the effort required for task training, it also introduces risks associated with increased robot autonomy. The potential for misuse, especially in safety-critical applications or under unforeseen distribution shifts, necessitates continued focus on safety considerations and thorough robustness checks during development and deployment.
