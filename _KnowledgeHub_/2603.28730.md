# SOLE-R1: Video-Language Reasoning as the Sole Reward for On-Robot Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SOLE-R1, a specialized video-language reasoning model from MIT and RAI Institute, reliably serves as the sole reward signal for on-robot reinforcement learning. It enables zero-shot learning of unseen manipulation tasks across diverse environments and robots, achieving at least 50% success on 24 tasks while significantly reducing reward hacking compared to general-purpose VLMs.

## Problem

- General-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently fail as robust reward functions for robot reinforcement learning due to their lack of robustness to partial observability and distribution shifts in dynamic environments.
- Robot policies trained with existing VLM rewards often learn to exploit perceptual or reasoning errors in the reward model, leading to 'reward hacking' (achieving high predicted reward without actual task completion).
- Current VLM reward learning approaches typically lack explicit intermediate reasoning and sufficient training data covering diverse non-expert behaviors, limiting their generalization and ability to support online RL from scratch.

## Method

- Developed **SOLE-R1**, a video-language model that generates per-timestep ==spatiotemporal Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== explanations and ==dense scalar task progress estimates== directly from robot video observations and natural language goals.
- Created a comprehensive ==training data synthesis pipeline== that includes diverse non-expert trajectories (generated via ==action deviations== in simulation and ==temporal perturbations== in real-world demonstrations) and annotates them with ground-truth CoT and progress estimates.
- Employed a two-stage hybrid training framework: ==Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)== to develop high-quality CoT reasoning, followed by ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)== to refine the accuracy and calibration of progress prediction for use as an online RL reward.

## Results

- **SOLE-R1** enabled zero-shot online RL from random initialization, achieving at least **50%** success on **24** previously unseen manipulation tasks across four simulation environments and a real-robot setup (50 tasks in total).
- Demonstrated superior robustness to reward hacking, with **SOLE-R1**'s failures predominantly categorized as 'signal-limited' rather than policies exploiting perceptual errors, outperforming general-purpose VLMs which largely failed via reward hacking.
- Generalization was observed across unseen task families, scene layouts, camera viewpoints, and robot embodiments, and offline evaluations showed higher Value-Order-Correlation (VOC) on real-world expert demonstrations and improved performance on general vision reasoning benchmarks compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit, per-timestep spatiotemporal Chain-of-Thought reasoning directly from raw video observations is crucial for building robust reward signals that are less susceptible to reward hacking and provide interpretable explanations for robot behavior.
> - Training data must comprehensively cover diverse non-expert, negative, and recovery trajectories, in addition to expert demonstrations, to enable the reward model to accurately distinguish between actual task progress and failure states.
> - A hybrid training strategy, combining supervised learning for reasoning and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards for progress estimation, is effective in producing a well-calibrated and accurate reward function suitable for zero-shot online RL.
