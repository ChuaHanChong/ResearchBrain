# SAC Flow: Sample-Efficient Reinforcement Learning of Flow-Based Policies via Velocity-Reparameterized Sequential Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> SAC Flow stabilizes off-policy reinforcement learning for expressive flow-based policies by reparameterizing their velocity networks as sequential models. This method, from Tsinghua, CMU, Li Auto, and Shanghai AI Lab, enables direct optimization and demonstrates state-of-the-art or comparable performance in continuous control and robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Continuous control tasks often require multimodal action distributions, which traditional unimodal Gaussian policies cannot represent.
- Directly combining expressive flow-based policies with sample-efficient off-policy reinforcement learning (RL) methods suffers from severe gradient instability (vanishing/exploding gradients).
- Previous solutions to stabilize training for flow policies, such as distillation or surrogate objectives, compromise the expressive capacity of the generative model.

## Method

- Identified that the multi-step ==Euler integration== in flow policies is algebraically equivalent to a residual recurrent neural network (RNN), explaining gradient pathologies.
- Introduced two novel velocity network architectures, Flow-G (GRU-gated) and Flow-T (Transformer-decoded), to stabilize gradient flow during backpropagation.
- Integrated these reparameterized flow policies into a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) framework, enabling direct, end-to-end off-policy optimization without compromising policy expressiveness.

## Results

- Empirically confirmed that Flow-G and Flow-T architectures maintain stable gradient norms during training, resolving the exploding gradient issue seen in naive SAC Flow baselines.
- Achieved state-of-the-art or comparable performance on dense-reward MuJoCo locomotion tasks, showing up to a **130%** improvement over strong baselines in challenging environments like HumanoidStandup.
- Demonstrated superior sample efficiency and rapid convergence in offline-to-online learning on sparse-reward manipulation tasks (OGBench), with up to **60%** improvement over baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The multi-step Euler integration used in flow-based policy action generation is algebraically equivalent to a residual recurrent neural network, clarifying the source of gradient instability.
> - Modifying the internal architecture of the flow's velocity network can directly address and mitigate vanishing/exploding gradients during off-policy optimization.
> - Modern sequential modeling principles, like gating mechanisms and Transformer-like decoding, can be adapted to stabilize generative policies for reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL) and generative models, specifically flow-based policies, for continuous control tasks like robotic manipulation.

Traditionally, continuous control RL has relied heavily on Gaussian policies, which, while simple and effective for many tasks, are fundamentally unimodal. This unimodal nature limits their ability to represent complex, multimodal action distributions often encountered in real-world scenarios (e.g., multiple valid ways to grasp an object or navigate a cluttered space).

In recent years, generative models, particularly diffusion models and flow-based models, have emerged as powerful alternatives for learning expressive, multimodal action distributions. Diffusion policies have shown state-of-the-art results in manipulation tasks, but their iterative denoising process makes both training and inference computationally expensive. Flow-based policies offer a compelling alternative, providing similar expressive power with potentially easier training and significantly faster, deterministic inference through ODE integration.

Early successes of flow-based policies in robotics primarily came from imitation learning (IL), where they learn from expert demonstrations. However, pure IL is constrained by the quality and coverage of the dataset and lacks the ability for environmental interaction and exploration, which is crucial for exceeding demonstrator performance or adapting to novel situations.

The natural progression is to combine flow-based policies with reinforcement learning. On-policy RL methods (e.g., PPO adapted to flows) have shown strong returns but are inherently sample-inefficient, making them impractical for real-world applications where data collection is costly. Off-policy RL methods (e.g., SAC, TD3) promise much higher data efficiency, a crucial factor for robotics. However, direct integration of flow-based policies with off-policy RL has been notoriously unstable. The core issue lies in the multi-step action sampling process of flow policies, which, when backpropagated through during off-policy updates, leads to severe gradient pathologies (vanishing or exploding gradients).

Prior work attempting to reconcile flow policies with off-policy RL has typically resorted to compromises:
- **Surrogate Objectives:** These avoid differentiating directly through the full flow rollout, thereby sidestepping the gradient issues.
- **Policy Distillation:** The complex flow-based policy is distilled into a simpler, one-step actor that can be optimized with standard off-policy losses.

The authors argue that both these strategies, while alleviating gradient stress, decouple the optimization from the original expressive generative model. This "blunts the benefits" of using multimodal flow-based policies in the first place, effectively sacrificing expressiveness for stability.

**This paper carves out a unique and significant niche by directly confronting and solving the fundamental instability issue.** By re-framing the flow rollout as a sequential model akin to an RNN, the authors provide a theoretical explanation for the gradient pathologies. They then introduce novel architectures (Flow-G and Flow-T) that stabilize the velocity network using principles from modern sequential models (GRUs and Transformers). This allows for direct, end-to-end optimization of expressive flow-based policies with sample-efficient off-policy RL (specifically SAC) without needing distillation or surrogate objectives. This work significantly advances the field by enabling the benefits of both expressive policy representations and data-efficient learning to be fully realized simultaneously.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a stable and sample-efficient off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm for training expressive flow-based policies in continuous control and robotic manipulation tasks.** The authors aim to overcome the notorious instability associated with directly optimizing flow policies using off-policy methods.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations and challenges in the current landscape of reinforcement learning:

1. **Expressiveness of Flow-Based Policies:** Flow-based policies demonstrate strong potential for challenging continuous control tasks, especially robot manipulation, due to their inherent ability to represent rich, multimodal action distributions. This is a significant advantage over traditional unimodal Gaussian policies.
2. **Limitations of Existing Training Paradigms:**
 - **Imitation Learning:** While successful in specific scenarios, imitation learning from static datasets is inherently limited by dataset coverage, quality, and the inability to explore and surpass expert performance.
 - **On-policy Reinforcement Learning:** On-policy methods adapted for flow policies achieve strong returns but suffer from poor sample efficiency, making them impractical for real-world applications where environment interaction is costly.
 - **Off-policy Reinforcement Learning:** Off-policy methods offer superior data efficiency, which is highly desirable. However, their direct integration with flow-based policies has been plagued by training instability.
3. **The Core Problem: Gradient Pathologies:** The multi-step action sampling process inherent to flow-based policies creates a deep computational graph. When off-policy RL updates attempt to backpropagate through this graph, it frequently leads to severe gradient pathologies (vanishing or exploding gradients), rendering training unstable or ineffective.
4. **Inadequacy of Current Workarounds:** Previous attempts to stabilize off-policy training for flow policies have involved using surrogate objectives or distilling the flow policy into a simpler one-step actor. While these mitigate gradient issues, they fundamentally decouple the optimization from the expressive generative flow model, thereby "blunting" the very advantages that flow policies offer (i.e., multimodal action representation).

The authors' key insight and motivation for their proposed solution is to **reconceptualize the flow rollout as a sequential model.** They formally demonstrate an algebraic equivalence between the Euler integration steps of a flow rollout and the recurrent computation of a residual Recurrent Neural Network (RNN). This theoretical link provides a clear explanation for the observed gradient instability, as RNNs are well-known to suffer from vanishing and exploding gradients.

This foundational insight directly motivates their approach: to **reparameterize the velocity network of the flow-based policy using principles from modern, stable sequential architectures**, specifically Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and Transformers. By addressing the root cause of gradient instability through architectural design, the authors aim to enable direct, end-to-end off-policy training of flow-based policies with SAC, thereby realizing their full expressive and sample-efficient potential without resorting to detrimental compromises.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in "SAC Flow" is built upon a novel theoretical insight, followed by practical architectural designs and an adapted off-policy reinforcement learning framework.

#### 3.1. The Sequential Model Perspective for Flow-Based Policies

The core theoretical contribution is the observation that the multi-step Euler integration process (Equation 4) used to generate actions in a flow-based policy is algebraically equivalent to a residual recurrent computation, much like an unrolled Recurrent Neural Network (RNN).
- **RNN Equivalence (Equation 5):** The update rule $A_{t_{i+1}} = A_{t_i} + \Delta t_i v_\theta(t_i, A_{t_i}, s)$ can be rewritten as $A_{t_{i+1}} = A_{t_i} + f_\theta(t_i, A_{t_i}, s)$, where $f_\theta(\cdot) = \Delta t_i v_\theta(\cdot)$ acts as the RNN cell. The intermediate action $A_{t_i}$ is seen as the hidden state, and $(t_i, s)$ as the input.
- **Explanation of Instability:** This equivalence provides a clear theoretical explanation for the notorious instability in off-policy training of flow policies: backpropagating through a deep recurrent stack (K steps of flow rollout) is prone to the same vanishing and exploding gradients observed in vanilla RNNs.

#### 3.2. Novel Velocity Network Reparameterizations

Building on the sequential model perspective, the authors propose two stable architectures for the velocity network $v_\theta$:

- **Flow-G (GRU-gated velocity):** (Figure 3b, Equation 6)
 - Inspired by GRU cells, Flow-G introduces a gated mechanism to the velocity update.
 - A gate network $g_i = \text{Sig}(z_\theta(t_i, A_{t_i}, s))$ adaptively controls the update strength.
 - The velocity is formulated as $v_\theta = g_i \odot (\hat{v}_\theta(t_i, A_{t_i}, s) - A_{t_i})$, where $\hat{v}_\theta$ is a candidate network.
 - This design regulates gradient flow across rollout steps, akin to how GRUs stabilize recurrent computations.

- **Flow-T (Transformer-decoded velocity):** (Figure 3c, Equations 7-9)
 - This architecture parameterizes the velocity using a Transformer decoder conditioned on the environment state $s$.
 - It creates independent embeddings for the current action-time token $A_{t_i}$ and a global state embedding $s$.
 - The action token is refined through a stack of L pre-norm residual blocks. Crucially, self-attention within these blocks uses a diagonal mask, ensuring position-wise transformations without mixing information across time steps.
 - Context integration occurs via a dedicated cross-attention module, where each action token queries the shared state embedding.
 - The final refined representation of the action token is projected to the velocity space. This "decoded velocity" allows for stable, depth-conditioned refinement while maintaining the Markov property of flow-based policies.

#### 3.3. A Practical SAC Framework (SAC Flow)

With the gradient stability achieved through Flow-G and Flow-T, the authors integrate these into a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) framework. A key technical challenge for SAC is computing policy likelihoods for the K-step flow rollout, essential for entropy regularization.

- **Noise-Augmented Rollout and Likelihood Computation (Appendix A):**
 - The deterministic K-step rollout is made stochastic by adding isotropic Gaussian noise with a compensating drift. This carefully chosen drift ensures that the final action distribution's marginal remains invariant.
 - This construction induces a product of per-step Gaussian transitions, making the joint path density $p_c(A|s)$ tractable. This $p_c(A|s)$ serves as a drop-in entropy term for the SAC objective.
 - The policy's final action $a$ is obtained by tanh-squashing $A_{t_K}$, and the Jacobian of this squashing is incorporated into the likelihood calculation for correctness.

- **From-Scratch Training (Algorithm 1, Equations 10-11):**
 - For dense-reward tasks, the SAC actor and critic losses are applied directly. The actor loss minimizes the Q-value and maximizes entropy using the tractable likelihood $p_c(A|s)$. The critic loss uses the standard TD error.

- **Offline-to-Online Training (Algorithm 2, Equation 12):**
 - For sparse-reward tasks where expert demonstrations are available, the actor loss is augmented with a proximity regularizer $\beta \|a_\theta^h - a_h\|^2_2$.
 - This approach begins with flow-matching pretraining on expert data, then transitions to online learning while maintaining closeness to the replay buffer data.

- **Fine-tuning with Adapters (Appendix F):**
 - Flow-G and Flow-T can be reformulated as lightweight adapters to fine-tune an arbitrary pre-trained flow-based policy. The adapters are zero-initialized such that they initially preserve the pre-trained policy's behavior, and only the adapter parameters are optimized during fine-tuning. A warm-up phase with behavioral cloning helps stabilize critic learning.

The overall approach provides a comprehensive solution for stable and efficient training of expressive flow-based policies across various RL settings, without compromising the policy's generative capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SAC Flow-G and SAC Flow-T across MuJoCo, OGBench, and Robomimic benchmarks yielded significant findings:

#### 4.1. Gradient Stability Validation

- **Key Insight Confirmed:** Ablation studies (Figure 6a, 12, 13) vividly demonstrate the core problem and the efficacy of the proposed solution. A naive SAC Flow baseline (without reparameterized velocity networks) suffers from severe exploding gradients, with norms escalating sharply along the backpropagation path (from K=3 to K=0).
- **Stable Gradients:** In contrast, SAC Flow-G and SAC Flow-T maintain stable gradient norms across the entire backpropagation path, with minimal variation. This confirms that the GRU-gated and Transformer-decoded velocity parameterizations effectively mitigate exploding gradients, enabling stable training. The instability of naive SAC Flow directly correlates with its poor performance in both from-scratch and offline-to-online settings.

#### 4.2. From-Scratch Training Performance (Dense-Reward MuJoCo Tasks)

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) or Comparable Performance:** SAC Flow-G and SAC Flow-T achieve superior or comparable performance across most MuJoCo locomotion tasks (Hopper, Walker2D, HalfCheetah, Ant, HumanoidStandup).
- **Outperformance of Baselines:**
 - Consistently surpass FlowRL (a state-of-the-art flow-based method), benefiting from direct optimization of the SAC objective.
 - Outperform DIME (a diffusion-based method) in Hopper, Walker, and HumanoidStandup, and achieve comparable results in HalfCheetah and Ant.
 - Demonstrate significant performance gains, up to a 130% improvement over strong baselines in challenging tasks like HumanoidStandup (Figure 4f).
- **Convergence Stability and Sample Efficiency:** The methods exhibit strong convergence stability across tasks. As expected, they also highlight the superior sample efficiency of off-policy algorithms compared to the on-policy PPO baseline.
- **Limitations in Hard Exploration Tasks:** All from-scratch methods, including SAC Flow, struggle with tasks featuring large exploration spaces and sparse rewards (e.g., Can from Robomimic, Cube-Double from OGBench), underscoring the necessity of offline-to-online training for such scenarios.

#### 4.3. Offline-to-Online Training Performance (Sparse-Reward Manipulation Tasks - OGBench, Robomimic)

- **OGBench (Cube-Double, Triple, Quadruple):**
 - SAC Flow, particularly SAC Flow-T, demonstrates rapid convergence and achieves state-of-the-art overall success rates on these challenging manipulation tasks.
 - Shows up to a 60% improvement over baselines, validating the effectiveness of the direct off-policy training approach in leveraging expert data and fine-tuning online.
- **Robomimic (Lift, Can, Square):**
 - SAC Flow-T and SAC Flow-G yield results comparable to QC-FQL (a SOTA off-policy method for action chunking). This similarity is attributed to the strong regularization (large $\beta$ value in Equation 12) applied during training, which limits the flow model's expressive capacity to stay close to the replay buffer, making its performance akin to a simpler one-step policy.
 - Nonetheless, SAC Flow-G and SAC Flow-T outperform ReinFlow (an on-policy baseline), showcasing the benefits of off-policy learning efficiency.

#### 4.4. Robustness to Flow Sampling Steps

- The ablation study on flow sampling steps (K=4, 7, 10) (Figure 7) reveals that SAC Flow-G and SAC Flow-T, especially Flow-T, are robust to an increased number of sampling steps. This indicates that the proposed velocity network designs maintain stability even when the "recurrent depth" of the flow rollout is increased, a testament to their robust gradient-handling capabilities.

#### 4.5. Fine-tuning Performance (Appendix F)

- The adapter-based fine-tuning methods achieve performance comparable to or even better than the offline-to-online methods in some instances (e.g., Cube-Triple-Task3, Task4, Robomimic-Square). This suggests that a randomly initialized critic, followed by a warm-up phase, might sometimes be more beneficial than a critic pre-trained via offline RL, especially when policy adaptation is key.

In summary, the main findings strongly validate the central hypothesis: reparameterizing the flow's velocity network with sequential model principles effectively stabilizes off-policy RL training, leading to state-of-the-art performance and sample efficiency for expressive flow-based policies without the need for compromises.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "SAC Flow" represents a significant advancement in reinforcement learning, particularly for continuous control and robotic manipulation, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Unlocking Expressive Policies:** The most critical significance is that this paper successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of gradient instability in training expressive flow-based policies with sample-efficient off-policy RL. By enabling stable, direct, end-to-end optimization, SAC Flow truly "unblunts the benefits" of flow policies, allowing them to effectively represent and learn complex, multimodal action distributions that are essential for intricate real-world tasks.

2. **Bridging the Gap between Expressiveness and Efficiency:** It successfully reconciles the power of generative models (flow-based policies) with the data efficiency of off-policy RL (SAC). This is a crucial step towards developing more capable and practical autonomous agents for complex scenarios where both factors are paramount.

3. **Elimination of Compromises:** The proposed method eliminates the need for common workarounds like policy distillation into simpler actors or the use of surrogate objectives. These prior solutions, while offering some stability, fundamentally diluted the expressive power of the flow policy. SAC Flow allows the original, expressive generative model to be optimized directly, maintaining its full capabilities throughout the learning process.

4. **State-of-the-Art Performance and Sample Efficiency:** The empirical results are compelling, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance and significant improvements in sample efficiency across a diverse suite of benchmarks. This includes challenging tasks in locomotion and robotic manipulation (MuJoCo, OGBench, Robomimic), highlighting the practical efficacy and robustness of the approach.

5. **Theoretical Clarity:** The novel insight that flow rollouts are algebraically equivalent to residual RNNs provides a clear theoretical foundation for understanding the observed gradient pathologies. This theoretical contribution offers a new lens through which to analyze and design generative models for control, potentially inspiring further research in stabilizing other complex policy architectures.

6. **Architectural Robustness:** The proposed Flow-G and Flow-T architectures are designed as "drop-in replacements" for the velocity network. This modularity means the principles can be readily applied to other off-policy RL algorithms beyond SAC, broadening the potential impact. Furthermore, their demonstrated robustness to an increased number of flow sampling steps suggests scalability to even more complex policy representations.

7. **Impact on Robotics and Autonomous Systems:**
 - **Real-world Deployment:** The enhanced sample efficiency is vital for robotic systems, where real-world data collection is expensive and time-consuming. This makes SAC Flow a promising candidate for practical deployment.
 - **Complex Manipulation:** For tasks requiring fine motor control, dexterous manipulation, or interaction with unstructured environments, the ability to model multimodal action distributions is crucial. SAC Flow provides a robust framework for learning such policies.
 - **Offline-to-Online and Fine-tuning:** The support for offline-to-online training and fine-tuning with adapters makes the approach highly adaptable to real-world pipelines that often leverage large datasets of demonstrations before online refinement. This is particularly relevant for applications like autonomous driving, where initial learning from vast datasets is then fine-tuned in specific operational domains.

Looking forward, the authors explicitly state their intention to validate SAC Flow on real robots and explore sim-to-real robustness. This underscores the immediate practical implications of their work. By providing a robust and sample-efficient method for training highly expressive policies, SAC Flow paves the way for the development of more intelligent, adaptable, and capable robotic systems and autonomous agents, ultimately contributing significantly to the advancement of applied artificial intelligence.
