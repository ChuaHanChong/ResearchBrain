# FetchBot: Learning Generalizable Object Fetching in Cluttered Scenes via Zero-Shot Sim2Real

> [!summary] Summary
> FetchBot introduces a framework for robots to perform generalizable object fetching in highly cluttered scenes via zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. The system integrates a voxel-based scene generator, a dynamics-aware oracle policy for data collection, and a vision-based policy leveraging foundation models for consistent depth prediction and learned 3D semantic occupancy, achieving real-world success rates up to 93.3% while minimizing environmental disturbance.

## Problem

- Robotic systems struggle with generalizable object fetching in densely cluttered and partially observable environments due to occlusion and diverse object properties.
- Learning-based methods face a significant sim-to-real gap, requiring costly real-world data, and are hindered by real-world depth sensor noise and inaccuracies with challenging materials.
- Existing scene understanding approaches often provide incomplete geometric representations in heavily occluded regions, complicating safe and precise action planning.
- Ensuring safe fetching, which minimizes disturbance to surrounding objects, is often overlooked or poorly addressed by current methods.

## Method

- ==Developed UniVoxGen==, a voxel-based scene generator that efficiently creates 1 million diverse and densely cluttered synthetic scenes with ground truth occupancy for training.
- Trained a dynamics-aware reinforcement learning oracle policy to generate 500,000 expert demonstrations, learning low-impact fetching maneuvers with minimal disturbance.
- Implemented a vision-based policy for imitation learning that utilizes DepthAnything for a sim-to-real-friendly intermediate depth representation and predicts complete 3D semantic occupancy (target, obstacle, robot) to infer occluded regions.
- Utilized a transformer-based architecture with a lightweight diffusion head to process 3D semantic occupancy features and robustly predict end-effector poses for fetching.

## Results

- FetchBot achieved significantly higher success rates in simulation (**81.46%** suction, **91.02%** gripper) and real-world scenarios (**86.6%** suction, **93.3%** gripper), outperforming all baseline methods.
- The system demonstrated superior safety by achieving the lowest environmental impact (minimal obstacle displacement) in cluttered scenes during fetching compared to other approaches.
- It exhibited strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities and robustness in handling transparent, reflective, and irregularly shaped objects in real-world environments.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the DepthAnything-predicted depth and the learned semantic occupancy representation are critical for the framework's high performance and effective sim-to-real transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inferring complete 3D semantic occupancy, even for occluded regions, is crucial for robust and safe object fetching in highly cluttered environments, outperforming methods relying on partial observations.
> - Leveraging a vision foundation model like DepthAnything to predict depth from RGB images effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap for visual input, handling real-world sensor noise and challenging materials.
> - Generating large-scale, densely cluttered synthetic scenes with accurate ground truth occupancy and expert demonstrations significantly improves the generalization and robustness of learned fetching policies.
> - A compact, task-appropriate Region of Interest (ROI) for 3D feature processing enhances policy generalization and accuracy by focusing on local context relevant to manipulation.
