# FetchBot: Learning Generalizable Object Fetching in Cluttered Scenes via Zero-Shot Sim2Real

> [!summary] Summary
> FetchBot introduces a framework for robots to perform generalizable object fetching in highly cluttered scenes via zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. The system integrates a voxel-based scene generator, a dynamics-aware oracle policy for data collection, and a vision-based policy leveraging foundation models for consistent depth prediction and learned 3D semantic occupancy, achieving real-world success rates up to 93.3% while minimizing environmental disturbance.

## Problem

- Robotic systems struggle with generalizable object fetching in densely cluttered and partially observable environments due to occlusion and diverse object properties.
- Learning-based methods face a significant sim-to-real gap, requiring costly real-world data, and are hindered by real-world depth sensor noise and inaccuracies with challenging materials.
- Existing scene understanding approaches often provide incomplete geometric representations in heavily occluded regions, complicating safe and precise action planning.
- Ensuring safe fetching, which minimizes disturbance to surrounding objects, is often overlooked or poorly addressed by current methods.

## Method

- ==Developed UniVoxGen==, a voxel-based scene generator that efficiently creates 1 million diverse and densely cluttered synthetic scenes with ground truth occupancy for training.
- Trained a dynamics-aware reinforcement learning oracle policy to generate 500,000 expert demonstrations, learning low-impact fetching maneuvers with minimal disturbance.
- Implemented a vision-based policy for imitation learning that utilizes DepthAnything for a sim-to-real-friendly intermediate depth representation and predicts complete 3D semantic occupancy (target, obstacle, robot) to infer occluded regions.
- Utilized a transformer-based architecture with a lightweight diffusion head to process 3D semantic occupancy features and robustly predict end-effector poses for fetching.

## Results

- FetchBot achieved significantly higher success rates in simulation (**81.46%** suction, **91.02%** gripper) and real-world scenarios (**86.6%** suction, **93.3%** gripper), outperforming all baseline methods.
- The system demonstrated superior safety by achieving the lowest environmental impact (minimal obstacle displacement) in cluttered scenes during fetching compared to other approaches.
- It exhibited strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities and robustness in handling transparent, reflective, and irregularly shaped objects in real-world environments.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the DepthAnything-predicted depth and the learned semantic occupancy representation are critical for the framework's high performance and effective sim-to-real transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inferring complete 3D semantic occupancy, even for occluded regions, is crucial for robust and safe object fetching in highly cluttered environments, outperforming methods relying on partial observations.
> - Leveraging a vision foundation model like DepthAnything to predict depth from RGB images effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap for visual input, handling real-world sensor noise and challenging materials.
> - Generating large-scale, densely cluttered synthetic scenes with accurate ground truth occupancy and expert demonstrations significantly improves the generalization and robustness of learned fetching policies.
> - A compact, task-appropriate Region of Interest (ROI) for 3D feature processing enhances policy generalization and accuracy by focusing on local context relevant to manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

FetchBot addresses a critical and long-standing challenge in embodied AI: **generalizable object fetching in cluttered scenes**. This problem is fundamental to many real-world applications, from warehouse automation and retail to domestic robotics. The paper frames its contribution by highlighting several limitations of existing approaches:

- **Difficulty with Clutter and Occlusion:** Prior works often struggle in densely packed environments. Heuristic or motion planning methods based on partial observations often fail when collision-free paths are difficult or impossible to find.
- **Generalization Gap with Learning-Based Methods:** While learning-based methods exist, they typically require large amounts of real-world data, which is prohibitively expensive to collect. This limits their ability to generalize across diverse objects, arrangements, and materials, a common necessity in cluttered environments.
- **Limitations of Simulated Data:** Existing simulated datasets for fetching often feature sparse, unrealistic scenes with minimal occlusion, preventing policies from effectively learning to handle real-world clutter.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap with Depth Sensors:** Many approaches use depth or point cloud inputs for their geometric information, which is beneficial for sim-to-real transfer over RGB. However, real-world depth sensors suffer from noise, inaccuracies (especially at object boundaries), and unreliability with transparent or reflective surfaces, creating a persistent sim-to-real gap.
- **Incomplete Scene Understanding:** Existing methods for scene modeling (e.g., voxel maps from RGB-D) inherit depth sensor limitations and often produce incomplete representations in heavily occluded regions, losing essential geometric details crucial for safe action planning.
- **Safety and Environmental Impact:** The need for "safe" fetching, minimizing disturbance to surrounding objects, is often overlooked or poorly addressed by methods that prioritize task completion without considering collateral damage.

FetchBot positions itself as a solution that systematically tackles these challenges by integrating a novel synthetic data generation pipeline, a robust oracle policy for demonstration collection, and a vision-based policy designed for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with advanced occlusion handling. It builds upon recent advancements in foundation models (like DepthAnything) and 3D vision, bridging them with robotic control, making it a timely and relevant contribution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of FetchBot are to:

1. **Achieve Generalizable Object Fetching:** Develop a robotic policy that can reliably fetch target objects from densely cluttered scenes, generalizing across diverse object categories, geometries, materials (e.g., transparent, reflective, irregular), layouts, and end-effectors.
2. **Enable Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Create a framework that allows policies trained entirely in simulation to perform effectively on real-world robots without any real-world fine-tuning.
3. **Ensure Safe Fetching:** Design policies that minimize collisions and disturbances to surrounding objects during the fetching process, even in highly occluded environments.
4. **Overcome Data Scarcity:** Develop an efficient method for generating large-scale, realistic synthetic data to train robust policies, mitigating the prohibitive cost of real-world data collection.
5. **Address Perception Limitations in Clutter:** Develop a scene understanding mechanism that can infer complete object geometry even under severe occlusion and handle challenging materials that confound standard depth sensors.

The motivation stems from the ubiquitous nature of cluttered scenes in practical applications and the limitations of current robotic systems in robustly interacting with such environments. The inherent difficulties of partial observability, data acquisition, and robust sim-to-real transfer for 3D visuomotor policies highlight the need for a comprehensive framework like FetchBot. The authors emphasize that minor collisions can lead to significant hazards, underscoring the importance of safe and precise manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FetchBot proposes a systematic sim-to-real framework comprising four main stages:

#### 3.1. Voxel-based Cluttered Scene Generator (UniVoxGen)

- **Problem:** Existing scene generators use computationally expensive simulator-based collision checks or object dropping simulations, leading to unrealistic or unstable cluttered scenes.
- **Solution:** UniVoxGen operates in a unified voxel space. It voxelizes objects and uses efficient, lightweight voxel operations (Union, Intersection, Difference, Transformation) for rapid collision detection and scene manipulation.
- **Outcome:** Generates 1 million diverse and densely cluttered scenes with tightly packed, frequently occluded objects, providing realistic training data and ground truth for occupancy prediction.

#### 3.2. Dynamics-Aware Oracle Policy

- **Problem:** Motion planning struggles in dense clutter; expert demonstrations are needed for imitation learning.
- **Solution:** A reinforcement learning (RL) policy is trained to generate "dynamics-aware" demonstrations. This oracle implicitly learns environment dynamics and selects low-impact maneuvers.
- **Architecture:** Employs a hierarchical scene encoder (inspired by PointNet++) to capture both local object details (from keypoints) and global scene context.
- **Reward Function:** A carefully designed reward system combines task completion, behavioral constraints (e.g., action range, action rate, pose maintenance, penetration penalty), and a crucial "environment change penalty" based on obstacle movement. A curriculum learning approach gradually reduces the allowable obstacle displacement to enforce precision.
- **Outcome:** Generates 500,000 representative demonstrations with minimal disturbance, serving as expert trajectories for the vision-based policy. Domain randomization enhances robustness.

#### 3.3. Vision-based Imitation Learning

- **Intermediate Depth Representation for Sim-to-Real:**
 - **Problem:** The sim-to-real gap for depth sensors (noise, transparency/reflectivity issues) hinders direct policy transfer.
 - **Solution:** Utilizes DepthAnything, a vision foundation model, to predict depth from RGB images (both simulated and real). This creates a shared, sim-to-real-friendly intermediate depth representation, ensuring the policy always receives consistent input from either domain.
- **Completion of Occluded Regions (Semantic Occupancy Prediction):**
 - **Problem:** Heavy occlusion in cluttered scenes leads to incomplete perception, making safe trajectory planning difficult.
 - **Solution:** Introduces semantic occupancy prediction as an auxiliary pre-training task. Multi-view predicted depth maps are processed by a ResNet-50 backbone. Features are aggregated into a 3D latent space using a Deformable Cross-Attention (DCA) mechanism, which projects 3D grid queries to 2D feature maps and samples features dynamically.
 - **Representation:** Within a Region of Interest (ROI) around the end-effector, the model predicts *semantic* occupancy (target, obstacle, robot) instead of just occupied/free space. The model is supervised with complete scene occupancy (from UniVoxGen) to infer occluded regions.
- **Action Generation Policy:**
 - **Architecture:** A transformer-based architecture processes the 3D semantic occupancy features. A learnable readout token aggregates these features.
 - **Output:** A lightweight diffusion head (Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model, DDPM) denoises Gaussian noise into the predicted action (end-effector pose).
- **Two-Stage Policy Learning Framework:**
 - **Stage 1 (Pre-training):** The 3D vision encoder is pre-trained on UniVoxGen's complete occupancy data, optimizing cross-entropy and scene-class affinity losses.
 - **Stage 2 (Policy Training):** The pre-trained vision encoder is frozen, and the policy head (transformer + diffusion) is trained via imitation learning, minimizing MSE between predicted and oracle actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FetchBot's effectiveness is rigorously evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Setup:** Evaluated on 3000 densely cluttered scenes using Isaac Gym, with both suction cup and parallel gripper end-effectors.
- **Metrics:** Success rate (target fetched with total obstacle displacement < 3 cm) and environmental impact (total obstacle translational and rotational displacement).
- **Baselines:** Heuristic methods, motion planning (CuRobo, AIT\*), and various learning-based methods with different input modalities (RGB, Point Cloud, Raw Depth, Predicted Depth, RGB-D Voxel).
- **Results:**
 - FetchBot (Occupancy) achieved significantly higher success rates: 81.46% (suction cup) and 91.02% (parallel gripper), drastically outperforming all baselines (e.g., CuRobo: 68.26%/73.05%; RGB-D Voxel: 71.38%/79.73%).
 - It also showed the lowest environmental impact (translation and rotation disturbance), demonstrating its ability to plan safe, low-impact actions.
 - **Key Insight:** Methods relying on partial observability (RGB, point cloud, depth-based, RGB-D voxel) struggled. FetchBot's occupancy representation, by inferring occluded regions, provided a more complete 3D scene understanding, crucial for navigating clutter and avoiding collisions (illustrated in Fig. 6). DepthAnything alone was hindered by scale-ambiguity and limited observations.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Setup:** Deployed on a Flexiv Rizon 4S robotic arm in a replicated retail store and other settings (tabletop, drawer), using two Intel D435i cameras (RGB streams only). 15 trials per scenario per end-effector.
- **Metrics:** Success rate (target acquired, no visible displacement of surrounding objects).
- **Baselines:** Learning-based methods with RGB, point cloud, raw depth, predicted depth, and RGB-D voxel inputs.
- **Results:**
 - FetchBot demonstrated strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and superior performance: 86.6% success rate (suction cup) and 93.3% (parallel gripper) in challenging cluttered environments.
 - It significantly outperformed real-world baselines (e.g., Point Cloud: 46.6%/53.3%; RGB-D Voxel: 73.3%/80.0%).
 - **Robustness:** Showcased excellent robustness in handling transparent, reflective, and irregularly shaped objects – materials often problematic for standard depth sensors – due to its use of predicted depth from a foundation model.
 - **Generalizability:** Demonstrated adaptability to diverse scenarios beyond shelves, including tabletop fetching and drawer extraction, with different end-effectors.
 - **Key Insight:** The combination of a foundation model for consistent depth prediction across domains and occupancy prediction for inferring complete geometry was critical for overcoming real-world complexities like sensor noise, challenging materials, and severe occlusion (illustrated in Fig. 7).

#### 4.3. Ablation Study for Sim2Real

- **Setup:** Real-world experiments evaluating the contribution of DepthAnything and occupancy representation.
- **Results:**
 - Removing DepthAnything (using raw RGB) drastically reduced success rates (e.g., from 86.60% to 60.00% for suction cup), confirming its role in bridging the visual sim-to-real gap.
 - Removing occupancy representation also degraded performance (to 73.33%), showing its necessity for inferring occluded regions.
 - Using only RGB inputs (removing both) resulted in the lowest success rate (33.33%), highlighting the synergistic benefits of these two modules.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Scaling Training Data

- Performance for both occupancy pre-training and policy learning significantly improved with larger datasets, up to 500k scenes/demonstrations, affirming the importance of large-scale synthetic data generation.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study on Region of Interest (ROI) Size

- A compact, task-appropriate ROI (20x20x30 cm) around the end-effector yielded the best performance, indicating that focusing on local context while filtering out distant clutter enhances policy generalization and accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FetchBot represents a significant advancement in generalizable robotic manipulation, particularly for object fetching in highly cluttered and partially observable environments.

- **Pioneering Zero-Shot Sim2Real in Clutter:** Its most impactful contribution is demonstrating robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities for a complex, safety-critical task. This mitigates the prohibitive cost and time of real-world data collection, accelerating robotic development and deployment.
- **Robust Clutter Handling:** By addressing fundamental limitations in scene understanding (occlusion, challenging materials) through learned occupancy prediction and leveraging foundation models for consistent depth representation, FetchBot offers a more robust solution than prior methods.
- **Practical Applicability:** The high success rates in real-world scenarios, adaptability to diverse objects and end-effectors, and performance in various environments (shelves, tabletops, drawers) highlight its strong practical value for industries like logistics, retail, and manufacturing, where autonomous handling of densely packed items is crucial.
- **Generalizable AI for Embodied Systems:** FetchBot pushes the boundaries of generalizable AI for embodied systems, demonstrating how synthetic data, advanced 3D vision, and reinforcement learning can be combined to teach complex skills.
- **Foundation Model Integration:** The effective integration of a foundation model (DepthAnything) for a robotics task opens new avenues for leveraging large pre-trained models to bridge real-world sensing gaps.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to future research directions:
1. **Joint Limit Awareness:** The policy can sometimes output complex actions that exceed robot joint limits, leading to failure. Integrating explicit joint limit constraints or more sophisticated motion planning could address this.
2. **Dual-Arm Collaboration:** Single-arm grasping is insufficient for very large or heavy objects, suggesting a need for dual-arm manipulation capabilities.
3. **Advanced Reasoning for Fully Occluded Targets:** FetchBot currently struggles with targets that are completely occluded and initially unreachable. This requires multi-step reasoning, such as strategically moving obstacles, fetching the target, and then potentially restoring the scene, which is a complex AI problem for future work.

Despite these limitations, FetchBot provides a robust and innovative framework that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in generalizable and safe object fetching in cluttered scenes, with clear pathways for future development.
