# Kinodynamic Model Predictive Control for Energy Efficient Locomotion of Legged Robots with Parallel Elasticity

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical kinodynamic Model Predictive Control framework integrates Unidirectional Parallel Springs for enhanced energy efficiency in legged robot locomotion. The approach achieved a 38.8% reduction in Cost of Transport in simulation and a 14.8% energy consumption reduction in preliminary hardware experiments on a monoped robot.

## Problem

- Legged robots require improved energy efficiency for sustained dynamic operation in unstructured environments.
- Integrating parallel elastic elements often restricts leg movement during the swing phase, impacting agility.
- Achieving real-time kinodynamic Model Predictive Control (MPC) for robots with elasticity is challenging due to the computational cost of non-convex optimization.

## Method

- A hierarchical control framework integrates a Spring-Loaded Inverted Pendulum (SLIP) model, a Single Rigid Body (SRB) ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC), and a detailed kinodynamic MPC.
- The kinodynamic MPC explicitly incorporates the dynamics of Unidirectional Parallel Springs (UPS) into its non-linear optimization problem.
- Warm-starting the kinodynamic MPC with solutions from the faster SRB MPC allows for robust real-time operation at 200 Hz despite the complexity.

## Results

- Simulation experiments demonstrated a **38.8%** reduction in Cost of Transport (CoT) across a range of forward velocities (**0.5** to **2.3** m/s) with UPS engaged.
- Preliminary hardware tests showed a **14.8%** reduction in total energy consumption during in-place hopping with UPS compared to without.
- The UPS mechanism notably shifted the knee motor torque distribution towards zero, decreasing the average knee torque from **11.4** Nm to **9.4** Nm in hardware experiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unidirectional Parallel Springs (UPS) effectively reduce energy consumption by sharing external loads and recycling kinetic energy, specifically during the stance phase without compromising swing phase.
> - A hierarchical MPC structure, leveraging warm-starting from simpler models, is effective for achieving real-time performance with complex kinodynamic models.
> - Explicitly incorporating UPS dynamics into the kinodynamic MPC framework optimizes motor torque distribution, shifting it towards zero and reducing peak loads on actuators.
