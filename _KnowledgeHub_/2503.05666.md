# Kinodynamic Model Predictive Control for Energy Efficient Locomotion of Legged Robots with Parallel Elasticity

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical kinodynamic Model Predictive Control framework integrates Unidirectional Parallel Springs for enhanced energy efficiency in legged robot locomotion. The approach achieved a 38.8% reduction in Cost of Transport in simulation and a 14.8% energy consumption reduction in preliminary hardware experiments on a monoped robot.

## Problem

- Legged robots require improved energy efficiency for sustained dynamic operation in unstructured environments.
- Integrating parallel elastic elements often restricts leg movement during the swing phase, impacting agility.
- Achieving real-time kinodynamic Model Predictive Control (MPC) for robots with elasticity is challenging due to the computational cost of non-convex optimization.

## Method

- A hierarchical control framework integrates a Spring-Loaded Inverted Pendulum (SLIP) model, a Single Rigid Body (SRB) ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC), and a detailed kinodynamic MPC.
- The kinodynamic MPC explicitly incorporates the dynamics of Unidirectional Parallel Springs (UPS) into its non-linear optimization problem.
- Warm-starting the kinodynamic MPC with solutions from the faster SRB MPC allows for robust real-time operation at 200 Hz despite the complexity.

## Results

- Simulation experiments demonstrated a **38.8%** reduction in Cost of Transport (CoT) across a range of forward velocities (**0.5** to **2.3** m/s) with UPS engaged.
- Preliminary hardware tests showed a **14.8%** reduction in total energy consumption during in-place hopping with UPS compared to without.
- The UPS mechanism notably shifted the knee motor torque distribution towards zero, decreasing the average knee torque from **11.4** Nm to **9.4** Nm in hardware experiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unidirectional Parallel Springs (UPS) effectively reduce energy consumption by sharing external loads and recycling kinetic energy, specifically during the stance phase without compromising swing phase.
> - A hierarchical MPC structure, leveraging warm-starting from simpler models, is effective for achieving real-time performance with complex kinodynamic models.
> - Explicitly incorporating UPS dynamics into the kinodynamic MPC framework optimizes motor torque distribution, shifting it towards zero and reducing peak loads on actuators.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Legged robots are being developed for applications in unstructured and remote environments, such as search-and-rescue operations and planetary exploration. These applications often require robots to operate dynamically and efficiently for extended periods without frequent access to power recharging. While significant progress has been made in achieving highly dynamic locomotion in quadrupedal and bipedal robots, research focusing specifically on improving their energy efficiency remains an active area of investigation.

One established approach to enhancing energy efficiency in legged locomotion is the incorporation of elastic elements. Parallel elastic elements, in particular, can reduce energy consumption by sharing external loads with motors, thereby counteracting gravitational forces and decreasing the required motor torque. In dynamic tasks like running, these elements can also store kinetic energy during landing and release it during push-off, facilitating energy recycling. However, traditional parallel elastic components can restrict leg movement during the swing phase, potentially hindering agility. Solutions like SPEAR and BirdBot address this through disengagement mechanisms, but these introduce additional hardware complexity. Unidirectional Parallel Springs (UPS) offer an alternative by providing assistance during the stance phase without impeding the swing motion, thus presenting a mechanism to reduce energy consumption without compromising kinematic freedom.

Controlling robots with parallel elasticity, especially during dynamic tasks like running, introduces complexities. Simplified models, such as the Spring-Loaded Inverted Pendulum (SLIP) model, have been widely used but often struggle to incorporate explicit constraints like motor torque saturation. Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a control paradigm suitable for these challenges because it can explicitly integrate complex system models and physical constraints (e.g., torque limits, kinematic limits) to generate trajectories that respect these boundaries over a finite prediction horizon. This capability is particularly relevant for robots with parallel springs, which manage energy storage and release within a gait cycle.

Within the spectrum of MPC approaches for legged robots, a trade-off exists between computational efficiency and model fidelity. Convex MPC, often employing simplified dynamics like the Single Rigid Body (SRB) model, offers fast computation suitable for real-time applications but may not accurately capture unmodeled aspects like kinematic constraints. Conversely, whole-body MPC models full dynamics and kinematics, offering maximum control potential but at a high computational cost. Kinodynamic MPC presents a compromise, leveraging simplified dynamics for speed while incorporating a full kinematic model to account for kinematic constraints. However, the inclusion of kinematic constraints often leads to non-convex optimization problems (Nonlinear Programming, NLP), which are computationally intensive to solve in real-time and prone to local minima or infeasible solutions. Warm-starting techniques, using solutions from simpler MPC problems as initial guesses, are commonly employed to mitigate these issues and improve robustness and convergence speed. This work situates itself within this landscape by proposing a hierarchical kinodynamic MPC framework that specifically addresses the integration of UPS dynamics for energy-efficient locomotion, aiming to overcome the real-time computational challenges through a structured warm-starting approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop and validate a kinodynamic Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework designed to improve the energy efficiency of dynamic legged robots by exploiting the characteristics of unidirectional parallel springs (UPS). The authors identify several motivations driving this objective:

1. **Exploiting Unidirectional Parallel Springs (UPS) for Energy Efficiency:** Legged robots demand significant energy, especially for dynamic locomotion in challenging environments. Parallel elastic elements are known to reduce energy consumption by assisting motors in carrying loads and recycling kinetic energy. The particular motivation for using Unidirectional Parallel Springs (UPS) is their ability to provide this elastic assistance specifically during the stance phase, where loads are high, without impeding the leg's free movement during the swing phase. This circumvents issues associated with traditional parallel elasticity that can hinder leg retraction or repositioning, thus offering a practical pathway to energy savings without compromising agility.

2. **Addressing Control Challenges for Elastic Robots:** Controlling dynamic legged robots with parallel elasticity presents difficulties for conventional control methods like the SLIP model, which typically lack the capability to incorporate explicit physical constraints such as motor torque limits or detailed joint kinematics. MPC is motivated as a suitable framework due to its inherent ability to integrate complex system dynamics and constraints, making it apt for managing the energy storage and release mechanisms of parallel springs throughout a gait cycle.

3. **Balancing Computational Efficiency and Model Fidelity with Kinodynamic MPC:** While whole-body MPC offers high fidelity, its computational demands make real-time deployment challenging. Simplified models, like the Single Rigid Body (SRB) model in convex MPC, are fast but sacrifice accuracy in kinematic constraints. The research is motivated by the desire to strike a balance, selecting kinodynamic MPC for its ability to incorporate joint kinematics and full-body dynamics (relevant for UPS integration) while maintaining a reasonable computational load compared to full whole-body control. This approach aims to explicitly reason about parallel spring torque, which is a function of joint angles, within the optimization framework.

4. **Enabling Real-time Performance through Hierarchical Control:** Kinodynamic MPC often involves solving non-linear programming (NLP) problems, which are computationally expensive and prone to issues like convergence to local minima or infeasible solutions in real-time applications. To address this, the authors are motivated to implement a hierarchical control architecture. This hierarchy uses simpler, faster models (SLIP and convex SRB MPC) to generate initial guesses (warm-starts) for the more complex kinodynamic MPC, thereby enabling robust, real-time operation at high frequencies (e.g., 200 Hz).

5. **Comprehensive Analysis and Validation:** A further motivation is to conduct a thorough investigation into the effects of UPS on the Cost of Transport (CoT) across various hopping speeds and frequencies. This study is intended to provide insights into optimal design and control strategies for robots with parallel elasticity. The ultimate motivation is to experimentally validate the proposed framework through both simulation and hardware tests, quantitatively demonstrating improvements in energy efficiency.

The main contributions of this paper, as stated by the authors, are:
- The development of a hierarchical kinodynamic MPC framework that explicitly incorporates UPS dynamics for improved energy efficiency in legged locomotion.
- A comprehensive study of UPS impact on CoT across hopping speeds and frequencies.
- Experimental validation showing a 38.8% reduction in CoT in simulation and a 14.8% reduction in energy consumption in preliminary hardware experiments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a hierarchical control framework designed to enable energy-efficient dynamic locomotion in legged robots equipped with Unidirectional Parallel Springs (UPS). This framework consists of three layers, each employing a different dynamic model with increasing fidelity, and a dedicated swing leg controller.

**A. Hierarchical Control Architecture:**
The overall architecture operates by generating motion references from simplified models and progressively refining them with more detailed models, ensuring real-time performance.
1. **SLIP (Spring-Loaded Inverted Pendulum) Model (Motion Sketch Generation):** This is the highest level of abstraction. Given a desired forward velocity, the SLIP model generates a preliminary Center of Mass (CoM) trajectory, ground reaction force (GRF), and touchdown foot location (parameterized by a touchdown angle α). This is achieved by finding an optimal apex state and touchdown angle that minimizes the difference in apex states over a return map, using a nonlinear least-squares problem. An offline gait library provides optimal state-control pairs and deadbeat feedback controllers for various desired apex velocities, allowing for real-time interpolation.
2. **SRB (Single Rigid Body) MPC (Fast Warm-Start):** The motion sketch from the SLIP model serves as the reference for this layer. This convex MPC uses the SRB model, which captures the rotational dynamics of the robot torso in addition to CoM dynamics. It linearizes the SRB dynamics around the desired state and foothold locations (computed from the SLIP output) and incorporates friction cone constraints. The objective is to minimize tracking errors for state and GRF. This problem is formulated as a Quadratic Program (QP) and solved efficiently, providing a warm-start for the subsequent kinodynamic MPC.
3. **Kinodynamic MPC for Robots with Elasticity:** This is the most detailed MPC layer and the core contribution. It takes the trajectories generated by the SRB MPC as an initial guess, enabling robust real-time control at 200 Hz. This layer explicitly accounts for the dynamics of elastic springs in the joint space during the stance phase.

**B. Dynamic Models:**
The hierarchical framework utilizes three dynamic models:
- **SLIP Model:** Represents the robot as a point-mass with a massless, prismatic spring leg. It alternates between ballistic flight and spring-driven stance phases. Equations describe ballistic flight and stance dynamics (m¨pc = mg + ks(∥r∥ − r0)ˆr).
- **SRB Model:** Builds upon the SLIP CoM trajectory by incorporating the rotational dynamics of the robot torso. It models the torso pitch angle (θ) and angular velocity (˙θ) and moment of inertia (I). Dynamics are described by a state-space model for CoM position, torso angle, and their velocities, influenced by GRF.
- **Kinodynamic Model:** Extends the SRB model by including joint angles (q) and motor torques (τ) as decision variables during the stance phase, crucial for reasoning about the UPS.

**C. Kinodynamic MPC Formulation:**
The kinodynamic MPC is formulated as a Nonlinear Program (NLP) with the following components:
- **Constraints:**
 - **Linearized Discrete Dynamics:** The nonlinear SRB dynamics are linearized around a reference trajectory to form a Linear Time-Varying (LTV) system.
 - **Nonlinear Kinematics Constraint:** Foothold position is constrained by forward kinematics: p_f = FK(p_c, θ, q).
 - **Joint Torque Constraint with UPS:** The effect of the parallel elastic springs is explicitly modeled: τ + τ_s(q) = S ⋅ J^T f, where τ_s(q) is the spring torque, J is the foot Jacobian, and S is a selection matrix.
 - **Joint Position and Torque Limits:** Box constraints are applied: q_min ≤ q ≤ q_max and ∥τ ∥ ≤ τ_max.
 - **Friction Cone Constraints:** Prevent foot slippage: −μ f_k,z ≤ f_k,x ≤ μ f_k,z and 0 ≤ f_k,z ≤ c_k f_max.
- **Objectives:** The objective function (ℓ_KD) minimizes a weighted sum of state tracking errors (∥x − x_des∥^2_Q), GRF tracking errors (∥f − f_des∥^2_R_f), and the sum of squared joint torques (∥τ ∥^2_R_τ). A terminal objective function (ℓ_f) is also included.
- **Formulation:** The NLP minimizes the objective function over a prediction horizon (N), incorporating a decay rate (γ) and respecting all defined constraints.

**D. Energy Consumption Modeling and Metric:**
- **Metric:** The Cost of Transport (CoT), defined as P/(mgv), is used for energy efficiency.
- **Power Model:** The instantaneous power consumption (P+) accounts for both mechanical power and Joule heating, assuming no battery regeneration (only positive power is accumulated): P+_k = max(τ^T_k ˙q_k, 0) + ∥τ_k/K_t∥^2 R, where R is winding resistance and K_t is the torque constant.

**E. Solution Strategy of the Optimization Problem:**
- SLIP and SRB MPC problems are solved efficiently offline (SLIP gait library) or in real-time (QP for SRB MPC).
- The kinodynamic MPC, being an NLP, is solved using the Sequential Quadratic Program (SQP) method. Each SQP iteration involves solving a QP subproblem, which is handled by the OSQP solver. This approach allows for trading solution accuracy for computational speed by solving a fixed number of iterations, enabling real-time operation through warm-starting from the SRB MPC solution.

**F. Swing Leg Control:**
A separate swing controller tracks the desired foot trajectory. This trajectory is generated using a B´ezier polynomial, interpolated between liftoff and touchdown positions, and influenced by the touchdown angle α from the SLIP deadbeat policy. Inverse kinematics provides desired joint angles for a proportional-derivative (PD) control scheme (τ = K_P(q_des - q) - K_D ˙q). A centroidal momentum-based algorithm assists in apex detection.

**G. Controller Details:**
Desired CoM states and GRFs are interpolated from the SLIP model, while desired rotational states are set to zero. An adaptive timestep segmentation is employed, considering a nominal discretization timestep and the predefined contact schedule to accurately capture discrete phase changes.

**H. Robot Platform (MUPS):**
The proposed framework is validated on the Monoped with Unidirectional Parallel Spring (MUPS), a custom hopping robot. MUPS features a hip and knee quasi-direct drive (QDD) motor setup, a free-rotating torso, and a gantry constraint for sagittal plane motion. The UPS mechanism is integrated into the knee joint via a four-bar linkage, utilizing a linear compression spring and slider to store and release energy.

**I. Computational Setup:**
The MPC formulations are implemented using CasADi, with C code generation for real-time performance. The controller runs on a desktop computer (Intel Core i7-13700) at 200 Hz, communicating with the robot via LCM. Low-level control and state estimation (Kalman filter) run on a Raspberry Pi.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents simulation and preliminary hardware experimental results to evaluate the performance and energy efficiency of the proposed kinodynamic MPC framework for the Monoped with Unidirectional Parallel Spring (MUPS) robot.

**A. Simulation Experiments:**
Simulations were conducted in the MuJoCo physics simulator using ground truth states for the controller.

1. **Velocity Tracking:**
 - The MUPS robot demonstrated effective velocity tracking, successfully converging to desired forward velocity profiles (e.g., accelerating from 0 m/s to 1.0 m/s, then 2.0 m/s, then 1.5 m/s) within approximately three jumps.
 - Torso pitch angle was regularized even during rapid velocity changes. The maximum pitch deviation observed was 0.21 rad during a swift acceleration from a stationary position to 1 m/s, attributed to fast leg retraction.

2. **Cost of Transport (CoT) vs. Forward Velocity:**
 - A study investigating CoT across a range of forward velocities (0.5 m/s to 2.3 m/s) showed that the MUPS robot, with UPS engaged, achieved consistently lower CoT compared to a monoped without UPS.
 - On average, incorporating UPS resulted in a 38.8% reduction in CoT across the tested velocity range. This indicates that the UPS effectively improves energy efficiency during high-speed hopping.

3. **CoT vs. Hopping Frequency:**
 - When commanded to run at a constant speed of 1 m/s for 10 continuous jumps, the robot with UPS exhibited lower CoT across hopping frequencies ranging from 1.9 Hz to 2.7 Hz (varied by adjusting the SLIP spring constant).
 - Without UPS, the monoped achieved a minimum CoT of 0.79 at a hopping frequency of 2.3 Hz. In contrast, the MUPS, with UPS, achieved a minimum CoT of 0.39 at a frequency of 2.1 Hz.
 - The results suggest that engaging UPS allows for a more compliant SLIP spring constant, which reduces the load on the motor.

**B. Hardware Experiment:**
Preliminary hardware experiments were conducted on the MUPS robot, constrained to sagittal plane motion by a gantry system, with a desktop computer running the MPC controller and a Raspberry Pi for low-level motor control and state estimation.

1. **Preliminary Hopping Results (In-place Hopping):**
 - An in-place hopping experiment, involving 10 consecutive hops while maintaining torso stability, was used to evaluate energy efficiency, measured as total energy consumption (CoT is not applicable due to zero horizontal displacement).
 - Each condition (with and without UPS) was tested three times. The average total energy consumed with UPS was 81.19 J (± 1.01 J), while without UPS, it was 95.32 J (± 3.43 J).
 - On average, the integration of UPS reduced energy consumption by 14.8% in hardware experiments.

2. **Torque Distribution:**
 - Analysis of motor torque distributions during the in-place hopping showed that the UPS mechanism specifically affected the knee motor.
 - The UPS shifted the knee motor torque distribution towards zero, indicating a reduction in both average and peak torques. Specifically, the average knee torque decreased from 11.4 Nm to 9.4 Nm, and the upper quartile torque was reduced from 16.4 Nm to 13.7 Nm. This suggests that the parallel spring effectively absorbed and redistributed a portion of the load, reducing the burden on the knee actuator.
 - The hip motor torque distribution remained largely unaffected by the UPS, implying that the primary influence of the UPS is concentrated on the knee joint dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates a significant advancement in the control of energy-efficient legged robots by successfully integrating a kinodynamic Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework with Unidirectional Parallel Springs (UPS).

**Significance of the Work:**
1. **Validation of Kinodynamic MPC for Elastic Robots:** The work establishes the effectiveness of kinodynamic MPC in leveraging joint elasticity to enhance energy efficiency. It showcases how this control paradigm can explicitly account for UPS dynamics within its optimization problem, a capability crucial for robots aiming for both dynamic performance and energy economy.
2. **Effective Hierarchical Control for Real-time Performance:** The proposed hierarchical control framework, utilizing SLIP for motion sketching, convex SRB MPC for warm-starting, and kinodynamic MPC for detailed control, addresses the computational challenges of solving complex non-linear programs in real-time. This hierarchical structure enables robust and agile running performance at high control rates (200 Hz), which is critical for dynamic locomotion tasks.
3. **Quantitative Energy Efficiency Improvements:** Both simulation and hardware results provide quantitative evidence of the benefits of UPS. A 38.8% reduction in Cost of Transport (CoT) in simulation and a 14.8% reduction in energy consumption in preliminary hardware experiments demonstrate the tangible impact of the UPS mechanism and the proposed control strategy on energy efficiency.
4. **Reduction in Motor Torques:** The analysis of torque distribution highlights that UPS effectively shifts the knee motor torque towards zero, reducing both average and peak torques. This is significant as it directly translates to less effort required from the motors, potentially leading to lower heat generation, increased motor lifespan, and the possibility of using smaller, lighter actuators for a given performance level.
5. **Comprehensive Analysis of UPS Impact:** The detailed studies on CoT across varying speeds and hopping frequencies provide valuable insights into the operating characteristics and optimal control strategies for robots with parallel elasticity. This information can guide future robot design and control parameter tuning.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Enhanced Autonomy and Operational Duration:** The demonstrated improvements in energy efficiency can significantly extend the operational time of legged robots, particularly those deployed in remote or challenging environments such as disaster zones, planetary surfaces, or inspection tasks, where frequent recharging is not feasible. This directly contributes to greater mission autonomy.
2. **Scalability to Complex Legged Systems:** The principles and the hierarchical control framework demonstrated on a monoped robot hold potential for scaling to more complex legged systems, including bipedal and humanoid robots. Achieving similar energy savings in these multi-limbed systems could unlock new capabilities for tasks requiring prolonged dynamic interaction with diverse terrains.
3. **Advancements in Robot Design and Actuation:** The effectiveness of UPS in reducing motor loads suggests avenues for optimizing robot hardware. Designers could potentially use less powerful or lighter motors, leading to overall lighter and more agile robot platforms, while maintaining or even improving dynamic performance. Further optimization of UPS design, such as adaptive stiffness or engagement mechanisms, is a promising future direction.
4. **Improved Robustness and Agility:** By reducing the burden on actuators and enabling better management of dynamic forces through elasticity, robots could achieve greater robustness to external disturbances and maintain high agility over longer durations.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified discrepancy between simulation and hardware energy reduction (38.8% vs. 14.8%) points to unmodeled real-world factors (e.g., joint friction, electrical losses, non-ideal spring behavior). This gap serves as a clear direction for future work, focusing on refining system models to better capture these non-idealities, which could further enhance prediction accuracy and control performance.
