# Distributional value gradients for stochastic environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at PSI and KU Leuven introduced Distributional Sobolev Reinforcement Learning, a framework that explicitly models the joint distribution of returns and their action-gradients in stochastic environments. The resulting DSDPG algorithm demonstrates enhanced robustness and performance in continuous control tasks, outperforming deterministic gradient-aware methods, particularly in noisy and multimodal settings, and achieving competitive results in standard MuJoCo environments.

## Problem

- Traditional gradient-aware continuous control methods struggle in stochastic or noisy environments due to their deterministic treatment of action gradient information.
- Existing distributional reinforcement learning approaches model the distribution of returns but do not account for uncertainty in the gradients of the value function, which are crucial for stable policy updates.
- The inherent randomness of real-world environments affects action gradients, leading to suboptimal or unstable policy learning if this uncertainty is not explicitly modeled.

## Method

- Developed Distributional Sobolev Reinforcement Learning, a novel framework that models the ==joint distribution== of returns and their action-gradients using a (1 + |A|)-dimensional random variable, addressing uncertainty in both.
- Introduced the Sobolev Distributional Bellman Operator, which bootstraps this joint distribution and explicitly integrates action-gradients into the temporal difference target for a comprehensive update signal.
- Implemented the Distributional Sobolev Deterministic Policy Gradient (DSDPG) algorithm, utilizing a reparameterized Sobolev critic, a one-step conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE) as a differentiable world model, and Max-sliced Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MSMMD) for distributional loss calculation.

## Results

- In a synthetic toy problem with increasing multimodality, DSDPG (MMD Sobolev and MSMMD Sobolev) consistently outperformed all baselines, including deterministic Sobolev methods, by effectively leveraging gradient information and exhibiting robustness to return distribution multimodality.
- Across various MuJoCo environments, DSDPG variants matched or surpassed competitors, showing particular advantages in tasks with multiplicative observation noise or additive Gaussian dynamics noise, notably improving performance in Ant-v2 and Humanoid-v2.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of overestimation bias correction for stable learning and demonstrated that DSDPG's benefits stem from its ability to exploit gradient information in stochastic settings, rather than the specific world-model architecture.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Sobolev-augmented Bellman operator is a contraction mapping with a unique fixed point, theoretically proven under both Wasserstein distance and Max-sliced Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MSMMD), ensuring convergence.
> - A fundamental "smoothness trade-off" (γκ < 1) exists for contraction, linking the discount factor (γ) and the smoothness assumptions (κ) of environmental dynamics and policy sensitivities to the stability of gradient-aware training.
> - Explicitly modeling the distribution of action-gradients provides a richer, uncertainty-aware signal to the actor, leading to more stable and effective policy optimization in stochastic continuous control tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically addressing challenges in continuous control environments using off-policy actor-critic algorithms. It integrates and extends two distinct lines of work: the use of action-gradients for policy optimization and distributional reinforcement learning.

Traditional value-based continuous control methods, such as DDPG, rely on a critic's value estimates to provide action-gradients for policy updates. However, these methods often implicitly learn these gradients through value prediction, which can suffer if the true value function lacks sufficient smoothness. Previous work, including methods like MAGE (D'Oro & Jaskowski, 2020) and Garibbo et al. (2024), attempted to improve this by explicitly incorporating gradient information into critic training, often by backpropagating through a learned, differentiable world model of transition dynamics and rewards.

Simultaneously, distributional RL (Bellemare et al., 2017; 2023) has emerged to address environments with irreducible uncertainty by modeling the entire distribution of returns rather than just their expectation. This approach provides a richer learning signal and has shown performance gains in various tasks.

The present work identifies a limitation in existing gradient-aware methods: their struggle in stochastic or noisy environments due to their deterministic treatment of gradient information. The paper posits that randomness inherently affects action gradients, and current methods do not account for this uncertainty. By combining distributional modeling with gradient-aware training, this research aims to overcome these limitations, providing a more robust and sample-efficient approach to continuous control in stochastic settings. It therefore aims to bridge the gap between deterministic gradient-informed learning and uncertainty-aware distributional RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and sample-efficient reinforcement learning framework for continuous state-action spaces that explicitly accounts for uncertainty in both returns and their gradients in stochastic environments. The authors identify a key limitation in current gradient-regularized value learning methods, such as MAGE, which leverage learned models to estimate return gradients: these methods struggle when environmental dynamics or rewards are stochastic or noisy. This struggle arises because existing approaches treat action gradients deterministically, failing to capture the inherent randomness that affects how optimal actions and their resulting returns change.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Addressing limitations of deterministic gradient methods:** In many real-world scenarios, environments exhibit irreducible uncertainty in transitions and rewards. When action gradients (which steer policy optimization) are themselves noisy or stochastic, deterministic gradient models may lead to unstable or suboptimal policy updates, diminishing the sample-efficiency benefits these methods typically offer. The authors argue that randomness impacts action gradients, particularly in high-dimensional action spaces.
- **Extending distributional reinforcement learning:** Distributional RL has demonstrated its capability to capture the full spectrum of returns, providing a richer signal than just expected values. The authors aim to extend this concept by not only modeling the distribution over scalar state-action value functions but also over their corresponding gradients. This allows for a more comprehensive understanding of uncertainty, including how it propagates through the gradient of the value function.
- **Improving credit assignment and policy optimization:** The quality of the critic's value and gradient estimates directly influences the effectiveness of policy optimization in continuous-action settings. By explicitly modeling the distribution of action-gradients, the proposed method seeks to provide a more accurate and uncertainty-aware signal to the actor, thereby boosting policy and value learning stability and performance, especially in uncertain environments.
- **Developing a novel theoretical foundation:** The integration of gradient information into a distributional framework necessitates a new theoretical foundation, including a novel Bellman operator that bootstraps both return and gradient distributions. This involves proving contraction properties under appropriate metrics to ensure the stability and convergence of the learning process.

In essence, the core motivation is to enhance the applicability and performance of gradient-aware RL in complex, stochastic environments by moving beyond deterministic gradient estimations to a comprehensive distributional modeling of both values and their sensitivities to actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces **Distributional Sobolev Reinforcement Learning** as a novel framework to address the limitations of deterministic gradient-regularized value learning in stochastic environments. This approach models the joint distribution of returns and their action-gradients.

#### 3.1. Core Theoretical Framework

- **Random Action Sobolev Return (Z_S_a (s, a)):** The method extends the concept of a random return to a joint random variable that encompasses both the discounted cumulative reward and its action-gradient. This is represented as a (1 + |A|)-dimensional vector, where |A| is the dimension of the action space.
- **Sobolev Distributional Bellman Operator (T_S_a_π):** A novel Bellman operator is defined to bootstrap the joint distribution of returns and action-gradients. The operator pushes forward the next-step law of the Sobolev return through a pointwise affine map. This map incorporates the immediate reward (r), its action-gradient (∂r/∂a), and scaled terms involving the next-state return gradient (∂_s Z(s', a')) and next-action return gradient (∂_a Z(s', a')), along with Jacobians of the transition dynamics (∂f/∂a) and policy (∂π/∂s). This formulation explicitly integrates the action-gradient into the temporal difference target, differentiating it from methods that use it solely as a regularization term. The paper also discusses a "complete" Sobolev operator that includes state-gradients (∂_s Z) but focuses on the action-gradient version for clarity and computational tractability.
- **Contraction Analysis and Fixed Point:** The paper provides theoretical proofs establishing that the Sobolev-augmented Bellman operator is a contraction mapping with a unique fixed point under certain conditions.
 - **Wasserstein Distance:** Contraction is first proven under the supremum-p-Wasserstein distance (Theorem 1), assuming bounded Jacobians for the transition, policy, and a Lipschitz coupling between state and return-gradients.
 - **Max-sliced Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MSMMD):** Recognizing the computational challenges of Wasserstein distance, the authors introduce MSMMD as a practical alternative. They prove that the Sobolev operator also contracts under the supremum-max-sliced MMD, given a kernel that satisfies translation invariance, scale-contraction, and mixture p-convexity (Theorem 2).
 - **Smoothness Trade-off:** The contraction proofs highlight a fundamental trade-off: contraction is achieved if γκ < 1, where γ is the discount factor and κ depends on the smoothness assumptions (bounded Jacobians of dynamics and policy sensitivities). This implies that either smoothness must be enforced (reducing κ) or the effective horizon shortened (reducing γ).

#### 3.2. Algorithmic Implementation (Distributional Sobolev Deterministic Policy Gradient - DSDPG)

To translate the theoretical framework into a practical algorithm, DSDPG employs several key components:

- **Sobolev Inductive Bias:** The method relies on the principle of modeling a gradient by a gradient. For a neural network critic, `f_φ`, approximating a target function `F`, the input gradient `∇_x f_φ(x)` is used to approximate `∇_x F(x)`.
- **Reparameterized Sobolev Critic:** The joint return-and-gradient distribution η_S_a_π (s, a) is modeled by a generative network, `Z_S_a_ϕ : (s, a, ξ) → (Z_ϕ(s, a, ξ), ∇_a Z_ϕ(s, a, ξ))`, where ξ is sampled from a standard normal distribution. This provides a purely sample-based approach for generating joint return-gradient samples, avoiding intractable likelihoods and scaling to high-dimensional actions.
- **Overestimation Bias Correction:** To mitigate overestimation issues common in value-based methods, the DSDPG algorithm adopts a strategy inspired by Truncated Quantile Critic (TQC). It trains two distributional critics, draws samples from both, and then discards the top p% (25%) of the values (based on return magnitude) from the concatenated samples to form the target distribution, thereby inducing an underestimation bias.
- **One-step World Model (Conditional Variational Autoencoder - cVAE):** To overcome the non-differentiability of real-world environments, a stochastic, differentiable world model `g(s, a, ε)` is learned. This model approximates the true transition-reward distribution `P(s', r | s, a)`. A cVAE (Sohn etal., 2015) is used for this purpose, predicting `(s', r)` from `(s, a, ε)`. The cVAE enables cheap sampling and computation of reparameterized gradients `∂(s', r)/∂(s, a)`, which are crucial for the Sobolev Bellman update.
- **Loss Function and Policy Update:** The critic is trained by minimizing the MSMMD (or plain MMD as an alternative) loss between its predicted Sobolev-return distribution and the bootstrapped Sobolev-Bellman targets. The actor (policy) is then updated by ascending the gradient of the critic’s *mean* estimated return.
- **Dyna-style Training:** The system operates in a Dyna setting, where new samples are drawn from the world model for every critic update, enhancing sample efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates DSDPG across a synthetic toy problem and several standard MuJoCo environments under various stochastic conditions.

#### 4.1. Toy Reinforcement Learning Problem

- **Environment:** A 2D point-mass task with multiple (N) possible bonus locations, one of which is randomly chosen and hidden from the agent. The task becomes increasingly multimodal and uncertain as N increases.
- **Findings:** DSDPG, implemented with both MSMMD and MMD losses (termed "MMD Sobolev" and "MSMMD Sobolev"), consistently outperformed all baselines. This includes a deterministic Sobolev method (Huber Sobolev, analogous to MAGE) and other gradient-agnostic critics. DSDPG demonstrated effective use of gradient information and robustness to the growing multimodality of the return distribution. MSMMD Sobolev exhibited a mild advantage over MMD Sobolev. The deterministic Sobolev baseline did not show a clear advantage over gradient-agnostic methods in this stochastic setting.

#### 4.2. MuJoCo Environments

- **Environments:** Standard MuJoCo tasks (e.g., Ant-v2, Humanoid-v2, Walker2d-v2, etc.).
- **Baselines:** TD3-Huber, deterministic Sobolev (MAGE), IQN, and standard MMD training.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Final evaluation performance and normalized Area Under the Curve (nAUC) over the training process.
- **Settings:**
 - **Normal (Noise-free):** DSDPG variants (MMD Sobolev and MSMMD Sobolev) generally matched the performance of all baselines across the six MuJoCo tasks.
 - **Multiplicative Observation Noise:** At the start of each episode, observations were scaled by a random factor `n ∼ U[0.8, 1.2]`, inducing partial observability and increasing task difficulty. In this setting, DSDPG outperformed every competitor in three out of six environments, particularly showing strong advantages in Ant-v2 and Humanoid-v2. The deterministic Sobolev method (MAGE) experienced significant performance drops and higher variance in tasks like Walker2d-v2 and Humanoid-v2.
 - **Additive Gaussian Dynamics Noise:** Zero-mean Gaussian noise was injected directly into positions and velocities. DSDPG again outperformed baselines in three out of six environments. Notably, on Ant-v2, DSDPG showed a more robust performance compared to other methods under the increased noise scale.
- **General Observation:** DSDPG variants performed on par with or better than competing methods, with notable advantages in harder tasks and under noisy environmental conditions.

#### 4.3. Additional Ablation Studies

- **World-model Ablation:** Replacing the cVAE world model with a normalizing flow model yielded qualitatively similar results. This indicates that the benefits of Distributional Sobolev TD arise from its ability to exploit gradient information in stochastic environments, rather than from the specific generative architecture used for the world model.
- **Overestimation Bias Correction:** Ablations confirmed that the overestimation bias correction (TQC-like truncation for DSDPG, double estimation for deterministic baselines) is crucial for stabilizing learning and achieving high performance.
- **Sensitivity Analysis:** Experiments explored the sensitivity of DSDPG to kernel bandwidth, noise scale, number of Sobolev samples, and world-model capacity. The method generally demonstrated robustness to these parameters, though some environments showed specific sensitivities (e.g., Ant-v2 to the number of samples).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of reinforcement learning, particularly in continuous control under stochastic dynamics.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Novel Framework for Uncertainty-Aware Gradient Learning:** The introduction of "Distributional Sobolev Reinforcement Learning" represents a conceptual advance by explicitly modeling the joint distribution of returns *and* their action-gradients. This addresses a critical limitation of previous gradient-aware methods, which struggled with inherent randomness in gradients. By capturing the uncertainty in gradients, the framework provides a richer and more stable signal for policy optimization in stochastic environments.
- **Theoretical Foundations for Gradient-Aware RL:** The development of the "Sobolev Temporal Difference" framework provides the first contraction proofs for gradient-aware RL, covering both the theoretically favored Wasserstein distance and the practically tractable Max-sliced Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MSMMD). This theoretical grounding validates the convergence properties of the proposed learning paradigm. The identification of a "smoothness trade-off" for contraction (γ κ < 1) offers a fundamental insight into the conditions required for stable gradient-aware training, linking environmental dynamics and policy sensitivities to the discount factor.
- **Algorithmic Innovation (DSDPG):** The proposed Distributional Sobolev Deterministic Policy Gradient (DSDPG) algorithm offers a concrete, practical instantiation of the theoretical framework. It integrates several advanced components: a reparameterized Sobolev critic, a one-step conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE) as a differentiable world model, and MSMMD (or MMD) for distributional loss calculation. The adaptation of overestimation bias correction techniques (e.g., TQC-like truncation) further enhances its stability.
- **Empirical Robustness in Stochastic Environments:** The empirical results demonstrate that DSDPG effectively leverages gradient information and exhibits superior robustness to noise and multimodality compared to deterministic gradient-aware methods and other distributional baselines. This is particularly evident in the controlled toy problem and under various noise perturbations in MuJoCo environments, especially in high-dimensional tasks.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Performance in Real-World Robotics and Control:** Many real-world applications, such as robotics, autonomous driving, or industrial control, operate in inherently noisy and uncertain environments. By providing more stable and accurate gradient information, DSDPG could lead to more robust and reliable policies in these domains, potentially improving the safety and efficiency of autonomous systems.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** The ability to learn a comprehensive distribution over gradients in a sample-efficient manner, particularly with the use of a differentiable world model (Dyna-style training), could reduce the amount of real-world interaction data required to train high-performing agents.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** The Sobolev Temporal Difference framework and its associated contraction proofs lay a foundation for further theoretical exploration in gradient-aware and distributional RL. The identified computational challenges (e.g., with the complete Sobolev operator) and the need for more efficient inductive biases suggest fruitful avenues for future work.
- **Cross-Domain Applications:** The core ideas of modeling aleatoric uncertainty in gradient information may extend beyond RL to other fields where derivatives of uncertain functions are critical. This includes areas such as Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) for solving stochastic differential equations, Neural Volume Rendering, and other applications of "double backpropagation."

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The paper also acknowledges several limitations and proposes directions for future research. A primary concern is the **high computational cost** of the current DSDPG implementation, as policy evaluation and improvement necessitate multiple samples from the distributional critic and their input gradients. Exploring more efficient inductive biases or architectural choices could mitigate this. Additionally, while the paper introduces the "complete" Sobolev operator (including state-gradients), the main algorithmic focus is on the action-gradient variant due to **computational tractability issues** with jointly handling both. Future work could aim to overcome these challenges, as a complete Sobolev operator would offer even richer information about return variations with dynamics. The authors also suggest further exploration of the applicability of their ideas to other fields, as mentioned above.
