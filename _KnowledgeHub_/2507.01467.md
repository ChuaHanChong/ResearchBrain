# Representation Entanglement for Generation: Training Diffusion Transformers Is Much Easier Than You Think

> [!summary] Summary
> Representation Entanglement for Generation (REG) introduces an image-class denoising paradigm, achieving up to 63x faster training for Diffusion Transformers while setting a new FID record of 1.8 on ImageNet 256x256 by structurally integrating a high-level class token with image latents.

## Problem

- High-fidelity image synthesis in diffusion models often requires extensive training iterations and computational resources.
- Previous methods, such as REPA, improve representation quality during training but lack an active mechanism to leverage discriminative information during the denoising inference process.
- Generative representations within diffusion models generally lag behind the discriminative power of dedicated, large-scale pretrained vision models like DINOv2.

## Method

- **REG** proposes an "==image-class denoising== paradigm" that injects Gaussian noise simultaneously into both image latents and a high-level class token extracted from a vision foundation model.
- The noised class token is spatially concatenated with patchified image latents, forming a unified input for Scalable Interpolant Transformer (SiT) blocks.
- The model is trained with dual objectives: a prediction loss for both the noised image latents and the noised class token, alongside an alignment loss for intermediate transformer layer features.

## Results

- REG achieved up to **63x** faster training convergence than the baseline SiT-XL/2 and **23x** faster than SiT-XL/2 + REPA, significantly reducing GPU hours required for training.
- A SiT-XL/2 + REG model attained a new record-low FID of 1.8 on ImageNet 256x256 at **4M** training steps without classifier-free guidance.
- These substantial improvements in training efficiency and generation quality were accomplished with negligible additional cost, specifically less than a **0.5%** increase in parameters, FLOPs, and inference latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structurally integrating a discriminative class token directly into the input and denoising process enables continuous, autonomous semantic guidance throughout the entire generative pipeline, including inference.
> - Jointly reconstructing image latents and their corresponding global semantics from noise leads to a more coherent and efficient generation process, improving both quality and convergence.
> - Leveraging rich, high-level discriminative signals from foundation models through entanglement significantly boosts generative model performance with minimal additional computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative models has seen rapid advancements, moving from early Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) and Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIMs) to more sophisticated architectures like Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) that operate in compressed latent spaces, and Diffusion Transformers (DiT) which enhance scalability through transformer-based designs. Scalable Interpolant Transformers (SiT) represent a further unification of these approaches using continuous-time stochastic interpolants. Despite these innovations, a persistent challenge remains: achieving high-fidelity image synthesis typically demands extensive training iterations and computational resources. Existing methods to accelerate training, such as masked training and multi-scale optimization, offer partial solutions but often involve significant architectural modifications.

A parallel line of research has explored the discriminative capabilities of generative models, demonstrating that their intermediate features encode rich semantic representations useful for downstream tasks like segmentation and depth estimation. However, these "generative representations" still lag behind those derived from dedicated, large-scale pretrained vision models like DINOv2 and CLIP in terms of discriminative power (as quantified by metrics like CKNNA). This performance gap has motivated approaches that leverage these powerful external "foundation model" representations to boost generative model training.

"Representation Alignment for Generation (REPA)" and its extension "REPA-E" exemplify this trend. REPA improves semantic representation quality by aligning the internal features of diffusion models with those from pretrained vision models during training. REPA-E further refines this by enabling end-to-end VAE tuning, quantitatively demonstrating that better alignment directly translates to improved generation fidelity. However, the critical limitation of REPA, as identified by the authors of this paper, is that its external alignment mechanism is entirely absent during the denoising *inference* process. This means the discriminative information, though learned, cannot actively guide generation when samples are actually being produced from noise, thus underutilizing its potential.

"Representation Entanglement for Generation (REG)" directly addresses this lacuna. It posits that current methods, by solely focusing on image denoising, miss an opportunity to continuously leverage discriminative semantic guidance. REG introduces an "image-class denoising paradigm," fundamentally shifting from pure image denoising to a joint process of reconstructing both image latents and their corresponding global semantics from noise. By entangling a high-level class token from a pretrained foundation model directly into the input and training objective, REG ensures that discriminative information is not just externally aligned but *structurally integrated* into the generative model's forward and inference passes. This enables the model to autonomously generate and utilize semantic guidance throughout the entire generative process, aiming for superior generation quality and significantly faster convergence without altering the core architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind Representation Entanglement for Generation (REG) stems from a critical observation about existing state-of-the-art diffusion models, particularly those leveraging external discriminative representations like REPA. While REPA successfully improves representation quality by aligning internal features with foundation vision models during training, this alignment mechanism is "absent during the entire denoising inference process," thereby limiting the full potential of the discriminative information learned. The authors explicitly state that this structural limitation likely "impedes further advancements in discriminative semantic learning and overall generative capability."

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of REG are multi-faceted:

1. **Fully Unleash Discriminative Potential:** The primary aim is to develop an efficient framework that can more effectively harness the power of discriminative visual representations from pretrained foundation models. Unlike prior methods, REG seeks to explicitly reflow this discriminative information into the *entire* generation process, including inference.
2. **Improve Generation Quality:** A core objective is to achieve significantly higher fidelity and semantic coherence in generated images. By integrating consistent semantic guidance, the model should produce more realistic and contextually appropriate outputs.
3. **Accelerate Training Convergence:** A major practical goal is to drastically reduce the computational resources and time required to train high-fidelity diffusion models. The paper seeks to demonstrate that "training Diffusion Transformers is much easier than you think" by achieving faster convergence rates.
4. **Enhance Discriminative Semantic Learning:** Beyond just generating images, REG aims to make the generative models themselves better "representation learners," capable of acquiring and utilizing rich discriminative semantic information internally.
5. **Minimal Computational Overhead:** All these improvements must be achieved with negligible additional cost during inference. The goal is to provide substantial gains by adding only a "single additional token" for denoising, ensuring practicality and scalability.
6. **Introduce a Novel Paradigm:** The overarching objective is to propose and validate a new "image-class denoising paradigm" that jointly reconstructs image latents and their corresponding global semantics directly from noise. This paradigm shift ensures that acquired semantic knowledge actively guides and enhances the image generation process continuously.

In essence, REG is motivated by the desire to bridge the gap between the discriminative power of foundation models and the generative capabilities of diffusion models, creating a more semantically aware and computationally efficient generative framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

REG builds upon the framework of Scalable Interpolant Transformers (SiT), which provides a unified perspective for flow and diffusion models. The core innovation of REG lies in its "Representation Entanglement for Generation," which explicitly entangles low-level image latents with a single high-level class token from pretrained foundation models, ensuring discriminative guidance throughout both training and inference.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries (SiT Overview)

The paper first reviews the basics of SiT, which unifies flow and diffusion models. Both transform Gaussian noise into data samples (`x*`) via a stochastic process `x_t = α_t x* + σ_t ϵ`. The goal is to learn a velocity field `v(x_t, t)` that estimates `E[x* | x_t = x]` and `E[ϵ | x_t = x]`. This velocity field is then used to integrate an ODE or SDE in reverse to generate samples. The model `v_θ(x_t, t)` is trained to minimize the squared error between its prediction and the true velocity components.

#### 3.2. Representation Entanglement for Generation (REG)

**REG Training Process:**
1. **Input Generation:**
 - Given a clean input image `x*`, it is first encoded into image latents `z*` using a VAE encoder.
 - Concurrently, a vision foundation encoder (e.g., DINOv2) extracts a high-level class token `cls*` from `x*`. This `cls*` is a single token `R^(1x D_vf)`, representing global semantics.
2. **Synchronized Noise Injection:** Unlike previous methods that might only noise image latents, REG injects Gaussian noise (`ϵ_z` and `ϵ_cls`) into *both* the image latents `z*` and the class token `cls*` simultaneously. This creates noised intermediate states `z_t` and `cls_t` at continuous time `t`.
3. **Spatial Concatenation:**
 - The noised image latents `z_t` are patchified into `z'_t`.
 - The noised class token `cls_t` is projected into the same embedding space as `z'_t` to obtain `cls'_t`.
 - These are then spatially concatenated to form a unified input `h_t = [cls'_t; z'_t]` which is fed into the SiT blocks. This single, high-level class token is positioned at the beginning of the sequence of image latent tokens.
4. **Dual Loss Objectives:**
 - **Prediction Loss (`L_pred`):** The model is trained to predict the velocity for *both* the noised image latents `z_t` and the noised class token `cls_t`. This is a crucial distinction, as the class token is now an active component of the denoising task. A weighting parameter `β` controls the relative importance of denoising the class token.
 - **Alignment Loss (`L_REPA`):** Similar to REPA, an alignment loss is applied at specific intermediate transformer layers (`n`). The projected hidden state `h_ϕ(H_n_t)` from these layers is aligned, using cosine similarity, with a reference representation `y*`. This `y*` is formed by concatenating the *clean* class token `cls*` and the *clean* image features `f*` (also from the vision foundation encoder). This step ensures that the internal representations remain semantically meaningful.
 - **Total Loss (`L_total`):** The final training objective combines both losses: `L_total = L_pred + λL_REPA`, where `λ` balances the prediction and alignment objectives.

**REG Inference Process:**
Crucially, REG operates *without* any auxiliary networks during inference.
1. **Joint Reconstruction:** Starting from random noise initialization, REG's denoising process concurrently reconstructs *both* the image latents *and* their corresponding global semantics (the class token).
2. **Active Semantic Guidance:** The class token, now an integral part of the input sequence, continually refines its semantic content alongside the image latents. The acquired semantic knowledge embedded within this reconstructed class token actively guides and enhances the image generation process through the attention mechanisms of the SiT transformer, from initial broad strokes to fine-grained details.

**Key Advantages of REG:**
- **Improved Utilization of Discriminative Information:** By making the class token an explicit input and a target for denoising, REG enables continuous, autonomous semantic guidance throughout the entire generative process, unlike REPA's inference-absent external alignment.
- **Minimal Computational Overhead:** The addition of a single class token introduces negligible overhead (<0.5% increase in FLOPs and latency), making it highly efficient.
- **Enhanced Performance:** REG delivers superior generation quality, significantly faster training convergence, and stronger discriminative semantic learning.

The methodology essentially introduces an "image-class denoising" paradigm, transforming the generative model into a joint image-and-semantic reconstructor, leading to a more coherent and efficient generation process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results overwhelmingly validate REG's effectiveness across multiple dimensions: generation quality, training efficiency, and discriminative semantic learning. All experiments were conducted on the ImageNet 256×256 dataset, with some extended to 512x512, using SiT as the backbone and various standard metrics like FID, sFID, IS, Precision, Recall, and CKNNA.

#### 4.1. Accelerating Training Convergence

- **Dramatic Speed-up:** REG demonstrates remarkable convergence acceleration. For the SiT-XL/2 model, REG achieves 63× faster training than the baseline SiT-XL/2 and 23× faster than SiT-XL/2 + REPA (Figure 2e, Table 1).
- **Outperforming Longer-Trained Models:** A SiT-L/2 + REG model trained for merely 400K iterations (10x fewer steps) outperforms a SiT-XL/2 + REPA model trained for 4M iterations. Similarly, SiT-XL/2 + REG achieves comparable FID scores to SiT-XL/2 (7M steps) and REPA-XL/2 (4M steps) in just 110K and 170K steps, respectively (Table 7). This translates to GPU hour reductions of 98.36% and 95.72%.
- **Record-Low FID:** At 4M training steps, SiT-XL/2 + REG achieves an FID of 1.8, setting a new record for this benchmark without classifier-free guidance.

#### 4.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

- **Competitive Performance:** With classifier-free guidance (CFG), REG achieves highly competitive performance. SiT-XL/2 + REG at 80 epochs matches the quality of SiT-XL/2 trained for 1400 epochs (17x faster). It also surpasses REPA's 800-epoch performance at just 480 epochs, with a lower FID (1.40 vs. 1.42) (Table 2, Table 8).
- **Robustness Across Scales:** REG consistently shows superior performance across SiT-B/2, SiT-L/2, and SiT-XL/2 model scales, achieving the lowest FID scores at comparable training iterations (Table 9).

#### 4.3. Computational Cost Efficiency

- **Negligible Overhead:** REG introduces a marginal increase in computational resources compared to REPA: a +0.30% increase in parameters, +0.38% in FLOPs, and +0.49% in inference latency. Despite this minimal overhead, REG yields substantial improvements in generation quality, achieving a 56.46% relative reduction in FID and a 50.19% increase in IS (Table 4).

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Discriminative Guidance (Table 3):** DINOv2-B as the source for the class token yielded the best performance (FID 15.22, IS 94.64), outperforming CLIP-L and DINOv2-L. All tested foundation models provided benefits over REPA, confirming the value of external discriminative signals.
- **Alignment Depth (Table 3):** Applying the REPA loss in earlier layers (e.g., depth 4 for SiT-B/2) was found to be most effective, consistent with REPA's findings. However, REG consistently outperformed REPA across all tested depths, attributed to the direct global guidance provided by the entangled class token.
- **Effect of β (Table 3):** The weighting parameter `β` for the class token denoising objective was optimized, with `β = 0.03` providing the best overall performance.
- **Entanglement Signal Variants (Table 5):** The DINOv2 class token proved to be the most effective entanglement signal, significantly reducing FID and increasing IS compared to using a learnable token or averaged latent/DINOv2 features. This highlights the importance of using a rich, high-level discriminative signal.
- **Effectiveness of Entanglement Alone (Table 6):** Even without the explicit representation alignment loss (`L_REPA`), simply entangling the DINOv2-B class token with the image latents improved FID by 19.18% and IS by 35.86% over the SiT-B/2 baseline. This demonstrates the robustness and inherent utility of the class token-based entanglement.

#### 4.5. Enhancing Discriminative Semantic Learning (Figure 3)

- **Strong Correlation with FID:** REG consistently achieves higher CKNNA scores (indicating better semantic alignment) while simultaneously yielding lower FID values (better generation quality) across training steps. This confirms a strong positive correlation between REG's semantic learning and its generation performance.
- **Robust Across Layers:** REG consistently achieves higher CKNNA scores across all network layers at 400K training steps, indicating that the entangled class token effectively propagates discriminative semantics throughout the network.
- **Timestep Stability:** The method demonstrates stable and superior CKNNA scores across all timesteps during denoising, confirming its consistent high-level semantic guidance capability irrespective of noise intensity.

In summary, REG achieves remarkable improvements in training efficiency and generation quality with minimal additional cost, primarily by pioneering an "image-class denoising" approach that effectively integrates discriminative semantic guidance throughout the entire generative process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Representation Entanglement for Generation (REG) marks a significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly for diffusion models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Denoising:** REG's core contribution is the shift from a "pure image denoising pipeline" to an "image-class denoising paradigm." By entangling low-level image latents with a high-level class token and jointly reconstructing both from noise, REG fundamentally redefines how generative models leverage semantic information. This holistic approach ensures that high-level conceptual understanding actively guides the pixel-level generation, leading to more coherent and contextually relevant outputs.

2. **Unprecedented Training Efficiency:** The most striking impact of REG is its ability to drastically accelerate training convergence. Achieving 63× faster training than SiT and 23× faster than REPA, while simultaneously outperforming models trained for significantly longer durations, democratizes access to high-fidelity generative model training. This efficiency drastically reduces the computational resources (GPU hours) and time required, making cutting-edge research and development more accessible to a wider range of institutions and researchers.

3. **Maximal Utilization of Foundation Model Knowledge:** REG provides an elegant and highly effective mechanism to tap into the rich discriminative knowledge encapsulated within large pretrained vision foundation models (like DINOv2). Unlike prior methods that relied on external or transient alignment, REG structurally integrates this knowledge directly into the model's operational flow, ensuring its continuous influence during both training and, critically, inference. This maximizes the synergistic benefits between discriminative and generative AI.

4. **Enhanced Generative Quality and Semantic Fidelity:** By providing consistent semantic guidance, REG not only accelerates training but also significantly boosts the quality of generated images. The improved FID and IS scores, alongside higher CKNNA scores, indicate that REG generates more realistic, diverse, and semantically consistent images. The ability to jointly reconstruct image content and its corresponding global semantics directly from noise leads to a deeper internal understanding of the generated content.

5. **Minimal Computational Overhead for Broad Applicability:** The fact that these substantial gains are achieved with negligible additional computational overhead (less than 0.5% increase in FLOPs and latency) makes REG a highly practical and generalizable enhancement. It can be readily integrated into existing transformer-based diffusion architectures (like SiT) without requiring complex architectural modifications, thus posing a widely applicable solution for improving current generative models.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:**
 - **Conditional Generation:** The success of class token entanglement strongly suggests its potential for other forms of conditional generation, such as text-to-image synthesis, where text embeddings could play an analogous role to the class token in guiding the generative process.
 - **Multi-Modal Entanglement:** This paradigm could be extended to entangle other forms of high-level information (e.g., depth maps, segmentation masks, audio embeddings) with image latents, paving the way for more controllable and versatile multi-modal generative models.
 - **Understanding Representation Flow:** REG's demonstrably improved discriminative semantic learning across layers and timesteps offers a new lens through which to study and understand how high-level semantics influence and are propagated through generative processes.
 - **Efficient Model Development:** The dramatic reduction in training costs will enable researchers to iterate faster, experiment with larger models, and explore more complex generative tasks, accelerating the pace of innovation in the field.

In conclusion, REG represents a significant step forward in making high-fidelity image generation both more efficient and semantically intelligent. By bridging the gap between discriminative knowledge and generative capabilities in a structurally integrated manner, it not only pushes the boundaries of current performance but also lays a robust foundation for future developments in AI-powered content creation.
