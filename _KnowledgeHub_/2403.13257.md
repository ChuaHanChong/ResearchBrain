# Arcee's MergeKit: A Toolkit for Merging Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Arcee's MergeKit provides an open-source, efficient, and user-friendly toolkit that facilitates the merging of Large Language Models (LLMs) from diverse architectures into a single, unified model. The toolkit has been widely adopted, enabling the creation of thousands of powerful open-source checkpoints and demonstrating improved performance in specialized domains like medicine while maintaining generalization.

## Problem

- Training Large Language Models from scratch is computationally expensive and beyond the reach of many researchers and organizations.
- Fine-tuning LLMs for specific tasks often leads to catastrophic forgetting of general capabilities, and managing numerous specialized models is inefficient.
- Combining knowledge from independently trained models or creating versatile, multi-task AI systems without retraining is challenging.

## Method

- ==MergeKit== offers a unified, open-source library supporting a wide array of existing and novel model merging techniques via an intuitive ==YAML configuration== or a graphical user interface.
- It employs an ==out-of-core approach with Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) scheduling== to efficiently handle large models across diverse hardware, from GPUs to memory-constrained CPUs.
- The toolkit is designed for modularity and extensibility, allowing for easy integration of new merging methods and fostering community contributions.

## Results

- MergeKit has facilitated the merging of **thousands** of models, contributing to powerful open-source checkpoints, demonstrating significant real-world adoption and utility.
- Merged models consistently outperformed base models in an empirical study, with merged Meditron-7B/Llama2-7B models showing improved performance across medical benchmarks (e.g., USMLE, MedMCQA, PubMedQA) and enhanced generalization in common LLM benchmarks.
- The toolkit enables the creation of novel model architectures like Franken-MoE and has been used to build models such as Goliath-**120b**, showcasing its capability to go beyond simple merging to create new model structures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abstracting complex merge operations into a user-friendly YAML configuration lowers the barrier to entry, democratizing advanced LLM development.
> - Efficient out-of-core processing and DAG scheduling enable large-scale model merging on commodity hardware, making the process accessible and scalable.
> - Supporting a diverse set of established and novel merging methods (like FrankenMerging and Franken-MoE) within an extensible framework accelerates research and practical application of model merging.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed an explosion of innovation, particularly with the proliferation of open-source models available on platforms like Hugging Face. These models, often trained on trillions of tokens and boasting billions of parameters, represent a significant leap in AI capabilities. However, this rapid growth also presents several challenges:

- **Resource Intensiveness:** Training LLMs from scratch requires immense computational resources, placing it beyond the reach of many researchers and organizations.
- **Specialization vs. Generalization:** While fine-tuning offers a path to task-specific models (e.g., for coding or medical applications), it often leads to catastrophic forgetting, degrading a model's general capabilities. Storing and deploying numerous task-specific models also becomes inefficient.
- **Knowledge Transfer:** Independently trained models struggle to leverage insights from related tasks, hindering the creation of truly versatile AI.
- **Alignment:** Aligning models with human preferences requires extensive and often scarce human preference data.

In response to these challenges, **model merging** has emerged as a transformative strategy. Instead of retraining or extensive fine-tuning, model merging combines parameters from multiple pre-existing checkpoints into a single unified model. This approach offers several compelling advantages:

- **Cost-Effectiveness:** Avoids the prohibitive cost of training large models from scratch.
- **Multitask and Continual Learning:** Enables the creation of models capable of performing multiple tasks or continually learning new information without forgetting old skills.
- **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting:** Helps preserve general capabilities while integrating specialized knowledge.
- **Resource Optimization:** Reduces the need to store and deploy multiple models.

The paper extensively categorizes the landscape of model merging techniques:

- **Models with Identical Architectures and Initializations:** This is the most common category, leveraging concepts like Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC). Techniques include linear averaging (Model Soups), Task Arithmetic, TIES merging, DARE, and Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP). Novel additions by MergeKit in this category include "FrankenMerging" (piecewise combination of layers) and "Franken Mixture of Experts" (building MoE models from dense ones). Evolutionary Model Merging is also mentioned as a novel approach optimizing both parameter and data flow spaces.
- **Models with Identical Architectures but Different Initializations:** This more advanced category deals with permuting weights to align models in the loss landscape, including methods like Git-Rebasin, Optimal Transport Fusion (OTFusion), and ZipIt. The paper notes these techniques are less widely adopted but hold significant promise.
- **Fusing Models with Different Architectures:** While not strictly merging, methods like CALM and FUSELLM aim to combine models with diverse structures, often requiring additional training.

MergeKit positions itself directly at the heart of this evolving landscape by providing a practical, efficient, and extensible toolkit to *implement* these diverse merging strategies. It addresses the practical gap between theoretical merging research and its widespread application. By providing an accessible platform, MergeKit democratizes the ability to experiment with and deploy powerful merged LLMs, thereby accelerating research and development in this critical area. Its direct integration with the Hugging Face ecosystem further solidifies its role as a central utility within the open-source AI community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MergeKit stems from the aforementioned challenges inherent in developing and deploying Large Language Models, especially within the open-source paradigm. The authors identify the need for a practical solution to leverage the growing repository of specialized LLM checkpoints effectively.

The key objectives of the MergeKit project are:

1. **Democratize Model Merging:** To provide an intuitive and accessible framework that allows users of all skill levels, from individual researchers to large teams, to perform complex model merging operations without requiring extensive coding expertise or high-end computational resources.
2. **Centralize Merging Strategies:** To create a unified, open-source library that supports a wide array of existing and future model merging techniques, making it a go-to resource for the community.
3. **Ensure Efficiency and Scalability:** To design a toolkit capable of handling large language models efficiently, even on memory-constrained hardware like CPUs, through optimized resource management.
4. **Foster Community Contribution and Extensibility:** To establish an extensible foundation that encourages the open-source community to contribute new merging methods and enhancements, thereby ensuring the toolkit's continuous evolution and relevance.
5. **Provide a Comprehensive Overview:** To offer a survey of current model merging research from an engineering perspective, helping users and developers understand the theoretical underpinnings of the techniques implemented in MergeKit.

In essence, MergeKit aims to transform the theoretical potential of model merging into a practical, widely usable capability, driving innovation and efficiency in the development of open-source LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MergeKit's approach is centered on creating a robust, user-friendly, and highly efficient open-source library that abstracts the complexities of model merging. The methodology is guided by several key design principles:

#### 3.1. User-Centric Design: Intuitive Interface and YAML Configuration Control

MergeKit prioritizes ease of use through a **YAML configuration file** interface. This design choice allows users to define intricate merge operations descriptively, without writing code. This not only lowers the barrier to entry but also promotes reproducibility, sharing, and remixing of merge recipes within the community. Parameters can be set globally, targeted to specific model components, or even defined as layer-varying interpolated gradients, offering flexibility for both simple and advanced users. To further enhance accessibility, the authors also developed **MergeKit-GUI**, a user-friendly graphical interface hosted on Hugging Face, allowing users to execute merges with a few clicks, upload configuration files, and monitor the process in real-time.

#### 3.2. Modularity: Plug-and-Play Components

The library is built with **composability and reusability** as core tenets. Merge methods are designed as interchangeable components, making it simple to add new techniques or customize existing workflows. Key components, such as the lazy tensor loading functionality, are designed to be useful standalone, allowing external scripts to leverage them independently. MergeKit maintains tight integration with the **Hugging Face Transformers library** and its model hub, ensuring seamless access to a vast ecosystem of LLMs and facilitating easy model input and output.

#### 3.3. Scalability: Efficiency and Performance Optimization

Addressing the challenge of merging large models with diverse computational resources, MergeKit employs an **out-of-core approach**. This means that only the tensors necessary for the current operation are loaded into working memory, allowing the toolkit to scale from high-end GPU clusters down to personal laptops with limited RAM and no dedicated GPU. The toolkit utilizes a **Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)** to represent and schedule merge operations. This computational graph scheduling minimizes redundancy and resource usage by optimizing the execution order of tasks and implicitly handling the eviction of intermediate values no longer needed. This infrastructure allows developers to build new merge methods that automatically benefit from MergeKit’s memory efficiency and hardware scalability.

#### 3.4. Supported Merging Techniques and Novel Additions

MergeKit supports a wide range of established merging techniques, including:
- **Linear Interpolation (LERP)**
- **Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP)**
- **Task Arithmetic**
- **Trim, Elect Sign & Merge (TIES merging)**
- **Drop And REscale (DARE)**

Furthermore, MergeKit introduces novel methods:
- **"FrankenMerging"**: A passthrough method allowing piecewise combination of layers from multiple models to create new models of unusual sizes, a technique used in models like Goliath-120b.
- **"Franken Mixture of Experts" (Franken-MoE)**: A script to build Mixture of Experts models from multiple dense models, using either prompt-based hidden state heuristics for semantic routing or randomly initialized gates for sparse up-cycling.

#### 3.5. Extensibility for Community Contributions

A significant part of MergeKit's methodology is its explicit design for **extensibility**. The paper provides a clear guide for community members wishing to integrate new merging methods. Contributors are directed to key Python modules—`merge_methods/base.py` (for defining new method interfaces), `graph.py` (for scheduling and data management), `plan.py` (for computational graph creation), and `architecture.py` (for handling model structures)—emphasizing a structured approach to foster collaborative development and ensure new methods benefit from the toolkit's core optimizations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's primary contribution is the introduction and validation of MergeKit as a practical, efficient, and extensible toolkit for merging Large Language Models, rather than presenting novel merging algorithms (though it does integrate and facilitate novel applications like FrankenMerging and Franken-MoE). The main findings and results can be summarized as follows:

1. **Successful Development and Adoption of MergeKit:** The most significant finding is the successful engineering and deployment of MergeKit itself. The abstract states that the library "has facilitated the merging of thousands of models, contributing to some of the world’s most powerful open-source model checkpoints." This indicates significant real-world adoption and utility within the open-source community, validating its design principles and effectiveness.
2. **Comprehensive Support for Diverse Merging Strategies:** MergeKit successfully integrates and makes accessible a wide array of existing model merging techniques, from simple linear averaging to more advanced methods like SLERP, TIES, and DARE. Its modular architecture proves effective in accommodating these diverse strategies and extending to novel applications.
3. **Demonstrated Efficiency and Scalability:** The out-of-core approach and DAG-based computational graph scheduling demonstrate MergeKit's ability to handle large models efficiently across various hardware configurations, from high-end GPUs to memory-constrained CPUs. This addresses a critical practical barrier for many researchers.
4. **Empirical Validation in Medical Domain:** The paper presents a practical example of MergeKit's utility by merging Meditron-7B (a medical-specific LLM) with the Llama2-7B chat model. The experiment, detailed in Table 1, shows concrete performance improvements:
 - **Improved Medical Performance:** All merged models (using LERP, SLERP, DARE-TIES, TIES) consistently outperformed the base Meditron-7B model across critical medical benchmarks such as USMLE, MedMCQA, and PubMedQA. This highlights MergeKit's capability to enhance specialized domain performance.
 - **Enhanced Generalization:** Models merged using LERP and SLERP techniques also exhibited superior performance over the Llama2-7B chat model in general benchmarks (Arc Challenge, HellaSwag, MMLU). This demonstrates that merging can lead to more generalized models without sacrificing specialized capabilities, addressing the catastrophic forgetting problem.
 - **Method-Specific Efficacy:** The SLERP method particularly stood out, achieving the highest scores in medical benchmarks (USMLE and PubMedQA) and competitive scores in general benchmarks, suggesting its effectiveness in preserving and blending model knowledge.
5. **Facilitation of Novel Architectures:** The integration of "FrankenMerging" and "Franken-MoE" techniques within MergeKit demonstrates its ability to not only merge existing models but also enable the creation of new, unconventional model architectures (like the 120B parameter Goliath model or sparse MoEs from dense models) through parameter manipulation.

In summary, MergeKit is presented not just as a concept but as a functional, widely-used, and empirically validated tool that effectively enables the creation of more powerful, versatile, and specialized LLMs by leveraging existing open-source assets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MergeKit stands as a significant contribution to the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Models, with potential impacts across research, development, and application:

1. **Democratization of Advanced LLM Development:** By providing an intuitive, open-source toolkit that can operate on diverse hardware, MergeKit significantly lowers the barrier to entry for advanced LLM development. Researchers, small teams, and even individuals with limited computational resources can now experiment with and deploy powerful merged models, which was previously a privilege of well-funded institutions. This fosters broader participation and innovation in the AI community.
2. **Acceleration of Research in Model Merging:** MergeKit serves as a standardized platform for implementing and evaluating model merging strategies. Its modular and extensible design encourages the open-source community to contribute new merging techniques, rapidly prototype ideas, and compare their efficacy. This accelerates the theoretical and practical advancements in understanding how to best combine models.
3. **Enabling Efficient Multitask and Continual Learning:** The ability to merge models allows for the creation of versatile LLMs that can handle multiple tasks or continually adapt to new information without undergoing expensive retraining or suffering from catastrophic forgetting. This is crucial for developing robust AI systems that can evolve and perform across various domains, as exemplified by the improved performance of merged models in both medical and general benchmarks.
4. **Optimization of Resource Utilization:** By reducing the need to train models from scratch or manage separate fine-tuned models for every task, MergeKit promotes efficient use of computational and storage resources. This is particularly relevant given the increasing size and complexity of LLMs, making their development and deployment more sustainable.
5. **Catalyst for Specialized LLMs:** The toolkit empowers the creation of highly specialized LLMs by combining domain-specific knowledge (e.g., medical, legal, coding) from different models into a unified, high-performing agent. This opens new avenues for AI application in niche areas where existing general-purpose LLMs might fall short. The success of models like BioMistral and Llama-3-SEC, mentioned in the paper, underscores this impact.
6. **Strengthening the Open-Source LLM Ecosystem:** As an open-source project tightly integrated with Hugging Face, MergeKit reinforces the collaborative spirit of the open-source AI community. It provides a shared infrastructure that leverages existing models and contributes back, creating a virtuous cycle of innovation and resource sharing.
7. **Ethical Considerations and Responsible AI:** The authors explicitly acknowledge the ethical implications of democratizing AI, including the need to address biases, ensure data privacy, and promote transparency. By fostering a community-driven approach, MergeKit implicitly encourages collective responsibility in developing and deploying AI systems ethically, aiming for solutions that benefit society.

In conclusion, MergeKit is more than just a software library; it is a foundational tool that redefines how the AI community can leverage and build upon the vast open-source LLM landscape. Its impact will likely be seen in the proliferation of more efficient, powerful, and specialized AI models across a multitude of applications.
