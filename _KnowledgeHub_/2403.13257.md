# Arcee's MergeKit: A Toolkit for Merging Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Arcee's MergeKit provides an open-source, efficient, and user-friendly toolkit that facilitates the merging of Large Language Models (LLMs) from diverse architectures into a single, unified model. The toolkit has been widely adopted, enabling the creation of thousands of powerful open-source checkpoints and demonstrating improved performance in specialized domains like medicine while maintaining generalization.

## Problem

- Training Large Language Models from scratch is computationally expensive and beyond the reach of many researchers and organizations.
- Fine-tuning LLMs for specific tasks often leads to catastrophic forgetting of general capabilities, and managing numerous specialized models is inefficient.
- Combining knowledge from independently trained models or creating versatile, multi-task AI systems without retraining is challenging.

## Method

- ==MergeKit== offers a unified, open-source library supporting a wide array of existing and novel model merging techniques via an intuitive ==YAML configuration== or a graphical user interface.
- It employs an ==out-of-core approach with Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) scheduling== to efficiently handle large models across diverse hardware, from GPUs to memory-constrained CPUs.
- The toolkit is designed for modularity and extensibility, allowing for easy integration of new merging methods and fostering community contributions.

## Results

- MergeKit has facilitated the merging of **thousands** of models, contributing to powerful open-source checkpoints, demonstrating significant real-world adoption and utility.
- Merged models consistently outperformed base models in an empirical study, with merged Meditron-7B/Llama2-7B models showing improved performance across medical benchmarks (e.g., USMLE, MedMCQA, PubMedQA) and enhanced generalization in common LLM benchmarks.
- The toolkit enables the creation of novel model architectures like Franken-MoE and has been used to build models such as Goliath-**120b**, showcasing its capability to go beyond simple merging to create new model structures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abstracting complex merge operations into a user-friendly YAML configuration lowers the barrier to entry, democratizing advanced LLM development.
> - Efficient out-of-core processing and DAG scheduling enable large-scale model merging on commodity hardware, making the process accessible and scalable.
> - Supporting a diverse set of established and novel merging methods (like FrankenMerging and Franken-MoE) within an extensible framework accelerates research and practical application of model merging.
