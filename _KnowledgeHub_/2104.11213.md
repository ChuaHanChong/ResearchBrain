# ManipulaTHOR: A Framework for Visual Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Allen Institute for AI introduced ManipulaTHOR, an extension to the AI2-THOR simulator, to enable embodied agents to perform low-level object manipulation in visually complex, physics-enabled environments. They developed the ArmPointNav task as a benchmark, achieving a 39.4% success rate in picking up and relocating objects without disturbance in previously seen scenes with familiar objects, while also demonstrating moderate generalization to novel objects.

## Problem

- Existing Embodied AI environments primarily focus on navigation, lacking robust support for low-level, physics-enabled object manipulation by mobile agents.
- Traditional robotics research often addresses manipulation in controlled settings or with static manipulators, not fully integrating mobile manipulation in cluttered, visually rich 3D environments.
- There is a need for standardized benchmarks and frameworks to train and evaluate agents on combined navigation and fine-grained object interaction tasks, particularly concerning generalization to novel objects and scenes.

## Method

- Developed ManipulaTHOR, an extension of AI2-THOR, integrating a simplified three-jointed robotic arm with 6-DOF hand grasper attached to the agent, supporting forward and inverse kinematics and physics-enabled interactions.
- Introduced the ArmPointNav task, a benchmark requiring agents to navigate to an object, pick it up, and transport it to a target location, involving continuous arm control and 3D collision avoidance.
- Created the ArmPointNav Dataset (APND) using 30 kitchen scenes and 12 object categories, enabling evaluation of end-to-end deep reinforcement learning agents for generalization to novel scenes and unseen object categories.

## Results

- The end-to-end model achieved a 39.4% Success Rate without Disturbance (SRwD) and an 89.9% Pick Up Success Rate (PuSR) for seen objects in test scenes, with successful episodes averaging 78.1 steps.
- Performance moderately decreased when generalizing to novel objects, with SRwD dropping to 32.7% for novel objects in unseen scenes and 32.2% for novel objects in seen scenes, highlighting challenges in handling unfamiliar object geometries.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the critical role of visual input, with a depth-only model (SRwD 39.4%) significantly outperforming an RGB-only model (SRwD 21.2%) and a no-vision agent (SRwD 10.3%).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An end-to-end deep reinforcement learning approach is more effective for complex combined navigation and manipulation tasks like ArmPointNav than disjoint multi-stage policies, which struggle with overall task completion despite specialized sub-task performance.
> - Depth information is more critical for spatial reasoning, collision avoidance, and overall task success in visual object manipulation compared to RGB information.
> - Generalization to novel object categories presents a greater challenge than generalization to novel scenes, suggesting that robust understanding of object geometry and interaction properties is a primary bottleneck for manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI has demonstrated advancements, particularly in agent navigation within virtual environments. This progress, exemplified by successes in tasks such as Point Navigation (PointNav), has established foundational capabilities for agents to interact with their surroundings. The current work positions itself as a progression from navigation-centric tasks to object manipulation, which represents a more complex and interactive aspect of embodied intelligence.

Object manipulation is an established domain within robotics research, traditionally confronting challenges related to manipulator motion planning, robust grasping, and long-horizon task execution. However, much of this research has often focused on controlled settings, such as tabletop manipulation, or simplified environments. Recent developments in Embodied AI environments, including iGibson, SAPIEN, and TDW, have begun to enable certain forms of object interaction, but often with limitations in supporting visually rich scenes, fine-grained physics, mobile manipulation, or generalization to novel objects and scenes.

This research addresses a gap by developing a framework that supports low-level object manipulation within visually complex and physically enabled environments, specifically designed for mobile agents rather than static manipulators. It distinguishes itself from prior work by integrating joint navigation and manipulation capabilities, requiring agents to manage dynamic changes in their own shape due to arm movement, avoid collisions in 3D cluttered spaces, and generalize learned policies to unfamiliar objects and environments. By extending a pre-existing, widely used simulator (AI2-THOR), the authors aim to bridge the gap between abstract interaction models and the granular control required for realistic object manipulation in diverse scenes. This contribution seeks to provide a platform for the Embodied AI community to pursue more sophisticated and real-world applicable tasks that necessitate active interaction with objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

This research paper outlines several key objectives and motivations, focusing on advancing the field of Embodied AI from navigation-focused tasks to complex visual object manipulation.

**Primary Objective: Introduce ManipulaTHOR Framework**
The central objective is to propose and develop ManipulaTHOR, an extension of the existing AI2-THOR framework. This extension is designed to imbue virtual agents with the capability to not only navigate but also actively manipulate objects within their environments using a robotic arm. The motivation for this framework stems from the observation that while Embodied AI has seen significant progress in navigation, the ability for agents to physically interact with and manipulate objects in complex, visually rich scenes remains a substantial challenge. ManipulaTHOR aims to provide the necessary infrastructure – including a physics-enabled environment, diverse scenes, an arm mechanism with kinematics, and specific interaction functionalities – to facilitate research in this domain.

**Secondary Objective: Present the ArmPointNav Challenge**
As a first application and benchmark for ManipulaTHOR, the authors propose a new challenge task called ArmPointNav. This task extends the established PointNav challenge by requiring an agent to locate, pick up, and transport a specified object from an initial source location to a designated target location within the scene. The motivation here is to establish a foundational, atomic manipulation task that encompasses both navigation and fine-grained object interaction, serving as a building block for more complex, long-horizon tasks in the future.

**Motivation for ManipulaTHOR's Design and Capabilities:**
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Simulators:** Many Embodied AI simulators, while strong in photorealism or navigation, often lack the fine-grained physics emulation, precise collision detection between manipulators and objects, and variety of interactive objects necessary for complex manipulation tasks. ManipulaTHOR aims to overcome these limitations by leveraging AI2-THOR's Unity-based physics engine and asset library, specifically adapting it for arm-based manipulation.
- **Enabling Low-Level Object Interaction:** Previous interactions within AI2-THOR often occurred at a high level (e.g., "open cabinet"). ManipulaTHOR is motivated by the need to enable low-level, continuous control over an agent's arm, allowing researchers to explore policies that require precise movements and collision avoidance.
- **Supporting Generalization:** A core motivation is to develop a platform where agents can learn policies that generalize to novel environments and previously unseen objects, which is a critical aspect for real-world applicability of embodied agents.

**Motivation for ArmPointNav's Challenges:**
- **Combined Agent and Arm Motion:** Unlike pure navigation, ArmPointNav requires simultaneous coordination of the agent's base movement and the arm's articulate motion, introducing a higher degree of control complexity.
- **3D Obstacle Avoidance and Occlusion:** The presence of an arm and potentially a held object necessitates sophisticated 3D collision avoidance. The task is designed to frequently involve scenarios where objects are behind occluders, requiring careful arm manipulation and planning to prevent collisions.
- **Multi-Object Manipulation and Long-Term Planning:** The challenge anticipates scenarios where agents may need to manipulate multiple objects (e.g., clearing a path) to achieve the primary goal, demanding advanced visual reasoning and long-term planning capabilities.
- **Dynamic View Obstruction:** The agent's arm and a carried object can frequently obstruct the agent's egocentric view, adding a perceptual challenge not present in navigation-only tasks.

In summary, the work is motivated by the recognized need to move beyond basic navigation in Embodied AI, providing both a robust simulation framework and a challenging benchmark task to foster research into intelligent agents capable of sophisticated, generalizable visual object manipulation in complex, interactive environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development of the ManipulaTHOR framework and its application to the ArmPointNav task, utilizing an end-to-end deep reinforcement learning approach.

**ManipulaTHOR Framework:**
ManipulaTHOR is presented as an extension of the AI2-THOR framework, which is a Unity-based, physics-enabled simulator offering visually rich indoor scenes and a library of manipulable objects. The key additions and functionalities include:

- **Arm Design:** A simplified three-jointed robotic arm is attached to the agent's body. It features equal limb-lengths and is composed of swivel joints, with the shoulder and wrist supporting 360 degrees of rotation. The hand grasper is specified with 6 degrees of freedom (6DOF). The arm rig supports both forward and inverse kinematics (IK), allowing for control via individual joint actuations or direct wrist position specification.
- **Grasper Mechanism:** Object grasping is abstracted as a spherical volume at the end of the arm. Any object intersecting this sphere can be "picked up." This design choice simplifies the complexities of detailed gripper mechanics to focus on broader manipulation challenges.
- **Arm Interface:** The framework provides functionalities for manipulating the wrist's location and orientation (with IK resolving joint rotations), adjusting arm height, querying arm state metadata, picking up/dropping objects, and changing the grasper sphere's radius.
- **Physics Engine:** NVIDIA's PhysX engine, integrated through Unity, is employed to simulate physically realistic object interactions, arm movements, and collision-induced force cascades.
- **Rendering Speed:** Despite the computational demands of accurate collision detection and physics, the framework achieves a training speed of 300 frames per second (fps) on a machine with 8 NVIDIA T4 GPUs and 40 cores.

**ArmPointNav Task:**
The ArmPointNav task involves moving a specific object from a source location to a target location within a scene. This entails a sequence of sub-tasks: navigating to the object, positioning the arm for pickup, grasping the object, navigating with the object to the target location, positioning the arm for placement, and releasing the object. Source and target locations are specified as (x, y, z) coordinates relative to the agent.

**ArmPointNav Dataset (APND):**
A dataset was generated using 30 kitchen scenes from AI2-THOR, containing over 150 object categories, 69 of which are interactable. Twelve specific pickupable object categories were selected as target objects. The scenes are split into 20 for training, 5 for validation, and 5 for testing. For generalization assessment, training was performed on 6 object categories, with evaluation including 6 novel object categories. The dataset includes tasks that present challenges such as objects being behind occluders or inside receptacles.

**Agent Model and Learning Approach:**
The agent utilizes an end-to-end learning approach, building on methods successful in PointNav tasks.

- **Sensors:** The agent receives egocentric RGB and Depth images, GPS and Compass coordinates indicating the relative positions of the arm and target locations, and ground truth instance segmentation maps (though only RGB, Depth, GPS, and Compass were used in this work).
- **Input Representation:** At each timestep, the model takes the egocentric frame (I_t), the current relative distance of the arm end-effector to the object (d_o_arm), and the current relative distance of the arm to the object's goal state (d_goal_o) as inputs.
- **Architecture (Figure 6):** Visual observations (RGB, Depth, or RGBD) are processed by three convolutional layers followed by a fully-connected layer to produce a 512-dimensional visual embedding. The relative coordinate distances are embedded by two fully-connected layers into a 512-dimensional feature vector. These two embeddings are concatenated and fed into a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) with a hidden size of 512, which acts as the controller. The GRU outputs a probability distribution over possible actions (policy) and a value estimate.
- **Action Space:** The agent's action space is discretized into 13 distinct actions:
 - Agent movement: Move forward (20cm), Rotate left (45 degrees), Rotate right (45 degrees).
 - Arm movement: Change wrist location by ±5cm along X, Y, Z axes relative to the agent (total 6 actions), Increase/Decrease arm height by 7cm (2 actions).
 - Interaction: Abstract grasp, Issue "Done" action.
- **Optimization Algorithm:** The network parameters are optimized using Distributed Proximal Policy Optimization (DD-PPO).
- **Reward Function:** The reward scheme combines terminal and intermediate rewards:
 - R_success = 10 (if task succeeds)
 - R_pickup = 5 (if object is picked up)
 - ∆_o_arm: Change in arm-to-object distance (positive for reduction).
 - ∆_goal_o: Change in object-to-goal distance (positive for reduction).
 This reward shaping aims to balance the importance of different phases of the task (pickup vs. placement).
- **Training Details:** Models were trained for 20 million frames, with a maximum episode length of 200 steps.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
Performance was assessed using six metrics:
- Success rate without disturbance (SRwD): Fraction of successful episodes where no other objects were disturbed.
- Success rate (SR): Fraction of successful episodes, less strict than SRwD.
- Pick up success rate (PuSR): Fraction of episodes where the object was successfully picked up.
- Episode Length (Len): Average length of all episodes.
- Successful episode Length (SuLen): Average length of successful episodes.
- Pick up successful episode length (PuLen): Average length of episodes with successful pickups.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted within the ManipulaTHOR framework using the ArmPointNav task yielded several findings regarding agent performance, generalization, and architectural considerations.

**Baseline End-to-End Model Performance (Depth-only):**
- **Seen Objects in Test Scenes:** The end-to-end model achieved a Success Rate without Disturbance (SRwD) of 39.4% and a Success Rate (SR) of 68.7% for objects and scenes seen during training. The Pick Up Success Rate (PuSR) was 89.9%, indicating that successfully picking up an object is significantly more achievable than completing the entire manipulation task by placing it at the target without disturbance. The average length of successful episodes (SuLen) was 78.1 steps.
- **Novel Objects in Test Scenes (Generalization):** When tested on objects not encountered during training but in unseen scenes, the SRwD decreased to 32.7%, and PuSR decreased to 84.3%. This indicates a moderate ability to generalize to novel objects, but also highlights the challenge that understanding object geometry and interaction properties poses for unseen instances.
- **Novel Objects in Seen Scenes:** Testing with novel objects within environments previously encountered during training resulted in an SRwD of 32.2% and a PuSR of 90.6%. The performance remained comparable to the Test-NovelObj setting, suggesting that generalization to new object categories is a primary bottleneck for this task, rather than generalization to new environments. The requirement to understand object geometry for collision avoidance and precise manipulation was identified as a contributing factor to this challenge.

**Impact of Distance to Goal:**
Analysis of SRwD based on the initial distance of the object from its target location indicated that success rates generally decreased as the distance increased. However, the model demonstrated relative robustness to longer distances. The performance gap between seen and novel objects persisted across different distances, with seen objects consistently yielding higher SRwD.

**Comparison to a Disjoint Model:**
A key experiment compared the end-to-end learning approach with a disjoint model consisting of two separate visual encoders and controllers—one for picking up the object and another for moving it to the goal.
- The disjoint model exhibited a higher PuSR (91.6%) and lower PuLen (40.8 steps) compared to the end-to-end model, suggesting that specializing a policy for the pickup phase can improve its efficiency.
- However, the disjoint model achieved an SRwD of 0.0% and an SR of 0.0%, indicating a failure to complete the overall task successfully. This outcome was attributed to the lack of shared parameters and skills between the two phases, leading to an inability to effectively navigate the object to the goal, particularly as the second stage received gradients only after a successful pickup. This result supports the efficacy of the end-to-end approach, which leverages shared underlying physical understanding across subtasks.

**Ablation Studies on Sensor Modalities:**
- **No-Vision Agent:** A model trained without visual modalities (only GPS and Compass for relative locations) performed significantly worse, achieving an SRwD of 10.3%. This demonstrates the critical importance of visual information for the ArmPointNav task, even when symbolic location information is provided.
- **RGB vs. Depth vs. RGBD:**
 - The depth-only model (SRwD 39.4%) outperformed the RGB-only model (SRwD 21.2%).
 - The combined RGBD model (SRwD 37.1%) performed comparably to, but slightly below, the depth-only model. This trend aligns with observations in prior PointNav tasks, where depth information can be particularly effective for spatial reasoning and collision avoidance.

In summary, the results demonstrate that the proposed end-to-end learning framework within ManipulaTHOR can achieve a moderate level of success in object manipulation, with a notable capacity to generalize to novel objects and environments. However, the task remains challenging, particularly in the placement phase and when dealing with unseen object geometries. The findings also underscore the importance of visual sensory input and the benefits of an integrated, end-to-end learning architecture for complex manipulation tasks over modular, disjoint approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces ManipulaTHOR, a framework for visual object manipulation, and the ArmPointNav task, contributing to the field of Embodied AI in several ways.

**Advancement in Embodied AI Simulation:** ManipulaTHOR extends the capabilities of existing simulation environments by integrating low-level, physics-enabled object manipulation with mobile agents in visually rich and complex scenes. This development addresses a gap in the availability of robust platforms that support both intricate navigation and fine-grained object interaction, which is crucial for training embodied agents for real-world tasks. The framework's ability to achieve 300 frames per second during training enables researchers to conduct large-scale experiments necessary for deep reinforcement learning.

**Novel Challenge and Dataset:** The introduction of the ArmPointNav task and its associated dataset (APND) provides a standardized benchmark for evaluating and advancing object manipulation policies. This task extends the foundational PointNav challenge to include complexities such as combined agent and arm movement, 3D obstacle avoidance, handling occlusions, potential multi-object manipulation, and dynamic view obstruction by the arm or carried object. The dataset's structure, including splits for novel scenes and novel objects, directly supports research into generalization, which is a key objective for practical Embodied AI.

**Insights into Learning Architectures:** The experimental comparison between an end-to-end model and a disjoint multi-stage model provides empirical evidence for the benefits of integrated learning in manipulation tasks. The superior performance of the end-to-end approach, despite the higher pickup success rate of the specialized first stage in the disjoint model, suggests that shared learning of underlying physical understanding across different phases of a manipulation task can be more effective for overall task completion. This finding informs future architectural designs for complex Embodied AI agents.

**Identification of Research Directions:** The results highlight that while current state-of-the-art models show promise, there remains substantial room for improvement in visual object manipulation. The observed performance degradation when generalizing to novel objects indicates that robust understanding of object geometry and interaction properties is a significant challenge. The research also suggests future work in relaxing dependencies on certain sensors (e.g., GPS, Compass) and exploring more complex network architectures or training paradigms for RGB and RGBD inputs.

**Community Engagement and Future Work:** By making the ManipulaTHOR framework, dataset, and code publicly available, the authors aim to encourage broader participation from the Embodied AI community. This open-source contribution is intended to accelerate research into complex problems such as long-horizon planning, robust grasping, and manipulation in highly dynamic and cluttered environments, ultimately paving the way for more capable and versatile embodied agents.
