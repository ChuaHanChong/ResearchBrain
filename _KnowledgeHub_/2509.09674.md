# SimpleVLA-RL: Scaling VLA Training via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SimpleVLA-RL is a reinforcement learning framework designed to improve Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by using outcome-based rewards and an efficient online training approach. This method significantly enhances model performance, generalization, and data efficiency in robotic manipulation tasks while also enabling the discovery of novel manipulation strategies.

## Problem

- VLA model training suffers from data scarcity and high costs associated with acquiring extensive, high-quality human-operated robotic trajectories for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- SFT-trained VLA models often exhibit poor generalization to new, unseen tasks, objects, or environmental configurations.
- Prior VLA-RL methods frequently relied on complex hand-crafted process rewards or intricate reward modeling, which limits their scalability and broad applicability.

## Method

- The research developed **SimpleVLA-RL**, an efficient ==online reinforcement learning== framework for VLA models, by extending the ==veRL== framework for LLMs and adapting the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== algorithm.
- It employs a scalable, sparse ==binary outcome-based reward== (1 for task success, 0 for failure) that is propagated uniformly across trajectories, simplifying reward design.
- The framework integrates VLA-specific adaptations, including ==token-based action generation==, closed-loop multi-round environment interaction, and parallel multi-environment rendering for faster trajectory sampling.

## Results

- SimpleVLA-RL achieved state-of-the-art performance on the LIBERO benchmark, boosting the average success rate from **91%** to **99.1%** and improving LIBERO-Long tasks by **12%**.
- It demonstrated robust data efficiency, increasing success rates from **48.9%** (One-Trajectory SFT) to **96.9%** on LIBERO tasks after RL training, approaching the performance of full-data SFT+RL.
- The policies trained in simulation showed strong sim-to-real transfer, improving real-world success rates on RoboTwin2.0 tasks from **17.5%** (SFT-only) to **38.5%** on average.
- The RL-trained policy discovered novel "pushcut" strategies in tasks like "move can pot," replacing the demonstrated grasp-and-place behaviors with more efficient pushing actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning with sparse, outcome-based rewards can drastically reduce the reliance on extensive demonstration data for VLA models, achieving near state-of-the-art performance with significantly fewer demonstrations.
> - RL training substantially enhances VLA models' generalization capabilities across spatial, object, and task variations, mitigating catastrophic forgetting observed in SFT-only methods.
> - RL can drive the discovery of emergent, more efficient behaviors (e.g., "pushcut" strategies) that were not present in human demonstrations, indicating optimization beyond pure imitation.
> - The effectiveness of VLA-RL is strongly correlated with the initial competence of the SFT-tuned base model; stronger initial priors lead to greater performance improvements after RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, large language models (LLMs) enhanced by reinforcement learning (RL), and robotic manipulation.
VLA models have recently emerged as a powerful paradigm for enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks by integrating visual perception, language understanding, and action generation. Current VLA models, such as OpenVLA, Octo, and UniVLA, typically rely on a two-stage training process: large-scale pretraining on diverse multimodal data (including human manipulation videos and robot datasets) followed by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on high-quality robot trajectories. This SFT phase is crucial for tailoring models to specific tasks and enhancing their capabilities.

However, the field faces two fundamental limitations with the SFT paradigm:
1. **Data Scarcity and Cost:** Acquiring large volumes of high-quality human-operated robotic trajectories for SFT is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. This significantly constrains the scalability and diversity of VLA model training datasets.
2. **Limited Generalization:** SFT-trained VLA models often struggle to generalize to new, unseen tasks, environments, or objects, especially those involving compositional, long-horizon, or real-world distribution shifts. Their performance degrades significantly when encountering scenarios outside the scope of their specific training data.

Concurrently, the field of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), a subset of LLMs, has seen remarkable breakthroughs through reinforcement learning, as exemplified by models like DeepSeek-R1. These models have demonstrated that RL, even with simple outcome-based rewards, can dramatically improve step-by-step reasoning capabilities. This success has sparked a critical question: Can RL similarly enhance the long-horizon, step-by-step action planning of VLA models, and thereby address the data scarcity and generalization challenges inherent in SFT?

SimpleVLA-RL directly aims to port the successes of RL for LLMs to the VLA domain. By building upon existing LLM-RL frameworks (specifically veRL and GRPO), the authors propose an efficient and scalable online RL framework for VLA. This work distinguishes itself from prior efforts in VLA-RL, which often relied on hand-crafted process rewards (limiting scalability), complex reward modeling (like RLHF), or dense reward designs. SimpleVLA-RL’s focus on straightforward outcome-based rewards and scalable interactive rollout for VLA models represents a novel approach to overcome the long-standing challenges in robotic learning. It seeks to prove that sparse, outcome-only RL can push the boundaries of VLA performance, data efficiency, and generalization, mirroring the paradigm shift observed in LLM development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind SimpleVLA-RL are clearly articulated:

1. **Address Data Scarcity in VLA Training:** The primary motivation is to reduce the heavy reliance on large-scale, costly human-operated robotic trajectories for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). The authors aim to demonstrate that reinforcement learning can effectively improve VLA model performance even with extremely limited demonstration data, thereby alleviating a major bottleneck in VLA scaling.
2. **Enhance Generalization Capabilities of VLA Models:** Current SFT-based VLA models often exhibit poor generalization to unseen tasks, objects, or spatial configurations due to their reliance on specific training distributions. SimpleVLA-RL seeks to improve VLA models' robustness and adaptability to distributional shifts, enabling them to perform effectively in novel scenarios.
3. **Investigate the Applicability of LLM-RL Success to VLA:** Inspired by the breakthroughs in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) where reinforcement learning, particularly with simple outcome rewards, has significantly enhanced step-by-step reasoning, the authors aim to explore if similar RL techniques can strengthen VLA models' capacity for generating accurate actions step-by-step in complex robotic manipulation tasks.
4. **Develop an Efficient and Scalable Online RL Framework for VLA:** Recognizing the unique challenges of applying RL to VLA (multi-round environment interaction, slower and costlier rollouts compared to LLMs), a key objective is to build an efficient, end-to-end online RL framework. This involves adapting existing LLM-RL methodologies with VLA-specific trajectory sampling, parallelization for faster sampling, multi-environment rendering, and optimized loss computation.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** The ultimate goal is to enable VLA models to surpass current state-of-the-art performance on challenging simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin 1.0 & 2.0) and demonstrate strong sim-to-real transfer capabilities in real-world robotic tasks.

In essence, the motivation is to leverage the power of RL, particularly outcome-based, on-policy methods, to circumvent the fundamental limitations of imitation learning in VLA, paving the way for more autonomous, adaptable, and generalizable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SimpleVLA-RL introduces an efficient online reinforcement learning framework specifically tailored for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, building upon the `veRL` framework for LLMs and the `Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)` algorithm. The methodology focuses on adapting LLM-RL techniques to the unique interactive nature of robotic tasks.

**Foundation:**
- **veRL (Volcano Engine Reinforcement Learning for LLMs):** SimpleVLA-RL extends this general-purpose RL framework, initially designed for LLMs, to handle the complexities of VLA.
- **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization):** The core RL algorithm employed is GRPO, a critic-free method that computes advantages by normalizing rewards within a group of trajectories. This removes the need for a separate value function, simplifying the training process. The authors modify GRPO by removing the KL divergence regularization, further reducing memory and accelerating training, and allowing for greater exploration.

**VLA-Specific Adaptations:**
1. **Interactive VLA Rollout:**
 - **Action Decoding:** Unlike LLMs that generate text tokens, VLA models deal with continuous robot actions. SimpleVLA-RL adopts a token-based action generation approach (similar to LLMs), where the VLA model outputs action token probability distributions. This makes it compatible with PPO-like RL algorithms that require action distributions for random sampling and policy gradient computation.
 - **Closed-Loop Interaction:** A critical distinction from LLM rollout is that VLA requires multi-round interaction with the environment. The policy generates an action chunk, the robot executes it, and the environment provides updated visual observations and robot states. This closed-loop feedback loop is essential as each action alters the environment for subsequent actions.
 - **Scalable Parallelization:** To address the slower and costlier nature of VLA rollouts, veRL is extended with parallel multi-environment rendering. This allows for faster, synchronous sampling of diverse trajectories across multiple simulated environments.

2. **Outcome Reward Modeling:**
 - **Binary Outcome Rewards:** SimpleVLA-RL uses a straightforward, scalable binary reward function: 1 for successful task completion, 0 for failure. This trajectory-level reward is uniformly propagated to all action tokens within that trajectory.
 - **Benefits:** This outcome-based approach avoids the complexities, non-transferability, and design challenges of hand-crafted process rewards, making it broadly applicable across diverse environments and tasks.

**Exploration Enhancements:**
Recognizing that VLA tasks often have multiple valid solutions and that VLA models can converge to narrow solution patterns, SimpleVLA-RL incorporates three strategies to boost exploration during RL training:
1. **Dynamic Sampling:** To mitigate vanishing gradients in critic-free RL (where all trajectories in a group might receive identical rewards), dynamic sampling ensures that only groups with mixed outcomes (i.e., some successes and some failures) are used for advantage computation. This guarantees non-zero advantage estimates and stable gradient flow.
2. **Clipping Higher PPO Parameter:** The clipping range in the GRPO objective is modified from the standard `[0.8, 1.2]` to `[0.8, 1.28]`. This increased upper bound for the importance sampling ratio allows low-probability but potentially valuable actions to increase their probability more aggressively, encouraging broader exploration.
3. **Higher Rollout Temperature:** The sampling temperature during trajectory rollout is increased from 1.0 to 1.6. A higher temperature promotes more diverse and exploratory actions, allowing the VLA model to discover novel strategies.

**Training Objective:**
The final policy optimization objective is based on GRPO, incorporating the modified clipping range (`𝜀_low = 0.2`, `𝜀_high = 0.28`), and the dynamic sampling constraint (ensuring mixed outcomes within a group). The KL regularization term present in standard GRPO is removed to accelerate training and prevent over-constraining the policy.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Backbone Model:** OpenVLA-OFT (a state-of-the-art autoregressive VLA model) is used as the base policy, modified for single-view images, language instructions, and robot proprioceptive states, with parallel decoding and action chunking using an LLaMA2 output head for action token generation.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluation is conducted on LIBERO (LIBERO-Goal, -Spatial, -Object, -Long, -90), RoboTwin1.0 (bimanual tasks), and RoboTwin2.0 (dual-arm tasks with domain randomization).
- **Training Paradigm:** A two-stage paradigm: SFT followed by SimpleVLA-RL on the SFT-tuned model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SimpleVLA-RL demonstrates significant advancements across multiple dimensions, validating the effectiveness of reinforcement learning for VLA models.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance in Simulation

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** SimpleVLA-RL dramatically improves the average success rate of the SFT-tuned OpenVLA-OFT model from 91% to 99.1%, achieving new state-of-the-art performance. Notably, on LIBERO-Long tasks, it achieves 98.5% success rate, a 12% improvement over the baseline and surpassing leading VLA models like $\pi_0$ and UniVLA.
- **RoboTwin 1.0:** On dual-arm tasks, SimpleVLA-RL shows a substantial 30.6% gain, increasing success rates from 39.8% to 70.4% compared to the SFT-tuned OpenVLA-OFT baseline.
- **RoboTwin 2.0:** Across 12 dual-arm tasks categorized by planning horizon, SimpleVLA-RL achieves an 80% relative improvement, boosting performance from 38.3% to 68.8%. It significantly outperforms other SoTA methods like $\pi_0$ (49.2%) and RDT (33.3%). Even on challenging Extra-Long-Horizon tasks, it yields gains of 11.1% to 18.7%. This indicates consistent improvements across varying task complexities and horizons.

#### 4.2. Overcoming Data Scarcity

- SimpleVLA-RL drastically reduces reliance on large demonstration datasets. With only *one* demonstration trajectory per task (One-Trajectory SFT), the average success rate across LIBERO task suites increases from a mere 48.9% (after SFT) to 96.9% after applying RL.
- This performance (96.9%) even surpasses that of Full-Trajectory SFT alone (91%) and is only 2.2% shy of Full-Trajectory SFT + RL (99.1%).
- On LIBERO-Long, success rates soar from 17.3% (One-Trajectory SFT) to 91.7% (One-Trajectory SFT + RL), a 430.1% relative increase. This highlights RL's potential to alleviate the data scarcity bottleneck.

#### 4.3. Strong Generalization Capabilities

- SimpleVLA-RL significantly enhances generalization across spatial configurations (LIBERO-Spatial), object types (LIBERO-Object), and task settings (LIBERO-Goal).
- In contrast to SFT, which often suffers from catastrophic forgetting and severe performance degradation on unseen tasks (with success rates dropping to 0%), SimpleVLA-RL consistently improves performance on unseen tasks. For instance, on LIBERO-Spatial, SFT degrades by 10% on one unseen task and completely fails on others, while SimpleVLA-RL improves performance by 28.5% on Unseen Task 1 and achieves gains of 7.1% and 13.3% on others. This indicates that RL training helps VLA models learn more generalizable skills.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment Capability (Sim-to-Real Transfer)

- Policies trained purely in simulation using SimpleVLA-RL exhibit strong sim-to-real transfer.
- On four RoboTwin2.0 real-world tasks, RL training improved success rates from an average of 17.5% (SFT-only) to 38.5%, outperforming the RDT baseline (23.5%).
- For tasks requiring high action precision like "Pick Bottle," the SFT model completely failed (0%), while SimpleVLA-RL achieved a 15% success rate, demonstrating improved precision in real-world manipulation.

#### 4.5. Emergence of New Patterns: The "Pushcut" Phenomenon

- A novel and striking finding is the "pushcut" phenomenon, where the RL-trained policy discovers new, more efficient strategies not present in the original demonstration data.
- For example, in tasks like "move can pot" or "place a2b left/right," where demonstrations exclusively used a "grasp–move–place" strategy, the RL-trained model autonomously learned to simply "push" objects into position. This highlights RL's ability to explore beyond imitation and uncover emergent, optimal behaviors driven by outcome rewards.

#### 4.6. Failure Modes and the Importance of Model Priors

- The research identifies that SimpleVLA-RL completely fails when the base model (0-trajectory SFT) has no initial task-relevant ability (0% success rate). In such cases, all trajectories receive zero reward, leading to no gradient updates.
- The effectiveness of RL is strongly correlated with the initial competence of the SFT-tuned model. Stronger initial priors (e.g., 1000-trajectory SFT vs. 100-trajectory SFT) lead to greater performance improvements after RL.
- There appears to be a performance threshold: when initial success rates are too low, RL provides only marginal gains, as effective exploration becomes challenging.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "SimpleVLA-RL: Scaling VLA Training via Reinforcement Learning" hold profound significance and potential impact for the future of robotic manipulation and embodied AI:

1. **Democratization of Robotic Learning:** By demonstrating that RL can achieve high performance with drastically reduced demonstration data (even one-shot SFT leading to near SoTA results), SimpleVLA-RL offers a path to mitigate the prohibitive cost and scarcity of high-quality robotic datasets. This could democratize access to advanced robotic capabilities, making them more attainable for researchers and practitioners without extensive data collection resources.

2. **Enhanced Autonomy and Adaptability:** The improved generalization capabilities of SimpleVLA-RL-trained models across various spatial, object, and task variations are critical for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments. Robots will be better equipped to handle novel situations and adapt to changes, moving closer to truly autonomous operation. The strong sim-to-real transfer further validates this, showing that policies refined in cost-effective simulations can directly translate to tangible performance gains in the physical world.

3. **Discovery of Emergent, Optimal Behaviors:** The "pushcut" phenomenon is a compelling demonstration of RL's capacity to transcend human-demonstrated solutions. It suggests that RL can enable robots to discover more efficient, non-obvious strategies by optimizing for task outcomes rather than merely imitating trajectories. This could lead to a new generation of robots that not only perform tasks but also innovate and refine their methods, much like humans do. This mirrors the "Aha Moment" observed in reasoning LLMs, indicating a powerful general principle for AI advancement.

4. **Scaling Real-World Policies:** The effectiveness of simulation-trained RL policies in real-world scenarios, without requiring any real robot data for RL, is a major step towards scaling up real-world robotic policy learning. It suggests that leveraging rich simulation assets and high-fidelity simulators for large-scale, cost-effective RL training can yield superior performance in real-world deployment, reducing the need for expensive and time-consuming real-robot experimentation.

5. **New Research Directions for VLA-RL:** This work serves as a strong empirical foundation for future research in VLA-RL. It highlights the importance of initial model priors for effective RL and opens avenues for developing methods to bootstrap RL when base models lack initial competence. Further exploration into diverse reward signals, advanced exploration strategies, and methods to reliably identify and harness emergent behaviors will likely follow. The explicit adaptation of LLM-RL techniques to the VLA domain also encourages cross-pollination of ideas between these two rapidly evolving fields.

In conclusion, SimpleVLA-RL presents a compelling case for the pivotal role of reinforcement learning in scaling and advancing Vision-Language-Action models. It not only addresses critical challenges of data scarcity and generalization but also unlocks the potential for robots to discover novel, more efficient behaviors, marking a significant stride towards more intelligent, versatile, and autonomous robotic systems.
