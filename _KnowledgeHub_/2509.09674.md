# SimpleVLA-RL: Scaling VLA Training via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SimpleVLA-RL is a reinforcement learning framework designed to improve Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by using outcome-based rewards and an efficient online training approach. This method significantly enhances model performance, generalization, and data efficiency in robotic manipulation tasks while also enabling the discovery of novel manipulation strategies.

## Problem

- VLA model training suffers from data scarcity and high costs associated with acquiring extensive, high-quality human-operated robotic trajectories for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- SFT-trained VLA models often exhibit poor generalization to new, unseen tasks, objects, or environmental configurations.
- Prior VLA-RL methods frequently relied on complex hand-crafted process rewards or intricate reward modeling, which limits their scalability and broad applicability.

## Method

- The research developed **SimpleVLA-RL**, an efficient ==online reinforcement learning== framework for VLA models, by extending the ==veRL== framework for LLMs and adapting the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== algorithm.
- It employs a scalable, sparse ==binary outcome-based reward== (1 for task success, 0 for failure) that is propagated uniformly across trajectories, simplifying reward design.
- The framework integrates VLA-specific adaptations, including ==token-based action generation==, closed-loop multi-round environment interaction, and parallel multi-environment rendering for faster trajectory sampling.

## Results

- SimpleVLA-RL achieved state-of-the-art performance on the LIBERO benchmark, boosting the average success rate from **91%** to **99.1%** and improving LIBERO-Long tasks by **12%**.
- It demonstrated robust data efficiency, increasing success rates from **48.9%** (One-Trajectory SFT) to **96.9%** on LIBERO tasks after RL training, approaching the performance of full-data SFT+RL.
- The policies trained in simulation showed strong sim-to-real transfer, improving real-world success rates on RoboTwin2.0 tasks from **17.5%** (SFT-only) to **38.5%** on average.
- The RL-trained policy discovered novel "pushcut" strategies in tasks like "move can pot," replacing the demonstrated grasp-and-place behaviors with more efficient pushing actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning with sparse, outcome-based rewards can drastically reduce the reliance on extensive demonstration data for VLA models, achieving near state-of-the-art performance with significantly fewer demonstrations.
> - RL training substantially enhances VLA models' generalization capabilities across spatial, object, and task variations, mitigating catastrophic forgetting observed in SFT-only methods.
> - RL can drive the discovery of emergent, more efficient behaviors (e.g., "pushcut" strategies) that were not present in human demonstrations, indicating optimization beyond pure imitation.
> - The effectiveness of VLA-RL is strongly correlated with the initial competence of the SFT-tuned base model; stronger initial priors lead to greater performance improvements after RL.
