# InternVL3: Exploring Advanced Training and Test-Time Recipes for Open-Source Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> InternVL3 establishes a new native multimodal pre-training paradigm for MLLMs, allowing the model to jointly acquire visual and linguistic capabilities from the outset. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source models, reaching 72.2 on the MMMU benchmark, and demonstrates strong competitiveness with leading proprietary models across a wide range of multimodal tasks.

## Problem

- Conventional Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) use a 'post-hoc' adaptation approach, adding vision to pre-trained text LLMs, which is resource-intensive and prone to alignment issues.
- Existing MLLM training paradigms struggle with efficient scalability for extended multimodal contexts and balancing computational load across modalities.
- Bridging the performance gap between open-source and proprietary MLLMs while maintaining strong linguistic capabilities is a challenge.

## Method

- Introduces a ==native multimodal pre-training== paradigm that simultaneously acquires multimodal and linguistic capabilities from diverse data and pure-text corpora in a single, unified stage.
- Integrates ==Variable Visual Position Encoding (V2PE)== to support extended multimodal contexts and employs a two-stage post-training strategy with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and ==Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO)==.
- Utilizes an optimized InternEVO training infrastructure for efficient scaling to thousands of GPUs, incorporating dynamic workload balancing for visual and textual modules.

## Results

- InternVL3-78B achieved a new open-source state-of-the-art score of **72.2** on the MMMU benchmark, demonstrating competitiveness with leading proprietary models like ChatGPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet.
- The models showed significant improvements across diverse benchmarks, including a **7.3-point** increase on MathVista (**79.6%**) and a **52-point** increase on OCRBench (**906**) compared to InternVL2.5.
- InternVL3 maintained and often surpassed the pure-language proficiency of text-only LLMs on benchmarks like MMLU and C-Eval, indicating that joint training does not compromise linguistic abilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Native multimodal pre-training, which jointly optimizes vision and language from scratch, is effective and comparable to or better than traditional multi-stage adaptation.
> - Variable Visual Position Encoding (V2PE) enhances performance across multimodal tasks by efficiently managing varied visual token densities and supporting extended contexts.
> - Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO) significantly improves reasoning performance by explicitly addressing the train-inference discrepancy in Chain-of-Thought reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) is a cornerstone of current AI research, driving progress towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). The prevailing paradigm in MLLM development, for both open-source and proprietary models, involves a "post-hoc" adaptation approach. In this common multi-stage pipeline, a text-only Large Language Model (LLM) is first pre-trained on vast text corpora, and then visual inputs are integrated by adding a vision encoder (e.g., a Vision Transformer) and a connection module (e.g., an MLP). This process often necessitates complex multi-stage fine-tuning, parameter freezing schedules, and supplementary data from specialized domains (e.g., OCR) to bridge the inherent modality gaps and prevent degradation of the LLM's core linguistic capabilities. While successful, this approach is resource-intensive, intricate, and prone to alignment challenges between modalities.

InternVL3 directly addresses these limitations by introducing a **native multimodal pre-training paradigm**. Instead of adapting a pre-trained text LLM, InternVL3 simultaneously acquires both multimodal and linguistic capabilities from diverse multimodal data and pure-text corpora during a single, unified pre-training stage. This fundamental shift aims to overcome the complexities and alignment issues of conventional multi-stage pipelines.

This work contributes to a broader trend of developing more integrated and efficient MLLMs, moving away from fragmented, sequential training. It positions InternVL3 as a direct competitor to leading proprietary models like ChatGPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Gemini 2.5 Pro, while steadfastly adhering to open-science principles by releasing models and data. By proving the viability and superior performance of native multimodal pre-training in an open-source context, InternVL3 influences the architectural and training philosophy for next-generation MLLMs. Furthermore, its focus on advanced techniques like Variable Visual Position Encoding (V2PE) for extended contexts, mixed preference optimization (MPO), and optimized training infrastructure (InternEVO) reflects the ongoing arms race in scaling MLLM capabilities, efficiency, and robustness, particularly in challenging areas like multi-image understanding, real-world comprehension, GUI grounding, and spatial reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind InternVL3 are clearly articulated in the paper's abstract and introduction:

1. **Develop a Native Multimodal Pre-training Paradigm:** The core objective is to move away from the conventional "post-hoc" adaptation of text-only LLMs. InternVL3 aims to jointly learn multimodal and linguistic capabilities from the outset, exposing the model to both diverse multimodal data and pure-text corpora in a single pre-training stage. This is motivated by the desire to effectively address the complexities, alignment challenges, and resource intensiveness of multi-stage pipelines.

2. **Enhance Performance and Scalability:** A central goal is to significantly improve performance across a wide range of multimodal tasks and ensure the model's scalability for extended contexts and larger models. This involves incorporating:
 - **Variable Visual Position Encoding (V2PE):** To support extended multimodal contexts and improve handling of varying visual token densities.
 - **Advanced Post-training Techniques:** Specifically, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO), to further refine conversational and reasoning abilities, especially mitigating distribution shifts during inference.
 - **Optimized Training Infrastructure:** Leveraging and extending the InternEVO framework to enable efficient scaling to hundreds of billions of parameters across thousands of GPUs, addressing computational load imbalances in MLLM training.
 - **Test-Time Scaling Strategies:** Employing techniques like Best-of-N evaluation with a critic model (VisualPRM) to further boost reasoning performance.

3. **Achieve State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance among Open-Source MLLMs:** The paper explicitly aims to establish InternVL3 as a new benchmark leader within the open-source community, significantly outperforming existing open-source models (e.g., InternVL2.5, Qwen2.5-VL) across various benchmarks.

4. **Maintain Competitiveness with Leading Proprietary Models:** While focusing on open-source SOTA, a crucial motivation is to narrow the performance gap and achieve highly competitive results against powerful proprietary models such as ChatGPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Gemini 2.5 Pro.

5. **Preserve Strong Pure-Language Proficiency:** Unlike some MLLMs that might sacrifice linguistic capabilities during multimodal adaptation, InternVL3 aims to maintain strong pure-language proficiency alongside its multimodal prowess, a critical aspect of generalist AI.

6. **Adhere to Open-Science Principles:** A fundamental motivation is to publicly release the training data and model weights. This commitment aims to foster transparency, reproducibility, and accelerate further research and development within the open-source community for next-generation MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InternVL3 builds upon the "ViT-MLP-LLM" architecture framework of its predecessors, but introduces several key innovations across its training pipeline, from pre-training to post-training and infrastructure.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

InternVL3 employs a standard "ViT-MLP-LLM" architecture. The Vision Encoder is initialized with pre-trained InternViT (300M or 6B parameters), and the Language Model component uses pre-trained base models from the Qwen2.5 series or InternLM3-8B, critically *without* instruction-tuned variants. A two-layer MLP connects the visual and language components. A pixel unshuffle operation is used for high-resolution image processing, reducing visual token count.

A notable architectural enhancement is the integration of **Variable Visual Position Encoding (V2PE)**. Unlike conventional positional encodings where indices increment uniformly, V2PE uses modality-specific increments. Textual tokens retain a standard increment of 1, while visual tokens increment by a smaller value, $\delta$, randomly chosen from a predefined set of fractional values (e.g., 1/2, 1/4..., 1/256) during training. This allows V2PE to accommodate longer multimodal contexts without excessively extending the position window, effectively compressing visual positional information. During inference, $\delta$ can be flexibly selected based on input length.

#### 3.2. Native Multimodal Pre-Training

This is the core distinguishing feature. InternVL3 consolidates language pre-training and multimodal alignment into a single, unified stage.
- **Unified Optimization:** Instead of a sequential process, all model parameters (ViT, MLP, LLM) are jointly optimized from the pre-training stage. This involves interleaving diverse multimodal data (image-text, video-text, interleaved image-text sequences) with large-scale text-only corpora.
- **Multimodal Autoregressive Formulation:** The model is trained with a left-to-right autoregressive objective, but loss computation is restricted *exclusively to text tokens*. Visual tokens serve as conditioning context, ensuring the model learns to embed multimodal information for language decoding. The paper adopts a "square averaging" strategy for token loss weights to mitigate biases towards short or long responses.
- **Data Strategy:** The pre-training data combines existing InternVL2.5 corpora (image captioning, QA, math, charts, OCR, documents, multi-turn dialogue, medical) with newly acquired real-world data (GUI, tool usage, 3D scene understanding, video comprehension). Pure language data, primarily from InternLM2.5 and other open-source text datasets, is integrated to preserve and amplify linguistic capabilities. An empirical two-stage sampling strategy determined an optimal 1:3 ratio of language to multimodal data, resulting in approximately 200 billion total training tokens.

#### 3.3. Post-Training

After native multimodal pre-training, a two-stage post-training strategy further enhances the model's capabilities:
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The model is trained on high-quality, diverse instruction-following data. Techniques like random JPEG compression, square loss re-weighting, and multimodal data packing (from InternVL2.5) are employed. The SFT data is significantly expanded to cover tool usage, 3D scenes, GUI, long contexts, video, scientific diagrams, and creative/multimodal reasoning.
- **Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO):** This phase addresses the train-inference discrepancy (ground-truth vs. model-predicted tokens) that can impair Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. MPO combines three loss components:
 - **Preference Loss ($L_p$):** Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is used to learn relative preferences between chosen and rejected responses.
 - **Quality Loss ($L_q$):** Binary Classifier Optimization (BCO) helps the model understand the absolute quality of individual responses (chosen vs. rejected).
 - **Generation Loss ($L_g$):** Standard LM loss is applied to encourage generation of preferred responses.
 MPO data consists of approximately 300K preference pairs covering various visual QA domains, generated by InternVL3 SFT versions.

#### 3.4. Test-Time Scaling

To boost reasoning and mathematics evaluation, InternVL3 employs the Best-of-N evaluation strategy, utilizing VisualPRM-8B as a critic model. VisualPRM assigns quality scores to each step of a solution, helping select the best response. The critic model is trained on VisualPRM400K, expanded with rollouts from InternVL3 variants.

#### 3.5. Infrastructure

Model training is supported by an extended **InternEVO framework**, originally designed for large-scale LLM training. This framework enables efficient scaling to thousands of GPUs by providing flexible and decoupled sharding strategies for ViT, MLP, and LLM components. It optimizes training efficiency by overlapping communication and computation and supports comprehensive parallelism strategies. Crucially, InternEVO includes techniques to dynamically balance computational workloads across visual and textual modules, addressing imbalances and achieving a 50% to 200% training speedup compared to InternVL2.5.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InternVL3 demonstrates significant advancements, setting new benchmarks for open-source MLLMs and proving highly competitive with leading proprietary models.

**Overall Multimodal Performance:**
- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Open-Source:** InternVL3-78B achieved a score of **72.2 on the MMMU benchmark**, establishing a new SOTA among open-source MLLMs.
- **Competitiveness with Proprietary Models:** InternVL3 is highly competitive with ChatGPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Gemini 2.5 Pro. In some benchmarks (e.g., AI2D, ChartQA), InternVL3 even surpasses these proprietary models, though Gemini 2.5 Pro retains an edge in certain areas like HallusionBench.
- **Consistent Improvements:** Across the InternVL series, InternVL3 consistently outperforms its predecessors (e.g., InternVL2.5) across a wide range of tasks, attributed to enhanced training, refined testing, and expanded training corpus.

**Specific Domain Performance Highlights:**
- **Multimodal Reasoning and Mathematics:** InternVL3-78B scored 79.6% on MathVista, a substantial 7.3-point improvement over InternVL2.5. Test-time scaling (VisualPRM-Bo8) further enhances reasoning, especially for smaller models (e.g., InternVL3-1B's MMMU score improved by 12 points).
- **OCR, Chart, and Document Understanding:** InternVL3-78B achieved an OCRBench score of 906 (a 52-point increase from InternVL2.5), and strong performance on DocVQA (95.4%) and ChartQA (89.7%), demonstrating robust visual-textual integration.
- **Multi-Image Understanding:** InternVL3 consistently improved performance on benchmarks like BLINK and MMT-Bench, showing enhanced capability to capture and reason over inter-image relationships.
- **Real-World Comprehension:** InternVL3-78B scored 78.0 on RealWorldQA and 77.4 on R-Bench, outperforming GPT-4o on RealWorldQA and MME-RealWorld.
- **Comprehensive Multimodal Evaluation:** InternVL3 models consistently showed superior performance on benchmarks like MME and MMBench (both English and Chinese).
- **Multimodal Hallucination Evaluation:** InternVL3 showed competitive performance, with InternVL3-78B reaching 59.1 on HallusionBench, indicating improved handling of multimodal hallucinations.
- **Visual Grounding:** InternVL3 demonstrated strong visual grounding capabilities on RefCOCO datasets, though larger models showed a plateau or slight decrease compared to InternVL2.5-78B, possibly due to a relative reduction in grounding-specific data in the expanded corpus.
- **Multimodal Multilingual Understanding:** InternVL3 achieved consistent improvements across various languages on MMMB, Multilingual MMBench, and MTVQA.
- **Video Understanding:** InternVL3 models showed clear performance improvements and strong scalability on video benchmarks like Video-MME and MVBench, with InternVL3-78B achieving a LongVideoBench score of 65.7.
- **GUI Grounding & Spatial Reasoning:** InternVL3-72B achieved 90.9% on ScreenSpot-V2, outperforming UI-TARS-72B. In spatial reasoning (VSI-Bench), InternVL3-78B scored 48.4, surpassing proprietary models like GPT-4o.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Native Multimodal Pre-Training:** Experiments showed that models with native multimodal pre-training (even without further post-training) achieved comparable performance to conventionally trained InternVL2-8B, and further gains were observed after instruction tuning. This validates the efficiency of the native approach.
- **Variable Visual Position Encoding (V2PE):** V2PE significantly improved performance across most multimodal benchmarks, even for short-context tasks, with smaller $\delta$ values often yielding optimal results. This highlights its efficacy beyond just long-context scenarios.
- **Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO):** Models fine-tuned with MPO demonstrated superior reasoning performance, with InternVL3-78B gaining 4.1 points and InternVL3-38B gaining 4.5 points on average across reasoning benchmarks, primarily due to the algorithm rather than data scale.

**Language Capability:**
- Despite multimodal pre-training, InternVL3 variants consistently demonstrated superior language capabilities compared to their corresponding Qwen2.5 chat models (which share the same base LLM initialization but use a pure-text training pipeline), across MMLU, CMMLU, C-Eval, GSM8K, and coding benchmarks. This confirms that the integrated training strategy does not compromise linguistic proficiency and can even enhance it.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of InternVL3 carries significant implications for the landscape of multimodal AI research and development:

1. **Paradigm Shift in MLLM Training:** InternVL3's native multimodal pre-training paradigm represents a fundamental departure from the prevailing "post-hoc" adaptation strategies. By jointly acquiring linguistic and multimodal capabilities from scratch, it offers a more elegant, efficient, and potentially more robust training approach, mitigating the inherent alignment challenges and complexities of multi-stage pipelines. This could inspire future MLLM development to adopt more unified training methodologies.

2. **Elevating Open-Source MLLM Capabilities:** InternVL3-78B's achievement of a new state-of-the-art on the MMMU benchmark for open-source models is a significant milestone. It demonstrates that open-source initiatives can compete directly with and, in some cases, even surpass leading proprietary models. This fosters a healthier, more competitive research environment and accelerates progress across the AI community.

3. **Advancing Scalability and Efficiency:** The integration of Variable Visual Position Encoding (V2PE) for extended multimodal contexts and the optimized InternEVO training infrastructure addresses critical scalability challenges. The demonstrated training speedup and efficient resource utilization make the development of larger, more capable MLLMs more feasible for research groups with substantial but not unlimited resources.

4. **Robustness and Generalization Across Domains:** The extensive empirical evaluations showcase InternVL3's superior performance across a wide array of multimodal tasks—from reasoning and document understanding to multi-image, video, GUI, and spatial reasoning. This broad applicability and strong generalization ability highlight its potential for diverse real-world applications. The continued strength in pure-language tasks further underscores its generalist capabilities.

5. **Fostering Open Science and Community Innovation:** The commitment to publicly releasing InternVL3's training data and model weights is a critical contribution. This transparency and reproducibility enable other researchers to build upon this work, conduct further experiments, and accelerate the pace of innovation in next-generation MLLMs. It democratizes access to advanced MLLM technology, reducing the barrier to entry for many researchers and developers.

In summary, InternVL3 not only pushes the performance boundaries for open-source multimodal models but also proposes a more integrated and efficient training philosophy. Its comprehensive evaluation and commitment to open science are poised to significantly influence the future direction and accessibility of multimodal AI research.
