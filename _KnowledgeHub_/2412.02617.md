# Improving Dynamic Object Interactions in Text-to-Video Generation with AI Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Google DeepMind, The University of Tokyo, and Stanford University advanced text-to-video generation by leveraging AI feedback from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to enhance dynamic object interactions. This approach led to a 14.7% increase in human preference for video quality and physical realism in challenging interaction scenarios.

## Problem

- Text-to-video (T2V) models frequently generate unrealistic, physically inaccurate, or static object movements, hindering practical utility.
- Existing metric-based feedback for T2V models does not specifically target the nuanced and complex aspects of dynamic object interactions.
- Scaling T2V models is challenging due to the high cost and labor intensity of curating video datasets with detailed labels for dynamic interactions.

## Method

- Developed a probabilistic framework to unify Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for offline ==reinforcement learning== (==RL==) ==fine-tuning== of video diffusion models.
- Introduced AI feedback from large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as Gemini-1.5-Pro, to provide perceptual feedback specifically tailored to object dynamics, physical accuracy, and task completion.
- Applied ==VLM==-guided RL fine-tuning to a 3D-UNet ==video diffusion model==, leveraging a custom dataset of prompts designed for challenging dynamic object movements.

## Results

- DPO fine-tuned with AI feedback (DPO-AIF) achieved absolute gains of +14.7% in human evaluation and +3.5% in Gemini-**1.5**-Pro evaluation for the quality of dynamic interaction scenes.
- The VLM-based AI feedback demonstrated the strongest positive correlation with human preference (Pearson R = **0.746**) among all evaluated feedback types.
- The method substantially improved video generation quality in challenging categories such as 'multiple objects' and 'falling down,' which are difficult for pre-trained models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - AI feedback from Vision-Language Models can effectively serve as a scalable proxy for human supervision, demonstrating a strong correlation with human preference in evaluating video quality.
> - Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) consistently outperforms Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR) for fine-tuning, showing better generalization and aligning outputs without signs of overfitting due to its explicit negative gradient.
> - Reinforcement learning fine-tuning, particularly when guided by VLM-based AI feedback, substantially enhances the realism and accuracy of dynamic object interactions in generated videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Text-to-video (T2V) generation models are recognized for their potential in applications such as creative content generation, game development, animation, and real-world simulation. However, a persistent challenge in the field is the difficulty these models face in accurately depicting dynamic object interactions. Current state-of-the-art T2V models often produce movements that are unrealistic, violate principles of physics, result in static scenes, or fail to incorporate specified movements from the input text prompts. This limitation hinders the practical utility of T2V systems.

The advancement of large language models (LLMs) has demonstrated that integrating external feedback can significantly improve model outputs, particularly in reducing undesirable behaviors. This success has inspired researchers to explore similar feedback mechanisms for generative models in other modalities, including text-to-image and text-to-video. While expanding datasets and model sizes has been effective for LLMs, video generation presents unique complexities related to labor-intensive data labeling and architectural scalability, making direct scaling approaches less straightforward.

Previous efforts to fine-tune text-to-video models with feedback have often relied on metric-based rewards, such as CLIP scores for text-image alignment, HPSv2 or PickScore for human aesthetic preference, and optical flow for motion dynamics. These metrics typically assess general visual quality, style, or overall coherence. However, they do not specifically focus on the nuanced and complex aspects of dynamic object interactions and physical plausibility, which remain critical failure modes for T2V models.

This research addresses the gap by investigating how different types of feedback, specifically AI feedback from Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and various reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning algorithms can be combined to overcome movement misalignment and enhance the realism of object interactions in generated videos. It positions itself at the intersection of generative AI, reinforcement learning, and multimodal understanding, aiming to develop scalable and effective post-training methods for improving the physical realism of generated video content.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the quality of dynamic object interactions in text-to-video diffusion models through the application of external feedback. The authors aim to identify the most effective types of feedback and self-improvement algorithms to mitigate issues such as movement misalignment and physically unrealistic object interactions.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:
- **Limitations of Current T2V Models:** Despite significant advancements in text-to-video generation, even sophisticated models frequently produce videos with unrealistic object movements, defy physical laws, generate static scenes, or ignore specified dynamic actions within prompts. Examples include objects failing to fall correctly or movements that do not logically follow multi-step interactions. These shortcomings restrict the deployment of T2V models in practical applications requiring high fidelity and physical accuracy, such as creative content, simulations, or robotics.
- **Challenges of Scaling for Video:** The successful scaling strategies observed in large language models, involving vast datasets and larger architectures, are not directly transferable to video generation. Video datasets with detailed, high-quality language labels for dynamic interactions are difficult and costly to curate. Furthermore, video generation architectures have not yet reached the same level of effective scalability as their language counterparts.
- **Success of Feedback in LLMs:** The integration of external feedback, particularly human feedback and later AI feedback, has been a pivotal development in improving the alignment and performance of LLMs. This success suggests a similar approach could be beneficial for T2V models if appropriate feedback mechanisms and integration strategies are developed.
- **Need for Targeted Feedback:** Existing metric-based feedback for T2V models often focuses on general visual quality or text-video alignment but does not adequately capture the correctness of dynamic object interactions. There is a need for feedback that specifically evaluates physical accuracy, task completion, and the consistency of object movements over time.
- **Exploration of Algorithmic Choices:** The paper is motivated to understand the theoretical underpinnings and practical implications of different offline RL fine-tuning algorithms, specifically Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). By showing their derivation from a unified probabilistic objective, the authors motivate an empirical comparison based on their properties, reward signals, and data characteristics rather than inherent algorithmic dominance.
- **Scalability of AI Feedback:** Human evaluation for video quality is resource-intensive. The rise of capable Vision-Language Models (VLMs) presents an opportunity to develop scalable AI feedback mechanisms that can interpret video scenes in a manner analogous to human perception, thereby providing valuable supervisory signals.

By addressing these motivations, the research seeks to provide a comprehensive "recipe" for fine-tuning text-to-video models that specifically improves the realism and accuracy of dynamic object interactions, thereby expanding their utility and reliability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involves a multi-faceted approach combining a foundational video diffusion model, a probabilistic framework for RL fine-tuning, various feedback mechanisms (including a novel AI feedback from VLMs), and a specialized dataset for evaluating dynamic object interactions.

#### 3.1. Base Model and Pre-training

The core generative model is a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM), implemented using a 3D-UNet architecture with approximately 3 billion parameters. This model is pre-trained on the Something-Something-V2 dataset, which contains 160,000 text-video pairs. The model supports text and first-frame conditioning, allowing for control over the generated content and initial state.

#### 3.2. Probabilistic Inference View of RL-Finetuning Objectives

The paper frames the denoising process in DDPMs as a T-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP). It introduces a binary event variable `O` representing the optimality of a generated video. From the log-likelihood of this optimal event `log p(O = 1 | c)`, a unified objective `J_RL` for RL-finetuning is derived. This objective maximizes a reward function `r(x_0, c)` under KL-regularization. This unified perspective reveals that two representative offline RL algorithms, Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), can be derived from the same objective with different design choices.
- **Forward-EM-Projection (leading to RWR):** This approach parameterizes the variational distribution as the target policy and the reference policy as the pre-trained model. It simplifies `J_RL` into a reward-weighted mean squared error objective (`J_f-EM`), where the model is fine-tuned to generate samples that match the pre-trained distribution but are weighted by their reward. This method aims to shift the distribution of generated videos towards higher-reward samples.
- **Reverse-BT-Projection (leading to DPO):** This method parameterizes the target policy as the current model and the reference policy as the pre-trained model. It incorporates the Bradley-Terry assumption for preference probabilities, leading to a surrogate objective (`J_r-BT`) that maximizes the likelihood of preferred videos over dispreferred ones. DPO effectively includes a negative gradient that penalizes undesirable generations.

#### 3.3. Feedback Mechanisms (Rewards)

The study compares several types of feedback for optimizing the RL fine-tuning process:
- **Metric-based Rewards:**
 - **CLIP score:** Measures text-image alignment using a ViT-B16 architecture.
 - **HPSv2 and PickScore:** Predict human preference for images, trained on large-scale preference datasets. They use OpenCLIP with ViT-H14.
 - **Optical Flow:** Quantifies the dynamic degree in videos by estimating motion between successive frames using RAFT.
 - These metric-based rewards are subjected to linear transformation (reward shaping) to control gradient scale.
- **AI Feedback from Vision-Language Models (Proposed):**
 - This novel approach leverages large-scale VLMs (primarily Gemini-1.5-Pro, with GPT-4o for generalization testing) to provide perceptual feedback specifically tailored to object dynamics.
 - VLMs receive a textual prompt and a sequence of 8 video frames. They are tasked with evaluating the video for overall coherence, physical accuracy, task completion, and consistency.
 - The VLM outputs a binary label: "Accept" if the video meets criteria and completes the task, or "Reject" otherwise.
 - Preliminary evaluations confirm that VLMs can interpret video scenes and reliably distinguish true videos from generated ones, suggesting their capability to simulate human supervision.

#### 3.4. RL-Finetuning Pipeline

The overall pipeline for RL-finetuning involves:
1. **Video Generation:** Videos are generated from the pre-trained model using a set of prompts designed for dynamic scenes.
2. **Feedback Collection:** The generated videos are then evaluated by VLMs (for AI feedback) or by computing metric-based scores.
3. **Model Update:** The collected feedback data is used to fine-tune the video diffusion model using either the RWR or DPO algorithm.
4. **Iterative Finetuning:** The process can be iterated by generating new videos from the *improved* model and collecting further feedback to resolve distribution shift issues and enhance alignment.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

- **Challenging Object Movements Dataset:** A custom dataset of 160 prompts and corresponding reference videos is curated from the Something-Something-V2 validation split. These prompts are categorized into five challenging types of dynamic object movements: Object Removal (OR), Multiple Objects (MO), Deformable Object (DO), Directional Movement (DM), and Falling Down (FD). This dataset is split into 120 training and 40 testing prompts.
- **Data for Finetuning:** 128 samples per prompt are generated from the pre-trained model for fine-tuning data collection.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **VLM Evaluation:** Binary acceptance rate from Gemini-1.5-Pro and GPT-4o.
 - **Human Evaluation:** Binary human ratings on video quality and task completion.
 - **VBench:** A comprehensive benchmark suite providing quantitative metrics for perceptual video quality, including smoothness, consistency (overall, subject, background), and imaging quality.
 - Conventional video quality metrics (FVD, FID, IS) and CLIP score are also reported on a larger validation set to assess overall model performance.

This comprehensive approach allows for a systematic investigation into the efficacy of different feedback types and RL algorithms in addressing the specific challenge of dynamic object interactions in text-to-video generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness of RL fine-tuning, the comparison of algorithms, and the impact of different feedback types on improving dynamic object interactions in text-to-video generation.

#### 4.1. RL-Finetuning vs. Supervised Finetuning (SFT)

- RL-finetuning algorithms, specifically RWR and DPO, consistently demonstrate improvements across most evaluation metrics, including AI feedback scores, when compared to the pre-trained baseline and a supervised finetuning (SFT) approach (which uses no explicit reward signal).
- RL-finetuning methods also exhibit improved generalization to unseen test prompts compared to SFT, suggesting that incorporating reward signals is beneficial for learning desired behaviors.

#### 4.2. Comparison of RWR and DPO Algorithms

- **DPO Performance:** DPO generally outperforms RWR, showing improvements across a wider range of evaluation metrics (7 out of 10 metrics). DPO-finetuned models achieve higher scores in VLM evaluations, human preference, and VBench scores, indicating a more robust and effective alignment with desired dynamic interactions.
- **Generalization vs. Overfitting:** RWR tends to perform well on the training split but exhibits overfitting, with performance on test splits sometimes degrading compared to the pre-trained model. In contrast, DPO consistently aligns outputs without signs of overfitting, which is attributed to its negative gradient component that explicitly discourages undesirable generations.

#### 4.3. Efficacy of AI Feedback from VLMs

- **Superiority of AI Feedback:** RL-finetuning guided by AI feedback from Gemini (RWR-AIF and DPO-AIF) yields the most significant improvements in the quality of dynamic interaction scenes. This is confirmed by evaluations from Gemini itself, GPT-4o, and human raters. For instance, DPO-AIF achieved absolute gains of +3.5% in Gemini evaluation, +1.8% in GPT-4o evaluation, and a substantial +14.7% in human evaluation compared to the pre-trained model.
- **VBench Improvements:** AI feedback also leads to the best improvements across several VBench scores, particularly those related to video consistency and motion smoothness.
- These results suggest that VLMs can effectively serve as proxies for human supervision, providing valuable signals for improving video quality, especially concerning complex dynamic events.

#### 4.4. Iterative RL-Finetuning

- When applied iteratively, RWR with AI feedback (RWR-AIF) shows continuous improvements in AI preference over subsequent iterations, indicating its potential for further scaling through on-policy sample generation.
- Conversely, DPO-AIF's performance appears to saturate after the initial fine-tuning, possibly because the quality of generated videos becomes uniformly high, making binary preference assignments for subsequent iterations less informative.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Open Models

- Preliminary evaluations against several state-of-the-art open text-to-video models (e.g., VideoCrafter, CogVideoX, Wan 2.1) demonstrate that these models still struggle significantly with generating coherent and physically accurate dynamic object interactions. They often produce static scenes or movements that do not align with prompts, despite sometimes achieving high visual quality. The AI feedback results quantify this observed insufficiency in open models.

#### 4.6. Analysis of Challenging Object Movement Categories

- Pre-trained models generally perform better on categories like deformable objects and directional movement, which often involve simpler 2D motions.
- They exhibit significant weaknesses in object removal, multiple objects, and falling down categories, which typically require modeling multi-step interactions, the appearance of new objects, and accurate 3D spatial relationships.
- RL-finetuning with AI feedback substantially improves video generation in the "multiple objects" and "falling down" categories, where VLMs can more readily discern correctness. However, some complex multi-step interaction failures still persist even after fine-tuning.

#### 4.7. Correlation with Human Evaluation and Over-optimization

- When comparing averaged metrics across different algorithm-feedback combinations, AI preference from VLMs demonstrates the strongest positive correlation with human preference (Pearson R = 0.746, statistically significant). This supports the use of AI feedback as a proxy for human judgment.
- However, a per-prompt analysis reveals a weaker correlation (R = 0.231), indicating that while VLMs provide strong guidance for overall improvement, their preference rationale is not perfectly aligned with human perception across all nuances of video quality.
- The study also identifies an "over-optimization" issue with metric-based rewards (e.g., HPSv2) when used with DPO. While the metric score might improve, the generated videos can visually degrade or contain incorrect events (e.g., simply darkening frames), highlighting a potential disconnect between some automated metrics and true perceptual quality. This further emphasizes the value of human-aligned feedback, even if imperfect, from VLMs.

#### 4.8. Impact on Conventional Metrics

- While targeted improvements in dynamic interactions were achieved, conventional video quality metrics such as FVD, FID, and IS showed a slight decrease after RL-finetuning. Conversely, the CLIP score for text alignment generally improved. This suggests a trade-off where fine-tuning for specific dynamic interaction quality might lead to minor changes in general video statistics, while improving semantic alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the field of text-to-video generation:

**Enhanced Dynamic Object Interactions:** The primary significance lies in providing a demonstrated method to improve the fidelity and physical realism of dynamic object interactions in generated videos. By addressing a known and critical failure mode of current text-to-video models, this work moves closer to producing more believable and functionally useful video content. This capability is crucial for applications requiring high accuracy in object behavior, such such as creating realistic simulations, interactive games, or precise content for educational and creative purposes.

**Validation of AI Feedback from VLMs:** The study rigorously evaluates and validates the use of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as scalable providers of AI feedback for video generation. Demonstrating that VLM feedback strongly correlates with human preference and drives significant quality improvements is a key advancement. This can substantially reduce the cost and labor associated with human annotation, making it more feasible to fine-tune and align large video models. It establishes VLMs as a promising tool for automated quality assessment and iterative improvement in complex generative tasks.

**Algorithmic Insights and Practical Guidance:** By unifying RWR and DPO under a single probabilistic objective, the research provides a clearer understanding of their underlying mechanisms. The empirical comparison highlights their respective strengths: DPO for robust generalization and RWR for iterative improvement within specific data distributions. This offers practitioners valuable guidance for selecting appropriate RL fine-tuning algorithms based on their specific constraints (e.g., budget for iteration, need for generalization, type of reward signal).

**Generality and Applicability to Advanced Models:** The proposed post-training method is described as model- and architecture-agnostic. This implies that it can be applied not only to existing open-source models but also to advanced proprietary models (e.g., Sora, Veo) that may still exhibit subtle physics violations or dynamic inconsistencies despite their high visual quality. This broad applicability suggests the potential to universally enhance the realism of dynamic scenes across diverse T2V platforms.

**Development of Challenging Benchmarks:** The curation of a specialized dataset focusing on "challenging object movements" from Something-Something-V2 provides a valuable resource for the research community. This benchmark can be used to rigorously evaluate future text-to-video models specifically on their ability to handle complex dynamic interactions, thus fostering targeted progress in this area.

**Mitigation of Hallucinations and Physics Violations:** The successful fine-tuning aims to reduce the common issues of hallucinations and violations of real-world physics in generated videos. By teaching models to better adhere to physical plausibility and instruction following, the research contributes to making AI-generated video content more trustworthy and predictable.

**Future Research Directions:** The work opens several avenues for future research, including exploring generative reward modeling from VLMs, integrating online reinforcement learning methods for continuous improvement, and further refining the alignment between VLM-based evaluations and nuanced human perception. The identified limitations, such as persistent failures in multi-step interactions, also guide future efforts toward more sophisticated temporal reasoning and object interaction modeling.
