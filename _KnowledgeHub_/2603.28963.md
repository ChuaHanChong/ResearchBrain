# AutoWorld: Scaling Multi-Agent Traffic Simulation with Self-Supervised World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoWorld, a multi-agent traffic simulation framework from the University of Toronto, leverages self-supervised world models trained on unlabeled LiDAR data to achieve top performance on the WOSAC leaderboard with an RMM score of 0.7865. The system integrates a motion-aware latent occupancy predictor and a world-model-conditioned diffusion model, complemented by a cascaded latent diversity strategy for generating realistic and diverse traffic scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing data-driven traffic simulators rely heavily on costly supervised learning from labeled trajectory data or semantic annotations, which limits scalability and scenario diversity.
- Current world models in autonomous driving often focus on photorealism or require semantic labels, preventing effective utilization of abundant unlabeled sensor data and potentially overlooking critical dynamic scene elements.
- Achieving both diversity and realism in simulated multi-agent behaviors is challenging, often leading to a trade-off that can hinder comprehensive testing of autonomous driving systems.

## Method

- A two-stage framework is developed: a ==self-supervised world model== for forecasting scene dynamics from unlabeled LiDAR data, and a ==world-model-conditioned diffusion model== for multi-agent behavior generation.
- A novel ==motion-aware latent supervision== strategy is introduced for the world model, training it purely on unlabeled ==LiDAR occupancy representations== to prioritize dynamic regions over static ones.
- A training-free ==cascaded latent diversity== strategy employs quality-aware ==Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs)== to guide sampling at both the scene (world model) and agent (motion generation) levels, promoting diverse yet plausible rollouts.

## Results

- **AutoWorld** achieved the first rank on the WOSAC leaderboard with a Realism Meta Metric (RMM) score of **0.7865**, demonstrating balanced high performance across kinematic, interactive, and map-based realism metrics.
- Motion-aware latent supervision significantly improved dynamic-region occupancy prediction accuracy (IoU_dynamic **0.71** vs. **0.58** for uniform supervision) and world model rollout fidelity (FVD **35** vs. **51**), leading to higher downstream simulation RMM (**0.7618**).
- Simulation realism, measured by RMM, consistently improved as the amount of unlabeled LiDAR training data for the world model increased, confirming the scalability of the approach and demonstrating enhanced utilization with diversity-guided sampling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised learning through motion-aware latent supervision in a world model can effectively capture dynamic scene evolution from unlabeled LiDAR data, reducing reliance on costly semantic annotations.
> - Conditioning multi-agent behavior generation on learned future latent occupancies from a world model provides a powerful 'coarse-to-fine' predictive context, significantly enhancing inter-agent coordination and overall scenario realism.
> - A cascaded application of quality-aware Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs) for diversity guidance at both the scene and agent levels is crucial for systematically generating a broad spectrum of realistic multi-modal traffic scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-agent traffic simulation constitutes a critical component in the development and validation of autonomous driving systems. The effectiveness of such systems is contingent upon their ability to generate a broad spectrum of realistic and interactive driving behaviors. Historically, heuristic-based simulators encountered limitations, particularly in their reactivity to ego vehicle behaviors and their reliance on pre-recorded logged trajectories.

In response, data-driven simulation approaches have gained prominence. These methods, often employing conditional variational autoencoders, Transformer-based architectures, or diffusion models, have demonstrated efficacy on established closed-loop behavior generation benchmarks. Recent advancements also include autoregressive formulations inspired by next-token prediction, with some extensions incorporating fine-tuning for enhanced performance. A shared characteristic among these prevailing data-driven paradigms is their substantial dependence on supervised learning from extensively annotated trajectory datasets or semantic annotations. This reliance presents a scalability challenge, as the acquisition and labeling of high-quality sensor data are resource-intensive and expensive, thereby limiting the diversity and coverage of scenarios available for training.

Concurrently, a significant volume of unlabeled sensor data, such as LiDAR point clouds, can be collected with relative ease and at scale. However, existing traffic simulation frameworks generally do not harness this data effectively. This gap has prompted exploration into "world models," which forecast sensor data and have shown capabilities in modeling high-dimensional environments. In autonomous driving, world models have primarily focused on improving photorealism in simulations through video-generative approaches or predicting semantic occupancy from LiDAR data. Limitations within this domain include the non-geometric nature of RGB-based representations, which can hinder reasoning about spatial layout and physical constraints crucial for control, and the continued need for semantic annotation in some LiDAR-based approaches. Additionally, uniform supervision across spatial regions in occupancy prediction may underemphasize motion-driven scene evolution, where dynamic elements are sparse but critically important.

AutoWorld positions itself within this landscape by addressing the scalability and data utilization challenge. It proposes a paradigm shift from solely supervised learning to a semi-supervised framework that leverages a learned predictive model of the environment, derived from unlabeled sensor data, as a foundation for behavior generation. The work seeks to integrate world modeling into the motion generation process, distinguishing itself from approaches that use world models solely for offline data generation, policy learning, or for improving downstream planning. By focusing on self-supervised learning of scene dynamics and transferring this predictive capacity to traffic simulation, AutoWorld aims to enhance realism and diversity in simulated traffic scenarios without incurring the high costs associated with increasing labeled datasets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that can leverage large amounts of unlabeled sensor data to improve the performance and scalability of multi-agent traffic simulation, thereby overcoming the limitations of current supervised learning-dependent approaches.

This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and translates into distinct objectives:

1. **Addressing Data Scalability and Cost**: Current data-driven traffic simulators are heavily reliant on supervised learning from labeled trajectory data or semantic annotations. This labeling process is costly, time-consuming, and limits the quantity and diversity of scenarios available for training. The motivation is to find a mechanism to utilize the abundant, readily available *unlabeled* sensor data (specifically LiDAR) to enhance simulation performance, thus enabling a more scalable and cost-efficient approach to improving simulator realism.

2. **Developing a Self-Supervised World Model for Scene Dynamics**: Existing world models in autonomous driving often focus on photorealism (RGB data) or require semantic labels for LiDAR data, which reintroduces annotation costs. A key objective is to learn a world model purely from unlabeled LiDAR occupancy representations. This requires developing a self-supervised training objective that can capture critical scene dynamics without relying on explicit semantic annotations. The motivation here is to accurately model the evolution of the driving environment, particularly dynamic elements, through implicit learning from raw sensor data.

3. **Integrating World Model Predictions into Behavior Generation**: The core hypothesis is that a predictive world model, by forecasting future scene evolution, can provide valuable contextual information that guides the generation of agent behaviors. An objective is to design a framework where a multi-agent motion generation model conditions its predictions on the learned future latent occupancies from the world model. This aims to provide a "coarse-to-fine" predictive scene context, where the world model implicitly informs the motion model about anticipated environmental changes, thereby enhancing inter-agent coordination and overall scenario realism. The motivation is to move beyond mere observation-based behavior generation to a more anticipatory, world-model-grounded approach.

4. **Enhancing Diversity and Realism of Simulated Scenarios**: An effective traffic simulator must produce diverse yet plausible driving behaviors. Generating only realistic (high-quality) trajectories without sufficient diversity can lead to brittle autonomous driving systems. Conversely, promoting diversity without realism can lead to unphysical or unsafe scenarios. An objective is to develop a training-free inference-time strategy that systematically promotes diversity in generated scenarios at both the scene level (world model outputs) and the agent instance level (motion model outputs), while simultaneously ensuring high realism. The motivation is to achieve structured multimodality in simulations, reflecting the inherent variability of real-world traffic, without compromising physical plausibility or safety constraints.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to pave the way for a more scalable and robust methodology for traffic simulation, directly impacting the development and testing cycles of autonomous driving technology.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AutoWorld proposes a two-stage framework for multi-agent traffic simulation, primarily leveraging self-supervised learning from unlabeled LiDAR data to inform supervised behavior generation. The overall approach is illustrated in Fig. 2 of the paper.

#### 3.1. Motion-Aware Latent Occupancy Forecasting (World Model)

The first stage involves training a world model to predict future scene occupancies from unlabeled LiDAR data.

- **Latent Occupancy Forecasting**: The world model is formulated as a LiDAR-based occupancy predictor operating in the latent space of voxelized occupancies. Raw LiDAR point clouds are voxelized into non-semantic occupancy grids, which are then encoded into latent representations using a Swin Transformer-based Variational Autoencoder (VAE). The VAE maps occupancy volumes of size (200, 200, 16) into latent representations of size (16, 16, 50).
- **Rectified Flow Formulation**: Building upon prior work, the model adopts a rectified flow formulation to predict future latent occupancies. It learns a time-dependent velocity field that transports samples from a Gaussian prior towards a target future latent occupancy. Training involves sampling a flow matching timestep and a Gaussian noise vector, constructing an interpolation, and optimizing an objective function to minimize the difference between the predicted velocity field and the displacement vector.
- **Motion-Aware Latent Supervision**: To address the imbalance where static structures dominate occupancy grids while dynamic elements are sparse but critical, a novel motion-aware supervision strategy is introduced.
 - Voxel-wise occupancy transitions are computed by comparing current and future occupancy grids after compensating for ego motion. This creates a "motion map" indicating regions of change.
 - This motion map is downsampled to the latent resolution to construct a latent weight map. This map assigns higher weights to regions exhibiting temporal occupancy changes.
 - The rectified flow objective is then modified to incorporate these weights, reallocating gradient magnitude towards dynamic regions. This enables fully self-supervised training of the world model without semantic annotations.

#### 3.2. World-Model-Conditioned Traffic Simulation (Behavior Generation)

The second stage utilizes the learned world model to condition a multi-agent motion generation model.

- **Conditional Diffusion Model**: Traffic simulation is framed as a conditional diffusion process that generates joint future agent trajectories in the action space. The diffusion model starts from Gaussian noise and iteratively refines trajectories by reversing a predefined noising process over discrete steps.
- **World-Model Conditioning**: The diffusion model is conditioned on the predicted future latent occupancies from the trained world model.
 - **Predictive Scene Context**: A "predictive scene context" is constructed from the world model's rollout of future latent occupancies. This involves temporally subsampling the predicted latent states and aggregating them via multi-head cross-attention (MHCA) pooling with a learned token. This context serves as a compact, structured summary of the anticipated scene evolution over the long horizon.
 - **Coarse-to-Fine Guidance**: The diffusion denoiser is conditioned at each future timestep using a combination of local and global predictive cues. This involves concatenating the per-step latent occupancy with the aggregated predictive scene context. These combined features are then fused with the scene encoding (derived from agent history, HD map, and traffic light states using a query-centric Transformer) through cross-attention to produce a refined step-wise conditioning representation. This provides a coarse-to-fine guidance mechanism, grounding agent behaviors in both immediate predicted scene changes and overall anticipated evolution.
- **Agent Identity Separation**: The world model learns only occupancy dynamics without explicit agent identities. Agent-specific behaviors are generated by the diffusion model, allowing the two components to play complementary roles.

#### 3.3. Cascaded Latent Diversity

To generate diverse yet plausible multi-agent behaviors, AutoWorld employs a training-free cascaded latent diversity strategy applied at inference time.

- **Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs)**: Diversity is quantified directly in latent space using two DPPs. A DPP encourages repulsion among similar samples, thereby favoring diverse sets. The standard DPP formulation is augmented with a quality-weighted kernel.
- **Quality-Aware Modulation**: To balance diversity with plausibility, per-sample quality weights are incorporated into the DPP kernel. For world model sampling, quality is based on Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of the latent rollouts. For motion generation, quality weights incorporate realism constraints in trajectory space, such as collision rate, kinematic infeasibility, and off-road violations.
- **Diversity-Guided Sampling**: During sampling from both the world model (rectified flow) and the motion generator (reverse diffusion), a repulsive guidance term, proportional to the gradient of the log probability of the quality-aware DPP, is added to the flow ODE or reverse-step mean. This term pushes candidate samples apart in latent space, promoting diversity while maintaining high-quality solutions.
- **Two-Stage Cascaded Procedure**:
 1. **Level 1 (World Modeling)**: N diverse future latent occupancies ($\hat{Z}^{(i)}$) are sampled from the world model using DPP-guided sampling, where each rollout represents a distinct plausible future scene development.
 2. **Level 2 (Behavior Generation)**: For each of the N sampled latent occupancy sequences, M agent behavior trajectories ($\tau^{(i,j)}$) are generated using the conditional diffusion model. DPP guidance is applied during this process to encourage trajectory diversity while ensuring consistency with the predicted scene dynamics and adherence to realism constraints.
- This cascaded approach yields N × M diverse and plausible rollouts, promoting multimodal coverage at both scene and behavior levels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of AutoWorld was evaluated through experiments on the WOSAC benchmark and comprehensive ablation studies.

#### 4.1. WOSAC Leaderboard Performance

- **Overall Ranking**: AutoWorld achieved the first rank on the WOSAC leaderboard based on the primary Realism Meta Metric (RMM) score of 0.7865. This position was achieved in a competitive environment where top-performing methods exhibited closely clustered RMM scores.
- **Balanced Realism**: AutoWorld demonstrated a balanced performance across various realism metrics. It attained the second-highest Interactive score (0.8143), suggesting effective inter-agent coordination without relying on adversarial or reinforcement learning-based policy optimization common in other top methods. This was coupled with strong Kinematic performance (0.4931). The results indicate that the world model's implicit learning of future interaction dynamics contributes to consistent multi-agent realism.
- **Map Adherence and Trajectory Accuracy**: While TrajTok and SMART-R1 showed slightly higher Map-based scores, AutoWorld's Map-based performance (0.9185) was competitive, without the reduction in Interactive scores observed in some other methods. AutoWorld also achieved a competitive minADE (1.3051), indicating accurate trajectory prediction, without sacrificing overall realism, in contrast to methods that optimize minADE more aggressively at the expense of RMM.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies on WOSAC (2% Validation Split)

Ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of the world model and diversity mechanisms:

- **Informativeness of World Model Rollouts**: When the world model was removed, simulation performance (RMM) decreased significantly (0.7472 with IID sampling, 0.7549 with motion diversity) compared to scenarios with world-model grounding (e.g., 0.7610 for 1x32 IID). This indicates that the world model provides informative future scene structure beyond what sampling strategies alone can achieve. Furthermore, without diversity, increasing world-model rollouts (8x4 vs 4x8 IID/IID) yielded modest RMM gains (0.7618 vs 0.7597), suggesting that additional rollouts encode distinct scene hypotheses, though IID sampling underutilizes them.
- **Motion Diversity and World Model Utility**: When motion diversity was applied while world-model sampling remained IID, increasing world-model rollouts (8x4 vs 4x8 IID/Pf) led to a clearer RMM improvement (0.7671 vs 0.7648). This suggests that motion diversity enhances the utilization of the structured variation encoded in world model predictions.
- **Interdependence of Scene and Motion Diversity**: Applying scene diversity (P$\mu$) while motion sampling remained IID (P$\mu$/IID) and increasing world-model rollouts (8x4 vs 4x8) led to a decrease in RMM (0.7539 vs 0.7563). This finding indicates that scene diversity alone is not sufficient; the benefits of richer future latents depend on adequate motion-level exploration to realize them.
- **Efficiency of Cascaded Diversity**: With full AutoWorld (cascaded diversity), allocating more motion samples per latent hypothesis (4x8) resulted in a higher RMM (0.7746) compared to more world-model rollouts with fewer motion samples (8x4, RMM 0.7728). This suggests that scene diversity effectively explores structured variation, and motion diversity efficiently exploits it, with diminishing returns for excessive world-model samples beyond a balanced allocation.

#### 4.3. Effect of Motion-Aware Latent Supervision

- **Improved Dynamic Occupancy Prediction**: Motion-aware latent supervision substantially improved dynamic-region accuracy (IoU_dynamic_: 0.71) compared to uniform supervision (0.58), nearly closing the gap to semantic baselines (0.73). Static-region accuracy remained comparable.
- **Enhanced Rollout Fidelity**: Rollout fidelity, measured by Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), improved from 51 (uniform) to 35 (motion-aware), indicating better temporal realism in world model predictions.
- **Downstream Simulation Benefit**: These world model improvements translated to enhanced traffic simulation realism. The motion-aware model achieved a higher RMM (0.7618) and lower minADE (1.4334) compared to the uniform model (RMM 0.7547, minADE 1.4826), closely matching the semantically supervised baseline.

#### 4.4. Performance Scaling with Unlabeled Data

- **Scalable Realism**: RMM consistently improved as the amount of unlabeled LiDAR training data for the world model increased (from 1x to 5x multipliers). This confirms that leveraging unlabeled data leads to scalable gains in traffic simulation realism.
- **Enhanced Utilization with Diversity**: The scaling trend was more pronounced for the full AutoWorld framework (with diversity-guided sampling) compared to the IID/IID variant. This suggests that the diversity mechanisms more effectively utilize the improved latent occupancy representations learned from larger unlabeled datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in AutoWorld offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the field of autonomous driving and beyond.

**Scalability and Cost Efficiency**: A primary impact of AutoWorld is its proposed solution to the scalability bottleneck in data-driven traffic simulation. By demonstrating that simulation realism can consistently improve with the inclusion of unlabeled LiDAR data, the framework offers a cost-efficient alternative to the current reliance on expensive and time-consuming manual annotation of trajectories or semantic information. This capability to leverage readily available unlabeled sensor data can accelerate the development and testing cycles for autonomous driving systems.

**Enhanced Realism and Diversity**: AutoWorld's top performance on the WOSAC benchmark, particularly its balanced high scores across kinematic, interactive, and map-based realism metrics, indicates an advancement in generating plausible and robust multi-agent traffic behaviors. The cascaded latent diversity strategy is critical in promoting multimodal coverage, ensuring that the simulated scenarios reflect the inherent variability and complexity of real-world traffic. The ability to generate diverse yet realistic scenarios is essential for comprehensively evaluating and improving the safety and robustness of autonomous vehicles, especially in rare or challenging edge cases.

**Methodological Advancements in World Modeling**: The introduction of motion-aware latent supervision provides a self-supervised mechanism to train world models to prioritize dynamic scene elements without requiring semantic labels. This is a technical contribution that enhances the fidelity of dynamic scene forecasting, which is critical for downstream tasks like behavior prediction and simulation. Integrating these self-supervised world models as a foundational element for behavior generation represents a paradigm shift from purely supervised approaches.

**Foundation for Future Research**: AutoWorld establishes a baseline and validates the concept of grounding behavior generation in learned world dynamics. This opens avenues for future research in several directions:
- **Multi-modal World Models**: The framework could be extended to incorporate other unlabeled sensor modalities (e.g., cameras, radar) to enrich the world model's understanding of the environment.
- **Improved Diversity Mechanisms**: Further refinements to diversity sampling, potentially integrating explicit topological reasoning or more sophisticated quality metrics, could enhance coverage of complex or rare scenarios.
- **Closed-Loop System Integration**: The insights gained from better-informed simulations could directly feed into the training and evaluation of perception, planning, and control modules for autonomous vehicles.

**Broader Applicability**: The principles of leveraging unlabeled data through self-supervised world models and employing diversity-guided sampling for complex generative tasks could extend beyond autonomous driving. Domains requiring realistic, diverse, and scalable simulations of multi-agent interactions, such as robotics, smart city planning, or even social simulations, could potentially benefit from similar methodologies.

In summary, AutoWorld presents a significant step towards enabling scalable and realistic traffic simulation, addressing a core challenge in the advancement of autonomous driving technology by effectively bridging the gap between abundant unlabeled data and the demanding requirements of high-fidelity, diverse simulations.
