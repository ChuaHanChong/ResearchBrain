# Doppler Correspondence: Non-Iterative Scan Matching With Doppler Velocity-Based Correspondence

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UNIST and KAIST developed a non-iterative scan matching framework, "Doppler Correspondence," which leverages Doppler velocity to establish point correspondences in 4D point cloud data. This approach demonstrated increased robustness and computational efficiency in challenging environments, achieving significantly faster processing times (e.g., 0.16-2.2 ms on radar data) and competitive or superior accuracy compared to traditional methods.

## Problem

- Traditional LiDAR odometry and 4D radar/LiDAR methods struggle with scan matching in adverse weather or geometrically repetitive environments due to their reliance on spatial features.
- Existing correspondence estimation methods for 4D sensors often underutilize rich Doppler velocity information, primarily depending on 3D geometric cues for initial matching.
- Iterative scan matching algorithms, like ICP, are computationally intensive, hindering real-time performance in autonomous systems.

## Method

- Introduced **Doppler Correspondence**, a novel mechanism that uses both range and Doppler velocity to non-iteratively establish correspondences between consecutive 4D point clouds.
- Developed a computationally efficient ==non-iterative scan matching== algorithm based on Doppler Correspondence, designed to estimate transformation in a single step.
- Proposed ==Doppler 4D-ICP==, a hybrid approach that integrates the fixed Doppler correspondences with dynamic spatial correspondences into a ==weighted iterative optimization== framework for enhanced robustness.

## Results

- The non-iterative Doppler Correspondence achieved substantially higher accuracy and was **5-30 times faster** (**0.16-2.2 ms** per frame) than point-to-point ICP on sparse and semi-dense 4D radar datasets.
- On dense 4D LiDAR data in repetitive environments (e.g., 'Curved Wall'), the method maintained comparable accuracy to DICP while being significantly faster (**5.8-8.6 ms** vs. **20.8-29.2 ms**).
- The hybrid Doppler 4D-ICP consistently provided robust and often superior odometry performance in challenging repetitive environments, where many traditional ICP variants diverged, by effectively fusing spatial and Doppler information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Doppler velocity, which describes the radial motion of points, provides a robust, motion-invariant cue for correspondence that can disambiguate matches in geometrically repetitive or sparse environments.
> - The core relationship derived for Doppler Correspondence is inherently invariant to translational motion and robust to small rotational motions of the sensor.
> - By leveraging Doppler information directly for correspondence, it is possible to achieve a computationally efficient, non-iterative scan matching process that relies on point flow rather than explicit spatial positions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate state estimation and odometry are fundamental requirements for autonomous systems. LiDAR odometry has been widely researched for its precision and environmental sensing capabilities. However, its performance, particularly its underlying scan matching process, can degrade in challenging environments. These challenges include adverse weather conditions (e.g., rain, smoke, fog) that scatter LiDAR beams, and environments characterized by repetitive geometric patterns (e.g., tunnels, highways) where geometric features are ambiguous. Traditional scan matching methods, such as Iterative Closest Point (ICP) and its variants, primarily rely on the geometric characteristics of point clouds.

The advent of Frequency-Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW) 4D LiDAR and 4D radar technologies has introduced a new dimension to environmental sensing. Unlike conventional 3D sensors, these 4D sensors provide point cloud data with not only range, azimuth, and elevation but also Doppler velocity. This additional Doppler information has been recognized for its potential to enhance robustness in challenging scenarios, including those with adverse weather and repetitive structures.

Within the existing research landscape, 4D radar and LiDAR odometry methods have explored Doppler velocity in several ways. Some approaches integrate Doppler velocity into optimization frameworks to improve accuracy and robustness, particularly in repetitive geometric settings or when dealing with dynamic objects. Examples include methods that combine 4D radar data with inertial measurements or refine point cloud registration by optimizing both geometric and Doppler objectives (e.g., DICP). Other research focuses on refining point correspondences by utilizing additional information like radar cross section (RCS) or point cloud uncertainty. A third category involves correspondence-free methods that attempt to estimate 6-Degrees of Freedom (6-DOF) motion directly from Doppler information. However, these correspondence-free methods often exhibit limitations in rotational observability, frequently requiring additional sensors like Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) or multiple LiDAR units.

A notable gap in existing correspondence-based 4D odometry methods is that despite the availability of Doppler velocity, the fundamental step of establishing correspondences between consecutive scans often continues to rely solely on 3D geometric information. The work presented in this paper addresses this specific limitation by proposing a novel correspondence mechanism that explicitly leverages Doppler velocity. This approach differentiates itself by focusing on Doppler information directly for correspondence establishment, rather than primarily for post-correspondence optimization or as part of a correspondence-free motion estimation. By doing so, it aims to provide a more robust and computationally efficient solution to scan matching, particularly in environments where traditional geometric matching is compromised.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and validate a novel correspondence mechanism, termed "Doppler Correspondence," for scan matching using 4D point cloud data. This mechanism explicitly utilizes Doppler velocity measurements to establish correspondences between consecutive scans.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current scan matching approaches for both conventional and 4D ranging sensors:

- **Degradation in Challenging Environments:** Traditional LiDAR odometry and many 4D radar/LiDAR odometry methods exhibit degraded performance in adverse weather conditions (e.g., rain, fog) and in environments with repetitive geometric patterns (e.g., tunnels, long hallways, large walls). This degradation is largely due to their reliance on spatial geometric features for establishing correspondences, which become unreliable or ambiguous in such settings.
- **Underutilization of 4D Data:** While 4D LiDAR and radar sensors provide rich Doppler velocity information in addition to spatial coordinates, most existing correspondence estimation methods for scan matching continue to primarily depend on 3D geometric cues. The kinematic information offered by Doppler velocity is often overlooked in the initial correspondence step or is integrated heuristically or only in subsequent optimization phases. This represents an untapped potential for enhancing robustness.
- **Computational Inefficiency of Iterative Methods:** Many prevalent scan matching algorithms, such as the Iterative Closest Point (ICP) family, operate through an iterative refinement process. This iterative nature, while often leading to high accuracy, can be computationally intensive, limiting their applicability in real-time or resource-constrained autonomous systems. There is a motivation to develop more computationally efficient methods that can perform direct scan matching without extensive iterations.
- **Need for Robustness Beyond Geometry:** In environments lacking distinct geometric features or containing repetitive structures, relying solely on geometric cues can lead to incorrect matches and pose estimation failures. Doppler velocity, which describes the radial motion of points relative to the sensor, provides an independent source of information that can disambiguate matches in such geometrically degenerate scenarios, offering a "flow-based" perspective rather than purely static spatial matching.
- **Addressing Rotational Observability Limitations:** While some correspondence-free methods leverage Doppler for motion estimation, they often face challenges in precisely determining rotational motion without additional sensors. This work is motivated by the potential to associate Doppler measurements with both translational and rotational motion from a single 4D sensor, offering a more complete motion estimation capability within a correspondence framework.

By addressing these points, the authors aim to develop a simple, non-iterative, and robust scan matching technique that fully exploits the Doppler velocity information available from 4D ranging sensors, thereby improving odometry performance in challenging real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper introduces "Doppler Correspondence" as a novel approach to establishing correspondences between consecutive 4D point clouds, followed by its integration into a non-iterative scan matching algorithm. It also explores a hybrid approach, Doppler 4D-ICP, for practical applications.

#### 3.1. A. Problem Definition and Background

The paper begins by outlining the standard Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm, which iteratively estimates the relative transformation (rotation $\Delta R$ and translation $\Delta t$) between two consecutive point clouds, $P$ (source) and $Q$ (target). A key step in ICP is "Correspondence Estimation," which typically involves finding the closest spatial point in $Q$ for each point in $P$. The authors highlight that this reliance on spatial information makes ICP susceptible to degradation in geometrically repetitive environments or with sparse and noisy data.

#### 3.2. B. Geometric Derivation of Doppler Correspondence

To overcome the limitations of purely spatial correspondence, the paper proposes Doppler Correspondence, which leverages both range and Doppler velocity information.
1. **Assumptions:** For two true corresponding points $p_i \in P$ and $q_j \in Q$, it is assumed that the sensor undergoes a motion during a short time interval $\Delta T$. The vector $\vec{P_iQ_j}$ is decomposed into radial and tangential components.
2. **Derivation:** By applying principles of similar triangles and relating spatial displacement to radial and tangential velocities, a core relationship is derived:
 $r_{p,i}^2 + r_{p,i}v_{p,i}\Delta T = r_{q,j}^2 - r_{q,j}v_{q,j}\Delta T$
 where $r$ denotes range and $v$ denotes Doppler velocity.
3. **Formulation:** This equation is recast as $f(p_i, v_{p,i}) = g(q_j, v_{q,j})$, where $f$ depends only on the source point's data and $g$ only on the target point's data. This formulation suggests that corresponding points should yield approximately equal values for these functions.
4. **Invariance:** A key characteristic of this derivation is the absence of $\Delta t$ terms, implying that Doppler Correspondence is inherently invariant to the sensor's translational motion. It is also stated to be invariant to small rotations of the sensor, which simplifies correspondence estimation. The reliance on the "flow of the point cloud" rather than geometry is posited to enhance robustness in repetitive environments.

#### 3.3. C. Kinematic Derivation of Doppler Correspondence

An alternative derivation of Doppler Correspondence is provided using kinematic relationships:
1. **Radial Velocity Approximation:** The derivation starts from approximations of radial velocity:
 $\frac{q_j - p_i}{\Delta T} \cdot \frac{p_i}{\|p_i\|_2} \approx v_{p,i}$
 $\frac{q_j - p_i}{\Delta T} \cdot \frac{q_j}{\|q_j\|_2} \approx v_{q,j}$
 These equations relate the change in point position to the measured Doppler velocity along the radial direction.
2. **Manipulation:** By algebraic manipulation, multiplication by $\Delta T$ and range terms, and adding the two resulting equations, the same core relationship as the geometric derivation is obtained:
 $r_{p,i}^2 + r_{p,i}v_{p,i}\Delta T = r_{q,j}^2 - r_{q,j}v_{q,j}\Delta T$
3. **Assumptions for Kinematic Derivation:** This derivation relies on two primary assumptions:
 - $\Delta T \|\omega\|_2 \ll 1$: The product of the time interval and the angular velocity magnitude is small, allowing linearization of the rotation matrix $\Delta R \approx I + \Delta T \omega^\times$.
 - Constant $\omega$ and $\rho$ (angular and linear velocities) over the short interval $\Delta T$.
 Violation of these assumptions, particularly during high angular velocities (e.g., sharp turns), can lead to degraded performance.

#### 3.4. D. Outlier Rejection

To handle potential ambiguities, such as symmetric points yielding similar $f$ and $g$ values, an outlier rejection mechanism is implemented. This mechanism uses two thresholds:
- **Spatial Distance:** $d_{spatial} = \|p_i - q_j\|_2 \leq \tau_{spatial}$
- **Doppler Distance:** $d_{doppler} = |f(p_i, v_{p,i}) - g(q_j, v_{q,j})| \leq \tau_{doppler}$
Only point pairs satisfying both spatial and Doppler constraints are retained as valid correspondences.

#### 3.5. E. Non-Iterative Scan Matching Algorithm (Algorithm 1)

The proposed non-iterative scan matching algorithm based on Doppler Correspondence is designed for computational efficiency:
1. **Input:** Source ($P$) and target ($Q$) point clouds, spatial ($\tau_{spatial}$) and Doppler ($\tau_{doppler}$) thresholds.
2. **Doppler Function Computation:** Calculate $f(p_i, v_{p,i})$ for all points in $P$ and $g(q_j, v_{q,j})$ for all points in $Q$.
3. **Correspondence Estimation:** Identify correspondences by finding the closest $g(q_j, v_{q,j})$ in $Q$ for each $f(p_i, v_{p,i})$ in $P$. This is a 1D search based on the scalar $f$ and $g$ values.
4. **Outlier Masking:** Apply the spatial and Doppler distance thresholds to filter out unreliable correspondences.
5. **Transformation Prediction:** Estimate the rotation ($\hat{R}$) and translation ($\hat{t}$) by minimizing the squared Euclidean distance between the transformed source points and their corresponding target points: $\arg\min_{R,t} \sum_{(p,q) \in C} \|q - (Rp + t)\|_2^2$.
This entire process is performed in a single step, without iterative refinement, thereby reducing computational load.

#### 3.6. F. Practical Use of Doppler Correspondence: Doppler 4D-ICP (Algorithm 3)

The paper also presents an extension, Doppler 4D-ICP, which integrates Doppler Correspondence into a traditional ICP framework to combine the strengths of both.
1. **Initial Doppler Correspondence:** $f(P)$ and $g(Q)$ are computed, and Doppler correspondences ($C_{doppler}$) are established once at the beginning, remaining fixed throughout iterations.
2. **Iterative Process:** Standard closest point correspondences ($C_{spatial}$) are dynamically updated in each ICP iteration based on the transformed source point cloud.
3. **Weighted Optimization:** The transformation is optimized by minimizing a weighted sum of errors from both $C_{spatial}$ and $C_{doppler}$, using a robust Huber loss function $\rho(\cdot)$:
 $\arg\min_{R,t} (1-\alpha) \sum_{(p,q)\in C_{spatial}} \rho(\|q - (Rp + t)\|_2^2) + \alpha \sum_{(p,q)\in C_{doppler}} \rho(\|q - (Rp + t)\|_2^2)$
 Here, $\alpha$ is a weighting factor. This hybrid approach aims to leverage both spatial and kinematic information for improved robustness and accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Doppler Correspondence through extensive experiments on various 4D ranging sensor datasets, comparing its performance against established methods like point-to-point ICP and DICP (Doppler Iterative Closest Point). The evaluation considered both accuracy using Relative Error (RE) and computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. A. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:**
 - **Sparse 4D Radar:** Campus dataset (ZF FRGen21, approx. 400-1,400 points/60ms)
 - **Semi-Dense 4D Radar:** Loop 1 dataset from NTU4DRadLM (Oculii Eagle, approx. 1,000-2,000 points/80ms)
 - **Dense 4D LiDAR:** Straight Wall, Curved Wall (CARLA simulator), and Baker-Barry Tunnel (Aeva Aeries I FMCW 4D LiDAR, approx. 40,000-120,000 points/100ms) from.
- **Comparison Methods:** Point-to-point ICP and DICP.
- **Conditions:** "No Seed" (no initial pose estimate) and "With Seed" (previous pose used as initial guess).
- **Metrics:** Relative Error (RE) for rotation (degrees) and translation (meters) over segment lengths of 8m to 48m, and average computation time per frame (ms).

#### 4.2. B. Experiment Results

The findings indicate varied performance across different point cloud densities and environmental conditions:

- **Sparse 4D Radar Data (Campus1, Campus2):**
 - The proposed non-iterative Doppler Correspondence method demonstrated substantially higher accuracy in terms of both rotation and translation errors compared to point-to-point ICP. DICP was unable to produce reliable results on this sparse data.
 - The method was significantly more computationally efficient, with average computation times around 0.16-0.17 ms, compared to 5.4-6.0 ms for point-to-point ICP. This is attributed to its single-step correspondence estimation, which relies on point flow rather than explicit spatial positions in sparse, noisy data.

- **Semi-Dense 4D Radar Data (Loop1 0, 2, 3, 4):**
 - The proposed method achieved performance comparable to DICP in several scenarios. For instance, translation errors often exhibited a lower median but higher variance than DICP. Rotation errors were generally higher in median and variance compared to DICP, especially in sequences with significant rotational motion (e.g., Loop1 4).
 - Despite these variations, the method maintained a computational advantage, operating faster (0.4-2.2 ms) than DICP (4.0-5.3 ms).

- **Dense 4D LiDAR Data (Straight Wall, Curved Wall, Baker-Barry Tunnel (Empty)):**
 - In static and geometrically repetitive environments, the proposed method showed accuracy comparable to DICP. Notably, it achieved lower rotation error than DICP in the "Curved Wall" sequence, suggesting that DICP's Doppler term might have limited effect on rotational constraint in optimization.
 - The method was considerably faster (5.8-8.6 ms) than DICP (20.8-29.2 ms) for dense LiDAR data.
 - In the "Baker-Barry Tunnel (Vehicles)" sequence, the performance of the proposed method degraded, which the authors attributed to a significant number of dynamic objects in the environment. DICP generally performed better in this dynamic scenario.

#### 4.3. C. Repetitive Geometric Environment

The paper highlights that Doppler Correspondence is designed to be robust in geometrically repetitive environments where traditional closest-point matching often fails due to ambiguity. By incorporating Doppler velocity, the method leverages the "flow" of points, providing more reliable alignment in featureless or structurally similar surroundings. Visualizations presented in the paper support this, showing accurate alignment where ICP struggles.

#### 4.4. D. Correspondence Visualization

Visualizations of established correspondences after a single iteration across sparse, semi-dense, and dense point clouds demonstrated that Doppler Correspondence produces more coherent and consistent matches compared to the closest-point approach. In sparse data, it yields fewer but higher-quality correspondences, aligning with the "less is more" principle. For dense, repetitive environments, it mitigates ambiguity effectively.

#### 4.5. E. Limitations

The paper acknowledges key limitations related to the underlying assumptions of the Doppler Correspondence derivation:
- **Small Rotational Motion:** The linearization of the rotation matrix ($\Delta R \approx I + \Delta T \omega^\times$) requires $\Delta T \|\omega\|_2 \ll 1$.
- **Constant Velocities:** The assumption of constant angular ($\omega$) and linear ($\rho$) velocities over the short interval $\Delta T$.
If these conditions are violated, particularly during scenarios with high angular velocity (e.g., sharp U-turns, as observed in Loop1 4), the method's performance can degrade significantly.

#### 4.6. F. Practical Use of Doppler Correspondence (Doppler 4D-ICP)

Integrating Doppler Correspondence into an ICP framework (Doppler 4D-ICP) showed promising results in enhancing overall odometry performance.
- **Semi-Dense 4D Radar:** Doppler 4D-ICP achieved competitive accuracy on Loop 1 datasets, in some cases outperforming or being comparable to point-to-point ICP, point-to-plane ICP, Generalized-ICP, VGICP, and DICP.
- **Dense 4D LiDAR:** In challenging environments like the Baker-Barry Tunnel, where many traditional ICP variants (point-to-point, point-to-plane, GICP, VGICP) diverged, Doppler 4D-ICP achieved robust results, significantly outperforming DICP in the "Empty" tunnel scenario and showing better performance in the "Vehicles" scenario. This demonstrates its potential to resolve geometric ambiguities through the fusion of spatial and Doppler information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of 4D ranging sensor-based odometry and scan matching by introducing Doppler Correspondence. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novelty in Correspondence Establishment:** The primary impact lies in demonstrating a novel method for establishing point correspondences that explicitly leverages Doppler velocity information. While Doppler data has been used in optimization steps or for direct motion estimation, its direct application to correspondence estimation in a non-iterative manner, as presented, represents an advancement in how 4D sensor data can be utilized.
- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** By enabling a non-iterative scan matching process, the proposed method offers a substantial reduction in computational time compared to traditional ICP-based approaches. This efficiency is critical for real-time applications in autonomous systems, where processing power and latency are key constraints.
- **Improved Robustness in Challenging Environments:** The method exhibits improved robustness in environments where geometric cues are insufficient or ambiguous, such as repetitive structures (e.g., tunnels, long corridors) or in the presence of sparse and noisy data (e.g., sparse 4D radar). This extends the operational domain for autonomous vehicles and robots into conditions where conventional methods often fail, contributing to safer and more reliable navigation.
- **Complementary to Existing Methods:** While the non-iterative approach has limitations in scenarios with high angular velocity or numerous dynamic objects, its unique strengths make it a valuable complement to existing techniques. Its integration into a hybrid ICP framework (Doppler 4D-ICP) demonstrates how Doppler Correspondence can enhance the overall performance and robustness of more complex odometry systems by fusing distinct information modalities.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundational concept for direct, Doppler-based correspondence. It may inspire further research into optimizing the integration of Doppler information for various tasks in 4D point cloud processing, including filtering, segmentation, and object tracking, potentially leading to a new class of algorithms for 4D sensor data.
- **Simplification of Scan Matching:** The simplicity of the Doppler Correspondence formulation and its non-iterative nature contribute to ease of implementation and integration. This accessibility could foster wider adoption and experimentation within the robotics and autonomous systems community.

In conclusion, this research provides a novel, computationally efficient, and robust approach to scan matching in challenging environments by effectively harnessing Doppler velocity from 4D ranging sensors. Despite certain limitations, particularly under conditions of rapid angular motion, its demonstrated advantages in specific scenarios and its potential for integration into hybrid systems underscore its significance for advancing robust odometry in autonomous applications.
