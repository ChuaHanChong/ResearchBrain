# UniHM: Unified Dexterous Hand Manipulation with Vision Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The UniHM framework enables unified dexterous hand manipulation by generating dynamic, physically feasible manipulation sequences from free-form language commands. It integrates a Vision-Language Model with a morphology-agnostic tokenizer and physics-guided refinement, achieving substantially higher real-world task success rates (e.g., 65% for "Grab" with seen objects) and reduced joint position errors (61.40 MPJPE on DexYCB seen data) compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Translating open-vocabulary language commands into precise, multi-step dynamic manipulation sequences for dexterous robotic hands.
- Ensuring physical plausibility and executability of generative model outputs for dexterous hands in contact-rich environments.
- Developing a scalable manipulation system that can generalize skills across heterogeneous dexterous robot hand morphologies without extensive re-training.

## Method

- An automated ==data annotation pipeline== augments human-object interaction (HOI) datasets with language instructions and retargets human poses to various robot hands, incorporating initial physical optimization.
- A Unified Hand-Dexterous Tokenizer, based on a morphology-agnostic VQ-VAE, establishes a shared latent space for diverse hand types, enabling skill representation and translation via knowledge distillation.
- A Qwen3-0.6B VLM, trained with a progressive masking curriculum, generates initial manipulation sequences, which are then refined by a physics-guided optimization module balancing contact, generative priors, and temporal smoothness.

## Results

- UniHM outperformed state-of-the-art baselines on DexYCB and OakInk datasets, achieving lower Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) (e.g., 61.40 for DexYCB Seen) and other metrics across seen and unseen objects.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated higher success rates for UniHM across various tasks (e.g., **65%** for "Grab" with seen objects) on different dexterous hand setups compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of depth input, the progressive masking curriculum, and the physics-guided dynamic refinement module for the framework's robust performance and physical feasibility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning dexterous manipulation from readily available human-object interaction videos, augmented with language and robot retargeting, significantly reduces the dependency on costly real-world robot teleoperation data.
> - A shared, morphology-agnostic latent space facilitates cross-morphology skill transfer, allowing a single framework to control various dexterous robot hands.
> - Combining a VLM's semantic understanding with a physics-guided post-processing step is crucial for generating manipulation sequences that are both semantically aligned with language commands and physically viable for real robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation and Embodied AI faces a significant challenge in enabling robots, particularly those with dexterous hands, to perform complex, physically feasible tasks guided by high-level human instructions. Current research efforts primarily fall into several categories, and UniHM strategically positions itself to address their collective limitations.

Historically, dexterous grasping research has often relied on object-centric cues (e.g., geometry, point clouds) or extensive simulation-based synthesis (e.g., UniDexGrasp, Xu et al., 2023; DexGraspNet, Wang et al., 2022). While these methods are adept at generating stable grasp poses or even simple manipulation sequences, they typically operate without the rich, compositional guidance of open-vocabulary language. They struggle with semantic intent, task diversity, and long-horizon planning. Another line of work involves transferring human-object interaction (HOI) videos to robots (e.g., DexMV, Qin et al., 2022), which can generate human-like motions but often results in fixed, pre-defined sequences that lack the adaptability required by free-form instructions.

More recently, the advent of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has begun to bridge the gap between language commands and robotic actions. Initial VLM-guided approaches in manipulation, such as SemGrasp (Li et al., 2024b) and AffordDexGrasp (Wei et al., 2025a), have focused predominantly on mapping natural language to static grasp poses or affordance predictions. While these methods demonstrate improved semantic controllability and open-vocabulary coverage, they inherently overlook the temporal dynamics and sequential nature of most real-world manipulation tasks, failing to produce smooth, multi-step interaction sequences. Other generative models, like MotionGPT (Zhu et al., 2025) and HOIGPT (Huang et al., 2025), have explored token-based generation for human or hand-object motions but often target simplified robotic hands (low-DoF grippers, digital hands) or still lack explicit guarantees for physical feasibility in complex dexterous-hand scenarios. Even works like DexGrasp Anything (Zhong et al., 2025b) incorporate physics-awareness but are often rooted in grasp generation rather than dynamic, sequential manipulation.

UniHM distinguishes itself by being the "first framework for unified dexterous hand manipulation guided by free-form language commands" that explicitly addresses the challenge of generating *dynamic, sequential, and physically feasible* manipulation trajectories for *heterogeneous dexterous hands*. It moves beyond static grasp generation by integrating a VLM with a novel morphology-agnostic tokenizer and a physics-guided refinement module. This comprehensive approach directly tackles the key limitations of prior work: the lack of open-vocabulary control over dynamic sequences, the absence of cross-morphology generalization, and the insufficient consideration of physical realism in VLM-generated actions. By learning from human-object interaction data, UniHM also aims to circumvent the prohibitive costs and difficulties associated with collecting large-scale real-world teleoperation datasets for diverse dexterous robot hands, thereby democratizing access to advanced manipulation capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of UniHM is to develop a unified framework capable of planning physically feasible dexterous hand manipulation sequences, guided by free-form, open-vocabulary language commands. This ambition is motivated by several critical challenges and gaps in the existing research landscape:

1. **Bridging Language and Dynamic Manipulation:** Current methods struggle to translate high-level, open-vocabulary natural language instructions into precise, multi-step dexterous hand movements. Most language-guided approaches are limited to generating static grasp poses, failing to capture the rich temporal structure and sequential nature of real-world manipulation tasks. UniHM aims to overcome this by enabling dynamic, language-guided manipulation that progresses along arbitrary planned trajectories with strong temporal consistency.

2. **Achieving Physical Feasibility and Realism:** While generative models can produce diverse motions, ensuring these motions are physically plausible, smooth, and executable by a robot hand in a contact-rich environment remains a significant hurdle. Prior methods often lack explicit physical-feasibility guarantees. UniHM is motivated to incorporate physics-guided dynamic refinement to ensure that the generated sequences are not only semantically aligned with instructions but also physically realistic, avoiding collisions, maintaining stability, and exhibiting smooth transitions.

3. **Enhancing Cross-Morphology Generalization:** Dexterous robotic hands come in various designs and kinematics. Developing a separate manipulation system for each hand type is inefficient and unscalable. UniHM seeks to create a "Unified Hand-Dexterous Tokenizer" that can represent and transfer manipulation skills across heterogeneous hand morphologies, enabling a single framework to control multiple robot hands without extensive re-training. This dramatically improves scalability to new hand designs.

4. **Reducing Data Dependency and Training Costs:** Training advanced dexterous manipulation systems typically requires massive, expensive real-world teleoperation datasets, which are arduous and costly to collect. UniHM is motivated to eliminate this dependency by learning dexterous manipulation skills directly from diverse and readily available human-object interaction (HOI) video data. This paradigm aims to significantly lower the barrier to developing and deploying such systems by leveraging inherent human expertise and interaction patterns.

5. **Robust Generalization to Unseen Scenarios:** For real-world utility, a manipulation system must generalize robustly to novel objects, environments, and instructions not encountered during training. UniHM is designed to achieve strong generalization by leveraging comprehensive human video data and a modular architecture that separates scene perception from HOI sequence generation, allowing for robust performance even in unseen scenes and with varied instructions.

In essence, UniHM's objectives revolve around creating a comprehensive, scalable, and practical framework that can empower dexterous robots to understand and execute complex manipulation tasks from natural language, moving towards more intelligent and human-like robotic capabilities in embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniHM proposes a unified framework comprising three core stages: automated data annotation, a vision-language model (VLM) coupled with a morphology-agnostic tokenizer, and physics-guided dynamic refinement.

#### 3.1. Auto Data Annotation

To address the scarcity of dynamic dexterous-hand manipulation data, UniHM leverages existing human-object interaction (HOI) datasets and augments them:
- **Open-vocabulary Language Annotation:** For each HOI sequence, keyframes (first, last, and pre-contact frames) are provided to GPT-4o, which then generates five distinct, open-vocabulary natural language instructions. This process enriches the dataset with semantic guidance.
- **Dexterous Hand Retargeting:** MANO poses from human HOI datasets (e.g., DexYCB, OakInk) are retargeted onto five distinct dexterous robot hands (Shadow, Allegro, SVH, Leap, Panda) using Dex-Retargeting (Qin et al., 2023). This involves solving a differentiable inverse-kinematics objective that maps MANO joint configurations to robot-specific joint limits and kinematics. Crucially, an energy-based physical optimization is applied *post-retargeting* to enforce immediate physical feasibility and consistency for the robot hands.

#### 3.2. Unified Hand-Dexterous Tokenizer

This module is central to enabling cross-morphology generalization.
- **Morphology-Agnostic Codebook:** A shared Vector Quantized-Variational AutoEncoder (VQ-VAE) codebook, $Z = \{e_k\}_{k=1}^K$, is established to discretize dexterous hand poses and short motion segments across all supported hand morphologies. Each hand type $h$ (e.g., MANO, Shadow Hand) has its own encoder $E_h$ and decoder $D_h$. An input sequence chunk $x^{(h)}$ is encoded by $E_h$, quantized to the nearest code vector $e_c \in Z$, and then reconstructed by $D_h$ to $\hat{x}^{(h)}$. This design ensures that all encoders map to the same discrete index space, and all decoders realize the token within their specific morphology.
- **Scalable Cross-Morphology Training:** The training is staged for scalability. A reference encoder-decoder pair (e.g., for Shadow Hand) and the shared codebook are initially established. When a new hand morphology is introduced, its encoder $E_{new}$ is first aligned to the pre-trained reference encoder $E_{ref}$ through **knowledge distillation** ($L_{distill} = \|E_{new}(x_{new}) - E_{ref}(x_{ref})\|^2_2$), using retargeted hand pairs. This distillation bypasses the non-differentiable quantization step. After alignment, the new encoder and its corresponding decoder are fine-tuned with reconstruction ($L_{rec}$) and VQ losses ($L_{vq}$), combining them with the distillation loss. A "cold-code" table mechanism is used to refresh infrequently used codebook entries, preventing codebook collapse.
- **Unified Hand Pose Translation:** Thanks to the shared latent space and codebook, translating a pose from one hand $i$ to another hand $j$ simply involves encoding the source pose $x^{(i)}$ with $E_i$ and decoding the resulting quantized representation with $D_j$.

#### 3.3. Dexterous-Hand Manipulation with VLM

This module generates the initial manipulation sequence tokens.
- **Network Structure:** UniHM employs Qwen3-0.6B as its base VLM, chosen for its stable convergence on HOI and dexterous-hand datasets. A decoupled architecture is adopted:
 - A **CLIPort-style vision module** (Equation 7) takes RGB-D images and language instructions ($I_{rgb-d}, T_{instruction}$) to infer a target object trajectory ($T_{tar}$).
 - **Point-SAM** (Equation 8) segments the object point cloud ($P_{obj}$) from RGB-D data based on semantics from the instruction.
 - The main VLM, conditioned on an initial hand pose token ($E_j(Qpos_0)$), the object trajectory ($T_{tar}$), object point cloud ($P_{obj}$), and text tokens ($T$), generates a sequence of tokens in the VQ-VAE latent space. The target hand's decoder $D_h$ then converts these tokens into joint positions ($\hat{Q}_{pos}$).
- **Training Stage:** The model is fine-tuned using a **progressive masking curriculum**. Initially, it's conditioned on ground-truth hand poses. As training progresses, a random portion of ground-truth poses is replaced with a unified learnable mask token. This strategy encourages the model to learn temporal relationships and reduces exposure bias, enabling robust sequence generation during inference.
- **Inference Stage:** During inference, the CLIPort module dynamically estimates $T_{tar}$ and $P_{obj}$ from RGB-D observations, decoupling spatial perception from HOI generation. This modularity improves data efficiency and robustness, as only CLIPort needs fine-tuning if scene distribution changes.

#### 3.4. Physical-Guided Dynamic Refinement

This is a post-processing step to ensure the physical feasibility of the VLM-generated sequences.
- **Posterior Optimization:** It formulates a frame-by-frame Gauss-Newton optimization problem with Levenberg-Marquardt damping to refine the generated trajectory $Q_{gen}$ given the object point cloud $P_{obj}$ and target pose trajectory $T_{tar}(t)$. This optimization balances physical plausibility with the semantic intent of the generative model.
- **Energy Terms:** The optimization minimizes a total energy function, $E_t$, which is a sum of three terms:
 - **Contact Energy ($E_{contact}$):** Uses an asymmetric, smooth point-to-plane penalty (Equations 11-13) for fingertip positions relative to the object point cloud. This penalty is continuous and slope-matched at $d=0$, providing robustness, especially in the presence of noisy point clouds.
 - **Generative HOI Prior ($E_{gen}$):** Penalizes deviations from the VLM-generated configuration ($q_{gen_t}$) (Equation 14), ensuring the refinement stays close to the VLM's semantic intent.
 - **Temporal Prior ($E_{time}$):** Regularizes first- and second-order temporal differences of joint positions (Equation 15) to enforce smooth velocity and acceleration profiles, preventing jerky or unrealistic movements.
The optimization proceeds sequentially, using previously optimized frames ($q_{opt_{t-1}}, q_{opt_{t-2}}$) as priors for the current frame $q_t$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniHM demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across multiple rigorous evaluations, affirming its efficacy in generating dynamic, language-conditioned, and physically feasible dexterous hand manipulation sequences.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance on Benchmarks

The framework was extensively compared against state-of-the-art methods like TM2T (Guo et al., 2022), MDM (Tevet et al., 2023), FlowMDM (Barquero et al., 2024), and MotionGPT3 (Zhu et al., 2025) on two widely used datasets: DexYCB (Chao et al., 2021) and OakInk (Yang et al., 2022). UniHM consistently outperformed all baselines across both seen and unseen objects and trajectories. The evaluation metrics used were:
- **Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE):** Measures accuracy of hand joint positions.
- **Final Position Location Error (FPL):** Measures accuracy of the hand's final placement.
- **Final Orientation Location Error (FOL):** Measures accuracy of the hand's final orientation.
- **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID):** Measures the realism/fidelity of generated sequences compared to real ones.
- **Diversity:** Measures the variability of generated sequences.

Key results from Tables 1 and 2 show UniHM achieving significantly lower (better) MPJPE, FOL, FPL, and FID scores compared to all baselines on both DexYCB and OakInk, for both seen and unseen data splits. For instance, on DexYCB Seen data, UniHM reduced MPJPE from MotionGPT3's 74.80 to 61.40 and FPL from 19.32 to 12.15. On Unseen data, similar improvements were observed, highlighting its strong generalization capability. While diversity scores are sometimes slightly lower than baselines (e.g., MotionGPT3), UniHM's diversity values are closer to the ground truth, suggesting more reasonable and controlled generation rather than arbitrary variability.

#### 4.2. Real-World Evaluation

UniHM's practical applicability was validated through real-world experiments on a dexterous hand setup (equipped with Panda Hand, XHand, and Inspire Hand). Qualitative and quantitative evaluations demonstrated marked improvements in grasp success rate and grasp quality for various tasks, including "Grab," "Pick&Place," "Pull&Push," and "Open&Close."
- As summarized in Table 3, UniHM achieved substantially higher success rates compared to MDM+Dex-Retargeting and MotionGPT3+Dex-Retargeting across all tasks and both seen and unseen objects. For example, in the "Grab" task, UniHM attained a 65% success rate for seen objects and 60% for unseen objects, significantly outperforming MotionGPT3's 30% (seen) and 45% (unseen).
- Visualizations (Figure 3 and Appendix D) further illustrate that UniHM generates physically consistent and executable manipulations in real-world scenarios.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Controlled ablation studies on DexYCB confirmed the necessity of each key component of the UniHM framework (Table 4):
- **"w/o Depth Input":** Removing depth information severely degraded performance across all metrics (e.g., MPJPE for seen objects increased from 61.40 to 85.47), underscoring the critical role of accurate 3D perception for object pose estimation and point cloud reconstruction.
- **"w/o Masked Training":** Disabling the progressive masking curriculum resulted in higher errors (e.g., MPJPE for seen objects increased to 73.41), indicating that this training strategy is essential for the VLM to learn robust temporal understanding and sequence generation, reducing exposure bias.
- **"w/o Physical Refinement":** Without the physics-guided dynamic refinement module, the generated sequences showed poorer physical feasibility (e.g., MPJPE for seen objects increased to 65.78, FPL to 15.35), validating its crucial role in adjusting poses, contacts, and timing to ensure physical validity and smoothness.

In summary, UniHM not only sets new benchmarks in language-conditioned dexterous manipulation quantitatively in simulation but also translates these improvements into tangible success rates in complex real-world robotic tasks, with each proposed module contributing significantly to its overall performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniHM framework represents a substantial leap forward in the field of robotic manipulation and embodied AI, with significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Dynamic, Language-Guided Dexterous Manipulation:** UniHM addresses a long-standing challenge by allowing dexterous robots to execute complex, multi-step manipulation tasks directly from free-form natural language instructions. This moves beyond the limitations of static grasp generation or pre-defined trajectories, paving the way for more intuitive and flexible human-robot interaction. The ability to understand "what to do" and translate it into "how to do it" dynamically is crucial for general-purpose robots.

2. **Democratizing Dexterous Manipulation Research:** By learning manipulation skills from readily available human-object interaction (HOI) videos rather than expensive and time-consuming real-world teleoperation data, UniHM significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing advanced dexterous manipulation systems. This paradigm shift can accelerate research and development in robotics, making these complex capabilities more accessible to a broader community.

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Adaptability:** The framework's strong generalization to unseen objects, trajectories, and even diverse hand morphologies is a critical step towards deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic real-world environments. The morphology-agnostic codebook and VLM architecture ensure that the learned skills are not confined to a single robot hand but can be transferred and scaled to various dexterous embodiments, increasing the versatility and applicability of robotic systems.

4. **Guaranteed Physical Feasibility and Robustness:** The integration of a physics-guided dynamic refinement module is paramount for ensuring that the generated manipulation sequences are not just aesthetically pleasing but also physically plausible, smooth, and executable by real robots without collisions or instability. This robustness is essential for reliable and safe operation in practical applications. The contact energy formulation, in particular, demonstrates robustness to noisy sensory inputs, which is a common challenge in real-world perception.

5. **Foundation for Advanced Embodied AI:** UniHM's ability to couple semantic intent with precise physical actions, across different robot bodies, provides a strong foundation for future embodied AI research. It lays groundwork for more sophisticated tasks involving long-horizon planning, bimanual manipulation, tool use, and adaptive behaviors in complex human-centric environments.

**Potential Applications:**
- **Personal Robotics:** Enabling domestic robots to perform a wide array of household chores and assistance tasks.
- **Industrial Automation:** Facilitating flexible manufacturing and assembly lines where dexterous manipulation is required for varied components.
- **Healthcare:** Assisting in delicate surgical procedures or patient care where fine-grained motor skills are essential.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Operating in hazardous or unstructured environments where precise, adaptive manipulation is needed.

**Future Directions (as outlined by the authors):**
While UniHM makes significant strides, the authors acknowledge areas for future improvement. These include incorporating richer contact priors and feedback mechanisms (like tactile or force sensing) to enhance robustness in complex contact-rich tasks, further scaling the unified codebook to an even wider array of hand morphologies, and integrating bimanual or tool-use scenarios. Ultimately, closing the loop with online adaptation will be crucial for real-time responsiveness and continuous learning in dynamic environments. These future advancements build directly on the strong foundation laid by UniHM, promising even more capable and intelligent robotic systems.
