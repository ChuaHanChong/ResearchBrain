# ActiveArena: Benchmarking and Understanding Active Perception in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ActiveArena is a simulator-benchmark-baseline suite for active perception in robotic manipulation. ActiveArena-Sim gives an Astribot S1 controllable head and torso over a 180-degree two-level workspace; ActiveArena-Bench adds 35 tasks unsolvable from passive views, with ID/OOD protocols; ActiveArena-VLA supplies 13 controlled configurations. Its best variant reaches 62.97% ID and 47.60% OOD success, far above memory-free and fixed-view baselines.

## Problem

- Manipulation benchmarks assume fixed-view observations already contain task-relevant evidence, so they never test whether a policy can seek out-of-view or hidden information.
- Memory-centric suites assume complete information is already retained, neglecting selective writing and updating of evidence as observations accumulate over long horizons.
- Active-perception QA benchmarks act through high-level semantic actions, abstracting away continuous dual-arm control; their simulators impose fixed viewpoints, confined workspaces, and limited interaction.

## Method

- **ActiveArena-Sim** rebuilds RoboTwin 2.0 around an Astribot S1 with controllable head pitch and torso yaw, an annular 180-degree two-level fan workspace, and frame-aligned search/anchor/action process annotations.
- **ActiveArena-Bench** defines 35 tasks in five categories under four design principles (initial information insufficiency, accessibility, historical dependence, ==closed-loop decision==), with 100 ID demonstrations per task and disjoint OOD backgrounds and clutter.
- **ActiveArena-VLA** is a 13-configuration StarVLA suite varying ==memory write policy== (subtask, motion, chunk keyframes), capacity, proprioceptive state tokens, VLM subtask supervision, and OFT/FAST/GR00T heads, plus a Qwen3-VL planner variant.

## Results

- ActiveArena-OFT reaches **62.97%** ID and **47.60%** OOD average success over **35** tasks, versus **35.31/23.43** for MemER and **16.86/1.71** for pi-0.5; the fixed-view ablation collapses to **0.34/0.17**.
- Chunk (uniform) writing beats subtask and motion triggers by wide margins (**47.60** versus **16.00** and **22.69** OOD); removing proprioceptive state conditioning costs **30.7** OOD points and raises chunk-boundary jump from **0.65** to **4.55** degrees.
- Across **eight** real-robot tasks ActiveArena-OFT averages **55.6%** success against **34.4%** for MemER and **23.8%** for pi-0.5; planner mediation retains **45.54** OOD while cutting peak training GPU memory **46%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Write-trigger stability matters more than memory size: extra capacity pays off only when frames are written on a schedule immune to prediction error and control jitter.
> - Uniform temporal sampling is a strong default for embodied visual history, echoing video-understanding results where learned keyframe scoring rarely beats regular spacing.
> - Strong in-distribution success does not imply generalization; policies exploit scene regularities, so benchmarks need disjoint backgrounds and distractor pools to expose the gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

In one robot manipulation task, the instruction is to grab the hammer with the left arm and hit the block. Swinging the hammer is only part of the job. The robot must also search for task-relevant evidence using its movable viewpoint, then carry that information into the manipulation.

The general problem arises when a target or a needed clue is hidden, outside the current view, or already out of sight. The robot must decide what to look at or interact with next, and it must remember what it learned well enough to act. ActiveArena evaluates acquiring information and maintaining it in memory together. The authors argue that either ability on its own gives only a partial test of active perception.

### 2. Giving the robot a reason to look

ActiveArena is organized around this problem. It includes a simulator, a benchmark, and a model study.

- **ActiveArena-Sim** combines controllable head and torso viewpoints with a large, roughly 180-degree workspace.
- **ActiveArena-Bench** has 35 tasks across visual exploration and interactive information acquisition. Its task taxonomy tests searching, carrying information across views, and interacting to reveal hidden evidence.
- **ActiveArena-VLA** comprises 13 configurations for controlled studies of memory and other policy choices.

Every task meets four requirements. The initial observation is insufficient. Missing evidence can be acquired. Past evidence remains relevant after it leaves view. New evidence affects later decisions.

### 3. Which frames get remembered matters

The authors compared three rules for writing observations into memory:

- **Subtask writing** stores a frame when a predicted subtask changes.
- **Motion writing** stores a frame at gripper transitions or thresholded robot motion.
- **Chunk writing** stores one frame after each action chunk.

The variants share the task instruction, 18-D proprioceptive state, OFT action head, and subtask-supervised VLM objective. Memory capacity is also matched. With 12-frame memory, chunk writing reaches 62.97% average success within the training distribution (ID). Under out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation, which adds scene and distractor shifts, it reaches 47.60%. Subtask writing reaches 28.11% ID and 16.00% OOD.

The authors interpret this as evidence that a stable write schedule matters more than merely adding slots. They also offer explanations for the weaker rules. Subtask-triggered writes can mistime updates when predicted boundaries are wrong. Motion-triggered writes can retain redundant frames when control is jittery.

More memory does not help uniformly. Increasing capacity improves six of ten chunk-policy task/evaluation settings, while extra capacity often hurts event-triggered policies. The result does not establish that chunk-based sampling is optimal in general.

### 4. Stronger, not solved

The benchmark protocol is shared across methods. Every method trains on 100 ID trajectories per task. Each method is evaluated on 50 seeds in each setting. OOD adds background and illumination changes, denser clutter, and a broader distractor pool.

Under this protocol, ActiveArena-OFT's average success rate is 62.97% ID and 47.60% OOD. MemER reaches 35.31% ID and 23.43% OOD. Here, the average is the mean of per-task success rates across all 35 tasks. External methods retain their method-specific inputs, so this is not a matched-architecture comparison. The advantage also does not extend to every category. For example, MemER's OOD score is higher in Multi-Object Decision, at 42.00% versus 35.33%.

The ID–OOD gap remains. Most unsuccessful ActiveArena-OFT OOD episodes fall into the "context retained" category. In these episodes, all task-relevant objects are jointly visible at the final observation, yet the task still fails. The authors interpret this as pointing to downstream reasoning or execution errors. This classification does not prove that either is the only remaining cause.

ActiveArena makes active search and memory testable together in manipulation. Its controlled study shows that the memory write schedule strongly affects results. It also exposes a substantial remaining gap between ID and OOD performance.
