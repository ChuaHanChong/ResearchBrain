# Behavior Foundations for Quadruped Robots: ABot-C0 Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> ABot-C0 establishes a comprehensive motion-control system for quadruped robots, integrating a scalable multi-source data pipeline, generalist behavior policies, and a unified deployment stack. The framework demonstrates improved motion tracking fidelity, robust omnidirectional locomotion, and basic scene interaction capabilities like hand-shaking on a physical platform.

## Problem

- Scarcity of large-scale, diverse quadruped motion data, hindering the development of generalist policies.
- Existing quadruped control research largely focuses on isolated skills, lacking unified, generalist motion-control systems.
- Challenges in directly transferring humanoid motion control advancements to quadrupeds due to morphological differences and data capture difficulties.

## Method

- Developed **ABot-C0**, a generalist motion-control system comprising a scalable multi-source data pipeline, including novel video-to-motion generation with quality filtering.
- Implemented robust policy learning for motion tracking (using ==DAgger distillation== and ==Flow-Matching==), all-terrain locomotion (with state estimation and ==constrained reinforcement learning==), and scene interaction (via compositional control).
- Created a unified deployment stack for reliable real-world operation on the physical **Tutu** robot, managing multi-policy execution, smooth transitions, and safety mechanisms.

## Results

- Reduced unseen Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) for motion tracking from **24.61 mm** to **14.79 mm** by scaling data, further improving to **12.53 mm** with Manifold-Calibrated Reference Conditioning (MCRC).
- Achieved **zero** torque and joint velocity violations during a high-speed sprint (**3.0 m/s**) and **zero** falls under unmodeled perturbations using the NP3O hardware safety constraints.
- Demonstrated an **83.2%** success rate in all-terrain locomotion, reducing 'Bad Impulse' from **90.0** to **18.5 N·s** and 'Unsafe Foothold' from **31%** to **14%** compared to a proprioception-only baseline.
- Successfully performed a hand-shaking interaction with an average active-foot-to-target error of **13.74 cm**, integrating perception, goal-directed locomotion, and IK-guided motion tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An empirical data scaling law was verified for quadruped motion tracking, where increased data volume consistently improved generalization and reduced the seen-unseen MPJPE gap from 10.17 mm to 2.41 mm.
> - Manifold-Calibrated Reference Conditioning (MCRC) significantly enhanced zero-shot generalization and tracking fidelity by leveraging a learned motion manifold.
> - The combination of implicit-explicit state representation and hardware-safe constrained reinforcement learning (NP3O) ensures robust control and prevents hardware damage under unmodeled disturbances.
> - A command-conditioned diffusion prior (Diff-CAST) with Symmetric Augmented Command Construction (SACC) effectively learns diverse, biomimetic, and omnidirectional gaits with minimal heading drift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Motion control is a fundamental aspect of embodied intelligence systems, enabling robots to translate high-level commands into physical movements while maintaining stability and adapting to the environment. Recent advancements in reinforcement learning, coupled with sophisticated physics simulators, have significantly propelled progress in this domain. In particular, humanoid robot control has seen notable developments, driven by large-scale human motion-capture data and motion-tracking paradigms. This has led to the emergence of "Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs)" for general whole-body control in humanoids.

However, the direct transfer of these advancements to quadruped robots presents distinct challenges. Animal motion data is comparatively scarcer and more difficult to capture at scale than human data, and cross-embodiment retargeting from human to quadruped models is often impractical or fragile due to inherent morphological differences. Existing quadruped research has largely concentrated on isolated skills such as all-terrain locomotion, biomimetic gaits, or extreme agility. This work aims to bridge the gap between these specialized skills and the comprehensive, generalist motion-control systems seen in humanoid research, envisioning quadruped robots that can move beyond basic functional demonstrations toward understanding, expression, and interaction. ABot-C0 positions itself as a Behavior Foundation Model for quadruped robots, addressing the need for a unified system that integrates diverse behavioral foundations for practical, product-level deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop ABot-C0, a generalist motion-control system for quadruped robots. This system is designed to establish three complementary behavior foundations:
1. **A scalable multi-source motion-data pipeline:** The motivation here is to overcome the scarcity and difficulty of acquiring diverse, high-quality quadruped motion data. Current datasets are limited in scale and behavioral coverage, hindering the development of generalist policies. The objective is to construct a data pyramid that combines various sources, including novel video-generation techniques, to provide a robust and diverse data foundation.
2. **Robust policy learning across motion tracking, locomotion, and scene interaction:** The goal is to move beyond isolated sub-skills and develop policies that can handle a broad spectrum of behaviors. For motion tracking, the objective is to create a generalist controller that can track diverse motions with high fidelity and generalize to unseen motions, exploring whether scaling laws observed in other domains apply to quadrupeds. For locomotion, the aim is to achieve both precise velocity tracking with biomimetic, energy-efficient gaits and robust adaptation to unstructured, all-terrain environments, which are crucial for real-world deployment. For scene interaction, the objective is to enable contact-rich behaviors like hand-shaking, requiring real-time perception, balance, and compliance.
3. **A unified deployment stack for reliable real-world operation:** The motivation is to integrate these learned capabilities into a cohesive system that can manage multi-policy execution, smooth behavior transitions, energy-efficient control, and safety mechanisms. This aims to transition quadruped robots from research prototypes to robust, product-level behavioral intelligence, enabling applications like autonomous navigation and human-robot interaction.

Collectively, these objectives are motivated by the vision of transforming quadruped robots from simple walking machines into expressive and interactive embodied agents capable of functioning effectively and safely in complex, human-centric environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ABot-C0 system is structured around three core pillars: a data engine, core motion capabilities (motion tracking, locomotion, scene interaction), and a unified deployment stack.

#### 3.1. Data Engine

To address the scarcity of quadruped motion data, a **scalable quadruped motion-data pyramid** was constructed from four complementary sources:
- **Teleoperation**: Provides high-quality, robot-executable demonstrations for initial policy training.
- **Artist Design**: Expands the library with expressive and dynamic behaviors.
- **Motion Capture**: Contributes natural, animal-like locomotion patterns.
- **Video-to-Motion Generation**: Serves as a scalable backbone for generating diverse motions conditioned on text and image prompts. This pipeline includes:
 - **Identity-Consistent Video Generation**: Fine-tuning a video diffusion model (Wan2.2) in an image-to-video (I2V) setup. An **Identity Consistency Loss (L_IC)** is introduced to maintain the robot's rigid body identity across frames, layered over the standard flow-matching objective.
 - **From Video to 3D Motion Trajectory**: Each generated video is converted into a 3D motion trajectory using a fine-tuned ViTPose model to predict 2D body landmarks. A 2D reprojection error is minimized to solve for per-frame root position, Euler angles, and joint angles, complemented by temporal smoothness and foot-contact constraints.
 - **Multi-Stage Quality Filtering**: Three sequential gates ensure physical feasibility:
 - **CLIP semantic gate**: Discards trajectories if the re-rendered video's CLIP image-embedding similarity to the original falls below a threshold (97.0% pass rate).
 - **Geometric gate**: Screens trajectories based on 2D reprojection error (mean < 20 px, max-frame < 100 px), identified as the primary bottleneck (70.2% pass rate).
 - **Physical feasibility gate**: Trains a specialist tracking policy for each remaining trajectory and discards those that trigger termination conditions (fall, root divergence, velocity explosion) during a full-length simulation rollout (97.6% pass rate).

The resulting dataset comprises 16,074 physically validated reference trajectories totaling 22.43 hours, with 7,488 clips (18.51 hours) from video generation alone.

#### 3.2. Core Motion Capabilities: Motion Tracking

A **generalist motion-tracking controller** for quadruped robots is developed based on this large-scale dataset. The methodology involves:
- **Specialist-to-Generalist Flow Policy Pipeline**:
 - **Specialist Training**: Individual specialist policies ($\pi_k^{\text{expert}}$) are trained for each motion clip using PPO in parallel simulation, optimizing for their assigned motion.
 - **DAgger Distillation**: The specialist ensemble is distilled into a unified student policy ($\pi_\theta$) using DAgger, where the student's rollouts are relabeled by corresponding specialists.
 - **Conditional Flow-Matching (FM) Model**: The student policy is parameterized as a conditional Flow-Matching model, which learns a time-dependent velocity field to transport noisy actions towards the specialist-action distribution.
 - **Residual RL Policy**: A lightweight residual RL policy is trained atop the frozen flow policy to refine robustness and recover performance gaps.
- **Dynamic-Aware Motion Curation**: Motions are stratified by a complexity measure and then ranked within each bin using a **PRF-score** (Physical feasibility, Rollout executability, Flow confidence). This prioritizes motions that are physically plausible, executable by a fixed policy, and consistently sampled by the flow policy.
- **Manifold-Calibrated Reference Conditioning (MCRC)**: A reference-window VAE is trained on curated reference windows to learn a compact, time-indexed latent code ($z_t(m)$) representing the local future motion segment. This latent code, potentially along with the VAE reconstruction uncertainty ($e_{\text{recon},t}(m)$), is used as an additional condition for the student policy observation.
- **Implementation Details**:
 - **Observation/Action Space**: A 69-dimensional observation vector includes proprioceptive state and motion-reference observations, augmented with a 32-dimensional VAE latent for MCRC. The policy outputs 12-dimensional scaled joint-position offsets.
 - **Model Architecture**: Specialists use MLPs. The reference-window VAE uses MLPs. The full-train flow policy student uses a Transformer-history architecture processing embedded tokens (proprioceptive block, reference command, VAE latent, past proprioceptive frames).
 - **Reward**: A dense tracking reward (exponential kernels over pose/velocity errors) is used for specialists and residual policies, with penalties for action rate, joint limits, and self-collisions.
 - **Domain Randomization and Noise**: Sensing noise, state-estimation errors, external disturbances, physical parameter variations, and reference-state initialization (RSI) are employed to reduce the sim-to-real gap.

#### 3.2.2. Locomotion

The locomotion framework focuses on two capabilities: biomimetic gait with velocity-tracking precision and all-terrain adaptability.
- **Robust Baseline**: Addresses partial observability and hardware safety.
 - **Self-Supervised Implicit Representation Learning**: Learns a proprioceptive latent from a 10-step buffer using a Barlow Twins-style temporal consistency objective to infer unobservable states.
 - **Explicit State Estimation**: Appends regression heads to estimate base linear velocity, payload mass, and CoM coordinates, supervised by privileged simulation data.
 - **Hardware-Safe RL via Constrained MDP (NP3O)**: Formulates control as a Constrained Markov Decision Process, with joint positions, velocities, and torques as hard constraint boundaries optimized via Normalized Penalized PPO (NP3O) to ensure zero safety violations.
- **Biomimetic Gait with Omnidirectional Ability**: Addresses limitations of GAN-based imitation learning for diverse gaits.
 - **Action-Agnostic Diffusion Prior (CC-Diffusion)**: Replaces the GAN discriminator with a command-conditioned diffusion state-transition model, modeling temporal state transitions to decouple stylistic learning from actuator domain. A bounded stylistic reward based on a classification probability ($D_\varphi(x_t)$) is used.
 - **Symmetric Augmented Command Construction (SACC)**: Injects geometric regularization through sagittal-plane mirror operations on the dataset and a structural mirror symmetry loss on actor-critic networks to improve omnidirectional velocity tracking and prevent heading drift.
- **All-Terrain Locomotion**: A three-stage privileged-to-perceptive teacher-student framework is employed:
 - **Privileged Teacher Training**: A teacher policy is trained with privileged observations (clean proprioception, height map, dynamics variables) on diverse terrains using curriculum learning and domain randomization.
 - **Clean LiDAR Memory Distillation**: A student is distilled from the teacher using clean LiDAR memory observations (8-frame voxelized 3D occupancy grid with scan-age and ego-motion compensation). The student's LiDAR memory encoder and auxiliary heads are optimized with teacher-guided matching and auxiliary predictions (base velocity, ego-motion, terrain geometry).
 - **Noisy On-Policy Student PPO Fine-tuning**: The distilled student is fine-tuned with PPO under noisy, domain-randomized LiDAR inputs, gradually decaying teacher regularization terms.

#### 3.2.3. Scene Interaction

Implemented through a **hand-shaking case study**, scene interaction is formulated as a compositional control problem.
- **Methodology**: Integrates existing locomotion and motion-tracking modules.
 - **Perception Module**: Estimates hand position from camera landmarks and LiDAR depth, maintained by odometry.
 - **Goal-directed Locomotion**: Converts the detected hand target into velocity commands for the locomotion policy to approach the human.
 - **IK-guided Motion Tracking**: Once near the person, Inverse Kinematics (IK) generates a front-leg reaching trajectory from a standing pose to a hand-shaking pose. This IK trajectory becomes the reference data for the motion-tracking policy, which then executes the local contact behavior.
 - **Adaptive Interaction**: During shaking, active thigh and calf gains are reduced for compliant response to human motion. Completion is based on a proprioceptive external-stimulus score, with safety guards active throughout.

#### 3.3. System

The **unified deployment stack** connects learned policies to the physical quadruped platform, Tutu.
- **Hardware Configuration**: The Tutu platform includes multi-view cameras, a 96-channel LiDAR, an audio module, and an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin 64GB module for real-time onboard computation. Cloud infrastructure supports heavier reasoning.
- **Runtime Architecture**: The on-robot compute module hosts the runtime stack. Locomotion and motion-tracking runners operate continuously. A coordination layer arbitrates active policy output, handles smooth blending during transitions, and incorporates safety gating. The motor-command loop runs at 200 Hz, while decision and inference modules run at 50 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results: Motion Tracking

Experiments validated the effectiveness of the generalist motion tracking system across a training pool of 7,076 reference motions, evaluating on both seen and held-out unseen motions using Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and success rate.
- **Specialist-to-Generalist Tracking Baseline**: The flow policy achieved 12.04 mm seen MPJPE and 16.51 mm unseen MPJPE, with 95.26% seen and 86.42% unseen success rates. This significantly outperformed direct multi-motion RL (22.18 mm seen, 22.20 mm unseen MPJPE), closing most of the gap to per-motion specialists while enabling generalist control. Residual RL provided a marginal refinement.
- **Flow Policy Scaling Law**: Increasing the training set from 30 to 7,076 motions consistently improved generalization. Unseen MPJPE decreased from 24.61 mm to 14.79 mm, and unseen success rate improved from 84.30% to 88.54%. The seen-unseen MPJPE gap narrowed from 10.17 mm to 2.41 mm, empirically verifying a data-scaling law for quadruped motion tracking.
- **Dynamic-Aware Motion Curation**: Under a fixed motion budget (70% of full data), PRF-ranked curation (Physical feasibility, Rollout executability, Flow confidence) yielded the best overall performance (11.17 mm / 94.81% seen, 15.56 mm / 85.66% unseen MPJPE/success), outperforming random subsampling and single-score curation variants. This demonstrated the value of reference quality for fixed-budget training.
- **Manifold-Calibrated Reference Conditioning (MCRC)**: Augmenting the student observation with the VAE latent code ($z$) representing the learned motion manifold significantly improved performance. Compared to the base observation, adding $z$ reduced unseen MPJPE from 14.79 mm to 12.53 mm and increased unseen success from 88.54% to 91.02%, demonstrating the value of manifold-conditioning.

#### 4.2. Locomotion

The progressive locomotion framework was evaluated for robustness, biomimetic gait, and all-terrain traversal.
- **Robust Baseline**:
 - **Implicit-Explicit State Representation**: The full model (Barlow-regularized latent + explicit estimates) achieved the lowest velocity error (0.14 m/s), fastest recovery time (0.82 s), and zero observed falls under unmodeled payload, CoM, and impulse perturbations, outperforming policies with only current observations or MLP history encoders.
 - **Hardware Safety Constraints (NP3O)**: During a high-speed sprint (3.0 m/s), NP3O strictly enforced hardware limits, achieving zero torque and joint velocity violations, in contrast to unconstrained PPO (124.8 torque, 318.5 joint velocity violations) and penalty-based PPO (17.2 torque, 54.6 joint velocity violations).
- **Biomimetic Gait with Omnidirectional Ability**:
 - **Motion Diversity and Gait Realism**: Diff-CAST successfully disentangled diverse semantic skills in the latent space and reduced Frechet Gait Distance (FGD) from 4173.66 (Vanilla AMP) to 489.13, indicating improved motion quality.
 - **Omnidirectional Velocity Tracking**: Diff-CAST with SACC (Symmetric Augmented Command Construction) achieved near-zero heading drift and low position deviation across forward, high-speed, lateral, and backward commands, outperforming a baseline without SACC that failed under out-of-distribution commands.
- **All-Terrain Locomotion**: The full perceptive model achieved an 83.2% success rate and a maximum level score of 7.8 across a diverse terrain curriculum. It significantly reduced "Bad Impulse" (from 90.0 to 18.5 N·s) and "Unsafe Foothold" (from 31% to 14%) compared to a proprioception-only baseline, indicating safer and more deliberate foothold selection. Ablations confirmed the critical roles of LiDAR memory, ego-motion compensation, and terrain reconstruction auxiliary tasks.

#### 4.3. Scene Interaction

The hand-shaking case study demonstrated the compositional approach to scene interaction.
- **IK Reference Planning Accuracy**: IK generated reaching references with a mean planning error of 11.83 mm (median 9.83 mm), confirming geometric feasibility.
- **Execution Error Analysis**: The final executed active-foot-to-target error ($e_{\text{exec}}$) was 13.74 cm on average (median 12.15 cm). This error reflects the combined effect of IK reference quality, dynamic tracking (median front-calf body tracking error of 3.44 cm), and compliant whole-body execution during the interaction hold phase.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ABot-C0 represents a systematic effort towards establishing behavioral foundations for quadruped robots, addressing key limitations in data availability, policy generalization, and real-world deployment. The research makes several contributions:

1. **Scalable Data Foundation**: The development of a multi-source motion-data pipeline, particularly through video-to-motion generation, offers a method to overcome the data scarcity in quadruped robotics. This pipeline's ability to produce a large, physically validated, and diverse motion corpus is significant for training advanced policies.
2. **Generalist Motion Tracking**: The demonstration of a scaling law for quadruped motion tracking, where performance consistently improves with increasing data volume, suggests a path towards highly capable and generalizable controllers. The introduction of Manifold-Calibrated Reference Conditioning (MCRC) further enhances zero-shot generalization and tracking fidelity, enabling robots to follow a broader range of unseen motions.
3. **Versatile and Robust Locomotion**: The locomotion framework addresses critical challenges for practical deployment. The robust baseline, incorporating implicit and explicit state representations with hardware-safe constrained RL (NP3O), ensures stable control under physical variations and prevents hardware damage. The Diff-CAST method, with its action-agnostic diffusion prior and Symmetric Augmented Command Construction (SACC), allows for biomimetic, energy-efficient gaits with precise omnidirectional velocity tracking, crucial for human-centric applications. The three-stage privileged-to-perceptive framework for all-terrain locomotion, leveraging temporal LiDAR memory and terrain-predictive supervision, enables robust traversal in complex, unstructured environments.
4. **Unified Deployment and Application Potential**: The integration of these capabilities into a unified deployment stack facilitates smooth policy transitions, multimodal command handling, and real-time safety. This system-level approach allows quadruped robots to move beyond isolated skill demonstrations towards product-level behavioral intelligence, as showcased in applications like interactive companionship and autonomous navigation in complex urban terrains.

The work identifies current limitations, such as the system operating as a coordinated multi-policy stack rather than a single unified model, the separation of low-frequency semantic reasoning from high-frequency onboard control, and the need for broader cross-platform evaluation and stronger hardware safety guarantees for more complex interactions. However, ABot-C0 serves as a foundation for future advancements, particularly towards self-improving quadruped control stacks that can autonomously collect real-world experience, identify failures, and refine policies through continual skill discovery, ultimately paving the way for extensible embodied agents.
