# CLAW: Composable Language-Annotated Whole-body Motion Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The CLAW system introduces a scalable method for generating language-annotated, whole-body motion data for humanoid robots, specifically demonstrated with the Unitree G1. It leverages composable motion primitives within a physics simulation to produce physically grounded trajectories, complemented by an automated, template-based engine that generates diverse natural language descriptions across multiple styles.

## Problem

- Traditional motion capture (MoCap) is expensive, time-consuming, and yields datasets with limited behavioral diversity, making large-scale data generation for robots challenging.
- Retargeting MoCap data to different robot morphologies introduces kinematic artifacts, while purely kinematic generative models often produce physically infeasible motions.
- Training language-conditioned robot controllers requires extensive manual annotation of motion clips, a labor-intensive process that does not scale efficiently with dataset size.

## Method

- **CLAW** generates physically grounded motions by composing ==parameterized motion primitives== from a ==kinematic planner== within a ==MuJoCo physics simulation==, tailored for the Unitree G1 robot.
- Two ==browser-based user interfaces== (Keyboard Mode and Editor Mode) enable both real-time exploratory control and reproducible, timeline-based batch generation of multi-segment trajectories.
- A ==deterministic, template-based language annotation engine== automatically generates diverse natural language descriptions in 8 distinct styles, utilizing the same motion-intent parameters that drive motion generation.

## Results

- The system successfully generates scalable, physically consistent, whole-body trajectories for the Unitree G1 robot, directly compatible with robotic control without requiring error-prone retargeting.
- CLAW automates language annotation, producing both segment-wise and full-trajectory descriptions in **8 distinct styles** (e.g., instructional, narrative) and up to **17 full-trajectory variations** for complex sequences.
- It demonstrates smooth stitching of semantically distinct motion primitives (e.g., walking to squat to crawl) into long-horizon sequences, with transient artifacts noted only for a limited number of extreme postural transitions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating the motion modes of a kinematic planner as composable, parameterized primitives allows for scalable and diverse generation of whole-body trajectories.
> - A kinematic planner capable of seamlessly transitioning between diverse motion modes by using the robot's current state can effectively stitch together complex, continuous behaviors.
> - Utilizing the same structured parameters for both motion generation and language annotation ensures inherent semantic accuracy and alignment between generated motions and their descriptive text.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of high-quality reference motions is an essential component for training humanoid robots to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks. The current research builds upon and addresses limitations within two primary paradigms for obtaining such data:

**Traditional Motion Capture (MoCap) Pipelines:**
The prevailing method involves acquiring human motion data through MoCap systems and subsequently retargeting it to the kinematics of the target robot morphology. While this approach has demonstrated utility, it presents several challenges:
- **Cost and Scale:** MoCap sessions are logistically complex and expensive, which limits the volume and behavioral diversity of the resulting datasets.
- **Kinematic Artifacts:** The process of retargeting motion across differing body types can introduce undesirable artifacts, such as foot sliding, self-penetration, and joint-limit violations, which can negatively affect the performance of downstream robotic policies.
- **Annotation Burden:** For tasks requiring language conditioning, each recorded motion clip necessitates manual annotation with natural language descriptions. This creates a linear scaling of annotation cost with dataset size, making large-scale data generation labor-intensive.

**Recent Generative Motion Models:**
Advances in generative artificial intelligence have led to models that can synthesize diverse whole-body movements from language prompts, often trained on extensive MoCap datasets like AMASS. Examples include text-to-motion diffusion models. While these models address some aspects of data scarcity:
- **Physical Feasibility:** The outputs of these generative models are typically purely kinematic. They do not guarantee physical feasibility, often requiring an additional physics-based tracking step before deployment on a physical robot.
- **Control Limitations:** It can be challenging to independently control meta-motion attributes such as speed, heading, pelvis height, or gait style using these models, which are critical for systematic data augmentation and fine-grained control.

**CLAW's Position:**
CLAW presents a complementary approach to existing methods. Instead of generating motions *from* language, it focuses on generating *language-annotated* motion data at scale by composing parametric motion primitives within a physics simulation environment. The core observation is that existing kinematic planners, when paired with low-level whole-body controllers, are capable of producing diverse and physically grounded motions. However, these planners typically expose only low-level command interfaces, which are not conducive to systematic dataset construction. CLAW bridges this gap by abstracting the planner's motion modes into composable, parameterized primitives, thereby enabling the scalable assembly of physically realistic, diverse trajectories that are automatically paired with natural language descriptions. This addresses the limitations of both MoCap (cost, artifacts, manual annotation) and purely kinematic generative models (physical infeasibility, lack of attribute control).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the CLAW project is to facilitate the scalable generation of language-annotated, whole-body motion data specifically for the Unitree G1 humanoid robot. This objective is driven by the identified challenges in creating large-scale, high-quality datasets necessary for training language-conditioned controllers for humanoid robots.

The detailed motivations and specific objectives include:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Diversity:** To overcome the limitations of traditional motion capture (MoCap) which are costly, time-consuming, and yield datasets with limited behavioral diversity. The goal is to enable the generation of a substantially larger and more varied corpus of motion data.
- **Ensuring Physical Feasibility:** To provide motion data that is inherently physically grounded. Unlike purely kinematic generative models which produce motions that may not be physically executable and often require subsequent physics-based tracking, CLAW aims to generate trajectories directly within a physics simulation, ensuring their realism and applicability to robotic control.
- **Eliminating Retargeting Artifacts:** To bypass the kinematic retargeting step often necessary when adapting human MoCap data to a robot's morphology. Retargeting can introduce undesirable artifacts such as foot sliding, self-penetration, and joint-limit violations. By generating motions directly for the Unitree G1 skeleton in simulation, CLAW aims to produce trajectories that are natively compatible with the robot, improving downstream policy performance.
- **Automating Language Annotation:** To remove the labor-intensive and costly requirement for manual language annotation. Training language-conditioned robot controllers necessitates pairing motions with descriptive text. CLAW seeks to automate this process through a template-based engine, generating diverse natural language descriptions at both segment and full-trajectory levels from the same parameters that drive motion generation.
- **Providing Flexible and Reproducible Data Collection:** To offer intuitive and efficient interfaces for human operators to control and compose motions. This includes a real-time interactive mode for exploratory data collection and a timeline-based editor for systematic, reproducible batch generation of multi-segment trajectories.
- **Developing a Modular and Extensible System:** To design an architecture that decouples data collection from the specific motion-generation backend. This allows for flexibility, enabling the integration or replacement of different kinematic planners and controllers without requiring extensive modifications to the entire pipeline.
- **Supporting Open Science and Robot Learning Research:** To make the entire system publicly available, thereby lowering the barrier for researchers and developers to generate large-scale, high-quality motion-language paired data for humanoid robot learning tasks.

In summary, CLAW is motivated by the need for scalable, physically grounded, and automatically annotated motion data for humanoid robots, aiming to overcome the practical and technical hurdles associated with existing data generation methodologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CLAW is designed as a pipeline for the scalable generation of language-annotated whole-body motion data for the Unitree G1 humanoid robot. It integrates interactive user interfaces with a physics-based simulation and a deterministic language annotation engine.

**A. Core Concept: Composable Motion Primitives**
The fundamental approach of CLAW is to treat the motion modes provided by a kinematic planner as composable primitives. These primitives are parameterized by attributes such as movement direction, heading, speed, pelvis height, and duration. By composing these primitives, the system can generate arbitrarily long and diverse whole-body motion sequences. The current pipeline instantiates this concept using the kinematic planner and whole-body controller from SONIC, though the architecture is designed to be planner-agnostic.

**B. User Interfaces**
CLAW offers two browser-based user interfaces that stream structured commands to the backend at 20 Hz:

1. **Keyboard Mode (Figure 3a):** This interface provides real-time, interactive control for exploratory data collection.
 - **Controls:** Standard keyboard inputs (W/A/S/D for movement, Q/E for heading adjustments, comma/period for strafing, R for halt) allow direct manipulation of the robot's movement.
 - **Parameter Adjustment:** A motion-mode selector categorizes 25 available behaviors (Locomotion, Ground, Boxing, Styled Walking). Sliders enable continuous adjustment of speed and pelvis height within the active mode's supported range.
 - **Application:** Suited for free-form exploration and rapid prototyping of motion sequences.

2. **Editor Mode (Figure 3b):** This interface facilitates the composition of multi-segment trajectories on a visual timeline for reproducible and batch data generation.
 - **Composition:** Operators can drag and drop motion clips from a library onto a timeline.
 - **Configuration:** Each segment can be configured with specific parameters, including duration, movement direction, turn angle, speed override, and pelvis height (for ground-level modes).
 - **Execution:** Once a sequence is defined, a single command dispatches it to the backend for execution, recording, and annotation.
 - **Application:** Designed for reproducible, large-batch data generation, enabling systematic expansion of training datasets by replaying identical recipes with varied parameters.

**C. Kinematic Planner and Whole-Body Controller**
The structured commands from the user interfaces are sent to a backend system comprising a kinematic planner and a low-level whole-body controller:

1. **Kinematic Planner:** This component acts as a motion-primitive engine. It receives meta-commands (motion-mode index, movement-direction vector, facing-direction vector, speed scalar, pelvis-height scalar) and the current robot state. From these inputs, it synthesizes short-horizon kinematic reference motions.
 - **Motion Modes:** The planner supports 25 distinct motion modes organized into four groups (Table I), each accepting a subset of continuous parameters.
 - **Composability:** A key feature is the planner's ability to smoothly transition between different motion modes. When a new mode index is received, the planner uses the robot's current state as the starting point for the new behavior, facilitating seamless stitching of heterogeneous primitives (e.g., walking to squat to crawl).

2. **Whole-Body Controller:** This controller tracks the kinematic references generated by the planner within a MuJoCo simulation environment. This step ensures that the resulting trajectories are physically consistent and grounded, producing joint trajectories recorded at 50 Hz.

**D. Language Annotation Engine**
Parallel to motion generation, CLAW employs a template-based engine to automatically generate natural language descriptions:

1. **Input:** The annotation engine utilizes the same motion-intent parameters (motion mode, movement direction, heading, speed, duration) that drive the kinematic planner.
2. **Stylistic Diversity:** It produces annotations at both per-segment and full-trajectory levels, supporting eight distinct styles. These styles vary along two factors:
 - **Register:** Instruction (imperative), natural (adverbial), narrative (third-person), and concise (keyword-only).
 - **Temporal Grounding:** Each register is rendered both with and without explicit duration.
3. **Linguistic Variation:** Linguistic diversity is achieved through synonym banks for verbs (associated with each motion mode), movement directions, and turn verbs. Speed values are mapped to tempo adverbs via linear interpolation, and turn magnitudes are discretized into descriptive buckets (e.g., "slight," "partial"). Connectives are varied for multi-segment descriptions.
4. **Reproducibility:** The generation process is deterministic given a seed, ensuring reproducibility of annotations while still offering substantial lexical variety. The design also allows for future integration of large language models (LLMs) for greater diversity if desired.

**E. System Design**
CLAW is implemented as a four-process distributed system (Figure 5), allowing for modularity and extensibility:

1. **Web UI Frontend:** Handles user interaction, sends UI commands (20Hz), and visualizes system states.
2. **Protocol Bridge (WebSocket–ZMQ):** Serves as the central coordinator, translating communication protocols, managing planner state, aggregating telemetry, and orchestrating command sequencing and trajectory management. It forwards planner commands to the controller via ZMQ at 20Hz.
3. **C++ Controller:** Performs kinematic planning and whole-body tracking, converting high-level commands into joint-level targets, executing control policies, and returning telemetry (~50Hz).
4. **MuJoCo Simulator:** Provides the physics execution environment, advancing system dynamics based on control inputs and returning state feedback.

The final output of each session is a structured data package containing time-aligned kinematic and dynamic trajectory data, coupled with multiple stylistic language annotations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The CLAW pipeline demonstrates capabilities in generating physically simulated, language-annotated whole-body trajectories through a composable, interactive system.

**A. Smooth Motion Stitching from Composable Primitives**
A core finding is that CLAW effectively enables the composition of semantically distinct motion primitives into continuous, long-horizon trajectories. The underlying kinematic planner manages transitions by using the robot's current state as the starting point for a new active mode, generally resulting in smooth transitions without requiring explicit blending or post-processing by CLAW itself.
- **Successful Transitions:** For a majority of motion-mode pairs, the system produces fluid and natural-looking transitions, even between behaviors that differ significantly in speed or body configuration (Figure 6a). This allows for chaining diverse actions like walking, squatting, and crawling into seamless sequences.
- **Transient Artifacts:** The paper acknowledges that for a limited number of motion pairs involving extreme postural differences, the planner may generate kinematically abrupt transitions, leading to unnatural intermediate postures (Figure 6b). Identifying and filtering these less ideal transitions in large-scale data collection remains an area for further investigation.

**B. Automated Language Annotation**
CLAW's template-based language annotation engine provides an automated solution for generating descriptive text for motion sequences, addressing the labor-intensive nature of manual annotation.
- **Accuracy and Alignment:** The engine operates on the same structured motion-intent parameters that drive the planner. This design ensures that the generated segment-wise and full-trajectory annotations are semantically accurate and inherently aligned with the recorded motion segments.
- **Diverse Descriptions:** For a given multi-segment trajectory, the engine can produce 17 full-trajectory descriptions and 8 per-segment annotations, capturing the semantic content of each stage. This includes descriptions for complex sequences such as a standard walk transitioning to a slow left turn, then to a ground-level crawl (Figure 7).
- **Efficiency:** The deterministic and automated nature of the annotation process eliminates human effort, making it suitable for generating large-scale, richly annotated motion datasets.

**C. Scalable Data Synthesis for Robot Execution**
CLAW offers a significant advantage over MoCap-based pipelines in terms of scalability and direct applicability to robotic systems.
- **Compute-bound Scalability:** Data collection scales with available computational resources rather than being constrained by physical recording sessions.
- **Reproducible Batch Generation:** The editor interface facilitates the definition of motion "recipes" that the pipeline can execute, record, and annotate repeatedly without further human intervention. These recipes can be replayed with variations in parameters (e.g., speed, heading) to systematically augment the training corpus. New recipes can be composed from the 25 available motion primitives.
- **Direct Robot Compatibility:** CLAW generates two forms of trajectory data: kinematic reference trajectories (planner output) and dynamic trajectories (MuJoCo simulation response). Crucially, because the planner operates natively on the Unitree G1 skeleton, the resulting joint trajectories are directly in the robot's configuration space. This eliminates the need for a retargeting step, which often introduces kinematic artifacts in MoCap-derived data, making the output directly usable for robot control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CLAW represents a development in the domain of humanoid robot control and data generation, with several areas of potential significance and impact:

- **Enabling Scalable Data Generation for Robot Learning:** The primary significance of CLAW lies in its ability to generate large-scale, physically grounded, and language-annotated motion data. This directly addresses the existing bottlenecks of traditional motion capture (cost, limited diversity, retargeting artifacts) and the physical infeasibility of purely kinematic generative models. By providing a scalable data source, CLAW can accelerate the training of robust and diverse language-conditioned whole-body controllers for humanoid robots.

- **Advancing Language-Conditioned Control:** The automated, multi-style language annotation engine is a key component for facilitating research in language-conditioned robot control. By providing consistently aligned and descriptively varied text annotations, CLAW simplifies the process of creating datasets necessary for training robots to understand and execute commands given in natural language, leading to more intuitive human-robot interaction.

- **Improving Data Quality and Applicability:** By generating motions directly for the Unitree G1 robot in a physics simulation, CLAW ensures that the output trajectories are physically feasible and directly compatible with the robot's morphology, without the need for error-prone retargeting. This can lead to higher quality training data and, consequently, more effective and reliable robot behaviors.

- **Reducing Development Costs and Time:** Automating both motion generation and language annotation significantly reduces the labor and time typically associated with dataset creation. This allows researchers and developers to focus resources on algorithm development and robot deployment rather than manual data collection and labeling.

- **Facilitating Reproducibility and Systematic Research:** The editor mode and deterministic annotation engine promote reproducible data generation. Researchers can define and share motion recipes, allowing for systematic exploration of parameter spaces and standardized evaluation of control policies, contributing to more rigorous scientific inquiry.

- **Open-Source Contribution:** The decision to make the system publicly available lowers the barrier to entry for researchers in humanoid robotics. This open-source release can foster collaboration, accelerate innovation, and establish a common platform for data generation in the field.

- **Foundational for Future Work:** The modular and planner-agnostic architecture means CLAW can evolve. It provides a foundation for integrating more advanced components, such as sophisticated large language models for enriched linguistic diversity in annotations or incorporating user-defined and learned skills as new motion primitives. The identified areas for future work, such as automatically filtering suboptimal transitions, also provide clear directions for further research and improvement in data generation quality.
