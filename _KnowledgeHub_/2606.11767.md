# Blind Dexterous Grasping via Real2Sim2Real Tactile Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for tactile-only blind grasping with a physical multi-fingered robotic hand is presented, operating without visual input or object pose estimates. The system achieved a 27% real-world grasp success rate across 20 diverse objects by leveraging calibrated tactile sensing and a privileged tactile encoder for effective sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- Dexterous manipulation typically relies on visual sensing, which is unreliable or unavailable in dark, cluttered, or occluded environments.
- The significant sim-to-real gap for tactile sensing, especially with sparse arrays, complicates transferring policies learned in simulation to real hardware.
- Learning robust, generalized tactile-only manipulation policies for diverse, unseen objects is challenging due to limited geometric information from sparse tactile observations.

## Method

- A lightweight ==Real2Sim== tactile calibration pipeline was developed to align simulated and real binary tactile events (onset/offset timing) using primitive motions.
- A layout-aware tactile encoder, enhanced by pretraining with privileged geometric supervision from simulation, was designed to improve the expressiveness of sparse tactile observations.
- An expert-to-diffusion policy learning scheme aggregates object-specific reinforcement learning expert trajectories into a unified, tactile-conditioned Diffusion Policy for robust generalization.

## Results

- The system achieved a **27%** overall real-world grasp success rate across 20 diverse objects (10 seen, 10 unseen) on a physical LEAP Hand, operating without visual input or real-world grasping demonstrations.
- Real2Sim tactile calibration reduced the average contact onset error from **426 ms** to **96 ms** and improved the activation F1 score from **64.2%** to **70.5%** between simulation and real hardware.
- Privileged tactile encoder pretraining significantly improved simulation grasping success rates from **36.2%** to **60.4%** for seen objects and from **20.0%** to **43.2%** for unseen objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing the tactile sim-to-real gap at the binary contact-event level through lightweight calibration is crucial for transferable tactile policies, rather than complex continuous modeling.
> - Pretraining tactile encoders with simulator-only geometric priors enables learning structured representations from sparse tactile signals, compensating for limited real-world observability.
> - Diffusion Policies effectively distill multimodal grasping strategies from diverse simulated experts, supporting generalization to novel objects with tactile-only feedback.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation in robotics often relies heavily on visual sensing to localize objects and guide grasping actions. However, environments where vision is unreliable, occluded, or unavailable present a significant challenge for robots. In such scenarios, blind grasping, which leverages contact feedback for reactive physical interaction, becomes a crucial capability. This approach is particularly relevant for tasks in darkness, clutter, narrow spaces, or with severe occlusion, where camera-based perception systems may fail.

Despite the recognized importance of tactile-only control for dexterous manipulation, its implementation on physical robotic hands faces several technical hurdles. Current methods often struggle with the "sim-to-real gap," a discrepancy between simulated and real-world sensor data and physics that makes policies learned in simulation difficult to transfer directly to hardware. For tactile sensing, this gap is exacerbated by the sensitivity of tactile signals to material properties, sensor placement, manufacturing variations, and complex contact mechanics, which are difficult to model precisely in simulation. Additionally, many tactile sensing systems, particularly distributed sparse arrays, provide limited geometric information and only partial observations of the hand-object state. This ambiguity means that a single tactile reading can correspond to multiple object poses or contact configurations, complicating the learning of robust manipulation policies.

Prior research in tactile-driven dexterous manipulation has explored tactile feedback for grasp stability estimation, slip detection, in-hand manipulation, and contact-rich control. While advanced sensors offer rich local contact understanding, much of this work still relies on visuotactile fusion, leaving vision-free dexterous grasping relatively underexplored. Attempts to bridge the sim-to-real gap for tactile policies have included identifying physical parameters for specific sensors or using domain-invariant sensory abstractions like binary tactile signals. However, even binary signals can exhibit temporal mismatches between simulation and the real world. Furthermore, while some methods incorporate geometric priors in tactile representation, these often depend on locally dense fingertip tactile readings or visual inputs, and are less applicable to sparse binary sensors distributed across a full hand. The challenge of aggregating diverse successful grasping behaviors into a single, robust policy for novel objects also persists.

This work addresses these gaps by focusing on developing a framework for tactile-only blind grasping that explicitly tackles the tactile sim-to-real gap, enhances the interpretability of sparse tactile observations, and enables generalization to unseen objects without real-world demonstrations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable reliable tactile-only blind grasping with a physical multi-fingered robotic hand, deployable in real-world scenarios without relying on visual input, object pose estimates, or prior knowledge of object geometry. This overarching goal is motivated by the limitations of vision-based systems in constrained or visually ambiguous environments, and the inherent scalability issues of real-world demonstration-based learning for dexterous tasks under blind conditions.

To achieve this, the authors identify three key challenges and propose specific objectives:

1. **Bridging the Tactile Sim-to-Real Gap:** A major motivation is the difficulty of transferring tactile policies learned in simulation to real hardware. Tactile sensors are highly sensitive, and even simplified binary contact signals can show significant discrepancies between simulated and real contact onset and offset timing. Therefore, a key objective is to develop a method for constructing a digital-twin simulator that accurately reproduces real tactile signals at the contact-event level, with minimal reliance on complex soft-body modeling or extensive real-world grasping demonstrations. This aims to ensure that policies trained in simulation behave predictably on physical hardware.

2. **Improving Expressiveness of Sparse Tactile Observations:** Sparse tactile arrays provide limited information about object geometry and hand-object configuration, making it difficult for policies to infer the spatial context of contacts. The motivation here is to augment these sparse signals to be more informative. The objective is to design a tactile encoder that incorporates sensor-geometry priors, such as the 3D location of each sensor on the hand, and to pretrain this encoder using privileged geometric supervision available only in simulation. This pretraining aims to encourage the learned latent representation to capture object- and contact-level structure that is not directly observable from raw sparse tactile inputs.

3. **Enhancing Generalization to Unseen Objects and Handling Multimodal Grasping Strategies:** Learning a single policy that can robustly grasp a diverse range of objects, including those not encountered during training, is challenging. Furthermore, tactile-only grasping is inherently multimodal; the same sparse contact history might permit several valid grasping motions. The motivation is to develop a policy learning scheme that can discover and aggregate successful grasping behaviors from simulation and generalize them to novel objects under partial observability. The objective is to train object-specific reinforcement learning (RL) experts in the calibrated simulator and then distill their successful trajectories into a unified, tactile-conditioned Diffusion Policy capable of generating diverse and effective grasping motions.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to provide a comprehensive framework that enables effective and scalable tactile-only blind grasping, enhancing robotic autonomy in visually uncertain environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for tactile-only blind grasping integrates three main components: a Real2Sim tactile calibration pipeline, a layout-aware tactile encoder with privileged pretraining, and a tactile-conditioned Diffusion Policy aggregated from simulated expert demonstrations.

#### 3.1. Hardware Setup and Contact Modeling

The physical platform consists of a 6-DoF xArm6 manipulator equipped with a 16-DoF LEAP Hand. This hand is instrumented with 44 binary tactile channels: four custom-built TwinTac sensors (eight channels each) on the fingertips and 12 commercial force-sensing resistors (FSRs) on the palm and finger surfaces. The robot receives proprioceptive feedback (joint states) and binary tactile activations.

For contact modeling in simulation, sensing regions are discretized into 3D surface nodes. FSRs activate if any node in their region has a Signed Distance Field (SDF) value below a calibrated threshold (λ_con). For TwinTac sensors, a penalty-based model computes raw contact pressure at surface nodes. This pressure is then spatially aggregated to virtual taxel locations using an exponential decay function, controlled by parameter α, to model contact-induced deformation and cross-talk. A taxel activates if its aggregated pressure exceeds a threshold (λ_pre).

#### 3.2. Real2Sim Tactile Calibration

To minimize the sim-to-real gap in binary tactile events, a lightweight Real2Sim calibration procedure is introduced. This involves:
1. **Data Collection:** Task-agnostic primitive motions (e.g., tapping, sliding against known surfaces like a sphere and a cylinder) are executed on the real hand, recording synchronized joint trajectories and binary tactile readings. Nine calibration trajectories are collected across the two objects at approximately 10 Hz, providing 5,674 frames of data.
2. **Simulation Replay:** The recorded joint trajectories are replayed in the calibrated simulator to obtain corresponding simulated tactile readings.
3. **Parameter Estimation:** The tactile simulation parameters (θ = (λ_con, α, λ_pre)) are estimated by minimizing the ℓ1 mismatch between the real and simulated binary event sequences. This low-dimensional optimization is solved using a grid search over bounded parameter ranges. This process aligns simulated and real contact events, particularly regarding onset and offset timing, without requiring complex soft-body models or real-world grasping demonstrations.

#### 3.3. Privileged Tactile Representation Learning

To enhance the expressiveness of sparse tactile observations, the approach employs a layout-aware tactile encoder.
1. **Kinematically Grounded Input:** For each tactile channel, its binary activation is augmented with the 3D position of the corresponding taxel, mapped from its local link frame to a common hand-centric frame using forward kinematics (FK). This creates a 4-dimensional vector ([Px, Py, Pz, Binary_Activation]) for each taxel. A short history window of these stacked vectors forms the tactile input (S_t).
2. **Self-supervised Pretraining:** A lightweight temporal Transformer serves as the tactile encoder. This encoder is pretrained in simulation using privileged geometric supervision (object shape, hand-object contact labels, object pose, robot joint configuration), which is unavailable in the real world. A query-based Transformer decoder is attached during pretraining to predict these simulator-only targets from the tactile history. The pretraining objective includes Chamfer distance for point clouds, L1 and quaternion loss for object pose, L2 loss for robot configuration, and focal loss for contact prediction, along with geometric regularizers and an empty frame loss. After pretraining, the decoder is discarded, and only the encoder is used for policy learning and deployment, ensuring that no privileged information is used during real-world operation.

#### 3.4. Policy Learning and Real-World Deployment

To handle diverse object geometries and multimodal grasping strategies, the framework combines reinforcement learning with diffusion-based policy learning.
1. **Expert Data Generation:** Object-specific Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) experts are trained in the calibrated simulator. These experts use an asymmetric actor-critic setup, where the actor receives deployable observations (proprioception and binary tactile activations), while the critic and reward computation access privileged simulator quantities for improved sample efficiency. After training, successful grasp-and-lift trajectories are rolled out from randomized initial object placements and retained, forming an offline dataset (D). These PPO experts are used solely for data generation and are then discarded.
2. **Diffusion Policy Distillation:** The successful trajectories from the RL experts are aggregated and distilled into a tactile-conditioned Diffusion Policy. At each control step, the pretrained tactile encoder maps the spatially grounded tactile history (S_t) to a feature (z_t), which is concatenated with the proprioceptive state (x_t) to form a condition (c_t). Conditioned on c_t, the Diffusion Policy predicts a future action chunk (A_t) and is trained using a standard diffusion denoising objective.
3. **Real-World Deployment:** The trained policy operates as a closed-loop receding-horizon controller on the physical robot. At each step, it updates proprioception and tactile history, computes the tactile feature, samples an action chunk, and executes the first few actions before replanning. Actions command incremental arm and hand joint motions, allowing the policy to react to new tactile contacts, refine finger coordination, and lift objects without visual input.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed framework was conducted on a physical LEAP Hand equipped with distributed tactile sensing, using a benchmark of 20 diverse objects (10 seen during simulation training and 10 unseen, held out for generalization assessment).

#### 4.1. Real-World Blind Grasping Performance

The complete system demonstrated the capability for real-world blind grasping, achieving an overall success rate of 27% across all 20 objects. This included successful grasps on both seen (16/50 trials) and unseen objects (11/50 trials), without the use of real-world grasping demonstrations or visual input during training or deployment. The policy was observed to exhibit tactile exploration behavior to align objects with the hand when objects were not initially centered.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Real2Sim Tactile Calibration

Evaluation of tactile event fidelity showed that the Real2Sim calibration significantly improved the consistency between simulated and real tactile observations. As presented in Table 2:
- **Contact Onset Error (∆t_on):** The calibrated simulator reduced the average contact onset error from 426 ms (nominal sim) to 96 ms, indicating better temporal alignment of initial contact events.
- **Activation F1 Score:** The calibrated simulator achieved a higher activation F1 score of 70.5% compared to 64.2% for the nominal simulator, suggesting improved taxel-level agreement in contact patterns.
- **False Positive Rate (FPR):** The FPR decreased from 4.2% (nominal sim) to 2.2% (calibrated sim), indicating fewer erroneous simulated activations when real sensors were inactive.
These metrics confirm that calibration enhances the fidelity of tactile events, which supports the learning of transferable policies.

#### 4.3. Impact of Privileged Tactile Encoder Pretraining

Simulation ablations (Table 3) demonstrated that privileged pretraining improved grasping performance:
- **Seen Objects:** Success rate increased from 36.2% (without pretraining) to 60.4% (with pretraining).
- **Unseen Objects:** Success rate increased from 20.0% (without pretraining) to 43.2% (with pretraining).
This indicates that simulator-only geometric supervision aids the encoder in learning structured representations from sparse contact events prior to policy learning. Real-world experiments (Appendix C.1, Table 5) further supported this, showing an improvement from 6% to 27% overall real-world grasp success when privileged pretraining was included, even when tactile calibration was disabled.

#### 4.4. Additional Ablations

- **Binary vs. Continuous Tactile Signals (Appendix C.2, Table 6):** Policies using binary contact events achieved a comparable simulation success rate (60.4%) to those using continuous tactile magnitudes (59.2%) on seen objects. This suggests that contact occurrence, timing, and location are sufficient for task execution, and binary contacts offer a more robust interface for sim-to-real transfer.
- **Policy Architecture (Appendix C.3, Table 7):** The Diffusion Policy demonstrated superior performance, achieving 51.8% average simulation success (60.4% seen, 43.2% unseen), outperforming deterministic Behavior Cloning (BC) (45.8%) and Action Chunking with Transformer (ACT) (34.9%). This supports the use of Diffusion Policy for multimodal action modeling.
- **Kinematically Grounded Tactile Inputs (Appendix C.4, Table 8):** Grounding tactile activations in the hand coordinate frame (FK-grounded tactile) improved simulation performance (28.1% average) compared to using a raw unstructured binary tactile vector (26.8%). This indicates that explicitly encoding spatial contact relationships enhances tactile representation.

#### 4.5. Real-World Failure Mode Analysis (Appendix D)

Analysis of 73 failed real-world trials revealed the most common failure modes:
- **Slip during Lifting (47.9% of failures):** The most prevalent issue, often occurring with smooth or rounded objects. This suggests challenges in maintaining stable grasps under real contact dynamics and a sensitivity to the contact-mechanics sim-to-real gap.
- **Emergency Stop (31.5% of failures):** Typically triggered by unsafe arm-hand configurations or workspace limit violations due to sparse tactile feedback and partial observability.
- **Empty Grasp (20.5% of failures):** More common for objects that are difficult to localize with tactile feedback alone, such as smooth objects where tactile exploration may fail to establish stable contact.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a comprehensive framework that demonstrates the feasibility of learning and deploying tactile-only blind grasping policies on a physical dexterous robotic hand without real-world grasping demonstrations or visual input. The work makes several contributions that have implications for future developments in robotic manipulation.

Firstly, the introduction of a lightweight Real2Sim tactile calibration procedure is significant. By aligning binary contact onset and offset timing between simulation and hardware, it effectively bridges a critical aspect of the tactile sim-to-real gap. This method avoids the complexity of continuous system identification and soft-body modeling, offering a more scalable approach for developing high-fidelity digital-twin simulators for tactile sensing. The calibrated simulator enables more reliable policy learning in a simulated environment, with improved transferability to real-world hardware.

Secondly, the development of a layout-aware tactile encoder, enhanced by privileged pretraining in simulation, addresses the challenge of limited expressiveness from sparse tactile signals. By incorporating the spatial layout of sensors and leveraging rich geometric and contact supervision during pretraining, the encoder learns more structured and informative representations of hand-object interactions. This approach allows the policy to reason about *where* contact occurs, not just *if* it occurs, which is crucial for dexterous manipulation, particularly under blind conditions where visual cues are absent.

Thirdly, the expert-to-diffusion policy learning scheme provides a robust method for aggregating diverse object-specific grasping behaviors into a unified, tactile-conditioned Diffusion Policy. This approach enables the deployed controller to generate multimodal grasping motions, enhancing its generalization capabilities to a wide array of objects, including those not encountered during initial expert training. This is a step towards more adaptable robotic systems that can handle novel objects in uncertain environments.

The overall 27% real-world grasp success rate on a diverse set of objects, including unseen instances, represents an advancement in tactile-only blind grasping. It suggests that robots can operate effectively in environments where vision is compromised or unavailable, such as in dark, cluttered, or confined spaces. The framework's ability to achieve this without real-world demonstrations also implies a reduction in the need for tedious and difficult manual data collection for complex dexterous tasks.

Future research directions, as identified by the authors, include improving tactile sensing quality and coverage (e.g., full-hand tactile skins, shear/slip sensing), developing two-timescale control architectures (combining high-level grasp formation with low-level reactive reflexes), and incorporating richer tactile representations, recovery behaviors, and more diverse simulated demonstrations. Addressing these limitations could further enhance the robustness and reliability of tactile-only blind grasping, broadening its applicability in various robotic domains. The work lays foundational groundwork for more capable and autonomous manipulation systems operating with an enhanced sense of touch.
