# Monet: Reasoning in Latent Visual Space Beyond Images and Language

> [!summary] Summary
> Monet, a framework developed by researchers from Peking University, ByteDance's Kling Team, and Amazon, enables multimodal large language models to perform abstract visual reasoning by directly operating within a continuous latent visual space. The approach achieves superior performance on real-world perception and reasoning benchmarks and demonstrates strong out-of-distribution generalization capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing "thinking with images" methods for MLLMs are rigid, tool-dependent, and lack generalization to complex visual operations beyond trained toolsets.
- Prior "reasoning in latent space" approaches for MLLMs suffer from poor scalability, loss of visual detail, and insufficient optimization for continuous latent embeddings.
- MLLMs generally lack a flexible, internal perceptual space for abstract visual thinking akin to human cognition.

## Method

- Introduces a ==three-stage Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) pipeline== to equip MLLMs with the ability to generate and reason with continuous latent embeddings.
- Develops the ==Monet-SFT-125K dataset==, providing high-quality, fine-grained supervision for learning latent visual observations.
- Proposes ==Visual-latent Policy Optimization (VLPO)==, a novel reinforcement learning algorithm tailored for optimizing continuous latent embeddings.

## Results

- Monet-7B consistently outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-7B and other state-of-the-art open-source methods on benchmarks including V*, HRBench, and MME-RealWorld-Lite.
- Achieves the best performance among open-source models on the VisualPuzzles benchmark, showcasing strong out-of-distribution generalization for abstract visual reasoning.
- Ablation studies confirm that ==dual supervision==, ==latent-only backpropagation==, and VLPO are crucial for the framework's effectiveness in learning and optimizing latent visual reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs can learn to generate and leverage internal continuous latent embeddings for abstract visual reasoning, eliminating the reliance on explicit external tools or auxiliary images during inference.
> - Dual supervision with representation-alignment loss on crucial observation tokens and direct attention from latent embeddings to auxiliary images is vital for effective latent feature learning.
> - Restricting gradients to flow solely through generated latent embeddings (latent-only backpropagation) is essential for genuinely improving latent representations during training.
