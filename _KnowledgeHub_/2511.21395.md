# Monet: Reasoning in Latent Visual Space Beyond Images and Language

> [!summary] Summary
> Monet, a framework developed by researchers from Peking University, ByteDance's Kling Team, and Amazon, enables multimodal large language models to perform abstract visual reasoning by directly operating within a continuous latent visual space. The approach achieves superior performance on real-world perception and reasoning benchmarks and demonstrates strong out-of-distribution generalization capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing "thinking with images" methods for MLLMs are rigid, tool-dependent, and lack generalization to complex visual operations beyond trained toolsets.
- Prior "reasoning in latent space" approaches for MLLMs suffer from poor scalability, loss of visual detail, and insufficient optimization for continuous latent embeddings.
- MLLMs generally lack a flexible, internal perceptual space for abstract visual thinking akin to human cognition.

## Method

- Introduces a ==three-stage Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) pipeline== to equip MLLMs with the ability to generate and reason with ==continuous latent embeddings==.
- Develops the ==Monet-SFT-125K dataset==, providing high-quality, ==fine-grained supervision== for learning ==latent visual observations==.
- Proposes ==Visual-latent Policy Optimization (VLPO)==, a novel ==reinforcement learning algorithm== tailored for optimizing continuous latent embeddings.

## Results

- Monet-7B consistently outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-7B and other state-of-the-art open-source methods on benchmarks including V*, HRBench, and MME-RealWorld-Lite.
- Achieves the best performance among open-source models on the VisualPuzzles benchmark, showcasing strong out-of-distribution generalization for abstract visual reasoning.
- Ablation studies confirm that ==dual supervision==, ==latent-only backpropagation==, and VLPO are crucial for the framework's effectiveness in learning and optimizing latent visual reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs can learn to generate and leverage internal continuous latent embeddings for abstract visual reasoning, eliminating the reliance on explicit external tools or auxiliary images during inference.
> - Dual supervision with representation-alignment loss on crucial observation tokens and direct attention from latent embeddings to auxiliary images is vital for effective latent feature learning.
> - Restricting gradients to flow solely through generated latent embeddings (latent-only backpropagation) is essential for genuinely improving latent representations during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Monet addresses a critical gap in the evolving field of visual reasoning for MLLMs, specifically within two major paradigms: "thinking with images" and "reasoning in latent space."

**"Thinking with Images" Paradigm:**
Recent advancements have shown that injecting visual evidence into intermediate reasoning steps, often termed "thinking with images" or "visual chain-of-thought (CoT)," significantly improves MLLMs' visual reasoning capabilities. Existing methods in this paradigm generally fall into two categories:
1. **Emphasizing visual content from the original image:** This includes techniques like grounding, cropping, or re-inputting selected image tokens.
2. **Creating new visual content:** This involves invoking external tools (e.g., grounding, depth estimation models), generating executable code to modify images (e.g., drawing lines, bounding boxes), or even generating entirely new images via text-to-image models.

Monet highlights a fundamental limitation of these existing approaches: their flexibility is "fundamentally limited by external tools." Models become tool-dependent, struggling to generalize to complex visual operations beyond their trained toolsets, requiring extensive supervision for valid tool calls, and suffering from increased latency due to asynchronous, multi-turn inference. Monet aims to overcome this rigidity by enabling MLLMs to move beyond explicit tools and reason internally.

**"Reasoning in Latent Space" Paradigm:**
A parallel line of research explores training large language models (LLMs) to reason with continuous latent embeddings, either by generating these embeddings directly or using reweighted combinations of text embeddings. This approach offers enhanced reasoning flexibility by exploring a continuous space, reducing the reliance on discrete linguistic tokens.

While some recent works have extended latent reasoning to MLLMs by aligning generated latent embeddings with those of auxiliary images, Monet identifies two key limitations:
1. **Poor scalability and detail loss:** Aligning over thousands of image tokens is computationally expensive. Compressing image tokens (e.g., via mean pooling) can distort detailed visual features.
2. **Insufficient optimization of latent embeddings:** Existing methods often rely on next-token prediction (NTP) loss on text tokens, providing weak supervision for latent embeddings. Furthermore, common reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms like GRPO are designed for text tokens and cannot directly optimize continuous latent embeddings.

Monet directly tackles these limitations by proposing a framework that allows MLLMs to generate continuous latent embeddings as "intermediate visual thoughts." This approach is designed to eliminate the need for explicit auxiliary images during inference and overcome the rigidities of external tools, thereby achieving a more flexible, human-like abstract visual thinking that directly operates within an internal perceptual space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Monet is to enable MLLMs to perform *human-like abstract visual thinking* by reasoning directly within a *continuous latent visual space*, moving beyond the constraints of external tools and explicit images or language alone.

This overarching goal is motivated by several critical observations and challenges in existing multimodal reasoning paradigms:

1. **Limitations of External Tools in Visual CoT:** Current "think with images" methods, while effective, are inherently constrained by the specific visual tools they employ (e.g., bounding box prediction, depth estimation). This limits their generalization to novel, complex visual reasoning tasks (like visual math or abstract spatial reasoning) and increases training complexity due to the need for extensive supervision for tool calls.
2. **Lack of Flexible, Internal Visual Reasoning:** Humans engage in abstract visual thinking that doesn't always rely on explicit external manipulations. Current MLLMs lack this internal, flexible perceptual space for reasoning. Monet aims to emulate this by allowing models to generate and manipulate continuous "visual thoughts."
3. **Computational and Supervision Challenges in Latent Visual Reasoning:** Early explorations into latent visual reasoning for MLLMs face significant hurdles:
 - **High Computational Cost:** Aligning generated latent embeddings with auxiliary image embeddings can be computationally expensive due to the large number of image tokens.
 - **Loss of Visual Detail:** Compressing image embeddings to manage computational cost can lead to the distortion or loss of crucial visual information.
 - **Weak Supervision for Latent Embeddings:** Existing training objectives (like next-token prediction during SFT) provide indirect and often insufficient supervision for the latent embeddings themselves.
 - **Incompatibility of Standard RL with Latent Space:** Reinforcement learning algorithms like GRPO are designed for discrete text tokens and cannot directly optimize continuous latent embeddings, leaving a critical reasoning component largely untrained.

Monet is explicitly designed to address these challenges by introducing a training framework that enables robust, scalable, and effectively supervised learning of latent visual reasoning, culminating in a model capable of abstract visual thinking beyond traditional image and language modalities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Monet proposes a novel training framework for MLLMs built upon a three-stage supervised fine-tuning (SFT) pipeline and a tailored reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm called Visual-latent Policy Optimization (VLPO). The base MLLM used is Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

#### 3.1. Inference Strategy

During inference, Monet allows the MLLM to autoregressively decide when to initiate latent reasoning by generating a special `<latent>` token. After generating a predefined number `K` of continuous latent embeddings, a `</latent>` token signals a return to language-based reasoning. The generated latent embeddings from the last decoder layer are fed back as the next input, forming an internal, continuous thought process.

#### 3.2. Monet-SFT-125K Dataset Construction

A high-quality dataset is crucial for training latent reasoning. Monet-SFT-125K is a text-image interleaved CoT dataset (125K samples) constructed through a three-stage pipeline to overcome limitations of existing datasets (unnecessary auxiliary images, inaccuracy, lack of fine-grained supervision):
1. **Filtering Unsolvable Samples (Necessity):** Raw CoT data from various sources (ReFocus, CogCoM, Zebra-CoT, Visual-CoT) are filtered. Only samples that Qwen2.5-VL-7B incorrectly answers *without* auxiliary images are retained, ensuring the necessity of intermediate visual steps.
2. **Ensuring Validity of Auxiliary Images (Correctness):** From the hard samples, only those that a stronger MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) *correctly solves* using *only* the auxiliary images are kept. This ensures the accuracy and utility of the intermediate visual information.
3. **Expanding Supervision (Fine-grained):** Advanced LLMs (Deepseek-V3.1 and Gemini 2.5 Pro) are used to identify and mark specific text tokens within the CoT that correspond to "crucial visual observations." These `<observation>...</observation>` tokens provide fine-grained supervision for learning latent embeddings.

The dataset includes a variety of problem domains (real-world, chart, OCR, geometry) and visual operations (cropping, grounding, highlighting, drawing lines, creating new visual states).

#### 3.3. Three-Stage Supervised Fine-tuning (Monet-SFT)

The SFT stage equips the model with the fundamental ability to generate and reason with latent embeddings, addressing the challenges of computational cost and insufficient supervision.

- **SFT Stage 1: Warm-up:** The base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is fine-tuned on the Monet-SFT-125K dataset using standard next-token prediction (NTP) loss. This adapts the model to the image–text interleaved reasoning pattern, ensuring it effectively leverages intermediate images and that "observation tokens" encode meaningful visual information.
- **SFT Stage 2: Obtain High-Quality Target Latent Embeddings:** This stage trains the model to generate latent embeddings that capture useful visual features. It involves a student model (initialized from M_warm-up) learning from a fixed teacher model (also M_warm-up), both processing the same CoTs.
 - **Controlled Attention Flow ("Auxiliary image → latent → observation"):** For the student, auxiliary image embeddings are inserted before latent embedding segments. A modified attention mask allows only latent embeddings to attend to these auxiliary image embeddings, enforcing a structured flow of visual information and ensuring latent embeddings directly access visual features without information loss to text.
 - **Alignment on Key Observation Tokens:** Inspired by text-based latent reasoning, the hidden representations of the crucial `<observation>` tokens in the *student's* CoT (conditioned on generated latent embeddings) are aligned with those from the *teacher's* CoT (conditioned on ground-truth auxiliary images). This is achieved through a cosine similarity-based alignment loss ($L_{align-obs}$). Crucially, gradients from this loss are restricted to flow *solely through the generated latent embeddings* to the model parameters (latent-only backpropagation), preventing the model from exploiting shortcuts.
 - **Next-Token Prediction (NTP) Loss:** A standard NTP loss is applied to text tokens to ensure the latent embeddings benefit subsequent reasoning.
- **SFT Stage 3: Learn to Generate Latent Embeddings without Auxiliary Images:** The model (re-initialized with M_warm-up) is trained to generate latent embeddings *without access to auxiliary images* in its CoT. These generated latent embeddings are aligned (using $L_{align-latent}$, a cosine similarity loss across all layers) with the *target latent embeddings* ($h^{*}_{latent}$) previously generated by the M_stage2 model from Stage 2. A standard NTP loss is also applied to text tokens. This stage bridges the gap between training with auxiliary images and inference without them.

#### 3.4. VLPO: Visual-latent Policy Optimization

Monet introduces Visual-latent Policy Optimization (VLPO) to address GRPO's inability to optimize continuous latent embeddings.
- **Limitation of GRPO:** GRPO computes policy gradients only for discrete text tokens, leaving the continuous latent reasoning component largely unoptimized during RL.
- **VLPO Mechanism:** VLPO estimates the output probability of continuous latent embeddings collected during rollout. It models a generated latent embedding ($h^{old}_{i,t}$) as a sample from a Gaussian distribution whose mean is the latent embedding generated by the current policy model ($\pi_\theta$) given the same context ($h^{\theta}_{i,t}$). This allows computing a "ratio" term ($r_{i,t}(\theta)$) for latent steps, similar to how it's done for text tokens, which is then incorporated into the policy gradient updates. Maximizing this ratio for latent embeddings effectively minimizes the distance between the policy's generated latent embedding and the "good-action" latent embedding that led to a positive outcome reward.
- **Reward Design:** VLPO uses a simple accuracy reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) and a format reward (for correctly boxed answers). Crucially, latent reasoning itself is *not* directly rewarded, preventing indiscriminate invocation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Monet demonstrates substantial improvements across various benchmarks, showcasing its effectiveness in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.

#### 4.1. Performance on Real-World Perception and Reasoning Benchmarks

- Monet-7B (Monet-SFT + VLPO) consistently outperforms the base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), vanilla SFT, and SFT + GRPO across benchmarks like V*, HRBench4K, HRBench8K, and MME-RealWorld-Lite.
- It achieves notable improvements, for example, boosting Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 6.81% on V*, 9.75% on MME-RealWorld-Lite (Overall), and significantly improving reasoning and perception scores.
- Monet-7B also surpasses other state-of-the-art open-source methods like Deepeyes (a cropping-based method) and LVR (a prior latent visual reasoning approach) on most tasks.

#### 4.2. Out-of-Distribution Generalization

- On the challenging VisualPuzzles benchmark, which demands abstract visual reasoning beyond domain-specific knowledge, Monet-7B achieves the best performance among open-source models, demonstrating strong OOD generalization capabilities.
- Compared to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Monet-7B shows a 2.31% overall improvement, with significant gains in algorithmic (6.09%) and analogical (8.78%) reasoning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Component Analysis

Ablation studies reveal the critical role of Monet's novel components:
- **Dual Supervision (SFT Stage 2):** Removing either the representation-alignment loss on observation tokens ("w/o obs token align") or the direct attention from latent embeddings to auxiliary image embeddings ("w/o auxiliary img") leads to substantial performance degradation. This confirms the necessity of dual supervision signals for effectively learning latent visual features.
- **Latent-Only Backpropagation:** Disabling latent-only backpropagation for the alignment loss ("w/o latent-only BP") results in a sharp performance drop across all tasks. This indicates that forcing gradients to flow solely through latent embeddings is crucial for the model to genuinely improve latent representations rather than finding shortcut optimizations.
- **VLPO vs. GRPO:**
 - GRPO does not consistently improve Monet-SFT, and in some cases, even degrades performance compared to Monet-SFT alone. This supports the hypothesis that GRPO is ill-suited for optimizing continuous latent embeddings.
 - VLPO, however, significantly enhances the performance of Monet-SFT, confirming its effectiveness in directly optimizing latent reasoning using reward signals.
- **Effect of Latent Embedding Number (K):**
 - For in-distribution tasks, models perform worst when no latent embeddings are used (K_test=0), indicating that abstract visual embeddings are indeed beneficial. Monet-SFT allows for performance scaling with K_test larger than K_train.
 - For OOD tasks (VisualPuzzles), only VLPO-enhanced models consistently benefit from latent reasoning (K_test > 0 better than K_test = 0), suggesting SFT alone is insufficient for strong OOD latent generalization.
 - VLPO also improves robustness to the choice of K_test, leading to more stable performance.
 - GRPO primarily strengthens *non-latent* reasoning (K_test=0 performance similar or better than SFT alone) but provides minimal benefits for *latent* reasoning (underperforms SFT for K_test > 0), further validating VLPO's necessity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Monet represents a significant step towards more sophisticated and human-like multimodal reasoning in MLLMs, with substantial potential impact in several areas:

1. **Advancing Abstract Visual Reasoning:** By enabling MLLMs to "think" in a continuous latent visual space, Monet moves beyond the limitations of explicit visual tools or textual descriptions. This fosters a more flexible and abstract form of visual reasoning, akin to human internal thought processes, which could unlock capabilities for tasks requiring nuanced interpretation, conceptual understanding, and generalization to unseen scenarios.
2. **Increased Flexibility and Generalization:** The framework's ability to operate without explicit tool calls or auxiliary images during inference makes it more adaptable to diverse and complex visual reasoning problems. Its strong out-of-distribution generalization on abstract visual puzzles is a testament to this enhanced flexibility, suggesting broader applicability in real-world scenarios where predefined tools might not exist.
3. **Novel Training Paradigms for MLLMs:** Monet introduces innovative solutions to critical challenges in training MLLMs for latent reasoning. The three-stage SFT pipeline with dual supervision and latent-only backpropagation, combined with the VLPO algorithm, provides a robust and effective methodology for teaching models to generate and leverage internal visual thoughts. These techniques could inspire future research in self-supervised or weakly supervised multimodal learning.
4. **Contribution of a High-Quality Dataset:** The Monet-SFT-125K dataset, meticulously curated to ensure the necessity and correctness of auxiliary images and featuring fine-grained supervision, is a valuable resource for the research community. It will facilitate further development and benchmarking of latent visual reasoning models, addressing a critical data bottleneck.
5. **Potential for More Autonomous AI Agents:** The ability to perform internal visual reasoning could contribute to the development of more autonomous and intelligent AI agents that can analyze complex visual information, plan actions, and solve problems without constant external feedback or pre-programmed tools.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge two primary limitations. First, the multi-stage SFT pipeline, while effective, adds to the overall training complexity and overhead. Simplifying this process or integrating stages more seamlessly could be an avenue for future work. Second, the impact of different reward designs on latent visual reasoning in MLLMs remains unexplored. Investigating more sophisticated reward functions could further enhance the capabilities of VLPO and the overall model performance. Despite these limitations, Monet establishes a promising direction for developing MLLMs capable of truly abstract and flexible visual thinking.
