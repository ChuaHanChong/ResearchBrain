# ActiveMimic: Egocentric Video Pretraining with Active Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> ActiveMimic introduces a two-stage pretraining framework that leverages active perception from in-the-wild egocentric human videos to enhance robot manipulation capabilities. The approach enables robots to learn synchronized viewpoint and manipulation actions, leading to improved performance on real-world tasks such as Restocking (90.1%), Reaching (88.9%), Finding (91.7%), and Pouring (93.3%), often surpassing state-of-the-art models pretrained on robot-specific data.

## Problem

- Robot foundation models pretrained on readily available egocentric human videos often show lower performance compared to those trained on costly robot-specific data.
- Existing methods for pretraining from human videos neglect 'active perception' (intentional viewpoint adjustments), treating camera motion as noise rather than a meaningful action.
- Decoupling and modeling synchronized camera and wrist motions from single-camera, in-the-wild egocentric videos is challenging without specialized motion capture hardware.

## Method

- **ActiveMimic** recovers synchronized egocentric camera and bimanual wrist trajectories from raw in-the-wild human videos using only off-the-shelf vision models, resolving camera-hand coupling by re-expressing poses in a chunk-relative frame.
- These motions are encoded into a unified 27-dimensional action vector, representing coupled camera translation/rotation and left/right wrist translation/rotation.
- A ==mix-of-transformers== architecture is trained in two stages: first on the human egocentric video dataset with the 27D action supervision, then fine-tuned on robot-specific data to transfer learned capabilities.

## Results

- ActiveMimic achieved competitive success rates across four real-world tasks, notably **90.1%** on Restocking and **91.7%** on Finding, outperforming state-of-the-art robot-data-pretrained baselines on tasks requiring significant active perception.
- The vision-based action recovery demonstrated high fidelity on the HOT3D dataset, with head pose recovery reaching **78.82%** and wrist recovery exceeding **60%** under strict error tolerances.
- The framework showed enhanced robustness, achieving **79.0%** success under flashing lighting and **72.2%** with unseen objects, conditions under which other human-video-pretrained baselines failed or had substantially reduced performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling active perception from egocentric human videos, which involves learning coordinated viewpoint and manipulation actions, is critical for enhancing robot task performance and generalization.
> - A purely vision-based approach can effectively and accurately derive high-fidelity 27D action labels (head and bimanual wrist poses) from in-the-wild egocentric videos, eliminating the need for specialized capture hardware.
> - The active perception capability is primarily acquired during the egocentric human video pretraining stage, manifesting through the robot's head camera and facilitating robust human-to-robot representational transfer.
