# RL-finetuning LLMs from on- and off-policy data with a single algorithm

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Meta GenAI and Meta FAIR present the Any-Generation Reward Optimization (AGRO) algorithm, a unified reinforcement learning approach for fine-tuning large language models using both on-policy and off-policy data. AGRO achieves a +7% performance increase on the MATH dataset with Llama 3 8B, demonstrating superior data efficiency and improved robustness in offline learning compared to traditional methods.

## Problem

- Existing RLHF algorithms for LLMs are largely compartmentalized into either computationally intensive online methods or static, often sub-optimal, offline methods.
- This dichotomy prevents the seamless integration of diverse data sources, such as iteratively collected online feedback and large, pre-collected offline datasets.
- Naive adaptations of on-policy KL-regularized policy gradient algorithms to off-policy settings do not reliably converge to the optimal target policy.

## Method

- AGRO introduces the theoretical concept of =="generation consistency"==, stating that at optimality, the KL-regularized reward is invariant across any generation for a given prompt.
- The algorithm derives ==unified loss functions== for both on-policy and off-policy settings, which minimize the ==variance of the regularized reward== to converge to the optimal policy.
- AGRO employs ==sample-based gradient estimates==, and for scenarios with multiple generations per prompt (n>1), it directly estimates variance, eliminating the need for an auxiliary value function.

## Results

- On-policy AGRO achieved a **+7%** performance improvement on the challenging MATH dataset compared to the supervised fine-tuned Llama 3 8B reference model.
- In on-policy settings, AGRO demonstrated superior data efficiency, making faster progress in training reward for the same number of learning iterations than standard regularized policy gradient methods.
- Off-policy AGRO consistently showed better overall performance and stability than adapted KL-regularized policy gradients in extreme offline learning scenarios, confirming its theoretical advantages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The core insight is that the optimal policy in a regularized RLHF problem ensures the quantity `r(x, y) - beta log(pi(y|x)/pi_ref(y|x))` is constant across all generated responses `y` for a given prompt `x`.
> - By minimizing the variance of this generation-consistent regularized reward, AGRO provides a single, theoretically grounded framework capable of learning from any mix of on-policy and off-policy data.
> - On-policy AGRO leverages both pathwise-derivative and likelihood ratio gradient estimates, leading to more efficient learning dynamics and faster training progress.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has revolutionized many areas of artificial intelligence, offering unprecedented capabilities in understanding and generating human-like text. However, these models often require extensive fine-tuning to align with human preferences and perform specific tasks effectively. While supervised fine-tuning (SFT) has been a prevalent paradigm, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has emerged as an increasingly crucial and powerful method for aligning LLMs with complex human values and instructions. RLHF has been shown to not only refine existing capabilities but also to instill entirely new behaviors and skills in LLMs, transcending the limitations of pre-training and supervised methods.

Within RLHF, the existing algorithms can be broadly categorized into two main groups: online and offline methods. **Online RL algorithms** (e.g., PPO-based methods like those by Christiano et al., Ouyang et al.) involve an iterative process of sampling responses from the current policy, evaluating them with a reward model (often trained from human preferences), and then updating the policy. These methods typically offer high performance but are computationally intensive due to the continuous sampling and reward model interactions. **Offline RL algorithms** (e.g., DPO, IPO, SLiC) aim to learn from a static, pre-collected dataset of (prompt, response, reward/preference) tuples without further interaction with the environment or a reward model. This makes them significantly cheaper and more efficient in terms of data collection but often leads to a performance ceiling lower than that of online methods.

A significant challenge in the current landscape is the inherent dichotomy between these two classes of algorithms. Techniques optimized for online settings often fail to perform optimally or even converge when applied naively to offline data, and vice-versa. This divergence prevents the seamless integration of diverse data sources—such as continuously generated online feedback and large, static datasets of human preferences or demonstrations. Researchers have observed that directly adapting on-policy KL-regularized policy gradient algorithms to an off-policy setting does not yield the optimal target policy, highlighting a fundamental theoretical gap.

The proposed AGRO algorithm directly addresses this gap by seeking a unified framework that can effectively leverage both on-policy (iteratively generated) and off-policy (statically available) data with a single, theoretically grounded approach. By introducing the concept of "generation consistency," AGRO aims to reconcile the learning dynamics across these different data paradigms, providing a more versatile and efficient solution for LLM fine-tuning. This work positions itself as a foundational advancement, striving to overcome a key limitation in current RLHF methodologies and potentially accelerate the alignment of LLMs with human goals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, **Any-Generation Reward Optimization (AGRO)**, designed for fine-tuning large language models. The overarching goal is to develop a single, unified algorithm that can seamlessly and effectively leverage both **on-policy** and **off-policy** data, thereby bridging the current performance and applicability gap between these two distinct categories of RLHF methods.

The motivation behind AGRO stems from several critical observations and challenges in the LLM fine-tuning landscape:

- **The Online/Offline Dichotomy:** Existing RLHF algorithms for LLMs are largely compartmentalized into either online (requiring iterative interaction and a reward model) or offline (learning from static datasets) categories. This creates a fragmentation where insights and techniques from one often do not directly transfer or perform optimally in the other.
- **Sub-optimality of Naive Adaptations:** The paper highlights that a naive adaptation of prominent on-policy algorithms, such as the KL-regularized policy gradient, to off-policy data does not reliably converge to the optimal policy (as illustrated in Figure 1). This demonstrates a fundamental theoretical issue that necessitates a new approach rather than mere algorithmic tweaks.
- **Demand for Data Versatility:** In real-world LLM deployment, data sources are often diverse. Continuous human feedback (online) might be collected alongside large, curated datasets (offline). An algorithm that can efficiently utilize all available data, regardless of its generation policy, would be highly valuable.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** The authors aim for an algorithm with strong theoretical foundations, ensuring convergence to the optimal policy under various data conditions. This is crucial for reliability and understanding the learning process.
- **Simplified Implementation and Practicality:** By providing a unified approach, AGRO seeks to simplify the algorithmic complexity for practitioners who currently might need to choose between or combine different specialized algorithms for different data types. Avoiding the need to learn an auxiliary value function in multi-sample off-policy settings also contributes to practical simplicity.

The core motivation is rooted in a novel theoretical insight: the concept of **generation consistency**. The authors posit that the optimal policy in a regularized RLHF problem inherently satisfies a specific notion of consistency across any possible generation. This insight is the cornerstone from which AGRO's loss functions and algorithmic updates are derived, promising a theoretically sound and practically effective solution to the online-offline dilemma in LLM fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of AGRO is built upon a fundamental theoretical insight: **generation consistency** at optimality for regularized reinforcement learning problems.

**Reinforcement Learning for Language Models:**
The problem is framed as a regularized policy optimization. An LLM acts as a policy $\pi$ that generates a response $y$ for a given prompt $x$. This response is evaluated by a reward function $r(x, y)$. The objective is to maximize a regularized objective function $G(\pi)$:
$G(\pi) = E_{x \sim \rho} [E_{y \sim \pi(\cdot|x)} [r(x, y)] - \beta KL(\pi(\cdot|x), \pi_{ref}(\cdot|x))]$
Here, $\rho$ is the prompt distribution, $\beta \ge 0$ is a regularization coefficient, and $\pi_{ref}$ is a reference policy (often a supervised fine-tuned model) that prevents the optimized policy from deviating too much.

**Generation Consistency at Optimality (Theorem 1):**
The core theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which states that for the optimal policy $\pi^*$, the quantity $r(x, y) - \beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)}$ does not depend on the specific generation $y$; it is solely a function of the prompt $x$. This quantity is equivalent to the KL-regularized value function, denoted as $\tilde{V}^{\pi^*}(x)$. This means that at optimality, the "regularized reward" for any generated sequence $y$ from a given prompt $x$ is constant.

**Deriving Loss Functions from Generation Consistency (Theorem 2):**
Based on this consistency, if the optimal policy is achieved, the regularized reward $R^\pi_\beta(x, y) = r(x, y) - \beta \log \frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)}$ should have zero variance across different generations $y$ for a fixed $x$. This leads to the formulation of two loss functions that aim to minimize this variance:
1. **On-policy Loss ($L(\pi)$):** Minimizes $V_{y \sim \pi(\cdot|x)} [R^\pi_\beta(x, y)]$. Here, the generations $y$ are sampled from the current policy $\pi$.
2. **Off-policy Loss ($L_\mu(\pi)$):** Minimizes $V_{y \sim \mu(\cdot|x)} [R^\pi_\beta(x, y)]$. Here, generations $y$ are sampled from a fixed behavior policy $\mu$ (e.g., from an offline dataset).
Theorem 2 formally proves that for both losses, the unique global minimizer is the optimal policy $\pi^*$.

**Gradient of the Consistency Losses:**
The paper then derives the gradients for these loss functions, essential for policy optimization.
- **Off-policy Gradient (Lemma 3):** The gradient $\nabla L_\mu(\pi)$ is derived, revealing a structure resembling a policy gradient with an intrinsic baseline term $\tilde{R}^\pi_\beta(x, \mu) = E_{y' \sim \mu(\cdot|x)} [R^\pi_\beta(x, y')]$. Notably, when the sampling policy $\mu$ is the current policy $\pi$, this gradient becomes equivalent to the standard RLHF gradient for the original objective $G(\pi)$.
- **On-policy Gradient:** The on-policy gradient $\nabla L(\pi)$ has two components: a pathwise-derivative (PD) estimate (which is effectively the off-policy gradient with $\mu = \pi$) and a likelihood ratio (LR) estimate. The full on-policy AGRO gradient combines these two terms. Proposition 4 proves that this combined gradient is an unbiased estimate of $\nabla L(\pi)$.

**Any-Generation Reward Optimization (AGRO) Algorithm:**
AGRO uses sample-based gradient estimates to optimize the policy.
- **Off-policy AGRO:**
 - **Single Generation ($n=1$):** When only one response per prompt is available, an auxiliary prompt-dependent value function $V(x)$ must be learned alongside $\pi$, minimizing a squared error loss (similar to approaches like Richemond et al.).
 - **Several Generations ($n > 1$):** This is the focus of AGRO. For $n$ responses $\{y_i\}_{i=1}^n$ per prompt, the algorithm uses an empirical average to estimate the variance, employing a leave-one-out baseline to reduce variance in the gradient estimate. For $n=2$, this simplifies to an elegant form reminiscent of contrastive policy gradient or IPO algorithms. Crucially, experiments demonstrate that off-policy AGRO converges to $\pi^*$ where naive KL-regularized policy gradient fails (Figure 1).
- **On-policy AGRO:** This variant utilizes both the PD and LR gradient estimates. The full on-policy AGRO gradient is presented with its specific formula (Eq. 6), which is an unbiased estimate. The paper notes that while both on-policy AGRO and standard RLHF gradients lead to the same optimum, their learning dynamics differ, and on-policy AGRO includes an extra term focusing on variance reduction of $R^\pi_\beta$.

**Implementation at the Token Level:**
For LLM fine-tuning, sequence-level generations are decomposed into token-level operations. The paper discusses how to compute gradients related to the regularization term by expanding $\log \pi(y)$ into its auto-regressive product form. It introduces three variants ($\hat{g}_1, \hat{g}_2, \hat{g}_3$) for estimating the regularization gradient, with $\hat{g}_2$ and $\hat{g}_3$ offering successively better variance reduction by eliminating zero-mean terms and computing conditional expectations, respectively. These variance reduction techniques are crucial for practical efficiency, although the authors primarily use the vanilla estimate in experiments.

**Connections to Related Work:**
The paper also discusses AGRO's connection to pairwise preference optimization (showing how DPO arises from generation consistency combined with Bradley-Terry models) and regularized value learning (interpreting $\log \frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)}$ as a Q-function).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of AGRO focuses on the **MATH dataset** (Hendrycks et al., 2021), a challenging benchmark for mathematical reasoning. The task involves solving mathematical questions, with a reward of 1 for a correct answer and 0 otherwise, automatically judged using Sympy. Experiments are conducted using the **Llama 3 8B model**, with a system prompt encouraging step-by-step reasoning. All algorithmic variants use a batch size of 64 prompts and generate 4 responses per prompt ($n=4$), with a temperature of 1.0.

#### 4.1. On-policy Learning Performance

- **Comparison of Algorithms:** The paper compares three on-policy variants:
 1. Standard regularized policy gradient (RLOO, using a leave-one-out baseline).
 2. Off-policy AGRO algorithm with on-policy sampling (i.e., $\mu = \pi$).
 3. Full on-policy AGRO algorithm.
- **Observations (Figure 2 - Training Performance):**
 - As theoretically expected, off-policy AGRO with on-policy sampling exhibits performance statistically identical to the RLOO baseline. This validates AGRO's theoretical alignment in the on-policy setting.
 - **On-policy AGRO demonstrates superior data efficiency**, achieving faster progress in training reward for the same number of learning iterations (epochs). This suggests that the additional likelihood ratio term in the on-policy AGRO gradient contributes to more effective learning dynamics.
- **Observations (Figure 3 - Evaluation Performance on Test Set):**
 - The test set performance closely correlates with training rewards.
 - **On-policy AGRO consistently achieves the best evaluation performance**, yielding a notable **+7% improvement** over the $\pi_{ref}$ (supervised fine-tuned) model. In contrast, RLOO and off-policy AGRO (with on-policy sampling) achieve about a +4% lift. This indicates that on-policy AGRO generalizes better and reaches a higher performance ceiling.

#### 4.2. Off-policy and Offline Learning Performance

- **Experimental Setup:** This scenario emulates an extreme off-policy setting where all samples are generated by the fixed reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ at the start of training.
- **Comparison:** Off-policy AGRO is compared against a KL-regularized policy gradient adapted for offline learning (using per-token KL regularization). Both use $\beta=0.01$.
- **Observations (Figure 5 - Evaluation Performance):**
 - Without regularization ($\beta=0$), the performance of an unregularized policy gradient crashes during off-policy training, highlighting the instability of purely off-policy learning.
 - **Off-policy AGRO consistently obtains better overall performance** than the adapted KL-regularized policy gradient, demonstrating both a superior peak performance and more stable learning over a fixed number of epochs. This empirically confirms AGRO's theoretical advantage in off-policy settings, where naive regularization strategies may be insufficient.

#### 4.3. Ablation and Comparison

- **KL-efficiency Comparison (Figure 4):** A synthetic off-policy setting using a replay buffer (replaying off-policy samples with probability $p=0.5$) reveals:
 - Off-policy learning (both AGRO and regularized policy gradient in this mixed setting) can be **as KL-efficient as on-policy learning**, meaning they achieve a similar reward gain for a given KL divergence from $\pi_{ref}$.
 - However, on-policy algorithms tend to drive up the KL divergence faster within the same number of updates, leading to quicker progress.
- **Ablation on Regularization Coefficient $\beta$ (Figure 6):**
 - As $\beta$ increases (stronger regularization), all algorithms become **more KL-efficient**. This means they achieve a given reward increase with less deviation from the reference policy.
 - Conversely, stronger regularization slows down policy optimization progress, resulting in slower performance improvement for a fixed number of learning steps.
 - Unregularized optimization ($\beta=0$) is generally KL-inefficient and, critically, causes **severe instability** during off-policy learning.
 - The paper suggests that for sequence-level updates, an effective $\beta$ scales as $O(1/T)$ (where $T$ is sequence length), while for token-level updates, it would be $O(1)$.

In summary, AGRO, particularly its on-policy variant, demonstrates superior data efficiency and achieves higher performance in fine-tuning LLMs for mathematical reasoning. Crucially, off-policy AGRO offers a more robust and effective solution for learning from static datasets compared to adapted traditional methods, validating its core theoretical premise of generation consistency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of the Any-Generation Reward Optimization (AGRO) algorithm represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model (LLM) fine-tuning, particularly within the domain of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized across theoretical, algorithmic, and practical dimensions.

**Theoretical Contributions:**
1. **Generation Consistency as an Optimality Condition:** The paper's most profound theoretical contribution is the identification and formalization of "generation consistency." This elegant property, stating that the regularized reward is invariant across generations from the optimal policy given a prompt, provides a novel and robust optimality condition for KL-regularized RL.
2. **Unified Theoretical Framework:** Generation consistency serves as a unifying principle, allowing the derivation of loss functions and gradient estimates that inherently cater to both on-policy and off-policy data regimes within a single theoretical framework. This addresses a long-standing challenge in RLHF, where algorithms were often specialized for one setting or the other.
3. **Theoretical Guarantees:** AGRO comes with strong theoretical guarantees, proving that the optimal policy $\pi^*$ is the unique global minimizer of its on-policy and off-policy loss functions. This provides a solid foundation for its convergence properties and reliability.

**Algorithmic Innovation:**
1. **Single Algorithm for Diverse Data:** AGRO's ability to operate effectively with both on-policy and off-policy data using a unified methodology is a major algorithmic breakthrough. This simplifies the development and deployment of RLHF systems, as practitioners no longer need to switch between or heuristically combine different algorithms.
2. **Avoidance of Auxiliary Value Functions (for $n>1$):** For scenarios with multiple generations per prompt ($n>1$), AGRO simplifies implementation by directly estimating the variance of the regularized reward, thereby eliminating the need to learn a separate auxiliary value function $V(x)$ common in some offline RL algorithms.
3. **Variance Reduction Techniques:** The explicit discussion and derivation of token-level variance reduction techniques further enhance AGRO's practical efficiency for LLM fine-tuning, which involves long sequences.

**Practical Benefits and Potential Impact:**
1. **Improved Performance and Data Efficiency:** Experiments on the MATH dataset demonstrate that on-policy AGRO achieves higher performance and is more data-efficient compared to traditional regularized policy gradient methods. This translates to faster training and potentially better-performing LLMs with less computational resources.
2. **Robustness in Off-policy Settings:** Off-policy AGRO shows superior stability and performance compared to adapted baseline algorithms in extreme offline learning scenarios. This opens up possibilities for effectively leveraging vast, static datasets of human feedback or demonstrations, which are cheaper to acquire than iterative online interactions.
3. **Enhanced LLM Alignment:** By providing a more versatile and robust fine-tuning mechanism, AGRO can contribute to developing LLMs that are better aligned with human preferences, values, and complex instructions across a wider range of tasks and data availability.
4. **Broader Applicability:** The unified nature of AGRO means it can be readily applied in mixed-data settings, where online feedback is continuously collected and combined with existing offline datasets. This adaptability is highly valuable in dynamic research and deployment environments.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** AGRO's theoretical framework and empirical success lay a strong foundation for future research in RLHF, inviting further exploration into its properties, extensions (e.g., dense rewards, importance sampling for extreme off-policy instability), and applications to other domains beyond mathematical reasoning.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge that off-policy learning, even with AGRO, can still exhibit instability under extreme off-policy data generation. This suggests future work could investigate complementary techniques such as importance sampling to further enhance robustness. Additionally, while variance reduction techniques for token-level implementation are discussed, their practical impact on large-scale training is left for future ablation by practitioners, indicating a potential area for further optimization.

In conclusion, AGRO provides a theoretically elegant and practically effective solution to a critical problem in LLM fine-tuning. By unifying on-policy and off-policy learning, it promises to make RLHF more efficient, robust, and accessible, ultimately accelerating the development of more capable and aligned artificial intelligence systems.
