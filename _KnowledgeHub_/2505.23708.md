# AMOR: Adaptive Character Control through Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> AMOR, developed by Disney Research, presents a multi-objective reinforcement learning framework that enables adaptive character control by training a single policy conditioned on reward weights. This approach allows for on-the-fly, post-training adjustment of character behavior and significantly facilitates sim-to-real transfer, exemplified by a bipedal robot executing a complex double pirouette.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning for physics-based character control often requires tedious, trial-and-error tuning of reward function weights due to conflicting objectives.
- Changes to reward weights necessitate full retraining of the RL policy, which is computationally expensive and time-consuming.
- Fixed reward weights limit a policy's versatility and present a significant challenge for sim-to-real transfer in robotics, as real-world deployment often demands different weight adjustments.

## Method

- **AMOR** trains a single policy that is explicitly conditioned on a vector of reward weights, allowing it to learn diverse behaviors by optimizing a vector-valued reward function.
- It extends ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== to Multi-Objective PPO (MOPPO), sampling different reward weight vectors during training to cover the entire space of possible objective prioritizations.
- A hierarchical framework is introduced where a high-level policy, trained with standard PPO, dynamically selects and adjusts the reward weights for a pre-trained low-level AMOR policy, often using implicit rewards from a discriminator.

## Results

- AMOR successfully approximates the Pareto front for various motions, quantitatively demonstrating the trade-offs between conflicting objectives and validating the need for adaptive weights.
- Post-training manual tuning of reward weights demonstrably alters character behavior and significantly reduces the sim-to-real gap, enabling a bipedal robot to perform a challenging double pirouette previously unachievable with fixed-weight controllers.
- A high-level policy (HLP) trained on an implicit reward dynamically adjusts weights in real-time, resulting in improved motion tracking performance (e.g., lower MAE in joint positions of **9.55** vs. **10.02** degrees) and providing interpretability of the implicit reward's preferences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a single policy conditioned on reward weights allows for on-the-fly behavior adaptation without requiring computationally expensive retraining.
> - Optimal reward weight trade-offs vary for different motions and tasks, necessitating adaptive, rather than fixed, reward configurations.
> - A hierarchical approach can dynamically adjust low-level reward weights, enabling the optimization of high-level or implicit objectives while providing interpretability.
