# AMOR: Adaptive Character Control through Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> AMOR, developed by Disney Research, presents a multi-objective reinforcement learning framework that enables adaptive character control by training a single policy conditioned on reward weights. This approach allows for on-the-fly, post-training adjustment of character behavior and significantly facilitates sim-to-real transfer, exemplified by a bipedal robot executing a complex double pirouette.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning for physics-based character control often requires tedious, trial-and-error tuning of reward function weights due to conflicting objectives.
- Changes to reward weights necessitate full retraining of the RL policy, which is computationally expensive and time-consuming.
- Fixed reward weights limit a policy's versatility and present a significant challenge for sim-to-real transfer in robotics, as real-world deployment often demands different weight adjustments.

## Method

- **AMOR** trains a single policy that is explicitly conditioned on a vector of reward weights, allowing it to learn diverse behaviors by optimizing a vector-valued reward function.
- It extends ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== to Multi-Objective PPO (MOPPO), sampling different reward weight vectors during training to cover the entire space of possible objective prioritizations.
- A hierarchical framework is introduced where a high-level policy, trained with standard PPO, dynamically selects and adjusts the reward weights for a pre-trained low-level AMOR policy, often using implicit rewards from a discriminator.

## Results

- AMOR successfully approximates the Pareto front for various motions, quantitatively demonstrating the trade-offs between conflicting objectives and validating the need for adaptive weights.
- Post-training manual tuning of reward weights demonstrably alters character behavior and significantly reduces the sim-to-real gap, enabling a bipedal robot to perform a challenging double pirouette previously unachievable with fixed-weight controllers.
- A high-level policy (HLP) trained on an implicit reward dynamically adjusts weights in real-time, resulting in improved motion tracking performance (e.g., lower MAE in joint positions of **9.55** vs. **10.02** degrees) and providing interpretability of the implicit reward's preferences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a single policy conditioned on reward weights allows for on-the-fly behavior adaptation without requiring computationally expensive retraining.
> - Optimal reward weight trade-offs vary for different motions and tasks, necessitating adaptive, rather than fixed, reward configurations.
> - A hierarchical approach can dynamically adjust low-level reward weights, enabling the optimization of high-level or implicit objectives while providing interpretability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper positions itself within the evolving landscape of **physics-based character control** and **robotics**, specifically leveraging **reinforcement learning (RL)**. Traditionally, RL methods for character control rely on a weighted sum of various reward functions to guide an agent towards a desired behavior, such as tracking a kinematic reference motion.

The broader research landscape for character control has seen significant evolution:
- **Early methods** focused on optimization and carefully designed cost functions for synthesizing locomotion.
- **Learning-based methods** emerged with the availability of motion capture data, moving towards controllers that learn from human motion.
- The advent of **efficient RL algorithms** (like PPO) enabled neural network controllers to learn continuous motor policies, scaling beyond simple locomotion to more complex behaviors.

However, a persistent bottleneck in these RL-based approaches is the **tedious and time-intensive process of tuning reward weights**. Reward functions often comprise conflicting objectives (e.g., accuracy vs. energy efficiency, or different body part tracking priorities). Choosing the right balance of these weights requires extensive trial-and-error, often necessitating complete retraining of the RL policy for each adjustment. This challenge is further exacerbated in **robotics applications**, where controllers trained in simulation must perform in the real world (the "sim-to-real gap"). Real-world deployment often demands different reward weightings (e.g., higher smoothness terms) than simulation, making fixed-weight policies impractical.

Existing attempts to address these limitations include:
- **Combining expert policies:** Training multiple specialized policies.
- **Incorporating future frames or latent spaces:** Enhancing policy awareness of future goals or abstract motion patterns.
- **Learned implicit rewards (adversarial imitation learning):** Using a discriminator to replace handcrafted reward functions, automating some reward design. However, these often suffer from training instabilities (mode collapse) and lose the interpretability of explicit rewards.
- **Constrained Multi-Objective RL (MORL):** Some works have explored MORL for character control, but typically with fixed weights, pre-specified stages, or only learning a single Pareto-optimal policy, limiting flexibility.

**AMOR distinguishes itself** by directly addressing the fixed-weight limitation through **Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL)**. Unlike prior MORL applications that might learn a set of distinct policies or focus on a single Pareto-optimal solution, AMOR trains a *single policy conditioned on a set of weights*. This allows for *on-the-fly tuning of reward weights after training*, effectively spanning the Pareto front of reward trade-offs without requiring computationally expensive retraining. This approach positions AMOR as a significant step forward in making RL-based character control more flexible, efficient, and practical for real-world robotic deployment, while also offering interpretability of reward trade-offs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind AMOR stems from the significant practical challenges associated with developing and deploying reinforcement learning (RL) based controllers for physics-based characters and robots.

**The primary problem identified is the "reward tuning bottleneck":**
- **Conflicting Objectives:** Real-world character control involves balancing multiple, often conflicting, objectives (e.g., tracking accuracy, balance, smoothness, energy efficiency).
- **Trial-and-Error Process:** Finding an optimal set of weights for these conflicting reward functions is typically a tedious, trial-and-error process.
- **Computational Cost:** Standard RL methods require these weights to be set *prior* to training. Any change necessitates a full retraining, which is computationally expensive and time-consuming (e.g., "5 days" of training mentioned for AMOR).
- **Lack of Versatility:** A single fixed set of weights cannot optimally cater to multiple motions with distinct styles or dynamics, limiting a policy's versatility.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** This issue is exacerbated in robotics, where policies trained in simulation must perform in the real world. The inevitable "sim-to-real gap" demands unknown adjustments to reward weightings (e.g., increased smoothness for real hardware), which, with fixed-weight methods, requires re-training on the real robot or with new simulation parameters, both being highly impractical.

**To overcome these limitations, the paper proposes AMOR with the following key objectives:**
1. **Enable Post-Training Weight Tuning:** Develop a multi-objective reinforcement learning framework that trains a *single* policy conditioned on a set of weights. This allows users or algorithms to select and tune reward weights *after* training, significantly speeding up iteration time and workflow.
2. **Achieve Zero-Shot Adaptation:** The trained policy should be capable of adapting its behavior to desired trade-offs among conflicting objectives without any further training.
3. **Facilitate Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Demonstrate how this improved workflow can be used to transfer challenging, dynamic motions onto physical robots by fine-tuning weights to mitigate the sim-to-real gap.
4. **Explore Hierarchical Control:** Investigate how weight-conditioned policies can be leveraged in hierarchical settings, where a high-level policy dynamically selects weights according to the current task or high-level objectives, including those derived from implicit rewards (like a discriminator). This also aims to provide interpretability of what implicit rewards are "looking for."
5. **Encode Diverse Behaviors:** Ensure that the multi-objective policy encodes a diverse spectrum of behaviors, facilitating efficient adaptation to novel tasks and motion styles.

In essence, the motivation is to make the process of developing and deploying robust, adaptive, and realistic physics-based character and robot controllers more efficient and practical by decoupling the reward tuning process from the computationally expensive training process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AMOR (Adaptive character control through Multi-Objective Reinforcement learning) proposes a novel framework that fundamentally alters how reward functions are handled in RL for character control.

**Core Idea: Weight-Conditioned Policy via Multi-Objective RL (MORL)**
Instead of training a policy to optimize a fixed scalar reward, AMOR trains a single policy, $\pi(a_t | s_t, c_t, w)$, that is conditioned on a vector of reward weights ($w$). This allows the policy to learn a diverse set of behaviors, each corresponding to a different trade-off between multiple objectives. The training process effectively spans the Pareto front of reward trade-offs.

**1. Problem Formulation:**
- **Vector-valued Reward:** Instead of a single scalar reward, the agent receives a vector-valued reward, $r_t \in \mathbb{R}^m$, where each element represents a distinct objective (e.g., tracking different body parts, smoothness).
- **Context Vector ($c_t$):** The policy is also conditioned on a context vector $c_t$, which encodes task-relevant information. In AMOR, this includes the current kinematic reference motion frame ($m_t$) and a latent vector ($z_t$) representing a compressed window of past and future motion frames. This latent representation is learned using a Variation Autoencoder (VAE).
- **Policy Function:** The policy takes the current state ($s_t$), motion context ($c_t$), and the reward weight vector ($w$) as input to output actions ($a_t$).

**2. Multi-Objective Proximal Policy Optimization (MOPPO):**
- AMOR extends the standard PPO algorithm to MOPPO.
- **Vector-valued Value Function:** MOPPO learns a vector-valued value function, $V^\pi(s, c, w)$, that estimates the expected discounted return for each objective, conditioned on the reward weights.
- **Policy Gradient:** The policy update relies on a scalarized advantage function ($A^\pi \cdot w$), effectively combining the multi-objective advantages based on the sampled weights.
- **Weight Sampling:** To ensure coverage of the entire space of possible reward weights (the $(m-1)$-dimensional simplex $\Delta^m$), a different randomly sampled weight vector $w$ (drawn from a Dirichlet distribution with $\alpha=1$) is assigned to every episode during training. This forces the policy to generalize across various objective prioritizations.

**3. Multi-Objective Tracking Reward:**
AMOR defines 7 specific motion tracking objectives for the reward vector:
- $r_{up}$: Upper body joint positions and height.
- $r_{lo}$: Lower body joint positions.
- $r_{feet}$: Ankle joint positions.
- $r_{rbs}$: Rigid body poses (end-effectors' position and orientation, and linear velocities).
- $r_{root}$: Root orientation.
- $r_{vel}$: Linear and angular velocities of joints and root.
- $r_{smooth}$: Penalizes high action rates, torques, and joint accelerations to mitigate vibrations and smoothen motion.
Each reward term includes a constant survival bonus and is scaled by prior factors (different for humanoid and robot) to prevent magnitude imbalances during summation.

**4. Training Infrastructure:**
- **Characters:** Experiments conducted on a 36-DoF humanoid and a 20-DoF bipedal robot, both torque-controlled (PD controllers).
- **Dataset:** Mixture of CMU motion capture data (large, general) and a smaller, high-quality processed motion dataset (Reallusion).
- **Neural Networks:** Multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) with ELU activations for policy and value functions.
- **Simulator:** GPU-accelerated Isaac Gym, running 8192 parallel environments at 250Hz on a single RTX 4090 GPU, allowing for large-scale training (300k iterations, ~5 days).

**5. Hierarchical Weight Adjustment (HLP):**
To dynamically select weights for complex tasks, AMOR introduces a hierarchical approach:
- **Low-Level Policy:** A *pre-trained* AMOR policy (with frozen parameters) serves as the low-level controller.
- **High-Level Policy (HLP):** A separate HLP, $\pi(w_t | s_t, c_t)$, is trained using standard PPO to output reward weights ($w_t \in \Delta^m$) dynamically during execution. The HLP is significantly faster to train than AMOR itself.
- **Implicit Reward for HLP:** The HLP is trained not on the explicit reward terms, but on an *implicit reward* derived from a discriminator. This discriminator distinguishes between simulated motions produced by the AMOR policy and real kinematic reference motions from the dataset. The reward for the HLP is then $-\log(1 - D(O_t | z_t))$, encouraging transitions that appear realistic. This framework allows for optimization of high-level objectives that are not directly represented by the low-level reward terms, and provides interpretability of the discriminator's preferences.

This comprehensive methodology allows AMOR to learn a highly versatile policy that can adapt its behavior post-training through direct weight manipulation, bridging the gap between simulated learning and real-world robotic performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AMOR demonstrates its efficacy across several key aspects:

**1. Pareto Front Approximation:**
- AMOR successfully approximates the Pareto front of tracking behaviors for various motions (Idle, Walking, Dancing) on a humanoid.
- Visualizations of pairwise reward comparisons clearly show the trade-offs between conflicting objectives (e.g., smoothness vs. lower-body tracking for dancing motion).
- Crucially, different motions induce different Pareto fronts, validating the need for adaptive weights rather than fixed ones. This highlights AMOR's ability to capture these complex relationships with a single policy.

**2. Training Comparison (MOPPO vs. PPO):**
- While PPO (trained with fixed, equal weights) converges faster than MOPPO, this is expected as MOPPO faces a harder learning task by dynamically adapting to a range of varying weights across the entire simplex.
- Despite the harder task, MOPPO's performance, when evaluated with average weights, is comparable to that of PPO, indicating its strong learning capability and ability to generalize across different weight configurations.

**3. Behavior Adaptation through Manual Weight Tuning:**
- Manual adjustment of reward weights post-training demonstrably alters the character's behavior. For instance, increasing the smoothness term for a dancing humanoid visibly results in a smoother motion, albeit at the expense of precise tracking performance.
- Quantitatively, the distribution of cumulative smoothness reward shifts significantly when its corresponding weight is increased, confirming the policy's successful adaptation. This validates the core premise of AMOR: on-the-fly, zero-shot adaptation.

**4. Sim-to-Real Transfer for Robotic Characters:**
- AMOR was successfully applied to a 20-DoF bipedal robot.
- **Reducing Sim-to-Real Gap:** For a stationary dancing motion, manual tuning of weights (specifically increasing the smoothness objective) on the simulated robot significantly reduced jitter in joint positions and velocities, thereby minimizing the sim-to-real gap observed with the physical robot. This demonstrates AMOR's practical utility for real-world deployment.
- **Enabling Challenging Motions:** For a double pirouette motion, fixed-weight state-of-the-art controllers (VMP) were insufficient. By dynamically tuning reward weights on the real system, including time-varying weights (e.g., high velocity weight initially for speed, higher smoothing towards the end for a smoother transition), the robot was able to perform this physically demanding motion. This highlights the critical advantage of post-training weight adjustment, noting that such tuning took approximately 1 day of experimentation compared to the 5 days required for full RL policy training, making retraining infeasible.

**5. High-Level Policy (HLP) Effectiveness:**
- **Dynamic Weight Adjustment:** The HLP successfully learns to dynamically adjust reward weights in real-time based on the current motion context. For example, during a walking motion, the HLP balances feet and lower body coordination with smoothing. For high punches, tracking weights spike, and for spinning motions, velocity terms are prioritized. This shows that optimal reward trade-offs can vary *within* a skill.
- **Interpretability of Implicit Rewards:** By observing the weights selected by the HLP (trained on a discriminator-based implicit reward), the paper demonstrates how the HLP allows for interpreting what the discriminator prioritizes at different times, providing insights into the "latent" objectives.
- **Improved Motion Tracking Performance:** Quantitative evaluation on a simulated humanoid showed that using HLP's dynamic weight predictions resulted in significant improvements over fixed, equally balanced weights. Specifically, HLP achieved a lower mean absolute error (MAE) in joint positions (9.55 vs. 10.02 degrees) and higher discriminator logits (-15.48 vs. -18.02), indicating more realistic and accurate motion tracking. This underscores the power of the hierarchical approach in leveraging AMOR's adaptability for better overall performance.

In summary, AMOR's findings validate its core claims: it provides a powerful, flexible, and practical framework for adaptive character control, drastically streamlining the development process, improving sim-to-real transfer, and enabling more sophisticated hierarchical control strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AMOR represents a significant advancement in physics-based character control and robotics, with profound implications across several domains. Its core contribution is solving the long-standing problem of rigid and time-consuming reward tuning in reinforcement learning.

**1. Revolutionized Development Workflow:**
- **Accelerated Iteration Time:** By enabling on-the-fly adjustment of reward weights *after* the computationally intensive training phase, AMOR drastically reduces the iteration time for developing and refining RL-based controllers. This shifts the bottleneck from training to a much faster post-training tuning process, allowing for rapid experimentation and fine-tuning.
- **Enhanced Usability:** The ability to tune behavior directly without retraining makes RL-based character control more accessible and practical for artists, animators, and robot engineers who may not have deep RL expertise or massive computational resources.

**2. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:**
- **Robust Real-World Performance:** This is perhaps one of AMOR's most impactful applications. The inevitable discrepancies between simulation and reality often necessitate fine-tuning of control parameters. AMOR allows engineers to quickly adapt a policy to a physical robot's unique characteristics or unexpected behaviors by adjusting reward weights in real-time, significantly improving the robustness and reliability of sim-to-real transfer. This is crucial for deploying bipedal robots in unstructured or dynamic environments.
- **Unlocking Complex Motions:** The demonstration of the double pirouette on a real robot, which was previously unachievable with fixed-weight controllers, underscores AMOR's potential to enable robots to perform highly dynamic and complex maneuvers that require nuanced and even time-varying prioritization of objectives.

**3. Versatile and Adaptive Controllers:**
- **Single Policy, Diverse Behaviors:** AMOR allows a single trained policy to encompass a vast spectrum of behaviors and trade-offs. This eliminates the need to train multiple specialized policies for different tasks or styles, leading to more generalized and efficient controllers.
- **Adaptive to Context:** The context conditioning ensures that the policy can adapt its behavior not only to user-defined preferences but also dynamically to the current task context, making the agents more intelligent and responsive.

**4. Advancements in Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning:**
- **Dynamic Objective Prioritization:** The hierarchical extension, where a high-level policy dynamically adjusts low-level reward weights, opens new avenues for solving complex, multi-stage tasks. This allows for fine-grained control over behavior that adapts to the nuances of a task in real-time.
- **Interpretability of Implicit Rewards:** Training the HLP with an implicit, discriminator-based reward, and then observing the explicit weights it chooses, provides a novel way to interpret what complex, learned rewards prioritize. This can lead to a better understanding of optimal behaviors and even aid in designing better explicit rewards.

**Potential Future Impacts and Directions:**
- **Broader Applicability:** The core concept of adaptive weight tuning is not limited to character control and could be applied to various other RL domains where multi-objective optimization and flexible trade-offs are crucial (e.g., autonomous driving, industrial automation).
- **Robot-in-the-Loop Learning:** AMOR could significantly enhance online reinforcement learning (ORL) by serving as a robust low-level policy, potentially reducing the high failure rates and long training times typically associated with ORL on physical robots.
- **User Interface and Artistic Control:** For character animation, AMOR could empower animators with unprecedented artistic control, allowing them to sculpt character performances by intuitively adjusting high-level sliders that correspond to reward weights, without needing to understand the underlying RL complexities.

In conclusion, AMOR fundamentally addresses a core bottleneck in RL for physical characters, enabling unprecedented flexibility, efficiency, and real-world applicability. Its implications extend from enhanced artistic control in animation to the deployment of more agile and capable robotic systems.
