# UnPose: Uncertainty-Guided Diffusion Priors for Zero-Shot Pose Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> UnPose introduces a framework for zero-shot, model-free 6D object pose estimation and 3D reconstruction by estimating pixel-wise epistemic uncertainty directly from pre-trained diffusion models. It uses this uncertainty to guide the adaptive refinement of 3D Gaussian Splatting representations, demonstrating improved pose accuracy and reconstruction fidelity on standard benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing 6D pose estimation methods often depend on pre-existing CAD models or extensive category-specific training data, limiting their application to novel objects in dynamic environments.
- Model-free 3D reconstruction from limited views frequently suffers from inaccuracies or 'hallucinations' in unobserved regions of the generated geometry.
- Current model-free reconstruction methods lack quantification of epistemic uncertainty, which hinders effective integration of new sensor data and leads to suboptimal refinement.

## Method

- **UnPose** utilizes pre-trained ==multi-view diffusion models== to synthesize initial 3D priors from a single RGB-D observation of a novel object.
- It quantifies pixel-wise ==epistemic uncertainty== for these diffused views at inference time, adapting a ==Last-Layer Laplace Approximation== on the diffusion model.
- The approach employs an uncertainty-guided refinement mechanism for a ==3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)== representation and integrates pose estimation and reconstruction into an ==incremental factor graph optimization== problem for global consistency.

## Results

- UnPose achieved superior 6D pose estimation accuracy, outperforming baselines like GigaPose by **71.66%** and FoundationPose by **36.6%** on average in single-view scenarios on YCB-Video and LM-O datasets.
- The method demonstrated higher fidelity and efficiency in 3D reconstruction, achieving lower Chamfer Distance and being up to **7 times** faster than other optimization-based methods like BundleSDF.
- Successful real-world robotic manipulation tasks using a PiPER robot arm validated UnPose's ability to incrementally update object geometry and refine grasp poses based on evolving observations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Epistemic uncertainty can be extracted from pre-trained multi-view diffusion models during inference, providing valuable pixel-wise reliability maps for 3D generation.
> - Guiding 3DGS-based reconstruction and multi-view integration with these uncertainty estimates enables more robust and accurate 3D model refinement, especially under sparse observations.
> - Formulating 6D pose estimation and reconstruction as an incremental factor graph optimization, incorporating both synthetic diffusion priors and real sensor data, ensures global consistency and continuous improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The estimation of 6D object pose (three dimensions for position and three for orientation) is a foundational capability for robotic manipulation, supporting tasks such as grasping and assembly. Prior research in this domain has largely been categorized into model-based or category-level approaches.

Model-based methods typically rely on access to pre-existing, textured Computer-Aided Design (CAD) models for specific objects. While these methods have shown progress, their reliance on CAD models limits their applicability in dynamic, open-world environments where such models may be unavailable or costly to acquire. Category-level approaches aim to generalize to unseen instances within known categories but require extensive category-specific training data, which can be expensive and restrict the range of objects that can be handled. Even more recent category-agnostic methods often still require ground-truth CAD models for rendering reference views during inference, indicating a persistent dependency on object models.

To address these limitations, model-free pose estimation methods have emerged. These approaches typically forgo explicit CAD models, instead utilizing reference views or reconstructing object models from single or multi-view images or videos. However, a notable challenge within existing model-free methods is their implicit reliance on internally generated 3D representations, which can suffer from inaccuracies or "hallucinations" in unobserved regions when derived from limited input observations. Crucially, most current methods do not quantify the epistemic uncertainty associated with these reconstructed 3D models. This lack of uncertainty awareness prevents the system from appropriately weighting the reliability of prior information against newly acquired sensor data, leading to suboptimal refinement and potentially inaccurate pose estimates and 3D reconstructions.

Uncertainty estimation itself is a recognized area of research in robotics and computer vision, often distinguishing between aleatoric uncertainty (inherent noise in observations) and epistemic uncertainty (due to lack of knowledge or limited data). While methods like Deep Ensembles and Bayesian Neural Networks exist for uncertainty quantification, their application to complex, high-dimensional models like image-to-3D diffusion models presents computational challenges, particularly during inference.

This research introduces UnPose to integrate uncertainty quantification with diffusion-model-based 3D reconstruction and 6D pose estimation. It aims to overcome the limitations of model dependency and address the issue of uncertainty in reconstructed geometry, thereby enhancing the robustness and accuracy of zero-shot, model-free 6D pose estimation in novel environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of UnPose is to achieve zero-shot, model-free 6D object pose estimation and reconstruction for novel objects, eliminating the need for pre-existing CAD models or category-specific training data.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several limitations observed in the broader research landscape:
1. **Reliance on Object-Specific Models:** Traditional 6D pose estimation methods often require CAD models or extensive category-level training data, which are costly, time-consuming to obtain, and limit the applicability of robots in dynamic, open-world environments with novel objects. UnPose seeks to bypass this requirement entirely.
2. **Inaccurate 3D Reconstruction from Limited Views:** Recent model-free approaches leverage image-to-3D reconstruction, often employing powerful foundation models like diffusion models. However, when observations are limited (e.g., a single RGB-D frame), these models can generate incomplete or hallucinated geometry for unobserved regions. Existing methods typically do not address or correct these inaccuracies, treating all generated regions with equal confidence, which compromises the quality of the 3D model and subsequent pose estimation.
3. **Lack of Uncertainty Quantification:** A critical gap in current model-free reconstruction methods is their failure to quantify the epistemic uncertainty inherent in 3D models reconstructed from sparse or limited observations. Without knowing which parts of a generated 3D model are reliable and which are uncertain, systems cannot effectively integrate new sensor data, leading to suboptimal refinement and potentially incorrect pose estimates.
4. **Suboptimal Multi-View Integration:** When additional observations become available, existing methods often struggle to effectively fuse new information with prior 3D representations. Approaches that rely on alternating optimization schemes between diffusion priors and neural representations (like NeRF) may yield suboptimal reconstruction quality and lack a mechanism to weigh the reliability of different data sources.
5. **Ensuring Global Consistency:** Incremental reconstruction and pose refinement processes require a mechanism to maintain global consistency of the object's 3D representation and its pose relative to the world. Without robust optimization frameworks, drift and inconsistencies can accumulate over time.

To address these motivations, UnPose sets out specific technical objectives:
- To leverage pre-trained multi-view diffusion models to generate initial 3D priors from a single RGB-D observation.
- To develop a method for estimating pixel-wise epistemic uncertainty directly from these pre-trained diffusion models during inference, without requiring additional training.
- To employ 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as an efficient and high-fidelity representation for the 3D object model.
- To implement an uncertainty-guided refinement mechanism that adaptively integrates new observations into the 3DGS model, prioritizing reliable measurements and refining uncertain regions.
- To formulate pose estimation and 3D reconstruction as an incremental factor graph optimization problem, integrating both diffusion priors and observed data to ensure global consistency and continuous improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UnPose is a framework for zero-shot, model-free 6D object pose estimation and reconstruction. It operates by continually refining an object's 3D model, represented as a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) field, in its canonical coordinate frame, along with its relative pose to the camera. The pipeline consists of four main modules: Initialization, Pose Estimation, Backend Optimization, and 3DGS Mapping.

#### 3.1. Initialization

The system begins with an arbitrary single RGB-D frame and corresponding object masks.
- **Multi-view Diffusion Synthesis:** A multi-view diffusion model, specifically Wonder3D, is used to synthesize a set of diverse multi-view images (e.g., 6 views) of the object from the initial observation.
- **Uncertainty Estimation:** Critically, UnPose estimates pixel-wise epistemic uncertainty for these diffused views. This is achieved by adapting the BayesDiff approach, which employs a Last-Layer Laplace Approximation (LLLA) on the pre-trained diffusion model (Wonder3D). LLLA approximates the predictive distribution of the diffusion model's noise prediction as a Gaussian, providing a predictive covariance. The diagonal elements of this covariance represent pixel-wise variance (uncertainty). To propagate this uncertainty through the deterministic DDIM sampling steps, an iterative variance update rule is derived, which also requires estimating the covariance between the noisy state and the model's prediction using Monte Carlo sampling (e.g., 20 samples per step across 50 denoising steps). This entire uncertainty estimation process occurs during inference without retraining the diffusion model.
- **Real-world Alignment:** The generated diffusion images, along with their uncertainties, are aligned with the initial real-world RGB-D frame. The VGGT network is used to output point clouds, confidence maps, and relative transformations for the diffusion images. The predicted confidence maps are modulated by the estimated diffusion uncertainties via a weighted exponential operation. To recover the correct scale and align the diffusion point clouds with the real-world observation, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is used to match covariance matrices, followed by Iterative Closest Point (ICP) for rigid transformation refinement. This process creates an initial pose graph, leading to a metrically consistent 3D reconstruction aligned with the real-world coordinate frame, while preserving uncertainty estimates.

#### 3.2. 3D Gaussian Splatting Mapping

Once poses are optimized, a compact 3DGS field is generated for each object. Each 3D Gaussian is modeled as isotropic, encoding RGB color, 3D position, a scalar radius, and opacity. Pixel-wise uncertainty estimates guide the updates of the 3DGS field when new observations become available. The mapping loss function explicitly incorporates the uncertainty at each pixel, along with visibility scores and rendered depth/color residuals, ensuring that regions with higher uncertainty are refined more aggressively while maintaining a complete shape.

#### 3.3. 6D Pose Estimation

For 6D pose estimation, UnPose leverages the Pose Refinement network from FoundationPose. This network takes two inputs: (1) an RGB-D view rendered directly from the optimized 3DGS field of the object (rather than a pre-reconstructed 3D model), and (2) a cropped RGB-D observation from the camera. A Siamese encoder extracts feature maps from both inputs, which are then processed by a transformer to iteratively refine the object's 6D pose by minimizing feature space discrepancies.

#### 3.4. Backend Optimization

UnPose employs a backend optimization module for continuous refinement and global consistency.
- **Keyframe Management:** When a new frame arrives, its correspondence to existing keyframes (real and virtual diffusion frames) is evaluated using Mast3R. If the number of inlier matches falls below a threshold, the new frame is added as a keyframe, and a bidirectional edge is established in the pose graph.
- **Geometric Pose Graph Optimization:** Loop closures are identified by encoding keyframe features using Aggregated Selective Match Kernels (ASMK). Upon loop closure detection, the geometric pose graph is optimized by minimizing a weighted geometric residual over keyframe correspondences. The per-match weighting function explicitly incorporates diffusion uncertainties and match confidence. This nonlinear least squares problem is solved using Gauss-Newton optimization. To ensure stability, the poses of all diffusion-rendered frames are fixed during optimization. The refined poses of real keyframes are then used to update the 3DGS mapping module, promoting a multi-view consistent object representation.
- **Relocalization:** If object tracking fails (due to occlusion, fast motion, etc.), a relocalization module is triggered. Failure is detected by a sharp drop in frame-to-keyframe correspondence or a large geometric discrepancy. The module retrieves top-k candidate frames from a database using ASMK features, and these are jointly optimized with the current frame to recover its pose, allowing tracking to resume. The completeness of the 3D model (even in unobserved parts) serves as a weak prior during relocalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UnPose was evaluated on two standard benchmarks for 6D pose estimation: YCB-Video and LM-O datasets, and further on T-Less and TYO-L datasets for additional quantitative results. Performance metrics included Area Under the Curve (AUC) of Average Distance of Model Points (ADD) and its symmetric variant (ADD-S) for pose estimation, and Chamfer Distance (CD) for reconstruction quality. Experiments were conducted under various observation settings (1, 8, and 16 reference views).

#### 4.1. Pose Estimation

- **Superior Accuracy:** UnPose consistently outperformed state-of-the-art baselines, including model-based GigaPose, zero-shot reference view-based SAM6D, and model-free reconstruction-based FoundationPose. In the challenging single-view scenario, UnPose demonstrated a 71.66% improvement over GigaPose and a 36.6% improvement over FoundationPose on average across the YCB-Video and LM-O datasets.
- **Consistent Improvement with More Views:** The performance of UnPose showed continuous improvement as the number of reference views increased, indicating the effectiveness of its multi-view integration and geometric pose graph optimization in refining pose estimates.
- **Outperformance across Benchmarks:** Quantitative comparisons on T-LESS and TYO-L datasets also indicated that UnPose consistently achieved higher ADD and ADD-S scores across all tested numbers of input images (1, 8, 16), demonstrating its robustness and generalizability.

#### 4.2. Reconstruction Quality

- **Higher Fidelity and Efficiency:** UnPose significantly outperformed baselines like BundleSDF, GOM (3D diffusion priors-based multi-view optimization), and Wonder3D (single-view diffusion-based 3D generation) in terms of reconstruction quality (lower Chamfer Distance).
- **Speed Advantage:** Compared to other optimization-based methods, UnPose was notably faster, achieving 7 times the speed of BundleSDF and 2 times the speed of GOM, while simultaneously being 2.3 times and 3.8 times more accurate on average, respectively. This efficiency is attributed to the uncertainty-guided reconstruction and global consistency provided by pose graph optimization.
- **Qualitative Improvements:** Qualitative results demonstrated that UnPose achieved improved geometry and appearance of reconstructed objects as the number of reference views increased, leveraging its uncertainty-aware fusion mechanism.

#### 4.3. Runtime Analysis

A detailed breakdown of runtime showed that uncertainty estimation was the most computationally intensive component (12.08 seconds), followed by backend optimization (1.70 seconds). Other components, such as 3DGS Mapping and Pose Estimation, were relatively fast (0.22 seconds and 0.12 seconds, respectively). The total reconstruction time for UnPose was reported at approximately 13.49 seconds.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirmed the importance of key components:
- **Uncertainty Guidance and Bundle Adjustment:** These components jointly improved performance by approximately 20% in scenarios with 16 input images.
- **Diffused Frames in Pose Graph:** While less impactful with abundant observations (16 images), incorporating diffused frames into the pose graph yielded a 15% performance gain in sparser observation scenarios (8 images), highlighting their role as strong 3D priors.

#### 4.5. Robotic Application

UnPose was successfully demonstrated in real-world robotic manipulation tasks using a PiPER robot arm. It was used to estimate an object's 6D pose and complete geometry, which then informed a grasp planning module (AnyGrasp). The system showed an ability to incrementally update the object's geometry and refine grasp poses as new views revealed previously occluded features (e.g., a mug handle), leading to more functional grasp solutions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UnPose represents a significant advancement in the field of 6D object pose estimation, particularly for novel objects in unconstrained environments. Its primary significance lies in addressing several core limitations of previous approaches:

1. **True Model-Free and Zero-Shot Capability:** By entirely eliminating the dependency on object-specific CAD models or extensive category-level training, UnPose enables robots to operate more flexibly in open-world scenarios with unseen objects. This broadens the applicability of robotic systems in tasks such as logistics, manufacturing, and service robotics where prior object models are often unavailable.
2. **Uncertainty-Aware Fusion:** The novel integration of pixel-wise epistemic uncertainty estimates from pre-trained diffusion models directly into the reconstruction and pose estimation pipeline is a key contribution. This mechanism allows the system to intelligently weigh the reliability of synthetic priors against real observations, leading to more robust and accurate 3D geometry and pose estimates, especially when observations are sparse or incomplete.
3. **Enhanced Reconstruction Fidelity and Efficiency:** The use of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) combined with uncertainty-guided refinement results in high-fidelity 3D reconstructions. The method achieves this with a notable efficiency advantage over existing optimization-based approaches, which can accelerate the deployment of such systems.
4. **Global Consistency through Pose Graph Optimization:** The formulation of pose estimation as an incremental factor graph optimization problem, incorporating both diffusion priors and new observations, ensures that the object's 3D representation and its pose are globally consistent over time, mitigating issues like drift and accumulated errors.
5. **Practical Robotic Applications:** The successful demonstration in a real-world robotic manipulation task highlights the practical utility of UnPose. Its ability to incrementally refine geometry and adjust grasp poses based on evolving observations provides a more adaptive and intelligent grasping solution, moving beyond static, pre-programmed interactions.

Despite its advancements, the paper also acknowledges limitations, such as reliance on correspondence for textureless objects, the computational overhead of Monte Carlo sampling for uncertainty estimation (which limits real-time deployment), and the current focus on individual objects. These areas suggest future research directions, including integrating direct matching losses from SLAM literature, training diffusion models that jointly predict mean and uncertainty, and incorporating scene-level priors for global consistency across multiple objects. Overall, UnPose provides a robust framework for autonomous systems operating in dynamic, novel environments, contributing to more capable and adaptable robotic solutions.
