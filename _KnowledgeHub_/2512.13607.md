# Nemotron-Cascade: Scaling Cascaded Reinforcement Learning for General-Purpose Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA's Nemotron-Cascade framework enables the development of general-purpose Large Language Models with enhanced reasoning capabilities by employing a cascaded reinforcement learning approach. This method sequentially fine-tunes models across diverse domains, achieving state-of-the-art performance in areas such as competitive programming, math, and software engineering, while supporting both "thinking" and "instruct" modes within a single unified model.

## Problem

- Integrating diverse reasoning tasks into LLMs with traditional reinforcement learning is complex due to varied response lengths, verification latencies, and reward computation methods.
- Developing a single, unified LLM capable of seamlessly operating in both 'instruct' (direct answer) and 'deep thinking' (step-by-step reasoning) modes without performance degradation remains a challenge.
- Efficiently combining different reinforcement learning objectives, like RLHF for alignment and RLVR for verifiable reasoning, within an LLM training pipeline.

## Method

- Introduced Nemotron-Cascade, a two-stage post-training pipeline consisting of multi-stage Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by ==Cascaded Reinforcement Learning (Cascade RL)==.
- Implemented ==sequential, domain-wise RL framework==, progressing from general alignment (RLHF) to specialized domains like instruction following, math, code, and software engineering.
- Utilized the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== algorithm with strict on-policy training and tailored data curation, reward models, and verification methods for each cascaded RL stage.

## Results

- Nemotron-Cascade models achieved state-of-the-art performance across a wide range of benchmarks, including MMLU, GPQA-Diamond, ArenaHard, AIME, LiveCodeBench, and SWE-bench Verified.
- The 14B-Thinking model attained silver medal performance in the 2025 International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI) with **343.37 points** and recorded a **43.1%** pass@1 on SWE-bench Verified.
- Progressive analysis across RL stages confirmed that RLHF improves token efficiency and accuracy, while subsequent domain-specific RL stages further enhance reasoning and problem-solving without degrading prior domain performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cascade RL effectively addresses the heterogeneity of multi-domain LLM training by enabling sequential specialization, minimizing catastrophic forgetting, and reducing engineering complexity.
> - Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), when applied as an initial step, unexpectedly boosts reasoning performance in subsequent math and code tasks, improving the efficiency of later RL stages.
> - A unified model, trained with a specific 'Half-Half' RLHF strategy, can successfully operate in both 'instruct' and 'deep thinking' modes, challenging the necessity for separate specialized reasoning models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) with general-purpose reasoning capabilities is a central focus in artificial intelligence research. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) are critical techniques for aligning LLMs with human preferences and enhancing their reasoning performance. However, applying these reinforcement learning (RL) techniques to models intended for diverse domains presents challenges due to significant heterogeneity in response lengths, inference-time, and verification latency across tasks such as mathematical reasoning, code generation, and general instruction following. Existing approaches often involve blending heterogeneous prompts for joint RL training, which can complicate infrastructure, slow training, and make hyperparameter tuning and curriculum design difficult.

A related trend in LLM development involves the creation of "thinking" models, which generate explicit reasoning steps (chain-of-thought) before providing an answer, versus "instruct" or "non-thinking" models that provide immediate responses. While dedicated thinking models often outperform unified models in reasoning tasks, there is a recognized desire to develop single, unified models capable of operating effectively in both modes to simplify deployment and advance towards artificial general intelligence. Previous attempts to create such unified models have sometimes resulted in performance degradation in the thinking mode compared to specialized counterparts. This research positions itself by proposing an open post-training recipe, using a cascaded RL approach, to address these challenges and develop unified models without compromising performance in either mode, while also striving for state-of-the-art results across a broad spectrum of reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop general-purpose reasoning models, specifically named Nemotron-Cascade, through a novel cascaded domain-wise reinforcement learning (Cascade RL) framework. This framework aims to overcome the complexities and performance limitations observed in conventional RL training approaches that blend heterogeneous prompts from various domains.

The motivation for this research stems from several challenges in the current LLM development landscape:

- **Cross-Domain Heterogeneity in RL Training:** Training general-purpose LLMs with reinforcement learning involves substantial variations across domains. These include significant differences in inference-time response lengths (e.g., short answers for instruction following vs. long reasoning chains for complex math problems) and verification latency (e.g., fast symbolic rule-based verification for math vs. slow execution-based verification for code). Such variability complicates the RL infrastructure, prolongs training cycles, and makes the design of training curricula and hyperparameter selection challenging. The paper highlights that conventional approaches of blending prompts from all domains for joint RL training often lead to suboptimal performance.
- **Performance Gap in Unified Reasoning Models:** The LLM community has seen the emergence of both dedicated "thinking" models (which generate extensive reasoning tokens) and "instruct" models (which provide concise answers). While there is an aspiration to build a single, unified reasoning model that can seamlessly operate in both modes, prior efforts have often faced a degradation in reasoning benchmark performance for the unified model compared to its thinking-only counterparts. This necessitates either maintaining separate models or complex routing mechanisms, adding to deployment complexity. The research is motivated to bridge this performance gap within a single model.
- **Enhancing Reasoning Capabilities:** The authors aim to improve the reasoning abilities of LLMs across various challenging domains, including mathematical problem-solving, competitive programming, and software engineering. They seek to demonstrate that RLHF can improve reasoning capabilities beyond mere preference alignment, and that sequential domain-wise RL can build upon previous gains without experiencing catastrophic forgetting.
- **Transparency and Knowledge Sharing:** The work is motivated by the desire to contribute an open post-training recipe, including training and data curation details, and release models. This approach promotes transparency, allows for direct comparisons with existing models, and facilitates knowledge sharing within the research community.

In summary, the key objectives are to introduce a scalable and robust Cascade RL framework that simplifies training for diverse reasoning tasks, enable a unified model to excel in both thinking and non-thinking modes without performance compromises, achieve state-of-the-art performance across a broad set of benchmarks, and provide a transparent, open-source methodology to the community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Nemotron-Cascade models are developed through a two-stage post-training pipeline: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Cascaded Reinforcement Learning (Cascade RL).

#### 3.1. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The SFT stage equips the base model with foundational skills. Qwen3-8B-Base and Qwen3-14B-Base models serve as starting points.

- **Training Framework:** A multi-stage SFT curriculum is employed:
 - **Stage 1 (16K tokens):** Focuses on general-domain data (dialogue, Q&A, creative writing, safety, instruction following), along with math, science, and code reasoning data. General-domain prompts include parallel responses for both thinking and non-thinking modes, while reasoning data only has thinking mode responses.
 - **Stage 2 (32K tokens):** Extends reasoning capabilities with longer responses, integrating tool use and software engineering skills. It combines general-domain data with new, longer math, science, and code reasoning data, plus tool use and software engineering datasets. All data, except general-domain, uses thinking-mode responses. Each stage involves one epoch of training.
- **Chat Template:** A modified ChatML template is used, incorporating `/think` and `/no_think` tags appended to individual user prompts for dynamic control over thinking/non-thinking modes within a conversation. For tool calling, available tools are listed in the system prompt within `<tools>` tags, and the model is instructed to generate tool calls within `<tool_call>` tags.
- **Data Curation:**
 - **General-Domain:** A corpus of 2.8M samples (3.2B tokens) from various datasets is curated. DeepSeek-R1-0528 (thinking) and DeepSeek-V3-0324 (non-thinking) are used to generate parallel responses, followed by quality filtering.
 - **Math Reasoning:** AceReason-Nemotron-1.1 prompts are used. DeepSeek-R1 generates Stage-1 (16K tokens) responses, while DeepSeek-R1-0528 generates Stage-2 (32K tokens) responses, focusing on harder problems with longer reasoning. Data is decontaminated.
 - **Code Reasoning:** AceReason-Nemotron-1.1 prompts (TACO, APPs, OpenCoder-Stage-2, OpenCodeReasoning) are used. DeepSeek-R1 generates Stage-1 (16K tokens) responses, and DeepSeek-R1-0528 generates Stage-2 (32K tokens) responses from OpenCodeReasoning and OpenCoder-Stage2. Data is decontaminated.
 - **Science Reasoning:** Prompts from S1K and Llama-Nemotron post-training datasets are curated, filtered for reasoning-intensive questions. DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeek-R1-0528 generate responses for Stage-1 (16K) and Stage-2 (32K), respectively.
 - **Tool Calling:** Llama-Nemotron dataset, covering single/multi-turn and multi-step interactions, with responses generated by Qwen3-235B-A22B.
- **Software Engineering (SWE) Task:** An Agentless framework is adopted, simplifying localization to relevant issue files. The task is decomposed into localization, code repair (generating diff-style patches), and patch validation (regression, reproduction tests, majority voting). Data is curated from SWE-Bench-Train, SWE-Fixer-Train, SWE-reBench, and SWE-Smith, with responses generated by DeepSeek-R1-0528. Data is deduplicated against evaluation sets and filtered for quality.

#### 3.2. Cascaded Reinforcement Learning (Cascade RL)

The Cascade RL process sequentially applies RL stages, starting from general domains and moving to specialized ones.

- **Training Framework:** The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm with strict on-policy training is used, based on the AceReason-Nemotron strategy. The GRPO objective is simplified to a REINFORCE-like objective with group-normalized rewards and token-level loss, with the KL divergence term removed to stabilize training.
- **Resistance to Catastrophic Forgetting:** The paper argues that Cascade RL mitigates catastrophic forgetting due to: 1) policy-dependent training data (LLM generates its own experience, preserving useful old behaviors); 2) optimization for expected cumulative reward (focus on long-term outcomes rather than exact token-level distributions); 3) substantial overlap in reward structures across domains (e.g., reducing verbosity benefits all); and 4) minimal prompt overlap and progressive ordering of domains (general to specialized).
- **Reward Modeling (for RLHF):**
 - **Data Curation:** An 82K preference pair dataset is used, combining HelpSteer2, HelpSteer3, and synthetically generated pairs (stronger LLM generating bad responses to slightly off-topic prompts, weaker LLM generating good responses).
 - **Training Recipe:** A scalar-output Reward Model (RM) is initialized from Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct and trained using the Bradley–Terry objective to maximize log-likelihood of human preferences. Ablations demonstrated that larger RM sizes (e.g., 72B) yield better performance and robustness.
- **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF):** This is the first stage.
 - **Data:** A subset of the RM's preference data, excluding math and competitive programming prompts, is used to avoid unstable reward signals.
 - **Training:** GRPO is applied to the SFT models. Dedicated thinking models are trained in thinking mode. Unified models are trained in both non-thinking and thinking modes with an equal prompt split per batch.
 - **Reward Function:** Uses scalar scores from the RM. For thinking mode, only the final summary (after `</think>`) is evaluated. A code-switching penalty is applied for mixed-language generations.
 - **Stabilization:** For smaller RMs, techniques like KL penalty, sequence-level loss aggregation, and reward shaping were found useful. With the 72B RM, these were largely unnecessary.
- **Instruction-Following Reinforcement Learning (IF-RL):**
 - **Data:** Llama-Nemotron instruction-following dataset (filtered), custom data (LMSYS-Chat-1M with IFEval constraints), and IF-RLVR training data (Pyatkin et al., 2025).
 - **Training:** Two stages with increasing difficulty. Dynamic filtering is used. Unified models undergo IF-RL only in non-thinking mode to avoid negative interference with RLHF, while still improving thinking mode performance. Dedicated thinking models use a combined reward function (rule-based IF verifier + sigmoid-scaled group-normalized RM score).
- **Math RL:**
 - **Data:** AceReason-Math dataset (DeepScaleR, NuminaMath, OpenMathReasoning), 18K high-quality math problems, filtered for suitability with rule-based verification and benchmark contamination.
 - **Training:** GRPO, on-policy, initialized from RLHF-trained models. A staged response length extension curriculum (e.g., 24K → 32K → 40K tokens) is applied, with dynamic filtering to manage problem difficulty.
 - **Reward Function:** Binary (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) from a rule-based verifier. Code-switching penalty (reward of -1).
- **Code RL:**
 - **Data:** AceReason-Nemotron coding corpus (TACO, APPS, DeepCoder, etc.), filtered for unit test coverage, duplicates, and difficulty. 9.8K samples.
 - **Training:** Single-stage, on-policy GRPO, initialized from Math RL checkpoints. Maximum response length 44K–48K.
 - **Reward Function:** Binary (1 for passing all test cases, 0 otherwise) from a parallelized code verifier. Code-switching penalty (reward of 0).
- **SWE RL:**
 - **Data:** Challenging instances from SFT datasets, focusing on code repair. Long prompts (up to 60K tokens) constructed with ground-truth and mixed localized files, using YaRN scaling.
 - **Training:** On-policy GRPO. Multi-stage for input context extension (16K → 24K tokens) to optimize long-context utilization.
 - **Reward Function:** An execution-free reward model is used. Reward is based on similarity between generated and ground-truth patches, using a combination of lexical similarity (Unidiff) and semantic similarity (Kimi-Dev-72B model, by querying for "YES/NO" on semantic equivalence). Penalties for unparseable or identical patches.
- **Test-Time Scaling (TTS) for IOI and SWE:** For competitive programming (IOI 2025) and SWE-bench, a multi-round generate-select-submit process is used. For IOI, it involves generating multiple candidates, filtering (incomplete, failing sample tests), selecting the best (Tail-10 heuristic), and iteratively refining prompts with official judge feedback. For SWE, TTS involves generating multiple candidate patches, followed by a patch-validation stage (regression, reproduction tests, majority voting) to identify reliable fixes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents Nemotron-Cascade models (8B unified, 8B-Thinking, 14B-Thinking) demonstrating performance across a range of benchmarks after the full Cascade RL pipeline.

- **Overall State-of-the-Art Performance:** The Nemotron-Cascade models achieve strong performance across knowledge reasoning (MMLU, MMLU-Pro, GPQA-Diamond), alignment (ArenaHard, IFEval, IFBench), mathematical reasoning (AIME 2024, AIME 2025), code generation (LiveCodeBench v5/v6/Pro), software engineering (SWE-bench Verified), and tool calling (BFCL V3). The 14B-Thinking model, in particular, often outperforms larger proprietary models and its SFT teacher, DeepSeek-R1-0528 (671B).
- **Unified Model Effectiveness:** The 8B unified reasoning model is shown to perform comparably to its dedicated 8B-Thinking counterpart across reasoning-related benchmarks while exceeding it on instruction-following tasks like IFEval. This suggests that the approach effectively closes the performance gap between unified and dedicated thinking models. The "Half-Half" RLHF training strategy (equal split of thinking/non-thinking prompts) for unified models provided the strongest overall performance.
- **RLHF Impact:** RLHF significantly improves overall response quality, reducing verbosity and repetition. It enhances reasoning performance on math and code benchmarks, even without math- or code-specific prompts in the RLHF dataset. This stage also improves token efficiency. However, RLHF can sometimes slightly degrade strict instruction-following performance if not carefully managed.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation:** The Cascade RL framework demonstrates robustness against catastrophic forgetting. Sequential domain-specific RL stages generally do not degrade performance attained in earlier domains and can often improve it (e.g., Math RL improving code benchmarks, and Code RL boosting performance across nearly all topics without significant degradation in others).
- **IF-RL Enhancements:** IF-RL significantly improves instruction-following capabilities (IFEval, IFBench) with controlled degradation on other benchmarks. For unified models, applying IF-RL in non-thinking mode is effective. For thinking models, a combined reward function (rule-based verifier + RM score) mitigates negative impacts on human alignment.
- **Math RL Performance:** Math RL leads to noticeable improvements on AIME 2024 and 2025 benchmarks, with minimal impact on knowledge reasoning and alignment. The multi-stage length extension and dynamic filtering stabilize training and enhance problem-solving.
- **Code RL Performance:** Code RL yields substantial gains on LiveCodeBench, with the 8B unified model matching DeepSeek-R1-0528 (671B) and the 14B-Thinking model surpassing it. The ablation study shows that higher training temperatures for Code RL can encourage exploration and improve accuracy, but may require careful management for stability.
- **SWE RL Performance:** SWE RL yields substantial gains on SWE-bench Verified. The 14B-Thinking model achieves a pass@1 resolve rate of 43.1%, outperforming several specialized open 32B models (e.g., DeepSWE-32B at 42.2%). An execution-free, LLM-based semantic similarity reward model is shown to be effective for scaling SWE RL training.
- **Test-Time Scaling (TTS):**
 - **IOI 2025:** The Nemotron-Cascade-14B-Thinking model achieves a total score of 343.37 on IOI 2025, corresponding to a silver medal performance. It achieves 90.37 points on Problem 2 Triples, outperforming OpenAI's internal IOI model (75.29) and DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale (82.00) using a feedback-driven, self-improving test-time scaling pipeline.
 - **SWE-bench Verified:** TTS combined with a patch-validation pipeline (best@k strategy) substantially boosts resolve rates on SWE-bench Verified. The 14B model reaches 53.8% best@k resolve rate.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of large language models:

- **Methodological Advancement in RL for LLMs:** The proposed Cascade RL framework offers a structured and scalable approach to training general-purpose LLMs across diverse, heterogeneous domains. By orchestrating sequential, domain-wise reinforcement learning, it addresses the challenges of varied response lengths, verification latencies, and complex infrastructure that hinder conventional blended RL methods. This framework provides a robust method for mitigating catastrophic forgetting, a common problem in sequential learning, through its policy-dependent data generation, focus on cumulative rewards, shared reward structures, and strategic domain ordering. This could simplify the development of future multi-skill LLMs.

- **High-Performance Open Models:** The Nemotron-Cascade models achieve new state-of-the-art results for open-source models across a comprehensive range of benchmarks including knowledge reasoning, alignment, mathematical problem-solving, competitive programming, and software engineering. The fact that the 14B model often outperforms significantly larger or specialized proprietary models (e.g., surpassing DeepSeek-R1-0528 and Gemini-2.5-Pro on LiveCodeBench) demonstrates the effectiveness and efficiency of the Cascade RL approach, particularly for smaller models. This can democratize access to high-performing reasoning AI.

- **Unified Model Capabilities:** The work successfully demonstrates the feasibility of building a single, unified reasoning model that can operate effectively in both "thinking" and "non-thinking" (instruct) modes without incurring a performance penalty in reasoning tasks. This addresses a recognized challenge in the field and simplifies model deployment, aligning with the long-term goal of developing more versatile and integrated AI systems. The insights into RLHF training strategies for unified models (e.g., the "Half-Half" approach) are valuable for future development.

- **Insights into RLHF and Reward Modeling:** The deep dive into RLHF provides empirical evidence regarding the importance of reward model size and robustness. It shows that larger reward models (e.g., 72B) lead to more stable and effective RLHF training, better human alignment, and improved performance on other tasks, even under adversarial conditions like style control. The "bag of tricks" for stabilizing RLHF training, while less critical for powerful RMs, offers practical guidance for researchers working with less robust reward signals.

- **Advancements in Software Engineering Automation:** The detailed methodology for SWE RL, including the adoption of the Agentless framework, the use of an execution-free reward model based on semantic similarity, and multi-stage training for long-context analysis, significantly contributes to advancing LLMs' capabilities in software engineering tasks. The achievement of competitive performance on SWE-bench Verified with smaller, general-purpose models, further boosted by test-time scaling, highlights the practical utility for automated code repair and development.

- **Transparency and Community Impact:** The transparent sharing of training and data curation recipes, along with the release of models and datasets, is a substantial contribution to open science. This fosters reproducibility, enables other researchers to build upon this work, and accelerates collective progress in developing general-purpose reasoning models.
