# Diverse Data Augmentation with Diffusions for Effective Test-time Prompt Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> DiffTPT, a method developed by researchers from A*STAR, MBZUAI, Australian National University, and Harbin Institute of Technology, improves Test-time Prompt Tuning by using pre-trained diffusion models for diverse data augmentation. This approach achieves a 5.13% average increase in zero-shot accuracy over prior TPT methods on out-of-distribution datasets and maintains robustness across various cross-dataset generalization tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Test-time Prompt Tuning (TPT) methods use simplistic data augmentation (e.g., random crops), leading to limited visual diversity and prompts that overfit narrow data variations.
- Conventional TPT's entropy-based confidence selection is often insufficient to guarantee prediction fidelity, potentially including misclassified augmented samples that mislead prompt optimization.
- Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP struggle with zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution or unseen domains due to distribution shifts.

## Method

- The **DiffTPT** framework leverages a pre-trained ==Stable Diffusion== model to generate diverse, semantically consistent augmented images for test samples, moving beyond basic parametric transformations.
- A ==cosine similarity-based filtration== mechanism is introduced to ensure prediction fidelity, retaining only augmented images whose features are semantically similar to the original test image.
- The method combines both conventional and ==diffusion-based augmentation==, integrating their complementary strengths, and uses a refined ==Test-time Prompt Tuning (TPT)== objective incorporating both entropy and cosine similarity filters.

## Results

- DiffTPT improved the average zero-shot accuracy by **5.13%** compared to the state-of-the-art TPT method on natural distribution shift datasets (e.g., ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-A) using a ResNet-50 backbone.
- The method achieved the highest average accuracy across 10 fine-grained cross-datasets, demonstrating performance gains of **5.1%** (ResNet50) and **2.3%** (Vit-B/16) over existing TPT approaches.
- Ablation studies confirmed that increasing the proportion of diffusion-based synthetic data and applying cosine similarity filtration with an optimal threshold of **0.8** enhanced performance, validating the dual augmentation and fidelity preservation strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained diffusion models can effectively generate visually rich and diverse augmented data that significantly enhances the robustness of prompts for zero-shot generalization.
> - Maintaining semantic consistency between original and augmented data via cosine similarity-based filtration is crucial to prevent spurious augmentations from degrading model performance.
> - Combining both traditional and diffusion-based augmentation strategies offers synergistic benefits, creating a more comprehensive augmented data pool for test-time adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper operates within the field of adapting large-scale pre-trained vision-language models, such as CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training), for various downstream tasks, particularly in zero-shot generalization settings. These models have demonstrated an ability to learn general representations and transfer knowledge across different tasks.

Traditional methods for adapting these models often involve either hand-crafted prompts or prompt tuning, which typically rely on the training data distribution of a specific domain. This can limit their generalization capabilities to out-of-distribution (OOD) data or unseen domains. To address this limitation, Test-time Prompt Tuning (TPT) was introduced. TPT aims to learn adaptive prompts for each test sample from an unseen domain dynamically, without requiring additional training data or annotations. This approach is practical for scenarios where labeled data for new distributions is scarce or unavailable.

Existing TPT methods, such as the one proposed by Shu et al., typically employ data augmentation combined with confidence selection (e.g., entropy minimization) to create a diverse set of views for a single test sample. However, the authors identify two primary limitations in these conventional TPT approaches. First, the data augmentation techniques often utilize simplistic parametric transformations (e.g., random resized crops), which restrict the diversity of generated data. This limited diversity can lead to prompts overfitting to narrow variations of the test data and consequently hinder generalization. Second, entropy-based confidence selection alone may be insufficient to guarantee prediction fidelity, as an augmented sample with a low-entropy prediction could still be misclassified, leading to the inclusion of unrepresentative samples in the augmented pool.

Concurrent advancements in generative models, particularly diffusion models, have demonstrated the ability to synthesize high-quality, photorealistic images with significant visual diversity. This paper positions itself at the intersection of TPT and diffusion models, exploring how these generative capabilities can enhance the diversity of augmented data for TPT. The challenge then becomes how to leverage diffusion models effectively while mitigating the risk of introducing spurious augmentations that could negatively impact prediction fidelity. This work aims to contribute a method that balances data diversity and prediction fidelity when applying diffusion-based augmentation for TPT, thereby improving zero-shot generalization performance under distribution shifts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective Test-time Prompt Tuning (TPT) method that enhances the zero-shot generalization capabilities of pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP, particularly when faced with test samples from unseen domains or those exhibiting distribution shifts.

This overarching objective is driven by several motivations and refined into specific aims:

1. **Address Limited Data Diversity in Existing TPT Methods:** Conventional TPT approaches often rely on basic data augmentation techniques (e.g., random resized crops). While these methods alleviate data scarcity, they generate variants of the test image that are visually simplistic and lack rich appearance variations. This limitation can cause the learned prompts to overfit to a narrow mode of the test data, thereby hindering generalization to diverse real-world scenarios. The authors are motivated to introduce a method capable of generating more diverse and informative augmented data.

2. **Improve Prediction Fidelity Beyond Entropy-based Selection:** Existing TPT methods use entropy-based confidence selection to filter out less reliable augmented samples. However, a low-entropy prediction does not inherently guarantee correct classification. An augmented image, even with a confident (low-entropy) prediction, might be misclassified into a different category than the original test sample. Such spurious augmentations can mislead the prompt optimization process, reducing its effectiveness. A motivation is to implement a more robust mechanism to ensure that augmented samples maintain semantic consistency with the original test image.

3. **Leverage Pre-trained Diffusion Models for Enhanced Augmentation:** Recent advancements in diffusion models have enabled the generation of high-quality, photorealistic images with significant diversity, often guided by text or image features. The authors are motivated to explore the potential of these models, specifically Stable Diffusion, to synthesize new images that preserve the key semantics of a given test sample while introducing richer visual appearance variations. This contrasts with traditional augmentation's fixed transformations.

4. **Balance Data Diversity with Prediction Fidelity:** While diffusion models can generate diverse images, they also carry the risk of producing spurious augmentations that deviate semantically from the original image. A key motivation is to devise a strategy that can effectively harness the diversity provided by diffusion models without compromising the fidelity of the generated data. This involves finding a balance where augmented samples are both varied and semantically consistent with the input.

5. **Integrate Complementary Augmentation Strategies:** The paper is motivated to combine the benefits of both conventional data augmentation techniques (which offer simple, robust transformations) and diffusion-based augmentation (which offers rich diversity). This integration aims to create a more comprehensive augmented data pool that benefits from the strengths of both approaches.

By addressing these points, the research seeks to develop a TPT framework that is more robust to distribution shifts and improves zero-shot generalization performance across a wider range of challenging datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, DiffTPT, aims to enhance Test-time Prompt Tuning (TPT) by integrating diverse data augmentation using pre-trained diffusion models and ensuring prediction fidelity through a cosine similarity-based filtration mechanism. The approach can be broken down into the following components:

#### 3.1. Test-time Prompt Tuning (TPT) Foundation

The work builds upon the TPT framework for pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP. CLIP utilizes an image encoder `f(·)` and a text encoder `g(·)` to align image and text features. For zero-shot classification, the prediction probability for a class `y_i` given an image `x` is computed based on the cosine similarity between the image feature `e = f(x)` and the class-specific text feature `w_i`.

In TPT, the objective is to optimize a learnable prompt `v` for each test sample `x_test` from an unseen domain, without access to training data or labels. The optimization typically involves minimizing the entropy of predictions over multiple augmented views of `x_test`. The original TPT formulation uses `N` augmented views `A_n(x_test)` generated by simple transformations and applies an entropy-based confidence selection mechanism, represented by `1[H(p_n) ≤ τ]`, to filter out augmented views with high-entropy (low-confidence) predictions. `p_n(y | A_n(x_test))` denotes the class probabilities for the `n`-th augmented view.

#### 3.2. Approach Overview (DiffTPT)

DiffTPT addresses the limitations of conventional TPT by focusing on two main improvements:
1. **Diffusion-based Diverse Data Augmentation**: To generate augmented images with richer visual appearance variations.
2. **Filtration with Cosine Similarity**: To maintain prediction fidelity by removing spurious augmentations.

#### 3.3. Diffusion-based Diverse Data Augmentation

This component introduces a novel way to generate diverse augmented images for a given test sample `x_test`. Instead of relying solely on conventional parametric transformations, DiffTPT leverages a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (specifically, Stable Diffusion-V2).

The process involves:
- **Feature Extraction**: The original test image `x_test` is first passed through the CLIP image encoder `f(x_test)` to extract its latent feature representation `z_0`.
- **Diffusion Model Input**: Traditionally, Stable Diffusion generates images from natural language descriptions (`g(t)`). In DiffTPT, the CLIP image feature `f(x_test)` is used as an alternative condition to `g(t)`. This leverages CLIP's ability to align image and text features, enabling the diffusion model to generate images semantically consistent with `x_test`.
- **Image Generation**: The Stable Diffusion model `G` generates `n`-th augmented image `D_n(x_test)` by taking the image feature `f(x_test)` and a sampled noise `n_n ∼ N(0, I)` as input: `D_n(x_test) = G(f(x_test), n_n)`. This process yields a set of diverse augmented images that preserve the key semantics of the original image while introducing variations in appearance.
- **Combined Augmentation**: DiffTPT combines augmented data from both the conventional methods (as in) and the newly introduced diffusion-based method. This dual approach aims to exploit the complementary merits of both types of augmentations. The augmented images `D_n(x_test)` are then incorporated into the TPT objective function.

#### 3.4. Filtration with Cosine Similarity

To mitigate the risk of spurious augmentations generated by the diffusion model—which could lead to misclassifications and negatively impact prompt tuning—DiffTPT introduces a cosine similarity-based filtration technique.

The steps are:
- **Feature Comparison**: For each augmented image `D_n(x_test)`, its CLIP image feature is extracted `f(D_n(x_test))`. The cosine similarity between this feature and the original test image's feature `f(x_test)` is calculated.
- **Similarity-based Masking**: A binary mask `M_n` is applied based on a predefined threshold `ε`. An augmented image is retained if its cosine similarity with the original test image is higher than `ε`: `M_n = (cos(f(D_n(x_test)), f(x_test)) > ε)`. This threshold `ε` results in a `ρ_C` percentage of augmented images being retained, ensuring higher prediction fidelity.
- **Refined TPT Objective**: The final TPT objective incorporates both the entropy-based confidence selection (from) and the new cosine similarity-based filtration. The combined filter `1[H(p_i) ≤ τ] · 1[M_n]` ensures that only augmented images that are both confident in their prediction and semantically similar to the original test sample are used for prompt optimization.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Implementation**: The method uses an NVIDIA Tesla V100 GPU. Prompts are initialized as "a photo of a" and optimized for 4 steps using Adam, with an initial learning rate of 0.005. The number of augmented images generated by Stable Diffusion is 63, in addition to 64 images from conventional augmentation.
- **Hyper-parameters**: The confidence selection threshold `ρ_H` (for entropy-based filtering) is set to 0.3, and the cosine similarity threshold `ρ_C` (for diffusion-based filtering) is set to 0.8.
- **Datasets**: Experiments are conducted across two scenarios:
 - **Scenario 1 (S1): Natural Distribution Shifts**: ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, and ImageNet-Sketch, which are out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets for ImageNet.
 - **Scenario 2 (S2): Cross-Datasets Generalization**: 10 fine-grained datasets (Flower102, OxfordPets, SUN397, DTD, Food101, StanfordCars, Aircraft, UCF101, EuroSAT, Caltech101), with ImageNet as the source.
- **Baselines**: Compared against TPT, few-shot prompt tuning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp trained with 16-shot on ImageNet), and zero-shot CLIP (with 80 hand-crafted prompts ensemble and with default prompt "a picture of a"). Experiments are performed with ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16 backbones.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that DiffTPT generally improves the zero-shot generalization performance of vision-language models across various challenging scenarios.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-arts

- **Natural Distribution Shifts (S1)**:
 - DiffTPT consistently outperformed all other methods across the five OOD datasets (ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch) and on ImageNet itself.
 - For the ResNet-50 backbone, DiffTPT improved the average zero-shot accuracy by 5.13% compared to the state-of-the-art TPT method.
 - Applying DiffTPT to prompts learned by CoOp or CoCoOp also improved their in-domain ImageNet accuracy and generalization to OOD data. For example, with ResNet-50, the average OOD generalization accuracy for CoOp improved from 43.08% to 47.42% with DiffTPT&CoOp, and for CoCoOp from 43.01% to 46.14% with DiffTPT&CoCoOp. Similar gains were observed with the ViT-B/16 backbone.
 - The improvements suggest that the increased diversity and semantic consistency provided by DiffTPT contribute to better robustness against natural distribution shifts.

- **Cross-Datasets Generalization (S2)**:
 - DiffTPT also achieved the highest average accuracy across the 10 fine-grained target datasets.
 - It demonstrated performance gains of 5.1% and 2.3% against TPT on the ResNet50 and Vit-B/16 backbones, respectively.
 - These results indicate that DiffTPT maintains robustness to diverse distributional variations even without relying on training data, outperforming few-shot prompt-tuning methods like CoOp and CoCoOp in this zero-shot generalization setting. Notably, for the EuroSAT dataset, TPT decreased accuracy compared to CLIP baselines, while DiffTPT showed significant improvement.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Balancing Synthetic Data vs. Standard Augmentation (Figure 4)**:
 - The study showed that increasing the proportion of diffusion-based synthetic data generally improved performance in Scenario 1 (Natural Distribution Shifts).
 - For Scenario 2 (Cross-Datasets Generalization), increasing the proportion of standard augmented data significantly enhanced classifier effectiveness, especially when combined with synthetic data. This suggests that the two augmentation types offer complementary benefits, supporting the method's design to incorporate both.

- **Analysis of Ratio ρ_H and ρ_C (Figure 5)**:
 - `ρ_C` (cosine similarity threshold): Classification accuracy generally improved as `ρ_C` increased, reaching optimal performance around 0.8. A lower `ρ_C` implies filtering out less data, suggesting that a large portion of the diffusion-generated data maintains high fidelity.
 - `ρ_H` (entropy threshold): Performance improved as `ρ_H` increased up to 0.3, but then decreased beyond 0.5. This indicates that an optimal balance is necessary for filtering out unconfident predictions without discarding too much useful information. The optimal values were identified as `ρ_H = 0.3` and `ρ_C = 0.8`.

- **Effect of the Generated Dataset Size (Figure 6)**:
 - Increasing the number of augmented views (N) improved accuracy, reaching a plateau around N=64.
 - DiffTPT maintained performance even at N=128, suggesting that the cosine similarity filtration effectively prevented performance degradation due to an excessive amount of synthetic data, validating its ability to preserve prediction fidelity. Even with a small N=8, DiffTPT provided notable accuracy gains.

- **Steps of Prompt Updating (Figure 7)**:
 - The optimal number of prompt optimization steps was found to be around 4, beyond which performance slightly decreased. This suggests that a few updates are sufficient for the prompt to adapt.
 - DiffTPT consistently outperformed TPT across all tested optimization steps, confirming the robustness and effectiveness of the proposed augmentation and filtration.

- **Inference Cost**: The paper acknowledges that original Stable Diffusion models can be time-consuming but points to recent advancements (e.g., ToMe, two-stage distillation, Consistency Models) and optimization strategies (e.g., TensorRT, Memory Efficient Attention) that significantly reduce inference time, making the approach more practical.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces DiffTPT, a method that enhances test-time prompt tuning by leveraging pre-trained diffusion models for diverse data augmentation while maintaining prediction fidelity. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Advancement in Zero-Shot Generalization**: DiffTPT demonstrates an improvement in the zero-shot generalization capabilities of vision-language models, such as CLIP, in challenging scenarios involving natural distribution shifts and cross-dataset generalization. This addresses a critical limitation of existing models that often struggle outside their training domain.
- **Enhanced Data Diversity**: By incorporating diffusion-based augmentation, the method generates visually richer and more diverse augmented samples than conventional techniques. This increased diversity can lead to more robust and generalized prompts, reducing the risk of overfitting to narrow visual variations.
- **Improved Prediction Fidelity**: The introduction of cosine similarity-based filtration directly addresses the issue of spurious augmentations that can arise from generative models. By ensuring semantic consistency between augmented and original samples, DiffTPT improves the reliability of the augmented data pool used for prompt optimization.
- **Practicality for Real-World Scenarios**: As a test-time adaptation method, DiffTPT does not require access to training data or annotations from the target domain, making it applicable to dynamic and privacy-sensitive real-world deployments where labeled data for new domains is often unavailable.
- **Complementary Augmentation Strategy**: The approach successfully integrates both conventional and diffusion-based augmentation, suggesting a synergistic effect that harnesses the strengths of both, leading to more comprehensive and effective test-time adaptation.
- **Generalizability**: The method is designed to be agnostic to the training data and can be integrated into various CLIP architectures, indicating its potential for broader application across different vision-language models.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: This work lays a foundation for further exploration into the synergy between generative models and adaptation techniques. Future research could investigate more efficient diffusion models, adaptive thresholding for filtration, or the application of similar principles to other forms of model adaptation. The study also highlights the importance of balancing diversity and fidelity in synthetic data generation for downstream tasks.
