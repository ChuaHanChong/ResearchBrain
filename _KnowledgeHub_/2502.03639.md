# Towards Physical Understanding in Video Generation: A 3D Point Regularization Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UCLA and Snap Inc. introduce a 3D point regularization approach for video diffusion models, augmenting 2D videos with pixel-aligned 3D point trajectories to improve physical understanding. This method enables the generation of videos with enhanced shape consistency and motion plausibility, effectively reducing artifacts like object morphing in complex interaction scenarios.

## Problem

- Current video diffusion models primarily operate on a 2D pixel-level understanding, lacking robust awareness of underlying 3D shapes and motions.
- This limitation leads to visual artifacts such as object morphing, unnatural deformations, and inconsistent object appearances in generated videos.
- Models struggle to accurately capture intricate 3D spatial relationships and movements in 'contact-rich' scenarios, like human hands manipulating objects.

## Method

- A novel 3D-aware video dataset, **PointVid**, is created by tracking 3D Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z) of sampled pixels from segmented foreground objects, aligning this 'pseudo-3D' representation with 2D video frames.
- Existing ==latent diffusion== models are fine-tuned to jointly learn and generate both RGB video and its corresponding 3D point representation, using a concatenated input (RGB + 3D points) and a cross-==attention mechanism== for modality alignment.
- A 3D point regularization strategy is applied during training, incorporating reconstruction loss (for fidelity, temporal, and acceleration smoothness) and rigidity loss (for local shape preservation) to guide the generation of physically coherent 3D points.

## Results

- Qualitative comparisons against baselines like I2VGen-XL and CogVideoX 1.5 show consistent mitigation of object morphing and unnatural deformations, leading to more visually plausible videos, especially in task-oriented scenarios.
- Quantitative evaluations on VBench demonstrated improvements in subject consistency, background consistency, and motion smoothness, while VideoPhy scores confirmed better adherence to physical commonsense (e.g., 0.41384 for DiT-based model vs. 0.36549 baseline).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of point augmentation and regularization, reducing the mean squared error (MSE) of predicted 3D point trajectories to 0.0020, and a user study revealed an 86.2% preference for the proposed method's physical realism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting 2D video diffusion models with explicit, lightweight 3D point trajectory information can significantly enhance their understanding of object geometry and dynamics without requiring full 3D reconstruction.
> - Regularizing the generative process with 3D priors (reconstruction fidelity, temporal smoothness, and local rigidity) effectively mitigates common non-physical artifacts like object morphing and unnatural deformations.
> - A balanced joint training strategy, incorporating a channel-wise cross-attention mechanism and appropriate weighting of diffusion and regularization losses, is crucial to preserve semantic content while improving physical plausibility across different diffusion model architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has experienced substantial advancements, particularly with the emergence of video diffusion models. These models, as exemplified by works such as, have demonstrated progress in generating expressive and aesthetically pleasing content, enabling applications in various industries. However, existing state-of-the-art models predominantly focus on the appearance and temporal smoothness of video content. They primarily learn and predict the movement of 2-dimensional pixels, wherein the perception of 3D object motion is approximated through changes in RGB values across frames.

A critical limitation arises from this 2D-centric approach: current models often lack a robust understanding of the underlying 3-dimensional shapes and motions that govern objects in the physical world. This deficiency manifests as common visual artifacts, such as objects arbitrarily changing shape, appearing or disappearing suddenly (termed "object morphing"), or exhibiting unnatural deformations. These issues are particularly pronounced in complex scenarios, such as human-object interactions where precise 3D geometry and contact dynamics are crucial, as seen when hands interact with delicate objects or perform tasks like tying shoelaces. The 2D projection and occlusion inherent in standard video data complicate the capture of true 3D shape, making it challenging for models to discern how objects occupy space, deform, or interact.

Previous research efforts have attempted to instill more physically plausible dynamics. Some approaches aim to control shape and motion directly within 2D space, but these are inherently limited by the 2D plane and cannot fully address out-of-plane motions or complex 3D dynamics. Other lines of work have explored explicit 3D modeling, such as generating full 3D geometries using Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) or 3D Gaussian Splatting, leading to 4D generation models. While these methods offer accurate 3D understanding, they are typically computationally intensive and often restricted to simpler scenes or specific object types. Furthermore, dynamics-aware video generation techniques have aimed to incorporate simple physical controls or optical flow. However, these methods often implicitly assume that the underlying diffusion model possesses an inherent understanding of object dynamics, an assumption that has been questioned by studies like VideoPhy, which highlights inconsistencies in current video generation models.

This work addresses the gap between advanced video generation capabilities and a fundamental lack of physical and 3D geometric understanding. It distinguishes itself by proposing a method that augments and regularizes video diffusion models using a pseudo-3D representation derived from tracked 3D point clouds. This approach aims to introduce out-of-plane spatial data into the model without the computational burden of full 3D reconstruction. Unlike concurrent works that focus solely on 2D optical flow or 2D point correspondences, this research explicitly models 3D geometry and motion to provide spatial guidance, enhancing visual plausibility and spatio-temporal consistency in generated videos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the observation that despite significant progress, contemporary video diffusion models primarily operate on a 2D pixel-level understanding of motion. This inherent limitation prevents them from accurately representing how objects exist and interact in a 3-dimensional physical world, leading to visual artifacts that undermine the realism and plausibility of generated videos. Specifically, models struggle with:
- **Lack of 3D Shape and Motion Awareness**: Objects often undergo non-physical deformations, arbitrary shape changes, or sudden appearances/disappearances (object morphing) because the models do not adequately learn their true 3D geometry and dynamic properties.
- **Inadequate Handling of Interactions**: In complex, "contact-rich" scenarios, such as human hands manipulating objects, the models frequently fail to capture the intricate 3D spatial relationships and movements. This results in dislocated limbs, distorted objects, or unnatural interactions.
- **Limited Physical Plausibility**: The generated videos often violate basic physical commonsense, indicating a deficiency in the model's understanding of how solids behave, retain their shape, or respond to forces.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Inject 3D Knowledge into Video Diffusion Models**: The core objective is to move beyond 2D pixel-based learning by integrating a partial 3D representation into the video generation pipeline. This involves augmenting 2D videos with out-of-plane 3D information, specifically through 3D point trajectories, without requiring full 3D reconstruction. The aim is for the model to learn not just the appearance but also the underlying 3D geometry and dynamics of objects.
2. **Develop a 3D-Aware Video Dataset (PointVid)**: To facilitate the injection of 3D knowledge, a dedicated dataset is required. An objective is to curate "PointVid," a 3D-aware video dataset that augments standard 2D videos with pixel-aligned 3D point trajectories and corresponding 3D dynamic states, specifically for segmented foreground objects.
3. **Enhance Generative Capabilities through Fine-tuning**: Another objective is to fine-tune existing latent diffusion models using the PointVid dataset. This process aims to extend their generative capabilities to encompass 3D shape and motion, thereby improving the model's capacity to understand and synthesize physically coherent object behavior.
4. **Regularize Generated Shape and Motion with 3D Priors**: The work aims to leverage the newly incorporated 3D information to regularize the generation process. This objective involves introducing specific regularization terms that guide the movement of generated 3D points, enforcing properties such as reconstruction fidelity, temporal smoothness, and local shape preservation (rigidity). This regularization is intended to implicitly improve the quality of the generated RGB videos by mitigating non-physical artifacts.
5. **Improve RGB Video Quality and Visual Plausibility**: Ultimately, the motivation is to enhance the overall quality of generated RGB videos, specifically by alleviating common issues like object morphing and unnatural deformations, and ensuring visually plausible outcomes, especially in scenarios involving complex object interactions.
6. **Ensure Seamless Integrability**: The approach is designed to be agnostic to specific diffusion denoiser architectures, enabling its integration into various existing video diffusion models to broadly improve their visual plausibility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates 3D geometry and dynamic awareness into video generation through a three-component pipeline: a novel 3D-aware video dataset, a joint training approach for latent diffusion models, and a 3D regularization strategy.

#### 3.1. PointVid Dataset Generation

The creation of the PointVid dataset addresses the challenge of providing 3D information in a format suitable for video diffusion models while maintaining alignment with 2D pixel data.
- **Representation Choice**: Instead of using explicit 3D representations like meshes or dense point clouds, which can have variable sizes and complicate diffusion training, the authors opted for a "pseudo-3D" representation. This involves tracking the movement of specific 2D pixels in 3D space, treating their 3D coordinates (x, y, z) as additional channels. This approach yields a point tracking tensor `P` of shape `[T, H, W, 3]`, directly matching the dimensions of the RGB video `V` (`[T, H, W, 3]`). The use of (u, v, d) (2D pixel coordinates and depth) further simplifies alignment, as (u, v) directly correspond to pixel positions in the RGB video.
- **Workflow**:
 1. **Reference Frame and Segmentation**: For each input video, the first frame is extracted as a reference. Semantic segmentation is performed on this reference frame using Grounded-SAM-2, guided by object-level labels inferred from the video's caption via LMDeploy. This process generates masks for foreground objects.
 2. **Pixel Sampling and 3D Tracking**: Pixels are randomly sampled on the reference image, with a bias towards those within the foreground object masks. These sampled pixels are then tracked throughout the video in 3D Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z) using SpaTracker. Sparse tracking (downsample=1, gridsize=80) is employed for efficiency while ensuring a sufficient number of tracked points.
 3. **Post-processing (Algorithm 1)**:
 - **Noise Filtering**: A Kalman Filter is applied to the raw 3D point tracking data to reduce high-frequency temporal noise, thus recovering the primary motion.
 - **Shape Alignment and Interpolation**: The tracked data, initially `T x N x 3` (where N is the number of sampled points), is reshaped and aligned to `T x H x W x 3`. Pixels corresponding to the background (outside the foreground mask) are set to zero. For foreground pixels that were not explicitly tracked (due to sparse sampling), their 3D trajectories are approximated by interpolating from their three nearest tracked pixels, determined by a KDTree search based on their initial (u, v) coordinates in the first frame.

#### 3.2. Point Augmentation in Diffusion Denoiser

This stage focuses on enabling the video model to jointly learn and generate both RGB video and its corresponding 3D point representation.
- **Joint Input Formulation**: The processed 3D point tensor `P` is concatenated with the RGB video tensor `V` along the channel dimension, forming an augmented tensor `VP` of shape `[T, H, W, 6]`. This `VP` is then fed as input to the latent diffusion model's denoiser.
- **Fine-tuning Strategy**: Instead of training from scratch, a pretrained video diffusion model is fine-tuned. This approach is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated with both UNet-based (I2VGen-XL) and DiT-based (CogVideoX 1.5) denoisers.
- **Weight Initialization**: To leverage pretrained RGB weights, the existing weights are loaded for the initial 3 RGB channels, and new projection layers for the additional 3 point channels are initialized with zeros.
- **Conditioning**: The model is conditioned on a text-image prompt, consistent with the base models. The initial RGB frame is used as the conditioning image, repeated to prompt the generation of both video and point tracking, ensuring the model does not rely on initial point positions during inference.
- **Cross-Attention Mechanism**: A cross-attention layer is introduced at the input of the latent diffusion model. This layer facilitates alignment between the two modalities (color and spatial location) by allowing them to attend to each other across their respective channel dimensions. It involves two passes where each modality alternately serves as the query, with the other providing the key and value.
- **Training Process**: The standard diffusion training pipeline is followed: `VP` is encoded into a latent space `z`, noise `ϵ` is added to obtain `z_t`, and the denoiser predicts this noise. DDIM is used for sampling.

#### 3.3. Point Regularization

To refine the generated results and mitigate non-physical artifacts, regularization terms are applied to the 3D point tracking component during training.
- **Noise-free Stage Recovery**: At each training step, a denoised latent `z_0` is iteratively recovered from the noisy latent `z_t` using DDIM denoising. Gradient checkpointing is employed to manage memory by freezing gradient flow except during the final step. The `z_0` is then decoded into an explicit 3D point trajectory `˜P`.
- **Regularization Losses**:
 1. **Reconstruction Loss (`L_recon`)**: This loss (Equation 1) aims to reduce high-frequency noise and enhance the fidelity of the generated point clouds. It comprises three terms:
 - L1 norm between the generated `˜Pτ` and the ground truth `Pτ` for generation fidelity.
 - L1 norm between consecutive time steps `˜Pτ` and `˜Pτ-1` to encourage temporal smoothness (penalizing velocity changes).
 - L1 norm between `˜Pτ`, `˜Pτ-1`, and `˜Pτ-2` to enforce acceleration smoothness (discouraging abrupt changes in velocity). Weights `c_i` are adjusted to balance the terms.
 2. **Rigidity Loss (`L_rigid`)**: This loss (Equation 2) addresses macroscopic shape deformation by enforcing local shape preservation. A kNN search is performed on the reference frame (`˜P0`) to identify local neighbor pairs. The loss penalizes changes in the distance between these pairs across different time steps, promoting the conservation of local rigidity.
- **Joint Optimization**: The regularization losses (`L_recon` and `L_rigid`) are jointly optimized with the standard diffusion denoiser loss `L_diff` using weighted coefficients (`λ`s) as shown in Equation 3. To prevent geometric regularization from dominating semantic aspects and to preserve larger motion dynamics, regularization loss is applied less frequently (e.g., once every 5 iterations).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed method involved both qualitative and quantitative assessments, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving the physical understanding and visual plausibility of generated videos.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **Comparison with Base Models (Figure 5, 7)**: Qualitative comparisons against baseline models, I2VGen-XL (UNet-based) and CogVideoX 1.5 (DiT-based), revealed that while base models frequently produced artifacts such as object morphing, unnatural deformations (e.g., distorted cars, gymnasts, or power saws), the proposed method consistently mitigated these issues. The generated videos exhibited smoother transitions in object shape and motion, leading to more visually plausible outputs.
- **Task-Oriented Videos (Figure 1, 8)**: In contact-rich, task-oriented scenarios (e.g., human hands interacting with objects like a shoelace, a container, or food), existing models (SVD, DynamiCrafter, LTX Video, I2VGen-XL, CogVideoX 1.5) often struggled to capture localized details, resulting in severe distortion or blurring of hands and objects. The proposed 3D regularization approach effectively preserved the shapes of both hands and objects, ensuring smooth and consistent interactions throughout the video, which represents a notable improvement.
- **General Categories (Figure 9)**: Across various non-specific categories including humans, animals, and general objects, the method consistently demonstrated improved shape and motion consistency, reducing common non-physical artifacts.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

- **VBench Evaluation (Table 1, 4)**: Using the VBench benchmark, the fine-tuned models generally outperformed their respective baselines across several metrics. Notably, the proposed method showed improvements in subject consistency (SC), background consistency (BC), and motion smoothness (MS). Aesthetic quality (AQ) and imaging quality (IQ) were also maintained or slightly improved.
- **VideoPhy Evaluation (Table 1)**: To assess adherence to real-world physics, the VideoPhy benchmark was utilized, which provides a physical commonsense (PC) score. The proposed models achieved higher PC scores compared to their baselines and other tested models, indicating a better alignment with physical principles such as solid object shape and size preservation. For instance, the UNet-based model scored 0.37805 compared to I2VGen-XL's 0.32876, and the DiT-based model scored 0.41384 compared to CogVideoX 1.5's 0.36549.
- **3D Point Trajectory Accuracy (Table 2)**: An evaluation of the mean squared error (MSE) of predicted 3D point trajectories demonstrated the efficacy of the 3D augmentation and regularization. An untrained model exhibited an MSE of 0.2125. After the augmentation stage, without regularization, the MSE dropped significantly to 0.0031. With the full regularization pipeline, the MSE was further reduced to 0.0020, indicating that the network effectively learns to predict 3D point trajectories and that regularization refines this prediction.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies conducted on the UNet-based model, evaluated qualitatively (Figure 6, 7) and quantitatively (Table 3), highlighted the contribution of each component:
- **Point Augmentation**: Training with RGB video only (Figure 6(i), Table 3 "No PA") resulted in lower subject and background consistency. The addition of point augmentation alone (Figure 6(ii)) injected 3D awareness and improved shape consistency.
- **Point Regularization**: The full model with point regularization (Figure 6(iii)) further enhanced quality by optimizing point generation and implicitly guiding RGB generation. Removing point regularization ("No PR" in Table 3) led to a drop in consistency metrics.
- **Channel Cross-Attention**: Without the channel-wise cross-attention mechanism ("No CS" in Table 3), video quality degraded (Figure 6(iv)), underscoring its role in ensuring cross-dimensional and cross-modality alignment.
- **Diffusion Loss**: Omitting the standard diffusion loss during joint optimization ("No DL" in Table 3, Figure 6(v)) caused severe degradation in results, indicating that 3D information could dominate semantics, leading to completely unrealistic outputs. This validates the importance of a balanced joint training strategy.

#### 4.4. User Study

A user study (Table 5) comparing the UNet model with its base (I2VGen-XL) on physical plausibility criteria (e.g., realistic object movements, absence of non-physical artifacts, consistent object properties, adherence to physics, overall coherence) indicated a strong human preference for the proposed method. Across five evaluation questions, human labelers consistently selected videos generated by the proposed method as superior in terms of physical realism and visual quality (e.g., 86.2% preferred the proposed method for physically realistic movements, while only 13.7% found more negative artifacts).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of video generation by introducing a framework that explicitly integrates 3-dimensional geometry and dynamic awareness into latent diffusion models. This approach moves beyond the limitations of purely 2D pixel-based video generation, which often struggles with producing physically plausible and visually consistent content.

The primary significance lies in its ability to **enhance the realism and coherence of generated videos**. By enabling models to "perceive" 3D shape and motion, the method effectively mitigates common artifacts such as object morphing, unnatural deformations, and inconsistencies in shape and appearance. This is particularly impactful for **contact-rich and task-oriented videos**, where accurate representation of object interactions and geometric properties is crucial. The demonstrated improvements in physical commonsense scores and qualitative evaluations, including user studies, suggest a notable advancement in addressing a persistent challenge in video synthesis.

Furthermore, the **methodological versatility** of the proposed approach is a key strength. The 3D point augmentation and regularization can be seamlessly integrated into existing video diffusion models with distinct architectural backbones (demonstrated with UNet- and DiT-based models). This indicates that the framework offers a general strategy for improving visual plausibility across various generative models, rather than being confined to a specific architecture.

The curation of the **PointVid dataset** also contributes a valuable resource to the research community. By providing pixel-aligned 3D point trajectories alongside video data, it facilitates further exploration into 3D-aware video understanding and generation.

The potential impact of this work extends to several domains:
- **Entertainment and Media**: Could enable more realistic visual effects, animation, and virtual content creation by minimizing non-physical artifacts.
- **Scientific Research and Simulation**: Improved physical plausibility in generated videos could benefit fields requiring accurate visual representations of dynamic systems, aiding in visualization and synthetic data generation for training other models.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI**: More realistic simulation of object interactions could serve as a valuable training ground for robotic manipulation tasks, where precise understanding of 3D physics is critical.

Future directions for research include exploring the use of higher-resolution and more accurate 3D point tracking techniques to further enhance 3D perception, as well as applying the framework to larger-scale base diffusion models for potentially higher generation quality. This research represents a step towards instilling a more fundamental understanding of the physical world within generative video models.
