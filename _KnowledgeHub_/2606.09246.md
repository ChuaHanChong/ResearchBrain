# SOMA: From Surface Observations to Muscle Anatomy

> [!summary] Summary
> SOMA is a person-specific model that infers dynamic 3D muscle and skin deformation from skeletal pose recovered by multi-view RGB capture. Two cascaded U-Net blendshape networks predict a muscle displacement field plus a skin residual, regularized by biomechanical priors for smoothness, stretch, tangential sliding and volume preservation. Paired with SKIM, an ArUco-suit dataset, it cuts extreme tracking error and restores volume without interpenetration.

## Problem

- Parametric body models capture only the skin surface, giving no access to the internal biomechanical structures that actually generate the observed motion.
- Anatomical reconstruction methods trained on MRI or CT scans work only for single static poses, so they lack real-world data on how muscles deform during motion.
- FEM and projective-dynamics muscle simulation is accurate but computationally expensive, non-scalable, and relies on generic physical parameters rather than in-vivo grounding.

## Method

- **SKIM** dataset: a stretchable ==ArUco-marker suit== captured in a 120-camera studio yields dense ==triangulated marker trajectories==, ground-truth 6D skeletal pose, and canonical marker-to-muscle bindings via ==normal ray casting==.
- ==Decoupled non-linear blendshapes==: one ==U-Net== maps pose features to a canonical ==muscle displacement field==, a second U-Net adds a ==skin residual offset== allowing sliding and inward compression over the muscle layer.
- ==Biomechanical priors== regularize the ill-posed disentanglement: ==area-normalized Laplacian smoothness==, ==biharmonic bending==, ==edge-stretch==, ==tangential-sliding== penalties, and ==signed-volume prism preservation== on subcutaneous and deep tissue.

## Results

- On unseen motions SOMA reaches **11.51 mm** MedPME and **19.20 mm** P90 versus LBS at **15.64** and **26.03**, halving tracking error in highly dynamic soft-tissue regions above the **25 mm** threshold.
- Subcutaneous volume deviation falls to **1.37 percent** against **2.99** for HIT and **9.37** without physical priors, while intersection ratio stays at **0.85 percent** versus HIT's **7.61 percent**.
- Ablations show U-Net beats MLP and linear blendshapes at **13.43 mm** MPME, and removing bending plus stretch energies causes the worst volume distortion at **10.54 percent**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Geometric physical priors act as structural anchors that improve fitting accuracy, not just plausibility, guiding the network toward better local minima than unconstrained marker regression.
> - Decoupling deformation into a structural layer and a sliding residual layer, with asymmetric regularization weights, is what makes volume preservation and outward bulging separable.
> - Person-specific training on high-quality tracking data matters more than architecture capacity; even linear blendshapes fit markers well, while generic templates suffer anatomical misalignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The creation of realistic and controllable digital humans constitutes a long-standing challenge in computer graphics, demanding increasing biomechanical fidelity. The broader research landscape has seen significant advancements in parametric body models, such as SMPL, which standardize the representation of human body shape and pose in a low-dimensional format, facilitating scalable human representations. Building upon these, recent vision-based approaches have focused on retrieving dynamic surface geometry from monocular or multi-view RGB inputs, bridging the gap between parametric abstractions and real-world visual data. However, a prevalent limitation of these models is their exclusive focus on the 3D surface of the skin, offering limited or no insight into the complex underlying bio-mechanical structures that generate motion.

As applications in virtual reality, sports science, and medical simulation evolve, the demand for virtual human models that extend beyond the skin surface to incorporate internal physiology has become evident. Current methods attempting to model internal anatomy face substantial limitations. Heuristic approaches, often used to fit anatomy to surface scans, rely on simplified assumptions, resulting in visually plausible but potentially anatomically inaccurate results. Conversely, data-driven methods, while capable of fitting anatomical structures using medical imaging (e.g., MRI, CT) to adapt volumetric templates (e.g., for skeletons or specific regions like the skull), are constrained by acquisition complexity, privacy concerns, and the inherent difficulty of capturing dynamic, full-body motion, especially for soft tissues. The challenge intensifies for dynamic muscle behavior due to its anisotropic nature and the practical impossibility of obtaining dynamic, full-body magnetic resonance images.

Physics-based simulations, such as those employing Finite Element Methods (FEM) or Projective Dynamics, can generate dynamic muscle behavior with high accuracy. However, these methods are often non-scalable and computationally expensive for most common applications. Some approaches attempt to achieve real-time performance by approximating physics with neural networks trained on offline FEM data or by using simplified line-actuator models like OpenSim, often sacrificing volumetric detail. A further limitation of many biomechanics frameworks is their focus solely on predicting mechanical behavior, neglecting the explicit modeling of muscle shape changes from surface observations. Consequently, there is a recognized lack of scalable methods for capturing internal muscle dynamics directly from accessible, real-world visual data, impeding the development of parametric models that accurately reflect internal shapes.

This paper addresses this gap by presenting SOMA, a novel approach that seeks to recover muscle deformations directly from visible surface observations—specifically, from skin and pose derived from multi-view RGB data. It aims to provide anatomically grounded animations without the computational complexity of traditional simulations, positioning itself as a scalable and cost-effective solution for modeling the interplay between external appearance and internal physiology.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a person-specific computational model capable of inferring spatio-temporal muscle behavior and deformation directly from visible surface signals, such as those obtained using RGB cameras. This objective is driven by several motivations stemming from the limitations of existing digital human models:

1. **Addressing Surface-Only Limitations:** Current parametric body models primarily capture the 3D surface geometry of the skin. This surface-only representation offers no direct insight into the complex biomechanical structures (e.g., muscles, bones) that underlie and generate human motion. As advanced applications in virtual reality, sports science, medical simulation, and entertainment demand greater biological fidelity, models that can represent and animate internal anatomy are increasingly necessary.

2. **Overcoming Computational Expense of Traditional Simulations:** Traditional soft-tissue simulations, particularly those based on Finite Element Methods (FEM), are known for their accuracy in modeling biomechanical structures. However, they are computationally intensive and non-scalable, rendering them impractical for real-time or widespread applications requiring dynamic internal anatomy. The motivation is to achieve anatomically plausible animations without incurring such high computational costs.

3. **Bridging the Gap Between Biomechanical Tools and Observable Anatomy:** Existing biomechanical tools can simulate muscular forces and activations but often do not explicitly model the corresponding changes in external shape. This disconnect restricts the ability to correlate internal muscle activations with actual observable anatomical deformations on the skin surface, limiting their utility in applications that require visual realism.

4. **Circumventing Limitations of Medical Imaging for Dynamic Data:** While medical imaging techniques like MRI and CT scans provide accurate anatomical data, their acquisition is complex, raises privacy concerns, and is particularly challenging for capturing dynamic, full-body motion, especially for anisotropic soft tissues like muscles. This limitation hinders data-driven approaches that rely on dynamic ground-truth internal anatomy.

5. **Solving the Inverse Problem:** The research is motivated by a novel inverse problem: inferring internal muscle deformations directly from external, visible surface observations (i.e., from skin shape and skeletal pose). This approach aims to bypass the need for direct internal measurement, which is often invasive or impractical.

To address these motivations, the authors present SOMA, a person-specific model designed to infer muscle deformations from surface signals. They also introduce SKIM, a subject-specific soft-tissue deformation dataset. The specific contributions of this work are threefold:
- **SKIM Dataset:** A multi-view RGB dataset designed to bridge surface observations and internal anatomy through high-resolution, spatio-temporal marker tracking and ground-truth pose data.
- **SOMA Model:** A parametric model that recovers muscle deformation from surface signals. It utilizes decoupled blendshapes supervised by physics-based geometric priors to ensure anatomical plausibility.
- **Propagation Framework:** A framework that propagates the learned volumetric deformations from a unified muscle layer to individual muscle meshes, enabling interactive internal anatomy in standard graphics engines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SOMA framework operates in three primary stages: a vision-based data acquisition framework, an end-to-end data-driven pipeline for deformation learning, and a mechanism for propagating learned deformations to individual muscle meshes.

### 4. SKIM Dataset (Skin-to-Internal Muscle Dataset)

To facilitate the inference of internal muscle deformation from RGB videos, which is an inherently ill-posed problem due to its reliance on indirect skin surface observations, the SKIM dataset was developed. This dataset couples high-precision, temporally consistent skin annotations with an individualized volumetric muscle template.

- **Capture Setup:** A custom skin-tight suit embedded with ArUco markers was utilized to capture dynamic skin deformations. This suit ensures robust spatio-temporal tracking of dense surface correspondences, consistent muscle-relative placement, and minimized occlusions. A 120-camera motion capture studio, operating at 25 Hz, captured both suit marker detections and ground-truth 3D skeletal poses. A separate 140-camera scanner reconstructed static body mesh templates.
- **Canonical Point Correspondences:** A subject-specific 3D canonical anatomical template (P_bind) was constructed, encoding the marker layout of the suit. This involved generating a high-resolution static skin mesh (S_bind) of the subject wearing the suit using the body scanner. Marker IDs and corner positions were detected by unwrapping the texture of S_bind into a 2D atlas, performing ArUco detection on the atlas, and re-projecting the detected corners back onto the 3D mesh. Barycentric interpolation was used to compute the 3D location of each marker corner on the mesh.
- **Internal Anatomical Modeling:** From the outer skin mesh S_bind, an internal canonical anatomy was reconstructed using volumetric fitting. This process yielded three additional representations: a global muscle mesh (M_bind) approximating aggregated muscle volume, a set of individualized muscle meshes ({M_m_bind}) for specific muscles, and a skeleton mesh (B_bind). All these layers shared the same topology as S_bind. A mapping function (ϕ) was established to associate each marker with its corresponding muscle by casting a ray from the marker position along the negative surface normal of its underlying triangle in S_bind, identifying the first intersection point with an individual muscle mesh.
- **Marker Tracking:** For each video frame, visible marker corners were detected in the 2D image plane of each camera. Corners identified across multiple views were triangulated to obtain optimal 3D coordinates (p*_{k,t}) by minimizing multi-view reprojection error. Post-processing steps, including temporal smoothing and consensus filtering, were applied to yield refined, temporally coherent ground-truth point clouds (P_GT(t)).
- **Residual Estimation:** To quantify non-rigid soft-tissue deformations, the static canonical point cloud P_bind was articulated using the ground-truth skeletal pose (θ_t) via Linear Blend Skinning (LBS), resulting in kinematic predictions p_k(θ_t). The deviation of the observed marker positions (p*_{k,t}) from these kinematic predictions was then isolated as pose-corrective displacements (δ_k,t) in the canonical space. This was achieved by computing the world-space residual and applying an inverse blended transformation. These displacements formed a sparse, temporally evolving field of canonical displacements D^(t), serving as ground truth for the muscle deformation model.

### 5. SOMA Model

The SOMA model builds on corrective pose-dependent blendshapes but extends them to a volumetric, multi-layer anatomy representation, incorporating the M_bind, B_bind, and S_bind meshes.

- **Decoupled Non-Linear Blendshapes Model:** To capture complex soft tissue dynamics, deformation was decoupled into interdependent structural muscle and volumetric skin layers. Two U-Net architectures, Ψ_M and Ψ_S, were employed to predict displacement fields.
 - **Muscle Layer Deformation:** Ψ_M computes a displacement field D_musc from pose features (relative rotation matrices minus identity). The deformed canonical muscle configuration (M_pred) is M_bind + D_musc.
 - **Skin Layer and Residual Offset:** Skin deformation (D_skin) is formulated as a residual function of the muscle layer: D_skin = D_musc + D_res, where D_res is a residual offset predicted by Ψ_S. This residual allows for soft tissue compression and sliding, providing degrees of freedom not present in rigid muscle following. The final posed skin mesh (S_pred) is obtained by transforming the canonical skin mesh (S_bind + D_skin) via LBS.
- **Muscle and Skin Surface Supervision:** The model is trained end-to-end by minimizing a combined energy function E_total = λ_data * E_data + E_reg. The data term E_data calculates the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted skin residuals (interpolated from D_skin using barycentric coordinates) and the ground-truth canonical marker residuals (δ_k,t). This canonical loss ensures invariance to global pose, forcing the network to learn local non-rigid deformations.
- **Bio-mechanically Inspired Deformation Priors (E_reg):** To resolve the inherent ambiguity in disentangling muscle and skin deformations and to enforce physical plausibility, several biomechanical priors are introduced as a regularization term E_reg:
 - **Spatial Smoothness (E_smooth):** An area-normalized Laplacian regularization is applied to both muscle and skin displacement fields to ensure smooth deformations. A higher weight for skin smoothness prevents high-frequency artifacts.
 - **Bending Resistance (E_bi):** A second-order Laplacian (biharmonic) regularization penalizes high-curvature deformations, enforcing wide, organic deformations characteristic of thick soft tissue and preventing localized vertex spikes.
 - **Surface Stretching (E_stretch):** Deviations of edge lengths from their canonical rest state are penalized to preserve structural integrity and prevent unrealistic expansion.
 - **Tangential Sliding (E_tang):** The tangential component of displacement relative to the canonical vertex normal is penalized. This ensures muscles remain structurally anchored, deforming primarily outwards, while allowing the skin to slide more freely.
 - **Soft Tissue Volume Preservation (E_vol):** To mimic the incompressibility of human soft tissue, volume preservation is enforced on the subcutaneous layer (skin-to-muscle) and deep tissue (muscle-to-bone). Deviations from rest volumes of volumetric prisms between these layers are penalized. This constraint operates in canonical space for the skin-to-muscle volume and posed space for the muscle-to-bone volume, aiming to counteract LBS compression. Signed volumes are used to prevent geometric inversions.
- **Individual Muscle Animation:** The learned canonical displacements from the unified muscle layer (M_bind) are propagated to the high-resolution individual muscle meshes via a pre-computed barycentric binding mechanism, ensuring physical synchrony during animation.

### 6. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both quantitative and qualitative evaluations of the SOMA model, demonstrating its ability to balance high-fidelity tracking with anatomical constraints compared to kinematic baselines and volumetric estimators.

### 7. Quantitative Evaluation

Experiments on unseen validation sequences, detailed in Table 2, assessed Global Surface Tracking (MPME, MedPME, P90), Dynamic Region Error (DRE), Intersection Ratio, and Volume Preservation.

- **Overall Performance:** SOMA exhibits competitive performance in global tracking while significantly improving anatomical plausibility. It achieves a global mean error (MPME) comparable to LBS, but crucially, improves the median tracking error (MedPME) and significantly reduces extreme errors (P90), indicating a more consistent and accurate deformation across the body.
- **Dynamic Region Accuracy:** In highly dynamic soft-tissue regions (identified by a threshold τ > 25 mm), where standard LBS experiences substantial degradation, SOMA reduces the tracking error by more than 50%. This indicates SOMA's effectiveness in capturing complex muscle bulging and soft-tissue dynamics that LBS cannot.
- **Anatomical Plausibility (Intersection and Volume):**
 - **LBS:** While LBS shows a low intersection ratio (0.85% for S → M and 0.93% for M → B), this is attributed to the "candy-wrapper effect," where skin and deep tissue collapse together due to shared skinning weights, failing to preserve internal structures and showing volumetric joint collapse. The "∆ Vol" metric for LBS is not applicable for skin-to-muscle as it doesn't apply pose-correctives, but for muscle-to-bone it shows 18.81% deviation, indicating significant volume loss.
 - **HIT:** The state-of-the-art uncoupled volumetric predictor HIT shows significantly higher global tracking errors (MPME of 115.34 mm) due to its reliance on generic templates that lack subject-specific proportions. More critically, HIT exhibits severe anatomical collisions, with an intersection ratio of 7.61% for skin-to-muscle, indicating a failure to maintain distinct anatomical layers. It also shows substantial volume deviation (2.99% for skin-to-muscle, 1.95% for muscle-to-bone).
 - **SOMA:** SOMA successfully addresses both tracking accuracy and internal physical plausibility. It maintains a low intersection ratio (0.85% for S → M and 0.93% for M → B) while restoring isochoric volume, leading to significantly lower volume deviations (1.37% for S → M and 10.35% for M → B) compared to HIT. This indicates that SOMA's coupled formulation effectively prevents interpenetration while enforcing realistic soft-tissue behavior.

### 8. Qualitative Evaluation

Qualitative results (Figure 6 and Figure 7) demonstrate SOMA's capability to predict finer-grained dynamic deformations compared to baselines like HIT. While static volumetric baselines can produce plausible resting volumes, they tend to over-smooth high-frequency dynamics during motion. SOMA, in contrast, captures active muscle bulging (e.g., quadriceps, triceps) with detailed dynamic deformations. A real-time visualization tool built in Viser allows for interactive full-body inference, per-muscle querying, and isolated single-muscle prediction.

### 9. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of different network architectures and loss components (Tables 3 and 4).

- **Network Architecture:** Comparing Linear Blendshapes, an MLP, and a U-Net architecture, the U-Net achieved the lowest tracking error (13.43 mm MPME). However, even linear and MLP models demonstrated effectiveness in fitting surface markers when trained on person-specific, high-quality tracking data, highlighting the benefits of individual model training over generic templates.
- **Regularization Terms:**
 - **"w/o Physics":** Removing all physical constraints resulted in the worst tracking (13.71 mm MPME) and highest volume distortion (9.37% for S → M, 13.54% for M → B), underscoring the necessity of biomechanical priors.
 - **"w/o Vector" (E_smooth, E_tan):** Removing spatial smoothness and tangential sliding regularization terms unexpectedly degraded subcutaneous volume preservation. This suggests that without tangential constraints, vertices can drift laterally to minimize marker error, leading to shearing of underlying volumetric prisms and hindering effective outward bulging.
 - **"w/o E_bi, str" (Bending Resistance, Surface Stretching):** Ablating these structural tension terms caused the most severe volumetric distortion (10.54% for S → M, 13.76% for M → B). The mesh artificially satisfied volume constraints through high-frequency wrinkling instead of smooth, organic bulging, indicating that membrane tension is crucial for preventing mesh artifacts.
 - **"w/o E_vol" (Volume Preservation):** Removing the volume preservation term significantly increased subcutaneous volume error to 8.28%, confirming its importance in correcting LBS-induced volumetric artifacts.
 - **Full Model:** The full SOMA model achieved the lowest tracking error (13.43 mm MPME) and the best subcutaneous volume preservation (1.37% deviation for S → M). A higher deviation for the deep muscle layer (10.35% for M → B) was observed, which is interpreted as a realistic physical compromise where the optimizer prioritizes strict non-intersection of the bone and muscle layers over full deep-tissue inflation to prevent skeletal collisions.

### 10. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SOMA as a significant step forward in the field of digital human modeling, addressing a long-standing challenge of integrating internal anatomy with external surface appearance derived from visual data. Its significance and potential impact span several domains:

1. **Advancing Digital Human Realism:** SOMA provides a methodology to create a new generation of digital humans with enhanced biomechanical fidelity. By moving beyond surface-only representations, it offers models that capture the complex interplay between skeletal motion, muscle deformation, and skin dynamics, leading to more realistic and plausible animations. This is crucial for applications that demand a high degree of visual accuracy and biological correctness.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between Surface and Internal Anatomy:** The work is notable as one of the first attempts to recover dynamic muscle deformations directly from accessible multi-view RGB data. This capability directly addresses the limitation of existing parametric models, which typically lack explicit internal anatomical structures, and circumvents the difficulties associated with acquiring dynamic internal data through medical imaging or the computational cost of traditional physical simulations.

3. **Scalable and Cost-Effective Biomechanical Modeling:** SOMA offers a scalable and computationally efficient solution for generating anatomically grounded animations. Unlike traditional Finite Element Method (FEM) simulations, which are accurate but prohibitively expensive for many real-time or interactive applications, SOMA's data-driven, blendshape-based approach allows for interactive internal anatomy in standard graphics engines using only user-defined skeletal poses. This reduces the barriers to incorporating biomechanical realism into diverse applications.

4. **Impact on Specific Applications:**
 - **Medical Simulation and Education:** More accurate virtual anatomical models can enhance medical training, surgical planning, and patient-specific biomechanical analysis, providing a realistic representation of internal tissue interactions during movement or surgical procedures.
 - **Sports Science and Biomechanics:** Detailed insights into individual muscle deformation during athletic performance can aid in analyzing movement efficiency, injury prevention, and optimizing training regimes.
 - **Entertainment and Virtual Reality (VR):** The creation of highly realistic and controllable digital characters with plausible internal dynamics can significantly improve immersion and visual quality in film, games, and VR experiences.

5. **Introduction of the SKIM Dataset:** The creation of the SKIM dataset is a valuable contribution in itself. By providing high-resolution, spatio-temporal marker tracking, ground-truth pose, and individualized volumetric muscle templates, it offers a unique resource for future research in human surface correspondence estimation and internal anatomy inference, particularly in a dynamic context.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for several promising future directions, including the joint optimization of baseline anatomy with dynamic displacements, the development of robust markerless skin tracking methods that leverage the collected data, and the extension of the current subject-specific model towards more generalizable, person-agnostic anatomical human models.

In conclusion, SOMA represents a crucial advancement towards accessible, data-driven biomechanical digital humans, offering a practical and effective method for creating anatomically accurate virtual characters from standard visual inputs.
