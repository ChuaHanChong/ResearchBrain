# State-Covering Trajectory Stitching for Diffusion Planners

> [!summary] Summary
> SCoTS introduces a reward-free trajectory augmentation framework that systematically generates diverse, long-horizon trajectories from short segments in offline datasets using temporal embeddings and diffusion-based refinement. This framework enables diffusion planners to achieve near-optimal success rates, such as 100% in PointMaze-Giant-Stitch, and enhances the performance of other offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning algorithms.

## Problem

- Diffusion planners and offline goal-conditioned RL algorithms are constrained by the limited length, diversity, and coverage of offline training data, impeding long-horizon planning and generalization.
- Collecting exhaustive, long-horizon offline data for complex real-world scenarios is often impractical and prohibitively expensive.
- Existing trajectory stitching approaches struggle with maintaining dynamic consistency and often rely on extrinsic rewards, limiting their applicability in sparse-reward environments.

## Method

- **SCoTS** learns a temporal ==distance-preserving latent== embedding to accurately represent state reachability, enabling efficient identification of viable trajectory segments.
- It iteratively stitches short trajectory segments from offline data, guiding the process with a combination of directional exploration and novelty to systematically cover and expand the latent state space.
- A diffusion-based model refines the stitched connections to ensure dynamic consistency, followed by an inverse dynamics model to infer actions for the augmented state trajectories.

## Results

- The SCoTS-augmented Hierarchical Diffusion planner achieved significantly higher success rates on OGBench tasks, reaching **100%** in PointMaze-Giant-Stitch and **87%** in AntMaze-Giant-Stitch, averaging **96.8%** across tasks.
- SCoTS-augmented datasets consistently boosted the performance of other offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning algorithms, with HIQL's average success rate increasing from **36.4%** to **73.4%**.
- Diffusion-based refinement proved critical by reducing Dynamic Mean Squared Error at stitching points, ensuring dynamic consistency, and enhancing the framework's robustness across diverse environments and low-level controller horizons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a temporal distance-preserving latent embedding is essential for robustly identifying dynamically coherent trajectory segments for stitching, particularly in complex, high-dimensional environments.
> - Combining directional progress with a novelty score during trajectory stitching effectively generates diverse and extensive state-space coverage, enabling more comprehensive long-horizon planning.
> - A diffusion-based refinement step is critical for ensuring dynamic consistency at stitched connections, substantially reducing errors and improving the feasibility of generated long-horizon plans.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) frequently encounters challenges in long-horizon planning, particularly in real-world applications where agents must execute hundreds of steps with sparse or delayed feedback. Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) attempts to address this by learning environment dynamics from data, offering benefits like sample efficiency and generalization. However, traditional autoregressive models in MBRL suffer from compounding errors, leading to the generation of suboptimal or infeasible trajectories over longer horizons.

Recently, diffusion-based generative models have emerged as a promising alternative for trajectory generation in sequential decision-making. These models, such as Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) and its successors, learn to generate entire trajectories by denoising samples from a simple prior. This approach inherently mitigates the compounding error issue present in autoregressive dynamics models and allows for flexible conditioning (e.g., goal-reaching).

Despite these advancements, diffusion planners face a fundamental limitation: their performance and generalization capabilities are closely tied to the quality, diversity, and coverage of the offline training data. Specifically, their effective planning horizon is typically bounded by the maximum trajectory length observed during training, and their ability to generalize to novel tasks is restricted by the specific behaviors and transitions represented in the dataset. Collecting exhaustive data for all possible scenarios is often prohibitively expensive.

Trajectory stitching, which involves composing novel, longer sequences from existing short segments, offers a potential solution. However, existing stitching methods often rely on extrinsic rewards for segment selection and struggle with maintaining dynamic consistency and feasibility in the stitched trajectories.

This paper introduces State-Covering Trajectory Stitching (SCoTS) as a novel approach to address these data-centric limitations. Unlike prior diffusion planner advancements that focus on architectural improvements or planning strategies, SCoTS is an *orthogonal* solution that directly augments the offline dataset. It contrasts with existing trajectory stitching methods by operating in a reward-free manner and explicitly incorporating mechanisms to ensure dynamic consistency. Furthermore, while other data augmentation methods exist for RL (e.g., pixel-based, state-based, transition-level, or reward-guided trajectory-level), SCoTS provides a reward-free, systematic, and dynamic-consistency-aware method for synthesizing diverse, long-horizon trajectories specifically designed to improve generalization for diffusion planners and other offline goal-conditioned RL algorithms. The work also builds upon research in temporal distance in RL, leveraging learned temporal distance-preserving representations, but applies them specifically to identify viable segments for trajectory stitching.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and generalization capabilities of diffusion planners and other offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL) algorithms by systematically augmenting their training data. This goal is motivated by several key challenges inherent in current planning paradigms:

1. **Limited Planning Horizon and Generalization from Offline Data:** Diffusion planners, while powerful, are fundamentally constrained by the extent and diversity of their offline training datasets. If the available data consists only of short trajectories or a limited variety of movement patterns, the planner struggles to generate coherent plans for longer horizons or novel tasks requiring different compositions of behaviors. This limits their applicability in complex real-world scenarios that demand planning over hundreds of steps.

2. **Prohibitive Cost of Data Collection:** Exhaustively collecting offline data that covers all conceivable scenarios and long-horizon tasks is often impractical and economically unfeasible. This necessitates methods that can extrapolate or synthesize new, longer trajectories from existing, shorter segments.

3. **Limitations of Existing Trajectory Stitching Approaches:** While trajectory stitching is a conceptual solution for extending planning horizons, existing methods often depend on extrinsic rewards to evaluate segment utility, making them less suitable for sparse-reward environments. Moreover, maintaining dynamic consistency and feasibility when concatenating segments remains a significant technical challenge, as simple concatenation can lead to unrealistic or unexecutable transitions.

4. **Compounding Errors in Model-Based RL:** Traditional autoregressive model-based RL approaches are prone to compounding errors during long rollouts, where small prediction inaccuracies accumulate. Diffusion planners avoid this by generating entire trajectories simultaneously, but they still require sufficient data to learn the distribution of long, feasible trajectories.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To propose State-Covering Trajectory Stitching (SCoTS), a novel reward-free trajectory augmentation framework.
- To incrementally stitch together short trajectory segments from an offline dataset to systematically generate diverse and extended trajectories.
- To learn a temporal distance-preserving latent representation that accurately captures the temporal structure of the environment, facilitating efficient segment identification.
- To develop an iterative stitching strategy guided by directional exploration and novelty to effectively cover and expand the latent state space.
- To refine stitched trajectories using a diffusion-based process to ensure dynamic consistency and feasibility.
- To empirically demonstrate that SCoTS significantly improves the performance and generalization capabilities of diffusion planners on offline goal-conditioned benchmarks, particularly those requiring extensive stitching and long-horizon reasoning.
- To show that the augmented trajectories generated by SCoTS also enhance the performance of other widely used offline goal-conditioned RL algorithms across diverse environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The State-Covering Trajectory Stitching (SCoTS) framework is designed as a reward-free trajectory augmentation method that iteratively constructs long and diverse trajectories from an offline dataset. This is achieved through a three-stage process:

1. **Temporal Distance-Preserving Embedding:**
 The first stage focuses on learning a robust representation of the state space that facilitates the identification of temporally viable trajectory segments. Standard Euclidean distances in raw state space can be misleading for temporal coherence, as dynamically distant states might appear geometrically close. To address this, SCoTS learns a temporal distance-preserving embedding, $\phi: S \rightarrow Z$, which maps raw states into a latent space $Z$. In this latent space, the Euclidean distance between embedded states, $||\phi(s) - \phi(g)||₂$, is trained to approximate the optimal temporal distance $d^*(s, g)$ (the minimum number of steps to transition from $s$ to $g$).
 This embedding is parameterized through a goal-conditioned value function $V(s, g) := -||\phi(s) - \phi(g)||₂$. The function is trained on the offline dataset $D$ using a temporal difference objective, specifically an expectile loss inspired by Implicit Q-Learning. This approach leverages local reachability information from the offline data to construct a latent space where temporal proximity is preserved, making it effective for local segment retrieval during stitching without requiring a globally perfect metric representation of the MDP.

2. **Directional and Exploratory Trajectory Stitching:**
 Once the temporal distance-preserving embedding $\phi$ is learned, SCoTS iteratively builds extended trajectories. The process begins by randomly sampling an initial trajectory segment $\tau_{comp}$ from the offline dataset. To encourage diversity and coverage of the state space, a random unit vector `z` is sampled as a fixed latent exploration direction for each new trajectory rollout.
 At each stitching iteration, SCoTS performs the following steps:
 - **Candidate Segment Retrieval:** It identifies $k$ candidate segments from the offline dataset $D$ whose initial states are the nearest neighbors in the latent space to the endpoint of the currently composed trajectory $\text{end}(\tau_{comp})$. The search uses the Euclidean distance in the latent space $\Vert\phi(\text{end}(\tau_{comp})) - \phi(s_{1,j})\Vert_2$. For efficient retrieval in large datasets, an Inverted File (IVF) index from the Faiss library is employed.
 - **Exploratory Segment Selection:** From the retrieved candidates, the best segment $\tau_{\text{best}}$ is selected based on a composite score $S_j = P_j + \beta N_j$. This score balances two objectives:
 - **Progress Score ($P_j$):** Measures the alignment of the candidate segment's latent-space direction (from its start to its end) with the randomly sampled global exploration direction $z$. A higher progress score indicates movement along the desired exploration path: $P_j = \langle \phi(\text{end}(\tau_j)) - \phi(s_{1,j}), z \rangle$.
 - **Novelty Score ($N_j$):** Promotes exploration of new state regions. It is calculated using a non-parametric particle-based estimator to estimate the entropy of the candidate segment's endpoint relative to previously visited latent states within the current trajectory rollout. A higher novelty score indicates that the segment leads to a less-explored region of the latent space.
 The parameter $\beta$ controls the trade-off between directional progress and novelty. The segment with the highest $S_j$ is selected for stitching.

3. **Diffusion-based Stitching Refinement:**
 After a candidate segment is selected, the point where it connects to the composite trajectory might still contain dynamic inconsistencies due to the approximate nature of the latent space and the local segment selection. To ensure smooth and dynamically feasible transitions, SCoTS incorporates a diffusion-based refinement step. A dedicated diffusion model, termed the "stitcher" ($p_{\text{stitcher}}_\theta$), is trained to generate intermediate states conditioned on the boundary states of the adjacent segments (the end state of $\tau_{comp}$ and the end state of $\tau_{best}$). This process generates a refined trajectory segment $\tau'$ that smoothly bridges the two parts, preserving dynamic coherence.
 Finally, an inverse dynamics model $a_t = f_\psi(s_t, s_{t+1})$, trained on the original offline dataset $D$, is used to infer the action sequences for these newly stitched and refined state trajectories. The resulting state-action trajectories are then aggregated into the augmented dataset $D_{aug}$.

**Experimental Validation:**
The methodology is evaluated on the OGBench benchmark, including PointMaze and AntMaze environments with "Stitch" datasets (short, sparse trajectories requiring significant stitching) and "Explore" datasets (noisy, low-quality exploratory trajectories). The primary evaluation metric is success rate. SCoTS is used to augment the training data for a Hierarchical Diffusion (HD) planner, which uses a high-level diffusion model for sparse waypoints and a low-level controller for dense trajectory execution. Ablation studies are conducted on key components, including the temporal embedding, novelty weighting, stitching refinement, and the impact of replanning. Further experiments assess SCoTS's impact on general offline GCRL algorithms and its applicability to dense-reward D4RL benchmarks and noisy robot navigation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that State-Covering Trajectory Stitching (SCoTS) significantly improves the performance and generalization capabilities of diffusion planners and other offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL) algorithms by generating diverse and extended trajectories.

1. **Diversity and State Coverage:**
 - Analysis of the novelty weighting parameter $\beta$ in the PointMaze-Giant-Stitch environment illustrated SCoTS's ability to promote diverse state-space coverage.
 - When $\beta=0$ (no novelty), stitched trajectories primarily follow directional guidance, resulting in limited coverage but distinct paths.
 - With a moderate $\beta=2$, trajectories exhibited a balanced trade-off between directional progress and coverage, achieving substantial diversity.
 - A higher $\beta=20$ led to broad state-space coverage but with less distinct and more overlapping paths. Based on these observations, $\beta=2$ was adopted consistently.

2. **Enhanced Diffusion Planning:**
 - SCoTS consistently improved the performance of the Hierarchical Diffusion (HD) planner across all OGBench tasks, achieving near-optimal success rates.
 - The performance gains were particularly pronounced in larger and more complex maze environments (e.g., "Giant" variants), where SCoTS augmentation allowed the HD planner to reach success rates of 100% in PointMaze-Giant-Stitch and 87% in AntMaze-Giant-Stitch, substantially outperforming baselines which often achieved 0%.
 - In the challenging AntMaze-Explore dataset, characterized by noisy and short-range exploratory trajectories, SCoTS augmentation significantly enhanced the planner's ability to synthesize coherent, long-range, goal-directed plans.
 - Quantitatively, SCoTS-augmented HD achieved an average success rate of 96.8% across all evaluated tasks, significantly higher than vanilla HD (25.4%) and other strong baselines like Compositional Diffuser (CD) (77.9%) and Generative Skill Chaining (GSC) (65.3%).

3. **Improvements for Offline GCRL Algorithms:**
 - When widely used offline GCRL algorithms (GCIQL, CRL, HIQL) were retrained on SCoTS-augmented datasets, their performance consistently improved compared to training on original data or data augmented by SynthER (a transition-level augmentation method).
 - For example, HIQL's average success rate increased from 36.4% on original data to 73.4% with SCoTS-augmented data.
 - This indicates that SCoTS's trajectory-level augmentation, which considers long-term dynamics and state-space diversity, provides effective supervision for learning robust stitching and long-horizon planning capabilities, even for non-diffusion-based methods.

4. **Ablation Studies and Component Effectiveness:**
 - **Low-level Controller Horizon:** SCoTS maintained strong and robust performance across various low-level controller horizon lengths, indicating that the generated subgoals are highly feasible.
 - **Diffusion-based Stitching Refinement:** This step was found to be critical. It substantially reduced Dynamic Mean Squared Error (Dynamic MSE) at stitching points, ensuring dynamic consistency. Without refinement, significant performance degradation was observed for both diffusion planners and GCRL algorithms, especially for methods sensitive to dynamic validity.
 - **Replanning:** SCoTS consistently outperformed the best baseline (CD) even without replanning, suggesting inherent reliability. The performance difference with and without replanning was minimal, further supporting the robustness of SCoTS-augmented plans.
 - **Temporal Distance-Preserving Embedding (Appendix C):** The learned temporal embedding was crucial. Stitching guided by raw state-space distances resulted in significant performance drops, particularly in high-dimensional and complex environments like AntMaze, highlighting the necessity of a metric that reflects true reachability.
 - **Sensitivity to Novelty Weight $\beta$ (Appendix C):** While directional stitching alone offered improvements, balancing it with the novelty score ($\beta > 0$) led to substantial further gains, demonstrating the importance of exploratory detours for finding valid paths in the imperfect latent space.
 - **Sensitivity to Sub-trajectory Length H (Appendix C):** Performance on Stitch datasets was robust. On noisy Explore datasets, shorter segments performed better, suggesting that SCoTS can be more selective in identifying high-quality, temporally extended segments from low-quality data.

5. **Broader Applicability:**
 - **D4RL Benchmarks (Appendix C):** SCoTS augmentation consistently improved the performance of the original Diffuser on standard dense-reward offline RL benchmarks (e.g., Hopper, Walker2d), showing its applicability beyond goal-conditioned tasks and its ability to generate novel, reward-increasing behaviors.
 - **Noisy and Partially Observable Navigation (Appendix C):** SCoTS enhanced performance on mobile robot navigation tasks in realistic, large-scale environments with fragmented and noisy data (LiDAR observations converted to latent states), demonstrating its robustness and practical utility for robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The State-Covering Trajectory Stitching (SCoTS) framework presents a significant advancement in the field of diffusion-based planning and offline reinforcement learning. Its contributions have several implications for future research and real-world applications:

1. **Overcoming Data Scarcity Limitations:** SCoTS directly addresses a fundamental limitation of diffusion planners and other offline RL methods—their reliance on high-quality, diverse, and long-horizon training data. By systematically generating extended and diverse trajectories from existing short segments, SCoTS provides a method to mitigate data scarcity, thereby broadening the applicability of these powerful planning tools to more complex tasks. This reduces the prohibitive cost and logistical challenges associated with exhaustive data collection.

2. **Enhanced Generalization and Long-Horizon Reasoning:** The capability of SCoTS to produce augmented datasets that enable diffusion planners to generalize to tasks outside their original training distribution and plan over significantly longer horizons is a key contribution. This is crucial for real-world robotic control and decision-making systems, where agents must often navigate novel situations and execute sequences of actions spanning many steps.

3. **Reward-Free Trajectory Augmentation:** A notable aspect of SCoTS is its reward-free nature. This means the augmentation process does not rely on extrinsic reward signals, making it particularly valuable for environments with sparse or delayed rewards, or where defining a precise reward function is difficult. This expands the utility of data augmentation to a wider range of challenging RL problems.

4. **Improved Dynamic Consistency:** The integration of a diffusion-based refinement step to smooth stitching points between trajectory segments is critical. This mechanism ensures that the augmented trajectories are not only longer and more diverse but also dynamically consistent and feasible. This enhances the reliability of plans generated from SCoTS-augmented data, which is paramount for deployment in safety-critical systems.

5. **Broad Applicability Across RL Algorithms:** While primarily designed for diffusion planners, the demonstration that SCoTS-augmented data also significantly improves the performance of various offline goal-conditioned RL algorithms underscores its broad utility. This suggests that the principles of state-covering trajectory generation can serve as a valuable preprocessing step for a wider spectrum of offline RL approaches.

6. **Practical Relevance for Robotics:** The evaluation on noisy, partially observable mobile robot navigation tasks further highlights the practical relevance of SCoTS. Its ability to perform robustly in environments with low-quality and fragmented data indicates its potential for improving learning and planning capabilities in real-world robotic systems.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges certain limitations. The iterative stitching and diffusion-based refinement processes can introduce significant computational overhead during data augmentation. Furthermore, the current temporal distance-preserving embeddings do not inherently capture asymmetric temporal distances, which might limit their effectiveness in highly asymmetric or disconnected Markov Decision Processes (e.g., tasks with irreversible actions or sparse connectivity). Future work could explore more computationally efficient augmentation strategies and methods to learn asymmetric temporal representations.

**Societal Impact:**
The research advances long-horizon reasoning in reinforcement learning. The authors do not identify direct negative societal impacts but emphasize the importance for practitioners to carefully assess safety and reliability before deploying such methods in real-world scenarios.
