# State-Covering Trajectory Stitching for Diffusion Planners

> [!summary] Summary
> SCoTS introduces a reward-free trajectory augmentation framework that systematically generates diverse, long-horizon trajectories from short segments in offline datasets using temporal embeddings and diffusion-based refinement. This framework enables diffusion planners to achieve near-optimal success rates, such as 100% in PointMaze-Giant-Stitch, and enhances the performance of other offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning algorithms.

## Problem

- Diffusion planners and offline goal-conditioned RL algorithms are constrained by the limited length, diversity, and coverage of offline training data, impeding long-horizon planning and generalization.
- Collecting exhaustive, long-horizon offline data for complex real-world scenarios is often impractical and prohibitively expensive.
- Existing trajectory stitching approaches struggle with maintaining dynamic consistency and often rely on extrinsic rewards, limiting their applicability in sparse-reward environments.

## Method

- **SCoTS** learns a temporal ==distance-preserving latent== embedding to accurately represent state reachability, enabling efficient identification of viable trajectory segments.
- It iteratively stitches short trajectory segments from offline data, guiding the process with a combination of directional exploration and novelty to systematically cover and expand the latent state space.
- A diffusion-based model refines the stitched connections to ensure dynamic consistency, followed by an inverse dynamics model to infer actions for the augmented state trajectories.

## Results

- The SCoTS-augmented Hierarchical Diffusion planner achieved significantly higher success rates on OGBench tasks, reaching **100%** in PointMaze-Giant-Stitch and **87%** in AntMaze-Giant-Stitch, averaging **96.8%** across tasks.
- SCoTS-augmented datasets consistently boosted the performance of other offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning algorithms, with HIQL's average success rate increasing from **36.4%** to **73.4%**.
- Diffusion-based refinement proved critical by reducing Dynamic Mean Squared Error at stitching points, ensuring dynamic consistency, and enhancing the framework's robustness across diverse environments and low-level controller horizons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a temporal distance-preserving latent embedding is essential for robustly identifying dynamically coherent trajectory segments for stitching, particularly in complex, high-dimensional environments.
> - Combining directional progress with a novelty score during trajectory stitching effectively generates diverse and extensive state-space coverage, enabling more comprehensive long-horizon planning.
> - A diffusion-based refinement step is critical for ensuring dynamic consistency at stitched connections, substantially reducing errors and improving the feasibility of generated long-horizon plans.
