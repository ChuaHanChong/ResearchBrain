# NaVILA: Legged Robot Vision-Language-Action Model for Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> NaVILA introduces a two-level Vision-Language-Action model that enables legged robots to navigate complex real-world environments by interpreting natural language instructions, leveraging training from human touring videos. The framework demonstrates high success rates on physical quadrupedal and humanoid robots and achieves substantial performance gains on established Vision-Language Navigation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Translating high-level natural language navigation instructions into precise low-level joint actions for complex, dynamic legged robots in real-world environments.
- Generalization limitations of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which often struggle to adapt across robot platforms or perform robustly in diverse, unseen real-world conditions.
- Inefficiencies and environmental vulnerabilities (e.g., strong sunlight affecting depth sensors) in training robust locomotion policies for legged robots.

## Method

- A two-level framework decoupling high-level VLA (==Vision-Language-Action==) reasoning from low-level visual locomotion execution, using mid-level language instructions as an intermediate.
- A high-level VLA component (based on VILA) trained on a diverse dataset including novel human touring videos, augmented simulation data, and auxiliary spatial reasoning tasks.
- A low-level visual locomotion policy developed using single-stage reinforcement learning in Isaac Sim, incorporating LiDAR-based height maps for robust terrain perception.

## Results

- Achieved over a **17%** improvement in success rate on R2R-CE and outperformed prior state-of-the-art models on RxR-CE using only single-view RGB input.
- Demonstrated an **88%** overall success rate on real-world navigation tasks with a Unitree Go2 quadruped and generalized the VLA model to a Booster T1 humanoid robot without retraining.
- Introduced VLN-CE-Isaac, a new high-fidelity simulation benchmark for legged robot navigation, showing a 14-**21%** improvement in success rate with vision-based policies over blind policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level vision-language understanding from low-level robot control through intermediate natural language commands enhances VLA generalizability across different robot platforms.
> - Training the VLA model directly on real-world human touring videos, leveraging metric-pose estimation, improves generalization capabilities for continuous navigation.
> - A single-stage reinforcement learning approach with LiDAR-based height maps provides a robust and efficient method for low-level visual locomotion, handling complex terrains and obstacles.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

NaVILA positions itself at the intersection of three rapidly evolving fields: Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), Robot Foundation Models, and Legged Robot Locomotion Learning.

#### 1.1. Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN)

VLN is a foundational challenge in embodied AI, where a robot must navigate an unseen environment based on natural language instructions and visual observations. Early VLN research primarily focused on **discrete navigation** in simulated environments (e.g., Matterport3D, Habitat), where agents moved between predefined nodes on a navigation graph. This setup emphasized high-level decision-making and language understanding but largely abstracted away the complexities of real-world motion control.

More recently, the field has shifted towards **continuous environments (VLN-CE)**, requiring agents to perform mid-level actions like moving forward by a certain distance or rotating by an angle. While this is more realistic, existing VLN-CE benchmarks (like R2R-CE and RxR-CE) often simplify robot physics and interactions, often relying on "oracle" low-level policies or pre-trained waypoint predictors that assume perfect command execution. NaVILA's contribution here is extending VLN-CE to **legged robots**, which introduces significantly more complexity due to their dynamics, balance requirements, and interaction with challenging terrains. It also addresses the limitation of previous models that struggle to generalize beyond simulator-specific data or require extensive sensor inputs (panoramic views, depth, odometry) by demonstrating strong performance with single-view RGB input.

#### 1.2. Robot Foundation Models

Inspired by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in general AI, robot foundation models aim to create unified frameworks that process multi-modal inputs (vision, language) and directly output robot actions for complex tasks. Existing efforts like RT-2 or OpenVLA have shown promising results, mainly in **robot manipulation** tasks. In navigation, several foundational models have emerged, but they often focus on **goal navigation** (e.g., go to a specific object or image target) rather than complex, long-horizon, natural language instruction following. Legged robot VLA models are an emerging area, with some prior work focusing on either direct low-level action generation from VLM or skill banks controlled by LLMs/VLMs. NaVILA distinguishes itself by proposing a two-level framework that *explicitly decouples* high-level reasoning from low-level execution, using *mid-level language instructions* as an intermediate representation. This aims to leverage the natural language capabilities of VLMs more effectively and improve generalizability across different robot platforms.

#### 1.3. Legged Robot Locomotion Learning

Legged robots offer superior mobility in challenging and cluttered environments compared to wheeled robots. Learning robust locomotion policies is crucial for their deployment. Prior work has relied on proprioceptive data or visual inputs (depth cameras, LiDAR). However, many vision-based approaches suffer from environmental vulnerabilities (e.g., strong sunlight affecting depth cameras) or rely on inefficient two-stage training paradigms (teacher-student distillation). NaVILA advances this area by integrating **LiDAR-based height maps** for robust terrain perception and employing a **single-stage reinforcement learning** approach in a high-fidelity simulator (Isaac Sim). This aims to improve efficiency, robustness, and safety for real-world deployment, enabling the robot to handle complex terrains and obstacles autonomously.

#### 1.4. NaVILA's Position and Novelty

NaVILA uniquely bridges these three fields by:
1. **Extending VLN to Legged Robots:** Addressing the critical challenge of translating high-level human instructions into low-level leg joint actions for complex, dynamic platforms in diverse environments.
2. **Introducing a Two-Level VLA Framework:** Decomposing the problem into a high-level VLM that outputs mid-level *language* actions (e.g., "turn right 30 degrees") and a low-level locomotion policy that executes these commands. This modularity is a key differentiator, enabling better generalizability and decoupling of concerns.
3. **Leveraging Human Videos for Continuous Navigation:** Pioneering the use of real-world human touring videos (processed with metric-pose estimation) to directly train VLA models for continuous navigation, improving generalization beyond simulated data.
4. **Developing a New High-Fidelity Benchmark:** Introducing VLN-CE-Isaac on Isaac Sim, a more realistic benchmark that accounts for detailed robot joint movements and environment interactions, crucial for validating legged robot navigation.
5. **Achieving Strong Real-World Performance:** Demonstrating impressive robustness and success rates on real quadrupedal and humanoid robots in diverse, challenging settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of NaVILA is to **solve the problem of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) for legged robots**, enabling them to navigate complex and cluttered real-world environments following natural language instructions from humans.

The motivations behind this objective stem from several current limitations and desired capabilities:

1. **Flexible Human-Robot Interaction:** Traditional robot control often requires precise, technical commands or pre-programmed paths. VLN offers a more intuitive and flexible human interface by allowing natural language instructions, making robots more accessible and adaptable.
2. **Navigation in Challenging Environments:** Wheeled robots are limited by terrain and obstacles. Legged robots, with their ability to traverse uneven ground, cluttered spaces, and even stairs, offer a path to deploying robots in significantly more diverse and challenging real-world scenarios (e.g., messy labs, multi-room houses, outdoor uneven terrains).
3. **Bridging High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Control:** Translating abstract human language instructions ("Turn right at the end. Proceed to the grass") into precise, low-level leg joint actions is non-trivial. Existing end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems often struggle when directly predicting low-level, non-verbal actions, as Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are primarily trained on natural language.
4. **Improving Generalization and Robustness:** Unified end-to-end VLA models can be prone to overfitting to specific low-level commands or simulated data. There's a need for systems that can generalize across different robot platforms and unseen real-world environments, while also handling real-time obstacle avoidance and sophisticated control.

NaVILA aims to address these motivations by proposing a novel framework that enhances generalizability, leverages VLM reasoning capabilities more effectively, and ensures robust real-world execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NaVILA employs a **two-level framework** that integrates a high-level Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model with a low-level visual locomotion policy.

#### 3.1. Two-Level Framework Overview

Instead of end-to-end prediction of low-level actions, NaVILA first generates **mid-level actions in the form of natural language** with spatial information (e.g., "moving forward 75cm", "turn right 30 degrees"). This language-based mid-level action then serves as input for a low-level visual locomotion Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy, which translates it into precise leg joint movements for real-time execution. This decoupling offers three key advantages:
1. **Robot Agnostic High-Level Policy:** The same VLA can be applied across different robots by simply swapping the low-level policy.
2. **Enhanced VLA Training:** Representing actions as language allows VLA training with diverse data sources, including real human videos and reasoning QA tasks, leveraging VLM's natural language strength and improving reasoning without overfitting to specific low-level commands.
3. **Dual Timescale Operation:** The VLA (computationally intensive) runs at a lower frequency for high-level commands, while the locomotion policy operates in real-time, handling sophisticated obstacle avoidance and increasing overall robustness.

#### 3.2. High-Level VLA for Vision-Language Understanding

The VLA component is built upon **VILA**, a family of efficient VLMs known for strong multi-image reasoning.

- **VILA Preliminary:** VILA consists of a vision encoder, a projector, and an LLM. It processes images into visual tokens, maps them to the language domain, and then uses the LLM for auto-regressive generation. It handles videos by uniformly sampling frames and concatenating their tokens before the text prompt.
- **Navigation Prompts:** NaVILA customizes VILA's input by differentiating between *current observation* (most recent frame) and *historical frames* (sampled preceding frames, including the first). These are explicitly distinguished in the task prompt using textual cues (e.g., "a video of historical observations:", "current observation:"). This design avoids introducing special tokens and aligns with the LLM's natural language processing strength.
- **Learning from Human Videos:** A novel aspect is the direct training on **human touring videos from YouTube**. This addresses the challenge of obtaining continuous action labels from real-world data. The data pipeline involves:
 - Processing 2K egocentric touring videos into 20K trajectories using entropy-based sampling.
 - Estimating camera poses using MASt3R to extract step-by-step continuous actions.
 - Generating natural language instructions for each trajectory using VLM-based captioning followed by LLM rephrasing.
 This enables leveraging human demonstrations for continuous navigation, a previously non-trivial capability.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) Data Blend:** To ensure a robust and generalizable VLA, a comprehensive data blend is curated:
 1. **Navigational data from real videos:** The human touring videos described above.
 2. **Navigational data from simulations:** R2R-CE and RxR-CE datasets within the Habitat simulator. Consecutive actions are merged to encourage continuous value labels and diversify actions, and a rebalancing technique addresses label imbalance (e.g., for "stop" actions).
 3. **Auxiliary navigational data:** Augmented instructions from EnvDrop and a novel auxiliary task of navigation trajectory summarization (LLM describes the robot's trajectory from video frames). ScanQA dataset (3D QA pairs) is also integrated to enhance spatial scene understanding.
 4. **General VQA datasets:** Standard VQA datasets are included to maintain the model's broad general capabilities.
- **Training and Inference:** The VLA is fine-tuned from a pre-trained VILA model using the SFT data blend, with all components (vision encoder, connector, LLM) unfrozen. During inference, a regular expression parser extracts action types and arguments from the LLM's natural language output.

#### 3.3. Low-Level Visual Locomotion Policy

This policy translates the VLA's mid-level commands into precise joint movements.

- **Robot Platform:** Primarily developed for the Unitree Go2 quadruped robot, equipped with a LiDAR sensor and 18 DoFs (12 controlled leg joints).
- **Interpreting High-Level Commands:** VLM outputs a fixed set of actionable words ({move forward, turn left, turn right, stop}) which are mapped to fixed command velocities (e.g., 0.5 m/s, π/6 rad/s) and executed for corresponding time durations.
- **RL Training and Observation Space:** A single-stage Reinforcement Learning approach using the PPO algorithm is employed in the Isaac Sim simulator (Isaac Lab). This is more time-efficient than two-stage policy distillation. The critic observes privileged environment information, while the actor only receives real-world sensor data. Observations include proprioception (linear/angular velocity, orientation, joint positions/velocities, previous action) and a terrain height map.
- **LiDAR-based Height Map:** The Unitree L1 LiDAR sensor generates point clouds (360°x90° FoV), from which a 2.5D height map is constructed. This is crucial for detecting transparent objects and performing robustly under strong sunlight, addressing limitations of other visual sensors. Randomization is applied during training to bridge the sim-to-real gap.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

NaVILA's experimental evaluation spans high-level VLA performance, low-level RL policy performance, a new simulation benchmark for legged robots, and real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. High-Level VLA Performance

- **VLN-CE Benchmarks (R2R-CE, RxR-CE):** NaVILA significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on both R2R-CE and RxR-CE (Val-Unseen split). It achieves over a **17% improvement in success rate** compared to previous approaches on R2R-CE. Remarkably, NaVILA, using only single-view RGB input, achieves comparable or superior results to models that rely on panoramic views, depth, odometry, or simulator-pretrained waypoint predictors.
- **Cross-Dataset Performance (RxR-CE):** When trained exclusively on R2R data and evaluated zero-shot on RxR Val-Unseen, NaVILA substantially outperforms the previous state-of-the-art (NaVid) with a **10% improvement in Success Rate (SR)**.
- **Spatial Scene Understanding (ScanQA):** NaVILA demonstrates strong spatial reasoning, outperforming previous VLA models (NaviLLM by 20 CIDEr points) and even state-of-the-art 3D-based large multi-modal models (which require 3D scans or RGBD data) on the ScanQA Validation benchmark. This highlights its robust object localization and referring capabilities.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Human Touring Videos:** Incorporating real-world human touring videos leads to approximately 5% gains in OS, SR, and SPL metrics on R2R-CE, validating the effectiveness of learning from human demonstrations.
 - **Memory Sizes:** 8 history frames are found sufficient for R2R-CE, with diminishing returns for larger memory sizes.

#### 4.2. Low-Level RL Policy Performance

- **Comparison to Policy Distillation:** NaVILA's single-stage visual locomotion policy significantly outperforms policy distillation-based methods like Regularized Online Adaptation (ROA) across metrics: lower linear velocity error (0.066 vs 0.161-0.189), lower angular velocity error (0.113 vs 0.152), and notably a significantly **lower collision rate (0.81 vs 3.09-3.25)**. This validates the efficiency and effectiveness of the direct RL training approach.

#### 4.3. Legged Robot Navigation Performance in Simulation (VLN-CE-Isaac)

- **New Benchmark Introduction:** The paper introduces VLN-CE-Isaac, a high-fidelity benchmark built on Isaac Sim, designed to simulate detailed robotic joint movements and environmental interactions for legged robots. This addresses the limitations of prior VLN benchmarks that did not account for realistic low-level control.
- **Vision-based vs. Blind Policy:** In the VLN-CE-Isaac benchmark, the vision-based policy (using LiDAR height maps) significantly outperforms the proprioception-only (blind) policy, showing a **14% improvement in SR for Unitree Go2** and **21% for Unitree H1**. This highlights the crucial role of visual perception for obstacle avoidance and robust navigation in realistic settings. The gap to an "Oracle" policy (perfect command execution) further emphasizes the introduced challenges of this new benchmark.

#### 4.4. Real-World Evaluation

- **Real-world Deployment on Unitree Go2:** NaVILA achieves an impressive **88% overall success rate** on 25 instructions (repeated three times), including a **75% success rate on complex, long-horizon instructions** across diverse environments (Workspace, Home, Outdoor).
- **Comparison to GPT-4o:** NaVILA significantly outperforms GPT-4o, a state-of-the-art VLM, in both success rate and navigation error in real-world tasks, demonstrating its specialized effectiveness for embodied navigation.
- **Impact of Human Videos:** Real-world results further confirm that training with human touring videos substantially improves generalization, particularly in challenging outdoor scenes.
- **Cross-Robot Generalization (Booster T1 Humanoid):** NaVILA successfully deploys on a Booster Dynamics T1 humanoid robot using the *same VLA model without any retraining*, despite differences in camera height and view angle. This showcases the strong generalization capabilities and the flexibility of the two-level framework, as only the low-level locomotion policy needed to be specific to the robot.
- **Voice Control Integration:** The system is demonstrated with speech recognition, allowing voice-controlled navigation.
- **Limitations:** The paper acknowledges limitations, including failures in some real-world cases due to insufficient error correction and the computational intensity of image-based VLMs, suggesting larger-scale training and advances in long-context LLMs as future work.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NaVILA represents a significant leap forward in embodied AI, particularly for real-world legged robot navigation. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Enabling Truly Embodied VLN:** By successfully bridging the gap between high-level language understanding and low-level physical execution on complex legged robots, NaVILA moves VLN from largely simulated academic exercises towards practical, real-world applications. This capability is crucial for deploying versatile robots in unstructured, human-centric environments.
2. **Advancing Robot Foundation Models:** The two-level framework offers a compelling paradigm for robot foundation models. It cleverly leverages the strengths of VLMs for high-level reasoning (which is best expressed in language) while abstracting away the intricacies of low-level control, making the VLA more generalizable and less prone to overfitting. This modularity could inspire future designs for multi-task, multi-robot foundation models.
3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The ability to learn from diverse data sources, including real human videos, and the robust LiDAR-based locomotion policy contribute to superior generalization to unseen environments and enhanced safety in challenging terrains (cluttered indoors, uneven outdoors, transparent obstacles). This is critical for practical robot deployment.
4. **New Benchmarking Standards:** The introduction of VLN-CE-Isaac on Isaac Sim is a crucial contribution to the research community. By emphasizing realistic robot physics and detailed joint movements, it provides a more accurate and challenging evaluation platform for future legged robot navigation research, pushing the field towards more practical solutions.
5. **Practical Accessibility and Human-Robot Interaction:** The framework's ability to interpret natural language instructions and potentially integrate with voice control makes robots more accessible and intuitive for human users, paving the way for more natural and flexible human-robot collaboration in various settings, from home assistance to industrial inspection and disaster response.

In conclusion, NaVILA demonstrates a robust and effective solution for legged robot vision-language navigation, showcasing impressive capabilities in both high-fidelity simulations and challenging real-world scenarios. Its innovative two-level architecture and data blend strategies set a new benchmark for generalizable and robust embodied AI agents.
