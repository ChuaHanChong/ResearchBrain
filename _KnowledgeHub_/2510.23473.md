# Video-Thinker: Sparking "Thinking with Videos" via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> VIDEO-THINKER, a new framework, empowers Multimodal Large Language Models to reason with videos by intrinsically developing temporal grounding and captioning abilities. The model establishes new state-of-the-art performance on various video reasoning benchmarks, achieving up to an 11.44% improvement on the VRBench out-of-domain dataset, while showcasing enhanced temporal localization (48.22% mIoU) and descriptive captioning.

## Problem

- Extending the 'Thinking with Images' paradigm to videos poses challenges due to inherent temporal dependencies and dynamic content.
- Existing MLLMs often treat videos as static inputs or rely on rigid, pre-designed prompting strategies, limiting dynamic manipulation and reasoning over temporal sequences.
- Many video reasoning models struggle to seamlessly integrate temporal grounding into the full chain-of-thought process and typically demand extensive datasets.

## Method

- A ==Hindsight-Curation Reasoning pipeline== synthesizes the ==Video-Thinker-10K dataset==, providing structured reasoning traces with explicit temporal, captioning, and thinking tags.
- A two-stage training strategy is employed, beginning with ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== to teach the MLLM to generate reasoning in the specified format.
- The SFT stage is followed by ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, a reinforcement learning technique, to intrinsically strengthen grounding and captioning capabilities for autonomous temporal navigation and robust reasoning.

## Results

- Video-Thinker-7B achieves new state-of-the-art results among 7B-sized MLLMs across in-domain and challenging out-of-domain video reasoning benchmarks, with up to an **11.44%** improvement on VRBench.
- The model demonstrates superior intrinsic grounding abilities with an mIoU of **48.22%** (a **75.5%** relative improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and enhanced captioning, showing a **31.2%** relative enhancement in overall captioning performance.
- Video-Thinker exhibits emergent 'aha moments' and self-reflective behaviors during reasoning, suggesting dynamic internal feedback mechanisms and metacognitive capabilities acquired with high data efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Empowering MLLMs with intrinsic temporal grounding and captioning allows them to autonomously 'think with videos,' moving beyond passive video processing to active, dynamic reasoning.
> - The hindsight-curation data synthesis, combined with the two-stage SFT and GRPO training, efficiently instills complex video reasoning abilities using a relatively small 10K sample dataset.
> - The GRPO stage is crucial for developing robust reasoning and out-of-domain generalization, transforming basic format-following into effective autonomous temporal navigation and understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of multimodal AI, specifically in extending the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to understand and reason with dynamic visual information like videos. The paper explicitly positions itself as an advancement of the "Thinking with Images" paradigm, which has recently revolutionized image understanding in MLLMs.

**Evolution from "Thinking with Images"**: Recent MLLMs have moved beyond treating images as static, passive inputs. Approaches like "Thinking with Images" (e.g., OpenAI's O3 and O4-mini, DeepEyes, Visual-ARFT) enable models to actively "localize, zoom in, and reason over image content" during the inference process by autonomously invoking visual tools or generating visual-interleaved chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. This dynamic interaction with visual data has led to significant performance gains in tasks like visual question answering and complex scene understanding.

**Challenges in Video Reasoning**: The natural next step is to extend this dynamic reasoning to videos. However, videos present unique challenges due to their inherent temporal dependencies, motion patterns, and evolving narratives. MLLMs traditionally struggle with dynamically manipulating and reasoning over temporal sequences without relying on pre-designed, often rigid, prompting strategies (e.g., "video-of-thoughts" methodologies). Existing video reasoning models (like Video-R1, VideoChat-R1, Temporal-R1) have started to incorporate implicit or explicit temporal reasoning, but often fall short of seamlessly integrating temporal grounding into the full CoT process or require extensive datasets.

**Addressing Key Gaps**: Video-Thinker aims to bridge these gaps by enabling MLLMs to "Think with Videos" through **intrinsic** "grounding" (temporal localization) and "captioning" (comprehension) capabilities. Unlike prior work that might rely on external, hand-crafted tools or sophisticated pre-designed CoT prompts, Video-Thinker's core innovation is to develop these temporal reasoning "tools" *within* the MLLM itself. This represents a significant shift towards more autonomous and human-like video understanding, where the model itself decides *when* and *how* to attend to specific temporal segments and interpret their content during a complex reasoning task. The paper also highlights its data efficiency, achieving strong results with a relatively small curated dataset (10K samples) compared to some existing methods requiring 160K+ samples. This aspect positions it as a more scalable and accessible approach to advanced video reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Video-Thinker is to **empower Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to "think with videos"** by autonomously leveraging their intrinsic "grounding" and "captioning" capabilities to generate reasoning clues throughout the inference process.

The key motivations behind this objective are:

- **Extending Dynamic Reasoning to Videos**: Inspired by the success of "Thinking with Images" in image reasoning, the authors aim to bring a similar dynamic, interactive reasoning paradigm to the more complex domain of video understanding.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Static Video Processing**: Traditional MLLMs often treat videos as monolithic inputs or rely on pre-defined, rigid chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting strategies, which limits their ability to dynamically manipulate and reason over temporal sequences.
- **Eliminating Reliance on External Tools**: Many approaches for enhanced MLLM reasoning involve calling external tools (e.g., for cropping, zooming). Video-Thinker seeks to integrate fundamental temporal visual operations (grounding and captioning) intrinsically within the MLLM, thereby simplifying the architecture and improving autonomy.
- **Enabling Structured Temporal Reasoning**: The goal is to allow MLLMs to autonomously navigate, analyze, and synthesize visual cues from specific temporal segments of a video, leading to a more coherent and robust understanding of dynamic narratives.
- **Data Efficiency**: To achieve robust video reasoning without the need for extremely large, expensive datasets, the authors aim to develop an effective training strategy and a carefully curated dataset that can efficiently spark these complex reasoning capabilities.

In essence, Video-Thinker seeks to enable MLLMs to perform complex video understanding by allowing them to "ask themselves" what is happening at specific times in a video and synthesize this information, much like a human would iteratively analyze a dynamic scene.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Video-Thinker framework introduces a novel approach to empower MLLMs with intrinsic video reasoning capabilities, comprising a specialized data synthesis pipeline and a two-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Data Synthesis via Hindsight-Curation Reasoning (Video-Thinker-10K)

The cornerstone of Video-Thinker is the meticulously curated **Video-Thinker-10K** dataset, designed to instill intrinsic "grounding" and "captioning" capabilities within MLLMs. This dataset consists of 10,000 samples spanning diverse video reasoning tasks.

**Source Data Collection**: Six prominent datasets were leveraged: ActivityNet, TutorialVQA, YouCook2 (caption-labeled, rich in temporal annotations), and STAR, ScaleLong, LVBench (QA-labeled, offering challenging reasoning questions).

**Core Principles for Curation**:
1. **Compelling Questions**: The training data requires questions that force MLLMs to localize multiple key segments, summarize their content, and synthesize information for comprehensive answers.
2. **Structured Reasoning Trace**: Each sample must provide supervision through a structured trace using specific annotation tags:
 - `<time></time>`: For precise temporal localization.
 - `<caption></caption>`: For comprehensive visual cue extraction and description.
 - `<think></think>`: For analytical reasoning and synthesis of extracted visual information.

**Systematic Data Transformation Pipeline**:
- **Quality Filters**: Initial filtering removed corrupted videos and those with fewer than 64 frames.
- **Question Generation (for Caption-labeled datasets)**: For datasets rich in temporal captions (e.g., ActivityNet), DeepSeek-R1 was used to generate complex multiple-choice questions requiring reasoning across multiple video segments, building upon existing detailed segment descriptions.
- **Visual Cue Generation (for QA-labeled datasets)**: For datasets with high-quality Q&A pairs but lacking granular segment descriptions (e.g., STAR), Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite was employed to produce answer-conditioned descriptive captions for video segments, ensuring relevance to the reasoning process.
- **Reasoning Trace Synthesis**: With both questions and segment-level visual descriptions available, DeepSeek-V3 was used for **reverse-curation generation**. The model received the ground-truth answer, generated captions, and temporal annotations to produce high-quality, step-by-step reasoning processes adhering to the `<time>`, `<caption>`, and `<think>` format.

**Hindsight Curation Process**: To ensure the quality and relevance of the generated grounding and captioning components and reduce manual inspection costs, a novel hindsight curation process was introduced. For each generated sample, the content within the `<time>` and `<caption>` tags was fed into Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. If this model failed to derive the correct answer, the reasoning trace was regenerated (up to three times). This iterative self-correction mechanism aimed to ensure that all samples effectively guide the model towards the correct solution.

#### 3.2. Training Strategy of Video-Thinker

The training of Video-Thinker employs a two-stage approach, building upon a base MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct).

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Format-Following**:
 - **Objective**: To bootstrap the MLLM's ability to generate structured reasoning traces in the specified format (`<time>`, `<caption>`, `<think>`). Pre-trained MLLMs lack exposure to this specialized format.
 - **Method**: The model is fine-tuned on the Video-Thinker-10K dataset by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the ground-truth reasoning trace (T) and final answer (Y). This teaches the model to predict each subsequent token of the trace, conditioned on the video, question, and preceding tokens.

2. **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for Autonomous Navigation**:
 - **Objective**: To strengthen the model's intrinsic "grounding" and "captioning" capabilities and promote autonomous temporal navigation for sophisticated video reasoning. This stage moves beyond simply following a format to truly understanding and generating effective reasoning.
 - **Method**: GRPO, a reinforcement learning technique, is applied. For each sample, the model generates multiple candidate reasoning traces. A reward `r^(i)` is assigned to each trace, combining correctness (`r_correct^(i)`) and adherence to the structured format (`r_format^(i)`).
 - **Advantage Calculation**: The advantages `A_i` are computed based on normalized rewards within each group of generated traces. This relative comparison mechanism allows the model to learn from the better-performing traces without requiring an explicit value function, promoting both accurate reasoning and proper temporal structure.
 - **Optimization**: The policy is optimized by maximizing a GRPO objective, incorporating a KL divergence term to prevent aggressive policy shifts from a reference policy.

This two-stage approach ensures that the model first learns *how* to structure its thoughts (SFT) and then *how to think effectively and autonomously* within that structure (GRPO), fostering both format adherence and robust reasoning generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate Video-Thinker-7B against various baselines demonstrate its superior performance and validate its core design principles.

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance**: Video-Thinker-7B consistently achieves new SOTA results among 7B-sized MLLMs across both in-domain and, crucially, challenging out-of-domain video reasoning benchmarks.
 - **Out-of-Domain**: Achieved 43.22% on Video-Holmes (4.68% improvement over best baseline), 33.25% on CG-Bench-Reasoning (3.81% improvement), and 80.69% on VRBench (11.44% improvement). These substantial gains highlight its strong generalization capabilities to unseen reasoning tasks.
 - **In-Domain**: Demonstrated superior performance across ActivityNet, Star, ScaleLong, YouCook2, and LVBench, reaffirming its effectiveness on tasks similar to its training distribution.

- **Impact of GRPO Stage for Generalization**: The GRPO stage is found to be critical for the model's out-of-domain generalization. Video-Thinker-7B (GRPO-trained) significantly outperforms Video-Thinker-SFT-7B (SFT-only) with improvements of 11.70% on Video-Holmes, 8.30% on CG-Bench-Reasoning, and 18.29% on VRBench. The SFT-only model often performs worse than baseline methods, indicating that while SFT is essential for learning the reasoning format, GRPO is indispensable for developing robust reasoning and generalization.

- **Performance Across Different Frame Counts**: Video-Thinker-7B consistently outperforms baselines (Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Video-R1-7B) regardless of the number of video frames (16, 32, or 64) used during inference. While more frames generally lead to better performance for all models, Video-Thinker's lead remains significant, indicating its superior ability to process and integrate temporal information effectively across varying temporal sampling densities.

- **In-Depth Analysis of Grounding and Captioning Capabilities**: Direct evaluation confirms that Video-Thinker-7B possesses enhanced intrinsic grounding and captioning abilities, which are posited as key "tools" for its reasoning gains.
 - **Grounding**: Achieved an mIoU of 48.22% (75.5% improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and nearly doubled Recall@0.3 and Recall@0.5 scores, indicating superior temporal localization. (Video-R1 was excluded due to its inability to generate temporal annotations in the required format).
 - **Captioning**: Demonstrated superior performance across METEOR (15.87%), ROUGE-L (20.11%), and BLEU@1 (15.34%), representing a 31.2% relative enhancement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and a 61.0% relative improvement over Video-R1-7B in overall captioning performance. This confirms its enhanced capacity for generating contextually accurate and temporally relevant video descriptions.
 - **Oracle Experiments**: Providing ground-truth grounding and captioning annotations to Video-R1-7B on the Video-Holmes benchmark significantly improved its accuracy (e.g., from 37% to 63% with both), further validating the critical importance of these capabilities for video understanding.

- **"Aha Moment" and Self-Reflection**: The model exhibits "aha moments" and self-reflective behaviors, periodically re-evaluating and refining its interpretations of video content during reasoning. This metacognitive process suggests dynamic internal feedback mechanisms, akin to Video-R1 but achieved with significantly less training data (10K vs. 160K samples), indicating greater data efficiency in acquiring such complex behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Video-Thinker represents a substantial advancement in multimodal AI, pushing the boundaries of what MLLMs can achieve in video understanding and reasoning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Paradigm Shift to "Thinking with Videos"**: By successfully extending the "Thinking with Images" paradigm to videos, Video-Thinker demonstrates that MLLMs can move beyond passive video processing to active, dynamic temporal reasoning. This unlocks new possibilities for more sophisticated and human-like video comprehension.
- **Intrinsic Tool-Use for Autonomy**: The development of intrinsic "grounding" and "captioning" capabilities within the MLLM itself is a key innovation. This eliminates the need for external, hand-crafted tools or rigid prompt engineering, leading to more autonomous, adaptable, and generalizable video reasoning agents. Models can decide *when* and *where* to focus their attention in a video, much like humans do.
- **Enhanced Temporal Understanding**: The demonstrated improvements in temporal grounding and captioning are critical. These capabilities allow MLLMs to precisely locate salient events and accurately describe their content, which are foundational for understanding complex video narratives, causal relationships, and action sequences.
- **Data Efficiency**: Achieving SOTA performance with a relatively small curated dataset (10K samples) compared to other video reasoning models (some requiring over 160K samples) is highly significant. This suggests that the hindsight-curation reasoning pipeline and the two-stage SFT+GRPO training strategy are highly efficient in instilling complex reasoning abilities, potentially lowering the computational and data annotation costs for developing advanced MLLMs.
- **Strong Generalization**: The substantial performance gains on challenging *out-of-domain* benchmarks (Video-Holmes, CG-Bench-Reasoning, VRBench) underscore Video-Thinker's robust generalization capacity. This indicates that the learned reasoning process is not merely memorizing patterns but acquiring a deeper, transferable understanding of video content.
- **Foundational for Future AI Agents**: The ability of an MLLM to "think with videos" through self-reflection and intrinsic temporal operations lays a strong foundation for developing more intelligent AI agents. These agents could autonomously analyze instructional videos, assist in complex surveillance tasks, or even contribute to creative video content generation and editing by understanding narrative flow and key events.
- **Aha Moments and Metacognition**: The observation of "aha moments" and self-reflective behaviors points towards emergent metacognitive capabilities in MLLMs. This is a crucial step towards models that can not only reason but also monitor and improve their own reasoning processes, leading to more robust and trustworthy AI systems.

In conclusion, Video-Thinker's framework offers a compelling vision for the future of multimodal AI, where models can interact with and understand dynamic visual information with a level of autonomy and sophistication previously unachievable. Its innovative approach to intrinsic tool-use and data efficiency positions it as a significant contribution to the field, paving the way for more capable and intelligent video-aware MLLMs.
