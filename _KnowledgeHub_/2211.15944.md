# The Effectiveness of World Models for Continual Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Oxford and affiliated institutions systematically explored the effectiveness of world models for Continual Reinforcement Learning, introducing "Continual-Dreamer" based on DreamerV2. Their method, which integrates persistent experience replay and task-agnostic exploration, mitigates catastrophic forgetting and achieves strong forward transfer, outperforming model-free baselines on challenging image-based benchmarks like Minigrid and Minihack.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning agents struggle to master multiple tasks sequentially without experiencing catastrophic forgetting, where performance on previously learned tasks degrades.
- Developing truly autonomous and adaptable agents requires task-agnostic Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) methods that do not rely on explicit signals about task changes or hand-crafted exploration schedules.
- Many existing CRL approaches are either model-free or rely on regularization techniques, lacking the sample efficiency and generative capabilities inherent in world models for complex, pixel-based environments.

## Method

- The authors adapted **DreamerV2** into **Continual-Dreamer**, extending its ==First-In-First-Out (FIFO) replay buffer== to persist experience across multiple tasks.
- They integrated ==Plan2Explore==, a task-agnostic exploration strategy that uses ==intrinsic rewards== derived from the world model's uncertainty, to guide policy learning in novel environments.
- A systematic study of ==Selective Experience Replay (SER)== methods was conducted, evaluating strategies like ==Reservoir Sampling== and ==50:50 Sampling== for populating and sampling from the persistent replay buffer.

## Results

- Continual-Dreamer, even with basic persistent replay, achieved higher sample efficiency and strong out-of-the-box performance on Minigrid tasks, often solving tasks that model-free baselines struggled with.
- When combined with Reservoir Sampling, Continual-Dreamer robustly mitigated catastrophic forgetting on Minihack benchmarks, outperforming all tested baselines in average return and demonstrating faster learning on new tasks.
- The inclusion of Plan2Explore significantly enhanced learning speed, particularly for exploration-intensive Minigrid tasks, validating the use of uncertainty-driven intrinsic rewards for task-agnostic exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World models provide a robust and sample-efficient paradigm for task-agnostic Continual Reinforcement Learning, effectively addressing catastrophic forgetting and facilitating positive knowledge transfer.
> - Reservoir Sampling proved to be a consistently effective strategy for managing persistent experience replay buffers, maintaining a balanced distribution of experiences from all encountered tasks.
> - Intrinsic uncertainty-driven exploration (Plan2Explore) from world models can successfully enable task-agnostic adaptation and learning without requiring explicit task boundaries or dedicated exploration schedules.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes in solving individual tasks across various domains, including games and robotics. However, developing intelligent systems capable of mastering multiple tasks sequentially, while retaining performance on previously learned ones, remains a significant challenge. This area of study is known as Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL). CRL agents are conjectured to be essential for scalable intelligent systems, requiring the ability to learn continuously without experiencing "catastrophic forgetting," a phenomenon where learning new tasks degrades performance on old ones.

World models, which combine generative models with RL, represent a paradigm that has demonstrated high sample efficiency and strong performance in single-task RL, particularly in environments with pixel-based observations. These models typically operate by training a generative model (the "world model") to predict environment dynamics from collected experience, and then training a policy within this imagined world. The iterative nature of world model training, often leveraging experience replay buffers, provides a natural point of integration for continual learning methodologies.

Existing CRL approaches can be broadly categorized into regularization-based methods (e.g., EWC, which regularizes parameter changes), expansion-based methods (which add new network components for new tasks), and memory-based methods (which replay data from past tasks). This work primarily positions itself within the memory-based paradigm, specifically exploring how model-based RL, through its inherent use of replay buffers and generative capabilities, can address the challenges of task-agnostic CRL. Unlike many prior CRL methods that require explicit task identification or task-specific parameters and exploration schedules, this research focuses on developing a task-agnostic approach, where the agent operates without explicit signals about task changes. This objective aligns with the broader goal of creating more autonomous and adaptable agents. The paper aims to demonstrate that the model-based RL paradigm is a viable and effective solution for task-agnostic CRL, an area where fewer state-of-the-art methods currently exist.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate the effectiveness of world models for Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL). The authors aim to showcase that world models can be harnessed to allow an agent to learn multiple tasks sequentially while satisfying traditional CRL desiderata: avoiding catastrophic forgetting, achieving knowledge transfer to new tasks, maintaining high average performance across all tasks, and being scalable.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this work include:

- **Leveraging World Model Capabilities for CRL**: World models are known for their sample efficiency, achieved by training policies within the imagined trajectories generated by the model. This efficiency is posited to be beneficial for CRL, where the agent needs to learn from potentially limited experience on each sequential task. The ability of generative models to create compact representations is also expected to facilitate policy training across diverse tasks.
- **Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting via Replay Buffers**: World models inherently use replay buffers to store and train on past experiences. The authors propose extending these replay buffers across multiple tasks to naturally prevent the forgetting of previously learned skills, drawing inspiration from similar strategies in continual supervised learning.
- **Achieving Task-Agnostic Learning**: A key goal is to develop a CRL approach that does not require explicit task identification or supervision regarding task boundaries or changes. This is considered an important requirement for truly autonomous and adaptable intelligent systems operating in dynamic environments. The authors specifically aim to demonstrate that world models can operate effectively without such external signals.
- **Implementing Task-Agnostic Exploration**: Many traditional RL exploration strategies, especially in CRL, rely on task-specific schedules (e.g., epsilon-greedy exploration in DQN). This work aims to utilize the intrinsic uncertainty quantification capabilities of world models (e.g., disagreement between model predictions) to provide an adaptive, task-agnostic exploration bonus. This allows the agent to implicitly adapt to task changes and seek out informative experiences without explicit task knowledge.
- **Systematic Study of Selective Experience Replay (SER) Methods**: While a persistent replay buffer can help, its management is crucial. The paper seeks to thoroughly explore how different strategies for populating the replay buffer and sampling from it affect performance, forgetting, and transfer in a world-model-based CRL setting. This includes evaluating methods like reservoir sampling, coverage maximization, uncertainty sampling, reward sampling, and 50:50 sampling.
- **Establishing a Strong Baseline for Model-Based CRL**: By demonstrating that a world model-based approach, specifically termed "Continual-Dreamer" (based on DreamerV2 with optimized replay strategies), can outperform state-of-the-art task-agnostic model-free CRL methods on challenging benchmarks, the authors aim to establish world models as a potent and promising direction for future CRL research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work involves adapting and extending the DreamerV2 algorithm, a discrete stochastic recurrent world model, for continual reinforcement learning. The approach, named Continual-Dreamer, integrates several mechanisms to address the challenges of sequential task learning, particularly catastrophic forgetting and effective exploration in task-agnostic scenarios.

#### 3.1. Core World Model Framework: DreamerV2

DreamerV2 serves as the backbone world model. It learns a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) which predicts the forward dynamics of the environment.
- **World Model Learning**: At each time step, the RSSM takes an observation (`o_t`) and previous actions, then reconstructs observations (`o_t`), rewards, and discounts. This is achieved through a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) (`h_t = GRU(h_{t-1}, z_t, a_t)`) where `h_t` is the hidden state and `z_t` represents discrete probabilistic latent states. Trajectories for training the world model are sampled from an experience replay buffer.
- **Policy Learning in Imagination**: After the world model is trained, the policy (`π`) is trained within the "imagination" of the world model (i.e., using its generated trajectories), while the world model's weights are frozen. An actor-critic method is employed, where the actor `(â_t ~ π(·|z_t, h_t))` and critic learn from predicted rewards `(r̂_{t+1})` and latent states `(z_t, h_t)` generated by the world model. This regime aims to generalize the policy to previously seen environments through the learned world model.

#### 3.2. Continual Learning Mechanisms

The following mechanisms are introduced to enable continual learning:
- **Persistent Experience Replay Buffer**: DreamerV2's standard First-In-First-Out (FIFO) replay buffer is extended to persist across multiple tasks. This means that experience from all previously encountered tasks is retained in the buffer, serving as a memory to prevent forgetting in the world model and facilitating policy training across tasks.
- **Task-Agnostic Exploration (Plan2Explore)**: The agent's policy is guided in the real environment not only by extrinsic rewards but also by an intrinsic reward derived from the world model's uncertainty. This is based on Plan2Explore, which uses an ensemble of neural networks to predict the next latent state features (`[z_{t+1}, h_{t+1}]`). The disagreement (variance) among these ensemble predictions serves as an intrinsic reward, encouraging the policy to explore regions where the world model is uncertain. This method is task-agnostic as it does not require explicit task knowledge to drive exploration, unlike `ϵ-greedy` schedules or task-specific entropy regularizers. The policy aims to maximize a combined reward `r = α_i * r_i + α_e * r_e`, where `r_i` is the intrinsic uncertainty reward and `r_e` is the extrinsic reward predicted by the world model.

#### 3.3. Selective Experience Replay (SER) Methods

To manage the content of the persistent replay buffer and optimize mini-batch sampling for learning, various SER strategies are systematically studied:
- **Replay Buffer Population Strategies**:
 - **Reservoir Sampling (RS)**: New experiences are added to the buffer with a probability that decreases as the total number of seen trajectories increases, ensuring a uniform distribution of experience from all tasks seen so far.
 - **Coverage Maximization (CM)**: Experiences are added based on their proximity to existing trajectories in the buffer, aiming to maximize the diversity of stored experiences. Trajectories are embedded using a convolutional LSTM, and distances are calculated based on hidden states.
- **Mini-batch Construction Strategies**:
 - **Uncertainty Sampling (US)**: Mini-batches are constructed by prioritizing episodes with high world model prediction uncertainty (measured as the sum of next-state uncertainties per trajectory).
 - **Reward Sampling (RWD)**: Mini-batches are sampled by prioritizing episodes with higher environmental rewards.
 - **50:50 Sampling**: A mini-batch is composed of an equal proportion of uniformly random samples from the replay buffer and samples drawn from a triangular distribution biased towards the most recent experiences, balancing stability (remembering old tasks) and plasticity (learning new tasks).

#### 3.4. Baselines and Experimental Setup

- **Task-Agnostic Baselines**: Impala (a powerful model-free RL algorithm) and CLEAR (a task-agnostic CRL method based on Impala, leveraging replay buffers with V-trace importance sampling).
- **Task-Aware Baseline**: L2 weight regularization of the world model and actor-critic parameters towards the optimal weights of the previous task.
- **Environments**: Experiments are conducted on two challenging image-based, partially observable, and sparse reward benchmarks:
 - **Minigrid**: 3 tasks (DoorKey-9x9, SimpleCrossing-SN9, LavaCrossing-9x9) with 0.75M interactions per task.
 - **Minihack**: 4 tasks (Room-Random-15x15-v0, Room-Trap-15x15-v0, River-Narrow-v0, River-Monster-v0) and an extended 8-task suite, with 1M interactions per task.
- **Metrics**: Average Performance, Average Forgetting, and Average Forward Transfer are used to quantify CRL effectiveness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence regarding the effectiveness of world models for Continual Reinforcement Learning, particularly in task-agnostic settings, and systematically evaluates various selective experience replay strategies.

#### 4.1. General Effectiveness of World Models for CRL

- **Out-of-the-Box Performance**: DreamerV2, even without specific CRL modifications beyond a persistent replay buffer, demonstrated strong out-of-the-box performance on Minigrid tasks. It exhibited higher sample efficiency compared to model-free baselines like CLEAR, often solving tasks that CLEAR struggled with even when given 10 times more environment interactions.
- **Forward Transfer**: DreamerV2 showed strong forward transfer, indicating that it effectively leverages knowledge from previous tasks to accelerate learning on new ones, particularly on Minigrid. This suggests that the learned world model can generalize across similar task structures.
- **Task-Agnostic Exploration**: The integration of Plan2Explore (P2E) significantly enhanced learning speed, particularly for exploration-intensive Minigrid tasks. This confirms the utility of uncertainty-driven intrinsic rewards for task-agnostic exploration in a continual learning context, obviating the need for task-specific exploration schedules.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Selective Experience Replay (SER) Methods

The evaluation on the harder Minihack tasks revealed the necessity of robust replay buffer management to mitigate forgetting.
- **Reservoir Sampling (RS)**: This method, when combined with DreamerV2 (termed "Continual-Dreamer"), consistently and robustly mitigated catastrophic forgetting across the Minihack benchmarks. Continual-Dreamer and Continual-Dreamer + P2E outperformed all baselines in terms of average return and exhibited faster learning on new tasks. RS proved effective in maintaining a relatively uniform distribution of experience from all past tasks in the replay buffer, as evidenced by analysis of buffer composition.
- **50:50 Sampling**: When combined with reservoir sampling, 50:50 sampling (balancing uniform random with recent-biased sampling) had mixed effects. For Continual-Dreamer, it increased forgetting slightly but maintained performance, suggesting improved learning of harder tasks and transfer. For Continual-Dreamer + P2E, it led to a decrease in both performance and forgetting.
- **Other SER Methods**:
 - **Uncertainty Sampling (US)**: Performed poorly, close to the Impala baseline, with low performance and high forgetting.
 - **Reward Sampling (RWD)**: Did not show improvement over random sampling of mini-batches.
 - **Coverage Maximization (CM)**: Reduced forgetting but showed inconsistent effects on performance (increased for DreamerV2, decreased for DreamerV2 + P2E). CM also struggled with task data distribution, retaining fewer samples from intermediate tasks.
- **Task-Aware Baseline**: An L2 regularization approach for a task-aware world model performed poorly compared to the task-agnostic methods, suggesting that rigidity from regularization can hinder efficient learning of new, substantially different tasks.

#### 4.3. Scaling to More Tasks (8 Minihack Tasks)

- On an extended suite of 8 Minihack tasks, Continual-Dreamer variants (especially with RS and 50:50 sampling) demonstrated strong knowledge retention and effective learning. They consistently outperformed CLEAR, which struggled with the latter, more difficult tasks (e.g., River-v0, River-Monster-v0, HideNSeek-v0).
- The results suggested that warm-starting the world model by learning easier, related tasks earlier in the sequence enabled DreamerV2 to improve its downstream performance on more complex tasks, indicating positive transfer.

#### 4.4. Observed Limitations

- **Interference**: Even with replay, world models can experience interference when tasks involve similar state spaces but differ in reward functions or goal locations. The paper demonstrated this on a Minigrid FourRooms-v0 environment, where the agent struggled to maintain performance on both tasks simultaneously.
- **Task Data Imbalances**: Current SER methods, including RS and CM, exhibited limitations in retaining an even distribution of task data in the replay buffer when task interaction lengths were imbalanced. Longer, more recent tasks could saturate the buffer, leading to forgetting of shorter, earlier tasks.
- **Stability-Plasticity Trade-off with Buffer Size**: Increasing the replay buffer size improved average performance and reduced forgetting but simultaneously decreased forward transfer and hindered the learning of harder exploration tasks, highlighting a trade-off between retaining old knowledge and acquiring new skills. Conversely, significantly reducing buffer size led to substantial performance degradation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Continual Reinforcement Learning by systematically demonstrating the viability and effectiveness of world models as a task-agnostic approach. The findings suggest a promising direction for developing intelligent agents capable of continuous learning in dynamic, multi-task environments.

The key significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Establishing World Models as a CRL Paradigm**: The paper provides empirical evidence that world models, specifically through the Continual-Dreamer configuration, can serve as a robust and sample-efficient baseline for task-agnostic CRL. This represents a paradigm shift from predominantly model-free or regularization-based CRL methods, opening new avenues for research into model-based CRL.
- **Addressing Key CRL Challenges**: By effectively mitigating catastrophic forgetting through persistent replay buffers and achieving positive knowledge transfer, the work directly tackles two fundamental challenges in CRL. The superior performance on challenging benchmarks like Minigrid and Minihack, compared to state-of-the-art task-agnostic model-free baselines, underscores this achievement.
- **Advancing Task-Agnostic Learning and Exploration**: The successful integration of Plan2Explore demonstrates a practical approach to task-agnostic exploration for CRL. This method, which derives intrinsic rewards from world model uncertainty, removes the need for explicit task identification or hand-crafted exploration schedules, moving closer to autonomous, adaptable agents for real-world scenarios where task boundaries are often unknown.
- **Practical Recommendations for Experience Replay**: The systematic study of various selective experience replay methods provides valuable insights and practical recommendations for managing experience buffers in model-based CRL. The identification of reservoir sampling as a robust strategy for maintaining memory across tasks is a tangible outcome that can be applied in future implementations.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work encourages further exploration of world models for CRL, particularly concerning:
 - **Continuous Control Benchmarks**: Extending the approach to environments like ContinualWorld, which involve continuous action spaces and complex physics.
 - **Advanced World Model Architectures**: Applying the principles to newer and more powerful world model variants (e.g., DreamerV3), which could further enhance performance and scalability.
 - **Addressing Interference and Imbalanced Tasks**: The identified limitations regarding interference in tasks with changing reward functions and the struggle with imbalanced task data distributions highlight crucial areas for future methodological development in world-model-based CRL.
- **Potential for Scalable Intelligent Systems**: By offering a sample-efficient and task-agnostic framework, this research contributes to the long-term goal of building truly scalable intelligent systems that can continually acquire and adapt skills without being reset for each new task.
