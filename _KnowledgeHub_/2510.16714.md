# SceneCOT: Eliciting Grounded Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in 3D Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> SCENECOT introduces a framework that facilitates step-by-step, grounded Chain-of-Thought reasoning within 3D scenes, supported by the creation of the SCENECOT-185K dataset. The approach enhanced grounding–QA coherence and yielded competitive question-answering results on 3D reasoning benchmarks, notably outperforming baselines on the Beacon3D benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing 3D vision-language models (3D-LLMs) exhibit 'poor grounding–QA coherence,' often generating verbally fluent answers without explicit connections to visual evidence in the 3D scene.
- Complex 3D reasoning, particularly in embodied and situated settings, lacks principled task decomposition and step-by-step processes, unlike human cognitive approaches.
- There is an absence of large-scale, high-quality datasets that provide explicit, stepwise reasoning traces necessary for training models capable of grounded 3D reasoning.

## Method

- ==SCENECOT== establishes a four-stage reasoning process: Task Recognition, ==Task-relevant Region Localization==, ==Entity Grounding==, and Grounded Reasoning, each designed to produce explicit grounding signals.
- The framework integrates a Multi-modal Large Language Model (LLaVA-1.5) with specialized 3D-VL and 2D-VL models, along with symbolic engines, into a modular learning and inference pipeline.
- The ==SCENECOT-185K dataset==, the first large-scale 3D-CoTs dataset with **185K** instances, was created to provide high-quality supervision for learning grounded, step-by-step reasoning in 3D scenes.

## Results

- SCENECOT achieved competitive overall performance on the MSQA benchmark (**55.6** GPT-Score) and demonstrated superior results on the Beacon3D benchmark for object-centric reasoning (**58.9** Case and **23.2** Obj. GPT-Scores), surpassing all baselines.
- The framework exhibited enhanced grounding–QA coherence, securing the highest Good Coherence (GC) score of **34.7%** on Beacon3D, a considerable improvement over previous methods (e.g., LEO: **1.6%**, Chat-Scene: **19.5%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of key components, showing that removing question type recognition or region localization led to substantial performance drops across various sub-tasks (e.g., **~20%** drop in overall GPT-Score for forced question typing).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decomposing 3D reasoning into human-like, step-by-step stages with clear grounding signals substantially improves both question-answering accuracy and the coherence between answers and their visual evidence.
> - Task-specific components, such as question type recognition and relevant region localization, are crucial for effectively narrowing the reasoning scope, reducing noise, and strengthening grounding in complex 3D environments.
> - Applying a dedicated grounding loss during training is important for ensuring accurate object grounding, which is vital for maintaining the consistency and overall correctness of the step-by-step reasoning process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D scene understanding is a critical area for the development of embodied artificial intelligence agents. Current research in 3D Large Language Models (LLMs) has demonstrated advancements in processing multimodal 3D vision-language information. Early approaches, such as 3D-LLM and PointLLM, aimed to integrate multi-view 2D features or point cloud data with text embeddings. Subsequent works like LEO, Grounded 3D-LLM, Chat-Scene, and LLaVA-3D have further refined object-centric or voxel-based 3D representations and their alignment with language models, often using end-to-end training paradigms.

Despite these developments, a notable limitation identified in benchmarks like Beacon3D is the struggle of existing 3D vision-language models to achieve "grounded" question-answering. This refers to the ability to link generated answers explicitly to specific entities or regions within the 3D scene, leading to what is termed "poor grounding-QA coherence." Models may produce syntactically plausible answers without transparently demonstrating the intermediate reasoning steps or the specific visual evidence supporting their conclusions. This contrasts with human cognitive processes, which typically involve step-by-step decomposition of complex problems and reliance on concrete evidence.

In parallel, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has emerged as a significant advancement in the language domain, enabling LLMs to decompose complex problems into manageable sub-problems, thereby improving performance on tasks requiring multi-hop reasoning in mathematics, science, and logic. While CoT has been explored in 2D vision-language reasoning, its application to the complexities of 3D environments, which involve navigating large spaces, interpreting intricate spatial relations, and handling partial observability, remained largely unaddressed prior to this work. This paper aims to bridge this gap by introducing a framework for explicit, grounded, step-by-step reasoning within 3D scenes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the grounded question-answering capabilities of 3D Large Language Models by introducing a novel framework that emulates human-like scene-object grounded reasoning.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing the Grounding-QA Coherence Gap:** Existing 3D LLMs often generate plausible answers that lack explicit grounding in the 3D scene. This deficiency, highlighted by benchmarks such as Beacon3D, indicates a failure to connect intermediate grounding steps with the final reasoning outcome. The paper aims to develop a mechanism that ensures answers are supported by transparent, grounded, and stepwise reasoning processes, thereby improving grounding-QA coherence.
- **Leveraging Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning in 3D:** Inspired by the success of CoT in text-based and 2D multimodal domains for problem decomposition, the authors are motivated to adapt this paradigm to 3D scene understanding. The goal is to enable models to break down complex 3D reasoning tasks into simpler, manageable sub-problems, mirroring human cognitive processes for tasks involving spatial relations and multi-hop reasoning in 3D environments.
- **Developing a Grounded Reasoning Framework (SCENECOT):** The paper seeks to propose SCENECOT, a novel framework that explicitly decomposes complex 3D reasoning tasks into distinct stages. This decomposition is intended to ensure that each step of the reasoning process is grounded in the 3D scene and contributes coherently to the final answer.
- **Creating a Large-Scale Grounded CoT Dataset (SCENECOT-185K):** To facilitate the training and evaluation of models capable of such step-by-step grounded reasoning, a key objective is the creation of a large-scale dataset. This dataset is designed to contain high-quality reasoning traces that capture the full stepwise trajectory, including task recognition, region selection, object grounding, and answer generation across diverse 3D QA benchmarks.
- **Demonstrating Performance and Interpretability:** The research aims to demonstrate through extensive experimentation that the SCENECOT framework achieves robust performance on challenging 3D reasoning benchmarks. Furthermore, a central objective is to show that the framework produces reasoning processes that are both interpretable and faithfully grounded in the visual content of the 3D scene.
- **Enabling Step-by-Step Human-like Reasoning:** Ultimately, the motivation is to take a foundational step towards enabling 3D LLMs to perform reasoning in a manner more analogous to human cognition, with potential for extension to broader 3D scene understanding scenarios and embodied AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SCENECOT framework integrates a Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) with specialized 3D-VL and 2D-VL models, and symbolic engines, to perform step-by-step grounded reasoning in 3D scenes. The core of the approach is a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning method that explicitly decomposes complex tasks.

#### 3.1. Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in SCENECOT

The reasoning process in SCENECOT is formalized as a sequence of step-by-step descriptions for a given 3D scene, agent situation, and question:

1. **Task Recognition and Analysis (`<think_type>`):** The initial step identifies the type of task required to answer the question (e.g., counting, navigation, attribute recognition). This analysis guides the subsequent reasoning steps and the selection of specialized models.
2. **Task-relevant Region Localization (`<think_rgn>`):** The framework reduces the reasoning space by localizing the relevant sub-region of the scene. This is achieved by parsing directional cues (e.g., left, right, front) or clock-based references (e.g., 1 o’clock) from the question relative to the agent’s situation.
3. **Entity Grounding (`<think_grd>`, `[OBJ]`):** This step grounds target objects relevant to the question. Detailed grounding instructions encoding object semantics, attributes, and relational context are generated. A special `[OBJ]` token triggers specialized grounding modules to localize the referenced objects.
4. **Grounded Reasoning (`<think_task>`, `<list_obj_prob>`, `<obj_loc_prob>`, `<obj_loc_plr_prob>`, `<highlight_obj>`, `<img_start>`, `<img_end>`, `<think_sum>`, `<answer>`):** Given the candidate objects, task-specific instructions are generated to retrieve or compute necessary information for downstream reasoning.
 - **Object Probability (`<obj_prob>`):** For tasks like counting or existence verification, class probabilities of grounded objects are recorded.
 - **3D Object Location with Probability (`<obj_loc_prob>`):** For spatial reasoning, object class probabilities and their 3D positions are recorded.
 - **2D Polar Coordinate Object Location with Probability (`<obj_loc_plr_prob>`):** For navigation tasks, object locations are represented in a 2D polar coordinate frame to facilitate direction-based reasoning.
 - **Object Image Tokens (`<highlight_obj>`, `<img_start>`, `<img_end>`):** For fine-grained attribute description, `<highlight_obj>` triggers image retrieval, inserting object-level image patches as visual tokens to support appearance-based reasoning.
 A summarization hint (`<think_sum>`) guides the final answer generation (`<answer>`) based on the gathered grounded information.

#### 3.2. SCENECOT Learning and Inference

- **Architecture:** SCENECOT is built upon an MLLM (LLaVA-1.5, based on Vicuna-7B) as the reasoning engine. It integrates two types of modular components:
 - **Specialized 3D-VL and 2D-VL Models:** These models, initialized with pre-trained weights, are jointly updated during SCENECOT training. A fine-tuned PQ3D model serves as the 3D visual grounding expert, and a 2D vision-language model processes object images.
 - **Symbolic Engines:** Off-the-shelf parsers and pre-trained models extract object-specific grounding information (e.g., location, coordinates, image patches) for region recognition and grounded reasoning. These modules remain fixed during training.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained using a dataset of annotated reasoning traces. The objective function combines three losses:
 - `L_CoT`: Causal language modeling loss for predicting the reasoning trace.
 - `L_ans`: Causal language modeling loss for predicting the final answer.
 - `L_ground`: Cross-entropy loss applied to the specialized grounding module for accurate object grounding.
 LoRA is used for fine-tuning the MLLM.
- **Inference:** During inference, SCENECOT follows the predicted 3D-CoTs. Special tokens trigger external module calls (e.g., Mask3D for object proposals, PQ3D for object grounding). The outputs from these modules are concatenated with prior predictions and fed back into the MLLM for subsequent reasoning steps. A two-expert routing mechanism is employed for MSQA evaluation, where models with best overall or partial sub-task validation performance are selected based on predicted question type.

#### 3.3. The SCENECOT-185K Dataset

To enable learning, SCENECOT-185K was constructed as the first large-scale 3D-CoTs dataset, comprising 185K instances across Situated Reasoning (MSQA) and Object-Centric Reasoning (GQA3D derived from Nr3D).

- **Metadata Collection:**
 - **Situated Reasoning (MSQA):** Object instances within sub-regions, question types, and grounding texts are collected. Rule-based procedures extract target objects based on agent location/orientation and directional cues (cardinal/clock-based).
 - **Object-Centric Reasoning (GQA3D):** Nr3D, a 3D Visual Grounding benchmark, is used as a metadata source. GPT-4o generates QA pairs based on object images and grounding texts, categorized as "Attribute" type.
- **Reasoning Trace Generation:** Full reasoning chains are constructed according to the defined steps:
 - **Counting, Existence, Refer, Room Type, Affordance:** Ground-truth thoughts use semantic labels and pseudo probabilities (random values between 0.5-1.0 for targets, 0-0.5 for non-targets), with object counts capped for token length.
 - **Spatial Relationship:** Relative coordinates are computed using agent and object 3D rectilinear positions.
 - **Navigation:** Object locations are represented in a 2D polar coordinate system.
 - **Attribute/Description:** An object image library (extracted from ScanNet RGB frames) supports appearance-based reasoning.
The dataset totals 145.6K instances for Situated Reasoning and 40K for Object-Centric Reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated SCENECOT on two primary 3D scene reasoning tasks: Situated Reasoning (MSQA benchmark) and Object-Centric Reasoning (Beacon3D benchmark). Evaluation metrics included GPT-score for QA, and for Beacon3D, GPT-Score (by case), GPT-Score (by object), and grounding-QA coherence metrics (GC, Type 1, Type 2, DF, R1, R2). Baselines included GPT-4o, LEO, MSR3D, Chat-Scene, and SplatTalk, with several baselines retrained on the SCENECOT-185K training set for fair comparison.

#### 4.1. QA Performance on Situated and Object-Centric Reasoning

- **Overall Performance:** SCENECOT demonstrated strong QA performance on both MSQA and Beacon3D.
 - On MSQA, it achieved an overall GPT-score of 55.6, comparable to the top baselines (Chat-Scene: 56.6).
 - On Beacon3D, SCENECOT significantly outperformed all baselines with a GPT-Score (by case) of 58.9 and GPT-Score (by object) of 23.2.
- **Sub-task Specific Performance (MSQA):**
 - SCENECOT showed notable gains on the **Counting** sub-task (47.9), outperforming all baselines, attributed to its explicit enumeration of objects in relevant sub-regions based on semantic similarity.
 - It performed comparably on **Spatial** tasks (Refer and Spatial Relationship).
 - Performance on **Existence** (82.1) and **Attribute** (49.6) was slightly lower than some baselines (LEO, MSR3D), which the authors attribute to grounding errors.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - SCENECOT outperformed object-centric baselines on Beacon3D due to its explicit scene-object grounded reasoning.
 - Chat-Scene, which directly predicts answers from object-centric tokens without explicit grounding, showed limitations on complex tasks like Counting where object-level reasoning is crucial.

#### 4.2. Grounding-QA Coherence

- **Superior Good Coherence (GC):** On Beacon3D, SCENECOT achieved the highest Good Coherence (GC) score of 34.7, substantially exceeding all baselines (e.g., SceneVerse: 20.4, Chat-Scene: 19.5, LEO: 1.6). This indicates SCENECOT's enhanced ability to combine correct grounding with correct QA reliably.
- **Highest Per-Object QA (QA (Obj.)):** SCENECOT also delivered the highest QA (Obj.) score of 23.2, showing a significant margin over other methods. This suggests stronger per-object QA capabilities and a tighter alignment between answers and the referenced objects.
- **Addressing Mismatch:** Baselines like LEO exhibited a large mismatch between correct answers and correct grounding (QA (Obj.): 7.8 vs. GC: 1.6), a gap SCENECOT's explicit grounding addresses.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analyses

Ablation studies investigated the importance of key components of the SCENECOT framework:

- **Question Type Recognition:** Removing question type recognition (e.g., forcing all questions to "existence" or "spatial relationship") led to performance degradation across various tasks, confirming its role in constructing appropriate grounded reasoning chains.
- **Region Recognition:** Disabling region recognition (i.e., providing all scene objects instead of task-relevant sub-regions) resulted in a substantial performance drop, particularly for Counting, Refer, and Attribute tasks. This highlights its importance for noise reduction and strengthening grounding-QA coherence.
- **Grounding Loss:** Ablating the `L_ground` term in the training objective caused a noticeable performance decline, especially for Counting, Refer, and Navigation. This indicates the grounding loss is crucial for maintaining step-by-step grounding consistency and improving reasoning accuracy.

#### 4.4. Oracle Upper Bound Analysis

Testing with oracle settings (removing semantic errors and grounding errors) on MSQA showed significant performance improvements, particularly for Counting, Existence, and Navigation. Removing both types of errors pushed results close to the oracle bound (e.g., Counting/Existence near 100%, Navigation 87.2%, Refer 84.9%). This suggests that accurate grounding and semantics are key to advancing 3D reasoning and that SCENECOT effectively translates these into better answers. Spatial Relationship remained the most challenging task, even with oracle information.

#### 4.5. Additional Grounding Performance Evaluation

SCENECOT achieved strong and balanced grounding performance across various benchmarks, including traditional grounding (Nr3D, Beacon3D) and QA-driven grounding tasks (MSQA, SQA3D, ScanQA).
- It performed competitively on Nr3D (Top-1: 57.7) and Beacon3D (Top-1: 67.8).
- It significantly surpassed prior methods on MSQA (F1@50: 52.1), SQA3D (F1@50: 51.6), and ScanQA (F1@50: 40.8) for QA-driven grounding, even without explicit fine-tuning on SQA3D or ScanQA. This indicates the generalizability and effectiveness of its grounding capabilities.

#### 4.6. Reasoning Chain Visualization

Qualitative examples illustrated SCENECOT's ability to construct interpretable reasoning chains. For Counting and Navigation tasks, the model correctly grounded objects, computed relative positions, and inferred answers. However, limitations were observed where the model failed in the final reasoning step despite accurate visual clues, suggesting a gap due to the base model's reasoning capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SCENECOT framework represents an advancement in 3D scene understanding by introducing a structured, interpretable approach to grounded reasoning. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

- **Enhanced Grounding-QA Coherence:** By explicitly decomposing complex tasks into task recognition, region localization, entity grounding, and grounded reasoning, SCENECOT ensures that answers are directly traceable to specific visual evidence within the 3D scene. This significantly improves grounding-QA coherence, addressing a known limitation of prior 3D vision-language models which often produced plausible but ungrounded responses. This transparency is crucial for the reliability and trustworthiness of AI systems operating in physical environments.
- **Pioneering 3D Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** This work is presented as the first successful application of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to 3D scene understanding. By enabling step-by-step, human-like reasoning, SCENECOT bridges a gap between the advanced reasoning capabilities of LLMs in 2D and textual domains and the complexities of 3D spatial intelligence. This paradigm shift can lead to more robust and generalizable 3D reasoning agents.
- **Novel Large-Scale Dataset:** The creation of SCENECOT-185K, the first large-scale dataset with stepwise grounded reasoning traces in 3D scenes, provides a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset, with its rich annotations linking language and 3D context, facilitates the training and evaluation of future models aiming for interpretable and grounded 3D reasoning.
- **Interpretability and Diagnosability:** The interpretable reasoning traces generated by SCENECOT make the decision-making process transparent. This enhanced interpretability is critical for diagnosing errors, understanding model behavior, and building safer AI systems, particularly in sensitive applications.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI:** The ability to perform grounded, step-by-step reasoning in 3D environments is a fundamental requirement for developing human-level embodied agents. SCENECOT lays a foundation for future advancements in embodied AI, enabling agents to operate more effectively, safely, and intelligently in real-world physical spaces, with potential applications in domestic robotics, assistive technologies, and autonomous navigation.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations. The current framework primarily focuses on tasks predefined in MSQA, which may not encompass the full complexity of long-horizon tasks found in embodied AI (e.g., embodied task planning). The dataset is also currently limited to ScanNet scenes, suggesting a need for extension to more diverse real-world environments. The design of 3D-CoTs, while effective, still faces challenges in certain sub-tasks like Spatial Relationship. Future work may involve exploring more advanced learning algorithms, such as Reinforcement Learning, to further enhance reasoning capabilities and extend the framework to broader 3D understanding scenarios.

Overall, SCENECOT represents a meaningful step towards building multimodal LLMs that can achieve more human-like, interpretable, and reliably grounded reasoning in complex 3D scenes.
