# GROVE: A Generalized Reward for Learning Open-Vocabulary Physical Skill

> [!summary] Summary
> GROVE is a generalized reward framework that enables simulated agents to learn open-vocabulary physical skills from natural language instructions. It integrates LLM-generated physical constraints with VLM-based semantic evaluations using a novel Pose2CLIP network, achieving 25.7% higher task completion and 22.2% better motion naturalness compared to baselines, while converging 8.4 times faster.

## Problem

- Manually designing task-specific reward functions for Reinforcement Learning (RL) is time-consuming and does not scale for diverse physical tasks.
- Existing LLM-based approaches struggle with holistic motion assessment and stylistic qualities, leading to unnatural movements, while VLM-based methods face a significant sim-to-real domain gap and cannot enforce precise kinematic constraints.
- Leveraging VLMs for simulated environments often necessitates computationally expensive high-fidelity rendering to bridge the visual domain gap, hindering efficient RL training.

## Method

- A generalized reward function combines an LLM-generated component for precise physical constraints (RLR_LRL) with a VLM-based semantic evaluation (RVR_VRV) from a frozen CLIP model.
- A lightweight neural network, Pose2CLIP, directly maps agent pose representations to CLIP's feature space, circumventing the need for computationally expensive rendering to address the sim-to-real gap.
- ==An Iterative Reward Design Process (RDP)== employs an adaptive rejection sampling strategy to dynamically refine the LLM-generated reward function, using the VLM-based reward as a fitness evaluator to ensure semantic alignment.

## Results

- GROVE achieved **25.7%** higher task completion scores and a **22.2%** improvement in perceived motion naturalness for open-vocabulary humanoid skill acquisition compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- It demonstrated robust generalizability across five diverse RL tasks and embodiments, exhibiting superior training efficiency (faster convergence) in three out of four tasks compared to directly optimizing expert rewards.
- The framework achieved **8.4** times faster convergence than previous methods and significantly outperformed VLM-RM while using only **11.4%** of its training time for the humanoid bowing task, highlighting its efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining LLM-derived precise kinematic constraints and VLM-derived holistic semantic evaluation offers a more complete and robust reward signal than either modality alone for open-vocabulary physical skill learning.
> - Directly mapping agent poses to VLM feature space via Pose2CLIP dramatically improves computational efficiency by bypassing costly rendering, making VLM-based rewards practical for real-time RL training.
> - An adaptive, VLM-guided regeneration process for LLM-based rewards ensures that optimization leads to both physically correct and semantically natural behaviors, preventing divergence towards unintended solutions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI, particularly the challenge of enabling simulated agents to learn diverse physical skills from natural language instructions, is a crucial frontier in AI, robotics, and computer graphics. Historically, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved impressive results in physical control, but its scalability is severely limited by the need for manually engineered reward functions, which are task-specific, time-consuming to design, and non-generalizable. Traditional imitation learning offers some generalization by learning from demonstrations, but it is fundamentally constrained by the distribution of its training data and cannot easily extend to open-vocabulary tasks.

GROVE positions itself at the intersection of recent advances in large-scale foundation models—specifically Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs)—and the persistent challenge of reward design in RL. Recent efforts have explored leveraging LLMs (e.g., Eureka) for generating precise, dynamic constraints and reward signals, and VLMs (e.g., AnySkill, VLM-RM) for providing semantic feedback and evaluating "naturalness." However, existing LLM-based approaches often struggle with holistic motion assessment and stylistic qualities, leading to technically correct but unnatural movements. Conversely, VLM-based methods, while strong in semantic evaluation, face significant domain gaps between simplified simulation renderings and natural images, struggle with temporal consistency, and cannot enforce precise kinematic constraints.

GROVE directly addresses these distinct limitations by proposing a synergistic framework. It aims to bridge the gap between LLM's strength in precise physical constraint generation and VLM's ability to evaluate overall motion semantics and naturalness. The paper also tackles the critical "sim-to-real" domain gap that plagues VLM-based methods by introducing **Pose2CLIP**, a novel, lightweight neural network that directly maps agent poses to CLIP's semantic feature space without requiring computationally expensive high-fidelity rendering. This contribution is crucial as it unlocks efficient VLM utilization in simulated environments. By combining these complementary strengths and resolving key technical hurdles, GROVE seeks to establish a more robust and generalizable foundation for open-vocabulary physical skill acquisition, moving beyond the limitations of single-modality approaches or those reliant on extensive demonstrations or manual reward engineering.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of GROVE is to develop a **generalized reward framework** that facilitates the learning of **open-vocabulary physical skills** for simulated agents. This means enabling agents to perform a wide array of complex physical actions specified through natural language, without the need for manual reward engineering, task-specific demonstrations, or extensive human intervention.

The motivation behind this ambitious goal stems from several critical limitations in current AI and robotics research:
1. **Lack of Scalability in Reward Design**: Manually designing reward functions for diverse physical tasks is a bottleneck, requiring significant human expertise and effort. This approach is not scalable for the growing complexity and variety of tasks expected of intelligent agents.
2. **Generalization Limitations of Demonstration-Based Methods**: While imitation learning offers an alternative, it is inherently limited by its training data. Agents trained via demonstrations struggle to generalize to novel tasks or conditions outside their pre-defined training distribution.
3. **Incompleteness of Single-Modality Foundation Models**: While LLMs can provide precise, symbolic constraints and VLMs can offer rich semantic evaluations, neither alone provides a complete solution for robust physical skill learning. LLMs lack holistic visual intuition for natural movement, and VLMs struggle with precise kinematic enforcement and the sim-to-real gap.
4. **Computational Inefficiency of High-Fidelity Rendering**: Leveraging VLMs for simulated environments often necessitates high-fidelity rendering to bridge the visual domain gap, which is computationally prohibitive for real-time RL training.

GROVE is motivated to overcome these challenges by combining the complementary strengths of LLMs and VLMs within an iterative, self-improving reward system. The framework aims to provide a robust and efficient mechanism for agents to interpret arbitrary natural language instructions and translate them into physically plausible, semantically accurate, and natural-looking motions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GROVE's methodology revolves around a novel generalized reward framework that integrates LLM-generated constraints with VLM-based semantic evaluation, dynamically refined through an iterative process.

#### 3.1. Generalized Reward Function

The core of GROVE is its reward function, $R_{GROVE}(s, a, I) = \omega_V R_V(s, I) + \omega_L R_L(s, a; I)$, a weighted combination of:
- **VLM-based semantic evaluation ($R_V$)**: This component uses a frozen CLIP model to assess the semantic alignment between the agent's behavior and the natural language instruction. Crucially, instead of computationally expensive rendering and direct CLIP similarity calculation, GROVE introduces **Pose2CLIP**.
- **LLM-generated constraint component ($R_L$)**: This component leverages a Large Language Model (specifically GPT-o1-preview) to translate natural language instructions into precise programmatic reward functions that capture physical and kinematic constraints. GROVE enhances the prompting process by including detailed agent specifications (joint nomenclature, indices) and explicitly instructing the LLM to "focus solely on capturing the essence of the task," assuming auxiliary mechanisms handle fundamental aspects like stability and locomotion.

#### 3.2. Pose2CLIP: Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap

To address the significant visual domain gap between simplified simulation environments and the natural images on which VLMs like CLIP are trained, GROVE introduces **Pose2CLIP**. This lightweight neural network directly maps agent pose representations to CLIP's feature space, circumventing the need for explicit rendering.
- **Training Data**: Pose2CLIP is trained on a diverse dataset of 1.7 million frames, combining SMPL poses from AMASS and Motion-X datasets. This dataset is continuously augmented with policy rollouts during RL training, ensuring comprehensive coverage of poses encountered during learning.
- **Ground Truth Features**: Each pose is rendered using high-quality Blender renderings (with realistic textures and lighting) from five strategic viewpoints (front, side, oblique, rear side, rear). These multi-view images are then processed through a pre-trained CLIP-ViT-B/32 model, and their features are mean-pooled to create a single, robust ground truth embedding.
- **Architecture**: Pose2CLIP is a lightweight two-layer MLP optimized to minimize MSE reconstruction loss between predicted and ground truth CLIP features. A balanced sampling strategy (k-means++ clustering followed by two-stage uniform sampling) is used during training to counter data imbalance and ensure representation of rare but important poses.

#### 3.3. Iterative Reward Design Process (RDP)

GROVE incorporates an adaptive rejection sampling strategy to dynamically refine the LLM-generated reward function ($R_L$). The VLM-based reward ($R_V$) acts as a fitness evaluator. If a consistent performance decline is detected (8 consecutive steps of decreasing average $R_V$ across environments, with the final average below a threshold of 0.1), the LLM-based reward function is regenerated. This mechanism ensures that the optimization towards $R_L$ consistently leads to visually and semantically correct behaviors, preventing divergence towards unintended solutions.

#### 3.4. Experimental Settings

GROVE's effectiveness is demonstrated in two distinct settings:
1. **Hierarchical Control**: Integrating GROVE with pre-trained low-level controllers (e.g., CALM) for complex humanoid skill acquisition. A high-level policy then optimizes the GROVE reward for instruction-guided coordination of motion primitives.
2. **Learning from Scratch**: Guiding standard RL algorithms (e.g., PPO in IsaacGym) to learn diverse control policies for various embodiments from open-vocabulary instructions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GROVE's experimental evaluations demonstrate its effectiveness, generalizability, and efficiency across diverse tasks and embodiments.

#### 4.1. Open-Vocabulary Humanoid Skill Acquisition

- **Qualitative Results**: GROVE successfully generates diverse and naturalistic motions for complex open-vocabulary instructions such as "conduct the orchestra," "position body in a shape of C," "playing the suona," "running while jumping hurdle," and "walking like a model." These motions are physically plausible and accurately reflect the textual descriptions (Figure 4).
- **Quantitative Results (User Studies & Metrics)**: Compared to state-of-the-art baselines (TMR, MoMask, MotionGPT, AnySkill, AvatarCLIP), GROVE achieves:
 - **25.7% higher task completion scores**.
 - **22.2% improvement in perceived motion naturalness**.
 - Competitive or superior performance in motion smoothness, physical realism, and CLIP text-image similarity (Table 1).
 - Crucially, GROVE operates within physical simulation constraints, a more stringent evaluation setting than many comparison methods that produce motions without such limitations.

#### 4.2. Standard RL Benchmarks

- **Generalizability**: GROVE demonstrates robust generalizability across five diverse RL tasks and embodiments (Cartpole, Ant, ANYmal walking, ANYmal jumping, Humanoid bowing).
- **Training Efficiency**: GROVE exhibits superior training efficiency (measured by "reward distance," indicating faster convergence to optimal behavior) in three out of four tasks compared to directly optimizing expert rewards (Table 2).
- **Efficiency vs. Baselines**: GROVE outperforms VLM-RM (a VLM-based approach that relies on texture enhancements for reward effectiveness) while using unmodified textures and requiring only 11.4% of its training time (47 min vs. 411 min for the humanoid bowing task) (Table 3). This highlights the efficiency benefits of GROVE's LLM-based reward formulation and Pose2CLIP.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Comprehensive ablation studies confirm the synergistic benefits of GROVE's components:
- **Pose2CLIP's Impact**: Replacing direct CLIP features with Pose2CLIP features significantly improves task completion and reduces convergence time, underscoring the importance of high-quality, efficient visual representations.
- **Complementary Strengths**: LLM-only approaches excel at naturalness, while Pose2CLIP-only methods better adhere to instructional details, validating the hypothesis that these modalities capture different, yet essential, aspects of performance.
- **Full Model Superiority**: The full GROVE model (Pose2CLIP + LLM with RDP) consistently outperforms all ablated variants across all metrics, confirming the synergistic interaction between its components.
- **Accelerated Convergence**: The integration of multi-modal rewards (LLM and VLM) accelerates the convergence of both reward functions compared to their uni-modal counterparts (Figure 7), demonstrating that they offer complementary guidance signals for more efficient skill acquisition.
- **Overall Efficiency**: GROVE achieves an impressive **8.4× faster convergence** compared to previous methods while maintaining high performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GROVE represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI, offering a generalized and efficient framework for physical skill acquisition. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

1. **Enabling Scalable Skill Learning**: By eliminating the need for manual reward engineering and task-specific demonstrations, GROVE offers a truly scalable approach to teaching complex physical skills to simulated agents. This paves the way for agents that can learn and adapt to an open-ended array of tasks simply by being given natural language instructions.
2. **Addressing Core Limitations of Foundation Models**: GROVE effectively combines the symbolic reasoning and precise constraint generation capabilities of LLMs with the holistic semantic understanding of VLMs, while mitigating their individual weaknesses (lack of naturalness for LLMs, sim-to-real gap for VLMs). This multimodal integration sets a new standard for leveraging foundation models in embodied control.
3. **Efficiency and Practicality**: The introduction of Pose2CLIP is a critical technical innovation that dramatically improves computational efficiency by bypassing expensive high-fidelity rendering. This makes VLM-based rewards practical for real-time RL training, accelerating research and development in this area. The 8.4x faster convergence further underscores its efficiency.
4. **Broad Generalizability**: Demonstrated success across diverse embodiments (humanoids, quadrupedal robots, simple inverted pendulums) and learning paradigms (hierarchical control with pre-trained policies, learning from scratch) highlights GROVE's robustness and versatility. This implies its potential applicability across various robotic platforms and simulation environments.
5. **Foundation for Advanced AI Agents**: GROVE's ability to interpret and execute open-vocabulary physical skills could lead to the development of more intelligent, adaptive, and human-friendly AI agents in virtual environments, robotics, and character animation. Such agents could learn new tasks on the fly, making them highly versatile for complex real-world applications.
6. **Future Research Directions**: The framework opens up avenues for further research into sophisticated multimodal reward generation, more advanced pose-to-semantic mapping techniques, and extending the framework to real-world robotic systems (sim-to-real transfer), which is hinted at by the robustness of Pose2CLIP. The iterative reward design (RDP) also suggests a powerful paradigm for self-improving AI systems.

In conclusion, GROVE establishes a robust and efficient foundation for open-vocabulary physical skill learning, addressing longstanding challenges in RL reward design and multimodal AI integration. Its contributions have far-reaching implications for creating more capable and adaptable intelligent agents across various domains.
