# Generative Recursive Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST, Mila, New York University, and Université de Montréal introduced Generative Recursive Reasoning Models (GRAM), a framework that injects stochasticity into recursive neural networks to enable multi-hypothesis exploration and generative capabilities. GRAM achieves improved performance on complex structured reasoning and multi-solution constraint satisfaction problems, outperforming deterministic recursive baselines and demonstrating high-quality unconditional generation of diverse data.

## Problem

- Existing Recursive Reasoning Models (RRMs) are predominantly deterministic, following a single latent trajectory and producing a single prediction.
- This deterministic nature limits their capacity to maintain uncertainty, explore alternative hypotheses, or address inherent ambiguity in complex reasoning problems.
- Deterministic RRMs struggle with multi-solution tasks, often exhibiting mode collapse and low coverage of possible solutions.

## Method

- **GRAM** formulates recursive reasoning as a stochastic latent trajectory, allowing for probabilistic, multi-hypothesis exploration.
- It employs a hierarchical recursive core with an inner loop for low-level refinement and an outer loop for high-level stochastic state transitions.
- Inference-time computation is scaled through both recursive depth and parallel sampling of multiple latent trajectories, with a Latent Process Reward Model (LPRM) used for effective candidate selection.

## Results

- GRAM consistently outperformed deterministic recursive baselines on structured reasoning tasks, achieving **97.0%** accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme and **52.0%** on ARC-AGI-1.
- It demonstrated superior performance and coverage on multi-solution constraint satisfaction problems (e.g., **99.7%** accuracy and **90.3%** coverage on N-Queens 8x8), where deterministic models showed mode collapse.
- GRAM achieved high-quality unconditional generation, producing **99.05%** valid Sudoku boards and diverse Binarized MNIST digits with competitive Inception and FID scores, showing improved efficiency over diffusion models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Introducing stochasticity into recursive latent state transitions enables the exploration of multiple reasoning paths and the maintenance of uncertainty, improving robustness in complex problems.
> - Computational scaling can occur not only through recursive depth but also by exploring multiple parallel latent trajectories at inference time, enhancing solution quality and robustness.
> - The proposed variational framework allows recursive models to function as effective unconditional generative models for structured data, producing diverse and constrained outputs beyond conditional reasoning tasks.
