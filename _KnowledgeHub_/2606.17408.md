# Where Should Action Generation Begin? A Learnable Source Prior for Generative Robot Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> LeaP, a learnable, proprioception-conditioned stochastic prior, improves generative robot policies by replacing the standard Gaussian action generation source with an informed yet uncertain starting point. This method achieved an 81.6% average success rate across 15 simulated manipulation tasks and 80.0% in real-world experiments, outperforming existing baselines with minimal overhead.

## Problem

- Generative robot policies typically initialize action generation from an observation-independent standard Gaussian distribution, requiring the generator to transform samples from an uninformed noise source.
- This leads to inefficient computational effort as the generator must perform fundamental "transport" tasks before fine-grained action synthesis.
- Prior attempts to use observation-derived starting points often neglect the inherent uncertainty of action generation, using deterministic point estimates.

## Method

- Introduces **LeaP**, a learnable, ==proprioception-conditioned diagonal Gaussian prior==, which replaces the default standard Gaussian source distribution.
- A lightweight MLP maps proprioceptive state features to the prior's mean and log-variance, anchoring the initial sample in a state-dependent region of the action space.
- The prior is trained jointly with the generative model using a combined loss function that includes flow matching, negative log-likelihood for the prior, and an instance-level alignment term.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **81.6%** across 15 simulated manipulation tasks, surpassing existing baselines by **6.5** to **25.5** percentage points.
- Demonstrated an **80.0%** average success rate on three real-world robot manipulation tasks, outperforming baselines by **11.7** to **33.3** percentage points.
- Introduced minimal parameter overhead (**1.6%** of total model parameters) and exhibited faster, more stable training convergence compared to other methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The source distribution is a critical and independently designable component within generative robot policies, not just the generative dynamics.
> - A learnable, proprioception-conditioned stochastic prior enhances efficiency by providing an informed yet uncertain starting point, allowing the generator to focus on action refinement.
> - State-adaptive uncertainty in the source prior is more effective for local anchoring of generation than fixed stochasticity or increased prior expressivity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot imitation learning, particularly visuomotor policies, has seen advancements with the adoption of generative models. These policies, including diffusion-based and flow-matching approaches, are designed to model complex action distributions from expert demonstrations. A common practice within these generative frameworks is to initiate action generation from an observation-independent standard Gaussian distribution, denoted as N(0, I). This default starting point implies that the generative model must perform the entire transformation from an uninformed noise source to a task-relevant action.

Recent research has begun to investigate alternatives to this standard Gaussian source by incorporating observational information. Methods such as A2A, VITA, and BridgePolicy derive the starting point from observations, often as a deterministic point estimate or a narrowly defined, observation-conditioned initialization. However, these approaches frequently overlook the role of uncertainty in the source distribution. Previous analyses suggest that the efficacy of generative robot policies may stem from iterative computation and stochasticity injection, not solely from distributional expressivity. While some methods have explored injecting fixed-variance Gaussian noise, this has not consistently improved nominal task performance or explicitly modeled source uncertainty.

This paper addresses the underexplored design aspect of the source distribution in generative robot policies. It investigates whether the source should be deterministic or distributional, if distributional, whether its variance should be fixed or state-adaptive, and which observation modality should parameterize it. By proposing a learnable, proprioception-conditioned stochastic prior, this work seeks to enhance generative policies by providing an informed yet uncertain starting point, thereby allowing the downstream generator to focus more efficiently on precise action refinement. This positions the work as an investigation into optimizing the initial conditions of generative models in robotics, complementary to advancements in generative dynamics themselves.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the optimal starting point for action generation within generative robot policies. The authors pose the question: "where should action generation begin?"

The motivation for this investigation stems from several observations regarding current generative robot policies:
1. **Inefficient Initialization:** Existing generative robot policies, such as those based on diffusion and flow matching, typically begin action generation from an observation-independent standard Gaussian distribution N(0, I). This source provides no information about the current robot state or the task context. Consequently, a substantial portion of the generator's computational effort and integration budget is expended on transporting samples from this uninformed noise source to a region of the action space relevant to the current observation and task. This process is considered inefficient as it burdens the generator with fundamental "transport" tasks rather than focusing on fine-grained action synthesis.
2. **Lack of Source Uncertainty Modeling:** While some recent methods (e.g., A2A, VITA, BridgePolicy) have attempted to derive the starting point from observations, they largely adopt a deterministic, observation-derived source. This approach treats the source as a point estimate or a tightly specified initialization, thereby neglecting the inherent uncertainty associated with generating actions. The authors highlight that a recent analysis by Pan et al. suggests the strength of generative robot policies may derive from iterative computation and stochasticity injection, implying that an explicit modeling of source uncertainty could be beneficial.
3. **Underexplored Design Axis:** The properties of the source distribution (e.g., deterministic vs. distributional, fixed vs. state-adaptive variance, choice of conditioning modality) remain an underexplored design space in generative robot policies. The authors aim to systematically investigate these aspects to determine if an optimized source distribution can enhance policy performance.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes LeaP (Learnable source Prior), which aims to:
- Replace the standard Gaussian N(0, I) with an observation-informed yet stochastic source distribution.
- Allow the generator to concentrate on precise action refinement by providing an initial sample within a proprioceptively plausible region of the action space.
- Be a plug-and-play module, modifying only the source distribution without requiring changes to the downstream generator architecture, generative dynamics, or inference solver.
- Systematically evaluate the impact of a learnable, distributional prior on various manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts flow matching as the underlying generative framework for policy learning. This framework involves learning a velocity field `v_theta` that transports a source sample `z_0`, drawn from a source distribution `q_0`, to a target expert action `a` along a linear interpolation path. The generator `v_theta` is trained to regress the ground-truth velocity `u_t = a - z_0` under observation conditioning `z_cond`.

The core of the proposed methodology, LeaP, involves the following components:

#### 3.1. LeaP: A Proprioception-Conditioned Gaussian Prior

- **Source Distribution Replacement:** LeaP replaces the conventional observation-independent standard Gaussian N(0, I) with a proprioception-conditioned diagonal Gaussian distribution, denoted `q_phi(z_0 | z_s)`. This distribution is designed to be shared across all `H` time steps of an action chunk.
- **Prior Network (g_phi):** A lightweight two-layer MLP, `g_phi`, maps the proprioceptive state feature `z_s` to the mean `mu_phi(z_s)` and log-variance `log sigma^2_phi(z_s)` of the diagonal Gaussian. The output dimension of `g_phi` is `2d_a`, split into `d_a` for the mean and `d_a` for the log-variance, where `d_a` is the per-step action dimensionality.
- **Sampling:** Samples `z_0,h` from this prior are generated using the reparameterization trick, `z_0,h = mu_phi(z_s) + sigma_phi(z_s) * epsilon_h`, where `epsilon_h` is drawn from N(0, I_da). This allows gradients to flow through `z_0` to update `mu_phi` and `sigma^2_phi`.
- **Conditioning Modality:** The prior `g_phi` is conditioned solely on the proprioceptive state feature `z_s`. This design choice reflects the prior's role in anchoring `z_0` in a state-dependent neighborhood of the action space, leaving finer-grained, scene-level disambiguation to the generator, which receives the full conditioning `z_cond = [z_vis; z_s]`.
- **Plug-and-Play Design:** LeaP is designed to be modular. It modifies only the source distribution `q_0`, leaving the architecture of the downstream velocity field `v_theta` (e.g., SimpleFlowNet), the generative dynamics, and the inference solver (e.g., forward-Euler integration) entirely unchanged.

#### 3.2. Joint Training Objective

The prior network `g_phi`, projection heads `phi_z`, `phi_a`, and the generator `v_theta` are optimized jointly using a combined loss function: `L = L_flow + beta L_NLL + alpha L_align`.
- **Flow Matching Loss (L_flow):** This is the standard objective for training the generator `v_theta`. It minimizes the squared error between the predicted velocity `v_theta(x_t, z_cond, t)` and the ground-truth velocity `(a - z_0)` along the interpolation path. Crucially, because `z_0` is reparameterized through `g_phi`, `L_flow` backpropagates gradients to `mu_phi` and `sigma^2_phi`, providing an implicit supervision signal for the prior.
- **Negative Log-Likelihood (L_NLL):** This term explicitly supervises `g_phi` by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the expert action chunk `a` under the proprioception-conditioned diagonal Gaussian `q_phi(z_0 | z_s)`. It penalizes residuals inversely by the predicted variance and includes a log `sigma^2_phi` term to prevent arbitrary variance inflation, thus coupling `mu_phi` and `sigma^2_phi`.
- **Instance-Level Alignment (L_align):** A symmetric contrastive loss (similar to CLIP) is used to encourage paired correspondence between the sampled source `z_0,i` and its corresponding target action `a_i` in a learned embedding space. This provides supervision that is orthogonal to `L_NLL`, coupling `z_0,i` to `a_i` at the sample level.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Environment:** Evaluation was conducted on 15 RoboTwin 2.0 bimanual manipulation tasks. Each task used 50 expert demonstrations, trained for 3000 epochs, and evaluated over 100 rollout episodes per task.
- **Real-World Environment:** Three tasks (Pick Cube, Close Box, Pick & Place Sandbags) were tested on a Franka Research 3 robot, using 100 teleoperated demonstrations and 20 evaluation rollouts with randomized object positions.
- **Baselines:** LeaP was compared against four representative baselines:
 - **A2A:** Uses a state-derived deterministic latent as the source.
 - **VITA:** Uses a visual latent as a deterministic source.
 - **NoPrior:** An architecture-matched counterpart to LeaP, which uses the same encoder and generator but initializes from N(0, I).
 - **BridgePolicy:** Redesigns both the source and generator dynamics through a diffusion bridge.
- **Shared Components:** All methods, except BridgePolicy, shared the same DP3 PointNet encoder and, for A2A and NoPrior, an MLP-based SimpleFlowNet velocity field. BridgePolicy was reimplemented from its original paper.
- **Ablation Studies:** Comprehensive ablations were performed on three core design dimensions: prior input modality, prior parameterization, and prior supervision, and generality across different generator backbones (flow matching vs. diffusion bridge).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the efficacy of LeaP across various evaluation criteria in both simulation and real-world settings.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance on Simulation Tasks

- LeaP achieved an average success rate of **81.6%** across 15 RoboTwin manipulation tasks.
- This represents an improvement over the tested baselines by **6.5 to 25.5 percentage points**.
- Specifically, LeaP outperformed A2A (73.8%), VITA (75.1%), NoPrior (56.1%), and BridgePolicy (61.4%).
- The largest performance gap was observed against NoPrior (25.5 pp), which shares the same encoder, generator, and flow-matching pipeline, differing only in the learned source prior. This highlights the contribution of the learnable prior itself.
- Deterministic observation-informed sources (A2A, VITA) performed between NoPrior and LeaP, suggesting that moving beyond a standard Gaussian is beneficial, but a learnable, distributional prior offers further advantages.

#### 4.2. Efficiency Analysis

- **Parameter Count:** LeaP utilized **13.22M** parameters, with the prior head `g_phi` accounting for only 0.21M (approximately 1.6%). This indicates a negligible overhead compared to the architecture-matched NoPrior (13.01M parameters). Other baselines like A2A and VITA used more parameters (16.92M, 16.88M, respectively) but achieved lower success rates. Larger models like ACT, DP3, and BridgePolicy (6-20x more parameters) also did not match LeaP's performance.
- **Training Convergence:** LeaP exhibited faster and more stable training convergence. For example, on the "open laptop" task, LeaP reached 91% success within 1000 epochs and remained stable, surpassing the final success rates of A2A, VITA, NoPrior, and BridgePolicy at 3000 epochs.

#### 4.3. Real-Robot Experiments

- On three real-world Franka Research 3 tasks, LeaP achieved an average success rate of **80.0%**.
- This performance exceeded A2A (68.3%), VITA (56.7%), and NoPrior (46.7%) by 11.7, 23.3, and 33.3 percentage points, respectively.
- The trend observed in simulation, where LeaP was the top performer and NoPrior was the lowest, was maintained in real-world deployment, reinforcing the robustness and effectiveness of the learned prior.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Prior Input Modality:** Conditioning the prior on **proprioceptive state alone** yielded the highest average success rate (85.3% across three tasks). Incorporating visual features (via FiLM, MLP, or cross-attention fusion) consistently degraded performance (e.g., FiLM 70.3%, CrossAttn 59.3%). Visualizations showed that the state-only prior placed source samples closer to the target action compared to vision-bearing variants.
- **Prior Parameterization:** The **NLL-trained diagonal Gaussian with state-adaptive variance** (LeaP, 85.3%) proved most effective. More expressive parameterizations like full covariance or Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) underperformed (79.7%, 78.7% respectively). Variants without state-adaptive variance, such as using only the mean (78.0%) or a fixed variance (62.7%), also trailed LeaP. The comparison between "only μ" and "μ+fixed σ" suggested that state-adaptive uncertainty is more critical than mere stochasticity. Visualizations confirmed LeaP's ability to place compact source distributions near the target action.
- **Prior Supervision:** All three loss terms (`L_flow`, `L_NLL`, `L_align`) contributed to performance. Removing either `L_align` or `L_NLL` individually reduced success rates (to 74.7% and 74.3% respectively). Removing both explicit prior supervision terms led to a further drop (59.7%), although still above the no-prior baseline (47.7%). Directly executing the prior's mean resulted in 0% success, confirming its role as a source for the generator rather than a replacement.
- **Generality Across Generators:** LeaP consistently improved performance when applied to different generator families. It enhanced a flow-matching generator (from 47.7% to 85.3%) and a diffusion-bridge generator (from 68.7% to 76.7%). This indicates that LeaP functions as a reusable source-distribution module independent of the specific generator architecture.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work highlights the **source distribution** as a critical and independently designable component within generative robot policies, complementary to the choice and design of the generative dynamics (e.g., flow matching or diffusion). The introduction of **LeaP, a learnable, proprioception-conditioned diagonal Gaussian prior**, provides a principled method for initializing action generation.

The significance of these findings can be summarized as follows:
- **Improved Policy Performance:** LeaP consistently improved success rates across a diverse set of manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world environments, demonstrating the practical benefits of an informed and stochastic initialization.
- **Enhanced Efficiency:** The approach achieved superior performance with fewer parameters and faster training convergence compared to several baselines. This suggests that optimizing the source distribution can be a computationally efficient lever for improving robot policy learning.
- **Task Decomposition:** By providing an observation-informed yet stochastic initialization, LeaP enables a natural division of labor. The prior places the starting sample within a plausible region of the action space, allowing the downstream generator to concentrate its efforts on precise, fine-grained action refinement.
- **Modularity and Reusability:** LeaP's plug-and-play nature, which only modifies the source distribution without altering the core generator architecture or inference solver, makes it broadly applicable. Its demonstrated effectiveness across both flow-matching and diffusion-bridge generators indicates its reusability as a universal component for generative robot policies.
- **Conceptual Advancement:** The research challenges the default use of an uninformed standard Gaussian source and emphasizes the importance of explicitly modeling uncertainty at the initiation stage of action generation. It suggests that state-adaptive uncertainty, rather than mere stochasticity or increased prior expressivity, is key to local anchoring of generation.

The potential impact extends to making robot learning more robust, efficient, and capable of handling complex manipulation tasks. By providing a better starting point, policies may require less data or simpler generator architectures to achieve high performance, accelerating the development and deployment of advanced robotic systems. The findings pave the way for future research into more sophisticated learnable priors and their integration into other generative models, including large-scale vision-language-action models and policies for more complex robot embodiments.
