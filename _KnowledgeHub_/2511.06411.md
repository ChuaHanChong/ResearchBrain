# SofT-GRPO: Surpassing Discrete-Token LLM Reinforcement Learning via Gumbel-Reparameterized Soft-Thinking Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> SofT-GRPO, a new reinforcement learning algorithm, enables large language models using the "soft-thinking" paradigm to achieve superior reasoning accuracy compared to discrete-token methods reinforced with GRPO. It demonstrates average accuracy improvements of +0.13% on Pass@1 and up to +2.19% on Pass@32 across numerical, scientific, and code reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Despite the theoretical advantages of "soft-thinking" over discrete-token Chain-of-Thought (CoT), prior attempts to apply Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to soft-thinking models failed to match or surpass discrete-token GRPO.
- Introducing controllable stochasticity into continuous soft-thinking tokens for effective exploration was challenging, often leading to generated inputs outside the pre-trained embedding space.
- Developing an effective policy optimization approach for continuous soft-thinking tokens, capable of accurate gradient estimation for reward attribution, proved difficult.

## Method

- Developed SofT-GRPO, a novel ==RLVR algorithm== specifically tailored for "soft-thinking" LLMs, integrating ==Gumbel-reparameterized policy optimization==.
- Employed ==Group Rollout with Gumbel Noise== by injecting Gumbel noise into log probabilities and applying ==Gumbel-Softmax== to create stochastic, continuous soft-thinking tokens while maintaining valid input representations.
- Utilized **Gumbel Reparameterization** within a modified GRPO loss function to enable accurate gradient estimation and policy updates for these continuous soft-thinking trajectories.

## Results

- SofT-GRPO achieved **+0.13%** higher average accuracy on Pass@1 and a notable **+2.19%** on Pass@32 compared to discrete-token GRPO across numerical reasoning benchmarks.
- The method significantly surpassed prior soft-thinking + GRPO attempts and demonstrated improved out-of-domain generalization on scientific and code reasoning tasks.
- SofT-GRPO reduced thinking length for correct queries, leading to improved token efficiency during training, and further boosted performance when combined with majority voting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gumbel reparameterization is an effective technique for introducing controllable stochasticity and estimating gradients for continuous, non-discrete representations in LLM reasoning.
> - Successfully applying RL to continuous reasoning processes requires careful consideration to ensure that generated representations remain within the meaningful pre-trained embedding space of the LLM.
> - When properly reinforced, continuous "soft-thinking" can surpass the performance of discrete-token Chain-of-Thought, especially for tasks benefiting from deeper exploration and diverse reasoning paths.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on SofT-GRPO is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving areas in large language model (LLM) research: LLM reasoning paradigms, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), and methods for enhancing model expressiveness.

Traditionally, LLMs rely on **discrete-token Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning**, where the model generates a sequence of explicit, human-readable tokens to arrive at an answer. This method has proven effective but is inherently limited by the discrete nature of tokens, which may not always capture abstract or nuanced concepts.

In response to this limitation, the **soft-thinking reasoning paradigm** emerged, which replaces discrete tokens with continuous representations—specifically, weighted sums of token embeddings based on output probabilities. This approach allows LLMs to operate in a "continuous concept space," enabling richer representation and implicit exploration of multiple reasoning paths. Previous work has shown that soft-thinking can outperform discrete-token CoT in various tasks without fine-tuning, demonstrating its potential for superior expressiveness.

Parallel to these reasoning advancements, **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)** techniques have become standard for fine-tuning LLMs to improve their reasoning capabilities. Methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) leverage verifiable rewards (e.g., correctness of the final answer) to reinforce successful reasoning trajectories. Notably, discrete-token CoT reasoning, when enhanced with RLVR, often surpasses the performance of un-fine-tuned soft-thinking models.

However, a significant challenge has been the effective integration of RLVR with soft-thinking. The deterministic nature of vanilla soft-thinking, which lacks inherent stochasticity and clear gradients for policy updates, makes it difficult to apply standard RLVR algorithms. Previous attempts to combine soft-thinking with RLVR, such as injecting Gaussian noise directly into soft tokens, have struggled to match the performance of discrete-token RLVR. These methods faced issues with accurately attributing rewards to the underlying LLM logits due to the non-invertibility of embedding transformations and the potential for generating "invalid" soft tokens outside the model's pre-trained embedding space.

SofT-GRPO directly addresses this gap. It aims to develop a powerful RLVR algorithm specifically tailored for soft-thinking that can overcome these challenges, thereby unlocking the full reasoning potential of LLMs operating in a continuous concept space and surpassing the performance of state-of-the-art discrete-token RLVR methods. This work advances the field by demonstrating that soft-thinking, when properly reinforced, offers a tangible advantage over discrete-token approaches, especially for complex reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind SofT-GRPO stems from the observed performance gap between reinforced discrete-token Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and existing attempts to reinforce soft-thinking. While soft-thinking has shown intrinsic advantages over discrete-token CoT without fine-tuning, the application of powerful Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) techniques like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to discrete-token CoT often results in superior performance that soft-thinking cannot match. This indicates that the full potential of soft-thinking remains untapped when it comes to fine-tuning with RL.

The paper identifies two core challenges in extending RLVR to soft-thinking reasoning:
1. **Introducing controllable stochasticity**: Unlike discrete-token CoT, where sampling naturally introduces randomness for exploration, vanilla soft-thinking generates deterministic soft tokens. Effective RL requires diverse exploration of reasoning paths.
2. **Enabling unbiased policy updates with accurate credit assignment**: It is challenging to properly assign credit from rewards to the LLM's output logits when dealing with continuous soft tokens, especially given the complexities of embedding spaces and the non-injectivity of token embedding transformations. Previous methods that inject noise into the soft tokens directly struggle with this, leading to suboptimal performance.

Therefore, the key objective of this research is to develop a novel and robust policy optimization algorithm, SofT-GRPO, that can effectively apply RLVR to the soft-thinking paradigm. Specifically, SofT-GRPO aims to:
- Inject controlled stochasticity into the soft-thinking rollout process to enable exploration of diverse soft-thinking paths.
- Develop a mechanism for unbiased policy updates that accurately attributes the advantage (reward credit) of high-performing soft-thinking samples back to the LLM's output probability distributions (logits).
- Ultimately, enable soft-thinking LLMs to surpass the reasoning performance of discrete-token LLMs fine-tuned with advanced RLVR techniques like GRPO, particularly on metrics like Pass@1 and Pass@K.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SofT-GRPO introduces a novel policy optimization algorithm designed to address the challenges of reinforcing soft-thinking reasoning in LLMs. Its methodology is built upon two core innovations: group rollout with Gumbel noise and Gumbel reparameterization for accurate credit assignment in the loss function.

#### 3.1. Group Rollout with Gumbel Noise

To introduce the necessary stochasticity for exploration in the soft-thinking reasoning process, SofT-GRPO adopts the **Gumbel-Softmax resampling technique**. Instead of generating a single deterministic soft token at each step, it samples a group of *G* soft-thinking Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paths for each query. This is achieved by:
- First, the LLM outputs a probability distribution `p` over its vocabulary for the next token, conditioned on the current state.
- Then, Gumbel noise `ϵ` (sampled from a Gumbel(0,1) distribution) is added to the logarithm of these probabilities: `g_i = log p_i + ϵ_i`.
- These perturbed logits `g_i` are then passed through the Gumbel-Softmax function with a temperature `τ_g` to produce a new, stochastic probability distribution `y_i`.
- Finally, the soft token `s_t` is constructed as a weighted sum of token embeddings, where the weights are the `y_i` probabilities.

This approach offers **controllable stochasticity**, allowing the model to explore diverse soft-thinking paths while maintaining **validity**. The Gumbel-Softmax technique ensures that the generated `y_i` distribution, and consequently the soft token `s_t`, remains within the convex hull of the pre-trained discrete-token embedding space. This is crucial because LLMs are typically trained on discrete tokens, and injecting noise in a way that leads to "out-of-vocabulary" or incomprehensible soft tokens can degrade performance. The paper highlights a theoretical advantage of the Gumbel-max trick in maintaining samples close to the discrete token distribution, mitigating risks associated with other noise injection methods (e.g., Gaussian noise on soft tokens, which can easily move outside the valid embedding space).

#### 3.2. Gumbel Reparameterization for Loss Function

The second, and perhaps most critical, innovation lies in how SofT-GRPO handles policy updates and credit assignment. Standard RLVR algorithms for discrete tokens can directly compute the probability of a generated token. However, in soft-thinking, where continuous soft tokens are generated via a stochastic process involving Gumbel noise, directly calculating the "probability" of a soft token for policy optimization is complex.

SofT-GRPO addresses this by employing the **Gumbel reparameterization trick**. Instead of trying to define a likelihood over the soft token `s_t`, it treats the *Gumbel noise `ϵ_t` itself as the action* taken by the RL agent. The generation of `s_t` from `ϵ_t` and the LLM's probabilities `p_t` is considered part of the environment transition. This allows for:
- **Accurate Gradient Estimation**: By viewing `ϵ_t` as the action, the density of the sampled Gumbel noise can be used to estimate the log-likelihood of a soft-thinking trajectory.
- **Off-Policy Probability Calculation**: For the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)-like clipping mechanism used in GRPO, SofT-GRPO reconstructs the soft-thinking paths and calculates the probability ratio between the current policy `π_θ` and the sampling policy `π_θ_old`. This is achieved by re-evaluating the Gumbel noise values (`ϵ_t`) based on the current policy's logits, rather than the original logits from the sampling policy. The log-likelihood under the current policy is derived by re-expressing the Gumbel noise as `(g'_t - log p_t)`, where `g'_t` are the perturbed logits collected during rollout and `p_t` are the current policy's logits.

This novel gradient estimation method allows SofT-GRPO to accurately assign reward credits to the LLM's output probability distributions (logits), even when dealing with continuous soft tokens. Unlike discrete-token GRPO, which updates the probability of only one token per step, SofT-GRPO updates the probabilities of *several* tokens at each LLM step, potentially leading to a more nuanced and effective policy improvement, especially for multi-path reasoning. The loss function is formulated to optimize the reasoning policy towards soft-thinking CoTs with higher rewards, incorporating the calculated probability ratios and a KL-divergence regularization term to maintain policy stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SofT-GRPO demonstrates its superiority across various LLM sizes and reasoning benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance on Numerical Reasoning Benchmarks

- **Outperformance on Pass@1**: SofT-GRPO consistently enables soft-thinking LLMs (1.5B to 7B parameters) to slightly outperform discrete-token CoT with GRPO on the averaged Pass@1 accuracy (averaging 32 runs), with an average uplift of +0.13%. This is a notable achievement, as previous soft-thinking RLVR attempts typically underperformed discrete-token GRPO.
- **Substantial Gains on Pass@K**: The most significant improvements are observed at higher sample rates (Pass@16 and Pass@32). SofT-GRPO brings a substantial uplift of +1.80% on average for Pass@16 and +2.19% on average for Pass@32. This indicates that SofT-GRPO effectively encourages the exploration of multiple, diverse, and high-quality reasoning paths, which are crucial for solving complex problems requiring several attempts. This also contrasts with discrete-token GRPO, which can sometimes lead to decreased Pass@K performance over un-fine-tuned models, despite improving Pass@1. Further analysis with Pass@K up to K=1024 on AIME2024 and AMC23 confirmed that SofT-GRPO consistently outperforms GRPO and No-Finetune counterparts across all K values.

#### 4.2. Strong Out-of-Domain Generalization

- Beyond numerical reasoning, LLMs fine-tuned with SofT-GRPO on numerical questions demonstrate advantages on out-of-domain tasks. Positive performance gains are observed from Pass@1 to Pass@32 on scientific reasoning (GPQA Diamond) and code generation benchmarks (HumanEval and MBPP). This suggests that the improved reasoning capabilities are not task-specific but reflect a more general enhancement of the LLM's underlying reasoning ability.

#### 4.3. Synergy with Majority Voting

- When combined with majority voting (selecting the most common answer from multiple runs), SofT-GRPO-fine-tuned LLMs further improve their performance, outperforming No-Finetune LLMs and discrete-token GRPO across Major@16 and Major@32 metrics. This reinforces the finding that SofT-GRPO generates a diverse set of high-quality reasoning paths that can be effectively aggregated for more robust answers.

#### 4.4. Comparison to Previous Soft-Thinking RLVR

- SofT-GRPO shows a clear improvement compared to the existing soft-thinking RLVR method proposed by Butt et al. (2025) (referred to as "Soft Tokens"). On MATH-500 and GSM8K, SofT-GRPO provides notable uplifts across Pass@1 and Pass@32 (e.g., +5.9% and +5.5% on MATH-500 Pass@1 and Pass@32 respectively, for LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct). This highlights the effectiveness of Gumbel noise injection and reparameterization over Gaussian noise directly on soft tokens.

#### 4.5. Improved Token Efficiency

- SofT-GRPO leads to a reduction in reasoning length (fewer tokens generated) compared to No-Finetune models, and does not significantly increase token usage compared to discrete-token GRPO. For the LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct model, SofT-GRPO significantly reduces thinking length, leading to computational cost savings.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies and Hyperparameter Analysis

- Ablation studies confirm that Gumbel noise is superior to Dirichlet noise or Gaussian noise for introducing stochasticity, demonstrating better training rewards and validation accuracies.
- Hyperparameter analysis reveals the sensitivity of SofT-GRPO to `top-p` and Gumbel-Softmax temperature `τ_g`. Higher values can cause "collapse" during training, attributed to the generation of soft-token inputs outside the pre-trained embedding space, as indicated by increased KL divergence from the reference model.

#### 4.7. Visualization Insights

- Visualizations of soft-thinking paths after SofT-GRPO fine-tuning show that high-probability paths maintain interpretability. Interestingly, new distributions emerge with high mass on conceptually opposite tokens (e.g., "Yeah" and "No"), suggesting the model's ability to represent abstract concepts encompassing multiple reasoning branches, a pattern not observed in un-fine-tuned LLMs.
- The entropy of token probability distributions is maintained or even improved with SofT-GRPO, unlike discrete-token GRPO where it tends to converge near zero. This explains SofT-GRPO's superior Pass@K performance by encouraging output diversity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SofT-GRPO marks a significant advancement in the field of large language model (LLM) reasoning, particularly in bridging the gap between expressive continuous reasoning paradigms and powerful reinforcement learning techniques. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

#### 5.1. Technical Breakthrough in Reinforcing Soft-Thinking

SofT-GRPO provides the first robust and highly effective method for applying Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to the soft-thinking paradigm. By ingeniously leveraging Gumbel noise for controlled stochasticity during rollout and Gumbel reparameterization for accurate credit assignment, the algorithm overcomes the long-standing challenges of gradient estimation and exploration in continuous token spaces. This is a crucial technical contribution that unlocks the potential of soft-thinking, which had previously been overshadowed by reinforced discrete-token methods.

#### 5.2. Superior Reasoning Performance

The empirical results unequivocally demonstrate that SofT-GRPO-enhanced soft-thinking surpasses discrete-token GRPO, not just on Pass@1 but, more critically, on Pass@K metrics (Pass@16, Pass@32, and even up to Pass@1024). This indicates a fundamental improvement in the LLM's ability to explore and converge on correct reasoning paths, especially for problems requiring multiple attempts or a broader exploration of solution strategies. This superior performance suggests that soft-thinking, when properly reinforced, can truly elevate LLMs beyond the capabilities of discrete-token reasoning.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Generalization and Robustness

The observed improvements extend beyond in-domain numerical reasoning tasks to out-of-domain benchmarks in scientific reasoning and code generation. This strong generalization ability underscores that SofT-GRPO is not merely overfitting to specific tasks but is fostering a more robust and adaptable reasoning capacity within the LLM. Furthermore, its synergy with majority voting techniques highlights the generation of diverse and qualitatively strong reasoning trajectories, which can be combined for even higher accuracy.

#### 5.4. Improved Efficiency and Interpretability

By reducing reasoning length (token count) in several cases, SofT-GRPO offers practical benefits in terms of computational cost and inference speed. While soft-thinking itself is more complex, the gains in efficiency from shorter reasoning chains are valuable. The visualization of soft-thinking paths, demonstrating both interpretability and the emergence of nuanced conceptual representations (e.g., blending antonyms), provides valuable insights into how LLMs might be learning to reason in a more abstract and flexible manner.

#### 5.5. Broader Impact on LLM Development and Research

- **New Directions for Model Architecture**: This work may inspire new LLM architectures or training methodologies that inherently support or optimize for continuous concept spaces, moving beyond the limitations of discrete token generation.
- **Advancing Reasoning Capabilities**: For applications requiring highly reliable and complex reasoning (e.g., scientific discovery, advanced problem-solving, complex code generation), SofT-GRPO offers a powerful tool to push the boundaries of LLM performance.
- **Rethinking RL for LLMs**: The success of Gumbel reparameterization in this context could lead to new explorations of differentiable sampling and credit assignment mechanisms in other challenging RL-for-LLM scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future Work**: The paper explicitly points to extending this method to other model types, such as Vision Language Models (VLMs), suggesting a wide range of future applications where continuous representations and robust reinforcement learning could yield significant benefits.

In conclusion, SofT-GRPO represents a pivotal step towards unlocking the full reasoning potential of LLMs. By providing a principled and effective way to reinforce soft-thinking, it not only pushes the state-of-the-art in various reasoning tasks but also opens new avenues for developing more intelligent, adaptable, and efficient language models.
