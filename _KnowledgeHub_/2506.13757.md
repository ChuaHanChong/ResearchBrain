# AutoVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Model for End-to-End Autonomous Driving with Adaptive Reasoning and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UCLA developed AutoVLA, a Vision-Language-Action model for end-to-end autonomous driving that unifies semantic reasoning and direct physical trajectory generation. The model employs an adaptive dual-thinking mode to switch between fast and slow reasoning, and achieves a 10.6% improvement in Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) while reducing runtime by 66.8% through reinforcement fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Traditional modular autonomous driving systems suffer from error accumulation and suboptimal holistic optimization.
- Early end-to-end methods based on imitation learning lack world knowledge and robust reasoning for complex or long-tail scenarios.
- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models generate abstract actions requiring downstream planners or use inflexible reasoning strategies, leading to inefficiencies or inadequate responses.

## Method

- **AutoVLA** integrates a VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) with novel ==physical action tokenization== to directly generate physically feasible driving trajectories.
- The model employs an adaptive dual-thinking mode (fast for simple scenarios, slow with Chain-of-Thought for complex ones) within a unified autoregressive framework.
- A two-stage training process uses supervised fine-tuning with automatically generated reasoning data from a larger VLM, followed by reinforcement fine-tuning (GRPO) with a reward function that penalizes unnecessary CoT.

## Results

- Reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) on AutoVLA improved PDMS by **10.6%** and reduced inference runtime by **66.8%** on the nuPlan NAVSIM test set.
- AutoVLA achieved competitive performance on nuPlan and Waymo benchmarks, notably ranking highest in the RFS Spotlight metric for Waymo's challenging scenarios.
- In CARLA closed-loop simulations, AutoVLA outperformed existing end-to-end models with a driving score of 78.84 and a **57.73%** success rate on Bench2Drive.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unified autoregressive generation of both reasoning tokens and physical action tokens enables truly end-to-end planning with adaptive capabilities.
> - Discretizing continuous trajectories into specific physical action tokens effectively allows language models to output directly executable, physically compliant maneuvers.
> - Reinforcement learning, with a CoT penalty, can successfully train a VLA model to adaptively select between fast and slow thinking, balancing performance and computational efficiency.
