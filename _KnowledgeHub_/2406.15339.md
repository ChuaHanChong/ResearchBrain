# Image Conductor: Precision Control for Interactive Video Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Image Conductor introduces a method for interactive video synthesis that enables precise control over camera transitions and object movements from a single input image. The approach employs a training strategy to decouple motion types and integrates a novel inference-time guidance technique. Quantitative evaluations demonstrate lower CamMC (33.49) and ObjMC (42.38) scores, signifying superior adherence to trajectories, and improved generation quality with lower FID (209.74) and FVD (1116.17) compared to previous methods.

## Problem

- Traditional filmmaking and animation production are labor-intensive for coordinating complex camera transitions and object movements.
- Current generative AI methods for video creation lack precise and fine-grained control over both camera and object motions simultaneously.
- The mixing of camera and object movements in readily available internet video data complicates training models to differentiate and control these motion types independently, leading to imprecision.
- A scarcity of large-scale, high-quality video datasets with precise trajectory annotations hinders the development of advanced motion control in video generation.

## Method

- **Image Conductor**, an image-to-video architecture built on Animatediff and SparseCtrl, was developed to animate static images by directing camera and object movements with user-provided trajectories.
- A high-quality, trajectory-oriented video motion dataset was curated, comprising 130,000 mixed videos and 62,000 camera-only videos, using a pipeline that includes dense point tracking with CoTracker.
- Controllable motion separation is achieved through distinct camera ==LoRA== and object LoRA weights within a motion ==ControlNet==, optimized via a collaborative, incremental strategy with an orthogonal loss.
- A camera-free guidance technique, inspired by ==classifier-free guidance==, was introduced during diffusion sampling to enhance desired object movements and mitigate unintended camera transitions.

## Results

- Qualitative comparisons demonstrated Image Conductor's superiority over state-of-the-art methods, generating more accurate and natural complex camera and object movements.
- The method achieved lower CamMC (**33.49**) and ObjMC (**42.38**) scores, indicating superior trajectory alignment, and better generation quality (FID **209.74**, FVD **1116.17**) compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that distinct LoRA weights successfully separated camera and object motion control, and camera-free guidance effectively mitigated unintended camera transitions.
- Image Conductor is compatible with personalized models from open-source communities, enabling controllable video generation while preserving personalized visual styles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Precise decoupling of camera and object motion control within diffusion models is feasible by utilizing distinct LoRA weights and a collaborative, incremental training strategy.
> - An orthogonal loss effectively encourages specialized learning for camera and object movements during LoRA training, accelerating convergence and improving disentanglement.
> - Inference-time guidance can selectively enhance specific motion types, like object movement, while suppressing undesired side effects such as unintended camera transitions.
> - The creation of a high-quality, trajectory-annotated video dataset is fundamental for training models capable of fine-grained motion control, addressing a critical data gap.
