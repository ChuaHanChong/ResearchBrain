# Image Conductor: Precision Control for Interactive Video Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Image Conductor introduces a method for interactive video synthesis that enables precise control over camera transitions and object movements from a single input image. The approach employs a training strategy to decouple motion types and integrates a novel inference-time guidance technique. Quantitative evaluations demonstrate lower CamMC (33.49) and ObjMC (42.38) scores, signifying superior adherence to trajectories, and improved generation quality with lower FID (209.74) and FVD (1116.17) compared to previous methods.

## Problem

- Traditional filmmaking and animation production are labor-intensive for coordinating complex camera transitions and object movements.
- Current generative AI methods for video creation lack precise and fine-grained control over both camera and object motions simultaneously.
- The mixing of camera and object movements in readily available internet video data complicates training models to differentiate and control these motion types independently, leading to imprecision.
- A scarcity of large-scale, high-quality video datasets with precise trajectory annotations hinders the development of advanced motion control in video generation.

## Method

- **Image Conductor**, an image-to-video architecture built on Animatediff and SparseCtrl, was developed to animate static images by directing camera and object movements with user-provided trajectories.
- A high-quality, trajectory-oriented video motion dataset was curated, comprising 130,000 mixed videos and 62,000 camera-only videos, using a pipeline that includes dense point tracking with CoTracker.
- Controllable motion separation is achieved through distinct camera ==LoRA== and object LoRA weights within a motion ==ControlNet==, optimized via a collaborative, incremental strategy with an orthogonal loss.
- A camera-free guidance technique, inspired by ==classifier-free guidance==, was introduced during diffusion sampling to enhance desired object movements and mitigate unintended camera transitions.

## Results

- Qualitative comparisons demonstrated Image Conductor's superiority over state-of-the-art methods, generating more accurate and natural complex camera and object movements.
- The method achieved lower CamMC (**33.49**) and ObjMC (**42.38**) scores, indicating superior trajectory alignment, and better generation quality (FID **209.74**, FVD **1116.17**) compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that distinct LoRA weights successfully separated camera and object motion control, and camera-free guidance effectively mitigated unintended camera transitions.
- Image Conductor is compatible with personalized models from open-source communities, enabling controllable video generation while preserving personalized visual styles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Precise decoupling of camera and object motion control within diffusion models is feasible by utilizing distinct LoRA weights and a collaborative, incremental training strategy.
> - An orthogonal loss effectively encourages specialized learning for camera and object movements during LoRA training, accelerating convergence and improving disentanglement.
> - Inference-time guidance can selectively enhance specific motion types, like object movement, while suppressing undesired side effects such as unintended camera transitions.
> - The creation of a high-quality, trajectory-annotated video dataset is fundamental for training models capable of fine-grained motion control, addressing a critical data gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Filmmaking and animation production conventionally rely on labor-intensive techniques such as real-world capturing and 3D modeling to achieve sophisticated coordination of camera transitions and object movements. The emergence of generative AI, particularly diffusion models, has initiated new pipelines for creating video assets. However, a significant limitation within these advancements is the inability to provide creators with precise and fine-grained control over motion, encompassing both camera transitions and object movements, which is crucial for expressing specific creative ideas.

Existing generative AI methods for video creation frequently lack efficient control interfaces and struggle to disentangle and accurately manipulate distinct types of motion. Previous attempts to introduce motion control signals (e.g., DragNUWA, DragAnything, MotionCtrl) often fail to offer fine-grained and accurate control over both camera and object motions simultaneously. A fundamental challenge contributing to this limitation is the nature of readily available internet data, which often mixes camera transitions and object movements. This blending creates ambiguities that complicate the training of models to differentiate and control these motion types independently. While some methods have explored data-driven decoupling, they often suffer from imprecision due to reliance on error-prone optical flow estimation or the difficulty in acquiring ground truth videos with controlled motion (e.g., fixed-camera viewpoints for pure object movement).

This work positions itself to address these identified gaps by proposing "Image Conductor." It aims to advance the field by providing a method for precise and fine-grained control over both camera transitions and object movements from a single input image. This is achieved by developing a specific training strategy that decouples different motion types through distinct model components and by introducing a novel inference-time guidance technique. Furthermore, the researchers contribute to the broader landscape by curating a high-quality, trajectory-oriented video motion dataset, directly tackling the scarcity of such annotated data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the interactive generation of video assets with precise control over both camera transitions and object movements, starting from a single image. This overarching goal is driven by several specific objectives and motivations:

- **Objective 1: Achieve precise and fine-grained control over camera transitions and object movements.** The current state of generative AI for video creation, despite its advancements, lacks the necessary precision for creative expression in filmmaking and animation. Professionals require granular control over visual elements and their motion to maintain narrative coherence and achieve specific visual effects.
- **Objective 2: Develop a mechanism to effectively separate distinct camera and object motions.** Real-world video data often contains a mixture of camera and object movements, making it challenging for generative models to learn to control them independently. This coupling leads to ambiguities and makes it difficult to generate videos where only the camera moves, only objects move, or both move in specific, directed ways. The researchers aim to overcome this by designing a system that can disentangle these motion types.
- **Objective 3: Address cinematographic variations arising from ill-posed trajectories.** Even with advancements in motion control, certain complex or ambiguous user-defined trajectories can lead to unintended camera movements or cinematic distortions in generated videos. The motivation here is to provide a flexible mechanism to refine motion output, particularly to enhance object movements while mitigating unwanted camera transitions.
- **Objective 4: Construct a high-quality, trajectory-oriented video motion dataset.** A significant impediment to developing advanced motion control in video generation is the lack of large-scale, high-quality, publicly available video datasets with precise trajectory annotations. Existing datasets either lack such annotations or are prone to inaccuracies from automated estimation methods. The motivation is to fill this data gap to enable robust model training.

The overarching motivation stems from the labor-intensive and costly nature of traditional video production workflows, which heavily rely on real-world capturing and 3D modeling for motion orchestration. By introducing Image Conductor, the researchers aim to offer an AI-driven alternative that empowers creators with tools for precise creative expression, potentially streamlining the production process for video media.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Image Conductor is designed to animate a static image by directing camera transitions and object movements based on user-provided trajectories. The methodology encompasses four main components: trajectory-oriented video data construction, a motion-aware image-to-video architecture, controllable motion separation, and camera-free guidance.

**Trajectory-Oriented Video Motion Data Construction:**
Recognizing the absence of large-scale, high-quality video datasets with trajectory annotations, the researchers developed a comprehensive data curation pipeline:
1. **Video Collection:** The pipeline begins by collecting videos from the WebVid dataset, which is a large-scale mixed dataset with textual descriptions, and the Realestate10K dataset, specifically chosen for its focus on camera-only movements. Video captions for Realestate10K are generated using BLIP2.
2. **Cuts Detection and Selection:** To ensure temporal consistency and prevent the model from overfitting to abrupt scene changes, a cut detection tool is employed to identify and filter out transitions between different shots. The longest consistent scenes are selected as video clips.
3. **Motion Estimation and Filtering:** RAFT is utilized to compute optical flow between adjacent frames, from which a Frobenius norm-based motion score is derived. Videos in the lowest 25% percentile of motion scores are filtered out to ensure a dataset with sufficient dynamic content. Videos are resized to have a shorter side of 256 pixels, and 32-frame sequences are randomly sampled for training.
4. **Cropping and Tracking:** Video frames are center-cropped to a standard resolution of 384x256x32. CoTracker (Karaev et al., 2023), a dense point tracking method, is then applied to record motion using a 16x16 grid, generating accurate accumulated point trajectories in the form of stacked flow maps, which are compatible with ControlNet. CoTracker is preferred over optical flow methods to mitigate drift-induced error accumulation.
5. **Sampling and Gaussian Filter:** To facilitate user interaction, sparse trajectories (1 to 8 trajectories per video) are sampled from the dense CoTracker output, with sampling probability based on normalized motion intensity. A Gaussian filter is subsequently applied to these sparse flow maps to enhance training stability.
This pipeline generated a dataset comprising 130,000 mixed videos (with both camera and object motion) and 62,000 videos containing only camera transitions.

**Motion-Aware Image-to-Video Architecture:**
The core architecture is built upon Animatediff (Guo et al., 2023b) combined with SparseCtrl (Guo et al., 2023a) for image-to-video generation.
- **Image-to-Video Backbone:** CLIP (Radford etal., 2021) text encoder extracts text embeddings for the UNet backbone via cross-attention. The input image acts as the first frame, conditioned by concatenating it with an all-zero frame matrix and a mask identifier. Video SparseCtrl (a ControlNet variant without skip-connections to the UNet encoder's input latents) is used to extract image information.
- **Motion-Controllable ControlNet:** An additional ControlNet acts as a motion encoder, processing the trajectory input (`c_trajs`) to capture multi-level motion representations. This ControlNet integrates distinct Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) weights to guide the diffusion model, differentiating between camera transitions and object movements. The removal of skip connections, consistent with SparseCtrl, is adopted to accelerate convergence.

**Controllable Motion Separation:**
To precisely decouple camera transitions and object movements, Image Conductor employs distinct camera LoRA (`θ_cam`) and object LoRA (`θ_obj`) within the motion ControlNet, optimized through a collaborative, incremental strategy:
1. **Camera Transitions Training:** The `camera LoRA` (`θ_cam = θ_0 + Δθ_cam`) is initially optimized using the curated camera-only motion dataset. This process utilizes the standard diffusion denoising objective (`L_cam`), empowering the ControlNet to direct cinematographic variations.
2. **Object Movements Training:** After the `camera LoRA` is well-trained and loaded, the `object LoRA` (`θ_obj = θ_0 + Δθ_obj`) is optimized using the mixed motion dataset. During this phase, the gradients to the `camera LoRA` are halted to ensure `object LoRA` learns object-specific motion without interfering with pre-learned camera controls. This also uses the standard diffusion denoising objective (`L_mixed`).
3. **Orthogonal Loss:** An orthogonal loss (`L_ortho`) is introduced as a joint optimization objective during `object LoRA` training. It imposes an orthogonality constraint (`||I - W_i,cam * W_i,traj^T||^2_2`) on the linear layer weights of the `camera LoRA` (`W_i,cam`) and `object LoRA` (`W_i,traj`). This encourages `object LoRA` to learn concepts distinct from `camera LoRA` and helps accelerate convergence.

**Camera-Free Guidance:**
To address instances where multiple object trajectories inadvertently introduce unwanted camera transitions, a camera-free guidance technique is proposed, inspired by classifier-free guidance. This technique iteratively performs an extrapolation fusion between different latents during the diffusion sampling process. It combines outputs from the model without any guidance (`ϵ_θ_0(x_t, ∅)`), the model with general conditioning (`ϵ_θ_0(x_t, c)`), and the model specifically trained with `object LoRA` (`ϵ_θ_trajs(x_t, c)`). By adjusting the `λ_trajs` parameter, this guidance flexibly enhances object movements while mitigating or eliminating unintended camera transitions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both qualitative and quantitative evidence to support the effectiveness of Image Conductor in achieving precise and fine-grained motion control for interactive video synthesis.

**Qualitative Evaluation:**
Comparative qualitative results (Figure 4) demonstrate Image Conductor's superiority over state-of-the-art methods like DragNUWA, DragAnything, and MotionCtrl.
- **Camera Transitions:** Image Conductor successfully utilized text, image, and trajectory prompts to generate natural content with accurate camera transitions, including complex movements such as panning up then down. In contrast, DragNUWA and DragAnything exhibited limitations in executing these complex transitions. MotionCtrl, while capable of certain camera movements, struggled to integrate natural content changes from text prompts and sometimes introduced distortion artifacts, lacking precise intensity control.
- **Object Movements:** Image Conductor demonstrated accurate and fine-grained control over object movements. Other methods, such as DragNUWA and DragAnything, frequently misinterpreted object movements as camera transitions, resulting in videos that did not align with user intentions. Their generated motion trajectories often showed poor correspondence to the desired paths due to errors from their labeled datasets. MotionCtrl, being text-to-video-based, lacked fine-grained content control due to its inability to use images as conditions and frequently produced camera transitions instead of pure object movement.

**Quantitative Evaluation:**
Automatic metrics (Table 1) consistently placed Image Conductor as a leading method.
- **Motion Control Accuracy:** Image Conductor achieved lower CamMC (33.49) and ObjMC (42.38) scores compared to DragNUWA (CamMC 48.72, ObjMC 51.24) and DragAnything (CamMC 66.54, ObjMC 60.97). Lower scores indicate better alignment with the given trajectories, confirming the method's precise motion control capabilities for both camera and object movements.
- **Generation Quality:** The method also demonstrated superior generation quality and temporal coherence with lower FID (209.74) and FVD (1116.17) scores, compared to baselines (e.g., DragNUWA FID 237.26, FVD 1283.85). This indicates the production of more realistic videos.
- **Human Evaluation:** A user study involving 31 participants confirmed these findings. Image Conductor received higher preference rates across overall performance (61.7%), sample quality (54.8%), and motion similarity (66.0%) when compared against DragNUWA and DragAnything. This suggests that the generated videos were perceived as more realistic, visually appealing, and exhibited better adherence to specified motions.

**Personalized and Controllable Video Synthesis:**
Figure 5 illustrates the method's compatibility with personalized models from open-source communities (e.g., CIVITAI). By loading personalized LoRA weights (e.g., from TuSun, HelloObject, CardosAnime checkpoints), Image Conductor demonstrated the ability to generate controllable video content assets while maintaining personalized visual styles, indicating its integration potential within existing generative AI ecosystems.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effect of Distinct LoRA Weights (Figure 6):** This study validated the effectiveness of the collaborative optimization strategy in separating motion types. When camera LoRA was loaded, a vertical upward trajectory resulted in the video panning up. Conversely, loading object LoRA with the same trajectory caused objects (e.g., waves) to move upward, demonstrating independent control over camera and object motion.
- **Effect of Camera-free Guidance (Figure 7):** The impact of camera-free guidance was shown to enhance object movements and mitigate unintended camera transitions. With `λ_trajs` set to 1 (no camera-free guidance), the generated video exhibited an unexpected pan-left transformation. Increasing `λ_trajs` to 1.1 resulted in reasonable object movements, albeit with some remaining artifacts. Further increases in `λ_trajs` led to more apparent and clear object movements, effectively eliminating the camera transition.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Image Conductor" introduces a method that addresses key limitations in interactive video synthesis, thereby holding several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Advancement in Generative AI for Video:** The development of Image Conductor marks a step towards more practical and controllable video generation using AI. By enabling precise and fine-grained control over both camera transitions and object movements from a single image, it surpasses the capabilities of many existing methods that often struggle with disentangled motion control.
- **Enhanced Creative Expression in Visual Arts:** For filmmakers, animators, and other visual content creators, this method offers a tool that can significantly streamline and empower their creative workflows. The ability to meticulously plan and execute complex motion sequences – such as specific camera pans, zooms, or object trajectories – without the extensive labor associated with traditional real-world capturing or 3D modeling, could revolutionize pre-visualization and content generation.
- **Improved User Interaction and Accessibility:** The adoption of trajectory-based inputs provides an intuitive and user-friendly interface for motion control. This accessibility could lower the barrier for creators who may not possess specialized skills in complex 3D animation software or cinematography.
- **Contribution to Research Data:** The construction of a high-quality, trajectory-oriented video motion dataset is a valuable contribution to the open-source community. The lack of such annotated data has been a bottleneck for advancements in this field, and this new resource can facilitate future research and development in motion-controllable video generation.
- **Novel Technical Solutions:** The proposed collaborative optimization strategy for distinct LoRA weights and the camera-free guidance technique represent novel technical approaches to address the challenges of motion decoupling and refinement in diffusion models. These methods could inspire further research in parameter-efficient fine-tuning and inference-time guidance for generative tasks.
- **Broader Applicability and Integration:** The demonstrated compatibility of Image Conductor with personalized text-to-image models from open-source customization communities (e.g., CIVITAI) suggests its potential for integration into a wider ecosystem of generative AI tools. This allows users to animate their personalized artistic styles with precise motion control, expanding its utility across various content creation domains.
- **Potential for Interactive Media:** Beyond traditional film and animation, the precise control offered by Image Conductor could find applications in interactive media, virtual reality, and gaming, where dynamic and user-controlled visual narratives are increasingly sought after.

In summary, Image Conductor contributes to making AI-driven video synthesis a more robust, controllable, and creatively empowering tool, moving it closer to practical application in diverse creative industries.
