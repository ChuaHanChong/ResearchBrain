# Failure Identification in Imitation Learning Via Statistical and Semantic Filtering

> [!summary] Summary
> The FIDeL framework introduces a policy-independent monitoring module for robotic imitation learning, leveraging statistical anomaly detection combined with semantic filtering. It successfully differentiates between benign deviations and genuine task failures, enhancing system robustness for real-world deployment and achieving superior anomaly detection performance on new benchmarks.

## Problem

- Imitation learning policies are brittle to out-of-distribution (OOD) states in real-world environments, leading to unpredictable failures.
- Existing anomaly detection methods in robotics can flag deviations but cannot semantically distinguish between benign anomalies and task-critical failures.
- There is a scarcity of comprehensive, multimodal datasets specifically designed for benchmarking failure detection in robotic tasks.

## Method

- FIDeL employs a two-stage approach: an offline phase for learning nominal behavior representation using optimal transport and calibrating thresholds with spatially-aware conformal prediction.
- An online phase performs real-time anomaly detection by comparing current observations to memorized nominal behavior, generating localized anomaly heatmaps.
- A Vision-Language Model (VLM) is integrated for semantic filtering, interpreting detected anomalies and heatmaps to classify them as 'False positive' or 'Failure', thereby bridging the anomaly-failure gap.

## Results

- FIDeL's anomaly detection module achieved a **70.50%** AUROC and **62.90%** F1-score on the novel BotFails dataset, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by a margin of **+5.30%** AUROC.
- Spatially-aware conformal prediction consistently improved anomaly detection accuracy and true negative rates compared to purely temporal CP and Gaussian thresholding.
- The integration of semantic filtering significantly improved end-to-end failure detection, preventing a substantial performance drop (e.g., mitigating a **23%** TNR loss on Real-π without filtering) by accurately distinguishing between benign and critical deviations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal Transport alignment provides a robust and compact representation for nominal behavior, effectively capturing deviations from expert demonstrations.
> - Spatially-aware Conformal Prediction (CP-time&space) adaptively sets anomaly thresholds, accounting for both temporal progression and patch-level visual variations, outperforming simpler thresholding methods.
> - Semantic filtering via a VLM is critical for reliable failure identification, substantially reducing false positives by distinguishing benign anomalies from genuine task failures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust and adaptable robotic systems leveraging imitation learning (IL) faces a significant challenge in real-world deployments: the brittleness of learned policies when encountering situations not explicitly covered in their training data. These "out-of-distribution" (OOD) states, which can arise from hardware faults, defective parts, unexpected human interactions, or environmental shifts, are difficult to anticipate exhaustively during training.

Existing strategies to address this issue include:
1. **Assuming sufficiently diverse training data:** This approach simplifies the problem by implicitly assuming no situation will be OOD, which is often unrealistic for rare events.
2. **Quantifying model uncertainty:** Methods like Bayesian models aim to provide uncertainty estimates for decisions. However, uncertainty quantification remains an open research challenge in IL and reinforcement learning (RL) and is not typically integrated into high-performing learning algorithms.
3. **Employing independent monitoring modules:** This involves a separate system that evaluates execution at runtime, providing a more practical direction for deployment. The current work aligns with this approach, framing the problem within the domain of Anomaly Detection (AD).

Vision-based Anomaly Detection (AD) methods have emerged as a relevant solution to detect anomalous states. Many AD techniques operate under a One-Class (OC) paradigm, where a model is trained exclusively on "normal" data, and significant deviations are flagged as anomalies. This is particularly suitable for IL, as expert demonstrations naturally define nominal behavior, allowing for a comprehensive definition of undesirable behavior.

However, a critical limitation in the current AD landscape is its inability to semantically distinguish between different types of anomalies. AD methods can reliably highlight deviations from nominal behavior, but they do not differentiate between benign deviations (e.g., a background object moving) and those that genuinely compromise task execution (i.e., failures). In robotics, this distinction is crucial for preventing unnecessary or costly interventions.

This paper addresses this specific gap by enhancing conventional anomaly detection with a semantic filtering mechanism. It builds upon representation-based AD methods, which are noted for their sample efficiency, interpretability through localized heatmaps, and suitability for robotic applications. Prior work like FAIL-Detect performed runtime failure detection but lacked the ability to distinguish benign anomalies from task-critical failures and assumed temporal consistency. The present work aims to overcome these limitations by introducing a mechanism for semantic interpretation and robustness to variable execution speeds.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, policy-independent monitoring module, named FIDeL (Failure Identification in Demonstration Learning), capable of identifying execution failures in robotic imitation learning policies. This involves not only detecting deviations from nominal behavior but also semantically distinguishing genuine failures from benign anomalies.

The detailed motivations and objectives are as follows:

- **Addressing Brittleness of IL Policies:** Current IL policies are effective in controlled environments but become unreliable in real-world scenarios due to the inevitability of rare, unforeseen events or out-of-distribution states. These events, arising from environmental shifts, hardware malfunctions, or human errors, can lead to task failures. The immediate motivation is to build a mechanism that can interrupt policy execution when such critical situations arise.
- **Leveraging One-Class Anomaly Detection:** Expert demonstrations inherently define what constitutes "normal" and desirable behavior. The research is motivated by the suitability of the One-Class (OC) anomaly detection paradigm, which trains models solely on nominal data, thereby implicitly defining any significant deviation as an anomaly. This avoids the impracticality of collecting exhaustive failure data.
- **Bridging the Anomaly-Failure Gap:** A significant limitation of existing vision-based AD methods is their inability to discern between harmless deviations (e.g., a person walking in the background) and true failures that compromise the task (e.g., dropping an object). The core motivation is to develop a system that semantically interprets detected anomalies, preventing unnecessary interventions or false positives that would reduce productivity.
- **Enhancing Robustness and Adaptability:** Robotic tasks often involve variations in execution speed or slight shifts in object positions. The module needs to be robust to such natural variability while still being sensitive to critical deviations. This motivates the development of adaptive thresholding mechanisms that account for both temporal progression and spatial variations in visual observations.
- **Improving Interpretability:** For reliable deployment and debugging, it is beneficial for a failure detection system to provide human-understandable reasons for its decisions. This motivates the generation of localized anomaly heatmaps and VLM-based textual explanations.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** A practical challenge in robotic failure detection research is the lack of comprehensive datasets featuring diverse failure scenarios. This motivates the creation of a new, multimodal dataset, BotFails, specifically designed for benchmarking anomaly and failure detection in robotics.

In summary, the key objective is to provide a comprehensive, interpretable, and effective framework for runtime failure identification in IL-driven robots, moving beyond simple anomaly detection to semantic understanding of critical events.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FIDeL framework operates in two main stages: an offline phase for model preparation and an online phase for real-time anomaly and failure detection. The approach leverages one-class anomaly detection, optimal transport for feature alignment, an extended conformal prediction for thresholding, and a vision-language model for semantic filtering.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The problem is defined as monitoring a generative robotic policy $f_\theta$ that maps an observation $O_t$ to an action $A_t = f_\theta(O_t)$. The monitoring module aims to identify execution anomalies and, specifically, failures that compromise task completion. This is decomposed into:
- **Anomaly Detection ($D_A$):** Assigns a score to $O_t$ indicating deviation from nominal behavior.
- **Failure Detection ($D_F$):** Filters out benign anomalies, flagging only failure-related ones.
The system is built on a one-class formulation, requiring only nominal expert demonstrations $X_N$. $X_N$ is split into $Y_N$ for detector construction and $Z_N$ for threshold calibration.

**B. Offline Phase**
1. **Memory Representation (Section III-C):**
 - Expert demonstrations from $Y_N$ are encoded using a pre-trained vision encoder (e.g., ResNet18 or DINOv2). This produces patch-level features $\phi(Y_N) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T \times P \times F}$, where $N$ is episodes, $T$ is horizon length, $P$ is patches per frame, and $F$ is feature dimension.
 - To create a compact representation and ensure efficient inference, these features are aggregated across episodes by computing Gaussian statistics (mean $\mu_{t,p,f}$ and standard deviation $\sigma_{t,p,f}$) for each feature dimension $f$, patch $p$, and timestep $t$. This forms the memory module $M$.
2. **Conformal Prediction (CP) Calibration (Section III-E):**
 - The expert calibration set $Z_N$ is split into two subsets, $E_A$ and $E_B$.
 - Anomaly calibration scores are computed using $D_A(\phi(Y_N), \phi(E_A))$ to determine the mean anomaly score $\mu_t^p$ over nominal examples.
 - Deviations are computed using $D_A(\phi(Y_N), \phi(E_B))$.
 - A threshold bandwidth $h$ is set as the $(1-\alpha)$-quantile of the maximum deviations observed on $E_B$.
 - This bandwidth, combined with a patch-wise modulation factor $\rho_t^p$ (capturing variability across time and space), yields a spatio-temporal upper bound $upper_{p,t} = \mu_t^p + \rho_t^p \cdot h$. This bound controls the false positive rate.

**C. Online Phase**
1. **Anomaly Detection Score Computation (Section III-D):**
 - During policy execution, an incoming observation $O_{t^*}$ is encoded by the same vision encoder to obtain query features $\phi_{query_{t^*}} \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times F}$.
 - Anomaly scoring is performed using Optimal Transport (OT) alignment between the query features and the memorized features (keys) from $M$. OT seeks the best global alignment, minimizing the "cost" to redistribute feature mass.
 - A ground cost function $c$ is defined as the normalized Euclidean distance (diagonal Mahalanobis distance), scaled by the standard deviations stored in $M$. This creates a cost matrix $M_c(t)$ for each memory timestep $t$.
 - The OT cost $W_t = \min_{\gamma \in \Pi(a,b)} \langle M_c(t), \gamma \rangle$ is computed using entropic regularization (Sinkhorn iterations), where $\gamma$ is the transport plan between uniform distributions $a,b$.
 - The final anomaly score $D_A(\phi(Y_N), \phi_{t^*})$ is the minimum transport cost across all nominal timesteps: $\min_{t \in [1,T]} W_t$. The timestep $t_{min}$ yielding this minimum cost is also stored.
 - Localized heatmaps ($M_c^{\dagger}{p^*}$) highlighting suspicious regions are derived from the cost matrix $M_c(t_{min})$ by extracting the minimum transport cost per query patch.

2. **Thresholding via Spatially-Aware Conformal Prediction (Section III-E):**
 - At runtime, the current observation's patch-level anomaly scores are compared against the pre-calibrated spatio-temporal upper bounds $upper_{p^*,t_{min}}$.
 - An observation is flagged as anomalous if the fraction of its patches exceeding their respective bounds surpasses a user-defined percentage. This parameter allows tuning detector sensitivity. Non-critical spatial regions can also be masked.

3. **Semantic Filtering (Section III-F):**
 - If an anomaly is flagged by the thresholding module, a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen 2.5-7b, is invoked to perform semantic filtering.
 - The VLM receives a prompt containing:
 - A task-specific textual description ($d$) provided by the user.
 - A reference expert demonstration frame $O(X_{N,t_{min}})$ from the closest nominal timestep $t_{min}$.
 - The current anomalous observation $O_{t^*}$.
 - The anomaly heatmap $M_c^{\dagger}{p^*}$, which localizes the anomalous region(s).
 - The VLM is tasked with:
 - Identifying the semantic nature of the anomaly by comparing the images and interpreting the heatmap.
 - Classifying the anomaly as either "False positive" (benign, robot may continue) or "Failure" (task-critical, requiring intervention).
 - This combination of structured context and localized visual cues helps constrain the VLM's reasoning space to make a more accurate failure judgment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of FIDeL was conducted on two datasets: the newly introduced BotFails dataset and the Real-π soldering dataset. FIDeL was benchmarked against several state-of-the-art anomaly detection and failure detection baselines.

**A. BotFails Dataset Introduction:**
- A novel multimodal dataset (vision, proprioception, natural language) specifically designed for robotic failure detection.
- Collected using master arm teleoperation with the LeRobot platform.
- Comprises 10 distinct tasks: 6 domestic (e.g., clearing dishes, making coffee), and 4 industrial (e.g., sorting screws, soldering).
- Features various anomaly types, including benign deviations and genuine failures, with corresponding anomaly and failure labels at the frame level.

**B. Anomaly Detection (AD) Module Performance (Pre-Thresholding):**
- **Comparison:** FIDeL's representation-based approach was compared against FAIL-Detect's lopO and logpZ0 (Conditional Normalizing Flows), a reconstruction-based AutoEncoder (AE), and STAC (Statistical Temporal Action Consistency, only for Real-π).
- **Metrics:** Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic (AUROC) and F1-score at the optimal threshold (F1@Opt).
- **Results (Table I):**
 - FIDeL consistently achieved superior performance in raw anomaly scoring across both datasets.
 - On the diverse BotFails dataset, FIDeL yielded an AUROC of 70.50% and F1@Opt of 62.90%, outperforming the next best method, logpZ0 (65.20% AUROC, 59.87% F1@Opt), by +5.30% AUROC.
 - On the Real-π soldering task, FIDeL achieved 86.72% AUROC and 78.29% F1@Opt, significantly surpassing logpZ0 (82.62% AUROC, 78.03% F1@Opt) and STAC (53.55% AUROC, 63.93% F1@Opt).
- **Conclusion:** The compact, representation-based approach of FIDeL, utilizing optimal transport, effectively captures deviations from expert behavior and outperforms likelihood- and reconstruction-based methods.

**C. Thresholding Module Performance:**
- **Comparison:** Anomaly scores from various AD methods were subjected to different thresholding strategies: CP-time (temporal Conformal Prediction), CP-time&space (FIDeL's spatially-aware CP), and Gaussian assumption.
- **Metrics:** True Positive Rate (TPR), True Negative Rate (TNR), balanced accuracy (Acc), and weighted accuracy (W.Acc).
- **Results (Figure 5):**
 - The combination of FIDeL's representation-based AD with CP-time&space consistently yielded robust performance.
 - Conformal Prediction (CP) strategies generally outperformed Gaussian thresholding due to their distribution-free nature, which better adapts to varied score distributions. Gaussian fitting sometimes led to over-flagging (low TNR), particularly for logpZ0.
 - CP-time&space consistently improved upon CP-time. This gain was particularly evident on the Real-π dataset, where anomalies are often spatially localized, demonstrating the benefit of considering patch-level variations. On BotFails, while accuracy was higher, weighted accuracy was marginally lower, suggesting a trade-off between TPR and TNR based on task characteristics.
- **Conclusion:** Spatially-aware conformal prediction provides a more adaptive and effective method for setting anomaly thresholds compared to simpler statistical models or purely temporal CP.

**D. End-to-End System Evaluation (Including Semantic Filtering):**
- **Comparison:** FIDeL (Representation + CP-time&space + Semantic filtering) was compared against AD modules without semantic filtering, FAIL-Detect (end-to-end configuration), and Sentinel (STAC + VLM).
- **Labeling:** This evaluation used "failure labels," meaning benign anomalies were re-labeled from 1 (anomaly) to 0 (nominal) to specifically assess failure detection.
- **Results (Figure 6):**
 - Without semantic filtering, AD modules experienced a significant performance drop, especially in TNR, when benign anomalies were re-classified as nominal. For instance, FIDeL's Representation method with CP-time&space lost 23% TNR on Real-π.
 - The integration of semantic filtering in FIDeL substantially mitigated this degradation, leading to more balanced detection performance (e.g., 85.8% TPR / 74.8% TNR on Real-π).
 - FAIL-Detect tended to overpredict anomalies as it could not differentiate between benign and critical deviations. Sentinel, conversely, favored nominal predictions, potentially missing visual failures not captured by proprioceptive signals.
- **Conclusion:** Semantic filtering via a VLM is a critical component for reliable failure monitoring in robotics, enabling the distinction between benign anomalies and genuine failures, thus reducing false positives and improving overall system accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents FIDeL, a framework designed to enhance the reliability and safety of robotic systems operating with imitation learning (IL) policies in real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Bridging a Critical Gap:** FIDeL directly addresses a known limitation in current anomaly detection (AD) methods for robotics—the inability to semantically distinguish between benign deviations and critical failures. By integrating a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for semantic filtering, the framework moves beyond simple anomaly flagging to intelligent failure identification, which is crucial for practical robotic deployment.
- **Enabling Robust and Reliable IL Deployment:** The brittleness of IL policies to out-of-distribution (OOD) states is a major hurdle for real-world applications. FIDeL provides a policy-independent monitoring module that can detect these critical states and trigger appropriate interventions. By reducing false positives associated with benign anomalies, it prevents unnecessary halts or costly resets, thereby maintaining productivity and improving the trust placed in autonomous systems.
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** The system generates localized anomaly heatmaps and leverages VLM-based explanations to provide human-readable justifications for detected failures. This interpretability is vital for debugging, understanding system behavior, and building user confidence, especially in human-robot collaboration scenarios.
- **Adaptive and Robust Detection:** The use of optimal transport for feature alignment makes the system robust to variations in execution speed. The spatially-aware Conformal Prediction (CP) for thresholding accounts for both temporal and patch-level visual variations, allowing for a more sensitive and context-aware detection of anomalies without strong distributional assumptions. This adaptability is particularly valuable in dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Introduction of a New Benchmark Dataset:** The creation of BotFails is a significant contribution to the robotics community. It addresses the scarcity of failure-oriented datasets by providing a multimodal resource for benchmarking anomaly and failure detection methods across diverse domestic and industrial tasks. This dataset will facilitate further research and development in this critical area.
- **Practical Applicability:** FIDeL offers a practical and modular interface for monitoring robot behavior. Its independence from the underlying IL policy means it can be readily integrated with various existing and future learned policies, offering a generalizable solution for improving the safety and robustness of robotic applications.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the VLM's computational cost, the system's reliance on demonstration diversity, and potential reduced sensitivity to fine-grained or non-Markovian anomalies. These areas suggest future research directions to further enhance the system's capabilities and address complex failure scenarios in robotics. Overall, FIDeL represents a step towards more reliable and safely deployable autonomous robotic systems.
