# RoboScape-R: Unified Reward-Observation World Models for Generalizable Robotics Training via RL

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboScape-R unifies reward and observation generation within a world model, serving as a self-contained simulation environment for training generalizable robotic policies via reinforcement learning. This framework demonstrated an average 37.5% performance improvement in out-of-domain scenarios compared to baselines and achieved balanced multi-task learning.

## Problem

- Generalizable embodied policies for robotics are hindered by overfitting in imitation learning and the challenge of designing consistent reward functions in traditional reinforcement learning.
- Current world models primarily predict observations and lack the intrinsic capability to generate explicit reward and termination signals needed for online policy optimization in RL.
- The reliance on exogenous, manually crafted reward functions in RL limits policy generalization across heterogeneous tasks and novel environments.

## Method

- A dual-world model pipeline is introduced: an Action World Model (extWMextact	ext{WM}_{	ext{act}}extWMextact) for action-conditioned observation/done signal prediction and a Text World Model (extWMexttxt	ext{WM}_{	ext{txt}}extWMexttxt) for generating goal observations from instructions.
- An endogenous, universal reward mechanism is derived directly from the world models, combining LPIPS similarity to a goal observation (dense) and the Action World Model's predicted done signal (sparse).
- The world model functions as a general, flexible RL training environment, providing all necessary signals (observations, intrinsic rewards, termination) for policy optimization across diverse scenarios.

## Results

- Policies trained with RoboScape-R achieved comparable in-domain success rates to those trained directly in physical simulators while requiring fewer resources.
- The framework delivered an average 37.5% performance improvement over baselines in out-of-domain scenarios, showcasing significantly enhanced generalization to novel scenes and object configurations.
- RoboScape-R enabled balanced performance improvements across multiple tasks in multi-task evaluation (e.g., P.&P. improved by 0.25 and M.A. by 0.47 for peach object), demonstrating superior task-level generalization with a unified reward.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World models can be transformed into comprehensive online RL environments by unifying reward and observation generation, moving beyond their traditional role as mere predictors.
> - Intrinsic, model-derived reward signals, rather than external handcrafted ones, are crucial for fostering policies that generalize robustly to unseen environments and tasks.
> - A dual-world model structure effectively separates the concerns of goal definition (via Text WM) and environment interaction (via Action WM) to enable the robust intrinsic reward mechanism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a central and persistent challenge in artificial intelligence: achieving **generalizable embodied policies** for robotics. This challenge is widely recognized as a critical step towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). The current landscape of robotics training is dominated by two paradigms: Imitation Learning (IL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

**Imitation Learning (IL)**, while effective for specific tasks and scenarios, often suffers from **overfitting** to expert trajectories and limited generalization to novel environments or unseen tasks. Policies trained via IL struggle when encountering situations not explicitly present in the training data, limiting their robustness in real-world, dynamic settings.

**Traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL)**, on the other hand, encourages more diverse exploration and can lead to robust policies. However, RL faces its own set of fundamental limitations regarding broad generalization. A major bottleneck is the **difficulty in designing universal and consistent reward functions** that are applicable across highly heterogeneous tasks and environments. Manually crafting task-specific reward functions is labor-intensive, often brittle, and inherently limits the policy's ability to generalize to novel tasks where such a reward function might not exist or be easily defined. Moreover, scaling RL to unified, multi-task policies becomes exceptionally complex due to this "exogenous" reward dependency.

**World models** have emerged as a promising alternative to tackle the generalization bottleneck. These learned predictive models can forecast future observations based on current states and actions, essentially providing an internal simulation of the environment. Trained on vast amounts of embodied data, world models can learn complex transition dynamics, making them ideal candidates for serving as "general environmental proxies." They have been successfully used for data generation to alleviate data scarcity and as offline policy evaluators.

However, existing world models typically focus solely on predicting observations. They **lack the inherent capability to generate explicit reward and termination signals** necessary for online policy optimization in an RL paradigm. Current attempts to integrate world models into RL often rely on "simplistic prediction heads" or "external reward models" to synthesize these signals. This reliance on *exogenous* reward systems means policies still optimize against artificially prescribed curves, inheriting the generalization limitations of extrinsic rewards.

RoboScape-R directly confronts this gap by proposing to use the world model itself not just for observation prediction, but also for **generating an *endogenous*, universal reward signal**. This innovative approach positions RoboScape-R as a significant advancement, aiming to transform world models into truly versatile and self-sufficient training environments for generalizable robotics, thus bridging the gap between world modeling's predictive power and RL's optimization capabilities for achieving broad generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **RoboScape-R**, a novel Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework that leverages the world model as the central simulation environment for training generalizable embodied policies.

The core motivations driving this work are:
- **Addressing the generalization bottleneck in embodied AI:** Both Imitation Learning (IL) and traditional RL struggle with policies that can effectively generalize across diverse and unseen scenarios. This limits their applicability in complex, real-world robotic tasks.
- **Overcoming the limitations of traditional RL reward design:** The reliance on task-specific, handcrafted reward functions in traditional RL severely hampers the development of truly generalizable, multi-task robotic policies. Designing universal reward signals for heterogeneous environments remains a significant challenge.
- **Unlocking the full potential of world models:** While world models are powerful for predicting observations and learning environmental dynamics, their utility as direct online RL environments is restricted by their inability to intrinsically generate reward and termination signals. The authors are motivated to enable world models to provide a *complete* training environment.
- **Providing a "unified and general reward signal":** The paper posits that the world model, with its intrinsic understanding of real-world state transition dynamics, is uniquely capable of serving as a universal environment proxy that can generate "endogenous" rewards. This intrinsic reward mechanism is seen as crucial for cultivating generalizability.
- **Enhancing multi-scenario policy training:** By providing a general reward that is derived from the world model's understanding rather than external sources, the framework aims to significantly improve a policy's ability to generalize to novel tasks and out-of-domain scenarios.

In essence, RoboScape-R aims to create a self-contained, versatile simulation environment from a trained world model that can generate both observations and a general, intrinsic reward, thereby enabling efficient and broadly generalizable RL training for robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboScape-R introduces a novel framework that integrates a world model as the primary simulation environment for generalizable embodied policy training. Its methodology centers around a dual-world model pipeline and an intrinsic, universal reward mechanism.

#### 3.1. World Model Architecture and Functionality

The proposed system comprises two distinct but structurally similar world models:
- **Action World Model ($\text{WM}_{\text{act}}$):** Responsible for receiving agent actions and generating instruction-following observation transitions (next frame observation $\hat{x}_t$ and a predicted done signal $\hat{d}_t$). This model predicts how the environment changes in response to robot actions.
- **Text World Model ($\text{WM}_{\text{txt}}$):** Primarily used to construct a "golden trajectory" and generate the goal observation ($\text{x}_{\text{goal}}$), which is then used in the reward calculation. This model predicts future observations and done signals conditioned on text instructions.

Both world models share an auto-regressive Transformer-based architecture but possess distinct parameters. Their forward process is formulated as $\hat{x}_t, \hat{d}_t = \text{WM}(x_{0:t-1}, c)$, where $x_{0:t-1}$ are historical observations and $c$ is the control signal (action sequence for $\text{WM}_{\text{act}}$, text instruction for $\text{WM}_{\text{txt}}$).

Key architectural components include:
- **Visual Tokenizer:** Utilizes MAGVIT-2 to compress RGB observations into discrete latent tokens.
- **Action/Instruction Encoder:** An MLP encodes 7-DoF robotic actions into dense embeddings for $\text{WM}_{\text{act}}$, while a T5 encoder processes text instructions into embeddings for $\text{WM}_{\text{txt}}$.
- **Spatial-Temporal Blocks (STBlock-T and STBlock-A):** These are conditioned Transformer blocks that predict the next visual tokens and the done signal. They incorporate a cross-attention mechanism to inject the control signal (text or action embeddings).
- **Visual Decoder:** Converts predicted tokens back into RGB frames.
- **MLP for Done Signal:** Processes the current frame to predict the done signal.

The models are optimized using a hybrid loss function consisting of a cross-entropy loss for visual token prediction ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{vis}}$) and an RMSE loss for done signal prediction ($\mathcal{L}_d$).

#### 3.2. World Model-based General Reward Mechanism

A central contribution is the **endogenous and unsupervised reward mechanism**, derived directly from the world model itself, rather than external handcrafted functions. This reward combines dense and sparse components:

- **Dense Reward ($\text{R}_{\text{den}}$):** Provides fine-grained guidance for faster policy convergence. It is calculated as the LPIPS similarity between the current observation $x_t$ and a generated target observation $x_{\text{goal}}$. Lower LPIPS (higher similarity) implies a better reward.
 - **Goal Observation Generation:** The $x_{\text{goal}}$ is crucial and is generated *once per episode* by the Text World Model ($\text{WM}_{\text{txt}}$). $\text{WM}_{\text{txt}}$ auto-regressively predicts next frames based on the initial observation and the task instruction. The first frame where the predicted done signal ($\hat{d}_{\text{txt}}$) exceeds a threshold $\theta$ is taken as $x_{\text{goal}}$, representing the accomplished task state.
- **Sparse Reward ($\text{R}_{\text{sps}}$):** Enhances generalization and helps avoid local optima. It's a binary reward (1 or 0) determined by the Action World Model's predicted done signal ($\hat{d}_{\text{act}}$). If $\hat{d}_{\text{act}}$ reaches a specified threshold $\theta_{\text{sps}}$, a sparse reward of 1 is given; otherwise, it's 0.

The total reward $R$ is a weighted sum: $R = \alpha \cdot \text{R}_{\text{sps}} + \beta \cdot \text{R}_{\text{den}}$.

#### 3.3. World Model-based RL Training Pipeline

The training pipeline integrates the world model as a flexible, general-purpose environment:
1. **Generalizable Environment Initialization:** Unlike physical simulators that initialize a single environment type, the world model, trained on massive data, can represent diverse scenarios. This allows the policy to interact with *multiple* environments simultaneously, enhancing generalizability. For each environment, $x_{\text{goal}}$ is generated by $\text{WM}_{\text{txt}}$.
2. **Policy Rollout in the Environment:** The policy ($\pi_{\Theta}$) generates an action sequence. The Action World Model ($\text{WM}_{\text{act}}$) receives these actions and generates the corresponding next observation ($x_t$) and sparse done signal. Simultaneously, the dense reward is calculated based on $x_t$ and the pre-generated $x_{\text{goal}}$. The combined reward $R$ is then provided to the policy.
3. **Policy Optimization:** The world model-based environment offers a general interface for observations, rewards, and done signals, making it compatible with various RL optimization algorithms (e.g., PPO) and policy architectures (e.g., MLP, OpenVLA). The policy parameters are updated using collected trajectories.

This methodology transforms the world model from a mere predictor into an active, reward-generating training environment, capable of fostering policies with enhanced generalization capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to validate RoboScape-R demonstrate its efficacy in enhancing the generalization capability of embodied policies, particularly in out-of-domain and multi-task scenarios.

#### 4.1. Experimental Settings

- **Dataset:** A custom dataset was collected using the ManiSkill physical simulator. It comprised 300,000+ optimal and suboptimal trajectories across 80 scenes, involving 20 objects, 2 containers, and 2 tables, for 4 tasks: Pick and Place (P.&P.), Push, Pull, and Move to Aim (M.A.). Suboptimal trajectories were generated to increase robustness and action space coverage.
- **World Model Training:** The action and text world models were trained on 80% of the collected scenes for 5 epochs (approx. 51 million 28-frame clips).
- **Policy Training:** Policies (MLP and OpenVLA) were trained using PPO within the RL4VLA framework, using either ManiSkill (physical simulator) or the RoboScape-R world model as the environment.
- **Evaluation:** Success Rate (SR) was used as the metric.
 - **In-domain (IND):** Policy trained and evaluated in the same 80% scenes (different initial states).
 - **Out-of-domain (OOD):** Policy trained in the 80% scenes and evaluated in the 20% unseen scenes, which included unseen objects and novel combinations of seen objects/containers.
 - **Multi-task:** Policy trained on two tasks (P.&P. and M.A.) simultaneously.
- **Baselines for Reward Module Comparison:**
 - **Rule-based reward:** Handcrafted rewards common in physical simulators.
 - **iVideoGPT (Embedding-based reward):** Uses a single reward head from hidden embeddings to fit manual reward labels.
 - **DiWA (Proxy-based reward):** Employs an external model (MLP) to provide visual image-based rewards.

#### 4.2. Overall Performance and Comparison

- **Reward Module Comparison (Fig. 2 & 3):**
 - RoboScape-R's world model-based reward achieved the highest success rate in training generalizable policies, converging efficiently, similar to rule-based methods.
 - Exogenous (embedding-based and proxy-based) rewards converged more slowly and yielded significantly poorer success rates, attributed to their instability from directly fitting manually designed rewards.
 - Fig. 3 (reward curve in OOD scenario) clearly showed that RoboScape-R's endogenous reward remained stable and reasonable in unseen environments, unlike proxy-based rewards which provided "meaningless" signals.

- **In-Domain Evaluation (Table 1 & Fig. 4):**
 - RL training (both in ManiSkill and RoboScape-R) consistently improved performance over Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) for both MLP and OpenVLA policies across all tasks.
 - RoboScape-R (RL w. World Model) achieved success rates comparable to RL trained directly in ManiSkill, confirming its effectiveness as an online training environment. For instance, OpenVLA's P.&P. SR was 92.5% with ManiSkill and 95.5% with RoboScape-R.
 - Qualitative visualization (Fig. 4) showed that RoboScape-R-trained policies performed comparably to rule-based ones in in-domain tasks, while embedding-based and proxy-based methods exhibited inferior performance (e.g., effector shaking, misclosing gripper).

- **Out-of-Domain Evaluation (Table 1 & Fig. 5):**
 - This is where RoboScape-R demonstrated its most significant impact. Policies trained with RoboScape-R achieved substantial performance improvements in unseen scenarios.
 - The paper reports an **average 37.5% performance improvement over baselines** in out-of-domain scenarios.
 - For MLP policies, OOD success rates dramatically increased, e.g., P.&P. from 32.1% (RL w. ManiSkill) to 74.2% (RL w. World Model); Push from 15.6% to 87.3%.
 - For OpenVLA policies, similar significant gains were observed, e.g., P.&P. from 54.5% to 84.1%; Pull from 39.6% to 85.8%.
 - The authors attribute this to the policy's ability to interact with a series of additional, diverse environments generated by the world model, fostering a deeper understanding of task dynamics rather than overfitting to specific environment properties.
 - Qualitative visualization (Fig. 5) strongly supported this, showing that *only* the policy trained with RoboScape-R could successfully complete tasks in OOD environments, while others failed (e.g., rule-based failed to place, embedding-based failed to pick, proxy-based performed meaningless motions).

- **Multi-Task Evaluation (Table 2):**
 - RL training in ManiSkill with separate, manually designed rewards led to unbalanced performance (e.g., P.&P. improved significantly, but M.A. dropped).
 - RoboScape-R, with its unified and general world model-based reward, achieved **balanced performance improvements** across multiple tasks (e.g., P.&P. ↑.25, M.A. ↑.47 for peach object), indicating superior task-level generalization. This is due to the policy interacting with a single, unified reward signal that consistently guides learning across diverse tasks.

- **World Model Controllability (Fig. 9 Supplementary):**
 - The robust design of RoboScape-R's world model, particularly its cross-attention-based action injection and comprehensive pre-training, enabled it to respond consistently and plausibly even to unseen or extreme actions generated by the policy. This is critical for stable online RL training, where policies might initially explore out-of-distribution actions.

In summary, RoboScape-R successfully transforms the world model into a versatile RL environment with an intrinsic, generalizable reward mechanism, leading to policies with significantly enhanced generalization capabilities across novel scenes and tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboScape-R framework marks a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotic learning, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Addressing the Generalization Challenge

The most significant contribution of RoboScape-R is its effective solution to the long-standing challenge of achieving generalizable embodied policies. By demonstrating substantial improvements (an average of 37.5%) in out-of-domain scenarios and balanced performance in multi-task learning, the paper shows a viable path toward creating robotic agents that can perform robustly in diverse, unseen real-world environments. This moves beyond the limitations of task-specific overfitting prevalent in imitation learning and the "exogenous reward problem" in traditional reinforcement learning.

#### 5.2. Pioneering World Model as an Online RL Environment

RoboScape-R establishes a pioneering methodology for integrating world models as online, self-sufficient simulation environments for RL training. Traditionally, world models were limited to observation prediction, data generation, or offline evaluation. This work shows that with a carefully designed dual-world model architecture and an intrinsic reward mechanism, world models can provide *all* essential environmental signals (observations, rewards, termination) for active policy optimization. This unlocks a new paradigm for data-efficient and scalable robotic skill acquisition.

#### 5.3. The Power of Endogenous, Universal Rewards

The introduction of a world model-derived, "endogenous" reward mechanism is a critical innovation. By generating rewards from the model's intrinsic understanding of real-world state transition dynamics, RoboScape-R bypasses the need for laborious and brittle manual reward engineering. This universal reward signal enables policies to learn fundamental task objectives that generalize across heterogeneous tasks, leading to more robust and adaptable behaviors. This principle could inspire new directions in intrinsic motivation and reward shaping for complex agents.

#### 5.4. Reducing the Sim2Real Gap

By training policies within a learned world model that captures real-world dynamics, the framework holds immense promise for reducing the notorious Sim2Real (simulation to reality) gap. If the world model accurately represents the complexities of the physical world, training in such a model could directly lead to policies that transfer more effectively to real robots. This could dramatically accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots in practical applications.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Embodied AI Research

RoboScape-R provides a strong foundation for future research in several areas:
- **More Complex Tasks:** While currently limited to shorter-horizon tasks, the core paradigm can be extended to support more complex, long-horizon tasks as world models improve in their temporal coherence and controllability.
- **Diverse RL Algorithms:** The flexibility of the world model-based environment, with its standardized interfaces, means it can readily support exploration with a wide array of existing and novel reinforcement learning algorithms and policy architectures.
- **Adaptive Learning:** The intrinsic reward generation could pave the way for adaptive learning systems where the "reward function" itself evolves with the world model's understanding.
- **Real-world Applications:** The enhanced generalization capabilities are crucial for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic environments, such as service robotics, manufacturing, and exploration.

#### 5.6. Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions

The authors transparently discuss the current limitations:
- **Long-horizon and Complex Tasks:** The autoregressive architecture of their world model can only maintain stable and high-quality rollouts for a limited number of frames (around 300), restricting the framework to short-duration tasks. Addressing this will require advancements in world model architectures, possibly through hierarchical modeling or non-autoregressive generation.
- **Reliance on World Model Performance:** The success of RoboScape-R fundamentally hinges on the accuracy and robustness of the underlying world model's learned dynamics. Errors or incompleteness in the world model's understanding will directly translate to limitations in the trained policies.

Despite these limitations, RoboScape-R presents a compelling vision for how world models can be leveraged to fundamentally reshape reinforcement learning for robotics, moving closer to the goal of generalizable and adaptable embodied intelligence.
