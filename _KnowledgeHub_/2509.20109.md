# Discrete Diffusion for Reflective Vision-Language-Action Models in Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> ReflectDrive, a collaboration between LiAuto and Tsinghua University, introduces a discrete diffusion framework for autonomous driving that uses a gradient-free reflection mechanism to generate safe and coherent trajectories. It achieved a 91.1 PDMS score on the NAVSIM benchmark, improving safety metrics like Drivable Area Compliance by 3.9 points and outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- End-to-End (E2E) autonomous driving models based on imitation learning struggle to inherently encode fundamental physical rules and safety constraints, leading to potentially unsafe trajectories.
- Existing safety enhancement methods, such as rule-based post-refinement, reinforcement learning (RL), or continuous diffusion guidance, face limitations including inflexibility, impracticality for real-world deployment due to unsafe online rollouts, or high computational costs during inference.
- There is a strong demand for verifiable and controllable E2E driving systems that can rigorously guide learned behaviors with prior knowledge and safety constraints, moving beyond 'black-box' planning.

## Method

- Transforms continuous 2D driving trajectories into ==discrete token sequences==, enabling compatibility with discrete diffusion models and facilitating efficient search for feasible solutions.
- Develops a discrete diffusion-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, fine-tuned from a pre-trained Diffusion Language Model, to generate tokenized trajectories conditioned on complex scene context.
- Implements a two-stage, gradient-free reflective inference mechanism (Goal-Conditioned Generation and Safety-Guided Regeneration) that iteratively corrects safety violations using specialized scoring functions and the diffusion model's inpainting capabilities.

## Results

- Achieved a PDMS score of 91.1 on the NAVSIM benchmark, demonstrating a 6.3-point improvement over the base discrete diffusion model without reflective inference and surpassing most listed baselines.
- Substantially enhanced critical safety metrics, with Drivable Area Compliance (DAC) increasing by 3.9 points to 99.3, Time-to-Collision (TTC) by 1.3 points to 93.5, and No-Collision Rate (NC) by **0.8** points to 97.7.
- When evaluated with a privileged ground-truth oracle for reflection, the system achieved a PDMS score of 94.7, remarkably close to human driving performance (94.8), indicating high potential given accurate perception and prediction systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discrete trajectory representation allows for the direct and inherent integration of hard safety constraints through search, masking, and inpainting, overcoming limitations of continuous optimization for safety-critical tasks.
> - The gradient-free reflection mechanism provides an efficient and practical method for iterative safety refinement during inference, avoiding the computationally expensive gradient calculations typical in continuous diffusion guidance.
> - By combining learned behaviors from a VLA model with explicit, external safety validation, the framework effectively mitigates the inherent safety gaps of imitation learning, creating a robust hybrid approach for autonomous driving.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving (AD) research has seen a significant shift from traditional modular designs and rule-based methods towards End-to-End (E2E) solutions. E2E systems, which directly map sensor inputs to driving actions, mitigate the error accumulation inherent in modular pipelines. Within this E2E paradigm, **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** represent a cutting-edge approach. These models leverage pre-trained multimodal knowledge from large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to interpret complex visual scenes and human instructions, outputting direct planning trajectories. This VLA paradigm promises enhanced generalization and adaptability, especially in challenging "long-tail" scenarios not frequently encountered in training data.

However, a fundamental challenge persists: most VLA models, like other E2E systems, are rooted in **imitation learning (behavior cloning)**. While effective for learning complex behaviors, imitation learning struggles to inherently encode inviolable physical rules (e.g., collision avoidance, drivable area compliance). This often leads to generated trajectories that are highly probable under the model's distribution but critically unsafe in reality.

The current landscape addresses this safety gap through several strategies:
- **Rule-based Post-Refinement / Trajectory Anchors:** Relying on human priors or rule-generated paths to provide a reliable initial solution or constraint. This often requires substantial post-processing and can be inflexible in complex situations.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Integrating human-designed reward functions to enhance causal reasoning and safety. While promising in simulation (e.g., self-play), RL faces significant hurdles for real-world deployment due to the need for unsafe online rollouts, training instability with large models, and the "sim-to-real" gap.
- **Diffusion Guidance:** Utilizing guidance mechanisms in diffusion models to enable controllable generation by injecting reward signals during inference. This approach offers flexibility but typically suffers from slow sampling speeds due to computationally expensive gradient calculations and sensitivity to parameter tuning.

**ReflectDrive** positions itself as a novel solution within this evolving landscape, specifically addressing the limitations of imitation learning and the computational overhead of gradient-based diffusion guidance. It pioneers the use of **discrete diffusion** for AD planning, which allows for the seamless incorporation of safety constraints through search, masking, and sampling in a discrete action space. By introducing a **reflection mechanism** that performs iterative self-correction *without gradient computation*, ReflectDrive offers a hybrid framework where learned behaviors are rigorously guided by prior knowledge, aiming for trustworthy, interpretable, and safer decision-making without the deployment complexities of RL or the computational costs of continuous diffusion guidance. This work represents a significant step towards practical and verifiable E2E autonomous driving systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ReflectDrive stems from the critical limitations of existing E2E autonomous driving solutions, particularly those based on imitation learning (IL):

1. **Inherent Safety Gaps in Imitation Learning:** IL models struggle to inherently encode fundamental physical rules and safety constraints (e.g., collision avoidance, staying within drivable areas). Trajectories generated, even if high-probability, can violate safety-critical rules, leading to deployment risks.
2. **Drawbacks of Current Safety-Enhancement Methods:**
 - **Rule-based methods/trajectory anchors:** While providing initial reliability, they demand extensive human engineering, are prone to complexity in diverse scenarios, and require significant post-processing.
 - **Reinforcement Learning:** Despite enhancing causal reasoning, RL is largely confined to simulation due to the infeasibility of unsafe online rollouts in real vehicles, along with training instability and the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Guidance mechanisms in continuous diffusion models:** These offer controllability but incur high computational costs due to gradient calculations during inference, leading to slow sampling speeds and parameter sensitivity.
3. **Need for Verifiable and Controllable E2E Driving:** There is a strong demand for autonomous driving systems that are not "black-box" but instead allow for the rigorous guidance of learned behaviors by prior knowledge and safety constraints.

To address these challenges, ReflectDrive sets forth the following key objectives:

- **Pioneer Discrete Diffusion for AD Planning:** Introduce and validate discrete diffusion models as a viable and advantageous paradigm for E2E autonomous driving trajectory generation, especially within a VLA framework.
- **Develop a Gradient-Free Reflection Mechanism:** Design and implement a novel inference-time guidance framework that integrates external safety validation with efficient discrete token optimization. This mechanism should enable iterative self-correction without relying on computationally expensive gradient computations.
- **Ensure Trajectory Safety and Coherence:** Create a system that can enforce hard safety constraints (e.g., collision avoidance, drivable area compliance) without compromising the overall behavioral coherence and progress of the generated trajectories.
- **Achieve Scalable and Reliable Solutions:** Offer a solution that is scalable in training (leveraging pre-trained DLMs) and reliable in deployment, particularly on real-world benchmarks, bridging the gap between learning-based approaches and verifiable safety.

In essence, ReflectDrive aims to deliver a robust, safe, and computationally efficient learning-based planning framework that can be seamlessly integrated into real-world autonomous driving systems by leveraging the strengths of discrete diffusion and a novel gradient-free reflection mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReflectDrive introduces a novel learning-based framework that integrates a reflection mechanism for safe trajectory generation using discrete diffusion. The methodology can be broken down into three main components: trajectory discretization, a discrete diffusion-based VLA model, and a two-stage reflective inference process.

#### 3.1. Trajectory Discretization

The first crucial step is to convert the continuous 2D driving space into a discrete format.
- **Action Codebook Construction:** Each 2D coordinate (x, y) of a waypoint is independently quantized by mapping its x and y values to the closest tokens in respective 1D codebooks. These 1D codebooks are uniform discretizations of a spatial range `[-M, M]` with a specific resolution `Δg`.
- **Discrete Trajectory Representation:** A full trajectory `τ` is transformed into a flattened sequence of token pairs `y = Q(τ) = (y1,x, y1,y..., yN,x, yN,y)`.
- **Rationale:** While seemingly leading to precision loss, this discretization is adjustable. Critically, it enables efficient search for feasible solutions in the Bird's-Eye View (BEV) space and makes the trajectory compatible with discrete diffusion models, facilitating constraint integration.

#### 3.2. Discrete Diffusion-Based VLA Model

The core planner is built upon a discrete diffusion model.
- **Model Initialization:** The VLA model backbone is initialized from a pre-trained Diffusion Language Model (DLM) (e.g., LLaDA-V), which has strong capabilities in understanding driving scenarios.
- **Planning Task:** This model generates a tokenized trajectory `y` conditioned on a scene context `c` (multi-view images, language instruction, ego state).
- **Training:** The model is fine-tuned using autonomous driving planning datasets, optimizing a denoising objective similar to standard discrete diffusion, enabling robust bidirectional inpainting capabilities essential for the reflective inference.

#### 3.3. Reflective Inference

This two-stage, gradient-free inference framework bridges the gap between learned behaviors and safety-critical deployment. It is guided by a set of specialized scoring functions:
- **Scoring Functions:**
 - `S_global(τ)`: Evaluates the overall quality (safety and coherence) of a complete trajectory, returning zero for critical rule violations.
 - `S_safe(τ)`: A safety oracle identifying specific points of failure by assigning a safety score to each waypoint based on local violations.
 - `S_local(ax, ay)`: Evaluates individual candidate token pairs for their impact on local safety and coherence.

#### 3.3.1. Goal-Conditioned Generation (GCG)

This initial stage generates diverse, high-level trajectory proposals to model multi-modal driving behaviors.
- **Terminal Waypoint Sampling:** The model first predicts a probability distribution for terminal waypoint tokens `pθ(yN | c, s)`.
- **Goal Candidate Selection:** From this distribution, a set of high-probability goal candidates is sampled. Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) is applied to obtain a spatially diverse set of `K` candidate goals `G = {G1..., GK}`.
- **Trajectory Generation:** For each selected goal `Gk`, a full trajectory `τk` is generated by sampling from `pθ(y1:2N−2 | Gk, c, s)` via inpainting.
- **Best Trajectory Selection:** All `K` trajectories are evaluated using `S_global(·)`, and the top-scoring trajectory `τ*` is selected for further refinement. This ensures the planner considers global intents beyond local adjustments.

#### 3.3.2. Safety-Guided Regeneration (SGR)

The selected trajectory `τ*` is then iteratively refined to ensure safety without compromising coherence. This is an iterative, gradient-free loop:
- **Trajectory Evaluation:** `S_safe(·)` evaluates the de-quantized `τ*`, identifying the earliest waypoint `t*` that violates a predefined safety threshold. If no violation, the process terminates.
- **Safety Anchors Search:** For the unsafe waypoint `t*`, an efficient local search is performed within a small Manhattan neighborhood `Nδ` of the original tokens. The `S_local(·)` function guides this search to identify an improved token pair that maximizes the local safety score. This corrected token pair becomes a fixed "safety anchor."
- **Trajectory Inpainting:** The diffusion model's inpainting capability is leveraged to regenerate the surrounding trajectory segments, conditioned on the newly fixed safety anchor. This single-pass regeneration re-establishes global coherence around the safety-driven edit.
- **Iterative Refinement:** This cycle of identifying violations, performing discrete corrections, and re-inpainting continues until the trajectory is fully safe or a computational budget (e.g., max iterations) is met. The process is efficient because local search is performed over a small discrete neighborhood, and inpainting is a single-pass operation, avoiding expensive gradient computations.

In summary, ReflectDrive combines discrete representations with the powerful generative capabilities of fine-tuned DLMs, further enhanced by an intelligent, gradient-free reflection mechanism that iteratively corrects safety violations, making it a robust and practical solution for autonomous driving.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ReflectDrive was evaluated on the NAVSIM benchmark, a large-scale real-world autonomous driving benchmark, assessing closed-loop performance using the PDMS score, which aggregates metrics for no-collision rate (NC), drivable area compliance (DAC), time-to-collision safety (TTC), comfort (Comf.), and ego progress (EP).

#### 4.1. Base Model Validation

- **ReflectDrive (w/o R.I.)** – The base discrete diffusion model without the reflective inference mechanism – achieved a PDMS score of 84.8. This score is comparable to vanilla End-to-End planners like UniAD (83.4) and PARA-Drive (84.0), and slightly lower than augmented E2E planners or other VLA models (e.g., AutoVLA at 89.1). This indicates that discrete diffusion alone provides a competitive base for trajectory generation but does not inherently offer significant performance advantages without further refinement. The authors attribute this to limited training data scale and potential for VLM improvement.

#### 4.2. Significant Improvements from Reflective Inference

- **Impact of Safety-Guided Regeneration (S.G.R.):** The introduction of the reflection mechanism, particularly the safety-guided regeneration, yielded substantial improvements. Compared to the base model (`ReflectDrive (w/o R.I.)`):
 - DAC: +3.9 points (from 95.4 to 99.3)
 - TTC: +1.3 points (from 92.2 to 93.5)
 - NC: +0.8 points (from 96.9 to 97.7)
 - EP: +7.9 points (from 79.0 to 86.9)
 - PDMS: +6.3 points (from 84.8 to 91.1)
- **Overall Performance:** The full ReflectDrive model (`ReflectDrive (Ours)`) achieved a PDMS score of 91.1, surpassing most listed baselines, including augmented E2E planners like DiffusionDrive (88.1) and GoalFlow (90.3), and significantly outperforming `AutoVLA (Post-RFT)` (89.1) in safety metrics, especially DAC (99.3 vs 95.6). This demonstrates that the reflection mechanism effectively addresses the safety limitations of imitation learning.

#### 4.3. Approaching Human Driving Performance

- **Privileged Oracle (`ReflectDrive†`):** To explore the upper bounds of the method, an evaluation was conducted using a privileged ground-truth oracle for reflection (assuming complete environmental information from ground-truth agents, rather than constant-velocity agents). In this ideal scenario, ReflectDrive achieved:
 - NC: 99.7
 - DAC: 99.5
 - TTC: 99.1
 - EP: 88.9 (higher than human at 87.5)
 - PDMS: 94.7
- These results demonstrate performance remarkably close to, and in some aspects even exceeding, human driving (Human PDMS: 94.8). This finding suggests that with accurate detection and prediction results—which are handled by specialized models in real-world deployments—ReflectDrive has the potential to match or surpass human-level performance in safety and efficiency.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

- Visualizations (Figure 3) effectively demonstrate the safety-guided regeneration process. Initial unsafe trajectories (e.g., prone to boundary violations or collision risks) are shown to be iteratively optimized towards safe regions, gradually darkening in color as they converge. The generated trajectories maintain kinematic feasibility and smoothness despite the discrete representation, validating the viability of discrete diffusion for AD planning.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Inference Parameters (Figure 4):**
 - Generation steps: Model performance peaks at 5 steps, indicating an optimal balance between refinement and computational cost.
 - Number of goal points: Increasing the number of goal candidates in G.C.G. improves performance (e.g., PDMS from 84.8 to 87.4 with 3 goal points), confirming the benefit of multi-modal behavior modeling.
 - Exploration steps & Max iterations: Performance scales with increased computational resources allocated to exploration and regeneration steps, suggesting room for further optimization.
- **Design Choices for Reflective Inference (Table 2):**
 - **Goal-Conditioned Generation (GCG):** Improves Ego Progress (EP from 79.0 to 83.8), validating its role in optimizing high-level driving intents.
 - **Safety-Guided Regeneration (SGR):** Significantly improves safety metrics (DAC from 95.4 to 98.9, TTC from 92.2 to 94.8, NC from 96.9 to 98.1) and also boosts EP (from 79.0 to 84.1).
 - **Full Model (GCG + SGR):** Achieves the best overall performance (PDMS 91.1), demonstrating the complementary nature of both components in ensuring both progress and safety.

In summary, ReflectDrive provides a strong base model using discrete diffusion, which is then dramatically enhanced by its novel reflective inference mechanism, particularly in safety-critical aspects. The privileged oracle results highlight the profound potential of this approach when integrated with robust perception and prediction systems, bringing it to the cusp of human-level autonomous driving performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReflectDrive represents a significant advancement in autonomous driving research, particularly in addressing the long-standing challenge of ensuring verifiable safety within learning-based E2E systems. Its core innovations and potential impacts are:

1. **Pioneering Discrete Diffusion for AD Planning:** This work introduces a novel paradigm by applying discrete diffusion models to continuous trajectory generation for autonomous driving. This is significant because it allows for the inherent integration of hard safety constraints through search, masking, and inpainting techniques within a discrete action space. This moves beyond the limitations of continuous optimization or complex post-processing for safety.
2. **Gradient-Free Reflection Mechanism for Practical Safety:** The proposed reflection mechanism is a breakthrough for real-world deployment. By performing iterative self-correction without computationally expensive gradient calculations, it offers an efficient and practical way to enforce safety constraints during inference. This mitigates the slow sampling speeds and parameter sensitivity that plague gradient-based diffusion guidance methods, making it more feasible for real-time autonomous driving systems.
3. **Enhanced Safety and Reliability:** ReflectDrive demonstrably improves critical safety metrics such as Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), Time-to-Collision (TTC), and No-Collision Rate (NC). The ability to achieve near-human-level safety performance, especially under privileged information, underscores its potential to build highly reliable autonomous vehicles that prioritize safety without sacrificing driving efficiency.
4. **Bridging the Imitation Learning Gap:** By integrating an external safety oracle and a robust self-correction loop, ReflectDrive effectively compensates for the inherent inability of imitation learning to encode inviolable physical rules. This hybrid approach offers a powerful solution to a core problem in learning-based autonomous driving.
5. **Scalability and Adaptability:** Leveraging pre-trained Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) within a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework allows for scalable training and enhanced generalization capabilities. The goal-conditioned generation further supports multi-modal driving behaviors, increasing the model's adaptability to diverse and complex scenarios.
6. **Trustworthy and Interpretable Decision-Making:** By explicitly integrating prior knowledge and safety constraints into the planning process, ReflectDrive shifts away from purely "black-box" planning towards a more trustworthy and interpretable decision-making framework. This is crucial for public acceptance and regulatory approval of autonomous systems.
7. **Future Directions and Optimization:** The paper openly discusses limitations and future work, such as incorporating historical images for better interaction understanding, refining reward functions, improving goal point selection through reinforcement learning, and optimizing sample efficiency. The observed "oscillation between boundaries" in failure cases provides clear directions for future research into discrete trajectory representation and search algorithms.

In conclusion, ReflectDrive offers a compelling vision for the next generation of autonomous driving systems. By successfully combining discrete diffusion models with an efficient, gradient-free reflective inference mechanism, it pushes the boundaries of how learning-based approaches can achieve both high performance and stringent safety requirements, paving the way for more robust and deployable autonomous vehicles.
