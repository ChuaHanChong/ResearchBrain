# LocoMamba: Vision-Driven Locomotion via End-to-End Deep Reinforcement Learning with Mamba

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Woven by Toyota and Trinity College Dublin developed LocoMamba, a deep reinforcement learning framework for quadrupedal robots that integrates a Mamba selective state-space model for efficient vision-proprioception fusion. The system achieved superior locomotion performance, faster training convergence, and robust generalization on diverse terrains compared to Transformer-based alternatives.

## Problem

- Traditional quadrupedal locomotion control relies on precise models and extensive manual tuning, limiting scalability and real-world deployment.
- Existing deep reinforcement learning (DRL) approaches are often 'blind' (proprioception-only) or face computational bottlenecks from Transformer-based cross-modal fusion (quadratic scaling) or limited long-horizon dependencies with RNNs.
- A need for foresightful, vision-driven control systems that can proactively navigate complex and dynamic environments without high computational and memory overhead for real-time robotic applications.

## Method

- **LocoMamba**, an end-to-end DRL framework, utilizes a ==Mamba selective state-space model== as its core ==cross-modal fusion== backbone, combining proprioceptive states and depth images.
- The Mamba backbone efficiently processes concatenated vision and proprioception tokens, enabling ==long-horizon modeling== with ==near-linear time and memory scaling==, overcoming the quadratic complexity of self-attention mechanisms.
- The control policy is optimized using ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)==, incorporating ==domain randomization== for physics parameters, visual perturbations, and an obstacle-density curriculum to enhance robustness and generalizability.

## Results

- LocoMamba significantly outperformed Transformer-based baselines, achieving **48.9%** higher returns, **48.9%** fewer collisions, and **30.4%** longer progress distance on trained obstacle terrains.
- The framework demonstrated faster training convergence, higher asymptotic returns, and improved stability with lower variance in value-function loss and estimated advantages compared to other fusion architectures.
- LocoMamba exhibited strong zero-shot generalization to unseen rugged terrains (**126.8%** higher return) and dynamic obstacle environments, while also showing superior computational throughput (**104.68 samples/s**) and a lower memory footprint (**207.2 MB**) than Transformer-based methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mamba selective state-space models offer a computationally efficient and scalable alternative to Transformers and RNNs for real-time cross-modal fusion in robotic control, effectively capturing long-range temporal dependencies.
> - Effective integration of both immediate proprioceptive state estimates and foresightful depth vision is crucial for developing policies that enable proactive obstacle avoidance and robust terrain negotiation in quadrupedal locomotion.
> - Comprehensive DRL training strategies, including extensive domain randomization and curriculum learning, are essential for training policies that exhibit strong zero-shot generalization to previously unseen and dynamic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of quadrupedal robot locomotion is critical for enabling robots to operate in complex, unstructured environments where wheeled platforms are ineffective. Traditionally, locomotion control relied on classical model-based approaches, which demand precise environmental and dynamic models, intricate contact planning, and extensive manual tuning, limiting their scalability and real-world deployment. The advent of learning-based methods, particularly Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), has revolutionized this domain by enabling end-to-end policies that can learn adaptive behaviors through interaction, reducing the burden of explicit modeling.

However, existing DRL approaches for quadrupedal locomotion face significant challenges. Many successful DRL policies are "blind," relying solely on proprioceptive feedback (e.g., joint angles, IMU data). While robust due to extensive simulation and domain randomization, these agents lack foresight, reacting only after physical contact and struggling with proactive obstacle avoidance or precise foot placement on irregular terrain.

To overcome this, researchers have increasingly turned to cross-modal fusion, integrating visual information (exteroception) with proprioception. Vision provides crucial look-ahead capabilities, enabling early perception of distant obstacles and anticipation of terrain changes. Yet, current fusion architectures have their own limitations:
- **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)**, such as LSTMs and GRUs, can model sequential data but often suffer from vanishing gradients and limited capacity for capturing long-horizon dependencies, making optimization challenging for extended sequences.
- **Transformer-based models**, while highly expressive and effective at modeling long-range dependencies through self-attention, incur a quadratic computational cost and memory usage with respect to the input sequence length. This quadratic scaling severely constrains their applicability in real-time robotic systems, where low latency, high frame rates, and potentially high-resolution visual inputs are crucial.

LocoMamba directly addresses these limitations by introducing the selective state-space model (SSM) Mamba as its cross-modal fusion backbone. Mamba offers an alternative sequence modeling paradigm that scales near-linearly with sequence length, addressing the computational inefficiencies of Transformers while preserving the ability to model long-range dependencies, a feature often lacking in traditional RNNs. This work positions itself at the forefront of combining efficient, modern sequence modeling architectures with DRL for robust and foresightful quadrupedal locomotion, offering a compelling solution to the trade-offs inherent in prior cross-modal fusion methods. It pioneers the use of Mamba in this specific application, distinguishing it from other Mamba-based multimodal fusion works that focus on perception-level alignment rather than real-time robotic control with its unique latency and causal dependency requirements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the LocoMamba framework are:

1. **Overcome limitations of existing cross-modal fusion architectures:** The central motivation is to bypass the quadratic computational and memory costs of Transformer-based models and the long-horizon dependency issues of RNNs, which hinder the scalability and efficiency of vision-driven DRL for quadrupedal robots.
2. **Enable foresightful and efficient quadrupedal locomotion:** By integrating visual input effectively, the goal is to allow robots to anticipate terrain changes and obstacles proactively, leading to smoother, safer, and more efficient navigation compared to "blind" proprioception-only controllers.
3. **Develop a robust and stable end-to-end DRL framework:** The authors aim for a system where perception and control are tightly integrated and optimized, ensuring stable learning dynamics and consistent performance across diverse and challenging environments.
4. **Improve locomotion performance metrics:** Specifically, the objective is to achieve higher episode returns, significantly reduce collision times, and enable longer progress distances, demonstrating superior control and navigation capabilities.
5. **Enhance training efficiency:** By leveraging Mamba's near-linear scaling, a key objective is to achieve faster convergence and reduce the overall training cost (e.g., less wall-clock time and compute budget) compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, facilitating rapid iteration and deployment.
6. **Ensure strong generalization to unseen conditions:** The framework aims to learn policies that can transfer effectively to novel, previously unencountered rugged terrains and dynamic obstacle scenarios, indicating a robust and adaptable control strategy.

In essence, the authors seek to build a DRL system for quadrupedal robots that marries efficient, causal, and long-range sequence modeling (via Mamba) with rich sensory input (proprioception and vision) to achieve superior, intelligent, and deployable locomotion.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LocoMamba proposes an end-to-end DRL framework built around the Mamba selective state-space model for cross-modal fusion. The methodology can be broken down into three main components: input encoding, Mamba-based fusion backbone, and policy optimization.

#### 3.1. Proprioception–Depth Input Encoding

The system takes two primary observations at each time step $t$:
- **Proprioceptive state ($s_{prop_t}$):** A 93-dimensional vector comprising IMU readings, local joint rotations, and the actions issued over the previous three time steps. This is processed by a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) into a compact proprioceptive token ($z_{prop_t}$).
- **Depth images ($I_{depth_t}$):** A stack of the four most recent 64x64 resolution first-person-view depth images. These are processed by a compact Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) that patchifies them into a sequence of spatial visual tokens ($z_{vis_t}$).

Both proprioceptive and visual embeddings are then projected to a common latent dimension and concatenated into a single cross-modal token sequence. Learned positional indices and modality tags are added, followed by layer normalization, to prepare the tokens for the Mamba backbone. This design ensures immediate state estimates from proprioception and look-ahead capabilities from depth.

#### 3.2. State-Space Modelling with Mamba (Cross-Modal Fusion Backbone)

The core of LocoMamba is a stack of $L_m$ Mamba SSM layers. Each Mamba layer processes the concatenated token stream using a selective state-space scanning mechanism.
- **Selective State-Space Scanning:** Unlike self-attention in Transformers, which has quadratic complexity, Mamba layers maintain a compact recurrent hidden state per token. This state is updated using input-dependent parameters ($\bar{A}, \bar{B}, \bar{C}, \bar{D}$), which are generated by lightweight affine transforms of the input token. This selective scanning allows for efficient long-horizon modeling with near-linear time and memory scaling with respect to sequence length.
- **Recurrent Updates:** The initial hidden state for each new time step is carried over from the final state of the previous time step, creating a causal, exponentially decaying memory. This promotes temporally consistent features and enhances robustness to variable-length inputs.
- **Stacked Architecture:** Two Mamba layers are stacked with residual connections and layer normalization to capture both short- and mid-term temporal dependencies.
- **Projection Head:** After fusion by the Mamba backbone, modality-aware pooling (average pooling for visual tokens, direct use of proprioceptive token) is applied to the final layer outputs. This results in a compact fused feature vector ($h_t$) that parameterizes the policy and value functions.

#### 3.3. Policy Optimization with PPO

The control policy is optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a widely used on-policy DRL algorithm.
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs a 12-dimensional vector of desired joint position targets for the robot's 12 actuated joints, which are then converted to torques by a PD controller.
- **Reward Function:** A unified, state-centric reward function balances three objectives:
 - **Forward progress ($R_{fwd}$):** Rewards linear velocity along the target direction.
 - **Energy efficiency ($R_{energy}$):** Penalizes excessive joint torques.
 - **Survival ($R_{alive}$):** Rewards staying upright and avoiding unrecoverable collisions.
- **Training Scheme:**
 - **End-to-End Optimization:** Gradients from the PPO objective flow through the entire network, from the policy/value heads back through the Mamba backbone and both encoders.
 - **Domain Randomization:** To improve robustness and sim-to-real transferability, physics parameters (e.g., Kp/Kd gains, link inertia, friction, mass, motor strength) are randomized at each episode reset.
 - **Visual Perturbations:** Lightweight depth noise (simulating sensor artifacts) is injected into depth frames.
 - **Obstacle-Density Curriculum:** For obstacle-based environments, the density of obstacles is gradually increased in a linear schedule during training, starting from easier settings and progressing to more challenging ones to stabilize on-policy learning.
 - **Hyperparameters:** Standard PPO hyperparameters (discount factor, GAE parameter, clip parameter, learning rates, etc.) are used, along with advantage normalization and gradient clipping for stable optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LocoMamba addresses four key research questions (RQs) and presents comprehensive comparisons and ablations.

#### 4.1. RQ1 (SOTA performance) & RQ4 (Training robustness)

- **Superior Performance:** LocoMamba significantly outperforms all baselines, including Transformer-based cross-modal fusion and proprioception-only controllers, on trained terrains (Thin Obstacle environment). It achieved 48.9% higher returns, 48.9% fewer collisions, and 30.4% longer progress distance compared to the Transformer Proprio-Vision baseline.
- **Faster Convergence and Stability:** LocoMamba demonstrated faster convergence (steeper early learning slope), higher asymptotic return, and lower variance during training (Fig. 4). Its training stability was further corroborated by having the lowest Coefficient of Variation (CoV) for both value-function loss (0.215) and estimated advantages (0.708) over the last 200 epochs, indicating more reliable optimization compared to other methods (Fig. 5).

#### 4.2. RQ2 (Cross-modal effectiveness)

- **Importance of Cross-Modal Fusion:** Ablation studies clearly showed that combining proprioception and depth inputs is crucial. Proprio-Only and Vision-Only variants (both Transformer and Mamba-based) performed poorly, highlighting the necessity of both immediate state estimation (proprioception) and foresight (depth) for effective obstacle negotiation. Mamba Vision-Only, in particular, struggled more than Transformer Vision-Only, suggesting that Mamba's selective recurrence benefits significantly from proprioceptive grounding to prevent state drift under partial observability.

#### 4.3. RQ3 (Mamba SSM efficiency)

- **Efficiency Gains:** LocoMamba's Mamba-based backbone demonstrated superior computational and memory efficiency. It achieved the highest throughput (104.68 samples/s) and a lower allocated memory footprint (207.2 MB) compared to the Transformer Proprio-Vision baseline (98.87 samples/s, 226.4 MB), despite having comparable FLOPs per step. This confirms that Mamba's architectural optimizations lead to better runtime efficiency.
- **Improved Learning Dynamics:** The Mamba backbone also led to a higher final reward, steeper early-learning slope (75% increase over Transformer), higher overall learning efficiency, and a larger area under the learning curve per epoch. This indicates that Mamba's near-linear scaling and causal memory enable faster and more effective learning within the same compute budget.
- **Token Resolution Trade-off:** An ablation study on visual token count (32, 64, 256) showed that increasing tokens improved perception accuracy on trained terrains but increased memory usage and degraded generalization on unseen rugged terrains. The 64-token configuration was identified as the optimal balance between spatial fidelity, computational efficiency, and cross-domain robustness.

#### 4.4. Generalization to Unseen Conditions

- **Robust Zero-Shot Transfer:** Policies trained solely in the Thin Obstacle environment were evaluated zero-shot on two challenging, previously unseen scenarios: Rugged Terrain and Dynamic Obstacle terrain. LocoMamba consistently maintained its advantage:
 - **Rugged Terrain:** Achieved 126.8% higher return, 20.8% fewer collisions, and 46.1% longer distance compared to Transformer Proprio-Vision.
 - **Dynamic Obstacles:** Showed approximately 9% higher return, roughly two-thirds fewer collisions, and about 15% longer distance than Transformer Proprio-Vision.
- These results highlight the strong generalization capabilities of LocoMamba, attributing it to the effective cross-modal fusion via the Mamba backbone and the robust PPO training protocol.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LocoMamba framework represents a significant advancement in vision-driven DRL for quadrupedal locomotion, with several key significances and potential impacts:

1. **Revolutionizing Cross-Modal Fusion in Robotics:** By successfully integrating the Mamba selective state-space model, LocoMamba demonstrates a viable alternative to Transformers and RNNs for real-time robotic control. Mamba's near-linear scaling addresses the long-standing computational bottlenecks of Transformer architectures, making high-resolution visual inputs and longer temporal dependencies practical for embedded robotic systems. This could lead to a broader adoption of Mamba-like architectures in various robotic perception and control tasks.
2. **Enabling More Capable and Autonomous Robots:** The superior performance in terms of higher returns, fewer collisions, and longer distances, coupled with strong generalization to unseen and dynamic environments, signifies a step towards more intelligent and autonomous quadrupedal robots. These robots can navigate complex terrains and dynamic obstacle fields more effectively, expanding their potential applications in areas like inspection, disaster response, logistics, and planetary exploration.
3. **Improving DRL Training Efficiency and Iteration Speed:** The faster convergence and improved sample efficiency demonstrated by LocoMamba reduce the time and computational resources required for training complex DRL policies. This is crucial for rapid prototyping, iteration, and deployment of learning algorithms in robotics, where training often involves extensive simulation.
4. **Strengthening End-to-End Learning:** The robust end-to-end PPO training scheme, incorporating domain randomization, curriculum learning, and a balanced state-centric reward, proves effective in achieving stable learning and balanced performance trade-offs. This holistic approach to DRL design provides a valuable blueprint for developing future robust robotic policies.
5. **Bridging Research and Application:** The collaboration between Woven by Toyota and Trinity College Dublin underscores the practical relevance of this work. The focus on efficiency, robustness, and generalizability aligns directly with the requirements for deploying advanced robotic systems in real-world industrial and commercial settings.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge current budgetary and hardware constraints, preventing real-world experiments. Future work aims to deploy LocoMamba on a physical quadruped platform to assess sim-to-real transfer, real-time latency, and safety under field conditions. Additionally, incorporating more realistic visual perturbations (e.g., occlusion, depth bias, motion blur) and modeling temporal latency/misalignment will further test the robustness of the Mamba-based fusion in real-world scenarios.

In conclusion, LocoMamba offers a compelling new paradigm for vision-driven robotic locomotion, leveraging the efficiency and long-range modeling capabilities of Mamba SSMs. Its demonstrated performance, efficiency, and generalization capabilities position it as a significant contribution to the field of robotic reinforcement learning, with the potential to accelerate the development and deployment of highly capable quadrupedal robots.
