# Panda-70M: Captioning 70M Videos with Multiple Cross-Modality Teachers

> [!summary] Summary
> Panda-70M is a 70.8M-clip video-caption dataset built by an automatic pipeline: semantics-aware splitting of 3.8M HD-VILA long videos into coherent clips, eight cross-modality teacher models generating candidate captions from frames, video, titles, descriptions and subtitles, and a human-finetuned retrieval model picking the best one. Pretraining on it improves captioning, retrieval and text-to-video generation.

## Problem

- Manual video captioning is prohibitively costly at scale, so existing hand-labelled video-text corpora stay under roughly 120K clips and cover narrow domains.
- ASR-annotated 100M-scale datasets substitute subtitles for captions, but speech rarely describes the main object or action, limiting multimodal training value.
- Prior automatic pipelines rely on a single captioner or an LLM summarizer that propagates errors from noisy vision-language outputs; long videos also mix multiple scenes.

## Method

- Two-stage ==semantics-aware splitting==: shot-boundary detection then ==ImageBind== embedding-similarity stitching of adjacent clips, with ==Max Running LPIPS== introduced to score a clip's semantic consistency.
- Eight cross-modality teachers (Video-LLaMA, VideoChat, VideoChat Text, three BLIP-2 variants, MiniGPT-4) caption each clip from video, single frames, titles, descriptions and subtitles.
- Fine-grained caption selection: ==Unmasked Teacher== retrieval finetuned on 100K human-picked captions with hard-negative mining; a two-branch student with a text ==Q-Former== distills the teachers.

## Results

- Video-LLaMA pretrained on the 2M subset raises zero-shot BLEU-4 from **5.8** to **23.5** on MSR-VTT and **12.7** to **31.2** on MSVD.
- UMT pretrained on the 5M subset lifts zero-shot text-to-video R@1 by **7.0** points on MSR-VTT; AnimateDiff cuts UCF101 FVD from **499.3** to **421.9**.
- Finetuned retrieval picks the human-chosen caption **35.90%** R@1 versus **21.82%** pretrained; the student reaches **18.4%** preference, beating every teacher's best **10.7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No single captioner is good on more than 31% of videos, but pooling diverse teachers yields at least one good caption for 84.7%.
> - Selecting among candidates with a finetuned retriever beats LLM summarization, which propagates errors from noisy vision-language outputs rather than filtering them.
> - A distilled student can outperform any individual teacher and approach the full ensemble, making expensive multi-model annotation pipelines cheap to replicate at scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal learning, particularly involving vision and language, has seen significant advancements, largely driven by large-scale computing, extensive models, and vast datasets. While substantial text corpora and image-text datasets are available, the collection of high-quality video-text data remains a challenge. This difficulty stems from several factors: manual annotation of videos is time-consuming, as annotators must watch entire videos; videos often contain dynamic, temporally varying content with multiple scenes and actions; and existing meta-information like subtitles or descriptions can be broad, misaligned, or imprecise.

Existing video-language datasets generally fall into two categories:
- **Large-scale datasets:** These often rely on automatic speech recognition (ASR) for annotation, such as HowTo100M (136M videos) and HD-VILA-100M (103M videos). While they offer scale, their captions frequently lack precision in describing the main visual content and actions, limiting their utility for fine-grained multimodal training.
- **High-quality datasets:** These are typically manually annotated, providing precise and rich captions. Examples include MSR-VTT (10K videos) and ActivityNet (100K videos). However, the manual annotation process is costly and consequently limits the scale of these datasets, usually to fewer than 120,000 samples.

This work addresses the gap between these two types of datasets by proposing an automatic method to generate a large-scale video-language dataset with high-quality, precise captions. It aligns with concurrent research efforts, such as VideoFactory, InternVid, and Stable Video Diffusion, which also leverage advanced vision-language models for video captioning. The paper aims to contribute a foundation dataset that combines the scale of ASR-annotated datasets with the descriptive quality often associated with manual annotations, thereby enhancing pretraining capabilities for various downstream vision-language tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to create a large-scale, high-quality video dataset with precise textual caption annotations, named Panda-70M. The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations in the existing landscape of video-language datasets:

- **Data Quality as a Limiting Factor:** The authors state that the quality of data and its annotations directly determines the upper bound for the performance of downstream models. Therefore, improving data quality is crucial for advancing multimodal learning.
- **Challenges of Video Data Collection:** Unlike text or image-text pairs, acquiring high-quality video-text data is particularly challenging. Manual annotation is prohibitively time-consuming and expensive due to the temporal nature of videos, which often feature multiple scenes and actions.
- **Limitations of Existing Large Datasets:** Current large-scale video datasets, such as HD-VILA-100M and HowTo100M, primarily rely on Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) for generating captions. These ASR-generated subtitles often fail to accurately or comprehensively describe the visual content and main actions within the videos. This imprecision diminishes their value for training models that require tight alignment between visual and linguistic modalities.
- **Inadequacy of Small-Scale High-Quality Datasets:** While manually annotated datasets offer high-quality, precise captions, their limited scale (typically under 120,000 samples) restricts their effectiveness for pre-training large foundation models that thrive on vast amounts of data.
- **Leveraging Multimodal Information:** The core insight motivating this work is that videos are frequently accompanied by rich multimodal information, including titles, descriptions, subtitles, and individual frames, in addition to the video content itself. The authors hypothesize that a single model cannot optimally leverage all this diverse information or produce consistently good captions across all video types. However, by combining outputs from multiple cross-modality models, a more comprehensive and accurate set of captions can be generated. An initial human evaluation supported this, showing no single model excelled for more than 31% of videos, but an ensemble could cover 84.7%.

In summary, the key objectives are to overcome the current trade-off between scale and quality in video-language datasets by developing an automated, efficient, and robust annotation pipeline that leverages diverse multimodal inputs to generate 70 million video clips with high-resolution, semantically coherent, and accurately descriptive captions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for constructing Panda-70M involves a multi-stage, automatic annotation pipeline designed to process a large corpus of videos and generate high-quality captions. The process begins with 3.8 million high-resolution long videos sourced from the publicly available HD-VILA-100M dataset and proceeds through three main steps, followed by the training of a student model.

#### 3.1. Semantics-aware Video Splitting

To ensure semantic consistency within each video segment, a two-stage splitting algorithm was developed. The goal was to balance semantic coherence with sufficient clip duration for meaningful motion content.

- **Stage 1: Initial Splitting based on Shot Boundary Detection:** Videos were initially segmented using PySceneDetect, a shot boundary detection tool. This step identifies points where significant visual changes indicate a new scene.
 - **Post-processing for Long Videos:** For clips longer than 5 seconds without detected cut-scenes or unedited footage, artificial scene cuts were introduced every 5 seconds.
 - **Redundancy and Transition Handling:** ImageBind embeddings of initial and final frames (10% and 90% marks) were used to identify and remove clips with overly dramatic semantic changes (L2 norm > 1.0), which often indicate complex transitions or abrupt content shifts.
- **Stage 2: Stitching based on Semantics Similarity:** Adjacent clips that were incorrectly separated but shared semantic content were merged. This was achieved by computing ImageBind feature similarity between the end frame of one clip and the beginning frame of the next. If the L2 norm of these features was below 0.6, the clips were concatenated.
- **Final Post-processing:**
 - Exclusion of clips shorter than 2 seconds or exhibiting minimal motion (L2 norm between start/end frames < 0.15).
 - Truncation of videos longer than 60 seconds to their first 60 seconds.
 - Removal of semantically redundant clips by comparing average ImageBind features (Euclidean distance > 0.3 from preceding clips) to enhance dataset diversity.
 - Trimming of the first and last 10% of each clip to remove unstable camera movements or transition effects.
This process resulted in 70.8 million semantically coherent video clips with an average duration of 8.477 seconds.

#### 3.2. Captioning with Cross-Modality Teachers

The core of the annotation process involved leveraging multiple existing vision-language models to generate a diverse set of candidate captions for each video clip.

- **Model Pool:** An initial pool of 31 captioning models was assembled, comprising various image/video visual-question-answering (VQA) and image captioning models. These included Video-LLaMA, VideoChat, VideoChat Text, Video-ChatGPT, BLIP-2, and MiniGPT-4, each with different pretraining weights and input configurations (vision, subtitles, metadata, or combinations thereof).
- **Teacher Model Selection:** To manage computational cost, a subset of eight well-performing teacher models was selected from the initial pool. This selection was based on a human evaluation study where annotators identified "every good caption" from the candidates generated by all 31 models for 1,000 randomly sampled videos. A greedy algorithm was then used to select the minimal set of models that collectively provided good captions for the widest range of videos.
- **Multimodal Input Strategy:** For VQA models, a structured prompt (e.g., "You are given some information about a video... Please faithfully summarize...") was used to integrate additional textual information such as video title, description, and subtitles alongside the visual input. For image captioning models (like BLIP-2), a randomly sampled video frame was used as input. This approach aimed to utilize the strengths of different models and modalities, hypothesizing that different models would excel at captioning different types of video content (e.g., video models for complex dynamics, image models for rare objects, VQA models for ambiguous visuals benefiting from textual clues).

#### 3.3. Fine-grained Video-to-Text Retrieval

With multiple candidate captions generated for each video clip, a mechanism was needed to select the most accurate one.

- **Problem with Generic Retrieval:** Standard video-to-text retrieval models, typically trained with contrastive learning to distinguish disparate samples, were found insufficient for this task, as all candidate captions were highly relevant, requiring fine-grained discernment.
- **Human Annotation for Finetuning:** A subset of 100,000 videos was manually annotated by human annotators tasked with selecting "the best caption" among the candidates, focusing on the most faithful and descriptive information.
- **Finetuning Unmasked Teacher (UMT):** The Unmasked Teacher (UMT) model, a video foundation model, was finetuned on this 100K human-annotated dataset. Hard negative mining was implemented for the contrastive loss, giving higher training weight to the seven unselected candidate captions (hard negatives) compared to random negatives.
- **Large-Scale Inference:** The finetuned UMT was then applied to the entire 70.8 million video dataset to automatically select the most precise caption for each video clip.

#### 3.4. Multimodal Student Captioning Model

To enable more efficient video captioning at a larger scale in the future, a student captioning model was trained using knowledge distillation from the multiple teacher models and the Panda-70M dataset.

- **Architecture:** The student model employed a two-branch architecture:
 - **Visual Branch:** Utilized the Video-LLaMA architecture, including a frozen pretrained visual encoder (EVA-CLIP ViT-G/14 and Q-Former) to extract frame-level features, followed by a temporal fusion module (position embedding, video Q-former) to aggregate into a video representation, and a linear projection layer.
 - **Text Branch:** Processed additional text inputs (metadata and subtitles) through a tokenizer, embedding layer, and a text Q-Former. This text Q-Former used the video representation as queries to extract a fixed-length text embedding, mitigating issues of long, noisy, or misaligned textual prompts.
 - **LLM Integration:** Both video and text representations were concatenated and fed into a large language model (LLM, Vicuna-7B) to generate the final caption.
- **Training Strategy:** The model was trained on Panda-70M, with gradients from the text branch blocked from propagating to the visual branch. Text inputs (metadata and subtitles) were randomly dropped during training (with a 0.5 probability) to enable the model to handle both video-only and video-text input scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented Panda-70M, a large-scale video dataset comprising 70.8 million video clips with corresponding captions. The dataset features high-resolution (720p) videos, an average clip duration of 8.5 seconds, and an average caption length of 13.2 words, making it an open-domain resource designed for improved semantic coherence compared to previous ASR-annotated datasets.

#### 4.1. Semantics-aware Video Splitting Performance

The proposed two-stage semantics-aware splitting algorithm demonstrated improved performance in balancing semantic consistency and video clip length.
- Quantitatively, it achieved a Max Running LPIPS score of 0.256, indicating better semantic consistency compared to subtitle alignment-based splitting (0.408).
- Simultaneously, it maintained a longer average video length of 7.9 seconds, which is more desirable than the 4.1 seconds resulting from vanilla shot boundary detection.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Multiple Cross-Modality Teachers

A human evaluation revealed that no single teacher model could generate a "good caption" for more than 31% of the videos, highlighting the limitations of individual models across diverse content. However, by combining outputs from the initial pool of 31 captioning models, at least one good caption could be identified for 84.7% of the videos. The refined set of 8 selected teacher models collectively achieved a good caption for 76.8% of videos, providing a balance between coverage and computational efficiency.

#### 4.3. Performance of Fine-grained Video-to-Text Retrieval

The finetuned Unmasked Teacher (UMT) model, tailored for fine-grained caption selection, showed a significant improvement over its pretrained counterpart.
- The finetuned UMT achieved 35.90% R@1 accuracy on the validation set for selecting the best caption, which was substantially higher than the 21.82% R@1 accuracy of a pretrained UMT.
- While human agreement for selecting a single "best" caption was 44.9% R@1 (indicating subjectivity when multiple captions are equally good), the finetuned UMT achieved 78.9% R@1 when considering any of three human-selected "good" captions.
- The selection distribution of the finetuned UMT for the final captions in Panda-70M aligned closely with human preferences. Over 89% of the selected captions in Panda-70M had a matching score above 0.43, indicating strong video-caption alignment.

#### 4.4. Downstream Task Performance with Panda-70M Pretraining

Pretraining models on Panda-70M (or its subsets) consistently led to improved performance across three evaluated downstream tasks:

- **Video Captioning:**
 - Video-LLaMA, when pretrained on Panda-2M (a 2.5M subset of Panda-70M), demonstrated substantial improvements over its official weights (pretrained on 2.5M videos + 595K images). For example, it showed a 17.7% improvement in BLEU-4 on MSR-VTT and 18.5% on MSVD. The generated captions were more relevant and less generic compared to the baseline.
 - The student model, trained on the complete Panda-70M, further enhanced captioning performance, achieving 25.4 BLEU-4 on MSR-VTT and 32.8 on MSVD.
 - A user study indicated that the student model outperformed any individual teacher model and achieved performance comparable to the "All Teachers" ensemble. The student model with both video and text inputs showed a 3.0% higher preference ratio compared to the video-only student, demonstrating the value of multimodal inputs.

- **Video and Text Retrieval:**
 - Unmasked Teacher (UMT) pretrained on Panda-5M (a 5M subset of Panda-70M) consistently outperformed the UMT with official weights (pretrained on 2.5M videos + 3M images) in both zero-shot and finetuned retrieval settings across MSR-VTT, DiDeMo, and MSVD benchmarks.
 - For instance, Panda-5M pretraining yielded a 7.0% R@1 lift for zero-shot text-to-video retrieval on MSR-VTT.
 - Notably, UMT with Panda-5M pretraining also surpassed several existing state-of-the-art methods (e.g., CLIP4Clip, X-CLIP, InternVideo), even those pretrained on significantly larger vision-text datasets (>100M).

- **Text-to-Video Generation:**
 - AnimateDiff, a text-to-video generation model, pretrained on Panda-2M consistently outperformed its officially released weight.
 - It achieved a lower Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of 421.9 on UCF101 (compared to 499.3 for the official weight), indicating higher quality and realism.
 - It also showed a slightly higher CLIP similarity (CLIPSim) on MSR-VTT (0.2880 vs. 0.2869), suggesting better alignment between generated videos and text prompts.
 - Qualitative results indicated that models pretrained on Panda-2M generated videos with more meaningful motion and photorealistic appearance, without watermarks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes Panda-70M, a large-scale video dataset with high-quality, automatically generated captions, addressing a critical bottleneck in multimodal learning: the scarcity of extensive, precisely annotated video-text data. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifold:

- **Advancement in Multimodal Learning:** Panda-70M fills a gap between small-scale, high-quality human-annotated datasets and large-scale, lower-quality ASR-annotated datasets. By providing 70 million semantically coherent video clips with rich, precise captions, it offers a robust resource for pretraining and developing more capable vision-language models. This can lead to improved understanding, generation, and retrieval capabilities across visual and textual modalities.
- **Scalable Data Annotation Paradigm:** The proposed automatic pipeline, leveraging multiple cross-modality teacher models and a fine-grained retrieval mechanism, demonstrates a scalable and efficient method for generating high-quality video annotations. This approach reduces the reliance on costly and time-consuming manual annotation, opening pathways for future dataset expansion and the creation of specialized multimodal datasets.
- **Improved Downstream Task Performance:** The empirical results consistently show that models pretrained with Panda-70M achieve substantial performance gains in diverse downstream tasks, including video captioning, video and text retrieval, and text-to-video generation. This validates the quality and utility of the dataset as a foundational resource for the community, enabling the development of more accurate, robust, and versatile vision-language applications.
- **Innovation in Model Architectures:** The development of a multimodal student captioning model, capable of distilling knowledge from multiple teachers and effectively integrating both visual and textual inputs, presents an efficient architecture for future large-scale video captioning tasks. Its ability to outperform individual teacher models and achieve comparable performance to their ensemble suggests a promising direction for designing more comprehensive multimodal models.
- **Resource for the Research Community:** Panda-70M provides an open-domain dataset characterized by high-resolution, semantically consistent, and watermark-free video content. Its release can foster further research and innovation in video understanding, generation, and retrieval, serving as a new benchmark for evaluating and developing next-generation multimodal AI systems.

Despite its contributions, the dataset has certain limitations, primarily related to its video source (vocal-intensive videos, e.g., news, tutorials) and its focus on fine-grained, semantically consistent clips, which might limit content diversity within single videos or overall average video duration. Future work could focus on expanding to unvocal videos and exploring datasets with longer videos and dense captions to support tasks like long video generation and dense video captioning. The authors also implemented risk mitigation strategies, including filtering for harmful or violent content and replacing personal names, to promote responsible AI development.
