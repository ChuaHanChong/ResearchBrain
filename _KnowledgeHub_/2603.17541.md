# Temporal Gains, Spatial Costs: Revisiting Video Fine-Tuning in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at SJTU, HKUST, and other institutions uncovered a "temporal trap" in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) where video supervised fine-tuning (SFT) often degrades static image understanding despite improving video performance. They introduce an instruction-aware Hybrid-Frame Strategy that adaptively allocates frames, mitigating this trade-off and improving image benchmark accuracy while maintaining strong video gains.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) widely use Video-SFT to enhance video understanding, but the assumption that this universally benefits or preserves static image capabilities is largely unexamined.
- The precise impact of Video-SFT on a model's broader visual skills, especially static image comprehension, remained poorly understood.
- A lack of systematic investigation into how progress in video understanding transfers to image understanding within unified visual frameworks.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic experimental design across various MLLM architectures (e.g., ==Qwen2.5-VL==, ==LLaVA-NeXT-Video==), model scales (**3B** to **72B**), and frame sampling settings (**8** to **64** frames) to analyze Video-SFT's impact on both image and video capabilities.
- Developed a theoretical framework explaining image degradation during ==Video-SFT==, attributing it to ==negative gradient alignment== between video and image objectives, and the increasing influence of ==temporally specialized updates== with larger temporal budgets.
- Proposed and evaluated an ==instruction-aware Hybrid-Frame Strategy== that adaptively allocates frames during Video-SFT based on ==task-relevant spatiotemporal demands== to reduce redundant temporal exposure.

## Results

- Video-SFT reliably improved video benchmark performance (e.g., Video-MME, MVBench) but consistently yielded limited gains or degradation on static image benchmarks (e.g., MME, POPE) across diverse MLLMs and frame settings.
- The instruction-aware Hybrid-Frame Strategy (using a Qwen3-VL-8B predictor) improved image benchmark accuracy on MMStar and POPE for Qwen2.5-VL-7B compared to fixed, larger frame budgets, while maintaining strong video performance gains.
- The Hybrid-Frame strategy also demonstrated robustness in video performance under fixed 8-frame inference constraints, outperforming models trained with higher fixed frame counts that often showed performance drops under downsampled inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video-SFT consistently leads to a "temporal trap," where improvements in video understanding often result in limited gains or degradation in static image understanding, indicating an intrinsic conflict.
> - The temporal budget (number of sampled frames) during SFT significantly influences this trade-off; increasing it can shift gradient alignment from cooperative to conflicting, exacerbating spatial degradation.
> - Larger model scales (e.g., 72B parameters) exhibit greater stability and partially mitigate the negative effects of the temporal trap on image understanding, though the phenomenon persists.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have significantly broadened visual understanding, extending capabilities from static images to dynamic video content. Current MLLMs are increasingly designed as unified language-vision frameworks, aiming to process both images and videos within a shared visual space using common visual encoders and unified alignment mechanisms. Models such as Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OneVision, Gemini 2.5, and Kimi K2.5 exemplify this trend, demonstrating strong performance across diverse tasks including captioning, visual question answering, and video reasoning.

Video-based supervised fine-tuning (Video-SFT) has become a widely adopted strategy to enhance video understanding in these MLLMs. A prevailing assumption in the field is that Video-SFT not only strengthens temporal modeling but also broadly benefits unified visual learning. This implies that improvements in video understanding should, at a minimum, preserve or ideally enhance a model's capabilities on static image tasks. However, the systematic examination of this assumption has been limited, leaving the transferability of video understanding progress to image understanding unclear.

This research positions itself by critically investigating this underlying assumption. It connects to broader challenges in MLLM post-training, where continual tuning can lead to gradient conflicts, negative transfer, and catastrophic forgetting—phenomena previously observed between text and vision modalities. This paper extends this inquiry to conflicts between image and video modalities within a unified visual framework, an area that has been underexplored. By systematically analyzing the effects of Video-SFT on the balance between spatial and temporal understanding, the work addresses a gap in the understanding of how visual capabilities evolve during this crucial fine-tuning stage.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate how Video-SFT reshapes the visual capabilities of MLLMs. Specifically, the authors aim to understand the fine-grained evolution of these capabilities, with a particular focus on the balance between spatial (image) and temporal (video) understanding.

The motivation for this study stems from a critical observation: despite the widespread adoption of Video-SFT as a post-training strategy to improve video understanding, its precise impact on a model's broader visual skills, especially its static image comprehension, remains poorly understood. The common assumption is that enhancing video understanding would either maintain or improve image understanding due to videos being sequences of images and the shared visual encoder. However, without systematic analysis, this assumption lacks empirical validation.

The researchers hypothesize that this assumption might not universally hold. They propose that video-centric fine-tuning, while beneficial for temporal tasks, could potentially lead to a trade-off or even degradation in spatial reasoning abilities. They term this potential conflict the "temporal trap."

The specific contributions outlined by the authors are threefold:
1. **Systematic Study:** To conduct a comprehensive investigation into how Video-SFT influences both image and video capabilities in MLLMs.
2. **Identification of Trade-off:** To identify and characterize a consistent image-video trade-off under Video-SFT, termed the "temporal trap," and to explore its relationship with the temporal budget (number of sampled frames).
3. **Theoretical Account and Mitigation Strategy:** To provide a theoretical analysis explaining this trade-off and to demonstrate that an adaptive frame allocation strategy can partially alleviate the observed negative effects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study adopted a systematic experimental design coupled with a theoretical analysis to investigate the impact of Video-SFT on MLLM visual capabilities.

**Problem Setting:**
The research specifically focused on MLLMs undergoing Video-SFT, aiming to analyze its effects on two core visual abilities: image understanding and video understanding. The authors posited that while improvements in video understanding during Video-SFT are expected, the concurrent impact on image understanding, given that videos are sequences of images, is the central question. The observed degradation in image understanding concurrently with improved video understanding is termed the "temporal trap."

**Study Dimensions:**
Experiments were structured along three key dimensions to ensure a comprehensive analysis:
- **Model Architecture:** Representative MLLMs were evaluated, including Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-NeXT-Video, and LLaVA-1.5. This allowed for assessing whether the observed phenomena are architecture-specific or more general.
- **Model Scale:** Four different parameter scales of Qwen2.5-VL (3B, 7B, 32B, and 72B parameters) were tested to explore the influence of model capacity on the image-video trade-off.
- **Frame Sampling Setting:** Videos were uniformly sampled with varying numbers of frames (8, 16, 32, and 64 frames) during Video-SFT. This dimension aimed to quantify the effect of temporal budget on both image and video performance.

**Datasets and Evaluation:**
- **Training Dataset:** A curated training dataset of 20,000 videos was used, drawn from 10 different sources within the LLaVA-Next-Video-178k dataset. This dataset incorporated diverse instruction formats, including textual descriptions, open-ended questions, and multiple-choice questions, to ensure broad data diversity.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** A comprehensive set of commonly used benchmarks for MLLMs was selected to evaluate core visual abilities:
 - **Image Benchmarks:** MME, MM-Star, MMBench, and POPE, covering aspects like perception (coarse-grained, fine-grained, OCR), cognition (reasoning), and hallucination.
 - **Video Benchmarks:** Video-MME, MVBench, TempCompass, and Video-MMMU, designed to assess various aspects of video analysis.

**Theoretical Analysis:**
A conservative theoretical framework was developed to provide a local sufficient condition for the observed image degradation.
- **Preliminaries:** The analysis defined population objectives for image loss ($L_{img}(\theta)$) and video loss ($L_{vid}^{(m)}(\theta)$) as functions of trainable parameters ($\theta$) and temporal budget ($m$). Gradient alignment was defined as the inner product of gradients.
- **First-Order Condition:** Proposition 1 established that if the gradient of the video objective ($g_{vid}^{(m)}$) is negatively aligned with the gradient of the image objective ($g_{img}$), then a single Video-SFT step can increase the image loss. This highlights a mechanism for negative transfer in shared-parameter learning.
- **Temporal Budget as Gradient Bias Source:** The video gradient was decomposed into a shared visual component ($g_{sh}$), a temporally specialized component ($g_{tmp}$), and a residual term ($\epsilon^{(m)}$). Assumptions were made about their average-case alignment: positive for $g_{sh}$ with $g_{img}$, non-positive for $g_{tmp}$ with $g_{img}$, and unbiased for $\epsilon^{(m)}$. Proposition 2 formalized how increasing temporal budget ($m$) could shift the expected transfer from cooperative to conflicting, by strengthening the influence of the temporally specialized component.
- **Justification for Adaptive Frame Allocation:** An assumption was made about the existence of a sample-wise minimal sufficient temporal budget ($m_{min}(V, Q)$). Proposition 3 demonstrated that choosing $m(v, q) = m_{min}(v, q)$ minimizes a smoothness-based upper bound on the image loss increase, thereby providing theoretical justification for adaptive frame allocation to reduce redundant temporal exposure.

**Hybrid-Frame Strategy:**
Motivated by the theoretical findings, an instruction-aware Hybrid-Frame Strategy was proposed and evaluated.
- **Principle:** Allocate sufficient frames to capture task-relevant temporal evidence while avoiding redundant temporal exposure.
- **Implementation:** Three frame allocation schemes were compared: a DINOv2-based strategy (using inter-frame similarity) and two VLM-based predictors (built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen3-VL-8B). The VLM-based predictors employed a prompt strategy to assess instructions based on five dimensions: event duration, motion continuity, causal relations, object interactions, and fine-grained visual attributes, to determine optimal frame counts (8, 16, 32, or 64).
- **Evaluation:** The best-performing Hybrid-Frame strategy (Qwen3-VL-8B-based) was applied to Video-SFT for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B, and its performance was compared against fixed frame budget settings on both image and video benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic analysis revealed a consistent pattern across architectural designs, parameter scales, and frame sampling settings, indicating a fundamental trade-off.

**The Temporal Trap:**
The central finding is the consistent "temporal trap" phenomenon. Video-SFT reliably improved performance on video benchmarks but frequently yielded limited gains or even degradation on static image benchmarks. This indicates an intrinsic conflict between temporal adaptation and spatial visual reasoning within unified MLLMs.

**Impact of Model Architecture:**
- Across all evaluated architectures (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT-Video, Qwen2.5-VL), Video-SFT consistently enhanced video performance while most image benchmarks showed degradation.
- The magnitude of this conflict varied; LLaVA-1.5 exhibited the largest performance drop on image benchmarks, while Qwen2.5-VL showed comparative stability, suggesting that stronger spatial-temporal alignment and mixed image-video pre-training could partially mitigate the conflict. However, the temporal trap persisted across all architectures.

**Impact of Model Size:**
- Increasing model scale was found to partially mitigate the negative effect of Video-SFT on image understanding, though this mitigation was not strictly monotonic.
- For models from 3B to 32B, image benchmark performance after Video-SFT showed noticeable fluctuations.
- The 72B Qwen2.5-VL model exhibited the most stable behavior, with post-SFT image performance becoming comparable to or slightly better than the base model on most image benchmarks.
- Attention visualizations corroborated this, showing that larger models (e.g., 72B) maintained more localized focus on target objects after Video-SFT, in contrast to smaller models where attention became more dispersed.

**Impact of Fine-tuning Frame Count:**
- Increasing the number of training frames consistently improved video benchmark performance, confirming the importance of temporal information. However, these gains showed saturation, indicating diminishing returns from additional temporal input.
- For image benchmarks, the performance on MME consistently underperformed the base model across all frame settings after Video-SFT. MMStar showed gradual improvement with more frames but slowed at higher counts. MMBench and POPE exhibited an initial increase followed by a decrease as frame counts rose.
- These results suggested that redundant temporal information during Video-SFT could disrupt the model’s static visual representations, contributing to the temporal trap. Furthermore, an experiment in the appendix showed that even when static images were duplicated to match video input length, performance degradation persisted and worsened with more replicated frames, reinforcing that the issue lies in the SFT process itself rather than just a modality mismatch during inference.

**Theoretical Analysis Outcomes:**
- Proposition 1 provided a local sufficient condition: negative alignment between the video gradient and the image gradient in shared parameter space could cause the image loss to increase during Video-SFT.
- Proposition 2 explained how increasing the temporal budget could shift the expected gradient alignment from cooperative to conflicting by amplifying the influence of temporally specialized updates.
- Proposition 3 justified adaptive frame allocation, demonstrating that selecting a sample-wise minimal sufficient temporal budget could minimize the upper bound on image loss increase, thereby avoiding redundancy.

**Hybrid-Frame Strategy Results:**
- Instruction-aware VLM-based predictors (Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen3-VL-8B) for adaptive frame allocation consistently outperformed a DINOv2-based method.
- The Qwen3-VL-8B-based Hybrid-Frame Strategy, when applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, achieved improved accuracy on image benchmarks (MMStar and POPE) compared to models trained with fixed, larger frame budgets (e.g., 32 or 64 frames), while maintaining strong gains on video performance.
- The strategy's effectiveness extended to LLaVA-1.5-7B across different Video-SFT budgets, indicating its broad applicability beyond a specific architecture.
- When evaluating video performance under a fixed 8-frame inference constraint, the Hybrid-Frame strategy maintained high accuracy rates, demonstrating its robustness compared to models trained with higher fixed frame counts, which often showed performance drops under downsampled inference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research significantly contributes to the understanding of Multimodal Large Language Models by challenging a prevalent assumption regarding Video-SFT. It empirically demonstrates that enhancing temporal understanding in MLLMs through Video-SFT does not reliably translate to improved or even preserved spatial understanding. Instead, a consistent "temporal trap" is identified, where gains in video performance are often accompanied by limited improvements or degradation in static image capabilities. This finding highlights a critical limitation in current unified training pipelines, suggesting that they have not yet achieved true image-video synergy.

The theoretical analysis provides a principled explanation for this observed trade-off. By formalizing how negatively aligned gradients and the increasing influence of temporally specialized updates with larger frame budgets can lead to spatial degradation, the work offers a deeper insight into the underlying mechanisms of shared-parameter optimization in multimodal contexts. This theoretical foundation is valuable for diagnosing and potentially predicting such conflicts in future MLLM architectures and training schemes.

The proposal and empirical validation of the instruction-aware Hybrid-Frame Strategy offer a practical intervention to mitigate the temporal trap. By adaptively allocating frame counts based on the spatiotemporal demands of individual instructions, this strategy demonstrates that unnecessary temporal exposure can be reduced, leading to a better balance between image and video performance without sacrificing video understanding. This approach not only improves model robustness but also reduces redundant computational costs during training.

The potential impact of this research is multifaceted:
- **Rethinking MLLM Training Paradigms:** It urges researchers and developers to reconsider the simplistic assumption that video training automatically benefits image understanding. Future MLLM training pipelines may need more sophisticated mechanisms to reconcile conflicting gradients and ensure robust performance across diverse visual tasks.
- **Guiding Architectural Design:** The findings, particularly the observation that larger models exhibit greater stability against the temporal trap, can inform the design of MLLM architectures that are more robust to inter-modality conflicts.
- **Enhancing Efficiency and Performance:** The Hybrid-Frame Strategy provides a blueprint for developing more efficient training methodologies. By avoiding redundant frame processing, it can lead to faster training times and reduced computational footprints, while simultaneously improving the quality of the resulting models.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations (e.g., the heuristic nature of Hybrid-Frame, focus on standard benchmarks) also open new avenues for research, such as developing more principled, continuous, and visually-aware adaptive frame allocation methods, exploring streaming/online training scenarios, and achieving truly universal multimodal training paradigms.

In conclusion, this paper delivers a critical empirical analysis and a theoretically grounded understanding of the image-video trade-off in MLLMs undergoing Video-SFT. It highlights a central challenge for joint image-video training and proposes a practical solution, thereby advancing the field towards more robust and truly synergistic multimodal AI systems.
