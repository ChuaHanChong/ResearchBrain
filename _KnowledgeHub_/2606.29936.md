# OpenSPM: An Environment-Transferable Robotic Key Spatial Pose Memory and Closed-Loop High-Frequency Flow-Matching Action Generation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenSPM introduces a robotic manipulation model that uses a decompositional approach, combining 3D perception, a key spatial pose memory, and a flow-matching action generator for robust, efficient, and environment-transferable control. It achieves an 85.6% success rate on tabletop tasks with significantly higher control frequency (1033.3 Hz) and fewer parameters (0.24M) compared to large-scale VLA models.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face limitations in few-shot learning, computational efficiency, and high-frequency control for open-environment robotic manipulation.
- End-to-end VLA policies often lack explicit object-centric geometric constraints, leading to unstable execution in tasks requiring precise pose alignment or physical contact.
- Accumulation of trajectory errors over long-horizon tasks can lead to task failures.

## Method

- A decompositional architecture separates semantic understanding and experience retrieval from geometrically constrained action generation.
- A ==key spatial pose memory== stores transferable, object-centric relative poses defining critical manipulation phase boundaries from demonstrations.
- A lightweight conditional ==flow-matching== model generates high-frequency, short-horizon action chunks between retrieved key spatial poses.
- ==Closed-loop control== with real-time proprioceptive state feedback and ==terminal residual correction== mitigates error accumulation during execution.

## Results

- OpenSPM achieved an **85.6%** task success rate on the LIBERO-GOAL benchmark, outperforming several large-scale VLA models.
- The model demonstrated high efficiency with **0.24M** parameters, **4.8 ms** inference latency, and an equivalent action output frequency of **1033.3 Hz**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the structured key spatial pose memory (dropping success to **23.8%**) and closed-loop execution with terminal residual correction (increasing success from **3.0%** to **85.6%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the complex Vision-Language-Action problem into specialized modules significantly improves efficiency and geometric precision for robotic manipulation.
> - Storing manipulation knowledge as object-centric relative "key spatial poses" enables robust environment transferability and offers interpretable priors for interaction.
> - Combining a lightweight flow-matching model for high-frequency local action generation with discrete geometric priors provides effective and efficient control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation in open environments requires systems capable of semantic understanding, precise geometric pose estimation, and the generation of high-frequency actions. Contemporary Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as RT-1, RT-2, Open X-Embodiment/RT-X, OpenVLA, Octo, and π0, have demonstrated progress in semantic generalization by learning end-to-end mappings from visual input and language instructions to actions. These models often leverage large-scale visual and vision-language pretraining (e.g., CLIP, ViT, DINOv2, SigLIP).

However, current VLA approaches frequently encounter limitations. Their deployment in settings with few-shot learning requirements, constrained computational resources, and high-frequency control demands remains challenging. Large-scale VLA policies typically necessitate extensive robot data and substantial training infrastructure. Furthermore, their end-to-end action outputs often lack explicit object-centric geometric constraints, which can lead to unstable execution, particularly in manipulation phases that require precise pose alignment or physical contact.

This paper positions its work as a decompositional approach to address these limitations. Instead of a monolithic end-to-end VLA system, OpenSPM proposes separating high-level semantic parsing and experience retrieval from low-level geometrically constrained action generation. The work integrates concepts from several established areas:
- **3D Perception and Spatial Manipulation Representations:** It utilizes advances in promptable segmentation (SAM, SAM 2) and object-level 3D reconstruction (SAM 3D) to establish robust 6D object poses and coordinate frames, which are crucial for precise manipulation. This contrasts with purely 2D vision-based approaches.
- **Demonstration Memory and Keyframe-Based Learning:** The system draws inspiration from methods that reduce long demonstrations into compact, interpretable structures, such as Transporter Networks and retrieval-based robotic systems (e.g., LIBERO). OpenSPM distinguishes itself by storing phase-labeled end-effector poses *relative* to object frames, rather than replaying entire trajectories, emphasizing transferability and semantic retrieval.
- **Diffusion and Flow-Matching Policies:** While diffusion models (Diffuser, Diffusion Policy) have been applied to planning and visuomotor control for modeling multimodal action distributions, they often involve iterative denoising. OpenSPM employs flow matching for action generation, a technique known for its simpler training objective and efficient sampling, but applies it specifically for short-horizon action completion between discrete key poses, rather than end-to-end.

By adopting this decompositional strategy, OpenSPM aims to bridge the gap between abstract task goals expressed in language and the continuous, physically feasible motor behaviors required for fine-grained robotic manipulation, while striving for efficiency and robustness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing OpenSPM are multifaceted, addressing specific limitations observed in current robotic manipulation systems, particularly end-to-end VLA models:

1. **Bridge the Gap between High-Level Semantics and Low-Level Physical Execution:** End-to-end VLA models often provide strong semantic understanding and generalization but struggle with the precise geometric constraints necessary for contact-rich manipulation tasks (e.g., grasping, lifting, placing). The motivation is to create an architecture that leverages semantic understanding for task planning while ensuring high geometric fidelity for execution.

2. **Enhance Geometric Precision and Stability in Fine-Grained Manipulation:** Existing VLA policies can exhibit instability in phases requiring accurate object-relative pose alignment. A key objective is to provide explicit geometric guarantees. This is addressed by using "key spatial poses" as an intermediate representation, marking critical manipulation phase boundaries (e.g., approach, grasp, lift, release). Representing these as relative poses between the end effector and the target object frame is intended to preserve local interaction geometry, making them transferable.

3. **Improve Environment Transferability:** The ability of robots to adapt to new scenes with different object arrangements and initial conditions is crucial for open-environment deployment. The motivation is to enable learned manipulation skills to be transferred effectively across varying scenes without requiring extensive re-training. This is facilitated by storing object-centric relative poses in memory, which can be adapted using SE(3) transformations based on the estimated poses of objects in a new scene.

4. **Reduce Computational Cost and Data Requirements:** Large-scale VLA models often demand significant computational resources for training and inference, along with vast datasets. OpenSPM aims for a lightweight and efficient system that can operate effectively in few-shot, low-compute settings, while still achieving high control frequencies. This is a central motivation for its modular design and the use of a lightweight flow-matching model for action generation.

5. **Suppress Trajectory Error Accumulation:** In continuous robotic execution, small errors in action generation can accumulate over time, leading to significant deviations and task failures, particularly over long-horizon tasks. The objective is to implement closed-loop control mechanisms to mitigate this error accumulation. This includes real-time proprioceptive state feedback and terminal residual correction, especially around critical interaction points.

6. **Develop a Structured, Interpretable Memory System:** Direct replay of full demonstration trajectories can be redundant, prone to temporal drift, and overfit to specific scenes. The motivation is to create a structured "spatial persistent memory" that compresses demonstrations into concise, phase-labeled, and semantically retrievable geometric priors, enhancing interpretability and reusability.

In summary, the motivation for OpenSPM is to develop a robust, efficient, and transferable robotic manipulation system by decomposing the problem into manageable, geometrically-aware components, thereby overcoming the limitations of purely end-to-end VLA models in scenarios demanding precision, efficiency, and adaptability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OpenSPM operates through an overall framework that comprises an offline memory construction phase and an online retrieval-execution phase. The system takes a natural language instruction, multi-view image sequences, robot proprioceptive state, and gripper state as input to generate a sequence of operational-space actions (translational and rotational increments of the end effector, and gripper commands).

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

- **Offline Phase:** The system extracts object-level and end-effector 6D poses from human demonstrations. Key spatial poses, representing phase boundaries of manipulation, are identified and stored as transferable, object-centric relative poses within a memory bank.
- **Online Phase:** Based on natural language instructions, relevant entries are retrieved from the spatial persistent memory. These key spatial poses are then transferred to the current scene using estimated object poses via SE(3) transformations. A conditional flow-matching model generates short-horizon action chunks between adjacent transferred key poses. Real-time state feedback and terminal residual correction are employed to manage error accumulation.

#### 3.2. Semantically Conditioned 3D Perception and Kalman State Estimation

- **Objective:** To provide continuous, scale-consistent 6D pose representations for objects, which are crucial for reliable cross-scene transfer and robust state estimation.
- **Pipeline:** This module is designed as a cascaded structure:
 1. **Semantic Segmentation:** Multi-view images (`I_v^t`) and text prompts (`q`) are processed by a segmentation model (e.g., Qwen, SAM 3) to output target masks, bounding boxes, and confidence scores.
 2. **3D Reconstruction:** Known intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters are used to lift 2D semantic observations into point-set constraints in the world coordinate frame. For single-view observations, object-level 3D reconstruction (e.g., SAM 3D) provides local shape and principal-axis directions.
 3. **Kalman Filtering:** To mitigate the effects of visual observation jitter, pose estimation is modeled as a noisy state-space problem. A Kalman filter is used to predict and update continuous 6D states for target objects and the end effector. The state vector includes translation, linear velocity, local orientation error, and angular velocity. Observation covariance is dynamically determined by segmentation confidence, mask area, multi-view consistency, and 3D reconstruction quality.
 4. **Smoothing:** A lightweight smoothing (linear interpolation for translation, slerp for orientation) is applied to the Kalman filter outputs to ensure continuity.
- **Purpose:** The 3D perception establishes a consistent geometric foundation during demonstration analysis and online initialization, while the Kalman filter maintains pose continuity during execution with low computational overhead.

#### 3.3. Construction of the Key Spatial Pose Memory Bank

- **Key Spatial Pose Definition:** A key spatial pose is defined as a discrete pose node that marks a manipulation phase boundary and geometrically dictates interaction success.
- **Extraction:** Candidate key spatial poses are identified using three types of evidence from demonstrations: gripper state transitions (open/close), significant changes in end-effector velocity or orientation, and phase-semantic judgments made by multimodal models (e.g., Qwen3-VL) analyzing multi-view images and trajectory data.
- **Memory Entry Structure:** Each entry `M_i` in the memory bank includes:
 - `k_i`: A retrieval key (e.g., operation type, target object, placement object, phase structure).
 - `l_i`: The original natural language description.
 - `∆T_o→e_i,j`: The relative pose of the end effector with respect to an anchored object (e.g., the target object for grasping, the placement target for placing) at the *j*-th key spatial pose. This relative pose (`(w_T_o)^-1 * w_T_e,j`) is central to transferability.
 - `c_i,j`: The phase label (e.g., "approach," "grasp," "lift").
 - `γ_i,j`: Confidence in the key spatial pose.
 - `r_i` and `ν_i`: Entry quality score and version information.
- **Online Retrieval:** During inference, structured task elements are parsed from the language instruction. The best-matching memory entry is selected based on a weighted sum of semantic key matching, consistency with current scene geometry, and historical success rate/quality score.

#### 3.4. Relative Pose Transfer

- **Mechanism:** Given a stored relative pose `∆T_o→e_j` from a demonstration scene (where `w_T_o` was the object pose and `w_T_e,j` was the end-effector pose), and the perception module's estimate of the target object's new pose `w_T_o'` in the test scene, the transferred world-coordinate key spatial pose `w_T_e',j` is computed as `w_T_o' * ∆T_o→e_j`.
- **Principle:** This process maintains the local interaction geometry between the end effector and the target object, making the memory entries transferable. A formal proof (Theorem 1) confirms that the local pose of the end effector relative to the target object remains unchanged after this transformation.
- **Feasibility Checks:** After transfer, the system performs reachability checks, pose-jump detection, and minimum-distance verification to ensure the sequence of key spatial poses is feasible and free of discontinuities in the new scene.

#### 3.5. Conditional Flow-Matching Action Generation

- **Purpose:** To generate continuous action chunks between the discrete, transferred key spatial poses.
- **Model Input Condition (`c_t`):** The model is conditioned on the current actual gripper pose, the next target key spatial pose, their relative pose error, and the progress within the current keyframe segment (`τ`).
- **Training:** The model learns a conditional velocity field `v_θ`. It is trained using mean squared error to map Gaussian noise (`a_0`) to a real action chunk (`a_data`) along a linear interpolation path. Training samples are derived from demonstration trajectories, divided into fixed-length (H=5) action chunks bounded by key spatial poses.
- **Inference:** Starting from a noise vector, the system integrates along the learned velocity field using ODE integration to predict the action chunk (`a_1`).
- **Architecture:** A lightweight model (approximately 240K parameters) with a conditional encoder that processes global conditions into a latent vector, and a shared step-wise decoder that predicts velocities. This design aims for high-frequency invocation and efficiency.

#### 3.6. Closed-Loop Execution and Terminal Residual Correction

- **Addressing Error Accumulation:** Open-loop action generation can lead to centimeter-level errors accumulating across segments.
- **Inter-segment Closed-Loop Control:** For each new action chunk, the flow-matching model's condition is reconstructed using the real-time gripper pose. This ensures that actions are generated based on the actual current state, rather than an idealized planned state.
- **Terminal Residual Correction (TRC):** Near critical grasping and placement nodes, the current gripper position is compared to the target key spatial pose. If the residual error exceeds a threshold, the system re-evaluates the condition and regenerates corrective action chunks iteratively until the error converges or a maximum iteration limit is reached. This is crucial for precise physical contact establishment.

#### 3.7. Complexity and Error Propagation Analysis

The paper analyzes the online computational complexity of its components: perception initialization (C_perc), memory retrieval (O(Dd) for linear scan, potentially faster with vector indexing), key spatial pose transfer (O(K)), and action generation (O(N_ode * H * P_θ), where P_θ is the model parameter scale). It also models error propagation, showing how OpenSPM's closed-loop mechanisms (reconstructing conditions and TRC) aim to mitigate the accumulation of errors compared to open-loop execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of OpenSPM was conducted on ten language-conditioned tabletop manipulation tasks from the LIBERO-GOAL benchmark, using 50 initial states per task for a total of 500 evaluation episodes. The primary metrics included task success rate, single action chunk generation latency, equivalent action output frequency, and average execution steps.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Comparison

- **Success Rate:** OpenSPM achieved an 85.6% success rate, which surpassed several representative baselines: Diffusion Policy (68.3%), TraceVLA (75.1%), SpatialVLA (78.6%), OpenVLA (79.2%), WorldVLA (83.4%), and Octo-Base (84.6%).
- **Efficiency:**
 - **Parameters:** OpenSPM utilizes a comparatively small model size of 0.24M parameters. Baselines ranged from 200M to 7B parameters.
 - **Inference Latency:** It achieved a 4.8 ms inference latency.
 - **Equivalent Action Frequency:** With an action chunk length (H) of 5, OpenSPM delivered an equivalent action output frequency of 1033.3 Hz. This is significantly higher than the baselines, which ranged from 2.2 Hz to 54.7 Hz.
- **Conclusion:** This performance indicates that OpenSPM's decompositional approach, leveraging 3D perception, key spatial pose memory, and lightweight flow-matching for local action completion, allows it to achieve competitive success rates with substantial advantages in computational efficiency and control frequency.

#### 4.2. Pose Consistency Evaluation of Kalman Prediction

- **Method:** The Kalman state estimation module was evaluated by comparing its predicted poses against frame-by-frame SAM 3 + SAM 3D reconstruction results (offline visual references).
- **Results (Table 2):**
 - The average translation Mean Absolute Error (MAE) across the ten tasks was 5.8 mm, with a maximum of 14.7 mm for one task.
 - The average orientation MAE was 2.12°, with a maximum of 6.4° for one task.
- **Conclusion:** These results suggest that the low-dimensional Kalman filter-based prediction-update mechanism can stably approximate high-precision visual references, even under noisy demonstrations and sparse visual observations, with low computational overhead.

#### 4.3. Ablation of the Key Spatial Pose Memory Bank

- **Method:** An ablation study was performed by removing the structured key spatial pose memory and transition priors. Instead, a multimodal model was directly used for zero-shot inference of end-effector 6D key spatial poses from multi-view images, object bounding box parameters, and point clouds.
- **Results (Table 3):** The success rate significantly dropped to 23.8% when relying solely on zero-shot multimodal inference for key spatial poses, compared to OpenSPM's full method (85.6%).
- **Conclusion:** This ablation indicates that the structured key spatial pose memory is not merely a cache but a critical component that explicitly encodes physically executable priors such as contact points, approach directions, release positions, and phase boundaries. This prior knowledge, extracted from successful demonstrations and stored in the object coordinate frame, reduces the need for a multimodal model to infer interaction points from scratch.

#### 4.4. Ablation of Flow-Matching Execution and Terminal Residual Correction

- **Method:** Three execution strategies were compared (Table 4):
 1. Open-loop flow matching.
 2. Inter-segment closed-loop flow matching (using real-time proprioceptive state at the start of each chunk).
 3. Inter-segment closed-loop flow matching with Terminal Residual Correction (TRC).
- **Results:**
 - **Open-loop flow matching:** Achieved a 3.0% success rate, demonstrating rapid error accumulation even with centimeter-level accuracy in single-step predictions.
 - **Inter-segment closed-loop flow matching:** Improved the success rate to 29.8%, highlighting the benefit of real-time state feedback in reducing error propagation.
 - **Inter-segment closed-loop flow matching + TRC:** Raised the success rate substantially to 85.6%.
- **Execution Steps:** TRC increased the average number of execution steps from approximately 167 to 278.5.
- **Conclusion:** Closed-loop control, particularly with terminal residual correction, is crucial for mitigating error accumulation and ensuring robustness in physical contact establishment and release phases. The increased steps with TRC suggest that the system engages in additional fine-tuning near critical nodes, which contributes to task success.

#### 4.5. Training Convergence, Action Error, and Failure Modes

- **Training:** The flow-matching model converged quickly (within ~200 epochs) due to its processing of low-dimensional input variables rather than high-dimensional image or language tokens, suggesting a smaller effective hypothesis space.
- **Action Error:** Validation-set action errors, even if at centimeter level, do not directly translate to task success rates because critical manipulation phases often demand sub-centimeter tolerances.
- **Failure Categories:**
 1. **Perception Errors:** Upstream 2D localization or segmentation errors propagated to 3D pose estimation, leading to incorrect anchor points for relative pose transfer (especially for visually similar, occluded, or round objects).
 2. **Small Operation Tolerances:** Tasks involving slender objects or those with narrow support regions, where centimeter-level flow-matching errors, even with TRC, were insufficient to compensate for instability.
 3. **Environmental Constraints:** Failures in pushing or drawer tasks due to conflicts between the planned path/gripper pose and scene objects or environmental boundaries, which residual correction could not overcome.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OpenSPM presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by demonstrating the efficacy of a decompositional approach, particularly in few-shot, high-frequency, and fine-grained manipulation scenarios.

1. **Validation of Decompositional Architecture:** The work validates the principle that decomposing complex Vision-Language-Action problems into specialized modules—semantic 3D perception, structured geometric memory, and local action generation—can overcome limitations of purely end-to-end VLA models. This suggests a pathway for creating more modular and interpretable robotic systems.

2. **Enhanced Geometric Fidelity and Transferability:** By explicitly encoding object-centric relative poses as key spatial memories, OpenSPM facilitates precise interaction geometry and enables robust transfer of learned skills across diverse scenes. This addresses a critical challenge for generalized robotic manipulation, where implicit geometric understanding in end-to-end models can be insufficient. The proven consistency of relative pose transfer provides a reliable mechanism for adapting manipulation plans to new environments.

3. **Efficiency and High-Frequency Control:** OpenSPM achieves state-of-the-art success rates with substantially reduced computational requirements (0.24M parameters) and significantly higher control frequencies (1033.3 Hz equivalent) compared to large-scale VLA models. This has the potential to make advanced robotic manipulation capabilities more accessible for deployment in resource-constrained environments and for tasks requiring real-time, reactive control.

4. **Robustness through Closed-Loop Control:** The integration of real-time proprioceptive state feedback and terminal residual correction is critical for mitigating error accumulation, which is a common failure mode in sequential action generation. This contributes to the system's robustness during contact-rich and precision-demanding manipulation phases.

5. **Interpretable and Reusable Knowledge Representation:** The structured key spatial pose memory bank offers an interpretable representation of manipulation knowledge, encoding essential physical contact points, approach directions, and phase constraints. This form of memory is more robust to scene variations and offers clearer insights into the robot's operational logic compared to opaque end-to-end policies.

**Potential Impact:**
OpenSPM's methodology offers a feasible path towards developing low-cost, high-frequency, and environment-transferable robotic manipulation systems. This could accelerate the adoption of robots in various domains, from industrial assembly to domestic assistance, where fine-grained object interaction, adaptability to changing environments, and efficient operation are paramount. The work highlights the complementary role of explicit 3D geometry and interpretable memory alongside semantic understanding, suggesting a hybrid approach for future robot learning architectures. Future research could build upon OpenSPM by enriching memory entries with more complex physical interaction parameters, such as contact forces, local surface normals, or obstacle distances, to further enhance robustness against unmodeled contact dynamics and scene complexities.
