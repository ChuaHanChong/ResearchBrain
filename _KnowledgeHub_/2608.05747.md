# GST-Bench: Can VLMs Develop Global Spatial Awareness from Video?

> [!summary] Summary
> GST-Bench introduces a video-based VQA benchmark and training dataset for evaluating global spatial intelligence in Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The benchmark reveals a significant performance gap between current VLMs (top VLM scored 42.68) and humans (79.08) in tasks requiring continuous spatial reasoning from novel viewpoints, while demonstrating that targeted fine-tuning on GST-Train substantially improves VLM capabilities (Qwen3-VL-8B from 25.89 to 53.52).

## Problem

- Existing VLM benchmarks primarily assess local spatial understanding, failing to comprehensively evaluate a model's ability to form and maintain a global representation of an environment from continuous, long-duration visual streams.
- Many video-based benchmarks do not explicitly disentangle tasks requiring true cross-frame integration from those solvable with single-frame cues, and often rely on coarse spatial metrics.
- Current benchmarks rarely evaluate the correspondence between egocentric video observations and explicit global visual representations, hindering assessment of a model's ability to map egocentric experiences to a broader scene context.

## Method

- The research introduces **GST-Bench**, a VQA benchmark featuring 12 subtasks across self-localization, object localization, and scene structure understanding, explicitly designed to enforce ==cross-frame integration== from long-horizon egocentric videos.
- The benchmark incorporates ==fine-grained numerical evaluations== for quantities like distances and angles, and integrates explicit global top-down scene representations at varying abstraction levels.
- A complementary large-scale training dataset, **GST-Train**, is provided, generated via an ==automatic simulation pipeline== built on OmniGibson to ensure scalability and precise geometric answer generation.

## Results

- Humans achieved an average score of **79.08** on GST-Bench, while the top-performing VLM, Gemini-3-Pro, scored **42.68**, highlighting a large performance disparity.
- Most open-source VLMs performed near or below random guessing (**20.01** overall baseline), indicating a general lack of global spatial competence beyond basic scene identification.
- Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on GST-Train led to a substantial improvement, raising its average score from **25.89** to **53.52**, which surpassed all zero-shot proprietary models on the benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art VLMs, including leading proprietary models, exhibit a substantial gap (36.4 points) in global spatial awareness compared to human performance (79.08).
> - Diagnostic experiments indicate that proprietary VLMs primarily struggle with integrating information across frames for global understanding, despite strong local perception, whereas open-source models show deficiencies in both local perception and global integration.
> - Targeted supervision using the GST-Train dataset significantly boosts VLM performance in global spatial reasoning, with a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B model outperforming all zero-shot proprietary models evaluated.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has demonstrated advancements in visual perception and reasoning tasks. A critical area for VLM development, particularly for applications involving embodied agents, is spatial intelligence—the ability to understand and navigate physical environments. This includes tasks such as perceiving scene layouts, tracking egocentric position, and maintaining a consistent global understanding of a space over time.

Existing benchmarks for spatial understanding in VLMs can be broadly categorized. Early benchmarks often focus on local spatial properties within single images or a limited number of discrete views. These typically evaluate categorical judgments (e.g., "left of," "under"), 2D understanding, depth ordering, or relative distances. Examples include those assessing prepositional statements or 3D-aware reasoning tasks such as height, location, and orientation. Some work has expanded to multi-image inputs, requiring models to integrate information across a few views, track motion, or relate entities not simultaneously present in a single frame. However, these settings primarily test localized spatial understanding and may not provide a comprehensive assessment of a model's ability to form and maintain a coherent global representation of an entire environment.

More recent advancements have introduced video-based benchmarks, aiming to evaluate broader scene-level understanding by requiring models to integrate observations over time. These benchmarks explore configurational reasoning, metric estimation, and spatiotemporal understanding over 3D spaces, sometimes involving long-horizon processing, visual spatial recall, or active exploration. While these approaches begin to probe global spatial understanding, the paper identifies several persistent limitations:
1. **Lack of Disentanglement:** Many video benchmarks do not explicitly separate tasks solvable from a single informative frame from those strictly requiring cross-frame integration of information. This can obscure a model's true global reasoning capabilities if it can rely on local cues.
2. **Coarse-Grained Metrics:** Spatial metrics, particularly for direction, often remain coarse (e.g., front/back/left/right) rather than employing precise numerical measurements like angles or distances. This limits the specificity of evaluation.
3. **Absence of Explicit Global Representations:** Existing benchmarks rarely evaluate the correspondence between egocentric video observations and explicit global visual representations, such as top-down images of a scene. This hinders the assessment of a model's ability to map egocentric experiences onto a broader scene context.

The presented work, GST-Bench, is positioned to address these specific gaps. It aims to provide a rigorous evaluation framework that necessitates global spatial awareness derived from continuous, long-horizon visual streams, particularly from novel viewpoints. By explicitly requiring cross-frame reasoning, utilizing precise numerical metrics, and incorporating explicit global top-down representations, GST-Bench seeks to push the boundaries of spatial intelligence evaluation for VLMs, thereby advancing their capabilities for effective interaction with complex physical environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce the Global-Spatial-Temporal Benchmark (GST-Bench), a video-based Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmark designed to comprehensively evaluate the global spatial intelligence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Complementing this, the research also aims to provide a training dataset, GST-Train, to facilitate future research in this area.

The motivation behind developing GST-Bench stems from critical limitations observed in the existing landscape of spatial intelligence benchmarks for VLMs:

1. **Requirement for Embodied Agent Intelligence:** For VLMs to effectively function as embodied agents in the physical world, spatial intelligence is a fundamental capability. This involves understanding surrounding layouts, tracking one's own position (egocentric localization), and maintaining a consistent global understanding of the environment over extended periods. For instance, a robot tasked with retrieving an object must build a scene representation from egocentric exploration, remember object locations, and infer their relative positions even when not directly visible. Current VLMs struggle with these aspects of spatial reasoning, which humans perform with apparent ease.

2. **Deficiencies in Existing Benchmarks:**
 - **Focus on Local Perception:** Most current spatial benchmarks, whether based on single images or a small number of images (typically 2-3 views), primarily evaluate local spatial properties. These include aspects like object size, relative depth, or relationships within a limited field of view. They often fail to assess a model's ability to integrate information across a continuous, long-duration visual stream to construct and maintain a global understanding of an entire scene.
 - **Lack of Disentanglement for Cross-Frame Reasoning:** Even some video-based benchmarks do not explicitly differentiate between questions answerable from a single, highly informative frame and those genuinely requiring integration of information across multiple frames or the entire video sequence. This compromises the evaluation of true global reasoning capabilities.
 - **Imprecise Spatial Metrics:** Evaluations for spatial reasoning often rely on coarse-grained judgments (e.g., "closer," "left/right") rather than requiring precise numerical answers for distances, angles, or coordinates. This limits the diagnostic power of such benchmarks.
 - **Absence of Global Scene Representation Evaluation:** Few benchmarks explicitly assess a model's capacity to map egocentric observations onto a global, top-down representation of the scene, which is crucial for a complete global spatial understanding.

To address these motivations and limitations, GST-Bench was designed around four specific principles:
- **Enforced Cross-Frame Integration:** All tasks are constructed such that the target object is guaranteed to be invisible from the query viewpoint, and questions solvable from a single video frame are excluded. This ensures that models *must* integrate information across views.
- **Fine-Grained Numerical Evaluation:** The benchmark relies on precise numerical answers for quantities like distances and angles, enabling unambiguous and granular assessment of spatial reasoning.
- **Integration of Global Scene Representations:** Top-down images are incorporated to directly evaluate a model's ability to construct and align a global scene representation from a continuous egocentric video stream.
- **Novel Viewpoint Reasoning:** Query viewpoints are sampled from independent points off the video's camera trajectory, increasing the difficulty and requiring models to generalize spatial understanding beyond observed paths.

By implementing these principles, the research aims to establish a more challenging and diagnostic benchmark that can reveal the true capabilities and limitations of VLMs in global spatial awareness, thereby guiding future advancements towards more spatially intelligent embodied AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces GST-Bench, a VQA benchmark for global spatial intelligence in video understanding, and GST-Train, a complementary dataset for training. Both are built upon a systematic data generation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Design (GST-Bench)

GST-Bench evaluates global spatial awareness across three core competencies crucial for embodied agents: self-localization, object localization, and scene structure understanding. These are instantiated into 12 distinct subtasks. The design emphasizes challenges beyond local perception, requiring models to integrate long-horizon egocentric observations, reason from novel viewpoints, and align egocentric evidence with explicit top-down scene representations.

- **Task Inputs:**
 - **Exploration Video:** A long egocentric video traversing the entire scene, serving as the primary source for models to build spatial memory.
 - **Object-Annotated Video:** The exploration video augmented with bounding boxes on a designated target object whenever it is visible. Used for visual target specification.
 - **Short Trajectory Video:** A clip from an independently collected trajectory, used for trajectory selection tasks.
 - **Top-Down Images:** Explicit global scene representations rendered at three abstraction levels:
 - **Easy:** Photo-realistic bird’s-eye-view with ceiling removed, preserving textures and colors.
 - **Medium:** Occupancy-style map showing object and wall footprints without appearance details.
 - **Hard:** Bare floor plan, retaining only wall structures.
 - **Current View:** A novel egocentric image rendered from an off-trajectory viewpoint, used as the query for localization tasks. For object localization, the target object is always explicitly invisible from this view.

- **Task Taxonomy (12 subtasks):**
 - **Self Localization:**
 - *Global Orientation:* Infer camera orientation of the current view on a medium-level top-down image.
 - *Global Position:* Infer camera position of the current view on a medium-level top-down image.
 - **Object Localization:** (Target always invisible from current view)
 - *Egocentric Direction (semantic/visual):* Infer egocentric direction from current viewpoint to target (specified by category name or visual bounding box).
 - *Egocentric Distance (semantic/visual):* Estimate metric distance from current viewpoint to target (semantic/visual).
 - *Global Position (semantic/visual):* Predict target object's position on a medium-level top-down image (semantic/visual).
 - **Scene Structure Understanding:**
 - *Top-Down Selection (easy/medium/hard):* Select the top-down image (at corresponding abstraction level) matching the explored scene from four candidates.
 - *Trajectory Selection:* Select the matching trajectory on a medium-level top-down image from four candidates, based on a short trajectory video.

#### 3.2. Data Generation Pipeline

An automatic pipeline built upon OmniGibson simulation with BEHAVIOR-1K is used for data generation. It leverages 50 diverse indoor scenes for GST-Bench, with evaluation scenes held out from GST-Train.

- **Enforcement of Global Reasoning:** The pipeline is designed to ensure global reasoning is required. Object-localization questions mandate that the target object is visible in the exploration video but *invisible* from the current query view. The current view is also sampled *off* the video trajectory, preventing simple image matching.
- **Scalability and Controllability:** Simulation allows for diverse video trajectories, arbitrary viewpoints, independent query viewpoint generation, and flexible control over camera poses, orientations, and rendering conditions. Geometric data (3D coordinates, distances, angles, top-down projections) are directly available for precise answer generation.
- **Human Verification:** For GST-Bench, an additional manual verification step confirms the answerability of each sample. Annotators check if the target object is identifiable in the video and if the off-trajectory current view can be localized by a human from the exploration video, ensuring benchmark quality and reliability.
- **Scene and Video Generation:** It involves scene preparation (diverse indoor assets), exploration video generation (multiple trajectories for broad scene coverage), object-annotated video generation (bounding boxes for visual targets), short trajectory video generation, and top-down image generation (three abstraction levels).
- **Current View Generation:** Off-trajectory current views are generated, filtered for sufficient visual overlap with the exploration video, and critically, for object localization tasks, filtered to ensure the target object is absent from the current view.
- **QA Generation and Automatic Filtering:** QA pairs are generated from inputs using task-specific templates, with answers computed from scene geometry. Automatic filtering removes invalid, ambiguous, or shortcut-prone samples.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Setup

- **Baseline Models:** 22 state-of-the-art VLMs were evaluated, including proprietary models (e.g., Gemini, GPT-5, Seed1.8), open-source models (e.g., LLaVA, Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, NVILA), and embodied-understanding models (e.g., Cosmos-Reason2, RoboBrain, Robix). Qwen3-VL-8B fine-tuned on GST-Train was also included. All models were evaluated zero-shot using recommended prompts and greedy decoding.
- **Metric Design:** Tailored metrics for different question formats:
 - **Distance Prediction:** Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) across thresholds.
 - **Angle Prediction:** Mean Accuracy over tolerance thresholds (15°, 30°, 45°).
 - **Point Prediction:** Mean Accuracy over Euclidean distance thresholds (in pixels).
 - **Multiple-Choice:** Standard accuracy.
 - **Overall Score:** Arithmetic mean of individual scores across all 12 subtasks.

#### 3.4. Diagnostic Experiments

To disentangle global reasoning from local perception, controlled local variants were constructed for Egocentric Direction (semantic) and Egocentric Distance (semantic) tasks:
- **Global:** The default GST-Bench setting (target absent from current view).
- **Local-Video:** Current view contains the target; exploration video is redundant.
- **Local-Image:** Exploration video removed, task reduced to single-image perception.

#### 3.5. Targeted Training (GST-Train)

GST-Train, a large-scale training dataset, was collected using the same pipeline but with different simulation scenes. Qwen3-VL-8B was fine-tuned on GST-Train, mixed with general multimodal instruction data, to assess the impact of targeted supervision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 22 state-of-the-art VLMs on GST-Bench yielded several key findings regarding their global spatial awareness capabilities.

- **Significant Gap Between VLMs and Humans:** Humans achieved an average score of 79.08 on GST-Bench. In contrast, the top-performing VLM, Gemini-3-Pro, reached only 42.68, demonstrating a substantial gap of 36.4 points. This discrepancy was observed across all three core competencies, being particularly pronounced in orientation estimation (Gemini-3-Pro: 16.89 vs. Human: 85.00) and global position estimation from visual input (Gemini-3-Pro: 42.23 vs. Human: 93.00). Egocentric distance estimation was an exception, where both human and model scores were comparatively low, suggesting an inherent difficulty in absolute metric distance recovery.

- **Struggling Performance of Open-Source Models:** The majority of open-source models exhibited performance close to random guessing. Qwen3-VL-32B (30.43) and InternVL3.5-38B (30.71) were the strongest in this category, but their advantage was largely driven by the easier Top-Down Selection subtasks. On the remaining ten tasks, these models generally scored only a few points above the random baseline. Several open-source and embodied-understanding models (e.g., LLaVA-OV-1.5-8B at 19.58, InternVL3.5-2B at 19.72) even performed below the random guessing baseline of 20.01. This indicates that, beyond basic scene identification, current open-source VLMs generally lack global spatial competence.

- **Modest Leadership by Proprietary Models:** Proprietary models, specifically Gemini-3-Pro (42.68), Gemini-2.5-Pro (40.95), and GPT-5 (40.85), formed the leading tier. They consistently outperformed the best open-source models by approximately 10 points and roughly doubled the random-guessing baseline. Each of the twelve subtasks saw a proprietary model achieve the highest score. Despite this, even these models achieved less than 55% of human performance, indicating significant remaining challenges for the field.

- **Embodied-Tuned Models Lack Advantage in Global Reasoning:** Models specifically tuned for embodied understanding, such as RoboBrain2.5-8B (24.61) and Cosmos-Reason2-8B (21.64), did not show an advantage over general-purpose VLMs of similar scale (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B at 25.89). Similarly, Robix-32B (29.26) performed comparably to Qwen3-VL-32B (30.43). This suggests that current embodied post-training approaches may prioritize local affordance or spatial relation prediction rather than the long-horizon spatial memory and cross-view alignment required for global spatial reasoning.

- **Disentangling Global Reasoning from Local Perception:** Controlled experiments with local variants of Egocentric Direction (semantic) and Egocentric Distance (semantic) revealed distinct bottlenecks:
 - **Proprietary Models:** These models demonstrated significant performance gains when the task was simplified to local perception. For Egocentric Direction (semantic), Gemini-3-Pro's score nearly tripled (from 22.11 to 61.20) in the Local-Image setting, with similar substantial improvements for other top proprietary models. This indicates that proprietary models possess strong local spatial perception but struggle specifically with the *integration of information across frames* to build a global understanding.
 - **Open-Source Models:** In contrast, open-source models did not consistently benefit from the local setting. Some even showed degraded performance when the task was simplified. While improvements were observed in Egocentric Distance (semantic) for all tested open-source models in local settings, their absolute local scores remained considerably lower than those of proprietary counterparts. This suggests that open-source models exhibit deficiencies in *both local perception and global integration*.

- **Effectiveness of Targeted Training:** Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on the GST-Train dataset resulted in a substantial improvement, raising its average score from 25.89 to 53.52. This fine-tuned model surpassed all zero-shot proprietary models evaluated. This finding indicates that targeted supervision with data specifically designed for global spatial reasoning can effectively narrow the performance gap, though a considerable difference from human performance still remains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of GST-Bench and GST-Train is significant for the advancement of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the broader field of embodied artificial intelligence.

- **Addressing Critical Gaps in Evaluation:** GST-Bench provides a novel and more rigorous framework for evaluating global spatial awareness in VLMs. By explicitly requiring cross-frame reasoning from long-horizon egocentric videos, posing queries from novel off-trajectory viewpoints, and utilizing explicit top-down scene representations, the benchmark moves beyond the limitations of existing datasets that often focus on local perception, coarse metrics, or single-frame solvable tasks. This specificity allows for a more accurate diagnosis of model capabilities in a critical area for embodied intelligence.

- **Revealing Fundamental Limitations of Current VLMs:** The comprehensive evaluation across 22 state-of-the-art VLMs reveals a substantial and pervasive gap between current model performance and human spatial reasoning abilities. This finding is critical as it highlights that even the most advanced proprietary models and those specifically tuned for embodied understanding still possess severe limitations in constructing and maintaining globally consistent spatial representations. This underscores a foundational challenge that needs to be addressed for VLMs to effectively operate in complex, dynamic real-world environments.

- **Diagnostic Insights into Model Failures:** The controlled diagnostic experiments, which disentangle global reasoning from local perception, offer valuable insights into the nature of these limitations. The observation that proprietary models struggle primarily with cross-frame integration while exhibiting strong local perception, whereas open-source models face challenges at both the local perception and global integration stages, provides a clear roadmap for targeted research and development efforts for different model architectures and training paradigms.

- **Catalyst for Future Research and Development:** By clearly defining and measuring "global spatial awareness," GST-Bench is anticipated to serve as a catalyst for new research. It provides a standardized metric and a challenging set of tasks that can drive the development of novel VLM architectures, training methodologies, and data augmentation strategies specifically aimed at improving long-horizon spatial memory, cross-view alignment, and robust scene understanding.

- **Enabling Targeted Training Solutions:** The accompanying GST-Train dataset offers a valuable resource for training VLMs to overcome the identified spatial reasoning challenges. The demonstrated success of fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on GST-Train, leading to a significant performance improvement, validates the utility of targeted supervision. This suggests that incorporating such spatially-focused training data can be an effective approach to enhance global spatial intelligence in VLMs, ultimately leading to more capable embodied agents.

In summary, GST-Bench and GST-Train are poised to significantly impact the field by providing a robust evaluation standard, diagnosing current model deficiencies, and offering resources to guide the development of future VLMs that can truly understand and interact with the physical world with a human-like grasp of global spatial context.
