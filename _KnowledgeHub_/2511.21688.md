# G$^2$VLM: Geometry Grounded Vision Language Model with Unified 3D Reconstruction and Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> G G ²VLM integrates 3D reconstruction and spatial reasoning within a single Vision-Language Model, addressing the spatial intelligence limitations of current VLMs. It learns explicit visual geometry from 2D data using a Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts architecture, leading to robust spatial understanding and strong performance on both 3D reconstruction and complex spatial reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often lack robust spatial intelligence, struggling with tasks requiring a deep understanding of 3D space.
- VLMs traditionally learn implicit knowledge of the physical world from 2D image-text datasets, without explicit learning of visual geometry to lift 2D perceptions into coherent 3D representations.
- Existing feedforward visual geometry models focus solely on 3D reconstruction, neglecting higher-level scene understanding and reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- Introduces G²VLM, a unified framework with a ==Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) architecture==, comprising a ==Geometric Perception Expert== and a Semantic Perception Expert.
- Employs a ==two-stage training strategy==: initial pretraining of the geometric expert on 3D-annotated datasets, followed by joint training of both experts on spatial understanding and general VQA datasets.
- The geometric expert predicts 3D attributes like depth, point clouds, and camera poses, with all tokens from both experts engaging in ==shared multi-modal self-attention==.

## Results

- Achieves competitive visual geometry performance, reducing the Absolute Relative Error (Abs Rel) for monocular depth estimation on Sintel from VGGT’s **0.335** to **0.297**.
- Demonstrates state-of-the-art spatial reasoning, with G²VLM-SR surpassing GPT-4o by **18.48** points on the SPAR-Bench and achieving the best results on MindCube and OmniSpatial benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirm the positive impact of explicit geometry pretraining and the dual-encoder design, showing that better geometric features directly correlate with improved spatial reasoning performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying low-level 3D reconstruction and high-level spatial understanding within a single VLM architecture significantly enhances the model's spatial intelligence.
> - A dual-expert design with shared attention allows for a rich interplay between geometric and semantic representations, enabling mutual improvement.
> - Explicitly learning 3D visual geometry from abundant 2D multi-view image and video data offers a scalable method to imbue VLMs with a genuine understanding of the 3D world, bypassing extensive 3D annotation needs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical limitation of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) within the rapidly evolving landscape of multimodal AI. Recent advancements have established VLMs as powerful foundation models, demonstrating impressive zero-shot generalizability across a wide array of tasks. However, as the authors and several cited studies point out, these models often exhibit a significant gap in robust **spatial intelligence**, struggling with spatial understanding and reasoning tasks crucial for applications like robotics and embodied AI.

Traditionally, VLMs acquire knowledge of the physical world implicitly, by training on massive 2D image-text datasets. This approach largely relies on language and 2D visual priors, treating inputs like multiple images or video frames as "flat" sequences of 2D data. While some specialized 3D-VLMs or models incorporating frozen geometry encoders (e.g., VLM-3R, Spatial MLLM) have emerged to address this, general VLMs still lack the explicit learning of visual geometry necessary to "lift" 2D perceptions into coherent 3D representations. Simultaneously, the field of feedforward visual geometry has seen significant progress, with models like DUSt3R and VGGT achieving accurate 3D reconstruction from 2D inputs. However, these models typically remain focused solely on geometric reconstruction, neglecting higher-level scene understanding and reasoning capabilities.

G²VLM positions itself as a pioneering effort to bridge these two previously distinct research streams: **spatial 3D reconstruction** and **high-level spatial understanding and reasoning**. Inspired by the "two-streams hypothesis" in human cognition (ventral for object recognition, dorsal for spatial location), G²VLM aims to natively unify these capabilities within a single, scalable VLM architecture. By directly integrating visual geometry learning into the VLM framework, G²VLM seeks to provide VLMs with an explicit understanding of 3D space, which is essential for true spatial intelligence, moving beyond implicit 2D-based reasoning. This unified approach represents a significant step towards creating more holistic and human-like AI systems capable of perceiving and interacting with the physical world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind G²VLM stems from the observed fragility of existing Vision-Language Models in comprehending and reasoning about spatial relationships. Despite their prowess in various multimodal tasks, current VLMs frequently falter when faced with questions requiring a deep understanding of 3D space. The authors attribute this deficiency to the inherent "flatness" of 2D data processing and the absence of a dedicated visual geometry learning process that can reconstruct the 3D world from 2D images.

Therefore, the **key objectives** of this research are:

1. **To bridge the gap between spatial 3D reconstruction and high-level spatial understanding within a single VLM framework.** This aims to overcome the limitation where models either excel at low-level geometry or high-level semantics, but rarely both cohesively.
2. **To develop a geometry-grounded VLM that natively leverages learned 3D visual geometry features.** This involves enabling the model to directly predict 3D attributes (like depth, point clouds, and camera poses) and utilize these explicit 3D representations to enhance spatial reasoning.
3. **To facilitate spatial reasoning tasks via in-context learning and interleaved reasoning.** By integrating explicit 3D geometry, the model can perform more robust and accurate spatial reasoning, as exemplified by tasks requiring navigation, object placement, or relative positioning.
4. **To design a unified architecture that is highly scalable for spatial understanding.** A crucial goal is to avoid reliance on hard-to-collect 3D annotated datasets by training primarily on abundant multi-view image and video data, while still benefiting from integrated 3D visual priors.
5. **To establish a strong baseline for the community.** By unifying a semantically powerful VLM with low-level 3D vision tasks, G²VLM aims to serve as a foundational model that can unlock new applications, such as advanced 3D scene editing, more capable robotics, and robust embodied AI agents.

In essence, the motivation is to imbue VLMs with a genuine, explicit understanding of the 3D world, moving beyond superficial 2D pattern matching to achieve true spatial intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

G²VLM adopts a novel and unified approach, centered around a **Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT)** architecture. This design is inspired by the two-streams hypothesis of human visual processing, comprising distinct but interacting pathways for "what" (semantic understanding) and "where" (geometric perception).

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The G²VLM architecture consists of two primary experts:
- **Geometric Perception Expert (the "where pathway"):** This expert is responsible for visual geometry learning and 3D reconstruction. It utilizes a **DINOv2 vision encoder** to extract low-level visual information, which is then processed by a series of transformer blocks that facilitate global attention. The hidden states from these blocks are fed into specialized **3D geometry heads** (lightweight transformer decoders) for task-specific predictions, including camera poses, pixel-aligned 3D point maps (local), and global point maps (for training stabilization). A linear projection layer aligns DINOv2 features with the LLM's hidden dimension. The design is permutation-equivariant and avoids camera tokens for scalability.
- **Semantic Perception Expert (the "what pathway"):** This expert handles multimodal understanding and spatial reasoning. It is built upon a **pretrained VLM, specifically Qwen2-VL-2B**, leveraging its native dynamic resolution and Multimodal Rotary Position Embedding (M-RoPE). This allows for easy integration with stronger VLMs.

A critical aspect of the architecture is that **all tokens from both experts engage in shared multi-modal self-attention** within each transformer block, enabling a rich interplay and mutual improvement between geometric and semantic representations.

#### 3.2. Training Strategy

G²VLM employs a two-stage training strategy to effectively train both experts:

1. **Stage 1: Geometric Perception Expert Pretraining:**
 - The semantic perception expert (Qwen2-VL) is **frozen**.
 - The geometric perception expert is initialized from scratch and trained on a vast collection of **large-scale 3D-annotated datasets** (e.g., ScanNet, Co3Dv2, MegaDepth).
 - The objective function, `L_VG`, is a weighted sum of three components:
 - **Point Cloud Reconstruction Loss (`L_points`):** Measures the L1 distance between predicted and ground-truth 3D points, scaled by depth for better performance.
 - **Camera Pose Loss (`L_cam`):** Combines rotation loss (geodesic distance between relative rotations) and translation loss (Huber loss for scaled relative translation).
 - **Normal Loss (`L_normal`):** Encourages smooth surface reconstructions by minimizing the angle between predicted and ground-truth normal vectors.
 - Training involves two phases: initial low-resolution (224x224) training for 100K iterations, followed by high-resolution (518x518) training for 20K iterations, using AdamW optimizer and cosine scheduler.

2. **Stage 2: Joint Training for Spatial Reasoning:**
 - The semantic perception expert is **unfrozen**, allowing it to learn to integrate the geometric features.
 - Both experts are jointly trained on **spatial understanding datasets** (e.g., SPAR-7M, OmniSpatial, MindCube) and general VQA datasets (LLaVA-One-Vision).
 - The primary optimization for the semantic expert is the standard **language modeling loss (cross-entropy)**.
 - The authors explore three joint-training strategies for the geometric expert:
 - **CE Loss Only (G²VLM):** Geometric expert is frozen; only the semantic expert updates using CE loss. This preserves geometric performance and scales well with abundant 2D video data. This is the main G²VLM variant.
 - **CE + CE Loss (G²VLM-SR):** Geometric expert is fine-tuned using CE loss, specifically for spatial understanding tasks. This variant shows the most significant improvement in spatial reasoning.
 - **VG + CE Loss:** Geometric expert is optimized with both VG loss and CE loss. While yielding strong results for both tasks, it requires 3D annotations during joint training, limiting scalability.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

The training incorporates gradient norm clipping, bfloat16 precision, and gradient checkpointing for stability and efficiency. A large number of A800 GPUs are utilized for both pre-training and joint-training stages, reflecting the computational intensity of such large-scale multimodal models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

G²VLM demonstrates proficiency across both visual geometry and spatial understanding tasks, establishing competitive performance against state-of-the-art models in both domains.

#### 4.1. Visual Geometry Results

G²VLM achieves results comparable to specialized feed-forward 3D reconstruction models, despite being part of a unified VLM:
- **Monocular Depth Estimation:** On the Sintel benchmark, G²VLM reduces the Absolute Relative Error (Abs Rel) from VGGT’s 0.335 to 0.297, showing significant improvement. It also performs competitively on NYU-V2.
- **Point Map Estimation:** The model achieves on-par completion (Comp.) and comparable accuracy (Acc.) on 7-Scenes and ETH3D datasets, indicating its ability to reconstruct high-quality 3D point maps.
- **Camera Pose Estimation:** G²VLM demonstrates comparable Relative Rotation Accuracy (RRA) and Relative Translation Accuracy (RTA) on the Co3Dv2 dataset. Notably, it achieves these results without employing camera tokens (unlike VGGT, which uses them as a strong prior) or requiring fine-tuning from pre-trained weights (unlike π³), highlighting the robustness of its geometry learning.

Qualitative results further illustrate G²VLM's ability to generate high-quality 3D reconstructions and generalize across diverse open-domain images, covering object-level, structure-level, indoor, and outdoor scenes, including both dynamic and static content.

#### 4.2. Spatial Understanding & Reasoning Results

Both G²VLM and its spatial reasoning-optimized variant, G²VLM-SR, show substantial improvements over the base model (Qwen2-VL-2B) on comprehensive spatial reasoning benchmarks:
- **SPAR-Bench:** G²VLM-SR achieves the best results among all existing works, surpassing the proprietary GPT-4o by 18.48 points. It outperforms human performance in the "low" category of spatial reasoning, as shown in the supplementary material.
- **MindCube & OmniSpatial:** G²VLM-SR also achieves the best results among all open-source and spatial expert models on these benchmarks.
- **OST-Bench (Online Spatio-Temporal Scene Understanding):** While the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B model achieves the overall best results, G²VLM-SR still significantly outperforms other spatial expert models of similar or larger sizes. This suggests that online spatio-temporal understanding might benefit more from larger architectures capable of storing vast amounts of knowledge.

These results underscore the effectiveness of integrating explicit 3D geometry for enhancing complex spatial reasoning capabilities in VLMs.

#### 4.3. Discussions and Ablation Study

The authors conducted several ablation studies to validate their design choices:
- **Encoder Design (Single vs. Dual):** A dual-encoder design (DINOv2 for geometry, Qwen2 for semantics) outperformed a single-encoder approach (CLIP for both). This suggests that DINOv2 provides complementary low-level visual information that significantly benefits both visual geometry and spatial understanding.
- **Attention Mechanism for Geometric Expert:** Global attention consistently outperformed frame-attention and mixed-attention variants during geometric perception expert training, demonstrating its effectiveness within an LLM-compatible framework.
- **Interplay Between Geometry and Reasoning:** The study confirmed a positive correlation: better geometric features (e.g., from the global attention design) directly led to greater improvements in spatial reasoning performance.
- **Impact of Geometry Pretraining:** The full G²VLM model significantly outperformed a baseline variant that was only finetuned on spatial understanding data, unequivocally demonstrating that the learned visual geometry representations are crucial for the model's effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

G²VLM represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal artificial intelligence, offering a robust and unified framework that addresses a fundamental limitation of current Vision-Language Models. Its contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Spatial Intelligence:** G²VLM is the first unified model to intrinsically bridge two critical aspects of spatial intelligence: low-level 3D reconstruction and high-level spatial understanding/reasoning. This moves beyond mere implicit learning from 2D data, endowing VLMs with an explicit, geometry-grounded comprehension of the physical world, akin to how humans perceive and reason in 3D space.
2. **Architectural Innovation:** The novel Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) architecture, with dedicated geometric and semantic perception experts interacting via shared self-attention, provides an elegant and effective solution for integrating diverse perception pathways within a single model. This design allows the model to learn 3D geometry directly from 2D multi-view data, a scalable approach that sidesteps the need for labor-intensive 3D annotations.
3. **Benchmark-Setting Performance:** G²VLM achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive results across both visual geometry tasks (depth, point map, camera pose estimation) and complex spatial reasoning benchmarks (SPAR-Bench, MindCube, OmniSpatial). Its ability to surpass powerful proprietary models like GPT-4o on certain spatial reasoning tasks is particularly noteworthy, validating the effectiveness of its geometry-grounded approach.
4. **Enhanced Scalability and Efficiency:** By learning 3D geometry from abundant 2D image and video data, G²VLM offers a more scalable solution for acquiring 3D priors compared to methods relying heavily on scarce 3D annotated datasets. This efficiency can accelerate future research and development in geometry-aware AI.
5. **Foundation for Future Applications:** The unified capabilities of G²VLM lay a strong foundation for a wide range of advanced applications. This includes:
 - **Robotics and Embodied AI:** More intelligent robots capable of intricate navigation, manipulation, and interaction within complex 3D environments.
 - **3D Scene Understanding and Editing:** Robust tools for creating, modifying, and interacting with realistic 3D virtual environments based on natural language commands.
 - **Augmented/Virtual Reality:** Improved understanding of real-world scenes for seamless integration of virtual content.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction:** More intuitive interfaces where AI can understand and respond to spatial queries in a human-like manner.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as training instability with larger models, which requires substantial computational resources and advanced optimization, the universality and effectiveness of G²VLM present a promising baseline. It opens new avenues for research into semantically rich spatial tasks, bridging the gap between low-level visual perception and high-level cognitive reasoning, pushing multimodal AI closer to a holistic understanding of our 3D world.
