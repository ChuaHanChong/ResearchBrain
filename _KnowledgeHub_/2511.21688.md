# G$^2$VLM: Geometry Grounded Vision Language Model with Unified 3D Reconstruction and Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> G G ²VLM integrates 3D reconstruction and spatial reasoning within a single Vision-Language Model, addressing the spatial intelligence limitations of current VLMs. It learns explicit visual geometry from 2D data using a Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts architecture, leading to robust spatial understanding and strong performance on both 3D reconstruction and complex spatial reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often lack robust spatial intelligence, struggling with tasks requiring a deep understanding of 3D space.
- VLMs traditionally learn implicit knowledge of the physical world from 2D image-text datasets, without explicit learning of visual geometry to lift 2D perceptions into coherent 3D representations.
- Existing feedforward visual geometry models focus solely on 3D reconstruction, neglecting higher-level scene understanding and reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- Introduces G²VLM, a unified framework with a ==Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) architecture==, comprising a ==Geometric Perception Expert== and a Semantic Perception Expert.
- Employs a ==two-stage training strategy==: initial pretraining of the geometric expert on 3D-annotated datasets, followed by joint training of both experts on spatial understanding and general VQA datasets.
- The geometric expert predicts 3D attributes like depth, point clouds, and camera poses, with all tokens from both experts engaging in ==shared multi-modal self-attention==.

## Results

- Achieves competitive visual geometry performance, reducing the Absolute Relative Error (Abs Rel) for monocular depth estimation on Sintel from VGGT’s **0.335** to **0.297**.
- Demonstrates state-of-the-art spatial reasoning, with G²VLM-SR surpassing GPT-4o by **18.48** points on the SPAR-Bench and achieving the best results on MindCube and OmniSpatial benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirm the positive impact of explicit geometry pretraining and the dual-encoder design, showing that better geometric features directly correlate with improved spatial reasoning performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying low-level 3D reconstruction and high-level spatial understanding within a single VLM architecture significantly enhances the model's spatial intelligence.
> - A dual-expert design with shared attention allows for a rich interplay between geometric and semantic representations, enabling mutual improvement.
> - Explicitly learning 3D visual geometry from abundant 2D multi-view image and video data offers a scalable method to imbue VLMs with a genuine understanding of the 3D world, bypassing extensive 3D annotation needs.
