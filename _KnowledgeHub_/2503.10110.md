# IMPACT: Intelligent Motion Planning with Acceptable Contact Trajectories via Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named IMPACT integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with contact-aware motion planning, enabling robots to make semantically acceptable contact with non-target objects in cluttered environments. This approach achieved a 73.75% success rate in simulation and 61% in real-world tests, while generating trajectories preferred by human evaluators.

## Problem

- Robot motion planning faces significant challenges in densely cluttered environments, where strictly collision-free paths may not exist or are highly inefficient.
- Defining and formalizing "acceptable contact" for robots is difficult due to the subjective, context-dependent nature of object properties like fragility and stability.
- Existing contact-rich manipulation methods are often domain-specific or rely on explicit programming, lacking generalization to diverse, unfamiliar objects in real-world settings.

## Method

- **IMPACT** employs ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)==, specifically GPT-4o, in a zero-shot inference setup to automatically assign common-sense contact tolerance costs to non-target objects based on visual and semantic cues.
- It converts VLM-generated object costs into a dense, anisotropic cost map that explicitly encodes directional push safety, guiding the robot on how and where to make contact with objects.
- A contact-aware A* planner utilizes this anisotropic cost map and incorporates estimated object displacement to compute paths that allow stable and semantically acceptable physical interaction.

## Results

- In simulation, IMPACT achieved a **73.75%** task success rate, outperforming strict collision-free baselines (e.g., A* at **15.75%**) and VLM-cost methods without directional awareness (**57.50%**).
- Real-world experiments demonstrated IMPACT's higher success rate of **61%**, exceeding a fine-tuned LAPP baseline (**49%** for seen, **40%** for unseen objects), highlighting its strong generalization for novel objects.
- Human user studies consistently showed preference for IMPACT-generated trajectories over baselines in both simulation and real-world scenarios, indicating alignment with human judgment of acceptable contact.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models can effectively provide robots with zero-shot common-sense knowledge about object fragility and contact tolerance, eliminating the need for manual labeling or explicit instructions.
> - Incorporating anisotropic cost maps for directional push safety significantly enhances the quality and robustness of contact-rich trajectories, reducing the risk of unsafe object displacements.
> - Intelligently permitting object contact through VLM-informed planning allows robots to overcome the limitations of strict collision avoidance, leading to more efficient and human-acceptable operation in cluttered spaces.
