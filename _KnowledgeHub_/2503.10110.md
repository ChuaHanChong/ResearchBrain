# IMPACT: Intelligent Motion Planning with Acceptable Contact Trajectories via Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named IMPACT integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with contact-aware motion planning, enabling robots to make semantically acceptable contact with non-target objects in cluttered environments. This approach achieved a 73.75% success rate in simulation and 61% in real-world tests, while generating trajectories preferred by human evaluators.

## Problem

- Robot motion planning faces significant challenges in densely cluttered environments, where strictly collision-free paths may not exist or are highly inefficient.
- Defining and formalizing "acceptable contact" for robots is difficult due to the subjective, context-dependent nature of object properties like fragility and stability.
- Existing contact-rich manipulation methods are often domain-specific or rely on explicit programming, lacking generalization to diverse, unfamiliar objects in real-world settings.

## Method

- **IMPACT** employs ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)==, specifically GPT-4o, in a zero-shot inference setup to automatically assign common-sense contact tolerance costs to non-target objects based on visual and semantic cues.
- It converts VLM-generated object costs into a dense, anisotropic cost map that explicitly encodes directional push safety, guiding the robot on how and where to make contact with objects.
- A contact-aware A* planner utilizes this anisotropic cost map and incorporates estimated object displacement to compute paths that allow stable and semantically acceptable physical interaction.

## Results

- In simulation, IMPACT achieved a **73.75%** task success rate, outperforming strict collision-free baselines (e.g., A* at **15.75%**) and VLM-cost methods without directional awareness (**57.50%**).
- Real-world experiments demonstrated IMPACT's higher success rate of **61%**, exceeding a fine-tuned LAPP baseline (**49%** for seen, **40%** for unseen objects), highlighting its strong generalization for novel objects.
- Human user studies consistently showed preference for IMPACT-generated trajectories over baselines in both simulation and real-world scenarios, indicating alignment with human judgment of acceptable contact.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models can effectively provide robots with zero-shot common-sense knowledge about object fragility and contact tolerance, eliminating the need for manual labeling or explicit instructions.
> - Incorporating anisotropic cost maps for directional push safety significantly enhances the quality and robustness of contact-rich trajectories, reducing the risk of unsafe object displacements.
> - Intelligently permitting object contact through VLM-informed planning allows robots to overcome the limitations of strict collision avoidance, leading to more efficient and human-acceptable operation in cluttered spaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditional robot motion planning predominantly focuses on generating collision-free paths for robot end-effectors to reach a target pose. This approach is effective in uncluttered environments but becomes highly restrictive, or even infeasible, in densely cluttered scenarios where incidental contact with non-target objects may be necessary for task completion or efficiency.

Existing approaches to address cluttered environments include:
- **Navigation Among Movable Obstacles (NAMO) and Minimum Constraint Removal:** These methods reason about relocating or removing obstacles to create clear paths. IMPACT differentiates itself by integrating with existing motion planning algorithms without requiring explicit object relocation.
- **Permeable Obstacles:** Some works modify cost functions to treat certain obstacles (e.g., foliage) as permeable, allowing contact. These are often domain-specific and not generalized to rigid objects in common cluttered settings.
- **Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Other research on contact-rich manipulation focuses on specific tasks like tight placement (e.g., peg insertion) or extrinsic dexterity (using environmental contacts for object reorientation). IMPACT, in contrast, addresses the broader scenario of robot parts touching non-target obstacles during a reaching task.
- **Language-Conditioned Path Planning (LAPP):** Closely related work like LAPP utilizes language instructions to enable semantically acceptable contact. IMPACT advances this by employing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to automate the inference of contact acceptability, thereby removing the need for explicit language commands.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in Robotics:** VLMs are increasingly leveraged for high-level planning, reward design, and affordance reasoning in robotics. IMPACT specifically utilizes VLMs to infer object contact tolerances, differentiating it from works like VoxPoser, which also use VLMs for semantic costing but require explicit user language commands for avoidance. IMPACT exploits the spatial and semantic reasoning capabilities of VLMs to determine contact tolerances in a more autonomous fashion.

This work positions itself at the intersection of VLM-driven semantic understanding and contact-aware motion planning, aiming to bridge the gap between strict collision avoidance and intelligently permissive contact in cluttered environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to efficiently achieve reaching tasks in densely cluttered environments by making "semantically acceptable" contact with non-target objects, rather than strictly avoiding all collisions. The authors define "contacts" in this context as any part of the robot touching any non-target object, distinguishing it from grasping the target.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges:
- **Limitations of Collision-Free Planning:** In dense clutter, collision-free paths may not exist or may be overly complex and inefficient (e.g., requiring long, parabolic motions to bypass obstacles). This restricts robot capabilities in realistic scenarios like confined spaces (cabinets, boxes).
- **Defining "Acceptable Contact":** The concept of acceptable contact is highly subjective and context-dependent. It relies on common-sense knowledge about object properties (material, fragility, size), their locations, and potential consequences of interaction (e.g., brushing a soft pillow versus toppling a glass vase). Characterizing which contacts are benign and which are dangerous is a significant hurdle.
- **Beyond Isotropic Costs:** Simply assigning a static "cost" to an object for contact is insufficient. The safety and outcome of contact often depend on the direction and manner of interaction. Pushing an object from one side may be safe, while pushing it from another could lead to cascading collisions.

To address these, the authors propose IMPACT, aiming to:
- Develop a framework that uses VLMs to infer environment semantics, specifically identifying which objects can best tolerate contact based on their properties and relative positions.
- Formalize "acceptable contact" by transforming initial VLM-inferred semantic costs into a dense, anisotropic cost map that encodes directional push safety.
- Integrate this directional cost map with a contact-aware A* planner to compute stable, contact-rich paths.
- Validate the effectiveness of this approach through extensive simulation and real-world experiments, using both objective metrics and human preference evaluations.

Ultimately, the motivation is to develop more flexible and capable robotic systems that can operate effectively and intelligently in complex, real-world cluttered settings, aligning robot behavior more closely with human intuition regarding acceptable physical interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The IMPACT framework operates in two primary stages: obtaining object costs using a VLM and then performing directional contact-aware motion planning.

#### 3.1. Obtaining Object Costs using a VLM

The first stage addresses the challenge of defining "acceptable contact" by leveraging the common-sense knowledge embedded in Vision-Language Models (VLMs).
- **VLM Selection:** GPT-4o (GPT) is utilized due to its reported strong spatial reasoning capabilities, though the approach is compatible with other VLMs.
- **Input to VLM:** The VLM operates in a zero-shot inference setting. Its input consists of:
 - An **annotated image (I')**: An RGBD camera captures a scene image (I). SAM2 is then used to segment and annotate objects within this image, labeling them with numbers.
 - A **text prompt (ℓ)**: This template includes a list of objects present in the scene (labeled consistent with the image annotations) and general principles related to contact tolerance.
- **Output from VLM:** GPT outputs a dictionary assigning an integer cost from {0, 1..., 10} to each queried object. A higher cost indicates lower tolerance to contact. For example, a fragile wine glass might receive a high cost (e.g., 8), while a soft toy bear might receive a lower cost (e.g., 3). The target object is assigned a cost of -1 to encourage the planner to reach it.

#### 3.2. Directional Contact-Aware Motion Planning

The VLM-generated object costs are then used to inform a contact-aware motion planner.
- **3D Voxel Grid (V):** A 3D voxel grid is constructed, where each voxel `V[x,y,z]` is assigned the cost of the object it belongs to. Target object voxels are set to -1.
- **2D Cost Map (M):** The 3D voxel grid `V` is projected into a 2D grid `M` using a top-down view, where `M[x,y] = max_z V[x,y,z]`. This simplifies planning while retaining object cost information. Obstacles are categorized as "low-cost" (cost ≤ 5) or "high-cost" (cost ≥ 6).
- **Motion Primitives:** The planner utilizes three discrete 2D motion primitives for the robot's end-effector:
 - **Move:** Translates the robot to one of eight neighboring positions. Assumed not to affect the environment.
 - **Rotate:** Changes the end-effector's orientation within a ±45° range. Assumed not to affect the environment.
 - **Push:** Involves end-effector translation while making contact with an object, potentially displacing it.
- **Anisotropic Cost Map Generation (M'):** To account for directional contact safety, the 2D map `M` is augmented to create an anisotropic cost map `M'`.
 - For each point `(x,y)` on the boundary of a low-cost object, `m` push outcomes are sampled. These samples have small variations in distance and angle, weighted by a 2D Gaussian centered on the object's reverse surface normal (the most direct push direction).
 - Each hypothesized push outcome is evaluated on `M` to check for overlap with other objects. Outcomes are classified into four categories: "safe" (no collision), "low-cost contact" (collision with low-cost object), "high-cost contact" (collision with high-cost object), or "contact with target."
 - Each category is assigned a discrete "safety score" `u ∈ (0, 1]`, with higher values for safer outcomes (e.g., Safe = 1.0, Target Contact = 0.025). The likelihood of each sampled outcome `l_i` is calculated using a 2D Gaussian decay function based on angular and distance variations from the optimal push.
 - An aggregated safety score `f_s(x,y)` is computed as a weighted average of `u_i` values, where `l_i` are the weights (Equation 1).
 - The anisotropic cost map `M'[x,y]` is then calculated by combining the original object cost `M[x,y]` with `f_s(x,y)`: `M'[x,y] = αM[x,y] + (1-α)[10 - 10f_s(x,y)]`, where `α` is a weighting parameter (Equation 2). This ensures costs in `M'` remain consistent with the original range and reflect directional safety.
- **Contact-Aware A* Planning:** A* search is employed to find a sequence of primitives.
 - **State Definition:** The planner's state `S = (p, r, D)` includes the robot end-effector's 2D position `p` and orientation `r`, and a list `D` tracking the cumulative 2D displacement of low-cost objects (only low-cost objects are considered displaceable).
 - **State Expansion:** Nodes are expanded using the three primitives. For "Push," object displacement `d` is estimated based on maximum displacement, angular deviation from optimal push, and a decay parameter. A valid "Push" creates a new search branch with updated robot pose and displaced object positions.
 - **Cost Function:** The A* cost function `f(s) = g(s) + h(s)` is used. `h(s)` is the Euclidean distance to the target. `g(s')` is the accumulated path cost: `g(s') = g(s) + C(a) + P(s')`.
 - **Action Cost (C(a)):** This depends on the primitive: linear with distance for `Move`, fixed for `Rotate`, and a combination of baseline effort `λ_p` and `M'[x,y]` at the contact point `(x,y)` for `Push` (Equation 5).
 - **Gripper Placement Penalty (P(s')):** This term penalizes undesirable end-effector poses. It includes: (i) average cost of `M'` voxels overlapped by the gripper, (ii) a collision penalty for gripper contact with low-cost objects, and (iii) a proximity penalty for closeness to high-cost objects. This ensures gripper safety and encourages lower-cost regions.
- **Trajectory Conversion:** The 2D planned path is converted back to 3D for execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluated IMPACT through extensive experiments in both simulation (PyBullet) and real-world settings, using quantitative metrics and a human user study.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Setup:** 20 cluttered scenes using a hybrid object dataset (YCB and TRELLIS models for fragility), with three cameras for cost map generation. The tabletop cost was set to 10.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Collision-Free Planning:** A*, A* w/o direction, RRT, RRT* with strict collision avoidance.
 - **VLM Cost + RRT/RRT*/A* w/o direction/A* (IMPACT):** These methods utilize VLM-generated costs. IMPACT specifically uses the anisotropic cost map and contact-aware A*.
 - **Language-Conditioned Path Planning (LAPP):** A baseline allowing collisions based on language instructions.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - `reach target`: Boolean indicating if the end-effector reached within 0.01m of the target.
 - `path cost`: Sum of VLM-assigned costs of all collided obstacles.
 - `contact duration`: Total time robot was in contact with obstacles.
 - `displacement`: Displacement of each object, particularly unsafe ones (pre-selected human annotator).
 - `Success Rate`: Defined as reaching the target with `path cost < 10`, `contact duration < 100s`, and `unsafe object displacement < 10cm`.
- **Simulation Results (Table I):**
 - IMPACT achieved the highest success rate (73.75%) compared to all baselines.
 - IMPACT exhibited lower path cost (5.93), shorter contact duration (4.18s), and smaller displacement of unsafe objects (2.51cm) among VLM-cost methods.
 - Collision-Free baselines had significantly lower success rates (e.g., A* at 15.75%), often failing to find paths or leading to unexpected collisions due to restrictive constraints.
 - `VLM Cost+A* w/o direction` performed worse than IMPACT (57.50% success vs. 73.75%), highlighting the benefit of the anisotropic cost map.
 - LAPP showed a lower reach target and success rate (25.00%), attributed to its tendency to push obstacles that prevent target access, leading to higher unsafe object displacement.
- **Ablation Study (Table II):** When all object costs were set to 0 (allowing all collisions), success rates decreased across all planners (e.g., IMPACT dropped from 73.75% to 65.00%). This demonstrates the importance of VLM-generated semantic costs in guiding safer and more effective contact.

#### 4.2. Human User Study (Simulation and Real World)

- **Methodology:** 25 participants evaluated 100 video pairs (simulation) and 10 video pairs (real-world) without knowing the underlying algorithm. They selected the "most acceptable" trajectory or "Cannot Decide."
- **Results (Fig. 6):** In simulation, VLM-assigned cost methods (VLM Cost + RRT, RRT*, A*, and IMPACT) were preferred over their Collision-Free counterparts. IMPACT was consistently preferred over LAPP and its Collision-Free A* baseline. This indicates that VLM-informed planning aligns better with human notions of acceptable contact.

#### 4.3. Physical Experiments

- **Setup:** Franka Panda arm, Intel RealSense D435 cameras, Grounded SAM2 for real-world object segmentation and cost map generation. No sim-to-real transfer was involved for IMPACT.
- **Baselines:** IMPACT and LAPP. LAPP was fine-tuned on a small real-world dataset (25 images) with explicit language-conditioned collision rules. Test scenes included both "seen" (during LAPP fine-tuning) and "unseen" objects.
- **Real-World Results (Table III):** IMPACT achieved a higher success rate (61%) compared to LAPP (49% for seen objects, 40% for unseen objects). The performance gap was larger for unseen objects, suggesting IMPACT's stronger generalization ability due to its zero-shot VLM approach.
- **Real-World User Study (Fig. 6):** IMPACT was preferred over LAPP in real-world scenarios, reinforcing the alignment with human preferences.
- **Failure Cases (Fig. 8):** Identified failures include the robot getting stuck on obstacles, gripper collisions during rotation, and VLM-predicted costs misaligning with human preferences.

In summary, the results indicate that IMPACT effectively leverages VLMs to interpret environmental semantics for contact tolerance, leading to improved task success rates and trajectories that are perceived as more acceptable by humans in cluttered environments. Its zero-shot nature also contributes to better generalization in real-world settings compared to fine-tuned alternatives.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The IMPACT framework represents a significant step towards enabling robots to operate more effectively and intuitively in densely cluttered real-world environments. Its primary significance lies in formalizing and operationalizing the concept of "semantically acceptable contact" through the integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with contact-aware motion planning.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**

- **Semantic Understanding for Contact:** By leveraging VLMs like GPT-4o, IMPACT automates the inference of object contact tolerances, eliminating the need for explicit human programming or language instructions for each object. This ability to imbue robots with common-sense knowledge about object fragility and stability is crucial for intelligent interaction in unstructured settings.
- **Anisotropic Cost Maps:** The development of an anisotropic cost map that incorporates directional push safety is a novel approach. This allows the robot to understand not just *whether* an object can be touched, but *how* and *where* to touch it to achieve a safe and effective displacement. This is critical for preventing cascading collisions or unintended damage.
- **Enhanced Robot Capabilities in Clutter:** The experimental results demonstrate that IMPACT significantly improves task success rates in cluttered environments where collision-free paths are often infeasible. This extends the operational envelope of robots, enabling them to tackle more complex manipulation tasks in human-centric spaces.
- **Alignment with Human Preferences:** The human user study consistently showed a preference for IMPACT-generated trajectories. This indicates that the VLM-informed semantic reasoning leads to robot behaviors that are perceived as more natural and acceptable by humans, a key factor for human-robot collaboration and deployment in sensitive environments.
- **Generalization and Zero-Shot Learning:** IMPACT's zero-shot VLM inference capability allows it to generalize to novel objects and scenes in the real world without extensive re-training or fine-tuning, a distinct advantage over methods requiring explicit object labeling or fine-tuning on new object sets. This reduces the deployment cost and increases adaptability.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Broadened Applications:** IMPACT could enable robots to perform a wider range of tasks in domestic settings (e.g., retrieving items from cluttered cabinets), industrial environments (e.g., assembly in confined spaces), or assistive robotics (e.g., helping individuals in complex home environments).
- **Improved Safety and Efficiency:** By intelligently determining when and how to make contact, robots can execute tasks more efficiently, avoiding lengthy detours, while simultaneously minimizing the risk of damage to valuable or fragile objects.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work inspires future research into more sophisticated VLM-robotics integrations, particularly for nuanced physical reasoning. It also highlights the need for closed-loop, reactive planning that can adapt to unexpected disturbances during contact-rich tasks.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

- **Open-Loop Execution:** IMPACT currently follows trajectories open-loop, limiting its ability to react to real-time disturbances or dynamic changes in the environment. Future work could integrate closed-loop control and active perception to address this.
- **Partial Observability:** The framework relies on relatively complete RGBD observations, which might be challenging under severe occlusions. Research into robust planning under partial observability or active sensing strategies could mitigate this.
- **VLM Reliability:** While VLMs offer broad knowledge, their predictions are not infallible. Refining VLM prompts or incorporating uncertainty quantification into cost assignments could improve robustness against VLM prediction errors.

Overall, IMPACT contributes a robust and semantically-aware framework for contact-rich motion planning, pushing the boundaries of what robots can achieve in complex, cluttered environments and setting a foundation for more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
