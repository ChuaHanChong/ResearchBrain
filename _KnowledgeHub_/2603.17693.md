# Learning Transferable Temporal Primitives for Video Reasoning via Synthetic Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> SynRL introduces a post-training framework that uses programmatically generated synthetic videos with verifiable ground-truth annotations to teach fundamental temporal primitives to Vision-Language Models. This approach significantly enhances VLM temporal reasoning across diverse real-world video benchmarks while achieving a 21x improvement in data efficiency compared to methods relying on noisy model-generated data.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often struggle with the temporal dynamics inherent in videos, relying on static cues rather than true temporal integration.
- Existing video datasets lack temporal-centricity, allowing models to answer questions from isolated keyframes without requiring deep temporal reasoning.
- Proprietary large multimodal models used for generating training data exhibit systematic errors in fundamental temporal perception, propagating flaws into training signals.

## Method

- A ==programmatic generation pipeline== creates temporal-centric synthetic videos (short-term motion, long-term perception) with precise, verifiable ground-truth annotations.
- A four-stage ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT) augmentation pipeline== generates, verifies, reflects, and polishes CoT annotations based on procedural metadata, ensuring high quality.
- A two-stage training strategy involves ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== with high-quality synthetic CoT data, followed by ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)== with ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== for robust refinement.

## Results

- **SynRL**-trained models achieved consistent performance improvements across **15** diverse real-world video benchmarks, including general understanding, temporal grounding, and complex reasoning tasks.
- The framework demonstrated a **21x** improvement in data efficiency, with **7.7K** synthetic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) samples outperforming **165K** noisy real-world CoT samples on multiple benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of initial Supervised Fine-Tuning and showed that iterative Reinforcement Learning with progressively challenging synthetic videos yielded consistent performance gains and higher ceilings, fostering explicit temporal analysis patterns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abstract temporal primitives learned from simple, programmatically generated synthetic videos can effectively transfer and generalize to complex real-world video reasoning tasks.
> - The verifiable correctness and quality of training data for temporal reasoning are more critical for model performance than the sheer volume of potentially noisy or flawed data.
> - A two-stage training approach, starting with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data before Reinforcement Learning (RL), is crucial for robust temporal skill acquisition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of vision-language models (VLMs) is undergoing a transition from static image understanding to dynamic video comprehension. This shift necessitates that VLMs acquire capabilities for reasoning over temporal dynamics, such as motion trajectories, speed changes, and state transitions, which are distinct from static pattern recognition. Current post-training methods for enhancing video understanding often rely on large-scale datasets and model-generated annotations.

The paper identifies two critical limitations within this existing landscape:
1. **Lack of Temporal-Centricity in Datasets:** Many prevalent video datasets contain questions that can be answered by analyzing isolated keyframes, thereby allowing models to bypass true temporal integration and rely on static visual cues. This prevents the development of robust temporal reasoning skills.
2. **Systematic Errors in Proprietary Model-Generated Data:** Contemporary approaches frequently utilize proprietary large language models (LLMs) or VLMs (e.g., GPT-4V, Gemini-2.5-Pro) to synthesize training data, either by generating question-answer pairs or Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations. However, the paper demonstrates that these proprietary models exhibit systematic failures in fundamental temporal perception, such as misidentifying motion directions or misjudging speeds. When such flawed descriptions are used to create training data, these errors propagate, leading to "fluent-but-wrong" reasoning styles that compromise the supervisory signal for learning.

This research positions itself as an effort to address these challenges by proposing an alternative data generation and training paradigm. Instead of relying on real-world datasets that may lack temporal focus or proprietary models prone to temporal errors, the authors advocate for the use of programmatically generated synthetic videos. This approach aims to provide high-quality, temporal-centric, and verifiable ground-truth annotations, thereby enabling models to learn fundamental temporal primitives more effectively and efficiently. The work seeks to establish a new methodology for VLM post-training that prioritizes the quality and specificity of temporal supervisory signals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed deficiencies in current methods for training Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to understand video. The authors highlight that while VLMs have made progress in image understanding, they often struggle with the temporal dynamics inherent in videos. This struggle is attributed to two core issues: datasets that do not adequately challenge temporal reasoning, and model-generated training data that propagates factual errors in temporal perception.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Address Deficiencies in Temporal Reasoning for VLMs:** The overarching goal is to equip VLMs with robust temporal understanding capabilities, moving beyond static pattern recognition to interpret motion trajectories, speed changes, and state transitions. This requires the development of "temporal primitives," which are defined as the fundamental building blocks of temporal perception, including concepts like direction, speed, acceleration, and state tracking.
- **Overcome Limitations of Existing Datasets:** The authors motivate their work by pointing out that many existing video datasets are not "temporal-centric." This means that answers to questions can often be inferred from single keyframes, rather than requiring the model to integrate information across a sequence of frames or reason about dynamic changes. The objective is to create data where holistic temporal integration is a prerequisite for correct answers.
- **Mitigate Errors from Proprietary Model-Generated Training Data:** A significant motivation is the identification of systematic temporal perception errors in proprietary large multimodal models (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro). These models, when used to generate CoT annotations or QA pairs for training, produce fluent but factually incorrect temporal descriptions, which then "poison" the training signal. The objective is to bypass this dependency on potentially flawed model-generated data by creating training data with guaranteed ground-truth annotations.
- **Introduce SynRL: A Framework for Learning Temporal Primitives:** The paper aims to present SynRL, a novel post-training framework designed to teach models these fundamental temporal primitives. The motivation behind SynRL is the hypothesis that abstract temporal primitives, despite being learned from simple, programmatically generated synthetic videos, can effectively transfer to complex real-world scenarios.
- **Decompose Temporal Understanding into Learnable Primitives:** A specific objective is to break down complex temporal understanding into more manageable, learnable components. This involves categorizing primitives into short-term perceptual skills (e.g., direction, speed) and long-term cognitive skills (e.g., state tracking, retrodictive inference). This decomposition guides the design of the synthetic video generation process.
- **Develop a Programmatic Generation Pipeline for High-Quality Data:** The motivation for programmatic generation is to obtain precise, frame-level procedural metadata (state snapshots, event timestamps, operation sequences). This metadata serves as the ground truth for annotations, ensuring verifiable correctness and eliminating the reliance on external models for labeling.
- **Demonstrate Transferability and Data Efficiency:** A crucial objective is to empirically validate that skills learned from abstract synthetic videos generalize to diverse real-world video benchmarks. Furthermore, the work aims to show that this synthetic data approach offers a more cost-efficient scaling path, achieving comparable or superior performance with significantly less data than methods relying on large-scale real-world datasets or model-generated annotations.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for more robust and accurate temporal reasoning in VLMs, addressing current data quality and annotation challenges through a novel synthetic data generation and training strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SynRL framework is structured into three main stages: Programmatic Generation, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Augmentation, and a two-stage Training Strategy. The core idea is to create temporal-centric synthetic videos with verifiable ground-truth annotations to teach fundamental temporal primitives.

#### 3.1. Programmatic Generation Pipeline

This stage focuses on creating synthetic videos and their corresponding ground-truth annotations through code. It distinguishes between short-term and long-term temporal primitives.

- **4.1.1. Short-Term Motion Videos:**
 - **Purpose:** To train fundamental perceptual abilities over brief time windows, requiring attention to frame-by-frame changes rather than static pattern recognition.
 - **Categories:** 12 types of videos are generated, including: collision event counting, direction identification, trajectory shape recognition (linear, circular, zigzag), speed perception, motion counting, attribute change detection (color, size, shape), rotation perception, relative position tracking, acceleration detection, velocity comparison, distance estimation, and sequential event ordering.
 - **Generation Process:**
 - Object properties (shape, color, size, position, velocity) are initialized programmatically.
 - Motion is simulated using basic physics equations, with iterative updates to positions and detection of boundary collisions.
 - Key events (e.g., wall contacts, direction changes) are tracked with frame-level timestamps.
 - Ground-truth answers are derived directly from these simulation logs (e.g., collision counts, trajectory analysis, angular displacement).
 - Videos are rendered at 30 FPS using Python's Matplotlib library and encoded as MP4 (H.264).

- **4.1.2. Long-Term Perception Videos:**
 - **Purpose:** To evaluate sustained reasoning over extended sequences where intermediate states are often latent, forcing models to maintain internal representations and perform step-by-step reasoning.
 - **Categories:** 6 scenario types are implemented: abstract data structure tracking (card stacks, chip containers, file systems, mathematical operations), grid-based object tracking with visual occlusion (shell games), and retrodictive identity inference (sliding number puzzles).
 - **Structure:** Each video typically shows an initial state (S0), followed by a sequence of state-changing operations {o1..., oT}, leading to a final state (ST). Crucially, only S0 or ST is visible, while the operation sequence is fully observable. This design prevents simple state comparison and necessitates internal simulation.
 - **Questions:** Target different reasoning modes: forward prediction (S0 + operations -> ST), retrodictive inference (ST + operations -> S0 by reversing), and historical queries (reconstructing intermediate states).
 - **Generation Process:**
 - Videos are programmatically generated using Python's Matplotlib.
 - Each video comprises three phases: initial reveal (S0 or blank), operation sequence (animated transitions), and final reveal (ST or blank).
 - Complete state histories with frame-indexed metadata (operation timings and effects) are maintained.
 - Multiple-choice question distractors are automatically generated by applying random alternative operations.
 - Videos are composited and encoded using FFmpeg.

#### 3.2. Chain-of-Thought Augmentation

This stage creates high-quality CoT annotations by leveraging the procedural metadata from the programmatic generation.

- **Four-Stage Pipeline:**
 1. **Generation:** A multimodal model (Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B) is prompted with the synthetic video, question, ground-truth answer, and the complete event timeline (metadata). It generates step-by-step reasoning that explicitly references temporal events.
 2. **Verification:** A separate LLM evaluator (Qwen3-235B-A22B) validates the generated CoT. It checks if the CoT: (1) leads to the correct final answer, and (2) accurately follows the event sequence with proper temporal alignment to the metadata.
 3. **Reflection:** If verification fails, the evaluator provides detailed feedback to the generator. Up to 5 iterative refinement attempts are allowed; failing CoT chains are filtered out.
 4. **Polishing:** Verified CoT chains are then refined by an LLM (Qwen3-235B-A22B) to improve naturalness and fluency while strictly preserving logical content and temporal dependencies.
- **Data Size:** This process generates 6.7K CoT samples (and 7K RL samples, preventing overlap in questions).

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

The model undergoes a two-stage training process:

- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Data:** 7.7K mixed CoT samples, consisting of the generated synthetic CoT data (6.7K) and 1K real-world video-QA pairs from LLaVA-Video. The LLaVA-Video samples are included without their original CoT annotations to avoid propagating potential errors from flawed reasoning chains in real-world data.
 - **Objective:** To teach the model explicit, step-by-step temporal reasoning structures.
 - **Curriculum:** Synthetic videos receive full CoT supervision, while general videos only receive direct answer supervision. This strategy aims to ensure that the model learns high-quality temporal reasoning from synthetic data without being contaminated by potentially flawed CoT from general datasets.

- **Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
 - **Data:** Approximately 7K synthetic video-QA pairs, covering both short-term and long-term scenarios.
 - **Algorithm:** GRPO is applied. The KL regularization term and entropy loss are removed to allow for more aggressive policy updates.
 - **Objective:** To refine the model's reasoning correctness, building upon the structured reasoning learned during SFT. The verifiable ground-truth nature of the synthetic data provides perfectly correct reward signals, allowing the model to improve under strictly reliable supervision.
- **Base Models:** Experiments are conducted using Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-4B/8B as base models.
- **Implementation:** All experiments run on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs. Training uses a global batch size of 512 and a learning rate of 1e-6. Nucleus sampling (temperature 1.0, 8 candidate responses) is used during training for exploration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the effectiveness and efficiency of using synthetic videos for training temporal reasoning in VLMs.

- **Strong Generalization to Real-World Benchmarks:**
 - Despite being trained predominantly on synthetic videos, the SynRL-trained models demonstrated consistent performance improvements across 15 diverse real-world video benchmarks (Tables 1, 2, 3, 4).
 - On general video understanding tasks, gains were observed on VideoMMMU and vinoground, indicating transferability of temporal skills to complex natural videos.
 - Temporal grounding tasks, specifically NExT-GQA, RexTime, and Charades-STA, showed substantial improvements in localization precision metrics (R@0.3, R@0.5, R@0.7, mIoU, mIoP). For example, SynRL achieved an increase of +12.6% on RexTime. These results suggest that the precise temporal annotations from programmatic generation foster robust temporal grounding.

- **Robust Task Transfer Across Capabilities:**
 - Improvements spanned temporal grounding, complex reasoning, and general video understanding.
 - Temporal reasoning benchmarks (TOMATO, TemporalBench, Video-TT, VCR-Bench, CVBench, TVBench, Minerva) showed enhanced performance, with notable gains on tasks requiring cross-video understanding.
 - General video QA benchmarks (MVBench, AoTBench) also improved. This indicates that the temporal-centric design of synthetic data forces models to develop fundamental temporal understanding that generalizes across different task types and does not compromise broader comprehension abilities.

- **Data Efficiency and Quality Over Quantity:**
 - The 7.7K synthetic CoT samples used in SynRL achieved superior results compared to Video-R1’s 165K real-world CoT samples, representing a 21x improvement in data efficiency (Table 6).
 - A comparative ablation (Table 6) showed that initializing with Video-R1’s 165K model-generated data led to performance degradation on several benchmarks, attributed to systematic errors and hallucinations in its reasoning chains. In contrast, SynRL’s 7.7K high-quality synthetic samples yielded consistent improvements, demonstrating that the verifiable correctness of training data is more impactful than sheer volume for temporal reasoning.

- **Ablation Studies and Analysis:**
 - **Necessity of Cold Start:** Ablations (Table 5) confirmed that an initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) phase with high-quality CoT data is essential before Reinforcement Learning (RL) training. Without this cold-start, models struggled to learn complex temporal reasoning from sparse reward signals.
 - **Efficient Scaling with Synthetic Videos:** Scaling analysis (Table 7, Figure 6) indicated that iterative RL training with progressively challenging synthetic videos (e.g., increased duration and state transitions) led to consistent performance gains and a higher performance ceiling compared to simply increasing data volume in a single round. No saturation was observed up to 50K samples.
 - **Emerging "Think with Time" Patterns:** Qualitative examples (Figure 5) illustrated that the SynRL-trained model exhibited more explicit and accurate temporal analysis in real-world scenarios. For instance, it correctly identified motion direction and speed by tracking frame-by-frame changes and referencing temporal landmarks, unlike the base model which made errors based on superficial visual cues.
 - **Synthetic vs. Real-World Data Comparison:** A controlled experiment (Table 8) demonstrated that training solely with real-world data containing noisy model-generated annotations led to performance degradation. Synthetic-only training outperformed this noisy real-world data, and a mixed training approach (synthetic CoT + real-world direct answers) achieved the best overall performance, combining precise temporal reasoning with visual diversity.
 - **Per-Category Ablation:** Decomposing synthetic data into spatial, temporal, short-term, and long-term primitives (Table 9) showed that each primitive type contributed to specific capabilities, with the full SynRL model providing complementary skill development across categories.
 - **Pass@k Analysis:** Pass@k performance (Table 10) revealed that SynRL exhibited a larger improvement margin from Pass@1 to Pass@4, particularly on complex reasoning tasks, suggesting an increased potential for exploring correct reasoning pathways.
 - **Effect of CoT Supervision:** An ablation (Table 11) indicated that CoT warm-up accelerated RL learning efficiency, making 7K CoT+RL training competitive with 15K RL-only training. While RL still benefited without CoT given more data, explicit CoT supervision provided a data-efficient shortcut for acquiring temporal primitive skills.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework, SynRL, that addresses identified limitations in current VLM post-training paradigms, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Paradigm Shift for Video Post-Training:** The work establishes a new paradigm where systematic temporal learning can be effectively achieved through carefully designed synthetic data. This contrasts with previous approaches that relied heavily on either real-world datasets, which may lack temporal-centricity, or large language models for annotation, which can introduce systematic errors in temporal perception.
- **Enhanced Temporal Reasoning in VLMs:** By teaching fundamental temporal primitives—such as direction, speed, and state tracking—through controlled synthetic environments, SynRL enables VLMs to move beyond static pattern recognition and develop a more robust understanding of dynamic video content. This capability is critical for a wide range of video applications, including surveillance, autonomous driving, content analysis, and human-computer interaction.
- **Improved Data Quality and Reliability:** The programmatic generation pipeline ensures that synthetic videos come with guaranteed ground-truth annotations, eliminating the propagation of errors seen in model-generated data. This approach provides a reliable supervisory signal that is crucial for learning complex reasoning skills accurately.
- **Increased Data Efficiency:** The research demonstrates that 7.7K synthetic CoT samples can outperform 165K real-world samples with model-generated annotations, achieving a 21x improvement in data efficiency. This finding suggests a more cost-effective and scalable path for VLM training, reducing the immense resources typically required for acquiring and annotating large-scale real-world video datasets.
- **Addressing the "Think with Time" Challenge:** The qualitative results, particularly the emergence of "think with time" patterns, indicate that SynRL-trained models develop a deeper, frame-by-frame temporal analysis capability that transfers to real-world scenarios. This moves models towards genuine temporal reasoning rather than superficial correlation.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The findings regarding the importance of cold-start SFT, the benefits of iterative curriculum-based RL scaling, and the superior quality of synthetic data over noisy large-scale real data provide valuable guidance for future research in VLM training and synthetic data generation. It highlights the potential of focused, high-quality synthetic data to complement or even outperform brute-force scaling with potentially flawed real-world data.
- **Broader Applicability of Synthetic Data:** The successful transferability of abstract primitives learned from simple geometric shapes to complex real-world scenarios suggests a broader applicability for synthetic data in teaching fundamental cognitive skills to AI models, potentially extending beyond video understanding to other domains where precise control over data generation is beneficial.
