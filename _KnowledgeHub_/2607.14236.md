# Never Too Late for Force: Accelerating VLA Post-Training with Reactive Force Injection

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named LIFT was developed to integrate 6D end-effector force feedback into pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, improving their performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks. This approach accelerated learning and achieved higher peak success rates, such as 0.825 for towel folding and 0.6 for book insertion, while preserving the VLA's general capabilities through a two-stage post-training pipeline.

## Problem

- Existing VLA policies primarily rely on visual observations, which become ambiguous in contact-rich manipulation due to occlusions or inaccurate depth, leading to brittle performance.
- Small force errors can cause execution deviations from offline demonstration distributions, degrading vision-only policy performance in tasks requiring precise contact.
- Large-scale collection of force-aware data for foundation model pretraining is costly, platform-dependent, and difficult to generalize, limiting the development of inherently force-aware VLAs.

## Method

- **LIFT** employs a two-stage training pipeline: initial offline pre-alignment with vision-only data, followed by online post-training with force-enabled corrective data collected via a ==DAgger loop== on a real robot.
- A ==reactive action expert== is grafted onto the VLA backbone, which uses ==causal force-injected cross-attention== to decode actions reactively within each chunk based on recent 6D end-effector force history.
- The framework preserves the pretrained VLA prior through ==output-equivalent reactive expert initialization== and ==zero-initializing== the force-injected cross-attention output, ensuring force feedback only influences the policy after learning.

## Results

- LIFT demonstrated accelerated learning and higher peak success rates compared to vision-only baselines across all tasks, achieving **0.825** for towel folding, **0.6** for book insertion, and **0.6** for Hanoi ring placement.
- Reactive force memory proved superior to single-frame force input, enhancing robustness and stability (e.g., LIFT's **0.6** peak for book insertion surpassed the single-frame baseline's **0.4** peak).
- The framework effectively preserved the VLA's original generalization capabilities, and online DAgger was found to be essential for robust reactive force injection, as offline-only training was insufficient for contact-rich tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reactive injection of high-frequency force memory, rather than single-frame input, is crucial for stable and effective real-time adaptation of actions within short execution chunks during contact.
> - It is possible to augment existing large pretrained models with a new modality by carefully designing architecture and initialization to preserve general capabilities while enabling specialized reactive control.
> - Effective post-training for force-aware manipulation requires jointly leveraging abundant offline vision-only data for broad task alignment and scarce, policy-dependent online force-enabled corrections for precise contact adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. Models such as RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA, and the π0/π0.5 family leverage large-scale vision-language priors from web and robot trajectory data to achieve language-conditioned scene understanding and general action generation. These foundation models are effective in transferring broad manipulation knowledge across various tasks.

However, a primary limitation of these VLA policies is their reliance on visual observations. In contact-rich manipulation scenarios, visual information can be ambiguous due to occlusions, partial observations, or inaccurate depth perception. Small force errors can also lead to execution deviations from the offline demonstration distribution, making purely vision-driven policies brittle in real-world contact states. Force sensing, conversely, offers direct physical feedback, provides insights into contact history, and is crucial for reactive control and robust policy learning in such situations.

Integrating force and torque feedback into large-scale pretraining efforts presents challenges due to the cost of data collection, dependence on specific robot platforms and end-effector hardware, and variability across different setups. This makes it difficult to scale force-aware data collection to the same extent as visual data.

This paper positions its work, LIFT, as a solution for force-aware *post-training* of existing VLA policies. Instead of developing a new foundation model from scratch, LIFT aims to adapt a pretrained VLA to a specific robot platform and its sensor stack, particularly incorporating 6D end-effector force feedback. The research addresses key questions regarding how to reactively inject high-frequency force information, how to preserve the valuable pretrained VLA prior during this adaptation, and how to effectively combine abundant vision-only data with scarce, policy-dependent online force data. By focusing on post-training, LIFT seeks to bridge the gap between general visual-linguistic manipulation capabilities and the precision required for physical interaction, leveraging the strengths of both without discarding existing knowledge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies in handling contact-rich manipulation tasks. While pretrained VLA models exhibit strong capabilities in language-conditioned scene understanding and general action generation, their primary reliance on vision becomes a drawback in scenarios involving physical contact.

Specifically, the authors highlight three main challenges:
1. **Visual Ambiguity in Contact States:** When manipulation tasks enter contact states, visual cues can become ambiguous. This includes situations where objects are occluded, depth perception is inaccurate, or partial observations hinder robust scene understanding. Force sensing provides direct, unambiguous feedback on physical interaction, which vision alone cannot reliably offer.
2. **Off-Distribution Execution:** Small errors in force estimation or execution can quickly push the robot's state away from the distribution observed in offline demonstrations. This covariate shift degrades policy performance, particularly in tasks requiring precise contact. Force feedback can help a policy remain robust or recover from such deviations.
3. **Difficulty of Large-Scale Force Data Collection:** Collecting synchronized force and torque data at the scale required for foundation model pretraining is resource-intensive. It is also highly dependent on specific robot platforms, end-effector hardware, and experimental setups, making generalization across diverse environments challenging.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out three technical objectives for its LIFT framework:

- **O1: Reactive force injection.** The objective is to enable the policy to react quickly to new contact events and maintain a useful force memory. This involves developing a mechanism to inject recent force memory into the policy, allowing it to adapt actions within a short execution chunk rather than committing to an open-loop sequence. The high-frequency, low-dimensional, and dynamics-coupled nature of force data necessitates a specific approach for its integration.

- **O2: Preservation of general capabilities.** The introduction of a new force-aware path could potentially disturb the existing pretrained VLA prior, leading to catastrophic forgetting of general manipulation knowledge. The objective is to design the architecture and initialization process such that the augmented policy preserves the pretrained VLA's general capabilities at initialization and throughout the adaptation phase, while still enhancing its ability to adjust actions based on contact.

- **O3: Simultaneous use of heterogeneous data.** Post-training often involves different types of data. There is typically an abundance of vision-only task-alignment data (e.g., from handheld devices), but scarce and highly policy-dependent force-enabled online corrections. The objective is to jointly exploit these two heterogeneous data sources effectively, ensuring the policy benefits from both broad visual knowledge and precise force-informed corrections, particularly when force distributions can shift sharply online.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LIFT framework, short for Late Reactive Injection of Force for VLA Post-Training, is designed to integrate 6D end-effector force feedback into pretrained VLA policies through a two-stage training pipeline and specific architectural and training innovations.

**Two-Stage Training Pipeline:**
1. **Stage 1 (Offline Pre-alignment):** A vision-only policy (π_V) is initially trained on a large, vision-only task-alignment dataset (D_v). This dataset is collected using a handheld device without a force sensor and helps the VLA learn coarse manipulation knowledge for the target tasks. During this stage, any force-related components of LIFT are masked out.
2. **Stage 2 (Online Post-training):** The system transitions to a real robot equipped with a 6D end-effector force sensor. An online DAgger loop is initiated to collect force-enabled corrective data (D_f) based on the robot's own policy rollouts. The post-training policy (π_V+F) is then continuously trained on a mixture of this online D_f and the static offline D_v dataset. This approach aims to mitigate covariate shift by collecting corrections from states visited by the current policy.

**Architectural Innovations to Address Objectives:**

- **O1: Reactive Force Injection:**
 - **Reactive Action Expert:** LIFT augments the pretrained π0.5 backbone by grafting a new "reactive action expert" alongside the original action expert. This new expert is designed to decode actions causally within each action chunk (from t to t+H-1), allowing for action updates as new sensor data arrives.
 - **Causal Force-Injected Cross Attention:** Recent 6D end-effector force history (F_t:t+H) is encoded into a "causal force memory" (m_t:t+H) using a single-layer causal GRU followed by a linear projection. This force memory is exclusively injected into the reactive action expert via a force-injected cross-attention mechanism. A latency-aligned causal mask is applied during cross-attention to ensure that each reactive action token only uses force measurements available up to a point, allowing real-time reaction without using future or outdated data.
 - **Cached Vision-Language Slow Context:** To enable rapid, within-chunk action updates, LIFT separates the computationally intensive vision-language context from the fast force-injected action update. The vision-language prefix and its KV cache are computed once at the beginning of each action chunk. Subsequently, within the chunk, only the latest latency-aligned force history is encoded and re-evaluated against the cached prefix, avoiding repeated full vision-language forward passes.

- **O2: Preservation of Pretrained VLA Prior at Initialization:**
 - **Output-Equivalent Reactive Action Expert:** To prevent disturbance of the pretrained VLA's capabilities, the weights of the newly introduced reactive action expert are initialized by copying the parameters from the original, pretrained action expert. The reactive action stream uses a "shifted causal attention" pattern (Figure 3). Each reactive token r_i attends to the vision-language prefix, *later* base-action tokens (a_i+1:H-1), and its own causal reactive prefix (r_0:i). This specific attention mechanism ensures that at initialization, with copied weights, the reactive token r_i effectively receives the same context as the base action token a_i in the original fully-attentive expert, making the reactive expert output-equivalent to the original VLA prior.
 - **Zero-Initialized Cross Attention Output:** The output projection of the force-injected cross-attention block is explicitly zero-initialized. This ensures that the force-injected residual update is exactly zero at the beginning of post-training (step 0). Consequently, force feedback does not influence the policy's output until the network learns a non-zero residual contribution during the post-training phase, thereby preserving the initial pretrained action output.

- **O3: Training with Heterogeneous Visual and Force Data:**
 - **Additive Flow-Matching Objective:** LIFT trains both the original and reactive action streams simultaneously using a shared additive flow-matching objective function. This allows gradients from both streams to accumulate and update shared parameters, anchoring the base stream to the pretrained prior while the reactive stream learns force-injected corrections. At inference, only the reactive action is sent to the robot.
 - **Selective Force Masking:** The training process accommodates both vision-only (D_v) and force-enabled (D_f) data. For vision-only batches, zero force placeholders are used, and the encoded force memory is masked before cross-attention, preventing gradients from updating the force encoder and force-injected attention. For online correction batches, measured force memory is active, updating the entire force pathway and reactive branch.
 - **Equal Sampling of Heterogeneous Data:** To balance the influence of both data sources, LIFT employs a 1:1 mixture of offline task-alignment batches and online corrective batches, following an adaptive symmetric sampling strategy. This ensures the model benefits from the complementary strengths of general visual knowledge and precise, on-policy force feedback.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of LIFT was evaluated across three real-robot manipulation tasks: towel folding, book insertion, and Hanoi ring placement. The experiments involved collecting stage-one vision-only data via a handheld device and stage-two force-enabled data using a Flexiv TDK (Teleoperation Development Kit) with a Flexiv Rizon 4S robot equipped with a 6D end-effector force sensor. Ten autonomous rollouts were used to evaluate each checkpoint.

**Policy Variants Compared:**
- **π0.5 w/ Online DAgger:** A vision-only VLA policy using the online DAgger loop, serving as a baseline for online post-training without force.
- **LIFT w/o Reactive Force Injection:** A variant of LIFT that uses a single-frame force input instead of reactive force memory, isolating the impact of force memory.
- **LIFT w/o Online DAgger:** LIFT trained only with offline data, without the benefits of continuous online updates and human corrections.
- **π0.5 w/ Offline Handheld Data:** A vision-only VLA trained solely on the larger offline handheld dataset, representing an offline-only adaptation baseline.
- **LIFT (Full Model):** The complete proposed framework.

**Q1: Does force accelerate or improve Post-Training?**
Across all three contact-rich tasks, LIFT demonstrated accelerated learning and achieved higher peak performance compared to vision-only post-training methods (π0.5 w/ Online DAgger).
- **Towel Folding:** LIFT achieved a peak success rate of 0.825 (at 2.8K samples) versus 0.725 for π0.5 w/ Online DAgger (at 3.1K samples). LIFT w/o Reactive Force Injection performed even better, peaking at 0.95. Force feedback was instrumental in resolving contact ambiguities, such as misestimating depth for grasping thin towels, leading to more reliable recovery from failed grasps.
- **Book Insertion:** LIFT reached the highest peak score of 0.6 (at 4.6K samples), while π0.5 w/ Online DAgger achieved 0.4 (at 5.6K samples). Force feedback was critical for precise contact during insertion into a constrained slot, enabling the policy to determine when to push, stop, or release, addressing vision-only failures like pushing after full insertion or releasing prematurely.
- **Hanoi Ring Placement:** LIFT achieved a peak success rate of 0.6 (at 1.7K samples) and maintained it, whereas π0.5 w/ Online DAgger peaked at 0.3. In this precision task with small clearances, force sensing allowed LIFT to identify and correct misalignment or jammed states, preventing continuous downward pushes that cause the ring to tilt.

**Q2: Does reactivity matter beyond single-frame force?**
Comparing LIFT with LIFT w/o Reactive Force Injection demonstrated the importance of reactive force memory.
- **Book Insertion:** LIFT's peak score of 0.6 surpassed the single-frame baseline's 0.4. The single-frame model often failed by continuously pushing after the book had bottomed out, lacking the historical force context to infer the completion of insertion and trigger release. LIFT utilized force memory to discern the contact phase, enabling appropriate actions.
- **Hanoi Ring Placement:** LIFT achieved 0.6 peak, while the single-frame baseline peaked at 0.5 but then significantly dropped. Instantaneous force feedback alone was susceptible to noise or sudden impact forces, leading to unstable motions. LIFT's reactive branch, integrating force memory over a short window, stabilized the force input and allowed for more robust, rapid action updates.

**Q3: Does LIFT preserve original VLA generalization?**
The evaluation of LIFT's final checkpoint under various generalization conditions (e.g., different objects, tablecloths, and lighting) indicated that LIFT largely preserved the original VLA's generalization capabilities. The performance remained similar across tested settings, suggesting that the prior-preserving initialization strategies (copied action expert, shifted causal attention, and zero-initialized cross attention) were effective in preventing catastrophic forgetting.

**Q4: Does online data matter for reactive force injection?**
The comparison with LIFT w/o Online DAgger showed that offline-only training was insufficient for robust reactive force injection. LIFT w/o Online DAgger consistently underperformed on all tasks, failing entirely on book insertion. This indicates that offline data often lack the specific force patterns encountered during real execution, and online DAgger's collection of corrections on on-policy failure states is crucial for adapting to policy-dependent contact distributions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents LIFT, a framework that addresses the challenge of integrating force feedback into pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies through a structured post-training approach. The work demonstrates that it is feasible to augment existing vision-driven VLA models with a physically grounded modality (force) without discarding their established general manipulation knowledge.

The significance of LIFT lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Robustness in Contact-Rich Tasks:** LIFT substantially improves the performance and learning speed of VLA policies in tasks that involve physical contact, such as towel folding, book insertion, and Hanoi ring placement. This enables robots to execute tasks requiring precise interaction, which are traditionally difficult for purely vision-based systems.
- **Effective Post-Training Strategy:** The framework provides a principled method for adapting large, generalist VLA models to specific robot platforms and sensor configurations. This is a practical approach that avoids the need to retrain foundation models from scratch, which would be prohibitively expensive and difficult for force-augmented data.
- **Prior Preservation:** The architectural design and initialization strategies ensure that the valuable, broad manipulation knowledge acquired during VLA pretraining is preserved, while new capabilities for force-aware reactive control are introduced. This mitigates the risk of catastrophic forgetting.
- **Optimal Data Utilization:** LIFT effectively combines diverse data sources – abundant offline vision-only data and scarce, policy-dependent online force-enabled corrections – ensuring that the policy benefits from both broad task alignment and specific contact-state adjustments. The emphasis on reactive force memory and online DAgger addresses key challenges in dealing with dynamic and unpredictable contact forces.

The potential impact of this work includes:
- **Broader Deployment of VLAs:** By making VLA policies more robust to physical interaction, LIFT contributes to their applicability in real-world scenarios beyond controlled, visually clear environments. This could accelerate the deployment of intelligent robotic systems in manufacturing, logistics, and service industries where contact is inherent.
- **Advancement in Robot Learning:** The methods for reactive force injection, prior preservation, and heterogeneous data training offer architectural and algorithmic insights that could inform future research in multimodal robot learning and adaptation.
- **Reduced Development Cycle:** Providing a post-training solution for force integration potentially reduces the development cycle for new robotic applications, as it leverages existing foundation models and focuses on targeted adaptation.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge two primary limitations of the current work:
- **Reliance on Human Corrections:** LIFT's online DAgger loop still depends on human intervention for collecting corrective data. This reliance can limit data throughput and scalability.
- **Single-Arm Manipulation:** The evaluation was limited to single-arm manipulation tasks. The applicability and performance of LIFT in multi-arm or more complex interaction scenarios remain unexplored.

**Future Directions:**
Future research could focus on reducing the human correction load, possibly through autonomous or semi-autonomous data collection methods, and expanding the evaluation to more diverse robot arms, force sensors, and end-effectors to demonstrate broader applicability.
