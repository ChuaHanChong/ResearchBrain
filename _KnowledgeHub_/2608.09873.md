# Sci-VBench: Evaluating Knowledge- and Reasoning-Intensive Video Generation in Science Domains

> [!summary] Summary
> Sci-VBench is a benchmark for knowledge- and reasoning-intensive text-to-video generation, with 1,253 expert-authored prompts spanning 60 subjects across four disciplines. Each prompt ships a reference guide and 1-5 anchored rubric, scored by VBench-based vision tools plus a rubric-conditioned MLLM judge. Benchmarking 16 frontier models shows perceptual scores saturating while scientific and causal correctness spreads from 1.24 to 3.34.

## Problem

- Prior video benchmarks measure perceptual quality, prompt alignment, and generic physical commonsense, never domain-specific scientific mechanisms that visually plausible clips can silently violate.
- Knowledge- and reasoning-intensive evaluation was studied mainly for video understanding, leaving generation without multidisciplinary tasks whose correctness demands expert curriculum knowledge and multi-step causal inference.
- Existing benchmarks release no reusable per-example evaluation specifications, so scoring scientific fidelity requires recruiting domain experts and stays inconsistent across raters and automatic metrics.

## Method

- ==Textbook-guided prompt annotation==: 61 expert annotators pick mechanism-driven curriculum concepts and write minimal prompts stating only setup and intervention, omitting outcomes so models must infer mechanism.
- ==Per-example evaluation specification== pairs a high-level reference guide (target concept, phase-based expected phenomena) with rubrics giving 1-5 observable scoring anchors for each of four dimensions.
- ==Hybrid automated protocol==: VBench-based Vision Tools score perceptual fidelity, while a ==rubric-conditioned MLLM judge== (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, three runs averaged) scores grounding, scientific-causal correctness, and spatiotemporal consistency.

## Results

- Releasing the specification lifts non-expert agreement with experts from **74.0** to **82.5** average Pearson correlation; independent expert re-rating gives Cohen's kappa **0.842**.
- Rubric-conditioned judging reaches **63.4** average correlation, beating VideoScore2 (**51.6**), VideoReward (**47.4**), and ETVA (**47.9**) on every dimension across three evaluator backbones.
- Across 16 models the perceptual proxy stays flat (**3.79-4.12**) while scientific-causal correctness spans **1.24-3.34**; prompt rewriting lifts correctness up to **51.7%** without closing the gap.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual realism saturates as a discriminator; mechanism-level correctness is where video generators actually differ, so benchmarks should score causal dynamics rather than appearance.
> - Externalizing expert knowledge as per-example rubrics makes judgment portable: non-experts and MLLM judges then approximate expert scores without domain training.
> - Better prompting recovers grounding and causal errors but barely fixes temporal incoherence, indicating consistency failures live in the generator, not the instruction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video generative models have demonstrated rapid advancements in visual fidelity, motion coherence, and controllability. Contemporary research in this field is increasingly shifting focus from whether a generated video appears plausible to whether it accurately represents specified events, constraints, and temporal dependencies. This shift is particularly critical in scientific domains, where visually convincing but mechanistically incorrect videos could misrepresent fundamental principles, such as violating conservation laws, reversing causal relationships, or depicting impossible state transitions. Such errors necessitate knowledge-grounded, temporally consistent synthesis that maintains mechanistic fidelity.

Existing evaluation benchmarks for video generation primarily concentrate on perceptual quality, prompt alignment, and compositionality. While some reasoning-oriented evaluation suites address generic physical commonsense, they generally do not extend to domain-specific scientific knowledge. Concurrently, knowledge- and reasoning-intensive evaluation has been extensively explored in the context of video understanding, but its application to video *generation* remains less developed. Although some recent work has begun to examine scientific reasoning in video generation, a comprehensive, multidisciplinary benchmark that assesses mechanistic understanding across various scientific disciplines and provides portable, expert-defined evaluation criteria has been identified as a missing component in the current research landscape. Sci-VBench aims to address this identified gap by providing a structured framework for evaluating the scientific and causal correctness of generated videos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that despite significant progress in visual realism, current video generative models often produce outputs that, while perceptually convincing, contain fundamental errors concerning scientific mechanisms and causal dynamics. This discrepancy highlights a critical need for evaluation methodologies that can assess the scientific validity of generated content, particularly in knowledge- and reasoning-intensive domains.

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:
- **Introduce a comprehensive benchmark:** The central objective is to present Sci-VBench, a new benchmark designed specifically for evaluating video generation tasks that demand significant scientific knowledge and reasoning capabilities across a broad spectrum of scientific disciplines.
- **Develop a robust and diverse dataset:** To support this evaluation, the researchers aimed to create a dataset comprising 1,253 expert-annotated examples. These examples are intended to span 60 distinct subjects across four core disciplines—Natural Science, Healthcare, Humanities & Social Sciences, and Engineering—ensuring wide domain coverage. Each example is formulated to require models to generate temporally rich videos grounded in scientific reasoning and domain-specific knowledge, moving beyond mere surface-level visual plausibility.
- **Establish a scalable and reproducible evaluation protocol:** A crucial objective is to define and validate a rubric-based evaluation protocol. This protocol aims to allow both non-expert human evaluators and Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM)-as-Judge systems to achieve a relatively high level of agreement with expert judgments. This design decision is intended to facilitate reproducible and scalable evaluation, mitigating the logistical challenges of relying solely on domain experts for large-scale assessments.
- **Benchmark frontier video generative models:** The project seeks to apply the developed benchmark and evaluation protocol to a selection of 16 frontier proprietary and open-source text-to-video models. This objective is to empirically assess their current capabilities in generating scientifically accurate and causally coherent videos.
- **Identify performance disparities and systematic limitations:** Through benchmarking, the researchers aim to analyze model performance across different evaluation dimensions. Specifically, they intend to identify whether advances in visual realism have translated into reliable modeling of scientific and causal dynamics, and to highlight any significant performance gaps between different types of models, such as proprietary versus open-source systems, particularly in reasoning-intensive tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The construction and evaluation process of Sci-VBench was designed around four desiderata: breadth of domain knowledge, depth of expert-level reasoning, completeness of spatiotemporal reasoning, and reliable and reproducible evaluation.

#### 3.1. Evaluation Dimensions

The benchmark defines four primary dimensions for evaluation:
- **Low-level Perceptual Fidelity (LPF):** Assesses the visual quality of the synthesized video, encompassing temporal coherence, motion dynamics, and the quality of individual frames.
- **Prompt Grounding (PG):** Determines if the video accurately instantiates the explicit conditions specified in the prompt, including key entities, initial states, spatial arrangements, and instruments.
- **Scientific and Causal Correctness (SCC):** Evaluates whether the generated dynamics are consistent with the scientific domain knowledge and causal mechanisms targeted by the prompt, as detailed in an accompanying reference guide.
- **Spatiotemporal Consistency (SC):** Examines if the video maintains a coherent temporal evolution and stable spatial relationships necessary for the intended mechanism to be interpretable.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction Pipeline

The Sci-VBench dataset was constructed through a multi-stage pipeline:
- **Preliminary Setup:**
 - **Subject Selection:** A user study involving 133 undergraduate and graduate students informed the selection of 60 subjects across four core disciplines: Natural Science (45.3%), Healthcare (23.9%), Engineering (22.6%), and Humanities & Social Sciences (8.2%). Subjects were chosen based on whether their core concepts were expert-level and visually verifiable through video evidence.
 - **Expert Annotator Recruitment and Training:** A total of 61 expert annotators (11 undergraduates, 45 graduate students, 5 authors), matched to subjects by expertise, were recruited. Each annotator underwent training on the annotation protocol.
- **Video Prompt Annotation:** Annotators crafted minimal prompts guided by canonical textbooks. Prompts specified only the observable initial setup and any explicit interventions, deliberately omitting expected mechanistic trajectories to compel models to infer the underlying scientific principles.
- **Evaluation Specification Curation:** To enable consistent scoring by non-experts, each prompt is accompanied by an evaluation specification:
 - **High-level Reference Guide:** This guide identifies the target concept(s), minimal mechanistic assumptions, and summarizes expected phenomena as a phase-based storyline. It also includes brief clarifications for non-expert evaluators.
 - **Evaluation Rubric:** Detailed 1-5 scoring anchors, tied to observable evidence, are provided for PG, SCC, and SC (per-example). LPF uses a single rubric across all examples for generic video-quality properties.
- **Data Quality Control:**
 - Every example underwent a second-pass review by an independent domain expert to verify clarity, visual testability, and mutual consistency of the prompt, reference guide, and rubric. Revisions were verified by an author.
 - An audit on 200 random examples involved a second annotator creating an independent specification. Two independent scorers then graded the same video under both specifications. Quadratic weighted Cohen’s kappa values of 0.79 for PG, 0.75 for SCC, and 0.73 for SC indicated strong agreement for independently authored specifications.
 - The benchmark is released with a full split (1,253 examples) and a testmini split (150 examples) for rapid iteration.

#### 3.3. Sci-VBench Evaluation Protocol

- **Human Evaluation:** Expert human ratings serve as reference labels. For each video, an expert in the corresponding discipline, distinct from the prompt author, assigns 1-5 scores across all four dimensions, guided by the prompt, video, and evaluation specification. Inter-expert agreement was measured at Cohen’s κ = 0.842 for reference label stability.
- **Automated Evaluation:**
 - **Low-level Perceptual Fidelity (LPF):** Assessed using VBench-based Vision Tools (VT), which aggregates six established metrics (Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Motion Smoothness, Dynamic Degree, Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality) into a single 1-5 composite score.
 - **Prompt Grounding (PG), Scientific and Causal Correctness (SCC), Spatiotemporal Consistency (SC):** Evaluated using a rubric-conditioned MLLM-as-Judge pipeline, specifically utilizing Qwen3.5-397B-A17B. The MLLM is provided with the prompt, video, reference guide, and the specific dimension's rubric, tasked with providing a justification and a 1-5 integer score. Scores are averaged over three independent runs to reduce variance.
- **Reliability Measurement:** The reliability of the evaluation protocol was assessed by measuring Pearson correlation with expert human ratings for:
 - Non-expert human evaluators, both with and without the evaluation specifications.
 - Different MLLM-as-Judge instantiations (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, Gemma-4-31B, Qwen3.5-9B).
 - Comparison against established automatic evaluation methods (VideoScore, VideoScore2, VideoReward, ETVA).

#### 3.4. Evaluated Models

The study benchmarked 16 text-to-video models: 8 proprietary (e.g., Sora-2, Gemini-Omni-Flash) and 8 open-source (e.g., HunyuanVideo-1.5, LTX-2). Proprietary models were evaluated on the testmini split due to cost, while open-source models were evaluated on both splits.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Reliability of Evaluation Protocol

- **Non-expert Agreement:** Providing evaluation specifications significantly improved non-expert human agreement with expert ratings across all dimensions. For Prompt Grounding, Pearson correlation rose from 74.1 to 83.3; for Scientific and Causal Correctness, it increased from 68.2 to 82.5; and for Spatiotemporal Consistency, it improved from 70.3 to 79.6. Non-experts utilizing the specification achieved closer agreement with experts than any MLLM-as-Judge instantiation tested.
- **MLLM-as-Judge Agreement:** Qwen3.5-397B-A17B demonstrated the highest correlations among the MLLM judges, and agreement generally increased with the MLLM's scale. Alignment was stronger for reasoning-centric dimensions (Prompt Grounding, Scientific and Causal Correctness) compared to Low-level Perceptual Fidelity and Spatiotemporal Consistency.
- **Comparison with Prior Automatic Methods:** The rubric-conditioned MLLM-as-Judge protocol showed higher correlation with expert ratings than prior methods such as VideoScore, VideoScore2, VideoReward, and ETVA across all dimensions.

#### 4.2. Model Performance

- **Discrepancy Between Appearance and Mechanism:** Automatic perceptual-quality scores (VT) were tightly clustered across all 16 models (3.79–4.12), while human ratings for Low-level Perceptual Fidelity showed a wider spread (2.66–3.76). In contrast, the reasoning-centric dimensions (Prompt Grounding, Scientific and Causal Correctness, Spatiotemporal Consistency) exhibited substantial performance differences. Automatic Scientific and Causal Correctness scores ranged from 1.24 to 3.34, and human Scientific and Causal Correctness scores ranged from 1.12 to 3.06. These results indicate that models vary more in their ability to correctly represent underlying scientific mechanisms than in generating visually plausible content.
- **Proprietary vs. Open-Source Performance Gap:** A notable performance gap was observed between proprietary and open-source models, particularly concerning Prompt Grounding and Scientific and Causal Correctness. Gemini-Omni-Flash achieved the highest automatic average score (3.38) and human average score (3.18). The leading open-source model, MiniMax-H3, scored 1.63 on Scientific and Causal Correctness, which is approximately half of the proprietary best. However, on Spatiotemporal Consistency, open-source models demonstrated comparable performance, with Wan2.2-5B achieving the highest automatic score (2.79) across all models.
- **Domain-Specific Strengths:** No single model demonstrated uniform strength across all disciplines. Gemini-Omni-Flash led in Engineering (3.33), Natural Science (3.13), and Humanities & Social Sciences (2.95), but Sora-2 performed best in Healthcare (2.97). This indicates that models may have varied proficiencies in preserving mechanisms across different scientific domains.

#### 4.3. Error Analysis

Errors observed in model generations were categorized into three groups:
1. **Poor Adherence to Instructions:** Models frequently misinterpreted core concepts or failed to render fine-grained details explicitly stated in the prompt, such as generating a bare fingertip touch instead of a momentary push-button switch.
2. **Inaccurate Simulation of Scientific Principles:** Models often prioritized visual aesthetics over scientific accuracy, leading to factually incorrect dynamics. Examples include depicting the incorrect leg kicking in a knee-jerk reflex or a lack of blinking response in a corneal reflex scenario.
3. **Deficiencies in Temporal Coherence and Visual Quality:** Generated videos exhibited issues such as objects merging or disappearing, discontinuous shifts in visual style (e.g., from realistic to animated), poor image quality with coarse textures, and weak or absent causal links between events (e.g., an LED switching without interaction from a motion sensor).

#### 4.4. Effect of Prompt Rewriting

An ablation study involving prompt rewriting (using Gemini-3-Flash to add visual concreteness without altering the scenario) demonstrated performance improvements for open-source models Wan2.2-5B and HunyuanVideo-1.5. The most significant gains were observed in Scientific and Causal Correctness (+23.3% and +51.7%), followed by Prompt Grounding (+12.4% and +26.7%). Spatiotemporal Consistency saw the smallest gains (+7.5% and +16.5%). While prompt rewriting helped, it did not eliminate the performance gap; HunyuanVideo-1.5's SCC score with enhanced prompts (2.20) remained below that of the strongest proprietary model with verbatim prompts (3.34). This suggests that some temporal failures and mechanistic inaccuracies are inherent to the generative models' capabilities rather than solely due to underspecified instructions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Sci-VBench contributes to the field of generative AI by establishing a benchmark that addresses the critical need for evaluating text-to-video models beyond surface-level visual realism. By focusing on knowledge- and reasoning-intensive scientific domains, the benchmark shifts the evaluation paradigm toward mechanistic fidelity and causally coherent temporal dynamics.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:
- **Advancing Evaluation Standards:** Sci-VBench provides a comprehensive and expert-driven framework for assessing the scientific validity of generated videos. The detailed, rubric-based evaluation protocol, validated for reproducibility with non-expert human and MLLM judges, offers a scalable method to conduct rigorous evaluations, reducing the dependence on specialized domain experts for routine assessments.
- **Guiding Model Development:** The benchmark's findings highlight that current text-to-video models, despite their progress in perceptual quality, still exhibit significant limitations in understanding and accurately simulating scientific principles and causal relationships. This identified gap between visual realism and scientific correctness points to specific areas for future research and development, particularly in improving instruction adherence, mechanistic consistency, and spatiotemporal stability.
- **Informing AI for Scientific Applications:** By challenging models to function as "expert-domain world simulators," Sci-VBench encourages the development of AI systems capable of generating scientifically accurate content. Such advancements could have implications for educational tools, scientific communication, and potentially for aiding scientific discovery by visualizing complex phenomena or experimental outcomes.
- **Characterizing Model Capabilities:** The benchmarking of 16 frontier models, revealing a pronounced performance gap between proprietary and open-source systems on reasoning-centric tasks, provides a clear characterization of the current state of the art. This insight can inform developers and researchers about the relative strengths and weaknesses of different model architectures and their capacity for scientific understanding.
- **Enabling Targeted Improvements:** The detailed error analysis, categorizing failures related to instruction adherence, scientific inaccuracies, and temporal inconsistencies, offers concrete diagnostic information. This level of detail can enable developers to prioritize specific areas for model improvement, moving beyond generic quality metrics to address fundamental flaws in scientific reasoning and world modeling.
