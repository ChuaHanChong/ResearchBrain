# Learn the Ropes, Then Trust the Wins: Self-imitation with Progressive Exploration for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Tencent Youtu Lab developed SPEAR, an adaptive reinforcement learning framework for large language model agents that balances exploration and exploitation using curriculum-scheduled self-imitation and intrinsic reward shaping. This approach yielded substantial success rate improvements, including up to 20.7% on WebShop and 19.6% on Sokoban, across diverse tasks and LLM scales with minimal computational overhead.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for LLM agents struggle with balancing exploration and exploitation, often leading to training instability, distribution shifts, and mode collapse when relying on policy entropy maximization.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or hybrid RL+SFT approaches, while improving stability, restrict an agent's ability to discover novel strategies beyond the initial training data.
- There is a need for an adaptive mechanism to dynamically schedule an agent's policy entropy, transitioning from broad skill-level exploration to focused action-level exploitation over time.

## Method

- **SPEAR** extends ==Self-Imitation Learning== (SIL) by incorporating prioritized experience replay with advantage recalibration to robustly learn from successful self-generated experiences, mitigating entropy collapse.
- It introduces curriculum-scheduled intrinsic reward shaping, using a progressively decaying tool-call reward to guide early skill-level exploration and later prioritize task completion.
- The framework integrates 'Dr.BoT', a suite of robust RL optimizations including covariance-based clipping, to stabilize training and prevent aggressive log-probability changes at the token level.

## Results

- SPEAR substantially improved success rates across diverse tasks, achieving up to **20.7%** on WebShop, **19.6%** on Sokoban, and up to **8.6%** on ALFWorld compared to strong baselines.
- It demonstrated efficacy in complex reasoning tasks, boosting performance by up to **6.1%** on AIME25 and enhancing search-augmented QA, indicating improved tool-integrated reasoning and problem-solving.
- The framework maintained policy entropy stability throughout training and incurred only 10-**25%** extra theoretical computational complexity, showcasing its efficiency and 'plug-and-play' scalability across LLM models from **1.5B** to **32B** parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An agent's policy entropy should dynamically evolve during training, starting with higher entropy for skill exploration and gradually converging for refined action exploitation.
> - Self-imitation, when strategically augmented with progressive experience utilization and advantage recalibration, can effectively guide LLM agents through complex tasks by reinforcing successful internal trajectories.
> - Progressively modulating intrinsic rewards, such as tool-call incentives, is crucial for encouraging early mastery of environment interaction while preventing reward hacking and optimizing for final task outcomes.
