# Learn the Ropes, Then Trust the Wins: Self-imitation with Progressive Exploration for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Tencent Youtu Lab developed SPEAR, an adaptive reinforcement learning framework for large language model agents that balances exploration and exploitation using curriculum-scheduled self-imitation and intrinsic reward shaping. This approach yielded substantial success rate improvements, including up to 20.7% on WebShop and 19.6% on Sokoban, across diverse tasks and LLM scales with minimal computational overhead.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for LLM agents struggle with balancing exploration and exploitation, often leading to training instability, distribution shifts, and mode collapse when relying on policy entropy maximization.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or hybrid RL+SFT approaches, while improving stability, restrict an agent's ability to discover novel strategies beyond the initial training data.
- There is a need for an adaptive mechanism to dynamically schedule an agent's policy entropy, transitioning from broad skill-level exploration to focused action-level exploitation over time.

## Method

- **SPEAR** extends ==Self-Imitation Learning== (SIL) by incorporating prioritized experience replay with advantage recalibration to robustly learn from successful self-generated experiences, mitigating entropy collapse.
- It introduces curriculum-scheduled intrinsic reward shaping, using a progressively decaying tool-call reward to guide early skill-level exploration and later prioritize task completion.
- The framework integrates 'Dr.BoT', a suite of robust RL optimizations including covariance-based clipping, to stabilize training and prevent aggressive log-probability changes at the token level.

## Results

- SPEAR substantially improved success rates across diverse tasks, achieving up to **20.7%** on WebShop, **19.6%** on Sokoban, and up to **8.6%** on ALFWorld compared to strong baselines.
- It demonstrated efficacy in complex reasoning tasks, boosting performance by up to **6.1%** on AIME25 and enhancing search-augmented QA, indicating improved tool-integrated reasoning and problem-solving.
- The framework maintained policy entropy stability throughout training and incurred only 10-**25%** extra theoretical computational complexity, showcasing its efficiency and 'plug-and-play' scalability across LLM models from **1.5B** to **32B** parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An agent's policy entropy should dynamically evolve during training, starting with higher entropy for skill exploration and gradually converging for refined action exploitation.
> - Self-imitation, when strategically augmented with progressive experience utilization and advantage recalibration, can effectively guide LLM agents through complex tasks by reinforcing successful internal trajectories.
> - Progressively modulating intrinsic rewards, such as tool-call incentives, is crucial for encouraging early mastery of environment interaction while preventing reward hacking and optimizing for final task outcomes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly expanding field of agentic Large Language Models (LLMs), where Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become the dominant paradigm for endowing LLMs with strategic tool-use capabilities to tackle long-horizon, sparsely-rewarded tasks. This aligns with the "reason-and-act" (ReAct) paradigm, which has powered diverse applications from robot navigation and mobile assistants to web navigators and deep searchers.

A fundamental challenge in applying RL to LLM agents is the delicate balance between exploration and exploitation. Agents need to leverage their pre-trained knowledge and past interactions (exploitation) while simultaneously discovering novel behaviors and tool-integrated reasoning (exploration). Existing approaches predominantly attempt to stimulate exploration by maximizing policy entropy. However, the authors argue that this "mechanical entropy maximization" is often fragile for LLM-driven agents. The multi-turn nature of interactions, coupled with low-probability token accumulation from environment feedback, can lead to severe distribution shifts, mode collapse, and training instability. While supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or hybrid RL+SFT schemes improve stability, they inherently limit the agent's ability to discover strategies beyond those present in the initial SFT corpus.

SPEAR directly addresses this critical gap by proposing an adaptive training framework designed to dynamically schedule the exploration-exploitation trade-off. It extends the concept of Self-Imitation Learning (SIL), which historically leverages successful past experiences for improvement, but specifically tailors it to mitigate the entropy collapse and instability issues prevalent in LLM agent training. By harmonizing SIL with intrinsic reward shaping and curriculum scheduling, SPEAR departs from purely entropy-based or SFT-limited exploration methods.

In the broader context of RL, SPEAR engages with several sub-areas:
- **RL Algorithms for LLMs:** The paper builds upon established algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), and GiGPO, and integrates "bag-of-tricks" to form a strong baseline (Dr.BoT). This signifies an iterative improvement over existing robust RL methods.
- **Optimization of LLM Agents:** It tackles specific challenges in optimizing LLMs for tool-use, multi-turn interactions, and reasoning, contrasting with methods like RAGEN (instance filtering), GiGPO (step-level advantages), and ARPO (adaptive trajectory branching). SPEAR's focus on progressive exploration through self-imitation offers a complementary or superior approach to these.
- **Exploration in RL:** Rather than relying on curiosity-driven, count-based, or skill acquisition methods that introduce external heuristics, SPEAR champions a curriculum-guided self-imitation approach. This allows the agent to intrinsically reinforce successful patterns based on its own experience, thereby avoiding handcrafted heuristics and better managing uncertainty in multi-turn interactions.
- **Experience Replay in RL:** SPEAR innovates upon classic Self-Imitation Learning (SIL), which is known to benefit long-horizon problems. The key contribution here is adapting SIL to prevent entropy collapsing in LLM agents, a limitation identified in vanilla SIL when applied to this context.

Thus, SPEAR positions itself as a significant advancement in robust and effective RL training for LLM agents, offering a novel solution to the exploration-exploitation dilemma by dynamically scheduling learning from self-generated successful experiences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **develop an adaptive training framework for LLM agents that can smoothly transition between exploration and exploitation, guided by the agent's own experiences, without succumbing to the extremes of entropy collapsing or runaway divergence.**

This core objective is motivated by several critical observations and a specific hypothesis:

- **Problem with Existing Exploration Methods:** Current RL approaches for LLMs often rely on maximizing policy entropy for exploration. However, in the context of multi-turn, tool-integrated agent tasks, this leads to instability, severe distribution shifts, and mode collapse. The accumulation of low-probability tokens from environment feedback makes entropy-based control fragile.
- **Limitations of Stability-Focused Methods:** While methods like cold-start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or hybrid RL+SFT improve training stability, they inherently compromise the policy's ability to discover novel strategies beyond what is present in the initial training data. This restricts the agent's true exploratory potential.
- **The Need for Adaptive Scheduling:** The authors hypothesize that an agent's policy entropy should not be static but rather maintain a dynamic, controlled range that evolves over time.
 - **Early Stage (Skill-Level Exploration):** At the beginning of training, increasing entropy is beneficial for broad skill-level exploration. The agent needs to rapidly develop tool-use capabilities, encounter unfamiliar observations, and engage in trial-and-error to understand the environment's dynamics.
 - **Later Stage (Action-Level Exploitation):** As training progresses and the agent gains familiarity, a shift towards converging entropy is required. This allows the agent to consolidate effective problem-solving heuristics and emphasize more refined action-level exploration, exploiting reward signals to select optimal actions and adapt to changing distributions for stability.
- **Leveraging Self-Generated Experience:** The motivation is to achieve this progressive balance by learning from the agent's *own successful experiences*, bypassing the costly and often domain-specific requirement for expert demonstrations. This self-imitation mechanism, if properly controlled, offers a scalable way to reinforce good behaviors.

In summary, SPEAR aims to answer the fundamental question of *how to intrinsically guide an LLM agent's learning trajectory from broad exploration to focused exploitation*, ensuring both stability and the discovery of advanced strategies through its own performance, without falling victim to the pitfalls of prior techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SPEAR (Self-imitation with Progressive Exploration for Agentic Reinforcement learning) is presented as a curriculum-based RL recipe designed to achieve a progressive exploration-exploitation balance. It fundamentally extends the vanilla Self-Imitation Learning (SIL) framework with several key innovations tailored for LLM agents. The underlying policy optimization algorithm is primarily based on GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), a variant of PPO.

The methodology can be broken down into the following components:

#### 3.1. Self-Imitation Learning (SIL) with Enhancements

SPEAR's core is self-imitation, where a replay buffer stores successful past experiences (state-action pairs with returns exceeding baselines) to encourage good actions and facilitate exploration of promising decision paths. To address the inherent challenges of SIL for LLM agents (particularly entropy collapse), SPEAR introduces:

- **Prioritized Experience Replay:** The replay buffer `D` stores trajectories $(\tau_j, R_j, \hat{A}_j)$ only if their advantages $\hat{A}_j$ are positive, ensuring that only "good" experiences are imitated.
- **Advantage Recalibration for Off-Policy Estimation:** To mitigate the off-policy nature of replayed experiences (where past returns might not reflect current policy values), SPEAR recalibrates advantages. It maintains a FIFO buffer `D_R` of intra-group baselines (average rewards) and uses the 50th percentile ($P_{50}(D_R)$) as a conservative yet robust estimate of the current policy's baseline performance (Equation 2). This avoids recomputing advantages from scratch and filters out outdated experiences that no longer yield positive advantages ($\tilde{A}_j \le 0$).
- **Progressive Experience Utilization with Curriculum Schedule:** A warm-up factor $\gamma$ is applied to the SIL term (Equation 5), ensuring that mechanical imitation of potentially immature early experiences is restricted. This $\gamma$ gradually increases from 0 to 1 over a `T_warm-up` period (Equation 13), allowing for initial broad exploration before emphasizing the consolidation of successful patterns.

#### 3.2. Intrinsic Reward Shaping with Curriculum Schedule

To guide skill-level exploration, SPEAR incorporates intrinsic rewards, primarily a tool-call reward that incentivizes interactions with the environment. This is crucial for familiarizing the agent with tool usage and accumulating diverse experiences.

- **Reward Composition:** The total reward $R_i$ for a trajectory $\tau_i$ is a compound of:
 - **Outcome Accuracy Reward ($R_{i}^{\text{outcome}}$):** A binary signal (1 for success, -1 otherwise) at the end of an episode (Equation 15).
 - **Continuous Tool-call Reward ($R_{i}^{\text{tool-call}}$):** Proportional to the number of valid tool calls, bounded to prevent reward hacking (Equation 16).
 - **Format Reward ($R_{i}^{\text{format}}$):** A small bonus for correct output formatting (Equation 17).
- **Progressive Reward Modulation:** A decay factor $\mu$ is applied to the tool-call reward (Equation 6), which gradually decreases from 1 to 0 over `T_decay` steps (Equation 14). This encourages initial tool mastery and exploration but prevents excessive tool calls that could hinder final outcome accuracy or exceed context limits, thus harmonizing tool learning with task completion.

#### 3.3. Dr.BoT (Bag-of-Tricks) as a Strong Baseline

To ensure a robust comparison and enhance the overall training process, SPEAR incorporates "Dr.BoT," a strong baseline built upon GRPO by integrating several industrial-validated RL optimizations:

- **Removal of KL Divergence:** Sets $\beta = 0$, allowing greater policy divergence for adaptation.
- **Clip-Higher:** Raises the upper clipping bound ($\epsilon_{ub} = 0.28$) in the PPO-style objective, promoting exploration of low-probability tokens.
- **Removal of Intra-group and Length Normalization:** Mitigates difficulty bias and simplifies advantage computation.
- **Filtering:** Masks out losses for over-long and "void-turn" (no tool calls) samples, improving training stability and preventing mode collapse.
- **Filtering of Low-Variance Groups:** Removes groups with low intra-group reward variance, ensuring policy updates are based on informative diverse behaviors.
- **Covariance-based Clipping:** A crucial regularization technique that, within the self-imitation objective, masks out tokens whose log-probabilities have high covariance with advantage gains (Equations 18-21). This prevents aggressive log-probability changes for advantage acquisition, stabilizing entropy and mitigating "reward hacking" at the token level.

The combined approach ensures that SPEAR effectively manages the exploration-exploitation balance by dynamically shaping the learning process through intrinsic rewards and self-imitation, grounded in robust policy optimization techniques. The methodology is designed to be "plug-and-play," making it compatible with existing RL algorithms for LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SPEAR demonstrates significant and consistent improvements across a diverse set of agentic tasks and LLM models, validating its effectiveness in balancing exploration and exploitation.

#### 4.1. Performance Gains Across Benchmarks

- **ALFWorld and WebShop:** SPEAR substantially boosts success rates for GRPO, GiGPO, and the Dr.BoT baseline. On ALFWorld, it increases GRPO/GiGPO/Dr.BoT success rates by up to 16.1%/5.1%/8.6%. On WebShop, these increases are even more pronounced, reaching up to 20.7%/11.8%/13.9%. These gains are observed across different Qwen2.5 model sizes (1.5B and 7B), highlighting its scalability and generalizability. The authors attribute this to the "walkthrough guide" effect of successful trajectories, which helps agents, especially smaller ones, accumulate tactics and reduce blind trials.
- **AIME24 and AIME25 (Code Interpreter Tasks):** For complex mathematical reasoning tasks requiring code interpreters, SPEAR improves Dr.BoT by up to 3.8% on AIME24 and 6.1% on AIME25, using Qwen2.5-32B and Qwen3-32B models. This confirms its efficacy in tasks requiring sophisticated tool-integrated reasoning and problem-solving.
- **Sokoban (Vision-Language Agents):** SPEAR shows strong generalization to visual agents, increasing success rates on the Sokoban puzzle game by up to 19.6% (GRPO) and 9.4% (Dr.BoT) for Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct. This indicates its applicability beyond purely text-based agents to multimodal settings demanding spatial comprehension and long-term planning.
- **Search-augmented QA Tasks:** On knowledge-intensive reasoning tasks (single-hop and multi-hop QA), SPEAR improves over the Search-R1 baseline, particularly on multi-hop QA datasets. The intrinsic reward encouraging multiple tool uses helps prevent arbitrary conclusions from limited searches, promoting detailed decomposition and cross-validation of complex queries.
- **Efficiency:** The observed performance gains come with a relatively low computational overhead, incurring only 10%–25% extra theoretical complexity and negligible runtime overhead in practice. This "plug-and-play scalability" makes SPEAR highly practical for real-world applications.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Self-Imitation (SI):** Ablations reveal that SIL alone consistently improves baselines, especially for models or tasks with low initial success rates (e.g., up to 17.4% on WebShop 1.5B GRPO). This underscores the value of learning from self-generated "good" experiences. However, on AIME24, SIL alone showed a slight performance drop, suggesting potential instability when not combined with intrinsic reward shaping, especially for reasoning-heavy tasks.
- **Intrinsic Reward (IR):** The intrinsic tool-call reward is shown to be critical, particularly for larger models and reasoning tasks like AIME. It encourages the transition from text-based to tool-integrated reasoning, promoting frequent tool calling, error correction, and self-reflection. Its curriculum schedule is vital for preventing reward hacking and optimizing for final outcome accuracy.

#### 4.3. Entropy Stability and Qualitative Insights

- **Policy Entropy:** While vanilla Dr.BoT often exhibits diverging policy entropy, SPEAR, due to its curriculum scheduling of self-imitation, maintains a steady entropy trend. It allows sufficient exploration early on (sometimes even increasing entropy beyond baseline) and then progressively strengthens the exploitation of successful experiences, resulting in smoother entropy variation during training.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Case studies demonstrate that agents trained with SPEAR develop more efficient coding skills (e.g., leveraging NumPy for matrix computation) and refine their action strategies (e.g., breaking down complex web searches into goal-oriented steps rather than pursuing "search query perfectionism"). This indicates a deeper understanding and more strategic behavior.
- **Context Budget Utilization:** Analysis on AIME tasks shows that SPEAR's design effectively manages tool-call frequency, even with increased context budgets (16K vs. 32K tokens). The agent focuses on quality reasoning and verification rather than excessive tool calls, with increased context allowing for longer thinking and reflection, contributing to performance gains.

In summary, SPEAR delivers substantial, generalizable, and efficient performance improvements for LLM agents across diverse challenging tasks by robustly managing the exploration-exploitation trade-off through its novel integration of curriculum-scheduled self-imitation and intrinsic reward shaping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SPEAR represents a significant step forward in the development of agentic Large Language Models, offering a robust and scalable solution to one of the most fundamental challenges in Reinforcement Learning: the exploration-exploitation dilemma. Its impact can be assessed from several angles:

#### 5.1. Addressing a Core RL Challenge for LLMs

The paper effectively tackles the instability and sub-optimal convergence often observed when applying traditional RL exploration techniques to LLM agents. By proposing a method that guides exploration and exploitation using the agent's *own experience* and a dynamic curriculum, SPEAR bypasses the limitations of fragile entropy-based methods and the strategic ceilings imposed by supervised fine-tuning. This enhances the practical applicability of RL for complex, multi-turn LLM agent tasks.

#### 5.2. Robustness and Training Stability

SPEAR's design, particularly the advantage recalibration, dual filtering, and covariance-based clipping, directly contributes to more stable RL training. It prevents policy entropy collapse, mitigates distribution shifts, and avoids excessive uncertainty that plagues existing methods. This increased robustness means less manual tuning and more reliable outcomes when training sophisticated LLM agents for various domains.

#### 5.3. Generalizability and Scalability

The "plug-and-play" nature of SPEAR, demonstrated by consistent performance gains across diverse tasks (embodied, web, code-interpreter, visual, search-augmented QA) and varying LLM scales (from 1.5B to 32B parameters), highlights its broad applicability. The minimal computational overhead (10-25% theoretical increase, negligible in practice) makes it an attractive and scalable solution for real-world industrial deployments, where efficiency is paramount. This can accelerate the adoption of RL for training highly capable LLM agents.

#### 5.4. Advancement in Self-Imitation Learning

SPEAR revitalizes and significantly extends the Self-Imitation Learning paradigm by specifically addressing its shortcomings when applied to LLM agents (e.g., entropy collapse). By integrating curriculum scheduling and robust advantage estimation, it enables agents to effectively learn from their own successes in complex, sparsely rewarded environments without being trapped by early, sub-optimal experiences.

#### 5.5. Strong Baseline for Future Research

The introduction of "Dr.BoT," a carefully engineered baseline combining established industrial RL optimizations, provides a valuable benchmark for future research in agentic RL. It consolidates many practical tricks, allowing new methods to be evaluated against a truly competitive and well-optimized foundation.

#### 5.6. Potential Impact on Agent Capabilities

By fostering a smoother and more effective learning trajectory, SPEAR has the potential to enable LLM agents to develop more advanced reasoning, strategic planning, and tool-integration skills. This could lead to more autonomous and intelligent agents capable of handling increasingly complex real-world problems in areas like software engineering, scientific discovery, and human-computer interaction.

#### 5.7. Future Research Directions

The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the vague definition of "good experiences" in noisy environments and the rigidity of current entropy control mechanisms. These limitations open promising avenues for future work, including dynamic scheduling (e.g., using entropy, performance, or curiosity as guiding mediums) and stepwise credit assignment (e.g., process reward models, meta-rewards, or world models). These directions could further enhance the adaptability and intelligence of RL-trained LLM agents.

In conclusion, SPEAR makes a substantial contribution by providing a practical, efficient, and highly effective framework for training LLM agents. Its innovative approach to managing exploration and exploitation through self-imitation and curriculum-scheduled intrinsic rewards positions it as a key development in enabling the next generation of capable AI agents.
