# Small Object Detection via Coarse-to-fine Proposal Generation and Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> CFINet, a two-stage framework for small object detection, is introduced, featuring a Coarse-to-fine RPN for improved proposal generation and a Feature Imitation branch for enriching feature representations. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on SODA-D and SODA-A benchmarks, showing particular improvements in detecting extremely small objects.

## Problem

- Existing object detection methods exhibit reduced performance on small objects due to their limited pixel areas.
- Difficulty in generating a sufficient quantity of high-quality region proposals for small objects, as conventional RPNs often miss them or produce low-quality candidates.
- Scarcity of discriminative information for small instances leads to uncertain predictions, and prior enhancement methods (e.g., GANs, static similarity learning) introduce complex training or risk feature collapse.

## Method

- Developed a ==Coarse-to-fine RPN (CRPN)== that uses an area-based dynamic IoU threshold for anchor selection and multi-level processing across ==FPN== layers to generate high-quality small object proposals.
- Integrated a ==Feature Imitation (FI)== branch that dynamically identifies "high-quality" exemplars based on an Instance Quality (IQ) indicator and enriches uncertain small object features via ==supervised contrastive learning==.
- Constructed **CFINet** as a two-stage detector built upon the ==Faster RCNN== architecture, enabling end-to-end training and balancing the contributions of proposal generation and feature enhancement.

## Results

- CFINet achieved an overall **AP of 30.7%** on the SODA-D test-set, surpassing the Faster RCNN baseline by **1.8** percentage points and outperforming specialized small object detector RFLA by **1.0% AP**.
- On the SODA-A test-set, CFINet achieved **34.4%** overall AP and demonstrated a **1.6** percentage point improvement in AP for extremely small objects (AP_eS) over the baseline.
- The Coarse-to-fine RPN (CRPN) component alone achieved a superior Average Recall (**42.6%**) for small objects compared to standard and Cascade RPNs, demonstrating its effectiveness in generating high-quality proposals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic, area-based anchor selection is more effective for ensuring sufficient high-quality proposals for small objects compared to fixed IoU thresholds.
> - An online, dynamic feature imitation strategy, guided by an Instance Quality (IQ) score and supervised contrastive learning, can effectively enrich feature representations for challenging small objects without relying on complex GANs or static feature banks.
> - Processing anchors from all FPN levels during coarse regression is crucial for mining potential anchors that are often missed for extremely small instances by single-level RPNs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This research addresses the challenges inherent in small object detection (SOD), a specialized area within computer vision's broader field of object detection. Generic object detection methods have achieved considerable success; however, they often exhibit reduced performance when applied to instances with limited pixel areas. The paper highlights two primary challenges for SOD: (1) the difficulty in generating a sufficient quantity of high-quality proposals for small objects, and (2) the scarcity of discriminative information for these small instances, leading to uncertain predictions.

Current object detection paradigms, particularly two-stage detectors, rely on Region Proposal Networks (RPNs) to generate candidate object regions. These RPNs frequently employ overlap-based or distance-based strategies to assign positive priors. For small objects, the small area inherently leads to low Intersection-over-Union (IoU) values between anchors and ground-truth boxes, which can result in a restricted sample pool for training. While reducing the IoU threshold can increase positive samples, it may compromise sample quality. Existing RPN variants, such as Cascade RPN, have demonstrated effectiveness for generic object detection but tend to favor larger objects, showing diminished returns or even performance degradation for extremely small instances.

To address the issue of limited discriminative information, several previous works have attempted to bridge the representation gap between small and large objects. Approaches include Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for feature super-resolution or restoration, and similarity learning techniques that align small object features with those of larger, "visually authentic" counterparts. However, the authors note that GAN-based methods can introduce sophisticated training strategies and potentially generate synthetic textures or artifacts. Similarity learning often requires cumbersome offline feature banks or risks feature collapse, where distinct object characteristics are lost due to homogenization in the feature space.

This work introduces a novel two-stage framework, CFINet, which seeks to improve small object detection by addressing both proposal generation and feature representation challenges. It positions itself by proposing modifications to the RPN mechanism and introducing a feature imitation learning strategy that operates in an online, dynamic manner, aiming to overcome the limitations of previous feature enhancement techniques.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the performance limitations of prevailing object detectors when encountering small objects. The authors identify two fundamental issues:

**a. Insufficient and Low-Quality Samples for Training:**
Current detectors often employ fixed IoU thresholds (e.g., 0.7) to define positive anchor samples. For small objects, which occupy limited pixel areas, the overlap between densely arranged anchors and ground truth boxes is frequently low, falling below these stringent thresholds. This leads to a significantly constrained number of positive samples available for model optimization, as illustrated in Figure 1. While reducing the IoU threshold can increase the quantity of positive samples, it risks incorporating low-quality samples that can disrupt optimization and lead to trivial regression solutions. The authors present empirical evidence from preliminary experiments using Cascade RPN (Table 1), a leading proposal network for generic object detection. These results indicate that while Cascade RPN refines priors, its region-based sampling strategy still biases towards larger instances, causing performance decreases on extremely small (AP_eS) and relatively small (AP_rS) objects. This observation underscores the need for a proposal generation mechanism specifically designed to provide sufficient and high-quality candidates for small objects.

**b. Paucity of Discriminative Information leading to Uncertain Predictions:**
Small objects inherently possess limited visual cues and may exhibit distorted structures, making it difficult for models to extract robust and discriminative features. This often results in ambiguous or incorrect predictions. The paper notes that larger instances typically embody clearer visual cues and better discrimination. Previous attempts to address this, such as GAN-based super-resolution or similarity learning, often have drawbacks. GANs require complex training schemes and can generate "fake" textures, while similarity learning often relies on static, offline feature banks or risks collapsing the feature space by forcing high similarity without preserving distinct characteristics. The authors argue that the definition of a "good example" for feature guidance should be dynamic and adapt to the model's current optimization state, rather than being solely based on object size or pre-defined examples. Such methods also tend to be computationally intensive or break the end-to-end training paradigm.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of CFINet are:
- To develop a **Coarse-to-fine RPN (CRPN)** that ensures the generation of both sufficient and high-quality proposals specifically for small objects, overcoming the limitations of conventional anchor-to-proposal paradigms. This involves a dynamic anchor selection strategy and multi-stage regression.
- To integrate a **Feature Imitation (FI) branch** into the conventional detection head to enrich the region representations of size-limited instances. This branch aims to guide the learning process by leveraging features from "high-quality" instances, defined dynamically based on the model's current performance, using a supervised contrastive learning paradigm to prevent feature collapse.
- To achieve state-of-the-art performance on large-scale small object detection benchmarks, demonstrating the effectiveness and generality of the proposed components.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed CFINet framework is a two-stage detector integrating a Coarse-to-fine Region Proposal Network (CRPN) and a Feature Imitation (FI) branch. It builds upon the Faster RCNN architecture.

#### 3.1. Coarse-to-fine RPN (CRPN)

CRPN is designed to generate high-quality proposals for small objects by addressing the limitations of traditional RPNs and Cascade RPN, which tend to neglect small instances.

- **Area-based Anchor Selection Strategy:** This strategy aims to ensure that objects of various sizes, particularly small ones, have an adequate number of potential anchors. Unlike fixed IoU thresholds, CRPN uses a dynamic positive IoU threshold ($T_a$) for coarse regression:
 $T_a = \max(0.25, 0.20 + \gamma \cdot \log(\frac{\sqrt{w \cdot h}}{12}))$
 where $w$ and $h$ are the width and height of the object box, $\gamma$ is a scale factor (default 0.15), and 12 corresponds to the minimal area in the SODA dataset. This continuous threshold adapts to object size, providing more suitable anchors for extremely small objects while preventing low-quality priors from overwhelming optimization.
- **Multi-level Anchor Processing:** Distinct from Cascade RPN, CRPN processes anchors from all Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) levels ({P2, P3, P4, P5}) during the first-stage (coarse) regression. This helps in mining sufficient potential anchors for extremely small instances that might otherwise be missed on a single pyramid level.
- **Cascade Regression with Adaptive Convolution:** After the initial coarse regression, the refined anchors (proposals) are fed into a standard RPN for a second-stage, fine-grained regression and foreground-background classification. Adaptive Convolution, inspired by Cascade RPN, is employed to align the features with the refined proposals, enhancing the quality of the final proposals.
- **Loss Function:** The training objective for CRPN ($L_{CRPN}$) combines cross-entropy loss for classification ($L_{cls}$) and IoU Loss for regression ($L_{\cdot reg}$) for both coarse (c) and fine (f) stages:
 $L_{CRPN} = \alpha_1 (L_{c\_reg} + L_{f\_reg}) + \alpha_2 L_{cls}$
 where $\alpha_1=9.0$ and $\alpha_2=0.9$ are loss weights.

#### 3.2. Feature Imitation (FI) Branch

The FI branch is introduced as an auxiliary head to the conventional detection network to enrich the representations of instances that are challenging for the model to predict accurately. It operates on an online, dynamic basis to prevent issues like feature collapse and the need for offline feature banks observed in prior methods.

- **Instance Quality (IQ) Indicator:** To dynamically identify "high-quality" exemplars, an Instance Quality (IQ) score is defined for each ground-truth object. For a ground-truth object $g$, given its label $c^*$ and bounding box $b^*$, and a set of model predictions $S = \{(C_i, IoU_i)\}$, the potential high-quality set $S'$ includes predictions where the highest confidence classification matches $c^*$. The IQ is then calculated as the average of the product of classification confidence for $c^*$ and IoU for predictions in $S'$:
 $IQ = \frac{1}{|S'|} \sum_{j=1}^{|S'|} C_{j,c^*} \cdot IoU_j$
 A threshold $T_{hq}$ (e.g., 0.65) is used to select instances with IQ $\ge T_{hq}$ as high-quality exemplars.
- **Exemplar Feature Set:** High-quality exemplars are collected and stored in an online, per-category exemplar feature set $E$. This set is updated in a queue-like (first-in, first-out) manner, ensuring diversity and relevance to the model's current optimization state. The maximum size of the feature set for each category is 256. For high-quality instances, positive samples are generated by augmenting their own features. For non-high-quality instances, positive samples are drawn from the corresponding category's exemplar feature set.
- **Feat2Embed Module:** Instead of directly comparing RoI features, these features ($x_i$) are first projected into a lower-dimensional embedding space by the Feat2Embed module. This module consists of three convolutional layers followed by a two-layer perceptron and a 128-dimensional embedding layer. Parameters are updated when processing current regional features but frozen when extracting exemplar features to improve stability. The "SharedConv-Embed" architecture, which reuses convolutional filters, is selected for its balance of performance and parameter efficiency (Appendix B).
- **Loss Function ($L_{FI}$):** The FI branch is optimized using a loss function based on Supervised Contrastive Learning (SCL). For a given proposal's embedding $v_j$, it encourages similarity with positive samples $v_p$ (exemplars from the same category or augmented versions of high-quality instances themselves) while pushing it away from negative samples $v_i$ (exemplars from different categories or background features).
 $L_{FI} = \frac{-1}{|P_{pos}|} \sum_j \sum_{v_p \in P_{pos}} \log \frac{\exp (v_j \cdot v_p / \tau)}{\sum_{v_i \in P} \exp (v_j \cdot v_i / \tau)}$
 Here, $P = P_{pos} \cup P_{neg}$ (positive and negative sample sets), and $\tau$ is a temperature parameter (optimized to 0.6).
- **Overall Loss:** The total loss function ($L$) for CFINet combines the CRPN loss, the standard classification and regression losses of the detection head, and the Feature Imitation loss:
 $L = L_{CRPN} + L_{cls} + L_{reg} + \alpha_3 L_{FI}$
 where $\alpha_3=0.5$ balances the FI branch contribution. The imitation process is active only during training and does not affect inference speed.
- **Training Details (Appendix B):** For high-quality instances, augmentation ($\Gamma$) involves random translation and zoom-in/out operations to generate multiple positive samples (16 pairs in practice). A low-quality threshold ($T_{lq}$) can be introduced to categorize instances into low-quality, mid-quality, and high-quality, allowing for differential loss weighting to stabilize training and improve amendment for genuinely low-quality instances.

### 4. Main findings and results

The effectiveness of CFINet was evaluated on two large-scale small object detection benchmarks: SODA-D and SODA-A, with objects typically ranging from 10 to 30 pixels in average size. The evaluation used COCO metrics, focusing on average precision (AP) across various IoU thresholds, particularly for extremely Small (eS), relatively Small (rS), and generally Small (gS) object subsets. All experiments utilized a ResNet-50 backbone with FPN, trained for 12 epochs (1x schedule).

#### 4.1. Performance on SODA-D Test-Set (Driving Scenario)

CFINet, integrated with Faster RCNN, achieved an overall AP of 30.7%, surpassing the Faster RCNN baseline by 1.8 percentage points (Table 2). Notably, CFINet demonstrated improvements on the most challenging metrics for small objects:
- **AP_eS:** 14.7% (vs. 13.8% for baseline), a 0.9 point improvement.
- **AP_rS:** 27.8% (vs. 25.7% for baseline), a 2.1 point improvement.
- **AP_gS:** 36.4% (vs. 34.5% for baseline), a 1.9 point improvement.
CFINet also outperformed RFLA (29.7% AP), a specialized small object detection method, by 1.0% AP, 1.5% AP_eS, and 0.9% AP_rS, addressing RFLA's noted performance sacrifice on extremely small instances.

#### 4.2. Performance on SODA-A Test-Set (Aerial Imagery)

On the SODA-A dataset, CFINet also achieved the highest overall AP of 34.4% among the compared methods (Table 3), matching the AP of Oriented RCNN. Crucially, CFINet showed a notable advantage in detecting extremely small objects:
- **AP_eS:** 13.5% (vs. 11.9% for baseline, 12.5% for Oriented RCNN), a 1.6 point improvement over baseline and 1.0 point over Oriented RCNN.
These results suggest the method's superiority and generality across different small object detection scenarios, including those with arbitrary orientations and high instance density.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of CRPN

- **Average Recall (AR):** CRPN achieved an AR of 42.6%, outperforming standard RPN (41.2%), RPN-0.5 (41.3%), GA-RPN (42.1%), and Cascade RPN (41.8%) on the SODA-D test-set (Table 4). Specifically, CRPN showed superior AR for small objects (eS, rS, gS) while maintaining comparable performance for normal-sized instances (N). This supports the claim that conventional refined RPNs tend to favor larger objects.
- **High-Quality Proposal Generation:** CRPN generated a greater number of high-quality proposals (IoU > 0.5) across all small object categories (eS, rS, gS) compared to baseline RPN and Cascade RPN (Figure 4). The analysis indicated a dynamic shift in focus during training, where CRPN initially prioritizes larger objects for early optimization but gradually allocates more attention to small instances as training progresses.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Discussions

Ablation studies on SODA-D confirmed the individual contributions of CRPN and the FI branch (Table 5):
- CRPN alone improved AP to 30.3% (from 28.9% baseline).
- FI branch alone (with baseline RPN) improved AP to 29.5%.
- Combining both CRPN and FI yielded the best result of 30.7% AP, with FI particularly contributing to recognition of size-limited instances.

Further analyses provided insights into component design choices:
- **Dynamic vs. Fixed Anchor Thresholds:** The proposed area-based dynamic anchor mining strategy for CRPN yielded better overall AP (30.3%) compared to fixed low thresholds (e.g., 0.20 or 0.40) (Table 6), which could introduce low-quality samples or bias towards large instances.
- **FI Loss Weight ($\alpha_3$):** An optimal weight of 0.5 for the Feature Imitation loss balanced its contribution, avoiding performance deterioration from either too little or excessive emphasis (Table 7).
- **Exemplar Criterion ($T_{hq}$):** An IQ threshold of 0.65 for selecting exemplars resulted in the best performance (AP 30.7%), indicating that a moderate stringency is beneficial. Too low or too high a threshold can degrade performance due to an abundance of low-quality exemplars or a lack of relevant, dynamic exemplars, respectively (Table 8).
- **Temperature ($\tau$):** The temperature parameter of 0.6 for the SCL loss was empirically determined to be optimal, preventing feature collapse while facilitating effective imitation (Table 9).
- **Feat2Embed Module:** The SharedConv-Embed design was selected for its performance and parameter efficiency (Table B2 in Appendix), demonstrating robustness to most designs except direct Global Average Pooling (GAP-Embed), which led to information loss.
- **Non-high-quality Instances (Appendix B):** Introducing mid-quality instances with specific loss weights proved beneficial for stabilizing training and amending low-quality instances effectively.

#### 4.5. Visualization

Qualitative results (Figure 5) on the SODA-D test-set visually demonstrated CFINet's capability in detecting small instances, even in dense scenes or with occlusions.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research contributes to the field of small object detection by proposing CFINet, a two-stage framework that addresses fundamental challenges related to proposal generation and feature representation for size-limited instances.

The significance of CFINet can be outlined as follows:

- **Improved Proposal Generation for Small Objects:** The Coarse-to-fine RPN (CRPN) introduces a dynamic, area-based anchor selection strategy and multi-level processing. This departure from conventional fixed-threshold or single-level RPNs results in a significantly increased quantity and quality of proposals for small and extremely small objects. This is critical for two-stage detectors, as high-quality proposals reduce the burden on subsequent stages and provide a more robust basis for classification and regression.
- **Enhanced Feature Representation through Dynamic Imitation Learning:** The Feature Imitation (FI) branch offers a mechanism to enrich the discriminative features of challenging small objects. By dynamically identifying "high-quality" exemplars based on the model's current performance (Instance Quality, IQ) and leveraging a supervised contrastive learning paradigm, CFINet can guide the learning of uncertain predictions without resorting to complex GAN architectures or static, offline feature banks. This approach mitigates the risk of feature collapse, a common issue in similarity-based methods, and enables end-to-end optimization.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** CFINet's consistent top-tier performance on the challenging SODA-D and SODA-A benchmarks, particularly for extremely small objects, demonstrates its practical effectiveness. The improvements over strong baselines and specialized small object detectors indicate a advancement in handling scenarios critical for applications like autonomous driving, intelligent surveillance, and aerial imagery analysis.
- **Methodological Robustness:** The comprehensive ablation studies validate the importance of each component (CRPN and FI) and provide empirical support for the design choices, such as the dynamic anchor strategy, the IQ threshold for exemplars, and the SCL temperature.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for real-world applications where precise detection of small objects is paramount. By providing a more robust and accurate method for small object detection, CFINet could enhance the reliability of:
- **Autonomous Driving:** Better detection of distant pedestrians, traffic signs, and other small obstacles.
- **Intelligent Surveillance:** Improved identification of individuals or objects in crowded or wide-area scenes.
- **Aerial and Satellite Imagery Analysis:** More accurate detection of small vehicles, ships, or infrastructure components.

The authors also acknowledge empirical limitations, noting that the FI branch's performance can exhibit instability due to the dynamic nature of exemplar selection. This suggests that future research into more flexible and general indicators of instance quality could further refine and stabilize such imitation learning paradigms, potentially expanding the applicability and robustness of this approach.
