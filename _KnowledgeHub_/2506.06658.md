# Self-Improving Loops for Visual Robotic Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Self-Improving Loops for Visual Robotic Planning (SILVR) framework enables in-domain visual robotic planners to continuously enhance their performance on novel tasks by iteratively learning from self-collected online experience. This approach yields up to 285% improvement in success rates on simulated MetaWorld tasks and substantial gains in real-world manipulation, outperforming baseline methods and demonstrating robustness to varying data quality.

## Problem

- Visual robotic planners struggle to generalize effectively to novel tasks or arbitrary robotic behaviors for which expert demonstrations are unavailable.
- Existing methods, even those leveraging large-scale offline datasets, lack continuous online adaptation capabilities to dynamically refine skills based on self-collected experience.
- Current online improvement techniques, such as reinforcement learning finetuning, often suffer from sample inefficiency and reliance on human-defined reward functions.

## Method

- **SILVR** introduces an ==iterative self-improvement loop== that fine-tunes an in-domain video model using successful trajectories gathered from online interactions.
- Integrates an optional ==internet-pretrained video prior== (e.g., ==AnimateDiff==) via ==Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation (IPA)== to enhance text-conditioned generalization during visual plan synthesis.
- An ==Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM)== translates synthesized visual plans into executable robotic actions, which are then deployed to collect new experience for iterative improvement.

## Results

- **SILVR** achieved significant success rate improvements, including up to **+285%** on 12 unseen MetaWorld tasks and substantial gains on real-world object pushing and drawer opening, over multiple self-improvement iterations.
- Consistently outperformed **DSRL** and **Behavior Cloning Improvement Loop** baselines in MetaWorld, showcasing higher **sample efficiency** for online self-improvement.
- The improved visual planner can be **distilled** into a lightweight action-predicting policy for efficient deployment, achieving comparable or superior performance while operating faster.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative finetuning of a visual planner with self-collected online experience, particularly when composed with internet-scale video priors, leads to continuous performance gains on novel robotic tasks.
> - Visual planning-based self-improvement demonstrates superior sample efficiency compared to direct reinforcement learning finetuning of action-predicting policies for online adaptation.
> - The framework exhibits robustness to suboptimal initial training data and varying feedback mechanisms, including VLM-based filtering or no explicit success filtering, in enabling sustained improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the field of visual robotic planning, where video generative models are increasingly employed to synthesize visual plans for robotic tasks. These plans, often text-conditioned sequences of video frames, are then translated into executable actions by inverse dynamics models (IDMs).

A primary challenge in this domain is the generalization of these visual planners to novel tasks that were not present in their initial training data. While advancements have been made in leveraging large-scale, pre-collected offline data (such as web-scale video datasets) to improve generalization (e.g., Adapt2Act, Luo et al., 2025), the current research landscape is moving towards agents that can continuously improve through online, self-collected experiences. Existing methods that incorporate large-scale priors often still rely on purely offline data, which limits their ability to adapt to new scenarios dynamically.

This work addresses the gap between offline data-driven generalization and continuous online adaptation. It acknowledges prior efforts in:
- **Video Generation for Decision Making:** Previous studies have utilized video generative models as reward functions, dynamics models, or pixel-based planners (e.g., UniPi, Du et al., 2024b). This paper positions itself by employing video models to predict text-conditioned visual plans, similar to some existing approaches, but focuses on improving these planners through iterative online learning.
- **Self-Improving Generative Models:** While the concept of generative models improving from their own outputs has been explored for Large Language Models (LLMs), similar applications for video generation models, particularly in a robotic context, remain less explored. The paper differentiates its approach from relevant work like VideoAgent (Soni et al., 2024) by emphasizing self-adaptation with internet-scale video priors and robustness to suboptimal initial data.
- **Reinforcement Learning Finetuning of Behavior Cloning Policies:** Approaches such as DSRL (Wagenmaker et al., 2025), ResIP (Ankile et al., 2025), and DPPO (Ren et al., 2024) finetune pretrained diffusion policies using online experience and rewards. This paper implicitly compares its sample efficiency to these reinforcement learning-based finetuning methods, suggesting an alternative path for iterative improvement.

In essence, this research aims to bridge the gap between powerful offline generalization capabilities and the demand for continuous, online self-improvement in robotic visual planning, particularly for tasks unseen during initial training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables visual robotic planners to continuously improve their performance on novel tasks through self-collected online experience, thereby overcoming the limitations of static, offline datasets.

The key motivations driving this work are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Generalization Challenges in Visual Planning:** Current visual planners, while capable when trained on expert demonstrations, struggle to generalize effectively to arbitrary robotic behaviors or tasks for which expert-quality demonstrations are unavailable or prohibitively expensive to collect. The goal is to allow these planners to adapt to new tasks without requiring new human-provided data for each specific scenario.
- **Moving Beyond Offline Data Limitations:** Even approaches that integrate large-scale internet-level video data (like Adapt2Act) are fundamentally limited by the static nature of offline datasets. The research is motivated by the need for intelligent agents that can extend their learning capabilities beyond pre-collected data, continuously refining their skills in an online manner based on their own interactions and feedback from the environment.
- **Enabling Self-Improvement on Unseen Tasks:** The core motivation is to design an agent that can iteratively enhance its visual planning capabilities for a specified task of interest, especially for behaviors and task variations that were not present in the initial in-domain training data. This includes adapting to novel objects, colors, or manipulation sequences.
- **Enhancing Sample Efficiency:** Existing methods for online improvement, particularly those based on reinforcement learning for finetuning behavior cloning policies, can be sample-inefficient. A key motivation is to develop a self-improvement framework that is more sample-efficient, making it practical for real-world robotic deployments where collecting large amounts of online interaction data is costly and time-consuming.
- **Reducing Reliance on Human-Provided Components:** The research seeks to reduce the dependency on human-defined ground-truth reward functions or pristine expert demonstrations. It investigates the robustness of the self-improvement process when using alternative, less constrained feedback mechanisms, such as Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for scoring trajectories or even operating without explicit success filtering.
- **Leveraging Internet-Scale Priors for Real-World Generalization:** Recognizing the power of large-scale pretrained models, a motivation is to naturally integrate these internet-scale video priors to facilitate stronger text-conditioned generalization and robustness in real-world visual settings and dynamics.
- **Balancing Performance and Inference Speed:** While visual planning can offer strong generalization, its execution speed may be slow due to the video generation process. The research is motivated to demonstrate that the benefits of self-improvement through visual planning can be combined with fast inference by distilling the learned capabilities into a lightweight policy for deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **Self-Improving Loops for Visual Robotic Planning (SILVR)**, aims to enable an in-domain video model to iteratively improve its visual planning performance for novel robotic tasks through self-collected online experience.

#### 3.1. Core Framework Components and Iterative Loop (Algorithm 1)

SILVR operates through an iterative cycle, initialized with pre-trained models:
1. **Initial Models:**
 - **In-domain Video Model ($\epsilon_\theta$):** Pre-trained on a general set of expert demonstrations within the target environment. This model is responsible for synthesizing visual plans. Its architecture is based on AVDC (Ko et al., 2024), augmented with a cross-attention layer for improved text-conditioning. It predicts 8 future frames.
 - **Inverse Dynamics Model (f_IDM):** A separate model trained to translate consecutive pairs of visual frames into executable robotic actions. Two implementations are considered:
 - **MLP-IDM:** An MLP network built on top of a VC-1 encoder, used for real-world experiments. It predicts end-effector positions.
 - **Diffusion Inverse Dynamics Model (DIDM):** A Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) modified to take current and future frames as input, outputting action chunks. This is used for simulated environments.
 - **Internet-pretrained Video Prior ($\epsilon_\text{general}$):** An optional, frozen large-scale text-to-video model (e.g., AnimateDiff, Guo et al., 2023) pretrained on web-scale datasets (e.g., WebVid-10M). This prior is used to enhance generalization.

2. **Iterative Self-Improvement Loop (K iterations):**
 - **Adaptation with Video Prior (Optional):** In each iteration, the current in-domain video model ($\hat{\epsilon}_\theta$) can be adapted with the frozen internet-pretrained video prior ($\epsilon_\text{general}$) using **Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation (IPA)** (Luo et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2023a). This training-free approach composes the score predictions of both models during the sampling process (Equation 1). The general model contributes strong text-conditioned generalization, while the in-domain model maintains environment-specific visual characteristics.
 - **Visual Plan Rollout:** The adapted video model ($\tilde{\epsilon}$) acts as a visual planner. Given a task prompt and current observation, it synthesizes a sequence of future frames (a visual plan). The f_IDM then translates these visual frames into robotic actions, which are executed in the environment.
 - **Control Loop:** The execution strategy varies. For MetaWorld, semi-open-loop control (executing half the plan before replanning) is used to balance quality and efficiency. For real-world tasks, open-loop control (executing all actions from a single plan) is employed.
 - **Data Collection:** A number of trajectories (N rollouts) are collected through these interactions.
 - **Filtering:** The collected online trajectories are evaluated for success. This filtering can be performed using:
 - **Ground-truth human-defined reward functions:** Available in simulated environments like MetaWorld.
 - **Human judgment:** For real-world robot experiments.
 - **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** Pretrained VLMs (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro) can score trajectories based on task descriptions, providing an automated success signal.
 - **No filtering:** In some experimental setups, all collected trajectories are used regardless of outcome.
 - **Finetuning:** Successful (filtered) trajectories are added to an accumulating dataset (D), which may initially include the original in-domain training data. The in-domain video model ($\hat{\epsilon}_\theta$) is then finetuned on this augmented dataset. The DIDM (for MetaWorld) is also iteratively finetuned. The MLP-IDM (for real-world) is kept frozen.

#### 3.2. Distillation for Deployment

After the self-improvement loop completes, the final, high-performing visual planner components can be distilled into a lightweight, action-predicting policy (e.g., a Diffusion Policy) via behavior cloning. This distilled policy is trained on a set of demonstrations collected by the improved visual planner, enabling faster inference during deployment.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Simulated Environment:** MetaWorld-v2 suite, focusing on 12 novel tasks unseen during initial training.
 - Initial training: 25 demonstrations from 8 *seen* MetaWorld tasks.
 - Per iteration: 30 trajectories collected, finetuning on successful data.
 - Internet prior: Disabled by default for MetaWorld.
- **Real-World Environment:** Franka Emika Panda robot arm, for two manipulation tasks.
 - **Task 1: Object Pushing:** Pushing colored cups (e.g., "Push the Orange Cup"). Generalization tested on novel color combinations (e.g., orange, purple, unseen during training). Initial training: 120 human-teleoperated videos (10 demos each for 12 tasks with seen colors).
 - **Task 2: Drawer Opening:** Selecting and opening a colored drawer. Generalization tested on a novel color (yellow). Initial training: 144 human-teleoperated videos.
 - Per iteration: Self-collected data combined with initial demonstrations for finetuning.
 - Success judgment and filtering: Human-evaluated.
 - Internet prior: Enabled by default (using AnimateDiff).

#### 3.4. Baselines for Comparison

- **DSRL (Wagenmaker et al., 2025):** Reinforcement Learning finetuning of a Diffusion Policy.
- **Behavior Cloning Improvement Loop (BCIL):** A Diffusion Policy iteratively finetuned on self-collected successful online experience.
 Both baselines are initialized with a Diffusion Policy trained on the same initial in-domain data as SILVR.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness and characteristics of the SILVR framework.

- **Continuous Self-Improvement:**
 - **MetaWorld (Simulated):** SILVR consistently achieved performance improvements over multiple iterations on 12 unseen tasks. Success rate increased by up to 285% over 10 iterations (e.g., from an average of 14.7% at Iteration 0 to 53.1% at Iteration 9 with ground-truth filtering). Improvements were noted to saturate around Iteration 5.
 - **Real-World (Panda Arm):** For novel object pushing and drawer opening tasks with unseen color combinations, SILVR also demonstrated continuous performance gains over iterations. For "Push the Orange Cup," success rates improved from approximately 43.4% at Iteration 0 to 76.7% at Iteration 2. For "Open the Yellow Drawer," success rates rose from 13.7% to 61.1% over 2 iterations.

- **Superiority and Sample Efficiency over Baselines:**
 - In MetaWorld, SILVR significantly outperformed DSRL and BCIL from Iteration 1 onwards. DSRL showed minimal to no improvement and sometimes decreased performance, while BCIL improved but quickly saturated at lower success rates (e.g., 23.2% vs. SILVR's 44.2% at Iteration 4).
 - This indicates that SILVR demonstrates higher sample efficiency for self-improvement compared to reinforcement learning finetuning of behavior cloning policies, which often require more experience to successfully bootstrap value functions for novel tasks.
 - Similar trends were observed in real-world experiments, where action-predictive behavior cloning exhibited lower base generalization and slower self-improvement compared to SILVR.

- **Robustness to Data Filtering Signals:**
 - **VLM-based Filtering:** In MetaWorld, using Vision-Language Models (GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Pro) for success filtering instead of ground-truth signals still enabled continuous self-improvement. Gemini-2.5-Pro performed comparably to ground-truth filtering in facilitating progress.
 - **No Filtering:** For real-world Panda arm tasks, continuous improvement was observed even when no data filtering was applied (i.e., finetuning on all collected trajectories regardless of success). This robustness is attributed to the score composition mechanism, where suboptimal demonstrations can still convey useful visual and dynamic information to the in-domain model.

- **Impact of Internet-Scale Video Prior (IPA):**
 - **Real-World Cruciality:** The integration of the internet-pretrained video prior (AnimateDiff) was found to be crucial for self-improvement in real-world robotic tasks. Without the prior, visual planners struggled to improve or even deteriorated, highlighting its role in enhancing zero-shot generalization and adaptability in visually complex real-world settings. Qualitatively, this led to clearer visual plans and successful task completion for novel scenarios (Figure 2).
 - **Simulated Environment Robustness:** In simulated MetaWorld tasks, the self-improving trend occurred with or without the internet video prior, suggesting that the benefits of the prior may be less pronounced when the sim-to-real gap is smaller.

- **Distillation for Efficient Deployment:**
 - After the self-improvement process, distilling the SILVR visual planning components into a lightweight Diffusion Policy resulted in a policy that significantly outperformed the BCIL baseline and, in some cases, slightly exceeded the final performance of the visual planner teacher (e.g., 44.2% for SILVR vs. 49.2% for SILVR-Distilled DP at MetaWorld Iteration 4). This demonstrates that SILVR can facilitate effective generalization and self-improvement without sacrificing inference speed for deployment.

- **Robustness to Initial Data Quality:**
 - SILVR exhibited robustness to the quality of initial in-domain training data. Even when initialized with suboptimal demonstrations (e.g., trajectories containing 70% random actions), SILVR demonstrated continuous self-improvement in MetaWorld. This suggests that the framework can still bootstrap a performant video planner even when expert-quality demonstrations are scarce. Specific tasks showed varied responses, with some benefiting more from expert initialization and others from the initial exploration of suboptimal data.

- **Component Ablations (Appendix F):**
 - For MetaWorld, finetuning both the video model and the DIDM was found to be critical for robust improvement, particularly when facing significant novelty in objects and motions. Freezing either component led to modest and quickly saturated performance gains. In contrast, for real-world tasks, the pre-trained MLP-IDM was sufficiently robust and did not require finetuning.

- **Long-Horizon Tasks (Appendix G.4):**
 - Improvements on individual atomic tasks through SILVR were observed to contribute to overall performance gains on sequential long-horizon tasks (e.g., pushing cups in a specified order), suggesting compositional improvement capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SILVR represents an advancement in robotic learning, offering a mechanism for visual robotic planners to continuously improve their capabilities online. This work has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy and Adaptability:** SILVR moves robotic agents beyond reliance on static, pre-collected datasets, enabling them to adapt and refine their skills in dynamic environments. This is a step towards more autonomous robots that can learn from their own experiences rather than requiring extensive human re-programming or data collection for every new task variant.
- **Improved Generalization to Novel Tasks:** The framework directly addresses the challenge of generalizing to tasks unseen during initial training. By iteratively learning from self-collected experience, robots can acquire proficiency in novel scenarios, reducing the "cold start" problem for new tasks where initial expert demonstrations are scarce.
- **Increased Practicality for Real-World Deployment:**
 - **Sample Efficiency:** The demonstrated superior sample efficiency compared to reinforcement learning finetuning makes SILVR more viable for real-world robotic applications, where collecting large amounts of interaction data is costly and time-consuming.
 - **Reduced Human Supervision:** Robustness to automated VLM-based filtering and even the absence of filtering in certain real-world scenarios lessens the dependency on human-defined reward functions and manual data curation, making the deployment process more scalable.
 - **Robustness to Suboptimal Data:** The ability to self-improve from suboptimal initial training data reduces the stringent requirements for expert-quality demonstrations, which are often expensive to acquire.
- **Synergistic Use of Foundational Models:** SILVR effectively integrates the generalization power of internet-scale pretrained video models (priors) with in-domain knowledge and online experience. This approach leverages the broad understanding of the world encoded in large models while grounding it in the specific visual and physical dynamics of the robotic environment.
- **Balanced Performance and Efficiency:** The framework offers a practical trade-off: the powerful self-improvement capabilities of visual planning can be leveraged during training, and the learned knowledge can then be distilled into a lightweight, fast-inference policy for efficient real-time deployment. This ensures that the benefits of advanced learning do not come at the cost of prohibitive execution speed.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens avenues for further research, including:
 - Developing principled exploration mechanisms to avoid local minima and discover more diverse task strategies.
 - Investigating methods to improve "cold start" performance when the initial success rate on a novel task is very low.
 - Exploring more advanced video generative models for enhanced visual quality and planning fidelity.
 - Combining SILVR with efficient subtask segmentation to tackle complex, long-horizon tasks more effectively by iteratively improving atomic behaviors.

In summary, SILVR contributes to the development of adaptable, self-improving robotic systems that can learn and generalize in challenging, open-ended environments, potentially reducing the need for extensive human intervention in robot task acquisition and refinement.
