# DecARt Leg: Design and Evaluation of a Novel Humanoid Robot Leg with Decoupled Actuation for Agile Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology introduce the DecARt Leg, a novel humanoid robot leg design that achieves agile locomotion through decoupled actuation while maintaining a human-like appearance with conventional rotational motors. The work also proposes the "Fastest Achievable Swing Time" (FAST) metric, demonstrating the DecARt Leg's superior agility with a FAST of 0.17 seconds and a theoretical walking velocity of 4.18 m/s, validated through simulations and hardware experiments.

## Problem

- Humanoid robot leg designs often face a trade-off between achieving anthropomorphic appearance (typically with coupled kinematics) and high agility (often found in decoupled designs).
- Existing highly agile decoupled leg designs frequently lack a human-like form or rely on specialized linear actuators, limiting their application in general-purpose humanoids.
- A lack of a standardized, objective metric to quantitatively compare the agile locomotion potential of diverse robot leg designs across the field.

## Method

- The **DecARt Leg** features a novel quasi-telescopic, pantograph-like kinematic structure with a geared knee, enabling decoupled actuation for leg length and pitch while using standard proprioceptive rotational motors.
- All motors are strategically placed near the leg's root to minimize swing inertia, and the design allows for an anthropomorphic appearance with a forward-facing knee and provides closed-form analytical inverse kinematics for simplified control.
- A new "==Fastest Achievable Swing Time==" (FAST) metric is introduced, which quantitatively evaluates a leg design's agility by estimating the shortest time required to complete a standardized swing trajectory under actuator limits, independent of upper-body dynamics.

## Results

- The DecARt Leg achieved a FAST of **0.17** seconds and a theoretical walking velocity of **4.18** m/s, outperforming other designs like Cassie (0.24s FAST) and Fourier GR1T2 (0.24s FAST) in agile swing capabilities.
- Simulations demonstrated the DecARt Leg-based robot's ability to walk at **2.2** m/s on flat terrain, recover from sagittal pushes up to 95 N, and perform complex loco-manipulation tasks like opening doors and climbing stairs.
- Preliminary hardware experiments with a prototype confirmed the design's practical feasibility, showcasing successful untethered walking using various control schemes (IK, WBC, RL) and dynamic jumping motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - It is possible to design a humanoid robot leg that combines the agility benefits of decoupled actuation with a human-like appearance, bridging a critical gap in current robot leg design paradigms.
> - Decoupled actuation significantly enhances agile locomotion capabilities, as evidenced by the DecARt Leg's performance compared to a coupled variant (DecARt-S) with identical motors and link masses.
> - Placing heavy actuators near the leg's root is a fundamental design principle for maximizing agility by minimizing swing inertia, and the FAST metric effectively quantifies these design impacts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of bipedal humanoid robots capable of agile and robust locomotion in human-centric environments remains a significant challenge in robotics. The broader research landscape for bipedal robot leg design is characterized by a fundamental trade-off between various design philosophies, primarily concerning actuation schemes and kinematic structures.

Historically, many commercially available or open-source humanoid robots, such as Unitree H1, Fourier GR1, and others, adopt a "serial" or "coupled" kinematic structure. In these designs, actuators for hip, knee, and ankle joints are often placed in a sequential chain, requiring all actuators to be involved in a leg's swing motion. This prevalence is often attributed to factors like simplified modeling and control, a human-like appearance (avoiding the "uncanny valley"), and compatibility with human environments (e.g., sitting on chairs). However, the paper argues that this coupled design can limit a robot's agility and dynamic performance due to higher leg inertia and complex control coordination for swift movements.

In contrast, other research streams have explored "decoupled" leg designs, which separate the control of leg length and leg pitch, often leading to more agile and energy-efficient locomotion. Notable examples include:
- **Pure Telescopic Legs:** Robots like the Raibert Hopper, Meltran V, and SLIDER utilize linear actuators for direct leg length control, pioneering dynamic legged locomotion. While demonstrating agility, these designs typically lack human-like morphology.
- **Quasi-Telescopic (Pantograph) Structures:** The ATRIAS robot and its successors, Cassie and Digit by Agility Robotics, have demonstrated remarkable agility, endurance, and dynamic capabilities, including impressive running and energy efficiency. Their pantograph structure effectively decouples leg pitch and length actuation. However, these designs often have a distinct "bird-like" appearance, with backward-facing knees, which may not be desirable for general-purpose humanoids interacting in human environments.

The current paper directly addresses a critical gap in this landscape: **the challenge of creating a bipedal robot leg that combines the agility benefits of decoupled actuation with a visually human-like morphology and the use of conventional proprioceptive rotational actuators.** Researchers seek to overcome the limitations of both traditional coupled designs (lack of agility) and existing decoupled designs (non-anthropomorphic appearance or reliance on specialized actuators). The DecARt Leg project aims to bridge this gap, offering a novel design that is both agile and anthropomorphic, thus contributing to the development of more versatile and socially acceptable humanoid robots. The formulation of the "Fastest Achievable Swing Time" (FAST) metric also contributes to the broader research by providing a standardized, objective tool for comparing the agile locomotion capabilities of diverse robotic leg designs, which has been a persistent challenge in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations in the current trends of humanoid robot leg design. While most modern humanoids adopt simple, human-inspired coupled kinematic structures for ease of modeling, control, and anthropomorphic appearance, this approach can compromise agility and dynamic performance. On the other hand, highly agile decoupled designs, such as those found in Cassie/Digit robots, often sacrifice a human-like appearance and may employ specialized actuation mechanisms, making them less suitable for general-purpose interaction in human-centric environments.

Given this context, the key objectives of the "DecARt Leg" project are multi-faceted:

- **Design a Novel Leg Kinematics:** To develop a new bipedal robot leg design that features **decoupled actuation** (similar to telescopic or pantograph designs) while simultaneously maintaining a **human-like appearance** with a forward-facing knee. This is a central objective, aiming to overcome the trade-off between agility and anthropomorphism.
- **Utilize Proprioceptive Rotational Actuators:** To achieve decoupled actuation using standard **proprioceptive rotational motors**, which are commonly used in humanoid robots due to their ease of modeling and control, avoiding the complexities associated with linear actuators or more exotic mechanisms.
- **Prioritize Agile Locomotion Principles:** To incorporate design principles that support agile locomotion, such as placing heavy motors near the root of the leg to minimize swing inertia, ensuring the leg remains compact and lightweight.
- **Develop a Quantitative Evaluation Metric:** To formulate a new, descriptive, and intuitive metric, named the "Fastest Achievable Swing Time" (FAST), specifically designed to quantitatively evaluate and compare the agile locomotion capabilities of various robotic leg designs, independent of upper-body dynamics or specific control strategies.
- **Validate the Design Numerically and Experimentally:** To thoroughly evaluate the proposed DecARt Leg design through extensive simulation across a range of agile locomotion and loco-manipulation tasks, and to conduct preliminary hardware experiments to demonstrate its practical feasibility and validate simulation results.
- **Demonstrate Advantages over Existing Designs:** To numerically compare the DecARt Leg's performance, particularly in terms of agile locomotion, against other competitive coupled and decoupled leg designs, highlighting the benefits of the proposed approach.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to build a "general-purpose humanoid robot" that is not only capable of agile and dynamic movements but also possesses a friendly, human-like appearance, facilitating its integration into everyday tasks and human environments without compromising performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-pronged methodology encompassing novel kinematic design, the development of a new performance metric, and comprehensive evaluation through both simulation and preliminary hardware experiments.

#### 3.1. DecARt Leg Design and Kinematics

The core of the methodology is the innovative **DecARt Leg design**, which aims to achieve decoupled actuation with an anthropomorphic appearance using rotational motors.
- **Quasi-Telescopic Structure:** The design utilizes a compact pantograph-like structure with a geared knee. A key feature is the combination of passive gears and a 4-bar parallel structure, which effectively translates the rotation of a "leg length motor" into a direct linear vertical motion of the foot, thereby mimicking a telescopic (prismatic) joint and achieving decoupled actuation.
- **Motor Placement:** All motors, including those for the 2-DoF ankle (pitch and roll), are strategically placed near the root of the leg (above the knee). This minimizes the leg's swing inertia, a critical factor for agile locomotion.
- **Anthropomorphic Appearance:** Despite its intrinsically telescopic nature, the design allows for a human-like appearance with a forward-facing knee, especially when internal links are covered.
- **Ankle Torque Transmission:** A novel multi-bar system is incorporated for ankle torque transmission, allowing the ankle motors to be placed above the knee. This system is designed to avoid singularity issues found in other multi-bar systems, enabling the leg to be fully compacted or stretched out, and to alter ankle position even with a fully crouched knee.
- **Analytical Inverse Kinematics:** A significant advantage of the DecARt Leg's design is the ability to derive a closed-form analytical solution for its inverse kinematics (Equations 1-7). This simplifies controller design, bridging the gap between complex parallel structures and simpler serial designs, and facilitating its integration into common robotic simulators. The paper notes that the ankle pitch is not fully decoupled, requiring some actuation during leg length variation, similar to Cassie/Digit robots.

#### 3.2. Fastest Achievable Swing Time (FAST) Metric

To quantitatively evaluate and compare leg designs for agile locomotion, the authors propose a novel metric called the **"Fastest Achievable Swing Time" (FAST)**.
- **Motivation:** The FAST metric addresses the limitations of maximum walking velocity (which depends on the controller and upper body) and other complex metrics (which are less intuitive). It focuses solely on leg design capabilities.
- **Estimation Procedure:**
 1. **Trajectory Generation:** A family of cubic spline swing-foot trajectories is generated, varying in duration but maintaining a consistent swing length (L) and apex height (h), scaled to the robot's leg size. The swing length is determined by tilting the leg 30 degrees backward and forward.
 2. **Inverse Dynamics Control:** These trajectories serve as references for a Quadratic Program (QP) optimization-based Inverse Dynamics (ID) controller (specifically, a Task Space Inverse Dynamics Controller - TSID). This controller calculates the required joint torques and accelerations, respecting actuator limits and using the full robot dynamic model (URDF). The robot base is assumed fixed to isolate leg performance.
 3. **Dynamic Integration:** The calculated joint accelerations are numerically integrated using a full robot dynamic model via the Pinocchio library.
 4. **Compliance Check:** The resulting operational space trajectories of the robot foot are evaluated. The shortest swing duration that meets strict positional (less than 3% of swing length error) and velocity (less than 0.1 m/s error) requirements is selected as the FAST value.
- **Theoretical Walking Velocity:** The FAST metric directly allows the calculation of a "theoretical walking velocity" by dividing the swing length by the FAST value, providing an intuitive measure of agile potential.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** An open-source package is provided to encourage adoption and reproduction of results, promoting standardization in leg design comparison.

#### 3.3. Simulation-Based Evaluation

- **Robot Model:** A full URDF model of the DecARt Leg, integrated with a preliminary torso and upper limbs (total weight ~35 kg), was developed.
- **Simulation Environment:** Evaluations were conducted in PyBullet, a physics simulation software.
- **Controllers:** An ALIPM-based controller combined with a TSID Whole Body Controller (WBC) with torque-controlled actuation was used.
- **Tasks:** The robot's performance was assessed across a variety of challenging tasks: fast walking on flat and rough terrain (5cm variations), recovery from pushes (sagittal and lateral), stair climbing (10cm height), opening doors, and picking/carrying objects.
- **Decoupled vs. Coupled Comparison:** A modified "DecARt-S (serial)" model was created by removing the closed kinematic chains and knee gears, converting the leg to a coupled configuration. This allowed for a direct comparison of the impact of decoupled actuation on the FAST metric.

#### 3.4. Preliminary Hardware Evaluation

- **Prototype:** Two DecARt Leg units were manufactured and assembled into a bipedal platform with a rudimentary pelvis. The full upper body is subject to ongoing research.
- **Control Schemes:** Three control schemes were tested: stiff position control, whole-body (WBC) torque control, and compliant PD control.
- **Walking Controllers:** Inverse kinematics-based, QP-based WBC (ALIPM framework), and reinforcement learning (RL) controllers were implemented.
- **Explosive Motions:** A simple jumping controller based on a predefined trajectory was also implemented and tested.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the efficacy of the DecARt Leg design and the utility of the proposed FAST metric.

#### 4.1. FAST Metric Evaluation and Comparison

The quantitative evaluation using the FAST metric provided compelling evidence for the DecARt Leg's agility:
- **Superior Agility:** The DecARt Leg achieved the **lowest Fastest Achievable Swing Time (FAST) of 0.17 seconds** and the **highest theoretical walking velocity of 4.18 m/s**. This significantly outperforms other prominent robot leg designs evaluated, including:
 - Cassie (Decoupled): 0.24 s FAST, 3.94 m/s theoretical velocity.
 - Fourier GR1T2 (Coupled, hip-actuated knee): 0.24 s FAST, 2.92 m/s theoretical velocity.
 - Unitree G1 (Coupled, knee-actuated knee): 0.34 s FAST, 1.87 m/s theoretical velocity.
 - Booster T1 (Coupled, knee-actuated knee): 0.33 s FAST, 1.55 m/s theoretical velocity.
- **Validation of Decoupled Actuation:** The "DecARt-S (serial)" model, a modified coupled version of the DecARt Leg, showed a substantial degradation in performance, with a FAST of 0.25 seconds. This direct comparison, keeping motor limits and link masses constant, strongly validates that the **decoupled actuation scheme is a key factor** enabling the superior agility of the DecARt Leg.
- **Importance of Actuator Placement:** The results also reinforced the principle that placing actuators closer to the root of the leg (hip) improves performance, as robots with hip-mounted knee actuators (Fourier GR1T2, DecARt) generally performed better than those with actuators directly in the knee joint (Unitree G1, Booster T1).

#### 4.2. Simulation Results

Extensive simulations with the full DecARt Leg-based robot model showcased its versatility and robust capabilities:
- **High-Speed Walking:** The robot achieved a maximum walking velocity of **2.2 m/s on flat terrain** and 0.8 m/s on rough terrain (with 5 cm height variations) using an ALIPM-based controller. This is a strong practical validation of the theoretical agility predicted by the FAST metric.
- **Push Recovery:** The robot demonstrated impressive resilience to external disturbances, recovering from sagittal pushes up to **95 N for 200 ms** and lateral pushes up to 50 N for 200 ms. This highlights the dynamic stability enabled by the agile leg design.
- **Complex Locomotion:** The robot successfully climbed stairs up to 10 cm in height.
- **Loco-Manipulation:** The DecARt Leg-based robot was shown to perform loco-manipulation tasks, including opening doors, picking boxes (up to 3.5 kg on exerted arms), and carrying additional torso weight (up to 8.5 kg), demonstrating its potential for general-purpose applications.

#### 4.3. Preliminary Hardware Evaluation

Preliminary experiments with a hardware prototype (two DecARt Leg units with a rudimentary pelvis) confirmed the practical feasibility of the design:
- **Successful Locomotion:** The robot successfully performed **untethered walking** using three different control schemes: inverse kinematics-based, QP-based WBC (ALIPM), and reinforcement learning. This validates the design's compatibility with diverse control strategies and the accuracy of its analytical inverse kinematics.
- **Explosive Motions:** The prototype successfully executed a simple jumping controller, further demonstrating its capability for dynamic and explosive movements.
- **Validation of Simulation:** The hardware results supported the promising locomotion capabilities observed in simulation, marking a crucial step towards a full humanoid implementation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DecARt Leg research carries significant implications for the future of humanoid robotics, impacting both design philosophies and evaluation methodologies.

#### 5.1. Bridging the Gap in Humanoid Leg Design

The most significant impact of the DecARt Leg is its successful demonstration of a novel kinematic structure that effectively **bridges the long-standing gap between anthropomorphic appearance and decoupled, agile actuation**. Previous designs either prioritized human-likeness (coupled) at the cost of agility or achieved high agility (decoupled) with a non-human appearance. By offering a solution that maintains a forward-facing knee and human-like form while achieving superior agility through decoupled actuation and conventional rotational motors, the DecARt Leg opens new avenues for general-purpose humanoid robots that are both high-performing and socially acceptable. This design could accelerate the deployment of humanoids in environments requiring interaction with humans, where aesthetics and performance are both crucial.

#### 5.2. Advancing Agile Locomotion Capabilities

The superior performance of the DecARt Leg, as quantified by the FAST metric and validated in simulation and hardware, indicates a clear path toward developing more agile and dynamically capable bipedal robots. Its ability to achieve high theoretical walking velocities, robust push recovery, and effective navigation on challenging terrains directly translates into robots that can operate more effectively and safely in dynamic, unstructured environments. This has implications for applications in logistics, disaster response, personal assistance, and manufacturing, where robots need to navigate complex spaces swiftly and robustly.

#### 5.3. Contribution of the FAST Metric

The introduction of the "Fastest Achievable Swing Time" (FAST) metric is a valuable methodological contribution to the robotics community.
- **Standardized Comparison:** FAST provides a rigorously defined, intuitive, and scalable metric for objectively comparing the agile locomotion potential of different robotic leg designs, independent of specific control implementations or upper-body characteristics. This addresses a critical need for standardized benchmarks in legged robotics research.
- **Design Insight:** The application of FAST to various robots, including the direct comparison between the decoupled DecARt Leg and its coupled "DecARt-S" variant, yields profound insights into the underlying principles of leg design. It quantitatively confirms the performance benefits of decoupled actuation and proper actuator placement (near the root), guiding future design choices. The open-source availability of the metric calculation package encourages its widespread adoption, potentially fostering more focused and comparable research in leg design.

#### 5.4. Simplification of Control and Modeling

The analytical inverse kinematics of the DecARt Leg significantly simplifies the control and modeling challenges often associated with complex parallel kinematic structures. This "bridge the gap" from complex mechanics to simpler control paradigms means that researchers and engineers can focus more on advanced control strategies rather than wrestling with kinematic complexities. The successful application of both classical (IK, WBC) and learning-based (RL) controllers in hardware demonstrates the versatility and ease of integration of this design.

#### 5.5. Future Directions

The research lays a strong foundation for future work, including the development of a full DecARt Leg-based humanoid robot with a complete upper body. Future investigations into additional actuation elements like parallel and serial springs could further enhance energy efficiency and locomotion performance, potentially leading to robots with even greater endurance and dynamic capabilities. This work is a significant step towards the realization of truly general-purpose, agile, and human-friendly humanoid robots.
