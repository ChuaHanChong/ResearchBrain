# Derivative-Free Guidance in Continuous and Discrete Diffusion Models with Soft Value-Based Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> Soft Value-based Decoding in Diffusion models (SVDD) is a derivative-free, inference-time method that guides pre-trained continuous and discrete diffusion models to generate samples optimizing non-differentiable reward functions. SVDD consistently achieved higher reward quantiles across image, molecule, and DNA/RNA generation tasks, outperforming baseline guidance methods while preserving sample validity and diversity.

## Problem

- Current gradient-based guidance methods for diffusion models require differentiable proxy models, limiting their use with intrinsically non-differentiable reward functions common in scientific discovery.
- Fine-tuning approaches for reward optimization are computationally intensive for large generative models and carry risks of model degradation or forgetting.
- Applying standard classifier guidance to discrete diffusion models is challenging due to the lack of continuous gradients in non-Euclidean spaces, and methods like 'Best-of-N' sampling are computationally wasteful.

## Method

- The **SVDD** (Soft Value-based Decoding in Diffusion) method iteratively refines noisy samples by sampling multiple candidates at each step and selecting one based on a ==soft value function== derived from an ==entropy-regularized Markov Decision Process== formulation.
- Two approaches are proposed for estimating the soft value function: ==SVDD-MC==, which uses a trained neural network to predict future reward, and ==SVDD-PM==, a training-free method approximating the value with the reward of the pre-trained model's predicted clean sample.
- The method operates ==derivative-free== and does not require fine-tuning the pre-trained diffusion model, making it applicable to both continuous and discrete data types without architectural modifications.

## Results

- Both SVDD-MC and SVDD-PM consistently outperformed pre-trained models, Best-of-N, DPS, and SMC-based methods by achieving higher **Top 10%** and **Top 50%** reward quantiles across image, molecule (e.g., **QED**, **SA**, **docking scores**), and DNA/RNA activity generation tasks.
- SVDD-generated samples maintained high validity, uniqueness, and naturalness, demonstrated by improved **Fréchet ChemNet Distance (FCD)** and other chemical metrics for molecules, and realistic visual fidelity for images.
- Ablation studies showed that SVDD maintained **higher diversity** in generated samples compared to SMC-based methods while achieving similar or superior reward levels, suggesting a more robust exploration of the high-reward design space.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sampling from a reward-optimized distribution can be formally linked to sequentially sampling from soft optimal policies within an entropy-regularized Markov Decision Process framework for diffusion models.
> - Effective guidance in diffusion models can be achieved at inference time by approximating and integrating soft value functions, representing expected future rewards, directly into the denoising process, circumventing the need for reward function gradients.
> - The SVDD framework, particularly SVDD-PM, efficiently guides generation without additional model training, demonstrating that the pre-trained diffusion model's inherent ability to predict clean samples can be leveraged for value function approximation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models have established themselves as proficient generative models, capable of capturing complex natural distributions across various domains, including image generation, natural language processing, molecule design, and biological sequence generation (e.g., DNA, RNA, proteins). While these models excel at producing realistic samples, many practical applications in scientific research and industrial design require not merely naturalness, but also the optimization of specific downstream reward functions. For instance, in drug discovery, the goal may be to generate molecules with high quantitative estimate of druglikeness (QED), synthetic accessibility (SA), or optimal docking scores. Similarly, in biological engineering, the objective could be to design DNA or RNA sequences with specific activity levels or translational efficiencies.

Addressing this objective typically involves guiding the generative process of pre-trained diffusion models toward desired outcomes. Existing approaches fall into two main categories: fine-tuning-based and non-fine-tuning-based methods.

Fine-tuning-based methods, such as classifier-free guidance and reinforcement learning (RL)-based fine-tuning, adapt the pre-trained diffusion model itself to incorporate reward signals. While effective, these methods often incur significant computational costs, especially with increasingly large "foundation models," and carry the risk of model forgetting or instability.

Non-fine-tuning methods aim to steer the generation process at inference time without modifying the underlying generative model. Classifier guidance and its variants are prominent examples in this category. These methods typically integrate a gradient of a "proxy" model (e.g., a classifier or a value function) into the diffusion model's denoising process. However, a significant limitation of standard classifier guidance is its requirement for differentiable proxy models. This constraint precludes the direct use of many non-differentiable reward functions or features common in scientific domains, such as physics-based simulations (e.g., molecular docking, protein stability calculations) or complex, non-differentiable molecular descriptors. Furthermore, applying standard classifier guidance to discrete diffusion models, which operate in non-Euclidean spaces, remains a challenge without specific adaptations. Another non-fine-tuning method, "Best-of-N" sampling, involves generating a large number of samples and selecting those with the highest reward. This approach is computationally inefficient as it requires generating many complete samples, only to discard most of them.

More recently, Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC)-based methods have been explored for guidance in diffusion models, offering an approach that does not necessarily require differentiable models. However, these methods often face challenges with parallelization and maintaining diversity, especially when applied to reward maximization with small batch sizes.

This work, Soft Value-based Decoding in Diffusion models (SVDD), positions itself within the landscape of non-fine-tuning, inference-time guidance methods. It seeks to overcome the limitations of existing approaches by providing a derivative-free framework that can directly incorporate non-differentiable reward feedback and be applied uniformly to both continuous and discrete diffusion models, all while avoiding the computational burden of fine-tuning the generative model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel method that enables pre-trained diffusion models to generate samples that optimize specific downstream reward functions, concurrently preserving the inherent naturalness of the design space captured by the pre-trained model. This is formalized as sampling from a distribution that maximizes an expected reward while minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence from the pre-trained model's distribution.

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges observed in current methods for guided generation:

1. **Computational Cost of Fine-tuning:** Existing fine-tuning approaches, such as classifier-free guidance or RL-based fine-tuning, necessitate extensive retraining of generative models. As diffusion models become increasingly large (e.g., "foundation models"), this fine-tuning becomes computationally intensive, limiting their practical applicability and increasing development time. The paper aims to circumvent this by proposing an inference-time technique.
2. **Restriction to Differentiable Reward Functions in Classifier Guidance:** A prevalent limitation of classifier guidance is its reliance on differentiable proxy models for gradient computation. This restricts its utility in numerous scientific and engineering fields where crucial reward feedback or features are intrinsically non-differentiable. Examples include molecular docking scores derived from physics-based simulations (e.g., Vina, Rosetta), or specific molecular/protein descriptors. The inability to directly utilize such non-differentiable feedback mandates the construction of approximate, differentiable proxies, which can introduce inaccuracies or loss of fidelity.
3. **Challenges with Discrete Diffusion Models:** The gradient-based nature of standard classifier guidance makes its principled application to discrete diffusion models problematic. Discrete spaces do not readily support continuous gradient calculations, requiring non-trivial transformations or approximations that may compromise theoretical foundations or practical performance. The objective is to develop a method applicable to both continuous and discrete domains without such conversions.
4. **Inefficiency of "Best-of-N" Sampling:** While simple, the "Best-of-N" strategy, which involves generating many samples and selecting the top ones based on a reward, is computationally wasteful. It generates complete, high-fidelity samples only to evaluate and discard most of them, contrasting with methods that guide the generation process incrementally.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes Soft Value-based Decoding in Diffusion models (SVDD), designed to provide a derivative-free, non-fine-tuning, and broadly applicable solution for reward-guided generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Soft Value-based Decoding in Diffusion models (SVDD), is an iterative sampling algorithm that integrates soft value functions into the standard inference procedure of pre-trained diffusion models. Its theoretical foundation is rooted in the equivalence between sampling from a reward-optimized distribution and sequentially sampling from soft optimal policies in an entropy-regularized Markov Decision Process (MDP) formulation of diffusion models, as established by Uehara et al. (2024).

**Core Principle:**
The method introduces a **soft value function** $v_{t-1}(x_{t-1})$, which quantifies the expected future reward at time $t=0$ given an intermediate noisy state $x_{t-1}$ at time $t-1$. Specifically, $v_{t-1}(x_{t-1}) := \alpha \log E_{x_0 \sim p^{pre}(x_0|x_{t-1})} [ \exp(r(x_0)/\alpha) | x_{t-1} = \cdot ]$. The paper leverages a key theorem stating that sampling from a sequence of these soft optimal policies, $p^{\star,\alpha}_{t-1}( \cdot | x_t) \propto p^{pre}_{t-1}( \cdot | x_t) \exp(v_{t-1}(\cdot)/\alpha)$, generates samples from the target reward-optimized distribution.

**The SVDD Algorithm (Algorithm 1):**
SVDD operates by iteratively refining a noisy sample from $t=T$ down to $t=0$. At each time step $t$:
1. **Multiple Sample Generation:** From the current noisy state $x_t$, $M$ candidate next noisy states, $\{x^{(m)}_{t-1}\}_{m=1}^M$, are sampled using the policy of the *pre-trained* diffusion model, $p^{pre}_{t-1}(\cdot|x_t)$.
2. **Value-Weighted Selection:** For each of these $M$ candidates, a weight $w^{(m)}_{t-1}$ is calculated as $\exp(\hat{v}_{t-1}(x^{(m)}_{t-1})/\alpha)$, where $\hat{v}_{t-1}$ is an estimated soft value function and $\alpha$ is a hyperparameter controlling the strength of reward optimization.
3. **Resampling:** A single next state, $x_{t-1}$, is then selected from the $M$ candidates by drawing an index from a categorical distribution parameterized by the normalized weights $\{\frac{w^{(m)}_{t-1}}{\sum_j w^{(j)}_{t-1}}\}_{m=1}^M$. This effectively performs importance sampling to approximate the soft optimal policy.

A common practical choice is to set $\alpha=0$, which simplifies the selection step to choosing the candidate with the maximum estimated value function, i.e., $x_{t-1} = \text{argmax}_{m \in [1,\ldots,M]} \hat{v}_{t-1}(x^{(m)}_{t-1})$. The number of duplications, $M$, typically ranges from 5 to 20 in experiments. While the generation of $M$ samples can be parallelized for efficiency in terms of wall-clock time (at the cost of increased memory), sequential computation of these samples results in computational time approximately $M$ times that of standard inference.

**Estimating Soft Value Functions:**
The core of SVDD relies on obtaining accurate estimates of the soft value function, $\hat{v}_{t-1}$. The paper proposes two main approaches:

1. **SVDD-MC (Monte Carlo Regression - Algorithm 2):**
 - This approach directly approximates the value function $v_t(x_t)$ with the expected future reward $E_{x_0 \sim p^{pre}(x_0|x_t)}[r(x_0)|x_t]$.
 - A dataset of trajectories is collected by rolling out the pre-trained diffusion model from $t=T$ to $t=0$, yielding pairs of $(x_t, r(x_0))$.
 - A neural network (e.g., CNN for images, GNN for molecules) is then trained as a regressor to predict $r(x_0)$ given $x_t$ and time $t$. This trained network serves as the estimated value function $\hat{v}'$.
 - Crucially, the value function model itself does not need to be differentiable, allowing for the use of non-differentiable features or architectures.

2. **SVDD-PM (Posterior Mean Approximation - Algorithm 3):**
 - This approach offers a simpler, training-free estimation of the value function.
 - It approximates $v_t(x_t)$ as $r(\hat{x}_0(x_t))$, where $\hat{x}_0(x_t)$ is the pre-trained diffusion model's prediction of the clean sample $x_0$ given the noisy sample $x_t$. Most diffusion models implicitly or explicitly learn such a predictor during training.
 - The primary advantage of SVDD-PM is that it requires no additional training or learning of a separate value function model; it directly utilizes the pre-trained model and the reward function.

**Advantages and Extensions:**
- **Derivative-Free:** SVDD does not require differentiable proxy models, enabling direct use of non-differentiable rewards and features.
- **No Fine-tuning:** It avoids the computational costs and stability issues associated with fine-tuning the generative model. SVDD-PM, in particular, requires no additional training.
- **Discrete Diffusion Compatibility:** The methodology is inherently applicable to both continuous and discrete diffusion models without modification.
- **Flexible Proposal Distributions:** The framework can be extended to use arbitrary proposal distributions (e.g., from classifier guidance) in the importance sampling step, potentially enhancing performance.
- **Distillation for Faster Inference:** The inference-time computational cost, which scales with $M$, could be mitigated in future work by distilling the SVDD policy into a new diffusion model.

**Comparison with SMC-based Methods:**
SVDD differs from "standard" SMC-based methods in its approach to resampling and weight calculation. SVDD is effectively an instantiation of nested-IS SMC. SVDD performs resampling on a "per-sample basis" within each time step, allowing for high parallelization and maintaining diversity. In contrast, standard SMC methods typically resample across the entire batch, which can complicate parallelization and potentially lead to a loss of diversity across generated samples, especially for reward maximization tasks where low $\alpha$ values can reduce effective sample sizes dramatically.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of SVDD was evaluated across diverse domains, encompassing image generation, molecule generation, and DNA/RNA sequence generation. The performance was assessed by comparing SVDD-MC and SVDD-PM against several baselines: pre-trained models (no guidance), Best-of-N, DPS (a training-free classifier guidance variant), and SMC-based methods. Key metrics included reward quantiles, sample validity, and computational resource usage.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance (Reward Optimization)

Table 2 in the paper presents the Top 10% and Top 50% quantiles of the reward scores for samples generated by each method.
- **Consistent Outperformance:** Both SVDD-MC and SVDD-PM consistently achieved higher reward quantiles across all evaluated domains (Image, Molecule, DNA/RNA) and all specific reward functions (e.g., image compressibility, aesthetic score, molecule QED, SA, docking scores, DNA/RNA activity levels). This indicates SVDD's ability to effectively guide diffusion models towards higher-reward regions of the design space.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **Pre-trained models** naturally showed the lowest reward quantiles, confirming the need for guidance.
 - **Best-of-N** improved upon pre-trained models but was generally outperformed by SVDD, highlighting SVDD's greater efficiency in leveraging reward information during the iterative generation process.
 - **DPS**, a gradient-based method, showed varied performance but was generally inferior to SVDD, particularly in molecule and biological sequence generation where non-differentiable rewards are prevalent. Its performance was hindered by the need for differentiable proxy models.
 - **SMC-based methods** also showed improvements over pre-trained models but were typically surpassed by SVDD, particularly in maintaining diversity while optimizing for rewards.
- **SVDD-MC vs. SVDD-PM:** The relative performance between SVDD-MC and SVDD-PM was domain-dependent. SVDD-PM, which requires no additional training, demonstrated competitive performance, and in some cases, slightly superior performance (e.g., for enhancers and 5’UTRs), suggesting its robustness due to direct utilization of reward feedback without an intermediary learned value function. SVDD-MC, when successfully trained, also delivered strong results.

#### 4.2. Sample Validity and Naturalness

Visual examples (Figure 2) and quantitative metrics (Table 3 for molecules) demonstrate that SVDD-generated samples maintained validity and naturalness comparable to or exceeding those from the pre-trained models.
- **Images:** Generated images retained high visual fidelity and realism while optimizing for compressibility or aesthetic scores.
- **Molecules:** For molecule generation, SVDD-generated compounds exhibited high validity, uniqueness, and novelty. Crucially, SVDD improved metrics such as Fréchet ChemNet Distance (FCD), Similarity to Nearest Neighbors (SNN), scaffold similarity, and molecule stability compared to the pre-trained model, indicating the generation of chemically plausible and diverse molecules that are relevant to the target properties.
- **DNA/RNA:** The generated sequences successfully optimized for activity levels while remaining biologically plausible.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Duplication Size (M)

Experiments investigating the impact of the duplication size $M$ (the number of candidate samples considered at each step of SVDD) revealed:
- **Performance vs. M:** Reward performance generally plateaued as $M$ increased (Figures 3a, 3b, 7a), suggesting that increasing $M$ beyond a certain point yields diminishing returns. Typical effective $M$ values were around 5-20.
- **Computational Cost vs. M:** GPU time scaled approximately linearly with $M$ (Figures 3c, 3d, 7b), consistent with the expectation that processing $M$ candidates sequentially on the GPU increases computation proportionally. Memory usage also scaled with $M$ (Figures 3c, 3d, 7b), reflecting the need to store multiple intermediate noisy samples.

#### 4.4. Comparison to SMC-Based Methods (Diversity)

A specific comparison with SMC-based methods (Figure 4) highlighted a key advantage of SVDD. While SMC could achieve high reward scores, the generated samples often exhibited reduced diversity (many similar samples), especially when $\alpha$ was set low for optimization. SVDD, by performing sampling on a "per-sample basis" and employing nested importance sampling, maintained higher diversity in the generated samples while still achieving comparable or superior reward levels. This suggests SVDD is more effective at exploring the high-reward regions of the design space without collapsing to a few modes.

#### 4.5. Value Function Training Performance

For SVDD-MC, the training of value function models demonstrated good performance (Figure 6), with high Pearson correlations for Enhancer and 5'UTR tasks and low MSE for molecular tasks. This indicates the models were adequately capturing the relationship between intermediate states and final rewards, enabling effective guidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SVDD, a novel inference-time algorithm that significantly advances the capabilities of pre-trained diffusion models for reward-guided generation. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Derivative-Free Reward Optimization:** SVDD's primary contribution is its ability to directly incorporate non-differentiable reward functions and features into the diffusion model's generation process. This is a critical breakthrough for numerous scientific domains, such as drug discovery, materials science, and biological engineering, where key performance indicators often derive from complex, non-differentiable simulations (e.g., physics-based docking scores, stability calculations) or proprietary black-box evaluators. By removing the need for differentiable proxy models, SVDD allows researchers to leverage realistic and high-fidelity reward signals directly, potentially leading to more accurate and impactful designs.

2. **Computational Efficiency and Scalability for Foundation Models:** SVDD operates purely at inference time, obviating the need for computationally intensive fine-tuning of large pre-trained generative models. This is particularly relevant in the era of "foundation models," where retraining colossal models is economically and environmentally costly. SVDD-PM further reduces overhead by requiring no additional training, directly utilizing the pre-trained model's capabilities. This efficiency makes reward-guided generation more accessible and scalable, accelerating design cycles in various applications.

3. **Unified Framework for Continuous and Discrete Data:** The method's seamless applicability to both continuous (e.g., images, some molecule representations) and discrete (e.g., biological sequences, text, discrete graph representations) diffusion models provides a unified and principled approach. This addresses a long-standing challenge with gradient-based guidance methods, which struggle with discrete spaces. This broad applicability extends the utility of guided diffusion to a wider array of data types and scientific problems.

4. **Preservation of Naturalness and Diversity:** Unlike some aggressive optimization strategies that might lead to samples diverging significantly from the original data distribution or collapsing to limited modes, SVDD is designed to optimize rewards while maintaining the naturalness and diversity inherent in the pre-trained model's distribution. This balance is crucial for generating viable and robust designs in real-world applications (e.g., drug candidates that are both potent and synthesizable, or functional biological sequences).

5. **Broad Impact on Scientific Discovery and Engineering:** The demonstrated effectiveness across image generation, molecule design (optimizing druglikeness, synthetic accessibility, and binding affinity to various proteins), and DNA/RNA sequence design (optimizing activity levels) highlights the broad potential impact of SVDD. This methodology could accelerate the design and discovery of novel therapeutics, advanced materials, and engineered biological systems by enabling more precise control over generative processes.

In conclusion, SVDD represents a significant step forward in the field of controlled generation with diffusion models. Its derivative-free and non-fine-tuning nature addresses critical limitations of prior work, opening new avenues for leveraging powerful generative AI in data-scarce and computationally demanding scientific domains.
