# PiL-World: A Chunk-Wise World Model for VLA Policy-in-the-Loop Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tongji University and AIRC, Midea Group developed PiL-World, a chunk-wise world model for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy-in-the-loop evaluation. This model significantly reduces the average absolute success-rate gap between imagined and real-robot rollouts from 63.2% to 12.0% and achieves a 0.94 Pearson correlation with real policy performance, while improving the Hallucination-Free Ratio from 41.5% to 70.1%.

## Problem

- Evaluating Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies on real robots is costly, time-consuming, and difficult to scale due to hardware limitations, safety concerns, and manual scene resets.
- Existing robot world models are primarily designed for open-loop prediction, which is unsuitable for the closed-loop, observe-act-replan feedback loop characteristic of VLA policy execution.
- Ensuring temporal and multi-view consistency in imagined rollouts is challenging; inconsistencies and hallucinations undermine the reliability of policy evaluations.

## Method

![[2606.05773-method.png]]
*Motivation for policy-in-the-loop world-model evaluation. (a) Policy-in-the-Loop Rollout Process. (b) Open-Loop vs. Closed-Loop Prediction. (c) Inconsistent Imagined Rollout vs. Consistent Imagined Rollout.*

- **PiL-World**, a ==chunk-wise world model==, generates multi-view future observations from VLA-predicted action chunks and feeds them back to the policy, enabling closed-loop evaluation.
- A deterministic Action-to-Control Projection converts robot actions into visual control sequences (e.g., gripper positions) to provide explicit visual guidance for image generation.
- A ==Latent History Memory== module incorporates latent representations of recent multi-view frames to maintain temporal and spatial consistency across successive prediction rounds.

## Results

- PiL-World reduced the average absolute success-rate gap between imagined and real-robot rollouts from **63.2%** (baseline) to **12.0%**.
- It achieved a Pearson correlation of **0.94** between real and imagined success rates across VLA policy checkpoints, indicating a strong ability to reflect relative policy performance.
- The model improved the average Hallucination-Free Ratio from **41.5%** to **70.1%**, signifying longer periods of believable and consistent generated rollouts compared to the baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly projecting robot actions into visual control signals (e.g., gripper markers) significantly enhances the accuracy and consistency of generated observations, particularly for robot-object interactions.
> - Leveraging a latent history memory to encode past observations is crucial for maintaining temporal coherence and preventing visual hallucinations across an imagined rollout.
> - Training the world model on diverse data, including teleoperated failure trajectories, allows it to realistically simulate a broader range of robot-environment dynamics and policy outcomes, improving evaluation fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) models, such as RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and $\pi_0$, have emerged as a significant approach for general-purpose robotic manipulation. The evaluation of these VLA policies in real-world robotics typically involves a closed-loop process where a robot observes a scene, executes an action, and then conditions its subsequent decision on the resulting observation. This observe-act feedback loop is fundamental to how robots interact with dynamic environments. However, performing such evaluations with physical robots is resource-intensive due to hardware safety considerations, the need for frequent scene resets, and limitations in experimental throughput.

World models offer a potential solution to mitigate the costs and challenges associated with real-robot evaluation by enabling imagined rollouts. Existing world models for robot action evaluation have largely focused on open-loop prediction. In open-loop scenarios, the world model predicts future states or observations based on a pre-collected, fixed sequence of actions, without the capacity to re-query the policy based on intermediate generated observations. This paradigm differs from the closed-loop nature of VLA policy deployment, where each action chunk is determined by the observation generated from the previous execution.

The research presented in this paper addresses this gap by focusing on the closed-loop, policy-in-the-loop evaluation of VLA policies. It aims to develop a world model that can accurately predict multi-view future observations from VLA-generated action chunks and then feed these predicted observations back to the VLA policy to inform subsequent decisions. This approach allows for a more faithful simulation of real-world robot-environment interaction compared to open-loop methods. Prior work on action-conditioned video prediction and robot world models (e.g., AVID, MTV-World, Ctrl-World) has explored generating future frames conditioned on actions for various purposes, including policy evaluation and learning. This work extends these efforts by specifically designing a chunk-wise world model to support the iterative, feedback-driven requirements of VLA policy-in-the-loop evaluation, ensuring consistency across generated observations and a reliable approximation of policy performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a chunk-wise world model, named PiL-World, that enables accurate and consistent policy-in-the-loop (PiL) evaluation for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. This objective is motivated by several key challenges and limitations in the current methods for VLA policy evaluation:

1. **Cost and Scalability of Real-Robot Evaluation**: Real-world evaluation of VLA policies is inherently expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale. It is constrained by physical robot limitations, safety protocols, manual scene resets, and the throughput of experimental setups. This hinders rapid iteration and comprehensive testing of VLA policies.
2. **Mismatch in World Model Interfaces**: Most existing robot world models are designed for open-loop prediction, where they predict outcomes along pre-recorded action trajectories. This contrasts with the closed-loop nature of VLA policy execution, where the robot continuously observes, acts, and re-plans based on the *resultant* observations. A world model intended for closed-loop evaluation must generate observations that can serve as valid inputs for subsequent policy queries, effectively "closing the loop."
3. **Ensuring Rollout Consistency**: Beyond merely generating visually plausible videos, a world model for policy evaluation must produce imagined rollouts that remain consistent with real-world physics and the robot's execution process. Inconsistencies, such as objects disappearing or non-physical interactions, can lead to unreliable evaluations of policy performance. Such a model must also maintain multi-view consistency across different camera perspectives, as VLA policies often rely on multi-view inputs.
4. **Capturing Diverse Execution Outcomes**: VLA policies do not always succeed. To provide a realistic evaluation, a world model should be capable of generating both successful and failed task trajectories. Learning from diverse outcomes, including teleoperated failures, can help the model better represent the varied behaviors and dynamics of real policy execution.

To address these motivations, PiL-World aims to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Closed-Loop Observation Generation**: Create a world model capable of taking an action chunk predicted by a VLA policy and generating a segment of future multi-view observations. The terminal observation of this segment must be suitable for feedback as the input to the VLA policy for its next decision.
- **Temporal and Multi-View Consistency**: Integrate mechanisms to ensure the generated observations are temporally consistent within a segment and across multiple prediction rounds, as well as spatially consistent across different camera views. This involves leveraging action-derived visual control signals and latent history memory.
- **Learning from Diverse Data**: Train the world model not only on successful demonstrations but also on failed teleoperated trajectories to expose it to a broader range of robot-environment dynamics and execution outcomes.
- **Accurate Policy Performance Estimation**: Enable the world model to estimate VLA policy success rates that closely align with those measured in real-robot executions, providing a reliable proxy for evaluation.

By fulfilling these objectives, PiL-World seeks to provide a scalable, cost-effective, and reliable alternative to real-robot closed-loop evaluation for VLA policies, thereby accelerating the development and refinement of general-purpose robotic manipulation systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PiL-World is designed as a chunk-wise world model for closed-loop VLA policy evaluation. Its methodology encompasses a specific problem definition, a structured framework with several key components, and an alignment strategy for training and evaluation.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The system defines a VLA policy $\pi$ that predicts an action chunk $A_t = \{a_{t+1}, \ldots, a_{t+H_{\pi}}\}$ of horizon $H_{\pi}$ given a multi-view observation $x_t = \{x_v^t\}_{v \in V}$ (where $V$ is the set of camera views), robot proprioceptive state $s_t$, and task instruction $g$.

PiL-World, denoted $W_{\theta}$, takes the current observation $x_t$, latent history memory $H_t$, an action-derived visual control condition $\Gamma(A_{\Delta,K}^t)$, and task instruction $g$ to predict a segment of future observations $\hat{x}_{t+\Delta:t+K\Delta:\Delta}$. Here, $A_{\Delta,K}^t = \{a_{t+\Delta}, a_{t+2\Delta}, \ldots, a_{t+K\Delta}\}$ represents stride-aligned actions from the policy's action chunk, where $K$ is the number of predicted frames and $\Delta$ is the stride, ensuring $K\Delta \leq H_{\pi}$. The terminal generated observation is then fed back to the policy for the next query, forming a closed loop.

#### 3.2. Framework

The PiL-World framework integrates several components to ensure temporal and multi-view consistency in generated rollouts:

- **PiL-World Closed-Loop Rollout Pipeline**: During inference, PiL-World operates in alternation with a frozen VLA policy over multiple rounds. In each round:
 1. The VLA policy predicts an action chunk based on the current (real or generated) observation, proprioceptive state, and task instruction.
 2. PiL-World converts a stride-aligned portion of this action chunk into visual control signals.
 3. Conditioned on the current observation, latent history memory, visual control signals, and instruction, PiL-World generates $K$ multi-view future frames.
 4. The terminal generated observation becomes the input for the policy's next query.
 5. The latent history memory is updated with recent frames (real initially, then generated).
 6. The policy-side proprioceptive state is updated based on the last stride-aligned action.
 This iterative process composes imagined execution segments into a dense, closed-loop rollout.

- **Action-to-Control Projection ($\Gamma$)**: Robot actions are absolute joint-space commands. To better condition the visual prediction model, these actions are converted into a head-view visual control sequence. This deterministic projection process maps stride-aligned robot actions (e.g., gripper joint positions) into 3D gripper positions via forward kinematics. These 3D positions are then projected into the head-view camera plane using calibrated intrinsics and extrinsics. The projected locations are represented as markers, with marker size encoding gripper state (open/close) and fading traces indicating motion continuity. This "control video" serves as a visual proxy for gripper motion, providing explicit visual guidance for generation in the head view.

- **Success/Failure Fine-Tuning Data**: To capture both general robot dynamics and task-specific execution nuances, PiL-World employs a two-stage training strategy:
 1. **Pretraining**: The model is first pretrained on RealSource World, a large-scale dataset of real-world dual-arm manipulation, to acquire general robot-environment dynamics.
 2. **Fine-tuning**: It is then fine-tuned on target-task trajectories. Crucially, this fine-tuning data includes both successful demonstrations and failed teleoperated executions. This exposure to non-goal-reaching trajectories helps the model learn a more comprehensive and realistic distribution of outcomes, including task failures, which is essential for accurate policy evaluation.

- **Latent History Memory ($H_t$)**: To maintain temporal context and consistency across successive prediction rounds, PiL-World incorporates a latent history memory. This memory encodes recent multi-view frames (real at rollout initialization, then updated with generated frames) into latent representations using a VAE encoder ($E_{\phi}$). These history latents ($Z_{v,h}^t$) are used as a conditioning input for future prediction alongside the current frame latents ($Z_{v,0}^t$), visual control, and instruction. This mechanism helps prevent generated segments from drifting away from the preceding visual context. The denoising objective is applied only to the future target latents ($Z_{v,f}^t$), while current and history latents serve as clean conditions.

#### 3.3. Training and Evaluation Alignment

Training involves splitting long robot trajectories into action-conditioned clips, each corresponding to a single world-model prediction step (recent history, current observation, stride-aligned actions, and future observations). This matches the inference-time prediction interface. For evaluation, imagined and real branches are initialized from the *same* subtask observation and task instruction, using the *same* VLA policy. This paired protocol directly tests how well imagined rollouts replicate real-environment behavior under matched policy inputs, moving beyond mere visual plausibility to assess functional agreement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PiL-World was conducted on three real dual-arm manipulation tasks: sorting cubes, stacking bowls, and stacking blocks, using the $\pi_0$ VLA policy. The results were assessed using multiple metrics to quantify closed-loop rollout agreement, reliability, and local visual prediction quality.

#### 4.1. Closed-Loop Rollout Agreement

- **Success-Rate Gap ($|\Delta SR|$):** PiL-World demonstrated a substantial reduction in the absolute success-rate gap between imagined and real-robot rollouts. Compared to the Ctrl-World baseline, PiL-World reduced the average $|\Delta SR|$ from 63.2% to 12.0%. This improvement was consistent across tasks, with notable reductions on Sort Cubes (71.8% to 15.0%), Stack Bowls (72.6% to 4.2%), and Stack Blocks (45.1% to 16.7%). These figures indicate that PiL-World's imagined rollouts better reflect the task-level outcomes of the VLA policy compared to the baseline.
- **Pearson Correlation across Checkpoints:** When evaluating across multiple VLA policy checkpoints, PiL-World achieved a Pearson correlation of 0.94 between real and imagined success rates. This strong correlation suggests that PiL-World can reliably reflect relative policy performance changes, enhancing its utility as an evaluation proxy during policy development.

#### 4.2. Hallucination-Free Ratio (HFR)

- PiL-World improved the average Hallucination-Free Ratio (HFR), a human-annotated metric measuring the duration an imagined rollout remains credible before obvious hallucination appears, from 41.5% for Ctrl-World to 70.1%. Significant gains were observed on Sort Cubes and Stack Bowls, indicating longer periods of consistent and believable generation. The gain on Stack Blocks was smaller (from 37.7% to 43.0%), attributed to the inherent difficulty of contact-rich manipulation tasks where small errors can rapidly amplify.

#### 4.3. Single-Step Visual Prediction Quality

- **LPIPS (Perceptual Similarity):** In single-step evaluations, measuring average multi-view LPIPS over a 15-frame prediction horizon under ground-truth action conditioning, PiL-World achieved lower overall LPIPS values than Ctrl-World on all three tasks. Relative reductions were 33.7% on Sort Cubes, 19.5% on Stack Bowls, and 5.4% on Stack Blocks. The most significant gains were observed in the head view, where the action-to-control projection provides direct guidance for gripper motion.
- **LPIPS Gain Across Horizon:** The LPIPS gain (Ctrl-World LPIPS minus PiL-World LPIPS) was more pronounced at later predicted frames. This indicates that PiL-World maintains lower perceptual prediction error over longer action-conditioned horizons, which is crucial for maintaining utility through a closed-loop rollout.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Rollout Consistency

- Qualitative examples demonstrated that PiL-World generated rollouts that better preserved consistency with reference executions compared to Ctrl-World. Inconsistencies (e.g., object disappearance, spatial conflicts) were less frequent and appeared later in PiL-World's rollouts, supporting the quantitative HFR results and highlighting improved closed-loop reliability.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study on Latent History Memory

- An ablation study showed that removing the latent history memory substantially increased single-step LPIPS across all three tasks (e.g., from 0.0965 to 0.3146 on Sort Cubes). This finding underscores the critical role of recent multi-view context provided by the history memory in preserving visual consistency during generation.

In summary, PiL-World demonstrated superior performance in generating consistent and reliable imagined rollouts for VLA policy-in-the-loop evaluation, leading to a closer agreement with real-world policy success rates and improved perceptual quality compared to the baseline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of PiL-World represents a notable advancement in the field of robotic learning and evaluation, particularly for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Scalable VLA Policy Evaluation**: The primary impact of PiL-World is its ability to facilitate closed-loop evaluation of VLA policies without continuous reliance on real-robot hardware. By reducing the need for costly and time-consuming real-world experiments, PiL-World can significantly accelerate the development, iteration, and benchmarking of VLA models. This scalability is critical for advancing general-purpose robotic manipulation.
2. **Improved Fidelity of Imagined Rollouts**: PiL-World's integration of action-derived visual control, latent multi-view history conditioning, and joint multi-view prediction contributes to generating temporally and spatially consistent imagined rollouts. The quantitative reduction in success-rate gap and the higher hallucination-free ratio demonstrate a higher fidelity to real-world robot behavior, making the imagined evaluations a more trustworthy proxy for actual policy performance.
3. **Better Understanding of Policy Performance**: By achieving a high Pearson correlation (0.94) between real and imagined success rates across VLA checkpoints, PiL-World offers a more accurate representation of how policy improvements or degradations manifest in a simulated environment. This allows researchers to assess the relative performance of different policy versions or training stages more effectively.
4. **Learning from Diverse Trajectories**: The training methodology, which includes both successful demonstrations and failed teleoperated trajectories, is crucial. This approach allows PiL-World to capture a broader spectrum of robot-environment interactions and outcomes, enhancing its ability to realistically simulate scenarios where policies may not succeed. This capability is vital for robust policy validation and understanding failure modes.
5. **Foundation for Future Robot Learning**: A highly consistent and reliable world model like PiL-World can serve as a powerful tool beyond just evaluation. It could potentially be used for data augmentation, model-predictive control, or even for training policies through imagined interactions, further reducing the reliance on real-world data collection.
6. **Addressing a Critical Interface Gap**: By specifically designing the world model to match the closed-loop, chunk-wise inference interface of VLA policies, PiL-World directly addresses a critical architectural mismatch prevalent in existing world models. This alignment ensures that the generated observations are directly usable by the policy for subsequent decision-making.

While the current evaluation is limited to specific dual-arm manipulation tasks, the principles and components introduced by PiL-World provide a foundation for developing more generalizable and robust world models for embodied AI, paving the way for more efficient and effective robot learning research and development.
