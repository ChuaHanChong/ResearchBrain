# Learning Soccer Skills for Humanoid Robots: A Progressive Perception-Action Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> A progressive perception-action framework enables humanoid robots to acquire human-like soccer kicking skills, demonstrating robust performance across varied ball positions and effective transfer to physical robots on diverse terrains.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with complex, dynamic tasks like soccer, particularly at human-like scales, due to amplified challenges in whole-body balance and precise manipulation.
- Existing modular robot control architectures suffer from 'inter-module representation gaps,' while end-to-end reinforcement learning frameworks often face 'training instability stemming from reward conflicts' across multiple objectives.
- Achieving high-fidelity, human-like kicking with robust positional generalization and reliable sim-to-real transfer for large humanoid robots remains a persistent challenge.

## Method

- ==Introduces PAiD== (Perception-Action integrated Decision-making), a progressive three-stage framework that sequentially handles motion acquisition, perception integration, and physics-aware sim-to-real transfer.
- Employs a unified policy with adaptive sampling and terrain randomization to learn diverse, high-fidelity human soccer kicking motions in a perception-free environment.
- Integrates lightweight perception for positional generalization by sampling randomized ball spawns with initial velocities and using an LSTM-based policy to implicitly predict rolling ball trajectories, guided by minimal task-specific rewards.

## Results

- PAiD achieved competitive or leading motion tracking quality against state-of-the-art baselines for both standard and stylized human kicking motions.
- In simulation, PAiD achieved a **91.3%** success rate for static ball kicks and **71.9%** for rolling ball interception within the effective workspace, significantly outperforming Pure RL, AMP-based, and Single-Stage baselines.
- On a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, the framework demonstrated successful, human-like kicks on both hard ground and grass, robustly intercepting rolling balls, confirming the efficacy of its physics-aware sim-to-real transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A 'divide-and-conquer' progressive learning strategy effectively mitigates reward conflicts and inter-module instability, making complex embodied skill acquisition more tractable and robust for humanoid robots.
> - High-fidelity human-like motion tracking can be robustly combined with lightweight perception to achieve versatile positional generalization without undermining the learned kinematics or introducing new instabilities.
> - Physics-aware sim-to-real transfer, incorporating real-world contact dynamics identification via CMA-ES and state-dependent observation noise, is critical for achieving consistent performance on physical robots across diverse real-world surfaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics, particularly in the context of complex, dynamic tasks like soccer, represents a frontier in embodied AI. Historically, the RoboCup initiative has served as a benchmark for integrated perception-action capabilities since 1997. Early successes in robot soccer predominantly involved simpler platforms like quadrupedal robots or smaller bipedal robots (e.g., Robotis OP3, 0.5m tall). However, the scaling up of humanoid robots to human-like dimensions (e.g., over 1.2m tall and 30kg mass, such as the Unitree G1 used in this study) introduces significantly amplified challenges in whole-body balance, dynamic locomotion, and precise manipulation due to increased gravitational and inertial effects.

Current approaches to teaching humanoid robots complex skills generally fall into two categories:

- **Modular Hierarchical Architectures:** These systems decouple components such as perception, motion planning, and low-level control. While offering design clarity, they often suffer from "inter-module representation gaps," where errors or inconsistencies in one module propagate and degrade the performance of subsequent modules.
- **End-to-End Reinforcement Learning (RL) Frameworks:** These frameworks attempt to jointly optimize visual inputs directly to motor control. While promising for seamless integration, they frequently encounter "training instability stemming from reward conflicts" when trying to balance multiple objectives (e.g., locomotion, kicking, balance, recovery) within a single optimization process. Moreover, many successful vision-driven approaches, even those leveraging adversarial motion priors (AMP), still rely on meticulously engineered rewards, limiting their scalability and robustness in diverse real-world scenarios.

This paper directly addresses these limitations. It positions itself as an advancement that combines the benefits of structured learning (like modularity) with the adaptability of RL, but critically, it rethinks the acquisition process itself. Instead of a monolithic or loosely coupled approach, PAiD proposes a **progressive, staged architecture** to learn complex humanoid soccer skills. This "divide-and-conquer" strategy aims to mitigate reward conflicts and inter-module instability by building skills incrementally: first mastering foundational motions, then integrating perception, and finally addressing the sim-to-real gap. This differentiates PAiD from prior work by offering a more stable and scalable framework for complex embodied skill acquisition, moving beyond the traditional dichotomies and offering a novel curriculum learning strategy for humanoid control. It specifically targets the challenge of achieving human-like kicking with robust performance in dynamic, unstructured environments, a goal that remains difficult for existing methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a humanoid robot to execute precise, human-like soccer kicks towards a target from arbitrary ball positions, under diverse environmental conditions. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific, motivated objectives:

1. **Acquire High-Fidelity Human-Like Kicking Motions:** The authors aim to allow humanoid robots to replicate the natural biomechanics and styles of human soccer players. This is motivated by the observation that existing methods often produce unnatural kicks or struggle with the coordination required for dynamic, expressive movements. The goal is to learn a diverse repertoire of kicking skills, including both standard and stylized motions.
2. **Achieve Robust Positional Generalization:** A key challenge in robot soccer is adapting to varying ball positions, including both static and moving balls. The motivation here is to develop a policy that can generalize kicking actions beyond a few fixed points, allowing the robot to approach and strike the ball accurately from any location within its operational workspace, without requiring extensive, task-specific reward engineering for each scenario.
3. **Ensure Stable Perception-Action Integration:** Traditional end-to-end RL often suffers from "reward conflicts" when trying to optimize perception-driven navigation, precise kicking, and whole-body balance simultaneously. The authors are motivated to decouple these complex objectives into stages to ensure stability. They seek a "lightweight perception integration mechanism" that guides the robot towards the ball without disrupting the underlying learned motion dynamics.
4. **Facilitate Reliable Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Bridging the "reality gap" between simulation and the physical world is a persistent challenge in robotics. Even minor discrepancies in physical properties (e.g., ball friction, restitution) or sensory noise can drastically degrade performance. The motivation is to develop a "physics-aware" transfer strategy that explicitly identifies and aligns key physical parameters in simulation with real-world measurements and incorporates realistic perception uncertainty models.
5. **Develop a Scalable Framework for Complex Embodied Skills:** Beyond just soccer, the authors are motivated to propose a generalizable "divide-and-conquer strategy" that could serve as a template for acquiring other complex, perception-based human skills in embodied agents. This addresses the broader challenge of making RL for complex robotic tasks more tractable and robust.

In essence, the core motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing monolithic and modular approaches by introducing a progressive, curriculum-based learning framework that builds complex skills incrementally, leading to more robust, versatile, and human-like humanoid robot capabilities for dynamic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Perception-Action integrated Decision-making (PAiD) framework adopts a progressive, three-stage architecture to achieve robust humanoid soccer skills. This "divide-and-conquer" strategy sequentially addresses motion acquisition, perception integration, and sim-to-real transfer. The overall problem is formulated as a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP) and solved using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The observation space is designed to include proprioception (robot's internal state), reference motion targets, and soccer-specific information (ball and goal positions).

**Stage I: Soccer-skill Acquisition via Motion Tracking**
This initial stage focuses on learning diverse, high-fidelity human-like kicking primitives in a perception-free environment.
- **Data Preparation:** A specialized motion dataset of 13 distinct human soccer kicking motions is curated using a motion capture system. This includes:
 - **Standard Kicks:** Recorded with uniformly distributed ball placements to cover various distances and shooting angles.
 - **Stylized Kicks:** Mimicking signature moves of professional players, involving highly dynamic movements.
 - Motions are retargeted to the robot (via GMR) and annotated with the striking leg, which acts as a gating signal for later rewards.
- **Unified Motion Tracking:** Instead of learning separate policies, a single, unified policy is trained to track a collection of reference motions.
 - **Yaw-only alignment** (from BeyondMimic) adjusts the robot's heading to strike the ball at various angles.
 - **Adaptive Sampling Strategy:** To handle varying task difficulties (e.g., stylized kicks are harder), a failure histogram tracks performance across motion indices and phase bins. This dynamically steers training towards the most difficult segments, promoting uniform proficiency.
 - **Terrain Randomization:** Irregular and uneven surfaces are introduced during Stage I training to enhance terrain adaptability from the outset, removing the need to explicitly manage terrain difficulty later.
- **Reward Design:** Primarily relies on motion-tracking rewards (e.g., anchor position/orientation, body position/orientation, linear/angular velocity, foot position tracking) to ensure high-fidelity imitation, combined with regularization terms (e.g., action-rate, joint-limit, undesired-contact penalties). Episodes terminate if safety constraints are violated.

**Stage II: Perception-Guided Positional Generalization**
After mastering foundational motions, this stage integrates lightweight perception to generalize kicking skills to varying ball positions while preserving the learned human-like kinematics.
- **Ball Position Sampling:** The ball is initially placed at a motion-consistent nominal location (terminal foot contact). Then, randomized ball spawns are generated by applying angular perturbations and radius offsets around this nominal location, creating a "curved arc" of feasible ball locations.
 - Crucially, an initial linear velocity is sampled for the ball, and an LSTM-based policy architecture is used for temporal aggregation, enabling the policy to implicitly predict short-horizon trajectories for rolling balls.
- **Perception Observation:** The policy receives continuous observations of the ball and goal positions relative to the robot's pelvis frame (g_ball, g_goal).
- **Lightweight Perception-Action Rewards:** Motion tracking rewards are retained, but global position constraints are relaxed (anchor position tracking weight set to zero) to allow for locomotion and stance adjustment. A small set of task-specific rewards is added:
 - **Ball proximity:** Exponential reward on horizontal base-to-ball distance, frozen after first contact to prevent continuous pursuit.
 - **Contact correctness:** One-time reward if the contacting foot matches the intended kicking leg.
 - **Strike direction prior:** Encourages foot swing alignment with the expected lateral striking direction.
 - **Post-kick outcome shaping:** A short-window reward after contact to align ball velocity with the target goal direction and encourage sufficient planar speed while penalizing excessive vertical speed.
- **Stabilization Terms:** Additional regularization terms are introduced to maintain balance during perception-driven adjustments (e.g., waist action-rate, pelvis uprightness, foot-separation reward).

**Physics-Aware Sim-to-Real Transfer**
This stage bridges the reality gap by aligning simulation physics with the real world and modeling perception uncertainty.
- **Contact Dynamics Identification:**
 - Real-world ball drop and rolling experiments are conducted to characterize normal impact and tangential friction behavior on different surfaces (hard ground, grass).
 - These experiments are replicated in IsaacSim, and a compact set of ball contact parameters (static/dynamic friction, restitution, linear/angular damping) are optimized using **CMA-ES (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy)** to minimize the trajectory matching loss between real and simulated data.
 - Two sets of nominal parameters are identified for hard ground and grass. During training, environments are split between these sets, and parameters are further perturbed with Gaussian noise to account for residual modeling errors.
- **Physics-Guided Domain Randomization (DR):** Beyond standard DR (robot friction, restitution, joint defaults, CoM, push forces), a tailored noise model is introduced for object-related observations.
 - **State-Dependent Observation Noise:** Zero-mean Gaussian noise is injected into ball and goal position observations, with magnitude dynamically adjusted based on the object's velocity and distance from the robot (σ_obj = c_min + ||v_obj||/c_vel + ||p_obj - p_robot||/c_dist). This models realistic perception uncertainty.

**Real-World Deployment:**
- **Perception Module:** Fuses depth camera (YOLOv8 for ball mask) and LiDAR (Fast-LIO for global pose estimation) to provide robust, continuous ball localization, even when the ball leaves the camera's field of view.
- **Decision Module:** A simple motion selection mechanism preloads candidate motions, selecting the one whose final-frame robot root position is closest to the estimated ball position for execution by the unified policy.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that PAiD systematically tackles the multi-faceted challenges of humanoid soccer, from fundamental motion control to real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments systematically evaluate PAiD across three key dimensions: motion tracking quality, soccer shooting proficiency, and real-world deployment performance on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

**Q1: Motion Tracking Quality**
- **Metrics:** Global Mean Per-Joint Position Error (G-MPJPE), Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE), Velocity Error (Vel Err), and AUJ Score (physical smoothness).
- **Comparison:** PAiD was benchmarked against state-of-the-art motion tracking approaches: GMT, Any2Track, TWIST2, and BeyondMimic, on both "Standard Kicks" (10 motions) and "Stylized Kicks" (3 motions).
- **Results:** PAiD achieved competitive or leading results across most metrics. While BeyondMimic showed slightly better performance on some metrics, PAiD demonstrated a balanced trade-off between precision and versatility, effectively supporting robust tracking of a diverse repertoire of soccer skills, including highly dynamic stylized kicks. This confirms the framework's ability to learn high-fidelity, physically feasible kicking primitives.

**Q2: Soccer Shooting Proficiency**
- **Experimental Protocols:**
 1. **Grid-Based Static Evaluation:** 11x13 grid of initial ball positions (0.5-3.0m depth, +/-1.5m width), 50 trials per point with randomized robot initializations.
 2. **Rolling Ball Interception:** Similar grid, but with balls initialized with velocities [0.1, 0.3] m/s.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (goals/total kicks) and Kick Accuracy (cosine similarity between outgoing ball velocity and target direction).
- **Comparison:** PAiD was compared against Pure RL (trained from scratch with manual rewards), AMP-based (adversarial motion priors), and Single-Stage (jointly optimizing tracking and task rewards).
- **Results:** Within the effective workspace ([0.5, 2.0] x [-1.0, 1.0] m), PAiD significantly outperformed all baselines:
 - **Static Evaluation:** 91.3% success rate and 0.9689 kick accuracy.
 - **Rolling Interception:** 71.9% success rate and 0.8892 kick accuracy.
 - Baselines struggled due to unnatural kicks (AMP), reward engineering complexity (Pure RL), or reward conflicts (Single-Stage).
- **Spatial Performance (Heatmaps):** Visualizations showed strong performance within the training range and reasonable generalization beyond it, with proficiency naturally decreasing at kinematic boundaries. This confirms that the perception-guided integration effectively enables positional generalization.

**Q3: Real-World Performance on Unitree G1**
- **Deployment:** The policy was deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot using a perception-and-decision framework combining a D435i depth camera (YOLOv8) and LiDAR (Fast-LIO) for robust ball localization, and a motion selection mechanism.
- **Soccer Shooting Proficiency and Terrain Robustness:**
 - The robot successfully kicked soccer balls on both hard ground and grass surfaces, achieving high success rates for most ball locations. Performance was slightly lower in extreme corner cases requiring large body rotations.
 - The robot demonstrated successful interception and kicking of rolling balls, a critical capability for dynamic gameplay.
 - Ablation studies on the real robot confirmed the effectiveness of the physics-aware sim-to-real transfer strategy:
 - Removing contact dynamics identification or observation noise led to noticeable performance degradation.
 - Combining both components yielded the best overall results, demonstrating their crucial role in bridging the sim-to-real gap and adapting to varying ground conditions.
- **Motion Human-Likeness Test:** The robot consistently executed professional-player–style shooting motions (Fig. 1b) in a stable and controlled manner, achieving accurate, goal-directed shots. This validated the high fidelity and expressiveness of the method in transferring diverse human motions to the real robot.

In summary, the main findings strongly support the efficacy of the PAiD framework. It successfully combines high-fidelity human motion tracking with robust perception-action integration and effective sim-to-real transfer, leading to superior and more human-like soccer skills in complex humanoid robots compared to existing methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Learning Soccer Skills for Humanoid Robots: A Progressive Perception-Action Framework" paper introduces a highly significant contribution to the fields of robotics, reinforcement learning, and embodied AI. Its primary significance lies in offering a robust and scalable solution to a long-standing challenge: enabling complex humanoid robots to perform dynamic, perception-dependent tasks with human-like proficiency in unstructured environments.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Novel Progressive Learning Framework:** The most impactful contribution is the progressive, three-stage decomposition of skill acquisition (motion acquisition, perception-action integration, and sim-to-real transfer). This "divide-and-conquer" strategy is a significant advancement over monolithic end-to-end RL approaches that struggle with reward conflicts, and modular pipelines that suffer from inter-module instability. By acquiring foundational motion skills first in a perception-free setting, PAiD establishes a stable base, then integrates lightweight perception without disrupting core dynamics, and finally addresses the sim-to-real gap methodically. This approach is intrinsically more stable and tractable for complex behaviors.
2. **Versatile Motion Tracking and Lightweight Perception Integration:** The ability to learn a diverse repertoire of human-like kicking motions (standard and stylized) through adaptive sampling is crucial for the expressiveness and versatility of the robot. The lightweight nature of perception integration, with minimal task-specific rewards and stabilization terms, ensures that the robot can generalize to varying ball positions (static and rolling) without undermining the learned human-like kinematics or introducing new instabilities. This demonstrates an elegant solution to the perception-action coupling problem.
3. **Physics-Aware Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The rigorous approach to bridging the reality gap is highly significant. Explicitly identifying and optimizing contact dynamics parameters through real-world experiments (ball drop, rolling) and incorporating physics-guided, state-dependent observation noise models a more realistic distribution of real-world uncertainties. This contrasts with less structured domain randomization techniques and is shown to be crucial for robust deployment, leading to consistent performance across varied terrains (hard ground, grass).

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancement in Humanoid Robotics:** PAiD pushes the boundaries of what humanoid robots can achieve in dynamic, unstructured tasks. By demonstrating high-fidelity human-like kicking with robust performance under diverse conditions on a large-scale humanoid platform (Unitree G1), it provides a compelling blueprint for developing more agile, adaptable, and capable humanoid robots.
- **Broader Application in Embodied AI:** The "divide-and-conquer" strategy is not limited to soccer. It offers a scalable framework for acquiring other complex, perception-based human skills in embodied agents, such as dancing, manipulation, or advanced locomotion tasks. This could accelerate progress in fields requiring robots to interact naturally and effectively with the physical world.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Methodologies:** The physics-aware sim-to-real transfer strategy, particularly the contact dynamics identification and state-dependent observation noise, provides a more principled and effective methodology for transferring policies from simulation to reality. This could significantly reduce the cost and time associated with real-world robot development.
- **Future of Robot Sports and Human-Robot Interaction:** The achievement of human-like, accurate soccer skills brings robot soccer closer to its long-term goals. Beyond competition, the ability for robots to execute natural human motions opens doors for more intuitive human-robot collaboration, rehabilitation robotics, and even entertainment.

While the paper acknowledges limitations regarding extreme ball positions and highly dynamic conditions, its contributions lay a strong foundation for future research. The progressive architecture, detailed in this work, represents a significant step forward in making complex, human-like behaviors achievable and robust for next-generation humanoid robots.
