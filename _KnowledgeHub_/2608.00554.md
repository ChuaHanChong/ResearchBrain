# DexMani: Human-Derived Manipulability Guidance for Dexterous Rotation

> [!summary] Summary
> The DexMani framework distills contact-conditioned rotational manipulability evolution from human demonstrations, providing an embodiment-agnostic prior to guide robot reinforcement learning for dexterous object rotation. This approach improves generalization capabilities across different robot hands and previously unseen tasks, leading to more robust and coordinated manipulation policies.

## Problem

- Dexterous object rotation requires complex sequential contact decisions, where each interaction influences both immediate object motion and the hand's long-term ability to sustain rotation.
- Existing learning methods often struggle with generalization across different robot hands or objects due to reliance on embodiment-specific data or a lack of explicit guidance on contact-conditioned rotational capabilities.
- Conventional manipulability measures do not fully account for the dynamic constraints imposed by object contacts, which are critical for sustained dexterous manipulation.

## Method

- A novel analytic descriptor, **'contact-conditioned manipulability,'** is introduced, derived from human visual-tactile data, explicitly accounting for active contacts to represent rotational capabilities.
- An ==energy-based prior model== is trained on human demonstrations to distill successful short-horizon manipulability evolution directions, assigning low energy to human-consistent changes.
- The frozen human-derived prior provides local action-space guidance as a ==residual bias== for a downstream robot ==reinforcement learning== policy, which then learns to achieve human-like manipulability evolution through its own actions.

## Results

- Achieved superior success rates (average **57.5%** on LEAP Hand) across rotation tasks and objects, demonstrating enhanced generalization to unseen objects and cross-task transfer with a single prior.
- Showed consistent superiority in cross-embodiment generalization on the Unscrew Cap task, with the same human-derived prior guiding four distinct robotic hands to higher success rates (average **43.4%**).
- Resulted in smoother, more coordinated robot motions and successful closed-loop deployment on a physical LEAP Hand, demonstrating practical applicability and effective sim-to-real transfer for dexterous manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human manipulation implicitly encodes an understanding of how contact transitions dynamically reshape the hand's rotational capabilities, which can be quantified as an 'evolution' and transferred.
> - Guiding robot learning with an abstract, contact-conditioned manipulability evolution prior is more transferable and effective across diverse robot hands and tasks than direct action retargeting or instantaneous manipulability maximization.
> - An energy model, trained contrastively with structured negative samples, can effectively distill human preferences for manipulability evolution into an embodiment-agnostic prior for robot control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous object rotation is a fundamental challenge in robotics, characterized by sequential contact problems where each interaction decision influences both immediate object motion and the hand's subsequent ability to maintain rotation. This work addresses a recognized gap in existing learning-based approaches for robot manipulation.

Current reinforcement learning (RL) methods, while capable of discovering movement patterns, often rely on extensive trial-and-error specific to particular robotic hand embodiments. These methods typically do not explicitly incorporate how each contact transition affects the hand's long-term capability to sustain object rotation, referred to in this paper as "contact-conditioned rotational manipulability." Consequently, learned policies may struggle with generalization across different robot hands or objects without significant re-training.

Similarly, imitation learning techniques that transfer human demonstrations often retarget human motions into embodiment-specific robot trajectories or joint commands. These approaches tend to be hardware-dependent, necessitating adaptation or re-learning for each new robotic hand with distinct kinematics and workspaces. While some methods incorporate contact priors or error correction models, they have not extensively utilized contact-conditioned manipulability evolution as an explicit, transferable guidance signal.

Prior research on manipulability has focused on characterizing the range of motion directions enabled by a robot's configuration. This work has explored the geometry of manipulability ellipsoids, transferred desired manipulability profiles, and integrated manipulability objectives into policy learning. However, these existing formulations frequently do not explicitly account for the dynamic constraints imposed by object contacts during manipulation, which are critical for tasks like dexterous rotation.

DexMani aims to bridge these limitations by introducing a framework that explicitly distills and leverages "contact-conditioned manipulability evolution" from human demonstrations. This concept provides an embodiment-agnostic representation that captures how successful contact transitions reshape the available object-rotation directions for a given hand. By integrating this human-derived prior into downstream reinforcement learning, DexMani positions itself as a method to enhance the generalization capabilities of dexterous manipulation policies across diverse robotic hands and tasks, reducing the need for extensive robot-specific exploration or direct action retargeting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent complexity of dexterous object rotation, which requires intricate sequences of support, release, and re-contact decisions. Each decision must not only contribute to the desired object motion but also configure the hand optimally for continued rotation. This capability, dependent on the hand's current configuration and contacts, is termed "contact-conditioned rotational manipulability." The authors identify that maintaining and reshaping this manipulability over successive transitions is crucial for sustained rotation, yet this aspect has received limited attention in existing learning-based robotic manipulation methods.

The key objectives of the DexMani framework are:

1. **To introduce Contact-Conditioned Manipulability**: Develop a novel, human-to-robot representation that explicitly captures how contact transitions reshape the task-space rotational capabilities of a hand. This aims to overcome the limitation of conventional manipulability measures that do not explicitly account for contact constraints during manipulation.
2. **To develop an Energy-Based Prior**: Create a model that distills successful human rotation demonstrations into a prior over the short-horizon evolution of this contact-conditioned manipulability. This prior should be capable of evaluating whether changes in manipulability are consistent with human-observed patterns and conducive to sustained rotation.
3. **To propose a Reinforcement Learning Framework with Manipulability Guidance**: Integrate the learned manipulability evolution prior into a downstream reinforcement learning process. This framework is designed to provide residual action guidance to robot policies, encouraging them to follow human-like manipulability evolution trends.
4. **To achieve Cross-Embodiment and Cross-Task Generalization**: Evaluate the effectiveness of this embodiment-agnostic prior in enabling the acquisition of rotation skills across different dexterous robot hands with distinct kinematics (e.g., Shadow Hand, Allegro Hand, XHand, LEAP Hand) and for various rotation tasks (e.g., Unscrew Cap, Rotate Object, Turn Faucet), including generalization to unseen objects and tasks.
5. **To demonstrate Practical Applicability**: Conduct closed-loop deployment of the proposed approach on a physical dexterous hand, highlighting its potential for real-world robotic systems.

In essence, the motivation is to leverage the implicit understanding of manipulability evolution demonstrated by humans to provide a more generalizable and efficient learning signal for robots, thereby overcoming the limitations of purely data-driven or embodiment-specific approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DexMani framework operates in two main stages: first, learning a manipulability-evolution preference prior from human demonstrations, and second, using this prior to guide downstream robot reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Human Demonstration Collection

High-quality human demonstrations are critical for distilling the manipulability prior. The authors developed a multimodal data collection system to capture synchronized hand pose, dense fingertip tactile pressure, and RGB observations from human participants rotating target objects. This system integrates a Meta Quest 3 controller (for global hand pose), a Manus Quantum MetaGlove (for finger joint poses), and a JQ-Industries tactile glove (for fingertip contact pressure). Over 100,000 frames of human rotation data were collected, providing synchronized recordings to determine active contact states and compute contact-conditioned manipulability labels.

#### 3.2. Analytic Manipulability from Human Data

This component involves processing the human data to derive a transferable representation of rotational capability.

- **Visual-Tactile Encoder**: A transformer-based network fuses visual (RGB images) and tactile (finger-level contact tokens derived from raw pressure readings) histories into a compact visual-tactile representation ($z_{VT_t}$). This encoder captures the current hand-object interaction and contact state.
- **Contact-conditioned Manipulability**: A novel analytic descriptor, $m_{hum_t}$, is introduced. This is a 6-dimensional log-Euclidean coordinate derived from the hand's rotational manipulability matrix ($M^\omega_t$). Unlike conventional manipulability, it explicitly accounts for active contacts. It is computed by extending the standard manipulability formulation with tactile-induced kinematic constraints, using the stacked Jacobian of active fingertip contacts ($J^c_{hum,t}$), tactile confidence weights ($W_t$), and a damped pseudoinverse of the grasp matrix ($G^+_t$). The rotational component is selected, and the result is converted to $m_{hum_t}$. Critically, fingertip positions and Jacobians are expressed in a shared wrist-frame convention for both human demonstrations and robot rollouts. This ensures that the dimensions of $m_t$ describe rotational capabilities about consistent x-, y-, and z-directions, making the descriptor cross-embodiment transferable without requiring joint-level correspondence or action retargeting.

#### 3.3. Manipulability-Evolution Energy Prior

Using the extracted contact-conditioned manipulability descriptors, an energy model $E_\theta(\hat{v} | c_t)$ is trained to learn a prior over the *evolution* of manipulability during successful human object rotation. The model assigns low energy to manipulability changes consistent with human demonstrations and high energy to incompatible directions.

- **Contrastive Energy Objective**: The model learns to differentiate between desired and undesired manipulability changes. A "positive direction" ($\hat{v}^+_t$) is defined as the normalized short-horizon change from the current manipulability ($m_t$) to a future state ($m_{t+\Delta}$). This focuses the model on the direction of evolution.
- **Structured Negative Sampling**: To train the model on incompatible directions, each positive sample is contrasted with K structured negatives. These negatives include random directions, reversed directions, and directions drawn from mismatched interaction contexts. This strategy helps shape a robust energy landscape.
- **Full Pretraining Loss**: The training objective combines a contrastive energy loss ($L_E$), an auxiliary loss for predicting the log-scaled magnitude of manipulability change ($L_\alpha$), and auxiliary losses ($L_{rec}, L_{pred}$) for the visual-tactile encoder. After pretraining, the visual-tactile encoder and the energy model are frozen.

#### 3.4. Manipulability Energy-Guided Residual Policy

The frozen human-derived prior is then used to guide robot learning. This guidance is applied as a local action-space hint for a residual policy, rather than directly executing the preferred direction.

- **Predicting Action-Induced Manipulability Change**: Given a robot's current observation ($V^r_t, T^r_t, q_t$) and a nominal action ($a^0_t$) proposed by a base policy, candidate actions around $a^0_t$ are generated. For each candidate action, a one-step estimate of the resulting manipulability change ($\hat{v}^r_t(a)$) is computed. This estimation uses the robot's current joint configuration to predict fingertip displacement and subsequently updates the contact Jacobian and grasp matrix to calculate the new rotational manipulability.
- **Energy-Guided Action Bias**: The frozen energy model $E_\theta$ evaluates each candidate action based on its induced manipulability-change direction and the current robot context ($c^r_t = [z^r_t, m^r_t, d_t]$). The candidate action that yields the lowest energy (most human-like evolution) defines a local action-space bias ($b^E_t$). A stop-gradient operator and clipping are applied to this bias to limit its influence and prevent it from overriding the base policy.
- **Residual Policy and Training**: A residual policy ($\pi_{res,\phi}$) is trained to output a correction ($\Delta a_t$), conditioned on the robot's state, nominal action, and the energy-guided bias ($b^E_t$). The final action executed is $a_t = clip_A(a^0_t + \lambda_R \Delta a_t)$. Both the base and residual policies are optimized using standard PPO with only the original task reward. This structure allows the robot to learn how to realize the human-derived manipulability evolution through its own specific actions and contact transitions.

#### 3.5. Sim-to-Real Transfer

To facilitate deployment on physical hardware, domain randomization is applied during simulation training. This involves perturbing various parameters such as joint angles and velocities, object colors, light directions, camera positions, image brightness/contrast, and tactile force readings. This approach aims to improve the robustness of the learned policies to discrepancies between simulation and the real world.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DexMani focused on task success, generalization capabilities, execution quality, and real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Generalization Across Tasks and Objects (LEAP Hand)

DexMani was evaluated on three contact-rich rotation tasks (Unscrew Cap, Rotate Object, Turn Faucet) using the LEAP Hand. The human-derived prior was pretrained using Unscrew Cap and Rotate Object data, with Turn Faucet reserved for cross-task generalization assessment.
- **Overall Success Rates**: DexMani achieved the highest success rates (SR) across all six task-split conditions (seen and unseen objects for each task), with an average SR of 57.5%. This represented an improvement of 5.6 percentage points over VTM, the strongest baseline.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects**: The performance gains were particularly notable on unseen objects, indicating that the learned manipulability guidance generalizes beyond the specific object instances and categories encountered during policy training.
- **Cross-Task Transfer**: DexMani performed strongly on Turn Faucet, even though this task was not included in the human-prior pretraining dataset. This finding demonstrates the transferability of the single frozen prior to previously unseen rotation tasks.
- **Baseline Comparisons**:
 - VT Pretraining and VTM (pretraining with visual-tactile representation and manipulability prediction, respectively) showed limited performance, suggesting that representation pretraining alone is insufficient without online policy guidance.
 - VTA and VTA-E (guiding with retargeted human actions) performed less effectively than DexMani, supporting the hypothesis that manipulability-based representations transfer more robustly across embodiments than direct action retargeting.

#### 4.2. Generalization Across Hands (Cap Unscrewing)

To assess cross-embodiment transfer, DexMani was evaluated on the Unscrew Cap task using three different dexterous hands: Shadow Hand, Allegro Hand, and XHand. The same task definition, reward function, and frozen human-derived prior model were used for all hands, with separate policies trained for each hand's native action space.
- **Consistent Superiority**: DexMani achieved the highest SR for both seen and unseen objects across all three robotic hands, with an average SR of 43.4%. This consistency indicated that the manipulability-based guidance is effective and transferable across distinct hand kinematics and morphologies.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to isolate the contributions of different components of the DexMani framework.
- **Importance of Guidance**: "Zero Guidance" (residual policy without the energy-guided bias) resulted in significantly lower success rates, confirming the benefits of manipulability-based guidance.
- **Context-Conditioning**: "Context-Shuffled" (guidance with mismatched interaction context) performed worse than "Zero Guidance," demonstrating the necessity of context-conditioned guidance.
- **Evolution vs. Instantaneous Maximization**: "Greedy-M" (guiding towards greedily maximizing instantaneous rotational manipulability) was less effective than DexMani, indicating that the value lies in guiding the *evolution* of manipulability according to human preferences, rather than simply maximizing instantaneous capability. DexMani also showed faster learning and better final performance on learning curves.

#### 4.4. Motion Quality and Deployment

- **Motion Smoothness and Rotational Capability**: DexMani achieved superior scores on metrics for motion smoothness (LDLJ and SPARC) across all three tasks, suggesting more coordinated object manipulation. It also demonstrated higher Task Compatibility Index (TCI) on Unscrew Cap and Turn Faucet, indicating a stronger ability to generate rotational motion aligned with the target axis.
- **Real-World Deployment**: DexMani was deployed on a physical 16-DoF LEAP Hand with TwinTac tactile sensors. It successfully performed closed-loop rotation tasks in the real world (e.g., 6/10 success rate for Unscrew Cap, 3/10 for Rotate Object, 1/10 for Turn Faucet). This demonstrated its ability for sim-to-real transfer, despite inherent discrepancies in sensing and dynamics. The lower success rate on the Faucet task was attributed to a combined task and object distribution shift compared to training data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexMani framework introduces "contact-conditioned manipulability evolution" as a novel and transferable representation of human manipulation experience. This approach provides a mechanism to convey rich, contact-aware guidance across different robotic embodiments, a significant departure from conventional methods that rely on embodiment-specific action imitation or extensive robot-generated interaction data.

The key significance of this work includes:

1. **Enhanced Generalization**: By distilling human preferences into an embodiment-agnostic manipulability prior, DexMani demonstrates a notable improvement in generalization capabilities. Policies learned with DexMani exhibit higher success rates on unseen objects and can transfer effectively to tasks not seen during the prior's pretraining. Crucially, the same human-derived prior can guide robot learning across diverse dexterous hands (e.g., Shadow Hand, Allegro Hand, XHand, LEAP Hand) with differing kinematics.
2. **Improved Learning Efficiency and Quality**: The guidance from the manipulability evolution prior facilitates faster learning and achieves higher final policy performance in reinforcement learning. Furthermore, the resulting robot motions are characterized by increased smoothness and rotational capability, suggesting more dexterous and human-like execution.
3. **Foundation for Human-Inspired Robot Skills**: This work establishes manipulability evolution as a viable, quantifiable way to transfer abstract human manipulation strategies to robots. This could reduce the data requirements for learning complex dexterous skills and accelerate the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
4. **Practical Real-World Applicability**: The successful closed-loop deployment on a physical LEAP Hand highlights the practical potential of DexMani. Despite sim-to-real gaps, the approach’s robustness, aided by domain randomization, suggests its applicability in real-world scenarios requiring dexterous in-hand manipulation.

While the current work requires a separately trained policy for each task-hand configuration, its findings lay groundwork for future research into unified policies capable of direct cross-embodiment transfer. This could further democratize access to advanced dexterous manipulation capabilities across a wider range of robotic hardware and contribute to the development of more general artificial intelligence for robotics.
