# PIE-NeRF: Physics-based Interactive Elastodynamics with NeRF

> [!summary] Summary
> PIE-NeRF introduces a framework for interactive, physics-based elastodynamic simulation of objects represented by Neural Radiance Fields. It enables physically accurate, nonlinear deformations at interactive rates by leveraging a meshless approach with quadratic Generalized Moving Least Squares (Q-GMLS) and a precise quadratic ray-warping scheme, preserving high visual quality.

## Problem

- Integrating traditional mesh-dependent physics simulation techniques with the implicit, continuous representations of NeRFs is challenging.
- Existing NeRF editing methods often rely on heuristic energy models like As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP), which are insufficient for realistic large deformations and material properties.
- Physically simulating NeRFs typically requires converting them into explicit mesh or voxel proxies, introducing computational overhead and complexity, especially for intricate or codimensional shapes.

## Method

- A meshless approach uses adaptively sampled Poisson disk particles and Quadratic Generalized Moving Least Squares (Q-**GMLS**) kernels to create a sparse, reduced-order geometric representation of the **NeRF**.
- Nonlinear elastodynamic behavior is formulated using Lagrangian mechanics with hyperelastic material models and an implicit Euler scheme, solved iteratively with Newton's method on the reduced Q-GMLS system.
- A quadratic ray-warping algorithm accurately maps deformed points back to their rest-pose for ==NeRF== rendering, which is critical for preserving high visual fidelity under large deformations.

## Results

- Achieved interactive-rate simulation and manipulation of NeRF scenes, enabling users to apply forces and constraints for novel, physics-grounded elastodynamic effects.
- Demonstrated superior physical realism compared to heuristic models, accurately simulating nonlinear hyperelastic materials and robustly handling topology changes without explicit mesh updates.
- Outperformed existing physics-based NeRF methods like PAC-NeRF in rendering quality under large deformations and provided significantly faster simulation speeds (up to two orders of magnitude).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive Poisson disk sampling combined with Q-GMLS kernels allows for effective meshless discretization of implicit NeRFs, enabling physics-based simulation without explicit mesh generation.
> - Quadratic Generalized Moving Least Squares (Q-GMLS) is essential for robustly capturing nonlinear dynamics and large deformations, particularly for codimensional shapes prone to locking artifacts.
> - Employing a quadratic ray-warping scheme, which leverages the Q-GMLS displacement field, is crucial for maintaining the original NeRF's visual quality even with substantial physical deformations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D reconstruction and representation has seen a significant shift with the advent of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [Mildenhall et al., 2020]. NeRFs implicitly encode 3D scene geometry and appearance, achieving impressive photorealistic novel view synthesis from multi-view images. Following NeRF, numerous advancements have improved its visual quality, performance, and reduced input requirements. A major line of research has also focused on extending NeRFs beyond static scene reconstruction to dynamic scenes and scene editing.

However, complex, nonlinear, and time-coherent elastodynamic motion synthesis that is genuinely grounded in real-world physics has remained largely unexplored within the NeRF ecosystem. Existing NeRF editing methods often rely on heuristic energy models, like As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) energy, which are less suitable for realistic large deformations and material properties. Furthermore, these methods frequently require converting the implicit NeRF representation into an explicit mesh or voxel grid, introducing computational overhead and complicating the pipeline, especially for objects with intricate or codimensional shapes. The innate incompatibility between continuous implicit representations (NeRF) and discrete physical simulation methods (like Finite Element Method, FEM, which typically requires meshes) poses a significant challenge.

This paper directly addresses this gap by proposing a framework that enables physically-grounded, interactive, and dynamic deformations of NeRF models. It differentiates itself from prior work in dynamic NeRFs, which often focus on *reconstructing* existing dynamic scenes from video rather than *generating* novel, physics-driven motions. It also moves beyond geometry-based editing by incorporating full nonlinear hyperelasticity. By leveraging a meshless approach, PIE-NeRF avoids the need for explicit mesh generation, a common bottleneck in physics-based NeRF editing, thus simplifying the pipeline and enhancing versatility for complex geometries. The work positions itself as a critical step towards a more comprehensive "what you see is what you simulate" (WS²) paradigm for neural scene representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of PIE-NeRF is to seamlessly integrate physics-based simulations with NeRF to generate high-quality elastodynamics of real-world objects interactively. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

- **Overcome incompatibility between implicit representations and physical simulation:** Address the fundamental challenge of applying traditional, mesh-dependent physics simulation techniques to the implicit, continuous representations of NeRF.
- **Eliminate auxiliary shape proxies:** Develop a method that obviates the need for intermediate explicit geometry proxies like tetrahedral meshes or voxel grids, which are typically required by existing simulation methods and introduce pipeline complexity.
- **Enable versatile simulation of complex and codimensional shapes:** Design a system robust enough to handle objects with intricate details and varying dimensions (e.g., thin structures), where traditional meshing can be problematic.
- **Capture nonlinear dynamics and large deformation:** Implement a robust numerical scheme that accurately models the complex, nonlinear behavior of materials under large deformations, moving beyond simpler, linearized deformation models.
- **Achieve interactive performance:** Ensure that the physics simulation and subsequent NeRF rendering can operate at interactive rates, allowing users to manipulate objects and observe responses in real-time.
- **Synthesize physically realistic animations:** Allow for the designation of real-world material parameters (e.g., Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio) to NeRF models, leading to animations that adhere to the laws of physics.
- **Improve rendering quality for deformed states:** Develop a robust ray-warping algorithm that accurately retrieves color and texture information from the original NeRF for deformed poses, even under large deformations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PIE-NeRF proposes an efficient and versatile pipeline that leverages meshless discretization and model reduction techniques to achieve interactive physics-based simulation with NeRF. The core steps are as follows:

1. **NeRF Training (NGP-NeRF):** The system begins by training an Instant Neural Graphics Primitives (NGP) NeRF [Müller et al., 2022] from a collection of multi-view images of a static 3D scene. NGP is chosen for its superior efficiency and quality in encoding positional and texture information.
2. **Augmented Poisson Disk Sampling (PDS):** To represent the 3D model's geometry without an explicit mesh, the authors employ an augmented Poisson disk sampling strategy. Particles are adaptively dispersed into the scene. Crucially, the sampling radius `r` is adjusted based on the magnitude of the NGP-NeRF's density gradient (∥∇σ∥). This ensures a higher concentration of particles at the object's boundaries, where geometry is most detailed and density changes are sharpest. Particles with low density are discarded.
3. **Q-GMLS Kernels and Integrator Points (IPs):**
 - **Q-GMLS Kernels:** The PDS particles are grouped using a Voronoi partition. The centers of these Voronoi cells act as "Q-GMLS kernels," which house the system's generalized coordinates (`q`). These kernels serve as a sparse, meshless proxy for the model, enabling model reduction.
 - **Quadratic Generalized Moving Least Square (Q-GMLS):** To capture nonlinear dynamics and large deformations robustly, especially for codimensional (thin) shapes prone to "locking" artifacts with affine GMLS, the method employs Q-GMLS. This technique assumes a quadratic displacement field per kernel. It generalizes standard MLS by incorporating local deformation gradient information, yielding shape functions that depend only on the rest-shape position. This leads to a generalized coordinate vector `q` that includes not only displacement but also its first and second derivatives at kernel centers.
 - **Integrator Points (IPs):** To efficiently compute kinetic and potential energies, additional "integrator points" (IPs) are placed. These include the Q-GMLS kernel centers and other PDS particles selected to accurately approximate energy integrals. Each IP is conceptualized as a small elastic cuboid, whose dimensions are derived from the covariance matrix of its K-nearest PDS particles, ensuring it captures local geometric anisotropy.
4. **Nonlinear Elastodynamic Formulation (Lagrangian Mechanics):** The dynamic equilibrium of the 3D model is formulated using classic Lagrangian mechanics, characterized by the Lagrangian `L = T - U` (kinetic energy minus potential energy). The system's dynamics are governed by a set of nonlinear equations derived from the implicit Euler time integration scheme.
5. **Energy Integration (Per-IP):**
 - **Kinetic Energy:** The total kinetic energy `T` is approximated by summing contributions from each IP, using the Q-GMLS shape functions to define the mass matrix `M`.
 - **Potential Energy:** The total potential energy `U` is computed by integrating the energy density `Ψ(F)` (which depends on the deformation gradient `F`) over each IP's cuboid. Crucially, the method leverages the quadratic nature of Q-GMLS displacement to assume a linearly-varying deformation gradient within each IP cuboid. This allows for accurate integration of hyperelastic material models (e.g., Neo-Hookean). The specific material model chosen defines `Ψ(F)`.
6. **System Assembly and Solve:** The generalized internal forces (`f_int = -∂U/∂q`) are computed using the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress. The assembled system, including external forces (`f_ext`), results in a dense, nonlinear system for the generalized coordinates `q`. This system is solved iteratively using Newton's method, which requires computing the tangent stiffness matrix (`∂f_int/∂q`). Due to the model reduction via Q-GMLS kernels, the system's dimension is significantly smaller than a particle-level or mesh-based system.
7. **NeRF Rendering with Quadratic Warping:** After solving for the deformed geometry, the deformed points (`˜x`) are warped back to their rest-pose positions (`x`) before querying the original NGP-NeRF for color and density. PIE-NeRF employs a **quadratic warping** scheme. Instead of a simple first-order Taylor expansion (linear warping) common in other methods, it uses the quadratic displacement field from Q-GMLS to solve for `x` more accurately, especially under large deformations. This involves solving a small nonlinear system for each queried point, typically converging quickly with Newton's method.
The implementation uses CUDA for simulation, PyTorch and Taichi for a modified instant-NGP for ray warping, optimizing for GPU acceleration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate PIE-NeRF's capabilities across several critical aspects:

- **Interactive and Dynamic NeRF Simulation:** PIE-NeRF successfully enables real-time, interactive manipulation of NeRF scenes. Users can apply external forces and position constraints with an intuitive user interface, observing novel, physics-grounded dynamic effects. The method's efficiency, stemming from the Q-GMLS based model reduction (requiring only a few dozen kernels for complex models), allows for this interactive rate on consumer-grade GPUs.
- **Physics-Grounded Pose Synthesis and Material Models:** The framework faithfully incorporates various nonlinear hyperelastic material models, providing a significant advantage over geometry-based heuristic models like ARAP. A comparison of chocolate jelly compression with Neo-Hookean material (which preserves volume) versus ARAP (which leads to significant volume loss) clearly illustrates the physical realism achieved by PIE-NeRF. This allows for animations that accurately reflect real-world material behaviors.
- **Robust Handling of Topology Changes:** Being meshless, PIE-NeRF is inherently more robust to topology changes than mesh-based methods. The paper demonstrates cutting a NeRF sculpture by modifying kernel weights, showing that the system handles the resulting dynamic separation without requiring explicit mesh connectivity updates.
- **Comparison with Ground Truth (FEM):** The results show that PIE-NeRF's simulations are nearly identical to those generated by a Finite Element Method (FEM) solver (used as ground truth), despite using a significantly lower number of kernels compared to FEM vertices or IPs compared to FEM tetrahedra. This validates the accuracy of the model reduction and meshless discretization.
- **Comparison with PAC-NeRF:** PIE-NeRF is compared against PAC-NeRF [Li et al., 2023], another recent method combining physics and NeRF using the Material Point Method (MPM). PIE-NeRF demonstrates superior rendering quality under large deformations, as PAC-NeRF's particle-grid hybrid approach suffers from excessive interpolation smoothing, leading to blurred details. Furthermore, PIE-NeRF's implicit time integration and model reduction yield significantly faster simulation (two orders of magnitude faster in reported examples) than PAC-NeRF.
- **Time Performance Breakdown:** The performance analysis reveals that matrix assembly, nonlinear solving, and quadratic warping are the three major computational tasks, all of which are manageable within interactive rates on the GPU. The quadratic warping, while slightly more expensive than linear warping, yields substantially improved visual results, justifying the overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PIE-NeRF represents a significant advancement in the integration of physics-based simulation with neural implicit representations. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Pioneering Physics-based Elastodynamics for NeRF:** The paper introduces a novel and robust pipeline for generating complex, nonlinear, and physically-grounded elastodynamic motions directly on NeRF models, filling a critical gap in the existing NeRF ecosystem.
- **Meshless Paradigm for NeRF Simulation:** By successfully implementing a meshless Lagrangian dynamics framework, PIE-NeRF eliminates the need for explicit auxiliary meshes. This simplifies the workflow, reduces computational overhead associated with meshing, and enhances the versatility of the simulation for complex or codimensional geometries that are challenging to mesh.
- **Enhanced Realism and Controllability:** The ability to specify real-world material parameters and simulate hyperelastic behavior allows for highly realistic animations that go beyond heuristic deformation models. This offers greater control and fidelity for applications requiring physically plausible object interactions.
- **Robustness to Topology Changes:** The meshless nature provides inherent robustness to topological changes, such as cutting or fracturing, which are notoriously difficult for mesh-based simulation methods to handle dynamically.
- **High-Fidelity Rendering with Quadratic Warping:** The meticulously designed quadratic warping scheme ensures that the visual quality of the original NeRF is preserved even under large deformations, addressing a common challenge in deformable NeRF rendering.
- **Interactive Performance:** Achieving interactive rates makes PIE-NeRF a practical tool for various applications, including virtual reality, interactive design, content creation, and real-time animation, where immediate feedback is crucial.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** PIE-NeRF lays the groundwork for further integration of advanced simulation and graphics techniques into deep 3D vision. The authors envision a future of "what you see is what you simulate" (WS²) and suggest exploring integration with other rapid rendering techniques like Gaussian Splatting, indicating a broad potential for future work in dynamic and interactive neural scene representations.

In summary, PIE-NeRF offers a powerful and flexible solution for interactive, physically-grounded deformation of neural radiance fields, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with implicit 3D representations in computer graphics and vision.
