# VISTA: Vision-Grounded and Physics-Validated Adaptation of UMI data for VLA Training

> [!summary] Summary
> VISTA introduces a framework to effectively adapt Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) data for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model training by constructing a large-scale fisheye-aligned VQA dataset and implementing a physics-validated trajectory scoring pipeline. The approach leads to policies with an average success rate of 0.598 on 20 real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming a π_0.5 baseline by 7.0 points.

## Problem

- VLA models, pre-trained on standard perspective images, struggle with the local, distorted views from wrist-mounted fisheye cameras in UMI data, leading to a visual grounding mismatch.
- Human-collected UMI trajectories often contain physically infeasible actions for target robot embodiments, due to a lack of kinematic, collision, and controller bandwidth constraints.
- Directly using raw UMI data for VLA training degrades performance and leads to unreliable robot behaviors.

## Method

- Developed ==UMI-VQA==, a large-scale Vision-Question Answering dataset with 8 million pairs, combining real-world fisheye frames and diffusion-edited images, to align VLM representations to distorted views.
- Implemented a trajectory scoring framework for physical validation, auditing UMI data for continuity, self-collision risk, and execution fidelity on target robot embodiments.
- Proposed a two-stage co-training recipe that first jointly trains the VLM backbone on UMI-VQA and discrete action tokens, then freezes the VLM to train a knowledge-isolated ==flow-matching action expert== for continuous control.

## Results

- VISTA achieved an average success rate of **0.598** on 20 real-world UMI-collected manipulation tasks, surpassing π_0.5 (**0.528**) by 7.0 points and LingBot-VLA (**0.313**) by 28.5 points.
- Co-training with UMI-VQA achieved an aggregate real-robot task success rate of **55.0%**, outperforming action-only training (**45.0%**) and standard-view VQA co-training (**31.7%**).
- Policies trained on high-score, physically validated UMI subsets demonstrated significantly higher Overall Success Rate (**0.65**) and Post-grasp Success Rate (1.00) compared to those trained on low-score subsets (**0.00**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Wrist-mounted fisheye observations significantly degrade both policy learning and robot-relevant visual reasoning in pre-trained VLMs.
> - A substantial portion of raw UMI trajectories are not physically executable by target robots, and data executability is highly dependent on the specific robot embodiment.
> - Perception-aligned VQA supervision and systematic physical validation are crucial for unlocking the full potential of UMI data for generalist VLA policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the field of large-scale robot learning, specifically concerning the effective utilization of data collected via the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) for training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

Current efforts in robot learning often rely on diverse, action-labeled datasets. Traditional data collection methods, which involve teleoperation on physical robot platforms, provide precise action supervision but are costly, labor-intensive, and often hardware-specific. This limits the generalizability of policies trained on one embodiment to others.

The UMI paradigm, introduced by Chi et al. (2024) and subsequently refined by works like FastUMI (Liu et al., 2024), offers a scalable alternative. It employs a handheld gripper equipped with a wrist-mounted fisheye camera and tracking sensors, enabling humans to collect first-person visual observations and end-effector trajectories without requiring a physical robot during collection. This approach has demonstrated effectiveness for training compact imitation-learning policies such as Diffusion Policy and ACT, facilitating real-world deployment across various tasks.

However, the paper identifies that directly leveraging UMI-collected data for training large-scale VLA models—such as OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024) and the π-series (Black et al., 2024, 2025)—presents fundamental mismatches. VLA models depend on powerful Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones pre-trained on vast internet-scale data, deriving their generalization from deep cross-modal alignment among vision, language, and action. Previous UMI-style efforts have largely treated the collected data as directly consumable by any downstream learner. This work contends that this assumption collapses for VLA systems due to:
1. **Visual Grounding Mismatch:** UMI data is characterized by wrist-mounted fisheye cameras that introduce severe radial distortion, non-uniform spatial resolution, and local, gripper-centric perspectives. These visual properties differ significantly from the standard perspective images and global scene contexts prevalent in VLM pre-training corpora.
2. **Physical Plausibility Mismatch:** Human operators collecting UMI data are not constrained by the kinematic limits, collision geometry, or controller bandwidth of target robot embodiments. Consequently, these trajectories may contain physically infeasible actions for a robot. While some prior UMI systems adopt hard-filtering for certain violations, a comprehensive, nuanced approach is largely unexplored for VLA training.

VISTA positions itself by explicitly identifying and systematically addressing these two critical gaps, aiming to unlock the full potential of UMI data for generalist robot policies learned by large-scale VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable the effective and scalable training of large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using data collected through the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI). The authors identify two primary, largely orthogonal, challenges that hinder the direct application of UMI data to VLA training: a visual grounding mismatch and a physical plausibility mismatch.

**Motivation for Addressing the Visual Grounding Mismatch:**
- **Problem Identification:** VLA models rely on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) pre-trained on vast internet-scale datasets, which typically consist of standard perspective images providing relatively global scene context. In contrast, UMI data is collected from wrist-mounted fisheye cameras, resulting in local, gripper-centric views with severe radial distortion and non-uniform spatial resolution. This discrepancy renders UMI observations "out-of-distribution" for pre-trained VLM backbones.
- **Impact:** This visual domain shift leads to degradation in the VLM's ability to accurately understand scenes, perform spatial reasoning, and ground language instructions within the distorted visual regime encountered during UMI-based robotic manipulation. Empirical evidence presented in the paper confirms that state-of-the-art VLMs and VLA policies suffer significant performance drops when faced with fisheye observations.
- **Objective:** To resolve this mismatch, a key objective is to align the VLM's visual representations to the distorted geometry and local first-person perspective inherent in wrist-mounted fisheye views. This alignment aims to establish a robust perceptual foundation for downstream action learning without forcing costly fine-tuning from scratch.

**Motivation for Addressing the Physical Plausibility Mismatch:**
- **Problem Identification:** UMI data is collected by humans manipulating a handheld gripper, unconstrained by the physical limitations of a target robot (e.g., joint limits, reachable workspace, self-collision avoidance, motion speed limits, or controller bandwidth). Trajectories may contain kinematically unreachable poses, tracking discontinuities, or motions exceeding robot capabilities.
- **Impact:** If a VLA model learns from physically infeasible trajectories, it internalizes action patterns that cannot be executed by the target robot, leading to systematic deployment failures. The paper empirically validates that a substantial fraction of raw UMI trajectories cannot be faithfully replayed on real robots.
- **Objective:** To ensure physical plausibility, an objective is to develop a systematic mechanism to audit and filter UMI trajectories, guaranteeing that the VLA model learns exclusively from embodiment-compatible and physically consistent demonstrations. This mechanism needs to be more nuanced than simple binary filtering, allowing for continuous quality control and adaptation to different target robot embodiments.

**Overall Objectives for VISTA:**
To bridge these dual gaps, VISTA aims to:
1. **Develop UMI-VQA:** Create a large-scale Vision-Question Answering (VQA) dataset specifically tailored to wrist-mounted fisheye observations, providing auxiliary vision-language supervision to align VLM representations.
2. **Establish a Physical Validation Pipeline:** Implement a systematic, trajectory-level validation process to score and select UMI trajectories based on kinematic reachability, self-collision risk, and execution fidelity for target robot embodiments.
3. **Propose a Two-Stage Co-training Recipe:** Design a training methodology that jointly learns vision-language grounding from UMI-VQA and action prediction from validated trajectories, employing a flow-matching action expert for continuous control generation.
4. **Demonstrate Empirical Effectiveness:** Validate through experiments that integrating UMI-VQA improves policy performance, that physical validation scores are predictive of real-world deployment success, and that the complete VISTA framework outperforms existing VLA baselines on diverse simulation and real-world manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VISTA is presented as a VLA adaptation framework designed to reconcile raw UMI-collected data with the perceptual and physical requirements of generalist robot policies. The methodology integrates three synergistic components: UMI-VQA for perception alignment, a physical validation pipeline, and a two-stage co-training recipe.

**UMI Hardware for Cross-embodiment Data Collection:**
The system utilizes the FastUMI Pro system (from Lumos Robotics) as the data-collection platform. This handheld gripper integrates a multi-sensor suite, including a central main fisheye camera (approximately 180° diagonal field of view), two auxiliary fisheye cameras, an onboard depth camera, a Vive Tracker for 6-DoF pose estimation, and a real-time visual-inertial SLAM pipeline. An internal encoder measures gripper width. This setup provides local, gripper-centric observations, which are subject to radial distortion and self-occlusion. For deployment, the same wrist-camera configuration and end-effectors are mounted on distinct dual-arm robot platforms, such as RealMan, AC one, and Galaxea R1 Pro, ensuring visual consistency between training and deployment.

**1. UMI-VQA for Perception Alignment:**
To address the visual grounding mismatch, UMI-VQA, a large-scale vision-language dataset, was constructed. It comprises 8 million question-answer pairs from two complementary visual sources:
- **Real-world UMI demonstration frames (3M pairs):** These frames are sampled from actual UMI manipulation videos and annotated using a semi-automatic pipeline. A large-scale VLM (Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct) generates structured descriptions covering scene content, object states, and spatial relations, followed by human verification. This portion is organized into five capability-oriented subsets to supervise specific perceptual abilities:
 - **Scene Captioning:** Provides concise contextual descriptions of wrist-fisheye observations.
 - **Scene-State Understanding:** Captures the task-relevant state, including gripper status, objects, obstacles, and constraints.
 - **Object Grounding:** Associates language references with task-relevant visual targets, localized with bounding boxes.
 - **Interaction Grounding:** Identifies actionable regions such as grasp sites or contact points.
 - **Spatial Reasoning:** Focuses on geometric and relational understanding (layout, position, depth, orientation, reachability).
- **Edited spatial-diversity images (5M pairs):** Images from RefSpatial (a dataset emphasizing 3D spatial reasoning, originally standard perspective) are transformed into fisheye-style views. Instead of classical geometric warping, a diffusion-based image-editing model (FLUX.2-dev) performs semantic-aware image-to-image translation. This model applies geometric distortion and *hallucinates physically plausible peripheral content* to create coherent wide-angle perspectives that mimic real fisheye lenses, preserving spatial-relation annotations. Original pixel-coordinate-based VQA annotations are filtered out.

**2. A Trajectory Scoring Framework for Physical Validation:**
To ensure physical plausibility and prevent physically infeasible demonstrations from entering the training corpus, a trajectory-level validation mechanism is proposed. This mechanism first performs a data-completeness pre-check and then scores each valid trajectory along three dimensions using a cross-embodiment trajectory replay system (MuJoCo simulation with Mink library for kinematics):
- **Trajectory Continuity (s_tc):** Measures the intrinsic smoothness of the recorded gripper motion in task space. It uses a three-regime piecewise scoring function, rewarding ideal smoothness, penalizing moderate deviations linearly, and imposing exponential decay for severe discontinuities. This score is embodiment-agnostic.
- **Self-collision Risk (s_sr):** Evaluates the minimum collision-pair distance between designated robot link pairs during trajectory replay on a target embodiment. Scores range from 100 (above a safety margin) to 0 (below a minimum distance, indicating a hard violation). This score is embodiment-dependent.
- **Execution Fidelity (s_ef):** Quantifies how faithfully a target robot can reproduce the demonstrated end-effector motion. It computes the tracking deviation between the desired and achieved robot poses during replay, aggregating factors like proximity to joint limits, kinematic singularities, and workspace boundaries. Scored similarly to continuity, and is embodiment-dependent.

An **overall cross-embodiment score** `S(ξ, e)` is calculated as a weighted product of these three scores, allowing for flexible emphasis (e.g., prioritizing collision-free trajectories during fine-tuning). For pre-training, trajectories are ranked by their average compatibility across multiple candidate embodiments, encouraging broad feasibility. For fine-tuning, a stricter threshold for the specific target robot is applied.

**3. Model Training and Deployment:**
The VISTA model is initialized from a π_0.5 checkpoint and pre-trained on the 8M UMI-VQA samples and 100K physically validated real-world UMI trajectories.
- **Stage 1: VQA-Action Autoregressive Co-training:** The VLM backbone is jointly trained on UMI-VQA (for vision-language grounding) and discrete action tokens derived from UMI trajectories (for action prediction). Continuous action chunks are converted into sequences of discrete FAST tokens. The objective is autoregressive next-token prediction, aligning cross-modal representations.
- **Stage 2: Knowledge-Isolated Flow Matching Action Expert:** To refine continuous control generation without catastrophic forgetting of Stage 1 knowledge, the VLM backbone is frozen. A separate continuous action expert (`f_ϕ`) is trained on top using a flow-matching objective to model the conditional generation of continuous action chunks.
- **Downstream Task Fine-tuning:** For specific tasks and robot embodiments, a strict physical validation threshold is applied to filter task data. The full model (VLM backbone and flow-matching expert) is then unfrozen and fine-tuned end-to-end.
- **Multi-robot Deployment System:** A pure Python-based distributed architecture is implemented, utilizing Zenoh middleware for transparent communication across various machine setups. This system aggregates state streams from multiple robotic arms, performs batched inference on distributed GPU nodes, and uses temporal ensembling with synchronous RPC calls for action dispatch, enabling a single VLA model to control heterogeneous robot arms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were structured into three tiers to validate the design choices of VISTA.

#### 4.1. Diagnostic Validation Experiments

- **Fisheye observations degrade policy learning:** Experiments on RoboTwin and LIBERO benchmarks demonstrated that switching from standard-view training to wrist-fisheye training consistently reduced policy performance across π_0.5, LingBot-VLA, and Wall-X models. For instance, π_0.5 saw an average success rate drop of 13.4 points, and LingBot-VLA a 15.7-point drop (Table 1), indicating a genuine perception bottleneck.
- **Fisheye observations degrade robot-relevant visual reasoning:** Evaluation of general and robot-specialized VLMs on four spatial-reasoning benchmarks (Where2Place, RefSpatial, ERQA, EmbSpatial) revealed a consistent mean absolute performance drop of 4.0 points (8.6% relative degradation) after fisheye transformation (Table 2). This finding confirmed that pre-trained VLMs struggle with distorted, local fisheye imagery, motivating the need for perception-aligned VQA.
- **Raw UMI trajectories are not always executable:** Audits of raw UMI demonstrations on tasks like "glue stick handover," "drawer pulling," and "stapler placement" using the RealMan robot showed that human-collected trajectories often led to significant deviations between desired and feasible robot poses, causing task failures (Figure 6). This empirically confirmed that unvalidated UMI data can provide physically infeasible supervision.

#### 4.2. Data-Level Validation Experiments

- **Effect of perception-aligned VQA:** Comparing different auxiliary VQA supervision sources during π_0.5 training on real-robot tasks (Stack Side Cubes, Stack Paper Cups, Stack Pen Holders) showed that co-training with UMI-VQA achieved the highest aggregate success rate of 55.0%. This was an improvement over action-only training (45.0%) and significantly outperformed co-training with standard-view VQA (31.7%), which even performed worse than action-only training (Table 3). This supports the efficacy of wrist-fisheye-aligned VQA for UMI-style policy learning.
- **Effects of physical score validation:** A score-controlled comparison on the "stapler placement" task with the RealMan robot demonstrated that policies trained on high-score, physically validated UMI subsets achieved significantly higher Overall Success Rate (OSR) and Post-grasp Success Rate (PSR) (0.65 and 1.00, respectively) compared to policies trained on low-score subsets (0.00 OSR and PSR), despite similar Grasping Success Rates (Table 4). Failure analysis showed low-score policies generated desired poses that were difficult for RealMan to execute, leading to tracking failures (Figure 8, Figure 9).
- **Embodiment-conditioned scoring:** Re-scoring the RealMan-selected low-score and high-score UMI subsets on R1Pro and ACone robots showed different score distributions across embodiments (Figure 7). The low-score RealMan subset, for example, received higher scores on R1Pro and achieved non-zero OSR (0.80) and high PSR (1.00) on R1Pro, whereas it failed on RealMan and ACone (Table 5). This supports that trajectory executability is embodiment-dependent, advocating for embodiment-conditioned data filtering.

#### 4.3. Model Evaluation Experiments

- **Simulation benchmark:** VISTA achieved the best performance on both RoboTwin-UMI (0.683 success rate) and LIBERO-UMI (0.943 success rate). Overall, VISTA averaged 0.813 success, outperforming π_0.5 by 5.5 points, LingBot-VLA by 15.5 points, and Wall-X by 38.7 points (Table 6).
- **Real-robot evaluation:** On 20 real-world UMI-collected manipulation tasks, VISTA achieved the highest average success rate of 0.598, surpassing π_0.5 (0.528) by 7.0 points and LingBot-VLA (0.313) by 28.5 points (Table 7).

#### 4.4. Ablations and Analysis

- **Component ablation:** Ablation studies on RoboTwin-UMI revealed the contributions of VISTA's key components (Table 8). The full model achieved 68.3% success. Removing the Stage-2 training (using a scratch expert or a π_0.5 expert) degraded performance to 52.4% and 60.2%, respectively, validating the learned action expert. Removing proprioceptive state input reduced success to 61.9%, indicating its importance. Replacing delta action prediction with absolute action prediction further reduced success to 53.1%, highlighting the criticality of relative action representation.
- **Attention-based analysis of visual grounding:** Qualitative visualization of attention maps (Figure 10) showed that VISTA produced more localized attention around task-relevant regions in auxiliary wrist views compared to π_0.5's more diffuse attention. This suggests VISTA develops stronger visual grounding under UMI-style observations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VISTA framework contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by systematically addressing fundamental challenges in integrating Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) data with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The work identifies and formalizes two critical bottlenecks: the visual grounding mismatch arising from wrist-mounted fisheye observations and the physical plausibility mismatch between human-collected trajectories and target robot embodiment constraints.

**Key Significance:**
- **Enabling Scalable VLA Training:** VISTA bridges the gaps that previously limited the effectiveness of UMI data for large-scale VLA model training. By providing methods for perceptual alignment and physical validation, it unlocks the potential of UMI's scalable real-world data collection for generalist robot policy learning.
- **Improved Model Performance and Reliability:** The framework leads to VLA policies that exhibit improved success rates across diverse simulation and real-world manipulation tasks. The strong correlation between physical validation scores and deployment success indicates that the methodology contributes to more reliable and physically executable robot behaviors.
- **Enhanced Visual Grounding:** The introduction of UMI-VQA, the first large-scale VQA dataset tailored to wrist-mounted fisheye observations, is a significant contribution. It provides vision-language supervision that aligns VLM representations with the distorted, gripper-centric visual regime, fostering better scene understanding and spatial reasoning in complex manipulation scenarios.
- **Robust Data Curation:** The systematic trajectory-level physical validation pipeline offers a nuanced approach to filtering and scoring UMI demonstrations. Unlike coarse filters, its continuous scoring along multiple dimensions (continuity, self-collision risk, execution fidelity) enables embodiment-compatible data curation, which is crucial for training policies that are genuinely executable on target robots. The embodiment-conditioned scoring further highlights the importance of adapting data curation to specific robot platforms.
- **Architectural Contributions:** The two-stage co-training recipe, combining autoregressive VQA-action learning with a knowledge-isolated flow-matching action expert, provides an effective method for learning robust continuous control while preserving visual-linguistic knowledge. The pure Python distributed multi-robot deployment system simplifies and scales the deployment of VLA models on heterogeneous robot arms, reducing operational overhead and dependency on specific middleware.

**Potential Impact:**
The release of the physical-validation pipeline, UMI-VQA dataset, validated trajectory data, and the pre-trained model provides valuable resources for the research community. This can accelerate further research in scalable robot learning, foster reproducibility, and encourage the development of more generalist robot policies. By making UMI data more usable for sophisticated VLA models, VISTA contributes to reducing the reliance on expensive and hardware-specific data collection methods, potentially democratizing access to large-scale robot learning research and development. The framework's ability to align human demonstrations with robot capabilities moves closer to a future where human intuition can more directly and reliably inform robotic actions across various embodiments.
