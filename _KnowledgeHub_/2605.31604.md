# Representation Forcing for Bottleneck-Free Unified Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Hong Kong and ByteDance Seed developed Representation Forcing (RF), a method for Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) to achieve high-quality pixel-space image generation by having the model internally predict and utilize its own high-level visual representations, thereby removing the reliance on external VAEs.

## Problem

- Existing Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) typically rely on separately pretrained, frozen Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) for image generation, which introduces a 'structural bottleneck' and limits the maximal achievable image quality.
- Directly applying pixel-space diffusion models to UMMs has historically led to degraded image generation quality, as these models struggle to learn both high-level semantic structure and fine-grained pixel details simultaneously from raw data.
- The use of external VAEs prevents UMMs from achieving truly end-to-end learning where perception and generation are grounded in a single, jointly learned representation space.

## Method

- The method, **Representation Forcing** (RF), leverages the UMM's own jointly trained understanding encoder to extract patch-level visual features, which are then discretized into 'representation tokens' using online vector quantization.
- The UMM's decoder is trained to autoregressively predict these representation tokens from text prompts using a cross-entropy loss, and these predicted tokens then serve as in-context conditioning for subsequent pixel-space image generation.
- A unified Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) backbone, initialized from Qwen3-A3B, processes a single token sequence of text, representation tokens, and pixel patches, with modality-specific feed-forward experts guiding multimodal understanding, representation prediction, and pixel generation via ==flow matching==.

## Results

- RF-Pixel achieved an overall GenEval score of 0.84 (without an LLM rewriter) and 0.88 (with rewriter), matching or slightly outperforming state-of-the-art VAE-based unified models on compositional generation benchmarks.
- Representation Forcing consistently improved multimodal understanding performance across both pixel-space and VAE-based generation pathways, showing gains up to +4.5 on MMMU and +8.0 on HalluBench.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that RF significantly improved pixel-space generation, boosting its GenEval score from 0.25 (without RF) to 0.76 (with RF), indicating its critical role in enabling high-quality direct pixel-space synthesis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly guiding pixel-space image generation with internally predicted high-level visual representations effectively overcomes structural coherence issues, closing the quality gap with VAE-based approaches.
> - Directly training the decoder to predict discrete visual representations (Representation Forcing) is more effective for structural guidance than implicit feature alignment methods like REPA.
> - Discrete representation tokens, derived from the understanding encoder and discretized through vector quantization, are crucial for robust sequential prediction and factorizing high-level structure from fine-grained pixel details, leading to improved generation quality.
