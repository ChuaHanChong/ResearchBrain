# Representation Forcing for Bottleneck-Free Unified Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Hong Kong and ByteDance Seed developed Representation Forcing (RF), a method for Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) to achieve high-quality pixel-space image generation by having the model internally predict and utilize its own high-level visual representations, thereby removing the reliance on external VAEs.

## Problem

- Existing Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) typically rely on separately pretrained, frozen Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) for image generation, which introduces a 'structural bottleneck' and limits the maximal achievable image quality.
- Directly applying pixel-space diffusion models to UMMs has historically led to degraded image generation quality, as these models struggle to learn both high-level semantic structure and fine-grained pixel details simultaneously from raw data.
- The use of external VAEs prevents UMMs from achieving truly end-to-end learning where perception and generation are grounded in a single, jointly learned representation space.

## Method

- The method, **Representation Forcing** (RF), leverages the UMM's own jointly trained understanding encoder to extract patch-level visual features, which are then discretized into 'representation tokens' using online vector quantization.
- The UMM's decoder is trained to autoregressively predict these representation tokens from text prompts using a cross-entropy loss, and these predicted tokens then serve as in-context conditioning for subsequent pixel-space image generation.
- A unified Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) backbone, initialized from Qwen3-A3B, processes a single token sequence of text, representation tokens, and pixel patches, with modality-specific feed-forward experts guiding multimodal understanding, representation prediction, and pixel generation via ==flow matching==.

## Results

- RF-Pixel achieved an overall GenEval score of 0.84 (without an LLM rewriter) and 0.88 (with rewriter), matching or slightly outperforming state-of-the-art VAE-based unified models on compositional generation benchmarks.
- Representation Forcing consistently improved multimodal understanding performance across both pixel-space and VAE-based generation pathways, showing gains up to +4.5 on MMMU and +8.0 on HalluBench.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that RF significantly improved pixel-space generation, boosting its GenEval score from 0.25 (without RF) to 0.76 (with RF), indicating its critical role in enabling high-quality direct pixel-space synthesis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly guiding pixel-space image generation with internally predicted high-level visual representations effectively overcomes structural coherence issues, closing the quality gap with VAE-based approaches.
> - Directly training the decoder to predict discrete visual representations (Representation Forcing) is more effective for structural guidance than implicit feature alignment methods like REPA.
> - Discrete representation tokens, derived from the understanding encoder and discretized through vector quantization, are crucial for robust sequential prediction and factorizing high-level structure from fine-grained pixel details, leading to improved generation quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of unified multimodal models (UMMs) capable of both understanding (e.g., text output) and generation (e.g., pixel output) within a single framework represents a significant area of research in artificial intelligence. Existing UMMs typically achieve image generation by integrating language models with diffusion models, often relying on a separately pretrained Variational Autoencoder (VAE) or Vector Quantized VAE (VQVAE) to compress images into a latent space. This VAE-based approach is prevalent in models like Transfusion, Show-o, and JanusFlow, as well as in methods where an LLM predicts visual representations (e.g., CLIP features) that condition an external diffusion model (e.g., Emu2, SEED-X).

While VAEs enable efficient high-resolution image synthesis by operating in a lower-dimensional latent space, they introduce a structural bottleneck. The latent space is optimized for reconstruction rather than the broader objectives of the unified model, and its lossy compression can impose an upper bound on generation quality that further training of the UMM cannot readily overcome. This dependence on an external, frozen component hinders true end-to-end learning and unification.

A natural alternative is to generate images directly in pixel space, a method shown to be feasible for standalone generation models (e.g., JiT, PixelFlow). However, applying naive pixel-space diffusion to UMMs has previously resulted in a quality gap. This is attributed to the more diverse image distributions and richer text conditioning found in UMMs, where the model must simultaneously learn high-level semantic structure and fine-grained pixel details from raw inputs.

This paper addresses the challenge of moving beyond VAE-based image generation in UMMs while maintaining or improving generation quality and enhancing multimodal understanding. It positions itself among a growing body of work that seeks to eliminate the pretrained VAE bottleneck, such as SenseNova-U1 and Tuna-2, but differentiates its approach by leveraging the UMM's internal understanding pathway for structural guidance in generation, rather than discarding the visual encoder or relying on external representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Eliminate the structural bottleneck of separately pretrained VAEs** in unified multimodal models for image generation. Existing UMMs rely on VAEs to compress images into a latent space, which is then used by the diffusion process. This dependency prevents true end-to-end learning, as the VAE's latent space is optimized independently for reconstruction, potentially limiting the overall quality and flexibility of the unified model.
2. **Close the quality gap observed in naive pixel-space generation** within UMMs. While direct pixel-space generation is feasible for standalone models, its application in UMMs often leads to lower quality compared to VAE-based methods. This is because UMMs must handle a broader image distribution and richer text conditioning, requiring the model to simultaneously learn high-level semantic structure and low-level pixel details, which is a complex task without explicit structural guidance.
3. **Make visual representation prediction a native capability of the unified model decoder.** Instead of relying on external or frozen representations, the goal is to train the UMM's decoder to internally predict high-level visual representations. These representations would then provide an explicit structural scaffold to guide pixel-space generation, integrating understanding and generation within a single, end-to-end learned framework.
4. **Demonstrate that this approach benefits both image generation and understanding**, ultimately paving the way for bottleneck-free, end-to-end UMMs where perception and generation share a single, self-learned representation space.

The motivation stems from the recognition that current UMMs, despite their unified architecture for language and image generation, remain structurally dependent on external components for image generation. Achieving truly general-purpose multimodal intelligence necessitates a model that can acquire all its capabilities directly from raw inputs without relying on independently trained, fixed modules. By enabling the model to learn and produce its own structural representations for generation, the authors aim to achieve a more cohesive and effective multimodal system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the proposed **Representation Forcing (RF)** technique, designed to integrate high-level visual structure into pixel-space generation directly within a unified multimodal model (UMM).

#### 3.1. Representations from Understanding

Instead of an external latent space, RF derives its intermediate representations from the UMM's own **understanding encoder**. This encoder, trained jointly with the rest of the model for visual comprehension, provides features that inherently capture high-level structure (e.g., object identity, spatial layout). To make these continuous features compatible with the decoder's next-token prediction objective and enable easy sampling during inference, they are **discretized into a sequence of visual representation tokens**.

The discretization process employs **online vector quantization**. An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) copy of the understanding encoder is used to extract patch-level features from the ground-truth image. This EMA copy provides stable targets as the primary encoder evolves during training. These features are then assigned to the nearest prototype embedding in a learnable codebook. The codebook itself is updated online via EMA, and to prevent codebook collapse, a Sinkhorn–Knopp normalization technique (similar to SwAV) is applied to balance prototype assignments. This yields one representation token per spatial patch, which retains the image's spatial layout and can be predicted in raster-scan order.

#### 3.2. Generating Pixels via Predicted Representations

The generation process leverages these representation tokens in a two-stage mechanism:

1. **Representation Prediction:** During training, the decoder learns to predict the ground-truth representation tokens (derived from the EMA encoder) autoregressively under a cross-entropy loss, mirroring the next-token prediction objective used for language. At inference, the understanding encoder is bypassed; the decoder autonomously produces the representation token sequence solely from the text prompt. This is termed "Representation Forcing" because the understanding encoder's features compel the decoder to learn structural visual patterns, and subsequently, the decoder's predicted representations guide pixel generation.
2. **Pixel-Space Diffusion:** Once predicted, the representation tokens remain in the sequence and serve as **in-context conditioning** for the subsequent pixel-space generation. The shared transformer backbone then performs iterative denoising to synthesize the final image directly from noise in pixel space. Following JiT, the approach adopts **x-prediction with velocity loss** (L_FM) for diffusion. Given clean patches `x` and Gaussian noise `ϵ`, noisy patches `z_t` are generated, and the decoder predicts `x_θ` to minimize the difference between the predicted velocity `v_θ` and the true velocity `v`.

#### 3.3. Architectural Integration and Training

The model adopts a **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture**, based on BAGEL. All tokens (text, representation, pixel) share the same multi-head self-attention layers but are routed to modality-specific feed-forward experts (understanding, representation prediction, pixel generation). Representation tokens are embedded as a sum of 2D spatial position embeddings and discrete token identity embeddings stored in a K-entry table.

The unified sequence structure is `[text tokens, representation tokens, pixel patches]`. Within the self-attention mechanism, text and representation tokens follow causal attention, while noisy pixel patches attend bidirectionally to each other and causally to all preceding text and representation tokens. This attention pattern allows the high-level structural information encoded in the representation tokens to flow into pixel generation without requiring separate cross-attention or injection modules.

The model is trained end-to-end using a combined objective function: `L = L_LM + L_FM + L_Rep`.
- `L_LM`: Cross-entropy loss for text next-token prediction.
- `L_FM`: Flow-matching loss for pixel generation.
- `L_Rep`: Cross-entropy loss for representation token prediction.

To support classifier-free guidance during inference, text conditioning and the representation token sequence are independently dropped with a probability of 0.1 during training.

#### 3.4. Training Strategy and Inference

The training involves a three-stage strategy:
1. **Alignment:** Freezing the backbone and encoder, only an MLP connector is trained.
2. **Joint Pre-training:** All components are unfrozen and jointly optimized on text and text–image pairs at resolutions up to 256.
3. **Continued Training:** Training extends to resolutions up to 1024.
Image resolutions are sampled dynamically and packed using NaViT-style variable-resolution batching. The image encoder used is DINOv3 ViT-H+/16 with NaViT support, jointly trained. A codebook size of K=16,384 is used for online vector quantization, with 2x2 pooling for representation tokens.

Inference proceeds in two stages:
1. The decoder autoregressively generates the full representation token sequence from the text prompt using top-k sampling.
2. Conditioned on both the text and the predicted representation tokens, the decoder performs iterative denoising (25 flow-matching steps with dynamic timestep shifting) from Gaussian noise to synthesize the final image directly in pixel space. Classifier-free guidance is applied with specific weights for representation token sampling (`w_rep=2.0`) and pixel patch denoising (`w_pix=3.0`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from evaluations on image generation and understanding, along with ablation studies to validate design choices.

#### 4.1. Image Generation Performance

The pixel-space model with Representation Forcing (RF-Pixel) was evaluated on standard text-to-image benchmarks: GenEval for compositional generation and DPG-Bench for dense prompt following.

- **Comparison with Baselines:** RF-Pixel was compared against both generation-only models (e.g., SDXL, DALL-E 3) and existing unified multimodal models (e.g., BAGEL, Emu3, Janus-Pro, BLIP3-o). Notably, all compared unified models rely on separately pretrained generative components (either VAE/VQVAE or external diffusion modules), whereas RF-Pixel operates entirely in pixel space without such dependencies.
- **GenEval:** Without an LLM rewriter, RF-Pixel achieved an overall score of 0.84, which slightly surpassed the BAGEL baseline (0.82) and matched other state-of-the-art VAE-based unified models like BLIP3-o (0.84). With an LLM rewriter, RF-Pixel reached 0.88, aligning with the highest scores among unified models.
- **DPG-Bench:** RF-Pixel scored 84.15 without a rewriter, a performance comparable to state-of-the-art VAE-based unified models.

These results indicate that Representation Forcing effectively mitigates the quality disparity between pixel-space and VAE-based generation, enabling an end-to-end pixel-space unified model to achieve performance levels comparable to or exceeding VAE-based counterparts. Qualitatively, images generated with RF demonstrated coherent structures, in contrast to the distorted objects and incoherent compositions often produced by pixel-space models without RF.

#### 4.2. Image Understanding Performance

The impact of different generation pathways (VAE-based vs. pixel-space, with and without RF) on understanding performance was evaluated across eight benchmarks, categorized into general visual understanding and document/diagram understanding.

- **Impact of RF:** RF consistently improved understanding across both generation pathways. For pixel-space generation, RF enhanced performance on 6 out of 8 benchmarks, with notable gains on MMMU (+4.3), MME (+3.6), BLINK (+3.6), AI2D (+4.5), and RealWorldQA (+2.7). Minor reductions were observed on DocVQA (−2.0) and ChartQA (−0.4). For VAE-based generation, RF improved 5 out of 8 benchmarks, with significant gains on HalluBench (+5.6) and MME (+8.0). The improvements were concentrated in benchmarks testing high-level visual understanding, consistent with the semantic nature of the representation tokens. Reductions in document-oriented tasks suggest less direct support for precise text recognition from representation-level guidance.
- **Pixel+RF vs. VAE+RF:** Pixel+RF generally outperformed VAE+RF on 6 out of 8 benchmarks. This is attributed to the removal of the external VAE latent space, fostering a more tightly integrated representation space for understanding and generation.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies provided insights into key design choices:

- **Representations are Critical for Pixel-Space Generation:** Without RF, pixel-space generation scored 0.25 on GenEval (at 256 resolution), while VAE-based generation reached 0.52. With RF, pixel-space generation dramatically increased to 0.76, matching the VAE-based RF variant (0.77). RF also improved VAE-based generation from 0.52 to 0.77, confirming its benefits for both settings, with the most significant impact in pixel space.
- **Decoder Prediction Outperforms Auxiliary Alignment:** Comparing RF (decoder prediction) with REPA (auxiliary alignment of diffusion features with encoder representations), RF achieved 0.76 on GenEval, substantially outperforming REPA's 0.43 and the baseline's 0.25. This indicates that direct, in-context conditioning via predicted representations is more effective than implicit feature alignment for high-dimensional pixel generation.
- **Discrete Prediction Outperforms Continuous Regression:** Replacing discrete representation tokens with continuous feature regression yielded only 0.26 on GenEval, offering no improvement over the baseline without RF. Discrete tokens achieved 0.76. This is attributed to the robustness of categorical choices in sequential prediction and the natural factorization of high-level structure from low-level detail encouraged by discretization.
- **Codebook Size:** Variations in codebook size (K=16,384 vs. K=32,768) showed comparable performance (0.76 vs. 0.77), suggesting insensitivity to codebook size within this range.
- **Understanding Encoder:** DINOv3 outperformed SigLIP2 as the image encoder on 4 out of 5 understanding benchmarks. DINOv3's self-supervised objective, prioritizing spatial and structural information, aligned more effectively with RF's goal of capturing visual structure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents Representation Forcing (RF) as a methodological advancement toward constructing more truly unified and end-to-end multimodal models.

- **Elimination of VAE Bottleneck:** The primary significance lies in demonstrating that high-quality pixel-space image generation can be achieved within a unified multimodal model without relying on a separately pretrained Variational Autoencoder (VAE). This removes a long-standing structural bottleneck that has constrained the development of fully integrated UMMs, allowing for a more cohesive training and inference paradigm.
- **Enhanced Multimodal Integration:** By training the decoder to predict visual representations derived from its own understanding encoder, RF fosters a closer integration of perception and generation. The same representation space is leveraged for both interpreting visual inputs and guiding their synthesis, suggesting a move towards a unified internal understanding of visual information within the model.
- **Improved Understanding Capabilities:** The finding that pixel-space generation with RF generally outperforms its VAE-based counterpart in image understanding benchmarks indicates that removing the external VAE can lead to a more compatible and potentially superior unified multimodal learning objective. This suggests that a direct, end-to-end learned visual pathway may be more beneficial for comprehension tasks.
- **Foundation for Future UMMs:** Representation Forcing offers an effective step towards bottleneck-free UMMs where all capabilities are learned directly from raw inputs within a single model, rather than inheriting from independently trained components. This direction could lead to more robust, flexible, and generalizable multimodal AI systems.

**Potential Impact:**
The proposed technique could influence the architectural design of future UMMs, encouraging the development of models that learn internal, self-consistent representations for both understanding and generation. It may accelerate research into fully end-to-end multimodal learning, reducing the reliance on complex, multi-stage pipelines involving external pretrained models. This could simplify model development and potentially improve performance across a wider range of multimodal tasks.

**Limitations:**
The study acknowledges limitations, including initialization from a pretrained large language model rather than full multimodal training from scratch, which might restrict the richness of joint representations. Additionally, the focus is currently limited to still-image generation, not extending to video or other temporal modalities.

**Broader Implications:**
As with any advanced generative AI system, models incorporating Representation Forcing could be misused to create misleading or harmful visual content, such as disinformation or deepfakes. The authors note that standard safeguards, including safety filters, output watermarking, and controlled access, would apply to RF-based systems, emphasizing the need for responsible development and deployment.
