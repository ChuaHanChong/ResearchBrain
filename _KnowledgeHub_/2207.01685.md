# Interaction Transformer for Human Reaction Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> InterFormer is a Transformer encoder-decoder that generates a person's reaction skeleton sequence from an input action sequence. It runs temporal and spatial attention in parallel, masks self-spatial attention with a skeleton adjacency graph, and biases interaction attention with an inter-person joint-distance prior. On SBU, K3HI and DuetDance it cuts FVD sharply and raises reaction classification accuracy over motion-prediction and interaction baselines.

## Problem

- Most human motion generation work models a single person's action and ignores two-person interaction, so no model produces a reaction conditioned only on a partner's action.
- Existing interaction methods predict future frames from historical sequences of both people, so they cannot start generation when only the action stream is available.
- Prior skeleton graph methods feed a fixed body graph as input, which discards cross-person joint relations, and prior evaluations cover only short, simple interactions.

## Method

- **InterFormer**: a six-layer ==Transformer== encoder-decoder where self-spatial skeleton attention over joints and self-temporal motion attention over frames run in parallel, plus interaction spatial and interaction temporal attention linking the two skeletons.
- ==Skeleton adjacency module== builds a mask from identity, inward and outward joint matrices, zeroing self-spatial attention entries absent from the body graph; ==interaction distance module== adds softmax of negative inter-person joint distances to interaction attention.
- Training uses an ==MSE== sequence loss plus a first-frame loss on the difference between the first two frames; autoregressive testing emits an end-of-sequence value, and encoder noise injection yields multi-modal reactions.

## Results

- FVD drops to **48.78** on SBU, **31.81** on DuetDance and **125.40** on K3HI, versus **113.61**, **789.23** and **195.47** for the VRNN baseline.
- Reaction classification accuracy reaches **80.0%** on SBU against **46.7%** for VRNN and **90.0%** ground truth; 20 users preferred its videos **29.2%** of the time versus **20.9%** and **11.4%**.
- Ablations on SBU stack gains: plain Transformer **53.3%**, plus spatial attention **66.7%**, plus skeleton adjacency **73.3%**, plus interaction distance **80.0%**; removing first-frame loss costs **3.3** points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting structural priors inside attention, as a mask or additive bias, beats feeding graphs as input: it keeps cross-entity relations that a hard graph input would erase.
> - Motion-prediction models transfer poorly to reaction generation because they suppress the input-output discontinuity and assume fixed horizons that variable-length interactions violate.
> - Deterministic Transformers need explicit noise to produce plausible response diversity, and distance-based ground-truth metrics mislead when many different reactions are equally valid.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion generation has seen significant advancements, largely driven by progress in deep learning. However, the majority of existing research primarily focuses on generating actions for a single person, often overlooking the complexities of human-to-human interaction. While some works have begun to address human interaction generation and prediction, they often deal with limited, simple, and short-term scenarios.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by tackling the more challenging and underexplored problem of **human reaction generation**. Unlike general interaction prediction, where both participants' future motions are anticipated, reaction generation specifically aims to synthesize a responsive motion based on a *given* input action. This problem is inherently difficult due to several factors: the non-linear temporal dynamics of human motion, the varying rates at which motions are performed, the extremely complex and non-periodic pose sequences involved in interactions (e.g., duet dancing), and the context-dependent nature of reactions (where the same action might evoke different responses).

The authors leverage the transformative power of **Transformer networks**, which have revolutionized Natural Language Processing (NLP) with their self-attention mechanisms and ability to model long-range dependencies. These architectures have rapidly permeated computer vision, showing success in tasks such as image classification, object detection, pose estimation, and single-person human motion generation and prediction. However, the authors explicitly highlight a critical gap: **no existing work, to their knowledge, has utilized a Transformer-based architecture specifically for the human reaction generation problem**. This paper directly addresses this void.

Furthermore, the work integrates insights from **graph representation learning**, which is widely used for 3D skeleton data due to the inherent graph-like structure of human bodies (joints connected by segments). While many methods use graph representations as direct input, the authors recognize that this approach might limit the modeling of interaction dynamics between *two* distinct skeletons. Instead, they propose a novel integration of graph-based information *within* the attention mechanisms to better exploit skeleton structure while simultaneously capturing inter-person interaction effectively.

By framing reaction generation as a "translation problem" (translating an action sequence into a coherent reaction sequence) and developing a Transformer-based solution, this work pushes the boundaries of human motion synthesis. It moves beyond simple predictions to generate dynamic, responsive, and contextually appropriate motions, particularly for complex and long-term interactions, which aligns with the growing demand for realistic virtual characters and intelligent interactive systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a novel framework capable of generating realistic and coherent human reaction motions in response to a given human action motion**. The authors aim to achieve this by learning the complex mapping from an action sequence to its corresponding reaction sequence, effectively formulating the problem as a "translation" task.

The motivations driving this work stem from several critical challenges and gaps in the existing research landscape:

1. **Addressing the Underexplored Problem of Reaction Generation**: Most research in human motion generation focuses on single individuals or predicts the future motion of both participants in an interaction. There is a significant lack of dedicated methods for explicitly generating a *reaction* to a specific *action*. The authors identify this as a challenging but crucial aspect of creating believable virtual characters and interactive systems.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Interaction Models**: Current methods for human interaction generation often handle only simple and short-term interactions. They struggle with the non-linearity, varying temporal evolution rates, and the complex, low-periodicity nature of activities like duet dancing. The goal is to generate more complex and long-term interactions.
3. **Harnessing the Power of Transformer Architectures**: Transformers have demonstrated unparalleled success in sequence-to-sequence tasks across NLP and various computer vision domains due to their ability to capture long-range temporal and spatial dependencies through self-attention. The authors are motivated to apply this powerful architecture to reaction generation, believing it is uniquely suited to model the intricate relationships within and between human motion sequences.
4. **Modeling Context-Dependent Reactions**: A key challenge is that the same action can elicit different reactions based on context (e.g., reacting to a punch depends on position and intent). The authors aim for a model that can capture these subtle dependencies.
5. **Integrating Structural Prior Knowledge**: While graph representations are beneficial for skeleton data, simply using them as input for interaction tasks can be limiting. The motivation here is to devise a method that benefits from the anatomical structure of skeletons (via graph adjacency) while simultaneously enabling flexible modeling of inter-person interactions based on spatial proximity.
6. **Enabling Long-Term and Complex Interaction Synthesis**: Specifically, the use of datasets like DuetDance highlights a motivation to tackle highly complex and extended interaction sequences that are beyond the scope of many existing approaches.

In essence, the authors are motivated to bridge the gap between single-person motion generation and truly dynamic, interactive human behavior synthesis, using a novel Transformer-based approach that can learn and translate complex spatio-temporal action-reaction patterns.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed solution is the **Interaction Transformer (InterFormer)**, a novel Transformer-based network designed for human reaction generation. Its architecture is composed of four main modules: a motion encoder, a motion decoder, a skeleton adjacency module, and an interaction distance module.

**Input Representation:**
Human motion is represented as a sequence of 3D joint positions. For a sequence of `T` frames, an action `X` (or reaction `Y`) is `P = {P_1, P_2, ..., P_T}`, where `P_t ∈ R^d` and `d = 3 × k` (k is the number of joints, each with 3D coordinates). Positional encoding, similar to, is added to the input sequences to incorporate temporal information.

**1. Motion Encoder:**
The encoder takes the input action sequence `X` and processes it to generate a latent encoding `Z`. It is designed to capture both the temporal evolution and the spatial configuration of the action motion.
- **Self Spatial Skeleton Attention**: This module processes each frame independently, focusing on the relationships between different joints *within* a single skeleton at a given time. It uses a multi-head attention mechanism where each head corresponds to one of the 3D coordinates of a joint, learning spatial dependencies.
- **Self Temporal Motion Attention**: This module considers the entire skeleton over time, learning relationships between joint positions *across* different frames. It captures the temporal dynamics of the action.

**2. Motion Decoder:**
The decoder receives the encoder's output `Z` (representing the action context) and the current (or previously generated) reaction sequence `Y` (for auto-regressive generation). It generates the future frames of the reaction sequence. It comprises four attention modules:
- **Self Spatial Skeleton Attention**: Similar to the encoder, but it models the internal spatial relationships *within* the reacting skeleton based on its current state.
- **Self Temporal Motion Attention**: Similar to the encoder, modeling the temporal evolution *of the reaction* itself.
- **Interaction Spatial Skeleton Attention**: This crucial module learns the spatial dependencies *between* the action and reaction skeletons at each frame. The queries come from the reaction sequence (`Y`), and the keys and values come from the encoder output (`Z`), enabling the reaction to be conditioned on the action's spatial configuration.
- **Interaction Temporal Motion Attention**: This module establishes temporal relationships between the action sequence (`Z`) and the reaction sequence (`Y`), ensuring that the generated reaction is synchronized and temporally coherent with the input action.

**3. Skeleton Adjacency Module:**
This module is designed to enhance the **self-spatial skeleton attention** by incorporating explicit anatomical knowledge of the human skeleton. Instead of feeding a graph directly as input, it uses adjacency matrices to create a mask for the attention matrix.
- It combines three types of adjacency matrices: an identity matrix (for self-connection), an inward relations matrix (from extremities to root), and an outward relations matrix (from root to extremities).
- These combined matrices form a mask `M`, which is applied to the attention matrix (element-wise multiplication, setting irrelevant attention scores to zero) to focus attention only on anatomically connected or relevant joints within a skeleton, thus improving the understanding of internal body structure.

**4. Interaction Distance Module:**
This module is introduced to provide prior knowledge to the **interaction spatial skeleton attention**, guiding the model to focus on meaningful interactions between two skeletons.
- It assumes that closer joints between the action and reaction characters are more likely to interact strongly.
- It calculates the Euclidean distance between all pairs of joints from the action and reaction skeletons at each time step.
- Unlike the skeleton adjacency module, which uses a binary mask, this module applies a `softmax` function to the negative distances (making shorter distances more important) and *adds* this scaled distance matrix to the attention matrix. This approach allows the model to prioritize spatially close interactions while still allowing for the discovery of non-distance-based relationships, preventing overly rigid constraints.

**Loss Functions:**
The model is optimized using two loss functions:
- **Sequence Loss (L_s)**: A Mean Squared Error (MSE) comparing the generated reaction sequence (`Ŷ`) directly with the ground truth reaction sequence (`Y`) over all frames and joints.
- **First Frame Loss (L_ff)**: An MSE on the difference between the first two frames `(J_i(2) - J_i(1))` of the generated and ground truth sequences. This is crucial for ensuring smooth transitions and preventing discontinuities between the initial input reaction frame and the subsequent generated frames, thus maintaining spatial coherency.

**Implementation Details:**
The encoder and decoder each consist of `N=6` layers. Inputs are normalized before attention modules, and outputs are normalized and added to residual connections. During inference, reactions are generated in an auto-regressive manner, conditioned on the input action sequence and the first ground-truth frame of the reaction. For long sequences, the generation can be segmented into sub-sequences, where subsequent sub-sequences use the last generated frame of the previous one as input, though this can lead to error accumulation. The authors also demonstrate multi-modality generation by adding noise to the encoder input, allowing for diverse reactions to the same action.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The InterFormer model was rigorously evaluated through comprehensive experiments on three challenging datasets: SBU interaction (simple interactions), K3HI (noisy interactions), and DuetDance (complex, long-term dance interactions). Performance was assessed using quantitative metrics (Classification Accuracy, Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), Diversity Score) and qualitative visualizations, supplemented by a user study.

**Comparison with State-of-the-Art Baselines:**
The InterFormer was compared against several baselines, including Zero Velocity (a simple no-motion baseline), Multimodal Variational Recurrent Neural Network (VRNN), Mix-and-Match Perturbation (MixMatch), Spatio-temporal Transformer (STT), and Progressively Generating Better Initial Guesses (PGBIG).

- **Quantitative Evaluation:**
 - **Classification Accuracy**: InterFormer consistently achieved the highest classification accuracy across all datasets. On SBU, it scored 80.0%, significantly outperforming the next best (VRNN at 46.7%). For DuetDance, InterFormer recorded 27.1% (closest to GT 28.0%), showing robust performance even on complex motions. On the noisy K3HI dataset, it led with 46.4%. This indicates that InterFormer generates motions that are not only realistic but also discernible and classifiable, closely matching the characteristics of ground truth data.
 - **Fréchet Video Distance (FVD)**: InterFormer demonstrated superior performance with significantly lower FVD scores on all datasets (SBU: 48.78; DuetDance: 31.81; K3HI: 125.40). Lower FVD signifies that the distribution of generated motion features is much closer to that of the ground truth, suggesting higher perceptual quality and fidelity.
 - **Diversity Score**: InterFormer generated reactions with diversity scores closest to the ground truth, outperforming baselines. For SBU, its score was 0.9, and for DuetDance, 0.4. This indicates that while the generated motions are realistic, they also maintain a natural range of variation, avoiding overly uniform or noisy outputs.

- **User Study:**
 A user study, comparing InterFormer against VRNN and MixMatch, revealed that users showed a greater preference for InterFormer's generated videos, judging them to be more realistic. This subjective assessment corroborates the quantitative findings on quality and realism.

- **Qualitative Evaluation:**
 Visualizations of generated sequences provided compelling evidence of InterFormer's capabilities:
 - For "shaking hands" on SBU, InterFormer accurately captured the full motion sequence (raising, shaking, returning to start), unlike baselines that showed partial or static movements.
 - In a "punching" scenario from SBU, InterFormer generated a realistic body recoil and return to a neutral position.
 - On the challenging "cha-cha" class from DuetDance, InterFormer generated motions that closely followed the action character while maintaining realistic leg movements and spatial relationships, whereas baselines often resulted in static, gliding, or disconnected movements.
 - For the "departing" class on K3HI, InterFormer generated a realistic walking motion with coordinated arm and leg movements.
 - Baselines like PGBIG and STT often produced highly unrealistic, discontinuous, or deformed skeletons, especially when applied to reaction generation without modification, highlighting their unsuitability for this specific task compared to their original design for motion prediction.

- **Multi-Modality Generation:**
 By introducing noise into the encoder input, InterFormer demonstrated the ability to generate diverse reaction motions for the same input action. This feature is crucial for creating more natural and varied AI behavior, addressing the inherent deterministic nature of Transformer models.

**Ablation Study:**
Comprehensive ablation studies on the SBU and K3HI datasets confirmed the effectiveness of each proposed component:
- **Effect of Spatial Attention**: Adding spatial attention significantly improved classification accuracy (+13% on SBU) and diversity, validating its role in modeling intra-skeleton dependencies.
- **Effect of Skeleton Adjacency**: Integrating the skeleton adjacency graph further boosted accuracy (+7% on SBU) and diversity, demonstrating the benefit of incorporating anatomical prior knowledge into self-spatial attention.
- **Effect of Interaction Distance**: The interaction distance module yielded additional improvements in accuracy (+7% on SBU) and diversity, confirming its effectiveness in guiding interaction spatial attention to focus on meaningful inter-skeleton relationships based on proximity.
- **Effect of Loss on First Frames**: The first frame loss was found to be critical for maintaining coherency and preventing discontinuities, with its removal leading to a decrease in generation quality (e.g., -3.3% accuracy and -5.2 diversity score on SBU).
- **Effect of Multihead Attention**: Using multihead attention for both temporal and spatial attention mechanisms (the full InterFormer configuration) consistently delivered the best performance (up to +20% accuracy and +9.7 diversity on SBU over no multihead attention), affirming its importance for capturing complex relationships.

In summary, InterFormer consistently outperforms existing methods across a range of metrics and datasets, demonstrating its ability to generate high-quality, realistic, and diverse human reactions, particularly for complex and long-term interactions. The ablation studies underscore the critical contributions of its novel architectural components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The InterFormer represents a significant advancement in the field of human motion generation, particularly in the challenging domain of human-to-human interaction. Its main significance lies in being the **first Transformer-based architecture explicitly designed for human reaction generation**, reframing the problem as a sequence-to-sequence translation task. This novel application of Transformers to a complex interaction problem opens up new avenues for research and development.

**Key Contributions and Strengths:**

- **Pioneering Transformer for Reaction Generation**: By successfully adapting the Transformer architecture, InterFormer demonstrates its capability to model the intricate spatio-temporal dependencies required for realistic reaction synthesis, a task previously not tackled by such models.
- **Robust Performance Across Diverse Interactions**: The extensive quantitative and qualitative evaluations on SBU, K3HI, and especially the challenging DuetDance dataset (featuring complex, long-term dance interactions) prove InterFormer's robustness and superior performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines. This includes significantly better classification accuracy, lower FVD (indicating higher perceptual quality), and more appropriate diversity scores.
- **Innovative Attention Mechanism Augmentation**: The integration of the Skeleton Adjacency Module and Interaction Distance Module directly within the spatial attention mechanisms is a key methodological contribution. This allows the model to leverage anatomical prior knowledge (intra-skeleton structure) and contextual interaction cues (inter-skeleton proximity) without imposing rigid constraints, leading to more informed and realistic motion generation.
- **Addressing Determinism with Multi-modality**: The ability to generate diverse reactions for a single action by introducing noise addresses a common limitation of deterministic models, moving towards more natural and varied AI-generated behaviors.
- **Clear Problem Formulation**: Defining reaction generation as an action-to-reaction translation problem provides a clear framework for future research in interactive motion synthesis.

**Potential Impact:**

The capabilities of InterFormer have far-reaching implications across several domains:

- **Computer Graphics and Animation**: It could revolutionize the creation of realistic non-player characters (NPCs) in video games, virtual reality, and augmented reality, allowing them to respond dynamically and naturally to user actions without extensive manual animation. This could lead to more immersive and engaging virtual experiences.
- **Robotics**: The ability for robots to predict and generate appropriate human-like reactions to human actions could enhance human-robot interaction, making robots more intuitive, safer, and collaborative in shared environments.
- **Virtual Avatars and Human-Computer Interaction**: InterFormer could enable more believable and empathetic virtual assistants or digital avatars that react with appropriate body language, leading to more natural and effective human-computer communication.
- **Behavioral Science and Psychology**: By synthesizing hypothetical reaction scenarios, the model could serve as a research tool for studying human social dynamics, interaction patterns, and non-verbal communication.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

Despite its significant advancements, the paper acknowledges several limitations:

- **Complexity of Motions**: Generating extremely subtle or highly complex motions, especially those with significant intra-class variability (as seen in DuetDance), remains challenging. The model's outputs can still diverge from ground truth in very long or intricate sequences.
- **Accumulation of Errors**: While InterFormer can generate long sequences by segmenting them, the auto-regressive nature of generation leads to an accumulation of errors over extended durations, causing divergence from the ground truth.
- **Limited Diversity**: Although a noise injection method is proposed to address determinism, the diversity of generated responses, while improved, could still be further enhanced to capture the full spectrum of natural human variability.
- **Data Scarcity**: A significant hurdle is the lack of large, high-quality interaction datasets specifically annotated to distinguish action from reaction, and for long-term motions. This limits comprehensive evaluation and the training of models for more robust, complex scenarios. Future efforts in data collection and annotation are crucial.

In conclusion, InterFormer represents a significant step forward in generating realistic human reactions, demonstrating the power of Transformer architectures for complex spatio-temporal modeling in human interaction. Its methodological innovations and strong performance lay a robust foundation for future research in virtual character animation, robotics, and advanced human-computer interaction, despite the inherent challenges posed by the complexity and variability of human behavior.
