# DROID: A Large-Scale In-The-Wild Robot Manipulation Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> The DROID project introduces a large-scale robot manipulation dataset collected "in-the-wild" across 16 institutions, featuring unprecedented diversity in scenes, tasks, and objects. Policies co-trained with DROID show an average 20% increase in success rate and enhanced robustness to out-of-distribution variations, outperforming existing dataset aggregations.

## Problem

- Existing robot manipulation datasets lack diversity in scenes, tasks, and objects, limiting the generalization capabilities of learned policies.
- Data collection for physical robots is complex due to logistical, safety, and hardware constraints, leading to a scarcity of high-quality, real-world data.
- Robot learning algorithms struggle to generalize beyond controlled laboratory environments, hindering real-world deployment.

## Method

- Over 80 authors from 16 institutions collaboratively collected data using 18 standardized robot platforms across diverse real-world environments.
- A rigorous ==in-the-wild data collection protocol== encouraged scene augmentations, random task selection, and systematic introduction of variations to maximize diversity.
- Post-processing included ==crowdsourced natural language annotations== and automatic camera calibration refinement using deep learning methods to enhance data quality and geometric accuracy.

## Results

- DROID consists of **76,000** successful trajectories (**350 hours**) with an order of magnitude more unique scenes (**564**) and tasks (**86**) than prior datasets.
- Policies co-trained with DROID achieved a **20%** average increase in success rate and significantly improved robustness on out-of-distribution tasks compared to in-domain baselines.
- DROID co-training outperformed co-training with the Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset, demonstrating that DROID's unique 'in-the-wild' diversity is more impactful than combining existing less diverse datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unprecedented scene diversity (564 unique scenes across 52 buildings) is a crucial factor for improving robot policy robustness and generalization to novel environments.
> - A distributed, multi-institutional data collection effort using standardized hardware effectively overcomes traditional bottlenecks in acquiring large-scale, 'in-the-wild' robot data.
> - High-quality natural language annotations and refined camera calibration are essential components for maximizing the utility of large-scale robot manipulation datasets.
