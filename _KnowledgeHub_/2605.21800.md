# stable-worldmodel: A Platform for Reproducible World Modeling Research and Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new open-source platform, stable-worldmodel, standardizes the research and evaluation pipeline for world models, from data collection to comprehensive testing. Its application revealed that contemporary world models exhibit limited zero-shot generalization and robustness, with planning performance degrading significantly even under minor distribution shifts.

## Problem

- World modeling research suffers from fragmentation due to disparate codebases, varying data pipelines, and inconsistent evaluation protocols, hindering reproducibility and fair comparisons.
- Inefficient data loading processes, especially for high-dimensional multimodal data like video, create bottlenecks and underutilize GPUs, slowing down research.
- A lack of standardized benchmarks for assessing generalization capabilities, particularly for out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, prevents rigorous evaluation of model robustness.

## Method

- Introduces `**stable-worldmodel**` (swm), an open-source, modular PyTorch and Gymnasium-based platform that unifies the entire world modeling pipeline.
- Implements a high-performance data layer using Lance for storage, offering fast random access, high compression, and efficient streaming, alongside robust planning solvers and ==world model== baselines.
- Provides a comprehensive evaluation suite with controllable Factors of Variation (FoV) at both simulator and boundary levels, enabling systematic studies of robustness and generalization.

## Results

- The Lance data format achieved approximately 4,815 samples/sec locally and 3,184 samples/sec from S3 for the Push-T environment, outperforming HDF5 and MP4 in loading throughput.
- Baseline world models like LeWM and DINO-WM reproduced reported in-distribution performance on Push-T, achieving **94%** and **92%** success rates respectively.
- Planning success rates for most models dropped sharply even under mild visual perturbations (e.g., a quadratic decay under occlusion) and changes in target anchor position or agent size.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current world models often exhibit limited zero-shot generalization and brittleness, relying on specific visual cues rather than robust underlying dynamics.
> - Raw prediction error magnitude shows a weak correlation with planning success, suggesting it is not a reliable indicator of task performance, especially under distribution shifts.
> - Efficient data management, particularly using columnar storage like Lance, significantly improves data loading throughput, addressing a critical bottleneck in world model training.
