# Ego-Foresight: Self-supervised Learning of Agent-Aware Representations for Improved RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Ego-Foresight introduces a self-supervised method for deep reinforcement learning that disentangles an agent's visual information from its environment by predicting future self-generated sensory changes. This approach, which dynamically adapts to new body schemas including tool use, consistently improved sample efficiency and performance for both model-free and model-based RL algorithms across various robotic control tasks.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents demand extensive training experience, hindering real-world deployment, particularly in robotics with costly data collection.
- Current approaches for disentangling agent and environment rely on explicit supervision (e.g., masks), limiting adaptability to dynamic body schemas such as tool use.
- General world models predict all future states, a computationally intensive task, without specifically prioritizing agent-induced changes.

## Method

- Develops **Ego-Foresight** (EF), a ==self-supervised framework== to disentangle agent features by predicting self-generated visual changes based on actions.
- Uses a feature-splitting mechanism with a low-dimensional bottleneck for agent features and a scene feature fast-forwarding strategy to enforce agent-specific prediction.
- Integrates this agent visuomotor prediction as an auxiliary loss into both model-free (DrQ-v2) and model-based (TD-MPC2) RL algorithms to improve feature learning and performance.

## Results

- Ego-Foresight achieved effective disentanglement of agent-related visual information, accurately predicting agent motion and dynamically incorporating tools into the agent's self-representation.
- Integration into DrQ-v2 yielded performance improvements in **21** out of **26** tasks, while integration into TD-MPC2 improved 8 out of **10** tasks, both showing enhanced sample efficiency and asymptotic performance.
- EF-augmented algorithms outperformed supervised baselines and were particularly effective in tasks involving tool use, validating its adaptability to changing body schemas.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An agent's self-generated motion serves as an intrinsic, self-supervised signal to learn effective disentanglement of agent-specific visual information from the environment.
> - Architecturally enforcing a focus on agent dynamics through bottlenecks and scene feature propagation enables robust learning of agent-aware representations without explicit supervision.
> - Integrating these self-supervised agent-aware representations as an auxiliary task enhances the feature learning and overall performance of both model-free and model-based RL systems, particularly for tasks involving dynamic body schemas.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable advancements over the past decade in various domains. However, a persistent challenge in both simulated and real environments is the substantial amount of training experience required for agents to learn effective policies. This limitation hinders the practical deployment of RL systems, especially in real-world robotics where data collection can be costly and time-consuming.

Previous research has explored approaches to enhance RL efficiency by modeling the agent and environment separately. These methods, exemplified by works such as Gmelin et al. (2023), Hu et al. (2022), and Mendonca et al. (2023), have demonstrated improvements in sample efficiency, zero-shot policy transfer, and environment exploration. A common characteristic of these existing methods is their reliance on supervisory signals to delineate the agent's appearance, often through explicit masks derived from geometric IDs in simulation, fine-tuned segmentation models, or CAD models of robots. This dependence on supervision introduces complexity in real-world applications and limits adaptability when the agent's physical form changes, such as when using tools.

In parallel, the field of World Models (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Finn et al., 2016; Gumbsch et al., 2024) focuses on learning predictive models of the entire environment to enable planning in latent spaces. While effective, these models often aim to predict all future states, which can be a more demanding task than focusing specifically on agent-induced changes.

This work draws inspiration from neuroscientific studies of human development, particularly the concept of motor prediction (Wolpert & Flanagan, 2001). Humans are capable of developing internal models of themselves and predicting the sensory consequences of their own movements without explicit supervisory signals, allowing them to rapidly acquire new skills. This ability to distinguish self-generated sensations from external phenomena, known as contingency awareness or self-other distinction, has also been explored in developmental robotics (Zhang & Nagai, 2018; Lanillos et al., 2020) and machine learning as a form of disentangled representation learning (Bengio, 2013; Wang et al., 2024), where the goal is to separate independent explanatory factors of variation in data. Specifically, in sequential data like video, this can involve disentangling content from motion (Villegas et al., 2017; Denton & Birodkar, 2017).

Ego-Foresight (EF) contributes to this landscape by introducing a self-supervised method for disentangling agent information. It aims to overcome the supervisory dependency of prior agent-environment separation methods by leveraging the agent's intrinsic motion as a cue for self-modeling. By integrating this self-supervised agent-awareness into feature learning as an auxiliary task, the research seeks to improve the sample efficiency and performance of both model-free and model-based RL algorithms, particularly addressing the challenge of adapting to dynamic body schemas like tool use.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the sample-efficiency and performance of Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms, both in simulated and potentially real-world environments. This is motivated by the observation that contemporary RL methods often require a substantial amount of training experience, which limits their applicability in scenarios where data acquisition is costly or time-consuming.

The core motivation for Ego-Foresight (EF) stems from principles observed in human cognitive development. Humans exhibit a remarkable ability to learn new motor skills efficiently, often without explicit supervision. Neuroscientific studies suggest that this efficiency is partly due to motor prediction—the capacity to develop an internal model of one's own body and anticipate the sensory consequences of one's movements. This process is crucial for distinguishing self-generated sensations from external events and for adapting to a changing body schema, such as when using tools.

The central insight guiding this work is that the movement of an artificial agent can provide an intrinsic, self-supervised cue for learning to differentiate between the agent and its environment. By attempting to predict the visual changes to its own body configuration based on its actions, an agent can implicitly learn representations that specifically encode agent-related information.

The key objectives derived from this motivation are:
1. **Develop a self-supervised method for agent information disentanglement:** The goal is to create a mechanism that can separate visual information pertaining to the agent from that of the environment, without relying on external supervisory signals like segmentation masks or CAD models.
2. **Demonstrate the ability to reconstruct and predict future agent configurations:** The method should be capable of accurately forecasting how the agent's visual appearance will change in response to its actions.
3. **Validate adaptability to changing body schemas:** A critical objective is to show that the proposed approach can adapt when the agent's embodiment changes, such as when it picks up and uses a tool, integrating the tool into its self-representation.
4. **Improve sample-efficiency and performance of existing RL algorithms:** The self-supervised agent-aware representations, when integrated as an auxiliary task, are hypothesized to regularize feature learning and allow RL algorithms to focus their capacity more effectively. This should lead to faster learning and potentially higher asymptotic performance in solving control tasks.
5. **Test generality across different RL paradigms:** The approach aims to be compatible with both model-free and model-based RL algorithms, demonstrating its broad applicability within the visual RL community.

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to advance the field towards more autonomous and robust learning systems that can operate efficiently in complex, dynamic environments, drawing parallels with the adaptability observed in biological systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper, named Ego-Foresight (EF), is a self-supervised approach designed to disentangle agent information based on its motion and self-prediction. This disentangled representation is then used as an auxiliary task to enhance existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms.

#### 3.1. Episode Partition

The training data consists of episodes, each containing a sequence of RGB frames (observations `x`) and corresponding actions (`a`). During training, a window of `C + H` steps is randomly sampled from each episode, where `C` is the number of context frames and `H` is the prediction horizon. The first `C` steps serve as context input to the model, along with actions for the entire `C+H` sequence. Frames from `t_C` to `t_{C+H}` are used as targets for prediction.

#### 3.2. Motion-Based Agent Disentanglement

The core of EF is an encoder-decoder model with a recurrent block designed for visuomotor prediction (Figure 3).
- **Encoder (E_ψ):** This convolutional network takes a limited number of context RGB frames (`x_{t_0:t_c}`) as input and produces a feature representation `h ∈ R^n`. In DrQv2-EF, an additional average pool and linear projection reduce the encoder output dimensionality to 2048. For TD-MPC2, the encoder architecture is fully preserved.
- **Feature Splitting:** The feature vector `h` is split into two components: `h_{t_c}^s ∈ R^m` representing scene features and `h_{t_c}^a ∈ R^l` representing agent features. The sum of `l` and `m` equals `n`.
- **Recurrent Block (FC_ψ):** The agent features `h_{t_c}^a` are fed into this recurrent network, along with the action `a_{t_{c+1}}` at the next time step. The network iteratively predicts future agent configurations, generating `h_{t_j+1}^a` from `h_{t_j}^a` and `a_{t_j+1}`, up to a randomly sampled time step `t_k` within the prediction horizon. The recurrent unit is based on the forward dynamics model from TD-MPC2.
- **Decoder (D_ψ):** The predicted agent features `h_{t_k}^a` are concatenated with the original scene features `h_{t_c}^s` (from the context frames). This combined feature vector is then passed to the decoder to reconstruct the future frame `x_{t_k}`. The decoder is based on DCGAN and, optionally, can include U-Net like skip-connections for improved reconstruction quality.
- **Reconstruction Loss (L_ef):** The model is trained using a mean squared error loss between the reconstructed frame `x_{t_k}` and the ground truth frame `x_{t_k}`: `L_ef(ψ, D) = E_{τ~D} ||x_{t_k} - x_{t_k}||^2_2`.

To encourage effective disentanglement, two key design choices are made:
1. **Bottleneck for Agent Features:** The dimensionality `l` of `h^a` is intentionally set to be a small fraction of `n`. This bottleneck forces the recurrent model to prioritize learning the most predictable dynamics, which are typically those of the agent itself.
2. **Scene Feature Fast-Forwarding:** By concatenating the original `h_{t_c}^s` (from context frames) with the predicted `h_{t_k}^a` for reconstruction, the recurrent block is discouraged from predicting the dynamics of the entire environment. This compels the agent features `h^a` to concentrate solely on the agent's configuration changes, as scene information is carried forward from the context.

#### 3.3. Agent Visuomotor Prediction as Feature Learning for RL

EF is integrated with existing RL algorithms by treating it as an auxiliary task for feature learning.
- **Shared Encoder:** The visual encoder of the baseline RL algorithm is shared with the EF framework.
- **Auxiliary Loss Integration:** An optimization step is performed by augmenting the loss function of the baseline RL algorithm with `L_ef`. The gradients from `L_ef` backpropagate to the shared encoder.
- **Model-Free RL (DrQ-v2-EF):** For the model-free DrQ-v2 algorithm, `L_ef` is added to the critic loss `L_critic`. The combined objective is `L(ϕ, ψ, D) = L_critic(ϕ, D) + βL_ef(ψ, D)`, where `β` is a weighting factor. Here, `L_ef` acts as a regularizer, imposing that learned features are predictive of agent motion, independent of task reward.
- **Model-Based RL (TD-MPC2-EF):** For the model-based TD-MPC2 algorithm, `L_ef` is added to the model objective `L_TDMPC`. The combined objective is `L(θ, ψ, D) = L_TDMPC(θ, D) + βL_ef(ψ, D)`.
- **Motor Babbling:** An optional initial motor-babbling stage is introduced, where the agent performs random actions (±1) for a fixed number of steps. This encourages exploratory movements and facilitates the learning of a diverse visuomotor mapping before goal-directed behavior might limit motion variety. EF continues to be optimized after this stage, allowing adaptation.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted in simulation using two benchmarks:
- **Meta-World (Yu et al., 2020):** Tasks involve robotic object manipulation, including those requiring tool use.
- **DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) (Tassa et al., 2018):** Tasks involve locomotion with diverse embodiments.
Inputs include camera observations and commanded actions.
**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Sample-efficiency:** Measured by the number of environment steps.
- **Performance:** Measured by success rate (Meta-World) or reward (DMC).
- **Efficiency Normalized Score (ENS):** A composite metric combining sample-efficiency and performance. It identifies the step at which 95% (or 90%, 85%) of maximum performance is reached and measures the algorithm's performance at that point.
**Baselines:** The paper compares DrQv2-EF against DrQ-v2, SEAR (Gmelin et al., 2023), CURL (Laskin et al., 2020), and Dreamer-v3 (Hafner et al., 2025). TD-MPC2-EF is compared against TD-MPC2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the Ego-Foresight (EF) method, its ability to disentangle agent information, and its impact on the sample-efficiency and performance of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms.

#### 4.1. Agent-Environment Disentanglement

- **Visualization of Encoded Features:** Visualizations show that EF effectively disentangles agent-related information from the scene. Early in training, both agent features (`h_a`) and scene features (`h_s`) contribute broadly to frame reconstruction. As training progresses, `h_s` becomes specialized in encoding static and variable background elements (including those introduced by image augmentation), while `h_a` concentrates on agent-specific visual information. This indicates that the bottleneck and scene fast-forwarding mechanisms successfully enforce this specialization.
- **Consistent Motion Prediction:** The model demonstrates the ability to correctly predict the motion of the robot arm based on sequences of actions.
- **Adaptation to Changing Embodiments:** A significant finding is EF's capacity to adapt its self-representation to include tools. In tasks like "Hammer," once the agent grasps a hammer, the model predicts the hammer's movement as an integral part of the agent's body schema. Conversely, in tasks like "Door Open," environmental objects such as the door, which do not move consistently with the agent across episodes, remain part of the scene and are not predicted as moving by `h_a`. This demonstrates a dynamic adaptation to the agent's effective embodiment without requiring explicit supervision, a capability that supervised methods typically lack.
- **Real-world Transferability (Qualitative):** Qualitative assessments on the BAIR dataset (real-world robot pushing objects) suggest that EF can separate robot-specific visual information from the scene and predict the robot's arm trajectory. While the model may blur objects the arm passes, indicating some expected interaction, it accurately captures the robot's self-generated motion. The method also demonstrates an ability to generalize predictions to previously unseen hand-crafted trajectories.

#### 4.2. Agent-Aware Representations for Improved Reinforcement Learning

- **Sample-Efficiency and Performance:**
 - **DrQv2-EF:** When augmenting DrQ-v2 with EF, improvements were observed in 21 out of 26 tested tasks. These improvements often included a reduction in the number of steps required to solve tasks and an increase in asymptotic performance. No degradation in results was reported for any task.
 - **TD-MPC2-EF:** Augmenting TD-MPC2 with EF led to improvements in 8 out of 10 tasks, with comparable results in the remaining 2 tasks.
 - **Efficiency Normalized Score (ENS):** Both DrQv2-EF and TD-MPC2-EF achieved a significant improvement in ENS compared to their respective baselines, indicating a combined gain in both sample-efficiency and performance. Rliable metrics further supported these statistical improvements.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** DrQv2-EF outperformed the supervised baseline SEAR and the contrastive self-supervised method CURL on both Meta-World and DMC benchmarks. It also demonstrated competitive performance against Dreamer-v3, a state-of-the-art model-based RL algorithm.
- **Tool Use Tasks:** A notable advantage of EF was observed in tasks requiring tool use. The performance gap between EF-augmented algorithms and baselines was greater in these tasks, supporting the interpretation that integrating tool movement into the agent's predictable body schema contributes to learning efficiency.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on DrQv2-EF investigated the impact of key hyperparameters:
- **Loss Weight (β):** Varying the weight of the `L_ef` term showed that the auxiliary loss has a regularizing effect, contributing to improved performance even without direct optimization for reward.
- **Prediction Horizon (H):** While horizon length did not show a uniformly consistent impact, horizons of 10 and 40 steps generally yielded stronger performance. A horizon of 10 was selected as the default due to its balance of performance and lower computational cost.
- **Agent Feature Dimensionality (h_a):** Larger dimensions for `h_a` were detrimental to performance. This supports the design principle of a "soft bottleneck" that compels the recurrent block to focus specifically on agent dynamics, preventing it from attempting to model broader, less predictable environment features.
- **Motor Babbling:** The initial motor-babbling stage was found to be a meaningful contribution, but prolonged babbling could delay the learning process.

#### 4.4. Computational Cost

EF introduces a wall-clock overhead, as evidenced by a reduction in environment steps per second (e.g., DrQv2-EF at 110 steps/s compared to DrQ-v2 at 155 steps/s). However, the paper posits that this computational penalty is justified by the gains in sample-efficiency, which is often a more critical factor in real-world applications such as robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper introduces Ego-Foresight (EF), a self-supervised method for learning agent-aware representations, and demonstrates its integration into deep reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks. This work holds several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Reduced Dependency on Supervision:** A primary contribution is the elimination of the need for explicit supervisory signals (e.g., segmentation masks, CAD models) to distinguish the agent from its environment. This addresses a critical limitation of previous agent-environment disentanglement methods, making the approach more practical and scalable for real-world robotic applications where obtaining such precise supervision can be challenging, expensive, or impossible.

2. **Improved Sample Efficiency and Performance in RL:** By using agent motion prediction as a self-supervised auxiliary task, EF effectively regularizes the visual encoder and provides richer, more focused features for the RL algorithm. The demonstrated improvements in sample efficiency mean that agents can learn effective policies with less interaction data, reducing training time and computational resources. The observed increases in asymptotic performance also indicate that EF contributes to learning more robust and capable policies.

3. **Adaptability to Changing Embodiments:** The ability of EF to dynamically integrate tools into the agent's body schema without prior knowledge or explicit retraining represents a substantial advancement. This adaptability is crucial for robots that interact with and manipulate various objects, allowing for flexible and intuitive control in complex manipulation tasks. This capability mirrors human motor learning, where tools are often seamlessly incorporated into the body's internal model.

4. **Generality Across RL Paradigms:** The successful integration and observed benefits with both model-free (DrQ-v2) and model-based (TD-MPC2) RL algorithms suggest that EF is a broadly applicable technique. This indicates that its underlying principles of self-supervised agent-awareness can benefit diverse RL architectures and learning strategies, potentially impacting a wider range of RL research and applications.

5. **Neuroscientific Inspiration for AI:** This work provides a concrete example of how insights from neuroscience, specifically the concept of motor prediction and self-modeling, can be translated into effective computational mechanisms for artificial intelligence. It reinforces the idea that understanding biological learning processes can inspire more robust and efficient AI systems.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Robotics:** Enhanced sample efficiency and adaptability to tools could significantly accelerate the deployment of robots in unstructured real-world environments, such as manufacturing, service robotics, or hazardous material handling, where flexibility and learning from limited demonstrations are paramount.
- **Autonomous Systems:** The ability to dynamically model the "self" could be beneficial for other autonomous systems, such as self-driving cars, where understanding one's own vehicle's state and interactions with the environment is crucial.
- **Foundation Models for RL:** Self-supervised learning of agent-aware representations could contribute to the development of more generalizable pre-trained models for RL, reducing the need for extensive task-specific fine-tuning.

**Acknowledged Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge limitations such as the computational overhead of pixel-wise reconstruction (suggesting contrastive loss as an alternative), the fixed nature of the babbling stage (proposing adaptive babbling), and the inherent instability of baseline RL algorithms. Future work aims to explore faster adaptation to new body schemas, improve modeling of moving objects, scale to higher-resolution real-world images with Transformer architectures, and apply the approach to other domains beyond robotics. These proposed directions indicate continued efforts to expand the capabilities and applicability of Ego-Foresight.
