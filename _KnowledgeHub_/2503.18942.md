# Video-T1: Test-Time Scaling for Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Video-T1 framework introduces Test-Time Scaling for video generation, enabling quality improvements by reinterpreting video synthesis as a search problem within the latent space. It leverages an ensemble of verifiers and a novel Tree-of-Frames search algorithm to guide existing models towards generating higher-quality, text-aligned videos, showing up to 70% efficiency gains in computation compared to a linear search baseline.

## Problem

- Improving video generation quality often requires costly scaling of models, data, and computation during training, presenting a barrier to further advancement.
- Existing video generation models struggle with maintaining temporal coherence and capturing complex dynamics from text prompts.
- There was a need for methods to enhance video generation performance without incurring the substantial costs of extensive retraining or model enlargement.

## Method

- The researchers propose **Video-T1**, a ==Test-Time Scaling (TTS)== framework that reinterprets video generation as a ==search problem in the Gaussian noise space==, guiding existing video generators.
- The framework utilizes an ensemble of ==multimodal reward models== (test verifiers) to provide feedback on generated video quality and text alignment.
- It introduces two search algorithms: a ==Random Linear Search== baseline and a more efficient ==Tree-of-Frames (ToF) search==, which incorporates hierarchical prompting and heuristic pruning along the temporal dimension.

## Results

- Test-Time Scaling consistently improved **VBench total scores** across six diverse video generation models, with performance gains converging after a certain computational budget.
- The Tree-of-Frames search algorithm achieved similar video quality metrics while reducing GFLOPs by approximately **66-70%** compared to random linear search for specific models.
- Smaller models augmented with TTS demonstrated performance comparable to, or exceeding, significantly larger single-run models (e.g., **Pyramid-Flow 2B vs HunyuanVideo 13B**) on various VBench dimensions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Allocating additional inference-time computation consistently improves video generation quality, demonstrating a viable alternative to solely scaling models during training.
> - The choice and combination of multiple verifiers significantly impact the effectiveness of TTS, providing more robust guidance for video selection.
> - Sophisticated search algorithms like Tree-of-Frames can achieve comparable quality to simpler linear searches with significantly reduced computational cost, making TTS practical for complex video tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative modeling has experienced significant advancements, particularly in text, image, and video synthesis. This progress is often attributed to the scaling of training data, model size, and computational resources during the training phase, a phenomenon known as Scaling Laws. Video generation, in particular, holds potential for revolutionizing digital content creation by enabling the production of high-quality videos from textual descriptions. However, generating videos that maintain temporal coherence and capture complex dynamics remains a challenge. Scaling video generation models during training, while effective, is computationally intensive and resource-demanding, presenting limitations for further improvement.

Concurrently, a parallel trend has emerged in Large Language Models (LLMs): Test-Time Scaling (TTS). In this paradigm, models are allocated additional computation during inference, leading to notable performance improvements without the need for extensive retraining or model enlargement. Techniques such as chain-of-thought prompting, self-consistency, and various reward-guided search methods have demonstrated the efficacy of TTS in LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI o1). The concept of TTS has also begun to be explored in computer vision, particularly in visual language models and image diffusion models, where it has shown promise in enhancing reasoning capabilities and generation quality.

This research aims to bridge the application of Test-Time Scaling from LLMs and image generation to the more complex domain of video generation. It acknowledges the unique challenges of video, specifically the need to handle both spatial quality and temporal consistency, alongside the iterative denoising processes inherent in diffusion models. By investigating TTS for video generation, this work seeks to provide an alternative pathway for improving video synthesis quality beyond the traditional and costly approach of scaling models at the training stage. It positions itself as an exploration of how inference-time computation can be effectively leveraged to enhance the capabilities of existing video generation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the efficacy of Test-Time Scaling (TTS) in video generation. The authors explicitly pose the question: "if a video generation model is allowed to use non-trivial amount of inference-time compute, how much can it improve generation quality given a challenging text prompt."

The motivation behind addressing this question stems from several factors:

- **Limitations of Training-Time Scaling:** While scaling models during training has been a key driver of progress in generative AI, including video generation, it is inherently expensive and resource-intensive. This poses a barrier to further advancements, especially for organizations with limited computational resources. The authors seek a method to enhance model performance without incurring the substantial costs associated with larger datasets, models, or training runs.
- **Success in Other Domains:** The observed benefits of TTS in Large Language Models (LLMs) and, to a lesser extent, in image diffusion models, suggest that similar improvements might be achievable in video generation. LLMs have demonstrated that allocating additional computation at inference time can lead to more contextually appropriate and higher-quality outputs.
- **Unique Challenges of Video Generation:** Video generation presents distinct challenges compared to text or image generation, primarily due to the temporal dimension. Maintaining consistency across frames, depicting realistic motion, and ensuring physical plausibility are complex tasks. Existing video generation models, largely based on multi-step denoising diffusion processes, add further complexity to directly applying scaling strategies. The research is motivated to develop TTS strategies that specifically account for these video-specific characteristics.
- **Reconceptualizing Video Generation:** To tackle these challenges, the authors propose a conceptual shift. They reinterpret the problem of generating a high-quality video under TTS as a "searching problem to sample better trajectories from Gaussian noise space to the target video distribution." This reinterpretation motivates the development of search-based algorithms capable of exploring a wider range of potential solutions at inference time.

In essence, the core motivation is to enhance the quality of text-conditioned video generation by strategically allocating and utilizing computational resources during the inference phase, thereby providing a more efficient and accessible path to improved video synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of Video-T1 involves reinterpreting Test-Time Scaling (TTS) for video generation as a search problem within the space of possible video trajectories, originating from Gaussian noise. This framework, termed Video-T1, aims to scale the search space during inference to identify higher-quality and text-aligned video outputs. The framework relies on three key components: a video generator, test verifiers, and heuristic search algorithms.

**Components of the Video-T1 Framework:**

- **Video Generator (G):** This component utilizes existing video generation models, which produce videos from given text prompts through a multi-step denoising process. The function of the generator is formally defined as $G : c \rightarrow R^{H \times W \times C \times T}$, where $c$ is the text condition and the output is a video with $T$ frames.
- **Test Verifiers (V):** These are multimodal evaluation models responsible for assessing the quality of generated videos and assigning a scalar score. This score provides feedback crucial for guiding the generation process. The verifier function is expressed as $V : R^{H \times W \times C \times T} \times c \rightarrow R$. The authors leverage an ensemble of three multimodal reward models: VisionReward, VideoScore, and VideoLLaMA3, to mitigate biases and enhance selection robustness. The final selection combines weighted ranks from these multiple verifiers.
- **Heuristic Search Algorithms (f):** These are optimization methods designed to leverage feedback from the verifiers to guide the search trajectory and identify superior video sequences. The search algorithm function is given as $f : G \times V \times (R^{H \times W \times C})^{N} \times c \rightarrow R^{H \times W \times C \times T}$, where $N$ represents the initial noise samples.

**Search Algorithms Implemented:**

1. **Random Linear Search:**
 - This is presented as a straightforward baseline approach.
 - It operates by sampling $N$ independent Gaussian noise candidates.
 - For each candidate, the video generator performs a full-step denoising process to produce a complete video sequence.
 - A test verifier evaluates each generated video, and the video with the highest score is selected (a "Best-of-N" strategy).
 - The computational cost for this method is analyzed as $O(T \cdot N)$, where $T$ is the video frame length and $N$ is the number of initial samples.
 - Limitations include its linear structure, which lacks efficient optimization mechanisms and the absence of feedback or interaction between independently generated video candidates, leading to increased randomness.

2. **Tree-of-Frames (ToF) Search:**
 - Designed as a more efficient and scalable alternative to random linear search, particularly for handling longer videos and larger search spaces.
 - It introduces inference-time reasoning along the temporal dimension, structuring the video generation process into three distinct stages, reflecting the sequential nature of video.
 - **Image-level alignment:** Within each stage, and even during the denoising of individual frames, a progressive evaluation strategy is employed. A verifier assesses the clarity of partially denoised frames and their potential to evolve into high-quality final images, allowing for early rejection of low-potential candidates. This dynamic scaling of computation aims for more efficient resource utilization.
 - **Hierarchical prompting:** A large language model (DeepSeek-R1-8B) is utilized to decompose the initial text prompt into three specific hierarchical prompts. These prompts are tailored for the static scene description of the initial frame, action/motion directions for intermediate frames, and the expected ending state for the concluding frame. Test verifiers use these stage-specific prompts to guide evaluation and feedback. Adaptive branching, through injecting additional initial noise samples, is introduced when transitioning between stages to maintain temporal coherence and diversity.
 - **Heuristic pruning:** The generation process is modeled as the dynamic growth of a forest of possible video paths. Starting with $N$ initial frames (roots), at each time step $t$, $k_{t-1}$ surviving parent nodes dynamically branch into $b_t$ candidate continuations. All $k_{t-1} \cdot b_t$ nodes are evaluated using a heuristic reward score from the test verifiers. Only the top $k_t$ nodes are retained for further growth, balancing local frame quality with global consistency.
 - **Complexity Analysis:** While the worst-case complexity can still be $O(T \cdot N)$, the ToF search algorithm, by dynamically pruning and branching only at specific prompt stages, aims to achieve a practical complexity closer to $O(N + T)$, significantly reducing computational costs compared to linear search.

The methodology integrates existing video generation models with advanced test-time evaluation and search strategies, specifically tailored to the spatio-temporal dynamics of video, to improve generation quality without costly retraining.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate several key findings regarding Test-Time Scaling (TTS) in video generation:

- **Consistent Performance Improvement with TTS:** Across all six evaluated video generation models (three diffusion-based: OpenSora-v1.2, CogVideoX-2B, CogVideoX-5B; and three autoregressive: NOVA, Pyramid-Flow (SD3), Pyramid-Flow (FLUX)) and various verifiers, an increase in inference-time computational budget consistently led to improvements in video quality, as measured by the VBench total score. The performance gains were observed to approach a convergence limit after a certain threshold of computation.
- **Impact of Verifier Choice:** The study noted that different verifiers yielded varying growth rates and extents of performance improvement, indicating that each verifier emphasizes distinct evaluation aspects. The use of multiple verifiers in an ensemble further boosted the performance of TTS, suggesting that combining diverse evaluation perspectives can mitigate biases and enhance overall selection accuracy.
- **Scaling Benefits for Larger Models:** A comparative analysis revealed that larger and more advanced foundation models (e.g., CogVideoX-5B, Pyramid-Flow (FLUX)) exhibited more substantial performance improvements with increased inference effort compared to lighter models (e.g., NOVA). This finding implies that models with greater inherent capacity can more effectively leverage the wider search space provided by TTS.
- **Efficiency of Tree-of-Frames (ToF) Search:** The ToF search algorithm demonstrated superior efficiency compared to the random linear search. It achieved comparable video quality metrics but at a significantly reduced computational cost (measured in GFLOPs). For instance, ToF search reduced GFLOPs by approximately 66-70% for Pyramid-Flow and NOVA models while maintaining similar performance levels. This efficiency is attributed to its practical time complexity of $O(N+T)$ versus the $O(T \cdot N)$ of random linear search in most experimental scenarios.
- **Variable Improvements Across Benchmark Dimensions:** TTS methods generally yielded significant improvements for easily assessable categories and common prompt sets within VBench (e.g., Scene, Object, Imaging Quality). However, improvements were less pronounced for complex or hard-to-evaluate latent properties, such as Motion Smoothness and Temporal Flickering. This suggests that while TTS can enhance adherence to general prompt requirements and visual fidelity, it may be constrained by the foundational model's intrinsic capabilities in generating highly nuanced or temporally consistent dynamics.
- **Competitive Performance Against Larger Models:** A notable finding was that smaller models, when augmented with TTS (e.g., Pyramid-Flow 2B), could achieve performance scores comparable to, or even exceeding, those of significantly larger single-run models (e.g., HunyuanVideo 13B) across many VBench dimensions. This indicates that strategically applied inference-time computation can partially compensate for differences in model scale.
- **Identified Failure Cases:** The research also acknowledged limitations, observing failure cases where increasing inference computation did not substantially improve video quality for specific, challenging details (e.g., realistic hand movements). This suggests that inherent limitations in the underlying video generation model can restrict the extent to which TTS can enhance performance.

In summary, the results indicate that Test-Time Scaling is an effective strategy for enhancing video generation quality, particularly when employing more sophisticated search algorithms like ToF and leveraging multiple verifiers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Test-Time Scaling (TTS) for video generation, as presented in Video-T1, carries several significances and potential impacts:

- **New Paradigm for Video Quality Improvement:** This work establishes a novel paradigm for enhancing video generation quality by reinterpreting the problem as a search process within the latent space. This offers an alternative to the traditional method of relying solely on expensive and resource-intensive training-time scaling, which is a significant bottleneck in the field.
- **Cost-Effective Performance Enhancement:** By demonstrating that significant quality improvements can be achieved through strategic allocation of inference-time computation, the research provides a more accessible and cost-effective pathway for improving video generation models. This could democratize access to higher-quality video synthesis for researchers and practitioners with limited resources, reducing the barrier to entry for advanced content creation.
- **Enhanced Generative Control and Alignment:** The framework, particularly through the use of test-time verifiers and hierarchical prompting, offers a mechanism for finer control over the generation process. By providing feedback and guiding the search toward better trajectories, TTS can lead to videos that are more consistent with textual prompts and exhibit improved spatial and temporal coherence.
- **Efficiency Gains in Search Strategies:** The development of the Tree-of-Frames (ToF) search algorithm demonstrates that sophisticated search methodologies can yield substantial efficiency gains in TTS. Its ability to achieve comparable quality at significantly lower computational costs than linear search makes the application of TTS more practical for longer videos and more complex generation tasks.
- **Bridging the Gap Between Model Sizes:** The finding that smaller models, when combined with TTS, can achieve performance comparable to or even surpass larger models on certain metrics, suggests a potential for smaller, more efficient foundation models to be competitive. This could influence future model development strategies, emphasizing optimized inference-time computation alongside model architecture design.
- **Broader Impact on Computer Vision:** The success of TTS in video generation extends the observed benefits from LLMs and image generation to a more complex multimodal domain. This reinforces the broader hypothesis that strategically leveraging inference-time computation can be a generalizable approach for achieving superior results across various computer vision tasks, potentially inspiring similar research in other generative or discriminative vision applications.
- **Future Research Directions:** The framework opens new avenues for research into optimizing test-time search algorithms, designing more robust and unbiased verifiers, and exploring adaptive computational allocation strategies within the video generation pipeline. Further investigation into the limitations of TTS, particularly for hard-to-evaluate latent properties, can guide the development of hybrid approaches that combine model improvements with intelligent inference strategies.
