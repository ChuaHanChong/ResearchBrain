# From Perception to Action: An Interactive Benchmark for Vision Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Causal Hierarchy of Actions and Interactions (CHAIN) benchmark introduces an interactive 3D, physics-driven testbed for evaluating AI's physical reasoning capabilities, revealing that state-of-the-art vision-language models achieve an overall success rate of only 22.9% and often struggle with constraint-aware, multi-step problem-solving. It further demonstrates that current world models systematically fail to maintain physical consistency during structured interactions.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) evaluations primarily rely on static, single-turn setups, failing to assess dynamic, multi-step physical reasoning about geometry, contact, and support relations.
- Current evaluations for diffusion-based models are largely confined to simplified 2D environments, neglecting the complexities of 3D geometry and multi-body physical constraints in problem-solving.
- A lack of interactive benchmarks to rigorously test AI's capacity to understand, plan, and execute structured action sequences inherently grounded in 3D physical constraints.

## Method

- Introducing **CHAIN**, an interactive ==3D physics-driven benchmark== comprising **109 distinct levels** across two task families: ==interlocking mechanical puzzles== and ==3D stacking/packing==.
- Utilizing a ==closed-loop evaluation protocol== where an agent iteratively observes, selects actions from a predefined space, and receives environmental feedback until task completion or a step budget is reached.
- Implementing environments using ==Unity== for complex puzzles and a lightweight 3D Python engine for stacking tasks, with a uniform, color-hinted interaction interface and ==multi-view observations==.

## Results

- The best-performing VLM, **GPT-5.2**, achieved an overall Pass@1 of **22.9%**, with significantly lower success on Puzzle tasks (**0.0-3.1%**) compared to Stacking tasks (**10.4-31.2%**).
- Performance on Stacking tasks degraded with increasing difficulty (e.g., GPT-5.2 achieved **100%** for easy but **6.3%** for hard), while Puzzle tasks remained challenging even at "easy" levels (peak **10.0%**).
- Stronger models achieved higher success rates but often showed increased plan inefficiency (more steps, higher Dist2Opt) and consequently higher token and cost consumption per solved task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art VLMs consistently struggle with interactive 3D physical reasoning, particularly with interlocking mechanical puzzles, demonstrating a limited capacity to infer and utilize hidden structural constraints.
> - Interaction and iterative feedback are crucial for improving performance, with the interactive setting yielding substantially higher accuracy than one-shot planning, indicating models leverage feedback to adapt plans.
> - Contemporary world models (video generation models) exhibit catastrophic failures in structured physical interactions, violating fundamental physics, suffering from representational collapse, and lacking object permanence.
