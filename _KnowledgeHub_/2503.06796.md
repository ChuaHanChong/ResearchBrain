# RoboDesign1M: A Large-scale Dataset for Robot Design Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FPT Software AI Center and the University of Liverpool developed RoboDesign1M, a large-scale multimodal dataset comprising 1 million image-text pairs extracted from scientific literature, aimed at training and enabling foundation models for robot design. Experiments demonstrated that models finetuned on RoboDesign1M showed up to 62% improvement in cross-dataset VQA generalization and reduced the FID score for text-to-design image generation from 45.83 to 39.42.

## Problem

- A significant lack of large-scale, specialized datasets for robot design hinders the application and development of foundation models in this domain.
- Existing engineering datasets are often generic, small-scale, or primarily focused on 3D CAD models without rich textual descriptions or multimodal instruction-following data.
- General-purpose foundation models struggle to understand and contribute to the complex, interdisciplinary process of robot design due to the scarcity of relevant training data.

## Method

- The authors created **RoboDesign1M**, a dataset of approximately 1 million multimodal samples (2D images/drawings and associated text) specifically for robot design, sourced from scientific literature.
- A semi-automated pipeline was developed for data acquisition from scientific documents, utilizing robotics-specific keywords and PDF content extraction tools.
- A robust filtering process including an ensemble image classifier for relevance and a text-verified k-==Nearest-Neighbor== method for deduplication was implemented, alongside LLM-driven construction of 1.3 million visual instruction-following (Q&A) pairs.

## Results

- Models finetuned on RoboDesign1M achieved 15-**62%** higher cross-dataset generalization in Visual Question Answering (VQA) when evaluated on Text2CAD and Ghezelbash datasets.
- In text-to-design image generation, finetuning Stable Diffusion XL on RoboDesign1M reduced the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) from **45.83** to **39.42**, indicating improved generation quality.
- Models finetuned on RoboDesign1M demonstrated superior cross-dataset performance in text-image retrieval and received higher ratings in a user study for text-image alignment in generated designs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sourcing data from scientific literature, combined with careful filtering, ensures high reliability and domain-specificity, addressing technical accuracy for robot design.
> - Automatically generating visual instruction-following data using LLMs significantly enhances the dataset's utility for training modern multi-modal models.
> - A large, diverse, and domain-specific multimodal dataset can significantly improve the generalization capabilities of AI models across various robot design understanding tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of RoboDesign1M is situated within the broader context of advancing Artificial Intelligence (AI) and machine learning, particularly foundation models, to address complex engineering and design challenges. Recent advancements in large-scale foundation models, such as Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT, have demonstrated capabilities across various domains, including an increasing number of applications in robotics. These models have been explored for their potential to lower costs, reduce human effort, and expand the boundaries of creativity in robot design processes.

However, a significant challenge in applying these general-purpose foundation models to specialized fields like robot design is the scarcity of large-scale, domain-specific datasets. While datasets exist for general engineering designs, often containing CAD models or technical drawings of generic objects, they typically lack the specificity, multimodal nature, and interdisciplinary scope required for comprehensive robot design understanding. Existing mechanical engineering datasets often focus on specific components like mechanisms and gears, or primarily provide 3D CAD models without rich textual descriptions or instruction-following data crucial for modern multi-modal AI.

This research addresses this data gap by creating RoboDesign1M, a dataset specifically tailored for robot design. It provides multimodal data (2D images/drawings and associated text) derived from scientific literature, covering a wide array of robotic disciplines. By providing such a dataset, the authors aim to enable the fine-tuning of foundation models for tasks like automated design generation, text-based design retrieval, and AI-powered design assistants, thereby contributing to the development of dedicated foundation models for robot design. This work positions itself as a foundational resource to facilitate further research into AI-driven robotic design automation, an area currently limited by data availability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the data scarcity gap that currently hinders the development and application of foundation models for robot design. The authors aim to enhance AI's capability to understand and contribute to the complex and interdisciplinary process of robot design.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving this work are:

- **Addressing the Data Scarcity Problem:** Acknowledging that the lack of large-scale, specialized robot design datasets is a major impediment to leveraging modern deep learning and foundation models in this field. Existing datasets are often generic, small-scale, or confined to specific engineering sub-domains, failing to capture the unique geometric properties, attributes, and interrelationships inherent in robotic designs.
- **Enabling Foundation Models for Robot Design:** Motivated by the potential of large foundation models to transform robot design by offering insightful feedback, suggesting improvements, streamlining workflows, and fostering innovation. To realize this potential, these models require training data that is specific to the robot design domain.
- **Creating a Large-Scale, Multimodal Dataset:** The central objective is to introduce RoboDesign1M, a dataset comprising approximately 1 million samples. This dataset is designed to be large-scale and multimodal, featuring 2D drawings and images of robot designs along with associated textual descriptions.
- **Ensuring Domain Specificity and Diversity:** To curate a dataset exclusively for robot design, covering a broad spectrum of robot types and design aspects from various robotic disciplines (e.g., bipedal, soft, mobile robots, prosthetics, robot manipulation, micro-robotics). This diversity is intended to enhance the generalization capabilities of models trained on the dataset.
- **Prioritizing Data Reliability:** To source data from scientific literature, including conference proceedings, journal articles, and dissertations. This approach aims to ensure that the figures and accompanying texts are technically accurate and representative of extensive research efforts within the robotics community.
- **Developing an Efficient Data Collection Pipeline:** Recognizing the challenges of manually collecting and labeling specialized technical data, the authors sought to propose and implement a semi-automated data acquisition, filtering, and labeling pipeline. This pipeline is designed to facilitate efficient and diverse data collection at scale.
- **Constructing Visual Instruction-Following Data:** To enhance the utility of the dataset for modern multi-modal learning frameworks, an objective was to automatically construct visual instruction-following data (question-answer pairs) by leveraging LLMs to interpret the visual content and associated texts.
- **Establishing a Benchmark for Robot Design Understanding:** To conduct extensive experiments across multiple robot design understanding tasks—including design image generation, visual question answering (VQA) about designs, and design image retrieval—to demonstrate RoboDesign1M's effectiveness as a challenging new benchmark and its potential to advance research in the field.

In essence, the motivation is to provide the necessary data infrastructure for AI to support and potentially transform the robot design process, moving beyond the limits of human intuition alone.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The creation of the RoboDesign1M dataset involved a multi-stage, semi-automated pipeline designed to efficiently acquire, filter, and process multimodal data for robot design understanding. The pipeline consists of data acquisition, data filtering, and visual instruction-following data construction.

#### 3.1. A. Data Acquisition

1. **Source Identification:** Scientific documents were collected from public domain sources, including Google Search, OpenAlex, and open-access publications from IEEE Xplore.
2. **Keyword-driven Retrieval:** A comprehensive list of approximately 1,000 robotics-specific keywords was compiled. This initial list was extracted from IEEE Robotics & Automation Letters and subsequently expanded using ChatGPT to generate semantically similar and conceptually related terms. After manual filtering, these keywords were used to query the identified data sources.
3. **Domain Expansion:** Beyond core robotics, publications from related fields such as mechanical and electrical engineering were also collected to ensure interdisciplinary coverage.
4. **Corpus Size:** This acquisition process resulted in a corpus exceeding 1 million documents, encompassing various publication types including conference proceedings, journal articles, and dissertations.
5. **Content Extraction:** The PDFFigures tool was employed to automatically extract images, their associated captions, and the full text from the collected PDF documents. This initial extraction yielded approximately 5 million raw image-text pairs.

#### 3.2. B. Data Filtering

The initial 5 million image-text pairs contained considerable noise and irrelevant data, necessitating a robust filtering process.

1. **Irrelevant Data Filtering:**
 - **Problem:** The extracted data included images not directly related to robot design, such as charts, tables, and human-robot interaction photographs.
 - **Solution:** An image classifier was trained to identify and filter out these irrelevant images. This classifier was an ensemble model utilizing pretrained OpenCLIP and EfficientNetV2 as backbones.
 - **Training Data:** To train the classifier, 32,000 images from the extracted data were manually annotated as either relevant or irrelevant.
 - **Performance:** The classifier achieved over 95% accuracy on a held-out test set before being applied to the full 5 million pairs.
2. **Deduplication:**
 - **Problem:** Duplicate samples, where visually similar images might represent the same design, needed to be identified and removed.
 - **Solution:** An approximate k-Nearest-Neighbor (kNN) method, implemented using Faiss, was applied. Images were embedded using a pretrained OpenCLIP model.
 - **Verification:** To avoid erroneously removing distinct designs that merely appear visually similar, the process incorporated a textual verification step. Only samples exhibiting both high visual similarity (via kNN) and nearly identical textual descriptions were identified as duplicates and removed.
 - **Result:** Following this filtering process, the dataset was refined to approximately 1 million unique image-text pairs relevant to robot design.

#### 3.3. C. Visual Instruction-Following Data Construction

To enhance the dataset's utility for training modern multi-modal learning models, visual instruction-following data (question-answer pairs) were constructed.

1. **Textual Enrichment:**
 - **Problem:** Many extracted captions provided insufficient descriptive detail for the images.
 - **Solution:** An LLM was used to extract "reference texts"—sentences from the full document that explicitly mentioned and described the image.
 - **Hallucination Mitigation:** To counter LLM hallucination, the extracted reference texts were cross-checked with the full document text for validation. The LLM was specifically prompted with short paragraphs containing the image number to reduce hallucination risk.
 - **LLM Selection:** GPT-4o, LLaMa 3.3 70B, and Qwen2.5 72B were experimented with, and LLaMa 3.3 70B was chosen for its balance of generation quality and affordability.
2. **Question-Answer Generation:** The selected LLM was then prompted to generate question-answer pairs based on the enriched captions and the validated reference texts. These pairs were designed to interpret the visual content of the image.
3. **Result:** This process generated approximately 1.3 million question-answer pairs associated with the design images.

#### 3.4. D. RoboDesign1M Statistics

- **Caption Length:** The dataset exhibits varied caption lengths, with an average of 15.14 words per caption, indicating concise yet descriptive textual data.
- **Caption Vocabulary:** After lemmatization, RoboDesign1M contains approximately 113,000 unique words (excluding stop words and numbers), which is substantially larger than comparable datasets like Ghezelbash et al. (16x larger) and Text2CAD (78x larger), suggesting high textual diversity.
- **Visual Content Diversity:** Images in RoboDesign1M cover over 1,000 distinct topics as classified by OpenAlex, and are associated with over 12,000 keywords from robotics and related disciplines. This confirms the dataset's broad visual diversity across numerous robot design categories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of RoboDesign1M was evaluated through extensive experiments across three robot design tasks: Visual Question Answering (VQA), Text-Image Retrieval, and Text-to-Design Image Generation.

#### 4.1. A. Visual Question Answering in Robot Design

- **Setup:** Pretrained LLMs (LlaVa-1.5 and Qwen2-VL) were finetuned on RoboDesign1M, Text2CAD, and Ghezelbash. GPT-4o and un-finetuned LLMs served as baselines. Evaluation metrics included BLEU, METEOR, and L3Score.
- **Results (Table II):**
 - General LLMs (without finetuning) performed poorly across all datasets, indicating a significant domain gap for engineering and robotic design.
 - Finetuning on domain-specific datasets improved performance, but considerable room for enhancement remained.
 - Models finetuned on RoboDesign1M achieved lower absolute metrics compared to those finetuned on Text2CAD or Ghezelbash when tested on their respective in-domain sets. This suggests that RoboDesign1M presents a more challenging learning environment.
- **Cross-dataset VQA Generalization (Table III):**
 - Models finetuned on RoboDesign1M demonstrated superior generalization capabilities, showing improvements of approximately 15-62% when tested on Text2CAD and Ghezelbash datasets compared to models finetuned on other datasets.
 - Qualitative examples (Fig. 5) showed models finetuned on RoboDesign1M providing correct technical answers (e.g., identifying "universal joints" in a delta mechanism) where models finetuned on other datasets provided incorrect or generic responses.

#### 4.2. B. Retrieving Design Images from Text

- **Setup:** A state-of-the-art multimodal encoder (BLIP-2) was finetuned on image-caption pairs from RoboDesign1M, Text2CAD, and Ghezelbash. Recall@k (R@1, R@5, R@10) was used as the evaluation metric.
- **Results (Table IV):**
 - Similar to VQA, models finetuned on RoboDesign1M achieved lower in-domain performance metrics compared to models finetuned on Text2CAD or Ghezelbash, again suggesting RoboDesign1M's challenging nature.
 - However, in cross-dataset testing, models finetuned on RoboDesign1M generally exhibited better generalization, retrieving relevant images from other datasets more effectively.
 - Qualitative results (Fig. 6) illustrated that the model finetuned on RoboDesign1M performed well across various design types, including both CAD images and technical drawings.

#### 4.3. C. Text-To-Design Image Generation

- **Setup:** A pretrained Stable Diffusion XL model was finetuned on image-caption pairs from RoboDesign1M. The Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) metric was used for quantitative evaluation (lower FID indicates better quality). A user study was also conducted.
- **Results (Table V):**
 - Finetuning Stable Diffusion XL on RoboDesign1M resulted in a significant reduction in FID, from 45.83 (un-finetuned) to 39.42, indicating improved generation quality in the robot design domain.
 - Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 7) showed that the finetuned model produced more realistic and technically relevant robot design images, whereas GPT-4o and the original Stable Diffusion XL often generated images with a focus on general aesthetics rather than specific design elements.
- **User Study (Fig. 8):**
 - A user study involving 12 robotics engineers evaluated 100 generated robot designs. Participants rated images based on alignment with input text.
 - The model finetuned on RoboDesign1M received higher ratings for text-image alignment compared to both the original pretrained Stable Diffusion XL and GPT-4o.

#### 4.4. D. Discussion

The experiments collectively demonstrate that RoboDesign1M is effective in supporting robot design tasks. Its diversity across various robotic domains appears to enhance the generalization capabilities of multimodal LLMs, as evidenced by superior cross-dataset performance despite presenting a challenging training environment for in-domain tasks. While RoboDesign1M significantly improves multimodal LLM performance in the robotic domain, the authors note that suboptimal performance levels still highlight a need for further advancements in model architectures to more effectively capture the intricate patterns within robot design data.

**Limitations identified by the authors include:**
1. **Textual Richness vs. Reliability:** The current approach prioritizes reliability by providing LLMs with short, explicit paragraphs for Q&A generation, potentially excluding richer, implicitly related textual descriptions.
2. **Lack of 3D Information:** RoboDesign1M currently lacks 3D information, which is a barrier for design fabrication. Future work could integrate parametric designs, CAD models, or 2D-to-3D conversion techniques.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboDesign1M addresses a critical gap in the field of AI for robotics by providing a large-scale, specialized, and multimodal dataset for robot design understanding. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Advancing AI in Robot Design:** The primary impact is enabling the development and refinement of foundation models specifically tailored for robot design. By providing 1 million high-reliability samples from scientific literature, RoboDesign1M offers the necessary data infrastructure for AI models to learn the complexities of robot morphology, functionality, and interdisciplinary design principles. This could lead to a deeper understanding of robot designs than currently possible with generic datasets.
- **Establishing a New Benchmark:** The dataset serves as a challenging new benchmark for various design understanding tasks, including VQA, text-image retrieval, and text-to-design image generation. This benchmark facilitates rigorous evaluation and comparison of new AI models, pushing the boundaries of research in this domain.
- **Facilitating Automated Design Generation:** With a robust dataset, AI models can be trained to automatically generate novel robot designs based on textual specifications. This could accelerate the design process, reduce human effort, and potentially lead to the discovery of designs that human designers might not conceptualize.
- **Enabling Intelligent Design Assistants:** The visual instruction-following data integrated into RoboDesign1M is particularly impactful for developing AI-powered design assistants or chatbots. These tools could provide insightful feedback, suggest improvements, answer specific design questions, and streamline workflows for engineers and students.
- **Enhancing Cross-modal Design Search:** The multimodal nature of RoboDesign1M, linking images/drawings with rich textual descriptions, can power sophisticated cross-modal search engines. Engineers could retrieve example designs based on functional descriptions, appearance, or specific components, fostering design analogy and inspiring new ideas.
- **Supporting Educational and Research Ecosystems:** By making RoboDesign1M publicly available, the authors contribute to an open research ecosystem, encouraging collaboration and further advancements. Its scientific sourcing ensures high reliability, making it a valuable resource for both academic research and educational purposes in robotics and engineering design curricula.
- **Potential for Downstream Applications:** Beyond the direct applications, RoboDesign1M can be utilized for developing high-quality data filters for other datasets, improving scientific image captioning, and potentially feeding into simulation-based design optimization.

In conclusion, RoboDesign1M is positioned to be a foundational resource that could significantly accelerate the integration of advanced AI into the practical and theoretical aspects of robot design, thereby contributing to the broader field of robotic innovation.
