# Enhancing Continual Learning of Vision-Language Models via Dynamic Prefix Weighting

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Yonsei University developed Dynamic Prefix Weighting (DPW), a framework that enhances continual learning for Vision-Language Models by applying dynamic, token-level weighting to prefixes and adapters. DPW achieved state-of-the-art performance on the Multi-domain Task Incremental Learning benchmark, outperforming existing methods by 2.0% (Avg) and 2.3% (Last) while maintaining parameter efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods for Vision-Language Model (VLM) continual learning apply task-specific information uniformly at the sample level, ignoring varying token relevance.
- Conventional attention mechanisms used in prefix-tuning can blur distinctions between task-relevant and task-irrelevant tokens, hindering fine-grained weighting and reducing task separability.
- Fixed normalization of prefix weights limits the dynamic adjustment of the total amount of task-specific information injected per token, potentially leading to under or over-adaptation.

## Method

- **DPW** introduces a gating module with ==Reparameterized Prefix Attention (RePA)== to replace conventional attention, creating task-specific projections for prefixes that directly capture token relevance.
- A ==Conditional Activation (CondAct)== module processes RePA scores, providing dynamic normalization and filtering of prefix weights to control the total amount and selection of information per token.
- A ==Residual Weighting Mechanism (RWM)== adaptively scales the contribution of an adapter (==LoRA==) based on residual prefix weights, ensuring adapters complement prefixes only when additional adaptation is needed.

## Results

- DPW achieved state-of-the-art performance on the MTIL (Order I) benchmark, with 'Ours' variant (**30.8M** parameters) outperforming GIFT by **2.0%** (Avg. score) and **2.3%** (Last score).
- On the challenging ODCL-CIL benchmark, DPW recorded a **78.6%** Avg. score and **86.6%** Last score, surpassing the next best method DPeCLIP by **2.5%** and **2.0%** respectively.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all components (RePA, CondAct, RWM) contribute to improved performance, with RWM providing the most significant gains across Transfer, Avg., and Last scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained attention query projections can reduce the distinction between task-relevant and task-irrelevant tokens, leading to increased cosine similarity and diminished task separability in feature space.
> - Dynamically adjusting the total information injected via prefixes per token (CondAct) is crucial for preserving zero-shot performance and mitigating forward forgetting in continual learning.
> - Adapters, when guided by a residual weighting mechanism, can effectively complement prefixes by addressing tokens that require further adaptation beyond what prefixes alone provide, enabling orthogonal contributions.
