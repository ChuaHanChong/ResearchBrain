# Multistep Quasimetric Learning for Scalable Goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Multistep Quasimetric Estimation (MQE) framework enables scalable offline Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning by integrating multistep value learning with quasimetric architectures. It demonstrates enhanced performance on long-horizon reasoning tasks in simulated environments and facilitates compositional generalization in real-world robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) methods struggle with long-horizon tasks due to compounding errors in Temporal Difference (TD) learning or convergence issues in Monte Carlo (MC) methods.
- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) finds it difficult to achieve long-horizon combinatorial generalization or 'stitching' from high-dimensional, unlabeled visual data.
- A recognized dichotomy exists between TD (local updates, optimality guarantees) and MC (global updates, practical effectiveness) value estimation, lacking an integrated approach for GCRL.

## Method

- ==Multistep Quasimetric== Estimation (**MQE**) parameterizes Q-functions as inverse exponentials of learned quasimetric distances, enforced by the Metric Residual Network (MRN) architecture.
- It extends Q-iteration to a multistep optimization using 'waypoints' sampled from future states, propagating value information across multiple steps with a Bregman divergence loss.
- An action invariance constraint, enforced by an exponential squared loss, ensures Bellman optimality and stabilizes training dynamics without needing a separate hyperparameter for gradient magnitude regulation.

## Results

- MQE achieved an overall success rate of **36.3%** on OGBench tasks, outperforming baselines like TMD (**13.0%**) and HIQL (**18.7%**), especially on 'colossal' AntMaze tasks (up to 4000 steps).
- It demonstrated strong horizon generalization on `antmaze-*-stitch` datasets, maintaining non-zero success rates in mazes up to **1000%** longer than training horizons where other methods failed.
- In real-world robotic manipulation (BridgeData), MQE enabled compositional generalization, achieving positive success rates on challenging tasks like 'quadruple PnP' (2/15) and 'drawer open and place' (5/15), without explicit hierarchical components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining multistep temporal difference updates with global value propagation through quasimetric architectures effectively addresses long-horizon challenges in offline GCRL.
> - Enforcing action invariance via a specific exponential squared loss is crucial for learning robust quasimetric distances and preventing trivial solutions.
> - Flexible waypoint sampling, balancing short-term and long-term horizons, significantly improves the recovery of structured temporal distance representations necessary for goal-reaching.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL), which aims to enable agents to learn policies for reaching diverse goals in an environment. A significant challenge within GCRL, particularly for modern methods operating in high-dimensional and stochastic settings, is reasoning over long horizons—tasks that require many sequential steps or a complex sequence of sub-goals.

The paper addresses a recognized dichotomy in value estimation methods: Temporal Difference (TD) learning and Monte Carlo (MC) methods. TD methods, which perform local updates, offer theoretical optimality guarantees for Q-functions but often exhibit degraded performance as horizon length increases due to compounding errors. In contrast, MC methods, which perform global updates using multi-step returns, lack such theoretical guarantees for optimal Q-functions but can perform well in practice, especially for longer horizons by propagating value information globally. Existing offline approaches often treat these methods separately.

Previous work in quasimetric distance learning has utilized either single-step TD updates (e.g., Wang et al., 2023) or contrastive learning-based Monte Carlo updates (e.g., Myers et al., 2024). Additionally, while offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown promise for scaling to short-horizon tasks, learning long-horizon tasks, especially those requiring complex reasoning or combinatorial generalization (often termed "stitching"), remains difficult. In robotics, offline RL for generalist policies often faces challenges, leading many to rely on methods like behavior cloning or data curation.

This paper positions its contribution as an integration of these distinct approaches. It proposes a method that leverages the advantages of both local and global value propagation to address the limitations of existing GCRL techniques, specifically in the context of offline learning from unlabeled visual data for long-horizon tasks and real-world robotic manipulation. The work aims to be the first end-to-end offline GCRL method capable of enabling multistep stitching in real-world manipulation by combining multistep TD returns with global value propagation through quasimetric architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and scalable offline Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) method that can effectively tackle long-horizon tasks by accurately estimating temporal distances between observations.

The core motivations and specific objectives driving this work are:

- **Addressing Long-Horizon Reasoning Deficiencies:** Current GCRL methods, whether based on TD learning or Monte Carlo (MC) approaches, exhibit limitations in tasks requiring extensive foresight. TD methods often suffer from compounding errors as the horizon increases, leading to suboptimal performance, while MC methods, despite propagating value globally, may struggle to converge to the optimal temporal distance. The motivation is to find a solution that overcomes these combined challenges.
- **Integrating TD and MC Advantages:** The research is motivated by the observation that both TD (local updates, optimality guarantees) and MC (global updates, practical effectiveness) methods possess distinct strengths. The objective is to integrate these approaches into a single, practical offline GCRL framework that performs both local and global value propagation concurrently.
- **Learning Quasimetric Distances:** The paper aims to fit a quasimetric distance function, which formally captures the concept of "temporal distance" or "progress" towards a goal. This is motivated by the human ability to instinctively use distance-like ideas to represent task progress and plan shortest routes (stitching).
- **Enabling Scalable "Stitching" and Combinatorial Generalization:** A key motivation is to enable agents to decompose complex problems into simpler sub-problems, allowing them to combine learned sub-tasks in sequence (stitching) or generalize combinatorially in novel environments. This capability is crucial for advanced AI agents and is difficult for current methods, particularly in high-dimensional, stochastic settings.
- **Enhancing Horizon Generalization:** The work seeks to develop a method that allows the learned policy to generalize effectively to task horizons significantly longer than those encountered during training.
- **Robustness to Noisy Data:** Developing a stable method that can extract reliable goal-reaching policies even from noisy offline datasets is a significant practical motivation, especially for real-world applications where data quality can vary.
- **Applicability to Real-World Robotics:** A specific objective is to apply the method to real-world robotic manipulation problems, demonstrating its ability to facilitate complex behaviors without requiring extensive additional design choices or explicit hierarchical structures. This includes achieving end-to-end learning from unlabeled offline datasets of visual observations.

In essence, the paper seeks to bridge the gap between theoretical optimality guarantees of local updates and the practical effectiveness of global value propagation, thereby enhancing the scalability and generalization capabilities of offline GCRL for complex, long-horizon problems, particularly in robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Multistep Quasimetric Estimation (MQE), is an offline Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) framework designed to integrate multistep value learning with quasimetric architectures. The approach is structured into three main components: multistep backup under a quasimetric architecture, enforcing action invariance for value learning, and practical policy extraction.

**Quasimetric Distance Representations:**
MQE utilizes quasimetric architectures to parameterize its value functions. It defines the goal-conditioned Q-function, `Q_g(s,a)`, and value function, `V_g(s)`, as inverse exponential functions of learned quasimetric distances: `Q_g(s,a) = V_g(g)e^(-d((s,a),g))` and `V_g(s) = V_g(g)e^(-d(s,g))`. This formulation implies that a smaller distance corresponds to a higher Q-value.
To enforce the quasimetric properties (non-negativity, identity, and triangle inequality), MQE employs the Metric Residual Network (MRN) architecture (Liu et al., 2023). MRN parameterizes the distance `d_MRN(x, y)` as a sum of asymmetric (ReLU-based) and symmetric (L2-norm-based) components of differences between learned embeddings `x` and `y`. The method learns state representations `ψ(s)` and state-action representations `φ(s, a)`, and then defines distances as `d((s,a),g) = d_MRN(φ(s,a), ψ(g))` and `d(s,g) = d_MRN(ψ(s), ψ(g))`.

**Multistep Returns with Quasimetric Architecture:**
MQE extends the concept of one-step Q-iteration to a multistep optimization procedure. Instead of only considering the immediate next state `s'`, it leverages "waypoints" `s_w_t` sampled from future states along a trajectory. The waypoint `s_w_t` is sampled from `s_{t+k'}` where `k'` follows a combined Bernoulli and geometric distribution: `k' ~ min(Geom(1-λ), K)` with probability `1-p`, and `k'=1` with probability `p`. This allows for flexible sampling of short-term (p=1 implies one-step) and longer-term waypoints.
The core objective is modified to `e^(-d((s,a),g)) <- E[γ^(k') * e^(-d(s_w_t,g))]`, which is denoted as `T_β`. This objective propagates value information across multiple steps. To optimize this, MQE uses a Bregman divergence with LINEX losses, `D_T(d, d') = exp(d - d') - d'`, which is selected to prevent vanishing gradients when distances become similar. This results in the loss function `L_Tβ`, which minimizes the divergence between the current state-action distance to the goal and the discounted waypoint-to-goal distance.

**Learning Value Functions via Action Invariance:**
To enforce the Bellman optimality property, `V_g*(s) = max_a Q_g*(s,a)`, MQE introduces an action invariance constraint. Under its construction, this property is satisfied if `d(ψ(s), φ(s,a)) = 0` for all actions `a`. This constraint is enforced by minimizing `L_I = (e^(-d(ψ(s_i),φ(s_i,a_j))) - 1)^2`. This exponential squared loss ensures that the enforcement becomes less strict as the deviation decreases, which helps prevent trivial solutions (e.g., all embeddings collapsing to zero) and stabilizes training dynamics without needing a separate hyperparameter for gradient magnitude regulation.

**Policy Extraction:**
The goal-conditioned policy `π(s, g)` is extracted using a behavior-regularized deep-deterministic policy gradient (DDPG+BC) approach. The policy objective `L_μ` minimizes `d((s, π(s, g)), g) - α log π(a | s, g)`. By minimizing the learned distance, the policy is implicitly trained to maximize the corresponding Q-value. The behavior cloning (BC) term `α log π(a | s, g)` regularizes the policy towards the observed actions in the dataset.

**Implementation Details and Algorithm:**
MQE is implemented without explicit hierarchical components. It learns a single critic `Q` (composed of `φ` and `ψ` networks) and a single goal-reaching policy `π_μ`. The overall algorithm iteratively updates `Q` using both `L_Tβ` (multistep backup) and `L_I` (action invariance), and then updates `π_μ` using `L_μ`. Waypoints are randomly sampled.

**Theoretical Analysis:**
The paper provides a theoretical result (Theorem 1) demonstrating that in a tabular setting with full support over state-action pairs by the behavior policy `π_β`, the extracted policy `π` from MQE satisfies `V_π(s) ≥ V_πβ(s)` for all states `s` and goals `g`. This implies policy improvement over the behavior policy, achieved by ensuring the learned distance `d` is less than or equal to the behavior-induced successor distance `d_πβ_SD`, and that the quasimetric projection operator `P` preserves this property.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated MQE's capabilities in horizon and compositional generalization across simulated benchmarks (OGBench) and real-world robotic manipulation tasks (BridgeData).

**Simulated Evaluation on Offline Goal-Reaching Tasks (OGBench):**
- **Overall Performance:** MQE demonstrated an overall success rate of 36.3% across a set of 90 challenging OGBench evaluation tasks, significantly outperforming baselines such as TMD (13.0%), HIQL (18.7%), CMD (8.7%), QRL (9.8%), GCIQL (11.8%), CRL (6.2%), and n-SAC+BC (8.5%).
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** On tasks requiring extensive reasoning, such as the newly introduced "colossal"-sized AntMaze (50% larger than "giant" mazes, up to 4000 steps), MQE exhibited superior performance. For instance, in `humanoidmaze-giant-stitch`, MQE showed a success rate of 26.5%, a substantial improvement over the previous best methods (e.g., HIQL at 3.3%, n-SAC+BC at 1.7%).
- **Horizon Generalization:** MQE maintained strong horizon generalization on `antmaze-*-stitch` datasets, where agents were trained on 4-meter trajectories but evaluated on mazes up to 1000% longer. MQE was the only method that showed non-zero success rates in colossal mazes under these conditions.
- **Visual Observations:** MQE also demonstrated strong performance with visual observations, although HIQL achieved higher success rates in some visual-based manipulation tasks (e.g., `visual_scene_play`, `visual_cube_double_noisy`, `visual_puzzle_4x4_play`).
- **Learned Distance Visualization:** Visualizations of learned distances `d(s, g)` in `antmaze-large-explore` indicated that MQE recovered a desired, structured distance representation, which smoothly propagated from the goal. This contrasted with other baselines, which often showed L2-like features, visual artifacts, or limited value propagation regions.

**Real-World Evaluation of MQE (BridgeData):**
- **Compositional Generalization (Stitching):** MQE was evaluated on multi-stage manipulation tasks, including sequences of up to four "Pick and Place" (PnP) actions and "Open Drawer and Place" (requiring sequential dependencies). The dataset did not contain explicit examples of these composite tasks.
- **Task Progress:** MQE maintained a relatively high task progress as the number of consecutive tasks increased, while the performance of GCIQL and TRA significantly regressed. For single-stage tasks (Single PnP, Open Drawer), MQE showed competitive or superior performance compared to GCBC, GCIQL, and TRA.
- **Challenging Tasks:** On the two most difficult tasks, "quadruple PnP" and "drawer open and place," MQE recorded positive success rates (2/15 and 5/15 respectively). TRA showed some success (1/15) on "drawer open and place," but other methods (GCBC, GCIQL) did not achieve any successes on these tasks. This demonstrated MQE's ability to compose multiple primitives without external guidance, which is challenging for end-to-end policies in this domain.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Action Invariance Objective (`L_I`):** The ablation study confirmed that `L_I` is integral to MQE's success. Without `L_I`, the success rate dropped significantly (26.5% with `L_I` vs. 7.9% without). Using expectile loss for value learning, as an alternative, also yielded lower success rates (11.3% for κ=0.7, 8.8% for κ=0.9).
- **Waypoint Sampling Distribution:** Sampling `k'` for waypoints from the combined Bernoulli and geometric distribution (Eq. 8) yielded the best performance (26.5% success rate). Alternatives such as a pure geometric distribution (22.1%) or uniform distributions (18.9% for `Unif[1, K]`, 17.8% for `Unif`) resulted in lower success rates. Fixed intervals (e.g., `k'=50` or `k'=1`) performed poorly (1.7% and 0% respectively).
- **Hyperparameters `λ` and `p`:** Analysis of `(p, λ)` pairs suggested that both hyperparameters for waypoint sampling need to be relatively high. A high `λ` promotes sampling distant waypoints for faster value propagation, while a high `p` ensures that local consistencies are maintained. The study also indicated that `T` (one-step backup with `p=1`) alone is insufficient for learning effective distance representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Multistep Quasimetric Estimation (MQE) method represents an advancement in Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL), particularly for tasks requiring long-horizon reasoning and compositional generalization in offline settings.

- **Addressing Long-Horizon Challenges:** MQE directly addresses a significant limitation of existing offline RL methods, which often struggle with long horizons due to cumulative errors or difficulties in global value propagation. By effectively integrating multistep returns with quasimetric distance learning, the method demonstrates improved performance on tasks requiring up to 4000 steps, a notable increase in task complexity. This suggests a potential for agents to learn and execute more complex, multi-stage behaviors.

- **Enabling Compositional Generalization ("Stitching"):** The successful application of MQE to real-world robotic manipulation tasks, particularly those requiring the "stitching" of multiple learned primitives into longer, novel sequences (e.g., quadruple Pick and Place, Open Drawer and Place), indicates progress towards more generalist robot policies. This capability, achieved from unlabeled offline data and without explicit hierarchical components, is important for developing adaptable robots that can combine basic skills to solve intricate problems.

- **Robustness and Practicality:** The method's demonstrated stability with noisy data and its applicability in real-world robotic environments without extensive domain-specific engineering or additional design choices (such as explicit hierarchies) contribute to its practical relevance. This suggests that MQE could be a valuable tool for extracting useful policies from large, diverse, and often noisy datasets collected in real-world scenarios.

- **Integration of Learning Paradigms:** MQE's approach of combining the advantages of Temporal Difference (TD) learning (local consistency, optimality guarantees) and Monte Carlo (MC) methods (global value propagation) within a quasimetric framework offers a new direction for value estimation in offline RL. The theoretical result on policy improvement over the behavior policy in tabular settings provides a foundation for understanding its performance.

- **Potential for Broader Impact:** The principles underlying MQE, such as flexible multistep backups and robust enforcement of action invariance in quasimetric spaces, could be extended to other areas of reinforcement learning. Future work might explore its application in offline-to-online RL, online RL, or even in contexts beyond traditional action-chunking policies, potentially leading to more efficient and scalable learning algorithms for various autonomous systems. The ability to learn robust temporal distances from visual observations also has implications for vision-based control and planning.
