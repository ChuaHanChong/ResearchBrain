# R2RGEN: Real-to-Real 3D Data Generation for Spatially Generalized Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> R2RGen presents a real-to-real 3D data generation framework for robotic manipulation that efficiently creates spatially diverse training data from minimal human demonstrations, enhancing visuomotor policies' generalization across object placements and robot viewpoints. It achieved an average success rate of 40.3% with one source demonstration, outperforming single-source policies (3.4%) and demonstrating robust cross-camera and mobile manipulation capabilities.

## Problem

- Visuomotor policies for robotic manipulation require extensive human demonstrations to achieve spatial generalization, particularly for mobile manipulators and varying viewpoints, leading to high data collection costs.
- Simulation-based data generation methods suffer from the sim-to-real gap, often necessitating additional real-world data for successful deployment.
- Existing real-to-real 3D data generation techniques are limited by restrictions to fixed-base scenarios, specific camera types, rigid input assumptions (e.g., cropped point clouds, limited objects), and susceptibility to visual mismatch during large augmentations.

## Method

- **R2RGen** implements a three-stage, simulator-free and rendering-free pipeline involving ==Source Demonstration Pre-processing==, ==Group-wise Data Augmentation==, and ==Camera-aware 3D Post-processing==.
- The framework employs template-based 3D object tracking (FoundationPose) for scene parsing and object point cloud completion in World Coordinate System (WCS), addressing inherent sensor data incompleteness.
- A ==group-wise backtracking== augmentation strategy generates new demonstrations by transforming object groups, environments, and robot bases while preserving causal relationships and spatial constraints, followed by camera-aware post-processing to align augmented point clouds with real sensor data distributions.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **40.3%** with one source demonstration across 8 manipulation tasks, significantly outperforming single-source (**3.4%**) and DemoGen-augmented policies (**16.7%**).
- Demonstrated robust cross-camera generalization, achieving **31.3%** to **50.0%** success in exterior-wrist setups and **28.1%** to **40.6%** in wrist-wrist settings with a single source demonstration.
- Successfully generalized to non-rigid object tasks (e.g., **53.1%** success on Cover-Object) and improved appearance generalization, in some cases surpassing policies trained with **40** human demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Completing object point clouds in WCS and combining them with environment and robot arm point clouds before augmentation is crucial for generating realistic 3D observations across diverse viewpoints.
> - A group-wise backtracking augmentation approach, where skills are associated with object groups and transformations are applied from the end backward, effectively preserves spatial and causal task constraints, enabling generalization for multi-object and bimanual tasks.
> - Camera-aware 3D post-processing, specifically projection with Z-buffering, is essential for transforming complete augmented 3D point clouds into sensor-realistic, incomplete, and view-dependent observations that match real RGB-D camera distributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly when utilizing visuomotor policies trained through imitation learning, has made progress; however, it remains significantly challenged by the substantial data requirements necessary for achieving generalized performance. A primary bottleneck is spatial generalization, which necessitates that a robot policy operates robustly despite variations in object placement, environmental layout, and the robot's own position or viewpoint. This challenge is amplified in advanced settings, such as those involving mobile manipulators, where viewpoint changes introduce additional data demand.

Prior research has explored data generation as a strategy to mitigate this data scarcity, aiming to synthesize diverse training data from a limited number of human demonstrations. Approaches like MimicGen and its successors augment object configurations and generate trajectories in simulation. While these methods can achieve reasonable performance in simulated environments, they often encounter the sim-to-real gap during real-world deployment, requiring time-consuming on-robot rollouts to collect real-world observation-action pairs.

More recent work, such as DemoGen, introduced a 3D-based, simulator- and rendering-free data generation method that operates directly on point cloud inputs. This paradigm aims to be efficient and to circumvent the sim-to-real gap. However, DemoGen exhibited several limitations, including restrictions to fixed-base scenarios with exterior cameras, specific input data assumptions (e.g., cropped environment point clouds, limited object counts, single-target object skills), and susceptibility to visual mismatch when substantial augmentation was applied.

R2RGen builds upon this 3D-based, real-to-real data generation paradigm by addressing the identified limitations of previous methods. It seeks to provide a more generalizable and practical framework that can accommodate mobile manipulators, diverse camera setups (exterior and wrist-mounted), raw point cloud observations without restrictive pre-processing, arbitrary numbers of objects, and complex interaction modes. The work positions itself as an advancement in enabling spatially generalized robotic manipulation through data augmentation directly in the 3D domain, thereby reducing the dependency on extensive human demonstrations and bridging gaps in existing techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a real-to-real 3D data generation framework, termed R2RGen, that enables visuomotor policies to achieve strong spatial generalization in robotic manipulation tasks. This framework aims to efficiently generate diverse, realistic point cloud observation-action pairs from minimal human-collected demonstrations, thereby reducing the significant data demands typically associated with imitation learning.

The motivation for R2RGen stems from several challenges in current robotic manipulation research:

- **High Data Requirements for Spatial Generalization:** Visuomotor policies often require a large volume of human demonstrations to cover a sufficient range of object spatial configurations and robot base positions. This is particularly true for complex tasks and mobile manipulation scenarios, where varying viewpoints further increase data demand. The collection of such extensive datasets is labor-intensive and costly, constituting a bottleneck for broader application and scaling of robotic systems.
- **Limitations of Simulation-based Data Generation:** While methods like MimicGen generate diverse trajectories in simulation, they suffer from the sim-to-real gap. Policies trained purely in simulation often struggle when deployed in the real world due to discrepancies between simulated and real physics, rendering, and sensor noise. Bridging this gap typically requires additional real-world data collection, negating some of the efficiency benefits.
- **Constraints of Existing Real-to-Real Data Generation:** Previous 3D-based real-to-real approaches, such as DemoGen, provided a simulator-free and rendering-free pipeline, which is efficient and avoids the sim-to-real gap. However, these methods had significant limitations:
 - **Fixed-Base and Camera Restrictions:** They were typically restricted to fixed-base robots and fixed exterior cameras, preventing generalization to mobile manipulators or scenarios involving wrist-mounted cameras and associated viewpoint changes.
 - **Strict Input Assumptions:** DemoGen required cropped point clouds of the environment, supported only a limited number of objects, and assumed that each skill involved only one target object. These assumptions limit applicability to more complex, multi-object, or bimanual tasks.
 - **Visual Mismatch Problem:** Large spatial augmentations in prior 3D data generation could lead to unrealistic point cloud observations, where expected points were missing or occluded points were visible, deviating from real-world sensor data distribution.
- **Need for Efficiency and Practicality:** There is a need for a data generation framework that is plug-and-play, efficient, and directly compatible with raw 3D sensor data, reducing setup complexity and manual intervention.

By addressing these issues, R2RGen seeks to enhance data efficiency, improve the spatial generalization capabilities of visuomotor policies across diverse tasks and platforms, and facilitate the deployment of robotic manipulation systems in varied real-world environments with minimal human effort.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

R2RGen proposes a three-stage, simulator-free and rendering-free pipeline for real-to-real 3D data generation. The framework takes a single human-collected source demonstration and augments it to produce diverse observation-action pairs, which are then used to train a spatially generalized 3D visuomotor policy. The selected visuomotor policy, iDP3, consumes egocentric point clouds and proprioception state without requiring camera pose calibration. The method operates under two main assumptions: the visuomotor policy only controls the robotic arm (mobile base is fixed during task execution), and actions are represented as a sequence of continuous end-effector poses and discrete gripper states.

The R2RGen pipeline follows a sequence of transformations: RoCS_DC (Data Collection Robot-Observation Coordinate System) → WCS (World Coordinate System) → WCS (Augmented) → RoCS_DP (Deployment Robot-Observation Coordinate System).

**A. Source Demonstration Pre-processing (SP)**
This initial stage prepares the raw source demonstration for augmentation by parsing both 3D observations and action trajectories.

- **Scene Parsing:** Given K relevant objects in a task, the point cloud observation for each timestamp is segmented into K object point clouds, one environment point cloud, and the robot’s arm point cloud. To address the inherent incompleteness of RGB-D sensor data (e.g., occluded sides of objects), the system completes the object point clouds using a template-based 3D object tracking system (FoundationPose). This system tracks objects and generates complete 3D models of objects in the WCS. Point clouds are converted to WCS using either a fixed transformation for an exterior camera or the inverse of the end-effector pose for a wrist camera. A complete environment point cloud is acquired by scanning the environment in WCS without objects. The robot's arm point cloud is generated based on robot dynamics and the measured base position/orientation.
- **Trajectory Parsing:** A lightweight annotation system is used. An annotator labels the start and end frames of skill segments in an RGB video. The segments between skills are classified as motion segments. For each skill, target object IDs and in-hand object IDs are specified. This process requires minimal time and effort.

**B. Group-wise Data Augmentation (Aug)**
This stage generates new demonstrations by augmenting the spatial configurations of objects, the environment, and the robot's base, along with corresponding action trajectories. This addresses the limitation of previous methods that struggled with multi-object interactions.

- **Group-wise Backtracking:** Instead of treating skills as single-object-centric, R2RGen assigns each skill to a "group" of objects, comprising both target and in-hand objects relevant to that skill. Augmentations are performed in a backtracking manner (from the last skill to the first) to preserve causal relationships and spatial constraints. For each skill, a random transformation (XY-plane translation and Z-axis rotation) is applied to its associated object group if the group does not intersect with the "fixed object set" (objects whose positions are constrained by subsequent skills). After augmenting a group, the fixed object set is updated to include the current group's objects (excluding any in-hand objects, which are considered released for prior skills).
- **Skill Augmentation:** For an augmentable skill, the sampled transformation is applied to the end-effector's pose and the point clouds of the objects within its group, while the gripper state remains constant.
- **Motion Augmentation:** For motion segments between skills, motion planning is employed to generate a trajectory that smoothly connects the augmented end-effector poses of the preceding and succeeding skill segments. Point clouds of in-hand objects during motion are transformed based on the relative pose change of the end-effector.
- **Base Augmentation:** The robot’s base position and orientation are augmented using a transformation to simulate viewpoint changes. The complete robot arm point cloud is then generated using Inverse Kinematics (IK) to determine joint angles for the augmented end-effector pose relative to the augmented base, converting the robot's URDF model to a point cloud.
- **Final Augmented Pointcloud:** The augmented point cloud for each timestamp is a combination of the complete environment, augmented arm, and augmented object point clouds.
- **Bimanual Task Handling:** For bimanual tasks, specific constraints are introduced to ensure the spatial relationship between the two arms is maintained when they jointly manipulate an object.

**C. Camera-aware 3D Post-processing (CAP)**
This final stage ensures that the augmented 3D point clouds resemble real RGB-D sensor observations, aligning their distribution for effective policy deployment.

- **Coordinate Transformation:** The augmented point clouds in WCS are first transformed into the RoCS_DP (camera coordinate system during deployment). This involves applying inverse transformations for exterior cameras (accounting for base augmentation) or the augmented end-effector pose for wrist cameras.
- **Distribution Adjustment:** Raw RGB-D observations are typically incomplete and view-dependent. The augmented point clouds, being completed in WCS, are over-complete from a single viewpoint. To address this, the transformed point clouds undergo a series of operations:
 - **Projection:** The 3D point cloud is projected onto an image plane using camera intrinsics.
 - **Crop:** Pixels outside the image boundaries are removed.
 - **Z-buffer:** Overlapping pixels are processed to keep only the one with the smallest depth value, effectively removing hidden points. A patch-wise Z-buffer operator is used to account for point cloud density.
 - **Fill:** Empty pixels near the image boundary, which might appear due to base augmentation, are filled. This can be achieved by either shrinking the image size (and applying this to real observations during deployment) or expanding the environment point cloud.
 - **Inverse Projection:** The processed pixels are projected back to the camera coordinate system, yielding an adjusted point cloud that matches the distribution of a real RGB-D camera.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of R2RGen was evaluated through extensive real-world experiments on multiple robotic platforms and diverse tasks.

**A. One-shot Imitation Learning (Table I & II)**
- **Performance with 1 Source Demonstration:** When trained with only one human demonstration, visuomotor policies typically exhibit limited generalization. R2RGen significantly improved performance, consistently outperforming policies trained on a single source demonstration and those augmented by DemoGen across 8 tasks (2 simple, 4 complex, 2 bimanual) in an exterior-exterior camera setup. For example, R2RGen achieved 50.0% success on "Open-Jar" compared to 3.1% for 1 Source and 18.8% for +DemoGen. Overall, R2RGen reached an average success rate of 40.3% compared to 3.4% (1 Source) and 16.7% (DemoGen on tested tasks).
- **Comparison to Human Demonstrations:** R2RGen, with one source demonstration, achieved performance levels comparable to policies trained with 25 human demonstrations, and in some complex tasks, it surpassed the performance of policies trained with 40 human demonstrations. This indicates R2RGen's effectiveness in generating high-quality, spatially diverse data.
- **Cross-Camera Generalization:** R2RGen demonstrated robust performance in challenging exterior-wrist and wrist-wrist camera setups (Table II). While naive cross-camera deployment (training on exterior, testing on wrist) resulted in failure, R2RGen achieved success rates of 31.3% to 50.0% on complex single-arm tasks in exterior-wrist and 28.1% to 40.6% in wrist-wrist settings with a single source demonstration. This highlights its ability to bridge domain gaps through 3D point cloud transformations.

**B. Performance-annotation Tradeoff (Figure 4)**
- Experiments on scaling R2RGen with more source demonstrations (1, 2, 3, and 5) showed that the success rate of the policy gradually saturates as the number of generated demonstrations increases. This saturation is attributed to the capacity of the iDP3 policy's PointNet encoder, suggesting that larger 3D backbones might be required for further performance gains.
- However, a notable observation was that increasing the number of source demonstrations led to a higher saturation performance, indicating that R2RGen effectively leverages additional human-provided data to improve its overall generalization capabilities.

**C. Ablation Studies (Table III & IV)**
- **Point Cloud Processing:** Removing critical components of the point cloud processing pipeline (e.g., object point cloud completion, environment point cloud, or robot arm point cloud) significantly degraded performance. Removing completion led to unrealistic data under large augmentations, removing environment point clouds reduced robustness to viewpoint changes, and removing arm point clouds severely impacted success (e.g., from 50.0% to 15.6% on Place-Bottle).
- **Camera-aware Processing:** Each component of the camera-aware post-processing (Crop, Z-buffer, Fill) was found to be critical. Removing any of these operations led to a substantial drop in success rate (e.g., from 50.0% to 15.6% for removing Z-buffer on Place-Bottle), confirming their role in aligning generated data with real sensor distributions.

**D. Extensions and Applications (Table V & Figure 5, 6)**
- **Non-rigid Objects:** R2RGen demonstrated generalizability to tasks involving non-rigid objects by integrating alternative 3D object completion models like SAM 3D. In tasks like "Store-Box" (articulated object) and "Cover-Object" (deformable object), R2RGen with one source demonstration achieved 31.3% and 53.1% success rates, respectively, outperforming policies trained on up to 40 human demonstrations for these specific challenges.
- **Appearance Generalization:** The spatial generalization enabled by R2RGen was shown to facilitate appearance generalization. For a "Place-Bottle" task with four distinct bottle-base appearance combinations, R2RGen, using only one demonstration per appearance (4 total), achieved a 43.8% success rate, outperforming a policy trained with 40 human demonstrations (10 per appearance combination), which achieved 25.0%.
- **Mobile Manipulation:** R2RGen enabled robust manipulation with mobile robots. By combining a navigation system with a manipulation policy trained via R2RGen, the robot successfully generalized to different docking points and viewpoints (up to 5cm deviation) without requiring specific camera calibration for raw RGB-D observations, which is impractical for real-world mobile scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

R2RGen represents a development in data generation for robotic manipulation, addressing several limitations present in prior work. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Data Efficiency and Reduced Human Labor:** The framework's ability to generate large volumes of high-quality, spatially diverse training data from a single human demonstration significantly reduces the human effort and time traditionally required for data collection in imitation learning. This directly addresses a major bottleneck in scaling robotic manipulation systems.
- **Robust Spatial Generalization:** R2RGen enables visuomotor policies to exhibit improved spatial generalization, allowing robots to perform tasks reliably despite variations in object positions, orientations, and the robot's own viewpoint. This capability is fundamental for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments.
- **Broader Applicability and Versatility:** By supporting mobile manipulators, various camera setups (exterior and wrist-mounted), raw sensor inputs, arbitrary numbers of objects, and diverse interaction modes (including bimanual tasks), R2RGen expands the practical applicability of data generation techniques. It moves beyond the constrained settings of many existing methods.
- **Overcoming the Sim-to-Real Gap:** As a simulator-free and rendering-free real-to-real framework, R2RGen inherently avoids the sim-to-real gap, which often hinders the deployment of simulation-trained policies in physical environments. This contributes to more direct and reliable transfer of learned skills.
- **Foundation for Multi-modal Generalization:** The work demonstrates that strong spatial generalization, facilitated by R2RGen, can serve as a foundation for achieving other forms of generalization, such as appearance generalization. This suggests a potential for building more comprehensive and adaptable robotic policies with reduced data for combined challenges.
- **Increased Practicality for Real-World Deployment:** The methodology's compatibility with raw RGB-D observations and its ability to handle viewpoint changes without requiring complex camera calibration make it more practical for integration into real-world robotic systems, particularly for mobile manipulation scenarios where precise docking is not always guaranteed.
- **Scalability and Extensibility:** R2RGen's modular design, including its ability to integrate different vision foundation models for object completion (e.g., SAM 3D for non-rigid objects), indicates its potential for future scalability and adaptation to a broader range of manipulation challenges and object types.

In conclusion, R2RGen contributes a robust and flexible data generation framework that could accelerate the development and deployment of generalized robotic manipulation capabilities by making imitation learning more data-efficient and practical across a wider spectrum of real-world scenarios.
