# Perception Test: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Multimodal Video Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Perception Test introduces a novel multimodal video benchmark diagnostically evaluating general perception and reasoning skills in advanced AI models using purposefully designed real-world videos. It reveals a substantial performance gap, with humans achieving 91.4% accuracy on multiple-choice video QA, while state-of-the-art models like fine-tuned SeViLA reach only 62.0%.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks are too specialized, failing to holistically evaluate the complex, multimodal capabilities of new general AI models.
- Prior evaluation methods often focus on in-dataset performance, inadequately assessing models' true generalization and transfer capabilities to novel situations.
- Few benchmarks genuinely define tasks requiring deep integration and reasoning across video, audio, and text modalities simultaneously.

## Method

- Created **The Perception Test**, a ==diagnostic benchmark== featuring 11,620 real-world, purposefully designed videos across 37 scripted scenarios to probe specific ==cognitive skills== (Memory, Abstraction, Physics, Semantics) and ==reasoning types== (descriptive, explanatory, predictive, counterfactual).
- Developed dense, ==multi-modal annotations== for each video, including ==object tracks==, ==point tracks==, ==temporal action and sound segments==, and two types of video question-answering (==mc-vQA== and ==g-vQA==).
- Designed an evaluation regime emphasizing ==zero-shot, few-shot, or limited fine-tuning== to assess ==transfer capabilities==, providing public data splits and a challenge server.

## Results

- A significant performance gap exists between humans (**91.4%** accuracy on mc-vQA) and state-of-the-art AI models, with fine-tuned SeViLA achieving **62.0%** and Flamingo-3B reaching **45.8%**.
- Current models often perform below simple baselines in key skill areas like Memory, Physics, and Abstraction, and struggle with complex reasoning types such as counterfactuals.
- Qualitative analysis shows models tend to 'latch on visible elements' for counterfactuals, and struggle with nuanced action understanding and precise spatial-temporal grounding for language queries.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The benchmark's design, inspired by cognitive science, moves beyond computational tasks to diagnose deeper cognitive skills (Memory, Abstraction, Physics, Semantics) and reasoning types (e.g., counterfactuals).
> - It acts as a diagnostic tool, pinpointing specific weaknesses in current multimodal models, particularly in intuitive physics, abstract reasoning, and nuanced understanding of actions.
> - The focus on generalization in evaluation is crucial for fostering the development of robust AI systems that can effectively transfer learned knowledge to novel, real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research landscape in AI has witnessed a transformative shift towards large-scale multimodal models, such as DeepMind's own Flamingo, Google's SeViLA, and OpenAI's GPT-4. These models exhibit remarkable versatility, capable of processing and reasoning across diverse data sources like images, video, audio, and text, often with only a handful of examples (few-shot learning). This marks a departure from earlier, highly specialized computer vision models (e.g., image classifiers, object detectors, action recognizers) that were trained and evaluated on narrow, task-specific benchmarks.

While existing benchmarks like ImageNet for image classification, Kinetics for video action recognition, Audioset for audio event classification, and VQA for image question-answering have driven immense progress in their respective domains, they are increasingly insufficient for evaluating the holistic capabilities of these new general multimodal models. The authors correctly point out several limitations of prior work:
1. **Specialization:** Most benchmarks target restricted aspects of perception, neglecting crucial dimensions like temporal dynamics (image benchmarks), nuanced scene understanding (basic VQA), or cognitive reasoning skills.
2. **Incompleteness for General Models:** Attempting to evaluate general models by "gluing together" multiple specialized datasets is computationally expensive and still misses important abilities like physics understanding or memory across modalities.
3. **Lack of Multimodality:** Few benchmarks genuinely define tasks across *both* audio and visual modalities, let alone complex combinations with text.
4. **In-Dataset Focus:** Many prior benchmarks provide large training sets, primarily evaluating a model's in-dataset capabilities rather than its ability to generalize to novel situations.

The Perception Test directly addresses these gaps by proposing a diagnostic benchmark that is explicitly designed to:
- **Evaluate general perception and reasoning:** It moves beyond computational tasks to probe underlying "skills" and "types of reasoning."
- **Integrate multiple modalities:** It covers video, audio, and text, requiring models to fuse information from all three.
- **Focus on transfer capabilities:** By offering a small training set and emphasizing zero-shot/few-shot or limited fine-tuning, it aims to assess true generalization and robustness, thereby preventing benchmark overfitting.
- **Draw inspiration from cognitive science:** The design principles are informed by diagnostic synthetic datasets (CATER, CLEVRER), behavioral tests (Visual Turing Test), and developmental psychology experiments, signaling a move towards assessing AI systems in a manner akin to evaluating human cognitive abilities.

In essence, the Perception Test positions itself as a next-generation benchmark, crucial for advancing the development of generalist AI models by providing a more comprehensive, diagnostic, and challenging evaluation framework that reflects the complexities of real-world perception and cognition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this work is to introduce **"The Perception Test" – a novel multimodal video benchmark** specifically designed to comprehensively evaluate the perception and reasoning skills of advanced pre-trained multimodal models (e.g., Flamingo, SeViLA, GPT-4).

The authors are motivated by the recognition that while large-scale multimodal models are demonstrating impressive versatility, the existing evaluation landscape has not kept pace. The key motivations can be broken down into several specific goals:

1. **Comprehensive Skill Assessment:** To move beyond mere "computational tasks" (like classification or detection) and probe deeper cognitive "skills" required for true understanding. These skills are categorized into:
 - **Memory:** E.g., visual discrimination, change detection, sequencing, event recall.
 - **Abstraction:** E.g., object/event counting, feature matching, pattern discovery/breaking.
 - **Physics:** E.g., object permanence, spatial relations, object attributes, motion, solidity, conservation, stability.
 - **Semantics:** E.g., understanding distractor actions/objects, task completion (including adversarial actions), object/action/sound recognition, state recognition, general knowledge, and language grounding.
2. **Diverse Reasoning Evaluation:** To assess different "types of reasoning" crucial for human-like intelligence, including:
 - **Descriptive:** What is happening?
 - **Explanatory:** Why is it happening?
 - **Predictive:** What will happen next?
 - **Counterfactual:** What would have happened if...?
3. **Multimodal Integration:** To necessitate models that can effectively integrate and reason across video, audio, and text modalities, addressing a significant limitation of many unimodal or loosely coupled multimodal benchmarks.
4. **Robust Generalization Evaluation:** To provide a robust and efficient evaluation tool that focuses on a model's transfer capabilities in zero-shot, few-shot, or limited fine-tuning settings. This aims to prevent benchmark-specific overfitting and truly assess how well models generalize to new, unseen scenarios, reflecting real-world utility.
5. **Diagnostic Insights:** To serve as a diagnostic tool that can identify specific strengths and weaknesses of current models, helping researchers understand *why* models succeed or fail, and pinpoint areas for future improvement. The dense, multi-faceted annotations per video are designed to facilitate this deeper analysis.
6. **Addressing the Human-AI Gap:** To highlight the substantial gap between human performance and state-of-the-art AI models in complex perception and reasoning tasks, thereby setting ambitious targets for future research.

In summary, the paper aims to provide a much-needed, holistic "stress test" for the next generation of AI models, shifting the focus from narrow task performance to a more profound understanding of perception and reasoning abilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology revolves around the creation of the Perception Test dataset and a multi-faceted evaluation framework.

#### 3.1. Dataset Design and Collection (Videos)

- **Real-World, Purposefully Designed Videos:** Unlike datasets collected from the web, the Perception Test features 11,620 real-world videos (average 23 seconds in length) that were *purposefully designed and filmed* to elicit perceptually interesting situations. This design choice is critical for systematically probing specific skills and reasoning types.
- **Scripted Scenarios:** The research team designed 37 distinct scripts, each with 2-5 variations, to cover the targeted skill areas (Memory, Abstraction, Physics, Semantics) and reasoning types (descriptive, explanatory, predictive, counterfactual). These scripts describe simple situations or games using common household items.
- **Diversity in Filming:** Approximately 100 crowd-sourced participants from various countries (globally diverse in gender, ethnicity, country) filmed the scripts. This ensures diversity in video content, appearance, lighting, and camera angles (static, moving, single/multiple viewpoints). Participants were instructed to avoid recording faces/voices for privacy.
- **Challenging Elements:** Scripts included:
 - **Distractor actions/objects:** Irrelevant elements to test causal understanding (e.g., clapping hands while making tea).
 - **Adversarial configurations:** Unusual object placements to challenge spatial reasoning beyond language biases (e.g., a shoe on a table).
 - **Adversarial actions:** Incorrectly executed actions to test task completion understanding (e.g., pouring from an empty kettle).

#### 3.2. Dense and Multi-modal Annotations

The videos are densely annotated with six distinct types of labels, enabling both language and non-language evaluations:
- **Object Tracks (189.9k):** Spatio-temporal bounding boxes for objects, annotated at 1 frame per second (fps). Focus on interacting objects, immediate vicinity, and background distractors. Uses an open vocabulary for object names (including attributes). All other annotations (except mc-vQA) are linked or grounded to these object tracks. A special "cups-game" subset (598 videos) targets object permanence and occluded interactions.
- **Point Tracks (8.6k):** Fine-grained 2D point trajectories on object surfaces, densely annotated at 30 fps. Used for understanding motion, deformation, and physical interactions of articulated or non-rigid objects.
- **Temporal Action Segments (73.5k):** Temporal segments of actions labeled from a fixed set of templated actions (e.g., "putting something into something"). Action boundaries are defined by contact with "action-relevant objects," which are linked to object tracks.
- **Temporal Sound Segments (137k):** Temporal segments of sounds labeled from 16 audio categories. Similar to actions, these are grounded by "sound-relevant objects" from the object tracks.
- **Multiple-Choice Video Question-Answers (mc-vQA) (38k):** High-level questions (designed by the research team) and three multiple-choice options (with one correct answer, sourced from crowd-workers). Questions are explicitly mapped to skill areas and reasoning types (descriptive, explanatory, predictive, counterfactual). This setup favors unambiguous evaluation over open-ended generation.
- **Grounded Video Question-Answers (g-vQA) (6k):** Questions (designed by the research team) where the answers are given as object tracks. This links high-level linguistic queries to low-level visual grounding.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Regime and Splits

- **Generalization-Focused Evaluation:** The benchmark is designed to assess the *transfer capabilities* of pre-trained models. It provides a small training split for optional fine-tuning of task decoders or prompting, but the primary focus is on zero-shot or few-shot performance.
- **Data Splits:** The 11,609 videos are divided into a training split (20%, 2,184 videos), a validation split (50%, 5,900 videos), and a held-out test split (30%, 3,525 videos). The splitting process ensures that all variations of a script filmed by a single participant remain within the same split to prevent data leakage and that video attributes and annotation types are proportionally distributed across splits.
- **Public Availability:** The training and validation splits (videos, annotations, baseline code) are publicly available under a CC-BY license. A challenge server is provided for evaluating models on the held-out test split.

#### 3.4. Computational Tasks and Baselines

Six computational tasks are defined based on the annotations:
- **Object Tracking:** Input: video, initial bounding box; Output: box track. Metric: Average IoU. Baseline: SiamFC.
- **Point Tracking:** Input: video, initial 2D point coordinates; Output: point track. Metric: Average Jaccard. Baseline: TAP-Net.
- **Temporal Action Localisation:** Input: video, audio; Output: list of action segments. Metric: mAP. Baseline: ActionFormer (fine-tuned).
- **Temporal Sound Localisation:** Input: video, audio; Output: list of sound segments. Metric: mAP. Baseline: ActionFormer (adapted, fine-tuned).
- **Multiple-choice videoQA (mc-vQA):** Input: video, audio, question, 3 options; Output: selected option. Metric: top-1 accuracy. Baselines: Flamingo (0/few-shot), SeViLA (0-shot/fine-tuned), Frequency dummy baseline, Human baseline.
- **Grounded videoQA (g-vQA):** Input: video, audio, language query; Output: list of box tracks. Metric: HOTA. Baseline: MDETR+Stark (combined detection and tracking).

This comprehensive methodological approach ensures that the benchmark is robust, diagnostic, and geared towards evaluating the holistic perception and reasoning capabilities of cutting-edge multimodal AI models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of state-of-the-art models on the Perception Test reveals a significant gap between machine and human performance, underscoring the benchmark's difficulty and the substantial room for improvement in multimodal AI.

#### 4.1. Pronounced Human-Machine Performance Gap

- For the multiple-choice video question-answering (mc-vQA) task, the **human baseline achieved an impressive 91.4% accuracy** in a zero-shot setting (without specific training on the task or dataset).
- In stark contrast, state-of-the-art video-language models performed significantly worse:
 - **SeViLA (0-shot):** 46.2% accuracy.
 - **SeViLA (fine-tuned on Perception Test training split):** 62.0% accuracy.
 - **Flamingo-3B (8-shot):** 45.8% accuracy.
 - Notably, in many skill areas, especially Memory, Physics, and Abstraction, **Flamingo and 0-shot SeViLA performed below a simple 8-shot frequency-based dummy baseline (51.0%)** and, in some cases (e.g., Piaget conservation task, collision, counterfactual reasoning), even below a pure random baseline (33.3%).

#### 4.2. Specific Model Limitations and Difficulties

- **Reasoning Deficiencies:** The qualitative analysis of Flamingo's performance on counterfactuals revealed a tendency to "latch on visible elements" in the video rather than imagining alternate realities. This highlights a fundamental weakness in abstract and hypothetical reasoning. Similarly, models struggled with intuitive physics tasks like stability, conservation, and occluded interactions, despite these being well-defined in the videos.
- **Generalization Challenges:** Both Flamingo and SeViLA demonstrated poor generalization in zero-shot/few-shot settings, failing to effectively transfer knowledge from their extensive pre-training to the novel, diagnostically designed tasks of the Perception Test. Even larger versions of Flamingo sometimes fared worse, suggesting potential overfitting issues.
- **Task-Specific Model Struggles:**
 - **Object Tracking (SiamFC):** While generally robust (0.67 Avg. IoU), performance significantly dropped when the camera or objects were moving, or for "action objects" and "sound objects," indicating challenges in dynamic and interactive scenarios.
 - **Point Tracking (TAP-Net):** Achieved 0.401 Average Jaccard, lower than reported on Kinetics, attributed to the longer video lengths in Perception Test. It notably struggled with fine-grained positional accuracy below a 4-pixel threshold, indicating limitations in precise tracking of surfaces.
 - **Temporal Action Localization (ActionFormer):** Achieved 15.56 mAP, lower than on other benchmarks like EPIC-KITCHENS. The model particularly struggled with rare action classes and confusing "pretend actions" with their actual counterparts, indicating a lack of nuanced understanding of intent or subtle distinctions. Multimodal inputs offered minimal improvement for action localization.
 - **Temporal Sound Localization (ActionFormer):** Achieved 15.46 mAP. Here, multimodal inputs (video + audio) did show a benefit over audio-only or video-only inputs, suggesting that fusing modalities is beneficial for localizing sounds.
 - **Grounded Video QA (MDETR+Stark):** The combined detection-tracking baseline yielded very poor performance (HOTA 0.1). This indicates that simply detecting objects in a single frame and then tracking them is insufficient for answering complex temporal-grounded queries, necessitating more integrated spatial-temporal reasoning for language grounding.
- **Dataset Difficulty:** The paper attributes the performance gap to the diversity of skills, reasoning types, and the inclusion of "hard negative options" in the mc-vQA tasks, making the Perception Test a significantly more challenging benchmark than many existing ones.

In essence, the findings unequivocally demonstrate that despite remarkable progress, current state-of-the-art multimodal AI models are far from exhibiting human-level perception and reasoning capabilities on a diagnostically challenging benchmark like the Perception Test, especially when evaluated for generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Perception Test benchmark carries significant implications for the future direction of multimodal AI research and its application. Its significance stems from several key aspects:

1. **A New Standard for General AI Evaluation:** The benchmark fundamentally shifts the paradigm of evaluation from task-specific performance to a holistic assessment of underlying cognitive skills (Memory, Abstraction, Physics, Semantics) and reasoning types (descriptive, explanatory, predictive, counterfactual). This provides a more robust and diagnostically rich measure of progress towards truly general AI, moving beyond superficial metrics.
2. **Highlighting Critical Research Gaps:** By revealing a substantial performance gap between humans and even the most advanced multimodal models, particularly in areas like counterfactual reasoning, intuitive physics, and nuanced understanding of actions (including adversarial ones), the Perception Test effectively pinpoints specific weaknesses that demand concentrated research efforts. It acts as a compass, directing researchers toward more challenging and cognitively relevant problems.
3. **Promoting Generalization and Robustness:** The emphasis on zero-shot, few-shot, or limited fine-tuning evaluation regimes is crucial. It discourages benchmark-specific overfitting and instead fosters the development of models with stronger transfer capabilities – models that can apply learned knowledge to novel situations, a hallmark of intelligent systems. This will lead to more robust and deployable AI in real-world, unpredictable environments.
4. **Driving True Multimodal Integration:** The benchmark's dense, interconnected annotations across video, audio, and text modalities necessitate models that can genuinely fuse and reason with information from all these sources, rather than simply processing them in isolation or in a loosely coupled manner. This will push the boundaries of multimodal fusion architectures.
5. **Diagnostic Tool for Model Interpretability:** The diverse and granular annotations (object tracks, point tracks, action/sound segments, and various QA types) on the same set of videos offer unprecedented opportunities for detailed failure analysis. Researchers can correlate successes and failures across different tasks and modalities, potentially uncovering model biases and offering insights into *why* models fail, thereby fostering more interpretable and trustworthy AI.
6. **Catalyst for Interdisciplinary Research:** Drawing inspiration from developmental psychology and behavioral tests, the Perception Test encourages a more interdisciplinary approach to AI research, bridging computer science with cognitive science to build systems that better emulate human-like understanding.
7. **Open Science and Community Engagement:** The public release of the training and validation splits, along with baseline code and a challenge server, fosters broad community engagement. This open approach is critical for accelerating research, encouraging diverse contributions, and establishing a widely adopted standard.
8. **Ethical Considerations and Responsible AI:** The paper proactively addresses potential negative societal impacts (e.g., intrusive surveillance) and explicitly states a stance against such uses. This demonstrates a commitment to responsible AI development, emphasizing the positive applications of enhancing perception for assistive technologies.

In conclusion, the Perception Test is not just another dataset; it is a meticulously designed diagnostic instrument tailored for the era of general multimodal AI. Its primary impact will be to steer research toward more cognitively sophisticated, robust, and truly intelligent systems, ultimately bridging the current chasm between machine perception and human understanding.
