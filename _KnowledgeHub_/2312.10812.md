# Learning to Act without Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent Action Policies (LAPO) learns interpretable latent action representations and policies purely from observation-only video data, bypassing the need for ground-truth action labels. This method enables efficient policy fine-tuning that surpasses policies trained from scratch and demonstrates high data efficiency in adapting to true action spaces, addressing a key challenge for scaling reinforcement learning with web-scale data.

## Problem

- Web-scale behavioral data (videos) lacks explicit action labels, preventing its direct use by traditional imitation learning or offline reinforcement learning methods.
- Existing Imitation Learning from Observation (IfO) methods often require some amount of ground-truth action-labeled data to train an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM).
- Scaling the success of large-scale pre-training from natural language processing and computer vision to reinforcement learning is hindered by the action label dependency in RL.

## Method

- Jointly trains an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) to predict a continuous latent action from a sequence of observations and a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) to predict the next observation from the current observation and the latent action.
- Applies Vector Quantization (VQ) to the continuous latent actions before they are fed to the FDM, serving as an information bottleneck to encourage learning of robust and disentangled action representations.
- Uses the trained IDM to pseudo-label action-free video data with inferred latent actions, then trains a latent policy via behavior cloning, which can be adapted to true actions via a small supervised decoder or online ==reinforcement learning== fine-tuning.

## Results

- LAPO learned highly interpretable latent action spaces, with UMAP projections showing distinct clusters corresponding to true actions without explicit action supervision during IDM training.
- Online fine-tuning of LAPO-derived policies achieved or exceeded expert performance in 9 out of 16 Procgen tasks within 4 million environment steps, significantly outperforming policies trained from scratch.
- Offline decoding, using fewer than 256 action-labeled transitions, allowed LAPO to match the performance of a PPO policy trained from scratch for 4 million steps, demonstrating extreme data efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent action information can be effectively inferred purely from observed state transitions by jointly optimizing an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) and a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM).
> - Vector Quantization (VQ) acts as a crucial information bottleneck, promoting the learning of interpretable, compressed latent action representations from continuous observation sequences.
> - Pre-training on observation-only video data yields a powerful policy initialization that allows for highly data-efficient adaptation to true action spaces via minimal supervised decoding or online fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a critical bottleneck in the application of large-scale pre-training paradigms to reinforcement learning (RL). In domains like natural language processing and computer vision, pre-training massive models on vast amounts of unlabelled web-scale data (e.g., text, images) has led to highly powerful and general models, such as large language models (LLMs) and vision transformers. However, this success has not yet fully translated to RL. The primary reason is that the most abundant form of embodied behavioral data on the web—videos—lacks the explicit action labels necessary for traditional imitation learning or offline RL methods.

"LEARNING TO ACT WITHOUT ACTIONS" directly confronts this challenge by proposing a novel method, Latent Action Policies (LAPO), that can infer latent action information purely from observation-only video data. This positions LAPO at the intersection of several key research areas:

- **Imitation Learning (IL) and Offline RL:** Traditional IL (e.g., Behavior Cloning) and offline RL require datasets with explicit action and often reward labels. LAPO extends these by demonstrating how policies can be learned from data *without* such labels.
- **Imitation Learning from Observation (IfO):** This subfield aims to learn policies from action-free demonstrations. Prior IfO methods often rely on some access to the true action space or require a small amount of ground-truth action-labeled data to train an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) for pseudo-labeling. LAPO significantly advances this by entirely removing the need for *any* ground-truth action labels for IDM training, inferring latent actions solely from observed dynamics.
- **World Models and Dynamics Learning:** LAPO's core mechanism involves jointly training an IDM and a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM). This aligns with research on learning predictive models of the environment, which are crucial for model-based RL. Unlike some prior works (e.g., FICC), LAPO focuses on distilling *behaviors* (via latent policies) rather than just dynamics, although it recovers both.
- **Self-supervised Learning and Representation Learning:** The method's ability to discover meaningful latent action representations without explicit supervision highlights its roots in self-supervised learning. By establishing predictive consistency between the IDM and FDM and using an information bottleneck, LAPO learns a compressed, actionable representation of state transitions.
- **Comparison to Related Work:**
 - **ILPO (Edwards et al., 2019):** A closely related work that also infers latent actions via dynamics modeling. However, ILPO learns a *latent policy* directly and uses *discrete* latent actions, which the authors show can lead to mode collapse and limited generalization in complex environments like Procgen. LAPO's use of an *IDM* and *continuous* latent actions (with Vector Quantization) offers greater robustness and expressive power.
 - **Semi-supervised IDM approaches (VPT, ACO, RLV, BCO, SS-ORL):** These methods use a small dataset with ground-truth action labels to train an IDM, which then pseudo-labels larger action-free datasets. LAPO fundamentally differs by inferring latent actions *without any prior action labels*, making it truly unsupervised in the initial latent action discovery phase.

By enabling the learning of actionable representations from unlabelled video, LAPO takes a significant step towards unlocking the web-scale pre-training paradigm for RL, paving the way for the development of more generalist and adaptable RL agents, analogous to the impact of LLMs and large vision models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the disparity between the success of large-scale, web-data pre-training in language and vision domains, and its limited application in reinforcement learning. While web data provides an enormous, inexpensive source of behavioral demonstrations, the absence of action labels in most video footage prevents its direct use by existing imitation learning or offline RL techniques.

The key objectives of this work are therefore to:

1. **Overcome the Action Label Requirement:** Develop a method that can learn meaningful action representations and policies directly from observation-only video data, without needing any ground-truth action labels for pre-training.
2. **Recover Latent Action Information:** Introduce a mechanism to infer an underlying "latent action" that effectively explains observed environment transitions, purely from visual input. This includes recovering the structure of the true action space itself.
3. **Produce Essential RL Models:** Beyond just latent actions, the method aims to yield latent-action versions of crucial RL components, specifically forward dynamics models (world models), inverse dynamics models, and most importantly, policies.
4. **Enable Rapid Fine-Tuning:** The learned latent-action policies should serve as powerful pre-trained models that can be efficiently fine-tuned into expert-level policies. This fine-tuning should be achievable either offline (with minimal true action-labeled data) or online (with environmental rewards).
5. **Advance Generalist Policy Learning:** Take a significant foundational step towards the long-term goal of pre-training powerful, generalist RL policies and world models on the vast, untapped resources of web-available video data, thereby moving RL closer to the generalist capabilities seen in NLP and vision.

In essence, the paper seeks to demonstrate the feasibility and utility of unsupervised learning of behavioral primitives from raw video, bridging the gap between abundant observational data and the action-centric demands of reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LAPO (Latent Action Policies) is designed to recover latent action information and train latent-action policies purely from videos. The core methodology is built upon two key insights: (1) a latent action explaining an environment transition can be inferred from observations alone, and (2) given these inferred latent actions, a policy can be trained using standard imitation learning.

The approach consists of three main stages:

#### 3.1. Learning a Latent Action Representation

This is the unsupervised pre-training phase where the latent action space is learned.
- **Components:**
 - **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM):** A neural network `p_IDM(z_t | o_t-k..., o_t, o_t+1)` that takes a sequence of observations (past, current, and future) and predicts a continuous latent action `z_t`. The future observation `o_t+1` is crucial for the IDM to infer the action that led to that state change.
 - **Forward Dynamics Model (FDM):** A neural network `p_FDM(o_t+1 | o_t-k..., o_t, z_t)` that predicts the next observation `o_t+1` given past observations, the current observation `o_t`, and the latent action `z_t` generated by the IDM.
- **Joint Training Objective:** Both models are trained jointly to minimize the next-state prediction error `||o_hat_t+1 - o_t+1||^2`. This objective encourages the latent action `z_t` to capture information about the state transition that is necessary for the FDM to accurately predict the future.
- **Information Bottleneck and Vector Quantization (VQ):** To prevent the IDM from simply copying the future observation `o_t+1` directly into `z_t`, LAPO introduces an information bottleneck. This is achieved by applying Vector Quantization to the continuous latent action `z_t` before it is fed to the FDM. VQ forces the IDM to compress the transition information into a discrete codebook, promoting the learning of a highly compressed, reusable, and disentangled representation that ideally corresponds to the true action. The IDM learns to encode the *difference* between `o_t+1` and `o_t` into `z_t`, which naturally tends to be the agent's action in deterministic environments.

#### 3.2. Behavior Cloning a Latent Action Policy

Once the latent IDM is trained, it can be used to pseudo-label the original action-free video dataset with inferred latent actions.
- **Process:** For each transition `(o_t, o_t+1)` in the dataset, the trained IDM predicts the corresponding latent action `z_t`.
- **Policy Training:** A new latent policy `π(z_t | o_t)` is then trained using standard behavior cloning, minimizing the `L2` loss `||π(o_t) - z_t||^2` between the policy's output and the IDM-predicted latent action. This effectively teaches the policy to imitate the observed expert's behavior in the latent action space.

#### 3.3. Decoding Latent Actions to True Actions

The final stage involves converting the latent-action policy into a policy that can operate in the true environment's action space. Two scenarios are considered:
- **Small Action-Labeled Dataset:** If a limited dataset with ground-truth `(o_t, a_t)` pairs is available, a simple decoder `d: Z → A` (e.g., a small neural network) can be trained in a supervised manner to map the latent actions `z_t` (predicted by the IDM for transitions where true actions `a_t` are known) to their corresponding true actions. The final policy becomes `d(π(o_t))`.
- **Online Environment Interaction:** If interaction with the online environment is possible, the decoder can be trained and fine-tuned using reinforcement learning. The latent policy's convolutional layers are kept frozen, and only the final layers (including the decoder) are fine-tuned using a general-purpose RL algorithm like PPO, directly optimizing for environmental rewards. This allows the policy to adapt to the true action space and potentially exceed the performance of the expert that generated the initial data.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setting

The methodology is evaluated on the challenging Procgen Benchmark (16 games), known for its procedural generation, visual complexity, partial observability, and stochasticity, which pose significant challenges for dynamics modeling. The training data consists of 8M frames from a PPO-trained expert policy, allowing for direct evaluation against ground truth. Architectural choices include IMPALA-CNN for policy/IDM and U-Net for FDM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Procgen Benchmark yielded several compelling findings that underscore LAPO's effectiveness:

1. **Highly Interpretable Latent Action Spaces:**
 - A remarkable result is LAPO's ability to learn latent action representations that exhibit clear, interpretable structures, closely corresponding to the true discrete action space. UMAP projections of the learned latent action spaces (Figure 5) show distinct clusters for most games, with each cluster aligning with a specific ground-truth action (e.g., move left, move right, jump), even though no ground-truth action labels were used during the IDM training.
 - The structure's complexity varied with environmental characteristics; environments with higher partial observability often exhibited more fragmented latent spaces, as the IDM had to encode more off-screen information into the latent action.
 - The ablation study demonstrated that **Vector Quantization (VQ)** is a critical component. Without VQ, the latent spaces were significantly more fragmented and less interpretable (Appendix A.2), indicating that VQ effectively acts as an information bottleneck, forcing `z_t` to capture cleaner, state-invariant action information.

2. **Superior Online Decoding and Fine-tuning Performance:**
 - When the latent policy was fine-tuned online using a combination of supervised decoding and reinforcement learning, LAPO significantly outperformed a policy trained from scratch with PPO. Within only 4 million environment interaction steps, LAPO-derived policies achieved or exceeded expert performance in 9 out of 16 Procgen tasks, whereas PPO from scratch only reached 44% of expert performance in the same timeframe (Figure 3). This highlights the immense benefit of LAPO's unsupervised pre-training, acting as a highly effective initialization for downstream RL.
 - LAPO demonstrated robustness compared to the primary baseline, ILPO. While ILPO showed positive results on single levels of CoinRun, it consistently collapsed when applied to the full, procedurally-generated Procgen environments, suggesting that LAPO's use of an IDM and continuous latent space (with VQ) is more suitable for handling the complexity and stochasticity of diverse environments.

3. **Extreme Data Efficiency for Offline Decoding:**
 - LAPO also proved highly efficient in adapting the latent policy using only a small, offline action-labeled dataset, without any online interaction. A decoder trained on fewer than 256 labeled transitions was sufficient to match the performance of a PPO policy trained from scratch for 4 million steps (Figure 4). This showcases LAPO's potential for scenarios where even limited online interaction is prohibitively expensive or unavailable.
 - However, performance plateaued with larger offline datasets and generally did not reach the level of online fine-tuning, likely because latent actions may not be perfectly state-invariant, and a purely offline decoder lacks the full state context.

In summary, LAPO successfully learned meaningful action representations from raw video, enabling data-efficient policy adaptation that outperformed strong baselines, marking a significant step towards unlocking web-scale observational data for reinforcement learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"LEARNING TO ACT WITHOUT ACTIONS" presents a pivotal advancement in reinforcement learning, addressing one of the most significant barriers to scaling RL models: the dependency on explicitly labeled action data. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Web-Scale Pre-training for RL:** The most profound impact of LAPO is its demonstration that comprehensive action information can be recovered from purely observational video data through unsupervised objectives. This directly unlocks the vast, unstructured, and readily available trove of web videos (e.g., YouTube footage of human gameplay, robotic demonstrations) for pre-training generalist RL policies and world models. This parallels the paradigm shift observed in NLP (with LLMs) and computer vision, where massive unsupervised pre-training has led to unprecedented capabilities.

2. **Bridging the Observation-Action Gap:** LAPO effectively solves the "action problem" in learning from observation, by inferring latent actions that accurately reflect the underlying true actions. This capability is crucial for any system aiming to learn complex behaviors from passive observational data, which is far more common than precisely annotated datasets.

3. **Highly Data-Efficient Adaptation:** The research shows that LAPO's pre-trained latent policies are incredibly efficient to fine-tune. Whether with a minuscule amount of action-labeled data offline or limited online interaction, the policies can be rapidly adapted to achieve or even surpass expert-level performance. This drastically reduces the data and computational resources required for training competent RL agents, making RL more practical and accessible for real-world applications where data labeling or environment interaction is costly.

4. **Foundation for Generalist Agents:** By learning generalizable latent action representations, LAPO lays a foundation for the development of "generalist agents" in RL. Such agents could learn a wide array of skills from diverse video data and then adapt quickly to specific tasks, much like how large language models can perform myriad tasks with few-shot learning after vast pre-training. This could lead to more robust, versatile, and transferable RL systems that can operate across various environments and tasks.

5. **New Research Avenues:** The work opens several promising avenues for future research. Scaling LAPO to more powerful architectures (e.g., Transformers capable of processing longer sequences and richer observations) is a natural next step for handling web-scale video. Further exploration into learning multi-task generalist policies within a single LAPO instance, and improving generalization to unseen tasks, will be crucial for realizing the full potential of this unsupervised pre-training paradigm.

In conclusion, LAPO represents a critical stride towards autonomous learning from the world's most abundant data source—videos. By enabling the learning of actionable intelligence from passive observation, it holds the potential to fundamentally transform the landscape of reinforcement learning, driving it towards the era of truly generalist and adaptive AI.
