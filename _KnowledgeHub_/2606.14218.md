# Universal Manipulation Exoskeleton: Learning Compliant Whole-body Policies with Real-time Torque Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> The Universal Manipulation Exoskeleton (UME), developed by Ant Group and Stanford University, provides real-time joint torque feedback and whole-arm configuration capture during human teleoperation, facilitating the learning of compliant, whole-body, and bimanual policies for robots. This system achieved autonomous task success rates of up to 95% on challenging, contact-rich manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming baselines lacking torque data.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with active compliant behaviors in contact-rich or constrained environments due to policies primarily trained on visual/linguistic data, often overlooking force/torque feedback.
- Current robotic data collection methods for teleoperation, such as ALOHA and GELLO, typically only capture end-effector poses and lack real-time joint torque data or comprehensive whole-arm configuration control.
- Teleoperation often lacks haptic feedback, making it less intuitive and safe for operators to regulate interaction forces, hindering the collection of high-quality data for learning compliant robot behaviors.

## Method

- A Universal Manipulation Exoskeleton (**UME**) was developed to capture human demonstrations with real-time joint torque signals and full whole-arm configurations.
- The UME features a low-cost, lightweight mechanical design using coaxial actuators and ==quasi-direct-drive motors== for transparent haptic feedback, built primarily from 3D-printed PLA plastic.
- An accompanying universal retargeting algorithm decouples kinematic chains for stable control across various robot degrees of freedom, and an integrated IMU enables portable mobile manipulation.

## Results

- UME-trained autonomous policies achieved high success rates on challenging tasks: **90%** for Box Pushing, **85%** for Box Flipping, **95%** for GPU Picking, and **95%** for Fridge Drink Retrieval.
- Ablation studies showed that `No-torque` and `UMI` baselines performed poorly or failed completely (e.g., **0%** success for Box Flipping and GPU Picking with UMI), underscoring the criticality of torque feedback and whole-arm data.
- The system successfully demonstrated universal retargeting by controlling bimanual 6-DoF X-ARMs in the real world and 7-DoF Franka arms in simulation, showcasing its cross-embodiment generality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time haptic torque feedback significantly enhances human operator intuition and safety during teleoperation, improving data collection efficiency by 3.3 times for complex, force-mediated tasks.
> - Policies trained with joint torque data, alongside visual and proprioceptive information, can learn active compliant behaviors necessary for robust force regulation and successful execution of contact-rich manipulation.
> - Capturing comprehensive whole-arm configurations during demonstrations is crucial for robots to learn effective collision avoidance and compliant sliding behaviors in tightly constrained environments.
