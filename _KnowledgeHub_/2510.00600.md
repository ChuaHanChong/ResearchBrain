# Hybrid Training for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Qualcomm AI Research and the University of Tübingen developed Hybrid Training (HyT), a framework that enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to internalize Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning during training. The approach allows models to achieve superior performance in complex robotic tasks, outperforming standard VLAs and ECoT methods by up to 20% in real-world settings, while maintaining fast inference speeds comparable to standard VLAs.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while powerful, struggle with generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios due to narrow robotic data distributions.
- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods like Embodied CoT (ECoT) improve VLA performance and interpretability but incur significant inference latency due to explicit thought generation, limiting real-time robotic deployment.
- A trade-off exists between the performance benefits of CoT reasoning and the practical need for fast action execution in robotics.

## Method

- A ==Hybrid Training== (HyT) framework is introduced, which allows a single VLA model to learn multiple conditional action distributions based on a 'modality' variable (act, think, follow).
- The learning objective is a weighted sum of negative log-likelihood losses corresponding to direct action prediction, thought-then-action prediction, and action prediction based on provided thoughts.
- At inference time, the model can operate in a fast 'act' mode (predicting actions directly without thoughts) or a slower 'think' mode for interpretability, leveraging internalized CoT knowledge for performance gains without overhead.

## Results

- HyT models consistently outperformed standard VLAs, ECoT, and hierarchical VLAs across all dataset sizes (300-3000 demonstrations) on the ClevrSkills benchmark, showing particular benefit for complex tasks.
- HyT achieved fast inference speeds of approximately **3Hz** on A100 GPUs, comparable to standard VLAs, while ECoT was **3x** slower and hierarchical methods were **4x** slower.
- In real-world robotic tasks, HyT achieved **63%** ± 7 overall success, significantly outperforming OpenVLA (**41%** ± 7 overall success), with even greater gains on out-of-distribution tasks (**54%** ± 10 vs. **29%** ± 9).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Performance benefits from Chain-of-Thought training primarily stem from the model's internalization of knowledge and improved representations, rather than requiring explicit thought generation at inference time.
> - Hybrid training enables a VLA to develop 'skilled intuition,' allowing it to effectively recognize patterns and generate actions directly, analogous to human cognitive processes.
> - The 'think' and 'follow' distributions within the hybrid training objective act as effective regularization or knowledge distillation mechanisms, enhancing the robustness and generalization of the VLA's learned policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the rapidly evolving field of robotics foundation models, specifically Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Recent advancements, driven by large-scale robot data collection and the development of open-source Vision Language Models (VLMs), have enabled the creation of VLAs capable of end-to-end control from language instructions and camera images. These models offer advantages such as multimodal prompting and leveraging pre-trained VLM knowledge, yet they often struggle with generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) settings due to narrow robotic data distributions.

To address generalization challenges and enhance performance, prior work has explored "thinking VLAs" that generate intermediate outputs, or "thoughts," before producing actions. A prominent example is Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) (Zawalski et al., 2024), where VLAs output linguistic reasoning traces. While these methods have demonstrated improved performance and interpretability, the generation of these intermediate thoughts significantly increases inference time. This latency poses a practical limitation for real-world robotic applications requiring timely action sequences.

Concurrently, research on hybrid reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) has explored distilling slow, deliberate reasoning capabilities into faster models (Deng et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024). Some related work in robotics, such as RFST (Zhu et al., 2024), uses discriminators to select between fast and slow reasoning systems. Another concurrent study (Chen et al., 2025) suggests that reasoning pre-training, co-training, or dropout can enhance VLA performance by improving learned representations. This paper contributes to this landscape by proposing a unified training framework that aims to leverage the benefits of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for performance improvement without incurring the associated inference overhead at test time. It posits that VLAs can internalize the knowledge from CoT traces to develop a "skilled intuition," enabling fast, direct action generation while retaining the performance gains of reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observed trade-off between performance gains offered by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) techniques in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and the increased inference time caused by generating intermediate reasoning steps. While techniques like Embodied CoT (ECoT) improve task success and interpretability, the additional token generation for thoughts significantly slows down the agent's action execution, which is a critical limitation for real-world robotic manipulation where long action sequences and high control frequencies are often required.

The paper sets forth a central hypothesis: the performance benefits derived from CoT training primarily result from the model's internalization of knowledge through thought prediction and thought-conditioned action prediction, rather than from the explicit generation of thoughts at inference time. This implies that a VLA, adequately trained, could develop a "skilled intuition" analogous to human cognitive processes (Kahneman, 2011; Kahneman & Klein, 2009; Simon, 1992), enabling it to recognize patterns and generate accurate actions directly, even when explicit thoughts are not produced during inference.

Based on this hypothesis, the key objectives of the research are:

1. **Develop a Hybrid Training (HyT) Framework**: Design a training methodology that allows VLA models to learn effectively from CoT reasoning traces and action data. This framework aims to integrate structured reasoning capabilities into a single model, supporting multiple operational modes.
2. **Enable Performance Gains without Inference Overhead**: Achieve performance levels comparable to or exceeding CoT-based methods (like ECoT) while maintaining the fast inference speed of standard VLAs. This means allowing the model to generate actions directly during test-time without intermediate thought generation.
3. **Support Flexible Inference Modalities**: Engineer the model to conditionally predict a diverse set of outputs, providing flexibility at inference time. Specifically, this includes an "act" mode for direct action prediction, a "think" mode for generating intermediate thoughts (similar to ECoT), and a "follow" mode for executing actions based on provided instructions or oracle thoughts.
4. **Investigate the Contribution of CoT Techniques**: Address the fundamental question of how CoT techniques contribute to VLA performance, specifically exploring whether the main utility lies in improving the model's learned representations rather than requiring explicit reasoning at test time.
5. **Empirical Validation**: Evaluate the proposed HyT framework across various benchmarks, including simulated environments (ClevrSkills, LIBERO) and real-world robotic tasks, to assess its effectiveness in terms of success rate, inference speed, and instruction-following capabilities.

These objectives collectively aim to bridge the gap between high-performance reasoning and real-time robotic action, making VLA models more practical and efficient for complex manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Hybrid Training (HyT), a framework designed to enable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to learn from Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning while maintaining fast inference speeds. The core idea is that VLA models can internalize the knowledge from CoT traces to develop an intuitive expertise, allowing them to directly predict actions with high accuracy, without the need for explicit thought generation at inference time.

#### 3.1. Hybrid Training (HyT) Formulation

HyT redefines the VLA learning objective to conditionally predict different types of outputs based on a "modality" variable ($m$). Given a task description $l$ and current observation $x_t$, the conditional distribution over actions $a_t$ is expressed as a marginalization over thoughts $\tau$ and modality values $m_j$:

$p(a_t|x_t, l) = \sum_i \sum_j p_{\theta}(a_t, \tau_i, m_j | x_t, l) = \sum_i \sum_j p_{\theta}(a_t, \tau_i | x_t, l, m_j) p(m_j)$

This formulation allows a single VLA model with parameters $\theta$ to learn multiple conditional action distributions. The paper focuses on three specific distributions, depending on the value of $m \in \{m_a, m_{\tau}, m_f\}$:

- **"Act" distribution**: Instructs the model to predict actions directly, akin to standard VLAs. This corresponds to the case where $\tau$ is an empty set, i.e., $p_{\theta}(\tau = \emptyset | m_a) = 1$.
- **"Think" distribution**: Instructs the model to first predict intermediate thoughts $\tau_t$ and then generate actions $a_t$, similar to ECoT. This models $p_{\theta}(a_t|x_t, l, \tau_t) p_{\theta}(\tau_t|x_t, l, m_{\tau})$.
- **"Follow" distribution**: Instructs the model to predict actions based on provided thoughts/instructions $\tau_t$, similar to a low-level policy in a hierarchical system. This assumes conditional independence of $l$ when $\tau_t$ is provided, i.e., $p_{\theta}(a_t|x_t, \tau_t, m_f) = p_{\theta}(a_t|x_t, l, \tau_t, m_f)$, and no thought generation, i.e., $p_{\theta}(\tau = \emptyset | m_f) = 1$.

The overall learning objective for the model is a weighted sum of negative log-likelihood losses corresponding to these three distributions:

$\min_{\theta} L_{hyt}(\theta) = w_a L_{act}(\theta) + w_{\tau} L_{think}(\theta) + w_f L_{follow}(\theta)$

#### 3.2. Hybrid Training Implementation

Instead of directly computing the weighted sum of losses for each data point, which could reduce batch variability, the HyT framework employs a Monte Carlo estimate. The weights $w_a, w_{\tau}, w_f$ are used to define the probabilities of sampling the corresponding inputs and outputs for the model during training. This ensures that each training batch contains a mix of modality tokens, actions, and thoughts according to these probabilities.

For the experiments, the authors adopt the distributions defined above, using simple text strings like "<act>", "<think>", and "<follow>" as modality variables. The default sampling coefficients used are $\{w_a: 0.25, w_{\tau}: 0.5, w_f: 0.25\}$, with an ablation study presented in the Appendix.

The VLA models are based on Transformer-based LLM architectures for language processing and a vision encoder (e.g., Vision Transformer) for visual inputs. Actions can be tokenized into discrete bins for LLM prediction (negative log-likelihood loss) or predicted as continuous values (L1 or L2 loss).

#### 3.3. Inference Time

A key aspect of HyT is its flexibility at inference time. The primary goal is to leverage the "act" distribution for fast, direct action prediction. This is achieved by providing the model with the task description, environmental inputs (e.g., camera image), and the modality variable $m_a = <act>$. This allows the model to utilize its internalized knowledge without additional inference costs, performing similarly to standard VLAs in terms of speed (e.g., 3Hz on A100 GPUs, compared to 3x-4x slower for ECoT and HiRobot).

Additionally, HyT enables other inference modes:

- **"Think" mode**: By setting $m_{\tau} = <think>$, the model generates intermediate thoughts before actions, providing interpretability.
- **"Follow" mode**: By setting $m_f = <follow>$, the model can follow externally provided, fine-grained embodied instructions (e.g., from a human or an oracle), acting as a low-level policy. This supports greater intervenability.

The modality variable is typically set at the beginning of a task and remains constant throughout the episode, although dynamic switching is noted as a direction for future work.

#### 3.4. Thought Extraction

- **ClevrSkills Benchmark**: Thoughts are extracted from oracle solvers, transforming subtasks into natural language instructions (e.g., "Move to X", "Pick up Y"). Motion directions (e.g., "left/right", "forward/backward", "up/down") are also derived from end-effector positions.
- **LIBERO Benchmark**: Since oracle thoughts are unavailable, CoT is extracted by first using the simulator to obtain labeled object bounding boxes and high-level motion primitives. This information, along with the task description, is fed to an LLM (Gemma-2 9B) to generate a plan of subtasks and associate them with temporal steps.
- **Real-world Experiments**: A heuristic-based approach is used, identifying keyframe moments (reaching grasping/releasing positions, grasping/releasing actions) to define subtasks like "Move to {obj1}", "Pick up {obj1}", "Move {position} {obj2}", and "Place object {position} {obj2}". Motion directions are also included.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the Hybrid Training (HyT) method across simulated benchmarks (ClevrSkills, LIBERO) and real-world experiments, comparing it against standard VLAs, ECoT-like thinking VLAs, and HiRobot-like hierarchical VLAs.

#### 4.1. ClevrSkills Benchmark

- **Performance Scaling with Data**: HyT models consistently outperformed standard VLAs, ECoT, and HiRobot across all evaluated dataset sizes (300, 750, 1500, 3000 demonstrations) on 9 diverse tasks. This supports the hypothesis that training with reasoning traces helps internalize knowledge, leading to performance improvements even when thoughts are not generated at inference time.
 - HyT demonstrated superior performance gains, particularly beneficial for more complex and longer-horizon tasks.
 - ECoT performed second best at all data scales.
 - Hierarchical VLAs (HiRobot) showed higher performance than standard VLAs with smaller datasets but were eventually outperformed by standard VLAs with 1500+ demonstrations.
- **Inference Time**: Standard VLAs and HyT models achieved inference frequencies of approximately 3Hz on A100 GPUs (with 4 models in parallel). In contrast, ECoT models were approximately 3x slower, and HiRobot hierarchical generation was about 4x slower. This validates HyT's ability to retain fast inference speeds while benefiting from CoT-based training.
- **Instruction-Following and Inference Modalities**:
 - Models trained with HyT exhibited similar performance in "act" mode (direct action) and "think" mode (generating thoughts) when self-generating thoughts. This suggests that explicit thought generation at test time may not be necessary for performance gains in the evaluated settings.
 - When provided with "Oracle thoughts" (externally sourced, precise instructions for moving subtasks), all tested methods showed improved performance. This indicates that HyT's "think" and "follow" inference modalities can be useful for leveraging external guidance.
 - An ablation study (Appendix A.1) showed that the "think" distribution is particularly important for smaller datasets, providing a stronger training signal. The "follow" distribution proved beneficial for larger datasets and for improving performance when following oracle instructions.

#### 4.2. LIBERO Benchmark

- **Integration with VLA Designs**: HyT was implemented in combination with the OFT fine-tuning recipe (Kim et al., 2025), which incorporates action chunking and continuous action prediction, starting from the Prismatic VLM (Karamcheti et al., 2024).
- **Comparative Performance**: HyT achieved a higher average success rate (93.7%) across the four LIBERO task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long) compared to VLA-OFT (92.1%) and other state-of-the-art VLA methods such as TraceVLA (74.8%), Octo (75.1%), OpenVLA (76.5%), CoT-VLA (81.1%), ThinkAct (84.4%), $\pi_0$-FAST (85.5%), and MolmoAct (86.6%).
 - HyT demonstrated notable improvements, especially in the more complex "Goal" and "Long" task suites.
 - When starting from the OpenVLA pre-trained model (7B parameters), both VLA-OFT and HyT nearly saturated the benchmark's performance (overall success: 95.3% ± 0.1), showing no significant difference. This suggests that HyT and other CoT strategies can compensate for a lack of extensive robotics pre-training or data scarcity.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

- **Experimental Setup**: A dataset of 320 trajectories was collected using a UFactory xArm 6. Models were fine-tuned using LoRA from the pre-trained OpenVLA model (7B parameters).
- **Performance in Real-World Tasks**: HyT (63% ± 7 overall success) significantly outperformed OpenVLA (41% ± 7 overall success) on a set of real-world manipulation tasks.
 - For in-distribution tasks, HyT achieved 72% ± 9 success, compared to OpenVLA's 52% ± 10.
 - For out-of-distribution tasks (novel objects or placements), HyT achieved 54% ± 10 success, substantially higher than OpenVLA's 29% ± 9.
- **Qualitative Observations**: While both models exhibited similar flaws (e.g., incorrect object orientation during picking), HyT demonstrated greater precision in reaching picking and placing positions and did not reach for incorrect objects, contributing to its improved performance. The lower inference time of HyT (similar to standard VLAs) was highlighted as a practical benefit for real-world use cases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Hybrid Training (HyT) as a framework that addresses a key trade-off in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: the balance between performance gains derived from Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and the computational cost of increased inference time. The primary significance of this work lies in its empirical support for the hypothesis that VLA models can internalize the "thinking process," leading to improved performance through enhanced internal representations, even without explicit CoT generation during inference.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**

1. **High Performance with Fast Inference**: HyT demonstrates that VLA models can achieve performance levels comparable to or exceeding CoT-based methods (like ECoT) while maintaining the fast inference speed of standard VLAs. This is a crucial advancement for real-world robotics, where high control frequency and timely action execution are paramount for usability and safety, particularly in long-horizon tasks.
2. **Flexible Operational Modes**: The introduction of a "modality variable" allows a single HyT-trained VLA to operate in different modes: direct action ("act"), reasoning with thoughts ("think"), and instruction-following ("follow"). This flexibility offers practical benefits such as enhanced interpretability (via the "think" mode) and improved intervenability or integration into hierarchical systems (via the "follow" mode, especially with oracle guidance).
3. **Improved Representation Learning**: The findings support the idea that learning to generate and condition on CoT improves the agent's understanding of the environment and task, akin to how auxiliary losses enhance representation learning in other contexts. This implies that CoT training acts as a regularization or knowledge distillation mechanism, leading to more robust and generalizable policies.
4. **Robustness across Data Scales and Domains**: HyT consistently outperformed baselines across various data scales in simulation and demonstrated significant gains in real-world scenarios, particularly for out-of-distribution tasks. This suggests HyT can be a valuable strategy for improving VLA performance when data is limited or when facing novel environmental configurations.
5. **Scalable Knowledge Distillation**: HyT offers a scalable framework for integrating knowledge from human annotations and large foundation models into VLA models. This is particularly relevant as automated reasoning approaches mature, potentially reducing the reliance on manual CoT labeling.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

- **Complexity of Reasoning**: While HyT shows that explicit thought generation might be unnecessary for primitive tasks, its applicability to more complex tasks requiring advanced reasoning or memory remains an open question for future investigation.
- **Dependency on CoT Labeling**: HyT, like other CoT methods, still requires additional human labeling to provide reasoning traces for training. Future work could explore automated or self-supervised methods for thought extraction to mitigate this reliance.
- **Dynamic Modality Switching**: The current implementation fixes the modality variable for an entire task. Exploring the benefits and tradeoffs of dynamically switching between inference modalities during execution could offer further advantages.

In summary, HyT represents a step towards developing more efficient and capable robotic agents by enabling them to benefit from structured reasoning during training without compromising real-time performance. Its practical utility and demonstrated effectiveness across diverse benchmarks highlight its potential to advance the state of VLA models for robotic manipulation.
