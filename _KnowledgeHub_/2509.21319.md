# RLBFF: Binary Flexible Feedback to bridge between Human Feedback & Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers introduced Reinforcement Learning with Binary Flexible Feedback (RLBFF), a new paradigm for large language model post-training that extracts explicit, binary principles from human feedback. LLMs aligned with RLBFF achieved competitive performance on general alignment benchmarks, such as a 9.50 MT-Bench score, at less than 5% of the inference cost compared to state-of-the-art proprietary models.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) lacks interpretability and is prone to reward hacking due to implicit human judgment criteria.
- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is precise but has limited applicability to tasks with objective correctness and can suffer from low recall.
- Prior approaches using binary feedback or generative reward models often lacked user control over principles or broad coverage beyond narrow domains.

## Method

- Proposed Reinforcement Learning with ==Binary Flexible Feedback== (**RLBFF**), a framework where a reward model evaluates a response against a specific principle in a binary (Yes/No) fashion.
- Created a training dataset by extracting 1,414 distinct, fine-grained principles and their binary fulfillment from natural language human feedback using an LLM, applying stringent filtering for consensus and accuracy.
- Trained both efficient Flexible Principles Scalar Reward Models and reasoning-capable Flexible Principles Generative Reward Models to provide explicit rewards based on these principles.

## Results

- Flexible Principles ScalarRMs achieved an **83.6%** score on RM-Bench, **76.3%** on JudgeBench, and **91.6%** on PrincipleBench, outperforming baseline and external scalar reward models.
- The Flexible Principles Generative Reward Model reached **86.2%** on RM-Bench and **81.4%** on JudgeBench, securing the top position on the JudgeBench leaderboard.
- An LLM aligned with RLBFF achieved competitive performance (e.g., 9.50 MT-Bench, **55.6%** on Arena Hard v2) compared to proprietary models, with a **24x** to **187x** reduction in inference cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly defined, binary principles extracted from human feedback offer a structured and interpretable bridge between broad human preferences and precise, verifiable rewards.
> - Training reward models on a diverse set of flexible principles significantly improves generalization, even when evaluating against a single specific principle.
> - Evaluating single responses against a principle is more aligned with natural human feedback patterns and effectively mitigates position bias inherent in pairwise comparison methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Model (LLM) post-training heavily relies on Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques, primarily Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLHF, exemplified by works like Ouyang et al. (2022) and Bai et al. (2022), utilizes human preferences to fine-tune LLMs, offering wide coverage across various tasks. However, it faces challenges with interpretability and susceptibility to reward hacking, as human judgments often lack explicit criteria. Reward models trained via RLHF, typically Bradley-Terry models, produce scores that are context-dependent and do not explain the rationale behind a given score.

Conversely, RLVR, explored by Lambert et al. (2025) and DeepSeek-AI et al. (2025), employs rule-based verifiers that provide binary correctness signals. This approach offers high interpretability and precision, as the criteria for reward are explicit. Nevertheless, RLVR is limited in scope, primarily applicable to tasks with objectively verifiable correct answers (e.g., math problems, competitive coding), and can suffer from low recall, potentially penalizing semantically equivalent but syntactically different correct answers.

The presented work, Reinforcement Learning with Binary Flexible Feedback (RLBFF), positions itself as a bridge between these two paradigms. It aims to combine the versatility and broad coverage of human-driven preferences (characteristic of RLHF) with the precision and interpretability of rule-based verification (characteristic of RLVR). By extracting explicit, binary principles from natural language human feedback, RLBFF seeks to mitigate the interpretability and reward hacking issues of RLHF while expanding beyond the narrow correctness focus of RLVR. This approach seeks to enable reward models to capture a wider range of nuanced response qualities that are often implicitly valued by humans but difficult to formalize. Previous work has explored binary feedback in narrower domains (e.g., safety, math), or used generative reward models with self-generated or manually curated criteria that lack user control or broad coverage. RLBFF distinguishes itself by deriving over 1,000 fine-grained principles directly from human feedback and allowing user-specified principles at inference time.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate Reinforcement Learning with Binary Flexible Feedback (RLBFF) as a novel paradigm for LLM post-training. This paradigm aims to address the limitations inherent in existing RLHF and RLVR approaches.

The detailed motivations for proposing RLBFF and its specific design choices are as follows:

1. **Addressing Limitations of RLHF:** RLHF struggles with interpretability because the underlying reasons for human preferences (e.g., "response A is better than response B") are often implicit. This lack of explicit criteria makes it difficult to understand *why* a reward model assigns a certain score, leading to opaque black-box models. Furthermore, RLHF is susceptible to reward hacking, where models may exploit superficial features (e.g., response length, matching user beliefs) to maximize reward without genuinely improving response quality according to intended criteria. RLBFF aims to mitigate these issues by grounding reward models in explicitly defined principles.

2. **Overcoming RLVR's Scope Restrictions:** RLVR, while precise and interpretable, is limited to tasks where correctness can be easily verified by objective rules. This restricts its applicability to a subset of LLM use cases. Additionally, rule-based verifiers can exhibit low recall, failing to recognize valid alternative solutions or paraphrases, thereby overly penalizing responses that are semantically correct but do not match a predefined template. RLBFF seeks to extend the benefits of verifiable rewards to a broader spectrum of qualitative attributes beyond mere correctness, by allowing principles to be derived from diverse human judgments.

3. **Rationale for Design Choices in RLBFF Formulation:**
 - **Why Principles?** The motivation stems from the observation that human judgments are often driven by diverse and context-dependent principles (e.g., humor on social media vs. correctness on scientific forums). Learning preferences without explicitly defining these underlying principles can lead to an unclear optimization objective and less effective training. RLBFF proposes to make these principles explicit, allowing for targeted feedback and clearer learning signals.
 - **Why Single Response Instead of Response Pair?** Traditional RLHF often relies on pairwise comparisons, which can be unnatural for how humans typically provide feedback (e.g., product reviews focusing on a single item). Pairwise comparisons are also prone to position bias, where the order of presentation influences judgment. Evaluating single responses against a principle aligns more naturally with human feedback patterns and avoids position bias.
 - **Why Binary (Instead of Likert Scale)?** Likert scales (e.g., 5-point ratings) are known to be difficult to calibrate consistently across different human annotators due to subjective interpretations of numerical scores. This variability makes it challenging to achieve consensus and consistent training signals. Binarizing the evaluation (e.g., "concise" vs. "not concise") reduces ambiguity and annotation disparities, leading to more reliable feedback.

4. **Empirical Validation and Contribution:** A key objective is to empirically demonstrate that reward models trained with RLBFF can achieve high performance on established benchmarks (RM-Bench, JudgeBench) and a newly introduced benchmark (PrincipleBench) designed to specifically assess adherence to explicit principles. Furthermore, the research aims to show that LLMs aligned using RLBFF can match or exceed the performance of state-of-the-art proprietary models on general alignment benchmarks (MT-Bench, WildBench, Arena Hard v2) while significantly reducing inference costs.

5. **Open-Source Contributions:** The project commits to providing a fully open-source recipe, including data, for aligning LLMs using RLBFF, thereby promoting reproducibility and broader adoption within the research community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involves a multi-step process, encompassing the formulation of RLBFF, the creation of a suitable training dataset through sophisticated processing of existing human feedback, the training and evaluation of various reward models, ablation studies, and finally, the alignment of a large language model using the developed reward models.

**RLBFF Formulation:**
The core of RLBFF is a three-part input: a prompt, a response, and a specific principle. The model is then tasked with indicating whether the response fulfills that principle in a binary (Yes/No) fashion. The reward signal for training is derived as the difference between the log-probability of generating "Yes" and the log-probability of generating "No" (`log P(Y) - log P(N)`). This approach allows the reward to be monotonic with the probability of satisfaction, while also putting more weight on responses that clearly satisfy or clearly fail the principle.

**Training Data Generation:**
Since no readily available dataset adhered to the RLBFF format, the researchers adapted the existing **HelpSteer3-Feedback** dataset (NVIDIA, 2025a). This dataset comprises 40,821 samples, each containing a prompt, two responses, and human-written paragraph-length textual feedback (2–10 sentences) for each response. The feedback covers General, STEM, Code, and Multilingual domains.

The process of converting this natural language feedback into binary flexible feedback involved several key steps:
1. **Principle and Fulfillment Extraction:** DeepSeek V3-0324, an open-weight LLM, was employed to extract principles from the human feedback. A zero-shot prompt template instructed the LLM to generate a JSON dictionary where each key is a principle and its value is a string containing a supporting text span from the feedback, followed by a binary (yes/no/partially) indication of whether the principle is fulfilled.
2. **Filtering Principles Unsupported by Feedback:** To reduce hallucinations, a stringent filtering step was applied. Principles were rejected if the LLM-generated supporting text span did not plausibly originate from the human feedback, identified using the `rapidfuzz.partial_ratio` string matching library with a threshold of 60. This removed 2.2% of extracted principles.
3. **Removal of "Helpfulness" Principles:** Principles related to global "helpfulness" were excluded (4.5% of raw principles) because "helpfulness" often represents an overall quality assessment rather than a specific, actionable principle.
4. **Removal of Partially Fulfilled Principles:** Principles marked as "partially" fulfilled were removed (13.8%) due to the inherent ambiguity in quantifying "partial" fulfillment, reinforcing the binary nature of RLBFF. The remaining principles showed a slight imbalance, with 64.6% "yes" and 35.4% "no."
5. **Obtaining High-Precision Consensus Principles:** To ensure high-quality principles and reduce annotator subjectivity, a consensus filtering mechanism was implemented. Qwen-3-8B Embedding was used to compute cosine similarities between principles. Only principles that had at least one semantically similar counterpart (cosine similarity > 0.8) from *each* of the other annotators for the same feedback were retained. This was a highly selective filter, reducing the dataset from an initial 1.2 million extracted principles to approximately 100,000 (33,000 unique meanings), averaging 1.27 principles per feedback. This prioritization of precision over recall aimed to avoid training on mis-specified criteria.
6. **Human Verification:** A small-scale human verification involving 3 volunteer annotators was conducted on 126 random samples. Annotators assessed whether the extracted principle and its binary fulfillment aligned with the natural language feedback. An inter-rater agreement (Fleiss’ κ) of 0.447 (moderate) was observed, and the majority annotator's judgment agreed with the extracted principle in 88.9% of cases.
7. **Principle Distribution:** The final dataset contained 1,414 unique principles, predominantly single-word (e.g., clarity, accuracy, relevance), indicating a diverse semantic coverage.

**Reward Model Training:**
Two types of reward models were trained:
1. **Scalar Reward Models (Flexible Principles ScalarRM):** These models, built on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, predict either "Yes" or "No" as a single token, given a prompt, response, and principle. The reward is calculated as `log P(Yes) - log P(No)`. This approach is computationally efficient, requiring minimal inference compute (<0.1 seconds per task). Training involved 1 epoch using the NeMo-Aligner framework, AdamW optimizer, with a global batch size of 128 and max sequence length of 4096.
2. **Generative Reward Models (Flexible Principles GenRM):** Initialized from Qwen3-32B, these models are designed for more complex tasks requiring step-by-step reasoning. They are prompted to reason through the task and principle before outputting "Final Judgment: Yes" or "Final Judgment: No." The reward calculation is identical to the Scalar RMs. Training utilized the GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) algorithm within the NeMo-RL framework for 3 epochs, with a batch size of 128 and max sequence length of 8192. These models are substantially more compute-intensive (>10 seconds per task).

**Evaluation Benchmarks:**
Reward models were evaluated on three benchmarks:
1. **RM-Bench (Liu et al., 2024):** A benchmark assessing reward models on correctness across diverse domains (Chat, Math, Code, Safety).
2. **JudgeBench (Tan et al., 2025):** Focuses on correctness in areas like Knowledge, Reasoning, Math, and Coding. It employs a pairwise evaluation, requiring models to correctly identify the chosen response regardless of its position to mitigate position bias.
3. **PrincipleBench (Novel Contribution):** A new human-annotated benchmark introduced by the authors to specifically evaluate reward models' adherence to explicit principles beyond correctness (e.g., clarity of expression, avoidance of repetition, language compliance). It comprises 487 samples derived from the HelpSteer3-Preference validation set, with 3-annotator consensus for binary principles.

**Baselines:**
Comparisons were made against Bradley-Terry models, fixed-principle models, and several external state-of-the-art Reward Models including Llama-3.3/3.1-Nemotron-70B-Reward, Llama-3.3-Nemotron-Super-49B-GenRM, RewardAnything-8B-v1, RM-R1-DeepSeek-Distilled-Qwen-32B, and R3-QWEN3-14B-LORA-4K.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Group Similarity Threshold:** The impact of varying the cosine similarity threshold (0.7, 0.8, 0.9) for principle consensus filtering on model performance was investigated.
- **Fixed Principle at Test Time:** The Flexible Principles ScalarRM was evaluated with a fixed principle ("Accuracy of Information") at test time to assess its performance when the user does not specify a flexible principle.

**Model Alignment:**
The Qwen3-32B LLM was aligned using Reinforcement Learning (GRPO algorithm) with the best-performing Flexible Principles GenRM as the reward model. The actor policy generated multiple candidate responses, which the GenRM then evaluated against the specific principle associated with each training sample. The actor was trained to optimize `log P(Yes) - log P(No)` as rated by the GenRM.

**Alignment Evaluation:**
Aligned models were evaluated on:
- **MT-Bench (Zheng et al., 2023):** Assessed by GPT-4-Turbo judge with human-verified references.
- **Arena Hard v2 (Li et al., 2024):** Evaluated using a Gemini-2.5-Pro judge. This benchmark focuses on challenging real-world, multilingual questions, particularly in coding and math.
- **WildBench (Lin et al., 2025):** Evaluated with a GPT-4o-05-13 judge, covering diverse real-world prompts.
Comparisons were made against Qwen3-32B (base model), a Bradley-Terry aligned Qwen3-32B, and external models like o3-mini, Claude-3.7-Sonnet (Thinking), and DeepSeek R1. Inference costs were also compared.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the performance of RLBFF-trained reward models and their utility in LLM alignment.

**Reward Model Performance:**
- **Top Performance on Benchmarks:** The **Flexible Principles ScalarRM** achieved an overall score of 83.6% on RM-Bench, 76.3% on JudgeBench, and 91.6% on the newly introduced PrincipleBench. This performance surpassed baseline Bradley-Terry models and other external scalar reward models. When scaled to a **Flexible Principles Generative Reward Model (GenRM)**, performance further improved to 86.2% on RM-Bench and 81.4% on JudgeBench. Notably, the Flexible Principles GenRM secured the top position on the JudgeBench leaderboard at the time of publication (September 24, 2025).
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **Bradley-Terry models:** Our Flexible Principles ScalarRM significantly outperformed the Bradley-Terry baseline (e.g., 83.6% vs. 78.5% on RM-Bench, 76.3% vs. 68.9% on JudgeBench).
 - **External Scalar RMs:** The Flexible Principles ScalarRM also outperformed external scalar RMs such as Llama-3.3-Nemotron-70B-Reward (83.6% vs. 79.9% on RM-Bench, 76.3% vs. 73.7% on JudgeBench).
 - **External Generative RMs:** The Flexible Principles GenRM achieved higher overall scores than all external Generative RMs on both RM-Bench (86.2% vs. 84.9% for R3-QWEN3-14B-LORA-4K) and JudgeBench (81.4% vs. 75.1% for Llama-3.3-Nemotron-Super-49B-GenRM).
- **GenRM Underperformance on PrincipleBench:** Despite excelling on RM-Bench and JudgeBench, GenRMs, including our Flexible Principles GenRM (83.8%), generally underperformed Scalar RMs on PrincipleBench. This is hypothesized to be due to GenRMs often being initialized from reasoning models, which may over-index on correctness and logical reasoning, leading to a lesser focus on other qualitative principles like clarity or absence of repetition. This highlights the utility of PrincipleBench in revealing nuanced aspects of reward model capabilities beyond correctness.
- **Mitigation of Position Bias:** Poor performance of some baseline GenRMs on JudgeBench was attributed to substantial position bias in pairwise evaluations. For instance, RewardAnything-8B-v1 achieved 77.1% when the chosen response was presented first but dropped to 65.1% when the rejected response was first, resulting in a lower overall score of 62.6% when both orders needed to agree. RLBFF's individual response rating approach effectively averts this bias.
- **User-Specified Principles at Low Latency:** The Flexible Principles ScalarRM is presented as the first Scalar RM capable of grounding reward assignment by user-specified principles, operating at inference speeds of <0.1 seconds per task. This contrasts with previous generative reasoning models that offered similar flexibility but required significantly more compute (e.g., >100x slower).

**Ablation Study Results:**
- **Group Similarity Threshold:** An ablation study on the cosine similarity threshold for filtering consensus principles found that a threshold of 0.8 achieved optimal performance on RM-Bench and JudgeBench, striking a balance between data quantity and quality.
- **Fixed Principle at Test Time:** Training with flexible principles (Flexible Principles ScalarRM) was found to be beneficial even when only a single principle (e.g., "Accuracy of Information") was used at test time. This model outperformed a Scalar RM explicitly trained only on that single fixed principle, suggesting that training on a diverse set of principles can generalize positively to specific principle evaluations.

**Model Alignment Results:**
- **Competitive Alignment with Cost Efficiency:** When Qwen3-32B was aligned using the RLBFF technique with the Flexible Principles GenRM, it achieved competitive performance compared to powerful proprietary models. The RLBFF-aligned Qwen3-32B scored 9.50 on MT-Bench, 55.6% (95% CI) on Arena Hard v2, and 70.33% overall on WildBench. These scores matched or exceeded those of o3-mini (9.26 MT-Bench, 50.0% Arena Hard v2), DeepSeek R1 (9.49 MT-Bench, 57.4% Arena Hard v2), and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (Thinking) (8.93 MT-Bench, 54.2% Arena Hard v2).
- **Significant Inference Cost Reduction:** This competitive performance was achieved at a substantially lower inference cost. The RLBFF-aligned Qwen3-32B maintains the inference cost of the base Qwen3-32B (0.018 $/M input tokens, 0.072 $/M output tokens), which is 24 to 187 times cheaper than the compared models (e.g., o3-mini at 1.1 $/M input, 4.4 $/M output). This translates to less than 5% of the inference cost compared to the cheapest alternative.
- **Superior to Baseline BT Alignment:** The RLBFF alignment also substantially outperformed the Qwen3-32B aligned with a baseline Bradley-Terry reward model (9.50 vs. 9.45 on MT-Bench, 55.6% vs. 47.5% on Arena Hard v2).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Reinforcement Learning with Binary Flexible Feedback (RLBFF) represents a notable advancement in the landscape of Large Language Model (LLM) post-training. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

1. **Bridging Paradigms for Holistic Feedback:** RLBFF effectively integrates the strengths of RLHF (wide coverage from human preferences) and RLVR (interpretability and precision from verifiable rewards). This hybrid approach addresses critical limitations of both, offering a more comprehensive and robust method for capturing nuanced aspects of response quality. It provides a structured way to distill implicit human judgments into explicit, binary principles, moving beyond the binary correctness focus of RLVR and the opaque preference judgments of RLHF.

2. **Enhanced Interpretability and Reduced Reward Hacking:** By explicitly defining and training reward models on specific principles (e.g., "accuracy of facts," "code readability"), RLBFF significantly enhances the interpretability of reward signals. This allows researchers and users to understand *why* a response is rated favorably or unfavorably, mitigating the "black-box" problem. This explicit grounding also inherently reduces the risk of reward hacking, as models are optimized against clearly articulated criteria rather than superficial correlations.

3. **Customizable and Flexible Reward Models:** A substantial impact of RLBFF, particularly with the Flexible Principles ScalarRM, is the ability for users to specify custom principles at inference time. This offers an unprecedented level of control and flexibility, allowing users to tailor the reward model's focus to specific use cases, preferences, or domain requirements without retraining. This capability is efficient, requiring minimal computational resources (<0.1 seconds per task), making it practical for latency-sensitive applications.

4. **New Standard for Reward Model Evaluation:** The creation of **PrincipleBench** fills a critical gap in existing evaluation benchmarks. While RM-Bench and JudgeBench primarily focus on correctness, PrincipleBench enables the evaluation of reward models' adherence to a diverse set of qualitative principles. This new benchmark encourages the development of more sophisticated reward models that can discern and optimize for a broader spectrum of desirable LLM behaviors, moving beyond mere factual accuracy. The findings on GenRM underperformance on PrincipleBench highlight the importance of such diverse evaluation.

5. **Democratizing High-Performance LLM Alignment:** The demonstration of aligning Qwen3-32B with RLBFF to match or exceed the performance of proprietary, state-of-the-art models (like o3-mini and DeepSeek R1) on general alignment benchmarks (MT-Bench, WildBench, Arena Hard v2) is highly significant. Crucially, this is achieved using an entirely open-source recipe and data, at a drastically reduced inference cost (less than 5% of alternatives). This advancement lowers the barrier to entry for developing and deploying highly aligned LLMs, promoting wider access to advanced AI capabilities and fostering further innovation in the open-source community.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens several avenues for future research, including exploring the optimal balance between reasoning capabilities and principle adherence in generative reward models, investigating RLBFF's application in highly domain-specific alignment tasks where qualitative principles are paramount (e.g., creative writing, character roleplay), and further refining the principle extraction and consensus mechanisms.
