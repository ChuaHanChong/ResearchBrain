# RLBFF: Binary Flexible Feedback to bridge between Human Feedback & Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers introduced Reinforcement Learning with Binary Flexible Feedback (RLBFF), a new paradigm for large language model post-training that extracts explicit, binary principles from human feedback. LLMs aligned with RLBFF achieved competitive performance on general alignment benchmarks, such as a 9.50 MT-Bench score, at less than 5% of the inference cost compared to state-of-the-art proprietary models.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) lacks interpretability and is prone to reward hacking due to implicit human judgment criteria.
- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is precise but has limited applicability to tasks with objective correctness and can suffer from low recall.
- Prior approaches using binary feedback or generative reward models often lacked user control over principles or broad coverage beyond narrow domains.

## Method

- Proposed Reinforcement Learning with ==Binary Flexible Feedback== (**RLBFF**), a framework where a reward model evaluates a response against a specific principle in a binary (Yes/No) fashion.
- Created a training dataset by extracting 1,414 distinct, fine-grained principles and their binary fulfillment from natural language human feedback using an LLM, applying stringent filtering for consensus and accuracy.
- Trained both efficient Flexible Principles Scalar Reward Models and reasoning-capable Flexible Principles Generative Reward Models to provide explicit rewards based on these principles.

## Results

- Flexible Principles ScalarRMs achieved an **83.6%** score on RM-Bench, **76.3%** on JudgeBench, and **91.6%** on PrincipleBench, outperforming baseline and external scalar reward models.
- The Flexible Principles Generative Reward Model reached **86.2%** on RM-Bench and **81.4%** on JudgeBench, securing the top position on the JudgeBench leaderboard.
- An LLM aligned with RLBFF achieved competitive performance (e.g., 9.50 MT-Bench, **55.6%** on Arena Hard v2) compared to proprietary models, with a **24x** to **187x** reduction in inference cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly defined, binary principles extracted from human feedback offer a structured and interpretable bridge between broad human preferences and precise, verifiable rewards.
> - Training reward models on a diverse set of flexible principles significantly improves generalization, even when evaluating against a single specific principle.
> - Evaluating single responses against a principle is more aligned with natural human feedback patterns and effectively mitigates position bias inherent in pairwise comparison methods.
