# UDM-GRPO: Stable and Efficient Group Relative Policy Optimization for Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> UDM-GRPO offers a stable and efficient framework for applying Group Relative Policy Optimization to Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models, enhancing text-to-image generation. This approach improved compositional generation scores (GenEval from 0.69 to 0.96), human preference alignment (PickScore from 20.46 to 23.81), and visual text rendering (OCR accuracy from 8% to 57%) on a 1.7B-parameter URSA model.

## Problem

- Pretrained Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models (UDMs) struggle with fine-grained human preference alignment and complex compositional image generation tasks.
- Direct application of reinforcement learning (RL) to UDMs leads to training instability, characterized by oscillating rewards and exploding KL divergence.
- Instability arises from using inaccurate intermediate denoising predictions as policy actions and a distribution shift when training on reverse-process generated trajectories.

## Method

- The **UDM-GRPO** framework redefines the policy action at all timesteps as the final clean image to provide a consistent and precise reward signal.
- It constructs training trajectories using the forward diffusion process, ensuring alignment with the pretraining data distribution and mitigating out-of-distribution learning.
- Efficiency is enhanced through reduced-step optimization, focusing gradient updates on early, high-noise diffusion stages, and Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)-Free training to simplify the process.

## Results

- UDM-GRPO significantly improved a 1.7B URSA model's compositional generation (GenEval from **0.69** to **0.96**), human preference alignment (PickScore from **20.46** to **23.81**), and OCR accuracy (**8%** to **57%**).
- The method achieved stable convergence with sustained reward improvement and bounded KL divergence, avoiding baseline RL collapse; ablations confirmed the novel action definition and forward-process trajectory reconstruction were critical to this stability.
- Efficiency strategies, including reduced-step and CFG-Free training, led to faster convergence with CFG-Free surpassing CFG-based methods for text rendering; UDM-GRPO also generalized effectively, improving the FUDOKI model across GenEval, PickScore, and OCR benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning policy actions with the ultimate reward target (the final generated image), and training on trajectories reconstructed via the true forward diffusion process, are both crucial for stable and effective RL fine-tuning of discrete diffusion models, preventing instability from distribution shift.
> - Concentrating gradient updates on the more impactful early diffusion timesteps can significantly accelerate convergence and improve efficiency for discrete generative models.
> - Eliminating Classifier-Free Guidance during RL fine-tuning can enhance training efficiency and policy exploration, leading to improved overall performance, particularly for specific tasks like OCR.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual generative models has experienced advancements, particularly with the emergence of diffusion models for synthesizing images. Within this domain, Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models (UDMs) have been established as a paradigm for discrete generative modeling, characterized by their use of parallel token updates and progressive refinement, which have been observed to outperform earlier mask-based methods. Examples of models employing this paradigm include Fudoki, Next-Omni, and URSA.

Despite these advancements, pretrained generative models often exhibit limitations in tasks requiring precise alignment with human preferences or complex compositional generation. Concurrently, Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), has demonstrated efficacy in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). This success has prompted extensions of GRPO to visual generation tasks, including autoregressive models and various diffusion models. Notably, Flow-GRPO has shown gains by framing the denoising process as a policy optimization problem within continuous diffusion settings.

However, the integration of RL, particularly GRPO, with Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models remains largely unexplored. This research addresses this gap by proposing a framework for stable and efficient reinforcement learning in the context of UDMs, aiming to leverage RL's strengths for preference alignment and complex generation while adapting it to the specific characteristics of discrete diffusion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to integrate Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models (UDMs) with Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), for text-to-image (T2I) generation. This objective is motivated by the observation that while UDMs demonstrate promising generative capabilities, they often fall short in tasks requiring fine-grained alignment with human preferences or intricate compositional understanding, areas where RL has proven beneficial.

The authors initially explored a direct adaptation of the Flow-GRPO framework to UDMs, defining the policy action as the intermediate predicted sample at each timestep and optimizing along the reverse sampling process. However, this preliminary integration encountered significant instability, characterized by violent reward oscillation and exploding KL divergence after an initial period of transient gains. The authors attribute this instability to two core issues:

1. **Inaccurate Intermediate Actions (Problem I)**: In the reverse denoising process, predictions made during early diffusion steps are often high-entropy and noisy. Treating these unreliable intermediate predictions as policy actions compels the model to learn from inconsistent or incorrect signals, despite the final output potentially receiving a positive reward. This misalignment in optimization signals destabilizes the training process.
2. **Biased Distribution of Backward Trajectory (Problem II)**: During pretraining, UDMs learn to predict a clean image from a noisy input generated via a defined forward diffusion process. When RL fine-tuning is performed on the model's self-generated trajectories (the backward process), a distribution shift occurs. The intermediate states encountered during the backward process deviate from the training manifold of the forward process, leading to out-of-distribution (OOD) training and a biased probability path.

Beyond addressing these stability challenges, a secondary motivation is to improve the efficiency of RL training for UDMs. Standard optimization across all diffusion timesteps can disperse gradients, leading to slow convergence. Additionally, the computational overhead associated with Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), commonly used in diffusion models, presents an efficiency concern. The research aims to develop strategies to mitigate these efficiency issues.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Develop a stable and effective framework for integrating GRPO with Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models.
- Resolve the instability issues arising from inaccurate intermediate actions and biased backward trajectories.
- Enhance training efficiency through targeted optimization strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, UDM-GRPO, addresses the identified challenges through a set of core strategies and efficiency enhancements. The method builds upon the foundations of Uniform Discrete Diffusion and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), while explicitly reformulating the RL problem for discrete generative models.

**Initial Pilot Integration and its Limitations:**
The research began by adapting Flow-GRPO, which is designed for continuous diffusion, to Uniform Discrete Diffusion. This involved:
- Defining the policy action ($a_t$) as the intermediate predicted clean sample ($x_t^1$) at each timestep $t$.
- Formulating the policy probability $\pi_\theta(a_t | s_t)$ as $p_\theta(x_t^1 | x_t, c)$, where $s_t=(c, t, x_t)$ is the state.
- Optimizing along the reverse sampling process ($X_{backward}$).
This direct adaptation was found to be unstable, exhibiting optimization collapse and exploding KL divergence.

**UDM-GRPO Core Strategies (Addressing Instability):**
To resolve the instability issues, UDM-GRPO introduces two main modifications:

1. **Accurate Action Definition (Key Insight I):** Instead of using the intermediate predicted sample ($x_t^1$) as the action, UDM-GRPO redefines the policy action ($a_t$) at all timesteps as the *final clean sample* (ˆx_1). This is motivated by the fact that the reward is typically defined solely on the final generated image. By making the action consistent with the ultimate reward target, the optimization signal becomes more precise and stable, avoiding reliance on noisy early-step predictions. The reformulated policy is $\pi(a_t | s_t) \triangleq p_\theta(\hat{x}_1 | \hat{x}_t, c)$.

2. **Forward-Process Trajectory Reconstruction (Key Insight II):** To mitigate the distribution shift caused by optimizing on the model's self-generated reverse trajectories ($X_{backward}$), UDM-GRPO constructs training trajectories using the *forward diffusion process* ($X_{forward}$). After an initial clean sample (ˆx_1) is generated by the model, it is then perturbed using the *true forward diffusion process* to obtain noisy intermediate states (ˆx_t). This ensures that the training trajectory (ˆx_t) aligns more closely with the distribution encountered during pretraining ($X_{pretrain}$), thus reducing out-of-distribution learning.

With these modifications, the T-step Markov Decision Process (MDP) is reformulated as:
- State ($s_t$): $( \hat{x}_t, t, c)$
- Action ($a_t$): $\hat{x}_1$ (the final clean sample)
- Policy ($\pi(a_t | s_t)$): $p_\theta(\hat{x}_1 | \hat{x}_t, c)$
- Reward ($R(s, a)$): $r(\hat{x}_1, c)$ (terminal-only, based on the final clean sample)
The policy optimization objective remains the standard GRPO form, maximizing the advantage-weighted policy ratio while regularizing with KL divergence to a reference policy.

**Efficiency Enhancement Strategies:**

1. **Reduced-Step Accelerated Optimization:** Recognizing that early diffusion timesteps exhibit higher noise and prediction errors, UDM-GRPO focuses optimization efforts on these critical stages. For each sample, three consecutive timesteps are randomly selected from the *first half* of the total diffusion timesteps for policy optimization. This strategy aims to concentrate gradients on more impactful stages, thereby accelerating convergence.

2. **CFG-Free Training:** To reduce computational overhead and simplify the training process, Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is eliminated during training. This removes the need for simultaneous optimization of conditional and unconditional objectives. While initial generation quality may be affected, the hypothesis is that RL training can compensate for this, potentially leading to a broader exploration of the policy distribution and improved final performance.

**Implementation Details:**
The framework fine-tunes a pre-trained URSA text-to-image model (1.7B parameters). Training involves group sampling (16 groups of 8 image samples per batch) using the AdamW optimizer with a constant learning rate of 1e-6. Training is conducted on 32 A100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of UDM-GRPO involved integrating the method with a 1.7B-parameter URSA model and assessing its performance across three tasks: Compositional Image Generation (GenEval), Human Preference Alignment (PickScore), and Visual Text Rendering (OCR).

**Overall Performance Improvement:**
UDM-GRPO demonstrated performance improvements over the base URSA model and established competitive or superior results compared to other state-of-the-art models in both continuous and discrete settings:
- **GenEval (Compositional Image Generation):** The overall GenEval score for URSA improved from 0.69 to 0.96. This represents a substantial gain, with UDM-GRPO achieving state-of-the-art performance. Notably, it attained scores of 1.00 for Single Object and Two Object categories, 0.95 for Counting, 0.97 for Colors and Position, and 0.85 for Attribute Binding.
- **PickScore (Human Preference Alignment):** The PickScore increased from 20.46 (for URSA without CFG) to 23.81, also achieving state-of-the-art performance.
- **OCR (Visual Text Rendering):** The accuracy on the OCR benchmark improved significantly from 8% to 57%, demonstrating the method's generalization capabilities to tasks beyond typical image generation.

**Stability and Convergence:**
Comparative reward-step training curves illustrated a marked difference in stability. The baseline model exhibited optimization collapse after approximately 500 steps, characterized by violent reward oscillation and exploding KL divergence. In contrast, UDM-GRPO achieved stable convergence with a sustained reward improvement and maintained a bounded KL divergence, validating the robustness of the framework.

**Qualitative Comparisons:**
Qualitative results (Figure 5) indicated that RL post-training with UDM-GRPO led to visual improvements in generated images. For compositional prompts from GenEval, the URSA model showed enhanced spatial arrangement, attribute binding, and object counting. On PickScore prompts, UDM-GRPO produced images with finer details, reduced artifacts, and preserved style consistency.

**Ablation Studies:**
Detailed ablation studies investigated the impact of key components:
- **Action Choice (Final Clean Sample vs. Intermediate Predicted Sample):** Replacing the intermediate predicted sample ($x_t^1$) with the final clean sample (ˆx_1) as the action consistently improved training performance on GenEval and PickScore (e.g., GenEval from 0.84 to 0.89) and resulted in lower KL divergence, indicating more stable optimization.
- **Trajectory Choice (Forward vs. Backward Optimization):** Utilizing the forward-process-based trajectory ($X_{forward}$) instead of the backward trajectory ($X_{backward}$) for state construction led to superior performance. While both methods showed similar early performance, backward optimization eventually suffered from slow convergence and collapse (GenEval reaching approx. 0.89), whereas forward optimization improved smoothly and stably (reaching 0.95). Forward optimization also consistently yielded lower KL divergence, suggesting improved stability.
- **CFG-Free Training:** The CFG-Free approach, despite initially showing lower sample quality, eventually surpassed CFG-based training. It converged faster and achieved a lower KL divergence, indicating enhanced training efficiency and stability. On the OCR task, CFG-Free training particularly outperformed its CFG-based counterpart, suggesting that removing CFG broadened the policy distribution, enabling more effective exploration during RL.

**Reduced-Step Accelerated Optimization:**
Experiments with different timestep optimization strategies showed that concentrating optimization on early high-noise timesteps yielded a clear advantage in convergence speed and performance for GenEval and PickScore, affirming the efficiency of this approach.

**Generalized Validation:**
The efficacy of UDM-GRPO was further validated on FUDOKI, another UDM-based multimodal large language model. UDM-GRPO consistently and significantly improved FUDOKI's performance across GenEval, PickScore, and OCR, confirming its broader applicability beyond a single base model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents UDM-GRPO as a framework that addresses a research gap by providing the first method for stably and efficiently integrating Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models (UDMs) for text-to-image (T2I) generation.

The primary significance of UDM-GRPO lies in its ability to overcome critical instability challenges encountered when directly applying RL to discrete diffusion processes. By re-conceptualizing the policy action as the final clean sample and reconstructing training trajectories via the forward diffusion process, the method ensures a more consistent and stable optimization signal, mitigating issues related to inaccurate intermediate actions and distribution shifts. This principled approach contributes to the robustness of RL fine-tuning for complex generative models.

The substantial performance improvements demonstrated across multiple T2I tasks, including compositional generation (GenEval), human preference alignment (PickScore), and visual text rendering (OCR), position UDM-GRPO as a method that enables base UDM models to achieve competitive or state-of-the-art results. The generalization capabilities of the method, as shown with its application to the FUDOKI model, suggest broad applicability across different UDM-based architectures.

Furthermore, the introduction of efficiency strategies, such as Reduced-Step and CFG-Free training, contributes to more practical RL fine-tuning. These strategies aim to reduce computational overhead and accelerate convergence without compromising final performance, which could be beneficial for deploying and iterating on large generative models.

The research expands the capabilities of discrete generative models, enabling them to align more effectively with complex objectives and human preferences. This advancement could lead to the development of more controllable and nuanced T2I systems. Potential future work includes extending the framework to text-to-video generation and optimizing for more challenging multi-reward tasks, indicating the foundational nature of this contribution to the field of machine learning.
