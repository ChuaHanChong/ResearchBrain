# UDM-GRPO: Stable and Efficient Group Relative Policy Optimization for Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> UDM-GRPO offers a stable and efficient framework for applying Group Relative Policy Optimization to Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models, enhancing text-to-image generation. This approach improved compositional generation scores (GenEval from 0.69 to 0.96), human preference alignment (PickScore from 20.46 to 23.81), and visual text rendering (OCR accuracy from 8% to 57%) on a 1.7B-parameter URSA model.

## Problem

- Pretrained Uniform Discrete Diffusion Models (UDMs) struggle with fine-grained human preference alignment and complex compositional image generation tasks.
- Direct application of reinforcement learning (RL) to UDMs leads to training instability, characterized by oscillating rewards and exploding KL divergence.
- Instability arises from using inaccurate intermediate denoising predictions as policy actions and a distribution shift when training on reverse-process generated trajectories.

## Method

- The **UDM-GRPO** framework redefines the policy action at all timesteps as the final clean image to provide a consistent and precise reward signal.
- It constructs training trajectories using the forward diffusion process, ensuring alignment with the pretraining data distribution and mitigating out-of-distribution learning.
- Efficiency is enhanced through reduced-step optimization, focusing gradient updates on early, high-noise diffusion stages, and Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)-Free training to simplify the process.

## Results

- UDM-GRPO significantly improved a 1.7B URSA model's compositional generation (GenEval from **0.69** to **0.96**), human preference alignment (PickScore from **20.46** to **23.81**), and OCR accuracy (**8%** to **57%**).
- The method achieved stable convergence with sustained reward improvement and bounded KL divergence, overcoming the optimization collapse observed in baseline RL attempts.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the novel action definition and forward-process trajectory reconstruction were critical for achieving improved stability and performance.
- Efficiency strategies, including reduced-step and CFG-Free training, led to faster convergence and better performance, with CFG-Free training surpassing CFG-based methods for text rendering tasks.
- UDM-GRPO generalized effectively, consistently improving performance on the FUDOKI model across GenEval, PickScore, and OCR benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning policy actions with the ultimate reward target (the final generated image) is crucial for stable and effective reinforcement learning in discrete diffusion models.
> - Training on trajectories reconstructed via the true forward diffusion process effectively addresses distribution shift problems, preventing instability during RL fine-tuning of UDMs.
> - Concentrating gradient updates on the more impactful early diffusion timesteps can significantly accelerate convergence and improve efficiency for discrete generative models.
> - Eliminating Classifier-Free Guidance during RL fine-tuning can enhance training efficiency and policy exploration, leading to improved overall performance, particularly for specific tasks like OCR.
