# Mind-VLA: Instruction-Aware Spatial Representation Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Mind-VLA enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating instruction-aware spatial representation alignment during training, focusing 3D supervision on the target object. This approach enables a 345M-parameter VLA model to achieve 93.9% average success on LIBERO and greater robustness to target occlusion in real-robot tasks compared to instruction-agnostic methods.

## Problem

- Existing 3D-aware VLA models are often instruction-agnostic, uniformly encoding entire scene 3D information without prioritizing the specific target object designated by language instructions.
- This instruction-agnostic approach weakens the connection between instructions and relevant 3D representations, hindering target discrimination and reliable manipulation of partially occluded objects.
- Achieving precise 3D understanding for tasks requiring fine-grained object manipulation, pose reasoning, and handling occlusions remains a challenge for generalist robotic manipulation models.

## Method

- **Mind-VLA** introduces ==instruction-aware spatial representation alignment== during VLA model training, using target-object specific 3D supervision.
- It employs pre-prepared canonical "==tri-views==" (top, front, side orthographic views) of the target object, encoded into latent representations and ==VGGT== features, to supervise dedicated object queries within the VLA backbone.
- The auxiliary supervision modules are removed during inference, ensuring the VLA model operates with standard inputs and negligible additional computational overhead.

## Results

- Mind-VLA achieved an average success rate of **93.9%** on the LIBERO benchmark and an average completed length of **4.47** on CALVIN ABC-D, matching or exceeding larger 7B-class VLA models with a **345M-parameter** backbone.
- In real-robot experiments, Mind-VLA demonstrated an **11 percentage point** improvement over its scene-image VGGT ablation and a **19 percentage point** improvement over the Seer baseline on normal tasks.
- Under **~25%** target occlusion, Mind-VLA's success rate degraded by only **13 percentage points**, outperforming instruction-agnostic baselines that saw degradation of **21-29 percentage points**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly focusing 3D supervision on the instruction-specified target object during training improves VLA models' ability to discriminate targets and perform fine-grained manipulations.
> - Providing a canonical-view prior through target-object tri-views enhances the VLA model's robustness against partial target occlusion in real-world environments.
> - Instruction-aware spatial alignment can enable compact VLA models (e.g., 345M parameters) to achieve performance comparable to or surpassing much larger instruction-agnostic models, while maintaining efficient inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a current approach for generalist robotic manipulation, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and visual observations to execute actions. A key challenge for these models involves achieving robust 3D understanding, which is crucial for tasks requiring precise object manipulation, reasoning about object pose and spatial relations, and handling occlusions.

Existing 3D-aware VLA methods generally follow two main paradigms to incorporate 3D information:
1. **Explicit 3D Information as Input:** Some methods provide explicit 3D data, such as point cloud features, RGB-D observations, or rendered orthographic views, directly as model inputs. While these approaches can enhance spatial reasoning, they often necessitate depth sensors, depth estimation, or additional 3D processing during inference, thereby increasing deployment costs.
2. **Training-Time Alignment with 3D Features:** Another category of methods extracts 3D scene features using frozen 3D foundation models and aligns the VLA model's internal representations with these features during training. This strategy aims to keep the model input unchanged at inference, reducing deployment complexity and cost, and has become a prevalent approach for developing 3D-aware VLAs.

The paper identifies a limitation within both existing paradigms: they are often "instruction-agnostic." This means that the 3D information, whether input or used for supervision, typically encompasses the entire scene uniformly, without specifically prioritizing the 3D geometry of the particular target object designated by the language instruction. This uniform scene encoding can weaken the connection between the instruction and the relevant 3D representation, potentially hindering the model's ability to discriminate between visually similar objects or reliably manipulate partially occluded targets. Success in such tasks often depends on a precise 3D understanding of the *target object* rather than the overall scene.

Mind-VLA positions itself within the second paradigm (training-time alignment) but introduces a modification: it proposes an "instruction-aware" spatial representation alignment. By focusing the 3D supervision on the instruction-specified target object, Mind-VLA aims to enhance the VLA model's target-specific 3D understanding, thereby addressing the limitations observed in prior instruction-agnostic approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that current 3D-aware Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit a fundamental limitation: they are instruction-agnostic in their spatial representation alignment. This means that the 3D information integrated into these models, whether as direct input or through training supervision, uniformly represents the entire scene. Consequently, the distinct 3D geometry of the specific target object, as designated by the language instruction, is not explicitly emphasized or prioritized within the model's representations.

This instruction-agnostic approach leads to several challenges:
- **Weakened Instruction-3D Connection:** Encoding the target object, distractor objects, and background structures together can dilute the explicit link between the language instruction and the precise 3D characteristics of the intended object.
- **Difficult Fine-Grained Manipulation:** For manipulation tasks requiring high precision or interaction with specific parts of an object, a lack of detailed, target-focused 3D understanding can result in failures.
- **Reduced Reliability with Occlusion:** When a target object is partially occluded, existing models may struggle because they have not been explicitly trained to maintain a robust 3D representation of the instructed object under incomplete visual information. The paper posits that success in these scenarios depends on accurate 3D understanding of the target object, not just the complete scene.
- **Target Discrimination Issues:** In environments with visually similar objects, instruction-agnostic supervision may complicate the VLA model's ability to differentiate the specified target from distractors.

Against this background, the key objectives of the Mind-VLA research are:
- To develop an instruction-aware spatial representation alignment method for VLA models.
- To enable VLA models to explicitly encode and understand the 3D geometry of the target object specified by the language instruction, rather than relying solely on scene-level 3D understanding.
- To improve the generalization and robustness of VLA models, particularly for tasks involving fine-grained manipulation and situations where the target object is partially occluded.
- To integrate this instruction-aware alignment as a training-only auxiliary objective, ensuring that it enhances the VLA model's capabilities without incurring additional computational overhead or requiring specialized sensors during inference time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mind-VLA is designed as an instruction-aware spatial representation alignment method that operates during the training phase of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The approach builds upon existing VLA architectures but augments their training with target-object specific 3D supervision.

**Base VLA Method (Preliminaries):**
The foundation of Mind-VLA utilizes a common VLA framework that maps a language instruction (ℓ), visual observations (I_t), and proprioceptive state (s_t) to a sequence of future robot actions (A_t).
- **Inputs and Tokenization:** At each timestep, two RGB images (from primary and wrist cameras) are processed by a frozen vision encoder. The language instruction is encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, and the proprioceptive state by a linear projection. These tokens, alongside learnable action queries, are fed into a causal transformer backbone.
- **Action Prediction:** The transformer's outputs at the action-query positions condition a diffusion action head, which predicts a chunk of future 7-D actions. This head is trained using an L2 loss to predict Gaussian noise added to the ground-truth action chunk.
- **Training-time Auxiliary Supervision (Existing VLAs):** Traditional VLA training often includes auxiliary losses for scene-level signals, such as per-patch RGB reconstruction (L_obs) and dense 2D motion prediction (L_traj), which aim to preserve workspace appearance and short-term dynamics. Some 3D-aware VLAs also use an additional loss (L_3D) to align image-token features with scene-level 3D targets provided by external teachers or estimators. Mind-VLA replaces this scene-level L_3D with its instruction-aware approach.

**Mind-VLA's Instruction-Aware Spatial Representation Alignment:**
The core contribution of Mind-VLA lies in its instruction-aware auxiliary supervision, which specifically targets the 3D geometry of the object referred to by the language instruction.
- **Query Introduction:** Mind-VLA introduces two new sets of learnable queries into the transformer backbone:
 - **Scene Queries (q_s_t):** These queries are decoded by auxiliary heads to supervise the scene-level objectives (L_obs and L_traj), ensuring preservation of general scene dynamics and appearance.
 - **Object Queries (q_tri_t):** These queries are designed to be supervised by instruction-aware 3D features of the target object.
- **Target Object Identification:** For each given language instruction (ℓ), the system implicitly identifies the target object, denoted as m(ℓ).
- **Target-Object Tri-view Preparation:** This is an offline preprocessing step. For each target object, a canonical "tri-view" (consisting of top, front, and side orthographic views) is generated.
 - In **simulation environments**, these views are pre-rendered from the object's 3D mesh.
 - For **real-robot experiments**, each object is photographed once from three approximately canonical viewpoints using a handheld camera.
 This offline preparation means the supervision is invariant to online viewpoint changes or occlusion during actual task execution.
- **Tri-view Prediction (L_tri):**
 - Each of the three views in the target-object tri-view is encoded offline using a frozen Stable Diffusion VAE into a compact, stacked latent representation, Z_m(ℓ).
 - The object query tokens (q_tri_t) from the VLA backbone are then decoded by a small Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) into a predicted latent representation, ˆZ_t.
 - A Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss (L_tri) is applied between ˆZ_t and the ground-truth target-object latent Z_m(ℓ). This loss encourages the VLA model's object queries to encode the geometry of the instruction-specified target object.
- **Multi-level Alignment with VGGT (L_geo):**
 - In addition to tri-view latent prediction, Mind-VLA applies feature-level supervision to four intermediate layers of the VLA backbone.
 - For each layer, VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer) features (g_m(ℓ),j) are extracted from the target-object tri-view (mean-pooled across views and spatial positions).
 - Corresponding mean-pooled image-token features (¯h_t,j) from the VLA transformer layer are projected (ϕ_j) and aligned with g_m(ℓ),j using a cosine distance loss (L_geo).
 - This component guides the intermediate VLA features to capture the specific 3D structure of the instructed object, differentiating it from prior methods that use VGGT on the full scene image.
- **Full Training Objective:** The final training objective combines the action prediction loss, scene-level auxiliary losses, and the instruction-aware spatial alignment losses:
 L_MindVLA = L_VLA + λ_tri * L_tri + λ_geo * L_geo,
 where L_VLA includes L_act, L_obs, and L_traj.
- **Inference Time:** Crucially, during inference, all auxiliary modules—the VAE encoder, VGGT, and auxiliary decoders for tri-view prediction and VGGT alignment—are removed. The VLA model then operates with the same RGB, language, and proprioceptive state inputs as a baseline VLA, incurring negligible additional computational cost from the auxiliary query tokens in the backbone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Mind-VLA included standard benchmarks and real-robot experiments, demonstrating its performance and robustness.

#### 4.1. Main Benchmark Results

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** Mind-VLA achieved an average success rate of 93.9% across the Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long task suites.
 - This performance matched or slightly exceeded that of significantly larger 7B-class VLA models, such as π0 (94.2%) and GLaD (94.1%), and outperformed CogACT (93.6%). Mind-VLA accomplished this with a compact 345M-parameter backbone, which is 9 to 20 times smaller than these large models.
 - Among compact-backbone methods, Mind-VLA surpassed DreamVLA by an average of 1.3 percentage points, with a notable +4.0 percentage point improvement on the LIBERO-Object suite.
- **CALVIN ABC-D Benchmark:** On the long-horizon CALVIN ABC-D benchmark, Mind-VLA achieved an average completed length of 4.47.
 - This result exceeded state-of-the-art methods including VPP (4.29), Seer (4.28), and the large-backbone π0 (3.92), indicating improved performance on complex, chained manipulation tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

A systematic ablation study on LIBERO investigated the contribution of Mind-VLA's components:

- **Progressive Component Addition:**
 - Starting from a baseline with only image reconstruction (89.5% average success), adding scene-level trajectory prediction yielded a +1.8 percentage point increase (91.3%).
 - Further adding target-object tri-view latent prediction contributed an additional +1.2 percentage points, reaching 92.5% average success.
- **Instruction-Aware vs. Instruction-Agnostic Alignment:**
 - Comparing Mind-VLA's target-object tri-view VGGT alignment (93.9% average) against an instruction-agnostic scene-image VGGT alignment (92.8% average) revealed that the instruction-aware approach led to a 1.1 percentage point overall improvement. The largest difference was observed on LIBERO-Object, where Mind-VLA achieved +4.0 percentage points. The scene-image variant showed a slight advantage on LIBERO-Long tasks, suggesting a potential benefit from broader scene context in certain long-horizon scenarios.
- **Object Focus vs. Canonical-view Prior:**
 - To differentiate the impact of merely focusing on the object versus providing canonical views, an ablation replaced the tri-view target with an instruction-aware SAM crop from the current image. This crop variant achieved 96.5% on LIBERO-Object, improving over scene-image VGGT (94.0%) but still trailing the full tri-view supervision (98.0%). This suggests that object focus contributes approximately +2.5 percentage points, while the canonical-view prior from the tri-view provides an additional +1.5 percentage points in clean simulation.

#### 4.3. Real Robot Experiments

Mind-VLA was deployed on a UFactory xArm 6 robot, evaluating three task categories (Pick, Place, Drawer) under normal and occluded conditions:

- **Normal Tasks (Pick, Place, Drawer):** Mind-VLA achieved an average success rate of 69% across five tasks. This represented an 11 percentage point improvement over its scene-image VGGT ablation and a 19 percentage point improvement over the Seer baseline.
- **Occluded Tasks (∼25% Target Occlusion):** Under conditions where approximately 25% of the target object was occluded by a physical obstruction, Mind-VLA demonstrated greater robustness. Its average success rate degraded by only 13 percentage points (from 67% to 54%). In contrast, the scene-image VGGT ablation degraded by 29 percentage points (from 57% to 28%), Seer by 23 percentage points, and OpenVLA by 21 percentage points.
 - This result indicates that the canonical-view prior embedded in the target-object tri-view supervision is a significant factor in Mind-VLA's robustness to occlusion, extending beyond the benefit of mere object focus.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Mind-VLA presents an advancement in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by introducing instruction-aware spatial representation alignment. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood through several points:

- **Addressing a Core Limitation in VLA Models:** The research directly addresses the instruction-agnostic nature of 3D understanding in prior VLA models. By explicitly conditioning 3D supervision on the language instruction, Mind-VLA enables the model to focus its spatial reasoning on the specific target object, rather than treating the entire scene uniformly. This targeted approach is particularly relevant for tasks requiring fine-grained interaction and precise object identification.

- **Enhanced Robustness to Real-World Challenges:** A key impact is the demonstrated improvement in robustness to target occlusion. Real-robot experiments showed that Mind-VLA sustained significantly higher success rates under occluded conditions compared to instruction-agnostic baselines. This capability is crucial for deploying robotic systems in unstructured and complex environments where objects may frequently be partially obscured. The finding that canonical views contribute significantly to this robustness suggests a pathway for more resilient robotic perception.

- **Improved Performance with Computational Efficiency:** Mind-VLA achieves performance comparable to or surpassing larger-scale VLA models (e.g., 7B-class models) on standard benchmarks like LIBERO and CALVIN, despite utilizing a considerably more compact 345M-parameter backbone. This efficiency indicates potential for developing more accessible, deployable, and computationally lighter robotic systems without sacrificing task performance.

- **Minimal Inference-Time Overhead:** The instruction-aware alignment is designed as a training-only auxiliary objective. This means that at inference time, all supplementary modules are removed, ensuring that Mind-VLA maintains a low deployment cost and does not introduce additional computational latency or sensor requirements (like depth sensors) compared to standard VLA models. This aspect enhances its practicality for real-world applications.

- **Advancement in Generalist Robot Policies:** By improving the ability of VLA models to integrate language instructions with precise 3D object understanding, Mind-VLA contributes to the development of more capable and reliable generalist robot policies. Such policies are essential for robots to operate effectively across a diverse range of tasks and environments.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies limitations, such as the requirement for a bounded object vocabulary with pre-prepared tri-views and potential trade-offs on long-horizon tasks that might benefit from broader scene-level modeling. These limitations delineate clear avenues for future research, including investigating methods for zero-shot generalization to unseen objects and more integrated scene-level and object-level reasoning for complex multi-object tasks.
