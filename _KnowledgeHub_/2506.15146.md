# TACT: Humanoid Whole-body Contact Manipulation through Deep Imitation Learning with Tactile Modality

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at CNRS-AIST JRL and Tokyo University of Science developed TACT, a deep imitation learning framework that enables a life-size humanoid robot to perform whole-body contact manipulation using integrated visual, tactile, and proprioceptive feedback. The system demonstrated 100% success in holding medium and large fragile paper boxes, outperforming baselines and generalizing to unseen objects.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots are typically limited to manipulation involving only hands and feet, restricting their versatility for complex tasks in unstructured environments.
- Existing model-based control methods face high computational costs for real-time generation of multi-contact, whole-body motions.
- Accurately perceiving and processing distributed tactile feedback across large robot body surfaces remains a significant challenge for complex manipulation.

## Method

- A two-layer control architecture combines a model-based lower layer for stable locomotion and kinematic retargeting with a learning-based upper layer for manipulation.
- The **TACT** (Tactile-modality extended ACT) policy, a Transformer-based ==Conditional Variational Autoencoder==, processes multimodal inputs including proprioception, vision, and distributed tactile/proximity sensor data.
- The policy is trained via deep imitation learning from human teleoperation demonstrations, predicting future human arm poses which are then translated into physically constrained robot movements by the lower layer.

## Results

- In real-world experiments, the RHP7 Kaleido robot achieved **100%** success rates in holding medium and large fragile paper boxes (3/3 trials each), significantly outperforming vision-only and tactile-only baselines.
- The TACT policy demonstrated improved generalization to unseen object sizes, successfully manipulating four out of five varied boxes, showcasing adaptability beyond training data.
- Analysis revealed that vision was crucial for object sizing and approach, while tactile feedback was indispensable for delicate contact maintenance, confirming the complementary roles of both modalities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal inputs, specifically the synergistic combination of vision and tactile sensing, are essential for robust and delicate whole-body contact manipulation on a humanoid robot.
> - Tactile feedback provides critical fine force control and contact maintenance capabilities, preventing both object crushing and accidental drops during manipulation.
> - A hybrid control architecture, integrating a learning-based manipulation policy with a stable model-based locomotion system, enables dynamic and versatile robot behavior in complex tasks.
