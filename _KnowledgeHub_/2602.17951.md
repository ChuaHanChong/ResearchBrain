# ROCKET: Residual-Oriented Multi-Layer Alignment for Spatially-Aware Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ROCKET, developed at the University of Maryland, introduces a framework to infuse robust 3D spatial reasoning into 2D-pretrained Vision-Language-Action models. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on the LIBERO benchmark with a 98.5% average success rate, requiring only 4% of the computational cost of previous methods.

## Problem

- 2D-pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack stable 3D spatial understanding, leading to poor generalization in robotic manipulation tasks requiring precise geometry.
- Existing representation alignment methods to inject 3D knowledge often use single-layer supervision, which is sub-optimal, sensitive to layer choice, and fails to capture hierarchical information.
- Naive multi-layer alignment attempts suffer from severe gradient interference when using independent projectors for each layer, causing training instability and performance degradation.

## Method

- ROCKET proposes a residual-oriented multi-layer representation alignment framework, leveraging a set of aligned layer pairs from the student VLA model and a 3D vision foundation model (teacher).
- A single, shared projector is used across all selected layers to map student representations to teacher representations, which theoretically and empirically reduces cross-layer gradient conflicts.
- Matryoshka-style sparse activation is integrated into the shared projector, allowing shallower layers to use a smaller capacity while deeper layers progressively activate more parameters, balancing alignment losses across depths.

## Results

- Achieves a 98.5% average success rate on the challenging LIBERO benchmark, matching previous state-of-the-art methods while using only approximately 4% of their compute budget.
- Demonstrates superior robustness on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark with an 81.7% average success rate, showing most pronounced gains under Robot and Layout shifts, indicating enhanced 3D spatial reasoning.
- Ablation studies confirm the shared projector's role, improving performance from 80.0% (naive multi-layer) to 98.2%, with Matryoshka-style activation further boosting it to 98.5%.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive multi-layer alignment with independent projectors leads to severe gradient interference due to structurally decoupled Jacobian matrices during back-propagation, causing performance collapse.
> - A shared projector imposes a common operator structure on cross-layer gradient interference terms, promoting gradient coherence, faster convergence, and stable multi-layer knowledge transfer.
> - Balancing alignment losses across hierarchical layers is crucial; Matryoshka-style sparse activation prevents shallower layers from dominating the shared projector's learning, enabling effective transfer of both local and global spatial cues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation, particularly instruction-following, demands models that can seamlessly integrate semantic understanding with precise 3D spatial reasoning. Modern Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown impressive capabilities in instruction-following by leveraging large vision-language backbones, but they primarily suffer from a critical limitation: their backbones are largely pretrained on 2D imagery. This leads to a deficiency in stable 3D spatial understanding, causing poor generalization in scenarios requiring accurate geometry, viewpoint invariance, or fine-grained spatial relations.

The research community has pursued three main strategies to address this "3D spatial grounding gap" in VLA models:
1. **Augmenting inputs with explicit 3D signals:** This involves incorporating additional sensor data like depth maps or point clouds (e.g., SpatialVLA, GeoVLA, 3D-CAVLA). While effective, this approach often necessitates specialized hardware or complex data processing pipelines.
2. **Recovering 3D structure from 2D observations:** Methods in this category employ techniques like depth estimation (e.g., Depth Anything) to infer 3D information from standard 2D camera feeds. This can add computational overhead during inference.
3. **Implicitly aligning intermediate VLA representations to spatial features from pretrained 3D foundation models:** This strategy, which ROCKET falls under, involves transferring 3D spatial knowledge from powerful 3D vision foundation models (teachers) to the 2D-centric VLA models (students) through representation alignment or feature distillation. This approach is favored for its inference efficiency and scalability, as it does not require additional sensors or runtime depth estimation.

Within the third category, existing representation alignment methods typically apply supervision at only a **single layer** of the VLA model (e.g., Spatial Forcing (SF), GLaD, REPA, 3DRS). The paper highlights a significant drawback of this single-layer approach: the optimal layer for alignment is highly sensitive to the task and data distribution, often requiring inefficient post-hoc search. Moreover, single-layer alignment fails to fully exploit the rich, hierarchical information distributed across multiple depths in deep neural networks, a benefit observed in general feature distillation studies.

A natural extension would be **multi-layer alignment**, which could leverage these hierarchical spatial cues. However, the paper critically identifies a major challenge: naively extending classic multi-layer distillation techniques to VLA settings, especially by using independent projectors for each layer, often leads to severe **gradient interference**. These separate projectors learn inconsistent mappings, causing training instability and performance degradation (a "collapse").

ROCKET directly addresses this critical problem by proposing a novel multi-layer alignment strategy that *solves* the gradient interference issue, thereby enabling stable and effective knowledge transfer across multiple layers. By doing so, ROCKET aims to provide a more robust, efficient, and comprehensive solution for endowing 2D-pretrained VLA models with robust 3D spatial understanding, pushing the boundaries of generalist robotic policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the ROCKET framework are twofold:

1. **To inject robust 3D spatial reasoning capabilities into 2D-pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.** The ultimate goal is to enable these models to perform complex robotic manipulation tasks that require a precise understanding of 3D geometry, object relations, and environmental constraints, which current 2D-centric VLA models often lack.
2. **To develop a stable and efficient multi-layer representation alignment strategy that overcomes the limitations of existing methods.** This includes addressing:
 - **The inefficiency and sub-optimality of single-layer alignment:** Current practices of aligning a VLA model to a 3D vision foundation model at only a single layer are limited. The optimal layer varies, requiring tedious empirical search, and a single layer cannot capture the full spectrum of hierarchical spatial information available across a deep network.
 - **The critical problem of gradient interference in naive multi-layer alignment:** Directly extending multi-layer knowledge distillation (using independent projectors for each layer) leads to performance collapse due to conflicting gradients. Separate projectors learn inconsistent transformations, disrupting stable optimization.

The motivation for ROCKET stems from these identified gaps and challenges:
- **Richness of multi-layer representations:** Representations in deep residual networks evolve with depth, encoding information at multiple levels of abstraction (from local cues in shallow layers to global context in deeper ones). Leveraging these complementary signals is theoretically more potent than single-layer supervision.
- **The "cone effect" and residual dynamics:** The paper posits that hidden states within deep residual networks tend to converge into a consistent feature space (the "cone effect"). This suggests that multi-layer alignment *should* learn a consistent "cone-to-cone" mapping between student and teacher residual streams. The failure of naive multi-layer alignment indicates a breakdown in this consistency.
- **Computational efficiency:** Traditional approaches involving explicit 3D inputs or runtime depth estimation add computational overhead. Implicit representation alignment, if done effectively, offers a more inference-efficient route. ROCKET seeks to make this route not only effective but also computationally frugal during training.

By addressing these points, ROCKET aims to provide a principled, robust, and computationally efficient method to enhance VLA models' spatial intelligence, facilitating their deployment in complex real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ROCKET introduces a novel residual-oriented multi-layer representation alignment framework built on three core technical designs to address the challenges of spatial grounding and gradient interference:

1. **Multi-layer Representation Alignment on Residual Streams:**
 - **Problem Formulation:** ROCKET frames the problem as aligning multiple layers of a student VLA model's visual backbone to multiple layers of a powerful 3D vision foundation model (teacher). Unlike single-layer methods, it explicitly leverages a set of `N` aligned layer pairs `{(s_i, τ_i)}` from the student (`h_S_s_i`) and teacher (`h_T_τ_i`) models.
 - **Layer Selection:** While robust to layer choice, ROCKET identifies that aligning approximately 8-16 layer pairs yields the best performance. The default strategy is "E2M-Last1," which samples layers uniformly from early to middle depths and includes the final layer, providing a practical, training-free method.
 - **Alignment Loss:** The overall objective combines the action learning loss (`L_action`) with a multi-layer alignment regularizer: `L_ROCKET = L_action + λ * (1/N) * Σ ℓ(p_Φ(h_S_s_i), h_T_τ_i)`, where `ℓ` is a similarity-based loss (e.g., 1 - cosine similarity) and `λ` balances the two objectives.

2. **Shared Projector to Reduce Gradient Conflict:**
 - **Theoretical Justification (Residual Dynamics and Gradient Coherence):** The paper provides a theoretical analysis rooted in residual dynamics. It highlights that in Pre-LayerNorm (Pre-LN) residual networks, back-propagation means early layers receive an additive mixture of gradient signals from all aligned future layers. With *independent* projectors, the Jacobian matrices (which govern gradient transformation) become "structurally decoupled" across layers. This can lead to cross-layer gradients that are inconsistent, poorly aligned, or even destructive (gradient interference), leading to performance collapse.
 - **Shared Projector Design:** ROCKET's key innovation is to use a *single, shared projector* `p_Φ` across all selected layers, rather than `N` independent projectors. This shared projector learns a common, layer-invariant mapping from the student's residual stream to the teacher's.
 - **Mechanism for Coherence:** The theoretical analysis shows that a shared projector imposes a common operator structure on the cross-layer interference terms. This biases cross-layer gradients towards constructive interference, promoting gradient coherence. Empirical evidence (cosine similarity of gradients) supports that the shared projector significantly increases gradient alignment throughout training, leading to faster convergence and better performance.

3. **Matryoshka-style Sparse Activation for Balancing Depths:**
 - **Problem:** Even with a shared projector, shallower layers tend to converge more easily to the teacher's signal, potentially dominating the shared projector's learning. This can hinder the projector's ability to learn effective mappings for deeper layers, which contain more abstract or global spatial cues.
 - **Solution (Matryoshka-style Activation):** Inspired by Matryoshka representation learning, ROCKET introduces a sparse activation scheme for the shared projector. The projector is parameterized (e.g., as a two-layer MLP) with a maximum internal width `m`. For each aligned layer `s_i`, only the first `m_i` hidden channels of the projector are activated, where `m_i` monotonically increases with depth.
 - **Width Schedule:** A simple linear width schedule `ρ_i = ρ_min + (ρ_max - ρ_min) * ((i - 1) / max(N - 1, 1))` is used to determine the activation fraction `m_i = ⌈ρ_i * m⌉`. This means shallow layers use a smaller, nested sub-projector, while deeper layers progressively activate more parameters, up to the full capacity (`ρ_max = 1.0`).
 - **Benefit:** This scheme balances alignment losses across depths. Shallow layers can quickly capture common local cues with a smaller capacity, while deeper layers are provided with greater effective capacity to refine more global and abstract information, preventing shallow-layer dominance and ensuring comprehensive knowledge transfer.

In essence, ROCKET combines a principled theoretical understanding of gradient dynamics in deep networks with practical architectural innovations to enable effective and efficient multi-layer spatial knowledge transfer in VLA models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ROCKET demonstrates superior performance and efficiency across multiple benchmarks and VLA models, establishing itself as a state-of-the-art method for spatial grounding.

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance on LIBERO:**
 - ROCKET achieves a 98.5% average success rate on the challenging LIBERO benchmark, matching the previous state-of-the-art method, Spatial Forcing (Li et al., 2025). This includes strong performance across all four task suites: Spatial (98.2%), Object (99.8%), Goal (98.8%), and Long (97.0%).
 - Compared to other 2D VLA models, ROCKET significantly outperforms them (e.g., VOTE at 98.0%, GR00T N1.6 at 97.0%). Even against 3D VLA models that use explicit 3D inputs, ROCKET matches or exceeds their performance without requiring additional sensor modalities.

2. **Significant Computational Efficiency:**
 - Despite matching SOTA performance, ROCKET achieves this with remarkably low computational cost. It requires only approximately **4% of the compute budget** compared to prior SOTA methods like Spatial Forcing or OpenVLA-OFT to reach comparable performance. This is achieved by using a single VLA model, smaller batch sizes, and fewer training steps (1x32x50k vs. 4x64x150k or 3x4x8x150k for others).
 - **Faster Convergence:** ROCKET converges quickly, outperforming the baseline by ~10% success rate at just 10,000 training steps and continuing to improve, demonstrating efficient exploitation of spatial cues.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization (LIBERO-Plus):**
 - On the LIBERO-Plus robustness benchmark, ROCKET achieves the best average success rate (81.7%), surpassing the baseline (80.0%).
 - Notably, ROCKET shows the most pronounced gains under **Robot and Layout shifts**, which are directly related to spatial geometry rather than just appearance variations. This strongly suggests that ROCKET genuinely enhances spatial reasoning and does not rely on superficial positional shortcuts.

4. **Performance Across Multiple VLA Backbones (PI0.5, PI0) and Bimanual Tasks (RoboTwin 2.0):**
 - ROCKET improves PI0.5 performance on LIBERO by 2.3% under full fine-tuning, indicating its effectiveness even for smaller-scale models.
 - On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark for bimanual manipulation, ROCKET achieves a clear advantage in the "Easy" setting and remains competitive in the "Hard" setting, showcasing its generalization to more complex multi-robot tasks.

5. **Ablation Study Validating Key Components:**
 - **Multi-layer (Naive):** Introducing multi-layer alignment with *independent projectors* significantly degrades performance to 80.0% (from 96.4% baseline), confirming the severe gradient interference problem.
 - **Shared Projector:** Replacing independent projectors with the *shared projector* (ROCKET Shared-only) drastically improves performance to 98.2%, validating its role in mitigating gradient conflicts.
 - **Matryoshka-style Activation:** Adding the *Matryoshka-style sparse activation* further boosts performance to 98.5%, confirming its effectiveness in balancing alignment losses and fully leveraging hierarchical information.

6. **Data Efficiency and Robustness to Layer Selection:**
 - ROCKET performs strongly even with limited data (1%, 5%, 10% subsets of LIBERO), highlighting its data efficiency, crucial for robotics.
 - The framework is robust to different layer selection strategies; most reasonable strategies yield consistent gains, eliminating the need for extensive hyperparameter search.

In summary, ROCKET not only pushes the boundary of VLA performance by integrating 3D spatial awareness but does so with unprecedented training efficiency and robustness, making it a highly practical and impactful contribution to embodied AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ROCKET represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, offering a novel, principled, and highly practical approach to enhance their 3D spatial reasoning capabilities. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap Effectively and Efficiently:** ROCKET provides a robust solution to the fundamental problem of 2D-pretrained VLA models lacking 3D spatial understanding. Unlike methods relying on additional hardware or runtime processing, ROCKET implicitly injects 3D awareness through representation alignment, making it inference-efficient and scalable. This is crucial for deploying VLA models in real-world robotic systems where computational resources and sensing capabilities might be constrained.

2. **Principled Multi-Layer Knowledge Transfer:** The core contribution of ROCKET is its ability to perform stable and effective multi-layer representation alignment. By theoretically identifying and empirically solving the gradient interference problem via a shared projector and balancing mechanisms like Matryoshka-style activation, ROCKET unlocks the full potential of hierarchical knowledge transfer from powerful 3D vision foundation models. This addresses a critical limitation of previous single-layer alignment approaches and naive multi-layer attempts.

3. **Unprecedented Computational Efficiency:** The fact that ROCKET achieves state-of-the-art performance with approximately 4% of the compute budget required by prior SOTA methods is a game-changer. This dramatically reduces the financial and environmental costs associated with training large VLA models, making advanced robotic manipulation capabilities more accessible to a broader range of researchers and practitioners. It also facilitates faster iteration and experimentation.

4. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** ROCKET's superior performance on robustness benchmarks like LIBERO-Plus, particularly under spatial perturbations (Robot and Layout shifts), indicates that it genuinely fosters deeper 3D understanding rather than superficial associations. This improved generalization is vital for real-world robotics, where environments and conditions are often dynamic and unpredictable. The demonstrated effectiveness across diverse VLA backbones (OpenVLA, PI0, PI0.5) and tasks (unimanual, bimanual) further underscores its generality and broad applicability.

5. **Data Efficiency for Embodied AI:** Robotic data collection is notoriously expensive and time-consuming. ROCKET's ability to achieve strong performance even with limited training data (e.g., 1% or 5% of the dataset) is a major advantage for embodied AI, where data scarcity is a common challenge.

6. **Implications for Future Research:**
 - **Gradient Coherence in Deep Learning:** The theoretical analysis of gradient interference and coherence in residual networks, and how a shared projector mitigates this, offers valuable insights beyond VLA models. It could inspire similar principled designs for multi-task learning, knowledge distillation, or transfer learning in other domains.
 - **Architectural Design for Alignment:** ROCKET's success with the shared projector and Matryoshka-style activation could lead to new architectural patterns for designing efficient and effective alignment modules in various deep learning contexts.
 - **Scalability of Spatial Understanding:** By making 3D spatial grounding more accessible and efficient, ROCKET paves the way for developing even more capable and generalized robotic policies, potentially leading to robots that can operate reliably in increasingly complex and unstructured environments.

In conclusion, ROCKET offers a simple, scalable, and highly effective framework for endowing VLA models with crucial 3D spatial awareness. Its combination of theoretical grounding and compelling empirical results positions it as a significant step towards more reliable, adaptable, and generalist robotic manipulation systems, with wide-ranging implications for both academic research and practical applications in embodied AI.
