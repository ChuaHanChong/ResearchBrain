# ROCKET: Residual-Oriented Multi-Layer Alignment for Spatially-Aware Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ROCKET, developed at the University of Maryland, introduces a framework to infuse robust 3D spatial reasoning into 2D-pretrained Vision-Language-Action models. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on the LIBERO benchmark with a 98.5% average success rate, requiring only 4% of the computational cost of previous methods.

## Problem

- 2D-pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack stable 3D spatial understanding, leading to poor generalization in robotic manipulation tasks requiring precise geometry.
- Existing representation alignment methods to inject 3D knowledge often use single-layer supervision, which is sub-optimal, sensitive to layer choice, and fails to capture hierarchical information.
- Naive multi-layer alignment attempts suffer from severe gradient interference when using independent projectors for each layer, causing training instability and performance degradation.

## Method

- ROCKET proposes a residual-oriented multi-layer representation alignment framework, leveraging a set of aligned layer pairs from the student VLA model and a 3D vision foundation model (teacher).
- A single, shared projector is used across all selected layers to map student representations to teacher representations, which theoretically and empirically reduces cross-layer gradient conflicts.
- Matryoshka-style sparse activation is integrated into the shared projector, allowing shallower layers to use a smaller capacity while deeper layers progressively activate more parameters, balancing alignment losses across depths.

## Results

- Achieves a 98.5% average success rate on the challenging LIBERO benchmark, matching previous state-of-the-art methods while using only approximately 4% of their compute budget.
- Demonstrates superior robustness on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark with an 81.7% average success rate, showing most pronounced gains under Robot and Layout shifts, indicating enhanced 3D spatial reasoning.
- Ablation studies confirm the shared projector's role, improving performance from 80.0% (naive multi-layer) to 98.2%, with Matryoshka-style activation further boosting it to 98.5%.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive multi-layer alignment with independent projectors leads to severe gradient interference due to structurally decoupled Jacobian matrices during back-propagation, causing performance collapse.
> - A shared projector imposes a common operator structure on cross-layer gradient interference terms, promoting gradient coherence, faster convergence, and stable multi-layer knowledge transfer.
> - Balancing alignment losses across hierarchical layers is crucial; Matryoshka-style sparse activation prevents shallower layers from dominating the shared projector's learning, enabling effective transfer of both local and global spatial cues.
