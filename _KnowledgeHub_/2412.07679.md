# RADIOv2.5: Improved Baselines for Agglomerative Vision Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> RADIOv2.5 from NVIDIA Research establishes new baselines for agglomerative vision foundation models by addressing multi-resolution inconsistencies and computational inefficiencies, achieving state-of-the-art performance across dense visual tasks and Vision-Language Model benchmarks. It demonstrates improved robustness by eliminating resolution-dependent mode switching and enhances efficiency through novel token compression techniques.

## Problem

- Existing agglomerative vision foundation models exhibit "mode switching" behavior, where feature distributions change dramatically with input resolution, leading to inconsistent performance.
- Teacher models have varying activation magnitudes and feature distributions, causing unbalanced learning in multi-teacher distillation.
- High-resolution visual encoders produce excessive output tokens, which significantly bottleneck the efficiency and performance of Vision-Language Models due to quadratic attention complexity.

## Method

- Implemented a ==Multi-Resolution Training (MRT)== schedule, augmented with Mosaic Augmentation, allowing the student model to learn consistently from all teachers across multiple resolutions.
- Applied ==PCA-Hadamard Isotropic Standardization (PHI-S)== to balance teacher contributions by normalizing activation magnitudes and distributions, improving training stability.
- Integrated a ==token merging technique== inspired by ToMeSD, which uses bipartite soft matching to efficiently compress visual tokens while preserving essential information for Vision-Language Models.

## Results

- Multi-resolution training eliminated "mode switching," resulting in consistent accuracy across input resolutions, with specific improvements such as **+2.8** mIoU on ADE20k and **+3.4** average VLM score.
- Outperformed prior agglomerative models (e.g., SAM-CLIP, Theia, UNIC) on dense tasks, with RADIOv2.5-B exceeding DINOv2-g-reg's ADE20k performance despite being significantly smaller.
- Achieved state-of-the-art VLM performance, with configurations showing a **6.7-point** average VLM score increase by using SigLIP as a teacher and a **5.9-point** average increase from efficient token merging.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simultaneously training across multiple resolutions with adaptive teacher handling (like mosaic augmentation for SAM) effectively mitigates resolution-dependent "mode switching" and improves model robustness.
> - Properly balancing teacher contributions via methods like PHI-S is crucial for stable training and allows seemingly challenging teachers, like SAM, to become genuinely beneficial for diverse tasks.
> - Token merging strategies, when applied judiciously, can drastically reduce the token count from high-resolution vision encoders while retaining sufficient information for significant performance gains in Vision-Language Models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) and multi-modal AI, particularly focusing on the technique of **knowledge agglomeration** or **multi-teacher distillation**. The core idea is to combine the strengths of several specialized, pre-trained VFMs (like CLIP for image-text alignment, DINO for self-supervised visual features, and SAM for segmentation) into a single, unified, and more versatile student model. This approach is motivated by the desire to create general-purpose vision encoders that can handle diverse tasks without the need for training entirely new models or complex ensemble methods.

Previous works in this area, such as SAM-CLIP, AM-RADIO (which this paper directly builds upon), UNIC, and Theia, have demonstrated the viability and benefits of multi-teacher distillation. SAM-CLIP pioneered combining SAM and CLIP, while AM-RADIO introduced label-free knowledge distillation from multiple teachers. UNIC advanced this by incorporating intermediate teacher matching projectors and dynamic teacher selection, and Theia applied it specifically to robot learning. PHI-S highlighted the importance of normalizing distinct teacher distributions, and Eagle leveraged a mixture of vision encoders for inference-time aggregation.

Despite these advancements, the authors identify several critical open challenges that existing agglomerative models fail to adequately address. These include:
- **Resolution balancing:** Teachers operate at varying resolutions, leading to feature granularity inconsistencies in the student model.
- **Teacher distribution balancing:** Different teachers have distinct feature distributions, which can bias the student's learning if not properly accounted for.
- **Generating multi-resolution features for diverse applications:** The need for a VFM that can flexibly produce features at any resolution to serve various tasks (e.g., fine-grained segmentation vs. image-level captioning).
- **Excessive output tokens:** High-resolution processing often leads to a large number of visual tokens, which can incur quadratic complexity in attention mechanisms for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), hindering performance and efficiency.

RADIOv2.5 directly tackles these limitations, positioning itself as a significant advancement that improves the robustness, efficiency, and versatility of agglomerative VFMs. By introducing novel solutions for multi-resolution training, teacher balancing, and token compression, it aims to establish new baselines for the field, particularly for integration into high-performance VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind RADIOv2.5 are clearly articulated in the abstract, introduction, and "Challenges" section:

1. **Address Critical Challenges in Agglomerative Models:** The overarching goal is to overcome the limitations observed in existing agglomerative VFMs. Specifically, the authors aim to solve:
 - **Resolution Mode Shifts:** Previous models, like AM-RADIO, exhibit undesirable "mode switching" behavior where feature distributions change dramatically with input resolution (e.g., DINO-like at low resolution, SAM-like at high resolution). The objective is to achieve **multi-resolution robustness** and consistent accuracy across a wide range of input resolutions.
 - **Teacher Imbalance:** Acknowledge and correct for the varying activation magnitudes and distribution moments among different teacher models to prevent biased learning.
 - **Excessive Output Tokens for VLMs:** Reduce the computational burden of high-resolution image processing in VLMs by efficiently compressing visual tokens while preserving essential information.

2. **Develop a Versatile Vision Encoder:** Create a single, unified vision encoder that can flexibly produce features at various resolutions and granularities, catering to diverse downstream applications from dense segmentation to image captioning, thereby reducing the need for separate, specialized models.

3. **Improve Baseline Performance and Efficiency:** By tackling the identified challenges, the authors aim to significantly enhance the performance, stability, and computational efficiency of agglomerative VFMs, setting new state-of-the-art baselines.

4. **Facilitate Downstream Applications:** Specifically improve the integration of vision encoders with Language Models (LLMs) for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which are often bottlenecked by the number of visual tokens.

The motivation stems from the practical difficulties and inefficiencies encountered when deploying current agglomerative models. The observed "mode switching" behavior makes models unreliable across different input scales, while the computational cost of high-resolution features limits their utility in sequence-heavy applications like VLMs. By addressing these fundamental issues, RADIOv2.5 seeks to unlock broader applicability and stronger performance for foundation models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose several novel solutions and refinements to existing agglomerative training paradigms, building upon a baseline similar to AM-RADIO. The core methodology involves distilling knowledge from multiple powerful teachers (DFN CLIP, OpenAI CLIP, DINOv2-g-reg, and SAM-H) into a Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone enhanced with Cropped Position Embeddings (CPE) for multi-resolution support.

The key components of their approach are:

1. **Multi-Resolution Training (MRT):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** The "mode switching" issue where student features adapt to different teachers at different resolutions (e.g., DINO at low-res, SAM at high-res).
 - **Solution:** Enable the student to learn from *all* teachers across *multiple* resolutions simultaneously. This is achieved through a multi-stage training schedule: an initial low-resolution stage, followed by a medium-resolution stage, and a final stage training concurrently at both medium (432x432) and high (1024x1024) resolutions.
 - **Adaptations for Teachers:** DINOv2 natively supports multi-resolution. For CLIP, student features are downsampled to match the teacher's native resolution. For SAM, which struggles with interpolation, smaller images are padded to 1024x1024, SAM inference is run, and then features are cropped to the effective unpadded size.

2. **Mosaic Augmentation:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** The high computational cost of running SAM inference on padded images for low-resolution student training.
 - **Solution:** Instead of padding individual small images for SAM, a mosaic of multiple smaller images (e.g., 𝑘 × 𝑘) is created to form a single 1024x1024 image. SAM inference is run once on this mosaic, and the individual feature maps are then extracted to train the student.
 - **Benefits:** Reduces computational cost significantly, increases image diversity, and qualitatively leads to cleaner features.

3. **Improved Teacher Loss Balancing (PHI-S):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Significant variations in activation magnitudes among teachers (e.g., SAM overshadowing CLIP/DINOv2), leading to unbalanced learning.
 - **Solution:** Adopt and apply the PCA-Hadamard Isotropic Standardization (PHI-S) method. This technique rotates teacher activations to evenly distribute variance across channels and scales them to unit variance, effectively balancing their contributions to the overall loss.
 - **Impact:** Enhances training stability and improves overall benchmark performance, as quantified by a fidelity measure.

4. **Token Compression (ToMeSD-inspired):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** The excessive number of output tokens from high-resolution vision encoders, which negatively impacts VLM performance and efficiency (quadratic attention complexity).
 - **Solution:** Integrate a token merging technique inspired by ToMeSD. This uses bipartite soft matching to merge similar tokens, effectively reducing the token count while retaining high-resolution information. Strided partitioning is used to ensure regional representation.
 - **Refinements:** Authors found using features directly for matching (instead of attention keys) reduces reconstruction error and applying merging at the vision encoder's output (rather than incrementally) yields better VLM performance.

5. **Teacher Set and Feature Selection:**
 - **Teacher Update:** Replace OpenAI-CLIP with SigLIP as a teacher, which demonstrated significant VLM performance improvements.
 - **SAM Re-evaluation:** Re-assess SAM's utility as a teacher, concluding that with proper balancing (PHI-S) and multi-resolution handling (mosaic augmentation), SAM is indeed beneficial for dense tasks and dense downstream applicability.
 - **Feature Selection for Downstream Tasks:** Investigate sparse (individual layers) and dense (averaged groups of layers) feature selection for downstream tasks. Found that intermediate layer activations benefit tasks, especially when a non-linear transformation (like a DPT head) is used.

The evaluation framework is comprehensive, covering image-level reasoning (ImageNet-1k zero-shot/k-NN), pixel-level semantic segmentation (ADE20k, Pascal VOC), instance segmentation (COCO with SAM adaptor), 3D understanding (depth, surface normals, multi-view, semantic correspondence via Probe3D), and Vision-Language reasoning (VILA benchmarks).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings and demonstrates substantial improvements over prior baselines:

1. **Resolution Robustness and Mode Switch Mitigation:** Multi-resolution training (config ℬ) effectively eliminates the "mode switching" behavior observed in AM-RADIO. The model exhibits consistent accuracy across a wide range of input resolutions (Figure 2), with a significant +2.8 mIoU on ADE20k and +3.4 average VLM score for config ℬ. This is a core success, making the model more reliable.
2. **Improved Scale Equivariance:** RADIOv2.5 models achieve significantly better scale equivariance compared to AM-RADIO and even tiling-based approaches, demonstrating consistency in feature representation across different scales (Table 2).
3. **Effectiveness of Mosaic Augmentation:** Mosaic augmentation greatly reduces the computational cost of training with high-resolution teachers like SAM while improving student quality. This makes SAM a more practical teacher to include.
4. **Balanced Teacher Learning via PHI-S:** PHI-S successfully balances the energy spent learning from each teacher, addressing the issue of disproportionately large activation variances (e.g., from SAM). This leads to improved training stability and benchmark performance (Table 3).
5. **SAM as a Beneficial Teacher:** Contrary to some recent trends, when properly handled with mosaic augmentation and PHI-S, SAM proves to be a valuable teacher. Its inclusion shows negligible but positive impact on classification and strong positive effects on dense tasks like semantic segmentation and 3D probing (Table 4, Finding 5).
6. **Optimal Partitioning:** Minimizing the number of training partitions benefits summarization tasks, but partitioning remains an effective strategy under compute constraints for dense tasks (Table 5, Finding 6).
7. **SigLIP Teacher Superiority:** Replacing OpenAI-CLIP with SigLIP as a teacher significantly improves VLM benchmarks, leading to a 6.7-point average increase (config 𝒞, Table 1).
8. **Superior Token Compression with ToMe:** Token merging (ToMe) is highly effective at retaining diverse information under high compression ratios, outperforming pixel unshuffling. It disproportionately benefits RADIOv2.5 compared to other encoders like SigLIP, likely due to RADIOv2.5's lower feature noise (Figure 7, Table 6). Config ℰ shows an average VLM score improvement of 5.9 points.
9. **Feature Selection for Downstream Tasks:** Intermediate layer activations benefit dense downstream tasks significantly, particularly when a non-linear transformation (like a DPT head) is employed. VLM tasks, however, show less sensitivity to this choice (Table 7, Finding 8).
10. **State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - **Dense Tasks:** Across various scales (-B, -L, -H, -g), RADIOv2.5 models consistently outperform other agglomerative models (SAM-CLIP, Theia, UNIC, UNIT) on semantic segmentation (ADE20k, NYUDv2, Pascal Context), depth estimation, and surface normal estimation (Table 8). RADIOv2.5-B even exceeds DINOv2-g-reg's performance on ADE20k despite being one-tenth the size.
 - **VLM Evaluation:** When integrated with an LLM (MN-Minitron-8B) and using token merging, RADIOv2.5 models consistently achieve higher accuracy across a suite of VLM benchmarks (TextVQA, ChartQA, DocVQA, InfoVQA, OCRBench, GQA, POPE, MME, SEED(I), MMMU, AI2D) compared to OpenAI-CLIP, RADIOv2.1-H, and SigLIP-SO400M (Table 9, Table A1, A2).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of RADIOv2.5 represents a significant step forward in the creation of robust, efficient, and versatile Vision Foundation Models. Its contributions have several far-reaching implications:

1. **Enhanced Model Robustness and Generalization:** By fundamentally addressing the "mode switching" issue through multi-resolution training and improving scale equivariance, RADIOv2.5 creates a vision encoder that performs consistently and reliably across varying input resolutions. This is crucial for real-world applications where input image sizes and aspect ratios are highly variable, reducing the need for application-specific fine-tuning or complex multi-pass inference strategies.

2. **Improved Efficiency and Resource Utilization:** Techniques like mosaic augmentation and token compression (ToMe) drastically improve the efficiency of training and inference. Mosaic augmentation lowers the computational overhead associated with high-resolution teachers, making training more feasible. Token compression, by reducing the number of visual tokens without significant information loss, directly tackles the quadratic complexity bottleneck in VLMs, enabling higher-resolution image processing and reducing latency—a critical factor for deployable AI systems.

3. **Broadened Applicability of Foundation Models:** The unified, multi-capable nature of RADIOv2.5 makes it a more "generalist" vision encoder. Its ability to simultaneously leverage strengths from diverse teachers (CLIP, DINO, SAM) for both high-level image understanding and fine-grained dense prediction tasks means a single model can serve a wider array of downstream applications, from robotic perception and 3D understanding (as hinted by its use in Semantic Keypoint tracking) to advanced Vision-Language Models.

4. **Advancement of Vision-Language Models:** The substantial performance gains observed across VLM benchmarks, combined with efficient token compression, position RADIOv2.5 as a strong backbone for the next generation of multimodal LLMs. This can lead to more accurate, detailed, and context-aware visual understanding in conversational AI, document analysis, and visual question answering systems.

5. **Open Science Contribution:** The release of top-performing model variants at multiple scales, along with inference code and pretrained weights, is a valuable contribution to the research community. This promotes reproducibility, facilitates further research, and accelerates the adoption and development of advanced vision models by a broader audience.

In summary, RADIOv2.5 provides a robust framework for building more capable agglomerative vision foundation models. Its innovations in training stability, multi-resolution consistency, and efficiency address critical practical challenges, paving the way for more powerful and deployable AI systems in computer vision and multimodal understanding.
