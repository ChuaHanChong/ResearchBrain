# Language to Rewards for Robotic Skill Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind researchers developed a system that translates natural language instructions into a diverse range of robotic skills by having Large Language Models generate parameterized reward functions, which are then optimized by a real-time MuJoCo Model Predictive Controller. This approach successfully synthesizes complex locomotion and dexterous manipulation behaviors on both simulated and real robots, reliably tackling 90% of designed tasks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to directly generate low-level robot commands due to the hardware-dependent nature of actions and their underrepresentation in LLM training data, creating a language-action gap.
- Existing LLM-robotics approaches are limited by the rigidity and scope of pre-defined robot primitives or the prohibitive data requirements of end-to-end learning methods.
- Manually designing effective and robust reward functions for complex robotic tasks is challenging and requires significant domain expertise.

## Method

- A system (**L2R**) that uses LLMs to generate ==parameterized reward functions== from natural language instructions, serving as an intermediate interface between high-level semantics and low-level control.
- A two-stage LLM-based **Reward Translator** that first uses a ==Motion Descriptor LLM== to expand user input into a structured natural language description, then a Reward Coder LLM to translate this into Python code for reward parameters and weights.
- A **Motion Controller** utilizing real-time ==MuJoCo Model Predictive Control (MPC)== to optimize robot actions based on the LLM-generated reward function, enabling interactive feedback and robust execution on real hardware with specific regularization.

## Results

- The system successfully generated **17** diverse and complex skills for quadruped and dexterous manipulation robots in simulation, reliably tackling **90%** of the designed tasks, including bipedal standing and dexterous faucet turning.
- It significantly outperformed baselines such as "Code-as-Policies" (primitive-based) and an ablated "Reward Coder Only" approach, demonstrating superior success rates and robustness in task completion.
- Interactive skill synthesis was achieved, allowing users to iteratively refine complex behaviors (e.g., a quadruped's "moon-walk" or placing an apple in a drawer) through natural language feedback.
- Successful sim-to-real transfer was demonstrated on a real mobile manipulator for non-prehensile tasks like object pushing, validating the practical applicability through a `sim2real_regularization_reward`.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward functions provide a semantically rich, modular, and robust intermediate interface for LLMs to define desired robot outcomes, effectively bridging the language-action gap more broadly than direct action generation or fixed primitives.
> - Structuring the LLM input through an intermediate natural language motion description template significantly stabilizes and improves the LLM's ability to generate accurate and complex reward function code.
> - The combination of LLM-generated rewards with real-time model predictive control facilitates interactive skill synthesis, allowing users to iteratively refine robot behaviors, and enables practical sim-to-real transfer with appropriate regularization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) with robotics is a rapidly evolving and highly active area of research. Traditionally, robotics has relied on meticulously engineered control policies, often requiring extensive domain expertise or vast amounts of training data. Recent advancements in LLMs, especially their ability to understand natural language, perform complex reasoning, and generate code, have opened new avenues for more intuitive and flexible robot control.

Existing efforts to apply LLMs to robotics primarily fall into a few categories:
1. **LLMs as Semantic Planners:** Many approaches use LLMs to generate high-level plans or step-by-step instructions (e.g., Zeng et al., Huang et al., Ahn et al.). The LLM acts as a "brain" that decomposes complex tasks into a sequence of simpler, pre-defined robot "primitives" or skills.
2. **LLMs for Code Generation:** Some works leverage LLMs' code-writing capabilities to generate robot-specific code directly (e.g., Liang et al.'s "Code as Policies", Singh et al.). This often involves generating scripts that call existing robot APIs or control primitives.
3. **End-to-End Language-to-Action:** More ambitious approaches attempt to directly map natural language instructions to low-level robot actions through end-to-end learning (e.g., BC-z, RT-1, Interactive Language). These methods typically require massive datasets of language-action pairs.

This paper identifies a critical limitation in these existing paradigms: LLMs, despite their semantic understanding, struggle with directly outputting *low-level robot commands* due to the hardware-dependent nature of actions and their underrepresentation in LLM training corpora. Consequently, methods relying on primitives are bottlenecked by the breadth and quality of these hand-engineered skills, while end-to-end learning demands prohibitive amounts of data.

The paper carves out a novel niche by proposing **reward functions as an intermediate interface** between high-level language and low-level robot control. While prior work has explored grounding language to rewards (e.g., Goyal et al., Lin et al., Kwon et al.), these typically involve training domain-specific reward models that map language to reward values, which itself requires considerable language-labeled data or incurs high LLM query costs during reinforcement learning. In contrast, this work *directly utilizes LLMs' code-writing ability to generate parameterized reward functions* that can then be optimized by an off-the-shelf, real-time controller (MuJoCo MPC) without requiring explicit reward model training or extensive LLM queries for reward values at each step.

By leveraging rewards, the system operates in a more semantic and modular space, aligning with how humans naturally describe desired *outcomes* rather than precise motor commands. This approach seeks to combine the best of both worlds: the semantic reasoning power of LLMs with the precise, real-time execution capabilities of model predictive control, while sidestepping the data hunger of end-to-end methods and the rigidity of fixed primitive sets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a **new paradigm for robotic skill synthesis** where Large Language Models (LLMs) are used to define reward parameters that can then be optimized to achieve a variety of robotic tasks.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Bridge the Language-Action Gap:** LLMs excel at interpreting complex natural language instructions and performing high-level semantic reasoning. However, they inherently lack detailed knowledge of robot hardware and the nuances of low-level control. This makes it challenging for them to directly generate the precise, hardware-dependent actions required for robots. The paper aims to effectively bridge this gap by using a more abstract, yet semantically rich, interface: reward functions.
2. **Harness Semantic Richness of Rewards:** Human language instructions often describe desired behavioral *outcomes* or *goals* (e.g., "make the robot stand up") rather than specific joint torques or positions. Reward functions naturally align with this high-level, goal-oriented specification. They are flexible representations that can be optimized for diverse tasks and are modular and compositional, allowing concise descriptions of complex behaviors.
3. **Overcome Limitations of Primitive-based Systems:** Existing LLM-robotics approaches that rely on pre-defined control primitives are limited by the scope and granularity of these primitives. Designing comprehensive and robust primitive libraries requires extensive expert knowledge or massive data collection, and they struggle to generalize to truly novel skills. By enabling LLMs to generate and modulate rewards, the system can synthesize novel behaviors without being constrained by a fixed set of pre-programmed actions.
4. **Enable Interactive Behavior Creation:** Manual reward design is notoriously difficult and requires significant domain expertise. This work aims to democratize reward specification by allowing LLMs to automatically generate reward code from natural language. Coupling this with a real-time optimizer like MuJoCo MPC facilitates an interactive experience where users can immediately observe robot behavior and provide iterative feedback, allowing for dynamic refinement of complex skills.
5. **Achieve Data Efficiency:** Unlike data-hungry end-to-end learning methods that require vast datasets of demonstrations, this approach leverages the pre-trained knowledge within LLMs and combines it with a model-based control strategy, thereby reducing the need for extensive task-specific training data. It avoids the need to train a separate language-to-reward model and reduces the number of LLM queries compared to methods that use LLMs as direct reward evaluators during RL training.

In essence, the research seeks to empower non-technical users to create and steer intricate robot behaviors using natural language, by intelligently combining the semantic understanding and code-generation capabilities of LLMs with the powerful, real-time optimization of model predictive control through a flexible reward interface.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work revolves around a novel system that translates natural language instructions into low-level robot actions by leveraging reward functions as an intermediate interface. The system comprises two main components: a **Reward Translator** (built on LLMs) and a **Motion Controller** (based on MuJoCo MPC).

#### 3.1. Reward Function Formulation

The paper defines a reward function within the standard Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework. Crucially, the reward `R(s,a)` is structured as a sum of a set of individual residual terms: `R(s,a) = - Σ w_i * n_i(r_i(s,a,ψ_i))`. Here, `w_i` are non-negative weights, `n(·)` is a twice-differentiable norm (e.g., L2 norm) that minimizes at 0, `r_i` is a residual term that achieves optimality when `r_i=0`, and `ψ_i` are the parameters of the `i`-th residual term. The innovation lies in using LLMs to compose these generic residual terms and modulate their parameters `ψ_i` and weights `w_i`.

#### 3.2. Reward Translator (LLM-based)

The Reward Translator is responsible for interpreting user intent from natural language and transforming it into executable reward function code. It addresses the challenge that generic LLMs cannot directly generate hardware-specific reward functions by decomposing the process and using in-context learning:

1. **Motion Description:**
 - A dedicated **Motion Descriptor LLM** takes the user's natural language instruction.
 - It then expands this input into a structured, natural language description of the desired robot motion, following a pre-defined template. This template specifies common robot movements (e.g., torso pitch, height, foot positions, locomotion style) with placeholders for numerical values or choices.
 - This stage is critical for stabilizing the LLM's output. By providing a structured template, the LLM can leverage its internal knowledge about motions in a predictable format, avoiding the complexities of direct code generation for diverse, implicit commands. The prompt for this LLM includes rules for filling the template and assumptions about the robot's capabilities.

2. **Reward Coding:**
 - A second LLM, the **Reward Coder**, receives the natural language motion description generated by the Motion Descriptor.
 - It translates this description into Python code that specifies the reward function parameters (`ψ_i`) and weights (`w_i`) by calling a set of pre-defined reward APIs. These APIs correspond to the generic residual terms (e.g., `set_torso_targets`, `set_feet_pos_parameters`).
 - The prompt for the Reward Coder is designed as a code-writing task, including: i) a clear description of all available reward APIs, ii) an example of the expected code output, and iii) rules and constraints for code generation (e.g., only use `numpy`, no new functions, call `execute_plan` at the end). This leverages the LLMs' strong capabilities in code generation and structured output.

#### 3.3. Motion Controller (MuJoCo MPC)

The Motion Controller takes the generated reward function code and synthesizes the optimal sequence of low-level robot actions `a_1:H`.

- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** The system uses MuJoCo MPC (MJPC), a real-time optimization tool. At each control step, MPC plans a sequence of actions that maximizes the accumulated reward over a specified horizon, then executes the first action, and re-plans based on the updated robot state.
- **Benefits of MPC:** This frequent re-planning provides robustness to system uncertainties and, crucially, enables **interactive motion synthesis**. Users can observe the robot's real-time response to the generated reward and provide iterative feedback, which the LLM can then use to modify the reward function.
- **Optimization Algorithms:** The paper uses `iLQG` (iterative Linear Quadratic Gaussian) for legged locomotion tasks, favored for its smoother and more accurate actions, and `Predictive Sampling` for manipulation tasks, which is better suited for exploring non-smooth optimization landscapes.

#### 3.4. Real-Robot Adaptation

For real-world deployment, the system incorporates:

- **State Estimation:** Object detection in image-space using an open-vocabulary detector (F-VLM) combined with point cloud processing to estimate 3D bounding boxes. An AprilTag provides robust table orientation.
- **Sim-to-Real Regularization:** To address the challenge that highly dynamic behaviors synthesized in simulation by MJPC might exceed real hardware capabilities, a specific `set_sim2real_regularization_reward()` term is introduced. This term encourages steady and stable robot movements by penalizing high end-effector and joint velocities, and encouraging the gripper speed to match object speed during interaction.

In summary, the methodology combines the semantic interpretation and code-generation strengths of LLMs with the powerful, real-time optimization capabilities of MPC, mediated by a modular and parameterizable reward function, all while being carefully adapted for interactive and real-world application.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of its proposed "Language to Rewards" system, demonstrating its efficacy across a range of simulated and real-world robotic tasks.

1. **Diverse and Complex Robot Motion Generation:**
 - The system was evaluated on 17 distinct tasks: 9 for a simulated quadruped robot (12 DoFs) and 8 for a simulated dexterous manipulator (7 DoF arm + 20 DoF hand).
 - Tasks for the quadruped included heading control ("Facing sunrise"), body pose control ("Sit down," "Roll Over," "Stand up on two feet"), limb control ("Lift one paw"), and locomotion styles ("Moon-walk").
 - Dexterous manipulation tasks involved interacting with diverse objects (lifting, moving, re-orienting) and articulated objects (opening a faucet, pulling a drawer).
 - The proposed method reliably tackled **90% of the designed tasks**, showcasing its ability to generate a wide variety of challenging locomotion and manipulation skills from natural language instructions.

2. **Superior Performance Compared to Baselines:**
 - **Code-as-Policies (CaP) Baseline:** This baseline used an LLM to generate plans based on a fixed set of pre-defined robot primitive skills. Our method achieved significantly better success rates in almost all tasks compared to CaP. CaP performed well on tasks directly expressible by its primitives (e.g., "Touch object", "Sit down") but struggled to generalize to novel low-level skills.
 - **Reward Coder Only Baseline:** An ablation of our approach that used only the Reward Coder (without the preceding Motion Descriptor). This baseline could solve some tasks but failed in ones requiring more complex reasoning. For example, when asked to "open a drawer," it often designed rewards for drawer opening but failed to include the necessary step of getting the robot hand close to the handle.
 - **Pass Rate Analysis:** A standard metric for code generation, measuring the percentage of tasks solved given `N` generated code pieces. The results clearly showed that using reward as the interface (our method) empowered LLMs to solve more tasks more reliably. The inclusion of the **Structured Motion Description** (our full system) further boosted performance significantly in terms of both stability (flatter curve) and overall task coverage (higher converged point).

3. **Demonstrated Interactive Motion Synthesis:**
 - A key benefit of using a real-time optimizer like MJPC is the ability for humans to provide iterative feedback.
 - Two compelling examples were shown:
 - Teaching a quadruped robot to perform a "moon-walk" in four interactive steps, with each instruction refining the behavior based on observed results.
 - Sequentially commanding a dexterous manipulator to place an apple inside a drawer, also through multiple rounds of interaction.
 - These demonstrations highlight the system's capacity for user-steered behavior shaping through natural language.

4. **Successful Real-Robot Experiments:**
 - The proposed method was successfully implemented and validated on a real mobile manipulator robot.
 - Despite simulation's ability to discover highly dexterous but often infeasible maneuvers, the system, with the aid of a `sim2real_regularization_reward()` term (designed to encourage steady, stable movements), achieved successful sim-to-real transfer.
 - Complex non-prehensile manipulation tasks, such as object pushing and object grasping, emerged on the real hardware from language instructions, validating the practical applicability of the approach.

In summary, the main findings confirm that using reward functions as an LLM-robot interface, especially when structured by a motion description stage, offers a powerful, flexible, and robust way to synthesize diverse and complex robot skills from natural language, outperforming direct action- or primitive-based methods, and demonstrating practical utility on real hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and human-robot interaction, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **A Novel and Powerful Interaction Paradigm:** The most significant contribution is the introduction of **reward functions as a robust and semantic intermediate interface** between high-level language understanding by LLMs and low-level robot control by real-time optimizers. This paradigm effectively bridges a critical gap in existing LLM-robotics research, moving beyond the limitations of fixed action primitives or data-intensive end-to-end learning. It allows LLMs to operate in a semantically rich space they excel at, while offloading the precise, physics-aware control to dedicated, high-performance optimizers.

2. **Enhanced Robotic Capabilities and Expressiveness:** By leveraging LLMs to define and modulate reward parameters, the system can synthesize a much wider range of diverse and complex robotic behaviors than previously possible with language-conditioned methods. It enables the emergence of novel skills (e.g., bipedal moonwalk, dexterous faucet turning) without explicit programming or extensive demonstrations for each new task, thereby unlocking greater expressiveness for robotic systems.

3. **Democratization of Robot Programming:** The ability to generate complex robot behaviors directly from natural language significantly lowers the barrier to entry for robotics. Non-technical users, who might lack expertise in robotics control, programming, or reward engineering, can now intuitively create and refine robot skills. This could greatly accelerate robot deployment in various domains, from manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics and personal companions.

4. **Improved Data Efficiency:** Unlike many deep reinforcement learning or imitation learning approaches that demand enormous datasets of robot interactions or human demonstrations, this method is comparatively data-efficient. It leverages the vast, pre-trained knowledge of LLMs and combines it with a model-based control framework (MPC), reducing the need for extensive task-specific data collection for each new skill.

5. **Interactive and Iterative Skill Learning:** The coupling of language-based reward generation with real-time model predictive control fosters an interactive feedback loop. Users can observe the robot's behavior in real-time, provide natural language corrections, and immediately see the refined outcome. This iterative process is crucial for rapidly developing and fine-tuning complex behaviors in dynamic and uncertain environments, making robot learning more agile and user-centric.

6. **Robustness and Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment on a real robot arm, despite the inherent sim-to-real gap, underscores the practical robustness of the approach. The introduction of specific regularization terms to bridge this gap highlights the practical considerations necessary for bringing advanced AI-robotics research into the real world. This demonstrates a path towards more reliable and deployable intelligent robots.

**Potential Future Impact:** This research lays foundational groundwork for future developments. It opens avenues for:
- More generalized robot instruction across various morphologies by automating or unifying motion description templates.
- Integrating multimodal inputs (e.g., video demonstrations, gestures) with language to provide even richer forms of user interaction and instruction.
- Advancing LLMs to design more sophisticated reward functions from scratch, rather than just parameterizing pre-defined terms, which would unlock even greater autonomy and creativity in robot behavior generation.
- Enabling robots to understand and execute highly abstract or qualitative instructions (e.g., "act gracefully"), pushing the boundaries of human-robot communication.

Overall, "Language to Rewards for Robotic Skill Synthesis" represents a pivotal step towards creating more intelligent, intuitive, and capable robotic systems that can be programmed and controlled through natural language, thereby accelerating their integration into human society and industrial applications.
