# Scaling Up RL: Unlocking Diverse Reasoning in LLMs via Prolonged Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NVIDIA demonstrate that prolonged reinforcement learning, coupled with specific algorithmic enhancements, can enable a 1.5 billion parameter language model to achieve significant improvements across diverse reasoning tasks like mathematics, coding, and logical puzzles. This work shows sophisticated reasoning can be unlocked in smaller, more resource-efficient models by refining training stability and duration rather than solely scaling model size.

## Problem

- Developing small language models with sophisticated, diverse reasoning capabilities without relying solely on increased model size, which is computationally expensive.
- Addressing common challenges in large-scale Reinforcement Learning for LLMs, such as entropy collapse, maintaining an exploration-exploitation balance, and overcoming performance plateaus during extended optimization.
- The lack of stable and scalable methodologies for prolonged RL training that can reliably enhance reasoning in general-purpose models across multiple domains.

## Method

- Applied prolonged ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to a 1.5 billion parameter Qwen-based model, enhancing it with novel stability mechanisms for extended training durations.
- Integrated ==decoupled clipping== for PPO-style objectives and ==dynamic prompt sampling== to promote exploration, maintain entropy, and improve sample efficiency.
- Introduced ==controlled KL regularization== between the current and reference policies, alongside ==periodic hard resets of the reference policy==, to prevent policy drift and overcome performance plateaus during long training runs.

## Results

- The Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B model achieved substantial performance gains over its baseline, including **+14.7%** in mathematics, **+13.9%** in coding, **+54.8%** in logic puzzles, and **+25.9%** in STEM reasoning.
- The trained model demonstrated strong generalization, performing competitively with larger or domain-specialized baselines (like DeepScaleR-1.5B and DeepCoder-1.5B) despite its smaller size and generalist approach.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of controlled KL regularization, decoupled clipping, dynamic sampling, and periodic reference policy resets in ensuring stable and continuous performance improvements over prolonged training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Controlled KL regularization and periodic resets of the reference policy are critical for mitigating entropy collapse and achieving stable, prolonged training in reinforcement learning for large language models.
> - A higher rollout sampling temperature (e.g., 1.2) and decoupled clipping (with higher upper bounds) effectively promote exploration and prevent early training instability, leading to better performance.
> - Utilizing dynamic sampling, which focuses on prompts of intermediate difficulty, improves sample efficiency and accelerates learning by providing a more informative signal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models has witnessed a rapid paradigm shift towards enhancing reasoning capabilities, exemplified by models like OpenAI's O1 and DeepSeek-R1. These advancements are characterized by two primary features: scaling test-time computation through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and iterative exploration, and leveraging large-scale Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly when combined with verifiable reward signals. Verifiable rewards, which allow for programmatic confirmation of correctness, have proven superior to learned reward models that can suffer from "reward hacking," providing objective and grounded supervision for complex tasks such as mathematics and code generation.

Against this backdrop, the NVIDIA research addresses a crucial niche: the application of *prolonged* reinforcement learning to *smaller* language models to unlock diverse reasoning capabilities. While the prevailing trend often emphasizes scaling model size to achieve better performance, this work explores the orthogonal dimension of scaling *training duration and efficacy* for more compact architectures. This is a significant contribution because it offers a pathway to develop highly capable reasoning models that are more resource-efficient, potentially reducing computational costs for training and inference, and increasing accessibility for a wider range of applications and researchers.

The paper differentiates itself by focusing on several key challenges in large-scale RL for LLMs: entropy collapse, maintaining an exploration-exploitation balance, and overcoming performance plateaus during extended optimization. It builds upon established RL algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and incorporates innovations from DAPO (Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization). However, it critically extends these by introducing novel stability mechanisms, such as controlled KL regularization and periodic reference policy resets. Notably, this work counters recent research advocating for the removal of KL penalties, arguing for their continued benefit when training from strong, pre-trained base models, thereby offering a refined perspective on RL training recipes. By demonstrating competitive performance against larger or domain-specialized models (like DeepScaleR-1.5B and DeepCoder-1.5B) despite its generalist approach and smaller size, this research carves out a significant space in the landscape, advocating for "smart scaling" through optimized RL training rather than solely relying on "brute force scaling" of model parameters.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to comprehensively investigate the impact of *prolonged reinforcement learning* on the performance, stability, and generalization of a *small language model* across a *diverse set of reasoning domains*. The authors aim to go beyond initial RL fine-tuning to understand how sustained optimization can further enhance complex reasoning abilities.

Several motivations underpin this investigation:
- **Leveraging Recent Advancements:** The success of models like OpenAI's O1 and DeepSeek-R1, driven by large-scale RL with verifiable rewards, serves as a strong precedent. The authors seek to extend these benefits to smaller models.
- **Addressing RL Training Challenges:** Despite progress, scaling RL for LLMs presents significant difficulties. These include:
 - **Entropy Collapse:** Where the model's output distribution becomes too narrow, limiting exploration and hindering learning.
 - **Exploration-Exploitation Balance:** The perpetual challenge of ensuring the model sufficiently explores new solutions while exploiting learned knowledge.
 - **Performance Plateaus:** The common issue of models stagnating in performance even with continued training.
 - **Lack of Stable and Scalable Recipes:** A need for robust methodologies that can reliably train reasoning models over long durations.
- **Democratizing Reasoning Capabilities:** By focusing on a 1.5 billion parameter model (Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B, initialized from DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B), the research aims to demonstrate that sophisticated reasoning is not exclusive to gargantuan, computationally expensive models. This enables more accessible and efficient AI development.
- **Identifying Key Training Ingredients:** The project seeks to pinpoint specific algorithmic and practical techniques that are critical for achieving effective, stable, and generalizable performance gains through prolonged RL.
- **Contributing to Open Science:** A stated motivation is to open-source the resulting model and the training methodology to foster continued research and development in the broader AI community, particularly in resource-efficient LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on applying and refining Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for prolonged training of a Qwen-1.5B based language model across a broad spectrum of reasoning tasks.

#### 3.1. Base Model and RL Algorithm

The starting point is a pre-trained model, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, which is already capable of generating coherent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) outputs. The core reinforcement learning algorithm employed is **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, chosen for its efficiency by foregoing a separate value model and estimating baselines from group scores, akin to PPO but without a critic.

#### 3.2. Enhancements for Stability and Efficiency

The authors integrate several key enhancements:
- **Decoupled Clipping:** Inspired by DAPO, the PPO-style objective's clipping bounds are separated into 𝜖𝑙𝑜𝑤 (for negative advantages) and 𝜖ℎ𝑖𝑔ℎ (for positive advantages). Setting a higher 𝜖ℎ𝑖𝑔ℎ promotes "clip-higher," which encourages the model to uplift probabilities of previously unlikely tokens, thereby fostering broader exploration and maintaining entropy.
- **Dynamic Prompt Sampling:** Also from DAPO, this technique filters out prompts where the model consistently succeeds or fails (accuracy 1 or 0). By focusing on intermediate difficulty examples, the approach ensures a more stable and informative learning signal, improving sample efficiency.
- **Controlled KL Regularization:** A crucial deviation from some recent works, the authors reintroduce a small but non-zero KL divergence penalty between the current policy (𝜋𝜃) and a reference policy (𝜋𝑟𝑒𝑓). This term (𝐿𝐾𝐿−𝑅𝐿(𝜃) = 𝐿𝐺𝑅𝑃𝑂(𝜃) − 𝛽𝐷𝐾𝐿(𝜋𝜃 ||𝜋𝑟𝑒𝑓)) is vital for maintaining entropy, preventing the online policy from drifting too far, and stabilizing learning, especially when starting from a strong pre-trained checkpoint.
- **Periodic Reference Policy Reset:** To mitigate issues of KL term dominance and performance plateaus during prolonged training, the reference policy (𝜋𝑟𝑒𝑓) is periodically hard-reset to a more recent snapshot of the online policy (𝜋𝜃), and optimizer states are reinitialized. This strategy reinvigorates learning dynamics, enabling continuous improvement.

#### 3.3. Diverse Training Data and Reward Mechanism

A critical aspect is the use of a wide variety of tasks with *verifiable reward signals*, spanning:
- **Mathematics:** 40K problems from DeepScaleR, using a binary reward via an improved `math-verify` system.
- **Code Generation:** 24K problems from Eurus-2-RL, with continuous rewards based on the fraction of test cases passed in a refined execution environment.
- **STEM Problem Solving:** 25K filtered problems from SCP-116K, using binary rewards verified by GPT-4o.
- **Logical Puzzles:** 37K synthetic samples from Reasoning Gym, with continuous rewards from the gym’s verifier.
- **Instruction Following:** 10K synthetic data from Llama-Nemotron, evaluated with the strict mode of the IFEval verifier.

To manage this diversity, a **sandboxed reward server architecture** is implemented. This design allows for customized execution environments per task, isolates code execution for security and fault-tolerance, and leverages multiprocessing and distributed reward servers for efficient parallelization and high throughput during reward computation.

#### 3.4. Staged Training Strategy

The training process is meticulously designed as a multi-stage, iterative refinement:
- **Iterative Refinement:** Sequential runs address specific challenges, incorporate new data, adjust hyperparameters, and perform resets.
- **Continuous Monitoring:** Key metrics (KL divergence, entropy, response length, validation scores) are closely monitored to guide interventions like reward shaping (e.g., penalizing non-termination), context window changes, or rollout count adjustments.
- **Hardware:** Training was conducted on 4 NVIDIA-H100-80GB nodes, accumulating approximately 16,000 GPU hours.
- **Hyperparameters:** Rollout sampling temperature of 1.2 (for exploration), batch size 256, mini-batch size 64, AdamW optimizer with a constant learning rate of 2x10⁻⁶. Decoupled clipping set to 𝜖𝑙𝑜𝑤 = 0.2, 𝜖ℎ𝑖𝑔ℎ = 0.4, and KL penalty coefficient 𝛽 = 0.0001.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded compelling results, demonstrating the efficacy of prolonged RL training and the proposed enhancements on a smaller LLM.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Gains and Generalization

The final model, Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B, achieved significant performance improvements over its baseline, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, across a diverse set of reasoning domains:
- **Mathematics:** Average improvement of +15.7% (specific claim of +14.7% in abstract).
- **Coding:** +13.9% improvement, particularly in competitive programming tasks.
- **Logic Puzzles:** A dramatic +54.8% improvement, highlighting significant gains in this challenging domain, although some harder categories still show room for improvement (e.g., arc, code, cognition, games).
- **STEM Reasoning (GPQA Diamond):** +25.1% improvement (specific claim of +25.9%).
- **Instruction Following (IFEval):** +18.1% improvement (specific claim of +22.0%).

Crucially, the model also demonstrated strong **generalization capabilities**. Despite being trained on a broad range of domains rather than being narrowly specialized, it achieved competitive performance with domain-specialized baselines like DeepScaleR-1.5B (for math) and DeepCoder-1.5B (for code). This suggests that the generalized RL approach can effectively bridge the gap between general-purpose models and specialized reasoning systems.

#### 4.2. Impact of Key Ingredients (Ablation Studies)

The ablation studies provided critical insights into the contribution of each proposed component:
- **Rollout Sampling Temperature:** A higher sampling temperature (1.2) consistently yielded more stable training and better performance, preventing early instability and entropy collapse. This holds true for both early and late stages of training, demonstrating that promoting exploration through higher temperature is beneficial even when the model is already strong.
- **Decoupled Clipping:** Setting 𝜖𝑙𝑜𝑤 = 0.2 and 𝜖ℎ𝑖𝑔ℎ = 0.4 significantly outperformed more conservative clipping ratios. The higher upper threshold (𝜖ℎ𝑖𝑔ℎ) was particularly effective in mitigating entropy collapse and achieving higher validation performance. While a smaller 𝜖𝑙𝑜𝑤 increased policy entropy, it did not translate to better learning, emphasizing the need for a balanced exploration-exploitation.
- **Dynamic Sampling:** Proved effective in improving sample efficiency and leading to faster improvements by filtering out prompts that offer no learning signal (zero advantage).
- **KL Regularization and Reference Policy Reset:** These were identified as critical for mitigating entropy collapse and enabling stable, prolonged training. The KL penalty helps maintain entropy and prevent policy drift, which is particularly beneficial when starting from a strong pre-trained model. Periodic hard resets of the reference policy and optimizer states were crucial for overcoming performance plateaus and recovering from degradation, allowing for continuous improvement over extended training durations.
- **Mitigating Entropy Collapse:** Comparisons with GRPO-KL=0, DAPO, and AdaptiveEntropy showed that all methods had varying degrees of effectiveness. The combination of DAPO and a small KL penalty proved to be a robust and balanced solution, preserving entropy while maintaining training stability without adding excessive hyperparameter complexity.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics

The staged training strategy, guided by continuous monitoring of metrics, proved essential. The interventions, such as initial adaptation to an 8k context window, followed by later extension to 16k, reward shaping for termination issues, and increasing rollout counts, all contributed to refining model behavior and sustaining performance gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research from NVIDIA carries substantial significance and potential impact on the development and deployment of advanced AI systems:

**Enabling Accessible and Resource-Efficient Reasoning LLMs:** By demonstrating that a relatively small 1.5 billion parameter model can achieve state-of-the-art reasoning capabilities competitive with larger and specialized models, this work challenges the prevailing "bigger is better" paradigm. This implies that highly capable reasoning AI can be developed and deployed with significantly reduced computational resources, making advanced LLMs more accessible to a wider range of researchers, developers, and organizations, and potentially leading to more efficient real-world applications.

**Advancing Robust Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** The paper provides a detailed and empirically validated blueprint for stable and effective prolonged RL training of language models. The identification and refinement of critical components—controlled KL regularization, decoupled clipping, dynamic sampling, and especially the novel periodic reference policy resets—address long-standing challenges like entropy collapse and training stagnation. These insights are invaluable for future RL research, contributing to more robust and scalable training methodologies. The counter-intuitive finding regarding the continued benefit of KL regularization for strong base models offers a nuanced perspective that could guide future algorithm design.

**Fostering Generalization Across Diverse Reasoning Domains:** The achievement of strong performance across a wide array of tasks—mathematics, coding, logical puzzles, STEM, and instruction following—underscores the model's generalized reasoning capabilities. This reduces the need for developing and maintaining highly specialized models for each distinct domain, leading to more versatile and adaptable AI systems. This generalizability is crucial for creating truly intelligent agents that can tackle unforeseen problems.

**Open Science and Community Contribution:** The public release of the "Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B" model on Hugging Face, coupled with a transparent exposition of the training methodology, is a substantial contribution to the open-source AI community. This facilitates reproducibility, encourages further research and innovation building upon their work, and potentially accelerates progress in areas like alignment, optimization, and reasoning within resource-efficient LLMs.

**Reinforcing NVIDIA's Position in AI:** This research strategically positions NVIDIA not merely as a hardware vendor but as a leading innovator in AI model development. It showcases their ability to leverage their advanced compute infrastructure to push the boundaries of AI capabilities, demonstrating the full potential of their platforms and strengthening their influence in the broader AI ecosystem. This vertical integration of hardware and cutting-edge model research enhances their strategic advantage.
