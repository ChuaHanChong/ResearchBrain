# HUMEMBR: Learning Human Routines for Predictive Embodied Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> HUMEMBR enables embodied robots to learn multi-day human routines and track persistent identities for predictive navigation and question answering in dynamic environments. The framework achieves 75.41% accuracy on the PersonEQA benchmark, outperforming baselines while reducing token usage by up to 83%, and demonstrates reliable performance in real-world robot deployments.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic navigation relies on static spatial information, failing to account for dynamic, human-centric environments shaped by daily routines.
- Existing embodied AI systems often lack mechanisms for modeling multi-day human routines or maintaining persistent, identity-aware memory of dynamic human behavior over extended periods.
- Robots need long-horizon reasoning and predictive capabilities to anticipate human behavior, such as typical locations or arrival times, for more effective and proactive assistance.

## Method

- The **HUMEMBR** system uses parallel ==Memory Building and Querying== processes to continuously accumulate structured, identity-aware data on human routines.
- A ==two-stage clustering framework== integrates ==facial recognition== and full-body ==Keypoint Promptable Re-Identification (KPR)== embeddings for robust, persistent human identity association.
- A ==retrieval-augmented LLM== strategy, leveraging five structured retrieval functions, iteratively refines answers to natural language queries and guides routine-conditioned navigation.

## Results

- On the PersonEQA benchmark, HUMEMBR with Gemini 3 Flash achieved **75.41%** total accuracy, surpassing the full-context baseline (**67.33%**) while consuming **83%** fewer tokens.
- Real-world deployments on a Boston Dynamics Spot robot showed reliable performance and high success rates (e.g., **90%** for 'Find Dory', **100%** for 'Pick up visitor') across diverse tasks, guided by routine-based predictions.
- Ablation studies confirmed that including Keypoint Promptable Re-Identification (KPR) for person association significantly improved overall accuracy, increasing it from **60.49%** to **66.01%** for the Qwen3-VL 235B configuration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured memory construction and targeted retrieval functions allow embodied systems to achieve higher reasoning accuracy while substantially reducing LLM token usage compared to full-context baselines.
> - Combining facial and full-body re-identification features is critical for building persistent, identity-aware memories of human routines, especially in dynamic environments with appearance changes.
> - Predictive, routine-based navigation leads to more efficient robot task execution in human-populated spaces, minimizing exhaustive searches and enabling goal-directed movement based on learned patterns.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotics research has progressed significantly in equipping robots to navigate and understand environments. Traditional robotic representations, such as metric maps and semantic scene graphs, primarily focus on static spatial information, enabling capabilities like obstacle avoidance and short-term navigation. However, these systems generally do not account for the dynamic, human-centric nature of real-world environments like offices or homes, where daily routines and behavioral patterns of individuals introduce significant temporal structure.

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have enabled robots to interpret natural language queries, decompose complex instructions, and generate actionable plans. Embodied Question Answering (EQA) frameworks and retrieval-augmented reasoning systems have shown that robots can access stored observations to answer questions about past events. However, these approaches often concentrate on object-centric reasoning or operate within limited temporal horizons, typically lacking explicit models for multi-day human routines, persistent identity tracking, or predictive reasoning about human behavior. This gap is particularly relevant for long-term robot deployments where understanding and anticipating human activities can enhance utility.

The present work, HUMEMBR, addresses this specific limitation by introducing a framework designed to build and leverage a human-centered, long-horizon memory. It contrasts with prior EQA benchmarks that primarily focus on object attributes or static scene states, and with existing long-term memory systems that do not explicitly model persistent human identities or recurring routines. While some previous methods incorporate long-term memory for retrospective state retrieval, they do not formulate semantic navigation as routine-aware prediction. Furthermore, existing person clustering and re-identification (ReID) research largely focuses on perception in static or controlled settings, whereas HUMEMBR integrates these techniques into an embodied, mobile robotic system for long-term, identity-aware question answering and navigation.

By combining continuous memory construction with retrieval-augmented LLM reasoning, HUMEMBR aims to bridge the gap between static environmental understanding and dynamic human behavior modeling. It seeks to enable robots to reason about human routines over multi-day observations, thereby supporting predictive capabilities not commonly found in current embodied systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenge of deploying robots in human-centered environments where successful operation requires more than just spatial awareness. Robots need to understand and adapt to the dynamic world shaped by human daily routines and temporal regularities. This necessitates reasoning about human behavior over extended periods, accumulating knowledge about recurring patterns, and leveraging this knowledge for various tasks.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Understanding and Navigating Dynamic Human-Centered Environments:** The paper highlights that real-world environments, such as offices and households, are characterized by human routines (e.g., arrival/departure times, preferred locations, object interactions). Conventional robotic representations, which are largely static, are insufficient for operating effectively in these dynamic settings. The objective is to equip robots with the capability to account for and adapt to these human-driven dynamics.
- **Addressing Limitations in Existing Embodied AI:** Current embodied question answering (EQA) and retrieval-augmented reasoning systems generally focus on object-centric reasoning or limited temporal horizons. They often lack mechanisms for explicitly modeling multi-day human routines or maintaining persistent, identity-aware memory of dynamic human behavior. This deficit prevents robots from answering queries like "When does a specific person typically arrive?" or "Where are they usually found at certain times?"
- **Enabling Long-Horizon Reasoning and Predictive Capabilities:** A core objective is to enable robots to reason over extensive histories of observations, capturing long-term behavioral patterns to support predictive tasks. This includes predicting where a person is likely to be or estimating their arrival/departure times, which current systems typically cannot achieve.
- **Developing a Human-Centered Memory Framework:** The research aims to design and implement a novel memory framework, HUMEMBR, that continuously accumulates structured representations of human routines. This memory must be persistent, identity-aware, and capable of integrating information across multiple days despite variations in appearance (e.g., clothing, hairstyles) and environmental conditions.
- **Supporting Embodied Question Answering and Routine-Conditioned Navigation:** A central objective is to allow robots to respond to natural language queries grounded in human routines and execute navigation tasks based on this accumulated knowledge. For example, a robot should be able to "Search Nemo" by inferring probable locations based on historical routine data.
- **Introducing a Benchmark for Human-Centered Long-Horizon Reasoning:** To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed system and stimulate further research, the authors aimed to create a new benchmark, PersonEQA. This benchmark is specifically designed to assess a robot's ability to identify individuals, reason about human routines, and answer diverse human-centered questions over extended temporal contexts.
- **Demonstrating Real-World Applicability:** The motivation extends to validating the system's performance in physical robot deployments under realistic conditions, thereby demonstrating its ability to handle diverse queries and navigation tasks in dynamic, live environments.

In summary, the motivation is to advance embodied robotics beyond static scene understanding, enabling robots to become more intelligent and helpful in human-populated spaces by actively learning and reasoning about the dynamic and routine-driven aspects of human behavior over long periods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HUMEMBR system is designed with two parallel, continuously operating processes: Memory Building and Querying. This concurrent design allows the robot to incrementally gather information about human activities while simultaneously supporting interactive, user-driven queries grounded in long-term behavioral patterns.

#### 3.1. Memory Building

The memory construction module focuses on building a structured, long-term representation of the environment that includes persistent, identity-aware information about humans and their activities.

- **Data Acquisition and Filtering:** The robot captures RGB observations at 2 Hz. To manage memory growth and reduce redundancy, a ResNet50-based feature encoder is used to filter incoming frames. Only visually distinctive frames, identified by a cosine similarity threshold against the most recently retained frame, are stored.
- **Memory Entry Components:** Each retained memory entry includes the RGB image, its corresponding spatial reference (from the Boston Dynamics GraphNav system), and a timestamp.
- **Semantic Context Generation:** Each image is annotated with a descriptive caption generated by Qwen-3-VL 235B. These captions are then embedded into dense vector representations using mxbai-embed-large-v1, facilitating efficient similarity-based retrieval.
- **Human Instance Detection:** Human instances are detected using a YOLO-based detector, conditioned on the visibility of a sufficient number of body keypoints.
- **Identity Representation:** To achieve robust and persistent identity tracking, two complementary embedding models are used:
 - **Facial Recognition:** InsightFace is employed to extract facial embeddings when a face is visible, serving as a primary, invariant identity anchor.
 - **Full-Body Appearance:** Keypoint Promptable Re-Identification (KPR) provides full-body appearance features, which are effective under pose variation, partial occlusion, or when faces are not visible.
- **Identity Association (Two-Stage Clustering):** An unsupervised two-stage clustering framework is used for identity association in dynamic environments:
 - **Stage 1 (Facial Clustering):** Detections with visible facial embeddings are clustered using an online DBSCAN algorithm. This stage establishes high-confidence identity anchors.
 - **Stage 2 (ReID-based Association):** Detections without visible faces are associated with existing identity clusters using their KPR ReID embeddings via nearest-neighbor matching, subject to a predefined similarity threshold. This balances minimizing incorrect associations with maximizing assignment coverage.

#### 3.2. Question Answering and Semantic Navigation

This module processes natural language queries by retrieving relevant information from the long-term memory and translating reasoning into executable actions.

- **Retrieval-Augmented Strategy:** Instead of processing the entire memory, a query-conditioned subset of memory entries is retrieved to enhance computational efficiency and focus relevant context.
- **Structured Retrieval Functions:** The language model interacts with memory through five structured retrieval functions:
 - **`Semantic observations f(Q, P, te)`:** Retrieves the top-n (n=30) observations semantically similar to query Q. A relevance score combines cosine distance (semantic similarity) with temporal recency, penalizing older observations. Retrieval can be filtered by person identity (P) and limited to observations prior to a specific time (te).
 - **`Waypoint observations f(W, ts, te)`:** Retrieves all observations recorded at a specific waypoint (W) within a given time interval ([ts, te]).
 - **`Person observations f(P, ts, te)`:** Retrieves chronologically ordered observations associated with a specified person identity (P) within a time interval ([ts, te]).
 - **`Persons per day f(d)`:** Returns the set of unique person identities observed on a particular date (d).
 - **`Daily summary for person f(d, P)`:** Aggregates all observations of person P on date d and uses an LLM to generate a summary of their activities.
- **Iterative Reasoning:** The LLM iteratively invokes these retrieval functions, progressively refining hypotheses about locations, time intervals, or activity patterns, until sufficient evidence is gathered or a function-call limit is reached.
- **Control Interface for Embodied Execution:** The model is provided with a control function for robot navigation:
 - **`Navigate to waypoint f(W, P)`:** Commands the robot to navigate to a specified waypoint (W). Optionally, it can verify the presence of person P upon arrival.

#### 3.3. Data Collection and Benchmark

- **Collaborative Office Behavior Dataset (COBD):** A new dataset was collected using a Boston Dynamics Spot robot in a real-world indoor office environment. It spans 20 days, totaling 31 hours of robot operation across 136 distinct waypoints. The dataset captures unconstrained human activity, with individuals following their normal routines, ensuring realistic appearance changes over time. Each sequence includes synchronized waypoints, visual observations (stitched front-facing camera images), and timestamps.
- **PersonEQA Benchmark:** Derived from the COBD dataset, PersonEQA is a human-centered, long-horizon embodied question answering benchmark. It consists of 200 temporally grounded question-answer pairs reformulated for a multi-day memory setting. Questions are categorized into six types: Spatial, Temporal (Duration, Time), Binary, Descriptive, and Person Identification. These categories evaluate both factual retrieval and higher-level reasoning about human routines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HUMEMBR involved both quantitative assessment on the PersonEQA benchmark and qualitative results from real-world robot deployments.

#### 4.1. PersonEQA Benchmark Results

- **Improved Accuracy with Reduced Token Usage:** HUMEMBR, when utilizing Gemini 3 Flash as the backbone LLM, achieved a total accuracy of 75.41%, surpassing the full-context baseline (67.33%) while consuming 83% fewer tokens per question (106,347 vs. 631,651). For open-source LLMs, HUMEMBR with Qwen3-VL 235B achieved a comparable accuracy (66.01%) to the baseline, but with a substantial reduction in token usage (11,589 tokens, approximately 2% of the baseline's token count).
- **Category-Specific Performance:**
 - **Spatial Reasoning:** HUMEMBR (Gemini 3 Flash) demonstrated significantly higher accuracy in spatial questions (92.31%) compared to the full-context baseline (34.62%). This suggests that structured retrieval mitigates context dilution, where the baseline struggled with attention diffusion across numerous waypoint identifiers in large, unstructured contexts.
 - **Semantic and Duration Reasoning:** HUMEMBR (Gemini 3 Flash) also performed well in semantic (81.48%) and duration questions (77.08%), outperforming the baseline in both.
 - **Person Identification and Time of Event:** The baseline slightly outperformed HUMEMBR on person identification (65.71% vs. 60.95%) and time-of-event questions (67.39% vs. 65.94%). This minor advantage for the baseline is attributed to its exhaustive access to all captions and timestamps, which might facilitate direct lookup for these specific question types.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Re-Identification (ReID):** The inclusion of the Keypoint Promptable Re-Identification (KPR) model for person association significantly improved overall accuracy. For instance, the Qwen3-VL 235B configuration without ReID achieved 60.49% total accuracy, which increased to 66.01% with ReID, demonstrating the robustness it adds to person tracking.
 - **Function Call Limits:** Varying the maximum number of retrieval function calls (5, 10, 15) did not show a consistent improvement in accuracy. A limit of 10 function calls appeared to offer an effective balance, providing sufficient information without introducing extraneous context.
 - **Caption Prompting Strategies:** Different prompting strategies for caption generation were evaluated. The "Interaction-centered" prompt generally yielded slightly better performance for the Qwen models, suggesting that the quality and focus of generated captions influence downstream retrieval and reasoning.

#### 4.2. Real-World Deployment Results

HUMEMBR was deployed on a Boston Dynamics Spot robot in the same office environment used for COBD data collection, with historical recordings preloaded into its memory. The system was evaluated on six representative tasks, each repeated over ten runs, across categories such as Routine-Based Search, Multi-Step Task Execution, Person–Object Interaction and Delivery, and Perception Robustness.

- **Reliable Performance:** The system demonstrated reliable performance across diverse real-world tasks (Table IV). High success rates were observed for tasks like "Find Dory" (90%), "Pick up visitor and show around" (100%), and "Pick up package and bring back" (100%).
- **Efficient Navigation:** The observed low number of visited waypoints and function calls per task indicated that navigation was frequently guided by accurate routine-based predictions rather than extensive, exhaustive search. Even for complex queries, navigation remained efficient, requiring minimal waypoints.
- **Perception-Related Limitations:** The primary source of failure was related to limitations in perception, particularly under challenging visual conditions. In tasks like "Are Early Birds already on-site" (60% success) and "Find people in partial occlusion" (50% success), failures occurred when target individuals were partially occluded (e.g., by a chair), observed from the back, or affected by strong backlighting or low illumination. These conditions reduced the reliability of face detection and person ReID, leading to lower "First-Visit Detection" rates and occasional search errors.
- **Occasional Hallucination:** In instances where the target person was genuinely absent, the model occasionally exhibited hallucination, inferring prior observations of that individual.

Overall, the real-world experiments indicated that HUMEMBR's long-horizon routine modeling supports efficient and goal-directed navigation. However, the robustness of underlying person perception in difficult visual scenarios remains a key area for potential improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HUMEMBR framework represents a notable advancement in equipping robots to operate effectively in dynamic, human-centered environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Predictive and Routine-Aware Embodied Navigation:** This work moves beyond traditional static or short-term memory approaches by enabling robots to build and reason over long-horizon, identity-aware memories of human routines. This capability allows for predictive reasoning about human behavior (e.g., estimating likely locations, arrival times), which is a crucial step towards more proactive and intelligent robotic assistance in human-populated spaces.
- **Addressing a Critical Gap in Embodied AI:** HUMEMBR directly tackles the limitation of existing embodied systems that typically lack persistent, identity-aware memory of dynamic human behavior over extended periods. This contributes to bridging the gap between understanding static environments and comprehending the temporal dynamics introduced by human presence and routines.
- **Enhanced Efficiency through Structured Memory and Retrieval:** The system demonstrates that by employing structured memory construction and targeted retrieval functions, robots can achieve higher reasoning accuracy while substantially reducing the computational overhead and token usage associated with large language models. This efficiency is critical for scalable and real-time deployment in real-world scenarios.
- **Foundation for Complex Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling robots to answer human-centered questions about individuals' activities and daily habits, and to execute person-search tasks based on accumulated routine knowledge, HUMEMBR lays a foundation for more sophisticated and intuitive human-robot interaction. This could lead to robots that are better integrated into daily life, performing tasks like finding colleagues, delivering items to specific individuals, or assisting with routine-based queries.
- **New Benchmark for Long-Horizon Reasoning:** The introduction of the PersonEQA benchmark provides a standardized tool for evaluating future research in human-centered, long-horizon embodied question answering. This can accelerate advancements in the field by offering a clear measure for comparing different methodologies.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment on a physical robot in live office environments validates the practical utility and robustness of the HUMEMBR framework under realistic conditions. This is an important step towards transitioning research concepts into deployable robotic systems.
- **Ethical Considerations and Responsible AI Development:** The paper acknowledges the privacy risks associated with modeling persistent human routines and outlines mitigation strategies, including informed consent, pseudonymization, and restricted data access. This proactive consideration of ethical implications is important for the responsible development of human-aware AI systems.

In conclusion, HUMEMBR contributes to the development of more context-aware and socially intelligent robots by providing them with the ability to learn and leverage human routines, thereby enhancing their navigation, question-answering, and overall utility in dynamic human environments.
