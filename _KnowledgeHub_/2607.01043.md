# DART-VLN: Test-Time Memory Decay and Anti-Loop Regularization for Discrete Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> DART-VLN introduces a training-free, test-time control framework for discrete Vision-Language Navigation agents that addresses stale memory and inefficient local movements. It leverages read-side memory decay and anti-loop regularization to reduce trajectory length and runtime by up to 65% while maintaining or improving navigation success on R2R and REVERIE benchmarks.

## Problem

- Memory-based discrete VLN agents struggle with stale or redundant historical evidence at memory readout, leading to noisy decision-making at inference time.
- Even strong pre-trained VLN models frequently exhibit inefficient local backtracking and short loops, which prolong trajectories and consume unnecessary steps.
- Enhancing existing VLN navigators often requires costly retraining, architectural redesign, or heavy planning modules, limiting practical application for deployed models.

## Method

- **DART-VLN** implements a ==Test-Time Memory Decay== module that reweights memory slot contributions based on recency, visit count, and novelty during readout, suppressing stale and redundant evidence.
- Introduces an ==Anti-Loop Regularization== module that applies a lightweight ==next-hop based penalty== to action scores, discouraging immediate reversals and repeated revisits.
- The framework operates without modifying the learned backbone or introducing new learnable parameters, making it a ==training-free==, low-intrusion intervention for existing memory-based VLN pipelines.

## Results

- DART-VLN, particularly with memory decay and anti-loop regularization, reduced average trajectory length (e.g., from `val unseen`3.27 to `val unseen`2.4`val unseen` on R2R `val unseen`) and significantly lowered runtime (e.g., from **937.99s** to **552.27s** on R2R `val unseen`).
- The framework consistently maintained or improved key navigation metrics such as Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) across R2R and REVERIE benchmarks (e.g., R2R `val unseen` SPL increased from **64** to **66**).
- Anti-loop regularization effectively reduced the immediate backtrack rate (e.g., from 3.5`decay+anti-loop`% for `decay-only` to 2.`decay-only``decay+anti-loop`% with `decay+anti-loop` on R2R), directly contributing to more efficient path execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conservative, training-free interventions applied purely at inference time can yield measurable improvements in VLN agent efficiency and reliability, even for strong, frozen backbones.
> - Selectively reweighting memory at readout based on age, visits, and novelty is an effective strategy for denoising historical evidence, leading to more focused decision-making and reduced runtime.
> - Simple graph-local penalties on immediate backtracking and repeated revisits are effective in reducing inefficient local movements without over-constraining the agent's exploration capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) is a field concerned with enabling embodied agents to navigate environments based on natural language instructions and visual observations. Discrete VLN, specifically, operates on explicit viewpoint graphs, allowing for step-wise decision-making. Recent advancements in VLN have stemmed from various directions, including the development of more robust pre-trained navigators, the incorporation of explicit memory or map representations, the utilization of extensive training datasets, and the implementation of sophisticated recovery or planning mechanisms. These efforts have collectively improved long-horizon reasoning and benchmark performance in VLN.

Despite these advancements, a gap persists at inference time, particularly when working with strong, pre-trained, and frozen backbone models. Such models, while competitive, can still exhibit unreliable behavior. Existing approaches to address these limitations often involve retraining the model, redesigning its architecture, or integrating heavier planning modules. However, these solutions are less practical when the objective is to enhance an already capable navigator with minimal intervention.

This paper addresses two specific failure modes observed in memory-based discrete VLN agents at test time:
1. **Stale or redundant historical evidence at memory readout:** Explicit navigation memory, used by agents to reason over longer trajectories, can become cluttered with outdated or repeatedly observed information. This can lead to noisy memory aggregation and less selective decision-making.
2. **Inefficient local backtracking during action selection:** Even robust frozen backbones can exhibit behaviors such as immediate reversals or short loops, which, while not always preventing task completion, prolong trajectories, consume unnecessary steps, and increase runtime.

DART-VLN is positioned as a lightweight, training-free test-time control framework that seeks to mitigate these issues without altering the learned backbone or introducing new learnable parameters. It distinguishes itself from prior work in several ways:
- **Memory Management:** Unlike methods that focus on constructing new memory structures, map encoders, or fusion architectures (e.g., GridMM, DUET, BEVBert, HAMT), DART-VLN operates on existing explicit-memory pipelines. Its contribution lies in improving *how* historical evidence is utilized at test time, specifically through read-side reweighting, rather than redesigning the memory itself or its write mechanisms.
- **Local Recovery:** While other research targets local navigation failures through regret-aware agents, foresighted navigation, or planning modules (e.g., The Regretful Agent, DREAMWALKER, NavQ), DART-VLN's anti-loop regularization module is intentionally narrower. It acts as a lightweight next-hop regularizer on graph-local action scores at inference time, avoiding the complexity of a full planner or learned self-correction policy.
- **Intervention Scope:** This work aligns with a growing interest in training-free or test-time interventions in VLN (e.g., TRAVEL, Active Test-Time Vision-Language Navigation). It contrasts with approaches that yield gains through architectural extensions, richer scene modeling, or heavier planning modules, which necessitate substantial retraining or redesign. DART-VLN's low-intrusion nature aims to improve deployed navigators without a new training cycle.

In summary, DART-VLN contributes to the VLN landscape by providing a method for enhancing the reliability and efficiency of memory-based discrete VLN agents through minimal, interpretable, and training-free adjustments at inference time, specifically targeting memory readout and local action selection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a training-free test-time control framework for discrete Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) agents that leverages existing memory-based backbones. The motivation stems from observed limitations in even strong, pre-trained, and frozen VLN models, which despite their capabilities, still exhibit vulnerabilities during inference. The authors aim to demonstrate that significant improvements in navigation performance and efficiency can be achieved through lightweight interventions at test time, without necessitating model retraining or architectural modifications.

Specifically, the key objectives and their underlying motivations are:

1. **Address Stale and Redundant Historical Evidence in Memory:**
 - **Motivation:** Memory-based navigation agents accumulate historical information to support long-horizon reasoning. However, as a trajectory unfolds, some stored memory slots may become outdated, repeatedly observed, or marginally relevant to the agent's current decision. If such "stale" evidence continues to influence memory aggregation with the same strength as more recent or pertinent observations, it can introduce noise and reduce the selectivity of the decision-making process at test time. This can lead to suboptimal path choices or increased uncertainty.
 - **Objective:** To implement a read-side memory control strategy that selectively suppresses the influence of stale and redundant historical evidence during memory readout. This intervention is designed to be conservative, reweighting memory contributions rather than altering the stored content itself, thereby preserving compatibility with the existing memory structure.

2. **Reduce Inefficient Local Backtracking and Short Loops:**
 - **Motivation:** A common failure mode in embodied navigation is inefficient local behavior, such as immediately reversing direction or engaging in short, repetitive loops. These actions consume additional steps, prolong trajectory lengths, increase computational runtime, and expose the agent to more decision points where further errors can accumulate. While such behaviors may not always prevent an agent from eventually reaching its goal, they diminish the efficiency and interpretability of the navigation process.
 - **Objective:** To introduce a lightweight action regularization mechanism that discourages immediate reversals and repeated revisits during action selection. This mechanism is intended to act as a graph-local next-hop penalty, improving path efficiency and reducing wasteful movements under frozen backbones, without serving as a complex planner or a learned self-correction policy.

3. **Achieve Improvements without Retraining or Architectural Redesign:**
 - **Motivation:** Many advancements in VLN require substantial modifications to the model architecture, extensive re-training, or the integration of heavy planning modules. These approaches are resource-intensive and often impractical for improving deployed or already competitive navigators where the goal is minimal intervention.
 - **Objective:** To demonstrate that a combination of the proposed memory decay and anti-loop regularization, acting purely at inference time, can yield a favorable quality-efficiency trade-off. The framework is designed to introduce no new learnable parameters and leave the learned backbone unchanged, thereby providing a low-intrusion method for enhancing existing VLN agents.

In summary, DART-VLN aims to provide an interpretable and efficient solution to common inference-time failures in memory-based discrete VLN, proving that targeted test-time control can yield practical benefits without the overhead of retraining.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DART-VLN is presented as a training-free test-time control framework designed to enhance the performance and efficiency of memory-based discrete Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) agents. The framework operates on an existing GridMM-style discrete VLN pipeline, intervening specifically at memory readout and action selection stages without modifying the learned backbone or introducing new learnable parameters.

#### 3.1. A. Problem Setting and Baseline Navigation Inference

The agent operates on a discrete navigation graph $G=(V, E)$, starting from an initial viewpoint $v_0$ and following a natural-language instruction $x$ to generate a trajectory $\tau = (v_0, v_1, \ldots, v_T)$. At each step $t$, the agent processes the current visual observation at $v_t$, retrieves historical information from explicit memory slots $\{m_i\}$, and computes scores $s_t(v)$ for candidate actions (target viewpoints $v$). The baseline GridMM framework encodes instructions and observations, aggregates historical evidence, and selects the next action based on these scores. The core challenge addressed is how stale or redundant historical evidence impacts this decision-making process at inference time.

#### 3.2. B. Test-Time Memory Decay

1. **Motivation**: As trajectories lengthen, some historical memory slots become outdated or repeatedly observed, contributing noise to memory aggregation. DART-VLN addresses this by reweighting memory contributions at readout rather than altering stored content.
2. **Slot Metadata**: For each memory slot $m_i$, three metadata variables are maintained:
 - **Slot Age ($a_i$)**: $t - t_{\text{last}, i}$, where $t_{\text{last}, i}$ is the most recent step the slot was refreshed.
 - **Visit Count ($c_i$)**: How often the corresponding region has been observed.
 - **Novelty ($n_i$)**: Captures recent feature changes. An instantaneous novelty score $\nu_i = \text{clip}(1 - \cos(f^{\text{old}}_i, f^{\text{new}}_i), 0, 1)$ is computed (cosine similarity between previous and current slot features), then smoothed over time using an exponential moving average: $n_i^{(t)} = \rho n_i^{(t-1)} + (1 - \rho) \nu_i$. (Default $\rho=0.5$).
3. **Memory Reweighting**: A heuristic readout weight $w_i$ is assigned to each memory slot:
 $w_i = \text{clip}\left(\exp(-\lambda a_i) \cdot \left(1 - \alpha \frac{c_i}{c_i + 1}\right) \cdot (0.5 + 0.5n_i), w_{\min}, w_{\max}\right)$.
 - The term $\exp(-\lambda a_i)$ downweights older slots (recency).
 - The term $(1 - \alpha \frac{c_i}{c_i + 1})$ reduces influence of repeatedly visited regions (repetition), where $\alpha$ controls suppression strength.
 - The term $(0.5 + 0.5n_i)$ favors slots exhibiting recent feature change (novelty).
 - Weights are clipped to $[w_{\min}, w_{\max}]$. These weights are applied during memory readout to aggregate a weighted memory representation, which is then fed into the original navigation pipeline.
4. **Boundary of Decay-Only**: This refers to the application of only the read-side reweighting (Eq. 3), leaving the stored slot representations and baseline memory update behavior unchanged. It is characterized as a read-side denoising rule.
5. **Write-Side Memory Update Variants (Exploratory)**: The paper also explored more aggressive write-side updates, where matched slots are refreshed based on novelty, age, and repetition, and unmatched observations append new slots. These variants are termed "update-only" (write-side update alone) and "full-mode" (write-side update combined with read-side decay), serving as boundary comparisons.

#### 3.3. C. Anti-Loop Regularization for Navigation Decision-Making

1. **Motivation**: Immediate backtracking and short loops are common local failures that degrade trajectory efficiency. This module aims to mitigate these through lightweight inference-time control.
2. **Next-Hop Based Penalty**: For a candidate target viewpoint $v$, the agent examines its graph next hop $h(v)$ (the first local transition). A penalty $p_t(v)$ is calculated:
 $p_t(v) = \beta_{\text{back}} \cdot I[h(v) = v_{t-1}] + \beta_{\text{rev}} \cdot I[\text{visit}(h(v)) \geq k]$.
 - The first term applies a penalty $\beta_{\text{back}}$ if the next hop $h(v)$ would return to the previously visited viewpoint $v_{t-1}$ (immediate backtracking).
 - The second term applies a penalty $\beta_{\text{rev}}$ if the next-hop viewpoint $h(v)$ has been visited $\geq k$ times (repeated revisits). In the mainline setting, this revisit term is weak and activated with $k=2$, making immediate backtrack suppression the dominant effect.
 This penalty is then subtracted from the original action score $s_t(v)$ to yield an adjusted score $s'_t(v) = s_t(v) - p_t(v)$.
3. **Inference Scope**: The anti-loop module is applied only at test time for deterministic, argmax-based inference, specifically to candidate action scores before action selection. It does not modify the stop head, introduce a separate planning component, or alter the learned backbone.

#### 3.4. D. Design Principle: Conservative Test-Time Control

DART-VLN emphasizes conservative test-time interventions. The practical mainline combines read-side memory decay with local anti-loop regularization. Read-side reweighting avoids perturbing stored representations, while the action-side regularizer is kept narrow to discourage wasteful loops without over-constraining exploration. Aggressive variants (like write-side updates) are treated as boundary comparisons. #### 3.5. E. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmarks**: Room-to-Room (R2R) and REVERIE.
- **Metrics**: Trajectory Length (TL), Navigation Error (NE), Success Rate (SR), Oracle Success Rate (OSR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), Remote Grounding Success (RGS), and Remote Grounding Success weighted by Path Length (RGSPL). Runtime is also reported for comparative efficiency analysis.
- **Baseline**: GridMM.
- **Variants**: `update-only`, `decay-only`, `full-mode` (exploratory write-side variants), and `decay+anti-loop` (the proposed mainline DART-VLN).
- **Implementation**: All runtime measurements were obtained on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti GPU using the same implementation and hardware settings for comparability among variants.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DART-VLN on the R2R and REVERIE benchmarks revealed consistent patterns regarding the efficacy of its components.

#### 4.1. A. Overall Trend

Across both benchmarks, the `decay-only` variant consistently provided stable gains through read-side memory control. The full `decay+anti-loop` configuration generally achieved the most favorable balance between navigation quality and execution efficiency, characterized by shorter trajectories, lower runtime, and improved navigation performance in key metrics.

#### 4.2. B. Results on R2R

Table I presents the results on R2R, covering both `val unseen` and `test unseen` splits.
- **`decay-only` Performance:** This variant demonstrated the value of conservative read-side control. Compared to the GridMM baseline, it improved Navigation Error (NE) from 2.83 to 2.59 on `val unseen` and from 3.35 to 3.19 on `test unseen`. Concurrently, runtime was reduced from 937.99 seconds to 742.61 seconds on `val unseen` and from 2312.52 seconds to 1621.34 seconds on `test unseen`. Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) were maintained or slightly improved, suggesting that suppressing stale evidence at memory readout contributed to a cleaner and more efficient inference process.
- **`decay+anti-loop` Performance:** This combined configuration yielded the strongest overall results on R2R. It achieved the shortest trajectory length (TL) and the lowest runtime across both splits, while also improving SR and SPL over the baseline. For example, on `val unseen`, TL decreased from 13.27 to 12.41, and SPL increased from 64 to 66. On `test unseen`, TL was reduced from 14.43 to 13.80, and SR/SPL improved from 73/62 to 74/63. While NE on `test unseen` did not show a monotonic improvement (3.38 vs. 3.35 for baseline), the overall performance indicated a favorable balance between navigation quality and efficiency.

#### 4.3. C. Results on REVERIE

Table II summarizes the results on the REVERIE `val unseen` dataset. Similar qualitative trends were observed.
- **`decay-only` Performance:** This variant provided a clean, conservative gain. Relative to GridMM, it marginally improved Oracle Success Rate (OSR), SR, SPL, Remote Grounding Success (RGS), and Remote Grounding Success weighted by Path Length (RGSPL). Critically, runtime decreased from 4329.67 seconds to 2998.49 seconds. This indicated that read-side memory control benefits not only standard navigation success but also the more complex task involving remote object grounding.
- **`decay+anti-loop` Performance:** This configuration delivered the strongest overall results on REVERIE. It shortened TL from 23.20 to 21.57, improved SR from 51.37 to 52.34, raised SPL from 36.47 to 37.53, and increased RGSPL from 24.56 to 25.44. The runtime reduction was substantial, dropping from 4329.67 seconds to 1497.98 seconds. This pattern suggested that anti-loop regularization was most effective when combined with the stable read-side decay.

#### 4.4. D. Ablation and Boundary Variants

The comparison among `update-only`, `decay-only`, `full-mode`, and `decay+anti-loop` clarified the contributions of each component:
- **Stability of `decay-only`**: The `decay-only` variant consistently provided the most stable single-module gain, improving or preserving main task metrics while reducing runtime across both benchmarks. This supports the hypothesis that lightweight read-side memory control can enhance frozen-backbone inference without altering stored memory representations.
- **Limited Benefit of Aggressive Intervention**: The `update-only` and `full-mode` variants, which involved write-side memory rewriting, showed less consistent gains and sometimes underperformed `decay-only` or `decay+anti-loop`. This suggested that directly rewriting stored memory under frozen backbones could potentially disrupt representations the baseline policy uses effectively.
- **Synergy of `decay+anti-loop`**: The robust performance of `decay+anti-loop` was attributed to building upon the stable gains of `decay-only`. Memory decay provided a dependable base improvement, with anti-loop regularization acting as a focused action-side controller that further refined the quality-efficiency trade-off.

#### 4.5. E. Navigation Behavior and Efficiency Analysis

Table III provides a behavioral diagnosis, quantifying immediate backtrack rate, average action steps, and average trajectory length.
- **Impact of Anti-Loop Regularization**: On R2R, `decay-only` showed a marginal change in average action count and a slightly higher immediate backtrack rate compared to the baseline, consistent with its role as a read-side denoiser. In contrast, `decay+anti-loop` lowered the immediate backtrack rate from 3.51% (for `decay-only`) to 2.01% while reducing average action steps and trajectory length. Similarly, on REVERIE, `decay+anti-loop` reduced the immediate backtrack rate from 8.45% (for `decay-only`) to 5.99% and shortened the executed trajectory.
- **Efficiency Improvement**: This analysis indicated that anti-loop regularization directly modified local trajectory execution, specifically by suppressing wasteful reversals. Such reductions contribute to improved efficiency, lower runtime, and potentially enhanced interpretability of the executed path, even if not every endpoint metric shows a monotonic gain.

In summary, the results demonstrate that DART-VLN, particularly in its combined `decay+anti-loop` configuration, offers a viable and efficient method for improving discrete VLN performance by addressing specific inference-time challenges through training-free control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DART-VLN framework contributes to the field of Vision-Language Navigation by demonstrating that lightweight, training-free test-time control can meaningfully improve the performance and efficiency of memory-based discrete VLN agents, even when utilizing strong, pre-trained, and frozen backbones. This work challenges the assumption that substantial architectural redesign or retraining is always necessary to enhance the capabilities of established navigation models.

The significance and potential impact of this research can be considered from several perspectives:

1. **Practical Enhancements for Deployed Agents:** DART-VLN offers a low-intrusion method for improving existing competitive navigators. By operating purely at inference time and introducing no new learnable parameters, it provides a means to refine the behavior of already deployed or robustly trained models without the computational cost and complexity associated with retraining. This could translate to more reliable and efficient autonomous agents in real-world applications where re-training is impractical or undesirable.

2. **Improved Resource Efficiency:** The framework demonstrably reduces trajectory length and runtime across multiple benchmarks. Shorter trajectories imply fewer actions taken, which can lead to energy savings in embodied agents and reduced computational load during inference. The significant reduction in runtime observed with DART-VLN indicates a potential for faster decision-making and overall operational efficiency.

3. **Enhanced Reliability and Interpretability:** By suppressing stale and redundant memory evidence, DART-VLN aims to make the agent's decision-making process cleaner and more focused on relevant information. The anti-loop regularization directly addresses inefficient local backtracking, leading to smoother and more direct path execution. This not only improves efficiency but can also enhance the interpretability of the agent's behavior, as its movements become more deliberate and less prone to oscillatory patterns.

4. **Insights into Model Limitations and Control:** This research highlights that even powerful, well-trained models can exhibit specific failure modes at inference time that are amenable to targeted, conservative control. It suggests that a part of navigation intelligence might reside not just in the learned representations, but also in how those representations are utilized and regulated during the decision-making process. This perspective can inform future research into the robustness and adaptability of embodied AI systems.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The study opens avenues for exploring similar training-free or test-time intervention strategies across a broader range of embodied navigation settings and with different backbone architectures. While the current work focuses on discrete VLN with a GridMM-style backbone, the principles of managing memory decay and local movement inefficiencies could be adapted to continuous navigation environments or other sequential decision-making tasks where partial observability and efficiency are critical.

In conclusion, DART-VLN provides a valuable demonstration of how focused, lightweight interventions can yield practical and measurable improvements in VLN, offering an alternative pathway for enhancing agent performance beyond traditional model training paradigms.
