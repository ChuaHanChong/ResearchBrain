# Wave-Based Bilateral Teleoperation between Nonlinear Manipulators with Direct Contact Force Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> Extends stabilizing USP wave-transformation communication laws to nonlinear multi-DOF Euler-Lagrange master-slave teleoperation with constant delay, enabling direct (not coordinating) environmental force feedback. An LMI-based passivity-shortage characterization of the remote subsystem sizes the excess passivity the channel must inject; simulations on nonlinear 2-DOF manipulators show stable position and force transmission across free-motion and hard-contact regimes.

## Problem

- Classical wave-transformation architectures transmit only a coordinating force rather than true contact force, degrading transparency and telepresence quality under delay.
- Prior stabilizing communication laws with direct force feedback (USP channel, force-receptive scattering) are limited to linear, single-DOF robot models, not realistic multi-DOF manipulators.
- Existing passivity-based delay-compensation schemes lack a systematic way to quantify how non-passive a nonlinear multi-DOF remote subsystem becomes when raw contact force is reflected.

## Method

- ==LMI-based passivity-shortage characterization==: a ==quadratic storage function== and ==linear matrix inequality== over bounded position/velocity sets quantify the remote Euler-Lagrange subsystem's non-passivity level alpha under direct force feedback.
- ==Vector-valued upper strictly passive (USP) communication law==: multi-DOF extension of scalar wave-transformation scattering weights that injects excess passivity to compensate the computed shortage under constant delay.
- ==Optimization-based tuning procedure==: offline ==patternsearch optimization== of channel parameters (impedance b, excess-passivity gain gamma_l) over a weighted position/force tracking-error cost to improve transparency while preserving stability constraints.

## Results

- LMI solved via grid-based approach over [-pi,pi]^2 x [-1,1]^2 with step **0.1** yields passivity-shortage level alpha = **5.7709** with lambda fixed at 0.001.
- Optimized channel parameters found as b = **0.06**, gamma_l = **-20**, giving gamma_r = **-69.4444**, from a search over b in [0.03, 0.09] and gamma_l in [-25, -15].
- Simulated 2-DOF teleoperation with **0.2 s** delay achieves stable position tracking and force transmission with force-tracking RMS error of **1.2993 N** across alternating free-motion and hard-contact phases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Passivity shortage from reflecting raw contact force can be explicitly quantified via LMI rather than assumed, turning an ad hoc stability risk into a computable design parameter.
> - Delay-independent stability and transparency guarantees generalize from linear single-DOF to nonlinear multi-DOF robots by keeping the same USP scattering structure with vector-valued ports.
> - Because the transparency cost is evaluated via nonlinear time-domain simulation, it is non-convex and non-smooth, so derivative-free search outperforms gradient-based tuning for channel parameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bilateral teleoperation systems enable human operators to interact with remote environments via master-slave robotic architectures. A fundamental challenge in these systems is maintaining stability and transparency in the presence of communication delays, particularly when force feedback is employed. Passivity-based approaches, originating from the scattering transformation framework, have become a cornerstone for addressing delay-induced instability. The wave-transformation (WT) interpretation of this framework has been further refined to manage various issues such as time-varying delays, wave reflections, and performance limitations.

A recognized trade-off exists between stability and transparency in delayed teleoperation. Classical WT-based architectures often transmit a "coordinating force" rather than the "true environmental contact force" to avoid rendering the remote robot non-passive, which could destabilize the system. While this approach preserves stability, it compromises transparency and the quality of telepresence.

Recent advancements have explored methods for stable environmental contact force feedback. One such approach, proposed in, introduced a stabilizing communication law based on an Upper Strictly Passive (USP) communication channel. This method deliberately injects an excess level of passivity into the communication channel to compensate for any passivity shortage in the remote subsystem, thereby ensuring delay-independent stability and improved transparency. Another work, proposed a force-receptive scattering transformation using additional communication ports and redundant scattering weights, which could be optimized to enhance transparency while maintaining passivity.

A significant limitation of these recent developments ( and) is their reliance on linear, time-invariant robot models with a single degree-of-freedom (DOF). In many practical teleoperation applications, robotic systems are characterized by inherently nonlinear dynamics and multiple degrees of freedom. This disparity between theoretical models and practical systems represents a gap in the existing research landscape.

The current paper addresses this gap by extending the stabilizing communication law framework to encompass bilateral teleoperation systems involving nonlinear, multi-DOF Euler-Lagrange robots. This work aims to bridge the gap between theoretical frameworks that ensure stable direct force feedback and the complex dynamics of real-world robotic manipulators, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and transparent teleoperation systems for practical applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges associated with providing direct environmental contact force feedback in bilateral teleoperation systems. While direct force feedback is crucial for enhancing teleoperation transparency and providing the human operator with high-fidelity haptic realism, its implementation can destabilize the closed-loop system, especially when communication delays are present. Previous research has primarily addressed this problem for simplified linear, single-DOF robot models, leaving a need for solutions applicable to the more complex and common nonlinear, multi-DOF robotic manipulators.

This paper is therefore motivated by the objective of developing a robust control and communication framework that can handle the inherent nonlinear dynamics and multiple degrees of freedom of modern robotic systems, while simultaneously preserving stability under constant communication delays and ensuring high transparency through direct contact force reflection.

Specifically, the paper pursues two main objectives:

1. **Extension of Stabilizing Communication Law:** To extend the framework of the stabilizing communication law, previously introduced in for linear, single-DOF systems, to bilateral teleoperation systems involving nonlinear, multi-DOF Euler-Lagrange robots. This involves adapting the passivity analysis and communication channel design to accommodate the complexities of such robotic dynamics.
2. **Optimization-Based Tuning for Transparency:** To investigate and develop an optimization-based procedure for tuning the parameters of the communication channel. Inspired by, this objective aims to systematically improve teleoperation transparency and overall performance while rigorously ensuring that delay-robust stability is maintained. This approach allows for a more principled selection of communication parameters beyond heuristic methods.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to enable the deployment of teleoperation systems that offer both robust stability in challenging communication environments and a high degree of transparency, thereby improving the operator's sense of presence and control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper involves a multi-faceted approach combining system modeling, passivity analysis using Linear Matrix Inequal (LMIs), the design of an Upper Strictly Passive (USP) communication law, and an optimization-based tuning procedure.

**System Modeling:**
The teleoperation architecture considers a local master robot interacting with a human operator and a remote slave robot interacting with an environment, all connected by a communication channel with constant delay *T*. Both master and slave robots are modeled as *n*-DOF nonlinear Euler-Lagrange systems, characterized by their inertia matrices *M*(q) and Coriolis/centrifugal terms *C*(q, ˙q). Gravity compensation and damping injection are incorporated into the control inputs for both robots. The human force is modeled with a spring coefficient and an exogenous muscle force, while the environment force is represented by a state-dependent stiffness matrix. A key aspect of the system design is the direct transmission of the environmental contact force *F_e* to the master side, rather than a coordinating force.

**Passivity-Shortage Analysis of the Nonlinear Remote Subsystem (Section III):**
The core challenge of direct contact force feedback is that it can render the remote subsystem non-passive, potentially leading to instability. To quantify this, the authors develop a passivity-shortage characterization for the remote subsystem.
1. **Augmented States and Storage Function:** Augmented state vectors *x* and *z* are defined, encompassing positions, velocities, and desired velocities of the slave robot. A quadratic storage function *V_s(x) = x^T P x* is chosen, where *P* is a symmetric positive definite matrix.
2. **LMI Condition:** By differentiating *V_s(x)* along the trajectories of the remote subsystem dynamics, a dissipation inequality (Equation 12) is derived. This inequality establishes that the rate of change of the storage function is bounded by the power input from the communication channel, augmented by a passivity shortage term *α˙q_sd^T˙q_sd* and an excessive dissipation term *−λ˙q_s^T˙q_s*. The existence of *P*, *α*, and *λ* is guaranteed if a specific LMI condition (Equation 13a) is satisfied.
3. **Assumptions:** This analysis relies on the boundedness of slave motion (Assumption 1) and standard properties of robotic systems, such as uniformly invertible inertia matrices and C1-smoothness of system matrices (Assumption 2). The LMI is solved over compact sets for position and velocity using a grid-based approach.

**Wave-Based Stabilizing Communication Law (Section IV-A):**
To compensate for the identified passivity shortage, a vector-valued USP communication channel is designed.
1. **Wave Variables:** The concept of scalar wave variables from classic wave transformation is extended to vector-valued variables *u_m, v_m, u_s, v_s* for multi-DOF systems. These are defined based on the physical variables (reflected force *F_md*, master velocity *˙q_m*, environmental force *F_e*, and desired slave velocity *˙q_sd*) and a characteristic impedance *b*, along with passivity excess levels *γ_l* and *γ_r*.
2. **Communication Channel Dynamics:** By combining the definitions of wave variables with standard delay relations between the master and slave communication ports, the communication law is expressed directly in terms of physical variables. This includes deriving delayed recursive equations that govern the desired slave velocity *˙q_sd* and the reflected force *F_md*.

**Stability and Transparency Analysis (Section IV-B):**
1. **Closed-Loop Stability:** A Lyapunov-Krasovskii functional is constructed, incorporating the master robot's energy, the slave subsystem's storage function *V_s(x)*, and the communication channel's stored energy *V_c*. By analyzing the derivative of this functional, the authors derive conditions on *γ_l* and *γ_r* (specifically, *γ_l ≤ 0* and *γ_r < -α*) that guarantee the closed-loop system's stability. Under these conditions, all system signals are proven to remain bounded, and through Barbalat-type arguments, the velocities of both robots (*˙q_m* and *˙q_s*) are shown to converge asymptotically to zero.
2. **Teleoperation Transparency:** The paper defines position and force tracking errors. It is shown that:
 - **Free Motion:** If the environment force is zero (*F_e ≡ 0*) and *γ_r* is specifically tuned, asymptotic position synchronization between the slave and the delayed master position is achieved.
 - **Hard Contact:** If the master acceleration converges to zero, asymptotic force synchronization is achieved, meaning the human operator accurately perceives the remote environmental force.

**Optimization-Based Tuning of Communication Law (Section IV-C):**
To improve transparency, the communication parameters *b* and *γ_l* are treated as tunable parameters.
1. **Objective Function:** An objective function *J(θ)* is defined as a weighted integral of the squared position and force tracking errors over a finite time window.
2. **Optimization Problem:** The parameters *b* and *γ_l* are optimized offline to minimize *J(θ)*, subject to constraints derived from the stability conditions (e.g., *0 < b < 1/(2√α)* and *γ_l ≤ 0*). The parameter *γ_r* is then set to *−1/(4b^2)* to achieve impedance matching for transparency during free motion. This optimization is performed using a derivative-free solver, such as MATLAB’s `patternsearch`, due to the non-convex and non-smooth nature of the objective function.

**Numerical Illustration (Section V):**
The proposed framework is validated through numerical simulations using a nonlinear 2-DOF planar manipulator for both master and slave robots. The environment includes a wall with a unilateral contact model. A periodic step function for the desired master position *q_md* is used to simulate transitions between free-motion and hard-contact scenarios. The LMI is solved using a grid-based approach to find *α*. The optimization routine is then applied to find suitable communication parameters, prioritizing force transmission (by setting *w_q = 0, w_f = 1* in the objective function).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several findings regarding wave-based bilateral teleoperation between nonlinear manipulators with direct contact force feedback:

- **Passivity Shortage Characterization for Nonlinear Systems:** The paper successfully derives a passivity-shortage characterization for the nonlinear, multi-DOF remote (slave) subsystem. This characterization is quantified by a parameter *α > 0* and an excessive velocity dissipation *λ > 0*, and its existence is formally established via a Linear Matrix Inequality (LMI) condition (Theorem 1). This provides a systematic method to analyze the passive properties of complex nonlinear robot dynamics in the context of teleoperation.
- **Multi-DOF Upper Strictly Passive (USP) Communication Law:** A vector-valued, multi-DOF extension of the USP communication law is developed. This law is specifically designed to compensate for the computed passivity shortage of the remote subsystem, thereby enabling stable direct environmental contact force feedback, which is crucial for enhancing transparency.
- **Delay-Robust Stability of the Closed-Loop System:** Theorem 2 establishes that the proposed framework, combining the nonlinear robot dynamics with the USP communication law, guarantees delay-robust stability. Under specific conditions on the communication parameters (*γ_l ≤ 0* and *γ_r < -α*), all signals within the teleoperation system are proven to remain bounded. Furthermore, the velocities of both the master and slave robots (*˙q_m(t)* and *˙q_s(t)*) are shown to asymptotically converge to zero as time approaches infinity, using a Lyapunov-Krasovskii functional approach and Barbalat's lemma. This stability is maintained irrespective of the magnitude of constant communication delays.
- **Enhanced Teleoperation Transparency:**
 - **Asymptotic Position Synchronization in Free Motion:** Theorem 3 demonstrates that in free-motion scenarios (where the environment exerts no force, *F_e ≡ 0*), and with a specific tuning of the communication parameter *γ_r = -1/(4b^2)*, asymptotic position synchronization is achieved between the slave robot and the delayed master robot position.
 - **Asymptotic Force Synchronization in Hard Contact:** During periods of hard contact with the environment, asymptotic force synchronization is achieved, provided the master robot's acceleration tends to zero. This ensures that the human operator accurately perceives the interaction forces from the remote environment.
- **Optimization-Based Tuning for Performance:** An optimization procedure is developed to tune the communication channel parameters (*b* and *γ_l*). This optimization minimizes a weighted objective function that combines position and force tracking errors, while adhering to constraints derived from the stability conditions. This systematic tuning allows for improving teleoperation transparency and overall performance.
- **Numerical Validation:** Numerical simulations involving a nonlinear 2-DOF planar manipulator demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the proposed method. The simulations confirm stable position trajectories, verify position synchronization in free-motion phases, and illustrate effective force transmission during hard-contact scenarios. The LMI-based analysis yielded a passivity shortage value of *α = 5.7709*. An optimization process, prioritizing force transparency, resulted in optimized communication parameters *b = 0.06*, *γ_l = -20*, and *γ_r = -69.4444*, which satisfy the established stability conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic teleoperation by addressing fundamental challenges associated with stability and transparency in the presence of communication delays, particularly for systems with complex dynamics.

One significant aspect is the **extension of stabilizing communication frameworks to nonlinear, multi-DOF robotic manipulators**. Previous advancements in enabling stable direct force feedback were often limited to simplified linear, single-DOF models. This paper expands the theoretical foundation to more accurately represent real-world robotic systems, which are typically nonlinear and possess multiple degrees of freedom. This broadens the applicability of robust teleoperation strategies.

The development of a **passivity-shortage characterization via an LMI condition** for nonlinear remote subsystems provides a systematic and quantifiable method for analyzing system stability. This mathematical rigor allows for the principled design of compensation mechanisms within the communication channel, moving beyond heuristic approaches.

The proposed **multi-DOF USP communication law** ensures **delay-robust stability** of the closed-loop teleoperation system. The finding that stability is maintained largely independently of the magnitude of constant communication delays is crucial for practical applications where delays can be significant and variable. This resilience allows for a focus on optimizing performance and transparency, knowing that fundamental stability is guaranteed.

Furthermore, the research directly enhances **teleoperation transparency and haptic realism**. By enabling stable direct reflection of environmental contact forces, human operators can experience a more accurate and intuitive sense of interaction with remote environments. This improvement in telepresence is critical for tasks requiring fine motor control, precise force application, or a detailed understanding of remote physical conditions.

The introduction of an **optimization-based tuning procedure** for communication channel parameters allows for a systematic approach to balance stability and transparency. This method moves beyond manual tuning by leveraging simulation-based evaluation to identify optimal parameters that improve position and force tracking performance while adhering to stability constraints.

The findings of this paper have potential implications for a range of **high-stakes applications**. These include telesurgery, where precise force feedback and stable operation are paramount; exploration of hazardous or inaccessible environments (e.g., nuclear facilities, space, deep-sea exploration), where robust remote control is essential; and advanced manufacturing or assembly tasks requiring dexterous manipulation with haptic feedback. By offering a more robust and transparent teleoperation framework for complex robotic systems, this research contributes to safer, more efficient, and more intuitive remote human-robot interaction across various domains. Future work, including addressing dimension mismatch and experimental validation, could further broaden the practical utility of these contributions.
