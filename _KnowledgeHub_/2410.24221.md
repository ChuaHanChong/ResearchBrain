# EgoMimic: Scaling Imitation Learning via Egocentric Video

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoMimic develops a framework that allows robots to learn manipulation skills by co-training on both human egocentric video and robot demonstration data. This approach enables a unified policy to generalize across diverse tasks, with human data proving more efficient for performance gains than additional robot-only data.

## Problem

- Robot learning, particularly imitation learning, is limited by the scarcity of diverse, high-quality demonstration data.
- Collecting large-scale robot datasets is expensive and time-consuming, hindering the generalization capabilities of robotic manipulation systems.
- Traditional approaches often treat human and robot demonstrations as distinct data types, preventing unified policy training.

## Method

- EgoMimic proposes a comprehensive framework that unifies human egocentric video and robot data to train a single policy for robot manipulation.
- It uses Project Aria glasses for natural human data capture and a custom bimanual robot designed to minimize the kinematic and visual gap with human embodiments.
- The framework employs cross-domain data alignment techniques, including action distribution normalization and visual masking of manipulators, to enable joint learning across different embodiments.

## Results

- EgoMimic significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baseline methods on three challenging real-world bimanual manipulation tasks: continuous object-in-bowl, laundry folding, and grocery packing.
- EgoMimic trained on 2 hours of robot data plus 1 hour of human data outperforms a baseline (ACT) trained on 3 full hours of robot-only data, demonstrating the efficiency of human data.
- The framework exhibits robust generalization to novel objects and unseen scenes, attributed to the diversity of human demonstration data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating human egocentric video as a first-class data source allows for scalable, passive data collection, addressing a key bottleneck in robot learning.
> - Careful cross-domain data alignment, including action distribution normalization and visual appearance masking, is crucial for effectively co-training a unified policy from human and robot data.
> - Human demonstration data provides more value for performance gains and generalization than an equivalent amount of additional robot-only data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Robot learning has been significantly limited by the scarcity of diverse, high-quality demonstration data. While imitation learning offers a promising path for robots to acquire complex skills, training robust policies requires large-scale datasets that are expensive and time-consuming to collect. This data bottleneck has prevented robotic manipulation systems from achieving the generalization capabilities seen in other domains like computer vision and natural language processing, where access to vast datasets has been transformative.

EgoMimic addresses this fundamental challenge by treating human egocentric video as a scalable source of embodied demonstration data. Instead of viewing human demonstrations and robot demonstrations as fundamentally different data types requiring separate handling, the framework treats them as equivalent sources of sensorimotor behavior that can jointly train a unified policy. This novel approach offers a pathway to dramatically scale up the quantity and diversity of training data available for robot learning.

### 2. The EgoMimic Framework

EgoMimic is a comprehensive framework designed to leverage human embodiment data for robot learning. It consists of four key components:

1. **Data Collection System**: Uses Project Aria glasses to capture egocentric human manipulation data, including first-person video, 3D hand tracking, and SLAM data.

2. **Low-Cost Bimanual Manipulator**: A custom robot designed to minimize the kinematic and visual gap between human and robot data.

3. **Cross-Domain Data Alignment**: Techniques to normalize action distributions and address visual appearance differences between human and robot embodiments.

4. **Unified Imitation Learning Architecture**: A model that co-trains on both human and robot data to learn a shared representation despite distinct action spaces.

The core innovation of EgoMimic is treating human egocentric video as a first-class data source rather than auxiliary information. This approach enables passive collection of large amounts of demonstration data without requiring explicit teleoperation or active human involvement in the robot training process.

### 3. Data Collection and Hardware Design

#### 3.1. Human Data Collection

EgoMimic employs Project Aria glasses to capture human manipulation data. These wearable devices provide:

- Egocentric video from front-facing cameras
- 3D hand tracking data from the device's tracking system
- Device SLAM for spatial awareness

The glasses enable natural, unintrusive data collection during regular human activities, creating a pathway for passive acquisition of large-scale datasets.

#### 3.2. Robot Design

To minimize the gap between human and robot embodiments, the researchers developed a custom bimanual robot with the following characteristics:

- Two ViperX 300 S arms for dexterous manipulation
- Intel Realsense D405 wrist cameras for detailed close-up views
- Project Aria glasses mounted on the robot to match the human visual perspective
- An inverted arm configuration similar to human arm kinematics

This design reduces the kinematic and visual discrepancies between human and robot data, making it easier to learn from both sources simultaneously.

### 4. Cross-Domain Data Alignment

A critical challenge in EgoMimic is addressing the differences between human and robot data distributions. The framework employs two key alignment techniques:

#### 4.1. Action Distribution Normalization

Human hand movements and robot end-effector trajectories have different kinematic properties. EgoMimic addresses this by:

1. Transforming both human hand and robot end-effector trajectories into camera-centered reference frames
2. Applying Gaussian normalization to standardize the action distributions

As shown in Figure 3(a), normalization significantly increases the overlap between human hand and robot action distributions, enabling more effective co-training.

#### 4.2. Visual Appearance Alignment

The visual differences between human hands and robot manipulators could mislead the vision-based policy. EgoMimic addresses this by:

1. Using the Segment Anything Model (SAM) to mask out both human hands and robot arms
2. Overlaying a consistent red line indicator to show the direction of the end-effector

This masking technique, shown in Figure 3(b), ensures the model focuses on the manipulation task rather than the specific appearance of the manipulator.

### 5. Unified Imitation Learning Architecture

EgoMimic employs a unified architecture that learns a joint representation from both human and robot data. The model builds upon the Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) architecture with modifications to handle the multi-embodiment setting.

The detailed architecture includes:

The key components include:

1. **Vision Encoder**: A ResNet-18 backbone processes the visual input from both human and robot data.

2. **Transformer Encoder-Decoder**: Processes visual features and proprioceptive information to predict future actions.

3. **Dual Output Heads**:
 - Pose Prediction Head: Predicts future hand/end-effector poses for both human and robot data
 - Joint Action Head: Predicts robot-specific joint actions (only trained on robot data)

The model is trained with a combination of pose loss (applied to both human and robot data) and joint action loss (applied only to robot data). This approach allows the model to learn a shared representation while accommodating the different action spaces.

### 6. Experimental Tasks

To evaluate EgoMimic, the researchers designed three challenging real-world manipulation tasks:

1. **Continuous Object-in-Bowl**: The robot must pick up toy animals and place them into a bowl, with both the bowl and objects varying in position.

2. **Laundry Folding**: The robot needs to fold a piece of clothing, requiring precise bimanual coordination.

3. **Grocery Packing**: The robot must pick up grocery items and place them into a bag, handling various item types and configurations.

These tasks were selected because they involve complex manipulation skills, require long-horizon planning, and benefit from bimanual coordination.

### 8. Broader Implications

EgoMimic addresses several fundamental challenges in robot learning:

#### 8.1. Scalable Data Collection

By leveraging human egocentric video, EgoMimic provides a pathway to scale up robot learning data similar to how internet data transformed computer vision and NLP. The passive nature of data collection through wearable devices means demonstration data can be gathered during everyday human activities without specialized recording setups.

#### 8.2. Cross-Embodiment Learning

The framework demonstrates that robots can learn effectively from demonstrations performed by different embodiments (humans), establishing a foundation for more general cross-embodiment learning approaches. This could enable knowledge transfer across different robot platforms and from humans to various robot designs.

#### 8.3. Cost-Effective Robot Learning

The combination of low-cost hardware (ViperX arms, Realsense cameras) with the ability to leverage human demonstration data makes the approach accessible and practical. This addresses the resource constraints that have traditionally limited robot learning research.

### 9. Conclusion

EgoMimic represents a significant advance in imitation learning by treating human egocentric video as a first-class data source for robot training. The framework's ability to learn a unified policy from both human and robot data addresses the critical data bottleneck in robot learning, enabling dramatic improvements in manipulation performance and generalization.

Key innovations include the full-stack approach that spans hardware design, data alignment techniques, and a unified learning architecture. Together, these components create a pathway for robots to benefit from the vast amounts of human demonstration data potentially available through wearable devices.

The performance improvements demonstrated across multiple manipulation tasks—with human data proving more valuable than additional robot data—suggest that EgoMimic's approach could fundamentally change how robot learning systems are developed. By enabling robots to learn from human demonstrations at scale, the work brings us closer to robots that can perform diverse manipulation tasks in unstructured real-world environments.
