# 3DWay: Generalizing Robot Manipulation via 3D Consistent Waypoints

> [!summary] Summary
> 3DWay is an intermediate representation for generalizable robot manipulation: a fine-tuned vision-language model predicts multi-view consistent 2D waypoints from RGB images and a language instruction, then geometric triangulation lifts them into 3D end-effector waypoints. These waypoints are directly executable or fed into a foundation VLA, lifting RLBench unseen success from 18.8% to 64.0% and real-world basic-task success from 21.7% to 65.8%.

## Problem

- Vision-language-action models forget vision-language priors during fine-tuning because token-based 2D perception and continuous 3D action modalities remain fundamentally mismatched.
- Trajectory representations predicted in 2D image space carry intrinsic depth ambiguity, so they give no explicit guidance about motion in physical 3D space.
- Lifting 2D trajectories with depth maps yields non-unique free-space points and stays sensitive to depth noise, limiting reliable 3D reasoning and scalability.

## Method

- **3DWay** reformulates 3D waypoint prediction as generating ==multi-view consistent 2D waypoints (2D-MCW)== from NVILA backbones, then recovering 3D points by ==geometric triangulation== without depth sensors.
- Training data pipeline computes raw tool-center-point trajectories, simplifies them with the ==Ramer-Douglas-Peucker algorithm==, projects to per-view 2D labels, and filters via ==open-vocabulary detector scoring==.
- ==Adaptive waypoint-guided integration== selects a local temporal window of waypoints by end-effector distance and concatenates it with robot proprioceptive state inside a pi-zero VLA.

## Results

- On RLBench unseen tasks, direct top-down execution reaches **64.0%** success versus **18.8%** for the strongest foundation-VLA baseline; VLABench average generalization improves by **6.4** points.
- With only **10** demonstrations per task, waypoint-augmented pi-zero fine-tuning scores **46.1%** versus **17.6%** standard, matching the **50-demo** baseline using **20%** of data.
- Ablations show removing multi-view consistency collapses success from **64.0%** to **22.0%**, while shrinking the backbone from **15B** to **2B** costs only about **14** points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating a 3D prediction task as multi-view 2D prediction plus geometry keeps outputs inside the pretrained model's native modality, preserving language priors.
> - Consistency across views during training, not merely triangulation at inference, supplies the signal; independent per-view supervision loses most of the benefit.
> - Compact intermediate representations transfer across embodiments and camera placements, making them a cheap generalization lever when in-domain demonstration data is scarce.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning faces the challenge of achieving generalizable performance across diverse application domains. Traditional methods like imitation learning and reinforcement learning often struggle to transfer effectively beyond their training distributions due to reliance on task-specific supervision and limited data coverage.

The emergence of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) has motivated the development of large vision-language-action (VLA) models for generalized robot manipulation. However, a significant obstacle in this paradigm is the "modality gap" between the discrete, token-based, 2D perceptual vision-language modality and the continuous, high-dimensional, 3D spatial robot action modality. This discrepancy can lead to forgetting of vision-language priors during VLM fine-tuning, which limits generalization and knowledge transfer.

To bridge this gap, researchers have explored intermediate representations that align with VLM data modalities. Two primary types are affordance-based and trajectory-based representations. Affordance-based methods excel at identifying *where* to act (e.g., a specific object part) but are less effective at defining continuous motion. Trajectory-based representations, conversely, capture global execution paths and motion patterns.

Prior trajectory-based approaches often predict trajectories in 2D image space, typically by drawing them on raw input images. While offering motion-intent guidance, these 2D trajectories inherently suffer from depth ambiguity, limiting their guidance in the 3D physical space. Attempts to lift 2D trajectories into 3D using depth information (referred to as 2.5D waypoints in this paper) introduce their own challenges, including sensitivity to depth noise and ambiguity in free-space 3D points, which restricts their reliability and scalability.

This work, "3DWay," aims to advance the field by proposing a more direct, robust, and extensible intermediate representation: 3D Consistent Waypoints. It positions itself as an improvement over 2D and 2.5D trajectory representations by explicitly addressing 3D ambiguity and providing clear 3D motion intent. It leverages multi-view RGB images to achieve 3D consistency, aligning with the growing trend of incorporating 3D awareness in robot learning through methods like point clouds, egocentric 3D coordinate systems, or implicit 3D awareness from multi-view inputs in foundation VLA models. By reformulating 3D waypoint prediction as generating multi-view consistent 2D waypoints followed by geometric triangulation, 3DWay seeks to exploit the strong priors of pretrained VLMs while enabling explicit 3D motion specification.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalizability of robot manipulation policies by developing a robust and spatially explicit intermediate representation. The authors identify a core problem in current robot learning paradigms: the difficulty of effectively leveraging powerful vision-language models (VLMs) for 3D robot control due to an intrinsic modality gap.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Address the limitations of existing intermediate representations:** Current trajectory-based intermediate representations, such as 2D waypoints, suffer from depth ambiguity, providing only a partial characterization of robot actions in the projected image domain. While 2.5D waypoints (2D trajectories lifted with depth) attempt to provide 3D cues, they are sensitive to depth noise and face ill-defined ambiguity for free-space points, severely limiting their reliability and scalability for consistent 3D reasoning. The authors are motivated to overcome these drawbacks.

2. **Develop a 3D consistent intermediate representation:** The central objective is to propose 3D Consistent Waypoints (3DWay), a simplified sequence of 3D points along the task execution trajectory. This representation is intended to capture explicit 3D motion information and provide clear spatial relations, which are crucial for robot manipulation tasks that operate in the physical 3D world.

3. **Bridge the VLM-Action modality gap effectively:** The research aims to align the intermediate representation with the data modalities and output structures of large pre-trained VLMs. By reformulating 3D waypoint prediction as generating multi-view consistent 2D waypoints, which are subsequently reconstructed into 3D using geometric constraints, the method seeks to leverage the strong vision-language understanding and world knowledge of VLMs while enabling explicit 3D motion specification. This approach is designed to preserve VLM priors, which are often compromised during direct fine-tuning of VLAs.

4. **Enable generalizable robot manipulation:** The ultimate goal is to improve the generalizability of robot manipulation policies. This is pursued in two ways:
 - **Direct executability:** The 3DWay representation should be directly executable on unseen tasks and new robot embodiments without requiring extensive additional policy training, demonstrating its inherent actionability.
 - **Integration with foundation VLAs:** The 3DWay should serve as a flexible intermediate representation that can be integrated with existing foundation VLA models (e.g., π0) to enhance their spatial reasoning and generalization capabilities, particularly in data-scarce or novel scenarios.

The key contributions listed by the authors encapsulate these objectives:
- Proposing a novel method utilizing multi-view spatial consistency for 3DWay generation.
- Developing a tailored VLM fine-tuning approach for consistent multi-view supervision that preserves VLM priors.
- Introducing an adaptive waypoint-guided integration strategy for foundation VLAs.
- Validating the executability, generalization potential, and flexibility of 3DWay through extensive experiments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in "3DWay" focuses on generating 3D consistent waypoints by leveraging multi-view visual information and pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs). The approach consists of a problem reformulation, a data processing pipeline, VLM fine-tuning, and strategies for policy execution.

#### 3.1. 3D Waypoint Definition and Generation Formulation

The 3D waypoints are defined as a simplified sequence of 3D points representing the Tool Center Point (TCP) trajectory and end-effector states in world coordinates during task execution.

The core idea is to avoid direct 3D point prediction, which is challenging due to varying camera poses and intrinsics. Instead, the task of generating 3D waypoints (W_3D) from multi-view RGB images (I), camera intrinsics (K), extrinsics (T), and a language instruction (ℓ) is reformulated. The model first predicts multi-view consistent 2D projections of waypoints (W_2D) in image space. These 2D waypoints are then reconstructed into 3D waypoints (W_3D) through geometric triangulation.
- During training, ground-truth 3D waypoints are projected to 2D-MCW (Multi-view Consistent 2D Waypoints) using standard perspective projection functions and camera parameters.
- A VLM is fine-tuned to predict these 2D-MCW from the multi-view images and language instruction.
- During inference, the predicted 2D-MCW from the VLM are used along with camera parameters for multi-view triangulation to reconstruct the 3D waypoints.

#### 3.2. VLM Fine-tuning for Multi-view Consistent 2D Waypoints

**Base VLM Selection:** The NVILA family of models (2B, 8B, and 15B parameters) is chosen as the base VLM, known for its performance in image and video understanding.

**Training Data Processing:** A two-stage data processing pipeline is implemented:
1. **Initial Fine-tuning Data:** The base VLM is initially fine-tuned on the RoboPoint dataset to improve its pixel-level point prediction accuracy.
2. **2D-MCW Data Generation:** For 2D-MCW prediction, data from RLBench (simulation) and DROID, RH20T (real-world) datasets are used. This data includes task initialization frames, task descriptions, and corresponding robot execution trajectories.
 - **Trajectory Simplification:** Raw TCP trajectories are first computed and then simplified using the Ramer-Douglas-Peucker (RDP) algorithm.
 - **2D Projection:** The simplified 3D points are then projected into 2D-MCW using the provided camera intrinsics and extrinsics.
 - **Data Filtering:** An automatic scoring mechanism, utilizing task-specific prompts and an open-vocabulary detector, is employed to filter out low-quality data.

**Training Strategy:**
- Pixel coordinate predictions are directly supervised within the VLM's output.
- 2D-MCW are represented in a text format, aligning with the language modeling objective of VLMs. This strategy is chosen to avoid complex loss or expert head designs and to better preserve the VLM’s vision-language reasoning capabilities.
- Training is conducted in two stages: one epoch on RoboPoint, followed by ten epochs on the processed 2D-MCW dataset, using cross-entropy loss and AdamW optimization.

#### 3.3. Policy Execution with 3D Waypoints

The generated 3DWay can be utilized in two primary ways for robot manipulation:

1. **Direct Execution with Fixed Strategy (3DWay-TD):**
 - For simple tasks, 3DWay can be directly translated into low-level control commands.
 - A top-down grasping strategy and waypoint-based motion planning are employed.
 - This approach aims for direct executability and generalizability on unseen tasks and new embodiments without additional policy training.

2. **Adaptive Waypoint-guided Integration for Enhanced Generalization:**
 - To enable dexterous manipulation and further unlock 3DWay's potential, an adaptive integration strategy is proposed for foundation VLAs.
 - The strategy uses a VLA backbone (specifically π0) that typically takes visual observation, robot proprioceptive state, and language instruction as input.
 - A local temporal window of waypoints (W_3D_t) is adaptively selected based on the robot's real-time state and its distance to the 3DWay.
 - This window of waypoints is concatenated with the robot state and integrated as an additional input to the VLA.
 - This adaptive integration allows VLAs to dynamically leverage nearby waypoints, aiming to improve spatial grounding and robust generalization in various manipulation tasks, particularly during in-distribution fine-tuning under data-scarce conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments presented in the paper evaluate 3DWay's effectiveness in generating 3D trajectories, its ability to inherit VLM world knowledge, and its capacity to improve the generalization of foundation VLAs.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of 3DWay Generation and World Knowledge Inheritance

- **RLBench Evaluation:**
 - On a subset of 10 rotation-invariant tasks, 3DWay-TD (direct execution with Top-Down strategy) achieved an 84.8% average success rate under seen settings and 64.0% under unseen settings.
 - In comparison, foundation VLAs like π0 showed significantly lower performance (39.6% seen, 12.0% unseen) and a noticeable drop in unseen scenarios.
 - These results suggest that 3DWay provides actionable and generalizable 3D trajectories.

- **VLABench Evaluation:**
 - Evaluations on VLABench across five dimensions (in-distribution, cross-category, common-sense, semantic instruction, unseen texture) demonstrated 3DWay-TD's strong generalization.
 - 3DWay-TD outperformed π0.5 by 6.4% in average success rate, indicating its ability to inherit strong vision-language reasoning capabilities from the base VLMs.

- **Qualitative Results:**
 - Visualizations of 2D-MCW predictions confirmed consistency across multiple views and generalization to novel scenes and abstract language instructions.

#### 4.2. Improving Foundation VLA Generalization via 3DWay

- **Simulation Experiments (RLBench Few-Shot Fine-tuning):**
 - On five representative tasks with limited training data (10 demonstrations/task), a standard fine-tuned π0 achieved a 17.6% average success rate.
 - Augmenting π0 with 3DWay substantially improved performance: 38.1% with zero-shot generated 3DWay and 46.1% with few-shot generated 3DWay.
 - This indicates that 3DWay enables more effective utilization of limited supervision, achieving comparable success rates to baselines with significantly less data.

- **Real-world Experiments (AgileX PIPER Robot Arm):**
 - Experiments on five basic tasks and extended tasks for visual generalization and language reasoning were conducted.
 - 3DWay-TD exhibited competitive zero-shot transferability across various tasks.
 - The 3DWay-augmented π0 significantly improved the average success rate on basic tasks from 21.7% (standard π0) to 65.8%.
 - For challenging extended tasks (novel objects, abstract instructions), standard π0 achieved very low success rates (6.7% for visual generalization, 2.5% for semantic description), whereas the 3DWay-augmented variant consistently achieved success rates exceeding 50%.
 - These results highlight 3DWay's potential to alleviate generalization limitations of foundation VLAs under data-scarce deployment on novel embodiments and tasks.

#### 4.3. Factors Influencing 3DWay Performance (Ablation Studies)

- **Backbone Size:** Using smaller NVILA backbones (8B or 2B) resulted in only moderate performance degradation compared to the 15B model, suggesting the approach is effective with more compact VLMs.
- **Camera Pose Variation:** The method demonstrated robustness to both small and large camera extrinsic shifts during testing, maintaining consistently high success rates, which is crucial for practical deployment.
- **Multi-view Consistency:** An alternative training strategy using independent-view supervision (without enforcing multi-view consistency during training) led to a significant performance drop (22.0% success rate compared to 64.0% with consistency). This emphasizes the importance of the multi-view-consistent dataset construction and training paradigm.
- **Robot Embodiments and Camera Configurations (Real-world):** 3DWay-TD maintained stable performance across different robot arms (AgileX PIPER, KUKA iiwa14) and camera setups (different external camera positions, or a wrist camera combined with an external camera). This suggests the framework's scalability to diverse hardware and observation configurations.

In summary, the main findings indicate that 3DWay effectively generates explicit 3D trajectories, leverages VLM priors for strong generalization, and significantly improves the performance of foundation VLAs in data-limited, novel, and cross-embodiment scenarios. The ablation studies further validate the robustness and critical role of multi-view consistency in the proposed framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "3DWay" research introduces a significant advancement in robot manipulation by proposing 3D Consistent Waypoints as an intermediate representation. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Effective Bridge for the Modality Gap:** The core contribution of 3DWay is its ability to bridge the intrinsic modality gap between the 2D perception of vision-language models (VLMs) and the continuous 3D actions required for robot manipulation. By reformulating 3D waypoint prediction through multi-view consistent 2D waypoints and geometric triangulation, the method provides a spatially coherent and actionable representation. This approach allows for the explicit specification of 3D motion intent while preserving the strong vision-language priors of large VLMs, which often suffer from catastrophic forgetting during direct VLA fine-tuning.

2. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The work demonstrates a substantial improvement in the generalization of robot manipulation policies.
 - **Zero-shot Transfer:** 3DWay-TD (direct execution) exhibits strong zero-shot transfer capabilities to unseen tasks and novel objects, significantly outperforming traditional foundation VLAs in such scenarios. This suggests a reduced need for extensive task-specific data collection and policy training.
 - **Augmenting Foundation VLAs:** The adaptive waypoint-guided integration strategy for foundation VLAs (e.g., π0) dramatically boosts their performance in data-scarce, few-shot, cross-embodiment, and visually/semantically generalized settings. This empowers existing powerful VLA models to adapt more robustly and efficiently to new environments and tasks.

3. **Robustness to Environmental and Hardware Variations:** The ablation studies highlight 3DWay's robustness to variations in camera poses (extrinsic shifts) and its consistent performance across different robot embodiments and camera configurations (e.g., different robot arms, external vs. wrist cameras). This adaptability is crucial for real-world deployment, where hardware setups and observation conditions can vary.

4. **Flexibility and Versatility:** 3DWay offers a versatile framework. It can function as a standalone, directly executable representation for simpler tasks or seamlessly integrate with and augment more complex foundation VLA models for dexterous manipulation. This flexibility broadens its applicability across a spectrum of robotic challenges.

5. **Foundation for Future Dexterous Manipulation:** The identified limitations and future work directions point towards extending 3DWay to include explicit SE(3) pose modeling, hierarchical sub-task reasoning, and self-calibrating multi-view triangulation. These advancements could pave the way for more dexterous, precise, and robust manipulation capabilities in cluttered and dynamically changing open-world robotic scenarios.

In summary, "3DWay" represents a step towards truly generalizable robot manipulation. By providing a reliable, spatially grounded, and VLM-compatible intermediate representation, it has the potential to accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robots capable of performing diverse tasks in unstructured real-world environments with less human intervention and data collection.
