# Beyond Short-Horizon: VQ-Memory for Robust Long-Horizon Manipulation in Non-Markovian Simulation Benchmarks

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces RuleSafe, a novel benchmark to evaluate robot performance on complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks in non-Markovian simulated environments. It also proposes VQ-Memory, an efficient, model-agnostic temporal memory module that encodes past proprioceptive states into discrete latent tokens, leading to a 31.3% increase in average success rate across all tasks and up to 40% improvement on specific long-horizon tasks for various policy architectures.

## Problem

- Existing robotic simulation benchmarks primarily focus on short-horizon, simple tasks, failing to adequately capture the complexity of articulated object interactions and non-Markovian characteristics prevalent in real-world scenarios.
- Policies for long-horizon, multi-stage manipulation tasks struggle with temporal reasoning; incorporating raw visual history leads to substantial computational overhead, while raw proprioceptive states are susceptible to low-level noise and overfitting to specific training trajectories.
- Current articulated manipulation benchmarks often rely on manually scripted rules for task logic and trajectory generation, which severely limits their scalability, extensibility, and the generation of diverse, long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- The authors introduce RuleSafe, an LLM-aided simulation framework in the SAPIEN engine, designed for scalable generation of diverse, non-Markovian, long-horizon articulated manipulation tasks featuring complex unlocking mechanisms for safes.
- They propose "**VQ-Memory**," a compact and structured temporal memory module that leverages a Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-==VAE==) to learn discrete representations of continuous past joint states, followed by K-means clustering to merge similar codebook entries into a robust, high-level task-phase context.

## Results

- VQ-Memory consistently and substantially improved performance across diverse base models on challenging long-horizon tasks, with policies like π0 achieving an SR of **45.0%** on 'rule 020' (from **0.0%** baseline) and DP3 improving from **5.0%** to **45.0%** SR.
- In multi-task learning settings, VQ-Memory significantly boosted overall performance, increasing the average success rate across all 20 RuleSafe tasks from **25.0%** to **56.3%** (+**31.3%**) and the average process score from **48.8%** to **76.5%** (+**27.7%**).
- Ablation studies demonstrated that clustering the VQ-VAE codebook was crucial for performance, reaching an optimal **45.0%** success rate with 4 clusters on 'rule 020' compared to **20.0%** without clustering, and that a memory token length of 40 provided superior results.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks in non-Markovian environments require robust temporal reasoning, as policies relying solely on current observations or noisy raw state histories exhibit significant performance limitations.
> - Encoding continuous proprioceptive states into compact, discrete, and clustered tokens (VQ-Memory) effectively filters low-level noise while preserving high-level task phase context, which is crucial for improving long-horizon planning and generalization across diverse tasks.
> - Effective memory representation requires careful design choices regarding vocabulary size and memory length; an optimal number of clusters for discrete tokens and sufficient look-back window are essential to capture distinct task stages without redundancy or loss of critical temporal information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic simulation has emerged as a scalable platform for evaluating and generating data for robotic tasks, driven by advancements in areas such as large language models (LLMs) for data generation, data augmentation for sim-to-real transfer, and the inclusion of complex embodiments like dual-arm and humanoid robots. However, current simulation benchmarks primarily focus on short-horizon tasks and simple manipulations, such as pick-and-place. They often do not adequately capture the complexity of articulated object interactions or the non-Markovian characteristics prevalent in real-world scenarios.

Previous articulated manipulation benchmarks have largely concentrated on assessing a model's ability to infer affordances and joint constraints of novel objects from visual observations. These benchmarks typically limit interactions to single-joint motions and rarely account for inter-joint dependencies or the interplay among multiple kinematic components of the same object. While some efforts have incorporated multi-joint dependencies (e.g., door locks), these methods often rely on manually scripted rules for trajectory collection, which restricts their scalability, extensibility, and the generation of diverse, long-horizon tasks.

Addressing these long-horizon dependencies necessitates temporal reasoning and memory-augmented policies, as a single current observation may be insufficient to gauge task progress or the current state of a multi-stage process. Existing approaches to temporal modeling in robotic policies include incorporating historical visual observations or raw robot proprioceptive states. However, using raw visual history leads to substantial computational overhead and scalability issues due to the large number of tokens involved. Conversely, while raw joint-state sequences offer a lightweight form of temporal context, they are susceptible to low-level noise and can lead to overfitting to specific training trajectories.

This work contributes to the landscape by introducing RuleSafe, a new benchmark designed to evaluate long-horizon, non-Markovian articulated manipulation tasks, addressing the limitations of simpler, manually scripted benchmarks. Furthermore, it proposes VQ-Memory, a novel and efficient temporal memory representation that aims to overcome the drawbacks of existing memory approaches by providing a compact, structured, and robust representation of past proprioceptive states, thereby enhancing the performance of robotic policies on these complex tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research is motivated by two principal limitations observed in the current state of robotic simulation and policy learning for manipulation tasks:

**Motivation 1: Limitations of Existing Robotic Simulation Benchmarks**
The high cost and practical challenges associated with collecting real-robot data have positioned robotic simulation as a crucial platform for both evaluating robotic systems and generating large datasets. Despite significant progress in simulation technologies, most existing benchmarks exhibit several limitations:
- **Focus on Short-Horizon and Simple Tasks:** The majority of current benchmarks concentrate on straightforward tasks like pick-and-place, which do not fully represent the complexity and sequential dependencies found in real-world manipulation.
- **Lack of Articulated Object Interaction Complexity:** Articulated objects (e.g., doors, drawers, safes) are common in human environments and introduce substantial manipulation challenges, yet their intricate interactions remain largely underexplored. Previous articulated manipulation benchmarks primarily focus on inferring affordances and joint constraints from visual observations, often limiting interactions to single-joint motions. This constraint prevents the evaluation of policies that require understanding inter-joint dependencies or multi-component interplay, such as unlocking mechanisms that precede opening.
- **Reliance on Manual Rule Scripting:** Benchmarks that incorporate more complex mechanisms, like locks, typically depend on manually scripted rules for task logic and trajectory generation. This manual process severely limits the scalability and extensibility of these benchmarks, hindering the creation of diverse and long-horizon tasks.

These limitations collectively mean that existing benchmarks often fail to capture the non-Markovian characteristics of realistic tasks, where the current observation alone is insufficient to determine the optimal action or task progress, thus not adequately challenging policies in temporal modeling and memory-based reasoning.

**Motivation 2: Challenges in Temporal Modeling for Long-Horizon, Non-Markovian Tasks**
Successful execution of multi-stage, articulated object manipulation tasks often requires policies to maintain an awareness of past states and task progression, as the current visual input might be ambiguous regarding the task phase or component states. This demand for temporal reasoning presents significant challenges for current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:
- **Difficulty in Differentiating Stages:** Policies relying solely on the current visual frame struggle to distinguish between visually similar but semantically distinct manipulation stages (e.g., whether to pull or rotate a handle based on prior actions).
- **Computational Overhead of Visual History:** Incorporating a history of raw visual observations as memory can mitigate this issue, but it introduces substantial computational overhead and scalability concerns due to the high dimensionality of visual data.
- **Noise and Overfitting with Raw Proprioceptive History:** Using a history of raw robot joint states (proprioceptive states) offers a lightweight alternative for temporal cues. However, raw joint states are continuous signals susceptible to low-level noise and can lead to overfitting to specific training trajectories, diminishing generalization capabilities.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

To address these motivations, the authors established the following key objectives:
1. **Introduce a Novel Articulated Manipulation Benchmark (RuleSafe):** To provide a scalable and systematically structured environment for evaluating policies on non-Markovian, long-horizon tasks that demand multi-stage reasoning and temporal modeling. This benchmark aims to feature diverse, complex articulated object interactions with unlocking mechanisms.
2. **Leverage LLM-Aided Generation for Scalability:** To minimize manual effort and ensure diversity, the benchmark should utilize LLMs for generating task rules and logic, enabling the creation of numerous complex scenarios.
3. **Propose a Compact and Robust Temporal Memory (VQ-Memory):** To develop an efficient and generalizable memory representation that can encode high-level task-phase context from noisy joint-state histories. This representation should effectively filter low-level variations while remaining lightweight and compatible with existing VLA models, thereby improving long-horizon planning and generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology comprises two main components: the development of the RuleSafe benchmark and the introduction of VQ-Memory for temporal modeling.

**RuleSafe Benchmark Construction**
RuleSafe is designed as a scalable, LLM-aided simulation framework focusing on long-horizon articulated manipulation tasks.

- **Locking Mechanism Generation:** The core of RuleSafe involves diverse unlocking mechanisms for safes, including key, password, and logic locks. These mechanisms are structured around two latent variables:
 - **Part-Phase:** This variable describes the discrete configuration states of articulated components (e.g., "open" or "closed" for a knob or handle), derived from their kinematic states (e.g., rotation angles). This allows for defining sequential dependencies between actions, such as a "knob open" condition before a "handle open" action.
 - **Task-Phase:** This provides a higher-level abstraction, tracking the overall progress of multi-step tasks. It does not directly map to individual joint configurations but encodes whether specific stages of a task have been completed. This enables complex locks like password or logic locks, which depend on combinations of actions or input sequences.
 - **LLM-Aided Lock Design:** To generate a wide variety of locking mechanisms efficiently, LLMs are utilized. The process involves providing LLMs with a few manually designed reference examples. The LLMs then generate two outputs for each lock: a natural language description of the rules (used as prompts for manipulation policies) and an executable Python program that verifies if the unlocking conditions are met. A template for the unlocking judgment program guides the LLM in structuring the logic based on part-phase updates influencing task-phase progression.

- **Demonstration Generation:** Demonstrations for RuleSafe are generated by extending the HumanoidGen framework for articulated manipulation, involving task decomposition and spatial annotation.
 - **Task Decomposition:** An LLM-based planner decomposes each lock rule into a sequence of atomic operations. Each step is either a hand operation (e.g., pinch, grasp, rotate, selected from a library) or an arm movement. Arm movements are formulated as constrained optimization problems where LLMs generate constraints specifying the target end-effector pose. An OMPL-based motion planner then generates intermediate poses to reach the goal.
 - **Spatial Annotation:** To ensure physically plausible interactions, both the hand's atomic operations and the articulated assets are spatially annotated. Key points and axes are marked on fingers (for contact/motion directions) and on manipulable joints of assets (for operational interfaces). These annotations establish geometric constraints (point constraints for position, axis constraints for orientation) that align the hand's motion with the object's articulation during the optimization process.

- **Benchmark Details:** RuleSafe is implemented using the SAPIEN simulation engine. It comprises 20 distinct lock rules applied to 10 types of safes. The manipulation is performed by Inspire hands attached to the arms of a Unitree H1-2 humanoid robot, providing a 13-dimensional action space (7 DoFs per arm, 6 DoFs per hand). Data collection includes RGB images from first- and third-person views and point clouds at 10 Hz, while the simulation runs at 100 Hz. The mean success rate for generating demonstrations is 71.7%, with an average trajectory length of 638 frames.

**VQ-Memory for Temporal Modeling**
VQ-Memory is a compact and structured temporal representation designed to encode past proprioceptive states, filtering noise while preserving high-level task context.

- **Problem Formulation:** The work frames the problem as learning a policy $\pi(a_t|o_t, m_t)$, where $a_t$ is a chunk of future actions, $o_t$ is the current observation (RGB images, language instruction, proprioceptive state), and $m_t$ represents memory tokens from previous timesteps. This explicitly addresses the non-Markovian nature of the tasks.
- **Discrete Joint-State from VQ-VAE:** A Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) is employed to learn a discrete and structured representation of continuous joint-state sequences. A window of past joint states $Q_t = \{q_{t-W+1}..., q_t\}$ is encoded into a latent embedding $z_t$, which is then quantized to the nearest entry $e_k$ in a learned codebook. A decoder reconstructs the original joint states from this quantized embedding, minimizing reconstruction and commitment losses. Unlike prior VQ-VAE applications that prioritize reconstruction fidelity for action tokenization (using small window/stride), VQ-Memory uses these tokens as auxiliary inputs for temporal context. This allows for a larger window size (50) and stride (20), achieving a high compression ratio (approximately 20x) of continuous joint states into discrete tokens, enhancing computational efficiency.
- **Compact Memory via Clustering:** To address potential overfitting and redundancy in the VQ-VAE's learned codebook, a post-hoc K-means clustering step is applied. This process merges similar codebook entries into a smaller, more compact vocabulary of cluster centroids $\{c_j\}$, where $J < K$. Each original VQ-VAE code $e_k$ is then mapped to its nearest centroid, with the centroid index serving as the final discrete memory token. This clustering emphasizes shared semantic patterns across trajectories rather than fine-grained variations, contributing to a more robust and compact memory representation.
- **Integration with Policies:** VQ-Memory is designed to be model-agnostic. For diffusion policies (like DP3) that take point cloud features, a small convolutional network maps the discrete memory tokens into a latent embedding, which then serves as an input. For Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (like RDT, CogACT, $\pi_0$), the discrete memory tokens are treated as special language tokens. The VQ-Memory's dedicated vocabulary is mapped to a set of IDs at the end of the VLM's original vocabulary, and the resulting sequence of memory tokens is concatenated with the policy's existing language tokens before being processed by the Transformer encoder.
- **Experimental Setup:** Experiments are conducted in both single-task (100 demonstrations per task) and multi-task (50 demonstrations per task across all 20 rules, 1000 total trajectories) settings. Performance is measured using Success Rate (SR) and Process Score (PS). Four base models (DP3, RDT, CogACT, $\pi_0$) are evaluated. VQ-Memory is configured with a vocabulary size of 4 (clustered from an initial 256) and a memory token length of 40.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the effectiveness, generality, and robustness of VQ-Memory across various learning scenarios and model architectures.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness and Generality of VQ-Memory in Single-Task Setting

Initial evaluations demonstrated the challenges posed by RuleSafe's non-Markovian tasks for policies relying solely on current observations. For instance, the $\pi_0$ policy struggled to differentiate visually similar yet semantically distinct manipulation stages.
- **Improvement over Baseline:** When $\pi_0$ was used without memory, it achieved a 30.0% success rate and 56.7% process score on the short-horizon 'rule 001', and 0.0% success rate and 10.6% process score on the long-horizon 'rule 020'. This indicated a deficiency in temporal reasoning.
- **Comparison with Raw Joint-State Memory:** Incorporating raw historical joint states improved $\pi_0$'s performance on 'rule 001' (SR 55.0%, PS 70.1%) but showed instability on 'rule 020' (SR 0.0%, PS 16.3%). This instability was attributed to the continuous nature of raw joint states, making them susceptible to noise and overfitting to specific trajectories.
- **Superiority of VQ-Memory:** Replacing raw joint states with VQ-Memory tokens led to substantial improvements:
 - On 'rule 001', $\pi_0$ + VQ-Memory achieved SR 80.0% and PS 89.3%.
 - On 'rule 020', $\pi_0$ + VQ-Memory achieved SR 45.0% and PS 67.3%.
- **Generality Across Architectures:** VQ-Memory consistently enhanced performance across diverse base models on the challenging 'rule 020' task:
 - DP3: Baseline SR 5.0% -> + VQ-Memory SR 45.0%.
 - RDT: Baseline SR 0.0% -> + VQ-Memory SR 35.0%.
 - CogACT: Baseline SR 0.0% -> + VQ-Memory SR 20.0%.
 - $\pi_0$: Baseline SR 0.0% -> + VQ-Memory SR 45.0%.
These results suggest that VQ-Memory is an effective and architecture-agnostic mechanism for improving temporal understanding and mitigating overfitting in non-Markovian manipulation tasks.

#### 4.2. Multi-Task Learning with VQ-Memory

The $\pi_0$ policy was evaluated with and without VQ-Memory across all 20 RuleSafe tasks in a multi-task setting.
- **Overall Performance Improvement:** VQ-Memory significantly improved multi-task learning. The average success rate across all tasks increased from 25.0% to 56.3% (+31.3%), and the average process score increased from 48.8% to 76.5% (+27.7%).
- **Task-Specific Effects:** While $\pi_0$ performed adequately on simpler tasks with fewer steps or visually inferable joint states (e.g., 'rule 002', 'rule 003'), it exhibited severe struggles (often near-zero success rates) on longer-horizon tasks. The integration of VQ-Memory led to consistent and substantial improvements across nearly all tasks, indicating its capability to capture temporal dependencies and maintain robust performance across diverse non-Markovian scenarios.
- **Remaining Bottlenecks:** Despite the improvements, the overall success rate of 56.3% suggests that precise action execution remains a challenge. Observations indicated that policies often generate correct high-level plans but may fail in executing individual operations with sufficient precision (e.g., finger misalignment or incomplete rotations), potentially due to the limited number of demonstrations per safe instance in the dataset.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments focused on the impact of key VQ-Memory hyperparameters on 'rule 020' performance.
- **Number of Clusters:** Clustering the VQ-VAE codebook was found to be crucial. Without clustering (vocabulary size 256), the success rate was 20.0%, attributed to redundant and noisy memory representations. Performance improved with clustering, reaching an optimal 45.0% success rate with 4 clusters. Further reducing the clusters to 2 slightly degraded performance (SR 30.0%), suggesting that overly coarse clustering may merge distinct stage-level patterns.
- **Memory Length:** Varying the memory token length demonstrated its impact on capturing long-term dependencies. A shorter memory (20 tokens) resulted in a 25.0% success rate. Increasing the length to 40 tokens improved performance considerably to 45.0%. Further increasing it to 60 tokens yielded negligible additional benefits (SR 40.0%), indicating a saturation point for temporal coverage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents two complementary contributions that address significant limitations in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly concerning long-horizon tasks and temporal reasoning.

- **Advancement in Benchmarking for Complex Manipulation:** RuleSafe introduces a novel articulated manipulation benchmark that moves beyond the short-horizon, simple tasks prevalent in existing simulations. By featuring non-Markovian, multi-stage tasks with diverse, LLM-generated unlocking mechanisms, RuleSafe provides a scalable and systematic platform for evaluating temporal reasoning and long-horizon planning capabilities. This is a critical step towards developing more generalized robotic policies capable of interacting with complex, articulated objects common in real-world environments. Its LLM-aided generation mechanism enhances diversity and reduces manual effort, promoting broader adoption and further research.

- **Robust and Efficient Temporal Modeling:** VQ-Memory addresses the challenge of temporal context in non-Markovian tasks by offering a compact and structured memory module. By leveraging vector-quantized variational autoencoders to encode past proprioceptive states into discrete latent tokens, VQ-Memory effectively filters low-level noise and preserves high-level task-phase context. This approach overcomes the limitations of computationally expensive visual history and noise-prone raw joint-state histories, enabling more efficient and robust long-horizon control. Its model-agnostic nature, demonstrated across various VLA models and diffusion policies, indicates broad applicability and potential for widespread integration into existing robotic architectures.

- **Catalyst for Future Research and Development:** The findings indicate that while VQ-Memory significantly enhances temporal understanding and planning, precise low-level action execution remains a bottleneck, particularly with limited per-configuration data. This insight highlights a clear direction for future research, suggesting that efforts could focus on improving data generation strategies, enhancing control policies for fine-grained manipulation, or incorporating targeted fine-tuning. Furthermore, the success of VQ-Memory paves the way for expanding its capabilities to encode additional sensory inputs, such as adaptive features or latent action embeddings from video, and for scaling RuleSafe with an even greater variety of rules and objects.

In summary, this work provides both a challenging new benchmark and an innovative tool for temporal memory, collectively contributing to the development of more capable, intelligent, and generalizable robotic systems for complex, real-world manipulation tasks.
