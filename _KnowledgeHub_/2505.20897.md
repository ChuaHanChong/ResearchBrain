# Cross from Left to Right Brain: Adaptive Text Dreamer for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Adaptive Text Dreamer (ATD) framework enhances Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) by employing a dual-branch Vision-Language Model for logical state estimation and abstract language-based future scene imagination. The system demonstrated competitive performance on the R2R benchmark, achieving up to a 6% increase in Success Rate (SR) and 3% in Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) over similar LLM-leveraged methods with a smaller parameter count.

## Problem

- Agents in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) suffer from partial observability, making it difficult to align natural language instructions with visual cues and infer unseen semantic information.
- Existing imagination-based VLN strategies, relying on visual generation, often incur high computational costs and produce redundant details, hindering efficient long-horizon planning.
- The challenge of effectively identifying and anticipating task-relevant semantics in future environments without reconstructing entire scenes remains prominent.

## Method

- Introduces the Adaptive Text Dreamer (ATD), a dual-branch, self-guided imagination policy built upon a Large Language Model (LLM) for adaptive language-based imagination.
- Employs a 'human-like left-right brain' VLM architecture, where the 'left brain' (State Estimation LLM) logically assesses current navigation state and the 'right brain' (Imagination LLM) predicts future key environmental semantics using abstract language.
- Integrates imagination with a graph-based navigation policy via a State Grounded Cross-Attention (SGCA) mechanism, which regularizes imagined outputs based on the current state and injects them into the action planning module.

## Results

- ATD achieved state-of-the-art performance among LLM-leveraged methods on the R2R benchmark, outperforming NaviLLM by 6% SR and 3% SPL on the test split with a significantly smaller parameter count (1.5B vs 7B).
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization on unseen REVERIE and R4R datasets, showing improvements of up to 4% in OSR, 3% in SR, and 4% in SPL over prior methods like DUET.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of both the State Estimation LLM and Imagination LLM, with their combined interaction leading to an 8% increase in SR and a 5% increase in SPL on the val unseen dataset compared to a baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language-based imagination offers a more efficient and semantically rich representation of future environmental states compared to purely visual imagination methods for VLN tasks.
> - A dual-branch architecture, where current state estimation (left brain) dynamically grounds and refines future imagination (right brain) through cross-attention, enhances task-relevant semantic filtering and prevents fixation on completed instructions.
> - Efficient activation of domain-specific LLM knowledge for both reasoning and imagination is achievable by fine-tuning only the Q-former module while keeping the main LLM and vision backbone frozen.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a prominent task within embodied artificial intelligence, requiring an agent to interpret natural language instructions and navigate through previously unseen 3D environments using visual cues. A fundamental challenge in VLN is the inherent partial observability of the environment, where the agent has a limited field of view at any given moment. This restriction complicates the alignment between visual perception and linguistic instructions, necessitating that the agent reason beyond immediate observations to infer unobserved semantic information relevant to its goal.

Earlier approaches to address this uncertainty included memory-based mechanisms, which helped agents aggregate past observations or construct spatial representations of traversed regions. While beneficial, these methods were often insufficient for anticipating critical, yet-unseen information, especially for long-horizon planning or targets outside the current or historical view.

More recent research has explored imagination-based strategies to extend an agent's perceptual horizon by simulating observations of unvisited locations. These methods aim to reduce uncertainty and improve long-horizon planning by allowing agents to proactively infer future states. However, existing imagination-based techniques predominantly rely on visually grounded generation. Such methods, whether generating pixel-level images along candidate trajectories (e.g., DREAMWALKER) or feature-level syntheses of panoramic views (e.g., HNR), often incur high computational costs and may introduce redundant details or noise. The paper argues that effective navigation hinges on identifying task-relevant semantics rather than reconstructing entire scenes, suggesting a need for more abstract and selective imagination.

Concurrently, the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into VLN has gained traction. LLMs have been used for tasks such as landmark extraction from instructions, generating detailed plans, acting as core planners, or assisting in task decomposition. Some works fine-tune Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with in-domain VLN data to enhance performance. This research positions itself within this landscape by proposing a language-based imagination approach, leveraging the abstract and compositional nature of language to provide targeted semantic cues, thereby aiming for a more efficient and semantically rich representation of future environmental states compared to purely visual methods. It seeks to bridge the reasoning capabilities of LLMs with specialized navigation policies to address the limitations of prior imagination methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent difficulties in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), particularly the problem of partial observability. Agents in VLN environments only perceive a limited field of view, making it challenging to align natural language instructions with environmental cues and to infer unseen semantic information required for successful navigation. Existing imagination-based strategies, while attempting to mitigate this, often suffer from high computational costs and the generation of redundant visual details, as they primarily rely on vision-based synthesis. The authors contend that effective navigation does not require a complete visual reconstruction of future scenes but rather the identification of key, task-relevant semantics. Language, with its capacity for abstract and compositional representation, is posited as a suitable modality for this targeted imagination.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Develop Adaptive Language-Based Imagination:** The central objective is to propose a method that can adaptively imagine crucial environmental semantics using language, thereby offering a more reliable and efficient navigation strategy compared to visual imagination.
2. **Introduce the Adaptive Text Dreamer (ATD):** To achieve language-based imagination, the research aims to introduce a novel architecture called the Adaptive Text Dreamer (ATD). This model is designed as a dual-branch, self-guided imagination policy built upon a Large Language Model (LLM).
3. **Implement a Human-like Left-Right Brain Architecture:** A specific objective is to structure ATD with a dual-branch VLM architecture analogous to human cognitive processes. The "left brain" is intended to focus on logical integration and current state estimation, while the "right brain" is responsible for imaginative prediction of future scenes.
4. **Efficiently Activate Domain-Specific LLM Knowledge:** The researchers aim to enable efficient activation of domain-specific knowledge within the LLM by fine-tuning only the Q-former module within both the left and right brains, while keeping the main LLM and vision backbone frozen. This approach is intended to allow for dynamic updates of both logical reasoning and imagination throughout the navigation process.
5. **Enable Dynamic Reasoning and Imagination Updates:** A core objective is to ensure that the imagination of the text dreamer can be continuously updated and refined during navigation. This dynamic adaptation is crucial for preventing the model from fixating on already completed parts of the instructions, which could hinder effective imagination for future steps.
6. **Integrate Imagination with Navigation Policy:** The work aims to introduce a cross-interaction mechanism that regularizes the imagined outputs and injects them into a navigation expert module. This integration allows ATD to jointly leverage the abstract reasoning capabilities of the LLM with the specialized action planning expertise of a navigation model.
7. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance with Efficiency:** A practical objective is to demonstrate that the proposed ATD framework can achieve state-of-the-art performance on standard VLN benchmarks, specifically the R2R benchmark, while requiring fewer parameters and lower computational costs compared to existing methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Adaptive Text Dreamer (ATD) framework integrates a dual-branch "left-right brain" reasoning structure with a graph-based navigation expert. The core idea is to leverage the abstract reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) for both state estimation and future scene imagination, then integrate these language-based insights into a policy for action selection.

#### 3.1. State Estimation LLM and Imagination LLM

The ATD model employs two branches of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), each utilizing a frozen InstructBLIP model with a fine-tuned Q-former module. The Q-former's role is to extract image tokens and fuse them with textual input for the LLM. Training data for these LLMs is collected within a discrete VLN simulation environment, allowing for the pre-extraction of visual features.

- **State Estimation LLM (Left Brain):** This component is designed for logical integration, specifically to estimate the agent's current navigation state. It determines the agent's position within the environment relative to the instruction and identifies which parts of the task have been completed.
 - **Data Collection:** GPT-4V is used to generate state descriptions. Given an observation and navigation instruction, GPT-4V provides a reasoning output about the agent's current position and navigation state. This output serves as the ground truth for fine-tuning the State Estimation LLM.
 - **Training:** The Q-former of the left brain processes the instruction (W) and current observation (O_t) to generate visual-textual fusion output (Q'_lb). This is then fed into the frozen LLM with a `StateEstimationPrompt` to predict the current reasoning (R_t) and generate a state embedding (State E_t).
 - **Loss:** A cross-entropy loss (L_leftbrain) is applied between the predicted reasoning (R_t) and the GPT-4V-generated ground truth.

- **Imagination LLM (Right Brain):** This branch is responsible for imaginative prediction, generating abstract language descriptions of future key environmental semantics for candidate pathways.
 - **Data Collection:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct generates detailed captions for panoramic images of candidate nodes (one step ahead) along sampled trajectories. These captions serve as the ground truth for the Imagination LLM.
 - **Training:** Similar to the left brain, the Q-former of the right brain processes W and O_t to produce Q'_rb, which is then input to the frozen LLM with an `ImaginationPrompt` to generate imagination output (I_t) and imagination embedding (Imagine E_t).
 - **Loss:** A cross-entropy loss (L_rightbrain) is computed between the generated imagination (I_t) and the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-generated ground truth captions.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Text Dreamer (Integration with Navigation Policy)

After training the individual LLM branches, they are integrated into a graph-based navigation policy.

- **State Grounded Imagination (SGCA):** To constrain the potentially broad imagination of the right brain with the logical context from the left brain, a State Grounded Cross-Attention (SGCA) mechanism is introduced.
 - **Mechanism:** The state estimation embedding (State E_t) acts as queries, while the imagination embedding (Imagine E_t) serves as keys and values. Cosine similarity is used to compute an attention weight matrix (A), which represents how state information grounds the imagination. This adaptive refinement process allows the model to filter and prioritize task-relevant details from the imagination based on the current navigation state.
 - **Output:** The SGCA module outputs a grounded imagination feature, V_ATD_t, which is then fed into the navigation policy.

- **Graph-based Navigation Policy:** The navigation policy operates on a dynamic topological graph, G = {V, E}. This graph is updated in real-time to represent the agent's navigation history, explicitly encoding environmental topology and spatial relationships.
 - **Node Representation:** Nodes in the graph include visited locations, navigable neighbors, and the current position. Each node has a visual embedding (V_vis_t) derived from the average pooling of observed view representations around it.
 - **Latent Embedding Injection (MCA):** The V_ATD_t embedding from the SGCA module is injected into the graph node embeddings. Multi-head Cross-Attention (MCA) is used to fuse V_vis_t (as query) with V_ATD_t (as key and value), resulting in a fused embedding (V_fusion_t).
 - **Cross-modal Transformer and GASA:** V_fusion_t is passed through a multi-layer cross-modal transformer to capture relationships between instructions and nodes. Following this, a Graph-aware Self-Attention (GASA) layer is applied. GASA incorporates spatial distances (derived from graph edges E_t) and visual similarities between nodes to enhance contextual understanding.
 - **Action Prediction:** An MLP takes the output of the GASA layer to predict the next goal action.

- **Loss Function for Navigation Policy:** The navigation policy is trained using a combination of two losses:
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC) loss:** L_BC supervises the model with ground truth actions (a*_t).
 - **Pseudo Interactive Demonstrator (PID) loss:** L_PID uses pseudo labels (~a*_t), which are derived from the shortest path to the destination based on the agent's partial on-policy generated graph (~G_t). This encourages the agent to learn from optimal or near-optimal paths.
 - **Total Loss:** The overall loss function is L = λL_BC + L_PID, where λ is a trade-off factor.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the Adaptive Text Dreamer (ATD) framework primarily on the R2R benchmark, using standard navigation metrics: Trajectory Length (TL), Navigation Error (NE), Success Rate (SR), Oracle Success Rate (OSR), and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL).

#### 4.1. Quantitative Analysis

- **R2R Benchmark Performance:** ATD, particularly the FlanT5-XL-1.5B model (when integrated with PREVALENT data), demonstrates competitive performance against previous state-of-the-art methods.
 - **Comparison with LLM-leveraged Methods:** ATD achieves the best performance among LLM-leveraged methods with a significantly smaller parameter count (1.5B). For instance, it outperforms NaviLLM (a 7B LLM fully fine-tuned for action generation) by 6% in SR and 3% in SPL on the test split. Compared to NavGPT2 (1.5B model), ATD shows gains of 3% in both SR and SPL on the test split. This indicates that the dual-branch, human-like left-right brain architecture is effective.
 - **Comparison with VLN Specialists:** ATD achieves comparable performance to BEVBert on the validation unseen split and surpasses it on the test split. BEVBert uses additional depth information for bird-eye-view map construction, suggesting that ATD's language-based imagination enhances the generalization of its navigation policy.
 - **Overall Metrics (with PREVALENT):** On the R2R `val unseen` split, ATD achieves an SR of 75.61% and an SPL of 67.49%. On the `val unseen` split, it achieves an SR of 74.63% and an SPL of 63.08%. On the `test unseen` split, ATD obtains an SR of 74% and an SPL of 63%.

- **Zero-shot Cross-Environment Evaluation:** ATD was evaluated for its generalization capabilities on the REVERIE and R4R datasets, trained on R2R.
 - On REVERIE, ATD outperformed DUET by 4% in OSR, 3% in SR, and 4% in SPL.
 - On R4R, ATD showed improvements over DUET with gains of 3.37 in nDTW, 2.42 in sDTW, 1.67 in OSR, 2.22 in SR, and 2.55 in SPL. These results indicate improved generalization of ATD to unseen environments.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Attention Visualization (SGCA):** Visualizations of the attention matrices within the State Grounded Cross-Attention (SGCA) layers demonstrate its function. Over successive layers, the SGCA mechanism increasingly emphasizes task-critical information while concurrently suppressing information related to already completed navigation steps. This suggests that SGCA effectively filters and highlights relevant imaginative details based on the current state.
- **Convergence Speed:** The convergence speed comparison with NavGPT2 on the validation unseen split reveals that ATD reaches NavGPT2's maximum SPL and SR values significantly faster, approximately 100,000 and 90,000 iterations quicker, respectively. This highlights the training efficiency of the ATD framework.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of LLM Model:** Comparing FlanT5-XL (1.5B parameters) and FlanT5-XXL (5B parameters), ATD with FlanT5-XL achieved slightly higher SR on both validation sets, while FlanT5-XXL showed a notable advantage in SPL. This may suggest that the larger model, with its potentially higher confidence in reaching the destination, sometimes stops earlier, leading to better SPL but slightly lower SR if the early stop is incorrect.
- **Effectiveness of State Estimation LLM (SELLM) and Imagination LLM (IMLLM):** Ablation studies confirmed the individual and combined effectiveness of the SELLM and IMLLM modules. Both modules individually led to significant performance improvements over a baseline model (DUET without its local branch and without BERT pretraining). The best performance, however, was achieved when both modules interacted within the full ATD architecture, demonstrating an 8% increase in SR and a 5% increase in SPL on the val unseen dataset compared to the baseline.
- **Effect of SGCA Layer Numbers:** Experimenting with 1 to 4 SGCA layers, the model achieved optimal performance for both SR and SPL with 3 or 4 layers. Notably, increasing layers significantly improved performance on `val seen`. The model's relatively strong performance even with a single SGCA layer on `val unseen` suggests a robust lower bound for its generalization capabilities.
- **Ablation of Left-Right Brain Structure:** Comparing ATD's SGCA design (State Estimation grounding Imagination) with alternative structures, such as "Cross from Right to Left" (Imagination as query) or "Parallel Structure" (summed embeddings without SGCA), demonstrated that ATD's original SGCA design achieved the superior performance. This validates the effectiveness of the proposed SGCA layer and the directed interaction between the left and right brains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that advances Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) by introducing an adaptive, language-based imagination mechanism. The Adaptive Text Dreamer (ATD) addresses fundamental challenges in VLN, particularly the limitations imposed by partial observability and the computational burden of existing vision-based imagination methods.

The primary significance of this work lies in its novel dual-branch, "human-like left-right brain" architecture. By dedicating one branch (State Estimation LLM) to logical state assessment and another (Imagination LLM) to abstract, language-based future scene prediction, ATD leverages the respective strengths of LLMs in reasoning and generation. This structured approach to imagination, constrained and guided by the current navigation state through the State Grounded Cross-Attention (SGCA) mechanism, allows agents to anticipate critical semantic cues more efficiently and effectively than relying on comprehensive visual reconstruction. This shift towards targeted, language-driven imagination represents a notable methodological departure and offers a pathway for reducing computational overhead while enhancing semantic grounding.

The demonstrated improvements in performance on the R2R benchmark, specifically achieving state-of-the-art results with fewer parameters and faster convergence compared to existing LLM-leveraged and specialist VLN models, underscore the practical efficiency and efficacy of the ATD framework. Furthermore, the robust zero-shot generalization capabilities exhibited on unseen environments like REVERIE and R4R suggest that ATD learns more transferable and adaptable navigation policies. This enhanced generalization is crucial for real-world embodied AI applications where agents frequently encounter novel settings.

The potential impact of this research extends to various domains within embodied AI, including autonomous robotics, human-robot interaction, and assistive technologies. By enabling robots to better understand and anticipate their environments through a more efficient, language-based internal model, ATD could contribute to the development of more intelligent, robust, and deployable navigation agents. For instance, in complex indoor environments, robots could make more informed decisions by "textually dreaming" about relevant landmarks or obstacles, leading to safer and more efficient task completion.

However, the authors also acknowledge limitations and broader impacts. The current imagination mechanism is primarily short-horizon (one-step-ahead candidate nodes), and extending this to long-horizon imagination could further unlock the potential of LLMs in this context. Ethical and safety considerations are also highlighted, emphasizing the need for rigorous evaluation in controlled simulations before any real-world deployment, given the inherent risks of hallucination, bias, and transparency challenges associated with large vision-language models. Addressing these aspects will be crucial for the responsible translation of such advancements into practical applications.
