# Cross from Left to Right Brain: Adaptive Text Dreamer for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Adaptive Text Dreamer (ATD) framework enhances Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) by employing a dual-branch Vision-Language Model for logical state estimation and abstract language-based future scene imagination. The system demonstrated competitive performance on the R2R benchmark, achieving up to a 6% increase in Success Rate (SR) and 3% in Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) over similar LLM-leveraged methods with a smaller parameter count.

## Problem

- Agents in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) suffer from partial observability, making it difficult to align natural language instructions with visual cues and infer unseen semantic information.
- Existing imagination-based VLN strategies, relying on visual generation, often incur high computational costs and produce redundant details, hindering efficient long-horizon planning.
- The challenge of effectively identifying and anticipating task-relevant semantics in future environments without reconstructing entire scenes remains prominent.

## Method

- Introduces the Adaptive Text Dreamer (ATD), a dual-branch, self-guided imagination policy built upon a Large Language Model (LLM) for adaptive language-based imagination.
- Employs a 'human-like left-right brain' VLM architecture, where the 'left brain' (State Estimation LLM) logically assesses current navigation state and the 'right brain' (Imagination LLM) predicts future key environmental semantics using abstract language.
- Integrates imagination with a graph-based navigation policy via a State Grounded Cross-Attention (SGCA) mechanism, which regularizes imagined outputs based on the current state and injects them into the action planning module.

## Results

- ATD achieved state-of-the-art performance among LLM-leveraged methods on the R2R benchmark, outperforming NaviLLM by 6% SR and 3% SPL on the test split with a significantly smaller parameter count (1.5B vs 7B).
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization on unseen REVERIE and R4R datasets, showing improvements of up to 4% in OSR, 3% in SR, and 4% in SPL over prior methods like DUET.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of both the State Estimation LLM and Imagination LLM, with their combined interaction leading to an 8% increase in SR and a 5% increase in SPL on the val unseen dataset compared to a baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language-based imagination offers a more efficient and semantically rich representation of future environmental states compared to purely visual imagination methods for VLN tasks.
> - A dual-branch architecture, where current state estimation (left brain) dynamically grounds and refines future imagination (right brain) through cross-attention, enhances task-relevant semantic filtering and prevents fixation on completed instructions.
> - Efficient activation of domain-specific LLM knowledge for both reasoning and imagination is achievable by fine-tuning only the Q-former module while keeping the main LLM and vision backbone frozen.
