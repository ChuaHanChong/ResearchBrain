# CogniPlan: Uncertainty-Guided Path Planning with Conditional Generative Layout Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> CogniPlan presents an uncertainty-guided path planning framework that uses a conditional generative model to predict multiple plausible environmental layouts from partial observations. This approach reduces travel length by 7.0% for autonomous exploration and 12.5% for point-goal navigation compared to strong baselines, exhibiting robust performance in simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Traditional and reactive planning methods often lead to myopic decisions, inefficient backtracking, and suboptimal paths in unknown or complex environments.
- Existing learning-based planners, while powerful, frequently operate without explicit foresight into unknown regions or struggle to capture global context effectively.
- Map prediction techniques often rely on single, deterministic layout predictions and decouple prediction from planning, failing to effectively model or leverage inherent environmental uncertainty.

## Method

- **CogniPlan** uses a ==conditional generative inpainting network==, built on ==WGAN-GP==, to generate multiple plausible future environment layouts from the robot's partial occupancy map, along with probabilistic uncertainty for each pixel.
- An ==uncertainty-guided graph attention planner network== constructs a collision-free graph from these predicted layouts, using augmented node features that encode predicted free space probabilities and navigation utilities.
- The planner leverages a ==graph attention network== for policy learning and a ==privileged-learning critic==, trained with ==Soft Actor Critic (SAC)==, to make informed decisions by reasoning over diverse spatial hypotheses.

## Results

- Achieved an average travel length reduction of **7.0%** for exploration and **12.5%** for navigation compared to state-of-the-art DRL baselines in simulated environments.
- Demonstrated successful zero-shot generalization to realistic KTH floor plans and robust performance when deployed on a real mobile robot in a cluttered indoor laboratory.
- Consistently produced shorter paths, higher exploration efficiency, and lower variance in travel distance in high-fidelity Gazebo simulations, even in significantly out-of-distribution environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly generating and reasoning over multiple plausible future environment layouts, rather than a single deterministic one, significantly improves non-myopic planning efficiency and robustness.
> - Integrating the uncertainty from generative layout predictions directly into a graph attention-based deep reinforcement learning planner allows for more informed decision-making under partial observability.
> - Drawing inspiration from human cognitive maps, the framework emulates foresight in robots, enabling more coherent and long-horizon path planning in unknown and complex settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Path planning in unknown environments remains a cornerstone challenge in robotics, fundamentally encompassing both autonomous exploration (mapping an entire unknown space) and point-goal navigation (reaching a specific target). The broader research landscape for these tasks can be categorized into:

- **Traditional, Rule-Based Methods:** These approaches, often relying on frontier detection and greedy heuristics (e.g., nearest frontier, information gain), are simple and effective in straightforward scenarios. However, they suffer from myopic decision-making, leading to inefficient backtracking and suboptimal paths in complex environments. Examples include seminal frontier-based exploration and various sampling-based methods.
- **Learning-Based Methods (Deep Reinforcement Learning - DRL):** DRL has emerged as a powerful paradigm, implicitly learning complex planning policies by encoding spatial knowledge within neural networks [5–9]. While capable of handling uncertainty and generalizing across environments, many DRL approaches operate directly on occupancy maps or graph representations, which can struggle to capture global context or fine-grained spatial details, especially in large-scale environments. Recent advancements, including graph attention networks, have improved scalability and reasoning but still primarily rely on observed data.
- **Planning with Explicit Map Prediction:** A growing subset of research focuses on predicting unknown layouts from partial observations using deep learning, especially generative models [10–14, 39, 42, 43]. These methods aim to provide "foresight" to the planner. However, most existing map prediction techniques often rely on a *single, deterministic* layout prediction and frequently *decouple* prediction from planning. This means they generate a map and then apply a conventional or learning-based planner, without effectively modeling or leveraging the *uncertainty* inherent in these predictions, nor allowing the planner to explicitly reason over multiple plausible hypotheses. Methods like UPEN and MapEx started incorporating uncertainty but often for conventional planners or without ensuring full coverage.

CogniPlan critically bridges the gap between explicit layout prediction and sophisticated DRL-based planning. It addresses the limitation of myopic, reactive planning by introducing a *conditional generative inpainting model* that generates *multiple plausible layouts*, effectively mirroring how humans rely on "cognitive maps" during navigation. This framework integrates the *uncertainty* inherent in these predictions directly into a *graph attention-based planner*, allowing the learned policy to reason over diverse spatial hypotheses. By doing so, CogniPlan aims to combine the scalability and contextual reasoning of graph networks with the high-fidelity spatial awareness and foresight offered by generative models, pushing the boundaries of autonomous navigation in unknown and complex environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of CogniPlan are twofold:

1. **Develop a unified, learning-based path planning framework:** The goal is to create a robust system capable of efficiently performing both autonomous exploration and point-goal navigation in previously unknown environments.
2. **Enhance planning under uncertainty through explicit, generative layout prediction:** The core objective is to leverage a conditional generative model to predict *multiple plausible* environmental layouts from partial observations. These predictions, along with their inherent uncertainty, should then explicitly guide a learned planner, enabling non-myopic and spatially coherent decision-making.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several critical limitations in existing approaches:

- **Myopic Planning:** Traditional rule-based planners often make decisions based on immediate observations (e.g., nearest frontier), leading to inefficient, zig-zagging paths and suboptimal long-term outcomes.
- **Implicit vs. Explicit Spatial Reasoning:** While DRL methods can implicitly encode spatial knowledge, they often lack explicit foresight into unknown regions. Conversely, explicit map prediction methods often fail to integrate uncertainty or deeply couple their predictions with the planning process.
- **Scalability and Detail Trade-offs:** Graph-based planners offer scalability but can overlook fine-grained spatial details. Occupancy map-based planners capture detail but struggle with large-scale environments. A solution is needed that combines the best of both worlds.
- **Human Cognitive Inspiration:** The authors are inspired by human cognitive navigation, where individuals build internal "cognitive maps" to reason about potential future states and plan accordingly, even with incomplete information. CogniPlan seeks to emulate this capability in robots.

By addressing these motivations, CogniPlan aims to enable robots to make more informed, long-horizon decisions, ultimately leading to more efficient and robust navigation and exploration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CogniPlan employs a novel two-stage framework comprising a conditional generative inpainting network and an uncertainty-guided graph attention planner network.

#### 3.1. Conditional Generative Inpainting Network

This component is responsible for predicting multiple plausible layouts from a robot's partial occupancy map.

- **Model Architecture:** A Wasserstein Generative Adversarial Network with Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP) is used. This choice mitigates training instability issues common in GANs, such as mode collapse, ensuring diverse and high-quality predictions.
- **Conditional Generation:** The generator takes the robot's current occupancy map belief, a mask indicating unknown regions, and a `layout conditioning vector (z)` as input. During training, `z` is a one-hot vector representing the ground-truth layout type (room, tunnel, outdoor). During inference, a `set of diverse conditioning vectors (Z)` (including uniform, one-hot, and soft one-hot variants) is used to generate multiple distinct layout hypotheses, capturing environmental uncertainty.
- **Training Objective:** The generator is trained with a multi-component loss function (Eq. 1):
 - **Adversarial Loss:** From the discriminator, ensuring predictions are realistic.
 - **Reconstruction Losses:** An L1 loss over the entire prediction, a spatially discounted L1 loss (weighted higher near known areas, decaying exponentially with distance) to emphasize critical regions for traversability, and a Dice (F1-score) loss to promote accurate overlap and spatial connectivity.
- **Efficiency:** The inpainting model is designed to be lightweight (fewer than 0.35M parameters), enabling real-time multiple forward inferences for uncertainty estimation, even on CPU, without significant computational overhead.
- **Post-processing:** Generated layouts are post-processed into binary free/occupied maps, followed by morphological operations to refine predictions (filling small holes, removing noise).
- **Uncertainty Representation:** The average of multiple binary predictions for each pixel, following a Bernoulli distribution, provides a probabilistic characterization of uncertainty, indicating the likelihood of an area being free space. This uncertainty is critical for downstream planning.

#### 3.2. Uncertainty-Guided Planner Network

This component leverages the predicted layouts and uncertainty to guide path planning.

- **Graph Construction:** Instead of directly using the occupancy map, a collision-free graph G'=(V', E) is constructed based on the *predicted* layouts (`M_hat`). Nodes (v) are uniformly distributed in predicted free areas and connected based on proximity and line-of-sight.
- **Augmented Node Features:** Each node `v'_i` is augmented with informative features:
 - `s_i`: A binary signal indicating if the node is in a known or predicted area.
 - `p_i`: The average predicted probability of free space (derived from the inpainting model's uncertainty).
 - `u_i`: Normalized utility based on observable frontiers (for exploration; -1 in predicted areas).
 - `g_i`: A binary guidepost indicating if the node lies on the shortest trajectory to the nearest frontier in *known* areas (useful for backtracking). For navigation, a direction vector towards the point-goal is added.
- **Policy Network:** A graph attention network, inspired by, serves as the backbone.
 - **Encoder:** Consists of a feed-forward layer followed by N stacked masked self-attention layers. This aggregates information from neighboring nodes, progressively expanding the receptive field to integrate local structure and global spatial dependencies, building a rich, uncertainty-aware representation (`h_tilde_n`).
 - **Decoder:** Selects the next waypoint. It uses the robot's current position feature as a query to an attention layer, whose output directly serves as the policy `pi`, indicating scores for neighboring nodes. This allows adaptation to varying graph connectivity.
- **Critic Network:** An identical architecture to the policy network, but trained with `privileged learning`. It receives the ground-truth graph (G*) (extracted from the full environment) to estimate state-action (Q) values, providing robust learning signals for the policy under partial observability.
- **Training:** Both exploration and navigation planners are trained using the Soft Actor Critic (SAC) algorithm with discrete actions. Reward functions encourage task completion and penalize travel distance, with specific bonuses for discovering new frontiers (exploration) or proximity to the goal (navigation).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CogniPlan demonstrates superior performance across various evaluation scenarios, consistently outperforming both conventional and state-of-the-art learning-based planners.

- **Simulated Maps Evaluation:**
 - **Exploration:** CogniPlan achieved an average travel length reduction of **17.7%** compared to Inpaint+TARE (an ablation using predictions with a conventional planner) and **7.0%** compared to ARiADNE+ (a state-of-the-art DRL planner without layout predictions) across 150 diverse maps (Table 1). The poor performance of Inpaint+TARE highlights the necessity of an uncertainty-aware planner that can robustly handle variable predictions.
 - **Navigation:** CogniPlan exhibited travel length reductions of **3.9%** against Inpaint+A* and **12.5%** against CA (a DRL navigation planner without layout predictions) across 100 maps (Table 2). It also slightly surpassed D*Lite, a near-optimal search-based planner.
 - **Impact of Number of Predictions:** Increasing the number of layout predictions (from 1 to 4 to 7) generally improved performance, with gains saturating, suggesting that a small set of diverse hypotheses is sufficient to guide planning effectively (Fig. 3).
- **Realistic Floor Plans Evaluation (Zero-Shot):**
 - CogniPlan successfully generalized to the KTH floor plan dataset without any additional training or fine-tuning. While MapEx (a state-of-the-art non-learning approach trained on KTH) initially excelled, CogniPlan maintained steady exploration and surpassed MapEx beyond 90% coverage (Fig. 4). This indicates CogniPlan's ability to provide robust long-horizon planning even with out-of-distribution layouts.
 - **Robustness to Starting Positions:** CogniPlan demonstrated greater robustness to varying starting points, achieving the lowest average travel distance and significantly reducing standard deviation (over 50% less than baselines at 90-95% coverage), indicating more consistent and globally coherent planning (Fig. 5).
- **Gazebo Simulation and Hardware Deployment:**
 - **Gazebo:** In high-fidelity Gazebo environments (medium-scale 54m x 34m and large-scale 130m x 100m), CogniPlan consistently yielded shorter paths and higher exploration efficiency compared to methods like TARE, HPHS, and DSVP (Table 3, Table 4, Fig. 7, Fig. 8). Notably, it showed lower variance in travel distance, indicating more stable and consistent trajectories. Even in significantly out-of-distribution Gazebo environments, the predicted layouts effectively supported planning (Fig. 9).
 - **Hardware:** CogniPlan was successfully deployed on a real mobile robot in a cluttered 30m x 10m indoor laboratory (Fig. 6), demonstrating its real-world applicability and high-quality path planning. The robot completed exploration within 6 minutes, covering approximately 100 meters.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CogniPlan represents a significant advancement in autonomous mobile robotics, particularly in planning under uncertainty. Its primary contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Novel Integration of Generative Models and DRL:** The framework's core strength lies in its synergistic combination of explicit, uncertainty-aware layout prediction with a sophisticated graph attention-based DRL planner. This addresses a critical gap in the literature where these two paradigms were often decoupled or where uncertainty was not effectively leveraged.
- **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** By enabling the planner to reason over multiple plausible future states, CogniPlan consistently achieves more efficient paths (reduced travel length) for both exploration and navigation. Its demonstrated robustness to diverse environment types, starting positions, and even out-of-distribution layouts (zero-shot generalization) is crucial for real-world deployment.
- **Inspiration from Cognitive Mapping:** The explicit use of multiple layout predictions, drawing inspiration from how humans use cognitive maps, offers a more intuitive and potentially more intelligent approach to autonomous navigation.
- **Practical Applicability:** The successful deployment on both high-fidelity simulators and real hardware underscores CogniPlan's practical viability for applications in logistics, search and rescue, surveillance, and other domains requiring autonomous operation in unknown or dynamically changing environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - **End-to-End Learning:** The identified limitation of a one-way pipeline (frozen generator) opens avenues for joint, end-to-end training of the prediction and planning components, potentially leading to continuous improvement and adaptation.
 - **Adaptive Conditioning:** Future work could involve active sampling of conditioning vectors in the latent space, moving beyond fixed sets to dynamically generate relevant layout hypotheses.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** Addressing the replanning bottleneck through incremental graph updates will be crucial for even larger and more dynamic environments.
 - **Scalability to Arbitrary Map Sizes:** Adapting the inpainting model to resolution-agnostic architectures would further enhance the framework's generalization capabilities.
 - **Beyond Single-Agent:** The explicit prediction of environmental topology and uncertainty could naturally extend to multi-agent planning and cooperation, where agents could share and refine collective cognitive maps.

In essence, CogniPlan pushes the frontier of intelligent autonomous systems by endowing robots with a form of predictive foresight and uncertainty-aware reasoning, moving closer to human-like cognitive capabilities in navigation and exploration.
