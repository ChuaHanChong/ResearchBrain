# Mastering Atari with Discrete World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamerV2 introduces an agent that achieves human-level performance on the full Atari benchmark by learning behaviors entirely within a learned discrete world model. This approach demonstrates that model-based reinforcement learning can outperform top model-free agents with a comparable computational budget.

## Problem

- Most state-of-the-art agents on the Atari benchmark are model-free, which are often sample-inefficient and require vast amounts of real-world interaction.
- Prior model-based RL attempts on high-dimensional visual inputs like Atari struggled with insufficient model accuracy or suffered from prohibitively high computational costs and complexity.
- Learning a sufficiently accurate world model from raw pixel inputs that could enable effective planning and achieve human-level performance on complex visual environments remained a challenge.

## Method

- A ==Recurrent State-Space Model== (RSSM) is learned from raw image inputs using a continuously growing experience dataset to predict future states, rewards, and discount factors.
- The world model employs discrete categorical latent variables for its stochastic state, optimized using straight-through gradients, combined with a "KL balancing" technique in its loss function.
- An actor (policy) and a critic (value function) are trained purely within the learned compact latent space (imagination MDP), without further real-world interaction, utilizing a combined Reinforce and straight-through gradient loss for the actor.

## Results

- DreamerV2 achieved human-level performance on the full Atari benchmark of **55** tasks, making it the first agent to do so purely by learning behaviors within a separately trained world model.
- It surpassed the final performance of top single-GPU model-free agents (e.g., IQN, Rainbow) across all evaluated aggregation metrics on Atari, within a comparable computational budget (single GPU, **10** days).
- The agent successfully learned to solve the challenging Humanoid Walk task from pixel inputs and showed performance comparable to dedicated exploration algorithms on Montezuma's Revenge without explicit exploration mechanisms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discrete categorical latent variables, optimized with straight-through gradients, are superior to continuous Gaussian latents for modeling Atari dynamics, contributing significantly to performance.
> - "KL balancing" in the world model's loss function is crucial for learning accurate prior dynamics, improving the model's ability to imagine future states.
> - For policy optimization in discrete action spaces like Atari, Reinforce gradients are more effective than straight-through gradients alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated at the forefront of reinforcement learning (RL) research, specifically addressing the long-standing challenge of applying model-based RL to complex, high-dimensional environments like Atari games.

Historically, the Atari benchmark, introduced by Bellemare et al. (2013), became a crucial testing ground for general RL agents. Most state-of-the-art results on Atari have been achieved by **model-free RL algorithms** (e.g., DQN, Rainbow, IQN). These algorithms learn directly from trial and error, mapping observations to actions without explicitly building a model of the environment dynamics. While highly successful, they are often criticized for their sample inefficiency, requiring vast amounts of real-world interaction.

In contrast, **model-based RL** aims to address sample inefficiency by first learning a world model – an explicit representation of the environment's dynamics. This model allows the agent to "imagine" future outcomes of its actions and plan in a simulated, often compact, latent space, thereby reducing the need for real-world interactions. Despite the theoretical advantages of model-based RL in terms of sample efficiency and generalization (Sutton, 1991), learning sufficiently accurate world models from high-dimensional visual inputs (like images) and using them for successful planning has remained a significant challenge. Previous attempts, such as SimPLe (Kaiser et al., 2019), learned pixel-space video prediction models but struggled to achieve competitive performance on Atari, often being evaluated on subsets of games or for fewer steps with diminishing returns. MuZero (Schrittwieser et al., 2019) from DeepMind demonstrated impressive performance on board games and deterministic Atari games by learning a task-specific model for planning via Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS). However, MuZero's approach is computationally intensive, requiring extensive resources (over 2 months on a GPU for one Atari agent) and its implementation is not publicly available, limiting its accessibility and reproducibility for many research groups.

DreamerV2 directly tackles these limitations. It positions itself as a significant breakthrough by being the *first agent* that achieves human-level performance on the full Atari benchmark of 55 tasks *purely by learning behaviors inside a separately trained world model*. This demonstrates that its world model is accurate enough to enable effective policy learning. Furthermore, it achieves this feat with a practical computational budget (single GPU, 10 days of training) comparable to top model-free agents, making model-based RL on high-dimensional visual tasks significantly more accessible and competitive. The work extends the authors' own prior success with DreamerV1 (Hafner et al., 2019), which had shown promise on continuous control tasks but not yet achieved human-level performance on Atari. By successfully demonstrating the efficacy of model-based RL on Atari, DreamerV2 paves the way for further research into areas like transfer learning, multi-task learning, and robust exploration using world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind DreamerV2 are multifaceted, aiming to advance the state-of-the-art in model-based reinforcement learning and demonstrate its viability for complex, high-dimensional tasks.

1. **Achieve Human-Level Performance on Atari with a Learned World Model:** The primary objective is to develop a reinforcement learning agent that can reach and surpass human-level performance on the challenging Atari benchmark, which consists of 55 distinct games. Crucially, this performance must be achieved by learning behaviors *purely within the compact latent space of a separately trained world model*, rather than directly interacting with the real environment during policy optimization. This demonstrates the learned world model's accuracy and predictive power.
2. **Increase Sample-Efficiency and Generalization:** A foundational motivation for model-based RL is its potential to generalize from past experience and increase sample-efficiency compared to model-free methods. By enabling planning through imagined outcomes, DreamerV2 aims to reduce the amount of real-world interaction needed for an agent to learn effective policies.
3. **Overcome Limitations of Previous Model-Based Approaches:** The authors explicitly acknowledge that prior model-based attempts on competitive benchmarks like Atari have fallen short, either due to insufficient accuracy (e.g., SimPLe) or prohibitively high computational costs and complexity (e.g., MuZero). DreamerV2 is motivated to bridge this gap, demonstrating a practical and competitive model-based solution for Atari.
4. **Demonstrate Computational Efficiency and Reproducibility:** A significant motivation is to show that such high performance is achievable within a reasonable computational budget. DreamerV2 is designed to run on a single NVIDIA V100 GPU using a single environment instance, completing training within 10 days. This contrasts sharply with resource-intensive agents like MuZero, aiming to make the research more accessible and reproducible for a wider research community.
5. **Validate Design Choices for World Models:** The paper seeks to investigate and validate specific architectural and training modifications that contribute to the success of world models, particularly the transition from continuous (Gaussian) to discrete (categorical) latent representations and the use of "KL balancing" in the loss function.
6. **Applicability to Diverse Tasks:** While focusing on Atari (discrete actions), the authors also motivate the agent's broad applicability by demonstrating its performance on tasks with continuous action spaces, such as solving complex humanoid robot control from pixel inputs. This suggests the learned world model generalizes across different control paradigms.
7. **Aid Exploration and Generalization:** The work implicitly motivates that accurate world models can inherently aid in challenging aspects of RL, such as exploring sparse-reward environments (demonstrated on Montezuma's Revenge) and learning generalizable representations from pixel inputs.

In essence, DreamerV2 aims to prove that model-based RL is not just theoretically promising but can be practically competitive with and even outperform state-of-the-art model-free methods on challenging, high-dimensional benchmarks, while offering advantages in terms of data efficiency and interpretability of the learned dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamerV2 employs a model-based reinforcement learning approach that fundamentally builds upon its predecessor, DreamerV1. The overall methodology is structured around three interconnected components: world model learning, behavior learning, and execution in the environment.

#### 3.1. World Model Learning

The core of DreamerV2 is its "world model," a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) that learns a compact, predictive representation of the environment from raw image inputs.
- **Experience Dataset:** The world model is trained offline from a continuously growing dataset of past interactions with the environment, comprising sequences of images, actions, rewards, and discount factors. Batches of fixed-length sequences (L=50) are sampled for training.
- **Model Components:** The RSSM uses a sequence of deterministic recurrent states ($h_t$) to compute two distributions over stochastic states: a posterior state ($z_t$) that incorporates the current image ($x_t$), and a prior state ($\hat{z}_t$) that attempts to predict the posterior without current image access. From the posterior model state, the model predicts the current image, reward, and discount factor.
 - **Recurrent Model:** $h_t = f_\phi(h_{t-1}, z_{t-1}, a_{t-1})$
 - **Representation Model (Posterior):** $z_t \sim q_\phi(z_t | h_t, x_t)$
 - **Transition Predictor (Prior):** $\hat{z}_t \sim p_\phi(\hat{z}_t | h_t)$
 - **Predictors:** Image ($\hat{x}_t$), Reward ($\hat{r}_t$), Discount ($\hat{\gamma}_t$) are predicted from $(h_t, z_t)$.
- **Key Innovation: Discrete Latent Variables:** Unlike PlaNet and DreamerV1 which used Gaussian latent variables, DreamerV2 employs a vector of multiple **categorical variables** for its stochastic state $z_t$. These are optimized using **straight-through gradients** for backpropagation, making the discrete sampling compatible with gradient-based optimization. This is a significant departure and a core contribution to its improved performance.
- **Loss Function:** The world model is optimized to maximize the log-likelihood of observed images, rewards, and discount factors. Additionally, it includes a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence term between the posterior ($q_\phi(z_t | h_t, x_t)$) and the prior ($p_\phi(z_t | h_t)$).
 - **KL Balancing:** A crucial modification is "KL balancing," where the KL loss is minimized faster with respect to the prior than the representations (using different learning rates, $\alpha = 0.8$ for prior and $1-\alpha$ for posterior). This encourages the prior to more accurately approximate the aggregate posterior, improving the temporal dynamics learned by the model.

#### 3.2. Behavior Learning

Once the world model is trained, DreamerV2 learns an actor (policy) and a critic (value function) purely within the learned latent space, without requiring further interaction with the actual environment. This "imagination MDP" allows for highly efficient policy optimization.
- **Imagination MDP:** Trajectories are generated by starting from observed model states and predicting forward using the world model's transition predictor and reward predictor. This process does not involve generating pixel images, making it computationally very efficient.
- **Model Components:**
 - **Actor:** A stochastic policy $p_\psi(\hat{a}_t | \hat{z}_t)$ that outputs actions based on imagined latent states.
 - **Critic:** A deterministic value function $v_\xi(\hat{z}_t)$ that estimates the expected sum of future rewards from an imagined state.
- **Critic Loss:** The critic is trained using temporal-difference learning, specifically a $\lambda$-target (Equation 4), which combines intermediate rewards with future critic predictions. This allows it to learn long-horizon values effectively. The loss is a squared error between the critic's prediction and the $\lambda$-target.
- **Actor Loss:** The actor aims to maximize the long-term future rewards predicted by the critic. DreamerV2 uses a combined loss (Equation 6) that includes:
 - **Reinforce gradients:** An unbiased but high-variance estimator, weighted by the advantage (V-lambda target minus baseline critic value).
 - **Straight-through gradients:** A biased but low-variance estimator that backpropagates directly through the sampled actions and state sequences of the world model. For Atari, Reinforce gradients are found to work substantially better ($\rho=1$), while for continuous control, straight-through gradients are preferred ($\rho=0$).
 - **Entropy regularizer:** Encourages exploration by penalizing low-entropy policies.

#### 3.3. Execution

The trained actor is executed in the real environment to collect new experience. This collected data is then added to the experience dataset, which is used to further train the world model, completing the loop. The agent periodically updates its world model and behavior models based on this growing dataset.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The efficiency gains come from the fact that policy learning occurs almost entirely within the fast-simulated latent space. For every 50M environmental frames, DreamerV2 learns its policy from 468 billion (10,000x more) compact states imagined under the model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DreamerV2 delivers a series of impressive results that significantly advance the state of model-based reinforcement learning.

#### 4.1. Human-Level Performance on Atari Benchmark

- **First of its kind:** DreamerV2 is the first agent to achieve human-level performance on the entire Atari benchmark of 55 tasks purely by learning behaviors within a separately trained world model. This confirms that its world model is accurate enough to enable effective policy learning from imagined trajectories.
- **Outperformance of Model-Free Baselines:** With the same computational budget (single GPU, single environment instance, 200M frames, 10 days wall-clock time), DreamerV2 surpasses the final performance of top single-GPU model-free agents like IQN, Rainbow, C51, and DQN across all four evaluated aggregation metrics (Gamer Median, Gamer Mean, Record Mean, Clipped Record Mean). The "Clipped Record Mean" is highlighted as the most robust and recommended metric for comparison, where DreamerV2 shows a substantial lead.
- **Specific Game Performance:** While generally superior, DreamerV2 exhibits comparable or higher performance on most games. Notably, it shows strong improvements on games like James Bond, Up N Down, and Assault. The main exception is Video Pinball, which the authors hypothesize is due to the reconstruction loss not adequately focusing on critical, single-pixel objects like the ball.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

The paper conducts a thorough ablation study to pinpoint the key architectural and algorithmic components contributing to DreamerV2's success:

- **Categorical Latents:** This is identified as a major contributor. Categorical latent variables significantly outperform Gaussian latents on 42 out of 55 tasks. Hypotheses for this benefit include:
 - A categorical prior's ability to perfectly fit the aggregate posterior (unlike Gaussian).
 - Beneficial sparsity enforced by the categorical representation.
 - Potentially easier optimization with straight-through gradients.
 - A better inductive bias for modeling non-smooth changes in Atari games (e.g., entering new rooms, disappearing items).
- **KL Balancing:** This technique, which scales the prior cross entropy relative to the posterior entropy, substantially improves performance, outperforming the standard KL regularizer on 44 tasks. It encourages the world model to learn more accurate prior dynamics, which are critical for effective imagination.
- **Model Gradients (Image vs. Reward):**
 - **Image Gradients:** Crucial for learning general representations; stopping them drastically reduces performance on 51 tasks.
 - **Reward Gradients:** Surprisingly, stopping reward gradients before they enter the model states can sometimes improve performance (on 15 tasks). This suggests that representations learned purely from images, without being biased towards previously encountered rewards, may generalize better to novel situations. This contrasts with approaches like MuZero, which train their model primarily through task-specific value gradients.
- **Policy Gradients (Reinforce vs. Straight-Through):** For Atari, Reinforce gradients are found to be substantially more effective than straight-through gradients alone. However, combining both can yield marginal improvements on some tasks, suggesting that Reinforce helps convergence while straight-through gradients might aid initial learning due to lower variance.

#### 4.3. Continuous Control and Hard Exploration Tasks

- **Humanoid Control:** DreamerV2 successfully learns to solve the challenging Humanoid Walk task from the DeepMind Control Suite, purely from pixel inputs, which the authors state is "the first published result of solving the humanoid environment from only pixel inputs." This demonstrates the agent's applicability beyond discrete action spaces and Atari.
- **Montezuma's Revenge:** On this notoriously hard-exploration Atari game, DreamerV2 (with a tuned discount factor) achieves performance comparable to dedicated exploration algorithms like ICM, without requiring any explicit exploration mechanisms. This suggests that the world model inherently assists in solving sparse reward tasks, possibly through improved generalization of reward prediction or more efficient policy optimization in the latent space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of DreamerV2 marks a pivotal moment in the field of deep reinforcement learning, offering several significant contributions and opening new avenues for future research:

1. **Validation of Model-Based RL Competitiveness:** DreamerV2 provides compelling evidence that model-based reinforcement learning can not only achieve but *outperform* state-of-the-art model-free algorithms on highly competitive benchmarks like Atari. This achievement is particularly impactful given the years of research and engineering effort that have gone into optimizing model-free agents. It fundamentally shifts the perception of model-based RL from being theoretically promising but practically limited to a viable and often superior alternative.
2. **Practical and Accessible Model-Based RL:** By achieving human-level Atari performance with a relatively simple algorithm, on a single GPU, and within a reasonable training time (10 days), DreamerV2 makes advanced model-based RL significantly more accessible and reproducible for researchers globally. This contrasts sharply with prior model-based breakthroughs like MuZero, which required vast computational resources and proprietary implementations. This democratizes research in world models and encourages wider adoption and experimentation.
3. **Demonstrated Efficacy of Latent Space Planning:** The core success of DreamerV2 lies in its ability to learn successful behaviors purely from predictions in a compact latent space. This emphasizes the power of learning accurate, abstract representations of the environment, enabling efficient and extensive planning without the computational burden of pixel-level simulations. The observation that policy learning benefits from 10,000 times more imagined states than real environment frames collected highlights the efficiency gain.
4. **Insights into World Model Design:** The ablation studies provide crucial insights into effective world model design. The findings regarding the superiority of **discrete categorical latent variables** over Gaussian ones, and the effectiveness of **KL balancing**, offer concrete guidance for future development of latent dynamics models. Furthermore, the discovery that representations learned from image reconstruction alone can be more generalizable than those explicitly trained for reward prediction is a significant finding that challenges conventional wisdom and suggests new directions for representation learning in RL.
5. **Pathways for Future Research:** DreamerV2 explicitly opens up several promising research directions:
 - **Efficient Transfer and Multi-Task Learning:** Accurate world models, by capturing general aspects of the environment, are inherently better suited for transferring knowledge between tasks or learning multiple tasks simultaneously with greater sample efficiency.
 - **Sample-Efficient Learning on Physical Robots:** The success on challenging continuous control tasks like Humanoid Walk from pixel inputs indicates strong potential for applying DreamerV2 to real-world robotic systems, where data collection is expensive and sample efficiency is paramount.
 - **Global Exploration:** World models can facilitate directed exploration by estimating uncertainty or novelty in unseen states, leading to more intelligent and less random exploration strategies. The strong performance on Montezuma's Revenge, a hard-exploration game, supports this potential.
 - **Integration with Advanced Planning Techniques:** The authors suggest that advanced planning components, such as Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) used in MuZero, are complementary and could be integrated with DreamerV2's accurate world models to potentially achieve even higher performance or solve more complex planning problems.

In summary, DreamerV2 is a landmark paper that not only pushes the performance frontier of model-based reinforcement learning but also provides a practical, accessible, and insightful framework for developing intelligent agents that can learn and plan effectively in complex, high-dimensional environments.
