# SpatialRGPT: Grounded Spatial Reasoning in Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SpatialRGPT, developed by UC San Diego and NVIDIA, enhances Vision-Language Models' grounded spatial reasoning by creating a scalable data pipeline to generate 3D scene graphs from single 2D images and integrating a flexible depth information plugin. It surpasses existing baselines on the SpatialRGPT-Bench for spatial understanding and shows utility as a region-aware reward annotator for robotics.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack robust and accurate grounded spatial reasoning capabilities, struggling with both qualitative and quantitative 3D relationships.
- Existing VLMs lack precise region-level parsing and the ability to reason about specific object instances in complex spatial contexts.
- Current VLM architectures do not effectively integrate 3D information, such as depth, from 2D images, which is crucial for true spatial understanding.

## Method

- A scalable data pipeline automatically generates ==3D scene graphs== from single 2D images, creating the ==Open Spatial Dataset (OSD)== with diverse region-aware spatial QA pairs.
- A novel =="plugin" module== flexibly integrates ==relative-depth information== into the visual encoder of existing VLMs without requiring depth for all inputs.
- A multimodal VLM architecture, ==SpatialRGPT==, combines a visual encoder, region-feature extractor, multi-modal connectors, and a language model, trained on the OSD and general VLM datasets.

## Results

- SpatialRGPT significantly outperforms other VLMs and LLMs on the new SpatialRGPT-Bench, achieving over **90%** success rates for qualitative spatial reasoning and substantially lower error rates for quantitative measurements.
- The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on the BLINK Relative Depth Benchmark, improving accuracy by over **20%** compared to previous models, while maintaining comparable performance on general VLM benchmarks.
- SpatialRGPT demonstrates practical utility as a region-aware dense reward annotator for robotic tasks, enabling precise distance-based feedback for control, and is capable of complex multi-hop spatial reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating flexible depth information into VLM architectures significantly improves 3D spatial reasoning, especially for depth-sensitive relationships like "behind/front" and metric distances.
> - Large-scale, automatically generated 3D scene graphs derived from 2D images can effectively teach complex, grounded spatial understanding to VLMs, overcoming limitations of manual annotation or multi-view data.
> - VLMs can develop robust generalization capabilities for novel spatial tasks and multi-hop reasoning when trained with precise region-aware and depth-informed data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress in bridging the gap between visual and linguistic understanding. Modern VLMs excel in tasks such as image classification, captioning, object detection, and even complex question answering in 2D visual environments. However, a significant limitation persists: their ability to perform accurate and grounded spatial reasoning, especially concerning 3D arrangements and metric properties of objects in a scene.

Prior research in spatial reasoning with Large Language Models (LLMs) has explored approaches like reconstructing 3D scenes from multi-view images (e.g., point clouds, neural fields) and integrating these into LLMs (e.g., 3D-LLM, ConceptGraph). While promising, these methods often face challenges such as the availability of multi-view data, computational intensity, and the modal gap between explicit 3D representations and language. Some works, like `ConceptGraph`, try to use scene graphs to bypass direct 3D input to LLMs, but even then, LLMs have been shown to struggle with effectively utilizing coordinate information provided textually (`BLINK`).

More recently, efforts like `SpatialVLM` have attempted to imbue 2D VLMs with spatial reasoning by generating spatially-aware visual question answering (VQA) data. This approach implicitly encodes spatial understanding. However, `SpatialVLM` relies on language descriptions of objects, potentially leading to ambiguity when multiple similar objects are present, and its training might over-rely on textual cues rather than true visual spatial learning. Crucially, it lacks the capability for precise region-level specification, which is vital for real-world interactions.

In parallel, advancements in "Region-level Visual Language Models" have enabled VLMs to understand specific areas of an image. Models like `KOSMOS-2`, `Shikra`, and `LLaVA` have achieved region-based understanding, but often through textual bounding box coordinates. More sophisticated approaches, such as `RegionGPT` and `Osprey`, utilize visual spatial-aware modules and pixel-level features, allowing for input masks of arbitrary shapes. However, these models have not specifically focused on *enhancing spatial reasoning* at the region level.

`SpatialRGPT` directly addresses these identified gaps. It builds upon the strengths of region-aware VLMs, specifically `RegionGPT`'s ability to process pixel-level inputs, and integrates a novel mechanism for incorporating depth information, which is critical for true 3D spatial understanding. By developing a scalable data curation pipeline that generates `3D scene graphs` from single 2D images and converting these into diverse, region-aware spatial Q&A tasks, SpatialRGPT aims to teach VLMs to reason about spatial relationships in a more grounded and precise manner. This work seeks to elevate VLMs from merely understanding *what* is in an image to understanding *where* objects are in relation to each other in 3D space, and *how* their metric properties relate, thereby paving the way for more robust and reliable AI systems in visually-grounded applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind SpatialRGPT stems from a critical limitation observed in current Vision-Language Models (VLMs): their inadequate ability to perform robust and accurate spatial reasoning. Despite their impressive performance in various 2D vision and language tasks, VLMs frequently struggle with fundamental spatial concepts like "left/right," "above/below," "behind/in front," and "near/far," as well as more complex quantitative relationships such as metric distances and object dimensions. This deficiency is a significant bottleneck, as grounded spatial understanding is not merely an academic challenge but a prerequisite for deploying VLMs in practical, real-world applications like robotics, augmented reality, and complex visual scene interpretation.

The authors identify two core challenges contributing to this limitation:
1. **Limited Regional Parsing and Specificity:** Most existing VLMs are designed to understand the global context of an image. When an image contains numerous instances, it becomes difficult for users to prompt the model to reason about specific spatial relations between particular instances. VLMs generally lack the capability to accurately parse and reason about *regional information*, especially when specific object instances need to be highlighted and compared.
2. **Lack of 3D Information Integration:** Accurately perceiving spatial relations, particularly directions and distances in a 3D environment, cannot rely solely on 2D RGB pixel data. True spatial understanding requires incorporating 3D inputs, such as depth information, into the VLM's architecture.

Based on these motivations and challenges, the key objectives of the SpatialRGPT project are clearly articulated:

- **Enhance VLMs' Spatial Perception and Reasoning:** Develop a framework, SpatialRGPT, that significantly improves VLMs' capabilities in understanding and reasoning about spatial arrangements, particularly at the region level and in 3D space.
- **Develop a Scalable Data Curation Pipeline:** Create an automated pipeline capable of generating large-scale, high-quality, region-aware 3D spatial annotations from existing 2D image datasets. This pipeline should construct 3D scene graphs and transform them into diverse spatial question-answering (QA) tasks to effectively teach spatial knowledge to VLMs.
- **Integrate Depth Information Flexibly:** Propose a novel architectural design, a "plugin" module, that allows for the seamless and flexible integration of relative-depth information into the visual encoder of existing VLMs without compromising their performance when depth inputs are unavailable.
- **Establish a Comprehensive Spatial Reasoning Benchmark:** Introduce `SpatialRGPT-Bench`, a new benchmark based on ground-truth 3D annotations across diverse environments (indoor, outdoor, simulated) to rigorously evaluate the 3D spatial cognition capabilities of VLMs, addressing the current lack of such dedicated benchmarks.
- **Demonstrate Practical Utility:** Showcase SpatialRGPT's effectiveness in real-world applications, specifically its ability to perform complex spatial reasoning, conduct multi-hop reasoning, and function as a region-aware dense reward annotator for robotic tasks.

In essence, the project aims to equip VLMs with a more "grounded" and precise understanding of the physical world, moving beyond superficial recognition to deep spatial cognition, which is crucial for embodied AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpatialRGPT addresses the limitations of current VLMs in spatial reasoning through a two-pronged approach: a novel data curation pipeline and an advanced VLM architecture that integrates depth information.

#### 3.1. Data Curation Pipeline: 3D Scene Graph from Single 2D Images

The core of SpatialRGPT's training is a scalable data pipeline that automatically generates 3D, region-aware annotations from 2D images. This process culminates in the creation of a 3D scene graph for each image, which is then converted into region-aware spatial QA tasks. The pipeline consists of the following steps:

1. **Filtering:** Initial images (e.g., from OpenImages) are filtered to remove unsuitable content (e.g., screenshots, paintings) using a CLIP-based open-vocabulary classification model, ensuring data suitability for spatial reasoning.
2. **Open-Vocabulary Detection & Segmentation:** To extract object instances and their precise regional masks, the pipeline utilizes state-of-the-art open-vocabulary models. An open-vocabulary image tagging model identifies object classes, `GroundingDino` detects corresponding bounding boxes, and segmentation models (e.g., `SAM`) refine these into highly accurate masks. This ensures precise contours critical for 3D bounding box accuracy.
3. **Metric Depth Estimation:** To obtain 3D information from 2D images, `Metric3Dv2` is employed. This model, trained on diverse indoor and outdoor scenes, predicts metric depth and surface normals, addressing scale ambiguity and robustly estimating depth "in-the-wild" images, enhancing geometric recovery around object edges.
4. **Camera Calibration:** Accurate 3D reconstruction requires camera intrinsics and a canonicalized scene space. `WildCamera` estimates four Degrees of Freedom (DoF) intrinsic parameters (focal point, focal length). `PerspectiveFields` provides per-pixel up-vectors and latitude values to derive camera extrinsics (pitch, roll), allowing the transformation of point clouds from camera to a canonicalized geodetic coordinate system. This avoids limitations of previous methods reliant on specific surface segmentations.
5. **Point Cloud Processing and 3D Scene Graph Construction:** The deprojected object points from the depth map are canonicalized and denoised (using statistical outlier removal and DBSCAN) to improve robustness. Finally, 3D axis-aligned bounding boxes (AABBs) are created for each object, from which metric width and height are calculated. The 3D scene graph is then formed, with nodes representing object instances (class, width, height) and edges denoting spatial relationships. These relationships are categorized into:
 - **Relative Relations:** `left`, `right`, `above`, `below`, `behind`, `front`, `wide`, `thin`, `tall`, `short`, `big`, `small`.
 - **Metric Relations:** `direction`, `direct distance`, `horizontal distance`, `vertical distance`.
 All object nodes are traversed to calculate these relationships using their point cloud centroids and bounding boxes.

#### 3.2. Learning Spatial-aware VLMs from 3D Scene Graph

The constructed 3D scene graphs are transformed into textual Q&A pairs for VLM training, creating the `Open Spatial Dataset (OSD)`:

1. **Template-based Question Answering:** Handcrafted templates are used to generate basic qualitative and quantitative QAs from node attributes (width, height) and edge attributes (relative, metric relations). Objects are referred to using `Region [X]` tags to ensure region-awareness.
2. **LLM-based Complex Reasoning Question Answering:** To enhance reasoning capabilities, `Llama3-70B` is used to generate complex spatial reasoning questions. Instead of inputting raw 3D coordinates (which LLMs struggle with), spatial descriptions derived from the scene graph are provided along with region tags. The LLM then creates context-matching, complex Q&A.

The resulting `OSD` contains 1M unique images, 5M open-vocabulary regions, 8M template-based QAs, and 700K LLM-based QAs.

#### 3.3. VLM Architecture: SpatialRGPT

SpatialRGPT is built upon a multimodal language model architecture:

1. **Visual Encoder:** A pre-trained `OpenAI CLIP-L` model is used as the backbone, processing images at 336x336 resolution.
2. **Region-feature Extractor:** Adopting the technique from `RegionGPT`, a 2-layer deconvolution network refines feature maps, and `MaskPooling` extracts and averages features from masked areas, enabling pixel-level region understanding. A separate, similarly initialized module is used for depth features.
3. **Multi-modal Connectors:** Simple linear layers bridge the visual (image and depth) features to the LLM's word embedding space. Separate connectors for RGB and depth prevent modality mixing. The depth connector's weights are initialized from the RGB connector and trained specifically on spatial QAs.
4. **Large Language Model:** `LLaMA2-7B` serves as the language processing core.
5. **Plugin Module for Relative-depth Inputs:** This is a key innovation. It allows SpatialRGPT to optionally incorporate depth maps. The same visual encoder processes the depth map, generating depth feature maps. An *additional depth-to-language connector* (initialized from the RGB connector) projects these features. This flexible design allows the pre-trained 2D visual encoder to leverage depth without extensive re-training or requiring depth for all inputs.
6. **Tokenization and Prompt Format:** A multi-turn conversation format (similar to `LLaVA` and `RegionGPT`) is used. Special tokens like `<image>`, `<region>`, and `<depth>` serve as placeholders for interleaved visual embeddings and text, fed auto-regressively to the LLM.

#### 3.4. Training and Inference Paradigm

SpatialRGPT's training follows a three-stage strategy:
1. **Connector Feature Alignment:** Pre-training the RGB connector using `CC3M` image-caption pairs.
2. **Visual Language Pre-training:** Pre-training the LLM and RGB connector on `MMC4` and `COYO`. Crucially, the depth connector is *then* pre-trained on the `OSD` dataset while other parameters are frozen.
3. **Visual Instruction-tuning:** Fine-tuning the entire pre-trained model on a blend of `LLaVA`, `RegionGPT` instruction tuning data, and the `OSD` dataset. A random sampling strategy for input modalities (box/mask) ensures versatility.

During inference, SpatialRGPT can accept both bounding boxes or segmentation masks as region inputs. If only bounding boxes are provided, SAM is used to segment the corresponding mask.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that SpatialRGPT significantly enhances VLMs' spatial reasoning capabilities across various benchmarks and real-world applications.

#### 4.1. 3D Spatial Reasoning Benchmarks (SpatialRGPT-Bench)

To address the lack of dedicated benchmarks for 3D spatial cognition, the authors introduce `SpatialRGPT-Bench`, a comprehensive VQA benchmark with ground-truth 3D annotations from diverse urban (nuScenes, KITTI), indoor (SUNRGBD, ARKitScenes), and simulated (Hypersim) environments. The benchmark includes 657 qualitative and 749 quantitative VQA pairs.

Key findings from SpatialRGPT-Bench (Table 1):
- **Superior Performance:** SpatialRGPT significantly outperforms all baselines (blind LLMs, VLMs with language referral, and other region-aware VLMs like GPT-4V+SoM, LLaVA-v1.6+SoM, KOSMOS-2, and RegionVILA) in both qualitative (relative relations) and quantitative (metric measurements) spatial reasoning tasks.
 - For qualitative QAs, SpatialRGPT-7B and its VILA variants consistently achieve success rates above 90%, with some categories like "Below/Above" and "Left/Right" nearing 100% accuracy. This is a substantial improvement over GPT-4V (58.14% avg) and LLaVA (43.98% avg).
 - For quantitative QAs, SpatialRGPT models exhibit significantly lower absolute relative errors (e.g., 0.25-0.33 for distances, 0.31-0.61 for width/height) compared to baselines, indicating much higher accuracy in metric estimations. Success rates for quantitative tasks are also much higher.
- **Value of Depth Information:** The `SpatialRGPT-7B` variant (with depth input) shows improved performance over `SpatialRGPT-7B(rgb)` (RGB only), particularly in tasks that benefit from 3D cues such as "Behind/Front" recognition (91.81% vs. 87.27%) and metric distance estimations. This confirms the effectiveness of the flexible depth plugin module.
- **LLMs' World Knowledge:** Blind LLMs (like GPT-4) and VLMs with language referrals surprisingly achieve commendable success rates for some quantitative QAs, especially related to object width and height. This suggests that LLMs can leverage their extensive world knowledge to infer some spatial properties even without direct visual grounding, but they lack the precision and generalizability of SpatialRGPT.

#### 4.2. Public Vision-Language Benchmarks

To ensure that the spatial training does not negatively impact general VLM capabilities, SpatialRGPT was evaluated on standard VLM benchmarks and specific region/spatial benchmarks:

- **General Benchmarks (Table 2):** SpatialRGPT variants (e.g., SpatialRGPT-VILA-1.5-3B) performed comparably to or slightly better than the baseline VILA-1.5-3B model on general VQA datasets (VQA v2, GQA, SQA, MMVet, etc.). This demonstrates that the specialized spatial training and depth integration do not compromise general VLM performance.
- **Region-level & Spatial Benchmarks:**
 - **Region-level Classification (COCO-2017 val set, Table 3):** SpatialRGPT models show strong region cognition, outperforming baselines like RegionGPT-7B and other region-aware VLMs in object classification with ground-truth boxes.
 - **BLINK Relative Depth Benchmark (Table 4):** SpatialRGPT achieved state-of-the-art performance on this challenging point-level depth assessment benchmark, significantly outperforming models like GPT-4V-Turbo by over 20% accuracy gain. This highlights the model's robust ability to generalize to novel spatial tasks without explicit training.

#### 4.3. Real-world Applications

SpatialRGPT's enhanced spatial reasoning enables several practical applications:

- **Complex Spatial Reasoning (Figure 4):** Unlike some systems that offload complex reasoning to a separate LLM, SpatialRGPT integrates these capabilities directly. It can answer intricate spatial questions (e.g., estimating age from height, number of people a sofa can seat, travel time based on distance) by leveraging its learned spatial knowledge, demonstrating a robust generalization of its intrinsic language reasoning abilities.
- **Multi-hop Reasoning (Figure 5):** The model shows the capacity to perform multi-hop spatial queries (e.g., "object in the basket to the right of Region and how far is it?"). This indicates a deep understanding of spatial relationships, even without explicit training on such complex sequences.
- **Region-aware Dense Reward Annotator (Figure 6):** SpatialRGPT can act as a precise reward annotator for robotic tasks. By directly specifying regions (e.g., fingertip and target object using bounding boxes), the model can annotate distance-based rewards. Results from a real robot experiment show estimated distance monotonically decreasing as the robot's fingertip approaches the target, validating its utility for precise robotic control, overcoming the ambiguity of language-only approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SpatialRGPT represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models by fundamentally enhancing their spatial perception and reasoning capabilities, particularly in a grounded 3D context. Its significance and potential impact span several critical areas:

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Bridging the Spatial Reasoning Gap:** The work directly addresses a long-standing limitation of VLMs, moving them beyond superficial 2D visual understanding to a more profound comprehension of 3D spatial arrangements and metric properties. This is crucial for truly intelligent visual agents.
- **Novel Data Curation Paradigm:** The introduction of a scalable, automated data pipeline to generate 3D region-aware scene graphs from single 2D images is a major methodological contribution. This pipeline effectively transforms readily available 2D data into rich 3D spatial knowledge, overcoming the scarcity and cost of directly acquiring large-scale 3D annotated datasets.
- **Innovative Architectural Design:** The flexible "plugin" module for integrating depth information is a clever architectural innovation. It allows existing 2D VLM visual encoders to learn 3D cues without requiring massive re-training from scratch or forcing a fixed dependency on depth input. This maximizes compatibility and efficiency.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** The creation of `SpatialRGPT-Bench` fills a critical void in VLM evaluation. By providing a rigorous benchmark with ground-truth 3D annotations across diverse environments, it establishes a new standard for assessing spatial cognition and will undoubtedly catalyze future research in this specific area.
- **Strong Generalization:** The model's exceptional performance on `BLINK Relative Depth Benchmark` (which measures point-level depths not explicitly trained for) and its ability to perform complex multi-hop reasoning (without explicit training) underscore its strong generalization capabilities and a deep, robust learned representation of spatial knowledge.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advanced Vision-Language Models:** SpatialRGPT pushes the frontier of VLM capabilities, enabling them to perform more human-like spatial reasoning. This will lead to more robust, reliable, and contextually aware VLMs that can interpret complex visual environments with greater precision.
- **Robotics:** The most immediate and profound impact is on robotics. SpatialRGPT's ability to act as a "region-aware dense reward annotator" directly addresses a key challenge in robotic control and learning. By allowing precise, region-specific spatial queries (e.g., "distance between fingertip and object"), it can generate dense rewards for reinforcement learning, enabling more accurate navigation, manipulation, and interaction in real-world settings, reducing ambiguity inherent in language-only instructions.
- **Augmented and Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** Enhanced spatial awareness is fundamental for immersive AR/VR experiences. SpatialRGPT could improve object placement, interaction, and scene understanding within virtual environments, leading to more realistic and responsive applications.
- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI):** By allowing users to ask complex spatial questions about specific regions in an image, SpatialRGPT could facilitate more intuitive and natural interactions with AI systems in domains like image analysis, design, and even everyday assistance.
- **Scientific Research Catalyst:** The release of the `OSD` dataset and `SpatialRGPT-Bench` provides invaluable resources for the research community. This will accelerate future work in 3D spatial cognition for VLMs, allowing researchers to build upon this foundation, test new hypotheses, and develop even more sophisticated spatial reasoning models.
- **Broader AI Development:** The methodology of generating grounded, region-aware spatial data and flexibly integrating multimodal inputs can inspire similar approaches for other challenging perception and reasoning tasks in AI, contributing to the development of more general and capable intelligent agents.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the use of Axis-Aligned Bounding Boxes (AABBs) instead of more accurate Oriented Bounding Boxes (OBBs) due to current open-world challenges, these are presented as avenues for future work, highlighting the ongoing nature of research in this complex domain. Overall, SpatialRGPT lays a strong foundation for future VLMs that can truly "see" and "understand" the spatial reality of the world.
