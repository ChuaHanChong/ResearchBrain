# Perception, Reason, Think, and Plan: A Survey on Large Multimodal Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen, surveyed Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (LMRMs), tracing their evolution through four stages from perception-driven modularity to language-centric reasoning. The survey highlights current limitations of state-of-the-art models in omni-modal and agentic benchmarks, and proposes Native Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (N-LMRMs) as a future paradigm for inherently multimodal and integrated AI systems.

## Problem

- AI systems require robust and adaptive reasoning for complex, open, and multimodal environments.
- Existing surveys on multimodal models lacked comprehensive analysis of reinforcement learning-enhanced reasoning and future technical prospects for large multimodal reasoning models (LMRMs).
- Current large multimodal models (LMMs) show limitations in omni-modal generalization, reasoning depth, and agentic behavior.

## Method

- The authors conducted a comprehensive, structured survey organized around a ==four-stage developmental roadmap== for multimodal reasoning models.
- The survey analyzes the evolution from ==perception-driven modularity== to language-centric short and long reasoning, and proposes the future concept of ==Native Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (N-LMRMs)==.
- It provides empirical insights into the capabilities and limitations of state-of-the-art models, including preliminary studies on OpenAI O3 and O4-mini, and updates existing datasets and benchmarks.

## Results

- The survey identifies a clear four-stage evolution of multimodal reasoning models, progressing from perception-driven modularity to language-centric "System-1" and "System-2" reasoning paradigms.
- State-of-the-art LMRMs show limited omni-modal generalization (e.g., GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro achieving below **20%** on OmniMMI) and struggle with agentic tasks (e.g., GPT-4o at **0.6%** on BrowseComp).
- Preliminary studies with OpenAI O3 revealed strong multi-modal Chain-of-Thought capabilities, but also limitations such as language knowledge interference, input file handling issues, and the fabrication of reasoning rationales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal reasoning models are evolving from modular, perception-driven designs to more unified, language-centric frameworks, with a future vision for native omni-modal integration.
> - Current state-of-the-art Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (LMRMs) exhibit limitations in omni-modal generalization, reasoning depth, and agentic behavior, failing to achieve human-level reliability in complex tasks.
> - Preliminary studies on models like OpenAI O3 suggest potential issues such as language knowledge interference over visual input, difficulties with file handling and multimedia generation, and the fabrication of reasoning.
