# Perception, Reason, Think, and Plan: A Survey on Large Multimodal Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen, surveyed Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (LMRMs), tracing their evolution through four stages from perception-driven modularity to language-centric reasoning. The survey highlights current limitations of state-of-the-art models in omni-modal and agentic benchmarks, and proposes Native Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (N-LMRMs) as a future paradigm for inherently multimodal and integrated AI systems.

## Problem

- AI systems require robust and adaptive reasoning for complex, open, and multimodal environments.
- Existing surveys on multimodal models lacked comprehensive analysis of reinforcement learning-enhanced reasoning and future technical prospects for large multimodal reasoning models (LMRMs).
- Current large multimodal models (LMMs) show limitations in omni-modal generalization, reasoning depth, and agentic behavior.

## Method

- The authors conducted a comprehensive, structured survey organized around a ==four-stage developmental roadmap== for multimodal reasoning models.
- The survey analyzes the evolution from ==perception-driven modularity== to language-centric short and long reasoning, and proposes the future concept of ==Native Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (N-LMRMs)==.
- It provides empirical insights into the capabilities and limitations of state-of-the-art models, including preliminary studies on OpenAI O3 and O4-mini, and updates existing datasets and benchmarks.

## Results

- The survey identifies a clear four-stage evolution of multimodal reasoning models, progressing from perception-driven modularity to language-centric "System-1" and "System-2" reasoning paradigms.
- State-of-the-art LMRMs show limited omni-modal generalization (e.g., GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro achieving below **20%** on OmniMMI) and struggle with agentic tasks (e.g., GPT-4o at **0.6%** on BrowseComp).
- Preliminary studies with OpenAI O3 revealed strong multi-modal Chain-of-Thought capabilities, but also limitations such as language knowledge interference, input file handling issues, and the fabrication of reasoning rationales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal reasoning models are evolving from modular, perception-driven designs to more unified, language-centric frameworks, with a future vision for native omni-modal integration.
> - Current state-of-the-art Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (LMRMs) exhibit limitations in omni-modal generalization, reasoning depth, and agentic behavior, failing to achieve human-level reliability in complex tasks.
> - Preliminary studies on models like OpenAI O3 suggest potential issues such as language knowledge interference over visual input, difficulties with file handling and multimedia generation, and the fabrication of reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen an explosive growth in large language models (LLMs) and, more recently, large multimodal models (LMMs). Reasoning is widely acknowledged as a core component of intelligence, enabling decision-making, conclusion-drawing, and generalization across diverse domains. As AI systems increasingly interact with complex, open, and multimodal environments, the demand for robust and adaptive reasoning capabilities becomes paramount.

This survey positions Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (LMRMs) as a critical paradigm that integrates various modalities—text, images, audio, video—to support complex reasoning. It recognizes the evolution of multimodal reasoning from early, modular, perception-driven pipelines to more unified, language-centric frameworks. While acknowledging significant progress through instruction tuning and reinforcement learning, the authors identify a crucial gap in the existing literature: previous surveys primarily focused on either multimodal large language models or reasoning methods centered predominantly on language. They often lacked a detailed analysis of recent reinforcement learning-enhanced multimodal reasoning and comprehensive technical prospects for future LMRMs.

By presenting a comprehensive and structured roadmap, this survey aims to fill this void. It synthesizes historical trends with emerging research, clarifying the current state and informing the design of next-generation systems. The work actively engages with the cutting edge, referencing models and trends up to mid-2025, demonstrating its intent to provide a timely and relevant overview in a rapidly evolving domain. It bridges the divide between foundational multimodal understanding and the advanced reasoning capabilities required for truly intelligent, agentic behavior in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this survey is to provide a comprehensive and structured overview of multimodal reasoning research, aiming to **clarify the current landscape and inform the design of next-generation multimodal reasoning systems.** The authors are motivated by the fundamental role of reasoning in intelligence, especially as AI systems increasingly operate in dynamic, uncertain, and multimodal environments, necessitating robust and adaptive behavior.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:
- **Addressing Current Limitations:** Despite rapid progress, multimodal reasoning remains a core bottleneck for large multimodal models, exhibiting limitations in omni-modal generalization, reasoning depth, and agentic behavior. The survey is driven by the need to understand these challenges and chart a path forward.
- **Developing a Coherent Framework:** To provide a structured understanding of the field's evolution, the authors propose a novel **four-stage developmental roadmap** for multimodal reasoning models. This roadmap reflects the shifting design philosophies and emerging capabilities, moving from perception-driven modularity to native multimodal reasoning.
- **Analyzing Reinforcement-Enhanced Reasoning:** A significant motivation is to provide a detailed analysis of recent advancements in reinforcement learning-enhanced multimodal reasoning, an area previously underexplored in existing surveys.
- **Projecting Future Directions (N-LMRMs):** A forward-looking objective is to introduce and discuss the conceptual direction of **Native Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (N-LMRMs)**. This paradigm envisions models where reasoning natively emerges from omni-modal perception and interaction, moving beyond retrofitted language models to support scalable, agentic, and adaptive reasoning and planning in complex, real-world environments.
- **Organizing Resources:** The survey also aims to reorganize and update existing datasets and benchmarks (to June 30, 2025) across multimodal understanding, generation, reasoning, and planning, thereby providing a valuable resource for researchers.

In essence, the authors seek to provide a definitive guide to the past, present, and future of multimodal reasoning, emphasizing the transition towards more integrated, adaptive, and intelligent AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a **comprehensive and structured survey methodology** to analyze the vast landscape of Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (LMRMs). Their primary approach is to organize the field's evolution and future trajectory around a **four-stage developmental roadmap**, which serves as the core framework for their analysis.

The roadmap is conceptualized as follows:
1. **Stage 1: Perception-Driven Modular Reasoning**: This early stage focuses on models that employed task-specific modules for reasoning, where reasoning capabilities were implicitly embedded within stages like representation, alignment, and fusion. This includes:
 - **Modular Reasoning Networks**: Early approaches using CNNs, LSTMs, and attention mechanisms (e.g., NMN, MAC, BAN).
 - **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)-based Modular Reasoning**: Models leveraging large-scale image-text pre-training with Transformer architectures (e.g., CLIP, BLIP-2, Kosmos-2) for enhanced perception-driven reasoning.

2. **Stage 2: Language-Centric Short Reasoning (System-1 Reasoning)**: This stage marks the pivotal shift to multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and focuses on "fast, intuitive" reasoning, often relying on language-centric frameworks. This includes:
 - **Prompt-based MCoT**: Utilizing carefully crafted instructions to elicit step-by-step multimodal reasoning (e.g., VoT, IPVR).
 - **Structural Reasoning**: Learning explicit procedural structures for reasoning through supervised training (e.g., Multimodal-CoT, TextCoT).
 - **Externally Augmented Reasoning**: Integrating advanced algorithms, auxiliary tools, or expert modules to compensate for inherent model limitations (e.g., search algorithms, RAG, visual experts).

3. **Stage 3: Language-Centric Long Reasoning (System-2 Thinking and Planning)**: This stage moves towards "slower, deliberate" cognitive processes, emphasizing extended reasoning chains, grounding in multimodal inputs, and training with supervised or reinforcement signals. This includes:
 - **Cross-Modal Reasoning**: Integrating and reasoning across modalities beyond text (e.g., VisProg, FAST, Image-of-Thought).
 - **Multimodal-O1**: Models like OpenAI O1 that rely on multi-stage, structured reasoning paths (e.g., Marco-o1, LLaVA-CoT) often enhanced by search algorithms like MCTS.
 - **Multimodal-R1**: Approaches leveraging reinforcement learning, particularly DPO and GRPO algorithms, to enhance long-chain reasoning capabilities (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, MM-EUREKA).

4. **Stage 4: Towards Native Large Multimodal Reasoning Model (Prospect)**: This is a forward-looking paradigm shift, where reasoning is natively integrated, moving beyond language-centric architectures to unify omni-modal understanding, generation, and agentic reasoning.

The survey's methodology also incorporates **empirical insights** from challenging benchmarks and experimental cases of OpenAI O3 and O4-mini. These preliminary studies serve to ground the theoretical discussion of future N-LMRMs by highlighting the current limitations of even state-of-the-art models in real-world omni-modal understanding and agentic capabilities. Furthermore, the authors extensively **reorganize existing datasets and benchmarks**, updating them to June 2025, and classifying them across understanding, generation, reasoning, and planning tasks, providing a valuable resource for the community. The paper reviews approximately 700 publications, indicating the depth and breadth of their literature analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey identifies a clear evolutionary trajectory in multimodal reasoning models, moving from task-specific modular designs to increasingly unified and complex language-centric frameworks, ultimately projecting towards truly "native" multimodal reasoners.

**Evolutionary Stages and Capabilities:**
- **Stage 1 (Perception-Driven Modular Reasoning):** Early models were constrained by data and architecture, leading to modular designs where reasoning was implicit. The rise of VLMs (e.g., CLIP, BLIP-2) integrated multimodal representation, perception, and fusion, but reasoning largely remained a classification-based paradigm, needing external modules for deeper inference.
- **Stage 2 (Language-Centric Short Reasoning):** MLLMs marked a shift to end-to-end, language-centric frameworks. The advent of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Multimodal CoT (MCoT) enabled explicit, step-by-step reasoning. Prompt-based, structural, and externally augmented MCoT methods improved contextualization and interpretability, though reasoning remained largely "System-1" – fast, intuitive, and often reactive.
- **Stage 3 (Language-Centric Long Reasoning):** Current research moves towards "System-2" reasoning, emphasizing slower, deliberate, and structured cognitive processes. This involves cross-modal reasoning chains, the emergence of "O1-like" models (multi-stage reasoning, self-reflection, error correction, and search algorithms like MCTS), and "R1-like" models (reinforcement learning, especially DPO and GRPO, for deeper, transferable reasoning and agentic behaviors).

**Limitations of Current LMRMs (Empirical Findings):**
The survey critically evaluates the performance of state-of-the-art LMRMs on omni-modal and agentic benchmarks, revealing significant limitations:
- **Omni-modal Benchmarks:** Models like Gemini-1.5-Pro and GPT-4o show average accuracy below 20% on OmniMMI, and open-source models perform even worse. Performance sharply declines as the number of modalities increases (e.g., BabelBench, OmnixR), indicating struggles with unified understanding and generating reasoning paths across image, video, and audio inputs.
- **Agent Benchmarks:** Despite notable progress, current LMRMs consistently fall short of human-level reliability in complex, open-ended tasks (e.g., AgentBench, OSWorld, BrowseComp). Common bottlenecks include failures in real-world grounding, coherent long-horizon planning, seamless tool integration, and robustness across diverse modalities and domains. For example, GPT-4o achieves only 0.6% accuracy on BrowseComp, highlighting weak tool-interactive planning.

**Preliminary Study with OpenAI O3 and O4-mini:**
The authors conducted a preliminary study on recently released OpenAI models, O3 and O4-mini, providing valuable real-world insights:
- **Strengths:** O3 demonstrated strong multi-modal Chain-of-Thought capabilities, effectively performing task decomposition (e.g., cropping sub-figures for a Chinese Civil Service Examination question) and leveraging subtle visual clues for puzzle-solving and location identification.
- **Challenges:**
 - **Language Knowledge Interference:** O3 sometimes prioritizes linguistic understanding over visual input, as seen in the "six-finger" case where it misidentified the number of fingers based on emoji conventions rather than the actual image.
 - **Input File Handling & Multimedia Generation:** The models struggled with processing input files (e.g., incorrect phone number parsing from a PDF resume) and generating multimedia content (e.g., failing to adhere to specific visual instructions for image frames). They also showed a lack of support for interleaved text-image generation.
 - **Fabricated Reasoning:** A critical finding was that O3 might "fabricate" reasoning, constructing incorrect rationales even for potentially correct answers, suggesting an incomplete grasp of the underlying logic and a potential for deceptive behavior if not addressed during post-training.

These findings collectively highlight that while current LMRMs have made strides, they are not yet truly capable of omni-modal or native agentic behavior, underscoring the need for the "Native Large Multimodal Reasoning Models" concept.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the future direction of AI research, particularly in the realm of multimodal intelligence. By comprehensively mapping the evolution of Large Multimodal Reasoning Models (LMRMs) and critically evaluating their current capabilities, the authors provide a vital, forward-looking roadmap for the field.

**Key Significance:**
1. **Clarification and Organization:** The structured, four-stage roadmap serves as an invaluable framework for understanding the complex and rapidly evolving landscape of multimodal reasoning. It helps categorize existing research and identify clear progression paths, reducing ambiguity in a crowded field.
2. **Highlighting Gaps and Bottlenecks:** The empirical findings, especially the detailed analysis of state-of-the-art commercial models like OpenAI O3 and O4-mini, rigorously expose the fundamental limitations of current LMRMs. These include insufficient omni-modal generalization, shallow reasoning depth, and underdeveloped agentic behavior, even with advanced Chain-of-Thought mechanisms and reinforcement learning. This is crucial for directing future research efforts towards high-impact areas.
3. **Proposing a Paradigm Shift: Native LMRMs (N-LMRMs):** The introduction of the "Native Large Multimodal Reasoning Model" concept is arguably the most significant contribution. It shifts the focus from retrofitting language models with multimodal capabilities to designing models that are inherently multimodal from their core, unifying understanding, generation, and agentic reasoning across *all* modalities. This vision aims to move AI beyond isolated task performance towards more holistic, human-like intelligence.

**Potential Impact:**
The conceptualization of N-LMRMs and the outlined technical prospects could drive several transformative impacts:
- **Multimodal Agentic Reasoning:** N-LMRMs are envisioned to exhibit proactive, goal-driven interactions with complex environments. This implies advanced capabilities like hierarchical task breakdown, real-time strategic adaptation based on environmental feedback, embodied learning through continuous interaction, and seamless tool use. This will accelerate the development of truly autonomous AI agents capable of operating in diverse real-world scenarios, from robotics to autonomous driving and sophisticated GUI automation.
- **Omni-Modal Understanding and Generative Reasoning:** The emphasis on unified representation spaces for heterogeneous data (text, image, audio, video, tactile, sensor streams) will lead to more robust cross-modal synthesis and analysis. This means AI systems could generate coherent, context-aware outputs across modalities, such as creating diagrams from spoken instructions, synthesizing video narratives from text descriptions, or performing complex spatial reasoning across various sensory inputs.
- **Advancing AGI:** The authors explicitly link the development of N-LMRMs to OpenAI's five-stage pathway to AGI. By addressing unified perception, advanced reasoning, and continuous learning from "world experiences" (including the development of "World Models"), N-LMRMs lay critical groundwork for subsequent stages like self-evolving innovators and multi-agent organizations. This frames the research within the broader, ambitious goal of creating highly intelligent and adaptive AI.
- **Guiding Technical Development:** The discussion on technical prospects—unified representations (e.g., MoE architectures), interleaved multimodal long CoT, learning from world experiences (lifelong learning, online RL, offline verification), and data synthesis pipelines (especially for multi-modal interactive chains and multi-tool coordination)—provides concrete directions for researchers and developers to build the next generation of multimodal AI.

In conclusion, this survey is more than a summary; it's a strategic blueprint that identifies critical current limitations and proposes a radical architectural shift towards native, omni-modal reasoning. By pushing for models that truly "perceive, reason, think, and plan" across all modalities, this work provides a compelling vision for achieving a more generalized, adaptable, and human-like artificial intelligence.
