# Diffusion Blend: Inference-Time Multi-Preference Alignment for Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Texas A&M University and Qualcomm AI Research introduce Diffusion Blend, a framework enabling diffusion models to dynamically adjust to multiple user-specified preferences and regularization strengths at inference time. The method, particularly its DB-MPA-LS variant, achieves Pareto-optimal trade-offs between conflicting objectives with performance comparable to retraining individual models, while maintaining the inference speed of the original pre-trained model.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning fine-tuning for diffusion models assumes static reward functions and fixed regularization weights, making it difficult to balance multiple conflicting objectives or adapt to dynamic user preferences.
- Accommodating varying preferences and regularization strengths traditionally requires retraining separate models, a computationally intensive and resource-prohibitive endeavor.
- Current methods lack the ability for post-hoc adjustment of model alignment parameters, limiting user control and requiring costly grid searches for optimal regularization.

## Method

- **Diffusion Blend** leverages theoretical derivations to blend the ==backward diffusion== processes of pre-trained and fine-tuned models at inference time, based on user-defined preference weights.
- DB-MPA (Multi-Preference Alignment) fine-tunes separate models for each basis reward and blends their drift terms during inference, while DB-KLA (KL Alignment) blends a single fine-tuned model with the pre-trained model for regularization control.
- The DB-MPA-LS variant achieves multi-preference alignment by stochastically sampling LoRA adapters, reducing inference cost to that of running a single base model and one adapter.

## Results

- DB-MPA and DB-MPA-LS consistently outperformed baseline methods in achieving Pareto-optimal trade-offs across multiple reward objectives, closely matching the performance of computationally intensive oracle baselines.
- DB-MPA-LS delivered nearly identical Pareto performance to DB-MPA while matching the inference speed of the base Stable Diffusion model (e.g., **5.64** sec/image), a significant efficiency improvement over other blending or guidance methods.
- DB-KLA effectively enabled smooth and continuous control over effective KL regularization strength at inference time, yielding images quantitatively and qualitatively similar to those from specifically fine-tuned models, and demonstrating scalability to SDXL.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The backward diffusion process of a fine-tuned model can be expressed as the pre-trained model's drift plus an "additional control term" related to the reward function, allowing for targeted manipulation.
> - Linearizing the control term with a Jensen gap approximation enables the blending of drift terms from models fine-tuned on individual basis rewards or with different regularization strengths.
> - Stochastic sampling of LoRA adapters, with probabilities proportional to preference weights, can approximate deterministic blending outcomes while significantly improving inference-time efficiency, as justified by theoretical propositions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models, exemplified by architectures such as Imagen, DALL·E, and Stable Diffusion, have demonstrated considerable proficiency in generating high-fidelity images from text prompts. These models are typically pre-trained on extensive datasets to learn a general data distribution. However, this pre-training does not inherently optimize them for specific downstream objectives, such as achieving particular aesthetic qualities, ensuring precise text-image consistency, or aligning with diverse human preferences.

To bridge this gap, recent advancements have focused on aligning diffusion models with task-specific objectives, primarily through reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning. This approach involves fine-tuning a pre-trained model to maximize a defined reward function, while simultaneously employing Kullback–Leibler (KL) regularization. The KL regularization term serves a dual purpose: it mitigates reward over-optimization (commonly referred to as "reward hacking") and helps preserve desirable attributes of the original pre-trained model, such as sample diversity and overall visual fidelity.

Existing RL fine-tuning methodologies, however, typically operate under the assumption of a static, fixed reward function and a predefined regularization weight. This static configuration presents practical limitations. Real-world applications often necessitate balancing multiple, potentially conflicting objectives (e.g., maximizing both aesthetics and prompt fidelity). Furthermore, user preferences are dynamic, varying significantly across different prompts, individual users, and deployment contexts. Accommodating such variability through traditional static fine-tuning would entail retraining separate models for each unique combination of preferences and regularization strengths, a computationally intensive and resource-prohibitive endeavor. Once trained, these models offer fixed trade-offs, precluding any post-hoc adjustments. Similar challenges arise with KL regularization, where inadequate regularization can lead to reward hacking, while excessive regularization may hinder effective alignment with the desired objective. Tuning these parameters typically involves computationally costly grid searches.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a framework for *inference-time multi-preference alignment*. This approach aims to provide a flexible and efficient mechanism for adjusting alignment to user-specified preferences and regularization strengths at the point of image generation, without the need for additional fine-tuning or extensive computation. The work positions itself as a principled solution to achieve Pareto-optimal trade-offs across multiple preferences, drawing inspiration from multi-objective RL and recent advancements in multi-preference alignment in large language models (LLMs), while specifically adapting these concepts to the unique characteristics of diffusion models. It contrasts with parameter-blending methods like Rewarded Soup by blending backward diffusion trajectories and with guidance algorithms by offering a more direct manipulation of the model's fundamental generation process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a methodology for inference-time multi-preference alignment in diffusion models. Specifically, the authors pose the following problem:

"Given a set of basis reward functions (r_i)_m_i=1 and a basis KL regularization weight α, can we design a fine-tuning procedure such that when the user specifies their reward or regularization preferences through parameters w and λ at inference time, the model generates images aligned with the linear reward combination r(w) = Σ_m_i=1 w_i r_i and regularization weight α(λ) = α/λ, without requiring additional fine-tuning?"

The motivation for addressing this problem stems from several practical and computational challenges inherent in current diffusion model alignment practices:

- **Balancing Multiple Conflicting Objectives**: Real-world scenarios frequently require generative models to satisfy several objectives concurrently, which are often in tension (e.g., image aesthetic quality versus precise adherence to a text prompt). Existing RL fine-tuning typically optimizes for a single, fixed reward composite, making it difficult to explore or dynamically adjust trade-offs between objectives.
- **Dynamic User Preferences**: User tastes and priorities are not static. They can change depending on the specific image being generated, the individual user, or the broader application context. A system that can adapt to these changing preferences in real-time offers a more personalized and valuable user experience.
- **Computational Efficiency**: Training separate diffusion models for every conceivable combination of reward weights and regularization strengths is computationally prohibitive. This limitation restricts the practical deployment of highly customized generative models. The objective is to achieve flexibility without incurring this substantial retraining cost.
- **Post-Hoc Adjustment**: Once a model is fine-tuned with a fixed set of alignment parameters, its behavior is largely fixed. There is a need for post-hoc adjustability, allowing users to interactively steer the model's output without re-initiating a lengthy training process.
- **Principled Solution**: The motivation includes developing a mathematically grounded approach that offers interpretable and predictable control over alignment, rather than relying on heuristic or trial-and-error methods for parameter tuning.
- **Real-Time Application**: The ability to perform multi-preference alignment at inference time, with minimal computational overhead, is crucial for integrating these models into real-time or resource-constrained applications.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to enhance the adaptability, efficiency, and user-controllability of diffusion models, moving beyond fixed-behavior generative AI to systems that can dynamically respond to diverse and evolving user needs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed Diffusion Blend, leverages theoretical insights to achieve inference-time multi-preference alignment without additional fine-tuning. The core approach involves blending the backward diffusion processes of pre-trained and fine-tuned models.

**Theoretical Foundation:**
The work begins by framing the alignment problem as maximizing a reward function `r(x_0)` while penalizing deviation from a pre-trained model `p_pre_0` using a KL divergence term `αKL(p_0 || p_pre_0)`. The paper derives a closed-form solution for the target distribution `p_tar(x_0) = p_pre(x_0) ⋅ exp(r(x_0)/α) / Z`, where `Z` is a normalization constant. Direct sampling from `p_tar` is intractable due to the high-dimensional nature of `Z` and the implicit nature of `p_pre` in diffusion models.

To address this, the paper establishes a critical relationship between the backward diffusion process of a fine-tuned model `f^(r,α)(x_t, t)` and that of the pre-trained model `f^pre(x_t, t)`:

- **Proposition 1**: It is shown that `f^(r,α)(x_t, t) = f^pre(x_t, t) - β(t)u^(r,α)(x_t, t)`, where `u^(r,α)(x_t, t) = ∇_x_t log E[exp(r(x_0)/α) | x_t]` is an "additional control term." This proposition, derived from analyzing the original alignment objective (Eq. 3), implies that fine-tuning effectively modifies the score function of the pre-trained model.

A key challenge lies in computing `u^(r,α)`. To enable practical implementation, the authors introduce an approximation by interchanging the expectation and the exponential function (a Jensen gap approximation):

- **Approximation**: `u^(r,α)(x, t) ≈ ¯u^(r,α)(x, t) = ∇_x E[r(x_0)/α | x_t=x]`. This linearizes the control term with respect to the reward function.
- **Lemma 1**: Quantifies the error `∆^(r,α)(x, t)` introduced by this approximation. The error bound depends on factors such as the local Lipschitz sensitivity of the stochastic reward component, the conditional coefficient of variation of `exp(r(x_0)/α)`, and the deviation of `p(x_0 | x_t)` from a pure shift family. The lemma indicates that the approximation is more accurate when the regularization coefficient `α` is not too small and when the reward function is more predictable from the noisy image `x_t`.

Leveraging this approximation, the authors derive key linearity properties for blending:

- **Lemma 2**: Demonstrates that the drift term for a linear combination of rewards `r(w) = Σ w_i r_i` can be approximated by a linear combination of the drifts from models fine-tuned on individual basis rewards: `f^(r(w),α)(x_t, t) ≈ Σ w_i f^(r_i,α)(x_t, t)`. Similarly, the drift for a scaled regularization weight `α(λ) = α/λ` can be approximated by blending the pre-trained and single-reward fine-tuned drifts: `f^(r,α(λ))(x_t, t) ≈ (1 - λ)f^pre(x_t, t) + λf^(r,α)(x_t, t)`. These approximations form the basis for the Diffusion Blend algorithms.

**Diffusion Blend Algorithms:**

1. **DB-MPA (Diffusion Blend-Multi-Preference Alignment)**:
 - **Fine-tuning**: For each basis reward `r_i`, a separate pre-trained model is fine-tuned using RL with a fixed regularization weight `α`, resulting in `m` fine-tuned models (`θ_rl_i`).
 - **Inference**: Given user-specified preference weights `w = (w_1..., w_m)`, the backward diffusion process is run using a blended drift term `f_mix = Σ w_i f^(r_i,α)(x_t, t)`. This involves evaluating all `m` fine-tuned models at each denoising step.

2. **DB-KLA (Diffusion Blend-KL Alignment)**:
 - **Fine-tuning**: A single pre-trained model is fine-tuned for a specific reward `r` and basis regularization weight `α`, yielding one fine-tuned model `θ_rl`.
 - **Inference**: Given a user-specified regularization modification factor `λ`, the backward diffusion process uses a blended drift term `f_KL = λf^(r,α)(x_t, t) + (1 - λ)f^pre(x_t, t)`. This blends the fine-tuned model's drift with that of the original pre-trained model.

**Inference-Time Efficient Variant: DB-MPA-LS (Diffusion Blend-Multi-Preference Alignment-LoRA Sampling)**:
To mitigate the `m×` computational overhead of DB-MPA, DB-MPA-LS is introduced.

- **Mechanism**: Instead of deterministically blending the drifts from all `m` fine-tuned models, DB-MPA-LS randomly samples one of the LoRA adapters (corresponding to a fine-tuned model) at each denoising step. The probability of sampling each adapter `i` is proportional to its assigned weight `w_i`.
- **Proposition 2**: Provides theoretical justification for this sampling approach. It states that for Lipschitz continuous drift functions, an SDE with a deterministically blended drift and an SDE with a stochastically sampled drift (where the sampling probabilities match the blending weights) yield the same marginal probability distributions over time. This implies that the sampling approach can approximate the blending approach while maintaining the desired distribution.
- **Benefit**: This reduces the inference cost to that of running a single model (the base Stable Diffusion model plus one LoRA adapter), thereby eliminating the multiplicative overhead.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental evaluations to demonstrate the performance of Diffusion Blend algorithms against relevant baselines.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Base Model**: Stable Diffusion v1.5 (SDv1.5) was primarily used, with an extension to Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL) for scalability validation.
- **RL Fine-tuning**: The DPOK algorithm (Fan et al., 2023) was used for RL fine-tuning.
- **Reward Models**:
 - ImageReward (text-image alignment)
 - VILA (aesthetic quality)
 - PickScore (human preferences)
 - JPEG compressibility (an adversarial reward, favoring smooth images over aesthetics)
- **Baselines**: Rewarded Soup (RS), CoDe (gradient-free guidance), Reward Gradient Guidance (RGG), and Multi-Objective RL (MORL), with MORL serving as an oracle baseline representing the theoretical optimum achievable by individual fine-tuning.
- **Prompt Datasets**: Short-DrawBench (25 color-object prompts for training, 1000 GPT-4 generated test prompts for generalization) and GenEval (550 compositional prompts for training, 700 additional test prompts).
- **Metrics**: Quantitative comparisons of reward scores, weighted rewards, Pareto fronts, and inference times. Qualitative visual comparisons of generated images.

### 5. Main Findings (DB-MPA and DB-MPA-LS)

- **Superior Performance on Multi-Reward Alignment**: Both DB-MPA and DB-MPA-LS consistently outperformed all baselines (RS, CoDe, RGG) across various preference weights and on both Short-DrawBench and GenEval test sets. They achieved Pareto fronts that were notably close to the MORL oracle, indicating highly effective trade-offs between conflicting objectives like text-image alignment and aesthetics.
- **Efficiency of DB-MPA-LS**: While DB-MPA incurred approximately 2x inference time compared to standard Stable Diffusion (due to evaluating two models), DB-MPA-LS achieved nearly identical Pareto performance to DB-MPA while matching the inference speed of the original Stable Diffusion model (e.g., 5.64 sec/image for DB-MPA-LS vs. 5.46 sec/image for SD, compared to 11.11 sec/image for DB-MPA, 185.26 sec/image for CoDe, and 121.58 sec/image for RGG). This makes DB-MPA-LS particularly appealing for practical applications.
- **Robustness to Conflicting Rewards**: In experiments involving a conflicting JPEG compressibility reward and VILA aesthetics, DB-MPA maintained superior performance compared to RS and CoDe, demonstrating its ability to balance strongly opposing objectives effectively.
- **Scalability with Number of Rewards**: The performance gains of DB-MPA and DB-MPA-LS remained stable as the number of reward models increased from two to four, in contrast to the significant performance degradation observed with Rewarded Soup.
- **Scalability to Larger Models (SDXL)**: The positive trends and superior performance of Diffusion Blend algorithms were observed to extend to the much larger Stable Diffusion XL backbone, suggesting broad applicability.
- **Qualitative Results**: Visual comparisons illustrated that DB-MPA and DB-MPA-LS generated images of higher visual quality and better alignment with user-specified preferences than other baselines. They exhibited smooth and continuous control over image characteristics as preference weights were adjusted, closely matching the outputs of individually fine-tuned MORL models.

### 6. Main Findings (DB-KLA)

- **Continuous KL Regularization Control**: DB-KLA effectively enabled smooth and continuous control over the effective KL regularization strength (`α/λ`) at inference time, without requiring additional fine-tuning for each `λ` value.
- **Alignment Quality**: Images generated by DB-KLA were visually similar and quantitatively comparable in reward scores to those produced by `λ`-specific MORL fine-tuned models.
- **Diagnostic Potential**: The ability to vary `λ` allows for exploration of the regularization landscape, potentially serving as a diagnostic tool to identify and mitigate issues of under- or over-optimization in reward-guided fine-tuning.

### 7. Significance and Potential Impact

The Diffusion Blend framework represents a notable advancement in the field of generative AI, specifically addressing the critical need for flexible and efficient alignment of diffusion models with diverse user preferences and dynamic objectives.

**Key Contributions:**
- **Inference-Time Multi-Preference Alignment**: The primary contribution is the introduction of a framework that enables users to dynamically specify combinations of reward objectives and regularization strengths at inference time, without necessitating retraining or additional fine-tuning. This fundamentally enhances the adaptability and user-centricity of diffusion models.
- **Theoretical Grounding**: The paper provides theoretical underpinnings, including Proposition 1, which elucidates the relationship between fine-tuned and pre-trained diffusion processes, and Lemma 2, which demonstrates the linearity of drift terms under approximation. Proposition 2 further provides a theoretical justification for the efficient sampling-based approximation. These theoretical results offer a principled basis for the proposed blending approach, distinguishing it from purely heuristic methods.
- **Novel Algorithms**: The development of DB-MPA, DB-KLA, and particularly DB-MPA-LS, offers practical and effective solutions to the problem. DB-MPA and DB-KLA provide direct blending mechanisms for multiple rewards and KL regularization, respectively.
- **Computational Efficiency**: The DB-MPA-LS algorithm is a significant advancement as it overcomes the linear scaling of inference time typically associated with multi-model blending, making multi-preference alignment computationally viable for real-time and large-scale applications. It achieves performance comparable to DB-MPA while matching the inference speed of the base pre-trained model.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced User Experience and Personalization**: The ability to steer image generation according to evolving user preferences—balancing aesthetics, prompt fidelity, and other factors—can lead to more personalized, relevant, and satisfactory outputs. This enables generative AI to move beyond a "one-size-fits-all" approach.
- **Reduced Development and Deployment Costs**: By eliminating the need for retraining or extensive grid searches for each new preference configuration, Diffusion Blend significantly lowers the computational and time costs associated with developing and deploying preference-aligned diffusion models.
- **Wider Applicability of Diffusion Models**: The framework makes diffusion models more versatile for applications where dynamic control over generation characteristics is essential, such as interactive design tools, creative content generation, and personalized media synthesis.
- **Improved Understanding of Model Behavior**: The ability to smoothly vary regularization strength via DB-KLA could serve as a diagnostic tool, providing insights into how models learn to align with rewards and identifying potential under- or over-optimization.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: This work sets the stage for further exploration into inference-time control mechanisms for generative models, potentially inspiring similar approaches for other generative architectures and modalities.

The authors do not foresee any immediate societal concerns that require specific highlighting, indicating that the enhanced control and performance offered by Diffusion Blend are primarily beneficial for advancing generative AI capabilities.
