# PDDL-ART: Autonomous Symbolic Abstraction From Demonstration For Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation Using Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PDDL-ART is a VLM framework that autonomously generates task-specific PDDL domain and problem files from a single expert demonstration, a natural language task description, and an action-name library, no templates, action signatures, or fine-tuning. A multi-stage syntactic, semantic, and execution-level correction pipeline, including tool-grounded predicate evaluation, yields 93.3% demo-aligned success versus 78.3% for a VLM planner baseline.

## Problem

- Hand-crafted PDDL abstractions require domain expertise, are time-consuming, and must be manually revised whenever task, object, environment, or robot capabilities change.
- Learned relational abstractions from demonstrations reduce manual specification but are hard to inspect or revise, obscuring whether planning failures stem from predicates, grounding, or sequencing.
- Prior VLM/LLM formalizer methods require ground-truth action signatures, assume a provided domain file, or need domain-specific fine-tuning for spatial reasoning, limiting generality.

## Method

- **PDDLGenVLM** generates ==PDDL== domain and problem files from a demonstration, task description, and action library, then iteratively corrects them via syntactic parsing, planning-feasibility checks, and initial-state and plan-semantic cross-validation.
- ==Execution-guided correction== stage runs the generated plan on the robot, using ==DTW subsequence matching== on end-effector trajectories to localize demonstration keyframes corresponding to each executed action.
- **PredicateEvalVLM** cross-validates action preconditions and effects using a Proximity Inspection Tool and a Temporal Tool, autonomously invoked for geometric and temporal reasoning beyond raw visual perception.

## Results

- PDDL-ART with GPT-5.5 achieves **95%** average planning feasibility and **93.3%** plan alignment across six tasks over 10 trials, versus **78.3%** alignment for the best CoT baseline.
- Ablations show stage-1-only correction reaches **96.67%** feasibility but just **76.67%** alignment, confirming execution-guided predicate evaluation in stage-2 is needed for demo-aligned plans.
- NL2PLAN baseline scores only **46.67%** feasibility and **6.67%** alignment, showing iterative text-only correction without visual grounding fails on complex long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding symbolic predicate evaluation in tool-invoked geometric and temporal reasoning, not raw vision alone, is necessary when relational or memory-dependent predicates are not visually discernible.
> - Separating correction into pre-execution (syntax/feasibility/semantics) and post-execution (demo cross-validation) stages catches distinct error classes; syntactic validity alone does not guarantee task alignment.
> - Autonomous corrective pipelines can still degrade previously valid PDDL components during later correction rounds, so minimal, targeted edits matter more than unconstrained regeneration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks necessitate sophisticated reasoning about actions, objects, and their causal relationships. Symbolic planning, particularly using formalisms like PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language), provides a structured framework for addressing such problems by synthesizing action sequences with guarantees of soundness and completeness. However, a significant challenge in deploying these methods is the manual construction of PDDL domain and problem descriptions. This process typically requires substantial domain expertise, is time-consuming, and necessitates repeated modifications when the task, objects, environment, or robot capabilities change.

Previous research efforts have attempted to mitigate this bottleneck by learning relational symbolic abstractions from robot data, such as demonstrations, kinematic trajectories, and interaction data. While these approaches reduce manual specification, the resulting learned abstractions can be difficult to inspect and revise, making error diagnosis in complex, long-horizon tasks challenging.

More recently, there has been increasing interest in leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for generating planning descriptions from natural language and visual inputs [3–7]. These models can propose predicates, action descriptions, and formalisms without requiring task-specific fine-tuning. However, generating PDDL descriptions solely based on these models without explicit interaction with the environment and downstream feasibility checks can lead to error-prone outputs. The generated descriptions must not only be syntactically correct but also define a feasible planning problem, infer initial and goal states aligned with the demonstration, and produce plans executable by a robot.

This work specifically addresses the problem of generating PDDL-based symbolic abstractions from a single expert demonstration and a natural language task description. It situates itself within the "Foundation Models as Formalizers" research direction, where models generate structured formal representations for classical planners to operate on [8, 15–18]. While other approaches in this area exist (e.g., LLM + P for problem files, NL2PLAN for domain/problem but requiring ground-truth action signatures, UniDomain pretraining generalized domains), PDDL-ART distinguishes itself by focusing on autonomous generation of *both* domain and problem files from minimal inputs (single demo, NL, action names) *without* domain templates, action signatures, fine-tuning, or candidate predicates. The most closely related work, VLMFP, also uses execution-guided correction for PDDL generation but requires fine-tuning a VLM on domain-specific data for spatial reasoning. PDDL-ART aims to overcome this by embedding geometric and temporal reasoning directly through a tool-use mechanism, thus enhancing applicability to unseen domains without specialized data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that autonomously generates task-specific PDDL domain and problem descriptions for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks, thereby addressing the significant bottleneck of manual PDDL construction.

The specific objectives are:
1. **Autonomous PDDL Generation from Minimal Inputs:** To create a VLM-based framework, named PDDL-ART, capable of generating comprehensive PDDL domain and problem files. The framework is designed to accept a single expert demonstration, a natural language task description, and a library of high-level action names as inputs. Crucially, it aims to achieve this without requiring manually provided domain templates, ground-truth action signatures, candidate predicates, or domain-specific fine-tuning of the VLM. PDDL-ART is responsible for inferring predicates, selecting actions, defining action schemas (including parameter types, arity, preconditions, and effects), and specifying initial and goal conditions.
2. **Systematic Validation and Correction:** To ensure the generated PDDL descriptions are not only syntactically valid but also semantically aligned with the demonstrated task, the framework incorporates a multi-stage validation and correction pipeline. This pipeline systematically checks for syntactic correctness, planning feasibility, consistency between the inferred initial state and visual observations, and semantic alignment between the generated plan and the task description.
3. **Enhanced Predicate Grounding with Tool Use:** To overcome limitations of relying solely on visual observations for predicate evaluation, PDDL-ART aims to leverage the tool-use capabilities of modern VLMs. This objective is particularly motivated by complex manipulation problems where predicates may involve relational, geometric, or abstract symbolic information (e.g., temporal dependencies or internal states not immediately visible). The framework is designed for the VLM to autonomously determine when to invoke external tools (such as proximity inspection or temporal tracking tools), how to infer their input arguments and thresholds, and how to interpret their outputs to accurately evaluate symbolic predicates. This allows for grounding predicates that are not directly discernible from images alone.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges in current robotic planning:
- **Manual Effort and Expertise:** The manual creation of PDDL models is resource-intensive and requires specialized knowledge, limiting the scalability and adaptability of symbolic planning systems.
- **Difficulty in Error Diagnosis:** When planning fails with learned abstractions, diagnosing whether the error originates from incorrect predicates, grounding, or action sequencing is difficult, especially in long-horizon tasks.
- **VLM Limitations:** While VLMs offer powerful reasoning capabilities, their direct application to generating PDDL without environmental interaction or rigorous validation is prone to errors, potentially leading to invalid PDDL, infeasible plans, or plans misaligned with the intended task.
- **Abstract Reasoning Needs:** Robotic tasks often involve abstract concepts, internal states, or temporal sequences that cannot be directly inferred from instantaneous visual data. A mechanism to integrate geometric and temporal reasoning is therefore crucial for accurate symbolic abstraction.

By addressing these points, the research aims to bridge the gap between high-level task understanding provided by VLMs and the precise, executable nature of symbolic planning, making long-horizon robot manipulation more accessible and robust.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PDDL-ART is a VLM-based framework designed to autonomously generate and validate PDDL domain and problem descriptions. Its methodology centers on a multi-stage correction and systematic validation pipeline, employing two complementary VLM components: PDDLGenVLM and PredicateEvalVLM.

**Inputs:**
The framework takes the following inputs:
- **Expert Demonstration (D):** A state trajectory, `ζ = {st}` containing robot end-effector poses, gripper states, and poses of task-relevant objects, accompanied by a sequence of RGB images `V = {vt}`. For long tasks, a subset of keyframes `I` from `V` is extracted using a proximity-based heuristic to represent important state transitions, reducing VLM context length.
- **Natural Language Task Description (d):** A summary detailing the overall task, initial robot state, task-relevant objects (`O_task`), and success criteria.
- **High-level Action Library (A):** A vocabulary of action names available to the robot, without predefined formal properties like parameter types or effects.

**Overall Pipeline:**
The PDDL-ART framework operates in two main stages, preceded by an initial generation step:

1. **Initial Generation:**
 PDDLGenVLM receives the demonstration `D`, task description `d`, action library `A`, and object list `O_task` to generate an initial set of PDDL domain (`D`) and problem (`P`) files.

2. **Stage 1: Syntactic and Semantic Correction (Pre-execution Validation)**
 This stage iteratively refines the generated PDDL files without robot execution. If any check fails, the error message is fed back to PDDLGenVLM, which is prompted to make minimal, targeted modifications.
 - **Syntactic Check:** Utilizes an off-the-shelf PDDL parser (VAL) to verify adherence to PDDL grammar, such as declared predicates and type consistency. Parser errors guide PDDLGenVLM corrections.
 - **Planning Feasibility Check:** The current `⟨D, P⟩` files are submitted to a classical planner (Fast Downward).
 - **Planning Failure (⊥):** Indicates issues within the domain or problem that prevent a plan from being found. The planner's output is returned to PDDLGenVLM for correction.
 - **Empty Plans:** Occurs if the goal is trivially satisfied in the initial state, pointing to incorrectly specified initial or goal conditions. This feedback is also used for correction.
 - **Initial State Cross-Check:** PDDLGenVLM is prompted with the initial demonstration frame (`v0`) and the generated problem file. It identifies mismatches between the visual scene and the declared initial state, feeding inconsistencies back for correction.
 - **Plan-Guided Semantic Check:** The VLM (PDDLGenVLM) is given the plan `πk` generated from the current PDDL files and the natural language task description `d`. It identifies misalignments between the plan and the intended task, providing feedback for a final round of corrections in this stage.

3. **Stage 2: Execution-Guided Correction (Post-execution Validation and Predicate Evaluation)**
 If Stage 1 is successful, the generated plan `πk` is executed. This stage involves physical execution and rigorous cross-validation against the expert demonstration, particularly focusing on predicate grounding.
 - **Robot Execution:** The plan `πk` is executed on the robot. A refinement function `Λ` maps high-level actions to robot configurations.
 - **Execution Failure:** If an action `aj` fails during physical execution (e.g., physically infeasible action), the failure message, successful prefix, current `⟨D, P⟩` files, and the full plan are provided to PDDLGenVLM to identify the cause and propose minimal PDDL corrections. A new plan is then generated and executed.
 - **Predicate Evaluation-Guided Correction (if no execution failure):**
 - **Keyframe Detection via Relational Trajectory Matching:** For each executed action `aj`, its execution trajectory (robot end-effector relative to the primary object) is compared to the full expert demonstration trajectory using Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) Subsequence Matching. This identifies the corresponding segment `[a, b]` in the demonstration, providing keyframes (`va`, `vb`) representing the world state before and after the action in the demo.
 - **Predicate Evaluation by PredicateEvalVLM:** PredicateEvalVLM independently evaluates each predicate from the preconditions `pre(aj)` and effects `eff(aj)` on both the demonstration keyframes (`va`, `vb`) and the execution keyframes.
 - **Tool-Use Capabilities:** To handle predicates not directly discernible from vision (e.g., relational, geometric, abstract symbolic, or memory-dependent states), PredicateEvalVLM leverages VLM tool-use. The model autonomously determines when to invoke these tools, which tool is appropriate, and how to infer their input parameters and interpret outputs:
 - **Proximity Inspection Tool:** Evaluates geometric and relational properties (e.g., `IsHolding(gripper, nut)`), with the VLM inferring appropriate thresholds.
 - **Temporal Tool:** Retrieves the most recent truth value of a logical state from a history, critical for tasks requiring memory or tracking states over time (e.g., `IsOpenedOnce cap`).
 - **Cross-Validation and Correction:** The results of predicate evaluations from both demo and execution instances are cross-validated. If a mismatch is detected, PredicateEvalVLM provides a brief reason. This information, along with the action `aj`, specific predicates involved in the mismatch, and the current `⟨D, P⟩` files, is fed back to PDDLGenVLM for a final correction round. If no mismatches are found across all actions, the current PDDL files are deemed validated.

The framework's output is the systematically validated PDDL domain and problem descriptions, along with the generated plan. This iterative and feedback-driven approach aims to produce PDDL descriptions that are empirically validated against the demonstrated task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The PDDL-ART framework was evaluated on six task scenarios, comprising two household tasks and four engine maintenance tasks, with task complexity ranging from 2 to 12 objects and 5 to 37 actions. The tasks were specifically chosen to require abstract predicates, memory, or involve goal states that are visually indistinguishable from initial states (e.g., replacing an oil cap after an oil change). Two VLM models, GPT-O3 and GPT-5.5 (both June 2026 checkpoints), were utilized.

The evaluation employed three metrics across 10 trials per task:
1. **Planning Feasibility (F):** The percentage of trials where a syntactically valid PDDL description leads to a non-trivial plan. Trivial plans (0-length, goal already satisfied) are noted.
2. **Number of Trivial Plans (T):** Counts cases where the goal state is incorrectly modeled as being met in the initial state.
3. **Plan Alignment (C):** The percentage of executed plans that are semantically consistent with the expert demonstration. This metric is for evaluation only and is not used in PDDL-ART's correction stages.

**Overall Performance (Table II):**
- **PDDL-ART with GPT-5.5** achieved an average planning feasibility (F) of 95% and an average plan alignment (C) of 93.3% across all tasks. It consistently produced 0 trivial plans.
- **PDDL-ART with GPT-O3** demonstrated an average planning feasibility of 91.62% and an average plan alignment of 80%, also with 0 trivial plans on average.

**Comparison with Baselines:**
- **Direct VLM-PLAN and VLM-PLAN (CoT):** These baselines, which directly generate action sequences, performed well on simpler tasks (e.g., 100% F/C for "Check Oil Levels" and "Cap Manipulation"). However, their performance decreased with increasing task complexity. The best VLM-PLAN (CoT with GPT-5.5) achieved an average F of 91.67% and C of 78.33%, which is lower than the full PDDL-ART framework on complex tasks.
- **NL2PLAN:** This iterative LLM-based approach performed significantly lower, with an average F of 46.67% and C of 6.67% for GPT-O3. This suggests that the visual information and execution-guided verification integrated into PDDL-ART are crucial for generating semantically correct and aligned PDDL descriptions, especially when compared to text-only reasoning.

**Ablation Analysis:**
An ablation study was conducted to assess the contribution of each stage of PDDL-ART:
- **Zero-shot PDDL Generation (without any corrections):** Achieved high success for compilation and feasibility on easy tasks but struggled with complex scenarios. For instance, PDDL-ART (GPT-O3) zero-shot showed an average F of 56.83% and C of 28.33%. This indicates that initial VLM output, while promising for simple cases, is insufficient for complex tasks without refinement.
- **PDDL-ART with Stage 1 Corrections only:** This configuration showed consistent improvement in compilation success across all tasks compared to zero-shot generation (e.g., PDDL-ART (GPT-O3) w/ stage-1 averaged 88.33% F). However, the plan alignment (C) did not improve as proportionally (41.67% C average), suggesting that syntactic and semantic validity checks alone do not guarantee plan executability or alignment with the demonstration's nuances.
- **Full PDDL-ART Framework (Stage 1 + Stage 2):** The integration of both stages, particularly the execution-guided correction in Stage 2, led to substantial improvements in both planning feasibility and, crucially, plan alignment. This configuration consistently achieved high success rates for both metrics across all tasks, demonstrating the efficacy of systematically validating and rectifying PDDL descriptions based on execution feedback and tool-augmented predicate evaluation. The results for "Cooking Prep II" illustrate this, where Stage 1 alone for GPT-O3 could make the problem infeasible, but the full PDDL-ART framework recovered robustly.

These findings indicate that while VLMs can initiate PDDL generation, the proposed multi-stage, execution-guided correction pipeline, augmented with geometric and temporal reasoning tools, is critical for achieving high rates of feasible and demonstration-aligned plans in long-horizon robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PDDL-ART framework represents an advance in the autonomous generation of symbolic planning abstractions for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Reduced Reliance on Domain Expertise:** PDDL-ART addresses the traditional bottleneck of manually constructing PDDL domain and problem descriptions, a process that historically demands significant domain expertise. By generating these descriptions autonomously from a single expert demonstration, a natural language task description, and a high-level action library, the framework lowers the barrier to entry for utilizing classical planning in robotics. This could democratize the application of symbolic AI in diverse robotic scenarios.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Semantic Alignment:** The multi-stage correction pipeline (syntactic, semantic, and execution-level) significantly improves the robustness and reliability of the generated PDDL. By systematically validating the descriptions against PDDL grammar, planning feasibility, initial state consistency, and semantic alignment with the task demonstration and execution, PDDL-ART produces PDDL that is not only formally correct but also genuinely representative of the intended task and executable on a robot. The ablation study confirms that the execution-guided stage is critical for achieving high plan alignment.

3. **Novel Predicate Grounding through Tool Use:** A notable contribution is the integration of tool-use capabilities within VLMs for symbolic predicate grounding. By enabling the PredicateEvalVLM to autonomously determine when to invoke geometric (proximity inspection) and temporal reasoning tools, the framework can evaluate complex relational predicates that are not directly discernible from static visual observations alone. This capability allows the system to handle tasks requiring abstract predicate inference, memory, or subtle state changes that were previously challenging for purely vision-based approaches. This expands the practical applicability of symbolic planning to a wider range of nuanced robotic tasks.

4. **Demonstrated Performance on Challenging Tasks:** The evaluation on diverse and challenging manipulation tasks, including engine maintenance and household scenarios, with requirements for memory and abstract predicate inference, validates the framework's effectiveness. Achieving high success rates (e.g., 93.3% plan alignment with GPT-5.5) on tasks involving numerous objects and actions demonstrates the framework's capability to generalize to non-trivial problems without domain-specific fine-tuning.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Task Deployment:** By automating PDDL generation, PDDL-ART could significantly reduce the time and effort required to program robots for new, complex manipulation tasks, accelerating the deployment of autonomous systems in industrial, service, and domestic environments.
- **More Adaptable and Flexible Robots:** The ability to generate task-specific PDDL from demonstrations could enable robots to adapt more quickly to variations in tasks or environments with minimal human intervention, fostering greater flexibility in robotic applications.
- **Foundation for Advanced Planning:** While the current framework focuses on fully observable, deterministic planning, it lays groundwork for future extensions to more complex planning paradigms (e.g., conditional, temporal, numeric planning).
- **Bridging AI Modalities:** The work effectively bridges the gap between high-level understanding from vision-language models and the rigorous logical reasoning of classical planners, showcasing a synergistic approach to AI for robotics.

**Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge that the current framework generates domain descriptions scoped to the provided demonstration, rather than general reusable domains. Expanding PDDL-ART to generate reusable domains across broader task instances is identified as a key direction for future work. Additionally, the framework currently assumes fully observable and deterministic planning, without supporting conditional, temporal, or numeric planning constructs, which are natural next steps for development. A potential limitation also lies in the VLM-guided predicate evaluation, which may be susceptible to hallucinations or visual noise, potentially leading to incorrect corrections or introducing errors. Addressing these aspects would further enhance the robustness and generality of the framework.
