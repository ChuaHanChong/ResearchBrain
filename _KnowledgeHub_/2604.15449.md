# Iterated Invariant EKF for Quadruped Robot Odometry

> [!summary] Summary
> An Iterated Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IterIEKF) has been developed for proprioceptive-only odometry in quadruped robots, demonstrating improved accuracy, statistical consistency, and faster convergence. This method achieved up to a 63% reduction in base velocity error and a 60% reduction in gravity direction error in simulations, alongside a 12% reduction in pitch angle estimation error in real-world scenarios compared to prior EKF variants.

## Problem

- Legged robot state estimation is challenging due to highly nonlinear dynamics and measurement models, often leading to statistical inconsistency in traditional Extended Kalman Filters (EKF).
- Existing Invariant EKF (IEKF) methods address nonlinear dynamics but typically rely on linear approximations for measurement updates, limiting accuracy for nonlinear measurement functions.
- The practical effectiveness of combining invariant filtering with iterative updates (IterIEKF) in hybrid contact-aided legged systems, where strict invariance might be partially violated, was not thoroughly evaluated.

## Method

- The research derives and implements an **Iterated Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IterIEKF)** based on the ==SE2(3) Lie group==, leveraging its structure for efficient propagation of robot pose and velocity.
- The filter utilizes solely proprioceptive measurements: IMU data for prediction and ==kinematic constraints of feet in contact== (zero velocity in the world frame) for the measurement update.
- An iterative ==Gauss-Newton scheme== is employed in the update step to find a ==Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimate== by repeatedly re-linearizing the nonlinear measurement function, enhancing accuracy and consistency.

## Results

- IterIEKF demonstrated significantly faster convergence, reducing the time to achieve the same error level by up to **2.5 times** in simulated large initial error scenarios and by **40-60%** in Monte Carlo simulations.
- In Monte Carlo simulations, IterIEKF achieved up to a **63%** reduction in base velocity error and a **60%** reduction in gravity direction error compared to the IEKF.
- On a real-world dataset, IterIEKF reduced the pitch angle estimation error by **12%** and exhibited superior statistical consistency (NEES values closer to ideal) across various observable states compared to other EKF variants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining invariant error dynamics with iterative refinement substantially improves state estimation accuracy and convergence speed, especially under large initial errors or when measurement noise aligns with filter assumptions.
> - Invariance is crucial for maintaining statistical consistency, preventing the overconfident estimates and false observability issues observed in non-invariant iterated filters.
> - The benefits of iterated filtering are most pronounced when measurement noise characteristics are well-modeled and the measurement covariance is relatively small; large or incorrect covariances can diminish these advantages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

State estimation is a fundamental challenge for mobile robots, particularly for legged systems operating in complex environments. Traditional Kalman filter-based algorithms, such as the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), offer computational efficiency but rely on assumptions of linear system dynamics and measurement models, coupled with Gaussian noise distributions. These assumptions are frequently violated in real-world robotic applications, where dynamics are inherently nonlinear and measurements can exhibit complex characteristics.

Previous research has extended the EKF to handle nonlinear dynamics through the Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF). The IEKF leverages the structure of Lie groups to model system dynamics that are group-affine, demonstrating improved statistical consistency and reduced sensitivity to linearization errors compared to the standard EKF. While the IEKF addresses the nonlinearity of the system model, the assumption of a linear measurement model often persists.

More recently, the Iterated Extended Kalman Filter (IterEKF) has been developed to address nonlinear measurement models by computing a maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate through iterative re-linearization. This approach has been extended to Lie groups (IterSO(3)-EKF) and, most recently, to incorporate invariant observer theory in the Iterated Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IterIEKF) by Goffin et al.. The IterIEKF aims to combine the benefits of invariant filtering for nonlinear dynamics with iterative updates for nonlinear measurements, demonstrating properties more compatible with the linear Kalman filter under ideal conditions.

However, the interaction between invariance and iteration in hybrid contact-aided legged systems remains an area of investigation. Legged locomotion introduces challenges such as hybrid contact switching and measurements (e.g., base-frame velocity) that may partially violate strict invariance assumptions. It has been unclear whether iterative refinement can effectively compensate for these structural inconsistencies or if it might amplify incorrect observability modeling. This work positions itself within this landscape by providing a systematic evaluation of the IterIEKF in the context of quadruped robot odometry, directly addressing these questions and assessing its performance against existing EKF, IEKF, and IterSO(3)-EKF variants under various conditions. The study specifically focuses on proprioceptive-only sensing to ensure robustness to external environmental conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and systematically evaluate a novel, open-source state estimation algorithm for quadruped robots, based on the Iterated Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IterIEKF). This algorithm relies solely on proprioceptive measurements to ensure robustness in diverse environmental conditions.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in state estimation for legged robots:
- **Nonlinearity and Consistency:** Legged robot dynamics are highly nonlinear, and traditional EKF-based methods often suffer from statistical inconsistency due to linearization approximations and a mismatch between the assumed error model and the true system symmetries. The IEKF partially addresses this for dynamics, but the measurement update step still typically relies on linearization.
- **Limitations of Existing Filters:** While IEKF improves consistency for dynamics, its update step still approximates the posterior distribution with a Gaussian whose mean may not be the most meaningful estimate (e.g., the mode). Iterated EKF variants attempt to find the mode (MAP estimate) by repeatedly linearizing the measurement function, but their compatibility with invariant error dynamics on Lie groups, especially in practical legged systems, requires further investigation.
- **Interaction of Invariance and Iteration:** The recently proposed IterIEKF theoretically combines invariant filtering with iterative updates for nonlinear measurement models. However, its practical performance in hybrid contact-aided legged systems, where contact events and velocity-based measurements may partially compromise strict invariance, has not been thoroughly evaluated. It is crucial to determine if iteration can compensate for these departures from ideal invariance or if it exacerbates existing issues.
- **Proprioceptive-Only Robustness:** The objective is to develop a state estimator that is inherently robust to external environmental conditions by relying exclusively on internal robot sensors (proprioception). This eliminates the dependency on external sensors that can be affected by factors like lighting, precipitation, or feature scarcity.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Kalman filter-based approaches are chosen for their computational efficiency, which is critical for real-time locomotion control.

The key contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
- Derivation and presentation of invariant and non-invariant iterated filtering formulations for contact-aided quadruped state estimation, specifically for IterIEKF and IterSO(3)-EKF, accounting for foot contact velocity measurements expressed in the base frame.
- A systematic study on the impact of iterative invariant updates on the accuracy and statistical consistency of legged robot state estimation.
- Demonstration, through Monte Carlo simulations, that IterIEKF improves NEES consistency and accuracy, with reported reductions of up to 60% in base velocity and gravity direction error. In real-world data, a reduction of approximately 12% in pitch angle estimation error is observed.
- Evidence that combining invariance with iterative refinement reduces convergence time under large initialization errors and measurement uncertainty, showing a reduction of up to 60% in convergence time for IterIEKF to reach the same error level as IEKF in some experiments.
- Provision of an open-source framework, including simulation tools, to facilitate reproducibility and further research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research develops and evaluates an Iterated Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IterIEKF) for quadruped robot odometry, comparing its performance against the conventional Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF), the SO(3)-based Extended Kalman Filter (SO(3)-EKF), and its iterated variant (IterSO(3)-EKF).

**Robot Model and State Representation:**
The robot is modeled as a Single Rigid Body, or a floating body. The state to be estimated includes the robot's orientation ($R \in SO(3)$), body velocity ($v \in \mathbb{R}^3$), and centroid position ($p \in \mathbb{R}^3$), all relative to an inertial frame.
- **IEKF and IterIEKF:** The state is embedded within the $SE_2(3)$ Lie group, represented as a $5 \times 5$ matrix $X = (R, v, p)$. A right-invariant error formulation is adopted, meaning the error is defined as $X \bar{X}^{-1}$, where $X$ is the true state and $\bar{X}$ is the nominal state. This aligns with the mathematical framework that simplifies IMU dynamics and error propagation.
- **SO(3)-EKF and IterSO(3)-EKF:** The state is represented as a tuple $\{R, v, p\} \in SO(3) \times \mathbb{R}^6$. A right error-state formulation is used, where errors are defined in the tangent space, e.g., $\delta\phi = \text{Log}_{SO(3)}(R\bar{R}^T)$ for rotation error and linear differences for velocity and position errors.

**Measurement Model:**
The filter relies exclusively on proprioceptive measurements:
- **Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU):** Provides angular velocity ($\tilde{\omega}_I$) and linear acceleration ($\tilde{a}_I$), assumed to be corrupted by white Gaussian noise. IMU biases are neglected for simplicity in this work.
- **Foot Velocity in Contact:** This is the primary measurement for the update step. It exploits the kinematic constraint that feet in contact with the ground have zero velocity in the world frame (static contact assumption). The base velocity ($\tilde{v}_{B,i}$) is estimated by averaging the negative of the individual foot velocities (derived from forward kinematics and joint encoders) for all feet in contact (Equation 17). This measurement is then transformed from the base frame to the IMU frame.
- **Rationale for Velocity-only Measurements:** The authors restrict the measurement model to foot velocity, excluding foot position measurements. This is justified by the sensitivity of iterated filtering techniques to covariance mischaracterization. Foot position measurements, unlike base velocity, can be affected by complex, state-dependent errors (e.g., joint compliance, kinematic parameter errors), which are challenging to model accurately with a fixed Gaussian covariance, potentially degrading iterated filter performance.

**Prediction Step:**
- **IEKF and IterIEKF:** The prediction step propagates the state $X$ using the IMU measurements, derived from the "natural frame dynamics" on $SE_2(3)$ (Equation 21). This formulation results in an autonomous error dynamic for the right-invariant error. The error covariance $P$ is propagated using a linearized model that incorporates the Adjoint map of the Lie group (Equation 25).
- **SO(3)-EKF and IterSO(3)-EKF:** The prediction step integrates the nominal state $\{R, v, p\}$ using IMU measurements. The error covariance $\Sigma$ is propagated using a linearized error-state model, which describes the evolution of $\delta x = \{\delta\phi, \delta v, \delta p\}$ (Equations 46-48). This error dynamic is not autonomous, as it depends on the nominal state.

**Update Step:**
- **Measurement Embedding:** The base velocity measurement is embedded as a noisy action of the true state $X_i^{-1}d + n_i$ for $SE_2(3)$ (Equation 26) or as a comparison against a predicted value $f(\delta x)$ for $SO(3) \times \mathbb{R}^6$.
- **IEKF:** The innovation is defined as the difference between the actual and predicted measurement transformed into the Lie algebra (Equation 28). This innovation is then linearized assuming small errors, and the state and covariance are updated using standard EKF equations.
- **IterIEKF:** Instead of simple linearization, IterIEKF aims to find the Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimate by minimizing a nonlinear cost function (Equation 34). This minimization problem is solved using a Gauss-Newton iterative scheme (Equations 37-41). The process iteratively updates the error estimate $\xi_i$ by re-linearizing the measurement model around the current estimate. A key property is that after convergence, the updated state is guaranteed to lie on the measurement constraint set, and there is no uncertainty orthogonal to this set, a property shared with linear Kalman filters under noise-free measurements. The final covariance update (Equation 44) uses the initial Jacobian. An optional post-update correction is implemented to mitigate abrupt yaw angle jumps by canceling the spurious z-axis rotation (Equation 75), preserving other observable quantities.
- **SO(3)-EKF:** The innovation is the difference between the measured base velocity and the nominal base velocity projected into the body frame (Equation 51). Standard EKF gain and covariance update equations are applied (Equations 53-56).
- **IterSO(3)-EKF:** Similar to IterIEKF, this filter formulates a nonlinear minimization problem to find the MAP estimate (Equation 57). A Gauss-Newton iteration (Equations 59-63) is used, iteratively re-linearizing the measurement function in the Euclidean error space. The final covariance update (Equation 66) uses the Jacobian from the last iteration.

**Observability Analysis:**
An observability analysis is performed for the invariant filters (IEKF/IterIEKF) using the linearized right-invariant error dynamics. The analysis confirms that global position and yaw angle are unobservable, while body-frame velocity, pitch, and roll angles are observable. This is a consistent finding across many proprioceptive-only legged robot state estimation methods.

**Experimental Setup:**
The filters are evaluated across four settings:
1. **MuJoCo Simulation (large initial error):** Robot performing an eight-shape trajectory on terrain with a ramp. Large initial state perturbation.
2. **Monte Carlo Simulation (synthetic data):** Constant linear acceleration and angular velocity, with specific noise parameters.
3. **Monte Carlo Simulation (simulated quadruped on irregular terrain):** Quadruped walking forward on a non-constant curvature terrain (Gazebo).
4. **Real-world Dataset Evaluation (Anymal D, Pilatus-Hike2):** Public dataset of an Anymal D robot traversing a zigzag trail with significant terrain inclination changes, using only proprioceptive sensors. This scenario includes three sub-scenarios:
 - Scenario 1: Complete trajectory, no initial error.
 - Scenario 2: Large initial pose error.
 - Scenario 3: Large initial pose error, but with artificially "clean" (white, small covariance) measurement noise for the base velocity.

Performance is assessed using Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for large initial errors, Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) otherwise, for observable states (base velocity, gravity direction, pitch, and roll angles). Normalized Estimation Error Squared (NEES) is used to evaluate filter consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation across simulations and real-world data demonstrates varying performance characteristics of the four filtering approaches.

**A. State Estimation in MuJoCo Simulation under Large Initial Error:**
- In a scenario with a ramp and a large initial state perturbation, the **IterIEKF exhibited a 2.5 times faster convergence rate** compared to the vanilla IEKF. The IterSO(3)-EKF showed a convergence rate similar to that of the IEKF. This suggests that the iterative approach, particularly when combined with invariance, is beneficial for faster acquisition of accurate state estimates under challenging initial conditions.

**B. Monte Carlo Simulation with Synthetic Data:**
- **Convergence and Accuracy:** Both IterIEKF and IterSO(3)-EKF demonstrated faster convergence than their classical counterparts. Specifically, IterIEKF required 40% less time to achieve the same error level as IEKF. In terms of Mean Absolute Error (MAE) over the first 5 seconds, IterIEKF reduced the velocity error by 27% and the gravity direction error by 10% relative to IEKF.
- **Consistency (NEES):** The IterIEKF maintained Normalized Estimation Error Squared (NEES) values close to the expected 9 (for a 9-dimensional error state), indicating consistent uncertainty estimates. In contrast, the SO(3)-EKF and IterSO(3)-EKF exhibited false observability issues, leading to overconfident estimates and high NEES values.
- **Iterations:** Both iterated filters typically converged within approximately six iterations.

**C. Monte Carlo Simulation with a Simulated Quadruped Robot on Irregular Terrain:**
- **Convergence and Accuracy:** On a challenging terrain with significant pitch variations, the **IterIEKF demonstrated substantial improvements**, requiring 60% less time to achieve the same error as IEKF. Its MAE over the first 5 seconds showed a 63% reduction in velocity error and a 60% reduction in gravity direction error compared to IEKF.
- **Comparative Performance:** In this scenario, the IterSO(3)-EKF performed worse than IEKF, indicating that iteration alone does not guarantee superior performance without the consistency provided by invariant error dynamics.
- **Consistency (NEES):** IterIEKF showed the best consistency, while IterSO(3)-EKF exhibited the poorest.
- **Pitch Angle Estimation:** IterIEKF achieved a very small error in pitch angle relative to the ground truth approximately 3 seconds into the simulation, outperforming other methods in transient response.
- **Iterations:** IterIEKF typically required around 5 iterations for convergence, while IterSO(3)-EKF generally converged faster, in about 3 iterations.

**D. Real-world Dataset Evaluation (Anymal D, Pilatus-Hike2):**
The real-world dataset presented challenges due to high, non-Gaussian measurement noise (large intermittent peaks, heteroscedasticity) in the base velocity, which potentially mitigates some of the benefits of iterated filters.

- **Scenario 1 (Complete Trajectory, No Initial Error):**
 - All filters performed similarly, with **IterIEKF achieving slightly lower RMSE values across all observable states** (base velocity, gravity direction, pitch, and roll angles).
 - Angular errors were generally less than half a degree, while velocity errors were around 15% of the average velocity, suggesting the impact of measurement noise characteristics.
 - Invariant filters (IEKF, IterIEKF) generally produced more accurate trajectory estimates than non-invariant filters (SO(3)-EKF, IterSO(3)-EKF), which were affected by false observability, leading to drift in unobservable states.

- **Scenario 2 (Large Initial Pose Error):**
 - For velocity components, all filters showed similar MAE over the first 5 seconds, with IEKF having the smallest error. IterIEKF performed slightly worse than IEKF, possibly due to the high level of real-world measurement noise.
 - For **pitch angle, IterIEKF achieved the smallest error (12% less than IEKF)** and converged faster.
 - For roll angle, IterSO(3)-EKF performed best (20% less error than IEKF).
 - IterIEKF showed faster convergence for the gravity direction compared to IEKF.

- **Scenario 3 (Large Initial Pose Error, Clean Measurement Noise):**
 - When the measurement function was artificially replaced with reference velocity data corrupted by small, white Gaussian noise, the MAE values for velocity significantly decreased across all filters.
 - In this ideal noise scenario, **IterIEKF consistently achieved the lowest error for all observable states**, demonstrating the superconvergent behavior observed in earlier simulations for pitch angle and gravity direction. This confirms that IterIEKF's advantages are most pronounced when measurement noise aligns with the filter's assumptions.

**Limitations Identified:**
The authors note two primary limitations for iterated filters: the requirement for a well-known and relatively small measurement covariance. If the covariance is incorrect or too large, the benefits of iteration diminish, and performance may even degrade compared to non-iterated counterparts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in state estimation for legged robots by proposing and thoroughly evaluating the Iterated Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IterIEKF). The work successfully extends the theoretical properties of the IterIEKF, established in prior literature, to practical and complex scenarios involving quadruped robots.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that the IterIEKF offers **tangible improvements in accuracy, statistical consistency, and convergence speed** in challenging conditions. The quantitative reductions in estimation errors (e.g., up to 60% in base velocity and gravity direction error in simulations, and 12% in pitch angle in real-world data) highlight its potential for more reliable robot operation. Its ability to converge faster under large initial errors is particularly valuable for robust system initialization and recovery from significant disturbances.

By systematically comparing IterIEKF with IEKF, SO(3)-EKF, and IterSO(3)-EKF, the study clarifies the individual and combined benefits of invariant error dynamics and iterative optimization. It reinforces that **invariance is crucial for statistical consistency** (as evidenced by NEES metrics), while iteration enhances accuracy by better approximating the MAP estimate, especially when the measurement model is strongly nonlinear and the noise characteristics are well-behaved. The observed degradation of IterSO(3)-EKF in certain scenarios, despite its iterative nature, underscores the importance of the underlying geometric structure provided by invariant filtering.

The decision to rely **solely on proprioceptive measurements** makes the proposed framework inherently robust to varying environmental conditions (e.g., lighting, texture, GPS availability), which is critical for autonomous legged robots operating in unstructured outdoor or indoor settings. This reduces reliance on external sensors that can be unreliable or unavailable.

The **open-sourcing of the proposed framework** and simulation tools is a valuable contribution to the robotics community. It promotes reproducibility, allows other researchers to build upon this work, and accelerates further development in robust state estimation for legged systems.

The potential impact of this research is broad:
- **Improved Robot Performance:** More accurate and consistent state estimates directly translate to better control, navigation, and overall autonomy for quadruped robots, enabling them to operate more effectively in complex and dynamic environments.
- **Enhanced Safety and Reliability:** Reduced estimation errors and faster convergence contribute to safer robot operations, particularly in scenarios requiring precise motion control or rapid recovery from unexpected events.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a strong foundation for future developments. The authors suggest extensions to SLAM applications, which could leverage the IterIEKF's superior accuracy in localization. Further work on incorporating accelerometer bias using specialized Lie groups (Two-Frame Group) and integrating other measurements like forward kinematics, while carefully modeling their uncertainties, could lead to even more comprehensive and robust state estimators.
- **Advancement of Geometric Filtering Theory:** By bridging the gap between theoretical invariant filtering and practical iterated estimation in hybrid systems, this research contributes to the broader field of geometric state estimation on Lie groups, validating its practical relevance.
