# Less is More: Clustered Cross-Covariance Control for Offline RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Clustered Cross-Covariance Control for TD (C4), a method that identifies and mitigates a harmful cross-time covariance of gradient features within the Temporal Difference (TD) learning objective in offline reinforcement learning. This approach improves average returns by over 30% on data-scarce D4RL benchmarks and enhances stability across various algorithms.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) suffers from distributional shift, where learned policies deviate from the behavior policy, leading to extrapolation errors and value overestimation with scarce data.
- Existing conservative offline RL methods can become overly cautious under weak data coverage, leading to over-conservatism that suppresses learning and degrades performance.
- The standard Temporal Difference (TD) learning objective implicitly induces a harmful bias, specifically a "cross-time covariance of gradient features," which causes gradient interference and training instability or collapse, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) areas.

## Method

- The Clustered Cross-Covariance Control for TD (C4) method partitions the ==replay dataset== into K clusters based on "stacked gradient pairs" (current and next-state Q-gradient features) to localize TD updates and suppress spurious coupling across unrelated modes.
- A modified critic objective integrates a K-component Gaussian mixture model fitted to the stacked gradient pairs and explicitly adds a gradient-based corrective penalty to shrink the magnitude of the harmful cross-covariance within each cluster.
- An EM-style training procedure iteratively updates clustering parameters and samples minibatches from single clusters, applying the corrective penalty during critic updates to stabilize optimization without compromising existing conservative offline RL mechanisms.

## Results

- C4 consistently achieved higher normalized returns, improving average performance by over **30%** compared to baselines on 10,000-sample D4RL MuJoCo locomotion tasks and recovering nearly **75%** of expert performance.
- The method demonstrated robust plug-and-play compatibility, boosting advanced offline RL algorithms like BPPO by **39.6%** and A2PR by **85.9%** in low-data settings, and improved performance even on full D4RL datasets.
- C4 maintained low computational overhead and low sensitivity to hyperparameters while empirically demonstrating successful suppression of the dimension-normalized trace of the empirical cross-covariance matrix throughout training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing the standard squared error TD objective (E[δ2]) implicitly induces a "harmful cross-time covariance of gradient features" that appears with a negative sign in the TD residual variance decomposition, leading to gradient interference and instability.
> - Partitioning the replay buffer into clusters based on these stacked gradient features allows TD updates to be localized, effectively vanishing the "between-cluster" component of the harmful cross-covariance and improving stability.
> - An explicit, gradient-based penalty applied within each cluster can further cancel the remaining within-cluster cross-covariance, ensuring that C4 mitigates excessive conservatism in OOD areas while preserving the inductive biases of underlying conservative offline RL algorithms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL) addresses the challenge of learning optimal policies from a fixed dataset of previously collected interactions, without further interaction with the environment. This paradigm is crucial in applications where online exploration is either risky, expensive, or impractical. A fundamental issue in offline RL is distributional shift, which occurs when the learned policy's state-action visitation distribution deviates significantly from the behavior policy that generated the dataset. This problem is exacerbated in scenarios with scarce data or datasets heavily dominated by out-of-distribution (OOD) areas, leading to extrapolation errors and value overestimation.

Existing offline RL methods commonly adopt conservative objectives to mitigate distributional shift. These approaches often constrain the learned policy to remain "close" to the behavior policy, regularize value functions on OOD samples, enforce strict in-sample learning, or employ pessimistic policy learning with uncertainty-based penalties. While these methods curb error accumulation, they can become overly cautious under weak data coverage, leading to over-conservatism that can destabilize policy learning and degrade performance. Some recent works attempt to address this by admitting carefully selected OOD samples, but these typically operate at the level of data selection rather than fundamental RL mechanisms.

A less explored failure mode in deep RL, which intensifies with scarce and noisy data, arises from the value fitting objective itself. Temporal Difference (TD) learning, the backbone of many RL algorithms, is known to induce detrimental bias and feature co-adaptation under distribution shift, which can lead to training instability or collapse. Prior work has identified various implicit regularizers in deep RL that contribute to these instabilities, with approaches like DR3 and LayerNorm attempting to mitigate symptoms or stabilize representations. However, these efforts have not explicitly linked the core TD loss objective to a specific harmful implicit regularizer driven by cross-time covariance in feature gradients, nor have they proposed a sampling-level solution.

This paper positions itself by identifying and directly addressing this specific failure mode: a harmful TD cross-covariance of gradient features induced by the standard squared error objective (E[δ²] minimization). It differentiates itself from existing conservative methods by focusing on the underlying mechanism within the TD update and from prior implicit regularization work by deriving the effect directly from the TD loss's second moment decomposition and proposing a novel solution operating at the data sampling level via clustering. It also relates to clustering-based RL methods, but specifically adapts clustering to manage gradient feature space, rather than solely for diverse policy training or trajectory partitioning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to identify, characterize, and mitigate a specific failure mode in offline reinforcement learning related to the temporal difference (TD) learning objective, particularly in data-scarce and out-of-distribution (OOD) heavy environments.

The motivation for this work stems from two main observations concerning the limitations of current offline RL approaches:

1. **Over-conservatism under Weak Data Coverage**: While conservative objectives are widely adopted to counteract distributional shift, they implicitly assume sufficient data coverage. When datasets are small or weakly covered, these methods can become excessively cautious. This over-conservatism can suppress learning in areas where policy improvement is needed, potentially leading to performance degradation or training instability. The authors aim to mitigate this without altering the fundamental behavior of existing conservative methods, but rather by ensuring that updates in OOD areas are less restricted unnecessarily.

2. **Harmful Bias from the TD Learning Objective**: The authors highlight that beyond distributional shift, the value fitting objective itself in TD learning can introduce detrimental bias and feature co-adaptation, especially under distribution shift and limited data, which can culminate in training collapse. Their detailed theoretical analysis is motivated by identifying the core cause of this phenomenon. They specifically find that minimizing the second moment of the TD residual (E[δ²]) induces a "harmful cross-time covariance of gradient features." This effect is particularly pronounced in OOD areas, leading to biased optimization and degraded policy learning. This covariance term, unlike other implicit regularizers, acts *against* the intended optimization objective, causing gradient interference and instability.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following key objectives:

- **Theoretical Characterization**: To formally analyze the TD learning objective (E[δ²]) and rigorously demonstrate how it induces a harmful implicit regularizer, specifically a cross-time covariance of gradient features, and explain its detrimental effects.
- **Method Development (C⁴)**: To develop a novel, two-pronged strategy, named Clustered Cross-Covariance Control for TD (C⁴), that operates locally on the geometry of replay data to counteract this harmful cross-covariance:
 - **Partitioned Buffer Sampling**: To restrict updates to localized replay partitions, thereby attenuating irregular covariance effects and aligning update directions.
 - **Explicit Gradient-Based Corrective Penalty**: To introduce an explicit penalty that cancels the covariance-induced bias within each update.
- **Preservation of Optimization Goals**: To mathematically prove that buffer partitioning preserves the lower-bound property of the maximization objective in policy improvement, ensuring that the proposed constraints mitigate excessive conservatism in extreme OOD areas without fundamentally altering the core behavior of policy-constrained offline RL.
- **Empirical Validation**: To empirically demonstrate that C⁴ improves stability and significantly enhances returns over prior methods, especially with small datasets and splits that emphasize OOD areas, showcasing its practical efficacy and "plug-and-play" compatibility with existing offline RL algorithms.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond generic conservatism by addressing a specific, detrimental bias inherent to TD learning, thereby enabling more stable and effective policy learning in data-constrained offline RL settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involves a theoretical analysis to pinpoint a failure mode in TD learning, followed by the development of a novel algorithmic approach, C⁴, that combines data partitioning and explicit regularization to address this issue.

**Theoretical Analysis of Cross-Covariance Effects (Section 4)**

The work begins by analyzing the standard Temporal Difference (TD) regression problem, which minimizes the squared error objective: $L_{TD}(\phi) = E_{(s,a,r,s') \sim D} [Q_\phi(s,a) - (r(s,a) + \gamma E_{a' \sim \pi(\cdot|s')} Q_{\phi'}(s',a'))]^2$. This is equivalent to minimizing the second moment of the TD residual, $E[\delta^2]$. The authors leverage the identity $E[\delta^2] = (E[\delta])^2 + Var[\delta]$, shifting the focus to understanding how $Var[\delta]$ changes under small feature space displacements, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) areas.

Using a first-order Taylor approximation for Q-values in feature space (e.g., $Q_\psi(x + kw) \approx Q_\psi(x) + k \langle w, \nabla_x Q_\psi(x) \rangle$), the authors derive **Theorem 1**. This theorem decomposes the variance of the TD residual ($Var[\delta]$) into three components:
1. **Term (A)**: $\gamma^2 (k')^2 Var[\langle w', \nabla_{x'} Q_{\phi'}(x') \rangle]$
2. **Term (B)**: $k^2 Var[\langle w, \nabla_x Q_\phi(x) \rangle]$
3. **Term (C)**: $- 2\gamma kk' Cov[\langle w', \nabla_{x'} Q_{\phi'}(x') \rangle, \langle w, \nabla_x Q_\phi(x) \rangle]$

Terms (A) and (B) are characterized as beneficial implicit regularizers, analogous to those seen in noisy supervised learning, which penalize large feature gradients and improve generalization by reducing Q-value variance in OOD regions. Crucially, **Term (C)** is identified as a unique "cross-time covariance of gradient features" specific to TD learning. Because this term appears with a negative sign in the variance decomposition and the TD loss is minimized, updates tend to *increase* its magnitude. This makes Term (C) a harmful implicit regularizer that can cause pronounced gradient interference and instability, particularly in severe OOD regimes. This analysis is distinct from prior work by directly deriving from the TD loss and treating next-state contributions explicitly.

**C⁴: Clustered Cross-Covariance Control for TD (Section 5)**

To counteract the harmful Term (C), the authors propose **C⁴**, which implements two complementary strategies:

1. **Clustering the Stacked Gradient Pairs (Section 5.2)**:
 - The core idea is that treating the entire dataset as a single entity mixes within-cluster spread and between-cluster offsets, making the TD cross-term unstable.
 - The methodology involves defining a "stacked gradient pair" $y_i = [g', g] \in \mathbb{R}^{2m}$ for each transition $i$, where $g = \nabla_x Q_\phi(x)$ and $g' = \nabla_{x'} Q_{\phi'}(x')$.
 - The dataset is then partitioned into $K$ clusters in this $y$-space.
 - **Theorem 2** proves that if each minibatch is drawn from a *single cluster*, the "between-cluster" component of the cross-covariance vanishes for that batch. This means that TD updates become localized, governed only by the "within-cluster" cross-covariance $C_z = Cov(g', g | Z=z)$. This suppresses spurious coupling across unrelated modes and improves stability in OOD directions.

2. **Mixture-Regularized Objective and Training Procedure (Section 5.3)**:
 - A $K$-component Gaussian mixture model is fitted to the stacked gradient pairs $\{y_i\}$.
 - The training objective for the critic is modified to: $min_{\phi,\{p_z,\mu_z,\Omega_z\}} L_{TD}(\phi) + \lambda \sum_{z=1}^K p_z \|C_z\|_F^2$. The Frobenius norm $\|C_z\|_F^2$ serves as a computationally tractable upper bound for the spectral norm $\|C_z\|_2^2$, aiming to shrink the magnitude of the harmful cross-covariance within each cluster.
 - **Algorithm 1** details the EM-style training procedure:
 - **E-step**: Compute responsibilities ($r_{iz}$) for each sample belonging to each cluster.
 - **M-step**: Update the Gaussian mixture parameters ($p_z, \mu_z, \Omega_z$) based on the responsibilities, and extract the within-cluster cross-covariance matrices $C_z$.
 - **Critic Minibatch**: In each training step, a cluster $z$ is sampled according to its probability $p_z$, and a minibatch $B$ is drawn from this cluster using the computed responsibilities as weights.
 - **Penalty and Update**: For the sampled minibatch $B$, an empirical cross-covariance $\hat{C}_z(B)$ is estimated. The critic is then updated by minimizing a batch loss $J(\phi, B) = L_{TD}(\phi, B) + \lambda \hat{R}_{cross}(B)$, where $\hat{R}_{cross}(B) = \|\hat{C}_z(B)\|_F^2 + \beta (\text{tr } \hat{C}_z(B))^2$ is the gradient-based corrective penalty with tunable coefficients $\lambda$ and $\beta$.

**Training on Clustered Buffers (Section 6)**

The paper further ensures that this clustering-based approach does not negatively impact the policy improvement objective, particularly for methods like CQL. It proves that:
- Buffer partitioning preserves a lower bound property of the maximization objective (Lemma 3).
- Optimizing a per-cluster surrogate objective and sampling clusters randomly yields an unbiased stochastic gradient of a computable lower bound to the mixture objective.
- This approach mitigates excessive conservatism in OOD areas by reducing unnecessary suppression, while crucially preserving the core behavior and known stabilizing effects (e.g., KL caps in CQL-style algorithms) of existing conservative offline RL methods (Proposition 3). This is achieved by exploiting the convexity of common $f$-divergences and other proximity measures in their second argument.

In summary, the methodology combines theoretical insight into TD learning's failure modes with a practical, EM-style algorithmic solution that locally controls gradient cross-covariance through clustered sampling and an explicit penalty, ensuring compatibility with existing offline RL frameworks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of C⁴, particularly in data-scarce and OOD-emphasized regimes, yielded several key findings:

1. **Superior Performance in Data-Scarce Regimes (Q1)**:
 - **Significant Improvement**: C⁴ (instantiated atop CQL or TD3+BC) consistently achieved higher normalized returns compared to various state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms across standard D4RL benchmarks, especially when datasets were limited to 10,000 state-action pairs (approximately 1% of full datasets). On MuJoCo locomotion tasks, C⁴ recovered nearly 75% of expert performance on average and demonstrated an average improvement exceeding 30% over the best alternative baseline (Table 1).
 - **Broad Applicability**: This performance gain extended to other challenging D4RL domains such as Adroit, AntMaze, and Maze2D under reduced data, demonstrating robust generalization capabilities across diverse task types (Tables 2, 3, 4, 5, Figures 8, 9, 10).
 - **Compatibility with Advanced Methods**: C⁴ also delivered substantial improvements when integrated as a plug-in module with more advanced offline RL algorithms, such as BPPO and A2PR. In the 10k-sample setting, C⁴ boosted BPPO's average normalized score by 39.6% (Table 7) and A2PR's by 85.9% (Table 8), indicating that the identified cross-covariance issue is a fundamental bottleneck even for sophisticated methods.

2. **Robustness and Efficiency (Q2)**:
 - **Moderate Computational Overhead**: Incorporating C⁴ into standard backbones like CQL or TD3+BC introduced only moderate runtime overhead, comparable to other lightweight regularizers (e.g., LayerNorm, DR3, SORL), and was considerably more efficient than complex data-efficient baselines (e.g., TSRL, DOGE) (Figure 4). Specifically, for data-scarce regimes (10^3–10^4 samples), clustering overhead was approximately 1% of total training time, rising to 15–20% for million-scale datasets (Table 13).
 - **Low Sensitivity to Hyperparameters**: C⁴ exhibited stable performance over a broad range of its regularization strength ($\lambda$) and the base algorithm's coefficient ($\alpha$) (Figure 4, Table 12), suggesting that it does not require delicate tuning.
 - **Optimal Number of Clusters**: Performance improved rapidly as the number of clusters (K) increased from 1, plateauing around K=3–5 and remaining stable up to K=20 (Table 10). For larger datasets, the effective number of occupied clusters was found to be smaller (Table 11), allowing for adaptive selection.
 - **Clustering Update Frequency**: A "sweet spot" for clustering frequency was identified around 100–200 critic updates between re-clustering runs. Updating too frequently or too infrequently degraded performance, highlighting the importance of balancing adaptability with stability (Table 14).

3. **Plug-and-Play Adaptability and Scalability (Q3)**:
 - **Algorithm Agnostic Enhancement**: C⁴ proved to be an effective plug-in module, providing consistent and substantial improvements throughout training for both CQL and TD3+BC (Figures 5, 6). This indicated that C⁴ effectively targets gradient-space variance components limiting offline RL performance while preserving the inductive biases of the underlying algorithms.
 - **Scalability to Full Datasets**: While primarily designed for data scarcity, C⁴ did not degrade performance on large, full-replay D4RL datasets. When integrated with CQL on full locomotion datasets, it improved the total normalized score from 695.5 to 742.1 (Table 6). C⁴ consistently achieved top trajectories and showed gains on replay datasets as the training set size increased, demonstrating adaptability to varying data budgets (Figures 9, 10).

4. **Effective Covariance Control**: Empirical monitoring of the dimension-normalized trace of the empirical cross-covariance matrix ($\text{tr}_n(\Sigma_B)$) showed that C⁴ successfully kept this value substantially lower than baselines (LN, CQL, DR3) throughout most of training, with the most pronounced difference in replay datasets (Figure 11). This provides direct evidence that the proposed mechanism effectively suppresses the identified harmful cross-covariance.

5. **Clustering Mechanism Validation**: Visualizations of the clustering structure (t-SNE plots of stacked gradient pairs) demonstrated that C⁴ effectively separated distinct gradient modes during different stages of training for both CQL+C⁴ and TD3BC+C⁴ (Figures 12, 13), supporting the efficacy of the gradient-space partitioning strategy.

In summary, C⁴ addresses a critical instability source in TD learning, offering a practical and effective solution that enhances the performance and robustness of offline RL algorithms, especially in challenging low-data scenarios, without compromising performance on larger datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of offline reinforcement learning by identifying and effectively addressing a fundamental source of instability within the temporal difference (TD) learning framework, particularly under conditions of scarce or out-of-distribution data.

The key significance and potential impacts include:

1. **Fundamental Theoretical Insight**: The work provides a detailed theoretical analysis identifying a "harmful TD cross-covariance of gradient features" as a distinct implicit regularizer induced by the standard squared error TD objective. This insight explains a less understood failure mode that leads to biased optimization and degradation of policy learning, especially in OOD areas. This deepens the understanding of implicit regularization in deep RL and its specific mechanisms in the offline setting.

2. **Enhanced Robustness in Data-Limited Settings**: The proposed C⁴ method offers a practical and effective solution for offline RL problems characterized by limited data coverage, small datasets, or datasets dominated by OOD samples. These scenarios are common in real-world applications where data collection is expensive, risky, or constrained. By improving stability and delivering substantial gains (up to 30% on benchmarks), C⁴ makes offline RL more reliable and applicable to practical deployments.

3. **Algorithmic Plug-and-Play Compatibility**: C⁴'s design as a "plug-and-play" module allows for easy integration with a wide range of existing offline RL algorithms, from foundational methods like CQL and TD3+BC to more advanced state-of-the-art approaches (e.g., BPPO, A2PR). This broad compatibility suggests that C⁴ can serve as a universal enhancement, improving the performance and stability of diverse offline RL pipelines without requiring extensive modifications or fine-tuning of the base algorithms.

4. **Mitigation of Excessive Conservatism**: By focusing on the intrinsic TD learning bias rather than solely applying generic policy constraints, C⁴ helps to counteract the problem of over-conservatism in extreme OOD areas. This allows policies to explore potentially beneficial actions in under-represented regions more effectively, without suffering from the instabilities caused by uncontrolled gradient interactions.

5. **Improved Generalization and Stability**: The empirical results demonstrate that C⁴ not only boosts average returns but also leads to smoother learning dynamics and greater stability across various benchmarks. This is particularly valuable in critical applications where robustness and predictable performance are paramount.

6. **Guidance for Future Research**: The identification of cross-time gradient covariance as a critical factor opens new avenues for research into implicit regularization in TD learning. Future work could explore alternative mechanisms for controlling this covariance, extend the analysis to other deep RL algorithms, or develop adaptive strategies for clustering and penalty application.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the state of offline RL by providing a new theoretical lens to understand instability and offering a robust, practical, and highly compatible solution. Its potential impact lies in making offline RL more accessible, reliable, and performant for real-world applications where data constraints are a primary concern.
