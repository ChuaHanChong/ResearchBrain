# Prisma: An Open Source Toolkit for Mechanistic Interpretability in Vision and Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Prisma is an open-source toolkit that adapts mechanistic interpretability methods from language models to vision and video transformers, providing a unified interface and pre-trained sparse autoencoders. The toolkit enables researchers to analyze the internal workings of vision models and has been used to observe that vision sparse autoencoders exhibit lower sparsity than their language counterparts and that sparse autoencoder reconstructions can sometimes improve model loss.

## Problem

- Progress in mechanistic interpretability (MI) for vision and video models lagged significantly behind that for language models due to a lack of accessible frameworks and pre-trained interpretability artifacts.
- Existing MI tools for language models were not directly transferable to vision/video domains due to architectural and data-specific differences (e.g., continuous visual input, patch tokenization, self-supervised objectives).
- Researchers lacked unified toolkits to easily access activations, train sparse coders, and perform circuit analysis on a wide range of vision and video transformer architectures.

## Method

- Prisma provides a unified, open-source Python toolkit featuring a ==HookedViT class== for easy access to activations and weights across over **75** vision and video transformer models.
- The toolkit offers comprehensive support for training and evaluating ==sparse coder architectures==, including Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs), transcoders, and crosscoders, with configurable training parameters.
- The project releases over ==80 pre-trained SAEs== for CLIP-B and DINO-B ViTs, along with tutorial notebooks and toy vision transformers, to lower the computational burden and entry barriers for vision mechanistic interpretability research.

## Results

- Vision Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) exhibit substantially higher activation density (lower sparsity) than language SAEs, with L0 values often exceeding **500** active features per patch in models like CLIP-B/32.
- Reconstructions from SAEs can sometimes decrease the original model's cross-entropy loss when reinjected into the forward pass, particularly in deeper layers of certain CLIP and DINO models, suggesting a potential denoising effect.
- Sparsity patterns and feature utilization in vision SAEs vary significantly between spatial patches and the CLS token, with the CLS token showing higher L0, especially in deeper layers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Successful mechanistic interpretability paradigms from language models, such as 'hooked' architectures and sparse autoencoders, can be adapted to vision and video transformers despite modality differences.
> - Providing comprehensive open-source tooling, pre-trained interpretability artifacts, and educational resources is crucial for accelerating and democratizing research in emerging AI interpretability subfields.
> - Preliminary analyses using newly developed tools can reveal fundamental and surprising properties about how vision models represent information, such as distinct sparsity patterns compared to language models.
