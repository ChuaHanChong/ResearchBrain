# Guava: An Effective and Universal Harness for Embodied Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Guava framework enables compact, open-source language models to achieve robust, generalizable robotic manipulation by employing iterative reasoning, semantic action abstractions, and multimodal observations. The 4-billion parameter Guava-Agent-4B attained 75.6% overall success in simulation and strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, often surpassing larger proprietary models while demonstrating emergent failure recovery.

## Problem

- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action policies require extensive and costly embodiment-dependent robot demonstration data, hindering scalability.
- Previous harness-based systems often rely on one-shot program generation, limiting robust, long-horizon behavior and failure recovery, and requiring powerful, proprietary models.
- A lack of systematic understanding existed regarding the specific design principles for an effective harness in robust embodied manipulation, especially concerning iterative workflows and action space abstractions.

## Method

- Developed **Guava**, a harness framework incorporating iterative ==Perception-Reasoning-Action loops== (ReAct-style), semantic action abstractions, and multimodal observations for robust embodied control.
- Created a data-efficient pipeline where frontier VLMs generate a dataset of interaction and recovery trajectories in diverse simulation environments (under 2,000 trajectories).
- Implemented a two-stage training process, combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and targeted Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to distill embodied tool-use behaviors into a compact 4B-parameter open-source VLM.

## Results

- Guava-Agent-4B achieved an overall success rate of **75.6%** across diverse simulation tasks, outperforming GPT-**5.4** (**70.2%**) and competitive one-shot methods (**62.7%**) in simulation.
- The compact Guava-Agent-4B demonstrated strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving **86%** success on in-distribution and **92%** on out-of-distribution real-world tasks without additional fine-tuning.
- RL post-training significantly improved success rates on long-horizon tasks, such as increasing 'shell game' performance from **6.7%** (SFT-only) to **60.0%** and 'place all red objects in basket' from **0.0%** to **93.3%**, by enhancing recovery capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative perception-reasoning-action loops, semantic action abstractions, and multimodal observations are critical design principles for effective and robust embodied manipulation harnesses.
> - Sophisticated embodied manipulation capabilities can be distilled from powerful proprietary models into compact (4B parameter) open-source models using a limited, simulation-generated dataset.
> - RL post-training, specifically on challenging long-horizon tasks, enhances iterative planning and emergent recovery behaviors, allowing compact models to handle complex, off-trajectory scenarios efficiently.
