# Guava: An Effective and Universal Harness for Embodied Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Guava framework enables compact, open-source language models to achieve robust, generalizable robotic manipulation by employing iterative reasoning, semantic action abstractions, and multimodal observations. The 4-billion parameter Guava-Agent-4B attained 75.6% overall success in simulation and strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, often surpassing larger proprietary models while demonstrating emergent failure recovery.

## Problem

- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action policies require extensive and costly embodiment-dependent robot demonstration data, hindering scalability.
- Previous harness-based systems often rely on one-shot program generation, limiting robust, long-horizon behavior and failure recovery, and requiring powerful, proprietary models.
- A lack of systematic understanding existed regarding the specific design principles for an effective harness in robust embodied manipulation, especially concerning iterative workflows and action space abstractions.

## Method

- Developed **Guava**, a harness framework incorporating iterative ==Perception-Reasoning-Action loops== (ReAct-style), semantic action abstractions, and multimodal observations for robust embodied control.
- Created a data-efficient pipeline where frontier VLMs generate a dataset of interaction and recovery trajectories in diverse simulation environments (under 2,000 trajectories).
- Implemented a two-stage training process, combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and targeted Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to distill embodied tool-use behaviors into a compact 4B-parameter open-source VLM.

## Results

- Guava-Agent-4B achieved an overall success rate of **75.6%** across diverse simulation tasks, outperforming GPT-**5.4** (**70.2%**) and competitive one-shot methods (**62.7%**) in simulation.
- The compact Guava-Agent-4B demonstrated strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving **86%** success on in-distribution and **92%** on out-of-distribution real-world tasks without additional fine-tuning.
- RL post-training significantly improved success rates on long-horizon tasks, such as increasing 'shell game' performance from **6.7%** (SFT-only) to **60.0%** and 'place all red objects in basket' from **0.0%** to **93.3%**, by enhancing recovery capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative perception-reasoning-action loops, semantic action abstractions, and multimodal observations are critical design principles for effective and robust embodied manipulation harnesses.
> - Sophisticated embodied manipulation capabilities can be distilled from powerful proprietary models into compact (4B parameter) open-source models using a limited, simulation-generated dataset.
> - RL post-training, specifically on challenging long-horizon tasks, enhances iterative planning and emergent recovery behaviors, allowing compact models to handle complex, off-trajectory scenarios efficiently.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalizable embodied agents capable of complex manipulation tasks is a prominent area within artificial intelligence and robotics. Recent advancements in language models (LMs) trained on large-scale vision-language data have demonstrated potential for driving such agents due to their semantic understanding, visual grounding, and reasoning capabilities.

Two primary paradigms have emerged for integrating LMs into robotic systems:
1. **End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies:** This approach involves finetuning vision-language models to directly generate robot actions from visual observations and language instructions. While capable of acquiring broad manipulation skills, these systems typically require substantial amounts of expensive, embodiment-dependent robot demonstration data, which can be challenging to scale across diverse objects, scenes, and long-horizon tasks.
2. **Harness-based or Tool-use systems:** This paradigm leverages LMs as high-level reasoning modules that invoke external, specialized tools for perception, planning, and control. This modular design aims to encapsulate low-level robot skills within tools, allowing the LM to focus on high-level reasoning, tool selection, and task decomposition. Previous efforts in this direction, such as Maestro and Cap-X, have demonstrated initial success by enabling robots to call various perception and control tools through program generation. However, many existing harness frameworks often rely on one-shot program generation and execution. This limits the agent's ability to react to execution outcomes, recover from failures, or maintain robust behavior in long-horizon tasks, often requiring powerful, proprietary frontier models.

This work, "Guava," positions itself within the harness-based paradigm. It addresses two specific gaps:
- A systematic understanding of the design principles that constitute an *effective* harness for robust embodied manipulation, particularly concerning iterative workflows, action space abstractions, and observation modalities.
- An investigation into whether such an effective harness can serve as a universal interface to empower *compact, open-source models* with strong embodied capabilities, thereby reducing reliance on costly proprietary models and extensive data collection. By developing an end-to-end training pipeline that distills embodied tool-use behaviors into smaller models with minimal simulation data, Guava aims to offer a scalable and accessible alternative for developing generalist manipulation agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research was to identify the fundamental elements contributing to an effective and universal harness for embodied manipulation and to demonstrate that these elements can enable strong emergent capabilities in compact, open-source models with minimal training data.

The motivation for this investigation arose from several challenges and observations in the field of embodied AI:
- **Ambiguity in Effective Harness Design:** Despite the potential of harnessing foundation models for complex domains, it was unclear what specific design choices make a harness effective for embodied manipulation. Existing systems often demonstrated limitations in robust long-horizon behavior and failure recovery, partly due to reliance on one-shot planning and powerful, often proprietary, models. The researchers aimed to systematically explore the design space of agent workflows, action spaces, and observation modalities to articulate concrete design principles for robust interaction with physical environments.
- **High Cost and Limited Accessibility of Frontier Models:** While frontier proprietary models (e.g., GPT-5.4) exhibit strong reasoning capabilities when integrated into harness frameworks, their direct deployment incurs significant costs and latency due to repeated multimodal API calls during closed-loop execution. This financial and computational barrier limits the widespread adoption and scalability of such systems. A key motivation was to determine if the embodied capabilities enabled by an effective harness could be distilled into smaller, open-source models, thereby making embodied manipulation systems more accessible and cost-effective.
- **Data Efficiency for Robot Learning:** Traditional robot learning often demands vast quantities of real-world demonstration data, which is expensive and time-consuming to collect. The research was motivated to explore a data-efficient training pipeline that could transfer complex embodied behaviors using a minimal dataset, specifically fewer than 2,000 trajectories collected entirely in simulation. This approach aims to mitigate the data scarcity problem in robotics.
- **Generalization and Robustness:** A recurring challenge in robot manipulation is ensuring generalization to unseen objects, novel instructions, and long-horizon tasks, as well as robust recovery from execution failures in stochastic real-world environments. The study aimed to design a harness that explicitly fosters these qualities, moving beyond mere task completion to adaptable and resilient agent behavior.

In summary, the key objectives were to uncover the critical components of effective embodied harnesses, integrate these components into a framework named Guava, and then demonstrate that this framework, combined with a data-efficient training pipeline, could empower compact open-source vision-language models with competitive, generalizable, and robust manipulation capabilities that transfer effectively from simulation to the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involved a two-pronged approach: first, the systematic design and validation of the Guava harness framework, and second, the development of a data-efficient pipeline to distill embodied capabilities into a compact open-source model.

#### 3.1. Designing the Guava Harness Framework

The Guava framework was developed through systematic exploration of the design space of embodied agents, identifying three key principles for effective manipulation:

- **Iterative Perception-Reasoning-Action Loops (ReAct-style):** The framework adopts a closed-loop reasoning process inspired by ReAct (Yao et al., 2023). Instead of predicting an entire trajectory from a single observation, the Vision-Language Model (VLM) continuously interleaves perception, reasoning, and action execution. This iterative workflow allows the agent to adapt to execution outcomes, re-plan when necessary, and recover from failures (e.g., missed grasps, object shifts) by incorporating updated observations throughout task execution.
- **Semantic Action Abstractions:** Guava provides the VLM with a set of high-level, semantic-level manipulation tools. These tools define actions with clear semantic meanings, focusing on task and object-oriented skills (e.g., `grasp(object)`, `align(object, direction, clearance)`). This design abstracts away the burden of low-level geometric and physical reasoning, such as direct joint-space controls or precise Cartesian poses, which are delegated to lower-level controllers. The toolset, detailed in Table 1, includes a combination of highly abstracted tools (e.g., `grasp`, `release`, `home_pose`) and tools allowing for fine-grained control when necessary (e.g., `move(x,y,z)`, `rotate(angle, axis)`).
- **Multimodal Observations:** The agent receives rich multimodal observations, combining visual input (images capturing spatial relationships and object configurations) with textual state representations. Textual representations provide compact symbolic descriptions of robot states, object positions/sizes, and task progress (e.g., current gripper pose). This combination aims to provide complementary information, improve grounding, and reduce ambiguity in sequential decision-making.

Controlled ablations on six long-horizon manipulation tasks in Robosuite (Zhu et al., 2020) with a frontier model (GPT-5.4) were conducted to validate these design choices, demonstrating that iterative workflows, semantic action spaces, and multimodal observations consistently lead to higher task success rates.

#### 3.2. Learning Efficient and Generalizable Agentic Embodied Reasoning

To address the cost and latency associated with frontier models, the research developed a data-efficient training pipeline to distill embodied tool-use behaviors into a compact open-source model (Qwen3.5-4B).

- **Guava as a Data Generation Engine:**
 - Frontier VLMs (e.g., GPT-5.4) interacted with diverse simulation environments (Robosuite) through the Guava harness.
 - This process generated a dataset of interaction trajectories, including observations, tool calls, and model reasoning traces.
 - To ensure robustness, the data generation explicitly incorporated **recovery trajectories**. Successful demonstrations were augmented with scenarios where execution states were perturbed (e.g., simulated missed grasps, object drops, wrong alignments). The frontier VLM then generated corrective actions from these perturbed states, exposing the model to off-trajectory scenarios. Randomly sampled states within successful trajectories were also used as starting points.
 - Scene randomization (pose, lighting, camera views) was applied to enhance data diversity.
 - A data cleaning and balancing process filtered unsuccessful episodes, invalid tool parameters, and low-quality samples, resulting in 1,934 trajectories (1,191 successful, 743 recovery) for 237 unique task prompts.
- **Two-Stage Training Pipeline:**
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The compact VLM (Qwen3.5-4B) was initially fine-tuned on the collected simulation trajectories, encompassing both successful and recovery behaviors. The vision encoder and aligner of Qwen3.5-4B were frozen during this stage.
 - **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Following SFT, RL post-training using GRPO was applied. Crucially, this stage was not applied to the entire dataset but *selectively* to a subset of the most challenging long-horizon tasks. This targeted approach aimed to enhance iterative planning, tool use, and adaptation to execution errors, particularly in scenarios requiring sequential reasoning and recovery. A sparse task-success reward signal was used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated Guava-Agent-4B, the 4-billion parameter VLM trained with the Guava pipeline, in both simulation and real-world environments. Comparisons were made against the base Qwen3.5-4B model (without post-training, but using the Guava harness), GPT-5.4 (a frontier proprietary VLM with the Guava harness), and CaP-Agent0 (a concurrent harness-based agent using one-shot code generation).

#### 4.1. Simulation Performance

- **Overall Performance:** Guava-Agent-4B achieved the highest overall success rate of 75.6% across diverse tasks in simulation, outperforming GPT-5.4 (70.2%) and CaP-Agent0 (62.7%) (Table 2). The base Qwen3.5-4B without post-training performed poorly (23.1%), indicating the necessity of the training pipeline.
- **In-Distribution (ID) Tasks:** Guava-Agent-4B demonstrated high performance, including 100% success on tasks like "place can in box" and "remove cube from tray," and 86.7% on "close drawer."
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** The model exhibited strong generalization capabilities:
 - **OOD Objects:** 100% success on "pick up carrot" and "lemon in bin," and 86.7% on "tomato near potato." It significantly improved "push pot" from 26.7% (GPT-5.4) to 73.3%.
 - **OOD Prompts:** Achieved 100% on "stack cube reverse order" and 66.7% on "arrange by size reverse direction."
 - **OOD Long-Horizon Tasks:** Demonstrated strong performance, achieving 93.3% on both "separate food and utensils" and "set table." Performance was lower on "shell game" (6.7%) and "place all red objects in basket" (0.0%).

#### 4.2. Real-World Transfer

- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Guava-Agent-4B transferred effectively from simulation to real-world environments without additional real-world fine-tuning. It achieved the highest overall success rates on both ID (86%) and OOD (92%) real-world tasks (Figure 4).
- **ID Real-World Tasks:** Perfect success rates were observed on "pick up orange," "place can in box," and "remove cube from tray." Performance on the "push basket" task improved to 60% compared to 40% for GPT-5.4.
- **OOD Real-World Tasks:** Maintained strong performance, with 100% success on "move object away" and 90% on "set table."

#### 4.3. Impact of RL Post-training

- **Improved Long-Horizon Reasoning:** RL post-training (GRPO) substantially improved performance on challenging long-horizon tasks. For instance, the "shell game" success rate increased from 6.7% (SFT-only) to 60.0%, and "place all red objects in basket" improved from 0.0% to 93.3% (Figure 6). This suggests that RL effectively strengthens recovery behaviors and the ability to handle off-trajectory states by focusing on complex, iterative planning scenarios.

#### 4.4. Role of Closed-Loop Execution and Emergent Recovery

- **Failure Recovery:** The ReAct-style closed-loop execution of Guava-Agent-4B was identified as critical for long-horizon manipulation. It enabled the agent to detect failures, revise plans, and recover from execution errors (Figure 7).
- **Emergent Recovery Behaviors:** The model demonstrated successful recovery from previously unseen real-world execution failures, such as those caused by joint limits or unreachable poses. The agent often generated plausible corrective actions, like replanning target poses or returning to a home configuration before retrying, indicating that recovery emerged from reasoning over execution feedback rather than simply memorizing predefined patterns (Figure 8).
- **State Awareness:** The agent demonstrated awareness of task progress, inferring which subtasks were completed and adapting its actions accordingly, rather than repeating already completed steps.

#### 4.5. Efficiency and Harness Universality

- **Token Efficiency:** Guava-Agent-4B utilized fewer tokens per episode compared to GPT-5.4, indicating improved efficiency (Figure 9).
- **Harness Versatility:** The Guava harness framework also proved effective with other frontier models, such as Gemini-3.1-Pro (89.8% overall success) and Claude-Sonnet-4.6 (88.9% overall success) in simulation (Table 3). However, a smaller 2B parameter open-source model (Qwen3.5-2B) exhibited poor performance (2.2% overall success) due to limitations in instruction following and tool calling, suggesting a minimum base model capability is required.

#### 4.6. Limitations

The authors noted several limitations:
- The current action primitives do not support dexterous manipulation.
- The system cannot directly correct tool-level errors (e.g., invalid grasp proposals, incorrect segmentations), though it can attempt recovery through retries or alternative actions.
- The single-view fixed camera setup is limiting due to occlusion and perspective effects.
- Future work involves scaling the dataset, model size, and toolset for broader tasks and improved performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Guava, a harness framework for embodied manipulation, and contributes to the field by systematically identifying and validating key design principles for robust robot learning with language models. The findings demonstrate several significant implications:

- **Clarification of Effective Harness Design:** The study concretely identifies three critical ingredients for effective embodied agents: iterative perception-reasoning-action loops, semantic action abstractions, and multimodal observations. This provides a structured framework for future development of robot control architectures. The empirical evidence from ablations supports these principles as fundamental for achieving robust, generalizable performance and failure recovery.
- **Empowering Compact Open-Source Models:** A core impact of this work is the demonstration that sophisticated embodied manipulation capabilities, traditionally associated with large, proprietary foundation models, can be distilled into much smaller (4B parameter) open-source models using a limited amount of simulation data (fewer than 2,000 trajectories). This capability reduces the financial and computational barriers to entry for developing and deploying generalist robot agents, fostering wider research and application.
- **Data-Efficient Robot Learning:** The proposed data generation and two-stage training pipeline (SFT followed by targeted GRPO) offers a data-efficient approach to robot learning. By effectively leveraging simulation data, including specially crafted recovery trajectories, the method minimizes the need for extensive and costly real-world demonstrations, which is a persistent challenge in robotics.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** Guava-Agent-4B exhibits strong generalization to previously unseen objects, novel instructions, and long-horizon tasks in both simulation and the real world. Critically, its closed-loop, ReAct-style execution enables robust failure recovery, even from unforeseen real-world execution errors. This indicates that the agent learns to reason about and adapt to dynamic environments rather than merely memorizing fixed action sequences.
- **Scalable and Model-Agnostic Interface:** The research suggests that a well-designed harness like Guava can act as a scalable and model-agnostic interface for embodied manipulation. Its compatibility with various frontier models, combined with the ability to transfer capabilities to compact models, positions it as a versatile foundation for future embodied AI systems.

The Guava framework and its associated training methodology represent a step towards more accessible, robust, and generalizable embodied agents. By demonstrating that high performance can be achieved with modest model sizes and data requirements, this work paves the way for further exploration into complex manipulation tasks, potentially reducing the development cycle and deployment costs for advanced robotic systems in diverse applications. Future work could build upon these identified principles to address current limitations, such as dexterous manipulation, direct tool-level error correction, and multi-view perception.
