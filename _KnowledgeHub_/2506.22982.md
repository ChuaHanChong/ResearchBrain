# Revisiting CroPA: A Reproducibility Study and Enhancements for Cross-Prompt Adversarial Transferability in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A study on Cross-Prompt Attack (CroPA) for Vision-Language Models validates the original framework's claims and introduces methodological enhancements. These improvements boost attack success rates, accelerate convergence, and enhance cross-model and cross-image transferability of adversarial examples.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) face vulnerabilities to adversarial attacks that perturb images to mislead models across various text prompts.
- The foundational Cross-Prompt Attack (CroPA) framework required thorough reproduction and validation to confirm its claims and understand its limitations.
- Existing CroPA methods exhibited limitations in attack success rate, convergence efficiency, and generalizability across diverse images and VLM architectures.

## Method

- A rigorous reproducibility study confirmed ==CroPA's== core claims regarding ==cross-prompt transferability== and performance across prompt numbers and few-shot settings.
- A ==semantically informed noise initialization strategy==, leveraging diffusion models, was developed to guide adversarial perturbations more effectively.
- A novel loss function was introduced to target the ==vision encoder's value vectors==, enhancing architectural sensitivity and improving attack generalization.
- Image augmentation techniques (SCMix, CutMix) were integrated during perturbation generation to improve the ==cross-image transferability== of adversarial examples.

## Results

- Reproducibility experiments validated CroPA's superior targeted ASR (Attack Success Rate) compared to baselines, demonstrating consistent cross-prompt misleading of VLMs.
- The CroPA+Init method, with diffusion-based initialization, achieved a **6-15%** average increase in overall ASR and faster convergence on BLIP-2.
- CroPA+D-UAP (value vector guidance) boosted targeted ASR by over **10%** on BLIP-2 and OpenFlamingo and improved intra-family cross-model transferability by **17-18%**.
- SCMix augmentation increased untargeted cross-image ASR from **43.55%** (base CroPA) to **70.55%**, showing progress in learning universal perturbations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The cross-prompt adversarial transferability of visual perturbations against VLMs is a verifiable and robust vulnerability.
> - Intelligent initialization, rather than random noise, is crucial for improving the efficacy and speed of bi-level adversarial optimization.
> - Targeting specific architectural components, like vision encoder value vectors, can create more potent and transferable adversarial examples, especially within similar VLM families.
> - Data augmentation during adversarial training partially enables the learning of universal perturbations that generalize across multiple images.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advent of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP, BLIP, Flamingo, and LLaVA has revolutionized AI, enabling sophisticated multimodal tasks such as image classification, captioning, and visual question answering by bridging the gap between visual and textual data. However, a significant and well-documented challenge in modern AI is the vulnerability of neural networks to adversarial attacks. These attacks involve subtle, often imperceptible, perturbations to input data that can lead models to make incorrect or harmful predictions.

Initially, adversarial research focused on single-modality models, primarily computer vision models, demonstrating "cross-model transferability" (attacks working across different architectures) and "cross-image transferability" (Universal Adversarial Perturbations, UAPs, working across multiple images). With VLMs, the problem space expands due to the interaction of visual and textual modalities, introducing new attack vectors. Prior works have explored adversarial image attacks on VLMs, textual adversarial perturbations, and methods to manipulate VLMs into executing harmful instructions.

This paper specifically targets a novel and relatively underexplored dimension: **Cross-Prompt Adversarial Transferability**. This concept, introduced by Luo et al. (2024) with their "Cross-Prompt Attack (CroPA)," describes the ability of adversarial images to remain effective across varying textual prompts. Unlike earlier works that treated visual and textual perturbations independently, CroPA employs learnable prompts that antagonistically optimize alongside image perturbations. This establishes a new paradigm for evaluating VLM robustness where the adversarial example's effectiveness is decoupled from specific prompt phrasing.

"Revisiting CroPA" fits into this landscape by:
- **Validating Foundational Work:** It acts as a crucial reproducibility study, confirming the core claims of the original CroPA paper. Reproducibility is vital in scientific research to ensure the reliability and robustness of findings.
- **Extending the State-of-the-Art:** Beyond replication, the paper proposes novel enhancements to CroPA, addressing its limitations and pushing the boundaries of adversarial effectiveness and generalization. This includes improving attack success rates, investigating cross-image transferability within the CroPA framework, and enhancing cross-model transferability by targeting architectural specifics of VLMs.
- **Highlighting VLM Security Concerns:** By demonstrating and improving the robustness of cross-prompt adversarial attacks, the work underscores the practical security implications for real-world VLM deployments, where models must be resilient to diverse and unforeseen inputs.

The paper thus contributes both to the robustness of scientific inquiry through reproduction and to the advancement of adversarial AI by developing more potent and generalizable attack strategies against multimodal models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are twofold: first, to meticulously reproduce and validate the findings of the original Cross-Prompt Attack (CroPA) paper, and second, to extend and enhance the CroPA framework to address identified limitations and expand its applicability.

The specific goals are:

**Reproducibility Study:**
1. **Claim 1: CroPA achieves cross-prompt transferability across various target texts.** This objective aims to confirm that adversarial examples generated by CroPA can consistently mislead VLMs to produce a specific target text, regardless of the accompanying input prompt.
2. **Claim 2: CroPA achieves the best overall performance across different number of prompts.** This seeks to verify that CroPA outperforms baseline methods (Single-P, Multi-P) in maintaining adversarial effectiveness as the diversity and number of prompts increase.
3. **Claim 3: CroPA converges towards higher ASR as the number of iterations increases.** This involves evaluating the attack success rate (ASR) over optimization iterations to confirm the stability and effectiveness of CroPA's training process.
4. **Claim 4: CroPA outperforms baselines under few-shot settings.** This objective investigates CroPA's robustness when additional in-context learning examples are provided, which typically makes adversarial attacks harder.

**Extended Work (Enhancements):**
1. **Better Initialization Strategy:** To propose and evaluate a novel initialization method for adversarial perturbations that significantly improves Attack Success Rate (ASR) and transferability, addressing the limitations of random noise initialization.
2. **Investigating Cross-Image Transferability and Image Augmentation:** To explore the possibility of learning universal perturbations within the CroPA framework that generalize across multiple images, and to assess whether transferability-promoting enhancements (like image augmentation) can aid this cause.
3. **Guiding Perturbations via Target Value Vectors:** To propose a novel loss function that specifically targets the vision encoder's attention mechanisms, particularly value vectors, aiming to improve generalization and ASR by exploiting architectural vulnerabilities.

The overarching motivation behind these objectives is to gain a deeper understanding of adversarial vulnerabilities in VLMs. By reproducing the original work, the authors reinforce the scientific validity of CroPA. The subsequent enhancements are motivated by the need to develop more robust, efficient, and broadly transferable adversarial attacks, which in turn provides critical insights for developing stronger defenses against such threats in real-world VLM applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a comprehensive methodology, beginning with a strict reproduction of the original CroPA framework, followed by the introduction of three distinct enhancement strategies. The experiments were conducted across a suite of prominent Vision-Language Models (VLMs): OpenFlamingo (9B), BLIP-2 (OPT-2.7b), InstructBLIP (Vicuna-7b), and LLaVA (Vicuna-13B), which represent both decoder-only and fusion-based architectural approaches. Images from the MS-COCO validation dataset and both general and specific VQA prompts were used for evaluation. Perturbations were constrained to an L∞-norm budget of 16/255 for visual imperceptibility.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation (Reproduced)

The core problem, as formulated by Luo et al. (2024), extends adversarial attacks to cross-prompt scenarios in VLMs. A VLM is represented by a function `f(xv, xt)` processing visual (`xv`) and textual (`xt`) inputs. An imperceptible adversarial perturbation `δv` is applied to the image, constrained by `∥δv∥p ≤ ϵv`.

Two main attack scenarios are considered:
- **Targeted Attack:** Aims to force the VLM to generate a specific predetermined text `T` for any input prompt. This is achieved by minimizing the language modeling loss `L(f(xv + δv, xit), T)` across multiple prompt instances.
- **Non-Targeted Attack:** Aims to maximize the discrepancy between outputs from clean and adversarial inputs, essentially causing the model to produce *any* incorrect output. This is defined by maximizing `L(f(xv + δv, xit), f(xv, xit))`.

Attack Success Rate (ASR) is the primary metric, requiring exact target text generation for targeted attacks and any deviation for non-targeted attacks.

#### 3.2. Baseline Approaches (Reproduced)

Given the novelty of cross-prompt transferability, the original paper introduced two baselines against which CroPA is evaluated:
- **Single-P:** Optimizes an image perturbation using a single, fixed textual prompt, minimizing `L(f(xv + δv, xt), T)`.
- **Multi-P:** Extends Single-P by using multiple prompts during optimization, minimizing `∑i=1^k L(f(xv + δv, xit), T)`. This aims to improve transferability by exposing the attack to diverse prompts during training.

#### 3.3. Cross-Prompt Attack (CroPA) Mechanism (Reproduced)

CroPA's innovation lies in its min-max optimization strategy (Algorithm 1, Figure 1). It simultaneously optimizes both visual perturbation `δv` and learnable prompt perturbation `δt`, but with opposing objectives:
- `δv` aims to minimize the language modeling loss for generating the target text `T`.
- `δt` aims to maximize this same loss.

This antagonistic relationship forces `δv` to develop stronger transferability across different prompts, as it must overcome the "challenge" posed by `δt`. The optimization is iterative, typically using Projected Gradient Descent (PGD). Visual perturbations are updated more frequently (N times) than prompt perturbations. During evaluation, only the optimized `δv` is applied, and `δt` is discarded, ensuring the attack's effectiveness is intrinsic to the image perturbation.

#### 3.4. Proposed Enhancements: Noise Initialization via Vision Encoding Optimization (Algorithm 2)

Recognizing the limitations of random noise initialization in gradient-based adversarial attacks, especially within CroPA's bi-level min-max optimization framework, the authors propose a semantically informed initialization strategy (Subsection 5.1).
- **Diffusion-Based Target Synthesis:** A state-of-the-art diffusion model (Stable Diffusion XL) generates a target image `x_target` that semantically embodies the desired target prompt `T`.
- **Vision-Encoder Anchored Perturbation:** The initial perturbation `δ_init` is derived by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the vision encoder's feature representation of the perturbed input (`f_v(x + δ)`) and the target image (`f_v(x_target)`) under an L∞ constraint. This ensures the perturbation is semantically aligned from the start.
- **Adversarial Optimization via PGD:** The initialized perturbation is then refined using PGD, where the loss function continues to be MSE between the vision encoder outputs.

This approach addresses the instability and slow convergence of random initialization in saddle-point optimization problems, guiding the perturbation towards semantically meaningful adversarial directions.

#### 3.4.2. Guiding Perturbations via Target Value Vectors (CroPA+D-UAP) (Algorithm 3, Figure 2)

To improve architectural sensitivity and target modality-specific weaknesses in the vision encoder, the authors integrate a modified Doubly-Universal Adversarial Perturbation (D-UAP) loss (Subsection 5.2). CroPA traditionally treats vision and language modalities uniformly. This enhancement focuses on disrupting the vision encoder's core attention mechanism, specifically the **value vectors** in its attention layers.
- **Motivation:** Perturbing value vectors (which hold information within patches) is hypothesized to disrupt the model's interpretative abilities more effectively than generic PGD-based perturbations. This is inspired by prior work identifying crucial components for adversarial influence.
- **Modified Loss Function:** Instead of solely maximizing the deviation of value vectors (as in standard D-UAP), the proposed `L_d-UAP` minimizes the cosine similarity loss between the perturbed image's value vectors and those extracted from a reference image corresponding to the target text `T`.
- **Joint Optimization:** This `L_d-UAP` is combined with the original CroPA loss ( `L_re-CroPA = L_CroPA - λL_d-UAP`), where `λ` controls their relative importance. The text encoder is frozen to ensure perturbations specifically degrade visual processing.

This aims to create more structured, interpretable, and transferable adversarial perturbations by focusing on the vision encoder's representational backbone.

#### 3.4.3. Transferability Across Images via Image Augmentation (SCMix and CutMix) (Algorithm 4, Figure 3)

Addressing CroPA's limitation in cross-image transferability (where perturbations typically overfit to individual images), the authors explore integrating data augmentation techniques during perturbation generation (Subsection 5.3).
- **SCMix (Self-mixing and Cross-mixing):** Diversifies training inputs by blending randomly cropped and resized patches of a base image (self-mixing) and then incorporating features from a secondary image (cross-mixing). This encourages perturbations to attack features invariant to intra-image variations and inter-image blending.
- **CutMix (Regional Replacement):** Generates composite images by cutting rectangular patches from one image and pasting them into another. This forces perturbations to target higher-level, spatially invariant semantic patterns rather than localized artifacts.

The goal is to learn a universal perturbation `δv` that works across a dataset of images, making the attack more computationally efficient and broadly applicable.

#### 3.5. Attack Setup and Threat Models for Transferability Experiments

- **Cross-Model Transferability (using D-UAP):** Evaluated perturbations trained on a source model (e.g., BLIP-2) against different target models (e.g., InstructBLIP, Flamingo, LLaVA). Two scenarios were tested: "intra-family transfer" (models with similar vision encoders like BLIP-2/InstructBLIP) and "cross-architecture transfer" (fundamentally different models like BLIP-2/Flamingo). Untargeted ASR was used for fair comparison across heterogeneous architectures.
- **Cross-Image Transferability (using Augmentation):** Universal perturbations were trained on a limited set of images (and their augmented variants) with white-box access to the target model, and then evaluated on unseen test images. Non-targeted ASR measured the generalization of the attack.
- **Evaluation Rationale:** Non-targeted ASR was chosen for transferability experiments due to the difficulty of pinpointing how perturbations influence final output token embeddings across models with distinct fusion mechanisms (e.g., decoder-only vs. fusion-based VLMs), which significantly impacts targeted attack feasibility. Context Injection Attack (CIA) was excluded from transferability comparisons due to its negligible performance and fundamental limitations in generating transferable perturbations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents results categorized into reproducibility findings and results from the proposed enhancements.

#### 4.1. Reproducibility Study Findings

The authors successfully reproduced and validated the four main claims of the original CroPA paper:

- **Claim 1: CroPA achieves cross-prompt transferability across various target texts (Table 1).** The reproduction confirmed that CroPA generates adversarial examples that effectively mislead the Flamingo VLM across diverse target prompts (e.g., "unknown," "I am sorry," "metaphor"), achieving significantly higher Targeted ASRs compared to Single-P and Multi-P baselines. However, it was noted that CroPA consistently underperforms for abstract target texts like "not sure" or "too late," especially in Classification and Captioning tasks.
- **Claim 2: CroPA achieves the best overall performance across different numbers of prompts (Figure 4).** Experiments showed that CroPA consistently yielded superior ASRs for the same number of prompts compared to baselines. Furthermore, as the number of prompts increased, baseline methods saturated at lower ASRs, failing to surpass CroPA's performance, thus confirming CroPA's robust transferability across prompts.
- **Claim 3: CroPA converges towards higher ASR as the number of iterations increases (Figure 5, Figure 6).** While the overall ASR showed a trend of convergence with increasing iterations (consistent with the original paper's aggregated view), a more granular analysis revealed instability in ASR convergence for individual tasks (VQA, Classification, Captioning). This nuanced finding highlights task-specific vulnerabilities and potential limitations of CroPA's convergence stability.
- **Claim 4: CroPA outperforms baselines under few-shot settings (Table 2).** CroPA maintained significantly better performance than Multi-P when visual in-context learning examples were introduced. This demonstrates CroPA's enhanced robustness to shifts in generation conditions caused by expanded context, confirming the efficacy of its min-max objective in dealing with wider prompt spaces.

#### 4.2. Results Beyond the Original Paper (Enhancements): Noise Initialization via Vision Encoding Optimization (Table 3, Table 4, Figure 7)

The proposed "CroPA+Init" method, employing diffusion-based semantically informed initialization, demonstrated substantial improvements:
- **Increased ASR:** CroPA+Init consistently outperformed both CroPA and Multi-P across all evaluated tasks (VQA general/specific, Classification, Captioning) on BLIP-2. Average gains in overall accuracy ranged from 6% to 15%, achieving an overall ASR of 92.09% compared to CroPA's 79.58% (Table 4).
- **Faster Convergence:** Empirical evidence (Figure 7a) showed that CroPA+Init achieved faster convergence during optimization compared to the base CroPA, validating the hypothesis that a principled starting point aids the bi-level optimization.
- **Semantic Alignment:** Quantifying feature similarity with CLIP scores (Figure 7b) showed a modest but positive increment in target features acquired by CroPA+Init's perturbations, suggesting better proximity to adversarial vulnerable regions from the outset.

#### 4.2.2. Investigating Cross-Image Transferability and Image Augmentation (Table 5)

Integrating image augmentation techniques (SCMix and CutMix) aimed to improve CroPA's cross-image transferability, which was inherently limited by its image-specific optimization:
- **Improved Cross-Image ASR:** Both SCMix and CutMix contributed to measurable improvements in untargeted cross-image ASR compared to base CroPA. SCMix-enhanced CroPA achieved an overall ASR of 70.55%, a significant increase over base CroPA's 43.55%. CutMix also showed improvement, reaching 55.65%.
- **Efficiency:** The augmented methods achieved their highest ASRs significantly earlier in the optimization process (1500-1600 iterations) compared to base CroPA (1700 iterations), suggesting improved efficiency in learning generalizable perturbations.
- **SCMix Outperformance:** SCMix generally outperformed CutMix, likely due to its soft blending (vs. hard masking) and explicit spatial/scale invariance, which encourages perturbations to target more generalized semantic features.
- **Fundamental Limitation Persists:** Despite these gains, the performance gap between these cross-image methods and CroPA applied to a single image indicates that CroPA still struggles with truly universal cross-image generalization, highlighting an inherent trade-off.

#### 4.2.3. Guiding Perturbations via Target Value Vectors (CroPA+D-UAP) (Table 6, Table 7, Table 8, Table D.4.5)

The CroPA+D-UAP framework demonstrated significant advancements in both attack effectiveness and cross-model transferability:
- **Enhanced ASR:** CroPA+D-UAP achieved an impressive overall Targeted ASR of 96.85% on BLIP-2 (Table 6) and 92.70% on OpenFlamingo (Table 7) for challenging target texts like "Bomb," representing a 13.5% and 10.37% increase, respectively, over the base CroPA method. Improvements were particularly strong in VQA_general (+13%) and Captioning (+28%) tasks.
- **Improved Cross-Model Transferability (Table 8):**
 - **Intra-family:** Perturbations trained on BLIP-2 showed notably improved transferability to InstructBLIP (and vice versa), with overall ASR increasing by approximately 17-18% compared to base CroPA. This aligns with the hypothesis that targeting vision encoder components aids transferability between models with similar encoder architectures.
 - **Cross-architecture:** Transferability to fundamentally different architectures like OpenFlamingo or LLaVA remained modest, although CroPA+D-UAP still consistently outperformed CroPA and Multi-P. For instance, BLIP-2 to Flamingo transfer saw a 9% increase.
- **Vision Encoder Specificity (Table D.4.5):** The results confirmed the hypothesis that the method's effectiveness is constrained by the dependency of value vectors on the specific vision encoder architecture. Transferability between models with dissimilar vision encoders (e.g., Flamingo to BLIP-2/InstructBLIP) was negligible, indicating a limitation in achieving truly encoder-agnostic universal perturbations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a multi-faceted contribution to the field of adversarial machine learning, particularly concerning Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Its significance and potential impact span both theoretical understanding and practical security implications.

#### 5.1. Validation and Reinforcement of VLM Vulnerabilities

The successful reproduction of the original CroPA paper's core claims robustly confirms that VLMs are highly vulnerable to cross-prompt adversarial attacks. This validates a critical dimension of VLM insecurity: that a single, subtle visual perturbation can mislead a model to produce a predetermined output regardless of the textual prompt. This finding underscores the need for continued research into VLM robustness and highlights a fundamental challenge for their secure deployment. The observed instability in task-specific ASR convergence during reproduction also offers a more nuanced understanding of CroPA's behavior, suggesting that certain tasks might be inherently more robust or susceptible.

#### 5.2. Advancements in Adversarial Attack Methodology

The proposed enhancements represent significant methodological improvements to the CroPA framework:
- **Improved Attack Efficacy and Efficiency:** The novel **Noise Initialization via Vision Encoding Optimization** substantially boosts Attack Success Rate (ASR) while simultaneously accelerating convergence. This means attackers can generate more effective adversarial examples more quickly, making the attack process more potent and practical.
- **Targeted and Generalizable Perturbations:** The **D-UAP-modified CroPA framework** (guiding perturbations via target value vectors) demonstrates impressive ASR gains and enhanced cross-model transferability, especially between architecturally similar VLMs. By precisely targeting the vision encoder's attention mechanism, the attack becomes more structured and powerful, even against harmful target texts that trigger model guardrails.
- **Progress Towards Universal Adversarial Perturbations (UAPs):** The exploration of **Image Augmentation (SCMix and CutMix)** for cross-image transferability offers a promising direction for generating universal adversarial perturbations within the cross-prompt context. While challenges remain, these techniques show that incorporating input diversity can help overcome the image-specific nature of many gradient-based attacks, paving the way for more efficient and broadly applicable adversarial examples.

#### 5.3. Practical Implications for VLM Security and Privacy

The findings have profound implications for both offensive (attacker) and defensive (defender) perspectives:

- **Attacker Standpoint:** The enhanced CroPA methods provide more robust and versatile tools for manipulating VLMs. Attackers could exploit this to:
 - **Disseminate Misinformation:** Manipulate VLMs into generating false or harmful content irrespective of the input query.
 - **Subvert AI Systems:** Compromise VLM-dependent systems (e.g., content moderation, decision support) by forcing them to produce specific, malicious outputs.
 - **Bypass Safety Mechanisms:** The success of CroPA+D-UAP even with "harmful" target texts like "Bomb" highlights a critical vulnerability in VLM guardrails.

- **Defender Standpoint:** Understanding these advanced attack vectors is crucial for developing resilient VLM defenses:
 - **Robustness Evaluation:** The improved attacks serve as stronger benchmarks for evaluating the adversarial robustness of new VLM architectures.
 - **Defense Strategies:** Insights into how perturbations transfer and which architectural components are most vulnerable (e.g., vision encoder value vectors, initialization sensitivity) can guide the development of more targeted defense mechanisms, such as adversarial training, certified robustness, or input sanitization.
 - **Privacy Enhancement:** The concept of embedding imperceptible perturbations to consistently output non-sensitive text offers a novel mechanism to safeguard sensitive information in personal images from unauthorized VLM analysis. This could be a proactive measure to enhance data privacy.

#### 5.4. Identified Trade-offs and Future Research Directions

The study thoughtfully highlights inherent trade-offs between different types of adversarial transferability:
- **Cross-Prompt vs. Cross-Model:** Optimizing for prompt diversity often anchors perturbations to specific model architectures.
- **Cross-Prompt vs. Cross-Image:** CroPA's strength in cross-prompt transferability comes at the cost of being image-specific, limiting cross-image generalization.
- **Role of Initialization:** Semantically informed initialization can help balance these trade-offs by guiding optimization towards more general adversarial directions.

These trade-offs emphasize that no single attack methodology can maximize all forms of transferability simultaneously. This insight is crucial for future research, which should focus on developing multi-objective optimization approaches or architecturally-aware attack designs that allow for a controlled balance between these competing objectives. The considerable lack of literature in cross-prompt adversarial transferability itself underscores a significant research gap, which this paper helps to fill.

#### 5.5. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge limitations, primarily concerning **computational resources**, which constrained the scale and depth of experiments, particularly in hyperparameter tuning and exploring a wider range of model architectures or ensemble attacks.

Future work is envisioned to:
- **Improve Cross-Model Transferability:** Develop encoder-agnostic methods to overcome the current dependency on similar vision encoder architectures.
- **Enhance Cross-Image Generalization:** Explore more complex approaches beyond simple augmentation, potentially involving latent representation feature-alignment attacks or direct perturbations in the latent space.
- **Practical Applicability:** Implement optimization with query-based strategies, which are more practical in black-box scenarios.
- **Broader Study:** Continue investigating the underexplored domain of cross-prompt adversarial transferability to develop robust VLMs against adversarial manipulations.

In conclusion, "Revisiting CroPA" is a significant contribution that not only validates a crucial finding in VLM adversarial robustness but also extends it with novel, effective enhancements. The work reinforces the urgency of understanding and mitigating adversarial vulnerabilities in VLMs, offering valuable insights for both offense and defense in the evolving landscape of AI security.
