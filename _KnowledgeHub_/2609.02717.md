# MV-dVRK: A Multi-Viewpoint Benchmark for Spatial Surgical Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> MV-dVRK is the first ex-vivo surgical dataset combining three exposure-synchronized stereo endoscopic viewpoints with dense SfM reference geometry and camera poses, validated to 0.6 mm against an industrial 3D scanner. It benchmarks zero-shot monocular, stereo, multi-stereo, and multi-view reconstruction as viewpoint count grows, showing geometry-refined multi-view methods reach 67% surface coverage within 1 mm at three viewpoints.

## Problem

- Clinical surgical telerobots carry only one stereo endoscope, so reconstructing occluded or unseen anatomy from a single viewpoint stays fundamentally ill-posed.
- Existing surgical datasets lack exposure-synchronized multiple viewpoints, and those with several cameras use software or capture-card timing plus marker-only poses without dense tissue geometry.
- Sparse multi-view foundation models are trained on data where endoscopic imagery is under-represented, so their reliability on real surgical scenes was never rigorously measured.

## Method

- Extends the da Vinci Research Kit with a second robot-controlled stereo endoscope plus a fixed third, custom global-shutter cameras, hardware-triggered exposure and ==PTP clock alignment== to microseconds.
- Builds dense reference geometry with the ==SfM== stage of GGPT, using ==VGGT== initialization and ==RoMa v2== dense correspondences, yielding over 14 million points per scene, externally validated by an Artec Eva scanner.
- Defines a benchmark protocol with fitted versus camera-anchored ==Sim(3) registration== and three metrics: 1 mm ground-truth coverage, pixel-aligned mean 3D error in a shared reference view, and relative pose error.

## Results

- Stereo beats monocular from the same viewpoint by **27 coverage points**; even **50** accumulated monocular frames leave stereo ahead by **15 points** across **6 of 8** scenes.
- Adding a second stereo viewpoint adds **14 coverage points** and a third adds **7.2** more, with registered multi-stereo most accurate in the two-endoscope reference view.
- At six synchronized views, geometry-refined methods reach **66-67%** coverage within 1 mm and **1.1-1.2 mm** error, versus **28-44%** for feed-forward foundation models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal accumulation from one moving camera cannot substitute for simultaneous viewpoints; occluded geometry needs genuine spatial diversity, not more frames.
> - Explicit geometric optimization pays off only once enough cross-view support exists; below that threshold, calibrated stereo priors dominate learned multi-view priors.
> - Reconstruction failures track co-visibility: with partial overlap, models fall back on learned priors and hallucinate geometry, which is the risk in constrained surgical rigs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate three-dimensional (3D) geometric reconstruction is a critical component for advancing robot-assisted minimally invasive surgery (RAMIS). It can facilitate advanced visualization tools for surgeons, enable geometry-aware robotic assistance, support autonomy, and enhance visuomotor policy learning. However, current clinical telerobots typically deploy only a single stereo camera inside the patient, which fundamentally limits the geometric coverage and spatial awareness of the surgical scene. This single viewpoint often leads to ambiguities in reconstructing unseen anatomy and challenges in handling occlusions and tissue deformation effectively.

The broader research landscape in surgical 3D perception has largely been shaped by this single-viewpoint constraint. Efforts have focused on recovering geometry from monocular video or single stereo pairs, with recent advancements in learned approaches improving robustness and generalization. However, even sophisticated single-view methods face inherent limitations due to the ill-posed nature of reconstructing complex, deforming anatomy from limited observations.

Multi-viewpoint imaging is recognized as a natural progression to address these limitations by improving both geometric coverage and spatial awareness. Yet, the physical constraints of minimally invasive surgery mean that only a sparse number of cameras can be deployed, often with large baselines and limited overlap. This sparse multi-view setting presents significant challenges for modern 3D reconstruction algorithms.

A key bottleneck for advancing multi-view surgical perception has been the scarcity of suitable datasets. Existing surgical datasets for 3D reconstruction generally suffer from several limitations:
- **Single viewpoint:** Most provide data from only one endoscope.
- **Lack of synchronization:** Datasets with multiple cameras often lack exposure synchronization, which is crucial for precise geometric evaluation, especially of deforming scenes.
- **Limited ground truth:** Accurate dense reference geometry of the tissue surface and precise camera poses are rare, often relying on less accurate methods like robot forward kinematics or requiring disruptive external scanning.
- **Limited diversity:** Datasets may not cover a wide range of surgical tasks, tissue appearances, or camera configurations.

This paper addresses this specific gap by introducing MV-dVRK, a novel dataset and benchmark designed to rigorously evaluate sparse multi-view 3D reconstruction methods on real ex-vivo surgical imagery. It provides a unique combination of features (multiple independently moving and exposure-synchronized stereo viewpoints, accurate dense surface geometry, and camera poses) that distinguishes it from prior work and allows for systematic analysis of multi-view perception in surgical contexts. By providing this benchmark, the work aims to bridge the gap between advanced 3D reconstruction techniques developed in computer vision and their application and evaluation within the unique constraints of robotic surgery.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged limitations of single-viewpoint 3D perception in robot-assisted minimally invasive surgery and the potential benefits of multi-viewpoint imaging. Despite significant advancements in sparse multi-view 3D reconstruction techniques within computer vision, their rigorous evaluation on real endoscopic images relevant to surgery has been hindered by a lack of appropriate datasets.

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

1. **To develop and present MV-dVRK, a multi-viewpoint surgical dataset:** The researchers aimed to create the first ex-vivo surgical dataset that integrates several critical features simultaneously. This includes combining multiple exposure-synchronized stereo viewpoints (three in total, two of which are robot-controlled) with accurate ground-truth surface geometry and camera poses. The dataset was designed to include both static scenes, providing dense reference geometry and camera poses validated against an industrial 3D scanner, and dynamic sequences that capture tissue deformation and camera motion under real surgical tasks. This addresses the critical need for high-fidelity, synchronized multi-view data with reliable ground truth in the surgical domain.

2. **To establish a sparse-view 3D reconstruction benchmark:** A core objective was to provide a standardized protocol for systematically evaluating different 3D reconstruction methods in a multi-view surgical context. This benchmark is designed to compare monocular, stereo, multi-stereo, and multi-view approaches as the number of synchronized input viewpoints varies from one to three. By using a consistent reference, input subsets, registration protocol, and evaluation metrics, the benchmark allows for objective and comparable assessment of method performance.

3. **To analyze and characterize the performance of current multi-view 3D perception methods:** The research sought to systematically investigate how reconstruction coverage, geometric accuracy, and camera-pose estimation evolve with an increasing number of viewpoints and varying view overlap. This objective aimed to gain insights into the strengths and weaknesses of different reconstruction paradigms (e.g., feed-forward foundation models vs. geometry-refined optimization-based methods) when applied to sparse multi-view endoscopic imagery. The goal was to identify which types of methods are most effective under specific conditions relevant to surgical constraints and to highlight promising avenues for future development in practical multi-view surgical perception.

In essence, the overarching motivation was to overcome the existing data scarcity, provide a robust evaluation framework, and contribute foundational insights into the capabilities of multi-view 3D reconstruction for enhancing spatial perception in robotic surgery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the development of a specialized data acquisition platform, the creation of a comprehensive dataset, and the establishment of a rigorous benchmark protocol for 3D reconstruction evaluation.

#### 3.1. Data Acquisition Platform

The MV-dVRK platform is an extension of the da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK). This customized setup includes:
- **Multiple Endoscopes:** Two stereo endoscopes are mounted on independent robotic arms, allowing for flexible positioning and large baselines. A third stereo endoscope is fixed, resulting in a total of three synchronized stereo viewpoints.
- **High-Resolution Cameras:** Custom-built endoscopes incorporate surgical-grade optics and industrial global-shutter cameras.
- **Exposure Synchronization:** A shared hardware trigger simultaneously exposes all six imaging sensors. Precision Time Protocol (PTP) is used to align camera clocks, achieving microsecond-level timestamp agreement across all cameras. This ensures that all viewpoints capture the same spatio-temporal state, which is crucial for evaluating deforming scenes.
- **Calibration:** Intrinsic and stereo calibrations were performed using ChArUco boards, yielding sub-pixel reprojection errors. Robot kinematics are logged at 100 Hz on the PTP network.

#### 3.2. Dataset Collection and Composition

The MV-dVRK dataset was collected during mock surgery on freshly excised abdominal organs from two adult pigs. Expert visceral surgeons performed representative surgical tasks such as cholecystectomy, tubular dissection, and small-bowel anastomosis. The dataset is divided into two subsets:

- **MV-dVRK I (Static):** This subset comprises eight geometric reference scenes. For these scenes, instruments were locked, and robotic endoscopes swept over stationary tissue. This allowed for the acquisition of data suitable for dense multi-view Structure-from-Motion (SfM) to recover reference tissue-surface geometry and camera poses. From each scene, 10 synchronized keyframes were selected to maximize diversity in relative camera poses, resulting in 80 static test instances, each with three stereo viewpoints. This subset forms the quantitative core of the paper, providing reference geometry, camera poses, and sparse synchronized inputs.
- **MV-dVRK II (Dynamic):** This subset contains ten long multi-view sequences recorded during active surgical manipulation. It includes synchronized dVRK kinematics and per-scene calibration offsets to link robot measurements to the endoscopes' optical frames. These sequences capture significant tool-tissue interaction and tissue deformation from both static and moving viewpoints, providing data for future research on dynamic surgical 3D perception, novel-view synthesis, and camera-pose estimation.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Design

MV-dVRK I provides the common protocol for evaluating sparse 3D reconstruction.
- **Input Variation:** Methods are evaluated on subsets of the synchronized inputs, varying the number of viewpoints from one to three. A viewpoint can contribute either a monocular image or a calibrated stereo pair, depending on the reconstruction family.
- **Consistency:** All predictions are evaluated against the same dense reference reconstruction, using the same sparse test inputs, registration protocol, and evaluation metrics.

#### 3.4. Dense Reference Reconstruction

- **Rationale:** To provide accurate dense tissue-surface geometry and camera poses, the reference was constructed directly from dense multi-view SfM over the static acquisition sequences. This approach avoids the difficulty of deploying external scanners during surgical acquisition and ensures pixel-aligned comparison with test images.
- **Process:** The SfM stage of GGPT was used. This pipeline leverages cameras and points predicted by VGGT for initialization and RoMaV2 for dense correspondences. A global bundle adjustment optimizes camera poses, followed by 3D point triangulation and filtering. This method proved more robust for endoscopic imagery, yielding over 14 million points per scene compared to classical SfM.
- **External Validation:** The dense SfM reconstructions were independently validated against an industrial 3D scanner (Artec Eva). Scaled ICP alignment showed a mean point-to-surface distance of 0.6 ± 0.4 mm, supporting sub-millimeter accuracy of the reference geometry.

#### 3.5. Synchronized Sparse Test Set

- For the 80 test samples (10 per scene), configurations were chosen to cover diverse relative endoscope poses, view overlap, illumination, and tool poses (Figure 2).
- E1L (left camera of endoscope 1) served as the common reference view for pixel-aligned 3D error due to its highest reference completeness.

#### 3.6. Prediction-to-Reference Registration

To account for different coordinate systems and unknown global scales, predicted reconstructions are aligned to the reference:
- **Fitted Registration:** A robust Sim(3) transformation is estimated using PyCOLMAP’s `estimate_sim3d_robust` function. This uses direct 3D correspondences between predicted and reference points corresponding to the same E1L pixels.
- **Camera-Anchored Registration:** For methods that jointly predict geometry and camera poses, the predicted E1L camera pose defines the rotation and origin of the alignment, with only the global scale estimated from robust 3D inliers. This transformation is consistently applied to all predicted geometry and camera poses.

#### 3.7. Metrics

Three complementary metrics are used for evaluation:
- **Ground-truth Coverage (Cov):** Measures the percentage of reference surface points that have a predicted neighbor within a 1 mm (primary) or 5 mm (secondary) distance threshold.
- **Mean 3D Error (ME):** Reports the Euclidean distance between registered predicted points and their pixel-aligned reference counterparts in the E1L (and sometimes E2L) field of view.
- **Relative Pose Error (Pose):** Quantifies the accuracy of predicted relative transforms between the left cameras of different endoscopes, reporting translation error in millimeters and geodesic rotation error in degrees.

#### 3.8. Statistical Analysis

The eight scenes serve as the units of statistical analysis. For pairwise comparisons, scene-level differences (∆) are computed, and their mean, 95% confidence interval (t7), and consistency across scenes (k/8) are reported. Mean values over all 80 test samples are provided for descriptive purposes in tables. The generalizability of observed effect magnitudes to new specimens or in-vivo conditions is acknowledged as a limitation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The benchmark analysis provided quantitative and qualitative insights into the performance of various 3D reconstruction paradigms as the number of synchronized viewpoints increased.

#### 4.1. Single-Viewpoint Reconstruction

- **Stereo vs. Monocular:** Dense stereo reconstruction (FoundationStereo) demonstrated substantially greater scene coverage compared to monocular reconstruction (Endo3R) from the same endoscopic viewpoint. FoundationStereo (using a two-frame input) achieved a 27 percentage point higher coverage than two-frame Endo3R (with a 95% confidence interval across all 8 scenes).
- **Temporal Accumulation:** Even with temporal accumulation over 50 monocular frames, Endo3R did not fully bridge the gap, with FoundationStereo retaining a 15-point coverage advantage (95% CI [5.8, 25] across 6/8 scenes). This indicates that simultaneous stereo observations provide geometric information not fully recoverable from extended temporal monocular observations.

#### 4.2. Multi-Stereo Reconstruction

- **Coverage Gains:** Adding a second endoscope significantly increased coverage by exposing previously occluded anatomy. Registering a second stereo reconstruction increased coverage by 14 percentage points (95% CI [7.2, 21] across all 8 scenes). A third viewpoint further contributed a 7.2-point gain (95% CI [5.2, 9.3] across all 8 scenes).
- **Consistency Challenges:** While individual stereo reconstructions maintained local accuracy, cross-endoscope registration could introduce duplicated structures and other inconsistencies, as illustrated in qualitative results (Fig. 4e).

#### 4.3. Multi-View Reconstruction

- **Joint Reconstruction Benefits:** Unlike multi-stereo methods that independently reconstruct and then register, multi-view methods jointly reconstruct all input images in a common geometric frame. This allows additional viewpoints to directly improve the overall reconstruction.
- **Error Reduction with More Views:** For geometry-refined multi-view methods, adding the third viewpoint consistently reduced the mean 3D error in the common reference view E1L by an average of 1.9 mm (95% CI [1.2, 2.6] across all 8 scenes), representing a 54% relative improvement. The effect on E2L was more variable, suggesting dependence on inter-endoscope geometry and co-visibility.

#### 4.4. Crossover in Performance with Viewpoint Count

The benchmark revealed a clear transition in performance superiority based on the number of available viewpoints:
- **Two Endoscopes (4 synchronized views):** Registered multi-stereo (FoundationStereo + Sim(3) registration) demonstrated superior accuracy in E1L compared to all evaluated multi-view methods. It achieved the highest coverage of 50% at 1mm tolerance.
- **Three Endoscopes (6 synchronized views):** Geometry-refined multi-view reconstruction methods, specifically Depth Anything 3 + GGPT, surpassed multi-stereo in E1L accuracy and achieved the highest overall coverage. These refined methods reached 66-67% coverage within a 1 mm tolerance, significantly outperforming feed-forward foundation models (which achieved 28-44% coverage in the same setting). This indicates that the benefits of explicit geometric optimization become most pronounced when sufficient cross-view support is available.

#### 4.5. Role of View Overlap

- **Sparse Geometric Support:** Reconstruction failures, particularly for multi-view methods, were strongly correlated with sparse geometric support. Reduced co-visibility between viewpoints, even at comparable working distances, was associated with increased reconstruction error for most methods.
- **Learned Priors vs. Constraints:** In regions with partial overlap and lacking strong cross-view geometric constraints, methods reliant on learned priors from point transformers tended to produce less accurate 3D predictions (Fig. 5c).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MV-dVRK dataset and benchmark represent a significant contribution to the field of robotic surgery and computer vision.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical Data Gap

MV-dVRK is the first ex-vivo surgical dataset to concurrently provide multiple robot-controlled, exposure-synchronized stereo viewpoints with validated dense reference surface geometry and accurate camera poses. This unique combination addresses a long-standing data scarcity, enabling more rigorous and realistic evaluation of 3D reconstruction algorithms than previously possible.

#### 5.2. Enabling Rigorous Evaluation and Comparative Analysis

The established benchmark protocol provides a standardized and controlled environment for systematically comparing various 3D reconstruction paradigms—monocular, stereo, multi-stereo, and multi-view—across different viewpoint counts on real surgical imagery. This allows researchers to quantitatively assess the trade-offs and performance characteristics of methods, fostering targeted development.

#### 5.3. Generating Insights into Multi-View Surgical Perception

The results provide crucial insights into the capabilities and limitations of current 3D perception methods in a sparse multi-view surgical setting.
- **Complementary Strengths:** The findings highlight that calibrated stereo methods are particularly effective in the sparsest two-endoscope scenario, providing robust local geometry. Conversely, geometry-refined multi-view methods demonstrate superior performance, especially in coverage and overall accuracy, when a third viewpoint provides sufficient cross-view support. This suggests that future surgical perception systems could benefit from hybrid approaches that leverage both paradigms.
- **Importance of Geometric Optimization:** The clear performance advantage of geometry-refined methods over feed-forward foundation models with increased viewpoints underscores the importance of explicit geometric optimization in achieving high accuracy and coverage in surgical environments.
- **Challenges of Sparse Overlap:** The identified issue of increased reconstruction error in regions with limited view overlap points to a key challenge that needs to be addressed in future research, potentially through improved learned priors or more robust sparse-view handling.

#### 5.4. Guiding Future Research Directions

The work points towards several promising avenues for future research:
- **Rig-Aware Optimization:** Developing optimization techniques that integrate the known geometry of individual stereo endoscopes with a joint refinement of scene structure and relative poses across multiple endoscopes could lead to more robust and accurate reconstructions.
- **Real-time Performance:** Practical deployment in surgery will require lower-latency dense matching and optimization, which is a critical area for ongoing development.
- **Dynamic Scene Reconstruction:** The MV-dVRK II (dynamic) subset explicitly provides a foundation for future research into multi-viewpoint surgical 3D perception under camera motion and tissue deformation, which remains a highly challenging problem.

#### 5.5. Limitations

The paper also acknowledges several limitations:
- **Ground Truth for Dynamic Scenes:** The dense reference geometry is available only for static tissue in MV-dVRK I; obtaining comparable ground truth for deforming tissue in MV-dVRK II remains impractical.
- **External Pose Validation:** SfM-estimated camera poses are not externally validated due to the difficulty of avoiding manual and optical calibration biases.
- **Generalizability:** The static benchmark comprises eight scenes from two porcine specimens and three surgical tasks. The measured effect sizes may not directly transfer to new anatomies, procedures, or in-vivo conditions.
- **Ex-vivo Conditions:** The ex-vivo, open acquisition environment with additional illumination differs from the in-vivo, closed abdominal cavity typically encountered in surgery.

Despite these limitations, MV-dVRK provides a foundational resource for advancing spatial surgical perception, offering a clear path for evaluating and developing next-generation 3D reconstruction technologies for robot-assisted surgery.
