# ProcVLM: Learning Procedure-Grounded Progress Rewards for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ProcVLM is a vision-language model that learns dense progress rewards explicitly tied to procedural task stages in robotic manipulation. It enhances embodied procedural understanding and improves downstream policy learning through better feedback, demonstrating strong one-shot generalization and stabilizing training with noisy real-robot data.

## Problem

- Existing large-scale robot datasets lack dense, procedural progress supervision, typically providing only sparse, trajectory-level success labels.
- Current reward models often rely on sparse outcomes or time-based interpolation, which inaccurately represents progress in complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- There is a need for transferable dense rewards explicitly grounded in the semantic procedural structure of a task, rather than relying on unreliable temporal proxies.

## Method

- **ProcVLM**, a ==progress-aware vision-language model==, was developed to learn and predict ==procedure-grounded progress== as a dense reward signal.
- A scalable, automated pipeline was created to synthesize frame-level procedural annotations (==ProcCorpus-60M==) from raw robot trajectories using large VLMs.
- Continuous progress targets were defined by combining ==subtask-level structure== with intra-subtask visual motion, and the model was pretrained with a ==two-stage strategy== (ProcVQA).

## Results

- **ProcVLM** achieved **superior performance** on the ProcVQA benchmark for embodied procedural understanding, securing the highest **BF1@5** for action segmentation and **VOC/EPR@50** for task progress estimation.
- It demonstrated strong one-shot generalization on unseen RoboFAC tasks, improving **VOC_succ by 85.7%** and reducing **MAE_fail by 38.0%** compared to zero-shot baselines.
- ProcVLM-guided reward fine-tuning yielded significant gains in success rates for downstream policy learning (e.g., **+25.0 pp** in real-robot Stack-Bowls task), stabilizing training with noisy teleoperation data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ProcVLM utilizes a 'reasoning-before-estimation' paradigm, inferring current execution stages and remaining atomic actions before predicting a continuous progress score.
> - Dense, continuous progress labels can be automatically derived by combining subtask-level structural duration with local visual changes, offering more nuanced feedback than temporal proxies.
> - Automated, hierarchical synthesis of frame-level procedural annotations enables the creation of large-scale, semantically rich datasets (ProcCorpus-60M) for training progress-aware VLMs.
