# Hybrid Reinforcement: When Reward Is Sparse, It's Better to Be Dense

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hybrid Ensemble Reward Optimization (HERO) framework integrates sparse, deterministic verifier signals with dense, continuous reward model scores to enhance large language model reasoning. This method, from FAIR at Meta and the University of Wisconsin–Madison, consistently outperforms both verifier-only and reward model-only baselines on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, achieving up to +11.7 points over RM-only training on hard-to-verify tasks, by providing stable and informative supervisory signals.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) suffers from reward sparsity and brittleness, leading to stalled learning when all generated responses are uniformly correct or incorrect.
- Reward Models (RMs) provide dense, continuous scores but can be noisy and misaligned with true correctness, potentially destabilizing training.
- Neither sparse, reliable signals nor dense, noisy signals alone are sufficient for robustly guiding Large Language Models (LLMs) on complex reasoning tasks, especially those that are hard to verify.

## Method

- ==HERO (Hybrid Ensemble Reward Optimization)== combines sparse rule-based verifiers with dense reward models using a two-pronged approach.
- ==Stratified normalization== partitions responses into correct/incorrect groups based on verifier feedback, then normalizes reward model scores within each group to ensure correct responses always score higher while retaining nuance.
- ==Variance-aware weighting== dynamically adjusts prompt importance during training, up-weighting challenging, high-variance prompts to focus learning on more informative examples.

## Results

- HERO consistently outperformed RM-only and verifier-only baselines across all training regimes and task types on mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- On hard-to-verify tasks, HERO achieved an average of **66.3** accuracy for Qwen3-4B-Base when trained on easy-to-verify data, representing a **+11.7 point** improvement over RM-only (**54.6**) and **+9.2 points** over verifier-only (**57.1**) training.
- Ablation studies confirmed that dense negative ranges and variance-aware reweighting were critical, with variance-aware reweighting adding **+3.8 points**, and that larger reward models (72B vs 7B) provided only marginal or no additional benefits, highlighting HERO's efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating sparse and dense reward signals through structured methods (stratified normalization) provides more effective gradients and prevents learning from stalling, especially when rule-based rewards are uniform.
> - Providing dense rewards within the 'negative' (incorrect) response range is crucial for stabilizing training and improving learning efficiency by guiding the model to better distinguish among errors.
> - Dynamically reweighting prompts based on the variance of candidate response scores (variance-aware weighting) improves robustness and generalization by prioritizing ambiguous and challenging examples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Positioning within the Research Landscape

This work is situated at the forefront of post-training methodologies for large language models, specifically within the paradigm of **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)** or similar reward-guided optimization strategies. As LLMs grow in scale and capability, fine-tuning them for specific complex tasks, such as mathematical reasoning, becomes crucial. The paper addresses a core challenge in this domain: how to design effective and robust reward signals to guide LLM policy improvement.

The current landscape for LLM post-training often relies on two primary types of reward signals:
1. **Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** This approach uses deterministic checkers (e.g., unit tests, exact string matches, symbolic equivalence checks) to provide binary (0 or 1) feedback on the correctness of an LLM's output. Systems like DeepSeek-R1 have leveraged this effectively for reasoning tasks. While highly reliable and unambiguous when applicable, RLVR suffers from *sparsity* and *brittleness*. Many tasks allow for partially correct answers or alternative valid formats that these strict verifiers fail to recognize (false negatives), leading to a "all-or-nothing" supervision. If all generated responses for a prompt receive the same binary label (all 0s or all 1s), relative advantage methods (like GRPO) yield zero policy gradients, stalling learning.
2. **Reward Models (RMs):** These are learned models that provide *dense*, continuous scores indicating the quality or preference of an LLM's response. RMs are typically trained on human preference data or synthetic data. They can capture nuances like partial correctness, clarity, or proximity to ground truth, offering richer supervisory signals. However, RMs can be *noisy* and *misaligned*, sometimes assigning high scores to incorrect responses or low scores to correct ones, which can destabilize training.

The paper argues that neither approach alone is sufficient, particularly for complex reasoning tasks that demand both precision and nuance, or for "hard-to-verify" scenarios where deterministic checkers fall short. This work builds upon and critiques existing methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which aims to alleviate gradient sparsity in RLVR but still struggles when all rollouts are uniformly labeled. It also engages with the emergent field of "LLM-as-a-judge" or model-based verifiers (e.g., TIGER-Lab/general-verifier), acknowledging their utility but highlighting their limitations in coverage and potential for sparsity.

By proposing a hybrid framework, the authors position their work as a critical advancement towards more robust and generalizable LLM reasoning. They seek to overcome the limitations of both sparse, reliable signals and dense, noisy signals, offering a more effective way to combine these complementary strengths.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a novel reinforcement learning framework that effectively integrates the strengths of sparse, deterministic verifiers and dense, continuous reward models to improve the reasoning capabilities of large language models. The authors named this framework **HERO (Hybrid Ensemble Reward Optimization)**.

The motivation stems from two critical limitations observed in the current LLM post-training landscape:

1. **The Brittleness and Sparsity of Verifier-only Rewards:**
 - **All-or-Nothing Feedback:** Deterministic verifiers provide binary (0-1) signals, which are reliable but lack nuance. They often under-credit partially correct solutions or alternative valid answers, leading to "false negatives."
 - **Gradient Sparsity:** In many scenarios, especially for difficult prompts, all generated responses might either fail or pass the verifier. In such "saturated" batches, group-relative RL algorithms (like GRPO) compute zero relative advantage, leading to stalled policy improvement. This means valuable learning opportunities from nuanced differences between incorrect (or even correct) responses are missed.
 - **Skewed Optimization:** The brittleness biases optimization towards easier, strictly verifiable cases, leaving the hardest and most informative prompts underutilized.

2. **The Noisiness and Misalignment of Reward Model-only Rewards:**
 - **Lack of Grounding:** Reward models, trained on preferences, provide continuous scores but can sometimes be misaligned with true correctness. They might assign high scores to incorrect solutions or low scores to correct ones, making them unreliable as a sole source of supervision.
 - **Destabilization:** Naively blending dense RM signals with binary verifier outputs can introduce noise and destabilize training, undermining the benefits of both.

Therefore, the key motivation is to design a hybrid reward mechanism that retains the **reliability and correctness guarantees of deterministic verifiers** while simultaneously leveraging the **richness and nuance of continuous reward models**. The goal is to create a supervisory signal that is both stable and informative, particularly for challenging reasoning tasks where traditional methods fail to provide meaningful gradients or suffer from misguidance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HERO (Hybrid Ensemble Reward Optimization) is designed to address the aforementioned challenges by structurally integrating sparse verifier signals with dense reward model scores. The framework comprises two primary innovations: **stratified normalization** and **variance-aware weighting**.

1. **Stratified Normalization:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** The challenge of combining binary (0 or 1) verifier outputs with continuous reward model scores without destabilizing training or losing the crucial correctness distinction. A naive combination leads to misalignment.
 - **Approach:** HERO first partitions responses into two distinct groups based on the rule-based verifier's output: `r_rule = 0` (incorrect) and `r_rule = 1` (correct). Within each of these groups, the continuous scores from the reward model (`r_RM`) are then subjected to min-max normalization.
 - **Formulation:** The final normalized reward `ˆr(x, y)` is calculated as:
 - If `r_rule = 0`: `−α + 2α · (r_RM − min r_RM) / (max r_RM − min r_RM + ϵ)`
 - If `r_rule = 1`: `(1 − β) + 2β · (r_RM − min r_RM) / (max r_RM − min r_RM + ϵ)`
 - Here, `α` and `β` are hyperparameters (between 0 and 1) that control the allowable ranges for scores within the incorrect and correct groups, respectively. `ϵ` is a small positive constant to prevent division by zero.
 - **Effect:** This scheme ensures that incorrect responses always receive a lower score than correct responses, preserving the fundamental correctness semantics dictated by the verifier. Simultaneously, within each group, the reward model provides fine-grained distinctions. For example, among incorrect responses, some might be "less incorrect" than others, receiving a higher normalized score within the `[-α, α]` range. Similarly, among correct responses, the reward model differentiates based on quality within the `[1-β, 1+β]` range. This introduces meaningful gradient flow even in batches where the rule-based verifier gives all 0s or all 1s, mitigating sparsity.

2. **Variance-Aware Weighting:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Not all prompts are equally informative. Easy prompts, where most responses are uniformly correct or incorrect, provide little additional learning signal. Challenging prompts, where candidate responses vary widely in quality, offer richer learning opportunities. Traditional RL (including GRPO) treats all prompts uniformly, leading to inefficient resource allocation.
 - **Approach:** HERO introduces a dynamic weighting mechanism that adjusts the contribution of each prompt based on the variance of reward-model scores across its generated candidate responses. High variance implies greater uncertainty and thus a more informative signal.
 - **Formulation:** For each prompt, `σ_u` is the standard deviation of reward-model scores for its candidate responses. A running mean `¯σ` of these standard deviations is maintained. The weighting function `w_difficulty(σ_u)` is defined using a bounded monotone sigmoid function: `w_min + (w_max − w_min) · 1 / (1 + exp(−k(σ_u − ¯σ)))`.
 - `w_min` and `w_max` set the minimum and maximum weights (e.g., 0.5 to 2.0).
 - `k` controls the steepness of the transition.
 - **Effect:** This mechanism up-weights difficult, high-variance prompts, emphasizing them during training. Trivial, low-variance prompts are down-weighted, preventing wasted computational capacity. The final shaped reward `r_final(x, y)` is `w_difficulty(σ_u) · ˆr(x, y)`. This ensures that learning effort is focused on the most challenging and informative parts of the dataset.

The underlying reinforcement learning algorithm used for policy optimization is **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, which is an extension of RLVR designed to stabilize learning by comparing groups of candidate solutions and assigning relative advantages. HERO builds upon GRPO by providing a more refined and informative reward signal to it.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

HERO was rigorously evaluated across diverse mathematical reasoning benchmarks, using two different LLM backbones (Qwen3-4B-Base and OctoThinker-8B-Hybrid-Base) and three training regimes: easy-to-verify, hard-to-verify, and mixed samples. The evaluation focused on both easy-to-verify and hard-to-verify test sets.

**Consistent Outperformance:**
- **Across Training Regimes and Task Types:** HERO consistently outperformed both RM-only and verifier-only baselines in all training settings (easy-to-verify, hard-to-verify, mixed) and on both easy-to-verify and hard-to-verify evaluation tasks.
- **Quantifiable Gains (Qwen3-4B-Base):** When trained on easy-to-verify data and evaluated on hard-to-verify tasks (HVM, TBR), HERO achieved an average of 66.3, a substantial +11.7 points over RM-only training (54.6) and +9.2 points over verifier-only training (57.1). Even when trained on hard-to-verify samples, HERO still led with 56.5 compared to 53.7 (RM-only) and 54.2 (rule-based). Mixed training also showed clear advantages for HERO.
- **Robustness Across Model Scales (OctoThinker-8B-Hybrid-Base):** For the weaker OctoThinker-8B-Hybrid-Base model, HERO delivered even larger *relative* gains, improving absolute scores significantly across the board, demonstrating its broad applicability and effectiveness for different backbone models.

**Specific Observations:**
- **Verifier-only Training Struggles on Hard-to-Verify Tasks:** As hypothesized, rule-based verifiers performed poorly on hard-to-verify tasks. For Qwen3-4B-Base, verifier-only training achieved 47.4 on hard-to-verify tasks, which was lower than both HERO (56.5) and RM-only (53.7), and even below the SFT baseline (47.1). This highlights the brittleness of pure binary rewards when faced with nuanced or open-ended problems, leading to stalled learning due to zero gradients.
- **Hybrid Reward Surpasses Model-Based Verifiers:** HERO also outperformed other state-of-the-art model-based verifiers (General Reasoner, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) across all training regimes, suggesting that structured reward integration is more effective than relying on larger, singular verifier models.
- **Naive Reward Combination is Ineffective:** Ablation studies showed that a simple, naive combination of rule-based and reward model signals without the structured stratified normalization performed significantly worse than HERO, confirming the necessity of HERO's careful integration approach.
- **Reward Hacking in RMs:** An analysis demonstrated that reward models on hard-to-verify samples exhibit rapid reward mean increases but catastrophic collapses in actual accuracy, indicating "reward hacking" where the RM learns spurious correlations rather than genuine reasoning. HERO’s approach counteracts this.

**Ablation Study Insights:**
- **Dense Negative Ranges are Crucial:** Providing dense rewards within the *negative* (incorrect) group was found to be more critical for stabilizing training and improving learning efficiency than dense rewards in the positive group. This indicates that penalizing diverse reasoning errors more effectively guides the model.
- **Reward Range Selection is Task-Dependent:** Smaller α, β ranges (e.g., 0.05) were optimal for easy-to-verify tasks, preserving verifier precision. Larger ranges (e.g., 0.1 or 0.2) were better for mixed or hard-to-verify tasks, providing richer signals from the reward model.
- **Variance-Aware Reweighting is Effective:** This component consistently improved performance, especially on hard-to-verify tasks (+3.8 points), confirming that dynamically focusing training on ambiguous, high-variance prompts leads to more robust and generalizable improvements.
- **Reward Model Size Not Critical for HERO's Gains:** Replacing the 7B reward model with a much larger 72B reward model yielded only marginal improvements or even slight underperformance. This significant finding demonstrates that HERO's benefits stem from its intelligent hybrid reward formulation, not merely from scaling the underlying reward model, implying better efficiency and deployability with compact RMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Hybrid Reinforcement: When Reward Is Sparse, It’s Better to Be Dense" paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of large language model training and reasoning.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Addressing Fundamental RL Challenges in LLMs:** HERO directly tackles the inherent trade-off between the reliability of sparse, binary rewards and the richness of dense, continuous rewards. This is a pervasive problem in applying RL to LLMs, particularly for complex, multi-step reasoning tasks where solutions can be partially correct or expressed in varied formats.
- **Enhancing LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** By providing a more stable, informative, and nuance-aware reward signal, HERO enables LLMs to learn more effectively from partially correct reasoning paths, distinguish subtle errors, and generalize better across diverse problem types. The substantial gains on "hard-to-verify" tasks are particularly impactful, as these are often the most challenging frontiers for current LLMs.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency and Robustness:** The stratified normalization ensures meaningful gradients even in saturated batches, where traditional verifier-only methods would stall. Variance-aware weighting intelligently allocates training resources, focusing on the most informative prompts, leading to more efficient learning and reducing overfitting on trivial examples. The framework’s robustness across different LLM backbones further underscores its practical utility.
- **Practical Implications for RLHF and Deployment:** The finding that HERO does not rely on extremely large reward models (7B vs. 72B RM showed marginal difference) is highly practical. It means strong performance can be achieved with more compact RMs, reducing computational costs and memory footprints, thereby making the method more efficient and deployable in real-world scenarios.
- **Guiding Future Reward Design:** HERO's structured approach to reward integration offers a powerful blueprint for future research in designing sophisticated reward functions for LLMs. It highlights that the *way* different reward signals are combined is more critical than their individual scale.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancing AGI:** Improved mathematical and general reasoning capabilities are crucial for progress towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). By making LLMs more capable of stable and nuanced reasoning, HERO contributes to this long-term goal.
- **Broader Applications:** While demonstrated on mathematical reasoning, the principles of hybrid reward design could be extended to other complex domains where evaluation involves both strict correctness and subjective quality, such as code generation, scientific discovery, creative writing, and complex problem-solving.
- **Better Human-AI Alignment:** By allowing for more nuanced feedback, HERO could lead to LLMs that better align with human intent and expectations, even when answers are not strictly binary.

**Limitations and Future Work (as acknowledged by authors):**
- **Verifier Dependency:** The method's effectiveness still relies on the availability and reliability of rule-based verifiers; if these are poor, the correctness groups could be biased.
- **RM Calibration:** Reward models, even when constrained, might still exhibit biases on non-verifiable data.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** HERO introduces additional hyperparameters (α, β, k) that require careful tuning.
- **Training Overhead:** Concurrent calls to verifiers and RMs add to training overhead.
- **LLM-as-Judge Limitations:** Evaluation on non-verifiable tasks often relies on LLM-as-judge protocols, which have their own sensitivities and potential for noise.

Future work aims to address these limitations by exploring hybrid symbolic-learned verifiers, incorporating process-level rewards (beyond just final answers), and developing adaptive range/weighting schemes. These extensions could further solidify the stability and generality of hybrid reward frameworks, pushing LLM capabilities even further.
