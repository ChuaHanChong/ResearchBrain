# Hybrid Reinforcement: When Reward Is Sparse, It's Better to Be Dense

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hybrid Ensemble Reward Optimization (HERO) framework integrates sparse, deterministic verifier signals with dense, continuous reward model scores to enhance large language model reasoning. This method, from FAIR at Meta and the University of Wisconsin–Madison, consistently outperforms both verifier-only and reward model-only baselines on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, achieving up to +11.7 points over RM-only training on hard-to-verify tasks, by providing stable and informative supervisory signals.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) suffers from reward sparsity and brittleness, leading to stalled learning when all generated responses are uniformly correct or incorrect.
- Reward Models (RMs) provide dense, continuous scores but can be noisy and misaligned with true correctness, potentially destabilizing training.
- Neither sparse, reliable signals nor dense, noisy signals alone are sufficient for robustly guiding Large Language Models (LLMs) on complex reasoning tasks, especially those that are hard to verify.

## Method

- ==HERO (Hybrid Ensemble Reward Optimization)== combines sparse rule-based verifiers with dense reward models using a two-pronged approach.
- ==Stratified normalization== partitions responses into correct/incorrect groups based on verifier feedback, then normalizes reward model scores within each group to ensure correct responses always score higher while retaining nuance.
- ==Variance-aware weighting== dynamically adjusts prompt importance during training, up-weighting challenging, high-variance prompts to focus learning on more informative examples.

## Results

- HERO consistently outperformed RM-only and verifier-only baselines across all training regimes and task types on mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- On hard-to-verify tasks, HERO achieved an average of **66.3** accuracy for Qwen3-4B-Base when trained on easy-to-verify data, representing a **+11.7 point** improvement over RM-only (**54.6**) and **+9.2 points** over verifier-only (**57.1**) training.
- Ablation studies confirmed that dense negative ranges and variance-aware reweighting were critical, with variance-aware reweighting adding **+3.8 points**, and that larger reward models (72B vs 7B) provided only marginal or no additional benefits, highlighting HERO's efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating sparse and dense reward signals through structured methods (stratified normalization) provides more effective gradients and prevents learning from stalling, especially when rule-based rewards are uniform.
> - Providing dense rewards within the 'negative' (incorrect) response range is crucial for stabilizing training and improving learning efficiency by guiding the model to better distinguish among errors.
> - Dynamically reweighting prompts based on the variance of candidate response scores (variance-aware weighting) improves robustness and generalization by prioritizing ambiguous and challenging examples.
