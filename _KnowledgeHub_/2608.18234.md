# GigaBrain-WBC-0.5: A Behavior World Model for Robust Whole-Body Control with Environment Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 is a Behavior World Model for humanoid whole-body control: a causal Transformer jointly predicts next action, next proprioceptive state, and next latent-command distribution, paired with automatic 3D terrain annotation mined from motion capture and an online Mahalanobis filter retracting implausible commands. It achieves 81.3% terrain-interaction success (4.3x best baseline) and 99.3% fall-recovery (16.8x best baseline).

## Problem

- Whole-body trackers train only on flat empty scenes, never learning how terrain or object contact reshapes their own dynamics, so they cannot exploit environmental contacts.
- The standard fix for command robustness, enlarging the reference-motion corpus, stops working once feasible behaviors become environment-dependent and the feasible set turns conditional.
- Fall recovery is usually a separate specialist controller, so handing control to it interrupts the operator's task exactly as the fall did.

## Method

- **Behavior World Model**: a 6-layer ==causal Transformer== with KV-cache jointly emits action, next-state prediction, and a ==Gaussian-Mixture distribution== over the next latent behavior command from one shared representation.
- ==Automatic spatial terrain-annotation pipeline== recovers full 3D contact geometry (not elevation maps) from retargeted motion via contact detection, penetration filtering, and primitive fitting, at motion-dataset scale.
- ==Mahalanobis-radius online filter== uses the world model's own predicted mixture to detect out-of-distribution commands and radially retract them onto the nearest learned behavior, with a single runtime-tunable safety radius.

## Results

- On terrain-interaction sim-to-sim tests the method reaches **81.3%** success rate versus **14-19%** for SONIC, HoloMotion-1, and Humanoid-GPT, a **4.3x** improvement, with **93.3mm** keypoint error versus 283-331mm.
- Fall recovery success is **99.3%** versus **0.7-5.9%** for baselines, and under implausible OOD commands success is **83.1%** versus 50.0-70.6%, with tracking error closest to the intended motion at **158.0mm**.
- Human audit of the automatic terrain annotation pipeline confirms **92%** overall correctness across stairs, chairs, boxes, and other contact categories on 200 sampled motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training a tracker to also predict its own next state and next-command distribution, not just the action, forces it to internalize contact dynamics and situational feasibility for free.
> - Distribution-based online command filtering, derived directly from a world model's own uncertainty, can replace both wider training-data coverage and separate fallback controllers.
> - Recovering usable 3D interaction geometry from ordinary motion capture in hindsight avoids the need for costly paired scene-capture data, letting contact-rich training scale with existing motion corpora.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics places a high value on low-level controllers that can execute whole-body commands online, allowing robots to operate in human-centric environments. Prior research has demonstrated significant progress in whole-body motion tracking, where policies learn to imitate extensive datasets of human motion, enabling complex behaviors like walking, running, and dancing while maintaining balance and physical feasibility. These trackers often serve as a crucial interface, abstracting complex dynamic control away from teleoperators or higher-level models.

However, a recurring limitation in existing large-scale motion trackers is their training environment: they are predominantly developed and evaluated in empty scenes with flat floors. Consequently, these policies do not acquire an understanding of how contact with varied terrains or objects alters their dynamics. They are not trained to exploit environmental contacts or adapt to changes in their center of mass due to interactions or payloads. The primary obstacle to training environment-interactive trackers has been the scarcity of large-scale motion datasets paired with consistent terrain and object geometry. While some work has explored scene reconstruction from motion, these often result in limited 2.5D elevation maps or rely on specialized, smaller datasets and per-link contact labels for control.

A second challenge is robustness to out-of-distribution (OOD) commands and unexpected states, such as falls. On flat ground, command robustness has often been pursued by expanding the reference motion distribution. However, this strategy becomes less effective in varied environments, as the set of feasible behaviors becomes highly conditional on the terrain. Standard reinforcement learning (RL) policies tend to exhibit unpredictable behavior when faced with commands outside their training distribution, which is exacerbated by environmental variability. Existing solutions for this often involve dedicated fall recovery controllers, which interrupt the robot's task.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a Behavior World Model (BWM) approach, aiming to equip a single policy with capabilities for environment interaction, robustness to implausible commands, and intrinsic fall recovery. It positions itself as a unified solution, contrasting with approaches that rely on specialized controllers for different skills or environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust whole-body controller for humanoids that can interact with complex environments and maintain operational stability under challenging conditions, including implausible commands and falls. The authors identify two main deficiencies in existing whole-body motion tracking policies that motivate this work:

1. **Lack of Environment Interaction:** Current large-scale motion trackers are primarily trained in empty, flat-ground environments. This training paradigm prevents them from learning how physical contact with varied terrains (e.g., stairs, chairs, boxes) and objects reshapes their body dynamics. As a result, these policies cannot exploit environmental supports, adapt to changes in contact forces, or handle situations where ground reaction forces or center of mass are altered by interaction. The motivation here is to move beyond purely reactive tracking to enable humanoids to functionally engage with environments built for humans, requiring the policy to internalize contact dynamics.

2. **Fragility to Out-of-Distribution Commands and Falls:** The common strategy to achieve robustness on flat ground, by continually expanding the reference motion corpus, is insufficient when the environment varies. A motion feasible on flat ground might be impossible on a narrow step. Furthermore, RL policies are known to behave unpredictably when given commands outside their training distribution, leading to emergency stops or freezes, which can be dangerous or task-aborting. Similarly, falls are typically handled by separate, specialized recovery controllers, which interrupt the ongoing task. The motivation is to create a unified policy that can gracefully handle implausible commands by executing a "best-effort" motion and recover from falls without transferring control to a different module, thereby preserving task continuity.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose the following key contributions:

- **Development of a Behavior World Model (BWM):** The core motivation is to train a causal Transformer not merely to reproduce actions, but to jointly predict its next action, next proprioceptive state, and the distribution over its next latent behavior command. This forces the network to model how the environment currently affects its dynamics and which behaviors are feasible, rather than just reacting to a reference.
- **Automatic Spatial Terrain Annotation:** Recognizing the bottleneck of scarce environment-interactive motion data, the authors aim to develop a pipeline that can automatically reconstruct full 3D contact geometry from existing retargeted human motion data. This is motivated by the need to scale terrain-paired training data to the magnitude of current large-scale motion datasets, enabling the policy to learn spatially grounded interactions beyond simple elevation maps.
- **Online Out-of-Distribution Command Filtering:** To address unpredictability with OOD commands, the authors aim to leverage the BWM's predicted next-command distribution. This mechanism is motivated by the need for an online, continuous filter that can identify implausible commands and retract them onto learned, feasible behaviors in a "best-effort" manner, rather than rejecting them entirely. This contrasts with approaches requiring separate classifiers or fallback controllers.
- **Unified Fall Recovery:** Motivated by the desire for continuous task execution, the authors aim to integrate fall recovery directly into the main tracking policy. By training with fallen initializations, standing up and resuming the task becomes an inherent behavior of the tracker, eliminating the need for a task-interrupting specialist controller.

Together, these objectives aim to produce a unified, robust whole-body control policy capable of real-time command execution, environment interaction, and resilience to unforeseen circumstances, thereby expanding the utility of humanoid robots in complex, human-centric settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 system is designed for a Unitree G1 humanoid robot with 29 actuated degrees of freedom, operating at 50 Hz. Its architecture is centered around a causal Transformer, which functions as a Behavior World Model (BWM).

#### 3.1. Behavior World Model (BWM) Architecture

The policy takes three inputs at each time step `t`: a 67-dimensional proprioceptive observation (`s_t`), the previous action (`a_{t-1}`), and a latent behavior command (`z_t`).

- **Reference Window and Latent Command Generation:** A 440-dimensional reference window (`c_t`) covering the next 10 frames is supplied to the system. This window includes reference joint positions, root rotations, and root translations (both relative to the previous frame and the current robot root), and the world down direction in the reference root frame. This input is perturbed with additive uniform noise, and the robot-relative root translation component is intermittently masked during training. An MLP encoder (`E`) maps this processed reference into a 64-dimensional continuous latent behavior command (`z_raw_t`).
- **Latent Space Regularization:** To shape a well-structured latent space, a finite scalar quantizer (FSQ) and a small kinematic MLP decoder are attached to the encoder *during training only*. The decoder reconstructs the clean reference window from the quantized latent. This auxiliary loss encourages the encoder to distribute information smoothly across latent dimensions, ensuring a well-behaved space for the mixture model. The policy itself always receives the unquantized latent (`z_t`).
- **Global Drift Handling:** To counteract global drift over long trajectories, the root-translation block (iv) is supplied during training approximately one-third of the time and zeroed otherwise, forcing the policy to learn to operate without continuous absolute root feedback. At deployment, this block is held at zero.
- **Causal Transformer Core:** The input `e_t = [s_t, a_{t-1}, z_t]` is projected to 256 dimensions and fed into a 6-layer causal Transformer with 4 heads and rotary position embeddings. It processes a 32-frame window, with masking enforcing causality and episode boundaries. A per-layer KV cache enables efficient single-step inference.
- **Multi-Headed Output:** The Transformer's final hidden state is read by three linear heads:
 1. **Policy Head:** Emits the joint targets (`a_t`).
 2. **State Head:** Predicts the next proprioceptive state (`s_{t+1}`), compelling the model to internalize contact dynamics.
 3. **Behavior Head:** Predicts a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) `G_t` with K=4 diagonal Gaussian components over the next latent command (`z_{t+1}`). This prediction models the distribution of feasible behaviors conditional on the current state and action.
- **Objective Function:** The full objective combines the PPO loss (`L_PPO`) with four auxiliary terms: a reconstruction loss (`L_recon`) for the reference window, a cycle-consistency loss (`L_cycle`) for the quantized latent, an `L2` loss for the predicted next state (`||s_{t+1} - s_{t+1}||^2`), and a negative log-likelihood for the predicted next latent command (`-log G_t(z_{t+1})`).

#### 3.2. Automatic Spatial Terrain Annotation

To overcome the data scarcity for terrain-interactive motions, the authors developed an automated pipeline to reconstruct 3D contact geometry from ordinary retargeted motion clips.

- **Contact Detection:** Points are uniformly sampled on the collision geometry of contact-relevant links (e.g., ankles, hands, knees). The retargeted trajectory is kinematically replayed in MuJoCo, and per-sample world positions and outward normals are obtained. Contacts are detected when normal speed and tangential speed fall below thresholds, accompanied by normal deceleration, distinguishing support events from incidental limb slowness.
- **Contact Cloud Formation:** Detected contacts accumulate into a 3D point cloud. Points below a certain height or with downward-facing normals (considered floor or non-supporting) are discarded.
- **Penetration Filtering:** Each remaining point is tested against the whole robot over the entire trajectory. A point is kept only if a support placed there (offset outward along its normal) never intersects any part of the robot's body. This ensures reconstructed geometry is collision-safe.
- **Primitive Fitting:** Points are grouped into planar supports using pairwise affinity based on normal agreement and mutual offset. DBSCAN separates distinct supports. Each cluster is fitted with a 1 cm-thick oriented box, with in-plane axes derived from PCA. Clusters that are too sparse are recursively split.
- **Lateral Expansion:** Each primitive is laterally expanded using a penetration-safe binary search to provide a margin around the robot's original footprint.
- **Simulator Integration:** These 3D primitives are spawned as static rigid bodies in Isaac Lab, co-located with their respective reference motions. Terrain is rebuilt at motion-resample boundaries, and motion sampling is frozen within an epoch to maintain consistency. Terrain and flat-ground motions are mixed at a configurable ratio (0.2 terrain to flat-ground).

#### 3.3. Filtering Out-of-Distribution Commands

The BWM's prediction of `G_t-1` (the mixture over the next latent command) is used at deployment to filter incoming commands.

- **Admissibility Test:** An incoming latent command `z_raw_t` is checked against the mixture `G_{t-1}` (predicted at the previous step). The goal is to determine if `z_raw_t` is consistent with the learned conditional behaviors.
- **Responsibility Head Selection:** The system first identifies the most plausible component (`k_star`) in the GMM that `z_raw_t` belongs to. This selection is biased towards modes considered more likely by the world model's prior (`log(pi_k)`).
- **Mahalanobis Radius Test:** For the selected component `k_star`, the squared Mahalanobis radius `M^2_{k_star}(z_raw_t)` is calculated. The command is deemed out-of-distribution if `M^2_{k_star}(z_raw_t) > R^2_safe`, where `R_safe` is a tunable safety radius.
- **Radial Retraction:** If the command is out-of-distribution, it is not rejected or replaced by the component mean. Instead, it is radially retracted towards the mean `mu_{k_star}` to land precisely on the boundary of the admissible ellipsoid (`Z_{k_star}`). This ensures the robot attempts a "best-effort" motion that is still aligned with the operator's intent but within the bounds of learned feasibility.
- **Efficiency:** The filter is stateless, memoryless, and computationally efficient (O(1) per step), operating within 1 ms, which is negligible compared to the 20 ms control period. The `R_safe` parameter can be tuned at runtime to adjust the trade-off between precision and robustness.

#### 3.4. Training Details

Training is performed using PPO in Isaac Lab with sequence-level updates, where the attention context is consistent with the policy's real-time experience.

- **Data Mix:** Three large retargeted human-motion corpora (Bones-Seed, MotionMillion, MotionDecode) are used, with identified terrain-interaction subsets (totaling ~72.57 hours) cleaned and mixed with flat-ground motion.
- **Robustness Curriculum:** A fraction of flat-ground episodes begin from synthetic fallen poses under a curriculum of trunk inclination, allowing the policy to learn to stand up. Persistent random external forces are applied to wrists and torso to simulate payloads and contact loads.
- **Reward Function:** Tracking terms use a Gaussian kernel, while other terms are plain penalties (e.g., action rate, joint limits, undesired contacts, anti-shake, ankle acceleration). Domain randomization is extensively used for physical parameters (friction, restitution, mass, CoM offsets) and external disturbances (pushes, forces, torques), along with input noise and masking.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The Unitree G1 checkpoint can be fine-tuned on a Maker L01 humanoid with a retargeted corpus, demonstrating transferability of the learned behavior structure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 against three large-scale tracker baselines (SONIC, HoloMotion-1, Humanoid-GPT) across four distinct evaluation regimes in MuJoCo, along with hardware trials.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup and Metrics

- **Benchmarks:**
 - **Standard:** AMASS test split (136 clips) for flat-ground tracking.
 - **OOD (Out-of-Distribution):** MotionMillion subset (136 clips) with physically implausible references (self-penetration, retargeting artifacts).
 - **Terrain:** Bones-Seed subset (150 clips) with motions traversing or interacting with terrain, with annotated geometry loaded.
 - **Fall:** Standard clips (136 clips) but initialized from fallen poses.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR), Mean Per-Keypoint Position Error (MPKPE) in mm, Root Position error (RootPos) in mm, Root Linear Velocity error (RootVel) in mm/s, and Joint Jerk in rad/s³. Specific metrics were chosen for each split to reflect the relevant performance aspects (e.g., RootVel for Standard, RootPos for Terrain, Jerk for Fall recovery). Fall SR specifically measures recovery rate (standing up and resuming tracking).
- **Protocol:** All policies were evaluated sim-to-sim in MuJoCo. The reference-root translation block, unavailable on hardware, was masked during evaluation. GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 was evaluated with its OOD filter active at `R_safe = 3`.

#### 4.2. Motion Tracking Across Regimes (Table 3)

- **Standard (Flat-Ground Tracking):** GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 achieved the highest accuracy and reliability, with an MPKPE of 76.6 mm and an SR of 96.3%. This performance surpassed SONIC (82.3 mm, 94.1%), indicating that environment-aware training did not compromise free-space tracking quality. The policy showed a slightly higher RootVel (211.1 mm/s vs. HoloMotion-1's 121.3 mm/s), which is attributed to its design for operating without continuous absolute root feedback, leading to small corrections rather than continuous velocity matching.
- **Terrain Interaction:** This regime highlighted a significant advantage. Baselines collapsed to 14-19% SR with MPKPEs between 283-331 mm, failing to treat terrain as a support. GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 maintained an 81.3% SR at 93.3 mm MPKPE, representing a 4.3x improvement in survival and a 3.0x reduction in tracking error compared to the strongest baseline. Its MPKPE increase from Standard to Terrain was only 22%, whereas baselines saw a 3-4x increase.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Commands:** When faced with physically impossible references, GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 survived 83.1% of clips, outperforming baselines (50.0-70.6%). Its executed motion also remained closest to the intent (158.0 mm MPKPE), indicating the filter effectively retracts commands to a "best-effort" feasible behavior.
- **Fall Recovery:** This showed a categorical difference. Baselines recovered from 0.7-5.9% of fallen initializations. GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 achieved a 99.3% recovery rate. Its recovery was also smoother (1050.6 rad/s³ Jerk) compared to the baselines' thrashing (1295-3598 rad/s³).

#### 4.3. Environment Interaction (Table 4, Figure 5)

- **Terrain Annotation Audit:** A human audit of the automatic terrain annotation pipeline (Section 3.3) on 200 randomly sampled motions showed an overall correctness rate of 92%. Boxes/platforms were easiest to annotate (98%), followed by chairs/seats (94%) and stairs/steps (92%). "Other" motions, dominated by hand supports, were weakest (84%) due to upstream retargeting noise. Failures were conservative (missing/misplaced supports, never collision-inducing obstacles).
- **Hardware Trials:** Qualitative hardware experiments on a Unitree G1 robot (Figure 5) demonstrated the policy's ability to interact with real environments. Comparisons with SONIC showed GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 using environmental contacts (e.g., sitting on a box, climbing a platform, lifting a heavy object, carrying an object while traversing terrain) where SONIC failed to establish load-bearing support or maintain stability. This provides evidence for a general interaction capability rather than scripted skills.

#### 4.4. Robustness (Figure 6, Figure 7)

- **Safety Radius Trade-off:** A sweep of the `R_safe` parameter (Figure 6) demonstrated a tunable trade-off between OOD success rate and Standard MPKPE. The deployed `R_safe = 3` was chosen at a knee point, gaining 2.9 points of OOD survival for 12.7 mm of Standard MPKPE, indicating effective balance between precision and robustness.
- **Qualitative Robustness:** Hardware trials (Figure 7) showed the policy producing stable, best-effort responses when expected environmental support was absent (e.g., removed chair or platform), rather than emergency stops. It also recovered from kick-induced falls without a separate controller. In simulation, the OOD filter prevented a fall during a challenging spinning-kick reference by retracting the command to a safer, still task-relevant, behavior.

#### 4.5. Whole-Body Motion Capability (Figure 8)

Beyond the specific claims, the controller demonstrated a wide range of agile whole-body motions (squat jumps, high kicks, Tai Chi) and practical tasks (table cleaning, cushion arrangement, door opening, logistics operations, payload carrying) under live whole-body command. The G1 checkpoint also successfully transferred to a Maker L01 robot after fine-tuning, performing several household and logistics tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 represents a notable advancement in humanoid whole-body control by introducing the concept of a Behavior World Model (BWM). Its significance lies in integrating capabilities that were previously disparate or limited, addressing critical bottlenecks in deploying humanoids in complex environments.

**Key Contributions and Their Impact:**

- **Unified Environment Interaction and Robustness:** By training a causal Transformer to predict its own next state and next command distribution alongside its action, the policy is compelled to internalize environmental contact dynamics and model the feasibility of future behaviors. This intrinsic understanding allows for robust interaction with diverse terrains and objects, and resilient operation under unexpected conditions. This unified approach reduces system complexity compared to reliance on multiple specialized controllers.
- **Scalable Terrain-Paired Data Generation:** The automatic spatial terrain annotation pipeline is a crucial innovation. By recovering full 3D contact geometry directly from kinematic motion data, it circumvents the laborious process of manual scene reconstruction. This capability allows for the creation of environment-interactive training datasets at scales comparable to existing motion capture libraries, which is essential for developing generalist policies.
- **Online Out-of-Distribution Command Filtering:** The BWM's predicted command distribution enables a real-time, stateless filter that identifies and retracts implausible commands to the closest learned, feasible behaviors. This "best-effort" approach prevents task interruption (e.g., emergency stops) and enhances operational safety, especially in dynamic and uncertain real-world scenarios. The runtime-tunable safety radius offers flexibility in balancing precision and robustness.
- **Integrated Fall Recovery:** Training the policy with fallen initializations means that standing up and resuming tasks is an intrinsic behavior, rather than requiring a separate, task-interrupting recovery controller. This significantly improves task continuity and overall robot autonomy in the face of disturbances.
- **Strong Performance Across Regimes:** The policy's superior performance across standard tracking, terrain interaction, OOD command handling, and fall recovery, compared to leading baselines, demonstrates the efficacy of the BWM approach. Its ability to maintain high fidelity on flat ground while excelling in interactive and robust scenarios indicates a significant step towards general-purpose humanoid control.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The successful fine-tuning and deployment on a different humanoid morphology (Maker L01) suggests that the learned behavioral structure is not entirely robot-specific, hinting at a more generalizable control paradigm.

**Potential Impact:**

The development of GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 has the potential to significantly impact the practical deployment of humanoid robots. It could serve as a foundational low-level controller for future generalist humanoids, enabling them to:
- **Operate in unstructured human environments:** By interacting robustly with common household objects and diverse terrains (stairs, chairs, platforms), humanoids could perform tasks in homes, offices, and industrial settings with greater reliability.
- **Simplify higher-level control:** Teleoperators or high-level AI models can issue coarse, high-level movement intents without needing to generate dynamically consistent, high-frequency control signals, simplifying the development of more complex autonomous behaviors.
- **Enhance safety and reliability:** The robust handling of unexpected commands and falls contributes to safer robot operation, reducing the likelihood of critical failures or hardware damage in uncertain environments.
- **Accelerate research:** The automatic terrain annotation pipeline could foster the creation of richer, more diverse datasets, accelerating research into environment-aware and interactive robot control.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The paper acknowledges several limitations. The OOD filter, while effective, flags commands based on training distribution similarity rather than direct physical risk, implying that interception of *all* physically infeasible commands is high-probability, but not guaranteed. The terrain annotation process only reconstructs geometry that was actually contacted, meaning unused environmental features are not captured. Future work could involve connecting the world model's state prediction to a more direct notion of physical risk and extending terrain reconstruction to include non-supporting geometry, thereby building more complete environmental representations.

Overall, GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 presents a coherent and effective framework for advancing humanoid whole-body control, particularly through its novel integration of world modeling for environment interaction and robustness.
