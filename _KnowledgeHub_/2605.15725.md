# DiLA: Disentangled Latent Action World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DiLA, a Disentangled Latent Action world model, resolves the trade-off in latent action models between action abstraction and video generation fidelity by co-optimizing disentanglement and latent action learning within a single framework. The model produces semantically structured, transferable latent actions and generates high-fidelity video, outperforming baselines in action transfer and visual planning tasks.

## Problem

- World models for autonomous agents require extensive action-labeled datasets, which are costly and difficult to acquire at scale.
- Existing Latent Action Models (LAMs) infer latent actions from unlabeled video but face a fundamental 'LAM Trade-off'.
- This 'LAM Trade-off' represents a tension between learning abstract, transferable actions (requiring strong predictive bottlenecks) and maintaining high-fidelity video generation quality.

## Method

- Introduces **DiLA**, a Disentangled Latent Action ==world model== that separates video into dynamics-relevant 'structure' and dynamics-irrelevant 'content' pathways.
- Leverages a strict information bottleneck, where an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) infers latent actions from temporal differences in structure embeddings, and a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) predicts future structure states.
- Incorporates a Mamba-based content pathway to aggregate static visual features and memory, and a Fusion Decoder to integrate predicted structure, content, and initial frame information for high-fidelity video generation.

## Results

- DiLA demonstrated robust action transfer capabilities across diverse embodiments (human-to-robot), semantic contexts, and domains (virtual-to-real navigation), confirming its abstract latent actions support robust transfer.
- The model generally surpassed state-of-the-art baselines in video generation quality, measured by SSIM and LPIPS, across SSv2 and RT-1 datasets for autoregressive rollouts of 16 frames.
- Analysis showed that DiLA successfully disentangled content and structure via rebinding experiments and learned low-dimensional, semantically continuous latent action manifolds, proving utility in visual planning for robotic control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The predictive bottleneck inherent in latent action learning can effectively drive the disentanglement of content and structure components within video data.
> - Co-optimizing disentanglement and latent action learning within a unified framework can simultaneously yield abstract, content-invariant latent actions and high-fidelity video prediction.
> - Employing a continuous information bottleneck, as opposed to rigid discrete (VQ) or variational (VAE) alternatives, is crucial for achieving superior generation quality and training stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models are a foundational component for autonomous agents, providing capabilities such as planning, simulation, and policy learning in dynamic environments. These models typically learn to predict future states by capturing underlying latent dynamics. However, their reliance on action-labeled datasets presents a significant challenge, as such data is scarce and expensive to acquire at scale. In contrast, unlabeled video data is widely available.

To address this data dependency, Latent Action Models (LAMs) have been introduced. LAMs infer abstract latent actions directly from unlabeled video sequences, serving as surrogates for explicit control signals. A typical LAM comprises two main components: an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) that extracts latent actions from consecutive frames, and a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) that predicts future states conditioned on these inferred actions.

Despite their potential, existing LAMs encounter a fundamental challenge, termed the "LAM Trade-off": a tension between the level of action abstraction and the fidelity of video generation. To achieve abstract and transferable actions, models often impose strong predictive bottlenecks, such as Vector Quantization (VQ) or variational bottlenecks. While these mechanisms encourage abstraction, they can compromise the intrinsic manifold of the latent action space, leading to over-simplified representations and degraded video generation quality. Conversely, relaxing these bottlenecks may improve generation fidelity but often results in entangled representations where latent actions capture irrelevant visual details rather than pure dynamics.

Current approaches to this trade-off often prioritize action abstraction. This frequently involves disjoint, two-stage training paradigms where the FDM is discarded in favor of a separate, pre-trained diffusion model for video generation. Some methods attempt to learn more abstract latent actions by using optical flow or depth maps as input or supervision targets, which implicitly separates motion-related spatial layouts from content details. While concurrent work has explored single-stage training, it primarily relies on latent action bottlenecks without explicit content-structure disentanglement. The present work aims to address this trade-off by proposing a joint learning framework for disentanglement and latent action modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to resolve the "LAM Trade-off," which describes the inherent tension between achieving a high degree of action abstraction and maintaining high-fidelity video generation quality within Latent Action Models (LAMs). The authors identify two pivotal questions motivating their work:
1. Can disentanglement learning and latent action learning be co-optimized within a single framework?
2. How can both abstract latent actions and high-fidelity video prediction be achieved simultaneously?

The central hypothesis guiding this research is that the key to reconciling the LAM Trade-off lies in effective disentanglement. The proposed model, DiLA (Disentangled Latent Action world model), is designed to decouple video sequences into two primary components: "structure," which pertains to dynamics-relevant spatial layouts, and "content," which encompasses dynamics-irrelevant visual appearance and texture.

The motivation behind this functional separation is rooted in a proposed co-evolutionary relationship: the predictive bottleneck inherent in latent action learning serves as a driving force for disentanglement. This bottleneck compels the model to distill only essential spatial layouts and motion dynamics into the structure pathway, while offloading rich visual details and appearance information to a separate content pathway for generation. This synergistic process is hypothesized to lead to the learning of content-invariant latent actions and a continuous, semantically structured latent action space, without compromising the quality of generated video frames. The authors aim to establish DiLA as a unified framework capable of simultaneously achieving both high-level action abstraction and high-fidelity generation, thereby advancing the field of self-supervised world model learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiLA operates by processing video sequences through two specialized pathways designed to disentangle structure and content, facilitated by a strict information bottleneck. The model integrates a frozen DINOv2 encoder for visual feature extraction and a frozen RAE decoder for visualizing reconstructed embeddings, operating entirely within the latent space without direct pixel-level reconstruction during training.

The processing begins by extracting visual embeddings from video clips, which are then refined by a spatial-temporal Transformer. This Transformer incorporates spatial attention for global dependencies and temporal attention with causal masking and rotary position embeddings to enforce causality and process historical contexts.

**The Structure Pathway:**
A structure encoder compresses visual tokens into structure embeddings, denoted as $s_{0:t}$. The Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) then computes abstract latent actions, $z_{0:t-1}$, directly from the temporal differences of these structure embeddings ($\Delta s_{0:t-1}$). The use of temporal differences serves as an information bottleneck, forcing the latent actions to capture only salient motion changes. The IDM utilizes 3D convolutional blocks, with spatial kernels capturing translation-invariant features and temporal kernels aggregating bidirectional context to form latent actions of dimension $d_z = 256$.
The Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) is implemented as a lightweight spatial-temporal Transformer. It predicts the next structural state, $\hat{s}_{1:t}$, as a residual update to the current state, $s_{0:t-1}$, conditioned on the latent actions $z_{0:t-1}$ using AdaLN-zero. The prediction is formulated as:
$z_{0:t-1} = \text{IDM}(\Delta s_{0:t-1})$
$\hat{s}_{1:t} = s_{0:t-1} + \text{FDM}(s_{0:t-1}, z_{0:t-1})$
This predictive task, constrained by the bottleneck of latent action learning, incentivizes the structure pathway to distill only abstract dynamics into the latent actions while ensuring the target structure embedding retains only dynamics-correlated spatial layouts, thereby driving content-structure disentanglement.

**The Content Pathway:**
A content encoder generates content embeddings, $c_{0:t}$. To accumulate historical content information, DiLA employs the Mamba architecture, which is selected for its parallelization capabilities and stable memory retention over long sequences. The Mamba module functions to aggregate static visual features over time, distinct from the structure pathway's focus on dynamics. This pathway is responsible for maintaining belief updates in partially observable environments and preserving information about occluded backgrounds or unobserved regions. The output of the memory module at time $t$ is formulated as:
$c^{\text{mem}}_t = \text{Mamba}(c_t, h_{t-1})$

**Fusion Decoder:**
A spatial-attention Transformer, acting as the Fusion Decoder, integrates the predicted structure with content and initial-frame conditioning. It uses the predicted structure $\hat{s}$ as queries, with the content memory $c^{\text{mem}}$ and the initial visual embedding $e_0$ serving as keys and values in a dual cross-attention mechanism. The initial embedding $e_0$ is crucial for reintroducing high-frequency details lost during compression. The decoding process at time $t$ generates the next visual embedding:
$\text{Dec}_{\theta}(\hat{s}_{t+1}, e_0, c^{\text{mem}}_t) = \hat{e}_{t+1}$

**Latent Rollouts:**
DiLA performs autoregressive rollouts directly within the latent structure space. At each step, the FDM predicts the subsequent structure state based on the previous predicted state and the current latent action. The visual embedding is then reconstructed by the Fusion Decoder, and the content memory is updated with this newly generated embedding for the next step.
$\hat{s}_{t+1} = \hat{s}_t + \text{FDM}(\hat{s}_t, z_t)$

**Training Objectives:**
DiLA is trained using a self-supervised teacher-forcing paradigm on unlabeled video sequences. The DINOv2 encoder and RAE decoder remain frozen. The total training objective is a weighted combination of four loss terms:
$L_{\text{total}} = \lambda_e L_e + \lambda_s L_s + \lambda_z L_z + \lambda_{\text{reg}} L_{\text{reg}}$
- $L_e$: Visual latent prediction loss, measuring the L2 difference between actual and predicted visual embeddings.
- $L_s$: Structure prediction loss, measuring the L2 difference between actual and predicted structure differences.
- $L_z$: Latent action consistency loss, ensuring consistency between IDM outputs from actual and predicted structure differences.
- $L_{\text{reg}}$: Regularization loss for latent actions, comprising:
 - L2 norm regularization for compactness.
 - A group symmetry term, using cosine similarity, to enforce that inverse temporal transitions yield opposite latent action vectors (applied only to non-static frames).
 - A variance regularization term to maximize information entropy.

**Training Protocol:**
DiLA is trained for 30,000 teacher-forcing iterations followed by 1,000 fine-tuning iterations using latent rollouts, on a diverse set of video datasets including Something-Something-v2 (SSv2), RT-1, RECON, and LoopNav.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DiLA focused on action transfer capabilities, video generation quality, the effectiveness of content-structure disentanglement, properties of the latent action space, and utility in visual planning.

**Action Transfer:**
DiLA demonstrated successful action transfer across various scenarios. Qualitatively, the model facilitated cross-embodiment transfer, such as mapping human activities to robotic arms, and semantic transfer across diverse objects and viewpoints. It also achieved intra-domain transfer (e.g., human-to-human, robot-to-robot) and navigation transfer between virtual and real-world environments. The content memory module was observed to contribute to generating plausible predictions for occluded regions, maintaining visual consistency. Quantitatively, the action cycle transfer metric showed that DiLA's latent actions are sufficiently abstract to support robust transfer across different contexts, indicating minimal increase in prediction error after a cycle of inference and re-inference.

**Video Generation Quality:**
DiLA was benchmarked against state-of-the-art methods including LAPA, Moto, AdaWorld, and Villa-X on the SSv2 and RT-1 datasets. Using SSIM for structural consistency and LPIPS for perceptual similarity, DiLA generally outperformed most baselines across these metrics for autoregressive rollouts of 16 frames. An ablation study comparing DiLA with a variant lacking the content model demonstrated a decrease in generation quality for the ablated model, underscoring the contribution of the content pathway and disentanglement to high-fidelity video generation.

**Content and Structure Disentanglement:**
The disentanglement mechanism was validated through rebinding experiments. By extracting structure embeddings from one video sequence and content memory from another, DiLA successfully synthesized new sequences that preserved the spatial dynamics of the structure source while inheriting the appearance attributes (colors, textures, landmarks) of the content source. A control experiment, where the structure embedding was frozen while content memory evolved, resulted in static generated sequences, confirming that the content pathway encodes only temporally invariant features and effectively isolates motion.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Without IDM + FDM:** An ablated DiLA variant lacking the IDM and FDM (where the structure pathway acted as a simple compressor) failed to achieve disentanglement. Its structure embeddings encoded redundant content details, and rebinding experiments exhibited texture leakage. This indicates that the predictive bottleneck imposed by latent action learning is crucial for driving content-structure disentanglement.
- **Without Content Pathway:** A variant of DiLA without the content pathway showed a significant reappearance of the LAM Trade-off. Despite having substantially more trainable parameters, this model exhibited more fragile latent action transfer (higher LPIPS increase), severely compromised generation quality, and slower convergence compared to the full DiLA model. This supports the hypothesis that disentanglement is essential for learning accurate and robust latent action world models.
- **Alternative Information Bottlenecks:** Replacing DiLA's continuous bottleneck with discrete Vector Quantization (VQ) or a Gaussian variational prior (β-VAE) resulted in inferior generation quality and slower convergence. This suggests that the rigid priors of VQ and VAE can distort the intrinsic low-dimensional manifold of the latent action space, hindering generative fidelity and training stability.
- **Linear Probing on OOD Datasets:** On four out-of-distribution (OOD) robotic benchmarks (Franka Kitchen, Block Pushing, Push-T, LIBERO Goal), DiLA achieved the lowest Mean Squared Error (MSE) in linear probing, indicating better alignment of its latent actions with ground-truth continuous control signals compared to VQ and Gaussian prior variants. This suggests DiLA learns more fine-grained and transferable actions.

**Latent Action Analysis:**
Analysis of the latent action space using the OmniObject3D dataset, an OOD synthetic benchmark with primitive affine transformations, revealed that DiLA captures low-dimensional, semantically continuous manifolds for translation, scaling, and in-plane rotation. UMAP visualizations showed distinct, topologically meaningful structures, and quantitative decoding achieved high accuracy. For compositional actions like Translation+Scaling, distinct clusters retaining scaling topology were observed, indicating semantic compositionality. In navigation tasks (RECON and LoopNav), DiLA learned a continuous spectrum for relative yaw and discrete clusters for fundamental motions, respectively, supporting its robust transfer performance.

**Visual Planning using Model Predictive Control (MPC):**
DiLA's utility in robotic control was evaluated using Model Predictive Control (MPC) on the VP² benchmark. After adaptation via a lightweight action MLP to bridge the domain gap and subsequent fine-tuning, DiLA served as the dynamics model for MPC, implemented with the MPPI algorithm. Comparative results showed that DiLA outperformed the AdaWorld baseline on the majority of tasks, with notable gains in tasks like "Push Button." This confirms DiLA's capability for visual planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces DiLA, a novel Disentangled Latent Action world model that addresses a fundamental challenge in self-supervised world model learning: the "LAM Trade-off" between action abstraction and generation fidelity. By proposing a co-evolutionary learning framework for content-structure disentanglement and latent actions, DiLA demonstrates that these two aspects can be mutually reinforcing, leading to a unified model with enhanced capabilities.

The significance of DiLA lies in its ability to simultaneously achieve high-level action abstraction, producing semantically structured and transferable latent actions, and high-fidelity video generation. This represents an advancement over prior LAMs that often sacrifice one aspect for the other or rely on multi-stage training with external generative models.

The demonstrated robustness and transferability of DiLA's learned latent actions across diverse embodiments (human-to-robot), domains (virtual-to-real navigation), and tasks (manipulation, navigation) have substantial potential impact. It suggests that DiLA can learn generalizable representations of dynamics from unlabeled video data, reducing the need for expensive action-labeled datasets. This capability is critical for scaling autonomous agents and robotic systems in complex, real-world environments.

Furthermore, the interpretability of DiLA's latent action space, as evidenced by its continuous and topologically meaningful manifolds for various transformations, contributes to a better understanding of the learned dynamics. Its strong performance in downstream visual planning tasks validates its practical utility for robotic control and decision-making, offering a more effective internal dynamics model for MPC.

Overall, DiLA represents a step forward in self-supervised learning for world models, potentially accelerating the development of more capable and autonomous AI systems by providing a framework that efficiently learns abstract, yet accurate, representations of physical interactions. Future work may address finer-grained control, multi-object dynamics disentanglement, and long-horizon generation stability.
