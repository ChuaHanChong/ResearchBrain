# AdaReasoner: Adaptive Reasoning Enables More Flexible Thinking in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> AdaReasoner is an LLM-agnostic framework that employs a reinforcement learning approach to dynamically configure reasoning hyperparameters, including instruction formats, generation temperature, and reasoning steps. This method consistently achieved higher average accuracy across six diverse LLMs and various reasoning tasks, demonstrating efficient few-shot training and robust out-of-distribution generalization.

## Problem

- Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning performance is highly sensitive to configuration parameters, leading to suboptimal outcomes when using fixed, general-purpose settings.
- Existing prompting strategies like Chain-of-Thought often rely on rigid reasoning structures that fail to achieve optimal performance across diverse tasks, especially creative or subjective ones.
- Manually identifying the best configuration for LLMs is impractical due to the vast, combinatorial search space of hyperparameters and the high computational cost of data collection for task-specific tuning.

## Method

- The authors developed **AdaReasoner**, an ==LLM-agnostic plugin== that uses a reinforcement learning (RL) framework to automate adaptive reasoning configurations for various tasks.
- The problem is formulated as a multi-armed bandit, where an RL agent selects a 3-dimensional action vector comprising generation temperature, reasoning instruction format, and the number of reasoning steps.
- AdaReasoner utilizes a neural architecture with a shared encoder and factorized policy heads, trained with the REINFORCE algorithm and Boltzmann exploration, using a pre-trained language judgment model for reward signals.

## Results

- AdaReasoner achieved the highest average accuracy across all six tested LLMs (e.g., **80.42%** with GPT-4o, **81.43%** with Claude-3.5-sonnet) and diverse reasoning tasks, outperforming standard baselines.
- Experiments demonstrated rapid convergence, showing "marginal gains beyond 100 shots," validating its efficiency in few-shot learning scenarios.
- The system exhibited strong out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, maintaining stable superior performance on unseen datasets, and highlighted the crucial role of adaptive reasoning instruction formats.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adapting reasoning configurations with an RL agent leads to consistently higher LLM performance across diverse tasks compared to static prompting strategies.
> - Factorizing the action space into discrete components (temperature, instruction, steps) and employing a shared encoder with specific policy heads enables efficient learning of coherent, adaptive policies.
> - Few-shot training, combined with Boltzmann exploration and a differentiable reward model, facilitates rapid convergence and efficient tuning, overcoming the impracticality of extensive data collection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated unprecedented capabilities in diverse natural language processing and reasoning tasks. A significant body of research has emerged to further enhance these capabilities, particularly focusing on how users prompt or configure LLMs to achieve better performance. AdaReasoner situates itself at the forefront of this effort by addressing a crucial, yet often overlooked, aspect: the dynamic and adaptive configuration of reasoning parameters.

Initially, advancements in LLM reasoning were driven by **prompting-based strategies**. Techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting enabled LLMs to decompose complex problems into intermediate steps, significantly boosting performance on logical tasks. This was further generalized by Tree-of-Thought (ToT), which allowed exploration of multiple branching reasoning paths. Sampling-based approaches, such as Best-of-N, aimed to improve robustness by selecting the most coherent reasoning path. More recently, automatic prompt optimization techniques sought to discover effective prompts systematically, while In-Context Learning (ICL) leveraged few-shot examples for advanced performance.

However, the authors identify a critical gap: despite these advancements, LLM reasoning remains "highly configuration-sensitive." Existing prompting strategies typically adopt "general-purpose, fixed configurations" that, while effective to some degree, "seldom achieve task-specific optimality." For instance, fixed CoT or ToT structures may fail on creative or subjective tasks, and automatic prompt optimization often overlooks crucial hyperparameters like temperature. This lack of adaptability leads to suboptimal performance, as illustrated by the paper's finding that a single LLM's accuracy can "swing wildly under different reasoning configurations" for the same task.

AdaReasoner directly addresses this limitation by proposing an "adaptive prompting configuration strategy." It positions itself within the emerging domain of **nuanced control of generation parameters and adaptive hyperparameter tuning**. While previous work has explored the impact of stochastic decoding settings (like temperature) and even some forms of LLM self-regulation (e.g., Hyperparameter-Aware Generation (HAG)), AdaReasoner provides a comprehensive, LLM-agnostic framework. It leverages a **Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigm** to dynamically manage a "toolbox of reasoning hyperparameters," including reasoning instruction formats, generation temperature, and the number of reasoning steps. This approach sets it apart by enabling a data-driven, task-specific optimization of these critical parameters, moving beyond static prompt engineering or generalized strategies. By doing so, AdaReasoner aims to unlock more flexible and optimal thinking in LLMs across a diverse range of tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an adaptive system that can automatically configure Large Language Models (LLMs) for optimal reasoning across a variety of tasks. The authors introduce **AdaReasoner**, an LLM-agnostic plugin designed to automate these adaptive reasoning configurations for tasks demanding different cognitive processes.

This objective is motivated by several critical observations and challenges:

1. **Configuration Sensitivity of LLM Reasoning:** The paper highlights that LLM performance on reasoning tasks is "highly configuration-sensitive." Simple changes in parameters like temperature or the number of reasoning steps can drastically alter accuracy, as depicted in Figure 1. This sensitivity implies that fixed, general-purpose configurations—the norm in many existing prompting strategies—are inherently suboptimal for diverse tasks.
2. **Limitations of Existing Prompting Methods:** Current strategies, despite their advancements, are often rigid:
 - **Fixed Reasoning Structures:** Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Tree-of-Thought (ToT) apply predetermined reasoning structures that may not generalize well to creative, subjective, or spatial planning tasks.
 - **Unguided Generation:** Approaches like Best-of-N suffer from potential "garbage in, garbage out" effects if the initial generations are poor.
 - **Overlooked Hyperparameters:** Automatic prompt optimization techniques tend to focus on static templates while neglecting dynamic hyperparameters such as generation temperature, which can significantly influence output diversity and reasoning quality.
 - **Brittleness of In-Context Learning (ICL):** ICL, while powerful, can be brittle under context perturbations and relies on implicit pattern matching, which may be less effective than structured reasoning for certain problems.
3. **Complexity of Identifying Optimal Configurations:** The task of manually identifying the best configuration for an LLM is non-trivial due to:
 - **Task Diversity:** Reasoning tasks span logical, creative, mathematical, and subjective domains, often in combination. A single, static configuration cannot optimally serve all these demands.
 - **Combinatorially Large Search Space:** The interplay of multiple configuration factors (temperature, instruction format, number of steps) creates a vast search space, making exhaustive manual tuning impractical.
 - **Computational Cost of Data Collection:** Building a data-driven decision-making model to tailor configurations would typically require extensive training samples for every possible configuration, which is computationally expensive and infeasible.

AdaReasoner aims to overcome these challenges by creating an intelligent agent that learns to dynamically adjust these configurations, thereby enabling more flexible and effective thinking from LLMs without requiring extensive manual prompt engineering or exhaustive data collection.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AdaReasoner is designed as an LLM-agnostic plugin that employs a reinforcement learning (RL) framework to automate adaptive reasoning configurations. This sophisticated approach enables LLMs to adjust their thinking processes based on the specific demands of each task.

**Problem Formulation:**
The core problem is formulated as finding the most effective hyperparameter configuration `a` for a given question `q` to optimize the LLM's ability (`Φ`) to generate the correct answer `Φ(q|a)`. AdaReasoner acts as a policy `Π_Θ` that, given a question `q` and the LLM `Φ` (considered as the state), selects an action `a`. The objective is to train the policy network weights `Θ` to maximize the expected reward, `E_{(q,R)~D_train} E_{a~Π_Θ(a|q,Φ)} [r(Φ(q|a), R)]`. This is conceptualized as a multi-armed bandit problem, where each question is an independent task, and the agent selects actions (configurations) to maximize reward.

**Hyperparameter Configuration (Action Space):**
AdaReasoner's action `a` is a 3-dimensional vector comprising three key hyperparameters, chosen for their substantial impact on reasoning performance and adaptability across LLMs:
1. **Generation Temperature (`a_t`):** Controls the randomness of outputs, influencing diversity and creativity. Discretized into a set `A_t` (e.g., 0.0 to 1.0 in 0.1 increments).
2. **Reasoning Instruction Format (`a_p`):** Guides the LLM's cognitive strategy. `A_p` is constructed from a compositional design (based on structure-mapping theory) combining a "base component" (e.g., creative thinking, self-audit) with a "variation" (e.g., clarify language, cross-reference). This allows for a rich and flexible set of instructions.
3. **Number of Reasoning Steps (`a_s`):** Influences the depth and thoroughness of the inference process. Discretized into a set `A_s` (e.g., integers from 3 to 10).

This factorization of the action space into discrete components significantly reduces the complexity of the optimization problem.

**Neural Architecture of AdaReasoner:**
The AdaReasoner neural network takes the input question `q`.
1. **Embedding:** The question `q` is first embedded (e.g., using a pre-trained BERT model).
2. **Shared Layer:** The embedding passes through a common, shared layer (`f_θ_c`) to extract a rich latent representation `h`, capturing features necessary for decision-making.
3. **Factorized Policy Heads:** `h` is then fed into three parallel policy heads (`f_θ_p`, `f_θ_t`, `f_θ_s`), each responsible for selecting one specific hyperparameter (`a_p`, `a_t`, `a_s`). Each head is implemented as a feed-forward network, outputting logits for its respective action values.
This design ensures that while each action is selected independently, they are all informed by a shared understanding of the input question, promoting coherent decision-making.

**Exploration Strategy:**
To efficiently navigate the large configuration space with few-shot training, AdaReasoner employs **Boltzmann exploration**. This mechanism probabilistically selects actions based on their estimated rewards, balancing exploration (trying new configurations) and exploitation (using known good configurations). The Boltzmann temperature `τ` is exponentially annealed (`τ_t = τ_0 * α^t`) during training, gradually shifting the policy from broad exploration to refined exploitation.

**Reward Signal:**
AdaReasoner uses a **pre-trained language judgment model** (DeBERTa-based) as a reward model. For a given LLM-generated answer `Φ(q|a)`, the reward model compares it against the ground truth reference `R`, providing a scalar score. This indirect, differentiable reward signal is crucial because there is no direct ground truth for the optimal configuration `a` itself.

**Training and Optimization:**
AdaReasoner's parameters `Θ = {θ_c, θ_p, θ_t, θ_s}` are optimized using the **REINFORCE algorithm**. The total loss `L` is the sum of head-specific losses `L_j = -r log Π_θ_j(a|q,Φ)`, where `j ∈ {p, t, s}`. Gradients for the shared layer (`θ_c`) are aggregated from all heads, while individual heads are updated based on their specific losses. This multi-task learning approach ensures joint optimization and coherence across action dimensions.

**Theoretical Guarantees (Appendix B):**
The paper provides theoretical guarantees for AdaReasoner's performance:
- **Fast Convergence:** Under conditions of L-smoothness and bounded gradient variance for the objective function, AdaReasoner achieves a low error bound (`O((J(Θ*) - J(Θ0))/(ηK) + Lησ^2)`) when trained with Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), signifying rapid convergence in few-shot settings.
- **Sublinear Regret:** By treating the configuration selection as a non-stochastic multi-armed bandit problem with softmax exploration, the cumulative regret `R(K)` is bounded by `O(√(K|A|log|A|))`. This implies that the average regret per step `R(K)/K` vanishes rapidly as training steps `K` increase, confirming its efficiency with limited training examples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AdaReasoner was rigorously evaluated across six different LLMs (GPT-4o, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct, Claude-3.5-sonnet, Deepseek-R1, GPT-o3-mini) and a diverse set of reasoning tasks, including:
- **Logical/Mathematical:** MMLU (Math), LogiQA
- **Figurative/Creative:** Metaphor
- **Factual Consistency:** TruthfulQA
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD):** BRIGHTER (multilingual emotion analysis), StepGame (spatial planning), CRoW (commonsense reasoning)
- **Knowledge-Intensive:** GPQA, MMLUChem, MedExQA

The evaluations consistently demonstrate AdaReasoner's superior performance and robustness:

#### 4.1. Performance Across Diverse Reasoning Tasks

- **Consistent Outperformance:** AdaReasoner achieved the highest average accuracy across every LLM group, significantly outperforming standard baselines such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thought (ToT), Best-of-N, Auto-CoT, In-context CoT (ICL), and Think Short (Table 1). For instance, it reached 80.42% average accuracy with GPT-4o, 81.43% with Claude-3.5-sonnet, and 86.28% with Deepseek-R1.
- **Task-Specific Adaptability:** The results underscore AdaReasoner's ability to tailor reasoning configurations to the specific demands of different tasks. While other methods might excel on particular task types (e.g., ToT on MMLU for complex, knowledge-intensive challenges; Auto-CoT on TruthfulQA for factual consistency), AdaReasoner's adaptive nature provided a more generalized and robust improvement.

#### 4.2. Few-shot Training Efficiency

- **Rapid Convergence:** Experiments showed that AdaReasoner exhibits "marginal gains beyond 100 shots" across all tested LLMs (Figure 3). This validates its efficiency in few-shot learning scenarios, where 50–100 demonstrations are often sufficient for the model to learn core reasoning patterns. This empirical observation aligns with the theoretical guarantees of sublinear regret and fast convergence.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Instruction Format (`a_p`):** Adapting only the reasoning instruction (`a_p`) resulted in the smallest performance drop compared to other single-parameter adaptations, highlighting its crucial role in adaptive reasoning (Table 2). The study also confirmed that adapting random seeds was not beneficial.
- **Effectiveness of Boltzmann Exploration and Reward Model:** AdaReasoner's performance dropped when Boltzmann exploration was replaced by Thompson sampling, or when the reward signal was rescaled, although it showed robustness to reward noise. This confirms the efficacy of the chosen exploration and reward mechanisms.
- **Model-Specific Adaptation:** Applying a policy trained on Qwen to GPT-4o resulted in a performance drop, indicating that the learned adaptive policies are somewhat model-specific due to variations in LLM reward landscapes. This suggests that per-model adaptation, even with few-shot data, is necessary.
- **Value of Integrated Design:** AdaReasoner's factorized policy with a shared encoder outperformed an ensemble of independently trained policy heads, validating its architectural design.

#### 4.4. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization

- AdaReasoner demonstrated "stable superior performance" over other reasoning methods on three OOD datasets (BRIGHTER, StepGame, CRoW) that it had never encountered during training (Table 3). This indicates strong robustness and generalization capabilities beyond the specific training domains.

#### 4.5. Performance on Knowledge-Intensive Datasets

- On knowledge-intensive tasks like GPQA, MMLUChem, and MedExQA, AdaReasoner showed a "modest yet consistent capacity" to adapt, outperforming conventional reasoning approaches (Figure 4). While adapting reasoning strategies alone cannot fully compensate for a lack of domain-specific knowledge, the selected adaptive prompts often involved "self-audit," "cross-reasoning," or "creative prompt variants" (Table 8). This suggests that even for knowledge-heavy tasks, tailoring the reasoning process helps, and that the agent intelligently selects configurations aimed at higher-level cognitive strategies. For example, MMLUChem preferred creative reasoning or systematic structure for validation.

In summary, the results strongly support AdaReasoner's effectiveness as an adaptive plugin for LLM reasoning, consistently delivering performance improvements across a broad spectrum of tasks and LLMs, underpinned by efficient few-shot learning and robust generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AdaReasoner represents a significant step forward in optimizing Large Language Model (LLM) performance by addressing the critical challenge of adaptive reasoning configuration. Its contributions have several important implications and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Enhanced LLM Reasoning and Performance

The most direct significance of AdaReasoner is its demonstrated ability to consistently achieve higher average accuracy across diverse reasoning tasks and multiple LLMs, compared to conventional fixed-strategy approaches. By dynamically adjusting key parameters—reasoning instruction formats, generation temperature, and the number of reasoning steps—AdaReasoner enables LLMs to think more flexibly and optimally. This leads to more accurate and reliable outputs, pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve in complex problem-solving.

#### 5.2. Efficiency and Scalability through Few-shot Learning

AdaReasoner's design, leveraging a reinforcement learning framework with a factorized action space and Boltzmann exploration, allows for efficient few-shot training. The rapid convergence observed (marginal gains beyond 100 shots) is a crucial advantage, as it minimizes the need for extensive, costly data collection and training. This makes the adaptive tuning process practical and scalable, even for specialized applications with limited task-specific data. The theoretical guarantees for fast convergence and sublinear regret further reinforce the model's efficiency.

#### 5.3. Democratization of Advanced LLM Usage

One of the most impactful aspects highlighted by the authors is the democratization of AI reasoning (Appendix G). By automating what is traditionally a laborious and expert-driven process of prompt engineering and hyperparameter tuning, AdaReasoner lowers the barrier for non-expert users. Individuals and organizations without deep expertise in LLM internals can now leverage the full potential of these models for diverse tasks, from academic research to creative content generation and complex data analysis, accelerating innovation and widening access to advanced AI capabilities.

#### 5.4. Robustness and Generalization

The consistent performance across different LLM architectures, coupled with strong out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, indicates that AdaReasoner learns robust adaptive strategies. This robustness suggests that the principles of adaptive configuration are transferable and can provide benefits even in unseen domains, making it a valuable tool for real-world applications where tasks can vary widely.

#### 5.5. Insights into LLM Cognitive Processes

The detailed analysis of AdaReasoner's action selections (e.g., specific `a_p` choices for different datasets) provides valuable empirical insights into how LLMs can be optimally guided for various cognitive tasks. Observing that "creative reasoning" prompts are favored for metaphor interpretation and knowledge-intensive tasks, while "step-by-step" or "self-audit" prompts are chosen for logical problems, deepens our understanding of effective prompting strategies and the underlying reasoning mechanisms of LLMs.

#### 5.6. Future Directions

While AdaReasoner marks a significant advancement, the authors also identify avenues for future research. Expanding the framework to incorporate continuous action spaces or gradient-based prompt generation could allow for even more fine-grained and expressive adaptation. Furthermore, integrating more domain-specific knowledge, particularly for highly knowledge-intensive tasks where reasoning strategy alone cannot fully compensate for factual gaps, presents another promising direction. Addressing the modest cross-model transferability to make policies more universally applicable would also be valuable.

In conclusion, AdaReasoner not only provides a highly effective solution for adaptive LLM reasoning but also paves the way for more intelligent, efficient, and accessible interaction with advanced AI systems. Its methodology and empirical success set a new standard for optimizing LLM performance through dynamic configuration, with broad positive implications for AI development and deployment.
