# Early science acceleration experiments with GPT-5

> [!summary] Summary
> A collection of expert-authored case studies documenting how GPT-5 assisted real research in mathematics, physics, astronomy, computer science, biology and materials science. GPT-5 rediscovered frontier results, ran deep cross-vocabulary literature searches, worked as a research partner, and helped produce four new, human-verified mathematical results, while also making confident errors that experts had to catch.

## Problem

- Many scientists remain unaware of what frontier AI models can actually contribute to ongoing research, so there is little concrete documentation of where they save expert time.
- Prior AI-for-science accounts, such as AlphaEvolve, target search problems with a well-defined objective that can be hill-climbed, not general-purpose assistance on open-ended research questions.
- Literature search across disciplines is hard because different fields use different vocabulary for the same concept, so relevant or forgotten results are missed by standard web search.

## Method

- ==Case-study protocol==: domain experts prompt **GPT-5** and **GPT-5 Pro** on live research questions, record ==full transcripts==, and report which human input was required and where the model failed.
- Four-part structure covers ==rediscovery of recent results== (for example, given v1 of a paper, can the model reach v2), ==deep literature search==, working in tandem, and obtaining new results.
- Every AI-produced proof or claim is ==verified by the human authors==; the search-based sections treat GPT-5 as a ==concept-level retriever== that also supplies missing connecting arguments.

## Results

- Given v1 of a convex-optimization paper, GPT-5 Pro improved the gradient-descent step-size condition for convex loss curves from **1/L** to **1.5/L** in about **17 minutes**. The optimal bound is **1.75/L**, which internal longer-thinking models derived from scratch.
- In the Erdős problems literature search, GPT-5 located previously published solutions to **10** problems not marked as known, including a German-language proof, and translated and explained it for verification.
- The paper reports **four** new human-verified mathematical results, including Erdős Problem 848, online algorithm lower bounds, tree subgraph-count inequalities and a COLT dynamic-networks problem, alongside documented hallucinated references and defended errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frontier models act best as accelerators under expert guidance: they turn imprecise ideas into concrete steps, but verification and problem selection stay with humans.
> - Concept-level literature search is a standout capability, bridging vocabulary gaps between fields and even supplying the missing deduction that links an old result to a new problem.
> - Models can confidently err and defend mistakes, and results depend on prompt details and are hard to reproduce, so outputs need independent checking and gains are modest in scope.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Early science acceleration experiments with GPT-5" positions itself at the vanguard of research exploring the potential of large language models (LLMs) to genuinely contribute to scientific discovery, moving beyond mere assistive tasks. Historically, AI's role in science has often been confined to data analysis, simulation, or hypothesis generation within well-defined, often computationally intensive, search spaces. For instance, the authors contrast their work with Google's AlphaEvolve, which focuses on search problems with objective functions that can be "hill-climbed."

This paper, however, represents a significant shift by demonstrating the intellectual contributions of a *general-purpose* LLM, GPT-5, capable of answering "any type of query" and engaging in complex reasoning across diverse scientific domains. It builds upon a growing trend of "AI-assisted proofs" and "LLM-assisted research" that has seen a surge in recent months (as evidenced by the cited arXiv preprints like [FK25; DMN25; IX25; AM25; JR25; Sal25; Geo+25]). This body of work collectively explores the nascent ability of frontier AI models to become "research partners" for human scientists.

The broader research landscape for LLMs has rapidly expanded from natural language understanding and generation, programming assistance, and planning, to now include complex problem-solving in mathematics, theoretical physics, and biology. This paper specifically aims to demystify the capabilities of frontier AI for a wider scientific audience, many of whom "remain unaware" of what such models can achieve. By showcasing concrete examples, the authors contribute to a crucial dialogue about integrating AI into standard scientific workflows, bridging the gap between AI development and its practical application in accelerating discovery. The work implicitly suggests that as AI models become increasingly sophisticated, they will not only automate routine tasks but also augment human intelligence in conceptualizing, proving, and discovering new scientific knowledge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this paper is to thoroughly document and exemplify the utility of GPT-5 as a tool for accelerating novel scientific research. The authors are motivated by the observation that despite the rapid advancements in AI, many scientists are still "unaware of the capabilities of frontier AI" and lack clear guidance on how to effectively integrate these models into their research workflows.

To address this, the paper sets out to achieve several key goals:
- **Showcase Concrete Contributions:** Present a collection of diverse case studies across mathematics, physics, astronomy, computer science, biology, and materials science where GPT-5 made "new, concrete steps" in ongoing research.
- **Highlight AI's Strengths and Weaknesses:** Clearly delineate "how AI accelerated their work, and where it fell short; where expert time was saved, and where human input was still key." This includes acknowledging GPT-5's tendency to "confidently make mistakes, ardently defend them, and confuse itself (and us) in the process," as well as challenges with reproducibility.
- **Provide Guiding Examples of Collaboration:** Document the detailed interactions between human authors and GPT-5, offering practical insights into "fruitful collaboration with AI." This involves sharing conversation transcripts and describing the necessary human input.
- **Demonstrate Novel Scientific Output:** Emphasize instances where GPT-5 contributed to "four new results in mathematics (carefully verified by the human authors)," underscoring its capacity to help settle previously unsolved problems, albeit of "modest in scope but profound in implication" given the rapid pace of AI progress.
- **Illustrate Diverse Modes of Assistance:** Categorize GPT-5's contributions into independent rediscovery, deep literature search, working in tandem with humans, and generating novel scientific results, thereby illustrating the breadth of its potential utility.

Ultimately, the motivation is to provide a "clear path for how researchers can use it to accelerate scientific discovery while keeping standards high," asserting that GPT-5 already offers "substantial value" and will become "even more powerful tomorrow."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is a qualitative, case-study-based approach, involving domain experts engaging GPT-5 with their active research problems. The authors deliberately chose problems spanning a wide array of scientific disciplines—mathematics, physics, astronomy, computer science, biology, and materials science—to demonstrate the general-purpose applicability of the model.

The core approach involves:
1. **Direct Interaction:** Human researchers posed specific queries or problems to GPT-5 (and in some cases, GPT-5 Pro), often uploading relevant documents or figures to provide context.
2. **Documentation of Interactions:** The interactions, including prompts, GPT-5's reasoning time, and responses, were meticulously documented. Where possible, ChatGPT conversation transcripts are provided as supplementary material, enhancing transparency and reproducibility for future researchers.
3. **Expert Evaluation:** The human authors, as experts in their respective fields, critically evaluated GPT-5's output for correctness, novelty, and utility. This included verifying mathematical proofs, assessing mechanistic biological hypotheses against unpublished lab data, and confirming literature search accuracy.
4. **Categorization of Contributions:** The case studies are organized into four distinct categories, reflecting different modes of AI-human collaboration:
 - **Independent Rediscovery of Known Results:** These "warm-up" experiments tested GPT-5's ability to reproduce recent (and thus out-of-training-data) frontier results, demonstrating its reasoning capabilities (e.g., convex optimization step-size, black hole symmetries, immune system mechanistic analysis).
 - **Deep Literature Search:** This focused on GPT-5's capacity to find non-obvious connections between disparate fields or locate obscure solutions to long-standing problems, often overcoming disciplinary jargon barriers (e.g., multi-objective optimization connections, solutions to Erdős problems). The authors also include a "cautionary tale" about initial attribution failures, highlighting a critical aspect of AI-assisted research.
 - **Working in Tandem with AI:** This category illustrates how GPT-5 acts as a collaborative partner, assisting with problem formulation, solving well-defined subproblems, refining ideas, or even refuting flawed approaches (e.g., Timothy Gowers' mathematical explorations, Brian Keith Spears' thermonuclear burn propagation modeling).
 - **New Scientific Results Obtained with AI:** This represents the highest level of contribution, where GPT-5 directly aided in generating novel, verified research findings (e.g., new proofs for Erdős Problem #848, online algorithms lower bounds, and inequalities on subgraph counts in trees).
5. **Focus on Human-AI Synergy:** Throughout the report, emphasis is placed on illustrating where human "expert oversight remains essential," identifying limitations, and suggesting best practices for maximizing fruitful collaboration. The use of "scaffolding" (e.g., providing simpler warm-up problems or specific problem-solving frameworks) is also implicitly shown to be a crucial part of the human's role in guiding the AI.

This empirical, expert-driven approach provides a rich, qualitative dataset on GPT-5's current capabilities, offering concrete examples that elucidate both the promises and pitfalls of integrating frontier AI into the scientific discovery process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a compelling collection of findings across diverse scientific domains, demonstrating GPT-5's emergent capabilities as a research accelerator:

**I. Independent Rediscovery of Known Results at the Scientific Frontier:**
- **Convex Optimization:** GPT-5 successfully improved the step-size condition in a recent convex optimization result from $\eta \le 1/L$ to $\eta \le 1.5/L$, a significant step towards the optimal $\eta \le 1.75/L$ derived by humans. Notably, its proof was distinct from the human-derived optimal one, and internal OpenAI models could even derive the optimal bound when given more time.
- **Black Hole Symmetries:** After a flat-space "warm-up," GPT-5 Pro (re)derived nontrivial Lie point symmetries, including an SL(2,R) algebra, for the stationary, axisymmetric wave equation on a Kerr background—a complex problem in theoretical physics. This matched recent human-derived insights.
- **Immunology Mechanistic Analysis:** GPT-5 Pro accurately analyzed experimental flow cytometry data on human T cells, providing novel mechanistic hypotheses (e.g., reduced IL-2 signaling via N-linked glycosylation interference) and making correct predictions for unpublished CAR-T cell cytotoxicity experiments. This demonstrated its ability to function as a "mechanistic co-investigator."

**II. Deep Literature Search:**
- **Interdisciplinary Connections:** GPT-5 rapidly surfaced non-trivial links between a newly proved geometric statement (on α-ratio covers in convex sets) and the concept of approximate Pareto sets in multi-objective optimization (Papadimitriou–Yannakakis's seminal work), bridging seemingly disparate fields.
- **Erdős Problems:** GPT-5 successfully located previously published solutions for 10 Erdős problems that were listed as "open" and identified significant partial progress for 10 others. This showcased its ability to semantically search and understand decades-old, often obscure, mathematical literature, overcoming challenges like differing vocabulary and scattered sources.
- **Attribution Cautionary Tale:** In the "clique-avoiding codes" problem, GPT-5 initially reproduced a known proof (by Noga Alon) without attribution, highlighting the critical need for vigilance and robust attribution mechanisms when using AI for research.

**III. Working in Tandem with AI:**
- **Mathematical Problem-Solving:** Timothy Gowers found GPT-5 useful for: 1) identifying existing definitions or observations in the literature, 2) solving well-defined subproblems (e.g., compactness of a specific function set in L2), and 3) rapidly identifying flaws in his own proposed solution strategies.
- **Physics Model Development:** Brian Keith Spears demonstrated GPT-5 Pro's capability to assist in building a reduced-physics model for thermonuclear burn propagation in inertial confinement fusion. It helped formulate the reaction-diffusion equation, suggest physical constants, and even implement a numerical solver. While requiring significant human guidance and correction for design and parameter exploration, it compressed months of work into hours, providing both numerical results and a theoretical explanation of burn front dynamics.

**IV. New Scientific Results Obtained with AI:**
- **Erdős Problem #848 (Combinatorial Number Theory):** GPT-5 Pro provided a key new idea for solving this long-standing open problem concerning subsets of integers where $ab+1$ is never square-free. The final proof was a collaboration between GPT-5's insight and human mathematical groundwork.
- **Online Algorithms Lower Bounds:** GPT-5 helped prove new bounds for the convex body chasing problem. It generated a non-obvious counter-example, showing that a natural "Follow-the-Leader" algorithm has an infinite competitive ratio. More significantly, it provided a crucial technical suggestion (using an $\epsilon$-hysteresis switching) and an extension idea (orthogonal decomposition) to improve the lower bound for general online algorithms from $\sqrt{d}$ to $\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor} \approx 1.11 \sqrt{d}$.
- **Inequalities on Subgraph Counts in Trees (Graph Theory):** With advanced scaffolding, GPT-5 autonomously reproved a known inequality ($Y - 9S - P \le 6$) with a novel, elegant proof, and then, building on that, proved a previously open conjecture ($29Y - 42P - 144S \le K$) for specific 5-vertex subgraph counts in trees. These proofs were entirely AI-generated (aside from minor clarity edits) and were notably different and more concise than existing human-derived arguments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "Early science acceleration experiments with GPT-5" carry profound significance for the future of scientific research, suggesting a paradigm shift in how discovery can be pursued.

**1. Unprecedented Research Acceleration:** The most immediate and striking impact is the demonstrated ability of GPT-5 to dramatically compress research timelines. Examples like reducing months of theoretical and numerical work in ICF to mere hours, or quickly solving mathematical problems that would take experts days or even years, underscore a potential for unprecedented velocity in scientific discovery. This acceleration can free up human researchers from arduous, time-consuming tasks, allowing them to focus on higher-level conceptualization and creative problem-solving.

**2. Augmentation of Human Intelligence and Expertise:** GPT-5 emerges not as a replacement, but as a powerful augmenter of human intellect. It acts as a "mechanistic co-investigator," a "knowledgeable research supervisor," or an "interdisciplinary bridge," allowing scientists to explore problems outside their primary domain of expertise, identify non-obvious connections, and quickly test and refute ideas. This broadens the scope of problems an individual researcher or a small team can tackle.

**3. Democratization and Accessibility of Knowledge:** The model's capacity for "deep literature search" that transcends disciplinary jargon can make complex, niche, or forgotten knowledge more accessible. It can help bridge the "language barriers" between scientific disciplines, fostering interdisciplinary insights and preventing redundant efforts, potentially accelerating the pace of convergence on complex challenges.

**4. Novel Avenues for Discovery:** The instances of GPT-5 generating genuinely new proofs and contributing key ideas to unsolved problems (e.g., Erdős problems, tree subgraph inequalities, online algorithms lower bounds) suggest its potential to autonomously drive novel scientific output. While currently requiring expert validation and guidance, these "modest in scope but profound in implication" contributions point towards a future where AI actively participates in generating fundamental scientific knowledge.

**5. Evolution of Research Workflows:** The paper highlights the emergence of new research workflows where humans and AI work "in tandem." This involves iterative prompting, scaffolding the AI with simpler problems, critically evaluating AI-generated solutions, and providing expert feedback to refine outputs. As models become more capable, these human-AI collaboration protocols will become increasingly sophisticated, integrating seamlessly into lab operating systems.

**Challenges and Future Directions:** Despite its remarkable capabilities, the paper also candidly addresses limitations: GPT-5 can make "confident mistakes," struggle with reproducibility, and initially fail to attribute sources. These necessitate robust human oversight, critical thinking, and a cautious approach to attribution. The challenge now lies in refining these human-AI interfaces, developing more robust error detection mechanisms, and establishing clear guidelines for AI-assisted research to maintain scientific rigor and ethics.

In conclusion, this work serves as a compelling early demonstration of how frontier AI models like GPT-5 are transitioning from mere tools to genuine intellectual partners in scientific exploration. The "exhilarating time in science and mathematics" predicted by the authors reflects a future where AI profoundly accelerates discovery, reshapes research methodologies, and expands the boundaries of human knowledge.
