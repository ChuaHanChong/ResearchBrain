# InterMimic: Towards Universal Whole-Body Control for Physics-Based Human-Object Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> The INTERMIMIC framework enables universal whole-body control for physically simulated humans interacting with diverse dynamic objects. It employs a teacher-student distillation approach to learn robust interaction skills from imperfect motion capture (MoCap) data, correcting physical inaccuracies and facilitating integration with kinematic generative models for applications in animation and robotics.

## Problem

- Existing physics-based methods for human-object interaction (HOI) lack scalability and diversity, typically confined to specific objects or simple actions, and rely on highly curated datasets.
- Large-scale MoCap data, while rich in human movement, often contains imperfections such as 'floating contacts,' penetrations, and missing hand details, which hinder direct imitation in physics simulators.
- A challenge exists in bridging the gap between kinematically generated motions, which often lack physical realism, and physically plausible simulations, and in enabling learned policies to generalize beyond reactive imitation.

## Method

- A two-stage curriculum-based teacher-student distillation framework where subject-specific teacher policies learn from imperfect MoCap subsets and actively 'perfect' the data.
- Teacher policies incorporate contact-guided rewards with adaptive reference markers, hand interaction discovery, ==Physical State Initialization (PSI)==, and Interaction Early Termination (IET) to robustly mitigate MoCap imperfections.
- A single student policy integrates skills from all teachers using 'reference distillation' (training on refined teacher rollouts) and 'policy distillation' (combining Behavior Cloning for bootstrapping with PPO for RL fine-tuning), employing a Transformer architecture for handling complex spatio-temporal dependencies.

## Results

- Teacher policies demonstrate superior robustness by achieving significantly longer tracking durations (42.6s vs. 12.2s) and lower object tracking errors (9.2cm vs. 13.4cm) compared to prior methods on single-subject tasks.
- The student policy attained high success rates (**88.8%** train, **98.1%** test) and long durations on the large-scale OMOMO dataset, showcasing strong generalization to objects with ten times greater weight and zero-shot generalization to novel objects.
- InterMimic successfully refines motions from kinematic generators (HOI-Diff, InterDiff), achieving high success rates (**12.5%** and **76.7%** respectively) and effectively bridging kinematic generation with physical plausibility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust, physics-based control policies for diverse human-object interactions can be effectively learned from large-scale, imperfect MoCap data through a multi-stage refinement and distillation process.
> - Adaptive reward mechanisms and simulation stabilization techniques (e.g., PSI, IET) are critical for overcoming inherent noise and limitations present in real-world motion capture data.
> - Distilling knowledge from multiple specialized 'teacher' policies into a single 'student' policy, supported by a Transformer architecture, provides a scalable and generalizable approach for mastering complex whole-body loco-manipulation skills across various objects and actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of character animation, particularly for human-object interactions (HOIs), has seen significant advancements with deep learning and physics-based simulation. INTERMIMIC positions itself at the forefront of this landscape by addressing critical limitations of prior work in both kinematic and physics-based interaction animation.

**Kinematic Interaction Animation:** Traditional kinematic methods excel at generating diverse human motions and interactions but often suffer from physical inaccuracies like "floating contacts" (lack of realistic physical contact) and penetrations between characters and objects. While recent diffusion models (e.g., HOI-Diff, InterDiff) have enabled advanced whole-body loco-manipulation generation from text or interaction prediction, they primarily produce body motion without explicit consideration for hand dexterity or physical plausibility. INTERMIMIC aims to bridge this gap by taking these kinematically generated motions as input references and refining them through physics simulation, thereby endowing them with physical realism and correcting inaccuracies.

**Physics-Based Interaction Animation:** Physics-based methods, typically leveraging Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL), offer inherent physical realism. Existing works in this area have focused on specific scenarios like human-human interactions, hand-object interactions, or human-static scene interactions. For dynamic objects, prior methods often concentrate on simpler tasks like punching/striking, sports-related actions, or basic box-carrying. A major limitation of these approaches is their lack of scalability; policies are typically trained for specific object types, actions, or rely on highly curated datasets like GRAB which features limited object diversity and low-dynamic full-body motion.

INTERMIMIC distinguishes itself by claiming to be the *first* framework to achieve "universal whole-body loco-manipulation simulation with diverse dynamic objects, beyond pick-and-place and grasping actions." It tackles the challenge of learning from *imperfect, large-scale MoCap data* (e.g., OMOMO, BEHAVE, HODome, IMHD, HIMO), which are rich in full-body interactions but prone to artifacts. By handling diverse dynamic objects and correcting MoCap errors, INTERMIMIC extends beyond the limitations of highly curated, single-object, or low-dynamic datasets that previous physics-based methods often depend on. Its teacher-student distillation framework specifically addresses the scalability issue by efficiently integrating skills learned from subsets of data, a novel approach for physics-based HOI imitation. Furthermore, the framework's ability to integrate with kinematic generators for downstream applications like text-to-interaction and interaction prediction marks a significant step towards generalizable and generative modeling of physically plausible human-object interactions. The explicit mention of its adaptability for real-world humanoid robot control also places it within the broader robotics research agenda, seeking to transfer human-like dexterity to physical agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of INTERMIMIC is to achieve realistic and universal control for physically simulated humans interacting with a wide range of diverse and dynamic objects. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and challenges:

1. **Addressing MoCap Imperfections:** Motion Capture (MoCap) data, while a rich source of human movement, is often imperfect, especially for complex HOIs. Common issues include inaccurate contacts (floating or penetration), missing hand details, and variability in human shapes requiring challenging motion retargeting. These imperfections make direct imitation in a physics simulator difficult, as accurately tracking flawed kinematics can lead to unrealistic dynamics. The paper explicitly aims to correct these errors and enhance the physical plausibility of captured actions.
2. **Scalability for Diverse Interactions:** Existing physics-based motion imitation methods struggle to scale to the complexity and diversity of whole-body interactions involving multiple, dynamic, and varied objects. Training separate policies for each object or action is unscalable. The motivation is to develop a single, robust policy capable of mastering a wide range of motor skills from hours of imperfect data, moving beyond simple grasping or single-object scenarios.
3. **Bridging Imitation and Generation:** The paper seeks to move beyond mere imitation by enabling the learned control policy to serve as a physically-grounded "execution layer" for kinematic generative models. This means integrating with methods that predict future interactions or generate interactions from text prompts, thereby elevating the framework from reactive imitation to proactive, generative modeling of HOIs.
4. **Enabling Downstream Applications:** A significant motivation is to facilitate versatile downstream applications. This includes translating whole-body loco-manipulation skills to humanoid robots, correcting and perfecting raw MoCap data for animation pipelines, and providing a physically plausible engine for advanced generative AI models.

In essence, INTERMIMIC is motivated by the desire to create a physically-aware, scalable, and generalizable control framework that can robustly learn complex human-object interaction skills from imperfect data, correct physical inaccuracies, and serve as a foundation for both advanced animation and robotic control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

INTERMIMIC employs a novel two-stage, curriculum-based teacher-student distillation framework to tackle the challenges of MoCap imperfection and scalability in physics-based HOI imitation.

**Task Formulation:** The goal is to learn a policy that produces simulated human-object motion matching reference MoCap data, while compensating for missing or inaccurate details. The state `s_t` includes human proprioception, object observations (position, rotation, velocity, angular velocity), object geometry (vectors from human joints to object surface), and contact markers (`c_t`). A crucial `goal state s_g_t` incorporates future reference poses, rotation, positions, and contact information. Actions are specified as joint PD targets for the human model's 51 actuated joints.

**Two-Stage Pipeline:**

**(i) Imitation as Perfecting (Teacher Policies):**
- **Subject-Specific Teachers:** Multiple teacher policies (`π^(T)`) are trained in parallel on small, imperfect subsets of data, typically per human subject. This modularity simplifies the learning problem for each teacher.
- **Imitation as Retargeting:** Teachers unify HOIs from different human shapes to a canonical human model. Rewards are designed with embodiment-aware components (for joint position `E_h_p` and rotation `E_h_θ`, and interaction tracking `E_d` weighted by distance to objects) and embodiment-agnostic components (for object tracking `E_o_p`, `E_o_θ` and contact tracking `E_c_b`, `E_c_p`). The goal is to preserve dynamics invariant to embodiment gaps while loosely aligning kinematics.
- **Reference Refinement:** Teachers actively "perfect" imperfect MoCap data by learning from it. The physics simulator inherently corrects inaccuracies.
 - **Contact-Guided Reward:** To mitigate MoCap contact artifacts, adaptive reference contact markers (`ˆc`) are inferred based on object acceleration and proximity. These define promotion, penalty, and neutral zones for contact (Figure 3i), allowing the policy to learn robust contact dynamics even with noisy ground truth. Foot-ground contact is also explicitly managed.
 - **Hand Interaction Discovery:** For MoCap data with limited hand detail, reference contact markers are activated for hand parts near objects, leveraging RL's exploratory nature to develop effective hand interaction strategies within constrained ranges of motion (Table B).
 - **Physical State Initialization (PSI):** To overcome roll-out failures caused by imperfect initial states, PSI stores successful simulation states from prior rollouts in a buffer. New rollouts are initialized from this buffer, increasing the likelihood of stable learning (Figure 3ii, Figure C). This is applied sparsely for efficiency.
 - **Interaction Early Termination (IET):** Standard early termination conditions are augmented with HOI-specific checks: object deviation, human-object distance, and prolonged loss of required contact. This prevents policies from learning invalid transitions.
- **Policy Learning:** Teachers are trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), maximizing expected discounted rewards.

**(ii) Imitation with Distillation (Student Policy):**
- **Scalable Skill Learning:** A single student policy (`π^(S)`) integrates all skills learned by the various teacher policies.
- **Reference Distillation:** Instead of using raw, noisy MoCap data, the student uses the refined rollouts from the teacher policies as high-quality references for defining its goal state and reward functions. This "cleaned" data significantly improves student learning (Figure 2, 5).
- **Policy Distillation with RL Fine-tuning:** The student learns from the teachers' actions (`a^(T)`) as online experts. This process starts with Behavior Cloning (BC) (via DAgger updates) to bootstrap learning, then gradually transitions to PPO for RL fine-tuning. This "demonstration-based pre-training refined by RL fine-tuning" approach, inspired by LLMs, allows the student to surpass mere demonstration replication and resolve conflicts/ambiguities among teachers, converging to optimal solutions.
- **Transformer Architecture:** Given the higher-dimensional observations and larger-scale data, the student policy utilizes a Transformer encoder, leveraging its inductive bias for sequential modeling and capacity for longer-term observations, outperforming MLPs for complex spatio-temporal dependencies.

**Integration with Kinematic Generators:** The framework is designed to integrate seamlessly with kinematic generators (e.g., HOI-Diff for text-to-HOI, InterDiff for interaction prediction) by using their generated motions as references for the physics-based agent.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate InterMimic's effectiveness in producing realistic, diverse, and physically plausible HOIs, showing strong scalability and zero-shot generalization.

**1. Teacher Policy Effectiveness (Imitation and Refinement):**
- **Qualitative Correction:** Teacher policies successfully correct MoCap imperfections. Figure 4 shows InterMimic faithfully imitating multi-body interactions from the BEHAVE dataset, correcting errors like incorrect hand positioning and floating contacts where PhysHOI fails. Figure 5 illustrates the recovery of plausible object rotations for symmetric objects that MoCap often misrepresents.
- **Quantitative Improvement:** Table 1 compares a teacher policy with SkillMimic on a single-subject, single-object task. InterMimic achieves significantly longer tracking durations (42.6s vs. 12.2s) and lower object tracking error (9.2cm vs. 13.4cm), demonstrating superior robustness to MoCap artifacts.
- **Ablation Study (PSI & IET):** The ablation in Table 1 confirms the importance of Physical State Initialization (PSI) and Interaction Early Termination (IET). Removing PSI or IET leads to decreased tracking duration and increased errors, validating their role in mitigating MoCap inaccuracies and optimizing trajectory collection.

**2. Student Policy Scalability and Performance:**
- **Large-Scale Data Imitation:** Table 2 presents the core quantitative evaluation of the student policy on the large-scale OMOMO dataset. InterMimic (Transformer, Line 5) achieves very high success rates (88.8% on train, 98.1% on test) and long durations (167.0s, 176.5s) with low human and object tracking errors.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** The student policy demonstrates strong generalization:
 - **Harder Test Set:** When tested on objects with ten times greater weight than during training ("OMOMO-Test (w x10)"), InterMimic maintains a reasonable success rate (56.8%) and duration (134.7s), albeit with slightly higher errors, showcasing robustness to dynamic variations.
 - **Kinematic Generators:** It seamlessly integrates with and refines motions from HOI-Diff (text-to-HOI) and InterDiff (interaction prediction), achieving high success rates (12.5% and 76.7% respectively) and long durations, effectively bridging kinematic generation with physical plausibility (Table 2, Figure 6).
 - **Novel Objects:** Figure 7 qualitatively demonstrates zero-shot generalization to novel objects unseen during training (from BEHAVE and HODome datasets), highlighting the effectiveness of its object geometry and contact-encoded representation.

**3. Ablation Study (Distillation and Architecture):**
- **Reference Distillation:** Table 2 shows that using teacher-refined references (Line 3, "Reference Distillation") significantly outperforms training from scratch with raw MoCap (Line 1, "PPO"), especially on the test set and for unseen shapes. This validates the importance of teachers in providing cleaner, more robust training data.
- **Joint PPO and DAgger Updates:** The full "Policy Distillation" strategy (Line 4) combining DAgger (behavior cloning) and RL fine-tuning (PPO) outperforms pure PPO (Line 3) and DAgger-only (Line 2). This confirms that supervised skill learning provides a good foundation, but RL fine-tuning is crucial for resolving ambiguities among teachers and converging to optimal, robust solutions.
- **Transformer Architecture:** The Transformer-based student policy (Line 5) consistently outperforms MLP-based approaches (Line 4) across all metrics, particularly for test-set and out-of-distribution cases. This is attributed to the Transformer's ability to handle complex spatio-temporal dependencies and leverage longer-term observations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

INTERMIMIC represents a significant step forward in the simulation and control of human-object interactions, with far-reaching implications across multiple domains:

**1. Advancing Physics-Based Animation and Character Control:** The framework successfully addresses long-standing challenges in creating realistic animated characters. By learning from diverse and dynamic interactions and correcting common MoCap artifacts, it enables the synthesis of highly plausible, whole-body loco-manipulation skills. This can drastically reduce the time and effort animators spend on manual correction, leading to more believable virtual characters in games, films, and virtual reality. The ability to recover missing physical details, like object rotations or hand contacts, further enhances animation fidelity.

**2. Bridging Generative AI and Physical Simulation:** A key impact is its seamless integration with kinematic generative models (e.g., text-to-HOI, interaction prediction). INTERMIMIC acts as a "physics engine" that can take high-level instructions or predictions from AI models and translate them into physically grounded actions. This bridges the gap between purely kinematic, often physically inaccurate, AI generation and the demands of realistic simulation, opening avenues for more intelligent and interactive virtual environments.

**3. Progress in Robotics and Humanoid Control:** The work has direct implications for real-world robotics, particularly for humanoid robots. By transferring complex human-like dexterity and interaction skills from MoCap data to a physics-based agent, INTERMIMIC provides a blueprint for training robots to perform intricate tasks involving diverse objects. The ability to handle embodiment differences and achieve retargeting inherently within the learning process is crucial for deploying such skills on varied robot platforms (as shown with Unitree G1). This could lead to humanoids capable of more nuanced, robust, and versatile manipulation in unstructured environments.

**4. Scalable Learning from Imperfect Data:** The curriculum-based teacher-student distillation framework offers a robust solution for learning complex skills from large, imperfect datasets. This approach, leveraging parallel teacher training and efficient student distillation, is a paradigm shift for physics-based learning, overcoming the sample inefficiency of traditional RL and the limitations of perfectly curated data. This methodology could be applicable to other domains where high-quality training data is scarce but large amounts of noisy data exist.

**5. Foundational for Future Research:** INTERMIMIC lays foundational groundwork for future research in several areas:
 - **More Dexterous Manipulation:** While current hand interaction discovery is effective, extending it to highly dexterous tasks would be a natural progression.
 - **Complex Scene Interaction:** Incorporating more intricate scene understanding and interaction beyond single or multiple objects could lead to even more intelligent agents.
 - **Real-World Transfer:** Further refinement of the sim-to-real gap for humanoid robots remains a critical area.

**Potential Negative Societal Impact:** The authors acknowledge that the ability to generate vivid human-object interaction sequences carries a risk of misuse, specifically in creating misleading content by fabricating scenarios. However, they mitigate this by designing the model with privacy in mind, using abstract geometric shapes for human and object representations. This abstraction reduces the inclusion of personally identifiable features, making it less likely for the synthesized data to be misused to compromise individual identities. This responsible consideration for ethical implications is an important aspect of high-impact research.
