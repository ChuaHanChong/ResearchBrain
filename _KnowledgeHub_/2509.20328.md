# Video models are zero-shot learners and reasoners

> [!summary] Summary
> Veo 3, a generative video model prompted with an image and text instruction, zero-shot solves tasks it was never trained for: segmentation, edge detection, image editing, physics understanding, affordance recognition, tool-use simulation, and maze/symmetry reasoning via frame-by-frame chain-of-frames generation, showing rapid Veo 2 to Veo 3 gains toward general-purpose vision foundation models.

## Problem

- Machine vision still relies on task-specific bespoke models like Segment Anything or YOLO, with no existing model solving arbitrary tasks purely by prompting.
- Prior unification attempts across vision tasks require fine-tuning or task-specific adaptation, unlike LLM zero-shot prompting that needs no inference-head changes.
- Whether the same web-scale generative training primitives that produced zero-shot LLMs also yield general-purpose visual understanding in video models is untested at scale.

## Method

- ==Black-box prompting protocol==: query Veo 2/Veo 3 via Vertex AI with an input image as first frame plus text instruction, treating the LLM-based prompt rewriter and video generator as one system.
- Four-tier ==qualitative taxonomy== (perception, modeling, manipulation, reasoning) organizing 62 tasks and 18,384 generated videos, each scored by success rate over 12 samples.
- **Chain-of-frames (CoF)** framing: frame-by-frame video generation treated as a visual analogue of chain-of-thought, evaluated quantitatively on 7 tasks with pass@k over best/last frame.

## Results

- Edge detection reaches **0.77** OIS pass@10 versus task-specific SOTA **0.90**, and segmentation reaches **0.74** mIoU pass@10, comparable to Nano Banana's **0.73**.
- Maze solving on 5x5 grids hits **78%** pass@10 versus Veo 2's **14%**; object extraction reaches **93%** pass@10 versus near-chance for Veo 2.
- Visual symmetry and analogy tasks show large Veo 2 to Veo 3 jumps, but analogy reflect/rotate transformations stay below the **33%** chance baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Consistent, large capability jumps between successive video model generations suggest video models are on an LLM-like trajectory from task-specific to generalist foundation models.
> - Reported performance is a lower bound since task and prompt representation choices (grid vs. photorealistic scene, etc.) strongly affect measured competence versus true ability.
> - Pass@k scaling with no plateau implies inference-time scaling and post-training with verifiers, mirroring the LLM playbook, could further boost zero-shot video reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper posits a transformative shift in machine vision, drawing a direct parallel with the revolution that Large Language Models (LLMs) brought to Natural Language Processing (NLP). Historically, both NLP and machine vision have relied heavily on task-specific models, where distinct architectures and training regimens were developed for problems like translation, question-answering, object detection, or image segmentation.

In NLP, this paradigm was disrupted by LLMs like GPT-3, which, through training on web-scale data with a simple generative objective, developed emergent zero-shot and few-shot learning capabilities. This allowed a single model to perform a wide array of language tasks without explicit fine-tuning, thereby establishing LLMs as "generalist foundation models."

The authors argue that machine vision is currently at a similar inflection point. While there are highly effective task-specific models in computer vision (e.g., Segment Anything for segmentation, YOLO for object detection), a unified model capable of solving diverse visual problems solely through prompting has remained elusive. Existing attempts to unify vision tasks often still require some form of adaptation or fine-tuning.

This paper proposes that large-scale generative video models, which share the "simple primitives" of LLMs (large, generative models trained on web-scale data, but for text/video continuation), are the key to unlocking general-purpose vision understanding. By demonstrating that Veo 3, a state-of-the-art video model, exhibits emergent zero-shot capabilities across a broad spectrum of visual tasks—from low-level perception to high-level reasoning—the authors position their work as a critical step towards establishing video models as the "foundation models" for machine vision. This would mark a departure from the fragmented, task-specific approach to a more unified, generalist paradigm, mirroring the success observed in NLP.

The work also subtly touches upon the broader multimodal AI landscape, where text acts as an interface to visual modalities. The fact that Veo is prompted via text and relies on an underlying LLM-based prompt rewriter for some tasks indicates a convergence of language and vision capabilities, pushing towards more holistic AI systems that can understand and interact with the world across different modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to affirmatively answer the question: **"Do video models develop general-purpose vision understanding, similar to how LLMs developed general-purpose language understanding?"** The authors are motivated by the profound paradigm shift observed in NLP, where LLMs transitioned from specialized tools to versatile, general-purpose foundation models capable of zero-shot problem-solving. They hypothesize that the same fundamental principles—large-scale training with a generative objective on web-scale data—are now being applied to generative video models, potentially setting them on a similar trajectory.

Specifically, the paper aims to demonstrate three core points:
1. **Broad Zero-Shot Task Solving:** To show that a capable video model, Veo 3, can solve a wide range of visual tasks across the entire vision stack (perception, modeling, manipulation, and reasoning) without explicit training or adaptation for those specific tasks. This involves analyzing 18,384 generated videos across 62 qualitative and 7 quantitative tasks.
2. **Emergent Visual Reasoning:** To illustrate that Veo 3, building upon its abilities to perceive, model, and manipulate the visual world, can exhibit early forms of visual reasoning, which the authors term "chain-of-frames" (CoF) reasoning, akin to "chain-of-thought" in LLMs.
3. **Rapid Advancement:** To highlight the rapid progress in video model capabilities by comparing Veo 3's performance against its predecessor, Veo 2, showcasing substantial and consistent improvements.

The underlying motivation is to accelerate the development of generalist AI in the visual domain, proposing that video models are the most promising candidates to become the unified foundation models for machine vision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research is intentionally minimalistic, mirroring the successful interaction paradigm with LLMs: **"We prompt Veo."** This black-box approach is crucial for demonstrating the zero-shot capabilities of the video model without any task-specific fine-tuning or adaptation.

**Model Selection:** The primary model under investigation is **Veo 3** (model ID: `veo-3.0-generate-preview`), a cutting-edge generative video model developed by Google DeepMind. For comparative analysis and to illustrate rapid progress, **Veo 2** (model ID: `veo-2.0-generate-001`), its predecessor, is also used. Both models are accessed via Google Cloud's Vertex AI API.

**Prompting Mechanism:** For each task, the models receive:
- An **initial input image**, which serves as the first frame of the generated video.
- A **text instruction** describing the desired task or manipulation.
The models then generate an 8-second video at 720p resolution and 24 frames per second (FPS).

**LLM-based Prompt Rewriter Consideration:** The authors acknowledge that the Vertex AI API utilizes an LLM-based prompt rewriter. While this means some task solutions might originate from the LLM, they treat the entire system as a single black box. To partially isolate the video model's reasoning abilities, they verify that a standalone LLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) cannot reliably solve key visual reasoning tasks (e.g., robot navigation, maze solving) solely from image input.

**Task Categories and Evaluation:** The tasks are hierarchically organized into four capabilities, building upon each other:
1. **Perception:** Understanding visual information (e.g., edge detection, segmentation, super-resolution).
2. **Modeling:** Forming a model of the visual world and its governing principles (e.g., intuitive physics, material properties).
3. **Manipulation:** Meaningfully altering the perceived and modeled world (e.g., image editing, background removal, novel view synthesis).
4. **Reasoning:** Applying changes across time and space over a sequence of manipulation steps (e.g., maze solving, visual symmetry, rule extrapolation).

**Evaluation Methods:**
- **Qualitative Assessment:** A comprehensive investigation across 62 tasks for Veo 3. For each task, 12 videos are generated, and a "success rate" (fraction of videos solving the task, as determined by authors) is reported as a ballpark estimate of capability.
- **Quantitative Assessment:** Seven specific tasks are evaluated quantitatively:
 - **Perception:** Edge detection (on BIPEDv2 using OIS F1-score), Class-agnostic instance segmentation (on LVIS using mIoU).
 - **Manipulation:** Object extraction (custom animal dataset using connected components count), Image editing (on Emu-edit using human ratings for fidelity and precision).
 - **Reasoning:** Maze solving (custom generated mazes using path correctness verification), Visual symmetry solving (custom grid dataset using incorrect cell count), Visual analogy completion (on KiVA using an autorater with Gemini 2.5 Pro for similarity).

**Performance Reporting:** For quantitative tasks, performance is reported for both "best frame" (optimal performance across any frame) and "last frame" (practical performance, as the optimal frame is unknown). This distinction addresses Veo's tendency to continue animating even after task completion. The `pass@k` metric (performance over `k` attempts, typically 10 videos per sample) is also used to account for variability. Comparisons are made against Veo 2, Nano Banana (a SOTA image editing model), and Gemini 2.5 Pro (an LLM) where appropriate.

**Prompt Engineering Best Practices:** The appendix details a prompt sensitivity study on visual symmetry, highlighting the critical role of clear, unambiguous instructions, specifying what *not* to change, providing "motion outlets" to prevent unnecessary animation, and scene/camera controls.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core finding of this research is the unequivocal demonstration of **emergent zero-shot learning and reasoning capabilities** in large generative video models, particularly Veo 3. The comprehensive qualitative and quantitative evaluations reveal a significant stride towards general-purpose vision understanding.

**Emergent Zero-Shot Capabilities Across the Vision Stack:**
- **Perception:** Veo 3 exhibits abilities in classical computer vision tasks such as edge detection, instance segmentation, keypoint localization, super-resolution, deblurring, denoising, and low-light enhancing, none of which it was explicitly trained for. It also shows nuanced perceptual understanding, interpreting ambiguous images like the dalmatian illusion and shape cue-conflict images.
- **Modeling:** The model demonstrates an understanding of intuitive physics (flammability, rigid/soft body dynamics, air resistance, buoyancy), object packing, material optics (refraction, reflection), and additive/subtractive color mixing. Furthermore, it can categorize objects and maintain a memory of world states across temporal and camera changes.
- **Manipulation:** Veo 3 performs a wide array of image editing tasks including background removal, style transfer, colorization, inpainting, outpainting, text manipulation, and doodle-based editing. It also showcases 3D-aware scene composition, novel view synthesis, object transfiguration, and simulates dexterous manipulation and object affordance.
- **Reasoning:** The paper highlights early forms of visual reasoning, termed "chain-of-frames" (CoF). Veo 3 can perform tasks like graph traversal, breadth-first search on a tree, completing visual sequences (Raven's progressive matrices inspired), connecting colors, shape fitting, sorting numbers, tool use, simple Sudoku, maze solving, robot navigation, and rule extrapolation.

**Quantitative Performance and Improvement:**
- **Significant Improvement from Veo 2 to Veo 3:** Across almost all quantitative tasks, Veo 3 consistently and substantially outperforms Veo 2, indicating rapid advancement in model capabilities within a short release cycle (half a year).
- **Edge Detection:** Veo 3 achieves an OIS F1-score of 0.77 (pass@10), which is remarkable for a zero-shot model, often generating more detailed edge maps than the ground truth.
- **Segmentation:** Veo 3 reaches a mean IoU (mIoU) of 0.74 (best frame pass@10), performing comparably to the state-of-the-art image editing model Nano Banana, despite lacking specific segmentation training. Prompting with a green background significantly boosted performance.
- **Object Extraction:** Veo 3 achieves a high pass@10 rate of 93%, demonstrating its ability to accurately perceive and extract multiple distinct objects, a significant leap from Veo 2's chance-level performance.
- **Image Editing:** Human ratings indicate that Veo 3 excels in fidelity (correct edits) and preserving details and textures, although controlling unintended animations (e.g., camera movement) remains a challenge.
- **Maze Solving:** Veo 3 shows strong zero-shot maze-solving abilities, with 78% pass@10 on 5x5 grids, far surpassing Veo 2. It also outperforms LLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro) when mazes are presented visually, highlighting the advantage of a visual medium for visual tasks.
- **Visual Symmetry:** Veo 3 significantly outperforms both Veo 2 and Nano Banana in completing symmetrical patterns, with performance heavily influenced by prompt phrasing.
- **Visual Analogy:** Veo 3 successfully completes analogies involving color and resize transformations, though it struggles with reflection and rotation tasks, indicating specific systematic biases.

**Limitations and Failure Cases:**
The paper transparently discusses current limitations, acknowledging that Veo 3's performance is often below that of specialized, task-specific models. It also presents several failure cases, including tasks requiring precise monocular depth/surface normal estimation, accurate force/motion prompting, complex physical simulations (e.g., tying knots, realistic glass breaking), detailed visual search for text, solving systems of linear equations, fine-grained spot-the-difference, complex visual IQ tests, and intricate manipulation like laundry folding or complex motion planning (e.g., the piano mover's problem). The authors attribute current performance partly to prompt sensitivity and suggest that reported metrics are a lower bound of the model's true capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant contribution to the field of artificial intelligence, particularly machine vision, by articulating and providing empirical evidence for the emergence of generalist capabilities in large generative video models.

**The "GPT-3 Moment for Vision":** The most profound impact of this work lies in its strong assertion that machine vision is on the cusp of a transformation akin to the "GPT-3 moment" experienced by NLP. By showcasing Veo 3's extensive zero-shot abilities, the paper builds a compelling case for video models becoming the unifying, general-purpose foundation models for vision. This paradigm shift would mean moving away from a landscape of numerous specialized models to a single, versatile model capable of handling a vast array of visual tasks, drastically simplifying development and deployment.

**"Chain-of-Frames" (CoF) as a New Reasoning Paradigm:** The introduction of "chain-of-frames" (CoF) as a visual analogue to "chain-of-thought" (CoT) in LLMs is a novel conceptual contribution. It highlights that the frame-by-frame generation process inherent in video models can enable step-by-step visual reasoning across time and space. This mechanism could unlock more sophisticated planning, problem-solving, and dynamic interaction capabilities in future AI systems, allowing them to simulate and predict outcomes in complex visual environments.

**Trajectory Towards More Capable AI:** The observed rapid and consistent performance improvement from Veo 2 to Veo 3 is a critical indicator of the field's rapid progress. While current performance may not always surpass task-specific models, the scaling properties (e.g., `pass@10` being consistently higher than `pass@1`) suggest that with further advancements, inference-time scaling methods, and post-training techniques like instruction tuning or Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), video models could significantly close the performance gap.

**Economic Implications:** The paper addresses the current high cost of video generation, drawing a parallel to the initial prohibitive costs of early LLMs. By citing the rapid decline in LLM inference costs, the authors predict a similar trajectory for video models, making them economically viable for broad adoption and further fueling the transition to generalist vision models.

**Future Research and Development:** This work opens numerous avenues for future research, including:
- **Advanced Prompt Engineering:** Developing more robust textual and visual prompting techniques to consistently elicit desired behaviors and control unintended animations.
- **Enhanced Reasoning:** Further exploring and refining CoF to tackle more complex, multi-step visual reasoning and planning tasks.
- **Bridging Performance Gaps:** Research into instruction tuning and RLHF specifically for video models to boost their zero-shot performance closer to or beyond specialized models.
- **Integrated Multimodality:** Advancing the synergistic capabilities of language and video models to create more human-like, interactive AI agents that can perceive, understand, and manipulate the world through both visual and linguistic inputs.

In conclusion, this research from Google DeepMind provides a compelling vision for the future of machine vision, positioning generative video models as the linchpins for developing highly versatile, general-purpose AI systems that can perceive, model, manipulate, and reason about the visual world with unprecedented flexibility.
