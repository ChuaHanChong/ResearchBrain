# General In-Hand Object Rotation with Vision and Touch

> [!summary] Summary
> RotateIt is a robotic system enabling continuous, multi-axis in-hand object rotation for diverse objects, trained purely in simulation and successfully deployed to the real world. It employs a two-stage reinforcement learning approach that distills an oracle policy with privileged information into a visuotactile policy capable of inferring object properties from vision and touch data.

## Problem

- Most advanced in-hand manipulation systems are tailored for specific objects or require extensive human demonstrations, limiting generalization to novel objects.
- Achieving continuous, large-angle, multi-axis object re-orientation with dexterous hands remains challenging, often limited to single-axis rotation.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for complex manipulation tasks that require inferring unknown object properties from noisy real-world sensory inputs is difficult.

## Method

- A two-stage training pipeline is used: an 'oracle' policy is trained in simulation with ground-truth object properties, then a visuotactile transformer is trained to infer these properties from realistic, multimodal sensor inputs.
- The system integrates robot proprioception, object depth images from an RGBD camera, and discretized contact locations from vision-based tactile sensors, processed by a transformer architecture for temporal modeling.
- The oracle policy explicitly encodes object shape information (via PointNet features) and physical properties to enhance robustness and generalization across a diverse set of objects.

## Results

- RotateIt successfully transferred from simulation to an AllegroHand robot, achieving continuous multi-axis rotation on diverse real-world objects, outperforming proprioception-only baselines.
- Explicit object shape encoding reduced the oracle policy's generalization performance drop on out-of-distribution (OOD) objects from 22% to 8%.
- Combining vision and rich tactile sensing reduced the sensorimotor policy's OOD generalization gap from 41% (proprioception-only) to 15%, demonstrating the critical value of multimodal input for real-world dexterity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly providing object shape information to the control policy substantially improves in-hand manipulation performance and generalization, especially for objects with irregular geometries.
> - Fusing rich visual and tactile feedback is crucial and synergistic for robust dexterous manipulation, significantly enhancing generalization to novel objects and closing the sim-to-real gap.
> - Transformer architectures are effective at processing the temporal history of multimodal sensory data to accurately infer complex object properties for precise control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and long-standing challenge in robotics: **generalizable dexterous in-hand manipulation**. It builds upon and significantly advances several key areas within the research landscape:

**a. Generalizable In-Hand Manipulation:**
Historically, in-hand manipulation research has often focused on reorienting specific objects (e.g., a cube or a cylinder) or required extensive human demonstrations. The broader goal is to enable robots to manipulate arbitrary, novel objects with the dexterity of a human hand. This work directly tackles this by demonstrating multi-axis continuous rotation for a diverse set of objects, a capability that has remained elusive for generalizable systems. It moves beyond the limitations of prior work that focused on single or a few objects.

**b. Sim-to-Real Transfer for Robot Learning:**
The paper heavily leverages the successful paradigm of training reinforcement learning (RL) policies in high-fidelity simulators and then transferring them to the real world without extensive real-world training (sim-to-real). It draws inspiration from and directly references pioneering work in this area, particularly methods involving privileged information, such as rapid motor adaptation (RMA) for legged robots and in-hand manipulation. Unlike earlier generalizable in-hand manipulation methods that struggled with real-world transfer, RotateIt achieves robust deployment by carefully managing the sim-to-real gap through realistic sensory modeling and domain randomization.

**c. Multimodal Sensing (Vision and Touch) in Robotics:**
A core contribution of this work is its explicit integration and demonstration of the importance of **multimodal sensory inputs**—vision, touch, and proprioception. While some prior works used proprioception or only vision, RotateIt argues for and empirically validates the necessity of fusing vision and touch. This aligns with biological systems, where humans rely heavily on both senses for dexterous tasks. The paper highlights that simpler tactile feedback (e.g., binary contact) offered limited benefits in previous work, whereas richer tactile information combined with vision significantly improves performance for complex manipulation. It leverages recent advancements in vision-based tactile sensors and robust visual perception (e.g., Segment Anything).

**d. Beyond Uniaxial Rotation:**
Many successful sim-to-real approaches for in-hand manipulation, including previous work from some of the authors and others, were primarily demonstrated for continuous rotation around a single axis (typically the z-axis). This work makes a significant leap by enabling **multi-axis object rotation**, which is far more challenging as it requires maintaining stable force closure and adapting to diverse object geometries simultaneously across multiple degrees of freedom.

**e. Deep Learning Architectures in Robotics:**
The paper effectively employs a **transformer architecture** for temporal modeling of multimodal sensory streams. Transformers, originally developed for natural language processing and later adapted for computer vision, are increasingly being applied in robotics for imitation learning and reinforcement learning. RotateIt demonstrates their efficacy in processing a history of proprioceptive, visual, and tactile inputs to infer crucial object properties, differentiating itself from approaches using transformers on different modalities like force/torque sensing.

In summary, RotateIt distinguishes itself by being one of the first systems to achieve continuous, multi-axis, generalizable in-hand object rotation in the real world *solely through learning from simulation and multimodal sensor fusion*, without requiring human demonstrations or object-specific fine-tuning. It pushes the boundaries of sim-to-real transfer and highlights the critical, complementary roles of vision and touch for advanced dexterous manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **RotateIt**, a robust robotic system capable of performing general in-hand object rotation along multiple axes using fingertip manipulation. A critical aim is to achieve this through a learning-based approach trained entirely in simulation and then directly deployed to the real world, generalizing to diverse objects without the need for fine-tuning.

The key motivations driving this research stem from significant challenges and limitations in the field of robotic manipulation:

**a. Overcoming Limitations of Single-Object or Limited-Object Manipulation:**
Despite progress, most advanced in-hand manipulation systems are still tailored for a single object or a small set of predefined objects. The ability to generalize to novel objects with diverse shapes and physical properties remains a major hurdle. This work aims to develop a system that can continuously rotate a wide variety of objects, moving towards "generalizable object manipulation."

**b. Enabling Continuous Multi-Axis Re-orientation:**
Achieving large-angle in-hand re-orientation requires continuous rotation capabilities along multiple axes. This task is inherently challenging because it demands maintaining stable force closure and dynamically adjusting finger positions and forces for objects of varying geometries and physical properties. Prior work often limited continuous rotation to a single axis (e.g., z-axis), which is insufficient for general re-orientation tasks. The authors are motivated to enable rotation over *arbitrary* axes.

**c. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Complex Tasks:**
While sim-to-real transfer has shown promise, successfully transferring complex, dexterous manipulation skills that require inferring unknown object properties in the real world is difficult. Prior methods often relied on privileged information (ground-truth object properties) during simulation training, but faced challenges in acquiring this information from real-world sensory inputs. The motivation is to effectively estimate these "extrinsics" from realistic sensor data.

**d. Harnessing Multimodal Sensory Information:**
Humans instinctively use both vision and touch for dexterous tasks, suggesting that integrating these modalities could be crucial for robotic systems. Many existing learning-based manipulation systems rely predominantly on proprioception or vision alone. The authors hypothesize that fusing visual and tactile feedback will lead to more robust perception, better generalization to novel objects, and ultimately, superior manipulation performance, especially for tasks requiring fine-grained interaction. They are motivated to empirically demonstrate the synergistic benefits of visuotactile sensing.

**e. Eliminating the Need for Real-World Demonstrations:**
Training complex manipulation policies often requires extensive and costly human demonstrations or real-world experimentation, which is sample-inefficient and time-consuming. A core motivation is to develop a system that can acquire these advanced skills purely through reinforcement learning in a high-fidelity simulator, bypassing the need for human demonstrations and directly transferring to physical robots.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to push robotic manipulation closer to human-like dexterity and generalization by integrating state-of-the-art learning techniques with multimodal perception, focusing on a challenging yet fundamental task of continuous multi-axis object rotation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RotateIt system employs a sophisticated two-stage training pipeline, entirely within simulation, to enable general in-hand object rotation with multimodal sensory inputs. The core idea is to distill a "privileged" oracle policy, which has access to ground-truth information, into a visuotactile policy that can operate on noisy, realistic sensory streams for real-world deployment.

**a. Two-Stage Training Pipeline:**

1. **Oracle Policy Training (Stage 1):**
 - **Goal:** Train a powerful control policy (`π`) in simulation that can efficiently and stably manipulate diverse objects over multiple axes, assuming access to complete, ground-truth information about the object.
 - **Privileged Information (Extrinsics, `z_t`):** This is the key "privileged" input. It comprises two main components:
 - **Object Shape Information (`z_shape_t`):** N points are sampled from the object's mesh and encoded into a feature vector using a PointNet architecture. This explicit encoding of shape is a crucial difference from prior work and found to be critical for complex objects.
 - **Object Physical Properties (`z_phys_t`):** A 7-dimensional vector containing mass, center of mass, coefficient of friction, scale, and restitution. This is concatenated with a 10-dimensional vector for object pose (position, orientation, angular velocity). These are projected into an 8-dimensional encoding.
 - The final `z_t` is a concatenation of `z_phys_t` and `z_shape_t`.
 - **Observations (`p_t`):** The oracle policy receives the robot's proprioception (a small temporal window of joint positions `q_t` and previous actions `a_t-1`) and the ground-truth privileged information `z_t`.
 - **Outputs:** The policy outputs 16-dimensional target positions for the robot's PD controller (`a_t`).
 - **Reward Function:** A carefully designed reward function encourages desired angular velocity (`r_rotr`) along a specified axis `k`, penalizes undesired angular velocities (`r_rotp` – critical for x and y-axis rotation), and includes terms for hand pose deviation, torque, energy consumption, and object linear velocity to promote stable and efficient manipulation.
 - **Optimization:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for training, sharing weights between the policy and critic.
 - **Simulation Setup:** Training occurs in IsaacGym using 32768 parallel environments. A diverse dataset of hundreds of objects (from EGAD, Google Scanned Objects, YCB, ContactDB) is used, with randomized physical properties and stable initial grasps.

2. **Visuotactile Policy Training (Stage 2 - Distillation):**
 - **Goal:** Train a separate module, the visuotactile transformer (`ϕ`), to infer the privileged information `z_t` from realistic, noisy real-world sensory inputs. This `ˆz_t` then conditions the pre-trained oracle policy for deployment.
 - **Multimodal Inputs:**
 - **Vision:** Object depth images (`o_depth_t`). In simulation, this is the foreground object depth. In the real world, Segment Anything is used to extract the object's depth map from an RGBD camera, making it robust to cluttered backgrounds. These depth images are encoded by a 3-layer ConvNet (`f_depth_t`).
 - **Touch:** Discretized contact locations. In simulation, these are parsed directly from the simulator. In the real world, four omnidirectional vision-based touch sensors (e.g., DIGIT-like sensors) on the fingertips track the deformation of high-intensity pixels, serving as a proxy for contact position. This 2D keypoint information is processed by an MLP (`f_touch_t`).
 - **Proprioception:** Robot joint positions (`q_t`) and previous actions (`a_t-1`).
 - **Visuotactile Transformer (`ϕ`):** A transformer architecture takes a sequence (history) of these encoded multimodal features (`f_depth_t`, `f_touch_t`, encoded `q_t`, encoded `a_t-1`) as input. Its output is the predicted extrinsic vector `ˆz_t`.
 - **Training Process:** The oracle policy is rolled out in simulation, and ground-truth `z_t` is recorded alongside the multimodal observations. The transformer `ϕ` is then trained to minimize the ℓ2 distance between its predicted `ˆz_t` and the ground-truth `z_t`. This process is iterated, where the oracle policy is sometimes conditioned on `ˆz_t` to refine the predictions. Adam optimizer is used for this regression task.

**b. Hardware Setup:**
The system uses an **AllegroHand**, a dexterous anthropomorphic robot hand with 16 degrees of freedom across four fingers. For vision, an **Intel RealSense D435** depth camera is used. **Omnidirectional vision-based touch sensors** are mounted on the distal end of each fingertip to capture tactile information.

**c. Evaluation:**
Performance is quantitatively measured using metrics like **Time-to-Fall (TTF)**, **Rotation Reward (RotR)**, and **Rotation Penalty (RotP)** in simulation. In the real world, **Radians Rotated (Rotations)** serves as the primary metric. Extensive ablation studies evaluate the impact of object shape information, vision, and touch. Generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) objects and multi-axis rotation capabilities are also tested.

This two-stage approach effectively disentangles the learning of robust control from the learning of robust perception, allowing the system to achieve complex manipulation skills by inferring necessary contextual information from noisy real-world sensory inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence for the efficacy of RotateIt, highlighting the critical roles of object shape information, multimodal sensing, and the transformer architecture for generalizable in-hand object rotation.

**a. Importance of Object Shape Information for Oracle Policy Training:**
- **Significant Performance Boost:** Explicitly encoding object shape information (via PointNet features) into the oracle policy substantially improved rotation reward (RotR), time-to-fall (TTF), and reduced rotation penalty (RotP) across all three principal rotation axes (x, y, z) compared to a baseline without shape information (Table 1).
- **Impact on Irregular Objects:** The performance gains were particularly pronounced for objects with non-uniform aspect ratios and irregular shapes (Figure 7, Stage 1 row), suggesting that shape awareness is crucial for manipulating complex geometries.
- **Improved Generalization:** When evaluated on 15 held-out, challenging out-of-distribution (OOD) objects, using point cloud information reduced the generalization gap (performance drop) from 22% to a mere 8% for oracle policies (Figure 8b).
- **Latent Space Learning:** Analysis of the latent representation `z_t` revealed that policies without explicit shape input tended to perceive objects as spherical or cuboidal, confirming the importance of shape for accurate object representation (Figure 9).

**b. Efficacy of Visuotactile Sensing and Transformer Architecture:**
- **Synergistic Multimodal Benefits:** Both vision and touch, when used individually, provided significant performance improvements over a proprioception-only baseline (Figure 6). Critically, combining vision and touch further boosted performance, enabling the visuotactile policy to closely match the performance of the privileged oracle policy.
- **Crucial for OOD Generalization:** For sensorimotor policies, proprioception-only input resulted in a substantial 41% performance drop on OOD objects, while the addition of visuotactile information drastically reduced this gap to only 15% (Figure 8c).
- **Finer Tactile Information Matters:** Detailed experiments showed that discretized contact location information was vital for improving performance, unlike binary contact (presence/absence of touch), which offered little benefit (Table 2). This highlights the need for richer tactile feedback beyond simple contact detection.
- **Transformer Superiority:** The transformer architecture demonstrated better sequence modeling ability and overall improved performance compared to temporal convolutions, confirming its suitability for processing historical multimodal sensory data (Table 4 in Appendix).

**c. Robust Real-World Deployment (Sim-to-Real Transfer):**
- **Successful Multi-Axis Rotation:** RotateIt successfully deployed to an AllegroHand robot, achieving continuous multi-axis rotation on various real-world objects, including those outside the training set (e.g., "Cocoon," "Squishy," "Stego"). It was able to rotate objects by multiple revolutions (~2π radians) within 20 seconds for the x-axis (Figure 10).
- **Superiority over Prior Work:** In a direct real-world comparison, the proprioception-only Hora policy failed to perform the task, unable to learn finger-gaiting or maintain object stability. This stark contrast strongly validates the importance of vision and touch for real-world dexterity.
- **Generalization to Real-World Physics:** The successful sim-to-real transfer to novel objects and real-world physics differences is a strong indicator of the policy's robustness and generalization capabilities, achieved without real-world fine-tuning.
- **Robustness to Sensor Failure:** Qualitative results (videos on the website) demonstrated that the policy maintained performance even with one touch sensor disabled, showcasing its robustness.

**d. Multi-axis Training Capabilities:**
- The study demonstrated that a single visuotactile transformer could be trained to perform multi-axis object rotation by conditioning on the desired rotation axis `k`. This distilled multi-axis policy achieved performance on par with specialized single-axis policies (Table 3), indicating the feasibility of a unified controller for diverse rotation tasks.

In summary, the results conclusively show that explicit object shape representation, combined with fused vision and rich tactile sensing, processed by a transformer, enables unprecedented generalizable multi-axis in-hand object rotation with robust sim-to-real transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RotateIt system represents a significant leap forward in the field of robotic dexterous manipulation, with profound implications and potential impact across several domains:

**a. Enabling General-Purpose In-Hand Manipulation:**
The ability to perform continuous, multi-axis rotation on diverse, previously unseen objects is a fundamental capability for general-purpose in-hand manipulation. By mastering this complex primitive, RotateIt opens the door for robots to execute a wider range of dexterous tasks, such as complex assembly, tool manipulation, object re-orientation to arbitrary poses, and fine adjustments, moving closer to human-level dexterity. This is a critical step towards robots that can adaptively handle novel items in unstructured environments.

**b. Paradigm Shift in Multimodal Perception for Robotics:**
This work definitively demonstrates the synergistic and indispensable role of fusing vision and touch for advanced manipulation. It empirically validates the long-held hypothesis that, much like humans, robots require rich multimodal sensory input to achieve robust and generalized dexterity, especially for complex object interactions. This finding encourages future research and development in multimodal sensor integration, driving advancements in sensor design, fusion algorithms, and learning architectures that can leverage diverse sensory streams effectively.

**c. Advancements in Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
RotateIt provides a robust framework for successful sim-to-real transfer for highly dexterous tasks. By meticulously modeling real-world sensory noise, employing domain randomization, and integrating modern perception techniques like Segment Anything, the system achieves impressive generalization without requiring costly or time-consuming real-world fine-tuning. This enhances the feasibility and scalability of developing complex robotic skills in simulation, accelerating the deployment of advanced robotic systems.

**d. Architectural and Methodological Contributions:**
The explicit encoding of object shape into privileged information is shown to be crucial for generalization, offering a valuable insight for future model-based or model-aware RL approaches. The successful application of transformers for temporal modeling of multimodal sensory history further solidifies their role as powerful architectures for complex robotic control, pushing the boundaries of what can be learned from sequential sensory data.

**e. Broader Impact on Embodied AI and Robotics Applications:**
The development of generalizable dexterous manipulation skills is a cornerstone for many applications. This work could impact:
- **Manufacturing and Logistics:** Robots capable of handling a wide variety of parts or packages without re-programming.
- **Service Robotics:** Robots performing household chores or assisting in care settings where objects are diverse and manipulation is key.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Robots operating in unknown environments, requiring adaptive interaction with novel objects.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** More fluid and natural interactions when robots can dynamically adjust object poses.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its significant contributions, the paper acknowledges several limitations, which also point to valuable future research directions:
- **Object Geometry Constraints:** The current method assumes objects are within the mechanical limits of the hand and not "too long" (e.g., pencils), suggesting a need to relax these constraints for truly universal manipulation.
- **Lifelong Learning:** The policy is frozen after training and cannot learn online in the real world. Integrating lifelong learning and real-world experience acquisition would enhance adaptability and robustness to unforeseen circumstances.
- **Enhanced Sensory Processing:** The tactile system currently uses low-dimensional contact locations, and vision could benefit from techniques like visual pre-training. Future work can explore leveraging the full information from image-based tactile sensors and advanced vision pipelines to further improve performance and robustness.

In conclusion, RotateIt stands as a landmark contribution demonstrating the power of integrating deep learning with multimodal vision and touch for generalizable, dexterous in-hand manipulation. Its success paves the way for a new generation of robots capable of interacting with the physical world with unprecedented versatility and adaptability.
