# HumanFlow -- Diffusion-Driven MAV Navigation Among Humans via Tightly-Coupled Motion Tracking, Forecasting, and Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed HumanFlow, a latent diffusion model for unified 3D human motion tracking and forecasting, explicitly conditioned on 3D scene context. The framework tightly integrates this perception capability with a flow-matching-based control policy, enabling Micro Air Vehicles (MAVs) to navigate safely and efficiently among humans, consistently achieving 100% collision avoidance in simulations.

## Problem

- Existing 3D human motion recovery methods are either computationally expensive (optimization-based) or prone to inaccuracy and physical implausibility (regression-based), especially under occlusions and dynamic camera movements.
- Prior human motion models often lack explicit 3D scene context integration and provide single-path deterministic forecasts, failing to account for the multi-modal nature of human behavior or incomplete scene data.
- Safe MAV navigation among humans faces challenges in balancing precise human motion reasoning with real-time computational efficiency and formal safety guarantees in dynamic, cluttered environments.

## Method

- The **HumanFlow** framework introduces a latent ==diffusion== model for joint 3D human motion tracking and multi-modal forecasting, operating on a compact latent representation.
- The model explicitly incorporates 3D scene context using a human-centric occupancy grid representation, distinguishing free, occupied, and unknown regions to guide motion predictions.
- A ==flow-matching==-based MAV control policy is trained to generate control inputs by leveraging frozen latent features from the human motion model, creating a tightly coupled perception-control loop for social navigation.

## Results

- HumanFlow achieved state-of-the-art 3D human motion tracking and forecasting performance, reducing GMPJPE by approximately **47%** compared to RoHM in occluded scenarios, while being about 268 times faster.
- The MAV navigation policy consistently demonstrated **100%** collision avoidance across all tested scenarios (noiseless and noisy human motion inputs) while maintaining low time-to-goal metrics compared to baselines.
- The system showed effective generalization to unseen motion types (e.g., dancing from the AIST++ dataset), maintaining **100%** collision avoidance with efficient navigation, indicating the robustness of its learned latent representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A compact latent diffusion model can efficiently perform robust 3D human motion tracking and multi-modal forecasting, maintaining physical plausibility even under noise and heavy occlusions, making it suitable for real-time robotic deployment.
> - Explicitly conditioning human motion prediction on 3D scene context, including incomplete scene information, significantly enhances accuracy and reduces physically implausible motions like foot skating or ground penetration.
> - Tightly integrating a control policy with a rich, compressed latent representation of human dynamics and scene context allows for proactive, safe, and efficient robot navigation without the control policy needing to explicitly learn complex human behaviors or scene interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Integrating robotic systems into human-populated environments necessitates robust and accurate perception of human motion, encompassing both tracking and forecasting. This capability is critical for ensuring robot safety and efficiency, particularly for mobile platforms like Micro Air Vehicles (MAVs) operating in dynamic, cluttered spaces. The current research landscape presents several challenges in achieving this goal.

Prior work on 3D human motion recovery generally falls into two categories: regression-based and optimization-based approaches. Regression methods offer real-time performance but often yield inaccurate, jittery, or physically implausible motion estimates, especially under long-term occlusions or partial observability. They typically focus on local motion and struggle with dynamic camera movements inherent in robotic applications. Optimization-based methods, while more accurate and robust to occlusions, are computationally demanding, making them unsuitable for real-time onboard deployment in resource-constrained robotic systems. Furthermore, neither of these traditional approaches intrinsically incorporates 3D scene context or provides capabilities for future motion forecasting, often requiring separate, subsequent models.

Generative models, particularly diffusion models, have emerged as a promising alternative for human motion synthesis, pose estimation, and forecasting due to their capacity to capture complex spatio-temporal dependencies and multimodal future behaviors. However, existing diffusion-based methods like RoHM, while robust to noise and occlusion, can be computationally slow due to iterative optimization over full sequences. Scene-conditioned human motion models have predominantly focused on forecasting, often assuming complete scene representations and noise-free input motion, which are unrealistic in real-time robotic scenarios with incomplete and noisy sensor data. These models are also frequently discriminative, producing only a single deterministic trajectory, despite the inherent multi-modality of human motion.

In the realm of safe navigation among humans, extensive research has been conducted for ground robots and, more recently, for aerial platforms. A key challenge is balancing accurate human motion reasoning with computational efficiency, especially under uncertainty and partial observability. Early work focused on modeling human cooperation and intent using methods like interacting Gaussian processes or Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs), but these often become computationally prohibitive in complex, multi-human environments or rely on simplified trajectory models. Safety-critical methods, such as those based on Hamilton–Jacobi reachability, offer formal guarantees but scale poorly and often require simplified dynamics. Efficient multi-agent approaches like Optimal Reciprocal Collision Avoidance (ORCA) simplify human motion assumptions, limiting their effectiveness when precise individual predictions are necessary. Learning-based approaches (e.g., Reinforcement Learning) can capture human motion patterns implicitly but often lack safety guarantees, struggle with out-of-distribution conditions, and are sample-inefficient. While some work has explored drone navigation, it frequently assumes stationary humans, perfect observations, or static environments. Recent work like HumanHalo introduced MPC frameworks for human-aware MAV navigation, constraining only initial control inputs for safety, but still assuming perfect knowledge of the human's current state.

This work addresses these gaps by introducing HumanFlow, a latent diffusion model designed for unified 3D human motion tracking and forecasting. It explicitly conditions on potentially incomplete 3D scene context and is engineered for real-time operation, robustness to noise and occlusions, and the generation of physically plausible, multi-modal predictions. Critically, HumanFlow learns a compact latent representation that facilitates its tight integration into a flow-matching-based control policy for MAV social navigation. This allows the robot to leverage rich, scene-specific human dynamics without explicitly learning them within the control policy itself, thereby enhancing safety and efficiency under partial observability without the high computational costs or conservative behaviors of many prior methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the fundamental challenge of seamlessly integrating robotic systems, particularly MAVs, into human-populated environments. For safe and efficient autonomous operation, robots must accurately perceive and predict human motion, even under challenging conditions such as heavy occlusions, partial visibility, and dynamic scene changes. Existing robotic perception and navigation systems often fall short in these aspects, leading to limitations in both safety and operational efficiency.

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

- **Develop a unified human motion perception model:** The core objective is to create a single, coherent model capable of jointly performing 3D human motion tracking and forecasting. This model needs to overcome the limitations of prior approaches by generating smooth, accurate, and physically plausible human motion estimates. A critical aspect is its robustness to adverse conditions commonly encountered in robotic applications, specifically noise, heavy occlusions, and partial visibility, which traditional regression or optimization-based methods often struggle with.

- **Incorporate 3D scene context:** A significant motivation is to condition human motion predictions on the surrounding 3D scene context. Unlike models that assume complete scene information, this work aims to explicitly account for incomplete or partially observed scenes, using a representation that distinguishes between free, occupied, and unknown regions. This scene awareness is crucial for generating human motion estimates consistent with the environment, such as avoiding collisions with furniture or walls.

- **Achieve computational efficiency for real-time deployment:** A major hurdle for complex human perception models in robotics is computational cost. The objective is to develop a model that operates in real-time, making it suitable for onboard deployment on resource-constrained platforms like MAVs. This involves leveraging techniques like latent space diffusion and efficient model architectures.

- **Tight coupling of perception and control:** Beyond just accurate perception, a key objective is to demonstrate how the advanced human motion model can be effectively integrated into a robot's control policy. The goal is to learn a rich, compact latent representation of human motion and scene context that can directly inform a control policy, reducing the policy's need to explicitly learn complex human dynamics. This tight coupling aims to enable proactive, human- and context-aware navigation.

- **Validate the system in MAV social navigation:** The practical application and validation of the proposed system are focused on the task of social navigation for MAVs in indoor environments. This specific scenario presents many of the target challenges: dynamic human motion, rapidly changing viewpoints, and frequent occlusions. The objective is to demonstrate that the integrated system allows MAVs to navigate safely and efficiently among humans, even under severe human occlusions, while maintaining computational efficiency.

- **Benchmark performance against state-of-the-art:** The work aims to quantitatively demonstrate superior performance in both human motion tracking accuracy and computational efficiency compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. For the MAV navigation component, the objective is to achieve high collision avoidance rates and efficient goal-reaching compared to various baselines, including those based on explicit optimization and reachable sets.

In summary, the motivation is to advance the state-of-the-art in human-robot interaction by providing robots with a robust, real-time, and context-aware understanding of human motion, which can then be directly leveraged for safe and efficient autonomous navigation in complex, dynamic human environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HumanFlow framework introduces a system for Micro Air Vehicle (MAV) social navigation that tightly integrates 3D human motion tracking and forecasting with control, utilizing a latent diffusion model conditioned on 3D scene context. The methodology is structured into three main components: a human motion autoencoder, a latent diffusion model for human motion, and a flow-matching-based MAV control policy.

**A. Context-Conditioned Human Motion Model**

The core of the perception system is a diffusion model designed to jointly denoise, complete, and forecast human motion, conditioned on observed (potentially noisy and partial) human body poses and 3D scene context.

1. **Human Motion Representation**: Human motion is represented as a sequence of 3D body joints ($h_{1:T}$). The model aims to predict this clean and complete sequence from noisy and partially observed input ($\tilde{h}_{1:T}^{\text{obs}}$) with a corresponding binary mask ($m_{1:T}^{\text{obs}}$) indicating missing observations, and 3D scene context ($S$).

2. **Autoencoder**: To facilitate efficient modeling of long temporal horizons, a temporal autoencoder (consisting of an encoder $E(\cdot)$ and a decoder $D(\cdot)$) is trained to map clean motion sequences to a compact latent representation ($z \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times T'}$). The autoencoder uses a standard L2 reconstruction loss on joint positions, augmented with a velocity loss for temporal smoothness and a bone length regularization to maintain skeletal consistency. The encoders and decoders are implemented as transformer encoders.

3. **Latent Diffusion Model**:
 - **Unified Tracking and Forecasting**: A single latent diffusion model is trained to perform denoising, inpainting, and forecasting. This is achieved through mask embeddings, which signal observed and unobserved portions of the input sequence. Random temporal masking is applied during training to enable the model to learn these diverse tasks.
 - **Noisy Input Encoding**: A separate encoder ($E_B(\cdot)$), sharing the architecture of $E$, encodes the noisy and incomplete observations ($\tilde{h}_{1:T}^{\text{obs}}$) into a latent representation ($z_{\text{noisy}}$). Missing joints are padded with a mean SMPL configuration, and masked temporal segments are filled using zero-order hold.
 - **Scene Context Integration**: Scene context is incorporated using a human-centric occupancy grid representation. A scene encoder ($E_S(\cdot)$), composed of 3D convolutions followed by average pooling, processes this grid to produce a global scene latent ($z_S$). Crucially, it explicitly represents free, occupied, and unknown regions, accommodating incomplete scene data. The encoded scene information is injected into the latent diffusion network via Feature-Wise Linear Modulation (FiLM).
 - **Training and Inference**: The diffusion model is trained using DDPM (Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models) with x0-prediction, employing 100 denoising steps during training and reduced to 10 steps during inference for efficiency. The training objective combines a standard diffusion denoising loss ($L_{\text{diff}}$) with regularization terms for velocity, foot-contact (skating), and bone length, similar to the autoencoder.

**B. Human-Aware MAV Policy**

The policy leverages the rich latent representations learned by HumanFlow for goal-directed, collision-free MAV navigation.

1. **Policy Formulation**: The control policy $\Pi$ generates MAV control inputs ($u_k$) conditioned on the initial MAV state ($x_0$), goal position ($g$), and the human motion and scene context. Instead of explicit, high-dimensional representations, the policy operates on the frozen latent features ($z_{\text{noisy}}$ and $z_S$) extracted by the pretrained HumanFlow encoders. These flattened latent features are concatenated with the MAV’s goal position (in MAV frame), initial velocity, roll, and pitch to form the policy input ($o$). The policy itself is implemented as a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).

2. **Offline Trajectory Generation (Supervision)**: To generate training data for the MAV policy, a two-step offline process is used:
 - **Human Motion Rollouts**: The trained human motion diffusion model is used to sample multiple plausible future human trajectories ($h_{1:T}^i$) by masking out future frames, capturing the conditional distribution of human motion based on past observations and scene context.
 - **Scenario Model Predictive Control (MPC)**: For each sampled human motion rollout (scenario), a nonlinear trajectory optimization problem is solved for the MAV. This optimization yields collision-free, goal-directed MAV trajectories that account for both the predicted human motion and the 3D scene structure. A key aspect is a scenario MPC formulation where the initial control action ($u_0$) is enforced to be identical across all sampled human motion scenarios. This ensures that $u_0^*$ is guaranteed to be safe with respect to the full conditional human motion distribution. The objective minimizes the expected MAV distance to the goal across scenarios, subject to MAV dynamics, safety distance constraints from human joints, and safety distance constraints from the scene (via Euclidean Signed Distance Function, ESDF). The constrained nonlinear optimization problem is solved using SLSQP.

3. **Policy Learning via Flow Matching**: The control policy is trained using flow matching to reproduce the distribution of these optimized MAV control sequences ($u_{0:T-1}^*$). Flow matching learns a vector field that interpolates between a base distribution and the target distribution (the optimized trajectories). This approach allows the lightweight MLP policy to generate control commands in real-time by leveraging the rich contextual information compressed into the latent space, without having to explicitly learn the complex dynamics of human behavior or scene interactions.

**C. Training Details and Data**

The models are implemented in PyTorch and trained on a single NVIDIA RTX A4000 GPU. The AMASS and GIMO datasets are used for training and evaluation. Motion sequences are downsampled to 10 fps, and models are trained on sequences of 48 frames. Various augmentation techniques, including Gaussian noise and stochastic dropout (for entire frames, body parts, and forecasting), are applied to simulate noisy and occluded observations during training. Hyperparameters for loss weights are determined via grid search.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed evaluations of both the human motion tracking and forecasting component and the MAV navigation policy, demonstrating the efficacy of the HumanFlow framework.

**A. Human Motion Tracking and Forecasting**

Evaluations were conducted using metrics such as mean-per-joint-position-error (GMPJPE), acceleration error (Accel), foot skating (Skating), mean ground penetration (Dist), and inference runtimes.

1. **Scene-Free Evaluation (AMASS Dataset)**:
 - **Robustness to Noise and Occlusions**: Two main experiments were performed: motion denoising with lower-body infilling (Occ-L) and motion denoising with in-betweening (Occ-10%). Gaussian noise was applied to SMPL-X parameters to simulate varying levels of input corruption.
 - **Accuracy and Efficiency**: HumanFlow demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across all tests. For instance, in the Occ-10% scenario (noise level 3), HumanFlow achieved a GMPJPE-all of 19.8, outperforming RoHM (29.2) by approximately 47%. This was achieved with significantly higher efficiency, being approximately 268 times faster than existing baselines (0.22 seconds inference time compared to 59-1800 seconds for others), due to operating in a compressed latent space without iterative optimization.
 - **Physical Plausibility**: The model produced smoother motion, substantially reducing joint acceleration errors (up to 6-fold compared to baselines) and exhibiting low foot skating and ground penetration errors. This indicates that the generated trajectories are more physically plausible.
 - **In-between Motion**: HumanFlow’s performance in the Occ-10% scenario highlights its ability to reason over in-between motion, a critical capability for MAV navigation when humans are temporarily occluded and non-visible.

2. **Scene-Conditioned Evaluation (GIMO Dataset)**:
 - Ablation studies on the GIMO dataset demonstrated that incorporating 3D scene information significantly improves tracking accuracy and reduces foot skating. HumanFlow with scene conditioning achieved a GMPJPE of 51.9 compared to 57.8 without it, and a skating error of 0.12 compared to 0.20, confirming the benefit of context awareness.

3. **Qualitative Examples**: Visualizations showed that HumanFlow effectively denoises and infills body motion, recovering smooth and temporally coherent full-body motion from noisy or occluded inputs, including diverse motion types like walking, jumping, sitting, and eating.

**B. MAV Policy Evaluation**

The MAV policy was evaluated in simulation using replays of AMASS sequences, focusing on safety (Collision Avoidance) and efficiency (Time-To-Goal).

1. **Collision Avoidance and Time-to-Goal (Noiseless and Noisy Conditions)**:
 - **Safety**: HumanFlow's MAV policy consistently achieved 100% collision avoidance across all sequences and both noiseless and noisy human motion inputs (noise level 5). This outperformed all distance-constraint (DC) baselines (ranging from 84-96% collision avoidance) and set-based approaches like "Forward Reachable Set" (90-100%) and HumanHalo (92-100%).
 - **Efficiency**: In addition to safety, HumanFlow maintained the lowest time-to-goal among all safe methods. For instance, in the noiseless setting, HumanFlow achieved a time-to-goal of 3.80s, compared to HumanHalo's 4.46s, indicating more efficient trajectories. Under noisy conditions, HumanFlow still achieved 3.91s compared to HumanHalo's 5.47s.
 - **Benefits of Latent Representation**: Ablation studies showed that policies conditioned directly on past human joint observations or a single forecasted trajectory were less safe (e.g., "Ours + Past Joints" at 86-87% collision avoidance) or less efficient ("Ours + Full Avoidance" at 4.45s). This highlights the efficacy of conditioning the policy on HumanFlow's rich, compressed latent representation, which captures complex, multi-modal human dynamics implicitly.

2. **Inference Efficiency**: The policy maintained its real-time performance by using only 10 denoising steps during inference, which was found to be sufficient for consistent 100% collision avoidance without significantly impacting efficiency.

3. **Distribution Shift (AIST++ Dataset)**:
 - The system demonstrated effective generalization to unseen motion types by evaluating on dancing motions from the AIST++ dataset, which was not part of the training data. HumanFlow achieved 100% collision avoidance with a median time-to-goal of 3.15s, indicating that the compressed latent representation captures motion primitives that transfer well across different action categories.

Overall, the findings indicate that HumanFlow effectively unifies robust human motion perception with efficient robotic control, leading to superior safety and navigation performance in complex human-robot interaction scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "HumanFlow" holds significant implications for the field of robotics, particularly in the domain of human-robot interaction and autonomous navigation. The primary significance lies in its comprehensive approach to addressing fundamental challenges in integrating robots into dynamic, human-populated environments.

**A. Advancements in Human Motion Perception:**
HumanFlow introduces a novel latent diffusion model that unifies 3D scene-conditioned human motion tracking and forecasting. This model achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and physical plausibility in motion prediction while being significantly more computationally efficient than prior methods. Its robustness to noise, heavy occlusions, and partial visibility is a critical advancement, as these conditions are ubiquitous in real-world robotic deployments but often hinder the performance of existing perception systems. The ability to model multi-modal human behaviors and infer full-body states from limited observations is a crucial step towards more intelligent and adaptive robots.

**B. Tight Coupling of Perception and Control:**
A key contribution is the demonstration of a tightly coupled perception-control loop. By leveraging the rich, compact latent representations learned by HumanFlow, the robot's control policy can reason about complex, scene-specific 3D human dynamics without the need for the policy itself to explicitly learn these dynamics. This reduces the computational burden on the control policy and simplifies policy learning, enabling real-time, proactive, and human-aware navigation. This tight integration paradigm offers a pathway for developing more responsive and context-aware autonomous systems.

**C. Enhanced Safety and Efficiency in Robot Navigation:**
The application of HumanFlow to MAV social navigation demonstrates a tangible impact on robot safety and efficiency. The system consistently achieves 100% collision avoidance, even under challenging conditions of noise and partial observability, while maintaining efficient goal-reaching capabilities. This surpasses the performance of several strong optimization- and reachability-based baselines. The ability of MAVs to navigate cluttered environments among humans safely and efficiently is a critical requirement for various applications, including logistics, inspection, and assistance, and HumanFlow provides a robust framework for achieving this.

**D. Generalizability and Future Potential:**
The framework's effectiveness in generalizing to unseen motion patterns (e.g., dancing from the AIST++ dataset) suggests that the learned latent representations capture fundamental motion primitives that are transferable across diverse human activities. While demonstrated in the context of MAVs, the underlying principles of HumanFlow – robust, scene-conditioned human motion modeling and its integration into control via latent representations – are broadly applicable to a wider range of robotic systems, including ground robots and manipulators. This opens avenues for extending its application to more complex human-robot interaction scenarios, such as collaborative tasks, and for scaling to more diverse and unstructured environments.

In conclusion, HumanFlow represents a substantial step towards robust, human-aware robotic operation in real-world environments. By addressing the limitations of existing human motion perception and robot navigation techniques through an innovative diffusion-driven, tightly-coupled approach, this work contributes significantly to the safe, efficient, and intelligent integration of robots into human society.
