# Reasoning in Space via Grounding in the World

> [!summary] Summary
> The GS-Reasoner framework enables 3D Large Language Models to intrinsically perform 3D visual grounding by predicting bounding boxes autoregressively as an intermediate step for spatial reasoning, without requiring external modules. This approach, supported by a novel semantic-geometric hybrid representation and the Grounded Chain-of-Thought dataset, achieves state-of-the-art performance in 3D dense captioning and complex spatial reasoning tasks, with competitive results in 3D visual grounding.

## Problem

- Existing 3D LLMs lack a unified representation that effectively captures both semantic and geometric information, leading to compromises in fidelity or weak integration of 3D cues.
- Current 3D LLMs heavily rely on external pre-trained modules (e.g., 3D detectors) for visual grounding, preventing intrinsic 3D scene understanding and seamless integration with higher-level reasoning.
- A scarcity of high-quality datasets exists that explicitly link 3D visual grounding with spatial reasoning through step-by-step annotations, hindering the training of models for complex, interpretable reasoning.

## Method

- Developed the ==Grounded-Spatial Reasoner (GS-Reasoner)==, an end-to-end framework enabling autoregressive 3D visual grounding as an intermediate Chain-of-Thought step within the LLM's output for spatial reasoning.
- Introduced a novel ==semantic-geometric hybrid 3D scene representation==, utilizing ==Dual-Path Pooling== to unify semantic, geometric, and positional features from multi-view images and point clouds, resolving feature misalignment.
- Created the ==Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) dataset==, which provides step-by-step reasoning paths with explicit 3D bounding box annotations, teaching the model to autonomously determine when and how to perform grounding during reasoning.

## Results

- GS-Reasoner is the first 3D LLM to achieve autoregressive 3D visual grounding without external modules, demonstrating performance comparable to state-of-the-art proposal-based methods and setting a new SOTA F1@25 on Multi3DRef.
- The framework achieved state-of-the-art performance on various spatial reasoning tasks within VSI-Bench, particularly for Relative Direction and Absolute Distance, and established a new **SOTA** for 3D dense captioning on Scan2Cap.
- GS-Reasoner exhibited strong zero-shot generalization capabilities, performing comparably to SOTA expert models on unseen 3D scenes from ARKitScenes and ScanNet++ validation splits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intrinsic, autoregressive 3D visual grounding by an LLM is achievable and leads to a more unified and robust understanding of 3D scenes without the need for external detection modules.
> - A hybrid 3D scene representation, combining semantic and geometric features with a dual-path pooling mechanism, effectively resolves misalignment issues and is crucial for accurate 3D localization and comprehensive scene understanding.
> - Explicitly integrating 3D visual grounding as an intermediate step within a Chain-of-Thought framework, supported by a tailored dataset like GCoT, significantly enhances the interpretability and accuracy of complex spatial reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of 3D computer vision and large language models, specifically focusing on **Visual-Spatial Intelligence (VSI)** and **3D Large Language Models (3D LLMs)**. The broader goal of VSI is to enable AI systems to perceive, interpret, and reason about 3D spaces, a capability fundamental for applications like embodied intelligence (e.g., robotics) and autonomous driving.

Recent years have seen a surge in the development of 3D LLMs, which extend the capabilities of multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) to incorporate 3D information. These models aim to perform tasks such as 3D Visual Question Answering (VQA), visual grounding, and captioning on 3D scenes. However, the authors identify a critical bottleneck: existing 3D LLMs primarily suffer from two key limitations:
1. **Absence of a unified 3D representation:** Current models struggle to jointly capture both semantic (meaningful categories, relationships) and geometric (shape, position, depth) information effectively within a single representation. This often leads to a trade-off between geometric fidelity and token efficiency, or the integration of weak geometric cues.
2. **Reliance on external modules for 3D visual grounding:** Many state-of-the-art 3D LLMs either depend on pre-trained 3D detectors (which generate object proposals) or external decoders to perform 3D visual grounding (linking textual descriptions to 3D objects and their bounding boxes). This reliance limits the LLM's intrinsic ability to understand 3D scenes holistically and hinders the seamless, self-contained integration of grounding with higher-level spatial reasoning.

The paper directly addresses these challenges. It aims to develop a 3D LLM that can perform **autoregressive grounding**—meaning the LLM itself generates the 3D bounding box coordinates as part of its natural language output, without needing external tools or pre-generated proposals. This approach seeks to establish a more unified and self-contained framework for 3D spatial reasoning, mirroring human cognitive processes where object identification (grounding) is a precursor to spatial reasoning.

Furthermore, the authors note a scarcity of high-quality datasets that integrate grounding as an intermediate step for spatial reasoning. Existing 3D VQA datasets often provide only short answers without explicit grounding annotations or reasoning steps, making it difficult to train models for complex, multi-step spatial intelligence. By introducing the **Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) dataset**, this work contributes to filling this data gap, encouraging models to learn a more interpretable and cognitively aligned approach to spatial reasoning.

In summary, this research pushes the frontier of 3D LLMs by proposing a novel representation and a dedicated dataset to enable intrinsic, autoregressive 3D visual grounding, thereby enhancing spatial reasoning capabilities and moving towards more unified and interpretable visual-spatial intelligence systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance visual-spatial intelligence in AI systems, particularly within the domain of 3D Large Language Models (LLMs). The authors articulate their goals and motivations clearly, stemming from identified limitations in current approaches.

The **key objectives** of this work are threefold:
1. **Enable Autoregressive 3D Visual Grounding:** To develop a 3D LLM capable of performing 3D visual grounding (identifying and localizing 3D objects described in text) in an autoregressive manner. This means the model should generate 3D bounding box coordinates as part of its sequential text output, without relying on external pre-trained object detectors or auxiliary modules.
2. **Enhance Spatial Reasoning Capabilities:** To leverage this intrinsic grounding capability to significantly improve the LLM's ability to reason about complex spatial relationships, layouts, sizes, and interactions of objects in 3D environments.
3. **Bridge Grounding and Spatial Reasoning:** To establish a unified and self-contained framework where 3D visual grounding is seamlessly integrated as a "cornerstone" or intermediate step within the broader spatial reasoning process, making the reasoning more interpretable and robust.

These objectives are motivated by several critical challenges observed in the current landscape of 3D LLMs:

- **Deficiency in Unified 3D Representation:** Existing 3D LLMs lack a single, comprehensive 3D representation that can effectively capture both **semantic information** (what an object is) and **geometric information** (where an object is, its shape, depth). Current methods often resort to compromises, sacrificing geometric fidelity for token efficiency or integrating only weak 3D positional cues from 2D vision models, which ultimately limits precise grounding and robust reasoning.
- **Over-reliance on External Modules:** Many state-of-the-art 3D LLMs delegate the task of 3D visual grounding to separate, pre-trained modules (e.g., 3D object detectors, mesh proposal generators). This modularity prevents the LLM from developing a deep, intrinsic understanding of 3D scenes and creates a disjointed pipeline, hindering true end-to-end spatial reasoning. The authors argue that a truly intelligent system should integrate these capabilities.
- **Scarcity of Integrated Datasets:** A significant hurdle is the lack of high-quality datasets that explicitly link 3D visual grounding with spatial reasoning. Current 3D Visual Question Answering (VQA) datasets typically offer short, direct answers without providing bounding box annotations for referenced objects or step-by-step reasoning paths that highlight the role of grounding. This makes it challenging to train models that learn to reason by first identifying relevant objects, similar to human cognition.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to develop a more powerful, integrated, and interpretable 3D LLM, exemplified by their **Grounded-Spatial Reasoner (GS-Reasoner)** and **Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) dataset**.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose the **Grounded-Spatial Reasoner (GS-Reasoner)** framework, which integrates 3D visual grounding as an intermediate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) step for spatial reasoning. Their methodology hinges on a novel semantic-geometric hybrid 3D scene representation and a specially curated dataset.

#### 3.1. GS-Reasoner Framework Overview

The GS-Reasoner processes a sequence of N RGB images, corresponding depth maps, camera intrinsics, and extrinsics of a 3D scene, along with a spatial reasoning query. Its goal is to autoregressively identify relevant objects (grounding) and then perform step-by-step spatial reasoning to derive an answer. The framework consists of three main components:
- **Semantic Encoder:** Extracts rich semantic features from input RGB images using a pre-trained vision foundation model (SigLIP).
- **Geometric Encoder:** Processes aggregated sensor point clouds (derived from depth maps) to capture structural information. Point Transformer v3 (PTv3) based Sonata is used as the backbone.
- **Video LLM:** A large language model (LLaVA-Video 7B based on Qwen2-7B) that integrates the fused 3D scene representation and text query to perform autoregressive grounding and reasoning.

The output format is structured as a Chain-of-Thought: reasoning steps are enclosed in `<think>...</think>` blocks, including autoregressively predicted 3D bounding boxes for relevant objects in `OBJECT_NAME OBJECT_COUNT <bbox>(x1, y1, z1, x2, y2, z2)</bbox>` format, followed by the final answer in `<answer>...</answer>`.

#### 3.2. Semantic-Geometric Hybrid 3D Scene Representation

This is the core innovation enabling intrinsic 3D scene understanding. The goal is to unify heterogeneous signals (semantic, geometric, positional) within an image patch-based representation without increasing input token count.
- **Geometric Feature Extraction:** Instead of processing points independently within small image patches, the full point cloud (P) derived from all depth maps is processed by the Geometric Encoder (Sonata). The encoder outputs geometric feature maps `{G_i}` and point maps `{P_sub_i}` aligned with image patches.
- **Dual-Path Pooling:** This novel mechanism addresses two critical issues with existing 3D representations:
 1. **Semantic-Geometric Misalignment:** Geometric features extracted from the whole point cloud can be misaligned with patch-specific semantic features. To mitigate this, a lightweight cross-attention network is used: semantic features of each patch act as queries, and the geometric features within the patch serve as keys and values. This selectively integrates geometric information most relevant to the patch's semantic context, creating "semantic-aligned geometric features."
 2. **Position-Geometric Misalignment:** Naive pooling of 3D points within a patch can lead to inconsistent positions and geometric features, especially if a patch spans foreground and background. To address this, the 3D point corresponding to each patch's center pixel is directly sampled for position encoding. Then, geometric features are interpolated based on this sampled 3D point's position to obtain "position-aligned geometric features." This ensures consistency, as features primarily come from foreground or background points depending on the sampled center point.
- **Fusion:** The semantic-aligned and position-aligned geometric features are concatenated and projected to form patch-level geometric features. These are then combined with the projected semantic features (from the Semantic Encoder) and the sampled 3D point positional encoding to yield the final **patch-level hybrid feature**. This holistic representation, preserving strong generalization from visual-semantic pretraining while incorporating robust geometric information, allows GS-Reasoner to accurately locate objects without external modules.

#### 3.3. GCoT: Grounded Chain-of-Thought Dataset

To train the model for both grounding and spatial reasoning, the authors introduce the GCoT dataset, addressing the lack of integrated datasets.
- **QA Pair Generation:** Initially, spatial reasoning QA pairs without CoT annotations are generated by following existing pipelines (e.g., Yang et al., 2025; Fan et al., 2025) using 3D datasets like ScanNet, ScanNet++, and ARKitScenes. This involves preprocessing point clouds, sampling RGB frames, and extracting detailed scene and frame metadata (object AABBs, room dimensions, object appearance times). Questions cover tasks like Object Count, Absolute Distance, Object Size, Room Size, Relative Distance, Relative Direction, Appearance Order, and Route Planning.
- **Chain-of-Thought Generation:** For QA pairs, 3D object bounding boxes, and bird’s-eye views of the scenes, GPT-4o is prompted to generate coherent, step-by-step CoT reasoning paths. To improve accuracy and prevent hallucinations, GPT-4o is provided with explicit annotations on the bird’s-eye view map, where relevant object bounding boxes are highlighted. Tasks that do not require complex spatial reasoning or grounding (e.g., Room Size, Object Count, Appearance Order) are excluded from CoT generation for efficiency.
- **Data Format:** The final dataset includes a "question analysis" part that guides the model to decide whether grounding is required before reasoning. This structured format teaches the model to autonomously determine when to perform grounding.

#### 3.4. Implementation and Training Details

- **Model Backbone:** LLaVA-Video 7B (Video LLM), SigLIP (Semantic Encoder), and Sonata (Geometric Encoder, built on PTv3) are used.
- **Training:** End-to-end, next-token prediction with cross-entropy loss.
 - **Pretraining:** On subsets of 3D visual grounding datasets (ScanRefer, Multi3DRef, SR3D, NR3D) to "warm up" object grounding.
 - **Finetuning:** On the GCoT dataset, remaining grounding data, and other 3D tasks (ScanQA, SQA3D, Scan2Cap).
 - **Data Augmentation:** Crucial for preventing overfitting, includes Z-axis rotations, random scaling, and translations to decouple geometric and positional cues, forcing the model to rely on both.
- **Inference:** For spatial reasoning, depth maps and camera parameters are estimated from multi-view images using VGGT-SLAM and MoGe-2, followed by metric alignment and axis-alignment transformations.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a novel representation, a tailored dataset, and robust training, enables GS-Reasoner to achieve its objectives of intrinsic 3D grounding and enhanced spatial reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of GS-Reasoner across various 3D vision-language understanding tasks, particularly highlighting its strong performance in 3D visual grounding and spatial reasoning.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on 3D Visual Grounding

GS-Reasoner was evaluated on four benchmarks: ScanRefer, Multi3DRef, SR3D, and NR3D.
- **Autoregressive Grounding:** GS-Reasoner is shown to be the first 3D LLM to achieve autoregressive grounding entirely without external modules, delivering performance comparable to state-of-the-art (SOTA) models that often rely on proposals or external grounding components.
- **Competitive Performance:** It achieves superior performance compared to earlier direct 3D LLM methods (e.g., 3D-LLM). Notably, it sets a new SOTA F1@25 on Multi3DRef, a multi-object grounding task.
- **Matching External Modules:** On ScanRefer Acc@50 and Multi3DRef F1@50, GS-Reasoner matches the performance of 3D LLMs utilizing external grounding modules, despite operating on potentially noisier, incomplete sensor point clouds instead of high-quality mesh inputs.
- **Comparison to Expert Models:** On ScanRefer, GS-Reasoner shows comparable results to specialized expert models. However, it lags behind on SR3D and NR3D, suggesting LLM-based methods might excel with complex queries, while expert models are better for precise localization with simpler descriptions. The authors attribute remaining gaps with proposal-based methods to the quality of mesh point clouds and mask supervision used in conventional 3D detectors.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Spatial Reasoning

Evaluated on VSI-Bench, covering eight spatial and temporal reasoning tasks with multiple-choice and numerical answers.
- **SOTA Performance:** GS-Reasoner achieves impressive and SOTA performance on most tasks, particularly those requiring complex spatial reasoning and precise 3D object localization, such as Relative Direction and Absolute Distance. It also attains SOTA results on the Appearance Order task.
- **Impact of Depth Quality:** Performance consistently improves when ground-truth depth inputs are used compared to predicted depths, with an average score exceeding 70 and yielding nearly a 10-point gain over previous SOTA. This underscores the importance of accurate geometric information.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on General 3D Tasks

Results on Scan2Cap (3D dense captioning), ScanQA, and SQA3D (3D VQA).
- **New SOTA for 3D Dense Captioning:** GS-Reasoner establishes a new SOTA for Scan2Cap across all metrics (CIDEr, BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE), significantly surpassing previous leading methods like ROSS3D. This is attributed to the model's explicit prediction of 3D coordinates for visual grounding, which forces it to better capture geometric and positional cues.
- **Comparable 3D VQA:** It achieves comparable performance on ScanQA and SQA3D. The authors explain that these datasets often contain ambiguous questions and strong answer biases, which can lead models to overfit textual patterns rather than deeply exploiting 3D tokens.

#### 4.4. Analysis and Ablation Studies

- **Zero-shot Generalization:** Trained solely on ScanNet data, GS-Reasoner demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization capabilities, achieving performance comparable to SOTA expert models on unseen ARKitScenes and ScanNet++ validation splits.
- **Effectiveness of Data Augmentation and Hybrid Representation:**
 - Without 3D input (LLaVA-NeXT), the model fails to predict 3D bounding boxes.
 - Adding average position encoding (Video-3D LLM) without data augmentation leads to poor performance due to overfitting.
 - Data augmentation significantly improves performance.
 - The introduction of geometric features from the geometric encoder and, crucially, the **Dual-Path Pooling** mechanism, leads to substantial gains in 3D visual grounding (Acc@25 and Acc@50). This confirms that the hybrid 3D representation and dual-path pooling effectively enhance 3D scene understanding and localization.
- **Effectiveness of GCoT Dataset:**
 - Ablation studies comparing models trained with and without the CoT part of the GCoT dataset show substantial performance improvements across all tasks that incorporate grounding within the CoT process (e.g., object absolute distance, relative direction, route planning).
 - This result emphatically demonstrates that explicitly integrating grounding as part of the Chain-of-Thought process, guided by the GCoT dataset, is vital not just for enabling grounding but also for teaching the model to *effectively leverage* grounding for complex spatial reasoning.

In summary, GS-Reasoner represents a significant step forward in 3D LLMs, demonstrating the viability and benefits of an intrinsically grounded, autoregressive approach to spatial reasoning, supported by a novel representation and a dedicated dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "Reasoning in Space Via Grounding in the World" introduces GS-Reasoner, a framework that marks a significant advancement in the field of 3D Large Language Models (LLMs) and visual-spatial intelligence. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

#### 5.1. Foundational Advancement in 3D LLMs

- **Unified and Self-Contained Framework:** GS-Reasoner is presented as the first 3D LLM to achieve autoregressive 3D visual grounding without relying on any external modules or pre-generated object proposals. This establishes a truly unified and self-contained framework where the LLM intrinsically understands and interacts with 3D space. This moves away from brittle, multi-stage pipelines and towards a more integrated, robust AI system.
- **Intrinsic Spatial Understanding:** By enabling direct 3D bounding box prediction as part of the LLM's output, the model is forced to develop a deeper, more inherent understanding of geometric and positional cues, rather than simply processing abstract features from external detectors.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Interpretability and Cognitive Alignment

- **Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT):** The introduction of the GCoT dataset and the model's ability to output a Chain-of-Thought that explicitly includes 3D visual grounding steps substantially improves the interpretability of spatial reasoning. This approach mimics human cognitive processes, where identifying relevant objects is a prerequisite for reasoning about their spatial relationships. This increased transparency is crucial for debugging, understanding model failures, and building trust in AI systems.
- **Richer Supervision:** The GCoT dataset provides richer, step-by-step supervision compared to traditional single-answer datasets, guiding models to learn more sophisticated problem-solving strategies.

#### 5.3. Novel Technical Contributions

- **Semantic-Geometric Hybrid 3D Scene Representation:** The proposed representation, which tightly aligns semantic, geometric, and positional information at the image patch level, is a key technical innovation.
- **Dual-Path Pooling:** This mechanism is particularly clever in addressing the critical challenges of semantic-geometric and position-geometric misalignment, enabling the fusion of disparate features into a coherent representation suitable for LLMs.

#### 5.4. Broad Applicability and State-of-the-Art Performance

- **Strong Performance Across Tasks:** Demonstrated SOTA performance in 3D dense captioning (Scan2Cap), highly competitive results in 3D visual grounding, and impressive SOTA scores in complex spatial reasoning tasks (VSI-Bench). This wide-ranging success validates the robustness and versatility of the GS-Reasoner.
- **Zero-shot Generalization:** The model's strong zero-shot generalization to unseen 3D scenes (e.g., ARKitScenes) is promising, suggesting its learned representations are robust and transferable.

#### 5.5. Potential Impact and Future Directions

- **Embodied Intelligence and Robotics:** The authors explicitly highlight the importance of spatial reasoning for robotics and embodied reasoning. GS-Reasoner's capabilities could serve as a crucial "embodied brain" for planning, task decomposition, and human-robot interaction in 3D environments, leading to more intelligent and adaptable agents. Joint fine-tuning with action data is a clear next step.
- **Autonomous Driving:** Accurate 3D visual grounding and spatial reasoning are paramount for autonomous vehicles to perceive their surroundings, understand traffic situations, and make safe navigation decisions.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality:** Enhanced 3D scene understanding could improve content generation, interaction, and user experience in AR/VR applications.
- **Computer Vision Research:** This work establishes a new paradigm for integrating grounding into LLMs, likely inspiring further research into more end-to-end, intrinsically capable multimodal foundation models. The GCoT dataset will be a valuable resource for the community.

#### 5.6. Ethical Considerations

The authors acknowledge the use of LLMs for dataset generation and the potential for synthetic data to inherit biases. Their commitment to releasing the dataset with clear guidelines for responsible usage is a positive step towards mitigating potential misuse and promoting ethical AI development.

In conclusion, "Reasoning in Space Via Grounding in the World" offers a significant leap towards developing truly intelligent AI systems that can reason about 3D environments with human-like proficiency and interpretability. Its innovations lay a strong foundation for future advancements in embodied AI and a wide array of applications requiring deep spatial understanding.
