# Incentivizing Agentic Reasoning in LLM Judges via Tool-Integrated Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> TIR-Judge, developed by researchers from Google and Emory University, introduces an LLM judge framework that integrates code execution tools through an end-to-end reinforcement learning approach. This method enhances judging accuracy on reasoning benchmarks by up to 7.7% and demonstrates self-evolution capabilities without relying on distilled data.

## Problem

- Traditional LLM judges, relying solely on text-based reasoning, struggle with tasks requiring precise computation, symbolic reasoning, or verifiable constraint checking.
- Existing tool-augmented LLM judges often treat tool invocation as an inference-time add-on, failing to deeply integrate tool-use strategies into the model's core reasoning process.
- Prior tool-assisted approaches were often limited by narrow task coverage, which hindered their general applicability across diverse evaluation domains.

## Method

- An end-to-end ==Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework==, based on DAPO, trains LLM judges to strategically generate and execute Python code during the training phase.
- The ==judgment trajectory== interleaves natural language reasoning, generated code, and execution results from an external interpreter, allowing for iterative refinement of decisions.
- A ==structured reward system== incentivizes correctness, proper output formatting, and error-free, budget-constrained tool execution, promoting effective and efficient tool integration.

## Results

- TIR-Judge consistently achieves superior judging accuracy, with gains of up to **6.4%** (pointwise) and **7.7%** (pairwise) over baselines on reasoning-focused benchmarks, and an **8B** parameter model often surpasses **32B** parameter reasoning reward models.
- The `TIR-Judge-Zero` variant, trained entirely without distilled judge trajectories, delivers comparable or even better performance than its distilled counterpart, demonstrating effective self-bootstrapping.
- When used for best-of-N inference, TIR-Judge consistently surpasses strong baselines, achieving absolute gains of **3.9–6.7%** on challenging code generation and math problems, validating its utility in enhancing policy model performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning is essential for effectively unlocking and optimizing tool-use capabilities in LLM judges, as merely providing tool access to base LLMs yields negligible gains.
> - Tool-augmented judges can self-evolve and self-improve their reasoning and tool-use skills through iterative RL (TIR-Judge-Zero), reducing reliance on costly supervision from frontier models.
> - Training with a diverse mixture of verifiable and non-verifiable tasks is crucial for the judge's generalization abilities and robust development of tool-use skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence and natural language processing: Large Language Models (LLMs), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR).

**LLM-as-a-Judge:** The paper begins by establishing that LLMs acting as judges are a "critical component in the LLM ecosystem," providing a scalable alternative to human evaluation. This approach is vital for various stages of LLM development: post-training alignment (e.g., through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback, RLHF), inference-time response selection (e.g., best-of-N decoding), and general evaluation for model reliability. However, the authors identify a significant limitation: most LLM judges rely solely on intrinsic text-based reasoning, which proves inadequate for tasks requiring "precise computation or symbolic reasoning" or the verification of "complex constraints." This highlights a recognized bottleneck in the current LLM evaluation paradigm, especially as LLMs are applied to increasingly complex and factual tasks.

**Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR):** To address the limitations of text-only reasoning, the paper leverages the burgeoning field of LLM tool-use. Recent advances have shown that equipping LLMs with external tools (like code interpreters, web search, databases) significantly enhances their capabilities by allowing them to perform exact validation, computation, and access external knowledge. This work extends the application of TIR to the domain of LLM-based judges, aiming to overcome the inherent challenges of text-only models in tasks requiring precision. Previous attempts at tool-augmented judges are acknowledged but critiqued for two main limitations: "inference-time restriction" (tools only used during inference, not deeply integrated into training) and "narrow task coverage" (tailored to specific domains). TIR-Judge explicitly aims to overcome these by integrating tool-use directly into the training process.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLMs:** The paper employs an end-to-end RL framework to train its LLM judge. RL has been instrumental in aligning LLMs with human preferences and improving their performance through iterative feedback. While previous work has used RL to enhance textual reasoning in judges (e.g., by encouraging longer chain-of-thought), TIR-Judge applies RL specifically to teach models *when and how* to invoke tools, execute them, and interpret their outputs to refine judgments. This represents a more advanced application of RL, moving beyond simply improving textual reasoning to optimizing agentic behavior involving external interactions.

**Self-Improvement and Bootstrapping:** The "TIR-Judge-Zero" variant is particularly noteworthy as it explores self-bootstrapping, where the judge improves iteratively through reinforcement learning without relying on distilled trajectories from a powerful teacher model. This aligns with a growing trend in LLM research focused on self-rewarding models, self-critique, and autonomous learning paradigms, which aim to reduce dependence on expensive human annotations or proprietary frontier models for data generation. The success of TIR-Judge-Zero positions this work at the forefront of scalable, self-evolving AI systems.

In essence, TIR-Judge contributes to the broader landscape by pushing the boundaries of LLM evaluation beyond superficial text matching, enabling more robust and verifiable judgments through deep integration of tool-use, powered by reinforcement learning, and a novel self-improvement mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of conventional LLM judges. While these judges offer a scalable alternative to human evaluation, their reliance on "intrinsic text-based reasoning" restricts their ability to accurately assess tasks demanding precise computation, symbolic reasoning, or verification against complex, often programmatic, constraints. This fundamental weakness leads to inaccuracies, particularly in domains like competitive programming, mathematical reasoning, or strict instruction following, where a text-only LLM might hallucinate facts or miscalculate.

Given this challenge, the **key objectives** of the TIR-Judge project are multi-faceted:

1. **Develop a Reliable Tool-Integrated LLM Judge:** The overarching goal is to create an LLM judge that can reliably integrate advanced reasoning capabilities with external code interpreter execution. This means the judge should not only generate textual reasoning but also intelligently decide when and how to invoke tools (specifically a Python code executor in this work), execute the generated code, and effectively incorporate the execution results into its final judgment.

2. **Enhance Judgment Accuracy and Verifiability:** By coupling reasoning with tool execution, the aim is to enable the LLM judge to deliver "more accurate and verifiable evaluations" across a diverse range of tasks. This is particularly crucial for verifiable domains where objective correctness can be programmatically checked, moving beyond subjective textual assessments.

3. **End-to-End Reinforcement Learning Framework:** To achieve deep integration of reasoning and tool-use, the paper aims to build an end-to-end Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework. This framework would teach the model to generate code, execute it, and iteratively refine its reasoning based on the tool's outputs during the training phase itself, rather than merely prompting tool-use at inference time.

4. **Promote Generalizability and Flexibility:** The authors set out to design the training of TIR-Judge based on three core principles:
 - **(i) Diverse Training Data:** To ensure the judge learns when to use tools and when pure textual reasoning suffices, training data must span both "verifiable domains" (e.g., math, coding) and "non-verifiable domains" (e.g., dialogue, safety).
 - **(ii) Flexible Judgment Formats:** The judge should accommodate various evaluation scenarios, supporting "pointwise, pairwise, and listwise" ranking formats.
 - **(iii) Data-Efficient Iterative RL:** To reduce reliance on expensive or inaccessible teacher models, the framework should allow the judge to "bootstrap directly from the initial model without distillation" through iterative self-improvement.

These objectives collectively aim to create a more robust, intelligent, and autonomous LLM judge capable of overcoming the inherent limitations of text-only models and providing higher-quality feedback essential for the continued advancement of LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of TIR-Judge is an end-to-end Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework designed to train LLM judges to integrate code execution tools into their reasoning processes. The methodology is structured around four main components:

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

The paper defines the task of LLM-based judgment for a user prompt and multiple model-generated responses. It formalizes three evaluation settings:
- **Pointwise evaluation:** Assigns a scalar score to a single response.
- **Pairwise evaluation:** Selects a preferred response between two candidates. This is the most common setting.
- **Listwise evaluation:** Identifies the best response from a list of more than two candidates.
Crucially, the judge model ($J_\theta$) is augmented with an external Python execution environment (I). A judgment trajectory ($s_k$) now interweaves natural language reasoning ($r_i$), generated code ($c_i$), and the execution result ($o_i = I(c_i)$), allowing the judge to ground its decisions in verifiable evidence.

#### 3.2. Training Procedure

The training of TIR-Judge comprises four key elements:

1. **Data Collection and Filtering:**
 - **Corpus:** A diverse collection of (prompt, responses) tuples is curated.
 - **Real Preference Pairs:** Human-labeled data is sampled from various domains, including general helpfulness (HelpSteer 3), reasoning (UltraInteract, S1), coding (CodeRM), instruction following (Tulu 3), and safety (Safe-RLHF). Each prompt includes a preferred (chosen) and one or more rejected responses.
 - **Synthetic Preference Pairs:** To scale the dataset, additional pairs are generated from verifiable prompts. Responses from multiple open-source models (Qwen3, Gemma-2, Gemma-3) are automatically evaluated against verifiable functions (for instruction following from Tulu-3) or ground-truth solutions (for reasoning from MATH, DAPO-Math, WebInstruct, Loong).
 - **Dataset Size and Quality:** The final dataset totals approximately 26k preference pairs across pointwise, pairwise, and listwise formats, covering a broad spectrum of tasks. Strict 8-gram decontamination is applied to prevent data leakage from evaluation benchmarks.

2. **Tool-Integrated RL with Verifiable Rewards:**
 - **RL Framework:** The training employs DAPO (Differentiable Advantage-weighted Policy Optimization), an improved variant of GRPO. The LLM judge ($J$) is parameterized by a policy ($\pi_\theta$).
 - **Policy Update:** The policy is updated using a clipped policy gradient objective. This involves sampling multiple rollouts from the current policy, assigning scalar rewards, and using token-level importance weights and advantages. A KL divergence penalty stabilizes training.
 - **Additional Designs:**
 - **Error Message Processing:** Interpreter outputs are truncated to only the final error line to manage context length while retaining critical feedback.
 - **Sandbox Output Masking:** Execution results ($o_i$) are masked during loss computation to prevent overfitting to exact strings and promote learning generalizable reasoning.

3. **Reward Design:** A structured reward system is defined to facilitate multi-turn RL with code execution:
 - **Correctness Reward ($R_c$):** A binary reward (1 or 0) indicating whether the judge's final prediction matches the ground-truth preference.
 - **Format Reward ($R_f$):** A binary reward (1 or 0) ensuring adherence to predefined structured output formats (e.g., `<score>`, `<preference>`, ```python``` tags). For safety and general helpfulness prompts, a positive reward is given only if a valid output is produced *without* invoking tools (discouraging unnecessary tool calls).
 - **Tool-Specific Reward ($R_t$):** A binary reward (1 or 0) encouraging accurate and efficient tool use. It's 1 only if code blocks are error-free and within a maximum call budget (set to 3).
 - **Final Reward ($R$):** Defined as $R = R_c \times (0.1 + 0.9 \cdot \mathbb{I}[R_t = 1 \land R_f = 1])$. This composite reward assigns full credit only when the decision is correct, the format is strict, and any tool usage is valid.

4. **Training Strategies for RL (Cold-Start and Iterative Training):**
 - **TIR-Judge-Distill:** This variant leverages a stronger teacher model (Gemini-2.5-Flash with code execution) to generate high-quality tool-integrated trajectories via rejection sampling. These ~10k trajectories are then used for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of the student judge, serving as a robust initialization before RL.
 - **TIR-Judge-Zero:** This innovative variant explores self-bootstrapping without distillation. It iteratively alternates between:
 - **RL:** Applying RL directly to the current model.
 - **Rejection Sampling (RS):** Sampling multiple trajectories from the RL-trained model and retaining only those that are correct, format-compliant, and error-free in tool execution. For efficiency, only the shortest valid trajectory with fewest tool calls is kept per prompt.
 - **SFT:** Fine-tuning the model on the newly collected high-quality trajectories.
 This cycle (RS → SFT → RL) allows TIR-Judge-Zero to progressively improve its reasoning and tool-use capabilities entirely through self-supervision.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

The backbone models used are Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507. Training is implemented with Verl-Tool, with specific hyperparameters for SFT and RL (batch sizes, learning rates, context length, epochs). Evaluation is performed on seven public benchmarks covering diverse tasks and formats, and baselines include off-the-shelf LLMs, standard reward models, text-based reasoning judges trained with RL, and other tool-augmented judges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on seven public benchmarks demonstrate the superior performance and efficiency of TIR-Judge, validating its design principles.

1. **Superior Judging Accuracy:**
 - TIR-Judge consistently outperforms strong reasoning-based judges. On the PPE benchmark, it shows significant gains of **up to 6.4% in pointwise accuracy and 7.7% in pairwise accuracy** over baselines with similar parameter counts. Specifically, it surpasses baselines by 4.8%-9.9% for pointwise and 4.5%-8.8% for pairwise judging on PPE.
 - On other benchmarks, TIR-Judge achieves competitive or even better performance compared to baselines with larger parameter counts and more training data. For example, the 8B TIR-Judge achieves similar accuracy to the 32B RRM model on PPE and RewardBench, showcasing remarkable parameter efficiency.

2. **Impressive Listwise Performance:**
 - In listwise evaluation on RewardBench2, the 8B TIR-Judge achieves performance comparable to Claude-Opus-4 (96% of its accuracy), a considerably larger and more advanced model. This advantage is particularly pronounced in instruction following and mathematical reasoning tasks, where the integration of code execution provides a distinct benefit.

3. **Reinforcement Learning is Critical for Tool-Use:**
 - A crucial finding is that simply providing LLMs with access to a code executor ("Qwen3-4B-Tool" or "Qwen3-8B-Tool") without specific training yields negligible or even negative gains. This highlights that base models lack the intrinsic capability for robust code generation and effective tool invocation, and **RL is essential for unlocking and refining this tool-use capability**. RL enables the model to learn *when* to use tools and *how* to generate correct and useful code.

4. **Success of Iterative RL and Self-Bootstrapping (TIR-Judge-Zero):**
 - The TIR-Judge-Zero variant, trained entirely without distilled judge trajectories, achieves comparable or even superior performance to its distilled counterpart (TIR-Judge-Distill). It outperforms the distilled variant on 4 out of 6 pointwise benchmarks and 3 out of 6 pairwise benchmarks.
 - Remarkably, the 4B TIR-Judge-Zero shows a **1.2% gain over its distilled counterpart**, demonstrating that tool-augmented judges can self-evolve and improve through iterative reinforcement learning, significantly reducing reliance on external, high-quality supervision.
 - The study on training stages shows substantial gains after the first round of RL, attributed to rejection sampling teaching the model to produce more format-correct and efficient tool use. Subsequent RL iterations further boost accuracy.

5. **Importance of Diverse Data Mixture:**
 - Experiments confirm that training exclusively on narrow task domains (e.g., only chat or reasoning) leads to poor generalization across subtasks, primarily due to insufficient exposure to tool-use prompts. **Unifying tasks that require and do not require tool use within a single training pipeline is crucial for improved generalization** and for the model to learn the appropriate context for tool invocation.

6. **Tool Use vs. Text-Only Judges:**
 - A controlled study comparing tool-augmented models against text-only models (with identical training data and settings) reveals that **tool-augmented judges consistently achieve higher accuracy on reasoning and instruction-following benchmarks**. While text-only models perform slightly better on purely text-centric tasks (e.g., Chat, Safety), the overall benefit of tool integration for complex, verifiable tasks is clear.

7. **Inference Efficiency:**
 - Despite integrating external tools, TIR-Judge introduces no additional inference-time overhead and, in some cases, is even more efficient than baselines. This efficiency stems from a training strategy that favors trajectories with shorter reasoning and fewer tool calls during rejection sampling.

8. **Positive Downstream Impact on Policy Models (Best-of-N):**
 - When used as a judge for reward-guided best-of-N inference, TIR-Judge consistently surpasses strong baselines like RRM and Majority Voting. It achieves absolute gains of **3.9–6.7% over RRM** on challenging benchmarks such as BigCodeBench (complex code generation) and AIME (competition-level math problems), demonstrating its ability to improve the quality of downstream LLM policy outputs.

9. **Illustrative Case Studies:**
 - A case study from IFEval vividly demonstrates TIR-Judge's capability to generate and execute Python code for precise verification (e.g., counting specific letters, checking capitalization), tasks where text-only judges often fail by producing "incorrect and hallucinated reasoning steps."

These findings collectively establish TIR-Judge as a highly effective, efficient, and innovative approach to LLM evaluation, capable of verifiable and precise judgments, and notably, with the potential for autonomous self-improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of TIR-Judge represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) evaluation and agentic AI, with profound potential impacts across various facets of LLM research and application.

1. **Enhanced Reliability and Verifiability of LLM Evaluation:**
 - The most immediate impact is the ability to conduct more accurate, robust, and verifiable evaluations of LLM outputs. By explicitly integrating a code executor, TIR-Judge moves beyond subjective or probabilistic textual assessments to objective, programmatic verification for tasks requiring precise computation, logical constraints, or factual correctness. This is crucial for high-stakes applications where accuracy is paramount, such as in scientific reasoning, legal document generation, or financial analysis.
 - This shift from "text-based reasoning" to "tool-integrated reasoning" addresses a critical limitation of previous LLM-as-a-judge approaches, making LLM evaluations more trustworthy and reliable.

2. **Scalable and Cost-Effective Alternative to Human Annotation:**
 - Human evaluation, while gold standard, is expensive, slow, and non-scalable. Traditional LLM judges offered scalability but lacked precision. TIR-Judge provides a solution that combines scalability with enhanced precision and verifiability. This can dramatically reduce the cost and time associated with evaluating and aligning new LLM models, accelerating the development cycle.

3. **Advancing LLM Development and Alignment:**
 - More accurate and reliable judges directly translate to better feedback signals for Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and other alignment techniques. Improved judge performance means more effective reward models, leading to policy models that are better aligned with complex instructions and user preferences.
 - The demonstrated improvement in downstream "best-of-N" inference showcases TIR-Judge's ability to select higher-quality responses, which can be critical for refining LLM outputs at inference time and improving user experience.

4. **Pioneering Self-Evolving AI Agents:**
 - The success of **TIR-Judge-Zero**, which learns to effectively use tools and reason without any distillation from a powerful teacher model, is a groundbreaking contribution. This validates the concept of **self-bootstrapping and iterative self-improvement** in LLM agents. It suggests a future where AI systems can autonomously develop and refine complex skills, including tool-use, by learning from their own experiences and outcomes. This could drastically reduce the need for extensive human supervision or reliance on proprietary frontier models for data generation.

5. **Parameter Efficiency and Accessibility:**
 - Achieving state-of-the-art or comparable performance with significantly smaller models (8B parameters matching 32B baselines and 96% of Claude-Opus-4) is highly significant. This makes advanced LLM judging capabilities more accessible and deployable on platforms with limited computational resources, fostering broader innovation.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research in Agentic AI:**
 - TIR-Judge's methodology opens new avenues for research into more sophisticated tool-using agents. Future work can expand the range of tools (e.g., web search, databases, APIs), diversify training tasks, and explore how such advanced judges can not only evaluate but also actively guide the training of even more capable policy models. It paves the way for LLMs that are not just language generators but intelligent, autonomous problem-solvers capable of complex, multi-modal reasoning.

In conclusion, TIR-Judge represents a substantial leap forward in the field of LLM evaluation by marrying the reasoning power of LLMs with the precision of external tools through an innovative RL framework. Its ability to self-evolve without heavy reliance on expert-annotated data or large teacher models marks a pivotal step towards building more robust, intelligent, and autonomous AI systems.
