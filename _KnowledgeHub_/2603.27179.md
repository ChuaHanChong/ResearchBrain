# Reasoning-Driven Anomaly Detection and Localization with Image-Level Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> An end-to-end multimodal large language model framework performs joint anomaly detection, pixel-level localization, and interpretable reasoning using only image-level supervision. This system, developed by researchers at Beihang University, eliminates the need for external vision modules or dense pixel-level annotations and achieves competitive performance on industrial anomaly detection benchmarks.

## Problem

- Most MLLM-based anomaly detection methods rely on external vision modules for pixel-level localization, leading to architectural complexity, error propagation, and misalignment between reasoning and localization.
- Advanced anomaly detection and localization approaches often require costly, time-consuming, and labor-intensive dense pixel-level mask annotations or detailed reasoning explanations.
- MLLMs, particularly under weak supervision, can produce inconsistent reasoning (e.g., correct detection but irrelevant explanation), undermining interpretability and reliability.

## Method

- A Reasoning-Driven Anomaly Localization (**ReAL**) module extracts pixel-level ==anomaly maps== directly from the MLLM's autoregressive reasoning by evaluating tokens based on semantic relevance to 'anomaly' and spatial attention concentration.
- A Consistency-Guided Reasoning Optimization (CGRO) module utilizes reinforcement learning with a class-conditional consistency reward to align the MLLM's textual reasoning with its visual attention, enhancing coherence and localization under image-level supervision.
- The framework is built upon a transformer-based MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and trained exclusively with image-level (normal/anomaly) labels, avoiding dense annotations and external vision components.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved the highest reasoning scores, with Qwen2.5-VL+CGRO (**7B**) reaching 27.1 ROUGE-L and 74.7 SBERT on MVTec-COT, outperforming commercial MLLMs like GPT-4.1.
- It demonstrated strong pixel-level localization, with an average AUROC of **80.7%** and ACC of **97.1%** across industrial benchmarks, performing competitively against methods requiring external vision modules or dense supervision.
- The Consistency-Guided Reasoning Optimization (CGRO) module significantly improved performance, boosting image-level AUROC from **63.4%** to **83.9%** and pixel-level AUROC from **61.7%** to **80.7%** for the Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs possess an intrinsic capacity for spatial grounding and pixel-level localization that can be activated and leveraged through careful analysis of reasoning token attention, even without explicit pixel-level annotations.
> - Reinforcement learning, guided by consistency rewards that enforce alignment between textual reasoning and visual attention, effectively enhances both anomaly detection and precise pixel-level localization under weak supervision.
> - Identifying anomaly-relevant reasoning tokens using a combination of semantic relevance and spatial attention concentration is crucial for accurately extracting high-fidelity anomaly maps from the MLLM's internal representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Anomaly detection, particularly in industrial inspection, is a critical area of research. Traditionally, industrial anomaly detection methods have been designed for single-product scenarios, requiring extensive normal samples to train product-specific models. These methods, often categorized into reconstruction-based, feature-embedding, and synthetic anomaly approaches, face limitations in real-world settings due to data privacy concerns, the need for large datasets, product-specific training, and high computational and deployment costs.

More recent advancements have explored zero-shot and few-shot anomaly detection, often leveraging multimodal models like CLIP. These approaches reduce data dependency by aligning image and text representations or employing prompt tuning and representation refinement. While these methods improve upon traditional techniques by offering better textual guidance and reducing training data requirements, they frequently lack explicit modeling of interpretable cues, such as anomaly categories, visual semantics, and causal factors, and are limited by the reasoning capacity of their underlying models.

The emergence of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has introduced new possibilities for industrial anomaly detection (IAD) due to their strong multimodal understanding and generation capabilities. Benchmarks like MMAD and Anomaly-OV have been established to evaluate and fine-tune MLLMs for IAD, enabling them to generate human-aligned explanations. Several MLLM-based methods, such as IAD-R1, AD-FM, and Triad, have adapted these models for industrial anomaly detection.

However, a significant limitation of most existing MLLM-based approaches is their confinement to image-level anomaly detection and textual reasoning. Pixel-level localization, which is essential for actionable inspection and precise intervention, typically relies on external vision modules (e.g., AnomalyGPT using a pretrained vision expert, EIAD employing SAM for mask refinement) or dense, pixel-level annotations (e.g., OmniAD). These staged designs introduce several challenges:
1. **Error Propagation:** Errors from one module can propagate to subsequent stages.
2. **Misalignment:** Potential misalignment between the MLLM's textual reasoning and the localization provided by an external vision module.
3. **Increased Complexity:** Higher deployment complexity due to multiple, non-differentiable components.
4. **Costly Supervision:** Reliance on dense pixel-level masks and high-quality reasoning annotations is expensive and labor-intensive to collect, sensitive to domain-specific bias, and can limit generalization to novel products and defect types.

This work positions itself to address these limitations by proposing an end-to-end MLLM framework that performs joint anomaly detection, pixel-level localization, and interpretable reasoning. Crucially, it aims to achieve this *solely from image-level supervision*, without requiring external vision modules or dense pixel-level annotations. By activating the intrinsic reasoning potential of MLLMs, the paper seeks to provide a more unified, efficient, and scalable solution for industrial anomaly understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reasoning-driven framework that enables multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to perform comprehensive anomaly understanding. This encompasses anomaly detection, pixel-level localization, and interpretable reasoning, all achieved *exclusively with image-level supervision*. The framework aims to eliminate the need for auxiliary vision modules or costly pixel-wise annotations, which are common dependencies in existing MLLM-based anomaly detection systems.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges in the current landscape of industrial anomaly detection:

1. **Dependency on External Vision Modules for Localization:** While MLLMs have demonstrated strong capabilities in image-level anomaly detection and textual reasoning, precise pixel-level localization of anomalies often requires integrating external vision modules (e.g., segmentation models like SAM or specialized vision experts). This introduces architectural complexity, potential for error propagation between modules, and misalignment between the MLLM's generated reasoning and the visual localization provided by a separate component. The researchers are motivated to activate the MLLM's *intrinsic* capacity for spatial grounding to achieve localization directly.

2. **High Cost of Dense Supervision:** Many advanced anomaly detection and localization methods, including some end-to-end MLLM approaches, rely heavily on dense supervision in the form of pixel-level masks and detailed reasoning annotations. Collecting such fine-grained data is time-consuming, expensive, and often requires domain-specific expertise, making it impractical for rapid deployment or generalization across diverse industrial scenarios. This motivates the development of a framework that can learn effectively from more readily available *image-level supervision* (i.e., whether an image contains an anomaly or is normal).

3. **Lack of Coherent Reasoning and Localization:** Without explicit constraints, the reasoning processes generated by MLLMs can sometimes be inconsistent with the visual evidence or the final detection decision. For instance, an MLLM might correctly classify an image as anomalous but provide reasoning that describes it as normal, or vice-versa. Such inconsistencies undermine the interpretability of the system and can lead to unreliable localization. The motivation here is to align the MLLM's textual reasoning with its visual attention, ensuring that its explanations are both correct and spatially grounded.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes two core components:
- **Reasoning-Driven Anomaly Localization (ReAL):** To automatically discover and leverage anomaly-relevant reasoning tokens and their attention patterns for pixel-level localization, moving beyond diffuse attention or irrelevant focus.
- **Consistency-Guided Reasoning Optimization (CGRO):** To deliberately activate and refine the MLLM's intrinsic reasoning potential, using reinforcement learning with a specific consistency reward to jointly improve anomaly detection, localization, and interpretability under limited supervision.

By tackling these issues, the research aims to pave the way for more scalable, efficient, and interpretable industrial anomaly detection systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Reasoning-Driven Anomaly Localization (ReAL), introduces an end-to-end MLLM-based approach for joint anomaly detection, pixel-level localization, and interpretable reasoning, operating solely under image-level supervision. The methodology comprises two main modules: ReAL itself, which extracts spatial anomaly information from MLLM reasoning, and Consistency-Guided Reasoning Optimization (CGRO), which refines this reasoning through reinforcement learning.

**Overall MLLM Architecture:**
The framework utilizes a transformer-based MLLM. Given an input image ($X_v$) and text prompts ($X_t$), the image is divided into patches and projected into visual embeddings ($E_v$), while the text is tokenized into embeddings ($E_t$). The concatenated embeddings $[E_v; E_t]$ are fed into the MLLM, which autoregressively generates an output token sequence, forming the reasoning process and the final anomaly answer. The multi-head self-attention mechanism within the transformer blocks models pairwise dependencies among all tokens, resulting in a multimodal attention matrix $A$ that encodes interactions between visual inputs, textual inputs, and generated reasoning tokens.

#### 3.1. Reasoning-Driven Anomaly Localization (ReAL)

The ReAL module is designed to extract pixel-level anomaly maps directly from the MLLM's autoregressive reasoning process. This is based on the hypothesis that some reasoning tokens, particularly those describing anomalies, exhibit focused attention on the actual anomalous regions in the image.

### 4. Identifying Anomaly-Relevant Reasoning Tokens

To distinguish informative tokens, each generated reasoning token ($t_r$) is evaluated based on two criteria:
- **Inter-modality Semantic Relevance ($S_r^T$):** This measures how semantically related a reasoning token is to the concept of "anomaly." It is computed by summing the attention weights from the reasoning token ($t_r$) to a predefined set of anomaly-related tokens in the input text (e.g., "defect," "anomaly," "abnormal"). A higher $S_r^T$ indicates stronger semantic association with anomalies.
- **Intra-modality Attention Concentration ($S_r^I$):** This quantifies how spatially focused a token's visual attention map ($A_{r,I}$) is on the image. A more concentrated attention suggests the token is focusing on specific, localized regions. $S_r^I$ is calculated as the spatial entropy of the binarized visual attention map. The visual attention map is binarized by thresholding attention values, and connected components are identified. Lower entropy values indicate more concentrated attention.

### 5. Aggregating the Reasoning-Driven Anomaly Map

A composite token weight ($w_r$) is assigned to each reasoning token. Tokens are retained only if their normalized semantic relevance ($\hat{S}_r^T$) exceeds a threshold $\tau_t$ and their normalized spatial attention concentration ($\hat{S}_r^I$) exceeds a threshold $\tau_i$ (where higher $\hat{S}_r^I$ means lower entropy). The composite weight $w_r$ combines $\hat{S}_r^T$ and $\hat{S}_r^I$ with tunable parameters $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
Finally, the visual attention maps ($A_{r,I}$) of these filtered, weighted reasoning tokens are aggregated to produce the Reasoning-Driven Anomaly Map ($A_{RDAM}$). This map highlights regions that receive focused attention from semantically relevant reasoning tokens, thus providing pixel-level anomaly localization.

#### 5.1. Consistency-Guided Reasoning Optimization (CGRO)

While ReAL enables localization, MLLMs, especially under weak supervision, may produce inconsistent reasoning (e.g., predicting an anomaly but describing a normal image). CGRO addresses this by using reinforcement learning to align reasoning tokens with visual attention, thereby enhancing reasoning coherence and localization accuracy.

### 6. Consistency Reward ($R_{cons}$)

The core of CGRO is a class-conditional consistency reward. For anomaly images, the reward encourages high spatial consensus among the top-$k$ anomaly-relevant reasoning tokens (selected by their composite weight $w_r$). Spatial consensus is measured by the Jaccard Index ($J$) of the binary support regions ($\Omega_r$) derived from these tokens' visual attention maps. For anomaly images ($y=1$), a high Jaccard Index ($J > \delta_1$) is rewarded to promote coherent localization of defects. For normal images ($y=0$), a low Jaccard Index ($J < \delta_2$) is rewarded to discourage spurious concentration on benign regions, promoting dispersed attention.

### 7. Training Procedure

The MLLM is optimized using a reinforcement learning framework, specifically a group-based policy optimization (similar to PPO). The total reward for a generated response is defined as:
$R_{total} = R_{fmt} + R_{acc} + R_{cons}$
- $R_{fmt}$: Ensures the generated output follows a predefined structure (reasoning followed by an answer).
- $R_{acc}$: Promotes correct image-level anomaly predictions (Yes/No).
- $R_{cons}$: Enforces consistency between the generated reasoning and the visual evidence, as described above.
The model learns to maximize this total reward, adjusting its policy to generate outputs that are well-formatted, accurate, and visually consistent. This mechanism allows the MLLM to regulate its own reasoning process, leading to semantically coherent and visually grounded interpretations, even with only image-level labels.

**Implementation Details:**
The framework is built upon Qwen2.5-VL-7B. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is applied to the language and cross-modal layers, while the vision encoder remains frozen. Training utilizes a curated dataset of 4K industrial images from various public benchmarks, annotated only with image-level (normal/anomaly) labels. Images are resized to 420x420 pixels. Experiments are conducted on NVIDIA A6000 GPUs.

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The research paper evaluates the proposed Reasoning-Driven Anomaly Localization (ReAL) framework, enhanced by Consistency-Guided Reasoning Optimization (CGRO), across four public industrial anomaly detection benchmarks: MVTec-AD, WFDD, SDD, and DTD. The evaluation adheres to a zero-shot industrial anomaly detection setting, meaning the model is trained on an auxiliary dataset without overlap with the target test domains, assessing generalization ability. Metrics include AUROC, AUPR, and ACC for image-level and pixel-level detection, as well as ROUGE-L and SBERT scores for reasoning quality on the MVTec-COT benchmark.

**Overall Performance (Table 1):**
- **Reasoning Quality:** The model, particularly the Qwen2.5-VL+CGRO variants, achieved the highest reasoning scores (ROUGE-L and SBERT) among all compared methods, demonstrating its ability to generate coherent and anomaly-aware explanations. For instance, Qwen2.5-VL+CGRO (7B) reached 27.1 ROUGE-L and 74.7 SBERT on MVTec-COT, outperforming even commercial MLLMs like GPT-4.1.
- **Image-Level Detection:** The proposed method exhibited strong image-level detection performance. The Qwen2.5-VL+CGRO (7B) variant achieved an average ACC of 86.9% across the four datasets, ranking as the second-best, closely behind GPT-4.1 (88.4%) but utilizing significantly less supervision (image-level vs. proprietary or dense).
- **Pixel-Level Localization:** A key finding is the framework's capability to perform pixel-level anomaly localization without external vision modules or dense pixel-level annotations. While most MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, LLaVA-1.6) cannot natively provide anomaly maps and report 'N/A' for pixel-level metrics, ReAL enables pixel-level output for its base MLLM. The CGRO component further significantly improved pixel-level metrics. The Qwen2.5-VL+CGRO (7B) variant achieved an average pixel-level AUROC of 80.7% and ACC of 97.1%, which is competitive with or superior to methods like AnomalyGPT (80.2% AUROC, 96.4% ACC) and EIAD (63.4% AUROC, 83.5% ACC), despite these baselines relying on external vision modules (e.g., SAM) and often dense pixel-level or task-specific instruction tuning data. This highlights the effectiveness of using only image-level supervision.

**Qualitative Comparisons (Figures 3, 5, 6):**
- Qualitative results visually confirmed the improvements. The base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model often showed limited anomaly perception and produced diffuse or irrelevant attention maps.
- The application of reinforcement learning (R1 variant) enhanced anomaly sensitivity and reasoning relevance, but could still lead to inconsistent reasoning and diffuse localization.
- The full Qwen2.5-VL-7B+CGRO framework demonstrated improved alignment between reasoning and visual evidence, concentrating attention more precisely on true anomalies. This resulted in cleaner and more focused anomaly maps.
- Comparison with other methods like GPT-4.1, Triad, AnomalyGPT, and EIAD showed that the proposed method produced high-fidelity pixel-level localization maps, comparable to or better than methods that required auxiliary modules or denser supervision, while also providing strong textual reasoning.

**Ablation Study on ReAL and CGRO (Table 2, 6):**
- **Impact of ReAL:** When ReAL was incorporated, pixel-level localization metrics improved significantly. Compared to a baseline of averaging all reasoning tokens' attention, ReAL's selective aggregation of anomaly-relevant tokens yielded a clearer spatial focus, improving pixel-level AUROC from 72.7% to 80.7% and ACC from 92.6% to 97.1% (average values for Qwen2.5-VL-7B + CGRO base).
- **Impact of CGRO:** CGRO consistently enhanced both image-level and pixel-level performance. For instance, for the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, adding CGRO to the vanilla model (which already included ReAL for localization) boosted image-level AUROC from 63.4% to 83.9% and pixel-level AUROC from 61.7% to 80.7%. This indicates that the consistency reward effectively aligns reasoning with visual evidence, leading to more accurate detection and localization.

**Ablation Study on Token Selection in ReAL (Table 3, 7):**
- The study showed that combining both semantic relevance ($S^T$) and spatial entropy ($S^I$) for filtering and weighting reasoning tokens yielded the best pixel-level localization performance. Using only $S^I$ or $S^T$ individually provided gains over using all tokens, but their combination using composite weights achieved the highest AUROC (80.7%) and ACC (97.1%), confirming their complementary roles in identifying anomaly-relevant tokens.

Overall, the findings indicate that the proposed framework effectively leverages the intrinsic reasoning capabilities of MLLMs to perform end-to-end anomaly detection and pixel-level localization with only image-level supervision, achieving competitive performance compared to methods requiring more extensive or dense annotations.

### 9. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper introduces a novel and significant contribution to the field of industrial anomaly detection, particularly in the context of multimodal large language models (MLLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Reduced Annotation Burden and Cost:** The most prominent impact is the successful demonstration of end-to-end anomaly detection and pixel-level localization using *only image-level supervision*. This eliminates the need for expensive, time-consuming, and labor-intensive pixel-level mask annotations and detailed reasoning explanations, which are typically required by many existing MLLM-based or vision-expert-dependent methods. This reduction in annotation burden can significantly lower deployment costs and accelerate the development cycle for industrial inspection systems.

2. **Simplified Architecture and Enhanced Efficiency:** By activating the intrinsic reasoning potential of MLLMs for pixel-level localization, the framework removes the dependency on external vision modules (e.g., segmentation models like SAM). This results in a more unified, streamlined, and end-to-end architecture. Such a simplification can mitigate issues like error propagation between distinct modules, reduce deployment complexity, and potentially improve inference efficiency in real-world industrial settings, although the paper notes MLLM autoregressive decoding latency as a current constraint for real-time applications.

3. **Improved Interpretability and Trustworthiness:** The Consistency-Guided Reasoning Optimization (CGRO) module ensures that the MLLM's textual reasoning is aligned with its visual attention and the final anomaly detection decision. This leads to more coherent, visually grounded, and reliable anomaly explanations. In industrial contexts, interpretable diagnostics are crucial for operator trust, informed decision-making, and targeted maintenance, making the system more actionable.

4. **Enhanced Generalization:** Relying on image-level supervision and the inherent reasoning capabilities of MLLMs, rather than domain-specific, pixel-level annotations, may lead to better generalization. This is particularly valuable in industrial settings where product lines frequently shift, new defect types emerge, and data privacy constraints limit extensive data collection. The approach could potentially adapt to novel products and defect categories with less retraining or fine-tuning.

5. **Activation of Intrinsic MLLM Capabilities:** The work highlights and leverages the latent spatial grounding abilities of MLLMs that are often underutilized when only text or image-level outputs are considered. By extracting and optimizing attention responses from reasoning tokens, the paper demonstrates a deeper understanding of how MLLMs process and interpret multimodal information.

6. **Competitive Performance with Weak Supervision:** The achievement of pixel-level localization performance competitive with methods trained with dense supervision, despite using only image-level labels, underscores the effectiveness and practical viability of the proposed reasoning-driven approach. This indicates that carefully designed optimization strategies can compensate for limited supervision.

In the future, the authors intend to extend the evaluation to a broader range of MLLMs to validate the generality and robustness of the framework. Addressing current limitations, such as refining boundary fidelity in complex scenes and optimizing for real-time deployment, would further enhance the practical utility and impact of this research. Overall, this work represents a significant step towards scalable, interpretable, and efficient anomaly understanding in industrial applications.
