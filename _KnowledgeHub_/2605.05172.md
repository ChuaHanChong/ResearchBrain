# When Life Gives You BC, Make Q-functions: Extracting Q-values from Behavior Cloning for On-Robot Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Q2RL introduces an algorithm that boosts Behavior Cloning (BC) policies by extracting implicit Q-values to guide online Reinforcement Learning (RL), enabling self-improvement for robotic tasks. The method facilitates rapid and safe adaptation on physical robots, achieving performance improvements up to 3.75x within 1-2 hours of interaction on contact-rich manipulation tasks, even without access to original BC training data.

## Problem

- Behavior Cloning (BC) policies cannot self-improve online, leading to performance degradation in novel situations or with insufficient data.
- Pure online Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically requires extensive and potentially unsafe exploration, which is costly and risky for real-world robot deployment.
- Existing offline-to-online methods often face the "unlearning" problem, discarding effective behaviors, or require large, diverse datasets not always available in robotics.

## Method

- **Q2RL** derives an estimated ==Q-function (Q̂_BC)== from a pre-trained BC policy by assuming its action distribution follows a ==Boltzmann distribution==, utilizing log-probabilities and entropy.
- ==Q-Gating==: initializes a learning RL Q-function (Q_RL) from Q̂_BC and employs a gating mechanism that executes the BC policy's action or the RL policy's action based on which has a higher Q-value.
- Integrates an ==auxiliary BC loss== into the RL actor's loss function to maintain safer behaviors and prevent the RL policy from deviating excessively.

## Results

- Achieved up to **3.75x performance improvement** on real robots (e.g., Peg Insertion, Pipe Assembly, Modified Kitting) within **1–2 hours** of online interaction, without requiring the original BC training data.
- Consistently outperformed or matched strong offline-to-online RL baselines like **IBRL, CalQL, and WSRL** in simulation, especially in scenarios with limited or no access to BC training data.
- Demonstrated enhanced **safety** in real-world experiments by avoiding costly exploratory actions and robot faults, a common issue with unguided online RL.
- Qualitatively showed intelligent action switching, leveraging BC for robust base movements and RL for fine, contact-rich manipulation and adaptation to task shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit value information within a pre-trained Behavior Cloning policy can be systematically extracted to serve as a strong initialization and guide for online Reinforcement Learning.
> - A Q-gating mechanism enables an RL policy to incrementally improve upon BC, preserving high-value expert actions while allowing exploration of potentially superior alternatives.
> - This combined approach provides a sample-efficient and safer pathway for on-robot learning, reducing exploration costs and mitigating risks in real-world deployments.
