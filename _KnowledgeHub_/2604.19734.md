# UniT: Toward a Unified Physical Language for Human-to-Humanoid Policy Learning and World Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified physical language, called UniT, allows robust policy learning and world modeling for humanoid robots by bridging the cross-embodiment gap using human data. This framework achieved a 66.7% success rate on the RoboCasa benchmark, demonstrated 10x data efficiency, and exhibited strong real-world generalization, including zero-shot task transfer.

## Problem

- The scarcity of high-quality robotic data hinders the development of general-purpose foundation models for humanoid robots.
- A "cross-embodiment chasm" exists due to fundamental differences in kinematics, DoF, and control paradigms between humans and robots, complicating skill transfer.
- Existing transfer methods like motion retargeting, action-only, and vision-only representations suffer from issues such as physical inconsistencies, distribution shifts, or entanglement with low-level visual confounders.

## Method

- **UniT** (Unified Latent Action Tokenizer via ==Visual Anchoring==) learns a discrete latent action representation by projecting heterogeneous human and humanoid behaviors into a shared token space.
- A ==tri-branch encoder== models visual transitions, actions, and fused visuo-motor features, which are then quantized using ==Residual Quantization (RQ-VAE)== into a shared, discrete codebook.
- A ==cross-reconstruction objective== forces tokens from any branch to be decoded by both a shared visual decoder and an embodiment-specific action decoder, aligning physical intent across modalities and embodiments.

## Results

- **VLA-UniT** achieved an overall success rate of **66.7%** on the RoboCasa benchmark, outperforming baselines, and demonstrated **10x data efficiency**, closely matching full-data baseline performance with **10%** of the data.
- The framework facilitated robust real-world human-to-humanoid transfer, including **60% zero-shot success** on an unseen stacking task, showcasing emergent complex coordination from human demonstrations.
- **WM-UniT** consistently improved controllable video generation quality and controllability (**lowest EPE**) for world modeling, enabling faithful cross-embodiment dynamics prediction and transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual observations serve as a universal anchor to ground and align disparate kinematic spaces, enabling the distillation of embodiment-agnostic physical intent despite kinematic differences.
> - The bidirectional cross-reconstruction mechanism effectively preserves the essential, embodiment-agnostic physical intent by filtering out uncorrelated noise from individual modalities.
> - The synergistic combination of visual and action modalities in a unified representation provides a more complete and transferable physical language for cross-embodiment learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of foundation models for humanoid robots is currently constrained by the limited availability of high-quality robotic data. In response to this data scarcity, there has been a growing interest in leveraging large-scale egocentric human data as a scalable alternative. However, a significant challenge arises from the "cross-embodiment chasm," which refers to the fundamental differences in kinematics, degrees of freedom (DoF), and control paradigms between humans and robots.

Previous approaches to bridging this gap have faced limitations. Traditional motion retargeting methods, which use kinematic solvers to map human motions to specific robots, are often labor-intensive, unscalable, and can result in physically inconsistent actions. More recent efforts in learning unified representations have explored modality-specific approaches. Action-only methods, relying solely on proprioceptive reconstruction, are susceptible to severe distribution shifts between humans and robots due to a lack of external grounding. Conversely, vision-only latent action frameworks infer intent directly from pixels, which can bypass kinematic mismatches but are prone to entangling low-level appearance confounders (e.g., textures, lighting), limiting fine-grained physical execution and under-utilizing structural priors from human pose data. Some multi-modal paradigms integrate both vision and action but typically employ independent tokenizers, leading to disjoint vocabularies without deep representational unification. Furthermore, many existing latent-action systems have been confined to simpler robot setups, leaving their scalability to dexterous humanoids underexplored.

This research aims to address these identified limitations by proposing a data-driven unified physical language. This language intends to project heterogeneous human and robotic data into a shared latent action space, thereby facilitating robust and scalable human-to-humanoid transfer for both policy learning and world modeling in complex humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to establish a unified physical language, termed UniT (Unified Latent Action Tokenizer via Visual Anchoring), to bridge the cross-embodiment gap between humans and humanoids. This unified language is intended to enable the distillation of vast human knowledge into general-purpose humanoid capabilities, particularly for policy learning and world modeling.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key challenges and observations:

- **Data Scarcity for Humanoid Foundation Models:** Scaling foundation models for humanoids is fundamentally bottlenecked by the limited availability of high-quality robotic data. This restricts the ability to train robust and generalizable policies and world models.
- **Potential of Human Data:** Massive, structured human motion sequences, readily available from low-cost capture methods, represent a scalable alternative rich in physical interaction priors. However, leveraging this data for robots is difficult due to fundamental biomechanical and hardware differences.
- **Limitations of Existing Transfer Methods:**
 - **Motion Retargeting:** Current methods are often unscalable, labor-intensive, and prone to physical inconsistencies when mapping human motions to diverse robot morphologies.
 - **Action-Only Representations:** These methods struggle with distribution shifts between human and robot action spaces because they lack external grounding, making cross-embodiment alignment difficult.
 - **Vision-Only Representations:** While bypassing kinematic mismatches, these representations can entangle task-irrelevant visual confounders, leading to a lack of precision for fine-grained physical execution and under-utilization of structural information from human pose data.
 - **Decoupled Multi-Modal Representations:** Approaches that process vision and action independently often fail to establish a deeply unified representation, resulting in disconnected vocabularies that hinder effective cross-modal and cross-embodiment transfer.
- **Core Insight – Visual Anchoring:** The central motivation for UniT's design is the philosophy that despite kinematic differences, the physical outcomes of human and humanoid intents share consistent visual representations. Therefore, visual observations can serve as a universal anchor to ground and align disparate kinematic spaces, allowing the distillation of embodiment-agnostic physical intent.

By addressing these points, the research aims to provide a more effective and scalable mechanism for human-to-humanoid skill transfer, overcoming the limitations of prior approaches and paving the way for more capable general-purpose humanoid robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces UniT (Unified Latent Action Tokenizer via Visual Anchoring), a framework designed to establish a unified physical language. This language is then integrated into two embodied AI paradigms: policy learning (VLA-UniT) and world modeling (WM-UniT).

#### 3.1. UniT: Unified Latent Action Tokenizer via Visual Anchoring

UniT learns a discrete latent action representation by mapping heterogeneous human and humanoid behaviors into a shared token space. This is achieved through a tri-branch architecture that jointly models visual transitions, actions, and fused visuo-motor features under a cross-reconstruction objective.

- **Tri-Branch Encoding:**
 - **Visual Branch ($E_v$):** This branch operates as an inverse dynamics model (IDM). It takes frozen DINOv2 features of an observation pair ($o_t, o_{t+k}$) as input, following the visual feature design of UniVLA, and produces a latent representation of the underlying physical transition. DINOv2 is chosen for its domain-invariant properties, serving as a stable visual anchor.
 - **Action Branch ($E_a$):** This branch encodes the current state ($s_t$) and an action chunk ($a_{t:t+k}$). To handle differing control modes, parameterizations, and DoF between human and humanoid actions, raw actions are first padded to a unified maximum length and then projected by embodiment-specific Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) before being summarized into a compact latent control representation.
 - **Fusion Branch ($E_m$):** This branch takes the features from both the visual and action branches as input. It then produces a fused visuo-motor latent representation, designed to capture complementary cross-modal structure for more compact and robust tokens.
- **Shared Discrete Quantization:** Continuous latent representations from all three branches are quantized using Residual Quantization (RQ-VAE) with a shared codebook. This ensures that tokens from all branches and all embodiments reside in a unified discrete space, progressively refining the approximation to capture both coarse physical intent and fine-grained motion detail.
- **Cross-Reconstruction:** This is a core mechanism of UniT. Every quantized token ($\hat{z}_i$) from any branch is decoded by both a shared visual decoder ($D_v$) and an embodiment-specific action decoder ($D_a$).
 - The visual decoder ($D_v$) functions as a forward dynamics model (FDM), conditioned on the current observation feature ($f_t$), and reconstructs the future visual feature ($f_{t+k}$). This process forces kinematic features to be anchored to their physical consequences, preventing memorization of embodiment-specific kinematics and filtering out visually unobservable artifacts.
 - The action decoder ($D_a$) is conditioned on the current state ($s_t$) and reconstructs the original action chunk ($a_{t:t+k}$). This process compels visual features to reconstruct kinematics, thereby stripping away low-level appearance confounders that do not contribute to physical motion.
 - This bidirectional constraint ensures that only the essential intersection of both modalities—the embodiment-agnostic physical intent—is preserved, while uncorrelated noise from either domain is discarded.
- **Training Objective:** The total loss function combines cosine similarity loss for visual feature reconstruction, an action reconstruction loss, and an RQ-VAE commitment loss, aggregated across all three branches.

#### 3.2. VLA-UniT: Cross-Embodiment Policy Learning

VLA-UniT integrates UniT into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architectures, specifically building upon the GR00T n1.5 framework with Qwen2.5-VL as the vision-language backbone. It decomposes policy learning into UniT token prediction and flow-matching action generation.

- **UniT Token Prediction:** The VLM, conditioned on the current observation ($o_t$) and language instruction ($\ell$), predicts UniT discrete codes via learnable queries. The target codes ($c_t$) are obtained by encoding ground-truth observation pairs, states, and action chunks using the pre-trained UniT tokenizer. A cross-entropy loss is used for token prediction.
- **Flow Matching Action Generation:** A lightweight flow matching head generates continuous, embodiment-specific actions ($A_t$). This head learns a velocity field conditioned on vision-language features from the VLM and the current observation encoding, utilizing a flow-matching loss.
Both UniT token prediction and action generation are conditioned on the same VLM vision-language features.

#### 3.3. WM-UniT: Cross-Embodiment World Modeling

WM-UniT employs UniT tokens as a universal condition for action-conditioned video generation, utilizing the Cosmos Predict 2.5 framework.

- **Unified Conditioning Interface:** Instead of using raw, embodiment-specific actions, WM-UniT uses the continuous pre-quantization features from UniT's action branch (${\tilde{z}^a_t}$). These features are projected through an MLP and injected via cross-attention alongside the current observation ($o_t$).
- **Dynamics Prediction:** The generation model is trained with flow matching, where a velocity field is applied to latent future frames, conditioned on the current observation and the projected UniT action features. This allows for autoregressive rollout for long-horizon video generation.
By using UniT features, which absorb physical priors during tokenization, WM-UniT provides an embodiment-agnostic control interface for dynamics prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments validated UniT across several aspects, including representation alignment, policy learning, world modeling, and architectural design.

#### 4.1. Unified Representation: Alignment and Robustness

- **Cross-Embodiment Token Alignment:** t-SNE analysis demonstrated that raw human and humanoid actions form clearly separated clusters. In contrast, UniT token embeddings for both embodiments were highly overlapping, indicating successful projection into a shared manifold of physical intent.
- **Robustness to Action Noise:** UniT exhibited superior robustness to Gaussian noise injected into action trajectories. At a relative noise level of $\sigma=0.2$, UniT degraded by 1.7x in reconstruction quality (MSE), significantly less than the action-only tokenizer (2.7x) and FAST (10.7x). This suggests that visual grounding provides an implicit denoising effect, filtering kinematic noise.
- **Downstream Representation Alignment:** Internal representations of downstream models (VLA vision-language features and WM cross-attention outputs) trained with UniT (VLA-UniT, WM-UniT) showed substantially more interleaved distributions for human and humanoid data compared to vanilla baselines, confirming that UniT induces embodiment-agnostic internal features.

#### 4.2. Policy Learning (VLA-UniT)

- **Benchmark Performance (RoboCasa GR1):** VLA-UniT achieved an overall success rate of 66.7% on the full-data RoboCasa benchmark, outperforming all compared baselines (e.g., FLARE at 55.0%, GR00T at 47.8%). This included 67.3% for Pick & Place tasks and 64.7% for Articulated tasks.
- **Data Efficiency:** With only 10% of the training data (few-shot setting), VLA-UniT achieved a 45.5% success rate, closely approaching the GR00T baseline trained on full data (47.8%). This indicates an approximate 10x reduction in data requirements for comparable performance.
- **Human-to-Humanoid Transfer (Simulation):** Co-training VLA-UniT with EgoDex human demonstrations in the few-shot simulation setting improved in-domain average performance from 45.5% to 50.0% and OOD average performance from 34.7% to 38.5%, with notable gains in Pick & Place and all OOD scenarios.
- **Real-World Generalization (IRON-R01-1.11):**
 - **In-Domain:** VLA-UniT without human data outperformed GR00T-Qwen2.5 on real-world Pick & Place (70% vs. 30%) and Pouring (35% vs. 5%). Incorporating human data further improved these to 78% and 75% respectively.
 - **OOD Generalization:** VLA-UniT with human co-training consistently achieved the strongest performance across five OOD scenarios (Geometry, Distractor, Target, Background, Combinational Generalization), with significant improvements in scenarios where human data introduced novel variations.
 - **Zero-Shot Task Transfer:** On an unseen stacking task, VLA-UniT with human co-training achieved a 60% success rate, demonstrating transfer of stacking logic and emergent upper-body coordination (waist rotation, head turning) from human demonstrations. Baselines and UniT without human data showed limited to no success (0-10%).

#### 4.3. World Modeling (WM-UniT)

- **Controllable Generation:** WM-UniT achieved the strongest controllability (lowest EPE) on the DROID dataset and improved PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS compared to Raw Action and WM-Action baselines. Similar performance advantages were observed during co-training on EgoDex and RoboCasa-GR1 data.
- **Human-Humanoid Transfer:**
 - **Co-Training:** WM-UniT consistently outperformed Raw Action in terms of video generation quality and controllability (EPE) on both human and humanoid subsets when co-trained on mixed data.
 - **Pre-Training:** Pre-training WM-UniT on EgoDex human demonstrations followed by fine-tuning on RoboCasa-GR1 data resulted in consistent gains across all evaluation metrics, with notable improvement in controllability, indicating transferable dynamics.
- **Cross-Embodiment Conditioning:** Qualitative and quantitative evaluation (using Gemini-3-Pro) of cross-embodiment conditioning showed that UniT tokens enabled more faithful video generation for the target embodiment than raw actions. UniT-conditioned generation preserved fine-grained action semantics, magnitude sensitivity, and temporal coherence (e.g., non-monotonic trajectories like "reach then retract") in both human-to-robot and robot-to-human transfer, achieving higher scores for Semantic, Temporal, and Geometric consistency.

#### 4.4. Tokenizer Design Ablation

- **Vision-Action Synergy:** VLA-UniT consistently outperformed single-modality variants (VLA-Vision and VLA-Action) across OOD scenarios, confirming that combining both visual and action modalities provides a more complete and transferable physical language.
- **Cross-Reconstruction:** VLA-UniT significantly outperformed VLA-UniT without the cross-reconstruction objective (49.9% OOD average vs. 30.3%), indicating that explicit cross-modal alignment is crucial for human-to-humanoid transfer.
- **Bidirectional vs. Unidirectional Reconstruction:** VLA-UniT (bidirectional cross-reconstruction) achieved higher in-domain Pick & Place performance (66.8%) compared to VLA-Villa (unidirectional V2A reconstruction, 63.1%), suggesting the benefit of mutual reconstruction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents UniT, a framework that provides a unified physical language for human-to-humanoid policy learning and world modeling. The findings suggest several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** By effectively bridging the cross-embodiment gap, UniT offers a scalable approach to leverage vast amounts of readily available human data. This could alleviate the bottleneck of scarce robotic data, which currently hinders the development of general-purpose humanoid foundation models.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Transfer:** The demonstrated improvements in data efficiency, out-of-distribution generalization, and zero-shot task transfer for humanoid robots in both simulation and the real world are significant. This capability is crucial for deploying humanoids in complex, unstructured environments where training data might be limited or where novel tasks need to be performed without explicit prior demonstrations. The emergent upper-body coordination observed in zero-shot tasks highlights the potential for learning sophisticated behaviors directly from human observation.
- **Robustness to Data Quality:** UniT's implicit denoising capability, a result of grounding actions in visual consequences, enhances the utility of noisy, in-the-wild human motion capture data. This improves the practical applicability of large, imperfect human datasets for robot learning.
- **Unified AI Paradigms:** UniT provides a shared interface for both policy learning and world modeling. This unification suggests avenues for deeper integration between these two core embodied AI paradigms, such as policies proposing latent actions that world models simulate, with imagined rollouts feeding back as reward signals or enabling test-time planning. Such closed-loop co-evolution within a shared latent space could accelerate the development of more intelligent and autonomous robots.
- **Scalability to Diverse Data Sources:** The data-driven alignment mechanism of UniT is designed to scale to internet-scale human motion data without requiring complex manual kinematic correspondence. This could enable the learning of a broad spectrum of upper-body coordination and dexterous control from diverse human demonstrations, moving beyond the limitations of single-embodiment or simple gripper setups.

In summary, UniT contributes a robust method for cross-embodiment transfer, opening new possibilities for leveraging human intelligence to advance the capabilities of generalist humanoid robots across various tasks and environments.
