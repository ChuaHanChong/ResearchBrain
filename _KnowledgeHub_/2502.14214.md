# Asymmetric Co-Training for Source-Free Few-Shot Domain Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Jilin University introduce Asymmetric Co-Training (ACT), a method for Source-Free Few-Shot Domain Adaptation (SFFSDA) that adapts pre-trained models to new target domains using only a small labeled support set without access to source data. ACT consistently outperforms state-of-the-art SFUDA and few-shot transfer learning approaches, achieving 52.56% accuracy at 1-shot on TerraIncognita compared to 31.01% for the leading SFUDA baseline.

## Problem

- Deep Neural Networks suffer significant performance degradation due to domain shift when trained on a source domain and applied to a target domain.
- Traditional Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) methods require simultaneous access to both source and target data, which is often unfeasible due to privacy or computational constraints.
- Source-Free Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (SFUDA) methods remove the need for source data but still depend on large volumes of unlabeled target data, which can be scarce or difficult to acquire in many real-world scenarios.

## Method

- The paper proposes ==Asymmetric Co-Training (ACT)==, a novel method for the ==Source-Free Few-Shot Domain Adaptation (SFFSDA)== setting, which leverages a ==two-branch network== architecture.
- ACT employs a ==strong-weak data augmentation== strategy, including AutoAugment, to artificially increase data diversity and prevent overfitting in limited few-shot target support sets.
- The approach utilizes a two-step iterative optimization process combining various loss functions (Label Smoothing Cross-Entropy, Entropy Minimization, Reverse Cross-Entropy, Classifier Determinacy Disparity) and ==Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM)== optimization.

## Results

- ACT consistently outperformed state-of-the-art SFUDA methods across all few-shot settings on benchmarks like Office31, OfficeHome, VisDA-C, and TerraIncognita, showing a **52.56%** accuracy at 1-shot on TerraIncognita compared to **31.01%** for the best SFUDA baseline.
- The proposed method demonstrated superior performance against existing few-shot transfer learning and SFFSDA techniques, particularly on label-imbalanced datasets, further confirming its robustness.
- An ablation study confirmed that all proposed components, including strong-weak augmentation, SAM optimization, and the specific loss functions, contribute positively and significantly to ACT's overall performance and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adapting models with only a small labeled support set from the target domain (SFFSDA) is a practical and efficient solution for domain adaptation when source data is unavailable and large unlabeled target datasets are scarce.
> - The strategic combination of strong-weak data augmentation with an asymmetric co-training framework effectively mitigates overfitting and enhances model generalization in data-limited, few-shot environments.
> - Integrating diverse loss functions for discriminative learning, source knowledge retention, and enforcing consistent, confident predictions across multiple classifiers is critical for robust and high-performing domain adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and evolving challenge in machine learning: domain adaptation (DA). Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) typically struggle when the training data (source domain) and test data (target domain) come from different distributions, a phenomenon known as domain shift.

Initially, **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA)** emerged as a solution, aiming to align feature distributions between a labeled source domain and an unlabeled target domain. UDA methods often rely on techniques like moment matching or adversarial training. However, UDA assumes simultaneous access to both source and target data during adaptation, which is often infeasible due to data privacy concerns, storage costs, or computational constraints.

To overcome these limitations, **Source-Free Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (SFUDA)** was introduced. SFUDA methods adapt a source pre-trained model to a large volume of unlabeled target data *without* direct access to the original source data. This paradigm has gained significant traction due to increasing data privacy regulations and the trend towards large-scale pre-training. Despite its advantages, SFUDA faces its own set of challenges:
- **Dependence on large unlabeled target data:** Sufficient unlabeled target data is not always available.
- **Closed-set assumption:** SFUDA often assumes that all target classes are present in the source domain, which might not hold in real-world "open-set" or "partial-set" scenarios.
- **Label distribution shift:** Discrepancies in class prevalence between source and target domains can degrade performance.

This paper proposes a novel and more practical setting: **Source-Free Few-Shot Domain Adaptation (SFFSDA)**. SFFSDA addresses the core limitations of SFUDA by requiring only a *small labeled support set* from the target domain, alongside a source pre-trained model, for adaptation. This is a significant shift because:
- Gathering a few labeled instances from the target domain is often more feasible and less costly than acquiring massive amounts of unlabeled data, especially in niche or sensitive applications.
- It moves away from the "large unlabeled data" assumption, making DA applicable in data-scarce environments.

SFFSDA thus carves out a new niche in the DA landscape, bridging the gap between SFUDA (which needs abundant unlabeled target data) and traditional Few-Shot Transfer Learning (which might rely on specialized architectures or joint training with source data, and can suffer from overfitting with very limited data). The work differentiates itself from existing few-shot transfer learning methods by specifically focusing on the *source-free* aspect and proposing a robust adaptation strategy to prevent overfitting in such data-limited scenarios. It builds upon the idea, as highlighted by Lee et al. (2023), that fine-tuning with a few labeled target instances is a practical solution for SFUDA limitations, but then introduces a more sophisticated co-training framework to enhance robustness and performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and practical method for domain adaptation in scenarios where source data is unavailable and labeled target data is scarce. Specifically, the authors aim to:

1. **Introduce and address the Source-Free Few-Shot Domain Adaptation (SFFSDA) setting:** The paper formally defines SFFSDA as a more pragmatic alternative to SFUDA, acknowledging the real-world difficulty of obtaining large volumes of unlabeled target data and the common occurrence of domain shifts with limited labeled samples.
2. **Mitigate performance degradation in data-scarce and distribution-shifted environments:** Traditional SFUDA methods struggle when the target data is insufficient, does not strictly adhere to a closed-set label distribution, or exhibits significant label shifts. The ACT method is designed to overcome these challenges.
3. **Enhance data diversity within limited support sets:** Given the few-shot nature of SFFSDA, the support set is inherently small. A key motivation is to prevent overfitting and improve model generalization by artificially increasing data diversity.
4. **Boost the model’s discriminative ability and mitigate overfitting:** With limited labeled data, ensuring that the model learns robust and discriminative features without over-specializing to the few examples is crucial.
5. **Ensure consistency and reduce redundancy in classifier predictions:** When using multiple classifiers, aligning their outputs and promoting confident predictions is essential for overall model stability and accuracy.
6. **Develop a method that is practical and dependable:** The overarching motivation is to provide a solution that is not only theoretically sound but also easily deployable in real-world applications where data acquisition is constrained.

By tackling these objectives, the authors seek to provide a novel and effective solution for adapting pre-trained models to new domains using minimal target-specific supervision, thereby extending the applicability of domain adaptation techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Asymmetric Co-Training (ACT)**, a novel method tailored for the SFFSDA setting. ACT leverages a two-branch network architecture, strong-weak data augmentation, and a two-step optimization process with multiple loss functions, along with an advanced optimizer.

#### 3.1. Overall Idea

The method begins with a source model (ΦS) pre-trained on a labeled source domain. In SFFSDA, the original source data is inaccessible; only the parameters of ΦS are available. The goal is to fine-tune this pre-trained source model into a target model (ΦT) using a small, labeled support set {XSUP, YSUP} sampled from the target domain in an N-way-K-shot manner. Unlike traditional SFUDA, target labels are available, eliminating the need for pseudo-labeling. ACT employs a feature extractor (F) and two classifier networks (C1, C2) for the target model.

#### 3.2. Data Diversity with Strong-Weak Augmentation

To combat overfitting and lack of diversity inherent in few-shot settings, ACT implements a strong-weak data augmentation strategy.
- **Weak Augmentation (AW):** Involves standard transformations like random cropping and flipping.
- **Strong Augmentation (AS):** Utilizes AutoAugment (Cubuk et al., 2019), an automated method that learns optimal augmentation policies, generating diverse synthetic data from ground-truth targets. This effectively increases the volume and variety of the limited support set.
The data-splitting strategy is also refined, systematically sampling few-shot support sets from the entire target dataset, with the remainder forming the test set.

#### 3.3. Model Structure

The ACT model comprises two modules, each containing a feature extractor and two classifiers.
- **Source Initial Model:** A feature extractor Fsrc and two classifiers Csrc1, Csrc2 are pre-trained on the source domain. Their parameters are then used to initialize the corresponding target model components (Ft, Ct1, Ct2). These source-initialized modules remain frozen during target domain fine-tuning, serving as a reference from the source knowledge.
- **Target Model:** The target model consists of a feature extractor Ft and two classifier heads Ct1, Ct2. Both are initialized with the source pre-trained parameters and subsequently fine-tuned using the target support set.

#### 3.4. Training Steps and Loss Functions

The fine-tuning process on the target domain is a two-step iterative optimization.

**Step 1: Simultaneous Training of Feature Extractor and Classifiers**
This step focuses on enhancing the model's discriminative ability and leveraging the available ground-truth labels.
- **Label Smoothing Cross-Entropy (LSCE) Loss:** $L_{lsce} = \lambda_{lsce} (L_{lsce1} + L_{lsce2})$
 - Directly uses ground-truth labels from the support set.
 - Label smoothing prevents overconfidence and improves generalization, which is beneficial with limited data.
 - Enhances model discriminability and aligns target data using semantic information.
- **Entropy Minimization (Entropy) Loss:** $L_e = \lambda_e (L_{e1} + L_{e2})$
 - Minimizes the entropy of the class-conditional distribution for both classifiers on the target support data.
 - Encourages confident predictions and aligns the classifiers with the low-density regions of the target domain, helping fit target-specific structures.
- **Reverse Cross-Entropy (RCE) Loss:** $L_{rce} = \lambda_{rce} (L_{rce1} + L_{rce2})$
 - Measures the discrepancy between the outputs of the *source-initialized frozen classifiers* (Csrc1, Csrc2) and the *adapted target classifiers* (Ct1, Ct2) on the support set.
 - Typically used for label noise, here it prevents model collapse and mitigates overfitting by maintaining consistency between the (potentially augmented) data and original labels, establishing a strong relationship between source and target models.
 - The combined objective function for Step 1: $\min_{Ft, Ct1, Ct2} \lambda_{lsce}(L_{lsce1} + L_{lsce2}) + \lambda_e(L_{e1} + L_{e2}) + \lambda_{rce}(L_{rce1} + L_{rce2})$

**Step 2: Training Classifiers (Feature Extractor Fixed)**
In this step, the feature extractor $F_t$ is frozen, and only the classifiers $C_t1, C_t2$ are trained.
- **Classifier Determinacy Disparity (CDD) Distance:** $L_{cdd} = \frac{1}{n_t} \sum_{i=1}^{n_t} \Gamma(p_1^i, p_2^i)$
 - Measures the consistency and confidence between the softmax outputs ($p_1, p_2$) of the two target classifiers (Ct1, Ct2).
 - The objective is to *maximize* this distance (equivalently, minimize $-L_{cdd}$), which encourages the classifiers to produce highly confident and consistent predictions for the same input. This helps refine predictions and reduce redundancy in the output space.
 - The overall objective function for Step 2: $\min_{Ct1, Ct2} \lambda_{lsce}(L_{lsce1} + L_{lsce2}) + \lambda_e(L_{e1} + L_{e2}) + \lambda_{rce}(L_{rce1} + L_{rce2}) - \lambda_{cdd}L_{cdd}$
Both steps are iteratively performed during training.

#### 3.5. Optimizer

ACT employs **Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM)** (Foret et al., 2020; Kwon et al., 2021) in conjunction with the Adam optimizer. SAM aims to find parameters that lie in neighborhoods with uniformly low loss, thereby improving generalization. While it increases training time due to its two-step gradient calculation, it significantly enhances accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the superiority and practicality of the ACT method across various domain adaptation benchmarks and settings.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** Office31, OfficeHome, VisDA-C, and TerraIncognita (Terra). These cover a range of scales and domain shift complexities, including datasets with long-tail distributions (Terra) and synthetic-to-real shifts (VisDA-C).
- **Few-shot settings:** 1, 3, 5, and 10 shots for most datasets; 10, 20, 30, and 50 shots for the larger VisDA-C.
- **Baselines:** State-of-the-art SFUDA methods (SHOT, SHOT++, AaD, CoWA-JMDS, NRC, G-SFDA), other few-shot transfer learning methods (L2-SP, DELTA, BSS, LCCS), and existing SFFSDA methods (FT, LP-FT).
- **Network Architecture:** ResNet-50 backbone for Office31, OfficeHome, and Terra; ResNet-101 for VisDA-C, both ImageNet pre-trained. A two-branch design with two classifiers.
- **Metrics:** Per-class average accuracy for VisDA-C, overall average accuracy for others.
- **Reliability:** Three independently pre-trained source models and a fixed seed for domain adaptation to ensure consistent comparison.

#### 4.2. Comparison with SFUDA Methods

- ACT consistently achieves superior performance compared to SFUDA methods, even with significantly less target data.
- **TerraIncognita:** ACT shows a remarkable advantage, outperforming all SFUDA methods across all few-shot settings (e.g., 52.56% at 1-shot vs. 31.01% for G-SFDA). This highlights ACT's effectiveness in mitigating the long-tail problem and ensuring balanced class representation, a critical issue for Terra's imbalanced label distribution.
- **VisDA-C:** Although ACT requires a 50-shot setting (0.08% of target data) to match or slightly surpass the best SFUDA baseline (SHOT++), it still demonstrates competitive results while using a substantially smaller amount of target data. This underscores its resource efficiency.
- The "No adapt" baseline (source pre-trained model without adaptation) is significantly improved across all datasets by ACT, even at 1-shot/10-shot, validating the efficacy of the adaptation process.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Transfer Learning Methods

- ACT consistently outperforms other established few-shot transfer learning techniques (L2-SP, DELTA, BSS, LCCS) across Office31 and OfficeHome datasets in 1, 3, and 5-shot settings. For example, on Office31 (5-shot), ACT achieves 92.58% vs. L2-SP's 90.20%. This validates ACT's superior ability to leverage limited labeled data for effective domain adaptation.

#### 4.4. Comparison with SFFSDA Methods (FT, LP-FT)

- Against the limited existing SFFSDA methods (Fine-Tuning (FT) and Linear-Probing Fine-Tuning (LP-FT) proposed by Lee et al., 2023), ACT demonstrates superior performance across all four benchmarks and few-shot settings.
- On label-imbalanced datasets like Office31 and Terra, ACT's advantage is even more pronounced, further confirming its robustness to long-tail distributions.
- The study also extends to **partial-set DA (PDA)** and **open-set DA (ODA)** scenarios on OfficeHome (Appendix A), where ACT again outperforms state-of-the-art SFUDA methods. This is attributed to ACT's direct access to target labels for better adaptation to sparse target distributions in PDA, and its use of reverse cross-entropy loss to establish a stronger source-target relationship in ODA, making it robust to unknown classes.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the Office31 1-shot setting confirms the contribution of each component:
- **CDD Distance:** Crucial for classifier matching, its removal led to a 0.07% accuracy drop.
- **Source Model Matching (RCE loss contribution):** Important for retaining source knowledge, its omission caused a 0.21% drop.
- **Strong-Weak Augmentation:** Essential for data diversity, its removal resulted in a 0.39% drop.
- **SAM Optimization:** Significant for training efficacy, its exclusion led to a 0.64% drop.
- **Label-Smooth Cross-Entropy:** Contributes to model robustness, its absence resulted in the largest drop of 2.45%.
This confirms that all proposed components contribute positively to the overall performance of ACT.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Results & Robustness to Few-Shot Sample Quality

t-SNE visualizations (Appendix C) show that ACT significantly improves the discriminative power of the adapted model, leading to better clustering of target data compared to the source pre-trained model. Furthermore, experiments with different random seeds for few-shot sample selection (Appendix E) indicate minimal performance variance (less than 1%), demonstrating ACT's robustness to the quality of the chosen few-shot samples.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Asymmetric Co-Training for Source-Free Few-Shot Domain Adaptation" paper introduces and effectively addresses the novel and highly practical **Source-Free Few-Shot Domain Adaptation (SFFSDA)** setting. This work holds significant importance and potential impact for several reasons:

1. **Bridging the Gap in Domain Adaptation:** SFFSDA directly tackles critical limitations of existing domain adaptation paradigms. While traditional UDA requires constant source data access, and SFUDA demands large volumes of *unlabeled* target data, SFFSDA demonstrates that competitive performance can be achieved with only a *small amount of labeled target data* and a pre-trained source model, without needing the source data itself. This makes domain adaptation much more feasible in real-world applications where data privacy, transmission costs, or collection efforts for large datasets (labeled or unlabeled) are prohibitive.
2. **Enhanced Practicality and Real-World Applicability:** The core insight that "gathering a few labeled target instances is more feasible than acquiring large volumes of unlabeled target data" is highly practical. Industries ranging from healthcare (where data privacy is paramount and labeling by experts is costly but possible for small sets) to industrial inspection (where new defect types emerge but can be sparsely labeled) can benefit immensely from this approach. The ability to adapt models with as few as one sample per class (1-shot learning) is a powerful capability.
3. **Robustness to Data Challenges:** The ACT method demonstrates strong resilience to common challenges in domain adaptation:
 - **Data Scarcity:** Strong-weak augmentation and specific loss functions (like RCE) prevent overfitting, a major concern in few-shot settings.
 - **Label Imbalance/Long-tail Distributions:** ACT shows particular strength on datasets like TerraIncognita, which are known for uneven class distributions, offering a practical solution for real-world imbalanced data.
 - **Specialized DA Scenarios:** Its superior performance in partial-set and open-set DA further extends its utility to complex, evolving label spaces beyond the traditional closed-set assumption.
4. **Methodological Contributions:** The ACT method's integrated approach, combining a two-step optimization, multiple loss functions (label smoothing cross-entropy, entropy minimization, reverse cross-entropy, and classifier determinacy disparity), and an advanced optimizer (SAM), offers a robust framework. The asymmetric co-training using two classifiers, and matching their determinacy, is a novel strategy for improving model confidence and consistency in target-specific adaptation.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** By establishing SFFSDA as a viable and crucial research area, the paper opens avenues for further exploration. The authors themselves suggest investigating "additional regularization techniques" as a direction for future work. This new setting could spur innovations in few-shot learning, domain generalization, and self-supervised learning tailored for scenarios with minimal target supervision.

In conclusion, this research marks a significant step forward in making domain adaptation more accessible, efficient, and applicable across a wider array of real-world contexts. Its emphasis on practical data constraints and robust solutions positions ACT as a valuable contribution to the machine learning community.
