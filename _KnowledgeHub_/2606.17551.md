# Reversal Q-Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Reversal Q-Learning (RQL) introduces an off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm that trains expressive flow policies from static datasets by utilizing a novel flow reversal technique to construct virtual trajectories within an expanded Markov Decision Process. The method mitigates training instability and the curse of horizon, achieving the highest average performance across 50 simulated robotic manipulation and locomotion tasks in the OGBench benchmark.

## Problem

- Training iterative generative models, like flow policies, with reinforcement learning often suffers from unstable backpropagation through time (BPTT) and suboptimal performance.
- Existing offline RL methods for flow policies either rely on computationally intensive regression or struggle to effectively leverage first-order gradient information from value functions.
- Applying the 'expanded MDP' framework to off-policy RL with offline data is challenging due to the lack of intermediate generative trajectories in datasets and the exacerbated 'curse of horizon' from increased effective horizon length.

## Method

- **RQL** employs an ==expanded MDP== framework where each iterative step of a flow policy is treated as a distinct action, circumventing ==BPTT==.
- A ==flow reversal== technique generates virtual, on-policy trajectories for the expanded MDP from standard offline state-action pairs, addressing the data mismatch.
- Unbiased ==multi-step returns==, derived from the deterministic virtual trajectories, effectively reduce the value horizon from T × F to T, mitigating the ==curse of horizon== in off-policy value learning.

## Results

- **RQL** achieved the highest average performance across **50** simulated robotic manipulation and locomotion tasks in the OGBench benchmark suite, outperforming **18** baseline algorithms.
- The algorithm demonstrated particularly strong performance on challenging, long-horizon tasks such as `antmaze-giant` and `humanoidmaze-large`.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the value horizon reduction technique is crucial for high performance, as a variant without it (TFQL) showed significantly inferior results or complete failure on certain tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The reversibility and determinism of flow ODEs can be exploited to synthesize virtual, on-policy trajectories from offline data, enabling the use of expanded MDPs without collecting new samples.
> - Treating intermediate generative steps as separate actions within an expanded MDP, combined with flow reversal, allows for efficient utilization of value function gradients for policy optimization while avoiding BPTT.
> - When intermediate trajectories are deterministic and on-policy, multi-step Q-learning can yield unbiased and zero-variance return estimates, significantly improving value function accuracy in long-horizon settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Reversal Q-Learning" operates within the field of reinforcement learning (RL), specifically addressing challenges in offline reinforcement learning. Offline RL aims to learn optimal policies from pre-collected, static datasets without further interaction with the environment, a crucial paradigm for real-world applications where data collection can be costly or dangerous.

Recent advancements in iterative generative modeling, such as denoising diffusion models and flow matching, have shown potential in offline RL by enabling the modeling of complex behavioral priors from datasets. These models can capture diverse actions and generate expressive policies. However, integrating these iterative generative models with off-policy RL algorithms poses specific challenges.

Previous approaches to training diffusion or flow policies with RL include:
- **Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT):** Direct gradient ascent on a learned value function through the iterative generative process. This method often suffers from training instability and suboptimal performance due, in part, to long gradient chains.
- **Regression-based techniques:** Methods like weighted regression, rejection sampling, and filtering avoid BPTT by using zeroth-order information from the value function. While more stable, these approaches can be computationally intensive or lead to suboptimal performance due to the limited use of value function gradients.
- **Expanded Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework:** This paradigm redefines each iterative refinement step of a generative model as a distinct action within an "expanded" MDP. This approach avoids BPTT and allows the use of standard RL algorithms. While effective for on-policy RL, its application to off-policy settings has been limited. The two main obstacles for off-policy application are the absence of expanded MDP trajectories in standard offline datasets and the exacerbation of the "curse of horizon" due to the significantly increased effective horizon length.

This research aims to overcome these limitations by proposing Reversal Q-Learning (RQL), an off-policy RL algorithm. RQL builds upon the expanded MDP framework but introduces novel techniques to adapt it for offline data and to mitigate the curse of horizon, thus offering an alternative to existing methods that struggle with BPTT or inefficient use of value function information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust and efficient off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm for training expressive flow policies using pre-existing offline datasets. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current methods for combining iterative generative models with RL:

1. **Addressing Training Instability from Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT):** Many iterative generative models, such as flow policies, generate actions over multiple sequential steps. When attempting to optimize these policies using reinforcement learning, directly backpropagating gradients through the entire sequence of iterative steps can lead to BPTT. This typically results in unstable training dynamics and often yields suboptimal policy performance, making it a significant barrier to effectively utilizing the expressive power of these models in RL.
2. **Improving the Utilization of Value Function Information:** Alternative approaches to BPTT, such as regression-based methods, typically avoid gradient propagation by relying on zeroth-order information from the learned value function. While this enhances training stability, it can limit the efficiency and quality of policy updates, potentially leading to suboptimal policies or requiring excessive computational resources. A key motivation is to leverage the richer first-order (gradient) information from the value function to achieve more effective policy optimization.
3. **Adapting the Expanded MDP Framework for Offline Data:** The "expanded MDP" framework, which treats individual iterative generative steps as discrete actions in an augmented MDP, has shown promise in on-policy RL settings. However, its direct application to off-policy RL using offline datasets presents two major challenges:
 - **Data Mismatch:** Standard offline datasets consist of state-action pairs from the original environment, lacking the detailed intermediate generative trajectories required by the expanded MDP. This makes it challenging to construct suitable training data for the expanded setting from existing offline sources.
 - **Curse of Horizon:** Expanding the MDP horizon by a factor `F` (the number of generative steps) significantly lengthens the effective horizon for value learning. In off-policy RL, this exacerbates the "curse of horizon," where biases in Temporal Difference (TD) targets accumulate over longer horizons, leading to inaccurate value estimates and hindering learning.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of RQL are:
- To enable the application of the expanded MDP framework to off-policy reinforcement learning with prior data.
- To develop a mechanism, "flow reversal," for generating virtual, on-policy trajectories within the expanded MDP using existing offline state-action data.
- To implement a bias-and-variance reduction technique using multi-step returns to mitigate the extended horizon issue in the expanded MDP, thereby enhancing the accuracy of off-policy value function learning.
- To achieve robust and high-performing flow policies without incurring BPTT issues.
- To empirically demonstrate that RQL surpasses the performance of current state-of-the-art flow-based offline RL algorithms across a diverse set of challenging robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Reversal Q-Learning (RQL) algorithm is developed to enable off-policy reinforcement learning with flow policies within the expanded Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework, while addressing specific challenges related to data availability and the effective horizon.

**Flow Policies and Expanded MDPs:**
The work utilizes flow policies, which are generative models that define action distributions through a time-dependent velocity field `v(s, x, f)`. This field induces a flow `ψ(s, x, f)` that transforms a prior distribution (e.g., standard normal) into the desired action distribution over `F` Euler integration steps. The flow policy is initially trained via a flow-matching loss to model behavioral action distributions from the offline dataset `D`.

The core of the approach relies on the "expanded MDP" framework. In this framework, each individual Euler integration step of the flow policy is treated as a separate action. This augments the state space to `eS = S × R^d × {0..., F-1}`, comprising the environment state `s`, a partially generated action `x`, and the discrete flow time `f`. Transitions in this expanded MDP involve iteratively updating `x` and `f` for `F-1` steps with zero reward, and then sampling the next environment state `s'` and a new initial `x'` from a prior distribution (and resetting `f` to 0) when `f` reaches `F-1`, providing the actual environment reward `r(s, x+a)`. The discount factor `eγ` is 1 for intermediate flow steps and `γ` for the final step of a flow trajectory.

**Addressing Challenges with Flow Reversal and Horizon Reduction:**
Two primary challenges hinder the direct application of the expanded MDP for off-policy RL with offline data:
1. **Lack of Expanded MDP Trajectories in Offline Data:** Standard offline datasets `D` only contain `(s, a, r, s')` tuples from the original MDP, not the intermediate flow integration steps.
2. **Exacerbated Curse of Horizon:** The expanded MDP increases the effective horizon length by a factor of `F`, which typically complicates off-policy value estimation due to the accumulation of biases in Temporal Difference (TD) targets.

RQL addresses these challenges using two key techniques:

1. **Generating Virtual Trajectories via Flow Reversal:** For each `(s, a, r, s')` transition available in the offline dataset `D`, RQL generates a "virtual" flow trajectory in the expanded MDP that is consistent with the *current* flow policy `v`. This is achieved by exploiting the reversibility and determinism of flow ODEs. The reverse flow `θ(s, x, f)` is computed by integrating the negative of the current velocity field: `d/df θ(s, x, f) = -v(s, θ(s, x, f), f)`. In practice, starting from `x_F = a` (the action observed in the dataset), the Euler method is applied in reverse: `x_{f-1} ← x_f - v(s, x_f, f)`. This process reconstructs the intermediate `x_f` values, yielding a sequence of transitions `eτ` for the expanded MDP, where the actions `a_f = x_{f+1} - x_f` are deterministic and on-policy with respect to the current `v`. This virtual dataset `eD` is recomputed for each batch during training.

2. **Reducing the Effective Horizon with Unbiased Multi-step Returns:** With the virtual trajectories `eD` available, a multi-step Q-learning objective is employed to reduce the effective horizon. Instead of performing `T × F` individual Bellman updates, RQL's value function learning directly calculates the TD target at the end of each flow trajectory. The value loss is defined as:
 `L(Q) = E_{eτ} [ Q((s, x_f, f), a_f) - (r + γ max_{a'} Q((s', x'_0, 0), a')) ]^2`
 Crucially, because the intermediate flow trajectories are deterministic and generated on-policy (with respect to the current `v`), these multi-step returns are *unbiased* and have *zero variance*. This effectively reduces the "value horizon" from `T × F` to `T` on average, significantly mitigating the curse of horizon.

**Practical Algorithm (RQL) Implementation:**

- **Value Learning:** RQL employs an Implicit Value Learning (IVL)-like approach using expectile regression to train a value function `V(s, x, f)`. This avoids explicitly computing `max_{a'}` over potentially out-of-distribution actions, a common issue in offline RL. The loss is:
 `L(V) = E_{eτ} [ ℓ_{κ}^2 ( V(s, x_f, f) - (r + γV(s', x'_0, 0)) ) ]`
 where `ℓ_{κ}^2(x) = |κ - I(x > 0)|x^2` is the expectile loss, and `κ` is the expectile, controlling the degree of optimism/pessimism.
- **Policy Learning:** The flow policy's velocity field `v` is trained to maximize the learned value function while being regularized towards the behavioral policy in the dataset. The policy loss is:
 `L(v) = -E_{eτ} [V(s, x_f + v(s, x_f, f), f+1)] + α L_{BC}(v)`
 The first term guides `v` to maximize the value, directly utilizing gradient information from `V`. The second term, `L_{BC}(v)`, is the flow-matching behavioral cloning loss, ensuring the policy remains close to the data distribution, which also helps stabilize the reverse flow computation. `α` is a hyperparameter balancing these two objectives.
- **Additional Implementation Details:** Action chunking is applied to process sequences of actions. An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the policy network is used for evaluation. A pessimistic critic backup, computed from an ensemble of value functions with a pessimism coefficient `ρ`, is incorporated into the TD targets to address out-of-distribution actions.

This methodology constructs an off-policy RL algorithm that avoids BPTT, efficiently leverages value gradients, and addresses the challenge of increased horizon in expanded MDPs by producing unbiased, low-variance multi-step returns through flow reversal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of Reversal Q-Learning (RQL) was conducted across 50 simulated robotic manipulation and locomotion tasks from the OGBench benchmark suite. The tasks included varying levels of difficulty and encompassed areas such as object manipulation (scene, puzzle, cube) and long-horizon locomotion (antmaze, humanoidmaze). The datasets used included large-scale 100M-sized datasets for some tasks and standard play/navigate datasets for others, all composed of task-agnostic trajectories. RQL was compared against 18 baseline algorithms, including a Gaussian policy baseline (ReBRAC) and a diverse set of flow-based off-policy RL methods spanning distillation, rejection sampling, weighted regression, backpropagation through time, Q-gradient-based methods, latent noise steering, residual editing, adjoint matching, and expanded MDP construction (BDPO). All experiments were averaged over four seeds, with 95% confidence intervals reported.

**Overall Performance:**
- RQL achieved the highest average performance across the 50 simulated robotic tasks.
- The algorithm demonstrated strong performance particularly on challenging, long-horizon tasks, including `antmaze-giant`, `humanoidmaze-large`, `puzzle-4x4`, and `cube-quadruple`.
- RQL's expanded MDP-based framework empirically surpassed the performance of several standard flow policy training paradigms, such as those relying on backpropagation through time (FBRAC), rejection sampling (IFQL), and weighted regression (FAWAC).

**Impact of Value Horizon Reduction:**
- To evaluate the contribution of the proposed value horizon reduction technique, RQL was compared against TFQL, a variant that uses the vanilla expanded MDP framework without the multi-step return mechanism.
- TFQL, which retains an effective horizon length of `T × F`, exhibited significantly inferior performance compared to RQL, including complete failure on certain benchmark tasks. This result indicates that the horizon reduction technique, which yields unbiased and zero-variance multi-step returns, is a crucial component for achieving high performance in this framework.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies were conducted on five representative tasks to assess the impact of two key hyperparameters:

- **Expectile κ:** The expectile `κ` in the expectile loss function (Equation 14) controls the degree of optimism in value estimation. The results showed that higher `κ` values (e.g., 0.7 or 0.9, representing "more RL" or a more optimistic Q-learning objective) generally led to improved performance across tasks. This suggests that for the OGBench datasets, which are often suboptimal, a stronger emphasis on maximizing returns is beneficial.
- **BC Coefficient α:** The behavioral cloning (BC) regularization coefficient `α` in the policy loss (Equation 15) balances value maximization with adherence to the behavioral prior from the dataset. The ablation study demonstrated that `α` is an influential hyperparameter, with performance variations observed across different values, indicating the importance of proper tuning for optimal results.

In summary, the results indicate that RQL provides an effective approach for offline RL with flow policies, largely due to its method of synthesizing virtual trajectories and its technique for mitigating the curse of horizon.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Reversal Q-Learning (RQL) algorithm represents a significant contribution to the field of offline reinforcement learning, particularly in its integration of iterative generative models like flow policies.

**Technical Significance:**
- **Resolution of BPTT and Value Gradient Utilization:** RQL offers a solution to the instability and suboptimal performance issues associated with backpropagation through time (BPTT) in training iterative policies. By treating individual flow steps as distinct actions within an expanded MDP, the algorithm circumvents BPTT. Furthermore, unlike regression-based methods that rely on zeroth-order value information, RQL effectively leverages first-order (gradient) information from the learned value function for policy optimization, which is typically more efficient and powerful.
- **Enabling Expanded MDPs for Offline RL:** The work successfully adapts the expanded MDP framework, previously primarily used in on-policy settings, for sample-efficient off-policy reinforcement learning with offline data. This adaptation is made possible through the novel "flow reversal" technique, which synthesizes virtual, on-policy trajectories compatible with the expanded MDP from standard offline datasets.
- **Mitigation of the Curse of Horizon:** A crucial technical advancement is the application of a bias-and-variance reduction technique for multi-step returns. By generating deterministic and on-policy virtual trajectories through flow reversal, RQL can apply multi-step Q-learning that yields unbiased and zero-variance return estimates, effectively reducing the computational horizon from `T × F` to `T`. This directly addresses a major challenge in off-policy value learning with expanded state spaces.

**Empirical Contributions:**
- The extensive experimental evaluation across 50 challenging simulated robotic tasks demonstrates RQL's high empirical performance. The algorithm consistently achieved the highest average performance compared to numerous state-of-the-art flow-based offline RL baselines, particularly excelling in complex, long-horizon manipulation and locomotion environments. This empirical validation underscores the practical utility and robustness of the proposed methodology.

**Potential Impact and Future Directions:**
- **Advancement in Generative Models for Control:** RQL's success in effectively training expressive flow policies with RL provides a more robust framework for leveraging advanced generative models in sequential decision-making. This could lead to the development of more sophisticated and adaptable robotic behaviors from offline datasets.
- **Broader Applicability:** The core ideas of RQL, based on the reversibility and determinism of iterative generative models, are not inherently limited to robotic control. The framework's generality suggests potential for application in other domains that utilize iterative generative processes, such as the fine-tuning of image generation models, sequence modeling, or other modalities. Exploring these applications is identified as a promising direction for future research.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness:** The study acknowledges that RQL's performance can be sensitive to hyperparameters like the behavioral cloning coefficient `α` and the expectile `κ`. Future work could focus on developing more adaptive or robust methods for setting these hyperparameters, potentially through meta-learning or automated tuning strategies, to reduce the need for extensive manual sweeps.
