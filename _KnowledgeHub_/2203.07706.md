# ActFormer: A GAN-based Transformer towards General Action-Conditioned 3D Human Motion Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> ActFormer is a GAN-trained Transformer for action-conditioned 3D human motion generation. A Gaussian Process latent prior supplies temporal correlation, stacked Interaction- and Temporal-Transformer encoders model person interactions and time, and a graph-convolution discriminator judges realism. It outperforms prior methods on NTU-13, NTU RGB+D 120, BABEL, and a newly contributed synthetic multi-person combat dataset, on both single-person and multi-person motion generation.

## Problem

- Autoregressive frame-by-frame recurrent generators suffer the mean-pose problem, collapsing to a static average posture after only a few generated frames.
- Sequence-level samplers trade off badly: convolutional generators impose heavy inductive bias, while a single sequence-level latent vector carries no explicit temporal correlation.
- Prior action-conditioned generators handle one person only, with no interaction mechanism, and no public dataset annotates action categories for two-to-five-person interactive motions.

## Method

- **ActFormer** generator encodes per-frame motion as ==Transformer== tokens with a class-embedding token and learnable positional encodings, decoded by output projection and trained adversarially with a ==graph-convolution discriminator==.
- ==Gaussian Process latent prior== samples a length-T latent sequence whose per-channel length-scales span many time scales, composing temporal correlations rather than using one static latent vector.
- Multi-person extension alternates ==I-Former== (self-attention across persons within a frame) with ==T-Former== (across frames per person), sharing one latent sequence plus factorized temporal and person positional encodings.

## Results

- Single-person: **99.9** recognition accuracy and **1.11** whole-FID on NTU-13, and **42.01** accuracy with **12.53** mean-FID on NTU-1P, beating ACTOR and CSGN.
- Multi-person NTU-2P reaches **69.65** accuracy and **3.77** mean-FID versus ACTOR's **63.04** and **13.56**; GTA Combat five-person aggregated FID falls from CSGN's **4.02** to **2.23**.
- Ablations: swapping the Gaussian Process prior for a Gaussian prior costs **4.72** mean-FID, and dropping explicit interaction modeling collapses to **7.62** aggregated FID at five persons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A latent prior carrying built-in temporal correlation can supply sequence structure that the decoder architecture would otherwise have to impose.
> - Explicit skeleton graph convolution in the generator is unnecessary once motion is tokenized; attention learns joint topology implicitly, as the ablation confirms.
> - Factorizing attention into per-frame interaction and per-person temporal passes scales to more people, where pooling or concatenation aggregation degrades as interactions grow sparse.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the task of action-conditioned 3D human motion generation, where the objective is to synthesize a sequence of 3D human motions based on a given semantic action label. This field is relevant to applications such as character animation, humanoid robot interaction, and data synthesis for computer vision tasks.

Historically, much of the research in human motion synthesis has focused on motion prediction, which involves generating future motions from previous motion frames. More recently, efforts have shifted towards generating motions from scratch, conditioned on action labels. However, existing works in this specific area present several limitations that this research aims to address:

- **Dataset Scale and Complexity**: Previous motion generation methods were often trained on relatively small-scale datasets, such as NTU-13 and HumanAct12, which typically covered a limited number of action categories (around 10). These datasets exhibit simpler data distributions. Larger and more complex datasets, like NTU RGB+D 120 and BABEL, which contain over 100 action categories, have not been fully leveraged for action-conditioned motion generation.
- **Motion Representation Adaptability**: Many prior works tended to perform optimally with motion data represented by SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear) pose parameters but showed reduced performance when handling other representations, such as skeleton joint coordinates. A solution capable of adapting to various motion representations is thus desired.
- **Scope of Actions**: Existing methods primarily focused on single-person motion generation, neglecting multi-person interactive actions, which constitute a significant portion of human activities.
- **Temporal Coherence**: Challenges exist in generating long, realistic, and diverse motion sequences. Auto-regressive models, which assume a Markovian dependency, can suffer from the "mean-pose" problem, where the generated motion converges to an average pose. Approaches that use sequence-level latent priors, like Gaussian Processes with convolutions (CSGN) or a single vector with positional encodings (ACTOR), have been proposed but are considered sub-optimal in balancing inductive bias and representation capacity.
- **Multi-Person Interaction Modeling**: While human interactions have been explored in motion prediction algorithms using pooling or self-attention mechanisms, they had not been systematically addressed in the motion generation task.
- **Data Availability**: There is a scarcity of large-scale motion capture (MoCap) datasets featuring fine-grained action labels, particularly for multi-person interactive actions.

The "ActFormer" work positions itself as a step towards a more general human motion generator by addressing these limitations through a novel combination of generative adversarial networks (GANs), Transformer architectures, and Gaussian Process latent priors, while also extending its capabilities to multi-person scenarios and introducing a new dataset.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general action-conditioned 3D human motion generator capable of synthesizing realistic and diverse human motions based on semantic action labels. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives, each motivated by the identified shortcomings of previous approaches:

1. **Enhance Realism and Diversity in Motion Generation**: The work aims to overcome issues like the "mean-pose" problem, common in auto-regressive models, by leveraging a sequence-level latent prior that inherently captures temporal correlations. The integration of a Transformer architecture is intended to exploit its capacity for modeling non-local correlations, contributing to more realistic and diverse long motion sequences.
2. **Increase Adaptability to Various Motion Representations**: A key motivation is to create a generation framework that can adapt to different human motion data formats, including SMPL pose parameters and skeleton joint coordinates. This flexibility would broaden the applicability of the generator across different datasets and use cases.
3. **Extend to Multi-Person Interactive Actions**: Recognizing that daily human behaviors frequently involve interactions between multiple individuals, a significant objective is to enable the generation of multi-person interactive motions. This extends the scope of motion generation beyond single-person actions, a domain largely unexplored in prior generative tasks.
4. **Leverage Larger and More Complex Datasets**: The researchers are motivated to evaluate and demonstrate the capability of their model on larger-scale datasets with a greater number of action categories and more complex data distributions (e.g., NTU RGB+D 120, BABEL), rather than being limited to smaller, simpler benchmarks.
5. **Address the Data Scarcity for Multi-Person Motions**: To facilitate research in multi-person motion generation, a specific motivation was to construct and contribute a new synthetic dataset. This dataset, GTA Combat, focuses on complex multi-person combat behaviors, which are underrepresented in existing MoCap collections.

The broader motivation stems from the utility of robust human motion synthesis in various fields. For character animation, it could streamline the creation of diverse and natural movements. In humanoid robotics, it could provide more sophisticated control policies for human-robot interaction. For computer vision, it offers a means to generate synthetic data for training models related to human action understanding, especially in complex multi-person scenarios. The authors note that prior works fall short of covering a complete domain of human motions, motivating a comprehensive solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, ActFormer, is a GAN-based Transformer framework designed for action-conditioned 3D human motion generation. It is equipped with a Gaussian Process (GP) latent prior and is extended to handle both single-person and multi-person interactive actions.

#### 3.1. Single-person Motion Generation

The generation process begins by sampling a random seed from a latent prior.
- **Gaussian Process Latent Prior**: To enforce temporal correlations and generate realistic long motion sequences, the method utilizes a Gaussian Process (GP) as its latent prior. A `(T, C0)` latent vector sequence `z` is sampled for each generation, where `T` is the target motion sequence length and `C0` is the channel dimension. Each of the `C0` channels is independently sampled from a GP, with characteristic length-scales spanning a spectrum of values. This design aims to model correlations at various time scales within the latent sequence.
- **ActFormer Generator Architecture**: The generator employs a Transformer architecture.
 - The `(T, C0)` latent vector sequence `z` is treated as `T` tokens and passed through an MLP layer for input embedding.
 - A class token embedding, representing the semantic action label `a`, is appended to these `T` tokens, resulting in `T+1` tokens.
 - Learnable positional encodings (PE) are added to these input tokens to preserve positional information, which is crucial in permutation-invariant Transformer encoders.
 - These tokens then pass through `L` layers of a Temporal-transFormer (T-Former) module. Each T-Former layer models temporal correlations among the various time steps (tokens) using a self-attention mechanism.
 - Finally, the class token is discarded, and each of the remaining `T` tokens is projected by an output layer into a `C`-dimensional vector. This vector represents a person's root translation and local body poses at a specific time step.

#### 3.2. Multi-person Motion Generation

The ActFormer framework is extended to the multi-person setting by adapting its architecture to handle an additional person-wise dimension `P`.
- **Shared Latent Prior for Synchronization**: To enforce synchronization among multiple persons in a group, a single latent vector sequence `z` from the GP prior is shared. The `T` input embedding tokens and the class token are duplicated `P` times to generate the input for all persons.
- **Positional Encoding for Person Distinction**: To distinguish between different persons, learnable positional encodings are again employed. Instead of a single 1D PE, a 2D combination of `T+1` Temporal Positional Encodings (TPE) and `P` Person-wise Positional Encodings (PPE) is learned. For a token indexed `(t, p)`, its PE is `concat(TPE(t), PPE(p))`. This 2D combination is reported to yield better results than completely independent PEs for each token.
- **Generator Architecture with Interaction Modeling**: The generator's `L` stacked layers are modified to alternately model human interactions and temporal correlations. Each layer consists of:
 - **Interaction-transFormer (I-Former)**: This module models interactions among different persons at each time step independently, using self-attention across the person dimension for a fixed time step.
 - **Temporal-transFormer (T-Former)**: Following the I-Former, this module models temporal correlations for each person independently, using self-attention across the time dimension for a fixed person.
 - After `L` such alternating layers, all class tokens are discarded, and the remaining `P * T` tokens are projected into the final `(P, T, C)` output, where `C` represents the motion of a specific person at a particular time step.

#### 3.3. Generative Adversarial Training

The ActFormer is trained using a conditional Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework, specifically with Conditional Wasserstein GAN loss functions.
- **Generator (G)**: The ActFormer generator synthesizes human motion sequences `G(z, a)` conditioned on `z` and `a`.
- **Discriminator (D)**: An ST-GCN (Spatial Temporal Graph Convolutional Network) is used as the discriminator. It receives human motion sequences and action labels as input, aiming to distinguish generated motions from real ones corresponding to the specified action.
 - For a single person, a `C`-dimensional motion vector at each time step is decomposed into a `(K, D)` part-wise representation (e.g., K nodes for skeleton joints, D dimensions per joint). This forms a `(T, K, D)` spatial-temporal graph.
 - For multi-person motions, the part-wise motions of multiple persons are concatenated along the `D` dimension, transforming a `(P, T, C)` sequence into a `(T, K, P * D)` graph.
 - The semantic condition `a` is integrated into the discriminator by projection.
- **Permutation Invariance for Multi-person Discriminator**: Since the concatenation operation for multi-person motions is not permutation-invariant, a data augmentation strategy is employed. During training, for each real multi-person sample, the persons within the sequence are randomly permuted in every iteration. This encourages the ActFormer to learn a distribution that accounts for various person orderings. This method is reported to be more robust than embedding symmetric functions directly into the discriminator.

#### 3.4. GTA Combat Dataset

To address the scarcity of multi-person interactive MoCap data, the authors constructed a new synthetic dataset called GTA Combat.
- **Collection Method**: Data was collected using the Grand Theft Auto V (GTA-V) gaming engine. Combat behaviors involving 2 to 5 persons were procedurally generated in real-time by combining over 10 atomic actions with variations based on combat arms and ragdoll physics-triggered reactions from attacked individuals.
- **Content**: The dataset contains approximately 7,000 motion sequences of complex combat behavior, which represents one of the most intricate types of human interaction.
- **Processing**: The JTA toolkit was extended to extract 3D human skeletons from the game.
- **Splits**: The dataset is divided into four splits based on the number of persons: 2P (~2.3K sequences), 3P (~1.9K sequences), 4P (~1.5K sequences), and 5P (~1.2K sequences).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ActFormer involved extensive experiments on both single-person and multi-person motion generation tasks across multiple datasets, including NTU-13, NTU RGB+D 120 (NTU-1P, NTURecon-1P, NTU-2P), BABEL (BABEL-120), and the newly introduced GTA Combat dataset.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics

Performance was quantitatively assessed using:
- **Action Recognition Accuracy (Acc.↑)**: A higher accuracy indicates better alignment with action labels.
- **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID↓)**: A lower FID score indicates higher realism and diversity. Two variants were used:
 - **Whole FID (FIDw)**: Calculated on statistics of the entire real/generated sample set.
 - **Mean FID (FIDm)**: Independently measured for each action category and then averaged, considered more suitable for action-conditional generation.
- **Aggregated FID (FIDa)**: For multi-person settings, this variant aggregated single-person features (via channel-wise max pooling) before FID calculation, offering an alternative perspective to whole-group FID (calculated on concatenated multi-person features).

#### 4.2. Comparison to State-of-the-Arts

ActFormer was compared against Action2Motion, ACTOR, and CSGN. Baselines were adapted or extended to conditional and multi-person settings where necessary.

- **Single-person Motion Generation (Table 1)**:
 - ActFormer demonstrated superior performance across all datasets (NTU-13, NTURecon-1P, NTU-1P, BABEL) in terms of higher accuracy and lower FID scores.
 - Action2Motion, which uses a frame-level VAE with GRU architecture, generally lagged behind, particularly on the NTU-1P dataset.
 - CSGN, originally designed for skeleton data, showed performance degradation when applied to SMPL-based data (NTURecon-1P).
 - ACTOR, a Transformer-based VAE, achieved strong results on NTU-13 but exhibited significant performance drops on larger and more challenging datasets (NTURecon-1P, NTU-1P, BABEL), especially when considering root translation in the evaluation metrics.
 - ActFormer exhibited strong adaptability to various motion representations and was the only method that showed workable performance on the challenging, long-tailed BABEL dataset.

- **Multi-person Motion Generation (Table 2)**:
 - On the NTU-2P dataset, ActFormer showed more significant advantages, outperforming all adapted baseline methods, which lacked specific designs for modeling human interactions.
 - On the GTA Combat dataset (involving 2 to 5 persons), ActFormer consistently outperformed CSGN across all person counts (2P, 3P, 4P, 5P). While performance for all methods decreased with an increasing number of persons due to increased complexity, ActFormer maintained a more gradual decline.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

A series of ablation experiments were conducted to evaluate the contributions of different components of the ActFormer framework.

- **Latent Prior (Table 3)**: Replacing the Gaussian Process latent prior with a simple Gaussian prior resulted in an increase in FIDm and FIDw scores on NTU-1P, indicating the importance of GP for capturing inherent temporal correlations.
- **Architecture Design (Table 3)**: An alternative design in the generator, explicitly modeling skeleton topology with Graph Convolution while keeping T-Former for temporal correlations, lagged behind the ActFormer's primary design. This suggests that the Transformer architecture effectively handles motion encoding without requiring explicit graph convolutions for skeleton topology within the generator.
- **Positional Encoding (Table 3 & 4)**: Learnable positional encoding (PE) proved more effective than fixed PE. For multi-person scenarios, a 2D combination of temporal and person-wise learnable PEs was superior to completely independent learnable PEs for each token, highlighting a trade-off between inductive bias and representation capacity.
- **Discriminator Design for Multi-person (Table 4)**: The proposed method of concatenating multi-person motions and applying random person-wise permutations as data augmentation during training (to compensate for permutation non-invariance) outperformed alternative discriminator designs that embedded permutation invariance with symmetric functions (e.g., AvgPool, MaxPool, Self-Attention pooling). The latter approaches often led to GAN training instability.
- **Interaction Encoding for Multi-person (Table 5)**: Both sharing the same latent vector sequence among multiple persons for synchronization and explicitly modeling human interactions with the I-Former were found to be indispensable.
 - Sampling independent latent vector sequences for different persons significantly degraded performance.
 - Modeling interactions solely by concatenation of multi-person motions (without the I-Former) also performed worse, especially collapsing for 5-person scenarios in GTA Combat, suggesting that the data-dependent self-attention in I-Former is crucial for handling sparse correlations in complex interactions.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Visualizations of generated motions provided further insights into the ActFormer's capabilities.
- **Single-person Motions**: ActFormer generated diverse motions for single-person actions from BABEL. For instance, in "Stretch," different body parts were stretched in various samples, and for "Dance," a range of dancing styles were presented.
- **Multi-person Motions**: Generated multi-person motions from NTU-2P and GTA Combat demonstrated natural synchronization. Examples included synchronized toasting and drinking in "Cheers and drink," coordinated posing in "Take a photo," and coupled stumbling in "Support somebody." In combat scenarios from GTA Combat, realistic attack and reaction sequences were observed, with participants maintaining fight postures even without direct contact.
- **Failure Cases**: A limitation was observed in complex multi-person combat scenarios with 4 or 5 persons. When interactions became sparse or persons formed separate groups (e.g., some falling without being attacked), ActFormer sometimes struggled, indicating a lack of mechanism to dynamically divide persons into distinct interaction groups.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents ActFormer as a notable advancement in action-conditioned 3D human motion generation, offering contributions across several key aspects:

- **Versatile and High-Quality Motion Generation**: ActFormer demonstrates leading performance on single-person motion generation tasks, exhibiting strong adaptability to various human motion representations (SMPL parameters, skeleton joint coordinates). This versatility is significant for broader application across diverse datasets and scenarios.
- **Pioneering Multi-Person Interaction Synthesis**: The research marks an early and substantial step in solving the multi-person motion generation problem. By effectively modeling temporal correlations and human interactions through its Transformer architecture and shared latent prior, ActFormer can synthesize realistic and synchronized multi-person behaviors, a domain largely unaddressed by previous generative models. This capability has the potential to transform character animation, interactive virtual environments, and the development of social robots.
- **New Benchmark Dataset**: The introduction of the GTA Combat dataset provides a valuable resource for the research community. This synthetic dataset, rich in complex multi-person interactive motions (specifically combat behaviors), addresses a critical data scarcity and offers a challenging benchmark for future work in multi-person motion understanding and generation.

The potential impact of ActFormer is considerable. It could significantly improve the efficiency and realism of creating animated content for entertainment, gaming, and virtual reality. In robotics, it could inform the design of more sophisticated and natural human-robot interaction behaviors. For computer vision, the ability to generate diverse and realistic synthetic human motion data, including complex interactions, can serve as a powerful tool for training and evaluating models for human pose estimation, action recognition, and human behavior analysis.

However, the authors also discuss limitations. The current ActFormer lacks a mechanism to dynamically segment persons into different interaction groups in highly complex scenarios with sparse interactions. Additionally, while the ActFormer generator is Transformer-based, the current GAN training setup, particularly the discriminator's concatenation-based input, prevents the learning of a shared model for a variable number of persons. Future work directions include exploring human-object interaction synthesis, which remains an open challenge.

The authors also include a statement on broader impacts, acknowledging the potential for malicious use of generative methods for creating non-existing content and emphasizing that the GTA Combat dataset, while containing fighting scenes, is intended solely for research on modeling multi-person interactive actions and not for promoting violence.
