# NIL: No-data Imitation Learning by Leveraging Pre-trained Video Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ETH Zürich and MPI-IS researchers develop NIL (No-data Imitation Learning), a framework that enables robots to learn complex motor skills using only generated videos from pre-trained video diffusion models, achieving comparable performance to methods trained on real motion capture data while eliminating the need for expert demonstrations.

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning requires expensive, high-quality expert demonstrations which are often unavailable, especially for non-humanoid robots
- Manually designing reward functions for reinforcement learning is time-consuming and can lead to unintended behaviors
- Existing skill-learning methods typically require morphology-specific data or retraining from scratch, limiting their ability to generalize across diverse robot body configurations.

## Method

- Uses pre-trained ==video diffusion models== to generate reference videos of desired skills from text prompts
- Develops a hybrid reward function combining video similarity, segmentation mask overlap, and physics-based regularization terms
- Trains policies using entropy-regularized reinforcement learning to match generated demonstrations

## Results

- Achieves locomotion skill learning for both humanoid and quadruped robots without any real demonstration data
- Matches or exceeds performance of methods trained on motion capture data in comparative evaluations
- Performance improves with better video diffusion models while maintaining zero requirement for expert demonstrations, successfully bridging 2D generated videos to 3D physical control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generated videos don't need perfect physical accuracy since the simulator refines motions to be physically valid
> - Combining visual and mask-based similarity metrics with regularization produces smooth, natural movements
> - The approach generalizes across different robot morphologies without requiring morphology-specific data

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several active areas within machine learning and robotics:

- **Imitation Learning (IL):** NIL addresses a key limitation of traditional IL, namely, the reliance on high-quality expert demonstrations. By generating demonstrations using video diffusion models, NIL circumvents the need for costly and often unavailable motion capture data, particularly for non-humanoid morphologies.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While RL is another popular method for training robots, it often requires extensive reward engineering, which can be time-consuming and lead to unintended behaviors. NIL reduces the reliance on hand-crafted reward functions by deriving reward signals from video comparisons.
- **Video Diffusion Models:** This work leverages recent advances in video diffusion models, which have demonstrated impressive capabilities in generating realistic video sequences. NIL explores a novel application of these models for robot learning, addressing the challenge of transferring knowledge from 2D generated videos to 3D robot control.
- **Video Understanding and Representation Learning:** NIL utilizes video encoders, specifically vision transformers, to extract meaningful features from both generated and simulated videos. This aligns with the growing body of research on learning robust video representations for downstream tasks.
- **Learning from Generated Data:** The research contributes to the growing trend of using generated or weakly supervised data for policy learning, reducing the dependence on curated expert demonstrations. However, NIL distinguishes itself by not requiring *any* curated data, a significant advancement in this area.

NIL directly addresses the limitations of existing approaches by providing a data-independent framework for skill acquisition. It's a novel approach that fills a critical gap in the field, particularly for scenarios where obtaining real-world data is impractical or impossible.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper's primary objective is to develop a method for learning physically plausible motor skills for robots and simulated characters without relying on manually collected expert data. The key motivations behind this work are:

- **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** Obtaining high-quality 3D motion capture data is expensive and challenging, especially for unconventional and non-humanoid robots and animals.
- **Reducing Reward Engineering Effort:** Traditional RL methods require significant manual effort in designing reward functions, which can be task- and body-specific.
- **Generalization to Diverse Morphologies:** The goal is to create a method that can easily adapt to different robot designs and body configurations without requiring retraining from scratch.
- **Bridging the Gap Between 2D Video and 3D Control:** Leveraging the power of video diffusion models to generate visual demonstrations and then translate this information into control policies for 3D agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NIL's methodology involves a two-stage process:

- **Stage 1: Video Generation:** A pre-trained video diffusion model (D) is used to generate a reference video (F) of the agent performing a desired skill. The diffusion model is conditioned on an initial frame (e0) of the agent in its environment and a textual prompt (p) describing the task ("The [robot name] agent is [skill], camera follows.").
- **Stage 2: Policy Learning:** A reinforcement learning agent is trained in a physical simulator to imitate the generated video. This is achieved by:
 - **Video Encoding:** A pre-trained TimeSformer is used to embed both the generated video (F) and a rendered video (E) of the agent in simulation into a latent space.
 - **Segmentation and Masking:** The agent is segmented out of the generated and simulated videos using SAM and simulator masks, respectively.
 - **Reward Function:** A reward function (R) is defined as a weighted sum of:
 - **Video Similarity (Sv):** The negative Euclidean distance between the embedded representations of the generated and simulated videos.
 - **Image-Based Similarity (SM):** The Intersection over Union (IoU) between the segmentation masks of the generated and simulated videos.
 - **Regularization (P):** A set of regularization terms that encourage smooth and physically plausible behavior. This includes penalties for joint torques, large action differences, angular joint velocities, foot movement during ground contact, and excessive torso tilting.
 - **Policy Optimization:** The agent's policy (π) is optimized using entropy-regularized off-policy actor-critic reinforcement learning (BRO).

**Key aspects of the approach:**

- **No Curated Data:** NIL does not require any manually collected expert demonstrations. The reference videos are generated autonomously.
- **Discriminator-Free Imitation:** The approach avoids the use of a discriminator, which can be unstable and prone to overfitting. Instead, it relies on direct comparison of video embeddings and segmentation masks.
- **Hybrid Reward Signal:** The combination of video similarity, image-based similarity, and regularization provides a robust and informative reward signal for learning.
- **Temporal Alignment:** A mapping is defined between simulation timesteps and video frames to align temporal dynamics between the simulation and the generated video.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several key findings based on experimental evaluations:

- **Effectiveness of NIL:** NIL is capable of learning locomotion skills for diverse robotic platforms (humanoids and quadrupeds) without relying on any curated data.
- **Importance of Reward Components:** Ablation studies demonstrate that all reward components (video similarity, image-based similarity, and regularization) contribute to the learning performance. Regularization helps to smoothen the learned motions, while both image-based and video-based similarity scores help the agent to understand the essentials of walking.
- **Impact of Diffusion Model Quality:** The performance of NIL is closely tied to the quality of the video diffusion model used to generate reference videos. Visually plausible generations are more important than perfect physical plausibility, as NIL refines motions within the simulator. Newer and higher-quality diffusion models lead to more natural gaits and smoother motion patterns.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** NIL achieves comparable or superior performance to state-of-the-art imitation learning methods trained on motion-capture data, highlighting its potential as a data-efficient alternative.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research has significant implications for the fields of robotics, animation, and machine learning:

- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** NIL makes robot learning more accessible by removing the need for expensive and time-consuming data collection. This could enable researchers and practitioners to develop controllers for a wider range of robots and simulated characters.
- **Accelerated Skill Acquisition:** The data-independent approach of NIL can significantly accelerate the skill acquisition process, allowing robots to learn complex behaviors more quickly and efficiently.
- **New Avenues for Research:** This work opens up new avenues for research at the intersection of generative modeling and reinforcement learning. It encourages further exploration of how generative models can be used to provide training signals for robot learning.
- **Potential Applications:** NIL has potential applications in various domains, including:
 - **Robotics:** Developing controllers for robots in unstructured environments, where data collection is challenging.
 - **Animation:** Creating realistic and physically plausible character animations.
 - **Simulation:** Generating realistic behaviors for agents in simulated environments.

The paper's most significant contribution is demonstrating that it is possible to learn complex motor skills without relying on any real-world data, bridging the gap between the visual realism of generated videos and the physical constraints of 3D environments. As video diffusion models continue to improve, NIL's performance is expected to increase, making it a promising approach for future robot learning and control.
