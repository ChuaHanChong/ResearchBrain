# PGDG: Physically Grounded Data Generation for Robust Bimanual Policy Learning from a Single Demonstration

> [!summary] Summary
> The Physically Grounded Data Generation (PGDG) framework synthesizes physically plausible and diverse recovery behaviors from a single expert demonstration, enabling robust bimanual policy learning for contact-rich tasks. This approach improved simulation success rates to 93% and real-world zero-shot transfer success rates to 82% for a ROTATEBOX-PITCH task compared to purely spatial augmentation.

## Problem

- Collecting diverse expert demonstrations, particularly for recovery behaviors in contact-rich bimanual tasks, is costly and time-intensive.
- Standard Behavior Cloning policies trained on limited nominal demonstrations often lack robustness to unforeseen test-time deviations, leading to task failures.
- Existing data augmentation methods either lack physical validity for contact-rich scenarios or incur high computational overhead with policy-dependent curation.

## Method

- **PGDG** iteratively generates physically plausible recovery trajectories by applying spatial randomization to a single demonstration and sampling control-point parameters in a physical simulator.
- A dataset curator selects informative and diverse successful trajectories using an adaptive reward function that defines a 'Goldilocks Zone' of recoverability, combined with Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) embeddings and Determinantal Point Processes (DPP).
- Selective local relabeling with the Cross Entropy Method (CEM) refines supervision at identified risky states within curated trajectories, ensuring locally optimal actions contribute to full-episode success.

## Results

- Policies trained with PGDG data achieved significantly higher simulation success rates, increasing from **38%** to **93%** on ROTATEBOX-PITCH and **68%** to **90%** on BARPASS compared to spatial augmentation.
- PGDG demonstrated improved zero-shot real-world transfer, with success rates increasing from **35%** to **82%** on ROTATEBOX-PITCH and **50%** to **80%** on BARPASS, outperforming baseline methods.
- The generated data effectively fine-tuned the GR00T N1.6 robotics foundation model, boosting its success rates (e.g., **77.5%** vs. **40%** for ROTATEBOX-PITCH) and enabling better adaptation to specific manipulation skills.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics-grounded data generation is crucial for creating robust policies in contact-rich bimanual tasks, as purely geometric augmentation often leads to physically infeasible behaviors under perturbation.
> - Curation that prioritizes both informativeness (challenging but recoverable behaviors) and diversity (distinct recovery modes) is more effective than simply generating a large volume of data for robust policy learning.
> - Refining local supervision at risky, off-manifold states through selective relabeling provides important corrective signals that further enhance overall policy robustness, even when the broader trajectory is successful.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses challenges in robot manipulation, specifically in the domain of bimanual, contact-rich tasks, through Behavior Cloning (BC). BC relies on expert demonstrations to learn control policies. A significant limitation of BC is its requirement for diverse demonstration datasets to ensure robust performance, particularly for recovery from unforeseen deviations during execution. Collecting such diverse demonstrations, especially those illustrating recovery behaviors in contact-rich settings, is costly and time-intensive.

Existing approaches to augment demonstration data fall into several categories:
- **Spatial and Contextual Augmentation**: Methods such as MimicGen and DexMimicGen expand data by re-anchoring demonstrated motions to varied object poses or scene geometries. However, these methods typically preserve the original motion pattern and contact schedule. In contact-rich tasks, geometric similarity does not guarantee physical feasibility or recoverability, as small pose perturbations can alter contact timing and forces, leading to failures.
- **Physics-Grounded Generation**: Works like PhysicsGen and SPIDER incorporate physical constraints during data generation to improve physical validity. While these methods generate feasible trajectories, they often do not explicitly focus on curating a compact and informative dataset that emphasizes diverse and recoverable behaviors.
- **Data Curation for Behavior Cloning**: Other studies, including Demo-SCORE and CUPID, aim to improve BC performance by filtering, reweighting, or selecting subsets from *existing* demonstration data. These methods frequently depend on policy queries, optimization rollouts, or learned scoring models during curation, which can incur substantial computational overhead and are typically applied to pre-existing datasets rather than *generating* new, targeted recovery data.
- **Recovery Supervision**: Approaches such as DAgger and RaC emphasize the importance of recovery-focused supervision. These often involve interactive learning paradigms, where humans provide corrective feedback online, or rely on policy-dependent feedback during data collection, which PGDG aims to avoid.

The presented work, PGDG (Physically Grounded Data Generation), aims to bridge the gap between these areas. It proposes an integrated framework that combines physics-grounded data generation with zero-shot, policy-free data curation. This enables the expansion of a single or few demonstrations into a compact dataset of physically plausible, successful, and diverse recovery behaviors. Unlike purely geometric augmentation, PGDG prioritizes physical validity and recoverability. Distinct from existing data curation methods, PGDG actively synthesizes new, informative recovery trajectories and curates them without requiring iterative policy training or human intervention, making it applicable in a pre-training, offline setting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework capable of generating a robust and compact dataset for Behavior Cloning in contact-rich bimanual manipulation, starting from a minimal number of expert demonstrations (ideally, a single one). This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **High Cost of Diverse Demonstration Collection**: Acquiring a sufficiently diverse dataset of demonstrations, particularly those illustrating recovery from deviations, is expensive and labor-intensive. Human operators typically need to perform repeated teleoperation, environment resets, and trial-and-error rollouts to capture valid recovery behaviors, which is particularly challenging in contact-rich bimanual tasks requiring precise coordination.
2. **Lack of Robustness in Standard Behavior Cloning**: Standard BC policies, trained on limited nominal demonstrations, often fail when encountering test-time deviations that push the system into off-manifold states. Without explicit recovery supervision for these states, the policy lacks guidance on how to correct errors, leading to task failure.
3. **Limitations of Existing Data Augmentation**:
 - **Geometric-only augmentation**: While methods like MimicGen can broaden coverage over initial object poses, they often fail to ensure physical validity and recoverability in contact-rich scenarios. Small geometric changes can disrupt critical contact sequences and force applications, rendering geometrically plausible augmented trajectories physically infeasible.
 - **Physics-grounded generation**: Current physics-grounded generation methods primarily focus on producing feasible trajectories but do not explicitly incorporate mechanisms to identify and select the *most informative* and *diverse* recovery behaviors for a compact training dataset.
 - **Policy-dependent curation**: Many data curation techniques require querying the policy or learning a policy-dependent scoring function, introducing computational overhead and making them less suitable for zero-shot data generation prior to policy training.
4. **Specific Challenges of Bimanual Manipulation**: Contact-rich bimanual tasks demand precise coordination, timing, and force application. Even minor deviations can destabilize objects or cause irreversible drift, making effective recovery highly localized and sensitive to physical interactions. Robust imitation in these settings necessitates supervision not only for nominal execution but also for physically valid corrective behaviors around the demonstrated trajectory.

To address these motivations, the PGDG framework aims to:
- Generate physically plausible and successful recovery behaviors in simulation.
- Curate these generated trajectories to emphasize informativeness (behaviors that are challenging but recoverable) and diversity (distinct recovery modes).
- Perform this generation and curation in a "zero-shot" manner, meaning without relying on additional human labeling, online human intervention, or queries to a trained policy during the data generation phase.
- Produce a compact and high-quality dataset that can enhance the robustness of both task-specific imitation policies and broader robotics foundation models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PGDG (Physically Grounded Data Generation) is an iterative framework designed to expand a single or few human demonstrations into a compact dataset of robust recovery behaviors. The process alternates between a physics-grounded sampler and a dataset curator, with a final step of selective local relabeling.

**A. Spatial Randomization**
The process begins by taking a single expert demonstration, typically collected via teleoperation (e.g., using a GELLO-style exoskeleton). To introduce initial diversity, MimicGen-style spatial randomization is applied. This involves re-anchoring the demonstrated end-effector trajectory to multiple randomized initial object poses. The motion is expressed relative to the object frame, then re-composed with a perturbed initial object pose, preserving the object-centric manipulation pattern while varying the absolute scene geometry. A short blending trajectory is appended to smoothly transition from the robot's reset pose to the re-anchored motion.

**B. Iterative Generation and Curation Loop**
The core of PGDG is an iterative process (for `K` iterations) consisting of a physics-grounded sampler and a dataset curator.

1. **Physics-Grounded Sampler**:
 - **Trajectory Parameterization**: To manage the high dimensionality of full action sequences, nominal plans are parameterized using `M` control points (`C`) that are decoded into an action sequence `a_nom` via a fixed decoder `G`.
 - **Sampling Distribution**: In each iteration `k`, control-point parameters `c` are drawn from a diagonal Gaussian proposal distribution `q^(k)(c) = N(μ^(k), diag((σ^(k))^2))`.
 - **Rollout and Success Filtering**: Each sampled control-point vector `c` is decoded into a nominal plan, which is then executed as a full-episode rollout in a physical simulator (e.g., Isaac Sim). Only *successful* rollouts are retained in a batch `B^(k)`.

2. **Dataset Curator**: The curator processes the successful rollouts from the sampler to identify informative, non-redundant behaviors.
 - **Curation Reward (R^(k)(τ))**: This reward guides the selection towards trajectories that visit a "Goldilocks Zone" of recoverability.
 - **Distance to Expert Manifold (d(s))**: For any state `s`, its distance `d(s)` is defined as the minimum Euclidean distance to any state in the expert demonstration's task-relevant subspace `ψ(·)`. This tolerates minor timing discrepancies.
 - **Peak Deviation (d_peak(τ))**: The maximum `d(s)` observed over a trajectory `τ`.
 - **Adaptive Recovery Tube**: Inner (`r^(k)_min`) and outer (`r^(k)_max`) tube radii are defined by quantiles (e.g., `q_min=0.1-0.3`, `q_max=0.8`) of successful peak deviations within the current batch `B^(k)`. This makes the "Goldilocks Zone" adaptive.
 - **Reward Function**: `R^(k)(τ)` is designed to be highest when a trajectory's deviations remain within the `[r^(k)_min, r^(k)_max]` range. It decreases if the trajectory stays too close to the demonstration (trivial success) or strays beyond the recoverable range.
 - **Diversity Selection**:
 - **Trajectory Embedding (ϕ(τ))**: Each successful rollout `τ` is represented by its task-relevant state-action sequence `x_t = [ψ(s_t); a_t]`. This sequence is then transformed using a Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) along the temporal dimension, retaining low-frequency coefficients to form a compact embedding `ϕ(τ)`. This embedding captures the overall state-action evolution while filtering high-frequency noise.
 - **Determinantal Point Process (DPP)**: To enforce diversity and remove redundancy, a subset `S^(k)` of `m` trajectories is selected from `B^(k)` by maximizing `log det(L_S + εI)`. This DPP-based selection favors sets of trajectories that are both high-coverage and non-redundant in the `ϕ(τ)` embedding space, promoting distinct recovery modes.
 - **Sampling Distribution Update**: The selected subset `S^(k)` identifies regions in the control-point space that consistently yield successful, informative, and diverse recovery behaviors. The proposal distribution `q^(k+1)(c)` for the next iteration is refined by performing weighted moment matching over the control points `c_i` corresponding to trajectories in `S^(k)`. The weights `w_i` are monotonic with the curation reward `R^(k)(τ_i)`. A small covariance floor (`δI`) is added to prevent premature collapse of exploration, ensuring continued discovery of new behaviors.

**C. Selective Local Relabeling with CEM**
After the iterative generation and curation loop, the collected dataset `B_sel` (all `S^(k)` subsets) may still contain states where actions are suboptimal or unstable, even if the overall rollout is successful. To refine supervision at these critical points, PGDG employs selective local relabeling:
- **Risky State Identification**: The `K_rel` most risky states (those with the largest `d_t(τ)` values) are identified across all curated trajectories in `B_sel`, with a minimum temporal separation imposed to avoid redundant relabeling.
- **CEM Relabeling**: For each selected risky state `(τ, t)`, a short action sequence `u_0:H-1` over a horizon `H` is optimized using the Cross Entropy Method (CEM).
 - The optimization objective `J(u; s_t)` penalizes full-episode failure, excursions outside the recoverable tube `r^(k)_max`, and significant deviations from the original nominal action segment.
 - A critical aspect is the *full-episode success check*: the candidate local action sequence `u_0:H-1` is evaluated by executing it and then resuming the original reference plan for the remaining steps. This ensures that the locally corrected actions contribute to overall task success rather than creating local fixes that are globally inconsistent.
 - The optimized corrective action sequence `y_t = (u*_0..., u*_H-1)` is then stored, paired with the observation `o_t` at the risky state.

**D. Policy Training**
The final dataset `D` comprises timestep-level observation-action pairs extracted from the curated successful trajectories, augmented with the locally relabeled corrective action chunks. This dataset is then used to train Behavior Cloning policies, such as ACT or to fine-tune foundation models like GR00T. The input to these policies includes depth images (encoded via high-low bytes for precision) and robot proprioceptive state history.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness of PGDG in simulation and real-world transfer across four bimanual manipulation tasks: ROTATEBOX-ROLL, ROTATEBOX-PITCH, ROTATEBOX-YAW, and BARPASS.

**A. Policy Evaluation in Simulation (ACT Policy)**
- **Superiority over Spatial Augmentation**: PGDG consistently increased the success rates of policies trained on its generated data compared to policies trained solely on MimicGen-style spatial augmentation. For instance, on ROTATEBOX-PITCH, success improved from 38% (MimicGen) to 93% (PGDG). Similar improvements were observed across other tasks: ROTATEBOX-ROLL (45% to 78%), ROTATEBOX-YAW (70% to 97%), and BARPASS (68% to 90%).
- **Role of Physical Validity**: The paper attributes the comparatively lower performance of MimicGen-style augmentation to its inability to preserve the correct contact sequence and timing under pose perturbations, which is critical for dynamic, contact-rich tasks. Small shifts in contact points or approach angles can lead to missed or unstable contacts, causing failures despite geometric plausibility. PGDG's physics-grounded generation, in contrast, yields dynamically feasible recovery trajectories.
- **Ablation Studies**:
 - **PGDG without DPP (Diversity Selection)**: Removing the Determinantal Point Process (DPP) for diversity selection consistently reduced performance across all tasks. This indicates that merely generating more successful trajectories without ensuring diversity can lead to redundant data, which can bias the training distribution towards narrow, stable solutions and limit overall robustness. Evaluation of policies trained on varying dataset sizes revealed that without DPP, larger datasets did not reliably improve performance, emphasizing the value of curated diversity over sheer quantity.
 - **PGDG without Relabel (Local Correction)**: Omitting the selective local relabeling step also led to decreased success rates. This suggests that refining local supervision at risky, off-manifold states provides valuable corrective signals that further enhance policy robustness, even when the overall trajectory is successful.

**B. Foundation Model Fine-tuning (GR00T N1.6)**
- PGDG-generated data demonstrated effectiveness in fine-tuning a robotics foundation model, GR00T N1.6. Policies fine-tuned with PGDG data achieved higher success rates compared to those fine-tuned with MimicGen-style data across all four tasks. For example, on ROTATEBOX-PITCH, success was 77.5% with PGDG data versus 40% with spatial augmentation data, indicating the benefit of PGDG's data quality for adapting generalist models to specific skills.

**C. Real-World Experiment (Zero-Shot Transfer)**
- **Successful Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Policies trained with PGDG-generated data in simulation exhibited higher success rates in zero-shot real-world deployment compared to those trained with spatial-only augmentation. For instance, on ROTATEBOX-PITCH, real-world success improved from 35% (MimicGen) to 82% (PGDG). Similar trends were observed for ROTATEBOX-ROLL (38% to 70%), ROTATEBOX-YAW (55% to 88%), and BARPASS (50% to 80%).
- **Real-World Failure Modes**: The dominant failure mode in real-world deployment was object slipping, which the authors attributed to sim-to-real discrepancies, particularly regarding friction and contact compliance (simulated rigid objects versus real-world deformable objects).
- **Interpretation of Robustness**: The real-world results suggest that PGDG does not eliminate the sim-to-real gap, but rather enables the learning of recovery behaviors that are more tolerant to the contact uncertainty and partial execution errors inherent in real-world environments.

**D. Limitations and Additional Findings**
- **Locality of Generation**: The data generation process is inherently local to the provided demonstration, meaning it excels at synthesizing diverse recoveries near the expert manifold but is not designed to discover entirely novel successful modes.
- **Action Representation Sensitivity**: The paper noted that converting demonstrated joint-space motions into Cartesian end-effector targets can degrade supervision for highly dynamic-sensitive behaviors due to information loss and mismatches with the robot's Cartesian controller.
- **Dataset Size vs. Quality**: The ablation studies reinforced that larger datasets are not necessarily superior without proper curation; redundant trajectories can skew the training distribution.
- **State-Dependent Recoverability**: Task success was found to be non-uniformly sensitive across different degrees of freedom, suggesting that recovery augmentation could benefit from structured and state-dependent application.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by offering a methodology to enhance the robustness of robot policies learned through behavior cloning. The significance and potential impact of PGDG include:

- **Enabling Robust Robot Skills from Limited Data**: PGDG provides a framework for generating a substantial, high-quality recovery dataset from a single or few human demonstrations. This significantly reduces the manual effort and cost associated with collecting diverse datasets, which is a major bottleneck for deploying learning-based robot control in complex, contact-rich tasks.
- **Improving Policy Robustness for Contact-Rich Tasks**: By focusing on physically plausible, informative, and diverse recovery behaviors, PGDG addresses a critical weakness of standard behavior cloning: its susceptibility to small deviations in dynamic, contact-rich environments. The increased success rates in both simulation and zero-shot real-world transfer demonstrate the framework's ability to yield more robust policies.
- **Advancing Data Augmentation and Curation**: PGDG integrates physics-grounded generation with a zero-shot, policy-free curation mechanism. This approach moves beyond purely geometric data augmentation, which often fails in physics-sensitive scenarios, and enhances data curation by actively synthesizing relevant data rather than merely selecting from existing examples. The use of adaptive recovery tubes, DCT embeddings, and DPP selection represents an advancement in how informative and diverse data subsets are identified and leveraged.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap**: While PGDG does not eliminate the sim-to-real gap, it contributes to learning policies that are more tolerant of real-world uncertainties, such as friction discrepancies and object deformability. The improved real-world performance suggests that learning diverse recovery behaviors in simulation can effectively equip policies to handle unmodeled dynamics in physical environments.
- **Facilitating Foundation Model Adaptation**: The demonstration that PGDG-generated data can be used to effectively fine-tune large robotics foundation models like GR00T highlights its utility beyond single-task policies. This capability could accelerate the deployment of generalist robot models by enabling them to quickly adapt to novel, specialized manipulation skills with minimal expert input.
- **Computational Efficiency**: The zero-shot and policy-free nature of the curation process means that PGDG does not require iterative policy training or human-in-the-loop interaction during data generation, which can lead to more computationally efficient data generation pipelines.

Overall, PGDG offers a principled approach to synthesize and curate high-quality training data for robust behavior cloning in complex robotic tasks, with potential implications for reducing development costs and accelerating the deployment of advanced manipulation capabilities.
