# PGDG: Physically Grounded Data Generation for Robust Bimanual Policy Learning from a Single Demonstration

> [!summary] Summary
> The Physically Grounded Data Generation (PGDG) framework synthesizes physically plausible and diverse recovery behaviors from a single expert demonstration, enabling robust bimanual policy learning for contact-rich tasks. This approach improved simulation success rates to 93% and real-world zero-shot transfer success rates to 82% for a ROTATEBOX-PITCH task compared to purely spatial augmentation.

## Problem

- Collecting diverse expert demonstrations, particularly for recovery behaviors in contact-rich bimanual tasks, is costly and time-intensive.
- Standard Behavior Cloning policies trained on limited nominal demonstrations often lack robustness to unforeseen test-time deviations, leading to task failures.
- Existing data augmentation methods either lack physical validity for contact-rich scenarios or incur high computational overhead with policy-dependent curation.

## Method

- **PGDG** iteratively generates physically plausible recovery trajectories by applying spatial randomization to a single demonstration and sampling control-point parameters in a physical simulator.
- A dataset curator selects informative and diverse successful trajectories using an adaptive reward function that defines a 'Goldilocks Zone' of recoverability, combined with Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) embeddings and Determinantal Point Processes (DPP).
- Selective local relabeling with the Cross Entropy Method (CEM) refines supervision at identified risky states within curated trajectories, ensuring locally optimal actions contribute to full-episode success.

## Results

- Policies trained with PGDG data achieved significantly higher simulation success rates, increasing from **38%** to **93%** on ROTATEBOX-PITCH and **68%** to **90%** on BARPASS compared to spatial augmentation.
- PGDG demonstrated improved zero-shot real-world transfer, with success rates increasing from **35%** to **82%** on ROTATEBOX-PITCH and **50%** to **80%** on BARPASS, outperforming baseline methods.
- The generated data effectively fine-tuned the GR00T N1.6 robotics foundation model, boosting its success rates (e.g., **77.5%** vs. **40%** for ROTATEBOX-PITCH) and enabling better adaptation to specific manipulation skills.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics-grounded data generation is crucial for creating robust policies in contact-rich bimanual tasks, as purely geometric augmentation often leads to physically infeasible behaviors under perturbation.
> - Curation that prioritizes both informativeness (challenging but recoverable behaviors) and diversity (distinct recovery modes) is more effective than simply generating a large volume of data for robust policy learning.
> - Refining local supervision at risky, off-manifold states through selective relabeling provides important corrective signals that further enhance overall policy robustness, even when the broader trajectory is successful.
