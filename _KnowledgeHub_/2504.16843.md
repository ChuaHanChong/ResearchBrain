# Physically Consistent Humanoid Loco-Manipulation using Latent Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from TUM and NYU develop a pipeline that uses latent diffusion models to generate human-object interaction scenes, which guide trajectory optimization for humanoid robots performing complex loco-manipulation tasks, enabling physically consistent motions while reducing the need for manual tuning and task-specific heuristics.

## Problem

- Programming humanoid robots for complex loco-manipulation tasks in unstructured environments is challenging due to high dimensionality and unstable dynamics
- Traditional methods struggle with long-horizon planning and require significant manual tuning
- Deep reinforcement learning offers an alternative to classical planning, but training stable policies for complex humanoid systems is difficult and generalization remains poor

## Method

- Use latent diffusion models to generate images of human-object interactions
- Extract contact locations and robot configurations from generated images to guide whole-body trajectory optimization that accounts for dynamics and constraints
- Combine semantic correspondence mapping and geometry-aware refinement for accurate 3D contact transfer

## Results

- Achieves physically plausible trajectories for long-horizon tasks with better collision avoidance than baseline methods
- Successfully demonstrates complex loco-manipulation sequences in simulation
- Reduces need for manual tuning while maintaining dynamic feasibility

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generated human poses can effectively guide robot motion planning through contact transfer and retargeting
> - Geometric information improves accuracy of contact transfer between generated and simulated scenes
> - Keyframes derived from generated images reduce penetration and improve optimization success rate

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work sits at the intersection of several active research areas:

- **Humanoid Robotics:** This field aims to develop robots that can operate in human environments, often requiring complex loco-manipulation skills.
- **Loco-Manipulation Planning:** This deals with the combined problem of locomotion (movement) and manipulation (interacting with objects). This is a challenging problem for humanoid robots due to their high dimensionality and unstable dynamics.
- **Generative Models (Latent Diffusion Models):** This is a rapidly advancing area in AI, focused on creating models that can generate realistic images, videos, and other data from textual prompts. LDMs have shown remarkable capabilities in generating diverse and high-quality content.
- **Trajectory Optimization:** A classic approach in robotics for planning dynamically feasible robot motions. It formulates the motion planning problem as an optimization problem, considering robot dynamics, constraints, and objectives.
- **Foundation Models:** Recent advances have yielded powerful AI models pre-trained on massive datasets that can perform various tasks with minimal fine-tuning. Examples include Vision Language Models (VLMs), visual segmentation foundation models, semantic-aware foundation models, and geometric foundation models.
- **Imitation Learning and Reinforcement Learning:** In recent years, imitation learning and reinforcement learning have been used to train robots to perform loco-manipulation tasks.

Traditionally, loco-manipulation planning for humanoids has been tackled using classical control methods, hierarchical approaches, or simplified models. These methods often struggle with long-horizon tasks or require significant manual tuning. Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has emerged as an alternative, but it can be challenging to train stable policies for complex humanoid systems and does not always generalize well.

This paper bridges the gap between these areas by using the power of LDMs to generate plausible human-object interaction scenes, which then guide the planning process for a humanoid robot. By leveraging human-like behavior generated by LDMs, the robot can overcome the challenges of long-horizon reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives of this research are:

- **Develop a pipeline for humanoid loco-manipulation planning that uses LDMs to generate realistic human-object interaction scenes.**
- **Extract relevant information (contact locations and robot configurations) from the generated images to guide a trajectory optimization framework.**
- **Generate physically consistent and geometrically feasible motions for humanoid robots performing complex loco-manipulation tasks.**
- **Demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach in long-horizon scenarios.**

The motivation behind this work stems from the difficulty of programming humanoid robots to perform complex tasks in unstructured environments. The authors argue that humanoids have the potential to replace humans in repetitive and dangerous tasks, but their complexity poses significant challenges. By leveraging the similarity between human and humanoid morphologies and the recent advances in generative models, they aim to create a more efficient and scalable approach to humanoid robot control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology can be broken down into the following steps:

1. **Image Generation:** Use a Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) to generate a collection of images demonstrating how a human would accomplish the long-horizon task. The LDM is provided with text prompts describing the desired interactions with objects in the scene. To ensure the generation of full-body humans, the authors append a static set of descriptive words.
2. **Contact Transfer:** Extract and map 2D contact information (hand and feet positions) from the generated images to corresponding 3D contact locations in the simulated scene. This involves:
 - Computing 2D semantic masks of objects in the generated images and the simulated scene using a Vision Language Model (VLM) and a visual segmentation foundation model.
 - Computing 3D point clouds of the objects by lifting the 2D information using depth information. Since LDMs do not provide depth, the authors use a zero-shot metric depth geometric foundation model to estimate depth. Camera intrinsics are empirically estimated.
 - Using a sampling-based optimization approach combined with a semantic-aware foundation model and object geometries to refine the semantic correspondences between the generated and simulated objects and transfer the 2D contact locations to 3D.
3. **Retargeting:** Use the depicted humans in the generated images and the extracted 3D contact locations to obtain a robot configuration. This is done via an Inverse Kinematics (IK)-based retargeting process. Human joint angles, foot positions, and base orientation are extracted from the generated images using a 3D human pose estimation module (WHAM). These are then remapped to a kinematically feasible robot configuration using IK, with hand and feet contact locations as constraints.
4. **Trajectory Optimization (TO):** Formulate a whole-body trajectory optimization problem that considers the robot's dynamics, contact constraints, and collision avoidance. The extracted contact locations and robot configurations from the previous steps are used to guide the optimization process. The optimization problem is solved using a semi-implicit Euler discretization of the dynamics and a task-generic cost function.
5. **Experimental Validation:** Validate the proposed approach in simulation using two long-horizon loco-manipulation scenarios. Perform an ablation study to analyze the effectiveness of different components of the pipeline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the paper are:

- **The proposed pipeline can generate physically plausible trajectories for long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks.** The authors demonstrate this by comparing their approach to a baseline that uses only contact information from a semantic-aware foundation model. The results show that their pipeline achieves significantly better collision avoidance and more realistic motions.
- **Geometry information improves contact transfer.** The authors show that using object geometries to refine the semantic correspondences between the generated and simulated objects leads to a more accurate contact transfer and reduces collision penetrations.
- **Keyframes reduce penetration and improve success rate of trajectory optimization.** The authors conduct ablation studies to show that using keyframes derived from the LDM improves the convergence and stability of the optimization process and the quality of the solution. They quantify the impact of keyframe base cost, keyframe foot position cost, and subtask-like warm start.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this work are:

- **Provides a novel and promising approach to humanoid robot control by leveraging generative AI.** This can lead to more efficient and scalable methods for programming robots to perform complex tasks.
- **Reduces the need for manual tuning and task-specific heuristics.** By using LDMs to generate human-like behavior, the robot can learn to perform tasks in a more autonomous and adaptable way.
- **Opens up new possibilities for human-robot collaboration.** By understanding how humans perform tasks, robots can better assist and collaborate with humans in various applications.
- **Advances the state-of-the-art in loco-manipulation planning.** The proposed pipeline can be extended and adapted to other robotic platforms and tasks.
- **Demonstrates the potential of combining generative AI with traditional robotics techniques.** This integration can lead to breakthroughs in robotics and AI.

The potential impact of this research extends to various fields, including:

- **Manufacturing:** Humanoid robots can perform complex assembly tasks in factories.
- **Logistics:** Humanoid robots can assist with warehousing and delivery.
- **Healthcare:** Humanoid robots can provide assistance to patients and healthcare providers.
- **Search and Rescue:** Humanoid robots can operate in dangerous and unstructured environments.

The future directions of this research include implementing the proposed work on a real humanoid robot, relaxing the assumption of being given the high-level plan, and developing a long-horizon task planner that can reason based on visual feedback using recent advances in multimodal large language models.
