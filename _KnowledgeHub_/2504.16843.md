# Physically Consistent Humanoid Loco-Manipulation using Latent Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from TUM and NYU develop a pipeline that uses latent diffusion models to generate human-object interaction scenes, which guide trajectory optimization for humanoid robots performing complex loco-manipulation tasks, enabling physically consistent motions while reducing the need for manual tuning and task-specific heuristics.

## Problem

- Programming humanoid robots for complex loco-manipulation tasks in unstructured environments is challenging due to high dimensionality and unstable dynamics
- Traditional methods struggle with long-horizon planning and require significant manual tuning

## Method

- Use latent diffusion models to generate images of human-object interactions
- Extract contact locations and robot configurations from generated images to guide trajectory optimization
- Combine semantic correspondence mapping and geometry-aware refinement for accurate 3D contact transfer
- Implement whole-body trajectory optimization considering dynamics and constraints

## Results

- Achieves physically plausible trajectories for long-horizon tasks with better collision avoidance than baseline methods
- Successfully demonstrates complex loco-manipulation sequences in simulation
- Reduces need for manual tuning while maintaining dynamic feasibility
- Shows potential for extending to real robot implementation through empirical validation

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generated human poses can effectively guide robot motion planning through contact transfer and retargeting
> - Geometric information improves accuracy of contact transfer between generated and simulated scenes
> - Keyframes derived from generated images reduce penetration and improve optimization success rate
