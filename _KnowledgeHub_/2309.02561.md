# Physically Grounded Vision-Language Models for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A physically grounded Vision-Language Model (VLM) was developed by fine-tuning InstructBLIP on PHYSOBJECTS, a novel dataset of common household objects annotated with physical concepts, to enable deeper understanding of material properties. This integration with LLM-based robotic planners led to a 31.3% increase in real-scene planning accuracy and a higher task success rate on a physical robot compared to ungrounded models.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with understanding physical object properties like material composition, fragility, or deformability, which are vital for nuanced robotic interaction.
- Robotic planners leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) lack deep physical grounding from visual input, limiting their ability to perform complex, context-aware manipulation tasks safely and effectively.
- Collecting extensive interaction data for physical property estimation is costly and challenging to scale, posing a practical hurdle for traditional robotic learning methods.

## Method

- ==Developed PHYSOBJECTS==, a new dataset of 225,466 object bounding boxes from household videos, annotated with 10 physical concepts using both automated and crowd-sourced preferences and labels.
- Fine-tuned the InstructBLIP VLM on PHYSOBJECTS, updating its Q-Former parameters to learn a score function for physical concepts, including material, fragility, and contents.
- Integrated the resulting physically grounded VLM (PG-InstructBLIP) with an LLM-based planner, enabling the LLM to interactively query the VLM about physical object properties for informed robotic task execution.

## Results

- The physically grounded VLM (PG-InstructBLIP) achieved an average test accuracy of **87.5%** on physical concepts from PHYSOBJECTS, a **21.3%** absolute improvement over the base InstructBLIP.
- When integrated into an LLM-based robotic planning framework, PG-InstructBLIP increased overall planning accuracy on unseen real scenes to **88.2%**, compared to **56.9%** for the base VLM.
- On a physical Franka Emika Panda robot, the system demonstrated a higher task success rate, completing 9 out of 10 manipulation tasks successfully compared to 4 out of 10 with the ungrounded VLM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs can be effectively endowed with an understanding of physical object concepts from visual appearance through fine-tuning on targeted human-annotated datasets.
> - Integrating such physically grounded VLMs allows LLM-based robotic planners to move beyond basic object identification to perform more sophisticated, context-aware manipulation tasks.
> - This approach offers a scalable method for acquiring physical property knowledge, leveraging robust visual reasoning from pre-trained VLMs rather than relying solely on extensive, costly interaction data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), vision-language models (VLMs), and robotic manipulation. Recent advancements have demonstrated the capability of LLMs to translate natural language instructions into sequential task plans for embodied agents. A fundamental challenge in deploying LLMs for real-world robotics is grounding their abstract linguistic understanding in the physical environment through sensory input, particularly vision.

Prior research has addressed this grounding challenge by employing VLMs to identify objects or provide feedback on scene events, such as task success detection. However, these applications typically rely on basic object detection or high-level visual summaries. The current work identifies a limitation: existing VLMs often lack a nuanced understanding of physical object concepts like material composition, fragility, or deformability. This deficiency restricts their utility in robotic tasks that necessitate physical interaction and reasoning about an object's properties, rather than merely its presence or category.

The paper contrasts its approach with traditional physical reasoning methods in robotics, which often involve self-supervised learning from extensive object interaction data. While such methods can provide precise physical property estimates, collecting large-scale, diverse interaction data for a wide array of common objects in varied settings remains a significant practical hurdle. The authors draw an analogy to human perception, where individuals can infer high-level physical concepts (e.g., a glass cup is more fragile than a plastic bottle) from static visual appearance, leveraging semantic knowledge of visually similar objects. This observation motivates the use of large-scale pre-trained VLMs, which exhibit broad visual reasoning and generalization capabilities from internet-scale data, as a scalable alternative to interaction-based learning for physical grounding.

The research also builds upon frameworks for VLM-LLM interaction, such as Socratic Models, where large models communicate via text to accomplish complex tasks. While previous work in this area has explored visual question answering or planning in simulated environments, this study focuses specifically on enhancing physical reasoning from real-world visual input to inform robotic planning, distinguishing it from approaches that rely on text descriptions or abstract simulations where physical concepts are not visually observable. The paper positions itself as extending the capabilities of VLMs for grounding LLM-based robotic planners by providing a deeper physical understanding of the environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the physical reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for application in robotic manipulation. This objective is motivated by several identified challenges and opportunities:

- **Addressing VLM Limitations in Physical Reasoning:** Current VLMs, despite their progress in tasks like visual question answering, exhibit limitations in understanding physical concepts pertinent to common objects in the real world. Concepts such as material composition (e.g., plastic vs. ceramic), fragility, deformability, and contents are not inherently well-understood by VLMs trained on general internet data, which lacks extensive object-centric physical reasoning examples. This gap restricts the direct application of VLMs in tasks requiring robots to interact physically with objects.
- **Enabling Deeper Physical Grounding for Robotic Planners:** For robotic manipulation, an understanding of physical object concepts is critical for intelligent planning. For example, a robot tasked with retrieving a cup of water needs to discern if a cup is already occupied, its material (e.g., glass vs. plastic) to assess fragility, and its capacity to hold liquid. Without this physical grounding, LLM-based robotic planners are limited to identifying objects and executing basic actions, lacking the nuanced understanding required for safe, effective, and context-aware interaction.
- **Overcoming Data Collection Challenges for Physical Properties:** Traditional methods for acquiring physical property information often rely on learning from object interaction data. However, collecting such data is resource-intensive and challenging to scale across a diverse range of objects and scenarios relevant to household robotics. The authors propose leveraging VLMs, which have demonstrated robust visual reasoning from static images, as a scalable alternative to infer high-level physical properties, akin to human visual inference. This approach aims to circumvent the need for extensive, task-specific interaction data.
- **Developing a Comprehensive Dataset for Physical Concepts:** A significant motivation is the absence of visual datasets specifically tailored for object-centric physical reasoning about common real-world objects, suitable for robotic applications. Existing datasets either focus on visual attributes (e.g., color), specific interaction scenarios, or a limited range of objects. The creation of a new dataset, PHYSOBJECTS, is therefore a key objective to benchmark and improve VLM performance in this domain. This dataset aims to capture human priors of physical concepts from visual appearance, making them accessible for VLM training.
- **Demonstrating Real-World Utility in Robotic Manipulation:** Beyond improving VLM accuracy on physical concepts, a core objective is to integrate these physically grounded VLMs into LLM-based robotic planning frameworks. The research aims to empirically demonstrate that such integration leads to improved planning performance on tasks requiring physical reasoning, both in simulated real-world scenarios and on a physical robot, thereby illustrating the practical utility of enhanced VLM physical understanding for embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves three main components: creating a novel dataset for physical reasoning, fine-tuning a VLM on this dataset, and evaluating the physically grounded VLM in robotic planning tasks.

#### 3.1. PHYSOBJECTS Dataset Creation

To address the lack of object-centric physical concept data, the authors developed PHYSOBJECTS.
- **Image Source:** The EgoObjects dataset was used, providing 117,424 images, 225,466 object bounding boxes, and 277 object categories from egocentric household videos. Images were selected based on object visibility using CLIP similarity.
- **Physical Concepts:** Ten physical concepts were defined, including eight main concepts and two held-out concepts for generalization evaluation (Density, Liquid Capacity).
 - **Continuous-valued (all objects):** Mass, Fragility, Deformability.
 - **Categorical-valued (all objects):** Material, Transparency.
 - **Categorical-valued (container objects only):** Contents, Can Contain Liquid, Is Sealed.
 - Specific definitions for each concept were provided to annotators.
- **Annotation Types:**
 - **Categorical concepts:** Direct label assignment from a predefined set (e.g., "plastic", "glass"). Open-ended labels were allowed for "material" and "contents" if predefined options were unsuitable.
 - **Continuous concepts:** Preference pairs, where annotators indicated if one object had a higher level of a concept than another, if they were roughly equal, or if the relationship was unclear. This approach was chosen due to the difficulty of assigning precise continuous values from static images.
- **Automatic Annotations:** An initial set of 417K annotations was automated. For categorical concepts, labels were assigned to entire EgoObjects categories. For continuous concepts, high and low tiers of categories were defined, and preference annotations were automatically generated for pairs of objects between these tiers.
- **Crowd-Sourcing:** 39.6K additional annotations were collected from 573 crowd-workers on the Prolific platform. Workers annotated object bounding boxes within the context of their full images via a web-based user interface. Each example received three annotations. Quality control included attention checks (10% of examples), requiring 80% accuracy for worker data retention.
- **Dataset Splits:** Object instances were split into training (73.0%), validation (14.8%), and test sets (12.2%), ensuring representation of each object category across splits.

#### 3.2. Physically Grounding Vision-Language Models

- **Base VLM:** InstructBLIP (FlanT5-XXL version) was used, chosen for its state-of-the-art open-source status. The VLM takes an object bounding box image and a text prompt (question) as input and produces a text output.
- **Fine-Tuning Process:** Only the Q-Former parameters of InstructBLIP were updated during fine-tuning.
 - **Categorical Concepts:** Training involved maximizing the likelihood of the annotated labels.
 - **Continuous Concepts (Preference Learning):** To train on preference pairs with a discrete text output VLM, a continuous score function was defined: $s(o, c) = \frac{p(\text{yes} | o, c)}{p(\text{no} | o, c)}$, where $p(\cdot|o,c)$ is the VLM's likelihood for "yes" or "no" for object $o$ and concept $c$. The Bradley-Terry model was then used to estimate the probability of preference $P(o_1 > o_2 | c) = \frac{s(o_1, c)}{s(o_1, c) + s(o_2, c)}$. The VLM was fine-tuned by minimizing a binary cross-entropy objective over preference annotations.
- **Training Details:** Hyperparameters were largely derived from BLIP-2 fine-tuning. A linear learning rate warmup and decay, along with mixed-precision bfloat16 training, were employed. Data augmentation included randomly sampling bounding box images for an object (except for concepts like "contents" where labels might vary per image). Prompt templates were used, with some training examples including object category labels to promote robustness. Q-Former text conditioning was removed during fine-tuning for improved generalization.
- **Validation:** Validation was performed periodically, with checkpoints selected based on lowest validation loss. Validation data was filtered for annotator agreement.

#### 3.3. Experimental Evaluation

The physically grounded VLM (PG-InstructBLIP) was evaluated across three settings:
- **Dataset Evaluation (PHYSOBJECTS):**
 - **Main Concepts:** Test accuracy on crowd-sourced examples was reported, comparing PG-InstructBLIP against baselines (Most Common, Text Only, base InstructBLIP, and single-concept fine-tuned InstructBLIP).
 - **Generalization:** Performance was assessed on held-out concepts and with paraphrased question prompts, as well as the impact on general VQA benchmarks (VQAv2, OK-VQA).
 - **Scaling Analysis:** The relationship between training data size and performance was investigated.
- **Real Scene Planning Evaluation:**
 - **Framework:** An LLM-based planner (GPT-4) was integrated with the VLM. The LLM received object detections (from OWL-ViT), a list of primitive robotic operations, and a task instruction. It then interactively queried the VLM about physical properties of objects and generated a plan using the primitives.
 - **Tasks:** 51 task scenarios across 8 unseen real scenes were evaluated by a human judge. Tasks were categorized as Single Concept, Multi-Concept, or Common Knowledge.
 - **Baselines:** Performance was compared against a planner using base InstructBLIP and a planner without any VLM interaction.
- **Real Robot Evaluation:**
 - **Hardware:** A Franka Emika Panda robot with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper was used.
 - **Primitives:** Pick-and-place primitives were executed based on kinesthetic demonstrations.
 - **Tasks:** 10 tasks across 2 real scenes were evaluated. Task success rates were assessed by a human evaluator from robot execution videos, isolating planning quality by ensuring primitive execution success.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the enhancement of VLM physical reasoning and its application in robotic manipulation.

#### 4.1. Dataset Evaluation

- **Improved Physical Reasoning:** Fine-tuning InstructBLIP on PHYSOBJECTS resulted in a substantial improvement in understanding physical concepts. PG-InstructBLIP achieved an average test accuracy of 87.5% on crowd-sourced main concepts, compared to 66.2% for the base InstructBLIP (Table II). Notable gains were observed across all concepts, with the largest improvement for "contents" (83.3% for PG-InstructBLIP vs. 35.1% for InstructBLIP).
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** PG-InstructBLIP consistently outperformed the "Most Common" and "Text Only" baselines, indicating that the improvements were due to visual reasoning and concept understanding rather than simple statistical priors or textual inference alone. The model also generally performed slightly better than versions fine-tuned on single concepts, suggesting potential benefits from a unified, general-purpose model.
- **Generalization to Held-Out Concepts:** PG-InstructBLIP demonstrated generalization capabilities, improving test accuracy on held-out physical concepts (density, liquid capacity) by an average of 11.9% compared to base InstructBLIP (71.7% vs. 59.8%, Table III). This suggests that the fine-tuning process captures underlying physical priors that transfer to related, unseen concepts.
- **Robustness to Prompt Variations:** The model maintained performance when presented with paraphrased question prompts, showing limited degradation from the original prompts and still outperforming base InstructBLIP (Table IV), indicating robustness to linguistic variations.
- **Dataset Scaling:** Performance generally scaled positively with increasing fractions of the PHYSOBJECTS dataset. The models showed significant benefits even when trained on only 10% of the dataset (Fig. 3), suggesting that meaningful improvements in VLM physical reasoning can be achieved with relatively moderate amounts of annotated data.
- **Limited VQA Degradation:** Fine-tuning on PHYSOBJECTS resulted in a limited decrease in performance on general VQA benchmarks (VQAv2 and OK-VQA) compared to the base InstructBLIP (Table XV). This suggests that the specialized physical reasoning capabilities were gained without significantly compromising general visual question answering abilities.
- **Model Size Comparison:** After fine-tuning on PHYSOBJECTS, a smaller InstructBLIP version (Flan-T5 XL) achieved comparable performance to the larger Flan-T5 XXL version (Table XIX), suggesting that targeted data and fine-tuning may reduce the reliance on extremely large model sizes for specific reasoning tasks.

#### 4.2. Real Scene Planning Evaluation

- **Improved Planning Accuracy:** Integrating PG-InstructBLIP into an LLM-based robotic planning framework led to a substantial increase in task plan accuracy on unseen real scenes. PG-InstructBLIP achieved an overall accuracy of 88.2%, compared to 56.9% for InstructBLIP and 33.3% for a planner without VLM interaction (Table V).
- **Enhanced Multi-Concept Reasoning:** The improvement was particularly pronounced in "Multi-Concept" tasks, where PG-InstructBLIP achieved 94.4% accuracy compared to 27.8% for InstructBLIP, indicating a greater ability to reason about multiple physical concepts simultaneously.
- **Necessity of VLM Grounding:** The notably lower performance of the "No VLM" baseline confirmed that the evaluated tasks required physical grounding beyond simple object detection.

#### 4.3. Real Robot Evaluation

- **Increased Task Success Rates:** On a physical Franka Emika Panda robot, the use of PG-InstructBLIP resulted in a higher task success rate (9 out of 10 tasks) compared to base InstructBLIP (4 out of 10 tasks) (Table VII). This demonstrates the practical benefits of the physically grounded VLM for real-world robotic manipulation.
- **Specific Improvements and Limitations:** Examples from the robot evaluation illustrated instances where PG-InstructBLIP corrected base VLM errors (e.g., correctly identifying a ceramic bowl as not plastic). However, both models occasionally exhibited errors, particularly with questions outside their specific training prompt formats or with inherently ambiguous visual properties (e.g., distinguishing translucent glass).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several points of significance and potential impact:

- **Enhanced VLM Capabilities for Embodied AI:** The development of PHYSOBJECTS and the subsequent fine-tuning approach demonstrably improve the physical reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models. This advancement addresses a key limitation in current VLMs, moving them beyond basic object recognition to a more nuanced understanding of physical properties crucial for interaction with the real world.
- **Foundational Dataset for Physical Reasoning:** PHYSOBJECTS contributes a large-scale, object-centric dataset of physical concept annotations for real household objects. This dataset serves as a valuable resource for benchmarking and training future models aiming to imbue AI systems with physical common sense, particularly for domestic robotics. Its focus on common household objects directly supports real-world applications.
- **Improved Robotic Manipulation and Planning:** By integrating the physically grounded VLM into LLM-based robotic planning frameworks, the research enables robots to execute more complex and context-aware manipulation tasks. The improved planning accuracy and real robot success rates indicate that robots can now better identify relevant objects, consider safety constraints (e.g., fragility), and infer affordances based on physical properties. This contributes to the development of more intelligent, adaptable, and safe robotic systems.
- **Scalable Approach to Physical Grounding:** The methodology of leveraging large-scale pre-trained VLMs with human annotations provides a scalable alternative to traditional methods of learning physical properties from extensive object interaction data. This approach can potentially accelerate the development of robotic intelligence by reducing the need for costly and time-consuming interaction data collection.
- **Demonstrated Generalization:** The ability of PG-InstructBLIP to generalize to held-out physical concepts and variations in question phrasing suggests that the learned representations are robust and transferable. This indicates that the model is learning underlying principles of physical reasoning rather than merely memorizing training examples.
- **Path for Future Research:** The work highlights directions for future development, such as integrating real physical measurements to ground model outputs in precise quantities, and expanding the scope of reasoning beyond physical properties to include geometric (e.g., fit) or social considerations. PHYSOBJECTS can serve as a stepping stone towards building VLMs that support more sophisticated and holistic reasoning for robotic applications.
