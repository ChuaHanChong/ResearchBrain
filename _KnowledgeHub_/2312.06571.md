# From Text to Motion: Grounding GPT-4 in a Humanoid Robot "Alter3"

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The University of Tokyo integrated GPT-4 with the humanoid robot Alter3 to enable the robot to generate spontaneous, contextually relevant motions directly from natural language commands. This system successfully translates abstract linguistic expressions into physical robot movements through zero-shot learning and allows for motion refinement via verbal feedback, demonstrating effective physical grounding of a large language model.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) possess vast textual knowledge but lack direct grounding in the physical world, limiting their expression to the digital domain.
- Controlling complex humanoid robots for low-level movements using LLMs typically requires extensive pre-programming or supplementary learning mechanisms.
- Developing fluid human-robot communication that incorporates contextually relevant gestures and expressions requires efficient motion generation and memory storage.

## Method

- A ==two-stage prompt engineering== process leverages GPT-4 to first generate detailed textual descriptions of desired motions and then translate these descriptions into Python code for **Alter3**'s 43 actuators.
- A ==verbal feedback== system allows human users to linguistically refine Alter3's motions, which are then autonomously revised, stored in a JSON database as ==motion memory==, and reused for subsequent interactions.
- A multi-personality '==social brain==' framework was implemented using six distinct LLM agents, enabling complex human-robot and robot-robot dialogues with integrated motion generation capabilities.

## Results

- Alter3 generated a variety of spontaneous, expressive motions and action sequences from natural language instructions using ==zero-shot learning==, including adopting poses like 'taking a selfie' and acting out narratives.
- Human subject evaluations confirmed that GPT-4 generated motions were perceived as significantly more meaningful and expressive than random control movements (**p <= 0.001**).
- The verbal feedback system successfully enabled autonomous revision of motion code and storage of refined movements, leading to improved action repertoire and higher user ratings for refined motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can directly control the low-level movements of a humanoid robot by generating executable code from natural language, demonstrating an implicit understanding of human actions and emotions.
> - Verbal feedback provides an effective mechanism for users to adapt and refine robot motions, contributing to the robot's developing 'body schema' without explicit parameter tuning within the LLM.
> - Conversations among multiple LLM agents can exhibit complex dynamics, but require human intervention and structured sequencing to sustain open-ended discussions and avoid repetitive cycles.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the context of advancements in artificial intelligence, particularly the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-4, which exhibit capabilities in generating contextually relevant text and code. While LLMs possess extensive knowledge and can simulate "human-like" behaviors in textual interactions, they typically lack grounding in the physical world, limiting their expressions to the digital domain.

The integration of LLMs with robots has emerged as a research frontier, with applications spanning human-robot interaction, task planning, navigation, and learning. Previous work in this area has utilized LLMs for interpreting complex instructions and facilitating basic communication. For instance, systems like Ahn et al.'s "SayCan" employ LLMs for high-level task planning, but often rely on supplementary learning mechanisms (e.g., TD-learning, reward functions) for low-level, hardware-dependent robot control, as this type of information is generally outside LLM training corpora.

This study specifically addresses the challenge of directly controlling robots with LLMs, particularly for humanoid platforms. It posits that humanoid robots, due to their human-like form, could benefit from the LLM's inherent knowledge of human motion descriptions, which are presumed to be more prevalent in training data than descriptions for other robotic forms. This approach aims to achieve precise movements through zero-shot learning, circumventing the need for extensive reward functions or interfaces commonly required in other robot control paradigms. The work also builds upon a history of developing humanoid robots, specifically the "Alter series," which previously demonstrated abilities in imitating human postures and generating varied movements through interaction. The authors consider embodied LLMs as a significant paradigm in cognitive science, human psychology, and the philosophy of embodied cognition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research was to develop a humanoid robot, Alter3, capable of generating spontaneous and contextually relevant motions using a Large Language Model (LLM), specifically GPT-4. This integration aimed to effectively ground the LLM's linguistic capabilities within the robot's physical body.

The study pursued several detailed objectives:

- **Direct LLM-based Robot Control:** To demonstrate the feasibility of directly controlling a humanoid robot's low-level movements using an LLM. This involves mapping abstract linguistic expressions of human actions into concrete robot body movements via generated program code, without requiring explicit, pre-programmed instructions for each body part.
- **Zero-Shot Learning for Motion Generation:** To enable the robot to perform a variety of poses and action sequences (e.g., "taking a selfie," "pretending to be a ghost") without specific prior training or fine-tuning for each action. This aimed to leverage the LLM's vast knowledge of human behaviors embedded in its training data.
- **Adaptive Motion Generation through Feedback:** To implement a system where verbal feedback from human users could be used to adjust and refine the robot's poses and movements, thereby obviating the need for parameter fine-tuning within the LLM itself.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Communication:** To investigate how LLM integration can enhance the cognitive and interactive capabilities of humanoid robots, allowing them to engage in dialogues that incorporate contextually relevant facial expressions and gestures. This included addressing challenges in conversation fluidity by implementing a motion memory system.
- **Exploration of a "Social Brain" Framework:** To explore the potential for emergent personality and sustained engagement in human-robot and robot-robot interactions by implementing a multi-personality framework using multiple LLM agents (a "society of mind" concept).
- **Contribution to Embodied Cognition:** To provide empirical insights into the necessity of embodiment for LLMs and to investigate the symbolic grounding problem within a physical robot context, thereby contributing to discussions in cognitive science and philosophy regarding consciousness and meaning-making in AI.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the recognition of LLMs' extensive knowledge and inferential power, coupled with their inherent limitation of being confined to the textual domain. The researchers sought to bridge this gap by enabling a humanoid robot to embody this knowledge, transforming abstract language into physical action. The human-like structure of Alter3 was considered advantageous for this endeavor, as human-centric motion descriptions are likely more common in LLM training data. Ultimately, the research was motivated by the potential to redefine human-robot collaboration by creating more intelligent, adaptable, and personable robotic entities, and to explore fundamental questions about embodied cognition in artificial systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Robot Platform

The research utilized Alter3, the third iteration in the "Alter series" of humanoid robots developed since 2016. Alter3 features 43 actuators that control facial expressions and limb movements, powered by compressed air. While it cannot walk, it is capable of simulating walking and running motions. The robot's control system transmits commands via a serial port, operating at a refresh rate of 100-150 ms. In prior work, Alter3 demonstrated the ability to imitate human postures using a camera and the OpenPose framework, with the effectiveness measured by transfer entropy.

#### 3.2. LLM Integration and Motion Generation

The core of the methodology involved integrating OpenAI's GPT-4 (specifically model gpt-4-0314) with Alter3 using a two-stage prompt engineering approach, described as a "Chain of Thought" (CoT) process without iterative learning (zero-shot learning).

1. **Prompt1 (Description Generation):** A user provides a natural language instruction for an action (e.g., "Let’s take a selfie with your iPhone!"). Prompt1 then instructs GPT-4 to generate a detailed, exaggerated, step-by-step textual description (approximately 10 lines) of the desired movement, including emotional and facial expressions. This prompt includes examples and guidelines for the LLM. A temperature of 0.7 was used for this stage.
2. **Prompt2 (Python Code Generation):** The detailed motion description generated by Prompt1 is then fed as input to Prompt2. Prompt2 instructs GPT-4 to translate this description into Python code that directly controls Alter3's 43 axes. A comprehensive list of axis definitions and their corresponding value ranges (0-255) for various movements (e.g., "Axis 1: Eyebrows. 255 = angry, 0 = surprised, 64 = neutral") is provided to the LLM. The generated Python code includes `time.sleep(0.5)` commands between operations, as each operation takes approximately 0.1 second. Examples of Python code corresponding to actions like "drink some tea" and guidelines for code generation are also provided. A temperature of 0.5 was used.

The resulting Python code is automatically executed by Alter3's android engine, controlling the amplitude of the air compressors to produce the physical motion. The system was tested with various instant actions (e.g., "take a selfie," "pretend a ghost") and actions with temporal sequences (e.g., "eating popcorn and realizing the mistake").

#### 3.3. Performance Evaluation

To quantify the effectiveness of GPT-4 in generating motions, videos of nine different generated movements were evaluated. A total of 107 subjects, recruited via the Prolific platform, watched these videos and rated the expressive ability of Alter3 on a 5-point scale (1 being the worst). For a control group, videos of random movements from Alter3, labeled with random motion notations generated by GPT-4, were subtly interspersed within the survey. Statistical analysis involved a Friedman test to identify significant differences among video ratings, followed by a Nemenyi post-hoc test for pairwise comparisons. Differences were considered statistically significant if the p-value was less than or equal to 0.001.

#### 3.4. Verbal Feedback and Memory

The methodology included a verbal feedback system to allow users to refine Alter3's motions. Users could provide linguistic instructions (e.g., "Set axis 16 to 255" or "Move your arm more energetically"). Alter3's system then autonomously revised the corresponding Python code. Once a movement was refined, it was stored in a JSON database with descriptive labels (e.g., "Holding the guitar") as motion memory. For subsequent motion generation via Prompt2, a `JsonToolkit` facilitated database searches, allowing the LLM to decide whether to reuse existing memories or generate new movements.

#### 3.5. Social Brain and Communication Framework

To enable Alter3 to engage in dialogue with contextually relevant gestures, a motion memory storage system was implemented to mitigate delays in generating new gestures. Pre-stored motions could be executed immediately, improving interaction fluidity.
The system also incorporated a multi-personality framework inspired by the "Society of Mind" concept. Six distinct LLM agents, each with a predefined personality (physicist, chemist, programmer, poet, artist, 10-year-old girl) and abilities for planning, long-term memory, and reflection, were created using LangChain's generative agents. These agents could converse with each other and with humans. An experimental environment was set up at a conference (ALIFE 2023) where the public could freely interact with Alter3.

#### 3.6. Conversation Analysis

Conversational data from the multi-agent system was analyzed using two methods:

1. **Trajectory of Embedding Vector:** Conversation data was embedded into 1536-dimensional vectors and then reduced to two dimensions using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) to visualize the conversational development and identify global structures.
2. **Semantic Analysis of Time Evolution:** Word Clouds were used to visualize the most frequently used words at different stages of the conversation (every 100 steps), illustrating how topics and language patterns evolved over time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Motion Generation

The study demonstrated that Alter3, integrated with GPT-4, was capable of generating a variety of spontaneous motions directly from natural language instructions without explicit prior programming for individual body parts. This zero-shot learning capability allowed Alter3 to adopt poses such as a "selfie" stance, "pretending to be a ghost," and "throwing a ball," as well as complex sequences of actions portraying narratives, like "eating popcorn and realizing the mistake." The LLM successfully inferred and expressed appropriate emotional states (e.g., a smile for a selfie, surprise for the popcorn scenario), reflecting them in Alter3's physical responses and facial expressions.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Performance Evaluation

A performance evaluation involving 107 human subjects rated the generated motions. Statistical analysis using the Friedman and Nemenyi tests showed a significant difference (p <= 0.001) in ratings, with GPT-4 generated motions being rated significantly higher than random control movements. This indicated that the motions generated by the system were perceived as meaningful, expressive, and distinct from arbitrary movements by human observers. The evaluation supported the conclusion that Alter3 could translate a broad range of linguistic representations of movements, including everyday actions, imitations of non-human entities, and emotional expressions, onto its physical body.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Verbal Feedback and Memory

The implemented verbal feedback system enabled users to linguistically instruct Alter3 to refine its movements (e.g., "Raise your arm a bit more"). Alter3 demonstrated the ability to autonomously revise its motion code based on this feedback, and store these refined motions in a database as "motion memory." This memory system allowed for the reuse of improved motions, suggesting the development of an effective body schema for the robot. Comparative evaluations indicated that motions refined with verbal feedback received higher scores than those without, confirming the utility of this adaptive mechanism.

#### 4.4. Insights from Multi-Agent Conversation

Analysis of conversations among the six generative agents and human participants revealed several dynamics:
- **Conversational Trajectory:** When the timing of contributions was fixed, conversations tended to become circular and repetitive. However, allowing random utterance timing led to more creative and engaging interactions among the agents.
- **"Good-bye" Attractor:** In prolonged conversations solely between GPT-4 agents, a phenomenon termed the "good-bye attractor" was observed. Agents frequently repeated "good-bye" and the conversation stagnated, requiring human intervention to introduce new topics and break the cycle.
- **Semantic Evolution:** Word Cloud analysis of conversations showed a gradual shift in frequently used words, indicating temporal evolution in discussion topics (e.g., from "art" and "learn" to "cultural," "human," and "will"). This evolution, however, eventually gave way to the "good-bye attractor."
- **Conformity:** GPT agents exhibited a tendency to agree with previous statements (e.g., using "indeed," "absolutely"), which the authors noted could hinder the development of more profound discussions.
- **Role of Human Intervention:** The study highlighted the importance of human intervention and conversation order in sustaining open-ended discussions among LLM agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in grounding Large Language Models within physical humanoid robots. By successfully enabling Alter3 to generate a wide array of spontaneous, expressive, and temporally sequenced motions directly from natural language commands using GPT-4, the study demonstrates a method for bridging the gap between an LLM's vast textual knowledge and physical embodiment.

The capability for **zero-shot learning** in motion generation is a key outcome, as it allows the robot to perform complex actions without task-specific programming or extensive training. This suggests that LLMs possess a detailed implicit understanding of human movements and emotions, which can be directly leveraged by humanoid forms. This approach simplifies robot programming, shifting control from low-level joint manipulation to intuitive linguistic instructions, thereby making human-robot interaction more accessible and natural.

The **verbal feedback and memory system** further enhances the robot's adaptability and learning. By allowing users to refine movements through natural language, the robot can progressively improve its action repertoire and develop a form of "body schema" through accumulated experience, without the need for complex internal parameter updates within the LLM. This provides a mechanism for continuous improvement in interaction quality.

The investigation into the **"social brain" framework** and multi-agent conversations contributes to understanding the dynamics of LLM agents. While it reveals challenges like the "good-bye attractor" and tendencies toward conformity in agent-only interactions, it underscores the crucial role of human intervention and structured conversation in fostering sustained and creative dialogues. This has implications for designing more robust and engaging AI conversational systems, particularly those integrated with physical bodies.

From a broader scientific perspective, the work contributes to the debate on **embodied cognition** and the **symbol grounding problem**. By embedding an LLM in a physical body, the research provides a platform to explore whether linguistic symbols can acquire meaning through physical interaction and how embodiment might influence an LLM's responses and emergent behaviors. The discussion touches upon philosophical concepts of consciousness (e.g., Searle's Chinese Room) and suggests that a multi-personality framework could be a practical implementation of modular theories of the mind.

The potential impact of this research includes:
- **More intuitive robot control:** Simplifying human interaction with complex robots for diverse applications, from service robotics to entertainment.
- **Foundation for empathetic AI:** Enhancing robots' ability to understand and express emotions, leading to more nuanced and relatable human-robot interactions.
- **Advancements in AI theory:** Providing empirical data for understanding the role of embodiment in AI, potentially informing future LLM architectures that integrate physical constraints for emergent spontaneity and less predictable, more innovative behaviors.
- **Scalability:** The demonstrated approach's adaptability to other androids with minimal modifications suggests a broad applicability across humanoid robotics platforms.

Overall, this study pushes the boundaries of LLM applications by demonstrating successful physical grounding in a humanoid robot, offering both practical advancements in robotics and significant theoretical insights into the nature of intelligence and embodiment.
