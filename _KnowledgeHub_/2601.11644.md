# Predicting When to Trust Vision-Language Models for Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A vision-based framework was developed at the University of Missouri Kansas City to predict the trustworthiness of Vision-Language Model (VLM) spatial reasoning outputs. This approach uses independent geometric verification through object detection, demonstrating a 34% relative improvement in AUROC for BLIP-2 predictions (0.674) compared to text-based confidence estimation methods.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit systematic failures and low accuracy (49-54%) in basic spatial reasoning tasks, making them unsuitable for critical applications.
- Existing confidence estimation methods for VLMs rely on text-based signals, which are ineffective for spatial reasoning and often yield AUROC scores near chance level due to VLM overconfidence.
- The lack of a robust method to predict VLM prediction trustworthiness for spatial reasoning hinders their safe and reliable deployment in high-stakes real-world systems.

## Method

- A ==four-stage pipeline== performs VLM spatial prediction, ==independent geometric validation== via object detection, ==multi-signal feature extraction==, and ==learned confidence fusion==.
- ==GroundingDINO-base== localizes objects to determine geometric relations and calculate ==geometric confidence (α_geo)==, providing an objective, external validation.
- An ==XGBoost classifier== combines four features (geometric confidence, separation confidence, detection quality, VLM token confidence) to output a final ==trustworthiness score== for VLM spatial predictions.

## Results

- Achieved AUROC scores of **0.674** for BLIP-2 and **0.583** for CLIP, representing **34.0%** and **16.1%** relative improvements over text-based baselines, respectively.
- At a **60%** target accuracy, the method achieved **61.9%** coverage for BLIP-2 predictions, outperforming geometric-only baselines by **2.2x** and text-based methods by approximately **19x**.
- Vision-based features collectively contributed over **80%** of the model importance, with geometric confidence being the most significant (**37.5%** for BLIP-2).
- Enabled more precise scene graph construction, increasing BLIP-2 edge precision from **52.1% to 78.3%** while retaining **68.2%** of edges, with a modest computational overhead of **87ms** per image.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - External, vision-based geometric verification provides a more reliable signal for VLM spatial reasoning trustworthiness than the VLM's internal self-assessment, which struggles with systematic overconfidence.
> - Fusing multiple complementary confidence signals (geometric, separation, detection quality, and VLM internal confidence) via a learned model significantly improves the accuracy of trustworthiness prediction.
> - Spatial relationships possess inherent objective verifiability, which can be leveraged through independent geometric analysis to effectively gauge VLM prediction reliability.
