# DexFuture: Hierarchical Future-State Visuomotor Targeting for Bimanual Dexterous Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> DexFuture, a hierarchical visuomotor targeting framework from UC San Diego, enables autonomous bimanual dexterous tool use by predicting future state targets directly from visuomotor history, thereby eliminating the need for privileged demonstration data. The system achieves approximately 90% of the success rate of privileged baselines and operates at 60 Hz, demonstrating a 250-fold speed advantage over action-conditioned planning methods.

## Problem

- Target-conditioned policies for dexterous manipulation rely on 'privileged' future targets from human demonstrations, which are unavailable during real-time robot deployment.
- Action-conditioned world models require computationally intensive online planning over high-dimensional action sequences, making them too slow for high-frequency, contact-rich control.
- Bimanual dexterous tool use involves high degrees of freedom, complex hand-tool-object dynamics, and intricate contact interactions, posing challenges for robust control.

## Method

- A hierarchical framework is proposed, separating long-horizon future-state prediction from high-frequency action execution.
- A high-level ==Future-State Visuomotor Target Predictor== (FθF_\thetaFθ​) forecasts coarse future state targets (hand-tool-object configurations) solely from egocentric RGB and proprioceptive history.
- A low-level Target-Conditioned Structured Dexterous Policy (πϕ\pi_\phiπϕ​) uses these linearly interpolated predicted targets to generate high-frequency bimanual actions, enabling stable, contact-rich control.

## Results

- DexFuture achieved an average success rate of **59.69%** across bimanual tool-use tasks, approximating **90%** of the success rate of privileged baselines (**66.52%**).
- The system operates at a policy control rate of **60 Hz**, demonstrating a computational speed advantage of approximately 250 times over an adapted action-conditioned world model planning baseline.
- Ablation studies showed that future targets are critical for dexterous manipulation, with 'no-target' policies exhibiting severely degraded performance (e.g., **4.16%** success rate for bread cutting vs. **83.49%** for DexFuture).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the prediction of future states from the execution of immediate actions allows for both efficient long-horizon guidance and reactive high-frequency control.
> - Structured visuomotor tokenization, focusing on physical entities like hands, tools, and objects, is effective for robustly encoding history and predicting future states.
> - Future state targets, even when predicted, provide essential guidance for high-degrees-of-freedom dexterous manipulation, significantly improving performance compared to policies without such targets.
