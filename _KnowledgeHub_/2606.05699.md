# DexFuture: Hierarchical Future-State Visuomotor Targeting for Bimanual Dexterous Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> DexFuture, a hierarchical visuomotor targeting framework from UC San Diego, enables autonomous bimanual dexterous tool use by predicting future state targets directly from visuomotor history, thereby eliminating the need for privileged demonstration data. The system achieves approximately 90% of the success rate of privileged baselines and operates at 60 Hz, demonstrating a 250-fold speed advantage over action-conditioned planning methods.

## Problem

- Target-conditioned policies for dexterous manipulation rely on 'privileged' future targets from human demonstrations, which are unavailable during real-time robot deployment.
- Action-conditioned world models require computationally intensive online planning over high-dimensional action sequences, making them too slow for high-frequency, contact-rich control.
- Bimanual dexterous tool use involves high degrees of freedom, complex hand-tool-object dynamics, and intricate contact interactions, posing challenges for robust control.

## Method

- A hierarchical framework is proposed, separating long-horizon future-state prediction from high-frequency action execution.
- A high-level ==Future-State Visuomotor Target Predictor== (Fθ) forecasts coarse future state targets (hand-tool-object configurations) solely from egocentric RGB and proprioceptive history.
- A low-level Target-Conditioned Structured Dexterous Policy (πϕ) uses these linearly interpolated predicted targets to generate high-frequency bimanual actions, enabling stable, contact-rich control.

## Results

- DexFuture achieved an average success rate of **59.69%** across bimanual tool-use tasks, approximating **90%** of the success rate of privileged baselines (**66.52%**).
- The system operates at a policy control rate of **60 Hz**, demonstrating a computational speed advantage of approximately 250 times over an adapted action-conditioned world model planning baseline.
- Ablation studies showed that future targets are critical for dexterous manipulation, with 'no-target' policies exhibiting severely degraded performance (e.g., **4.16%** success rate for bread cutting vs. **83.49%** for DexFuture).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the prediction of future states from the execution of immediate actions allows for both efficient long-horizon guidance and reactive high-frequency control.
> - Structured visuomotor tokenization, focusing on physical entities like hands, tools, and objects, is effective for robustly encoding history and predicting future states.
> - Future state targets, even when predicted, provide essential guidance for high-degrees-of-freedom dexterous manipulation, significantly improving performance compared to policies without such targets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bimanual dexterous tool use in robotics presents significant challenges due to the high degrees of freedom (DoF) of robotic hands, the complex dynamics involved in hand-tool-object interactions, and the intricate nature of contact-rich manipulation. Existing approaches in robot learning have made progress, particularly by leveraging human demonstrations to guide dexterous control.

One prominent strategy involves "target-conditioned policies," which provide the robot policy with future reference targets extracted from demonstration trajectories. These targets can encode future hand motion, object pose, fingertip relations, or higher-level task cues. While effective in simplifying high-DoF manipulation, a key limitation of these methods is that the future reference targets are "privileged" – they are derived from ground-truth future demonstration states and are thus unavailable to the robot during real-time deployment. This creates a bottleneck in generating dynamically consistent future reference trajectories without relying on such privileged information.

An alternative approach involves learning "action-conditioned world models." These models aim to learn predictive representations of the environment, enabling counterfactual rollouts and facilitating decision-making through online planning. Methods like DexWM learn world models from large-scale videos and utilize techniques such as the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) for planning subsequent actions. However, for dexterous tool use, online planning over high-dimensional action sequences is computationally intensive and often cannot operate at the high frequencies required for stable, contact-rich control. This computational burden restricts their practical applicability in real-time scenarios.

The present work, DexFuture, addresses the limitations of both these paradigms. It seeks to retain the performance benefits of target-conditioned policies while eliminating the dependency on privileged future demonstrations. Simultaneously, it aims to avoid the computational overhead associated with online action-conditioned planning. DexFuture proposes a hierarchical solution that predicts future *state* targets directly from visuomotor history, rather than planning over future actions, thus providing long-horizon guidance at a slower rate while maintaining fast, fine-grained action execution. This distinguishes it from direct action prediction methods or action-chunking approaches by separating the prediction of *what* should happen in the future from *how* the fine motor control is executed.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient framework for bimanual dexterous robot tool use that overcomes two primary limitations of current robot learning methods:
1. **Reliance on Privileged Future Targets:** Many successful target-conditioned policies depend on future configuration references derived directly from human demonstrations. These "privileged" targets provide critical guidance for high-DoF control but are not available to the robot at deployment time. The motivation is to enable target-conditioned control without this reliance.
2. **Computational Cost of Online Action Planning:** Action-conditioned world models, while capable of counterfactual planning, necessitate computationally intensive online optimization over high-dimensional action sequences. This makes them impractical for the high-frequency control loops (e.g., 60 Hz) required for stable contact-rich dexterous manipulation. The motivation is to achieve efficient, high-frequency control without this computational bottleneck.

To address these, the authors propose DexFuture, driven by a hierarchical design principle. The specific key objectives and their underlying motivations are:

- **Generate Dynamically Consistent Future Reference Trajectories:** The primary technical objective is to predict a meaningful future target trajectory solely from the robot's visuomotor history. This prediction must be dynamically consistent enough to guide dexterous actions effectively, replacing the need for privileged demonstration targets.
- **Decouple Coarse Future Planning from Fine-Grained Execution:** The framework is motivated by the idea that long-horizon semantic prediction (i.e., *what* the robot should achieve in the future) can occur at a slower rate than the high-frequency control (*how* the robot executes immediate contact-rich actions). This decoupling aims to simplify both prediction and control tasks.
- **Leverage Visuomotor History for Target Prediction:** Instead of predicting actions or raw pixels, the high-level predictor is designed to infer a coarse future *state* trajectory. This is motivated by the observation that policies benefit from state-level guidance, and predicting states is potentially less complex than predicting actions or raw visual data over long horizons. The use of egocentric RGB, proprioceptive, and geometric history provides rich context for this prediction.
- **Maintain the Advantages of Target-Conditioned Policies:** The research is motivated by the documented effectiveness of target-conditioned policies for high-DoF manipulation. The aim is to create a mechanism that supplies these policies with a robust, unprivileged target signal, thereby preserving their performance benefits.
- **Achieve High-Frequency Control:** By moving the slow, long-horizon prediction to a high-level module and having a low-level policy handle fast execution with interpolated targets, the system is designed to operate at frequencies suitable for stable contact interactions, such as 60 Hz. This directly contrasts with the slow speeds of online planning methods.
- **Structured Representation for Visuomotor Data:** The motivation for structured visuomotor embeddings is to focus the predictor on relevant physical entities (hands, tool, object) and their relationships, rather than processing dense, potentially irrelevant, image patches. This aims to improve prediction accuracy and efficiency by exploiting kinematic structure.

In summary, DexFuture is motivated by the need for autonomous, real-time, and high-performance bimanual dexterous tool use, achieved by intelligently separating the concerns of future-state prediction and immediate action execution within a hierarchical framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexFuture implements a hierarchical system comprising two main components: a high-level Future-State Visuomotor Target Predictor ($F_\theta$) and a low-level Target-Conditioned Structured Dexterous Policy ($\pi_\phi$).

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Problem Formulation

The system operates such that the predictor ($F_\theta$) observes a history of $K$ egocentric RGB observations ($I_\tau$) and proprioceptive/geometric cues ($p_\tau$) to generate a multi-step future target trajectory ($\hat{g}_{t+h}$). The low-level policy ($\pi_\phi$) then tracks these predicted targets, which are interpolated for intermediate timesteps ($\tilde{g}_{t+\delta}$), to produce high-frequency bimanual actions ($a_{t+\delta}$). This decouples coarse future reference generation from fine-grained action control and slow, long-horizon prediction from high-frequency execution. Unlike action-conditioned world models, the predictor does not require future actions as input, avoiding online optimization and circular dependencies.

#### 3.2. Future-State Visuomotor Target Predictor ($F_\theta$)

This module is responsible for predicting future target states. It processes visuomotor history to generate coarse guidance for the low-level policy, focusing on hand-tool-object states rather than raw pixels or actions.

#### 3.2.1. Structured Visuomotor Tokenization

To encode the visuomotor history effectively, DexFuture constructs structured embeddings instead of using dense image patches. This exploits the physical relationships between visual and proprioceptive information.

- **Visual Encoder:** A frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 visual encoder ($\Psi$) extracts patch-level image features ($V_{raw,\tau} \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times d_v}$) from each RGB frame $I_\tau$. These raw features are then projected into a structured token space ($V_\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times d}$) with a learned patch-position embedding. The hidden dimension $d$ is 256.
- **Hand-Link Tokens:** For each of the $N_h=56$ hand links (for two hands), its 3D position ($x_{t,\ell}$), 2D projected image coordinate ($u_{t,\ell}$), and finite-difference velocity ($\dot{x}_{t,\ell}$) are used to form a link-conditioned geometric feature ($\xi_{t,\ell}$). This feature, along with a learned identity embedding ($e_\ell$), forms a base link query ($q_{0,t,\ell}$). Feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) conditioned on $\xi_{t,\ell}$ adapts the query based on current link geometry and motion. The hand-link token ($z_{hand,t,\ell}$) is computed via cross-attention from this modulated query to a local $5 \times 5$ visual patch neighborhood around $u_{t,\ell}$, including relative 2D offset encodings.
- **Tool and Object Tokens:** These are constructed for scene entities. A fixed maximum number of entity slots ($E_{max}$) is used with a binary validity mask. For each entity $e$, its state ($r_{t,e}$), static geometry descriptor ($m_e$), type label ($c_e$), projected center ($x_{c,t,e}, u_{c,t,e}$), and a set of anchor points ($A_k$) are used. Each anchor point samples a visual patch feature. These anchor features are averaged ($\bar{a}_{t,e}$) and combined with entity type, state, geometry, and center information to form an entity token ($z_{ent,t,e}$). Finally, these entity tokens are pooled by type (tool or object) to yield a single tool token ($z_{tool,t}$) and a single object token ($z_{obj,t}$) per frame, resulting in a fixed-size structured token set $Z_t$ with $N=58$ tokens.

#### 3.2.2. Horizon-Conditioned Target Transformer

This transformer module predicts future structured embeddings ($\hat{Z}_{t+h}$) for a set of sparse horizons $H=\{0, 2, 4..., 16\}$.

- **Input & Memory:** The observed structured history $Z_{t-K:t}$ (where $K=8$) is projected into a transformer hidden space and flattened to form the memory ($M$).
- **Future Queries:** For each horizon $h_j \in H$, future query tokens ($Y^0_{h_j,i}$) are initialized from the latest observed structured state ($Z_t$), combined with a learned physical token slot embedding ($E_{slot}$) and a learned future output slot embedding ($E_{frame}$).
- **Horizon Conditioning:** A numeric prediction horizon $h_j$ is explicitly encoded using Fourier features ($c_{h_j}$).
- **Transformer Blocks:** Adaptive Layer-Normalization (AdaLN) conditioning, inspired by CDiT blocks, modulates transformer updates. Each block performs self-attention among future queries (MSA), cross-attention from future queries to observed memory tokens (MCA), and a feed-forward network (FFN). Gating functions ($\alpha_{sa}, \alpha_{ca}, \alpha_{ff}$) are also conditioned on the horizon. The model directly regresses future structured tokens without iterative denoising. The transformer hidden dimension is 384.
- **Output Decoding:** After $L$ blocks, the final future query tokens ($Y^L_h$) are projected back into the structured token space ($\hat{Z}_{t+h}$). These predicted structured embeddings are then decoded into auxiliary physical states (e.g., link positions, velocities) and the final 900-dimensional policy target representation ($\hat{g}_{t+h}$), which matches the layout of the demonstration target.

#### 3.2.3. Predictor Training Objective

The predictor is trained with supervised learning using demonstration data. The loss function $L_{pred}$ combines three terms:
- **Latent Consistency Loss ($L_z$):** Measures the L2-norm between the predicted current structured embeddings ($\hat{Z}_t$) and the observed current structured embeddings ($Z_t$).
- **Structured-State Loss ($L_{state}$):** Supervises future hand-link, tool, and object state predictions (positions, UV coordinates, velocities) against ground-truth values.
- **Target Loss ($L_{target}$):** Supervises the decoded policy target ($\hat{g}_{t+h}$) against the ground-truth demonstration target ($g^{demo}_{t+h}$), using component-wise Smooth-L1 distance, with special handling for quaternion components.

#### 3.3. Target-Conditioned Structured Dexterous Policy ($\pi_\phi$)

DexFuture can be integrated with any structured, target-conditioned low-level controller. For this work, a target-conditioned per-link transformer policy, inspired by PhysGraph, is used.

- **Input:** The policy receives the current simulator state ($s_t$) and the predicted/interpolated target ($\tilde{g}_t$).
- **Tokenization:** The bimanual hand-tool-object system, along with the target, is tokenized into $N_p$ hand-link tokens, scene tokens (tool and object), and a policy token.
- **Policy Network:** A transformer encoder processes these tokens. The final policy token parameterizes a Gaussian action distribution ($a_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(s_t, \tilde{g}_t), \Sigma_\phi)$), where $\mu_\phi$ is the action mean and $\Sigma_\phi$ is a learned diagonal covariance.
- **Training:** The policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with imitation-style tracking rewards. Rewards are a weighted sum of exponential tracking terms for wrist pose, hand-link/fingertip positions, object pose/velocity, and energy regularization. Training involves two stages: first using offline causal RGB from demonstrations, then switching to online rendered RGB from rollouts for closed-loop stability.

#### 3.4. Hierarchical Receding-Horizon Execution

During inference, the system executes in a receding-horizon manner:
- At a refresh time $t_j$, the predictor ($F_\theta$) processes the recent history and generates a sparse future target sequence ($\hat{g}_{t_j:t_j+H}$).
- For each low-level control step ($t_j + \delta$) before the next refresh, the policy ($\pi_\phi$) receives a linearly interpolated target ($\tilde{g}_{t_j+\delta}$) and computes an action from the current state.
- This allows the predictor to operate at a slower timescale (e.g., refreshes every $S$ steps, with $S=16$ policy steps) while the policy remains reactive at the control timescale (60 Hz). The predicted targets provide coarse future state guidance, enabling the low-level policy to perform fine contact-level corrections.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DexFuture focused on its performance on challenging bimanual dexterous tool-use tasks from the OakInk2 dataset, including cutting, pouring, wiping, shearing, and stirring.

- **Performance with Predicted Targets:**
 - DexFuture, utilizing predicted future targets, achieved approximately 90% of the average success rate of privileged baselines (59.69% for DexFuture versus 66.52% for privileged baselines ManipTrans and PhysGraph). This indicates that the system largely retains the benefits of target-conditioned dexterous control without requiring ground-truth future states.
 - In the fruit-knife cutting task, DexFuture demonstrated comparable or slightly superior performance, achieving an 89.79% success rate and 0.61cm tool/object translation error, compared to the privileged PhysGraph baseline's 87.87% success rate and 0.98cm error.
- **Impact of Future Targets:**
 - Ablation studies comparing DexFuture to a "no-target" variant of PhysGraph demonstrated the critical role of future targets. The no-target policy exhibited significantly degraded performance, often rendering high-DoF dexterous tool use nearly infeasible (e.g., 4.16% success rate for bread cutting compared to 83.49% for DexFuture on the same task). This finding supports the claim that future target guidance is essential for dexterous manipulation.
- **Future-State Visuomotor Target Prediction Quality:**
 - Independent evaluation of the predictor's accuracy showed varied performance across tasks. It performed accurately on cutting and stirring tasks (e.g., 3D error 0.87cm, PCK@10 99.78% for fruit-knife cutting) but faced greater challenges in tasks involving narrow contact regions or abrupt motion changes, such as shearing (e.g., 3D error 5.54cm, PCK@10 32.93% for scissor shearing). The predictor's performance generally correlated with the overall policy's success rate on those tasks, implying that prediction difficulty directly impacts downstream control. All tested tasks were unseen during training.
- **Ablation on Prediction Horizon:**
 - Analysis of different receding horizon schedules (h8, h16, h24) indicated that a medium horizon (h16, corresponding to horizons {0, 2..., 16}) provided the most effective trade-off between prediction accuracy and the extent of future guidance. Shorter horizons were easier to predict but offered less guidance, while longer horizons were more challenging to predict reliably. This supports the hierarchical design choice of sparse, long-horizon prediction.
- **Qualitative Results:**
 - Visualizations of robot rollouts confirmed that DexFuture produced stable and coordinated hand-tool-object interactions, closely resembling the behavior of oracle target-conditioned policies. In contrast, the no-target policy frequently failed to maintain stable coordination. This suggests that DexFuture not only improves scalar success metrics but also restores the structured coordination that is fundamental to dexterous tool use.
- **Comparison with Action-Conditioned Planning:**
 - DexFuture was compared against a DexWM-style action-conditioned world model planning baseline, adapted to use state-based CEM planning for feasibility on a single GPU. While DexWM-style planning with ground-truth goal images and fine-tuning on OakInk2 could achieve some success (e.g., 69.57% SR with horizon 1), its execution speed was approximately 0.24 Hz on a single 3090Ti GPU.
 - In contrast, DexFuture operated at the policy control rate of 60 Hz, demonstrating a computational speed advantage of approximately 250 times. This substantial speed difference is crucial for practical, contact-rich dexterous manipulation, where high-frequency updates are necessary. This finding underscores the practical advantage of amortizing future target generation into a low-frequency predictor over online action-sequence optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DexFuture represents a notable advancement in the field of robot learning for dexterous manipulation, particularly for bimanual tool use. Its significance and potential impact stem from addressing critical limitations of prior methods:

- **Enabling Autonomous Dexterous Manipulation:** By removing the dependency on "privileged" future demonstration targets, DexFuture moves robotic systems closer to fully autonomous operation in complex manipulation tasks. Previous target-conditioned policies, while effective, were fundamentally limited by their reliance on unavailable future information at deployment. DexFuture demonstrates that high performance can be achieved using only observable visuomotor history.
- **Practical Real-time Performance:** The framework's ability to operate at 60 Hz, approximately 250 times faster than state-of-the-art action-conditioned planning methods like DexWM-style CEM, is a significant practical advantage. This high control frequency is essential for stable and reactive control in contact-rich manipulation tasks, making DexFuture more viable for real-world robotic applications where real-time feedback is crucial.
- **Efficient Learning and Control Paradigm:** The hierarchical design effectively decouples the computationally intensive, long-horizon prediction task from the high-frequency, fine-grained action execution. This modularity not only simplifies the design and training of each component but also allows for optimized resource allocation, with the predictor operating at a slower semantic timescale and the policy at a faster control timescale. This paradigm offers a pathway to scaling dexterous robot skills.
- **Generalizability of Target-Conditioned Control:** DexFuture validates that the performance benefits of target-conditioned control can be largely retained even when targets are predicted rather than directly provided from demonstrations. This finding suggests a broader applicability for target-conditioned policies beyond demonstration-guided scenarios.
- **Structured Visuomotor Representation:** The introduction of structured visuomotor tokenization allows the high-level predictor to focus on semantically relevant physical entities and their relationships, leading to more robust and efficient future-state prediction compared to processing raw, dense visual information.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The research identifies remaining challenges, particularly regarding future target prediction under difficult contacts, narrow contact regions, or abrupt motion changes. This points towards future research directions in uncertainty-aware, contact-aware prediction, stronger visual grounding under occlusion, and the ultimate deployment to real robotic hardware, which would further amplify the impact of this foundational work.

In conclusion, DexFuture offers a methodologically sound and practically efficient framework that enhances the autonomy and real-time capabilities of bimanual dexterous robots for complex tool use, thereby contributing to the development of more capable and deployable robotic systems.
