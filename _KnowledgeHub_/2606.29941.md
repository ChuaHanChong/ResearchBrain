# Seeing Touch from Motion: A Unified Modality-Aware Visuo-Tactile Policy with Tactile Motion Correlation

> [!summary] Summary
> ViTacMotor, a visuo-tactile policy from Fudan University, integrates a Tactile Motion Correlation (TMC) representation with a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. This system improves robotic manipulation in contact-rich tasks by resolving perceptual ambiguity in optical tactile sensing and achieving modality-aware fusion, demonstrating higher success rates across diverse real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing optical tactile representations (raw images, cumulative motion fields) suffer from perceptual ambiguity, failing to distinguish subtle, fine-grained contact states critical for precise manipulation.
- Current visuo-tactile fusion methods either oversimplify cross-modal interactions (losing modality characteristics) or neglect complementarity (separate policies), leading to suboptimal performance in contact-rich tasks.
- Robotic policies struggled to robustly interpret nuanced contact information for tasks requiring precise physical interaction and fine-grained force control.

## Method

- Proposed ==Tactile Motion Correlation (TMC)==, a representation computed as the ==pixel-wise dot product of transient and cumulative tactile motion==, explicitly distinguishing fine-grained contact states and encoding contact intensity.
- Designed **ViTacMotor**, a unified visuo-tactile policy that uses a ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture for multimodal fusion.
- The MoT architecture employs ==modality-specific parameter decoupling== and ==shared self-attention== to balance cross-modal interaction with the preservation of individual modality characteristics.

## Results

- ViTacMotor achieved superior success rates on four real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, including Tube Collection (**73.3%** whole task success) and Whiteboard Erasing (**86.7%** whole task success), outperforming existing baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Tactile Motion Correlation (TMC) representation significantly improves fine-grained contact state perception and implicit force inference, reducing performance without it.
- The Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) fusion mechanism proved more effective than simpler concatenation or cross-attention methods, and the approach exhibited robustness to environmental and object variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The dynamic correlation between transient and cumulative tactile motion provides explicit, unambiguous cues for distinguishing fine-grained contact states, such as making contact versus releasing contact.
> - The magnitude of the Tactile Motion Correlation (TMC) can implicitly represent the applied contact force, which is valuable for tasks requiring fine-grained force control.
> - A Mixture-of-Transformers architecture effectively integrates visual and tactile data by enabling rich cross-modal interactions while preserving the distinct properties of each sensory modality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation, particularly in tasks requiring precise physical interaction, frequently relies on integrating visual and tactile sensory feedback. The broader research landscape in this area focuses on developing robotic policies that can perceive global scene context through vision and local contact details through touch. Optical tactile sensors, which employ internal cameras to monitor the deformation of an elastic gel surface, have gained prominence due to their high spatial resolution and multi-dimensional force sensing capabilities.

Previous approaches to utilizing optical tactile sensors have largely concentrated on two main types of tactile representations: raw tactile images and cumulative motion fields. Raw tactile images primarily capture appearance changes, while cumulative motion fields reflect the aggregate deformation of the gel surface. However, the existing literature indicates that both of these representations are susceptible to perceptual ambiguity, making it challenging for robots to distinguish subtle, fine-grained contact states crucial for tasks like precision insertion or object wiping. This ambiguity limits the robustness and accuracy of contact-rich manipulation policies.

In parallel, research on multimodal fusion for robotic control has explored strategies ranging from simple feature concatenation to separate modality-specific networks. While feature-level fusion can enable cross-modal interactions, it often struggles to preserve the unique characteristics of each modality. Conversely, decision-level fusion respects modality individuality but may not fully leverage cross-modal complementarity. This presents a challenge in designing fusion architectures that can effectively balance these aspects.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a novel tactile representation that aims to resolve perceptual ambiguity and by introducing a fusion architecture designed to optimize the integration of visual and tactile information. It contributes to the field by investigating dynamic priors in tactile motion and applying a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture, which represents an advancement in how multimodal sensory data can be processed for complex manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance a robot's ability to perform contact-rich manipulation tasks by improving both tactile perception and visuo-tactile fusion. The authors identify two main challenges that motivate their work:

**Challenge 1: Ambiguity in Existing Tactile Representations for Fine-Grained Contact States.**
- **Motivation:** Contact-rich manipulation tasks demand precise perception of subtle physical interactions. Existing methods that use raw tactile images or cumulative motion fields struggle to distinguish fine-grained contact states (e.g., making contact versus releasing contact). Raw images mainly capture appearance changes, which can be visually similar for distinct contact states. Cumulative motion fields represent overall gel deformation but also exhibit similar patterns for different contact dynamics. This perceptual ambiguity hinders a robot's ability to interpret nuanced contact information accurately, leading to reduced precision and robustness in manipulation. The authors hypothesize that analyzing the *dynamics* of tactile motion, rather than just its static appearance or cumulative state, might provide more discriminative cues.

**Challenge 2: Ineffective Fusion of Visual and Tactile Modalities.**
- **Motivation:** Successful contact-rich manipulation typically requires the complementary cooperation of global visual perception (for coarse motion planning and context) and local tactile feedback (for fine-grained interaction). Current visuo-tactile fusion strategies often fall into two categories:
 1. **Feature-level concatenation/simple attention:** These methods risk overlooking the distinct properties of each modality, potentially allowing one modality to dominate or dilute the unique information of the other.
 2. **Decision-level fusion (separate policies):** While respecting modality-specific differences, these approaches may neglect crucial cross-modal interactions, preventing the policy from exploiting synergies between vision and touch.
- The motivation here is to develop a unified fusion framework that can simultaneously model cross-modal interactions to exploit complementarity *and* preserve modality-specific characteristics to maintain the integrity of each sensory stream. This is critical for achieving a robust and truly synergistic visuo-tactile policy.

Based on these challenges, the key objectives are:
1. **To discover and leverage dynamic priors in tactile motion:** Specifically, to identify how the correlation between transient and cumulative motion can explicitly distinguish fine-grained contact states.
2. **To propose a novel tactile representation:** To develop "Tactile Motion Correlation" (TMC) as a motion-aware representation that resolves perceptual ambiguity and captures contact intensity.
3. **To design a unified, modality-aware visuo-tactile policy:** To introduce "ViTacMotor," an architecture based on the Mixture-of-Transformers, capable of capturing cross-modal complementarity while preserving modality-specific properties.
4. **To validate the proposed method:** To demonstrate ViTacMotor's superior performance and robustness on a variety of challenging real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks using different optical tactile sensors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named ViTacMotor, integrates a novel tactile representation called Tactile Motion Correlation (TMC) within a unified modality-aware visuo-tactile policy framework built upon the Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture.

#### 3.1. Tactile Motion Correlation (TMC) Representation

The authors address the ambiguity of existing tactile representations by exploring the dynamic priors of tactile motion.
1. **Motion Definitions:**
 - **Cumulative Motion ($\text{M}^{\text{cumu}}_{\text{t}}$):** Defined as the optical flow between the current tactile frame ($\text{T}_{\text{t}}$) and an initial non-contact frame ($\text{T}_0$). This captures the overall deformation relative to the initial state.
 - **Transient Motion ($\text{M}^{\text{tran}}_{\text{t}}$):** Defined as the optical flow between consecutive tactile frames ($\text{T}_{\text{t-1}}$ and $\text{T}_{\text{t}}$). This captures instantaneous deformation.
 Optical flow is estimated using an efficient algorithm (DIS) to maintain real-time performance.

2. **Discovery of Correlation Priors:** Through analysis experiments with optical sensors, the authors observe distinct correlation patterns between transient and cumulative motion for various contact states (e.g., no contact, making contact, stable contact, releasing contact, sliding contact).
 - **No Contact/Out of Contact:** Both motions are nearly zero.
 - **Making Contact:** Both motions exhibit the same direction, reflecting continuous gel deformation accumulation.
 - **Stable Contact:** Transient motion nearly disappears, while cumulative motion remains, indicating deformation stabilization.
 - **Releasing Contact:** The two motions show opposite directions, signifying gel recovery.
 - **Sliding Contact:** The dot product exhibits positive-negative separation, with the transition reflecting the sliding direction.

3. **TMC Computation via Dot Product:** Based on these observations, TMC is proposed as a pixel-wise dot product of the transient and cumulative motion vectors:
 $\text{Corr}_{\text{t}}(\text{x}) = \text{M}^{\text{tran}}_{\text{t}}(\text{x}) \cdot \text{M}^{\text{cumu}}_{\text{t}}(\text{x})$
 where $\text{x}$ is a pixel location. The resulting value encodes the correlation between instantaneous and aggregate gel deformation.

4. **Physical Interpretability:** The sign of the dot product differentiates contact states (positive for making contact, negative for releasing contact, near zero for no contact/stable contact). The magnitude of the dot product is found to correlate positively with the applied contact force during making contact, indicating its ability to reflect contact intensity. TMC is also presented as sensor-agnostic due to its reliance on underlying gel deformation principles.

#### 3.2. Unified Modality-aware Visuo-Tactile Fusion

The ViTacMotor policy uses a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture to fuse multimodal observations, respecting modality-specific properties while enabling cross-modal interactions.

1. **Multimodal Observation Space:** At each time step, the robot receives:
 - **Visual Input ($\text{I}_{\text{t}}$):** RGB images from wrist and third-view cameras.
 - **Tactile Input ($\text{T}_0, \text{T}_{\text{t}}, \text{T}_{\text{t-1}}$):** Initial, current, and previous tactile frames.
 - **Proprioceptive State ($\text{p}_{\text{t}}$):** Robot's end-effector pose and gripper width.

2. **Feature Encoding:**
 - Visual raw images are processed by pre-trained encoders to yield visual embeddings ($\text{e}^{\text{I}}_{\text{t}}$).
 - Tactile raw images ($\text{T}_{\text{t}}$) are processed by pre-trained encoders for tactile embeddings ($\text{e}^{\text{T}}_{\text{t}}$).
 - The computed TMC representation ($\text{Corr}_{\text{t}}$) is passed through a ResNet encoder to generate motion correlation embeddings ($\text{e}^{\text{Corr}}_{\text{t}}$).
 - Proprioceptive state ($\text{p}_{\text{t}}$) is projected into a latent space via an MLP encoder to obtain proprioceptive embeddings ($\text{e}^{\text{p}}_{\text{t}}$).

3. **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Fusion:**
 - **Modality-Specific Projections:** For each modality $m \in \{I, T, Corr, p\}$, separate learnable projection matrices ($\text{W}^{(m)}_{Q}, \text{W}^{(m)}_{K}, \text{W}^{(m)}_{V}$) are applied to compute query, key, and value vectors ($\text{Q}^{(m)}_{\text{t}}, \text{K}^{(m)}_{\text{t}}, \text{V}^{(m)}_{\text{t}}$). This decouples parameters to maintain modality individuality.
 - **Shared Self-Attention:** All modality embeddings are jointly processed through a *shared* self-attention mechanism. This allows for rich cross-modal interaction and information exchange.
 - **Modality-Specific Refinement:** The output of the self-attention is then refined through modality-specific output projections ($\text{W}^{(m)}_{O}$), feed-forward networks (FFN$^{(m)}$), and layer normalization (LN$^{(m)}$). This re-emphasizes modality-specific processing after the shared interaction.

4. **Action Prediction:** The aggregated fused embeddings are fed into a Transformer-based decoder, which autoregressively predicts the action chunk.

#### 3.3. Training Details

- **Objective Function:** The model is trained using a $\beta$-VAE objective, combining an L1 prediction loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}$) for action accuracy and a KL divergence loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}$) to regularize the posterior distribution towards a standard Gaussian prior.
- **Optimization:** The Adam optimizer is used, with a batch size of 16 and an initial learning rate of $2 \times 10^{-4}$ with a cosine decay schedule.
- **Hardware and Duration:** Training takes approximately 5 hours per task on a single RTX 3090 Ti GPU.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware:** Two 6-DoF Agilex robotic arms with 1-DoF parallel grippers. Two wrist-view cameras and one third-view camera for visual input. Two types of optical tactile sensors are used: one with random-distributed dense markers and one with regularly-arranged sparse markers.
- **Tasks:** Four real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks are used for evaluation: Tube Collection, Lightbulb Insertion (requiring precise contact state), Whiteboard Erasing, and Pencil Sharpening (requiring fine-grained force control).
- **Baselines:** The method is compared against Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT), Diffusion Policy (DP), ACT + Tactile Image (ACT+T), Policy Consensus, and TactileACT.
- **Evaluation:** 15 trials are conducted for each method per task, and average success rates are reported.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the efficacy and robustness of ViTacMotor across various challenging contact-rich manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Comparison

Table 1 presents the success rates of ViTacMotor against several baselines across four tasks, categorized by their primary requirements.

- **Tasks Requiring Precise In-Hand State Information (Tube Collection, Lightbulb Insertion):**
 - **Tube Collection (35 demos):** ViTacMotor achieved 93.3% success in grasping, 80.0% for inserting into the first hole, 73.3% for the second hole, and 73.3% for the whole task. This outperformed all baselines, with the closest being TactileACT (93.3% grasp, 73.3% 1st hole, 60.0% 2nd hole, 60.0% whole task). Visual-only policies (ACT, DP) showed significantly lower performance (53.3% and 40.0% whole task success, respectively), indicating the crucial role of tactile feedback.
 - **Lightbulb Insertion (60 demos):** ViTacMotor achieved 60.0% success in aligning with the socket and 40.0% for insertion and light activation, resulting in a 40.0% whole task success rate. This matched TactileACT's performance and surpassed other baselines (e.g., ACT at 13.3%, DP at 6.7%).

- **Tasks Requiring Fine-Grained Force Control (Whiteboard Erasing, Pencil Sharpening):**
 - **Whiteboard Erasing (50 demos):** ViTacMotor achieved 100.0% grasp success, 86.7% for the first erase, 86.7% for the second erase, and 86.7% for the whole task. This represents an improvement over TactileACT (73.3% whole task) and other baselines (ACT at 46.7%, DP at 40.0%). The ability of TMC to implicitly encode force magnitude is cited as a reason for this enhanced performance.
 - **Pencil Sharpening (40 demos):** ViTacMotor obtained 60.0% success for insertion and sharpening, and 60.0% for placing into the holder, leading to a 60.0% whole task success rate. This again demonstrated superior performance compared to TactileACT (46.7% whole task) and other baselines (ACT at 33.3%, DP at 33.3%).

These results indicate that policies incorporating tactile information generally outperform visual-only approaches. Furthermore, ViTacMotor, with its specific tactile representation and fusion strategy, generally achieved the highest success rates across the challenging contact-rich manipulation tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation and Discussion

- **Effectiveness of TMC (Table 2):** Removing TMC from ViTacMotor resulted in a performance drop. For whiteboard erasing, success fell from 86.7% to 66.7%. For tube collection, success decreased from 73.3% to 60.0%. This supports the claim that TMC facilitates fine-grained contact state perception and implicit force inference, which are critical for tasks requiring consistent pressure or precise alignment.

- **Effectiveness of MoT-based Fusion (Table 2 & 4):** Replacing the MoT-based fusion strategy with simpler concatenation or cross-attention methods led to performance degradation. For instance, in whiteboard erasing, concatenation yielded 73.3% success compared to MoT's 86.7%. In tube collection, MoT achieved 73.3% while concatenation reached 66.7%. This indicates that the MoT architecture, by capturing cross-modal interactions while preserving modality-specific properties, provides a more effective fusion mechanism than conventional approaches.

- **Universality of TMC (Table 1 & B.1 in Appendix):** The experiments utilized two types of optical tactile sensors (dense-marker and sparse-marker sensors) across different tasks. ViTacMotor consistently showed strong performance with both sensor types, supporting the universality of the TMC representation. The authors attribute this to TMC's focus on underlying gel deformation rather than sensor-specific raw visual appearance.

- **Flexibility of TMC (Table 3):** When TMC was embedded into existing visual-only policies like ACT and DP, it led to performance improvements on the whiteboard erasing task. For ACT, success improved from 60.0% (raw image) to 73.3% (TMC). For DP, it improved from 53.3% (raw image) to 66.7% (TMC). This suggests that TMC can be flexibly integrated into various policy architectures to enhance tactile perception.

#### 4.3. Robustness to Environment and Object Variations

Qualitative results (Figure 6 and Figure 10 in Appendix) demonstrate ViTacMotor's robustness to various disturbances:
- **Environment Variations:** The policy successfully completed tasks under both non-uniform and uniform temporal-varying lighting conditions.
- **Object Variations:** The policy adapted to changes in objects, such as different colored erasers and altered ink appearances in the whiteboard erasing task, and the replacement of a transparent tube with a cylinder in the tube collection task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and implications for the field of robotic manipulation and multimodal sensing:

- **Advancement in Tactile Perception:** The paper identifies and formalizes the principle that the correlation between transient and cumulative tactile motion offers explicit discrimination of fine-grained contact states. This insight addresses a fundamental limitation in existing optical tactile representations, which often suffer from perceptual ambiguity. The proposed Tactile Motion Correlation (TMC) representation provides a more informative and physically interpretable signal, distinguishing subtle contact dynamics (e.g., making vs. releasing contact) and implicitly encoding contact force magnitude. This enhances a robot's microscopic understanding of its interaction with the environment.

- **Unified and Modality-Aware Multimodal Fusion:** The introduction of ViTacMotor, leveraging the Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture for visuo-tactile fusion, represents a novel approach to integrate heterogeneous sensory data. By employing modality-specific parameter decoupling alongside a shared self-attention mechanism, the policy effectively balances the capture of cross-modal complementarity with the preservation of unique modality characteristics. This fusion strategy overcomes limitations of prior methods that either oversimplify multimodal interactions or neglect inter-modal synergies.

- **Improved Performance and Robustness in Contact-Rich Tasks:** The extensive experimental validation on four challenging real-world tasks demonstrates the superior performance and robustness of ViTacMotor compared to existing baselines. The demonstrated ability to achieve higher success rates in tasks requiring precise contact state information and fine-grained force control signifies a step towards more dexterous and reliable robotic manipulation in complex scenarios.

- **Potential for Generalization and Adaptability:** The sensor-agnostic nature of TMC, derived from fundamental gel deformation principles rather than specific visual patterns, suggests its potential universality across different optical tactile sensors. This could simplify the deployment of advanced tactile perception capabilities across diverse robotic platforms and hardware. The MoT-based fusion framework, being a general-purpose multimodal architecture, could also be applicable to other multimodal sensing problems in robotics beyond visuo-tactile integration.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future investigations into dynamic tactile priors and advanced multimodal learning architectures for robotics. It suggests avenues for exploring more complex contact phenomena and for developing manipulation policies that are even more adaptable to unseen objects and environments. The identified limitations regarding extreme visual disturbances and object position variations also provide clear directions for subsequent research focused on improving generalization in contact-rich manipulation.

In summary, this research advances the state of the art in visuo-tactile robotic manipulation by introducing a more discriminative tactile representation and a sophisticated, modality-aware fusion policy. This contributes to the development of robots capable of performing fine-grained, contact-rich tasks with increased precision, robustness, and adaptability.
