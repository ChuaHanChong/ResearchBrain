# GRPO-Guard: Mitigating Implicit Over-Optimization in Flow Matching via Regulated Clipping

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Kling Team at Kuaishou Technology and Sun Yat-Sen University introduced GRPO-Guard, an approach that mitigates implicit over-optimization in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for flow-matching generative models. By rectifying the importance-ratio clipping mechanism and balancing policy gradients, GRPO-Guard enables stable policy learning, preventing reward hacking and yielding consistently higher 'gold scores' for image quality and human preference.

## Problem

- GRPO-based reinforcement learning for flow-matching models frequently suffers from 'implicit over-optimization' or 'reward hacking,' where proxy reward scores increase while actual generation quality deteriorates.
- The importance-ratio clipping mechanism, crucial for stabilizing PPO-style algorithms like GRPO, fails in diffusion models due to a fundamental design mismatch between discrete token probabilities (LLMs) and continuous Gaussian state transitions.
- This mismatch leads to a left-shifted mean and inconsistent variance in the importance-ratio distribution across denoising timesteps, preventing effective clipping of overconfident positive updates and causing imbalanced gradient regulation.

## Method

- **GRPO-Guard** introduces ==Ratio Normalization== (RatioNorm) to standardize the log-importance ratio distribution at each denoising step, ensuring its mean is near zero and its variance is consistent, thus restoring symmetric clipping.
- A gradient reweighting strategy is applied to balance policy gradient magnitudes across different denoising timesteps, preventing any single step from dominating the optimization process and alleviating single-step over-optimization.
- The proposed method integrates RatioNorm and gradient reweighting into a modified GRPO policy loss function, designed to fundamentally correct algorithmic flaws in continuous state spaces.

## Results

- GRPO-Guard successfully mitigates reward hacking, maintaining or improving 'gold scores' (HPSv2, ImageReward, UnifiedReward) while baseline methods exhibit a sharp decline, e.g., achieving an Average Gold Score of **0.89** compared to **0.84** for Flow-GRPO on GenEval.
- Qualitative analysis and human evaluation confirm GRPO-Guard's superior visual quality, detail richness, text-prompt consistency, and overall preference over baselines, which often produce distorted or artifact-ridden outputs.
- The RatioNorm component effectively re-centers the importance-ratio distribution to a mean near 1 and aligns its variance, enabling balanced clipping of both positive and negative updates, while gradient reweighting reduces gradient magnitude variation from **20x** to **2.5x** across timesteps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A rigorous mathematical analysis reveals that directly applying GRPO to diffusion models introduces a quadratic term in the log-importance ratio, causing a timestep-dependent negative bias and inconsistent variance in the importance ratio distribution.
> - This biased and inconsistent importance ratio prevents the PPO-style clipping mechanism from effectively constraining positive advantage samples, leading to unchecked gradient updates and implicit over-optimization.
> - Policy gradient magnitudes vary significantly across denoising timesteps, with low-noise steps producing disproportionately large gradients, which can lead to overfitting and multi-step degradation of model capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is strategically positioned at the intersection of advanced generative models and reinforcement learning (RL) for alignment, addressing a critical challenge known as "reward over-optimization" or "reward hacking."

**Generative AI and Diffusion Models:** The foundation of this work lies in the remarkable progress of flow-based diffusion models for visual generation. State-of-the-art models like Stable Diffusion 3.5 (SD3.5), Flux, Qwen-Image for images, and Kling for video generation, have revolutionized synthetic media creation. These models decompose the generation process into iterative denoising steps, learning to predict velocity fields or noise.

**Reinforcement Learning for Alignment (RLHF/RLAIF):** A significant trend in modern AI, particularly with large models, is the shift from supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to interactive, reinforcement learning-style alignment. This paradigm, famously known as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or its automated counterpart RLAIF, has been highly successful in aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences and instructions (e.g., PPO, DPO).

**Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Within the RL for alignment landscape, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has emerged as an effective and stable approach, particularly in large-scale LLM deployment (e.g., Deepseek-R1, OpenAI O1 System Card). GRPO distinguishes itself by performing policy updates based on the relative performance of a group of samples, rather than individual samples, which contributes to its stability.

**GRPO for Diffusion Models:** Building on GRPO's success in LLMs, recent works like Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO have adapted this approach to flow-matching models. This adaptation involves introducing stochasticity into the deterministic ODE-based flow matching via Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) formulations, enabling exploration and group-based optimization. Other related works, such as MixGRPO, Flow-CPS, TempFlowGRPO, and G2RPO, have focused on improving the efficiency of GRPO-based methods, ensuring noise consistency, and addressing reward sparsity in multi-step SDE trajectories.

**The Problem of Reward Over-optimization:** Despite these advancements, a persistent and critical challenge across both LLMs and diffusion models undergoing RL-based alignment is "reward over-optimization" or "reward hacking." This occurs when models learn to exploit imperfections in the proxy reward model (which approximates true human preferences or task-specific metrics), leading to inflated proxy scores while simultaneously degrading actual generation quality, diversity, or alignment with the true objective. Common mitigation strategies include heavy KL-divergence regularization, importance ratio clipping, ensemble reward models, or early stopping. However, these often come with trade-offs, such as slowing down learning or failing to completely address the root cause.

**GRPO-Guard's Contribution:** GRPO-Guard specifically identifies and addresses an overlooked fundamental issue within the GRPO framework when applied to diffusion models: the failure of the importance-ratio clipping mechanism. It argues that this failure stems from a design mismatch between the discrete token probabilities in LLMs (for which PPO/GRPO were originally formulated) and the continuous Gaussian state transition probabilities in diffusion models. While previous works on GRPO for diffusion often focused on efficiency or reward accuracy, GRPO-Guard hones in on the algorithmic stability of the clipping mechanism itself, offering a robust solution to a widespread problem that severely limits the practical utility of aligned generative models. By rectifying this core algorithmic flaw, GRPO-Guard fills a critical gap, enabling more reliable and high-quality alignment in the burgeoning field of RL-optimized generative AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the GRPO-Guard paper is to mitigate the pervasive issue of "implicit over-optimization" or "reward hacking" that plagues GRPO-based reinforcement learning frameworks when applied to flow-matching generative models.

This objective is motivated by a critical analysis of the current state of GRPO applications in diffusion models:
1. **Observed Problem: Implicit Over-Optimization:** While GRPO-based methods like Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO have shown promise in improving alignment with task-specific rewards in diffusion models, empirical observations reveal a systematic problem. As training progresses, these models often enter an "implicit over-optimization stage." In this phase, the proxy reward score (from the learned reward model) continues to increase, creating an illusion of improvement. However, critical "gold scores" – essential metrics reflecting actual image quality, diversity, detail richness, and text-prompt consistency (human-evaluated or robust objective metrics) – sharply deteriorate. This makes the learned policy impractical for real-world use, as demonstrated vividly in Figure 1, where high proxy scores correlate with drastically degraded visual outputs.

2. **Identified Root Cause: Flawed Importance-Ratio Clipping:** The core of the problem is traced back to a failure in the importance-ratio clipping mechanism, a crucial component of PPO-style algorithms (including GRPO) designed to stabilize training by bounding policy updates.
 - **Systematic Mean Shift:** The empirical analysis shows that in diffusion models, the importance-ratio distribution systematically shifts leftward, meaning its mean consistently falls below 1. This is fundamentally different from the ideal scenario where the ratio should be centered around 1 for symmetric clipping.
 - **Inconsistent Variance:** Furthermore, the variance of this distribution varies significantly across denoising timesteps. At low-noise steps, clipping occurs excessively, while at high-noise steps, it is rarely activated.
 - **Consequence:** This left-shifted and uneven distribution prevents samples with positive advantages (those that performed better than the group average) from reaching the upper clipping bound (1 + ε). As a result, gradients from "overconfident positive updates" remain largely unconstrained. Conversely, negative advantage samples are more heavily clipped. This imbalance in gradient regulation is identified as the root cause of the implicit reward hacking observed in Flow-GRPO and similar methods.

3. **Fundamental Design Mismatch:** The authors pinpoint the underlying reason for these distributional anomalies: a fundamental design mismatch. GRPO's formulation, particularly how it computes log-probabilities and the subsequent importance ratio, was originally conceived for LLMs, which operate on discrete token probabilities. When directly applied to diffusion models, which deal with continuous Gaussian state transition probabilities, a quadratic term is introduced in the log-importance ratio calculation. This term is responsible for the timestep-dependent negative bias and variance inconsistency.

4. **Limitations of Existing Solutions:** Existing mitigation strategies, such as introducing a heavy KL-divergence penalty, help reduce drift from the reference policy and thus over-optimization. However, they can also slow down the improvement of both proxy and true performance metrics, potentially leading to under-optimization or degraded overall performance. The motivation is to find a simpler, more direct, and effective algorithmic solution that addresses the clipping failure without such trade-offs.

In summary, the key motivation is to diagnose and provide a robust, generalizable solution to the "implicit over-optimization" problem in GRPO-based flow-matching models, by correcting the fundamental algorithmic flaws in the importance-ratio clipping mechanism that arise from the modality-specific differences between language and diffusion models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GRPO-Guard introduces a two-pronged approach, consisting of ratio normalization (RatioNorm) and a gradient reweighting strategy, to fundamentally rectify the issues observed in importance-ratio clipping within GRPO-based flow-matching models.

#### 3.1. Analysis of Inherent Distributional Anomalies

The methodology begins with a rigorous mathematical analysis to understand why the importance-ratio clipping mechanism, which functions ideally in LLMs, fails in diffusion models.
- **Log-Probability Derivation:** The authors derive the log-probability for diffusion models based on Gaussian probabilities, contrasting it with discrete token probabilities in LLMs.
- **Log-Importance Ratio Derivation:** They then meticulously derive the expression for the log-importance ratio (`log r_t(θ)`) for diffusion models. This derivation reveals a critical quadratic term that introduces a timestep-dependent negative bias. The expected value of `log r_t(θ)` is shown to be negative (`- ||Δμ_θ||^2 / (2σ_t^2 dt)`), meaning the importance ratio `r_t(θ)` is consistently below 1.
- **Variance Inconsistency:** The analysis also shows that the variance of `r_t(θ)` is dependent on denoising scheduler parameters (`σ_t`, `dt`), leading to substantial differences across timesteps.
- **Conclusion:** This analysis mathematically confirms the empirical observation that the importance-ratio distribution is left-shifted (mean < 1) and has inconsistent variance (Figure 2(b) and 2(c)). This directly explains why positive-advantage samples rarely hit the upper clipping bound (`1 + ε`), leading to unconstrained updates and over-optimization.

#### 3.2. Regulated Clipping with Ratio Normalization (RatioNorm)

To address the left-shifted mean and inconsistent variance, GRPO-Guard proposes RatioNorm:
- **Purpose:** RatioNorm aims to standardize the `log r_t(θ)` distribution at each denoising step, ensuring its mean is close to zero (and thus `r_t(θ)`'s mean is near 1) and its variance is consistent across timesteps. This allows the PPO clipping mechanism to function as intended, symmetrically constraining both positive and negative updates.
- **Mathematical Formulation:** The key transformation is:
 `log ˆr_t(θ) = σ_t * sqrt(dt) * (log r_t(θ) + ||Δμ_θ||^2 / (2σ_t^2 dt))`
 This transformation simplifies to `log ˆr_t(θ) = -Δμ_θ · ϵ`, which has an expected mean of zero.
- **Mechanism:** By removing the negative bias (the quadratic term) and accounting for noise coefficients, RatioNorm ensures that `ˆr_t(θ)`'s distribution is centered and more uniform across timesteps. This restores the clipping mechanism's ability to truncate gradients from overconfident positive samples, mitigating imbalance-induced over-optimization and stabilizing policy learning.

#### 3.3. Gradient Balancing Strategy

Even with RatioNorm, the authors observe that policy gradients can still vary significantly across timesteps, with low-noise steps producing disproportionately large gradients. This can lead to the policy overfitting to specific noise conditions and single-step over-optimization.
- **Gradient Analysis:** The paper re-examines the policy gradient formulation, showing how it is influenced by terms like `∆μ_θ`, `σ_t`, and `dt`. After RatioNorm, the gradient scale becomes proportional to `β dt ϵ`.
- **Reweighting Factor:** To equalize gradient magnitudes across different timesteps, GRPO-Guard introduces a reweighting factor `δ = 1/dt` into the policy loss (or `δ = β/dt` for DanceGRPO, where `β` is a timestep-dependent coefficient).
- **Combined Policy Loss:** The final policy loss incorporates both RatioNorm (through `ˆr_t(θ)`) and the gradient reweighting factor `δ`:
 `J_policy(θ) = (1/G) * Σ_i=1^G * (1/T) * Σ_t=0^(T-1) * [δ * min(ˆr_i_t(θ) * ˆA_i_t, clip(ˆr_i_t(θ), 1 - ε, 1 + ε) * ˆA_i_t)]`
- **Impact:** This strategy normalizes the influence of gradients from different timesteps, preventing any single step from dominating the optimization process and further alleviating over-optimization (Figure 3).

#### 3.4. Experimental Design

- **Baselines:** Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO.
- **Backbone Models:** SD3.5-M and Flux.1-dev.
- **Proxy Tasks:** GenEval (instruction following), TextRender (text rendering), and PickScore (human preference/aesthetic quality).
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Proxy Scores:** Measured by the reward model trained for each task.
 - **Gold Scores:** A composite score based on independent, high-fidelity image quality metrics (HPSv2, ImageReward, UnifiedReward) to genuinely assess perceptual quality and human preference, which is crucial for detecting reward hacking.
 - **Qualitative Analysis:** Visual comparisons of generated images at different training stages.
 - **Ablation Studies:** To disentangle the contributions of RatioNorm (mean correction, variance alignment) and gradient reweighting.
 - **Human Evaluation:** Paired comparisons to assess image quality, text alignment, and overall preference.
 - **Algorithmic Analysis:** Visualization of importance ratio distributions, gradient magnitudes, and clipping percentages across timesteps.

This comprehensive methodology, combining theoretical derivation with practical algorithmic components and extensive empirical validation, allows GRPO-Guard to effectively diagnose and solve the problem of implicit over-optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various settings consistently demonstrate the effectiveness of GRPO-Guard in mitigating implicit over-optimization and enhancing the stability and quality of generative models.

1. **Mitigation of Over-optimization and Improved Gold Scores:**
 - **Proxy vs. Gold Scores:** A central finding is that while baseline methods (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) achieve high proxy scores, they simultaneously suffer a sharp decline in "gold scores" (metrics like HPSv2, ImageReward, UnifiedReward that reflect true image quality and human preference). This clear divergence signifies severe reward hacking (Figure 1, Figure 4).
 - **GRPO-Guard's Performance:** GRPO-Guard, in contrast, maintains or even improves these gold scores even as proxy scores increase, indicating successful mitigation of over-optimization. Table 1 quantitatively shows that GRPO-Guard consistently yields higher composite gold scores (e.g., Average Gold Score of 0.89 vs. 0.84 for Flow-GRPO on GenEval, and 1.02 vs. 0.88 for DanceGRPO on GenEval).

2. **Superior Visual Quality and Consistency:**
 - **Degradation in Baselines:** Visual comparisons (Figure 5, Figure 6) vividly illustrate the degradation in baseline models due to reward hacking. Outputs from Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO exhibit severe issues such as loss of detail richness, poor text-image consistency, distorted proportions (Figure 7), and introduction of artifacts (e.g., stripes in DanceGRPO, Figure 6).
 - **GRPO-Guard's Preservation:** GRPO-Guard preserves high visual quality, maintains diverse outputs, and ensures better adherence to instructions and realistic object generation, even under challenging prompts.

3. **Stable Importance Ratio Distribution:**
 - **RatioNorm's Effect:** The RatioNorm component successfully corrects the importance-ratio distribution. Figure 2(b) and 2(c) show that, unlike FlowGRPO's left-shifted and inconsistent distributions, GRPO-Guard achieves a balanced mean (near 1) and consistent variance across denoising timesteps. This ensures that the PPO clipping mechanism can properly constrain both positive and negative updates.
 - **Balanced Clipping:** Figure 12 provides statistical evidence, showing that FlowGRPO exhibits clipping `r(θ) < 1 - ε` only at later steps and zero clipping for `r(θ) > 1 + ε`. GRPO-Guard, however, demonstrates balanced clipping for both upper and lower bounds across all steps, confirming the restored functionality of the clipping mechanism.

4. **Balanced Policy Gradients:**
 - **Reduced Imbalance:** GRPO-Guard's gradient reweighting strategy significantly reduces the imbalance in gradient magnitudes across timesteps. Figure 3 demonstrates that FlowGRPO has a gradient magnitude variation of roughly 20x across timesteps, leading to single-step dominance. GRPO-Guard limits this variation to about 2.5x, promoting a more stable and balanced optimization process.

5. **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Component Importance:** The ablation study (Table 2, Figure 9) confirms the individual contributions of GRPO-Guard's components. Mean correction alone significantly alleviates the gold score decline. Adding variance alignment (RatioNorm) further enhances over-optimization mitigation, albeit with a slight slowdown in proxy score growth due to more effective clipping of high-advantage positive ratios.
 - **Comparison with Previous Reweighting:** The study also highlights that a previous reweighting strategy (Temp-Reweight from TempFlowGRPO) accelerates proxy score growth but leads to earlier and more severe over-optimization (rapid gold score drop), unlike GRPO-Guard's more moderate but stable improvements.

6. **Human Preference Validation:**
 - **Clear Superiority:** Human evaluation (Figure 10) provides strong evidence that GRPO-Guard is significantly preferred over baseline methods across image quality, text alignment, and overall quality for both GenEval and OCR tasks. This underscores the practical and perceptual benefits of the proposed method.

7. **Deeper Understanding of Hacking Mechanisms:**
 - **Multi-step Degradation:** Analysis of "hacking models" (Figure 11) reveals that over-optimization leads to pervasive capability degradation across all denoising stages, not just specific ones. At high-noise steps, models prematurely generate simplistic structures, while at low-noise steps, they lose the ability to refine fine details, resulting in residual noise and artifacts. GRPO-Guard's balanced clipping addresses this multi-stage degradation.

These findings collectively establish GRPO-Guard as a robust and general solution for stable policy optimization in flow-matching models, effectively preventing reward hacking while maintaining or improving generation quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GRPO-Guard represents a significant contribution to the field of generative AI, particularly in the domain of aligning powerful flow-matching models with complex human-defined objectives. Its potential impact extends across several key areas:

1. **Enhanced Reliability and Practical Applicability of GRPO for Generative Models:**
 - By addressing the core algorithmic flaw in importance-ratio clipping, GRPO-Guard transforms GRPO-based methods from potentially unstable frameworks (prone to reward hacking) into robust and reliable tools. This significantly boosts the practical applicability of reinforcement learning for fine-tuning text-to-image and text-to-video models. For developers and researchers deploying such models, GRPO-Guard offers a critical safeguard against producing high-scoring, yet visually degraded, outputs.

2. **Deeper Algorithmic Understanding of RL in Different Modalities:**
 - The paper's meticulous analysis of the fundamental "design mismatch" between GRPO's origins in discrete token probabilities (LLMs) and its application to continuous Gaussian probabilities (diffusion models) provides invaluable theoretical insight. This understanding is crucial for designing future RL algorithms that are truly "modality-aware," leading to more effective and stable optimization across diverse generative architectures. It highlights that direct inheritance of RL algorithms without careful adaptation can lead to subtle yet profound failures.

3. **Improved Generation Quality and User Experience:**
 - The consistent preservation and even enhancement of "gold scores" (true quality metrics) and strong human preference for GRPO-Guard's outputs directly translate to higher-quality generated content. This means models trained with GRPO-Guard will produce images and videos that are more aesthetically pleasing, faithful to prompts, and visually coherent, significantly improving the user experience and the utility of generative AI in creative and industrial applications.

4. **Reduced Reliance on Heavy Regularization:**
 - GRPO-Guard mitigates over-optimization without relying on computationally expensive or potentially performance-limiting heavy KL-divergence regularization. This makes the training process more efficient and allows the policy to explore and improve more effectively without being overly constrained by a reference model.

5. **Generalizable Solution for Diverse Generative Architectures:**
 - The demonstration of GRPO-Guard's effectiveness across multiple GRPO variants (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO), different diffusion backbones (SD3.5-M, Flux.1-dev), and various proxy tasks (GenEval, TextRender, PickScore) underscores its generalizability. This suggests that the principles behind RatioNorm and gradient reweighting could be broadly applicable to other RL-based fine-tuning scenarios involving continuous output spaces.

6. **Foundation for Future Research in Reward Modeling and Alignment:**
 - While GRPO-Guard effectively addresses the algorithmic stability of clipping, the paper acknowledges that it doesn't eliminate reward hacking entirely if the *reward model itself* is fundamentally flawed or misaligned with true human preferences. This points to a crucial direction for future research: the development of more comprehensive, efficient, and robust reward models that minimize the gap between proxy and gold scores. GRPO-Guard provides a more stable foundation upon which such advanced reward models can be integrated and optimized without the confounding factor of clipping mechanism failures.

In conclusion, GRPO-Guard offers a timely and impactful solution to a critical problem in generative AI alignment. By stabilizing the core RL optimization process, it paves the way for more reliable, higher-quality, and practically deployable generative models, while also deepening our understanding of modality-specific challenges in reinforcement learning.
