# GRPO-Guard: Mitigating Implicit Over-Optimization in Flow Matching via Regulated Clipping

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Kling Team at Kuaishou Technology and Sun Yat-Sen University introduced GRPO-Guard, an approach that mitigates implicit over-optimization in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for flow-matching generative models. By rectifying the importance-ratio clipping mechanism and balancing policy gradients, GRPO-Guard enables stable policy learning, preventing reward hacking and yielding consistently higher 'gold scores' for image quality and human preference.

## Problem

- GRPO-based reinforcement learning for flow-matching models frequently suffers from 'implicit over-optimization' or 'reward hacking,' where proxy reward scores increase while actual generation quality deteriorates.
- The importance-ratio clipping mechanism, crucial for stabilizing PPO-style algorithms like GRPO, fails in diffusion models due to a fundamental design mismatch between discrete token probabilities (LLMs) and continuous Gaussian state transitions.
- This mismatch leads to a left-shifted mean and inconsistent variance in the importance-ratio distribution across denoising timesteps, preventing effective clipping of overconfident positive updates and causing imbalanced gradient regulation.

## Method

- **GRPO-Guard** introduces ==Ratio Normalization== (RatioNorm) to standardize the log-importance ratio distribution at each denoising step, ensuring its mean is near zero and its variance is consistent, thus restoring symmetric clipping.
- A gradient reweighting strategy is applied to balance policy gradient magnitudes across different denoising timesteps, preventing any single step from dominating the optimization process and alleviating single-step over-optimization.
- The proposed method integrates RatioNorm and gradient reweighting into a modified GRPO policy loss function, designed to fundamentally correct algorithmic flaws in continuous state spaces.

## Results

- GRPO-Guard successfully mitigates reward hacking, maintaining or improving 'gold scores' (HPSv2, ImageReward, UnifiedReward) while baseline methods exhibit a sharp decline, e.g., achieving an Average Gold Score of **0.89** compared to **0.84** for Flow-GRPO on GenEval.
- Qualitative analysis and human evaluation confirm GRPO-Guard's superior visual quality, detail richness, text-prompt consistency, and overall preference over baselines, which often produce distorted or artifact-ridden outputs.
- The RatioNorm component effectively re-centers the importance-ratio distribution to a mean near 1 and aligns its variance, enabling balanced clipping of both positive and negative updates, while gradient reweighting reduces gradient magnitude variation from **20x** to **2.5x** across timesteps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A rigorous mathematical analysis reveals that directly applying GRPO to diffusion models introduces a quadratic term in the log-importance ratio, causing a timestep-dependent negative bias and inconsistent variance in the importance ratio distribution.
> - This biased and inconsistent importance ratio prevents the PPO-style clipping mechanism from effectively constraining positive advantage samples, leading to unchecked gradient updates and implicit over-optimization.
> - Policy gradient magnitudes vary significantly across denoising timesteps, with low-noise steps producing disproportionately large gradients, which can lead to overfitting and multi-step degradation of model capabilities.
