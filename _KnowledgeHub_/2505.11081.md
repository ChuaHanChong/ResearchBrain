# ShiQ: Bringing back Bellman to LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Cohere researchers introduce ShiQ, an off-policy Q-learning framework for fine-tuning Large Language Models that enables token-wise learning from existing data, achieving competitive performance on UltraFeedback and BFCL-V3 benchmarks while maintaining low divergence from reference policies through theoretically-grounded Bellman equations.

## Problem

- Current RLHF methods rely on computationally expensive sampling and on-policy learning
- Existing approaches struggle with token-wise reward signals and multi-turn interactions
- Direct application of Q-learning to LLMs faces challenges with model size and initialization

## Method

- Developed **ShiQ** ==algorithm== based on ==modified Bellman equations== specific to LLM characteristics
- Leveraged ==reference policy== for improved initialization and ==multi-step reward propagation==
- Implemented ==token-wise learning== capability while maintaining ==off-policy training==

## Results

- Achieved competitive performance on UltraFeedback while maintaining reasonable KL divergence
- Outperformed existing methods on multi-turn function-calling tasks in BFCL-V3
- Demonstrated the ability to learn from fine-grained reward structures in synthetic experiments, and enabled sample-efficient learning from existing data without requiring new on-policy samples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can be effectively modeled as regularized Markov Decision Processes
> - Careful modification of Bellman equations enables practical Q-learning for LLMs
> - Multi-step extensions help handle sparse rewards common in LLM tasks

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **RLHF and LLM Alignment:** This work addresses the central problem of aligning large language models with desired behaviors and preferences. The field of RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) has become the dominant approach for this, but it often relies on computationally expensive sampling and on-policy methods. This paper tackles this challenge head-on.
- **Q-Learning in RLHF:** The mainstream approach in RLHF involves policy gradient methods (like PPO). This paper explores a relatively under-explored paradigm: adapting Q-learning methods to LLMs.
- **Off-Policy and Offline RL:** A key theme is moving towards more sample-efficient and offline RL techniques for LLMs. The extremely high cost of sampling from LLMs makes off-policy learning critical. This paper directly contributes to that effort, addressing shortcomings of existing off-policy methods.
- **Bandit vs. MDP Perspectives:** The paper contrasts bandit-based approaches (which treat LLM completion as choosing from a set of possible arms) with a more structured Markov Decision Process (MDP) viewpoint. This positions ShiQ as an attempt to leverage the temporal structure of language and the sequential nature of LLM generation.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Contrastive Approaches:** The paper explicitly contrasts ShiQ with DPO and other contrastive learning methods, highlighting ShiQ's ability to work with token-wise reward signals and avoid the need for paired trajectory data.
- **Bellman Equations and Q-Value Estimation:** The central idea is to carefully derive theoretically sound loss functions based on Bellman equations, tailored to the specific characteristics of LLMs. This positions ShiQ as a theoretically grounded alternative to more heuristic approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Primary Objective:** To develop a practical and theoretically grounded Q-learning-based algorithm (ShiQ) for fine-tuning LLMs, capable of off-policy, token-wise learning, and demonstrating effectiveness in both single-turn and multi-turn LLM settings.
- **Motivations:**
 - **Sample Efficiency:** The high computational cost of sampling with LLMs motivates the need for off-policy and offline learning methods that can learn from existing data.
 - **Limitations of Policy Gradient Methods:** Policy gradient methods are inherently on-policy, requiring new samples for each update.
 - **Limitations of Bandit-Based Approaches:** Bandit methods often cannot process token-wise reward signals and require careful handling of sequence-level losses.
 - **Challenges of Naive Q-Learning:** Applying standard Q-learning algorithms directly to LLMs is ineffective due to LLM-specific characteristics (e.g., large model size, the importance of initialization).
 - **Desire for Theoretical Guarantees:** The authors seek a method with a strong theoretical foundation, derived from Bellman equations, rather than relying on ad-hoc heuristics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Framing LLMs as Regularized MDPs:** The authors model LLMs as regularized Markov Decision Processes (MDPs).
- **Deriving Bellman Consistency Equations:** The core contribution is a series of Bellman consistency equations designed to address LLM-specific challenges.
- **ShiQ Algorithm:** Based on these equations, they develop a practical algorithm called ShiQ (Shifted-Q).
- **Key LLM-Specific Considerations:**
 - **Easing Sampling:** Modifying the Bellman equation to avoid needing both the learned and reference models during inference.
 - **Improved Initialization:** Leveraging the reference policy (the pre-trained LLM) for a smarter Q-learning start.
 - **Multi-Step Extension:** Propagating rewards more effectively across multiple steps to handle sparse rewards often associated with LLM tasks.
- **Loss Function Development:** The paper outlines a sequence of loss functions (Ltry1, Ltry2, Ltry3, LShiQ), each building upon the previous one to address specific challenges.
- **Evaluation:** The algorithm is evaluated on synthetic datasets to characterize its behavior and on real-world benchmarks (UltraFeedback, BFCL-V3) to demonstrate its effectiveness in single-turn and multi-turn LLM settings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Synthetic Data:** Experiments on synthetic data demonstrate ShiQ's ability to learn with fine-grained reward structures.
- **Real-World Benchmarks:**
 - **UltraFeedback:** ShiQ achieves competitive performance compared to existing methods, optimizing reward while maintaining a reasonable KL divergence from the reference policy.
 - **BFCL-V3:** ShiQ outperforms existing methods in multi-turn function-calling scenarios, demonstrating its effectiveness in handling complex, sequential tasks.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies demonstrate the importance of the improved initialization and multi-step extension components of the ShiQ algorithm.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Advancement of Off-Policy RL for LLMs:** This work provides a new and theoretically grounded approach to off-policy reinforcement learning for LLMs, addressing the limitations of existing methods.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** By enabling learning from existing data, ShiQ can significantly reduce the computational cost of fine-tuning LLMs.
- **Token-Wise Learning:** ShiQ's ability to handle token-wise reward signals opens up new possibilities for fine-grained control over LLM behavior.
- **Multi-Turn Capability:** The demonstrated effectiveness in multi-turn settings is crucial for building more interactive and capable LLM applications.
- **Practical Applicability:** The paper emphasizes the simplicity and ease of implementation of ShiQ, making it accessible to practitioners.
- **Potential Applications:** This work has implications for a wide range of LLM applications, including dialogue systems, code generation, summarization, and other tasks where aligning model behavior with desired outcomes is critical.

In summary, this is a significant research paper that addresses a key challenge in the field of LLM alignment: developing sample-efficient and theoretically grounded methods for fine-tuning LLMs using reinforcement learning. The ShiQ algorithm represents a promising alternative to existing approaches, particularly in multi-turn and fine-grained reward settings.
