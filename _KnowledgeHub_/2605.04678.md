# From Pixels to Tokens: A Systematic Study of Latent Action Supervision for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A systematic study by Renmin University of China empirically compared latent action supervision strategies for Vision-Language-Action models, revealing that explicit discrete token supervision consistently improved performance by enhancing long-horizon reasoning with image-based actions and motor coordination with action-based actions.

## Problem

- Heterogeneous action spaces in large-scale VLA training data lead to semantic mismatches and hinder model generalization across diverse robotic platforms and human demonstrations.
- Lack of systematic investigation into how different latent action integration strategies comparatively impact VLA policy learning.
- Existing latent action integration approaches are fragmented, spanning discrete token supervision to auxiliary learning objectives, without clear guidance on optimal methods.

## Method

- Established a unified VLA baseline built on **Qwen3-VL-2B**, enabling systematic empirical comparison of ==latent action supervision== strategies while keeping the VLM backbone consistent.
- Categorized latent action supervision into two perspectives: ==image-based latent actions== for trajectory regularization and ==action-based latent actions== for unifying the target action space.
- Proposed and evaluated four integration strategies (**LA-Align, LA-Direct, LA-Cond, LA-Tok**) that incorporate pre-trained, frozen ==VQ-VAE== latent action models.

## Results

- Image-based strategies improved LIBERO-Long benchmark performance by **+8.4% to +10.8%** over the baseline and achieved a **70%** average success rate on real-world OOD pick-and-place tasks (LA-Cond).
- Action-based strategy (LA-Tok) enhanced the average success rate on motorically complex RoboTwin 2.0 tasks by **+17.5%** over the baseline (**78.0% vs. 60.5%**).
- Discrete token supervision consistently outperformed continuous regression, yielding **+2.2% to +2.7%** higher average success rates across various strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent action supervision consistently improves VLA model performance and generalization across diverse benchmarks and real-world tasks, including benefits in multi-task training and data efficiency.
> - Image-based latent actions are more effective for tasks requiring long-horizon reasoning and scene-level generalization, while action-based latent actions excel at motorically complex coordination tasks.
> - Explicit supervision of discrete latent action tokens generally yields stronger performance gains compared to implicit representation alignment or continuous latent action regression.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models has emerged as a paradigm for developing generalist robotic policies capable of performing diverse tasks across various environments by training on large-scale datasets. However, a significant challenge in VLA training stems from the inherent heterogeneity of available data, which includes inconsistent action spaces originating from different robotic platforms and human videos. This heterogeneity can lead to performance degradation due to semantic mismatches in actions across different domains.

Latent actions have been proposed as an intermediate representation to address this challenge. They abstract motion from either visual transitions or raw actions, providing a unified supervision signal for training VLA models on diverse datasets. While recent VLA models have begun to incorporate latent actions to enhance generalization, their integration strategies remain fragmented. Existing approaches encompass a range from discrete token supervision to auxiliary learning objectives, often targeting the VLM backbone. This diversity suggests differing assumptions regarding the role of latent actions, leaving the optimal integration method largely unexplored.

This research aims to systematize the study of latent action supervision. Instead of evaluating latent action models themselves, the paper focuses on empirically comparing how different latent action supervision choices impact VLA policy learning when implemented within a unified VLA baseline. This approach addresses a gap in the literature by isolating the effects of integration strategies and providing a structured comparison, contrasting with prior works that often entangle latent action formulation and integration with specific VLA architectural designs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is the challenge posed by heterogeneous action spaces in large-scale VLA model training. Vision-Language-Action models, designed to learn generalist robotic policies, often encounter training data from various sources (e.g., different robots, human demonstrations), each characterized by distinct action space semantics. This inconsistency can hinder the models' ability to generalize and maintain performance across diverse tasks and environments. Latent actions offer a potential solution by acting as an abstracted, unified representation of motion.

However, the methods for integrating and supervising VLA models with these latent actions are varied and lack systematic investigation. Researchers have adopted different strategies, such as using discrete tokens or applying auxiliary learning objectives, without a comprehensive understanding of their comparative effectiveness or the contexts in which each approach is most beneficial. This fragmentation necessitates a structured study to guide future VLA model development.

The key objectives of this work are therefore delineated as follows:
- **Systematic Study of Latent Action Supervision**: To conduct a systematic investigation of latent action supervision strategies within a unified VLA baseline. This is intended to isolate and analyze the impact of different latent action formulations and their architectural integration choices on policy learning, rather than focusing on the performance of the latent action models themselves.
- **Categorization of Integration Strategies**: To categorize latent action supervision into two complementary perspectives based on the mapping direction between vision-language (VL) space and action spaces. These perspectives are:
 - **Regularizing the Trajectory**: Leveraging image-based latent actions to provide high-level visual plans that guide the VLM's decision-making process.
 - **Unifying the Target Space**: Employing action-based latent actions to inversely map continuous actions into the VLM's semantic token space, thereby bridging semantic mismatches across heterogeneous action spaces.
- **Proposal and Evaluation of Integration Strategies**: To propose and instantiate four distinct supervision strategies arising from these two perspectives. These strategies are then empirically compared to identify their respective strengths and weaknesses.
- **Empirical Analysis and Insights**: To provide extensive empirical analysis that reveals:
 - **Formulation-Task Correspondence**: Identifying which type of latent action formulation (image-based or action-based) is more effective for specific types of tasks, such as long-horizon reasoning or complex motor coordination.
 - **Comparative Effectiveness**: Determining which specific integration strategy yields the most effective performance across various benchmarks.
 - **Robustness under Mixed Data Training**: Assessing the benefits of latent action supervision when training VLA models on mixed-task datasets, particularly regarding its impact on mitigating negative transfer and enhancing data efficiency.
 - **Representation Choice**: Investigating whether discrete token supervision or continuous representation is a more effective signal for latent action supervision.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to provide practical guidance for selecting and implementing latent action supervision, contributing to the development of more capable and robust generalist robotic policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research establishes a systematic framework for studying latent action supervision within VLA models by defining two core perspectives and instantiating four distinct integration strategies.

#### 3.1. Unified VLA Baseline

The foundation for all experiments is a unified VLA baseline built upon Qwen3-VL-2B. This model employs a parallel decoding formulation, processing a composite input sequence that includes visual observations (from multiple cameras, resized to 224x224), a language instruction (appended with action placeholders), and robot proprioceptive states (projected via an MLP). To enable parameter-efficient fine-tuning, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is applied to all linear layers of the VLM. A custom action head, inspired by VLA-Adapter, regresses a chunk of H continuous action vectors from aggregated hidden states of the [ACTION] placeholder tokens. The model is trained end-to-end by minimizing the L2 loss between predicted and ground-truth action chunks. All methods share the same VLM backbone, placeholder design, aggregation function, and action head, with the only variation being the supervision imposed on VLM representations.

#### 3.2. Latent Action Models

Two types of latent action models are pre-trained and kept frozen during VLA training:

- **Image-based Latent Action Models**: These models learn discrete latent actions (z_img) that represent compact action-relevant dynamics from visual transitions (o_t to o_t+δ). The model architecture is based on UniVLA, using a frozen DINOv2 visual encoder and a Transformer-based VQ-VAE. It is trained with a standard VQ-VAE objective (reconstruction, codebook, commitment terms). An auxiliary regularization, leveraging a small amount (5%) of ground-truth action supervision during latent action learning, is incorporated via a lightweight action prediction MLP attached to the pre-quantization latent representation. This action prediction head is discarded during downstream VLA policy learning.
- **Action-based Latent Action Models**: These custom VQ-VAE models discretize continuous action chunks (a_t) into sequences of shared discrete latent tokens (z_act). The encoder combines frequency-domain features (FFT) and time-domain representations (1D temporal convolutions), followed by a Transformer encoder. The decoder is a lightweight MLP. The model is trained with an action chunk reconstruction objective and VQ regularization, augmented with a latent consistency loss (L_mask). This loss randomly masks a subset of latent timesteps, decodes from the masked sequence, and enforces re-encoded latents to match originals at masked positions, stabilizing tokenization.

#### 3.3. Latent Action Supervision Strategies

The study proposes four distinct VLM supervision strategies, derived from two mapping perspectives, to incorporate these frozen latent actions:

- **Perspective 1: Image-based Latent Actions Regularizing the Trajectory** (using z_img, c_img)
 - **Strategy 1 (LA-Align): Implicit Representation Alignment**: An auxiliary objective aligns internal VLM placeholder hidden states (ν_t^(k)) at a mid-to-late layer with the continuous latent action embedding (c_img) using cosine similarity.
 - **Strategy 2 (LA-Direct): Explicit Direct Decoding**: The VLM is directly supervised to predict discrete image-based latent action tokens (z_img) as an intermediate planning representation. The VLM outputs a probability distribution over codebook indices from the final-layer placeholder states, maximizing the log-likelihood of the ground-truth sequence. The resulting latent action representation (h_latent) is then used by the action head to infer continuous actions.
 - **Strategy 3 (LA-Cond): Explicit Conditional Decoding**: The VLM jointly models latent actions and action representations. Placeholders are split into a latent segment (predicting z_img as in LA-Direct) and an action segment (forming action representation). Action prediction is explicitly conditioned on the predicted latent actions, enforced by a causal attention mask.

- **Perspective 2: Action-based Latent Actions Unifying the Target Space** (using z_act)
 - **Strategy 4 (LA-Tok): Action-to-Token Mapping**: Continuous action chunks (a_t) are discretized into action-based latent tokens (z_act) using the pre-trained action-based latent action model. The VLM is then directly supervised to predict these discrete action tokens, similar to LA-Direct but with action-based targets.

#### 3.4. Unified Objective

All strategies use a generalized training objective: L = L_action + λ L_latent, where L_action is the L2 loss for continuous action prediction, and L_latent is the strategy-specific latent supervision loss. The hyperparameter λ balances the strength of latent guidance, with different values used to account for the magnitude of alignment vs. token-level cross-entropy losses.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted on two simulated benchmarks: LIBERO (focusing on LIBERO-Long for long-horizon multi-stage manipulation) and RoboTwin 2.0 (for motorically complex, coordinated dual-arm tasks). Real-world evaluations are performed on a JAKA robotic arm across manipulation tasks (bowl stacking, stain wiping) and pick-and-place tasks with in-domain (ID) and out-of-domain (OOD) scene configurations. Performance is measured by success rates (%) and normalized completion scores. Ablation studies investigate the impact of placeholder length, regularization depth, and discrete vs. continuous latent representations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic study of latent action supervision revealed several empirical findings regarding their integration into VLA models.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvement

Latent action supervision consistently improved performance across all benchmarks compared to the baseline VLA model. On simulated benchmarks (LIBERO and RoboTwin 2.0), the proposed strategies achieved performance competitive with, and in some cases surpassing, state-of-the-art VLA models.

#### 4.2. Formulation-Task Correspondence

The study identified a correspondence between the type of latent action formulation and the tasks it benefits most:

- **Image-based Latent Actions**: Strategies utilizing image-based latent actions (LA-Align, LA-Direct, LA-Cond) demonstrated greater effectiveness for **long-horizon reasoning** and **scene-level generalization**. On the LIBERO-Long benchmark, these strategies yielded improvements ranging from +8.4% to +10.8% over the baseline, surpassing the action-based strategy (LA-Tok, +6.8%). In real-world manipulation, LA-Direct achieved a score of 79 on the challenging "Stack 4 Bowls" task, compared to 48 for the baseline. Image-based strategies also showed a clear advantage in real-world pick-and-place tasks under out-of-domain (OOD) scene variations, with LA-Cond achieving the highest OOD average score of 70, significantly outperforming the baseline's 17.
- **Action-based Latent Actions**: The action-based latent action strategy (LA-Tok) excelled at **motorically complex tasks**. On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, LA-Tok achieved the best performance, improving the average success rate by +17.5% over the baseline (78.0% vs. 60.5%). In real-world easier tasks, such as stacking 2-3 bowls and in-domain pick-and-place, LA-Tok consistently ranked among the top methods.

#### 4.3. Integration Strategy Effectiveness

Analysis of the architectural integration strategies revealed the following:

- **Explicit Supervision Outperforms Implicit Alignment**: Strategies with explicit latent action supervision (LA-Direct, LA-Cond, LA-Tok) generally delivered stronger gains compared to implicit representation alignment (LA-Align). LA-Direct, for instance, achieved 96.6% on LIBERO-Long versus LA-Align's 94.8%.
- **Direct Decoding vs. Conditional Decoding**: Within explicit image-based designs, Explicit Direct Decoding (LA-Direct) generally outperformed Explicit Conditional Decoding (LA-Cond) in most experiments, particularly in long-horizon settings and real-world manipulation. This suggests that decoupling latent plan prediction from action representation learning can be more effective. However, LA-Cond was competitive in complex settings like RoboTwin 2.0.
- **Directly Supervising Discrete Tokens is Most Effective**: The strategies that directly supervise the VLM to predict discrete latent actions (LA-Direct and LA-Tok) consistently exhibited the most effective performance across experiments. LA-Direct led on LIBERO, and LA-Tok led on RoboTwin 2.0. In real-world experiments, LA-Direct was the top performer, with LA-Tok being highly competitive as the second-best.

#### 4.4. Discrete Tokens vs. Continuous Representation

An ablation study demonstrated that **discrete token supervision consistently outperformed continuous regression** for latent action supervision. Using discrete tokens resulted in +2.7% and +2.2% higher average success rates for LA-Direct and LA-Tok, respectively, compared to their continuous counterparts. Despite this, continuous latent action supervision still provided substantial improvements over the baseline, indicating the general informativeness of latent actions.

#### 4.5. Benefits in Multi-task Training and Data Efficiency

- **Reduced Negative Transfer**: Latent action supervision was shown to reduce negative transfer in multi-task joint training. While the baseline exhibited instability and performance degradation on specific tasks when trained on a mixture of 10 RoboTwin tasks, LA-Cond achieved consistent gains across all tasks without instances of negative transfer, leading to a +20.9% improvement in average success rate over the baseline.
- **Data Efficiency**: Latent action supervision improved sample efficiency in low-resource regimes. For example, LA-Tok achieved 94.0% success on LIBERO-Long with only 50% of the training data, outperforming the baseline by +14.0%.
- **Architectural Ablations**: Increased action placeholder length alone did not explain performance gains, confirming that improvements were due to structured latent supervision. Aligning LA-Align at mid-to-late VLM layers was found to be optimal, as early layers lacked semantic abstraction and final layers were too specialized.
- **Sensitivity to Supervision Weight**: The performance of latent action supervision was found to be relatively stable across different values of the weighting coefficient λ.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a systematic empirical investigation into the role and optimal integration of latent actions for training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The findings offer practical guidance for the development of generalist robotic policies, contributing to the advancement of embodied AI systems.

The core significance of this work lies in its structured comparison of different latent action supervision strategies, addressing a previous fragmentation in the field. By demonstrating consistent performance improvements across diverse benchmarks and real-world tasks, the research validates latent actions as an effective intermediate representation for VLA training. This includes benefits for critical aspects of robotic control, such as long-horizon reasoning, where image-based latent actions were found to be particularly advantageous, and complex motor coordination, which benefited from action-based latent actions.

A notable impact is the observed enhancement in **generalization capabilities**, especially under out-of-domain (OOD) scene variations in real-world pick-and-place tasks. The robustness imparted by latent action supervision under distributional shifts suggests a pathway towards more adaptable robotic systems in uncontrolled environments. Furthermore, the findings on **reduced negative transfer** during multi-task joint training are significant for scaling VLA policies to diverse task distributions. By enabling more stable and consistent performance across heterogeneous task mixtures, latent actions can facilitate the development of truly generalist robots.

The identification of **discrete token supervision** as the most effective representation signal offers a clear architectural recommendation for VLA model designers. This finding, combined with the demonstrated **data efficiency** of latent action supervision, is valuable for scenarios with limited training data, potentially lowering the resource requirements for developing capable robotic systems.

In summary, this research contributes to a deeper understanding of how to leverage latent actions effectively within VLA architectures. The practical guidance provided on selecting appropriate latent action formulations and integration strategies can inform future research and engineering efforts, potentially leading to more robust, generalizable, and data-efficient robotic manipulation systems in both research and real-world applications. The authors acknowledge limitations, including the scope of integration strategies studied, the potential influence of stronger latent action models, and the use of a single-arm robotic platform for real-world validation, indicating directions for future work to broaden the applicability of these findings.
