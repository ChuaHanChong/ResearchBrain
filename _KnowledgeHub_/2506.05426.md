# Mixture-of-Experts Meets In-Context Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The T2MIR framework, from researchers at Nanjing University and the University of Technology Sydney, enhances in-context reinforcement learning by incorporating Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) to explicitly address the multi-modal nature of RL data and the challenges of diverse task distributions. This approach yields consistent performance improvements and stronger generalization across multiple multi-task environments.

## Problem

- Current in-context reinforcement learning (ICRL) methods struggle with the intrinsic multi-modality of state, action, and reward tokens within input sequences.
- Broad and heterogeneous task distributions in multi-task RL lead to "gradient conflicts," hindering learning efficiency and generalization.
- Monolithic Transformer architectures are not optimally suited for processing diverse data types and managing varied task demands within RL contexts.

## Method

- The **T2MIR** framework replaces standard transformer feedforward layers with ==dual Mixture-of-Experts== (MoE) components: token-wise and task-wise MoE.
- A token-wise MoE layer processes individual multi-modal input tokens (state, action, reward) with specialized experts, guided by a router and regularization.
- A task-wise MoE layer, employing a novel contrastive learning method, routes entire task trajectories to specialized experts to mitigate gradient conflicts across diverse tasks.

## Results

- T2MIR consistently outperformed five competitive ICRL baselines across diverse benchmarks, including discrete grid-worlds, 2D navigation, MuJoCo locomotion, and Meta-World manipulation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both token-wise and task-wise MoE layers are essential, with their combined presence yielding the highest performance.
- The framework demonstrated robustness against varying offline dataset qualities and generalized effectively to out-of-distribution test tasks and prompts.
- Visualizations showed that token experts clustered by modality, task experts specialized for similar tasks, and the system effectively mitigated gradient conflicts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) can effectively specialize parts of a Transformer to process distinct semantic modalities (states, actions, rewards) within RL data.
> - Utilizing task-wise MoE, complemented by contrastive learning, can alleviate gradient conflicts by enabling specialized experts to handle specific task distributions.
> - Architectural enhancements like MoE offer a scalable solution for improving ICRL's generalization and adaptability across varied environments.
