# Physics-informed Neural Network Predictive Control for Quadruped Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sun Yat-sen University and collaborators develop a unified control framework (OPI-PINNPC) for quadruped robots that combines online payload identification with physics-informed neural networks, achieving 35% better tracking precision than existing methods while handling payloads from 25-100kg through innovative integration of adaptive control and physics-based learning.

## Problem

- Traditional control methods struggle with unknown or varying payloads on quadruped robots
- Existing adaptive control approaches are computationally expensive and have slow convergence rates

## Method

- Developed unified framework combining online payload identification, physics-informed neural networks, and nonlinear ==model predictive control==
- Incorporated identified payload parameters into physics-informed loss functions to ensure physical consistency
- Created composite controller merging NMPC output with PID control

## Results

- **35%** improvement in tracking precision compared to baseline adaptive control
- Faster convergence when adapting to payload changes
- Successfully handled diverse payload conditions from 25-100kg
- Demonstrated robust performance in real-world testing on Kirin quadruped robot

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics-informed neural networks can effectively learn and adapt to changing robot dynamics
> - Integration of payload identification with physics-based learning enables faster convergence
> - Real-time optimization possible through PINN surrogate models within NMPC framework

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research falls within the intersection of several key areas:

- **Quadruped Robotics:** The paper directly addresses the control challenges associated with quadruped robots, which are becoming increasingly important in various applications, including search and rescue, inspection, and logistics.
- **Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC):** NMPC has emerged as a prominent control strategy for legged robots due to its ability to handle complex, nonlinear dynamics and constraints. This paper builds upon existing NMPC techniques.
- **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs):** PINNs represent a relatively recent and rapidly growing field that combines the strengths of traditional physics-based modeling and machine learning. This paper leverages PINNs to address the limitations of purely physics-based or purely data-driven approaches.
- **Adaptive Control:** The paper builds on previous work in adaptive control, particularly in the context of unknown payloads. The OPI component is derived from the existing Adaptive Control for Quadruped Locomotion (ACQL).

The research addresses a significant gap in the existing literature: the challenge of maintaining accurate and efficient control of quadruped robots under varying and unknown payload conditions. Traditional NMPC relies on accurate models, which are difficult to obtain when the payload is unknown. While adaptive control methods exist, they can be computationally expensive or have slow convergence rates. This work aims to bridge this gap by using PINNs to learn the dynamics of the robot while incorporating real-time payload identification.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the research are:

- **Develop a unified control framework (OPI-PINNPC) for precise quadruped locomotion in the presence of unknown payloads.** This framework should integrate online payload identification, physics-informed neural networks, and nonlinear model predictive control.
- **Incorporate identified payload mass and orientation parameters into the physics-informed loss function of the PINN.** This aims to improve the accuracy of the PINN's predictions by ensuring physical consistency and adapting to changing load conditions.
- **Achieve faster convergence and higher tracking accuracy compared to previous adaptive control methods.** This highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed OPI-PINNPC framework.

The main motivation behind this work is to address the limitations of existing control methods for quadruped robots operating in real-world scenarios where payloads can vary significantly. Maintaining stable and accurate locomotion under such conditions is crucial for the successful deployment of these robots in diverse applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs the following methodology:

- **System Modeling:** The researchers start with a detailed model of the quadruped robot's dynamics, including the effects of payload.
- **Online Payload Identification (OPI):** They use an online identification algorithm (taken from their previous work) to estimate the mass and orientation parameters of the unknown payload in real time.
- **Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN):** A PINN is trained to learn the dynamics of the robot, with the identified payload parameters incorporated into the physics-informed loss function.
- **Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC):** The PINN serves as a surrogate model within an NMPC framework, allowing for real-time optimization of control inputs.
- **Composite Controller:** The control input is a composite of the NMPC output and a PID controller.
- **Experimental Validation:** The OPI-PINNPC framework is tested on a real quadruped robot (Kirin) with varying payloads. The performance is compared to that of a baseline adaptive control algorithm (ACQL).

The approach is based on the integration of several techniques: adaptive control for identifying unknown parameters, physics-informed neural networks for learning the system dynamics, and model predictive control for generating optimal control inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the research are:

- **The OPI-PINNPC framework demonstrates improved position and orientation tracking accuracy compared to the baseline ACQL algorithm.** The experimental results show an average of 35% improvement in tracking precision across diverse payload conditions (25-100 kg).
- **The OPI-PINNPC framework exhibits faster convergence compared to ACQL.** This indicates that the proposed method is more efficient in adapting to changes in payload.
- **The PINN is able to accurately predict the dynamics of the robot under varying payload conditions.** This is attributed to the incorporation of identified payload parameters into the physics-informed loss function.

The results suggest that the OPI-PINNPC framework is an effective solution for controlling quadruped robots with unknown payloads.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Improved Quadruped Locomotion:** The OPI-PINNPC framework can enable more robust and reliable locomotion of quadruped robots in real-world environments where payload variations are common.
- **Enhanced Adaptability:** The framework's ability to adapt to unknown payloads without sacrificing computational efficiency makes it suitable for applications that require versatility and real-time performance.
- **Advancement of PINN Applications:** The research demonstrates the effectiveness of PINNs in a challenging control problem, contributing to the growing body of knowledge on PINN applications in robotics.
- **Potential for Emergency Response:** The research team's affiliation with the Guangdong Provincial Key Laboratory of Fire Science and Intelligent Emergency Technology suggests that this work could contribute to the development of quadruped robots for use in emergency response scenarios, where they could navigate complex terrain and carry essential equipment.
- **Broader Applicability:** The approach of combining online parameter identification with physics-informed learning could be extended to other robotic systems and control problems where accurate modeling is challenging due to uncertainties or variations in system parameters.

The successful implementation of the OPI-PINNPC framework could have a significant impact on the future development and deployment of quadruped robots in a variety of applications.
