# SeededGrasp: Language-Guided Grasping in Complex Scenes with Multiple Embodiments

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces SeededGrasp, a modular framework for language-guided, multi-embodiment robotic grasping in complex, cluttered scenes. It decouples high-level semantic reasoning using a Vision-Language Model for seed point prediction from low-level geometric grasp generation, achieving a 78% success rate in real-world cluttered environments with an unseen gripper and outperforming baselines in simulation by up to 35% in success rate.

## Problem

- Robotic grasping systems struggle to integrate robust 3D spatial understanding with task-specific requirements given through natural language, especially in cluttered, multi-object scenes.
- Existing VLM-based grasping approaches are either imprecise, require expensive end-to-end training with substantial data, or lack generalization across diverse robotic end-effectors.
- A significant gap exists in datasets providing multi-embodiment grasp poses within cluttered, multi-object scenes, hindering the development of holistic grasping solutions.

## Method

- A modular, two-stage framework, **SeededGrasp**, that decouples ==VLM==-based semantic seed point prediction from a dedicated ==flow-matching model== for multi-embodiment grasp generation.
- A large-scale synthetic dataset of 2.56 million multi-embodiment grasp poses in cluttered scenes was created for training, covering Franka Panda, Robotiq 3-Finger, and Allegro hands.
- The grasp generation model utilizes point cloud encoding with ==Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs)==, learnable gripper query vectors, and a ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)==-based flow-matching approach for robust pose sampling conditioned on the scene and predicted seed point.

## Results

- In simulation, SeededGrasp achieved up to a **35%** higher grasp success rate compared to baselines (e.g., Geomatch) in cluttered scenes and outperformed DGN2.0 by **13%** when using internal seed generation.
- With VLM-predicted seed points (Gemini 3.1 Flash), SeededGrasp achieved **72.97%** grasp success in simulation and **89.6%** object and part recognition success for language-conditioned prompts, outperforming ShapeGrasp and GraspMAS.
- Demonstrated robust sim-to-real transfer with a **78%** overall success rate in real-world cluttered scenes using an unseen Delto DG-3F-B gripper, mapped from Robotiq 3-Finger predictions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level semantic reasoning (VLM for seed point) from low-level geometric grasp generation is data-efficient and allows leveraging off-the-shelf VLMs without fine-tuning.
> - Incorporating both robot point clouds and learnable gripper-specific query vectors into the model significantly improves performance and generalization across diverse robotic embodiments.
> - Using an initial pose state as a fixed canonical grasp adjacent to the seed point, combined with flow-matching for generation, effectively handles complex, multi-modal grasp distributions in cluttered environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic grasping and manipulation is challenged by the need for robots to interact with objects precisely, stably, and in a manner that aligns with task requirements, particularly in complex, multi-object environments. A significant aspect of this challenge involves enabling robots to understand and execute commands specified through natural language, and to generalize these capabilities across diverse robotic end-effectors (embodiments), ranging from simple two-finger grippers to highly dexterous multi-finger hands.

Existing research has addressed various facets of this problem. Some methods focus on grasp generation for isolated objects, which simplifies the perception and contact reasoning but does not fully reflect the complexity of real-world cluttered scenes. Language-grounded grasping systems typically fall into two categories: those that integrate Vision-Language Models (VLMs) directly into an end-to-end training pipeline, often requiring substantial annotated datasets and computational resources, or those that leverage pre-trained VLMs to infer high-level semantic representations which then feed into complex, often heuristic-driven, pose optimization processes. Both approaches frequently exhibit limitations in precision and scalability, especially in cluttered environments with occlusions or when attempting to support multiple gripper morphologies.

Furthermore, a critical gap in the existing literature is the lack of comprehensive datasets that simultaneously provide multi-embodiment grasp poses within cluttered, multi-object scenes. While datasets exist for large-scale grasping, cluttered scenes, or multi-embodiment grasps, none combine all these attributes, hindering the development of holistic grasping solutions.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a modular framework that decouples high-level semantic reasoning from low-level geometric execution. By utilizing an off-the-shelf VLM to predict a task-specific "seed point" and then conditioning a lightweight grasp-generation model on this point, the proposed method bypasses the need for expensive VLM fine-tuning or end-to-end training. It also contributes a new, large-scale multi-embodiment dataset for grasping in cluttered scenes, aiming to facilitate broader research in this integrated problem space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified challenges in achieving practical robotic grasping in real-world scenarios. The authors highlight several key needs:
- **Integration of Spatial Reasoning and Task-Specific Requirements**: Robotic grasping systems require both robust 3D spatial understanding of objects and scenes, as well as the ability to interpret and act upon task-specific requirements provided through natural language. Existing VLMs offer a natural interface for language-based command, but their direct application to grasping faces performance and scalability issues.
- **Addressing Limitations of Current VLM-based Approaches**: Current approaches either predict grasps directly using VLMs, which often results in limited spatial awareness and precision, or require training VLMs together with grasping models. The latter necessitates significant data and computational resources, impeding scaling to complex scenes and multiple robotic embodiments. The existing use of pre-trained VLMs often relies on complex post-processing or heuristics, leading to imprecision.
- **Support for Multiple Robotic Embodiments**: Robots operate with a variety of end-effectors, from simple parallel-jaw grippers to complex dexterous hands. Developing specialized grasping models for each embodiment is inefficient and data-intensive. The ability to support multiple embodiments with a single, generalizable model is crucial for improving utility and data efficiency.
- **Performance in Cluttered Scenes**: Real-world environments are often cluttered, presenting challenges such as object occlusions and dense interactions that simpler, isolated-object grasping models cannot handle effectively.
- **Lack of Suitable Datasets**: The absence of large-scale datasets that provide multi-embodiment grasp poses in cluttered, multi-object scenes creates a bottleneck for training and evaluating advanced grasping models.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the SeededGrasp project are:
- To propose a novel, data-efficient framework, SeededGrasp, that enables language-guided, multi-embodiment grasping in cluttered scenes.
- To decouple high-level semantic reasoning (using a VLM to predict a seed point) from low-level geometric grasp execution (using a dedicated grasp-generation model), thereby eliminating the need for expensive end-to-end VLM training.
- To develop and release the first multi-embodiment tabletop grasping dataset containing over 2.5 million grasps in cluttered scenes, covering a diverse set of objects and grippers.
- To demonstrate that the proposed SeededGrasp framework outperforms existing baselines in simulation and exhibits robust sim-to-real transfer capabilities in real-world grasping experiments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SeededGrasp framework operates as a modular, two-stage pipeline designed for language-grounded, multi-embodiment grasping in cluttered scenes.

#### 3.1. Dataset Generation

To train the grasp prediction model, a new synthetic dataset was created, building upon the MultiGripperGrasp (MGG) dataset which provides stable mid-air grasp poses.
- **Gripper Selection**: Three grippers were chosen for their data volume and diverse kinematics: Franka Panda (parallel-jaw), Robotiq 3-Finger, and Allegro hand (dexterous).
- **Scene Generation**: 504 training and 106 evaluation scenes were constructed. This involved randomly sampling one to ten objects from the Google Scanned Objects and YCB datasets, initializing them in mid-air, and dropping them onto a tabletop. This process generated scenes ranging from sparse to dense clutter.
- **Grasp Transformation and Filtering**: Mid-air grasps from MGG were transformed into the global scene frame. To determine feasibility in cluttered environments, a set of heuristic collision checks were applied. These checks ensured the gripper approached from a viable angle (≥ 0.5 rad), maintained clearance above the table (≥ 0.5 cm), and avoided excessive interpenetration with other objects (≤ 10 mm). This approach bypassed computationally intensive dynamic lift tests for millions of grasps.
- **Scene Representation**: For each scene, a 2048-point point cloud was generated by fusing four RGB-D images captured from cardinal directions and downsampling using farthest point sampling biased away from the table. A bird’s-eye view (BEV) image was captured for VLM seed point selection during inference.
- **Dataset Statistics**: The final dataset comprises 2.56 million grasp poses, distributed across the three grippers, with a notable skew towards the Franka Panda due to its simpler kinematics and higher pass rate during filtering.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

The core of SeededGrasp integrates a VLM for high-level semantic reasoning with a flow-matching model for low-level grasp generation.

- **Point Cloud Encoding**:
 - Input: Gripper (pr ∈ R^(1024×3)) and scene (pc ∈ R^(2048×3)) point clouds.
 - Feature Enrichment: Point coordinates are Fourier-encoded and concatenated with local normal vectors and curvature, estimated via Principal Component Analysis. Scene point cloud representations also include the distance to the predicted seed point.
 - Feature Extraction: Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) are used, considering sixteen nearest neighbors, to produce per-point feature vectors (fp ∈ R^512).
 - Aggregation: Global representations for each point cloud are obtained through max and mean pooling of per-point features. Local geometry around the seed point is captured by concatenating features from the sixteen nearest neighbors to the global scene features.
 - Robot Query: A learnable query vector (qr ∈ R^512) is introduced for each robot to encode specific gripper geometry.
 - Output: Final feature vectors for the robot (fr ∈ R^(3×512)) and scene (fc ∈ R^(18×512)).
- **Grasp Pose Parameterization**: Each grasp pose is parameterized as a vector g = (T, R, θ), where T ∈ R^3 is translation, R ∈ SO(3) is rotation (parameterized with Euler angles), and θ represents the joint configuration. The joint configuration vector is padded to accommodate the maximum degrees of freedom (16 joints for the Allegro hand).
- **Grasp Pose Generation**:
 - A flow matching model, uθ, is employed to model and sample from the complex, multi-modal distribution of stable grasp poses conditioned on robot and scene features, p(g | fr, fc).
 - The model is trained to predict a ground truth vector field.
 - During inference, an initial noisy grasp pose sample (gt) is iteratively transformed into a clean grasp pose (g1) by numerically solving the corresponding Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE).
 - The underlying network architecture is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT), which uses cross-attention mechanisms to incorporate the robot and scene conditioning signals.

#### 3.3. Model Training

The flow matching model is trained on the synthesized grasp dataset with specific data preparation and loss formulation.
- **Data Preparation**: Ground-truth seed points are derived by sampling one of eight points on the target object closest to the gripper's palm center. To prevent overfitting to simpler geometries and grippers, grasp poses are sampled uniformly across grippers, scenes, and objects.
- **Augmentation**: Random Z-axis rotations, XY-plane translations, and Gaussian noise are applied to point clouds and grasp poses to enhance generalization.
- **Normalization**: All grasp poses are min-max normalized to a range of [-1, 1], with distinct bounds for joint angles specific to each gripper to account for variations in joint limits.
- **Loss Function**: A conditional flow-matching loss (L_cfm) is used, employing an L1 loss (instead of L2) for translation, rotation, and joint angle components to improve precision. The total loss is a linear combination of these components, with scaling factors (λ = (0.4, 0.04, 0.8)) to balance their contributions. Unused joint dimensions for specific grippers are masked during joint loss computation.
- **Training Dynamics**: The time step 't' is sampled from a Beta distribution to bias the loss towards later time steps (t → 1), where high accuracy is crucial for final grasp pose refinement. Classifier-Free Guidance is integrated by randomly dropping conditioning features during training, enabling the network to learn both conditional and unconditional vector fields. During inference, these fields are extrapolated to strengthen conditioning (using w = 1.1).

#### 3.4. Model Inference

The inference process combines VLM-based seed point prediction with the trained grasp generation model.
- **VLM Seed Point Prediction**: During inference, a VLM (e.g., Gemini 3.1 Flash) is prompted in a zero-shot manner to identify a stable grasping location based on a BEV scene image and user instructions. The predicted 2D pixel coordinate is then projected onto the 3D scene point cloud, yielding the seed point. This modular design means the VLM does not require fine-tuning.
- **Generating Grasp Poses**: An initial pose state (g0) is set as a fixed canonical grasp pose adjacent to the seed point (rather than pure noise). Forward Euler Integration with a step size of 0.2 is then used to numerically integrate g0 through the predicted vector field uθ from t=0 to t=1, producing the final grasp pose conditioned on the seed point, scene point clouds, and the target robot query vector.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SeededGrasp framework was evaluated in both simulation and real-world experiments across three distinct axes: seed point selection and conditional grasp prediction, multi-embodiment performance, and language-conditioned grasp prompting.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Seed Point Selection and Conditional Grasp Prediction**:
 - Comparison with DGN2.0 (single embodiment): When trained on Allegro hand data and conditioned on seed points generated by DGN2.0's Graspness Score Module, SeededGrasp achieved a 66.22% success rate, outperforming DGN2.0's 53.15% by 13%.
 - VLM-generated seed points: When seed points were predicted by Gemini 3.1 Flash, both SeededGrasp and DGN2.0 demonstrated higher success rates (72.97% for SeededGrasp, 66.67% for DGN2.0) compared to using DGN2.0's dedicated seed generation module. This indicates the effectiveness of VLMs in providing grasp-relevant seed points. SeededGrasp's design allows it to leverage these VLM-predicted seeds without requiring explicit object segmentation masks or additional feature vectors, while also supporting multiple end-effectors.
- **Multi-Embodiment Performance**:
 - Comparison with Geomatch: SeededGrasp achieved success rates of 71.38% for Franka Panda, 72.16% for Robotiq 3-Finger, and 72.07% for Allegro grippers in cluttered scenes. In contrast, Geomatch, designed for mid-air single-object grasping, yielded success rates between 25.35% and 37.97% across the grippers. SeededGrasp demonstrated an approximate 35% higher success rate, attributed to its ability to handle complex objects and cluttered scenes more effectively than Geomatch's Inverse Kinematics (IK) optimization, which struggled with partial point clouds and non-convex geometries.
- **Language-Conditioned Grasp Prompting**:
 - Comparison with ShapeGrasp and GraspMAS: On a test dataset of 226 scene-prompt queries (categorized as 'Easy' or 'Difficult'), SeededGrasp consistently outperformed both baselines, which are limited to parallel-jaw grippers and rely on Bird's Eye View (BEV) images.
 - Object & Part Recognition Success: SeededGrasp achieved 89.6%, while ShapeGrasp and GraspMAS achieved 57.5% and 42.5%, respectively.
 - Overall Grasp Success (Difficult Prompts): SeededGrasp obtained 58.2%, significantly higher than ShapeGrasp (31.1%) and GraspMAS (24.5%). The baselines' reliance on BEV images limited their spatial reasoning and ability to identify precise 3D grasp locations, especially for complex or occluded objects.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Gripper Encoding**: The study showed that using both the robot point cloud and a learnable gripper-specific query vector is important for robust performance. Removing either component resulted in decreased success rates, particularly for the kinematically more complex Robotiq 3-Finger and Allegro grippers, indicating their role in capturing specific geometric intricacies and promoting generalization.
- **VLM Backbone**: An evaluation of different VLM families for seed point prediction revealed that Gemini 3.1 Flash significantly outperformed GPT 5.4 and Qwen 3.5 Flash, achieving an average success rate of 71.87% compared to 42.61% and 54.42%, respectively. This highlights the impact of VLM choice on overall system performance. Using higher VLM reasoning levels did not yield additional benefits.
- **Dataset Scale**: Retraining the model with reduced dataset sizes (25%, 50%, 75%) indicated that performance generally improves with more data but begins to saturate around 71.5%. The performance of Robotiq 3-Finger and Allegro grippers degraded more significantly with smaller datasets compared to the Franka Panda, likely due to the Franka's higher density of viable grasps per scene.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation

- SeededGrasp was evaluated in a real-world setup using a Delto DG-3F-B three-finger gripper, a model not included in the training dataset. Robotiq 3-Finger predictions were mapped to the Delto gripper using constant joint offsets.
- Across ten trials for each of fifteen unseen objects in cluttered scenes, the model achieved an overall success rate of 78%.
- The primary cause of failure in real-world experiments was the gripper being too far from the object, leading to closure without effective grasping, sometimes exacerbated by angled grasp predictions. Despite this, the results demonstrated the model's sim-to-real transfer capabilities and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SeededGrasp framework represents a significant advancement in robotic grasping by addressing several critical challenges simultaneously: language-guided control, multi-embodiment support, and robust operation in cluttered scenes.

- **Bridging Semantic and Geometric Reasoning**: The modular approach, which utilizes a VLM to predict a high-level semantic "seed point" that then conditions a low-level geometric grasp generator, effectively bridges the gap between natural language commands and precise robot actions. This decoupling enhances both the interpretability and efficiency of the system.
- **Data and Computational Efficiency**: By eliminating the need for expensive end-to-end VLM training or large-scale language-annotated grasp datasets, SeededGrasp offers a more data-efficient solution. This efficiency is critical for deploying robotic systems in diverse, real-world environments where extensive bespoke dataset collection is often impractical.
- **Generalization Across Embodiments**: The framework's ability to support multiple, morphologically diverse robotic end-effectors with a shared model is a notable contribution. This reduces the need for gripper-specific training and development, making robotic manipulation systems more versatile and scalable.
- **Performance in Complex Scenes**: Demonstrating high success rates in cluttered, multi-object scenes, both in simulation and real-world experiments, indicates SeededGrasp's potential for practical applications in unstructured environments like warehouses or homes.
- **Contribution to Research Resources**: The release of the first multi-embodiment tabletop grasping dataset, comprising over 2.5 million grasps in cluttered scenes, provides a valuable resource for the robotics community. This dataset can facilitate future research and benchmarking in areas requiring integrated spatial, semantic, and multi-embodiment grasping capabilities.
- **Future Directions**: While demonstrating robust performance, the authors acknowledge limitations such as the current lack of awareness of robotic arm kinematics for motion planning and the sole reliance on single-image VLM conditioning. Addressing these limitations, along with enhancing data diversity for different grasp types (e.g., flat objects), suggests clear avenues for future research that could further amplify the impact of this work, pushing towards more general-purpose robotic manipulation.

Overall, SeededGrasp advances the state of the art in language-grounded robotic grasping by proposing an efficient and scalable architecture, providing a foundational dataset, and demonstrating strong performance, thereby paving the way for more capable and adaptable robotic systems.
