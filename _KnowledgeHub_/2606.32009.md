# Human-as-Humanoid: Enabling Zero-Shot Humanoid Learning from Ego-Exo Human Videos with Human-Aligned Embodiments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Human-as-Humanoid, a framework that translates ego-exocentric human video demonstrations into 60-DoF robot-executable action labels for humanoid robots. This method generates supervision at near-real-time speeds, achieving a 4.8–7.2x throughput gain over teleoperation and enabling zero-shot generalization of policies to real-robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- High-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) humanoid robots lack large-scale, high-quality observation-action supervision data for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model training.
- Traditional robot teleoperation for data collection is low throughput, labor-intensive, and limited in diversifying scenarios for complex manipulation.
- Abundant human video demonstrations cannot be directly used for robot training due to fundamental morphological and kinematic differences between humans and robots, and the lack of executable robot action labels.

## Method

- A custom-designed 60-DoF upper-body humanoid, **PrimeU**, was developed with human-aligned morphology and sensing to minimize the embodiment gap.
- A near-real-time pipeline converts synchronized camera-only human ego-exo videos into 60-DoF robot joint commands using ==staged Inverse Kinematics (IK) retargeting==.
- **PhysDex**, a ==VLA== policy, is trained on these human-derived actions, incorporating ==Dual-Space Hierarchical Kinematic Constraint (DS-HKC)== supervision to enforce task-space consistency (wrist and fingertip positions) during joint-space prediction.

## Results

- The Human-as-Humanoid pipeline achieved a **4.8–7.2x** higher demonstration throughput compared to motion-capture teleoperation methods.
- PhysDex policies, trained exclusively with **1,500 hours** of human-derived 60-DoF action labels, successfully generalized to real-robot deployment for multiple manipulation tasks, including zero-shot scenarios.
- PhysDex policies demonstrated higher composite scores across **seven** real-robot manipulation tasks compared to GR00T N1.7, especially in tasks where only human-derived data was used for adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-aligned robot embodiments significantly reduce retargeting errors and facilitate the conversion of human behaviors into robot actions.
> - A staged IK solver can accurately recover and retarget complex 60-DoF human upper-body and hand motions from camera-only videos, generating controller-compatible robot actions in near-real-time.
> - Integrating forward kinematics (FK)-aware, dual-space hierarchical supervision improves VLA training by explicitly constraining joint predictions to maintain crucial task-space geometry for dexterous manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents an approach for general robot control, enabling robots to interpret visual observations and language instructions to execute tasks. A fundamental challenge in training these VLA models, particularly for complex, high-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) humanoid robots, is the acquisition of large-scale, high-quality observation-action supervision data. Current methods for obtaining such data often involve direct robot teleoperation, which provides precise, controller-aligned supervision but is characterized by low throughput, high labor intensity, safety constraints, and difficulty in diversifying scenarios.

Concurrently, human demonstrations offer a rich source of bimanual manipulation behaviors, including complex hand-shape changes, contact timing, regrasping, and coordination. Egocentric human videos, in particular, provide a perspective similar to a humanoid robot's head-mounted camera, making them potentially suitable as policy inputs. However, raw human video data typically lacks direct robot action labels, and significant morphological and kinematic differences exist between humans and humanoids in terms of body scale, joint structure, hand morphology, DoF, sensing viewpoints, and reachable workspace. Previous research has explored using human video data for various purposes, such as generating perception labels, tracking hand trajectories, or providing pretraining signals for robot policies. However, directly converting these human behaviors into executable, high-DoF robot actions for real-time application remains an active area of investigation.

This work contributes to the landscape by addressing the problem of bridging the gap between abundant human video demonstrations and the requirement for robot-executable, high-dimensional action supervision. It proposes a framework for generating controller-aligned 60-DoF upper-body humanoid joint supervision directly from synchronized camera-only human videos in a near-real-time manner. This approach aims to reduce the dependence on labor-intensive robot teleoperation for VLA training, particularly for dexterous humanoids, and enables generalization to real robot tasks, in some cases without requiring target-task-specific robot demonstrations. The proposed system distinguishes itself by focusing on the generation of *executable robot actions* rather than just perceptual or high-level motion cues, and by integrating this with a human-aligned robot embodiment and specific training methodologies for kinematic consistency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenge of scaling observation-action supervision for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly for high-DoF humanoids. While VLA policies are critical for general robot control, their learning success is significantly constrained by the availability and quality of executable action data. Traditional methods, such as direct robot teleoperation, are slow, resource-intensive, and inherently limited in terms of data diversity and throughput for complex manipulation tasks performed by high-DoF humanoids.

Human demonstrations, captured through egocentric and exocentric videos, represent a potentially vast and diverse source of manipulation data. These demonstrations inherently contain rich bimanual behaviors, including intricate hand movements and object interactions. However, raw human videos are not directly suitable for robot training because they do not provide executable robot actions and there are fundamental differences between human and robot embodiments.

The key objectives of this work are to overcome these limitations by developing a framework, named Human-as-Humanoid, that enables the conversion of human demonstrations into high-DoF, robot-executable observation-action supervision. This involves addressing four specific, coupled requirements:

1. **Embodiment Alignment:** To reduce retargeting errors, the target robot's morphology (body scale, reachable workspace, hand morphology) and sensing layout must be compatible with human demonstrations.
2. **Observation-Motion Compatibility:** The system needs egocentric video streams for deployment-aligned policy inputs and complementary exocentric views to facilitate robust upper-body and hand motion recovery, especially under occlusion.
3. **Action-Interface Alignment:** The converted action labels must be compatible with the robot's specific joint ordering, URDF convention, joint limits, and controller interface, while also capturing the task-space motion intent.
4. **Joint-Task Consistency:** The generated executable joint commands must preserve crucial task-space geometry, such as wrist and fingertip positions and orientations, which are critical for contact-rich manipulation tasks.

The overall objective is to establish a human-to-humanoid supervision pipeline that facilitates near-real-time generation of controller-aligned 60-DoF humanoid action chunks from synchronized camera-only human videos, thereby significantly improving data throughput and enabling VLA models to generalize to real-robot tasks with reduced or zero target-task robot demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Human-as-Humanoid framework integrates a custom-designed humanoid embodiment, a multi-stage data processing pipeline, and a VLA learning architecture incorporating kinematic constraints.

**Human-Aligned Humanoid Embodiment (PrimeU):**
The system is built upon PrimeU, a 60-DoF upper-body humanoid designed to reduce the embodiment gap between humans and robots.
- **Morphological Alignment:** PrimeU's upper body proportions (shoulder width, arm length, overall height, hand scale) are designed to align with standard adult-male anthropometric references (e.g., ANSUR II), ensuring that common human demonstrations correspond closely to the robot's reachable workspace. It features two 7-DoF arms, a 3-DoF neck, and a 3-DoF waist.
- **Dexterous Hands:** It is equipped with two 20-DoF Wuji dexterous hands, enabling fine manipulation and detailed finger articulation.
- **Sensing Alignment:** The robot employs head-view and wrist-view Intel RealSense D435 cameras. These viewpoints are configured to mirror the human ego-exo camera streams used during data collection, ensuring consistency between policy observations during training and deployment.
- **Action-Interface Design:** PrimeU's control interface exposes a unified 60-DoF joint-space action vector. This design ensures that human demonstrations are directly converted into a format executable by the robot's controller, encompassing arms, hands, neck, and waist.

**Near-Real-Time Human-Centric Action Generation Pipeline:**
This pipeline converts synchronized camera-only human demonstrations into robot-executable 60-DoF action labels. It operates at approximately 20 FPS, close to typical video capture rates.
- **Data Capture:** Human demonstrations are recorded using a head-mounted egocentric camera (for policy observation) and one or more exocentric RGB cameras (for robust upper-body and hand motion recovery, especially under occlusion). All camera streams are time-aligned.
- **Stage 1: Human Tracking:** The human demonstrator is tracked in the exocentric video, and a target mask is propagated across short temporal windows.
- **Stage 2: Mesh-Aware Human Reconstruction:** A dedicated module recovers the upper-body pose and hand motion from the tracked video, generating a compact camera-coordinate skeleton with keypoints for the upper body and hands.
- **Stage 3: Skeleton Alignment and Smoothing:** The recovered skeleton is smoothed in a root-relative coordinate frame and mapped into PrimeU's specific kinematic convention. This involves axis alignment, interpolation of torso and neck joints, and the construction of palm frames for both hands.
- **Stage 4: Staged Inverse Kinematics (IK) Retargeting:** To convert the human skeleton into PrimeU's 60-DoF joint commands, a staged IK solver is employed. This method decomposes the complex 60-DoF IK problem into manageable parts:
 - **Hands First:** Fingertip points and finger-segment directions from the human demonstration are targeted. A calibrated similarity transform aligns observed human fingertip positions to robot targets, which are then minimized by a seed retargeter followed by per-finger Levenberg–Marquardt refinement.
 - **Arms and Wrists:** Arm motion is solved using damped Jacobian IK with wrist, palm, and elbow targets. Wrist orientation is extracted from the hand frame and refined.
 - **Neck and Waist:** Motion is resolved using semantic head and torso frames, minimizing an SO(3) residual.
 - **Post-Processing:** The resulting joint trajectory is filtered to adhere to joint limits, velocity bounds, and acceleration bounds. A guarded rule ensures that local wrist-palm refinements only improve task-space residuals.
- **Output:** The pipeline outputs robot training tuples (o_t, ℓ, q_t, q\*_{t+1:t+H}), where o_t is the egocentric observation, ℓ is the language instruction, q_t is the current robot state, and q\*_{t+1:t+H} is a future 60-DoF robot joint action chunk, all aligned with the PrimeU's URDF and joint convention.

**PhysDex: VLA Learning from Human-Derived High-DoF Actions:**
PhysDex is the VLA policy trained using the human-derived action labels.
- **Action Space:** The policy predicts future-relative 60-DoF joint offsets, which are decoded back to absolute joint positions. This joint-space representation avoids deployment-time IK and preserves the null-space structure of dexterous hands.
- **Action Backbone:**
 - Visual-language context (objects, hands, contact cues) is encoded by PhysBrain, a manipulation-aware VLM.
 - The action model, a conditional flow-matching Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model (DiT), directly incorporates proprioception and diffusion noise. It is trained to predict the velocity field required to transform a noisy chunk into the target action chunk.
 - At inference, the velocity field is integrated to yield a 60-DoF action chunk for direct control.
- **Dual-Space Hierarchical Kinematic Constraint (DS-HKC):** To address the challenge of precise task-space geometry within a joint-space action output, DS-HKC is introduced.
 - It applies differentiable Forward Kinematics (FK) using the PrimeU URDF to the predicted joint states, generating wrist positions (W(q)), wrist rotations (R(q)), and fingertip positions (P(q)).
 - **Hierarchical Supervision:** Losses are applied at two levels:
 - **Wrist Level (L_wrist):** Constrains the proximal hand pose by supervising wrist positions and orientations.
 - **Fingertip Level (L_tip):** Constrains distal contact geometry by supervising fingertip positions.
 - **Overall DS-HKC Loss (L_dshkc):** Combines L_wrist, L_tip, and a joint-limit feasibility term (L_lim). This objective implicitly weights joint-space errors through the robot's Jacobian, ensuring that joint deviations causing larger task-space displacements receive stronger corrective gradients.
- **Training Objective:** The total loss function combines the flow-matching term (L_fm), absolute pose error (L_abs), action delta error (L_∆), temporal smoothness (L_sm), and the DS-HKC term (L_dshkc) with a warm-up coefficient for DS-HKC.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments focused on validating the Human-as-Humanoid conversion pipeline and the PhysDex VLA policy across multiple dimensions.

**Embodiment Alignment:**
- PrimeU’s upper-body dimensions (shoulder breadth, arm reach, hand length) were found to be closely aligned with 50th-percentile male anthropometrics (e.g., shoulder-to-middle-fingertip reach: 80.3 cm for PrimeU vs. 78.6 cm for human). This structural compatibility minimizes the morphological gap between human demonstrations and the robot's capabilities, facilitating retargeting.
- Qualitative end-to-end replay (Figure 6) confirmed that the recovered human motion, after retargeting into PrimeU joint commands, preserved the main phases of bimanual manipulation in both simulator and real-robot environments. This indicated that the human-aligned design of PrimeU supported the conversion process.

**Ego-Exo Motion Recovery (Observation-Motion Compatibility):**
- A projection-based diagnostic compared camera-only ego-exo motion recovery with wearable inertial motion capture. The ego-exo recovery method maintained closer projected alignment with the human body and hands in the video compared to the wearable suit, which exhibited visible localization drift (Figure 5).
- This finding supports the stability and accuracy of the camera-only approach for recovering manipulation-relevant joints, allowing the egocentric stream to serve as the policy input without being compromised by the motion recovery process.

**Action-Interface Compatibility:**
- A discrete action tokenizer, trained exclusively on 60-DoF robot-action chunks derived from human data, was evaluated on held-out real-robot trajectories. This cross-domain test showed low reconstruction error, with a mean normalized MAE of 0.0080 and a 95th-percentile normalized MAE of 0.0097 (Table 3).
- In terms of task-space impact, the human-only tokenizer reconstructed unseen robot windows with a mean bi-manual end-effector error of 5.34 mm. For comparison, a tokenizer trained solely on robot data yielded 4.09 mm, and one trained on both robot and human-derived data yielded 4.86 mm. This indicated that the human-derived actions generated by the staged-IK pipeline produced a normalized action manifold compatible with real PrimeU demonstrations, preserving millimeter-scale replay accuracy in task space.

**FK-Aware VLA Training (Joint-Task Consistency):**
- The inclusion of Dual-Space Hierarchical Kinematic Constraint (DS-HKC) supervision during VLA training led to smoother and lower loss curves compared to training with joint-space action losses alone (Figure 7).
- This suggests that FK-aware supervision enhances the optimization geometry by leveraging the robot's kinematic chain to constrain joint predictions and enforce consistency with task-space wrist and fingertip geometry. The joint-limit term also contributed to physically valid solutions.

**Data Efficiency and Target-Task Deployment:**
- **Throughput Gain:** The Human-as-Humanoid pipeline demonstrated a 4.8–7.2x raw demonstration-throughput gain compared to motion-capture teleoperation methods in data collection analysis.
- **Zero-Shot Deployment:** PhysDex policies, post-trained exclusively with 1,500 hours of human-derived 60-DoF action labels (without any target-task robot demonstrations), successfully generalized to real-robot deployment across several manipulation tasks, including ring placement, magic-cube packing, cup stacking, and water pouring (Figure 8).
- **Performance Comparison:** PhysDex achieved higher composite scores than GR00T N1.7 across seven real-robot manipulation tasks (Figure 9). The performance improvement was more substantial in tasks where only human-derived data was used for adaptation, demonstrating the direct transferability of the generated labels. For tasks requiring precise contact (e.g., temperature-gun measurement, light-bulb loosening, bottle-cap loosening), where limited real-robot data was provided for both methods, PhysDex still showed higher composite scores, suggesting that the human-derived pretraining reduced the reliance on extensive robot-specific data.
- These results collectively support the conclusion that human demonstrations can serve as a stable and scalable source of executable action supervision for VLA learning, complementing or reducing the need for target-task robot data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a fundamental bottleneck in the development and scaling of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for high-DoF humanoid robots: the acquisition of diverse and executable observation-action supervision data. By introducing Human-as-Humanoid, the work offers a method to convert large-scale human video demonstrations into robot-compatible action labels, which has several implications:

- **Enhanced Data Scalability:** The pipeline's ability to convert ego-exo human videos into 60-DoF humanoid action chunks at near-real-time rates (4.8–7.2x throughput gain over teleoperation) provides a more scalable and cost-effective approach to data generation. This can significantly accelerate the training data acquisition process for complex humanoid manipulation tasks.
- **Broader Utilization of Human Data:** The framework transforms human demonstrations from a source of perceptual or high-level information into direct, executable robot commands. This allows VLA models to leverage the inherent diversity and complexity of human bimanual manipulation behaviors, including fine dexterous movements, directly as training supervision for robot control.
- **Reduced Reliance on Robot-Specific Data:** The demonstrated capability for zero target-task robot-demonstration deployment for several tasks is a notable contribution. For tasks that are more sensitive to hardware grounding and contact forces, the framework also shows that human-derived pretraining can reduce the amount of real robot data required for successful deployment. This allows robot resources to be focused on critical hardware grounding and refinement rather than extensive general data collection.
- **Advancements in Humanoid VLA Training:** The introduction of PrimeU, a human-aligned 60-DoF humanoid embodiment, and the Dual-Space Hierarchical Kinematic Constraint (DS-HKC) for FK-aware VLA training are methodological advancements. DS-HKC specifically addresses the challenge of maintaining task-space geometric consistency (wrist and fingertip positions) while learning in a joint-space action domain, which is crucial for dexterous manipulation.
- **Facilitating Generalist Robot Development:** By making high-DoF action supervision more accessible and scalable, this research contributes to the development of more capable and generalist humanoid robots. These robots could potentially learn a wider range of manipulation skills from human examples and adapt more quickly to new tasks with less robot-specific fine-tuning.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work also identifies limitations and pathways for future research, such as improving egocentric-only motion recovery under occlusion and exploring the synthesis of hand-object interaction data to further expand the corpus of humanoid-executable action supervision.

Overall, Human-as-Humanoid represents a step towards making human expertise more directly transferable to complex robotic systems, potentially accelerating the deployment of versatile humanoid robots in various applications by offering a practical solution to the robot data bottleneck.
