# RL-augmented Adaptive Model Predictive Control for Bipedal Locomotion over Challenging Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Institute of Technology and Northeastern University developed an RL-augmented Model Predictive Control framework for bipedal robots, enabling robust locomotion over challenging terrains. This method adaptively modifies system dynamics, swing leg trajectories, and gait frequency, achieving significantly higher success rates (e.g., 86% on 10cm stairs vs. 1% for baseline MPC) and better tracking across stairs, stepping stones, and slippery surfaces in simulation.

## Problem

- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) for bipedal robots suffers from model mismatch due to simplified dynamics, limiting robustness on rough or uneven terrain.
- Fixed swing leg trajectories and static gait frequencies in conventional MPC lack adaptability, increasing collision risk and instability on varied surfaces.
- Pure reinforcement learning (RL) methods lack explicit constraint satisfaction, raising safety concerns, and often require extensive, sample-inefficient training.

## Method

- A hierarchical **RL-augmented** ==MPC== framework was developed where an RL policy learns to predict residual parameters that adaptively adjust three core MPC components: system dynamics, swing leg trajectory, and gait frequency.
- The RL policy outputs residuals for linear/angular accelerations, swing foot apex height and control point coefficients, and a coefficient for MPC sampling time to modulate step duration.
- A GPU-accelerated batched MPC controller was implemented using CusADi to improve training efficiency, allowing for parallelized computation across numerous simulation environments.

## Results

- The RL-augmented MPC achieved an **86%** success rate on 10 cm pyramid stairs compared to **1%** for the baseline MPC, and **74%** success on surfaces with a friction coefficient of **0.05** compared to **5%** for the baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the full RL-augmented MPC framework consistently yielded the highest success rates and lowest tracking errors, with swing leg trajectory adaptation proving to be the most critical module for robust locomotion.
- The GPU-accelerated batched MPC using CusADi demonstrated significantly lower computation times and better scalability, reducing training time for RL policies while maintaining comparable locomotion performance to CPU-based methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting MPC with learned residuals for system dynamics, swing leg trajectory, and gait frequency significantly enhances bipedal robot adaptability and robustness on challenging terrains.
> - Swing leg trajectory adaptation, specifically adjusting swing height and control points, is critical for successfully navigating rough terrain like stairs and preventing tripping.
> - The RL policy's residuals are event-driven, showing pronounced changes at foot contact on stairs for obstacle clearance and dynamically adjusting gait parameters to mitigate slip on low-friction surfaces.
