# RL-augmented Adaptive Model Predictive Control for Bipedal Locomotion over Challenging Terrain

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Institute of Technology and Northeastern University developed an RL-augmented Model Predictive Control framework for bipedal robots, enabling robust locomotion over challenging terrains. This method adaptively modifies system dynamics, swing leg trajectories, and gait frequency, achieving significantly higher success rates (e.g., 86% on 10cm stairs vs. 1% for baseline MPC) and better tracking across stairs, stepping stones, and slippery surfaces in simulation.

## Problem

- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) for bipedal robots suffers from model mismatch due to simplified dynamics, limiting robustness on rough or uneven terrain.
- Fixed swing leg trajectories and static gait frequencies in conventional MPC lack adaptability, increasing collision risk and instability on varied surfaces.
- Pure reinforcement learning (RL) methods lack explicit constraint satisfaction, raising safety concerns, and often require extensive, sample-inefficient training.

## Method

- A hierarchical **RL-augmented** ==MPC== framework was developed where an RL policy learns to predict residual parameters that adaptively adjust three core MPC components: system dynamics, swing leg trajectory, and gait frequency.
- The RL policy outputs residuals for linear/angular accelerations, swing foot apex height and control point coefficients, and a coefficient for MPC sampling time to modulate step duration.
- A GPU-accelerated batched MPC controller was implemented using CusADi to improve training efficiency, allowing for parallelized computation across numerous simulation environments.

## Results

- The RL-augmented MPC achieved an **86%** success rate on 10 cm pyramid stairs compared to **1%** for the baseline MPC, and **74%** success on surfaces with a friction coefficient of **0.05** compared to **5%** for the baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the full RL-augmented MPC framework consistently yielded the highest success rates and lowest tracking errors, with swing leg trajectory adaptation proving to be the most critical module for robust locomotion.
- The GPU-accelerated batched MPC using CusADi demonstrated significantly lower computation times and better scalability, reducing training time for RL policies while maintaining comparable locomotion performance to CPU-based methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting MPC with learned residuals for system dynamics, swing leg trajectory, and gait frequency significantly enhances bipedal robot adaptability and robustness on challenging terrains.
> - Swing leg trajectory adaptation, specifically adjusting swing height and control points, is critical for successfully navigating rough terrain like stairs and preventing tripping.
> - The RL policy's residuals are event-driven, showing pronounced changes at foot contact on stairs for obstacle clearance and dynamically adjusting gait parameters to mitigate slip on low-friction surfaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Legged locomotion, particularly for bipedal robots, presents significant control challenges, especially in dynamic and unstructured environments. Historically, two primary paradigms have been employed: model-based control (MBC) and learning-based control (LBC).

Model Predictive Control (MPC), a prominent MBC technique, is favored for its ability to incorporate explicit constraints and guarantee stability, which is crucial for robot safety and performance. However, MPC often relies on simplified dynamic models (e.g., single rigid body dynamics), leading to model mismatch. This simplification, combined with the need for predefined contact sequences and swing leg trajectories, limits its adaptability and robustness when encountering rough, uneven, or slippery terrain. While whole-body dynamics models can offer higher accuracy, their computational cost makes them less suitable for real-time control.

In contrast, reinforcement learning (RL), a key LBC method, has demonstrated considerable success in training agile and robust locomotion policies over diverse terrains. RL policies, typically parameterized by neural networks, can learn complex, reactive behaviors and exhibit zero-shot transfer from simulation to reality. Nevertheless, RL methods typically lack explicit constraint satisfaction, which can raise safety concerns. Furthermore, training robust RL policies often requires extensive environmental interactions and careful reward shaping, making them sample-inefficient and challenging to deploy.

Recent research efforts have focused on combining MPC and RL to leverage the strengths of both approaches while mitigating their individual limitations. These hybrid strategies aim to integrate MPC's safety and constraint satisfaction with RL's adaptability and robustness. The existing literature on combined approaches in legged robotics generally falls into three architectural categories:
1. **Parallel architecture:** RL refines or augments MPC's output actions (e.g., adding corrective footholds or joint commands). While flexible, this can bypass MPC's feasibility constraints, potentially compromising safety.
2. **Imitation architecture:** MPC acts as an expert, generating trajectories or actions that an RL policy then imitates. This can improve training efficiency but may incur significant computational overhead for online trajectory generation and can limit generalizability if trajectories are pre-generated.
3. **Hierarchical architecture:** RL parametrizes specific components or parameters within the MPC framework, and MPC then computes the final control inputs. This approach maintains the optimization structure and constraint satisfaction of MPC, as the RL policy's outputs are evaluated before the optimization problem is solved.

Despite these advancements, most combined MPC-RL approaches, particularly for bipedal robots, have largely been restricted to relatively flat terrains or emphasize improving tracking accuracy rather than broad adaptability to challenging environments. Prior work often focuses on quadrupedal robots or limited bipedal scenarios such as short staircases or small stepping stones. The integration of MPC and RL specifically for enhancing bipedal locomotion adaptability over *genuinely rough and slippery terrain* remains an area requiring further investigation. This work addresses this gap by proposing a hierarchical RL-augmented MPC framework specifically tailored for robust bipedal locomotion in such challenging environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of traditional model-based control (MPC) in enabling adaptive and robust bipedal locomotion over complex, real-world terrains, and the safety and sample efficiency issues associated with pure reinforcement learning (RL) approaches. While MPC offers explicit constraint satisfaction and stability guarantees, its performance degrades significantly on challenging terrain due to:
1. **Model Mismatch:** Simplified dynamic models (e.g., Single Rigid Body Dynamics - SRBD) used in MPC for computational efficiency do not fully capture whole-body dynamics, foot-terrain interactions, and inertial forces, leading to inaccuracies.
2. **Predefined Swing Leg Trajectories:** Conventional MPC often relies on heuristic or predefined swing leg trajectories, which lack the adaptability required to navigate obstacles, steps, or uneven surfaces without collision or instability.
3. **Static Gait Frequency:** Fixed gait frequencies limit the robot's ability to adjust its stepping behavior (e.g., step length, duration) in response to varying terrain conditions or disturbances, hindering agility and robustness.

The central objective of this work is to develop an RL-augmented MPC framework that overcomes these limitations for bipedal robots. Specifically, the authors aim to:
- **Enhance Adaptability:** Enable the humanoid robot to exhibit reactive and robust behaviors when facing terrain disturbances, such as rough surfaces, stairs, stepping stones, and slippery environments.
- **Integrate Whole-Body Information:** Use an RL policy to implicitly incorporate rich whole-body dynamics information into the simplified SRBD model of the MPC, thereby reducing model mismatch.
- **Modulate Stepping Behavior:** Allow the RL policy to adapt the gait frequency, dynamically adjusting step length and duration to suit the terrain.
- **Improve Swing Foot Clearance and Placement:** Enable the RL policy to modify the swing foot trajectory in real-time to ensure collision-free movement and stable foot placement on irregular terrain.
- **Validate Performance:** Demonstrate through simulation that the proposed RL-augmented MPC framework significantly improves robustness and adaptability compared to baseline MPC and vanilla RL methods across a range of challenging bipedal locomotion tasks.
- **Analyze Contributions:** Conduct ablation studies to understand the individual contributions of each RL-augmented module to the overall performance.

By pursuing these objectives, the research seeks to combine the safety and stability benefits of MPC with the adaptive capabilities of RL, specifically addressing critical weaknesses that restrict bipedal robots from operating effectively in diverse and challenging real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a hierarchical RL-augmented Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework designed for bipedal locomotion. An RL policy learns to adaptively parametrize three key components of a Single Rigid Body Dynamics (SRBD) MPC controller: the system dynamics, the swing leg trajectory, and the gait frequency. This framework is implemented and validated in simulation using the HECTOR robot within NVIDIA IsaacLab.

**A. Core MPC Controller**
The foundation is an SRBD MPC controller that minimizes a cost function (tracking error, input cost) subject to constraints.
- **Dynamics:** The SRBD is formulated as a nonlinear system, then linearized to `˙x = Ax + Bu` for efficient convex optimization. The state `x` includes CoM position, orientation, linear velocity, and angular velocity. The control input `u` includes ground reaction forces and moments.
- **Optimization Problem:** The MPC solves a constrained optimization problem over a prediction horizon `N` to compute optimal contact wrenches. Constraints include discrete-time dynamics, contact state, friction pyramid (μ|Fz|), force saturation (Fmax), and line contact constraints.
- **Low-Level Control:** MPC outputs are converted into joint torque commands. Stance foot torques are computed via Jacobian mapping. Swing foot torques use a Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller with target setpoints from analytical inverse kinematics (IK). Foothold planning is based on Raibert heuristics, and the reference swing foot trajectory uses a cubic Bézier curve, defined by start, end (foothold), and two control points. The nominal swing height (`êh`) and swing phase duration (`Ts`) are predefined.

**B. RL Augmentation Modules**
The RL policy learns to output residual parameters that augment the nominal MPC.

1. **System Dynamics Adaptation:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Model mismatch from SRBD simplifications (e.g., ignoring leg masses, varying inertia, unmodeled foot inertia, ground impact).
 - **Approach:** The RL policy learns *residual dynamics* `fr(x,u)` that act as offset terms to the linear and angular accelerations. These residuals are structured as an affine control system, specifically modeling perturbations at the acceleration level.
 - **Parameterization:** `fr` is simplified into residual state terms (`f(1)x`, `f(2)x` for linear and angular accelerations) and simplified diagonal residual actuation matrices (`f(1)u`, `f(2)u`). This results in a total of 12 residual parameters (3 for `f(1)x`, 3 for `f(2)x`, 3 for `f(1)u` diagonal elements, 3 for `f(2)u` diagonal elements) that the RL policy predicts. These residuals are added to the linearized nominal dynamics.

2. **Swing Leg Trajectory Adaptation:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Fixed swing leg trajectories are not adaptive to rough or varied terrain, increasing collision risk.
 - **Approach:** The RL policy adapts the shape of the cubic Bézier curve used for swing leg trajectory generation.
 - **Parameterization:** Two residual parameters are learned:
 - `Δh`: A residual apex height, which adjusts the overall height of the swing foot trajectory relative to the nominal swing height (`êh`).
 - `Δcp`: A control point coefficient, which modifies the positions of the Bézier curve's intermediate control points (`p1`, `p2`), shifting the trajectory forward or backward to optimize foot clearance and placement.

3. **Gait Frequency Adaptation:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Static gait frequency limits the robot's ability to adjust step duration and stride length on different terrains.
 - **Approach:** The RL policy adjusts the MPC sampling time (`dtmpc`), which directly influences the swing phase duration (`Ts`).
 - **Parameterization:** A single MPC sampling time coefficient `s` is learned. The `dtmpc` is updated as `êdtmpc(1 + s)`. This affects the double-support and single-support durations, allowing the robot to take shorter or longer steps as needed.

**C. Reinforcement Learning Formulation**
- **MDP:** The problem of finding optimal residual parameters is framed as a Markov Decision Process.
- **Action Space:** The RL agent's action `a_t` is a vector consisting of the 12 dynamics residuals, 2 swing foot controller residuals (`Δh`, `Δcp`), and 1 MPC sampling time coefficient (`s`). These raw policy outputs are scaled to predefined ranges (Table I).
- **Observation Space:** The policy receives proprioceptive data (base linear/angular velocity, joint positions/velocities), command velocities, projected gravity, and foot states from the MPC (current/reference foot positions, planned footholds, swing phase). The previous action (`a_t-1`) is also included for smoothness.
- **Reward Functions:** A combination of standard locomotion rewards (velocity command tracking, CoM height tracking, joint velocity minimization, action smoothness, reduced feet slide, knee collision avoidance) and problem-specific penalties (base-to-stance-foot angle, step width) are used (Table III).
- **Algorithm:** Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), an off-policy model-free DRL algorithm, is used for its sample efficiency. The actor and critic networks are three-layer MLPs.
- **Training:** Training is conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 using the GPU-accelerated NVIDIA IsaacLab simulation platform. MPC and RL operate at 100 Hz, while low-level control and physics simulation run at 400 Hz. A squared action loss is applied to prevent action saturation and promote exploration.
- **Terrain Curriculum:** During training, a terrain curriculum adapts the robot's initial position to progressively more difficult regions based on its walking success.

**D. Batched MPC on GPU**
For improved training efficiency, the authors develop a batched MPC controller implemented on GPUs using CusADi. This sparse quadratic programming (QP) solver, based on the primal-dual interior point method, enables parallelized computation across thousands of environments, accelerating RL policy training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed RL-augmented MPC framework was conducted through extensive bipedal robot simulations in NVIDIA IsaacLab, comparing its performance against a baseline MPC controller and a vanilla RL policy.

**A. Per-Terrain Evaluation (Table V)**
The framework was tested across four challenging terrain types: pyramid stairs, random stairs, stepping stones, and slippery surfaces, with varying levels of difficulty (step height or friction coefficient).
- **Overall Performance:** The RL-augmented MPC consistently achieved significantly higher success rates and maintained better velocity and orientation tracking accuracy compared to the baseline MPC across all challenging terrains.
- **Pyramid Stairs:** On 10 cm stairs, the baseline MPC's success rate dropped to 1%, while the RL-augmented method maintained an 86% success rate. Tracking errors for linear velocity and orientation were also notably lower for the proposed method.
- **Random Stairs:** Similar trends were observed, with the proposed method sustaining an 85% success rate on 10 cm random stairs compared to 15% for the baseline. The overall success rate was slightly lower than pyramid stairs due to complex ascending-descending patterns.
- **Stepping Stones:** The RL-augmented MPC consistently outperformed the baseline in success rate, while tracking errors were comparable, suggesting that stepping stones presented a comparatively lower challenge for both methods.
- **Slippery Surface:** On surfaces with a friction coefficient (μ) as low as 0.05, the baseline MPC's success rate was 5%, whereas the proposed method achieved 74%. The RL-augmented MPC also demonstrated better velocity tracking and effectively stabilized the torso's roll angle, which is critical for resisting lateral slip.

**B. Role of RL Residuals (Fig. 3)**
Analysis of the RL policy's corrective actions provided insight into its adaptive behaviors:
- **On Stairs:** At foot contact, the policy produced pronounced spikes in `Δh`, `Δcp`, and `s`. `Δh` increased by up to 10 cm, raising the swing foot height to prevent tripping. `Δcp` decreased to approximately -0.2, shifting the swing trajectory backward, an adaptation critical for navigating stair edges. Residuals in linear and angular accelerations also increased, particularly in the vertical direction, to enhance normal force at contact. During steady walking on flat terrain, these residuals remained small and periodic, indicating event-driven activation.
- **On Slippery Terrain:** The amplitude and frequency of residuals increased with reduced friction. The MPC sampling time coefficient `s` caused `dtmpc` to decrease (e.g., from 1.0 to 0.75), leading to shorter step durations and reduced stride length to mitigate lateral slip. `Δh` decreased to around -2.5 cm, lowering the swing foot for faster step-down motions. Residual pitch and yaw accelerations exhibited large oscillations, indicating active balance control against slipping.

**C. Ablation Study (Table VII)**
An ablation study on 10 cm pyramid stairs evaluated the contribution of each adaptation module (system dynamics, swing leg trajectory, gait frequency) by testing various combinations:
- The study demonstrated that the full RL-augmented MPC (`res-all`) achieved the highest success rate (86%) and lowest tracking errors.
- Configurations lacking swing foot trajectory adaptation (`dyn-gait`, `dyn`, `gait`) exhibited catastrophic failure (1-2% success rates), indicating the critical importance of swing leg adaptation on rough terrain.
- While individual modules contributed, combinations like `swing-gait` and `dyn-swing` showed lower success rates and higher tracking errors than `swing` alone in some metrics, suggesting that optimal performance requires the combined action of all three modules.
- The baseline `vanilla-MPC` and a `vanilla-RL` policy (trained purely in joint space) performed significantly worse than `res-all`, with `vanilla-RL` showing poor orientation tracking.

**D. Batched MPC on GPU (Fig. 4)**
Preliminary results from a GPU-accelerated batched MPC controller using CusADi showed substantial computational performance improvements:
- The custom QP solver maintained significantly lower computation times across various batch sizes compared to qpOASES (CPU-based) and qpth (PyTorch-based batched QP solver), especially for larger batch sizes where other solvers experienced memory issues or substantial slowdowns.
- This GPU implementation achieved locomotion performance comparable to the CPU baseline, with the policy attaining an 85% success rate on 10 cm pyramid stairs, validating its effectiveness for efficient training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant step towards enabling robust and adaptive bipedal locomotion in highly challenging, unstructured environments. The proposed RL-augmented MPC framework addresses key limitations of traditional model-based controllers and pure reinforcement learning approaches, offering a balanced solution that combines the strengths of both paradigms.

The **significance** of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Bridging the Gap:** It effectively bridges the gap in combined MPC-RL research for bipedal robots by demonstrating successful locomotion over genuinely rough and slippery terrains, a domain where prior methods were largely limited.
- **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness:** By allowing the RL policy to adapt system dynamics, swing leg trajectories, and gait frequency, the framework enables a humanoid robot to react dynamically to unforeseen terrain changes and disturbances, achieving significantly higher success rates and more accurate tracking than conventional methods.
- **Preserving MPC Benefits:** The hierarchical architecture ensures that the core MPC optimization problem retains its structure and constraint satisfaction, mitigating the safety concerns often associated with pure RL or parallel control architectures. The RL policy *augments* MPC rather than overriding it.
- **Structured Learning:** Learning *residual parameters* for specific, physically interpretable components of the MPC controller provides a more structured and potentially more stable learning process compared to learning raw control actions or full dynamics.
- **Insights into Adaptive Mechanisms:** The analysis of RL residuals provides valuable insights into how the policy achieves adaptation, showing specific adjustments to swing height, step length, and reactive forces/moments in response to different terrain challenges.

The **potential impact** of this research is substantial, particularly for advancing robotics in real-world applications:
- **More Capable Bipedal Robots:** The framework can lead to the development of bipedal robots that are more robust and versatile, capable of navigating complex and unpredictable terrains beyond controlled laboratory settings. This is crucial for applications in disaster response, exploration, and domestic assistance.
- **Foundation for Real-World Deployment:** By enhancing robustness and adaptability in simulation, this work lays a critical foundation for future hardware implementations. The planned hardware experiments will be a crucial next step towards practical deployment.
- **Improved Training Efficiency:** The development of a GPU-accelerated batched MPC solver represents a significant contribution to the broader field of robot learning. This acceleration can drastically reduce the time and computational resources required for training complex RL policies, facilitating faster iteration and development of advanced robotic behaviors.
- **Future Research Directions:** This work naturally opens avenues for further research, including incorporating perception into the locomotion controller to enable proactive adaptation and extending the framework to full real-world hardware validation.

In conclusion, this research provides a comprehensive and effective solution for enabling bipedal robots to traverse challenging terrain robustly, combining the strengths of model-based and learning-based control in a principled manner.
