# PhysX-Omni: Unified Simulation-Ready Physical 3D Generation for Rigid, Deformable, and Articulated Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysX-Omni provides a unified framework for generating diverse simulation-ready 3D objects, encompassing rigid, deformable, and articulated structures, complete with physical attributes. The research also introduces PhysXVerse, a large-scale physical 3D dataset, and PhysX-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating generated physical properties and geometry.

## Problem

- Most existing 3D generation methods prioritize visual realism and geometric detail, neglecting crucial physical attributes needed for physics-based simulations.
- Current approaches for physical 3D generation are limited, often focusing on single object types or a narrow set of physical attributes, restricting versatility.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, physically annotated 3D datasets for training generalizable models, and an absence of robust benchmarks for assessing simulation-ready 3D generative models across diverse "in-the-wild" scenarios.

## Method

- Developed **PhysX-Omni**, a unified VLM-based framework generating simulation-ready physical 3D assets for rigid, deformable, and articulated objects, using a novel template-based Run-Length Encoding (RLE) geometry representation that directly models high-resolution 3D structures without additional special tokens.
- Constructed PhysXVerse, the first general simulation-ready 3D dataset comprising over 8.7K assets across 2.9K categories, with human-verified physical annotations.
- Proposed PhysX-Bench, a comprehensive VLM-based benchmark for evaluating six key physical attributes (geometry, scale, material, affordance, kinematics, description) in diverse settings.

## Results

- PhysX-Omni demonstrated superior geometric quality on PhysXVerse (PSNR **21.52**, Chamfer Distance **2.95×10⁻³**, F-score **91.28×10⁻²**), reduced absolute scale error to **2.79** (vs. over **298** for baselines), and achieved a kinematic score of **0.9185** with top scores for material, affordance, and description.
- The framework's generalization ability on PhysX-Bench showed strong performance across all attributes, with high scores for absolute scale (64.26), material (59.89), affordance (70.57), and kinematics (80.72).
- Generated simulation-ready assets were successfully deployed in downstream tasks, including robotic policy learning and simulation-ready scene generation, demonstrating reliable physical accuracy and coherent articulated behaviors in dynamic interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A coarse-to-fine, global-to-local VLM-based reasoning process effectively infers high-level global context before predicting detailed part-level geometry and physical attributes.
> - The template-based RLE geometry representation leverages spatial redundancy across 3D slices, significantly reducing token redundancy and sequence length while preserving high-resolution geometric details.
> - Building large-scale, human-verified datasets like PhysXVerse is crucial for training generalizable physical 3D generative models, and VLM-based evaluation (as in PhysX-Bench) provides a robust, human-aligned method for assessing generated physical attributes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of three-dimensional (3D) assets is a significant area of research, with most existing methods primarily focused on achieving photorealistic appearance and detailed geometric structures. While these appearance-centric approaches have demonstrated advanced generative capabilities, the resulting 3D assets often lack essential physical attributes, such as material properties, absolute scale, and kinematic information, which are necessary for deployment in physics-based simulations or real-world applications like robotics and embodied artificial intelligence (AI).

To address this gap, some prior works have begun to explore the generation of physical 3D assets. These efforts typically concentrate on specific asset categories, such as articulated objects or deformable objects, modeling a limited subset of physical attributes relevant to that particular type. Pioneering works in simulation-ready physical 3D generation have enabled the synthesis of richer physical properties but have been constrained by a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality annotated 3D datasets. This data limitation restricts the diversity and practical utility of generated assets for downstream tasks. Furthermore, the absence of comprehensive and robust benchmarks for evaluating physical attributes in "in-the-wild" scenarios has hindered meaningful assessment of generative models in this domain.

PhysX-Omni positions itself as an advancement in this landscape by proposing a unified framework that supports the generation of diverse object types—rigid, deformable, and articulated—with a comprehensive set of physical attributes. It aims to overcome the limitations of single-category focus and data scarcity by introducing a new large-scale dataset and a dedicated evaluation benchmark. This work builds upon previous endeavors like PhysXGen and PhysX-Anything, which also focused on simulation-ready assets, but seeks to provide a more general, efficient, and robust solution for generating physically plausible 3D content suitable for a wider range of downstream applications, including embodied AI and physics-based simulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing demand for high-quality simulation-ready (sim-ready) 3D assets across various domains, including gaming design, robotics, embodied AI, and interactive simulation. The authors identify several limitations in existing 3D generation methods that impede their practical applicability in these fields:
1. **Neglect of Physical Properties:** Conventional 3D generation primarily targets visual realism and geometric detail, often overlooking crucial physical attributes necessary for realistic interaction within simulated environments.
2. **Limited Scope of Physical 3D Generation:** Current approaches that do incorporate physical properties typically specialize in a single type of asset (e.g., articulated or deformable objects) or a limited set of physical attributes, thereby restricting the diversity and versatility of the generated content.
3. **Data Scarcity:** A significant challenge is the lack of large-scale, high-quality 3D datasets that are extensively annotated with a wide range of physical properties, which is essential for training generalizable physical 3D generative models.
4. **Inadequate Evaluation Benchmarks:** The absence of effective and comprehensive benchmarks for evaluating physical attributes in diverse, real-world scenarios makes it difficult to objectively assess the performance and generalizability of sim-ready 3D generation methods.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of the PhysX-Omni research are:
- To develop **PhysX-Omni**, a unified generative framework capable of producing simulation-ready physical 3D assets for a diverse range of object types, encompassing rigid, deformable, and articulated structures.
- To introduce a novel and efficient **geometry representation** specifically designed for Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This representation aims to directly encode high-resolution 3D structures without relying on data compression techniques that might reduce fidelity or additional segmentation modules that could introduce bottlenecks, thereby improving generation performance.
- To construct **PhysXVerse**, presented as the first general simulation-ready 3D dataset. This dataset is intended to provide a large-scale collection of diverse indoor and outdoor object categories with high-quality physical attribute annotations, mitigating the issue of data scarcity.
- To propose **PhysX-Bench**, the first benchmark for simulation-ready physical 3D generation. This benchmark is designed to offer a comprehensive and flexible evaluation framework, assessing both generative and understanding capabilities across six key physical attributes: geometry, absolute scale, material, affordance, kinematics, and description, in "in-the-wild" scenarios.
- To validate the effectiveness of PhysX-Omni through extensive experiments, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art methods using both conventional metrics and the newly proposed PhysX-Bench.
- To demonstrate the practical applicability and potential impact of the generated simulation-ready assets in downstream tasks, specifically focusing on simulation-ready scene generation and robotic policy learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysX-Omni adopts a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based generative paradigm that proceeds through a coarse-to-fine, global-to-local reasoning process. Given a single image, which can be complete or partially occluded, the framework first performs a holistic understanding to infer high-level global information about the object. This initial stage extracts overall details such as object category, semantic identity, absolute scale, component hierarchy, and preliminary physical properties. This global context serves as a structural and semantic prior for subsequent detailed generation.

Following global understanding, PhysX-Omni predicts the detailed geometric structure and physical attributes for each individual part of the object. For the global representation, the system utilizes a tree-structured and VLM-friendly formulation, organizing object-level and part-level information hierarchically to be compatible with autoregressive vision-language modeling.

A core technical contribution is the **novel geometry representation**. Recognizing the limitations of prior methods that often rely on vertex quantization, VQ-GAN representations, or text-based voxel indices (which may introduce special tokens, limit fidelity, or struggle with high-resolution structures), PhysX-Omni introduces a text-based geometry representation that directly models high-resolution 3D structures without requiring additional special tokens in the language model vocabulary. This is achieved through a **template-based Run-Length Encoding (RLE) approach**:
1. **Voxelization:** Simulation-ready assets are first voxelized and decomposed into part-level voxels based on annotated object structures.
2. **2D Slicing:** Each part-level voxel is sliced along the z-axis, generating a sequence of 2D binary masks.
3. **Compact 2D RLE:** A compact 2D RLE formulation is applied to each slice to encode occupied regions into text tokens.
4. **Template Layers:** To leverage spatial redundancy inherent in 3D structures, especially across neighboring slices, the method introduces template layers. This allows multiple slices to share a common structural template, with only their relative variations or residual differences being stored. This mechanism significantly reduces token redundancy and sequence length while preserving detailed geometric information and maintaining explicit structural representations.

The framework benefits from this representation by enabling direct modeling of complex geometries, preserving explicit structural information, and being compatible with pre-trained voxel-based 3D decoders (such as TRELLIS) for generating high-quality meshes without needing additional mesh segmentation.

To address the issue of data scarcity, the authors constructed **PhysXVerse**, a general simulation-ready physical 3D dataset. This dataset was built by leveraging human-verified segmentation annotations from PartVerse. For physical property annotations, a human-in-the-loop pipeline, adapted from PhysXGen, was employed:
1. **Preprocessing:** Original data was filtered, and excessively small or noisy parts were merged.
2. **VLM-based Annotation:** Multi-view images of 3D assets were rendered and a powerful VLM (GPT) was used to generate preliminary physical annotations, including absolute scale, affordance, material, functional descriptions, and kinematic information.
3. **Human Verification:** These automatically generated annotations were then verified and refined by human annotators to ensure physical plausibility and quality.
PhysXVerse comprises over 8.7K high-quality simulation-ready 3D assets across more than 2.9K categories, exhibiting a wide range of object types and structural complexities (1 to 65 parts).

For comprehensive evaluation, the research proposes **PhysX-Bench**, the first benchmark for simulation-ready physical 3D generation. PhysX-Bench uses an open-source VLM (Qwen3.5-122B-A10B) to evaluate generated physical attributes, utilizing rendered images or videos as inputs instead of raw attribute data, to align with human perception. The benchmark assesses six key dimensions:
- **Geometry:** Evaluated via CLIP alignment (generated output vs. conditioning image), 3D consistency (across multi-view renderings), and visual quality (using a five-level reference grading table).
- **Absolute Scale:** Measures the plausibility of generated dimensions by comparing them with VLM-estimated real-world dimensions using symmetric percentage error.
- **Affordance:** Assesses the human common-sense plausibility of object functionalities, considering local and global rationality, relative ranking, and salient misranking of typical parts.
- **Description:** Evaluates the semantic match between part-level masks (rendered on generated objects) and human-annotated reference descriptions using a VLM.
- **Material:** Indirectly evaluated through physics-based simulation videos (e.g., free-fall for Young's modulus/Poisson's ratio, water-drop for density) to reflect physical behaviors.
- **Kinematics:** Assesses the physical plausibility of motions from rendered videos. Metrics include prior-part motion consistency (for visible parts), revealed-entity plausibility (for newly observable parts), and global articulation coherence.

For training, the model utilized a combination of PhysXNet, PhysX-Mobility, and PhysXVerse datasets, totaling over 42K assets. The VLM backbone was Alibaba Cloud Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, trained for 5 epochs on 64 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. The maximum sequence length was set to 16,384 tokens to support high-resolution structures and long physical descriptions. High-quality 3D meshes were reconstructed from generated voxel representations using TRELLIS.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive evaluations using both conventional metrics and the newly proposed PhysX-Bench, along with qualitative comparisons and application demonstrations.

**Conventional Metrics Evaluation:**
Experiments were conducted on both the PhysXVerse and PhysX-Mobility datasets, comparing PhysX-Omni with Articulate-Anything, MonoArt, PhysXGen, and PhysX-Anything.
- **Geometric Quality:** PhysX-Omni consistently demonstrated superior performance. On PhysXVerse, it achieved a PSNR of 21.52, Chamfer Distance (CD) of 2.95 (×10⁻³), and an F-score (0.05 threshold) of 91.28 (×10⁻²), surpassing previous state-of-the-art methods. This improvement is attributed to the template-based geometry representation, which facilitates cleaner object boundaries, detailed local structures, and coherent articulated components.
- **Physical Attribute Prediction:** Significant improvements were observed across all physical attributes:
 - **Absolute Scale:** A remarkable reduction in error was achieved. On PhysXVerse, the absolute scale error for PhysX-Omni was 2.79, substantially lower than 309.31 for PhysXGen and 298.19 for PhysX-Anything. Similar reductions were seen on PhysX-Mobility (2.78 error).
 - **Material, Affordance, and Description:** PhysX-Omni consistently delivered the best performance. On PhysXVerse, material score improved from 15.65 (PhysX-Anything) to 27.23, and affordance score from 10.50 to 21.47.
 - **Kinematics:** The most substantial gains were in kinematic evaluation. PhysX-Omni achieved a kinematic score of 0.9185 on PhysXVerse (compared to 0.4191 for PhysX-Anything) and 0.8603 on PhysX-Mobility, indicating accurate inference of articulation structures and motion constraints.

**PhysX-Bench Evaluation:**
The framework's generalization ability was assessed on PhysX-Bench, which includes real-world photographs and rendered images.
- PhysX-Omni demonstrated strong overall performance, particularly excelling in physical attributes. It achieved the highest scores for absolute scale (64.26), material (59.89), affordance (70.57), kinematics (80.72), and description (39.02). The high kinematic score suggests robust physical reasoning and semantic understanding capabilities.
- While MonoArt achieved slightly higher scores in some geometry-related metrics (CLIP similarity, 3D consistency, visual quality), this was attributed to its reliance on a pre-trained geometry generation pipeline, which limited its performance on physical properties and articulated behaviors. PhysX-Omni achieved a more balanced performance across both geometric and physical reasoning tasks.

**Qualitative Results:**
Visualizations indicated that PhysX-Omni robustly generated complex structures and challenging articulated objects. Compared to baselines, it produced higher-quality simulation-ready assets with more accurate geometry, plausible material/affordance predictions, and coherent articulated behaviors. The method also demonstrated capability in generating diverse rigid, deformable, and articulated objects within simulation-ready scenes.

**Human Alignment of PhysX-Bench:**
To validate PhysX-Bench's alignment with human perception, Spearman's rank correlation coefficient was measured between automatic evaluation scores and human preference scores. PhysX-Bench showed consistently strong correlations across all dimensions. Absolute scale, affordance, material, and description achieved a Spearman coefficient (ρ) of 1.0. Kinematic evaluation reached ρ=1.0 with a Pearson correlation coefficient (r) of 0.992. Geometry evaluation also showed a strong correlation (ρ=0.8, r=0.803), validating the benchmark's reliability for both physical attributes and geometric quality.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the tailored template-based geometry representation. Compared to a baseline using text-based voxel indices, PhysX-Omni showed substantial improvements in kinematic and absolute scale metrics. Qualitatively, the proposed representation enabled the generation of more detailed and structurally coherent assets, reducing ambiguities and incomplete geometry often seen in baselines for complex objects.

**Applications:**
- **Robotic Policy Learning:** Generated simulation-ready 3D assets were successfully deployed in a physics simulator for robotic interaction tasks. The assets exhibited reliable geometric accuracy, plausible material properties, and coherent articulated behaviors under dynamic interactions, suggesting direct applicability for robotic training environments and a reduction in manual asset creation costs.
- **Sim-Ready Scene Generation:** PhysX-Omni demonstrated potential for scene-level generation. By integrating generated assets with 3D scene layouts (reconstructed from input images using image-to-depth estimation and 2D segmentation), the framework could automatically build physically plausible simulation-ready scenes. Its unified support for rigid, deformable, and articulated objects enabled the construction of diverse and realistic simulation environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PhysX-Omni research presents several contributions and potential impacts across multiple fields:

- **Unified Simulation-Ready 3D Generation:** By developing a single framework capable of generating rigid, deformable, and articulated objects with comprehensive physical attributes, PhysX-Omni addresses a fundamental limitation in current 3D generation. This unification streamlines the creation of diverse and functionally rich 3D content, which is crucial for complex simulated environments.
- **Novel Geometry Representation:** The introduction of a template-based Run-Length Encoding (RLE) geometry representation for Vision-Language Models is significant. This approach allows for direct, high-resolution 3D structure modeling, circumventing the fidelity limitations of compression-based methods and the performance bottlenecks of segmentation-dependent pipelines. It improves geometric accuracy, structural coherence, and computational efficiency, particularly for complex topologies.
- **Resource Contribution to the Community:** The construction of **PhysXVerse**, a large-scale (over 8.7K assets across 2.9K categories) and richly annotated simulation-ready physical 3D dataset, fills a critical data scarcity gap. Similarly, the development of **PhysX-Bench**, the first comprehensive benchmark for physical 3D generation with ground-truth-free evaluation across six key dimensions, provides a robust and reliable tool for assessing future research in this domain. Both resources are expected to accelerate research and development in physical 3D generation.
- **Enhanced Practical Deployability:** The validation of generated assets in real-world simulation environments, specifically for robotic policy learning and simulation-ready scene generation, underscores the practical utility of PhysX-Omni. The ability to automatically generate physically plausible and functional 3D assets directly from images reduces the manual effort and cost associated with creating complex simulation environments.
- **Advancements in Embodied AI and Robotics:** The framework has direct implications for embodied AI and robotics. High-fidelity, simulation-ready assets enable more realistic training of robotic agents and development of intelligent systems that can interact with virtual worlds in a physically consistent manner. This facilitates the transfer of learned policies from simulation to the real world.
- **Broad Applications:** Beyond robotics, PhysX-Omni has potential to advance gaming design, interactive simulation, virtual reality (VR), and augmented reality (AR) by providing tools for generating dynamic, physically aware 3D content at scale.

While the research acknowledges potential areas for improvement in geometric fidelity for extremely complex structures, particularly when compared to purely appearance-focused methods, the overall contributions significantly advance the field of physical 3D generation. The work lays a foundation for future research to further integrate geometric quality with physical consistency, aiming for even more realistic and functional virtual worlds.
