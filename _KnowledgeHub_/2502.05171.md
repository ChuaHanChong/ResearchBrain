# Scaling up Test-Time Compute with Latent Reasoning: A Recurrent Depth Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ELLIS Institute Tübingen, University of Maryland, and Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory introduce a recurrent depth transformer architecture that scales reasoning abilities by implicitly processing information in a continuous latent space. The 3.5 billion parameter model, Huginn-0125, trained on the Frontier supercomputer, demonstrates significant performance gains on reasoning benchmarks with increased test-time iterations, sometimes matching or exceeding larger models without requiring specialized Chain-of-Thought training data.

## Problem

- Traditional LLM scaling relies on increasing model parameters, leading to immense computational and memory demands during training and inference.
- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning requires specialized training data, consumes significant context window memory, and is limited to reasoning types that can be explicitly verbalized.
- Existing LLM architectures may prioritize memorization over learning generalizable algorithms, limiting deeper reasoning abilities.

## Method

- Introduces a ==recurrent depth transformer architecture== with a Prelude, an iteratively applied ==Core Recurrent Block==, and a Coda, enabling arbitrary scaling of effective depth at test-time.
- Trains the model using ==randomized unrolling== of recurrent iterations with a heavy-tailed log-normal Poisson distribution and ==truncated backpropagation== to manage memory and encourage extrapolation.
- Employs large-scale training on the Oak Ridge National Laboratory’s Frontier supercomputer, utilizing **4096** AMD MI250X GPUs, with careful parameter initialization and a custom distributed data parallel implementation.

## Results

- The **3.5B** parameter Huginn-0125 model shows dramatic performance improvements on reasoning benchmarks with increased test-time recurrent iterations, sometimes matching or exceeding **50B** parameter open-source models.
- It achieves strong performance on mathematical and coding benchmarks (e.g., surpassing most compared models on GSM8K and MATH), demonstrating an inclination towards reasoning over general knowledge.
- Analysis reveals complex emergent computational patterns in the latent space, such as context-dependent convergence, 'orbits,' and 'sliders,' suggesting sophisticated non-linear information processing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time recurrent depth offers a "third axis" for scaling LLM performance, complementary to increasing parameter count and explicit Chain-of-Thought methods.
> - Implicit, latent reasoning within the model's continuous high-dimensional space can capture complex thought processes not easily verbalized and does not require specialized CoT training data.
> - The recurrent architecture naturally enables zero-shot capabilities like adaptive compute, KV-cache sharing, continuous latent CoT, and self-speculative decoding, simplifying advanced LLM functionalities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has primarily pursued two main avenues for scaling performance:
1. **Scaling Model Size:** Increasing the number of parameters (e.g., GPT-3, PaLM), which demands immense computational resources for training and often leads to models that are memory-intensive during inference.
2. **Scaling Test-Time Computation via Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** This approach, popularized by models like PaLM-2 and GPT-4, involves prompting LLMs to verbalize intermediate steps of reasoning (e.g., "Let's think step by step") within their context window. While effective, it requires specialized, carefully curated CoT training data and consumes significant context window memory, which can be inefficient and limit the scope of complex problems.

This paper proposes a *third, complementary axis* for scaling LLM performance: **recurrent depth for implicit, latent reasoning.** It draws inspiration from a long history of recurrent neural networks and adaptive computation in machine learning, where the idea of an iterative unit performing computations over time or depth has been explored since the 1970s (e.g., recurrent neural networks, universal transformers, deep equilibrium models).

The key distinctions and contributions in the broader landscape are:
- **Implicit vs. Explicit Reasoning:** Unlike CoT, which relies on explicit, verbalized intermediate steps, this work focuses on reasoning that occurs entirely within the model's continuous, high-dimensional latent space. This is analogized to how humans perform much of their thought before uttering a single word.
- **Reduced Data Dependency:** A major advantage over CoT is that this approach does not require bespoke, specialized training data. The model can be trained on standard text corpora with a variable compute budget, and its reasoning abilities emerge and improve with increased test-time iterations.
- **Memory Efficiency:** By performing reasoning in latent space, the model avoids the need for extremely long context windows, which are a memory bottleneck for CoT approaches, potentially requiring specialized parallelization methods.
- **Computational Efficiency (FLOPs per Parameter):** The weight-sharing nature of recurrent depth models means they perform more FLOPs per parameter than standard transformers, which can significantly reduce communication costs during large-scale training, especially on systems with slower interconnects.
- **Encouraging "Thinking" over Memorization:** The authors hypothesize that a compute-heavy, parameter-light architecture naturally biases the model towards learning meta-strategies, logic, and abstraction rather than simply memorizing facts, aligning with the "deep thinking" literature.
- **Broader Reasoning Capabilities:** Latent reasoning is posited to capture facets of human thought that are difficult to verbalize, such as spatial thinking or physical intuition, potentially unlocking new types of complex reasoning behavior.

While related to deep equilibrium models and diffusion models due to their iterative nature, the paper distinguishes itself by using a truncated unrolling training objective rather than fixed-point solving or surrogate objectives. It also stands in contrast to recent works that fine-tune existing fixed-depth transformers to incorporate some form of latent deliberation, as this paper proposes training such a recurrent architecture *from scratch* for inherent latent reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **introduce and validate a novel language model architecture capable of scaling its reasoning abilities by implicitly processing information in its continuous latent space through recurrent computation at test-time.**

The primary motivations driving this objective are:
- **Mimic Human Cognition:** To explore architectural designs that more closely resemble human thought, where internal mental effort precedes verbalization, and non-verbal reasoning plays a significant role.
- **Overcome Limitations of Current Scaling Approaches:**
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Deficiencies:** Address the reliance on specialized, expensive CoT training data, the substantial memory footprint due to long context windows, and the restriction to reasoning types that can be easily verbalized.
 - **Parameter Scaling Challenges:** Offer an alternative to simply increasing model size, which demands extreme data and computation, and may not inherently foster deeper reasoning beyond memorization.
- **Enhance Computational Efficiency:** Develop an architecture that maximizes FLOPs per parameter, reducing inter-accelerator communication costs and potentially improving device utilization during training.
- **Promote Algorithmic Learning:** By designing a compute-heavy, parameter-light model, the authors aim to encourage the model to learn abstract meta-strategies and algorithms rather than relying heavily on memorization. This aligns with findings in the "deep thinking" literature that suggest recurrent networks are well-suited for learning generalizable algorithms.
- **Unlock New Reasoning Dimensions:** Investigate if iterative processing in high-dimensional latent space can enable the model to explore multiple reasoning paths simultaneously, leading to more complex and novel reasoning behaviors.
- **Establish a "Third Axis" of Scaling:** To demonstrate that test-time recurrent depth can serve as a distinct and effective mechanism for improving LLM performance, complementary to scaling parameter counts and verbalized inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology is the design and large-scale training of a novel "recurrent depth" transformer architecture.

#### 3.1. Architecture Design: Recurrent Depth Transformer

The model is a decoder-only transformer with a tripartite structure:
- **Prelude (P):** Consists of several transformer layers (l_P = 2 in the large model) that embed the input tokens into a latent space.
- **Core Recurrent Block (R):** The central unit of computation, comprising multiple transformer layers (l_R = 4 in the large model). This block is applied iteratively. At each iteration `i`, it takes the input embedding `e` (from the Prelude) and the previous latent state `s_{i-1}` to produce a new latent state `s_i`. A key design choice is to inject the input embedding `e` at *every* recurrent step, which is crucial for stabilizing the recurrence and promoting convergence to a state independent of initialization. The initial latent state `s_0` is sampled from a truncated normal distribution.
- **Coda (C):** Contains several layers (l_C = 2 in the large model) that un-embed the final latent state `s_r` (after `r` iterations) and contain the prediction head to produce next token probabilities.

The large model has 3.5 billion parameters in total, but its "effective depth" can be arbitrarily scaled at test-time by increasing `r`. For instance, 32 iterations (¯r=32 during training) unfold to an effective depth of 2 + (4 * 32) + 2 = 132 layers, making it computationally deeper than many larger fixed-depth transformers. The microscopic design within each block follows standard transformer layers (causal self-attention with RoPE, gated SiLU MLP, RMSNorm), but a specific "sandwich" normalization format (n_2(x+Attn(n_1(x))) followed by n_4(x_hat+MLP(n_3(x_hat)))) and a learned adapter matrix for concatenating `s_i` and `e` were found crucial for stability at scale.

#### 3.2. Training Objective: Unrolling with Random Iteration Counts and Truncated Backpropagation

- **Randomized Unrolling:** To enable the model to function with varying compute at test-time, the number of recurrent iterations `r` for each input sequence is randomly sampled during training. A log-normal Poisson distribution `Λ` is used for `r`, ensuring a heavy tail of occasional high-iteration events, which is critical for extrapolation.
- **Truncated Backpropagation:** To manage computational and memory costs, gradients are backpropagated through only the last `k=8` iterations of the recurrent unit. This makes memory usage independent of `r`, allowing for the heavy-tailed distribution. The prelude block, however, receives gradient updates at every step. This method is analogous to truncated backpropagation through time in RNNs but applied to depth.

#### 3.3. Large-Scale Training Setup

- **Data:** A specialized mixture of 800 billion tokens, heavily skewed towards code (25.36%) and mathematical reasoning (6.14% math, 18.73% scientific-text) data, augmented with general webtext, Q&A, and instruction data. All sources are publicly available.
- **Tokenization:** A 65536-token BPE vocabulary was trained specifically on the instruction data split for efficiency in the target domain. Documents were packed into 4096-length sequences.
- **Initialization:** Careful parameter initialization using schemes like Takase et al. (2024) and Goyal et al. (2018) was found to be critical for stable training at scale, as prior attempts led to representation collapse or inability to leverage recurrence.
- **Optimizer:** Adam with decoupled weight regularization, update clipping, and a constant learning rate after a 4096-step warm-up.
- **Compute Infrastructure:** Training was performed on Oak Ridge National Laboratory’s Frontier supercomputer, utilizing 4096 AMD MI250X GPUs. The authors developed a hand-crafted distributed data parallel implementation and specific strategies to circumvent critical AMD interconnect issues, achieving 41%-51% "Achievable Flop Utilization" (AFU). They note this might be the largest LLM training run completed on an AMD cluster.
- **Training Timeline:** The main model (Huginn-0125) was trained for 800 billion tokens over 21 segments of up to 12 hours. A non-recurrent baseline was also trained for 180 billion tokens under similar settings for comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings regarding the performance and emergent properties of recurrent depth models:

- **Dramatic Performance Improvement with Test-Time Compute:** The core finding is that the 3.5B parameter Huginn-0125 model dramatically improves its performance on reasoning benchmarks by simply increasing the number of recurrent iterations at test-time. This scaling of test-time compute allows it to achieve performance levels sometimes comparable to, or even exceeding, open-source models with up to 50 billion parameters in terms of compute load. The performance gain is particularly pronounced on tasks requiring deeper reasoning.
- **Task-Dependent Saturation:** The degree to which performance benefits from increased recurrence is highly task-dependent. Easier tasks (e.g., OpenBookQA, HellaSwag) saturate quicker, requiring fewer iterations to reach near-peak performance, while harder reasoning tasks (e.g., GSM8K) continue to benefit significantly from more compute.
- **Competitive Benchmark Performance:**
 - **General Benchmarks (lm-eval-harness):** While not outperforming the very latest, larger OLMo-2 models (which benefit from more curated data and higher training token counts), Huginn-0125 (r=32) consistently outperforms older Pythia series models and is comparable to the first generation of OLMo-7B models on standard language modeling tasks (e.g., ARC-C, MMLU, HellaSwag, PiQA, SciQ, WinoGrande).
 - **Mathematical and Coding Benchmarks:** The model shows exceptional strength in mathematical reasoning, significantly surpassing all compared models except the latest OLMo-2 on GSM8K and MATH benchmarks. In coding, it beats other general-purpose open-source models on MBPP and HumanEval, though it naturally lags behind dedicated code models trained on trillions of tokens. This highlights the model's inclination towards reasoning abilities.
- **Recurrence and Context Interaction:** The model's ability to leverage recurrence is correlated with the amount of context provided. On tasks like ARC-C, increasing few-shot examples (from 0-shot to 50-shot) leads the model to utilize more recurrent iterations (e.g., saturating around 32 iterations with 25-50 shots vs. 8-12 iterations with 0-shot), demonstrating its capacity to deeply process and reason about contextual information. Similarly, in "open-book" QA settings (where relevant facts are provided), the recurrent model significantly closes the performance gap to larger, memorization-heavy models.
- **Importance of Stable Training and Initialization:** The paper details initial failed training runs where lack of proper normalization and initialization led to "hidden state collapse" or inability to leverage recurrence. This underscores the non-trivial engineering and architectural choices required to train such recurrent models at scale and extract their benefits.
- **Emergent Zero-Shot Capabilities for LLM Usecases:** Recurrent depth naturally simplifies several advanced LLM functionalities that typically require specialized training or tuning in fixed-depth transformers:
 - **Adaptive Compute:** The model can perform per-token adaptive compute in a zero-shot manner, stopping iteration when the KL-divergence between successive hidden states falls below a threshold, indicating convergence. The number of steps varies adaptively based on task difficulty (e.g., faster for high school math than moral scenarios).
 - **KV-Cache Sharing:** The architecture naturally supports sharing KV-cache entries between layers, reducing memory footprint, without needing specific pre-training.
 - **Continuous Chain-of-Thought:** By warm-starting the current token's latent state with the final state of the previous token, the model can perform a "latent CoT," reducing required iterations and improving reasoning by leveraging information from prior computations.
 - **Self-Speculative Decoding:** The model can perform speculative decoding internally by drafting tokens with fewer iterations and verifying them with more, eliminating the need for a separate draft model.
- **Insights into Latent Space Computation:** Analysis of latent space trajectories reveals complex emergent computational patterns:
 - **Context-Dependent Convergence:** The rate and nature of convergence for latent states vary significantly across different tokens within a sequence and are highly context-dependent, with "key" parts of questions showing more "deliberation."
 - **Emergent Orbits and Sliders:** Beyond simple convergence to fixed points, the model learns to trace out complicated orbital patterns (e.g., for numerical computations) and "slider" movements (directional drifts), suggesting sophisticated, non-linear ways of processing information in its high-dimensional latent space. These patterns differ from discrete symbolic reasoning.
 - **Path Independence:** Despite complex dynamics, the models maintain path independence, meaning different initial latent states quickly converge to the same consistent trajectory, indicating robust internal computations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the architectural design of language models and offers a compelling alternative to current LLM scaling paradigms. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

- **New Paradigm for LLM Scaling:** The paper successfully demonstrates that recurrent depth is a viable and powerful "third axis" for scaling LLM performance, complementing the well-established approaches of increasing parameter count and explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning. This opens a new frontier for architectural innovation and scaling laws.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Accessibility:** By shifting compute from parameter count to recurrent depth, the model becomes "compute-heavy" but "parameter-light." This could lead to models with smaller memory footprints that are more efficient to train (due to reduced communication costs from weight sharing) and potentially deployable on more commodity hardware, making advanced LLM capabilities more accessible.
- **Rethinking Reasoning in LLMs:** The emphasis on implicit, latent reasoning challenges the prevailing view that reasoning must be explicit and verbalized. This approach suggests that LLMs can develop sophisticated internal "thought" processes that are not directly represented by tokens, potentially enabling them to tackle problems that defy easy verbalization (e.g., spatial reasoning, complex planning).
- **Inherently Flexible and Adaptive Models:** The emergent zero-shot capabilities—such as adaptive compute, KV-cache sharing, continuous latent Chain-of-Thought, and self-speculative decoding—are highly impactful. These features are not explicitly trained but arise naturally from the recurrent architecture, simplifying the development and deployment of flexible, efficient, and intelligent LLMs. This suggests that models designed for deep, internal deliberation may be inherently more versatile.
- **Advancing Interpretability of LLMs:** The visualizations of latent space trajectories provide an unprecedented glimpse into the internal workings of an LLM. Observing emergent geometric patterns like orbits and sliders offers new avenues for understanding *how* these models reason, beyond just *what* they produce. This is crucial for developing more transparent, controllable, and reliable AI systems.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work lays a strong foundation for numerous future research avenues, including:
 - Optimizing training schedules and data mixtures for recurrent models.
 - Exploring novel post-training schemes (e.g., fine-tuning for recurrence compression, reinforcement learning with diverse reasoning hardness).
 - Integrating recurrent depth with other architectural innovations like Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models or linear attention, potentially combining the strengths of both parameter-heavy and compute-heavy designs.
 - Further analyzing and interpreting the learned computational patterns in latent space.

In conclusion, "Scaling up Test-Time Compute with Latent Reasoning: A Recurrent Depth Approach" is a landmark paper that not only presents a compelling new architecture for LLMs but also fundamentally redefines how we might think about and scale their reasoning capabilities. By prioritizing implicit, continuous deliberation, it promises to lead to more intelligent, efficient, and versatile generative AI models.
