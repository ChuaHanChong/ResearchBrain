# Semi-Supervised Few-Shot Adaptation of Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich developed SS-Text-U, a semi-supervised method that enhances Vision-Language Model adaptation in few-shot medical imaging by leveraging unlabeled data through text-informed pseudo-labeling and Optimal Transport. The approach achieved average class-wise balanced accuracy gains of up to 10.9% in 1-shot settings, effectively reducing the required labeling effort by 50% to 75% compared to state-of-the-art few-shot adapters.

## Problem

- High cost and scarcity of expert-level annotations in specialized domains like medical imaging hinder VLM development and deployment.
- Existing few-shot adaptation techniques for VLMs degrade significantly in extremely low-shot regimes (e.g., 1-2 shots) and struggle with pronounced class imbalances.
- Abundant unlabeled data in clinical settings is often underutilized by current few-shot VLM adaptation methods, which primarily rely on limited labeled data or text-based priors.

## Method

- Introduced ==SS-Text-U==, a ==semi-supervised text-informed linear probe== that extends few-shot VLM adaptation by systematically incorporating unlabeled data.
- Developed an inexact ==Block Coordinate Minimization (BCM)== strategy to iteratively update class prototypes (W) and generate pseudo-labels (z) for unlabeled samples.
- Leveraged ==Optimal Transport==, solved by the ==Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm==, to enforce consistency between the estimated pseudo-label distribution and the true label distribution, thereby coherently propagating textual priors to unlabeled samples.

## Results

- Achieved average class-wise balanced accuracy gains of **10.9%** for 1-shot and **7.1%** for 2-shot adaptation over the best training-free baseline, SS-Text+.
- Reduced the required labeling effort by approximately **50%** to **75%** for K ∈ {2, 4, 8} shots, with 1-shot SS-Text-U performing comparably to 4-shot SS-Text+.
- Demonstrated computational efficiency, running orders of magnitude faster than gradient-based approaches, processing large datasets in approximately **25 ms** on standard hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating readily available unlabeled data via text-informed pseudo-labeling significantly improves VLM performance in annotation-scarce, few-shot medical imaging scenarios.
> - Enforcing distributional consistency on pseudo-labels using Optimal Transport is crucial for stable and effective learning from unlabeled data, preventing label drift.
> - Adaptive weighting of labeled and unlabeled data contributions, based on class counts, ensures robust performance across varying shot regimes, emphasizing underrepresented classes where labeled data is minimal.
