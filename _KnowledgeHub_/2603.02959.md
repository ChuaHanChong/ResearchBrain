# Semi-Supervised Few-Shot Adaptation of Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich developed SS-Text-U, a semi-supervised method that enhances Vision-Language Model adaptation in few-shot medical imaging by leveraging unlabeled data through text-informed pseudo-labeling and Optimal Transport. The approach achieved average class-wise balanced accuracy gains of up to 10.9% in 1-shot settings, effectively reducing the required labeling effort by 50% to 75% compared to state-of-the-art few-shot adapters.

## Problem

- High cost and scarcity of expert-level annotations in specialized domains like medical imaging hinder VLM development and deployment.
- Existing few-shot adaptation techniques for VLMs degrade significantly in extremely low-shot regimes (e.g., 1-2 shots) and struggle with pronounced class imbalances.
- Abundant unlabeled data in clinical settings is often underutilized by current few-shot VLM adaptation methods, which primarily rely on limited labeled data or text-based priors.

## Method

- Introduced ==SS-Text-U==, a ==semi-supervised text-informed linear probe== that extends few-shot VLM adaptation by systematically incorporating unlabeled data.
- Developed an inexact ==Block Coordinate Minimization (BCM)== strategy to iteratively update class prototypes (W) and generate pseudo-labels (z) for unlabeled samples.
- Leveraged ==Optimal Transport==, solved by the ==Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm==, to enforce consistency between the estimated pseudo-label distribution and the true label distribution, thereby coherently propagating textual priors to unlabeled samples.

## Results

- Achieved average class-wise balanced accuracy gains of **10.9%** for 1-shot and **7.1%** for 2-shot adaptation over the best training-free baseline, SS-Text+.
- Reduced the required labeling effort by approximately **50%** to **75%** for K ∈ {2, 4, 8} shots, with 1-shot SS-Text-U performing comparably to 4-shot SS-Text+.
- Demonstrated computational efficiency, running orders of magnitude faster than gradient-based approaches, processing large datasets in approximately **25 ms** on standard hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating readily available unlabeled data via text-informed pseudo-labeling significantly improves VLM performance in annotation-scarce, few-shot medical imaging scenarios.
> - Enforcing distributional consistency on pseudo-labels using Optimal Transport is crucial for stable and effective learning from unlabeled data, preventing label drift.
> - Adaptive weighting of labeled and unlabeled data contributions, based on class counts, ensures robust performance across varying shot regimes, emphasizing underrepresented classes where labeled data is minimal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of foundation models, specifically focusing on Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These models, pre-trained on extensive and diverse data, have demonstrated robust multi-modal embedding capabilities, enabling efficient transfer to various downstream tasks. A particularly relevant application area is few-shot adaptation, where models are adapted using only a limited number of annotated examples, often facilitated by multi-modal linear probes due to their computational efficiency compared to prompt learning techniques.

In the medical imaging domain, specialized VLMs have emerged as valuable tools for zero- and few-shot image classification. This is largely driven by the high cost and labor intensity associated with obtaining expert-level annotations. While promising, existing few-shot adaptation methods face challenges, especially in extremely low-shot regimes. Medical datasets frequently exhibit pronounced class imbalances, leading to insufficient representation of certain categories within the small support sets available for adaptation. This limitation can significantly degrade overall model performance.

This paper addresses the identified gap by proposing a semi-supervised approach. While some prior work has explored leveraging unlabeled data for VLM adaptation, these often involve specific scenarios like transduction or test-time adaptation. The authors note that the specific approach of propagating text-informed pseudo-labels from readily available unlabeled data during few-shot adaptation, particularly in the context of medical VLMs, remains largely unexplored. By integrating unlabeled data and textual priors, this research aims to enhance the annotation efficiency and performance of VLMs in low-shot medical imaging scenarios, thereby contributing to the development of more practical and resource-efficient medical AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and annotation efficiency of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) when adapted to new tasks, specifically in few-shot learning scenarios where labeled data is scarce. This is particularly pertinent in specialized domains like medical imaging, where the cost of obtaining high-quality, expert-level annotations is substantial.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges inherent in the current state of VLM adaptation for medical applications:
1. **High Annotation Costs in Medical Imaging**: Expert annotations are expensive and time-consuming to acquire in medical imaging. Few-shot learning presents a promising avenue to mitigate this, allowing models to adapt with only a handful of examples. However, even few-shot methods can be limited by the availability of even these few examples.
2. **Performance Degradation in Extremely Low-Shot Regimes**: Existing few-shot adaptation techniques, while effective, often struggle in scenarios where the number of labeled examples per class (shots) is extremely low (e.g., 1 or 2 shots). This limitation becomes pronounced when encountering rare diseases or conditions.
3. **Impact of Class Imbalance**: Medical datasets frequently exhibit severe class imbalances, meaning some categories are naturally underrepresented. In a few-shot setting, this imbalance is exacerbated, leading to support sets that may lack any examples for certain classes or contain very few, thus penalizing the model's ability to learn robust representations for these categories.
4. **Underutilization of Unlabeled Data**: While acquiring labeled data is costly, unlabeled data is typically more abundant in clinical settings and data management pipelines. Current few-shot VLM adaptation methods primarily rely on the limited labeled data or text-based priors, without effectively leveraging this readily available unlabeled information to improve model generalization. The authors specifically highlight that using unlabeled data for propagating text-informed pseudo-labels during few-shot adaptation is a largely unexplored area, differentiating it from existing works on test-time adaptation or transduction.

To address these motivations, the research sets forth several specific objectives:
- To formally introduce "semi-supervised few-shot learning" as a novel setting for VLMs, aiming to enable more annotation-efficient model transfer by systematically incorporating unlabeled data.
- To develop and propose a principled semi-supervised solver, named SS-Text-U, which is a text-informed linear probe. This solver is designed to simultaneously learn robust class prototypes and generate pseudo-labels from both limited labeled and abundant unlabeled data.
- To achieve this, SS-Text-U integrates an efficient block-wise optimizer with Optimal Transport techniques, allowing for a coherent and constrained propagation of textual priors to unlabeled samples.
- Through comprehensive experimentation, the authors aim to demonstrate that SS-Text-U can significantly improve predictive performance, particularly at lower annotation budgets, ultimately reducing the required labeling effort by a substantial margin (e.g., ≥ 50%) in low-shot regimes compared to state-of-the-art few-shot adapters.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, SS-Text-U, extends existing few-shot adaptation techniques for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by integrating semi-supervised learning principles, specifically leveraging unlabeled data through text-informed pseudo-labeling.

The foundation of the approach involves pre-trained contrastive VLMs, similar to CLIP, which comprise visual and textual encoders. These encoders project inputs into a shared, ℓ2-normalized D-dimensional feature space, yielding visual (v) and text (t) embeddings. Zero-shot predictions are enabled by defining class prototypes (W) as the ensemble of domain-specific textual descriptions for each category.

The baseline for few-shot adaptation is built upon existing multi-modal linear probes, particularly those that minimize cross-entropy (CE) loss on a small labeled support set (S) while regularizing the learned prototypes (W) to remain close to the textual priors (t_c). The method specifically focuses on the "tightness term" (H_t) of the CE loss, which encourages prototypes to align with their respective labeled embeddings. This formulation, referred to as SS-Text, has a closed-form solution, bypassing the need for hyperparameter tuning common in gradient descent methods.

**Proposed SS-Text-U Solver:**
The core innovation is to extend this few-shot scenario to an "extended adaptation set" D = S ∪ U, where U is an unlabeled set. This introduces the concept of learning from unlabeled data to generate pseudo-labels (z_i) for samples in U.

1. **Objective Function (L_SEMI)**: The overall objective function combines the few-shot loss (L_FEW-SHOT) from the labeled data with a new unlabeled loss term (L_U), weighted by λ_U:
 L_SEMI(W, z) = L_FEW-SHOT(W) + λ_U L_U(W, z).
 The unlabeled loss term L_U aims to minimize the tightness-CE loss between predictions based on W and the pseudo-labels z_i for unlabeled samples.

2. **Constraint on Unlabeled Loss**: A critical aspect of L_U is a constraint that the label distribution estimated from the pseudo-labels in the unlabeled set (m̂) must be consistent with the true label distribution (m) derived from the labeled support set. This constraint helps to regularize the pseudo-labeling process and prevent drift.

3. **Block-wise Optimization**: Given that L_SEMI depends on two coupled variables, W (class prototypes) and z (pseudo-labels), an inexact Block Coordinate Minimization (BCM) strategy is employed. This iterative process alternates between updating z while W is fixed, and then updating W while z is fixed.

 - **Z-block updates**: When W is fixed, the optimization for z focuses on maximizing the similarity between unlabeled visual embeddings and current prototypes, subject to the label distribution consistency constraint. This is formulated as an Optimal Transport (OT) problem. The Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm is utilized to solve this efficiently, yielding entropy-regularized, normalized soft pseudo-labels (z_t) by iteratively computing renormalization vectors.
 - **Closed-form updates for W**: When z is fixed, the objective function becomes convex with respect to W. The gradient of L_SEMI with respect to each class prototype w_c is set to zero, leading to a closed-form solution for W^(t). This solution incorporates information from labeled samples, pseudo-labeled unlabeled samples, and the initial textual priors.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Initialization**: The BCM process starts with prototypes initialized to textual priors (w_0_c = t_c).
- **Iterations**: The solver performs T=3 BCM iterations, and T_OT=10 Sinkhorn iterations for each z-block update, ensuring convergence of both iterative processes.
- **Label-marginal distribution (m) correction**: To address situations where certain classes are completely absent in extremely low-shot support sets (K ∈ {1, 2}), a small baseline value (b) is added to all class marginals. This ensures all classes have a non-zero probability for pseudo-labeling. The baseline is set as a fraction (r=1/4 by default) of the least-observed frequent category's marginal.
- **Weighting Parameters (λ_T_c, λ_U_c)**:
 - λ_T_c is set as the inverse of the number of samples for class c in the support set (1/K_c), providing a data-driven weight that emphasizes well-represented classes while allowing textual priors to dominate for underrepresented ones.
 - λ_U_c is set to 2λ_T_c, which ties the influence of unlabeled data to the context of the labeled data and simplifies solver design, ensuring significant unlabeled data contribution in low-shot regimes.

**Experimental Setup:**
The evaluation involved three medical imaging modalities: histology (using CONCH), ophthalmology (using FLAIR), and radiology (using CONVIRT). These VLMs were adapted to a total of 12 downstream tasks across various medical datasets (e.g., NCT-CRC, MESSIDOR, CheXpert). Imbalanced, realistic labeled support sets were sampled with K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} shots per class, and an unlabeled data pool of M = C × 24 samples was used. The performance metric was class-wise balanced accuracy (ACA), averaged over 50 random seeds. Baselines included gradient-based linear probes (ZS-LP, CLAP, LP++) and training-free methods (SS, SS-Text, SS-Text+).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SS-Text-U demonstrated its effectiveness in improving few-shot adaptation of Vision-Language Models, particularly in low-shot medical imaging scenarios.

- **Overall Performance Improvement**: SS-Text-U consistently exhibited performance gains across all evaluated few-shot baselines. When compared to SS-Text+, which was identified as the best training-free solver, SS-Text-U achieved notable improvements in average class-wise balanced accuracy (ACA): 10.9% for 1-shot, 7.1% for 2-shot, 2.7% for 4-shot, 1.3% for 8-shot, and 0.3% for 16-shot adaptation. These performance improvements signify a practical reduction in required labeling effort by approximately 50% to 75% for K ∈ {2, 4, 8}, indicating that, for example, 1-shot SS-Text-U can achieve performance comparable to 4-shot SS-Text+.

- **Computational Efficiency**: The proposed solver introduced a minimal computational overhead. SS-Text-U was observed to be orders of magnitude faster than gradient-based few-shot approaches. For instance, processing large datasets like Skin took approximately 25 ms on a standard laptop without specialized hardware, indicating its practicality for deployment.

- **Data-Efficiency of Unlabeled Data**: An analysis of the quantity of unlabeled data required showed that even a relatively small amount (M = C × 8 unlabeled samples) could yield significant improvements in low-shot regimes, demonstrating the efficiency with which the method leverages additional data.

- **Importance of Constrained Pseudo-Labels**: The inclusion of Optimal Transport for enforcing a consistent label distribution in pseudo-labels (Eq. 5) proved crucial. Comparing scenarios with (T_OT ≥ 1) and without (T_OT = 0) Sinkhorn Optimal Transport showed average performance gains of approximately 5.1% for 1-shot and 1.0% for 4-shot adaptation when the distributional constraint was applied. This highlights the value of structured pseudo-labeling.

- **Convergence Properties**: Both the Block Coordinate Minimization (BCM) solver and its Optimal Transport (OT) stage within the z-block updates demonstrated stable convergence. The default iterations chosen (T=3 for BCM and T_OT=10 for Sinkhorn) were determined to be sufficient for these iterative processes to converge.

- **Hyper-parameter Ablation Studies**:
 - **Adaptive λ_T_c and λ_U_c**: The adaptive design, where λ_T_c was set inversely proportional to the class count (1/K_c) and λ_U_c = 2λ_T_c, outperformed fixed hyper-parameter values across varying shot regimes. Fixed high values for λ_T_c led to diminished performance in larger-shot settings, and vice-versa. Additionally, using fixed λ_U_c values resulted in considerable performance reductions.
 - **m correction ratio (r)**: For extremely low-shot scenarios (K ∈ {1, 2}), a correction ratio 'r' was applied to the target label distribution 'm' to account for missing categories. Values like r ∈ {1/2, 1/4, 1/8} produced ACA results comparable to an oracle scenario (where 'm' is perfectly known) and consistently improved over the SS-Text+ baseline. The default r=1/4 was selected as a balanced choice, as overly large 'r' values could penalize overall accuracy.

- **Dataset-Specific Performance Analysis**: While SS-Text-U showed consistent gains across most datasets, certain tasks (e.g., mBRSET) presented challenges for all tested solvers, where performance remained moderate. An exploratory analysis revealed a strong linear correlation between solver performance and the Silhouette score of the embeddings with respect to true labels. Tasks with lower Silhouette scores (closer to 0, indicating severe cluster overlap) corresponded to lower solver performance, suggesting that the intrinsic quality of embedding representations or labels might be a limiting factor in these difficult cases. This contrasts with zero-shot predictions, which can sometimes be high even in difficult tasks, indicating a dependence on pre-training data priors that are not accessible to few-shot adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of few-shot adaptation for Vision-Language Models, particularly within the context of medical imaging. The proposed SS-Text-U method carries several implications and potential impacts:

1. **Addressing Annotation Bottlenecks in Medical AI**: The most direct and substantial impact is on mitigating the high cost and scarcity of expert annotations in specialized domains like medical imaging. By enabling "lower-budget annotation pipelines" and demonstrating a reduction in labeling effort by 50% to 75% in low-shot regimes, SS-Text-U directly addresses a critical barrier to the development and deployment of medical AI applications. This makes advanced models more accessible for rare diseases or conditions where large labeled datasets are inherently difficult to compile.

2. **Enhanced Performance and Robustness in Low-Shot Settings**: The consistent and significant performance gains observed in extremely low-shot scenarios (K=1, 2 shots) highlight the method's ability to extract more information from minimal labeled data. Furthermore, by explicitly managing class imbalances through text-informed pseudo-labeling and optimal transport, the approach contributes to greater robustness, which is crucial given the imbalanced nature of many real-world medical datasets.

3. **New Paradigm for VLM Adaptation**: The work introduces and validates the concept of "semi-supervised few-shot learning" for VLMs. This new setting integrates the strengths of foundation models, few-shot learning, and readily available unlabeled data, opening up a novel and promising direction for future research in data-efficient model adaptation across various domains, not just medical imaging.

4. **Computational Efficiency for Practical Deployment**: The demonstrated computational efficiency of SS-Text-U, being orders of magnitude faster than gradient-based alternatives, is a key practical advantage. This allows for rapid prototyping, iteration, and potentially real-time adaptation in clinical or research settings without requiring extensive high-performance computing resources.

5. **Leveraging Textual Priors More Effectively**: The method’s ability to propagate rich textual priors to unlabeled data, even in the absence of visual labels, underscores the power of multi-modal foundation models. This shows how language understanding can directly inform and improve visual classification even when visual examples are scarce.

6. **Foundation for Future Research**: The discussion on challenging datasets and the correlation with embedding quality (Silhouette score) provides valuable insights and clearly points to future research directions. These include exploring more sophisticated semi-supervised frameworks (e.g., multi-view augmentations, confidence filtering) to further enhance performance, especially when intrinsic data separability might be limited. The current work establishes a strong baseline and a framework for such explorations.

In summary, SS-Text-U represents a tangible step towards more annotation-efficient, robust, and deployable VLM adaptation solutions for complex, data-scarce domains like medical imaging.
