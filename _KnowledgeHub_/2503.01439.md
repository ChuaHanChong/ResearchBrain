# AVR: Active Vision-Driven Precise Robot Manipulation with Viewpoint and Focal Length Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The AVR framework introduces an active vision system that dynamically adjusts both camera viewpoint and optical zoom for robotic manipulation. This system enhances the precision, robustness, and generalization of imitation-learned policies, demonstrating over 25% increases in success rates for precision tasks and more efficient data collection.

## Problem

- Current imitation learning frameworks struggle with high-precision tasks due to reliance on fixed or suboptimal viewpoints, which impair detailed perception.
- Conventional camera setups, including fixed and wrist-mounted cameras, provide insufficient high-fidelity visual cues and are susceptible to occlusions, hindering policy generalization.
- Third-person teleoperation for data collection often yields suboptimal or failed demonstrations due to limited situational awareness and delayed feedback.

## Method

- Developed the **AVR** framework, a bimanual robotic system integrating an active vision module with a 2-DoF gimbal-mounted motorized-zoom camera.
- Implemented a teleoperation framework featuring VR-projected egocentric view with head-tracked active viewpoint control and button-to-zoom focal length adjustment for high-quality data collection.
- Utilized a ==Diffusion Policy== network to learn from multi-camera observations (including the active vision module) and proprioceptive states, predicting future actions, gimbal angles, and zoom settings.

## Results

- Simulation experiments with the AVR Plugin showed 5–**17%** increases in task success rates across various manipulation tasks by augmenting observations with ROI-conditioned active vision.
- Real-world precision tasks achieved over **25%** higher success rates compared to static-view baselines, and data collection efficiency improved with fewer erroneous teleoperation attempts.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both dynamic viewpoint and focal length contribute to overall performance, with dynamic zoom providing marked benefits for precision tasks like small-hole insertion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic control over both camera viewpoint and optical zoom is crucial for achieving fine-grained perception and mitigating occlusions in precise robotic manipulation.
> - An active vision-driven teleoperation system significantly improves the quality and efficiency of data collection for imitation learning, leading to more robust policies.
> - Policies trained with explicit, detail-aware observations, enabled by active vision, demonstrate improved robustness against environmental perturbations and enhanced generalization across varied scenes and objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has made progress through imitation learning (IL), which allows robots to acquire complex behaviors by learning from expert demonstrations. This approach bypasses the need for detailed dynamics modeling or manual reward function design, distinguishing it from reinforcement learning. However, current IL frameworks encounter performance limitations when applied to tasks requiring high precision, particularly in complex or cluttered environments.

A significant bottleneck in traditional imitation learning for dexterous manipulation lies in the quality of data collection and perceptual capabilities. Third-person teleoperation often suffers from limited situational awareness and delayed feedback, leading to suboptimal or failed demonstrations, especially in fine manipulation. Furthermore, conventional camera setups, typically comprising fixed external cameras and wrist-mounted cameras, provide insufficient high-fidelity visual cues for precision tasks. Fixed cameras offer broad context but lack resolution for fine details, while wrist cameras provide localized views that are susceptible to occlusion and unstable viewpoints. These limitations hinder the ability of state-of-the-art IL algorithms to capture essential interaction details, thereby restricting policy generalization and robustness.

Existing research has explored VR-based teleoperation systems and the creation of large-scale datasets. While these efforts contribute to the development of robotic manipulation, simply increasing data volume has shown diminishing returns for fine-grained manipulation, with success rates often plateauing. This suggests that the issue is not merely data quantity but rather the inadequate capture and representation of critical details.

Inspired by human operators' natural ability to dynamically allocate sensory resources, focusing attention on task-relevant regions and magnifying critical features, researchers have explored active vision in robotics. Prior active vision approaches primarily focus on "attention guidance," such as controlling camera viewpoints to mitigate occlusion and field-of-view limitations, or jointly learning sensory and motor policies. Other encoder-centric methods emphasize spatially selective processing to highlight relevant areas without explicit camera movement. However, these strategies often fail to adequately represent fine, manipulation-critical cues when fixed or suboptimal viewpoints cause occlusions or miss small details.

The concept of "learning from details" has been explored in various domains to improve perception and decision quality. Examples include models that mine subtle cues in fine-grained recognition, zoom-in pipelines in medical imaging for lesion-level evidence, and super-resolution techniques for small-object detection in low-resolution or cluttered scenes. In robotics, detail-centric perception has also shown benefits. This work positions itself by extending these concepts beyond implicit attention mechanisms, introducing an explicit detail-observation modality. By actively adjusting both viewpoint and optical zoom, the proposed framework aims to deliver high-resolution local evidence to the policy, thereby addressing the limitations of existing methods and improving precision and robustness in fine manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges faced by robotic manipulation systems in complex and precision-demanding tasks. Current imitation learning frameworks are often limited by fixed or suboptimal viewpoints, which impair the precise perception of task-relevant details and frequently result in occlusions. These perceptual constraints restrict the effectiveness and generalization capabilities of imitation-learned policies.

The core objective of this work is to develop a robotic system that overcomes these visual limitations by integrating an active vision module. Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

- **Enhance Fine-Grained Perception:** To enable robots to perceive critical task details with high fidelity, especially in scenarios involving cluttered environments, small objects, or intricate interactions. The motivation is that human operators inherently use dynamic focusing and magnification, and replicating this capability in robots could significantly improve performance.
- **Mitigate Occlusions:** To address the problem of occlusions that arise from static camera positions or erratic wrist camera movements, which often obscure crucial interaction points. By allowing dynamic viewpoint adjustment, the system aims to maintain an unobstructed view of the target.
- **Improve Data Quality for Imitation Learning:** To collect high-quality demonstrations for imitation learning by providing operators with enhanced situational awareness and precise visual feedback during teleoperation. The authors hypothesize that superior sensory input during data collection leads to more effective and robust learned policies, reducing the number of erroneous attempts typically seen in teleoperation.
- **Increase Task Success Rates:** To develop policies that achieve higher success rates across a diverse range of manipulation tasks, particularly those requiring high precision. This is motivated by the observation that success rates often plateau with conventional IL frameworks, suggesting a need for better data capture and representation.
- **Enhance Policy Generalization and Robustness:** To enable learned policies to perform effectively in varied conditions, including cluttered scenes, different lighting conditions, and with unseen objects or environments. The goal is to move beyond simple trajectory cloning towards a deeper task understanding facilitated by detailed, adaptive perception.
- **Integrate Active Viewpoint and Focal Length Control:** To design a bimanual teleoperation and learning framework, named AVR, that unifies head-tracked viewpoint control with motorized optical zoom. This integration is motivated by the understanding that both changes in perspective (viewpoint) and magnification (focal length) are crucial for comprehensive detail observation.

In essence, the research aims to equip robots with an adaptive perception capability akin to human visual attention, thereby enabling more precise and robust manipulation behaviors than currently achievable with static or suboptimally configured visual systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AVR (Active Vision-driven Robotics) framework integrates an active vision module into a bimanual robotic manipulation system, focusing on dynamically adjusting both camera viewpoint and optical zoom. The methodology spans hardware design, teleoperation for data collection, and policy learning.

#### 3.1. A. Hardware System

The manipulation platform comprises two 6-DoF Galaxea robotic arms, each equipped with a parallel-jaw gripper. For environmental observation, three Intel D435i depth cameras provide side and wrist views, ensuring comprehensive workspace coverage. The active vision module, mounted atop the platform, is central to the system. It consists of a motorized-zoom industrial camera integrated with a 2-DoF gimbal. This setup facilitates real-time capture of human head motion and provides live visual feedback during teleoperation, intended to enhance fine-grained perception and the recording of task details.

The system supports two control modalities for the robotic arms:
1. **Host–slave joint-angle mapping:** A traditional teleoperation approach.
2. **6-DoF end-effector control via VR controllers:** Providing a more immersive and intuitive control experience.
Both modalities allow for real-time zoom control of the active camera; a dedicated zoom button is provided on the teach pendant for host–slave mode, while VR controller buttons are mapped for VR mode.

#### 3.2. B. Teleoperation for Data Collection

To generate high-quality manipulation data, a teleoperation framework is implemented with two key features:
- **VR-projected Egocentric View with Head-Tracked Active Viewpoint Control:** The optical zoom camera's live stream is projected to a Virtual Reality Head-Mounted Display (HMD). The operator's head pose, captured by the HMD, is mapped to control the 2-DoF gimbal, allowing for real-time viewpoint adjustment. This maintains the target centered in the operator's view and aims to yield higher-fidelity demonstrations. A One-Euro filter is applied to the raw unit quaternion head motion data to mitigate jitter and ensure smoother gimbal tracking, thereby stabilizing the operator's view. The gimbal rotation angles (yaw/pitch) are extracted from the filtered rotation matrix.
- **Camera-to-HMD Projection:** The live 60 fps camera stream is spherically rendered onto a 110-degree curved surface at a 1-meter radius around the HMD viewpoint. This spherical projection, combined with an approximate 80 ms end-to-end latency, aims to preserve vestibular–visual consistency and reduce motion sickness.
- **Button-to-Zoom Camera Control:** Both teleoperation modes incorporate a button-to-zoom mapping. Operators can dynamically adjust the active vision camera's focal length using a keypad button (host–slave) or VR controller buttons. This capability allows for real-time field-of-view adjustments during bimanual operations. During policy deployment, the system is designed to autonomously modulate the camera's focal length based on learned zoom behavior.

#### 3.3. C. Learning Policy

A policy network based on the Diffusion Policy framework is utilized for learning. This network integrates observations from external cameras, the active vision module, and proprioceptive states to predict control actions.
- **Inputs:** At each time step, the policy receives RGB image observations from four viewpoints: two wrist cameras, one side camera, and the active vision camera. These images are processed by a pretrained DINOv2 ViT as a visual encoder, generating 16x22 tokens as a scene representation for each image. Additionally, a 19-dimensional proprioceptive state is included, which comprises the end-effector poses (position and quaternion) of both arms, gripper widths, the 2-DoF gimbal angles, and a scalar representing the camera zoom setting.
- **Outputs:** The policy outputs a sequence of future actions (a trajectory of length 'n'), where each action set includes the end-effector poses and gripper widths for both arms, as well as the desired gimbal angles and zoom setting, all expressed in the world frame.

#### 3.4. D. RoboTwin-based Simulation Evaluation (AVR Plugin)

To assess the benefit of detailed observations in a controlled environment, the researchers extended RoboTwin, a simulation data collection platform, with an "AVR Plugin."
- **Offline Processing:** The plugin processes pre-collected RoboTwin datasets containing RGB images, joint angles, and end-effector poses.
- **Simulating Active Vision:** The front camera view from the dataset, which covers the entire workspace, serves as the input to the AVR Plugin.
 1. **ROI Detection:** Task-conditioned Region of Interest (ROI) detection is applied to target objects, simulating dynamic camera viewpoints.
 2. **Dynamic Cropping and Zoom Ratio:** An aspect-ratio-preserving crop is performed around the detected ROI, and a relative zoom ratio is computed to simulate focal length variation.
 3. **Super-Resolution:** To emulate the expected detail level after physical zooming, Real-ESRGAN, a super-resolution algorithm, is applied to the cropped region, upscaling it back to the original image resolution.
- **Dataset Augmentation:** The processed super-resolved images, along with their corresponding zoom ratios, are integrated into the dataset as additional detailed observations for policy learning.
- **Evaluation:** 50 expert demonstrations are collected for each task, and models are trained using Diffusion Policy and evaluated in simulation, comparing performance against baselines without the AVR Plugin.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The AVR framework was evaluated through experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, assessing its impact on task success rates, data collection efficiency, robustness, and generalization.

#### 4.1. A. RoboTwin-based Simulation Evaluation

- **Task Success Rate Improvement:** In simulation experiments, the AVR Plugin, which augments observations with ROI-conditioned viewpoint changes, aspect-ratio-preserving crops with explicit zoom ratios, and super-resolution, demonstrated a 5–17% increase in task success rates compared to the baseline across diverse manipulation tasks (Fig. 4). This finding indicates that incorporating detail-aware observations, even through simulated active vision, can enhance the policy's ability to interpret and execute manipulation goals.

#### 4.2. B. Real Robot Performance

A suite of real-world bimanual manipulation tasks was designed, including grasping and placing a mango, folding a towel, scrubbing a dish, stacking three cubes, and inserting a screwdriver into a small hole (Fig. 6).

- **Data Collection Efficiency:** The AVR framework significantly improved the efficiency and reliability of data collection. Operators using AVR completed demonstrations in less time and with fewer erroneous attempts compared to traditional ALOHA and VR-control teleoperation settings (Fig. 5). Table I shows that AVR reduced the number of failed trials needed to achieve 50 successful demonstrations, indicating improved data quality and ease of use for operators.
- **Task Success Rates:** When both data collection and deployment were augmented with AVR, the system achieved higher success rates on most tasks. Notably, precision tasks experienced over 25% gains in success rate compared to a static-view baseline.
- **Differential Impact of Viewpoint and Focal Length:**
 - For coarse pick-and-place tasks (e.g., mango handover, towel folding, dish scrubbing), maintaining target visibility primarily required substantial top-camera viewpoint changes, with minor zoom (≤ 2x) providing limited additional benefit.
 - For precision tasks (e.g., three-block stacking and small-hole insertion), viewpoint changes alone were insufficient for accurate localization of boundaries or apertures. The addition of dynamic focal length to acquire high-resolution local detail markedly improved alignment and positioning during critical task phases, leading to higher success rates. This suggests that coarse tasks benefit primarily from viewpoint control, while precision tasks benefit significantly from dynamic zoom.

#### 4.3. C. Extended Analysis

- **Ablation Study:** An ablation study compared three visual observation settings: (i) no active vision (static view), (ii) dynamic viewpoint only, and (iii) AVR (dynamic viewpoint with focal length). As shown in Fig. 8, AVR consistently achieved higher success rates across all tasks, with more pronounced improvements on fine-grained manipulations. This quantitatively confirms the contribution of both dynamic viewpoint and, critically, dynamic focal length to overall performance.
- **Robustness:** The policies' robustness was evaluated under various perturbations: (i) limited observability via viewpoint occlusion, (ii) cluttered scenes, and (iii) illumination disturbances, with randomized object start poses (Fig. 7, 9). The results indicated that occlusion induced the most significant performance degradation on visually demanding tasks (e.g., cloth folding), highlighting the importance of detail-aware observation in maintaining robustness under challenging visual conditions. The AVR framework demonstrated improved robustness across these conditions.
- **Generalization:** Generalization capabilities were assessed in two settings: (i) unseen environments (varying scene layout and tabletop background) and (ii) unseen objects (new instances of the same category) (Fig. 7). The method maintained higher success rates in unseen settings and exhibited smaller performance declines from seen to unseen conditions. This demonstrates robust cross-environment and cross-instance generalization, attributed to the viewpoint- and focal length-enabled active vision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AVR framework introduces a comprehensive approach to active vision for precise robot manipulation, demonstrating how dynamic control over both camera viewpoint and optical zoom can enhance robotic capabilities. This work provides several contributions to the field of robotics:

- **Enhanced Precision and Dexterity:** By integrating active vision, AVR enables robots to achieve higher precision in manipulation tasks, particularly those requiring fine-grained detail observation, such as stacking small blocks or inserting components. This advancement moves towards developing robot capabilities that approach human-level dexterity.
- **Improved Imitation Learning Performance:** The framework significantly improves the success rates of imitation-learned policies. The findings from both simulation (5–17% gains) and real-world experiments (over 25% gains for precision tasks compared to static views) indicate that integrating active vision is a critical factor in overcoming performance bottlenecks in complex tasks.
- **Higher Quality Data Collection:** The intuitive teleoperation experience with head-tracked viewpoint control and real-time focal adjustment leads to more efficient and reliable data collection. Operators using AVR produce higher quality demonstrations with fewer errors, which in turn leads to more robust and effective learned policies. This addresses a common limitation in imitation learning where data quality can impede progress.
- **Increased Robustness:** Policies trained with AVR demonstrate enhanced robustness against common real-world perturbations, including occlusions, cluttered environments, and varying lighting conditions. This is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic settings, where such disturbances are prevalent. The study highlights that detailed observation is particularly important for maintaining robustness in visually demanding tasks.
- **Superior Generalization:** The framework contributes to better generalization capabilities for learned policies, allowing them to perform effectively in unseen environments and with novel objects of the same category. This suggests that active vision helps policies learn more fundamental task understanding rather than merely memorizing specific trajectories or object instances.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The work establishes the critical role of detail-centric observation in robotics and provides a foundation for future advancements. Proposed future directions, such as refining teleoperation with AR glasses and data gloves, improving viewpoint control mechanisms, and integrating instruction-conditioned policies, aim to further enhance active perception and policy learning.

Overall, the AVR framework demonstrates a method for addressing fundamental challenges in robotic perception and control, paving the way for more capable and adaptable robotic systems in various application domains that require precise manipulation.
