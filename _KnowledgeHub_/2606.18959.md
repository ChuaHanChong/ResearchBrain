# TactSpace: Learning a Physics-enriched Shared Latent Space for Tactile Sim-to-Real Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> TactSpace establishes a shared latent space that aligns heterogeneous tactile observations from real sensors, rigid-body simulations, and finite-element analysis, facilitating zero-shot transfer of robotic policies from simulation to reality. This multi-modal approach enables models trained on synthetic data to generalize to physical hardware for various contact-rich tasks.

## Problem

- Lack of scalable, diverse, and realistic tactile interaction data for learning-based robotic manipulation systems.
- Existing robot simulators struggle to accurately model complex tactile sensor deformation and transduction, leading to a substantial sim-to-real gap.
- Developing high-fidelity, sensor-specific simulations is resource-intensive and often does not generalize across different tactile sensor types.

## Method

- A multi-modal representation learning framework called **TactSpace** uses Vision Transformer (ViT) encoders to project real tactile sensor data and diverse physics simulations into a shared latent space.
- The latent space is aligned using a combination of ==InfoNCE contrastive loss== and cross-modal reconstruction loss, ensuring embeddings from different modalities for the same physical stimulus are consistent.
- A GPU-accelerated tactile simulation plugin for NVIDIA Isaac Lab generates scalable contact geometry and pressure distributions to enrich the simulated data.

## Results

- Achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, allowing models trained solely on simulated data to generalize to real tactile sensor measurements across in-distribution and out-of-distribution tasks.
- The InfoNCE contrastive alignment loss consistently yielded superior performance compared to MSE-aligned or unaligned baselines across all downstream tasks (classification, reconstruction, force prediction).
- Rigid-body simulation data was more effective for geometric tasks, while finite-element analysis data proved more beneficial for accurate force prediction, leveraging complementary physics for improved performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contrastive alignment (InfoNCE) is essential for effectively intermixing heterogeneous tactile data in the latent space, achieving modality-invariance based on the underlying physical stimulus.
> - Combining complementary physics simulations (rigid-body for geometry, finite-element analysis for stress) enriches the latent space, capturing a more comprehensive understanding of contact interactions.
> - Incorporating temporal history from real sensor data significantly improves cross-modal alignment by accounting for capacitive and material hysteresis effects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Tactile sensing is considered fundamental for robust contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks, providing direct measurements of surface deformation and contact forces. The field of tactile robotics utilizes various sensor types, including vision-based, capacitive, and piezoresistive arrays, for applications such as slip detection, texture recognition, and material classification.

A significant bottleneck in developing learning-based tactile manipulation systems is the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and contact-rich interaction data. Collecting such data on physical hardware is time-consuming, expensive, and potentially hazardous. Consequently, simulation is increasingly viewed as a necessary component for scalable tactile learning.

However, incorporating tactile sensing into sim-to-real pipelines presents challenges. Prior research has attempted to bridge the sim-to-real gap for tactile sensors through several approaches:
- **High-fidelity, sensor-specific simulations:** These often focus on vision-based sensors (e.g., Digit, GelSight) and involve complex rendering pipelines to model soft-body deformations and optical responses. While effective for specific sensors, these methods require substantial engineering effort, rely on detailed modeling assumptions, and typically do not generalize to other tactile sensor types, particularly those with complex electromechanical transduction mechanisms like capacitive sensors.
- **Image-to-image translation and domain adaptation:** Generative models and neural rendering are used to map tactile images between real and simulated domains. These methods are often highly engineered for the specific optical properties of vision-based sensors.
- **Physics-based and differentiable models:** These models aim to simulate elastic deformation and generate dense force/stress fields. However, they can rely on simplifying material assumptions or require extensive sensor calibration based on localized physical quantities.
- **Tactile representation learning:** This area focuses on embedding tactile signals, often integrating them with other modalities like vision or language. Common approaches include self-supervised or contrastive learning to align tactile data with pre-trained visual or textual embeddings. These methods frequently abstract away dynamic contact mechanics and interaction forces, which are crucial for low-level manipulation. Another line of research involves cross-sensor alignment to learn a unified latent space or translate raw outputs between different types of *real* tactile sensors, addressing hardware fragmentation but not the sim-to-real gap itself. Some multi-modal methods for manipulation train latent spaces entirely within simulation, assuming ideal tactile feedback in both training and deployment, thereby bypassing the sim-to-real problem.

The "TactSpace" work positions itself by directly addressing the limitations of existing sim-to-real transfer methods for tactile sensing. It proposes a framework that avoids the need for accurate raw-signal simulation and extensive hardware calibration. Instead, it aims to align heterogeneous tactile modalities—from diverse simulation sources and real sensors—within a shared latent space. This approach is distinct from previous works that focus on direct signal translation or sensor-specific high-fidelity models, as it seeks to abstract the underlying physical interaction rather than matching superficial signal characteristics. By integrating complementary physics simulations (rigid-body and finite-element analysis), it aims to create richer, more broadly applicable representations than single-modality or purely optical simulation approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the recognized bottleneck in developing robust, learning-based robotic manipulation skills that rely on tactile feedback: the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and realistic tactile interaction data. While simulation offers a scalable solution for data generation, existing simulators frequently lack the fidelity required to accurately model the complex deformation and transduction mechanics of various tactile sensors. This discrepancy creates a significant sim-to-real gap, hindering the transfer of policies trained in simulation to real-world robots.

The specific problems identified are:
- **Lack of fidelity in current simulators:** Rigid-body simulators, commonly used in hardware-accelerated robot learning workflows, cannot accurately model complex material deformation, detailed contact mechanics, or realistic force propagation. This results in simulated geometric proxies that diverge substantially from real tactile sensor outputs.
- **Sensor-specific simulation limitations:** Developing high-fidelity simulations for diverse tactile sensors (e.g., capacitive, piezoresistive) requires extensive engineering, modeling assumptions, and often doesn't generalize across sensor types due to their unique electromechanical interactions.
- **Difficulty in raw-signal alignment:** Directly matching raw sensor signals between simulation and reality is challenging due to fundamental differences in signal structure, noise characteristics, and physical interpretation.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Develop a multi-modal representation learning framework:** The core objective is to learn a shared latent space that aligns heterogeneous tactile modalities, encompassing both simulated observations and real-world sensor measurements. This alignment is intended to eliminate the necessity for accurate raw-signal simulation while preserving essential contact information.
2. **Enable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer:** The framework aims to allow models trained exclusively in simulation, using the learned embeddings, to generalize directly to real sensor measurements without any fine-tuning on real data. This would bridge the sim-to-real gap at the representation level.
3. **Integrate complementary physics simulations:** A specific objective is to leverage the strengths of different simulation types—scalable contact geometry from rigid-body simulators (e.g., NVIDIA Isaac Lab) and detailed stress fields/material responses from finite-element analysis (FEA) solvers (e.g., ABAQUS). By doing so, the framework seeks to construct a latent space that encodes a richer set of contact phenomena than either modality could achieve in isolation.
4. **Validate on diverse downstream tasks:** The learned embeddings must be evaluated on multiple contact-rich prediction tasks (e.g., indenter shape identification, force prediction, geometric reconstruction) to demonstrate their expressiveness and transfer capability across various aspects of tactile interaction.
5. **Release a scalable tactile simulation tool:** A practical objective is to contribute an efficient, GPU-accelerated tactile simulation plugin for Isaac Lab. This tool is designed to facilitate scalable tactile data generation and seamless integration into modern robot learning workflows, making it easier for others to generate contact geometry and force estimates.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the data scarcity and sim-to-real transfer problems in tactile robotics by learning a physics-enriched, modality-invariant representation of contact interactions, thereby enabling scalable robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a two-stage process: (1) multi-modal latent alignment, which establishes a modality-invariant embedding space, and (2) downstream evaluation, which assesses the utility of these representations for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The central premise is that instead of aligning raw sensor signals, which differ significantly in structure and characteristics between simulation and reality, a shared latent space (termed TactSpace) should be learned. In this space, observations from different modalities (real sensor measurements, simulated representations) corresponding to the same underlying physical stimulus are embedded consistently. The framework assumes access to multiple sensing modalities, each producing an observation for a given physical stimulus. The goal is to learn encoders that map these heterogeneous observations into a common embedding space where stimuli are represented similarly regardless of their source modality.

**B. Multi-Modal Latent Alignment**
1. **Architecture:**
 - **Encoders:** Each input modality is processed by a dedicated Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder. ViT’s self-attention mechanism is utilized to model global spatial structure, which is considered suitable for grid-structured tactile observations where long-range correlations convey contact information. These encoders project observations into a shared latent space Z.
 - **Decoder:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) decoder, featuring a shared backbone and modality-specific heads, is used to reconstruct all input modalities from any given latent embedding. This cross-reconstruction design mandates that an embedding generated by one modality’s encoder must be capable of recovering the observations of all other modalities, acting as a regularization mechanism.

2. **Training Objectives:** The framework is trained using a combination of two complementary objectives:
 - **Alignment Loss (InfoNCE):** A pairwise InfoNCE contrastive loss is applied to all pairs of modalities within the latent space. For each training batch, embeddings corresponding to the same physical stimulus across different modalities are encouraged to cluster together, while embeddings from distinct stimuli are pushed apart. This promotes a latent space organized by the physical stimulus rather than by individual modality characteristics.
 - **Reconstruction Loss (MSE):** A mean-squared error (MSE) loss is applied to all cross-modal reconstructions. This ensures that the latent embeddings retain sufficient physical information to enable the recovery of any modality's original observation from any other modality's embedding. This objective, combined with the alignment loss, helps prevent representational collapse and grounds the shared embedding space in the observable content of each modality.
 - **Overall Objective:** The total training objective is a weighted sum of these two terms: L = λ_align * L_align + λ_recon * L_recon, where λ_align and λ_recon are hyperparameters balancing their contributions.

**C. Downstream Evaluation**
To evaluate the quality and transferability of the learned representations, a set of downstream tasks (classification and regression) is defined. For each task, a lightweight MLP is trained on top of the frozen encoder embeddings. Critically, this training is performed exclusively using simulated data. The trained network is then evaluated directly on real sensor measurements without any fine-tuning. This zero-shot transfer protocol assesses the success of the alignment in abstracting away modality-specific artifacts and bridging the sim-to-real gap.

**D. Scalable Tactile Simulation**
The work introduces a custom plugin for NVIDIA Isaac Lab to facilitate scalable tactile data generation.
- **Implementation:** The plugin is built upon a penalty-based formulation for contact modeling and employs GPU-accelerated raycasting to determine contact geometry and pressure distributions across tactile sensor surfaces. All computations are implemented using NVIDIA Warp, enabling native execution on the GPU.
- **Scalability:** This GPU-accelerated implementation allows for massive parallelization of tactile simulations across thousands of concurrent environments, making it suitable for large-scale reinforcement learning and synthetic data generation.
- **Output:** The simulation framework generates geometric proxies like indenter penetration depth. While designed for scalability and integration into modern robot learning workflows, it acknowledges that the generated data may not perfectly match real-world measurements due to unmodeled mechanical deformation and transduction effects.

**E. Data Collection**
A "physical stimulus" is defined by indenter geometry, probing location (x, y), and probing displacement (z-axis). Paired data is collected for three modalities:
- **Real-world Capacitance (M_c):** An artificial silicone fingertip sensor with a 12x12 array of capacitive taxels is used. Each taxel produces a signal related to local surface deformation. To mitigate hysteresis, a temporal buffer of 10 recent frames (n_hist=10) is maintained, forming the capacitance modality (M_c ∈ R^(n_taxel × n_taxel × n_hist)). Data is collected using a 5-axis CNC milling machine for precise probing, and a 6-DOF force-torque (F/T) sensor provides ground-truth contact forces.
- **Rigid Body Simulation (RBS) - Penetration Depth (M_p):** Using the custom Isaac Lab plugin, raycasting from each taxel's surface normal measures indenter penetration depth (M_p ∈ R^(n_taxel × n_taxel) ≥0).
- **Finite Element Method (FEM) - Taxel Stress (M_s):** ABAQUS is used for high-fidelity FEM simulations of the probing process, yielding taxel stress fields (M_s ∈ R^(n_taxel × n_taxel × 6)). FEM simulations also provide indenter reaction forces, used as ground-truth for force prediction tasks.

**F. Alignment and Downstream Data Splits**
The dataset comprises 840 probing trajectories, yielding 32,942 samples for M_c and M_p, and 26,107 for M_s. The physical stimuli include 19 indenters across 12 distinct shapes.
- **Alignment Dataset (D_A):** Contains nine indenter geometries for training the multi-modal encoders (Fig. 4a).
- **Downstream Dataset (D_D):** Reserved for task training and evaluation, partitioned into:
 - **In-distribution holdout:** Same geometries and sizes as D_A, but with held-out probing locations.
 - **OOD-Size:** Six indenters with same shapes but previously unseen sizes and orientations (Fig. 4b).
 - **OOD-Shape:** Four entirely new indenter geometries (Fig. 4c).
The 840 trajectories are split into 462 in-distribution, 210 OOD-Size, and 168 OOD-Shape. In-distribution trajectories are further divided for D_A (231) and D_D (231). OOD-Size and OOD-Shape trajectories are exclusively for D_D. Within each subset, a 75%-25% split based on probing location is used for training and test sets.

**G. Downstream Tasks**
Three tasks are defined to evaluate cross-modal transferability, with task networks trained on simulated data and evaluated zero-shot on real capacitance measurements:
- **Indenter Shape Classification:** Predicts the indenter shape category (multi-label classification), measured by sample-weighted accuracy (Acc. [%]).
- **Indenter Shape Reconstruction:** Predicts per-taxel penetration depths, offering a geometric reconstruction of the contact surface, measured by pointwise mean absolute error (Pen. [μm]).
- **Indenter Force Prediction:** Estimates total reaction force on the indenter, measured by mean absolute error (Force [mN]).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of the TactSpace framework yielded several findings regarding latent space alignment, zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, representation expressiveness, and the effects of data scaling.

**A. Latent Space Alignment**
- **Effect of Alignment Loss:** t-SNE projections (Fig. 5) demonstrated that without an explicit alignment loss, embeddings from different modalities formed distinct, modality-specific clusters. An MSE alignment loss reduced separation but did not achieve coherent joint structuring. In contrast, the InfoNCE objective resulted in a uniform intermixing of embeddings from all three modalities (RBS, FEM, Real), indicating that the learned latent space was organized by the underlying physical stimulus rather than superficial modality characteristics.
- **Role of Temporal History:** Including temporal (capacitive) history in the real sensor data maintained consistently high pairwise cosine similarity between cross-modal embeddings throughout a probing trajectory (Fig. 6). Without this temporal context, a significant drop in similarity was observed, particularly between FEM-Real and RBS-Real embeddings during indenter retraction, attributed to capacitive and material hysteresis effects that history-dependent models can capture.

**B. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer**
- **General Transfer Capability:** The aligned latent space enabled zero-shot sim-to-real transfer across all evaluation splits, including out-of-distribution (OOD) indentations (Table I). Task networks trained exclusively on simulated data (M_p from Isaac Sim, M_s from FEM) showed performance on real capacitance measurements (M_c) that was comparable to their performance on simulated inputs, despite never having been fine-tuned on real data. This indicates that the latent alignment successfully allowed the model to interpret raw hardware measurements.
- **Complementary Modality Strengths:** The results indicated that rigid-body representations (M_p) were more effective for geometric prediction tasks (e.g., shape classification, reconstruction), while finite-element representations (M_s) excelled in force estimation. This supports the hypothesis that the framework captures modality-agnostic contact physics by leveraging the complementary information from different simulation types.
- **InfoNCE Superiority:** Across all evaluation splits and metrics, task networks trained on InfoNCE-aligned latent spaces consistently outperformed those trained on MSE-aligned or unaligned spaces (Table II), confirming the qualitative observations from t-SNE analysis and demonstrating the direct impact of alignment quality on sim-to-real transfer.

**C. Representation Expressiveness and Modality Ablation**
- **Comparison to Real-Data Baseline:** A supervised baseline trained directly on real capacitance measurements achieved the highest overall performance, as expected. However, the proposed latent representation model delivered competitive results, with a small performance gap in force prediction attributed to the baseline using ground-truth force-torque measurements versus FEM approximations in the proposed model.
- **Multi-Modality Benefits:** The model pre-trained with all three modalities (capacitance, penetration depth, stress) and full cross-reconstruction supervision achieved the best or near-best performance across tasks (Table III). This suggests that each modality contributes complementary physical information, enriching the shared latent space. While joint training might incur a slight performance trade-off compared to the best specialist configuration for a single task, it yields a more broadly capable representation.
- **Task-Specific Modality Contributions:** Penetration-depth supervision was particularly beneficial for geometric tasks (shape reconstruction and classification), whereas FEM-based stress data provided strong signals for force prediction. This indicates that task-relevant simulation sources can be leveraged for specialized performance, though at the cost of generality.
- **Necessity of Reconstruction Loss:** Removing the reconstruction loss while retaining the contrastive alignment objective resulted in a measurable drop in downstream task performance. This implies that the reconstruction objective is crucial for preventing representational collapse and preserving task-relevant information across modalities.
- **Impact of Data Diversity:** Including out-of-distribution (OOD) indenters in the latent alignment phase significantly improved performance on OOD splits, demonstrating the benefit of data diversity for the learned representation.

**D. Scaling Up with Simulated Data**
- **Geometric Tasks:** Increasing the volume of rigid-body simulation (RBS) data, specifically by generating more probing locations, positively impacted geometric tasks (indenter classification and penetration depth estimation). Performance improved and approached the real-data upper bound on the in-distribution set (Table IV).
- **OOD Generalization and Limitations:** While scaling simulated data improved performance on OOD splits, the performance gap to the real upper bound was wider compared to the in-distribution set. This indicates that while increased simulation data helps, an underlying alignment gap persists when transferring to entirely novel shapes.
- **Force Prediction:** Data scaling for single-modality RBS data provided negligible benefits for force prediction. In contrast, incorporating complementary multi-physics modalities, such as FEM-simulated stress, yielded substantial gains for force metrics, outperforming the benefits of simply increasing the volume of single-modality data. This highlights the importance of physics-enriched modalities for specific prediction tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by offering a novel approach to address the sim-to-real transfer problem for tactile sensing. The development of a multi-modal representation learning framework that aligns heterogeneous tactile observations within a shared latent space (TactSpace) carries several significances and potential impacts:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** By learning modality-invariant embeddings that abstract away sensor-specific artifacts and signal characteristics, the framework enables zero-shot transfer of policies and task networks trained solely on simulated data to real-world tactile sensors. This bypasses the need for high-fidelity, sensor-specific raw-signal simulation, which is often difficult and resource-intensive, particularly for complex sensors like capacitive arrays.
- **Scalability for Robot Learning:** Decoupling representations from raw sensor outputs and enabling training on synthetic data substantially lowers the barrier to data-intensive paradigms such as large-scale reinforcement learning. Robot learning algorithms can leverage vast amounts of simulated tactile data without requiring corresponding real-world data collection for every scenario or sensor type, accelerating the development of complex manipulation skills.
- **Physics-Enriched Representations:** The integration of complementary multi-physics simulation modalities (rigid-body mechanics for geometry and finite element analysis for stress fields) allows the learned latent space to capture a richer and more comprehensive understanding of contact interactions. This enriched representation can support a wider range of downstream tasks, from geometric reasoning to precise force estimation, leading to more versatile and robust robotic behaviors.
- **Sensor Agnosticism:** The design principle of aligning diverse modalities suggests that the framework is inherently sensor-agnostic. This implies that it could potentially be extended to different types of tactile sensors, reducing hardware fragmentation and enabling greater reusability of learned models across various robotic platforms.
- **Practical Tool Release:** The release of an efficient, GPU-accelerated tactile simulation plugin for NVIDIA Isaac Lab directly facilitates scalable tactile data generation. This tool can be integrated into existing robot learning workflows, providing a practical resource for the research community to generate physics-based contact information.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research, including relaxing the requirement for strictly paired observations to improve scalability when full simulation coverage is unavailable, extending the approach to dynamic manipulation sequences to leverage temporal contact evolution, and validating the framework's transferability across different tactile sensor platforms.

Overall, TactSpace offers a promising direction for creating more robust and data-efficient robotic manipulation systems by fundamentally altering how tactile information is acquired, represented, and transferred between simulated and real environments.
