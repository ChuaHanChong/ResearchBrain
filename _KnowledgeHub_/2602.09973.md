# RoboInter: A Holistic Intermediate Representation Suite Towards Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The RoboInter Suite introduces a comprehensive resource for robotic manipulation, featuring a large-scale dataset with dense, per-frame annotations of intermediate representations, alongside benchmarks and models. This suite enhances Vision-Language Model capabilities for embodied reasoning, leading to improved generalization and robustness in robot control.

## Problem

- Existing large-scale robot manipulation datasets are costly, embodiment-specific, and lack diverse, fine-grained intermediate representations (e.g., subtasks, object bounding boxes, gripper poses).
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) currently lack specialized embodied understanding and generation capabilities necessary for robust robot planning and execution in manipulation tasks.
- There is a need for integrated frameworks that can effectively leverage intermediate representations to bridge high-level planning with low-level robot action execution.

## Method

- Developed **RoboInter**-Data, a large-scale dataset integrating existing raw teleoperated videos with dense, per-frame annotations of over ten intermediate representation categories using a semi-automatic tool.
- Created RoboInter-VQA, a benchmark with diverse spatial and temporal VQA tasks, to systematically evaluate and improve VLMs' embodied reasoning capabilities for manipulation.
- Proposed RoboInter-VLA, a plan-then-execute framework that employs VLMs as Planners and Vision-Language-Action models as Executors, utilizing intermediate representations for both modular and end-to-end control.

## Results

- RoboInter-trained VLMs (e.g., RoboInter-Qwen-**3B**) demonstrated enhanced embodied reasoning, showing a **49.1%** improvement over RoboBrain2.0 on RoboRef It and **12.7%** on RoboVQA.
- In open-loop evaluations, the modular RoboInter-Te-Modular achieved the best performance among learning-based models with a mean Open-Loop Score (mOLS) of **0.3543**, surpassing end-to-end variants.
- Real-world closed-loop tests showed superior generalization, with RoboInter-IC-E2E achieving a **77.3%** success rate (vs. **65.0%** baseline) for in-distribution tasks and RoboInter-EC-E2E demonstrating greater robustness to out-of-distribution shifts (**8.3%** vs. **19%** performance drop).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training VLMs with RoboInter's intermediate representations significantly enhances their embodied grounding and reasoning abilities, outperforming existing embodied VLMs on relevant benchmarks.
> - Explicitly modeling and utilizing intermediate representations provides greater benefits for robot action generation and robustness in novel scenarios compared to implicitly incorporating them.
> - Finer-grained intermediate representations, particularly dense temporal traces, yield more substantial performance gains in robot action generation than coarser-grained representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is currently undergoing a transformative phase, heavily influenced by the remarkable generalization capabilities of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs). These advances have inspired the development of **vision-language-action (VLA) systems** for robotics, aiming to extend the success of large-scale pretraining to enable robots to understand and execute complex instructions in diverse environments.

However, a significant bottleneck in this evolution is the **limitation of existing robot manipulation datasets**. Current datasets, despite massive collection efforts (e.g., Droid, OXE, RH20T), are often costly to curate, highly specific to particular robot embodiments, and lack the coverage and diversity needed for generalizable VLA models. More critically, these datasets typically provide only high-level instructions paired with low-level robot actions, largely omitting the **fine-grained intermediate representations** that are crucial for robust planning and execution.

To mitigate these limitations, recent research has gravitated towards a **plan-then-execute paradigm**. This approach involves two main stages: first, generating high-level plans (e.g., subtasks, visual traces, 2D/3D grounding, affordances), and then translating these plans into low-level robot actions. While this modularity promises better generalization and robustness, its effectiveness is critically reliant on the availability of high-quality intermediate supervision, which is largely absent in existing datasets. Some efforts have explored automated or semi-automated annotation (e.g., LLARVA, ECoT, ShareRobot), but these often suffer from sensitivity to distribution shifts, limited scale, or misaligned labels.

RoboInter positions itself directly at this intersection, seeking to bridge the gap by providing a **unified resource of intermediate representations**—including data, benchmarks, and models—to facilitate the development of advanced VLA systems within the plan-then-execute framework. It aims to address the scarcity of diverse, high-quality, and densely annotated intermediate data, which is essential for grounding VLMs in embodied reasoning and guiding robotic action execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the RoboInter Manipulation Suite is to establish a practical and comprehensive foundation for advancing robust and generalizable robotic learning through fine-grained and diverse intermediate representations. The authors are motivated by several key challenges in current robotic manipulation research:

1. **Scarcity of Intermediate Supervision**: Existing large-scale robot datasets lack dense, per-frame annotations of intermediate representations (e.g., subtasks, object bounding boxes, gripper poses, motion traces). This absence hinders the training of VLA models that can effectively leverage a plan-then-execute paradigm.
2. **Cost and Specificity of Data Curation**: Manually curating robot data is expensive and time-consuming, leading to datasets that are often limited in diversity of scenes, tasks, and robot embodiments. Automated annotation methods have shown promise but often lack reliability or scale.
3. **Limited Embodied Reasoning in VLMs**: While general VLMs excel in broad semantic reasoning, they often lack the specialized embodied understanding and generation capabilities required for robot manipulation tasks. Benchmarking and enhancing these specific abilities is crucial.
4. **Lack of Integrated Frameworks**: There is a need for integrated frameworks that can systematically leverage intermediate representations to bridge high-level planning with low-level execution, allowing for both modular and end-to-end VLA variants.

To address these, RoboInter sets out to:
- **Introduce RoboInter-Data**: A large-scale dataset with dense, per-frame annotations across over ten categories of intermediate representations for robot manipulation, collected semi-automatically using a specialized tool.
- **Develop RoboInter-VQA**: A systematic benchmark and dataset comprising spatial and temporal embodied VQA categories to expose, evaluate, and enhance the embodied reasoning capabilities of VLMs.
- **Propose RoboInter-VLA**: An integrated plan-then-execute framework that leverages these intermediate representations to support both modular and end-to-end VLA models, facilitating efficient adaptation from planning to execution.

Ultimately, the motivation is to accelerate the development of robotic systems that can reason more effectively, generalize across tasks and environments, and operate with greater robustness by providing the necessary granular data and tools.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboInter suite is structured into three main components: RoboInter-Tool for data annotation, RoboInter-Data for the curated dataset, and RoboInter-VQA/VLA for benchmarking and modeling.

#### 3.1. RoboInter-Data: Large-Scale Intermediate Representation Annotation

The foundation of RoboInter is its large-scale, high-quality dataset of intermediate representations.
- **Data Collection**: To ensure diversity, RoboInter-Data integrates raw teleoperated video recordings from existing datasets: Droid (in-the-wild, diverse scenes/instructions), OXE (large-scale, diverse sources), and RH20T (table-top, high quality/skill diversity). After rigorous screening, this yields over 230k manipulation episodes.
- **Annotations & Check with RoboInter-Tool**: A custom, lightweight GUI called **RoboInter-Tool** facilitates semi-automatic, per-frame annotation. Key annotation processes include:
 - **Task Decomposition & Key-frame Annotation**: Human annotators segment videos into clips based on 15 predefined primitive skills (e.g., pick, place, push, pull, twist). ChatGPT provides preliminary language annotations, which humans refine. Contact frames (first robot-object contact) are also recorded.
 - **Manipulated Object Recognition**: Annotators identify the manipulated object. RoboInter-Tool then leverages SAM2 (Segment Anything Model 2) for automatic object segmentation and tracking, with human review and re-annotation mechanisms to ensure quality.
 - **End-Effector Localization**: To obtain accurate 2D end-effector traces, a calibration matrix is estimated to improve 3D-to-2D projection accuracy from robot states. For episodes lacking parameters, gripper detection and point tracking are used to reconstruct reliable 2D traces.
- **Post-processed Annotations**: From the raw annotations, additional intermediate representations are derived:
 - **Grasp Annotation**: Grasp affordance boxes are inferred from 2D end-effector locations at contact frames, and contact points (pre-defined gripper keypoints) along with 6D end-effector poses define grasp poses.
 - **Placement Annotation**: Object positions at the end of subtasks are identified as target placement locations.
 - **Gripper Annotation**: Anchor points enclosing the gripper are identified and projected to 2D image coordinates to form gripper bounding boxes.
- **Scale and Diversity**: RoboInter-Data provides dense, per-frame annotations for 230k episodes across 571 distinct scenes, involving 6 robot arm types and 15 primitive skills. This includes 61M object grounding annotations, 70M gripper trace annotations, 190k affordance/placement annotations, and 760k language clip annotations across over 10 categories of intermediate representations.

#### 3.2. RoboInter-VQA: Benchmarking Embodied Reasoning

To expose and enhance VLM capabilities for embodied tasks, RoboInter-VQA converts the dense annotations into diverse Visual Question Answering (VQA) tasks:
- **Spatial VQA (Understanding)**: Tasks like selecting correct object bounding boxes or grasp poses, matching scenes to instructions, and determining contact occurrence.
- **Spatial VQA (Generation)**: Tasks requiring the generation of spatial intermediate representations such as object bounding boxes, grasp poses, placement proposals, key points, and gripper bounding boxes.
- **Temporal VQA (Understanding)**: Tasks to evaluate understanding of motion traces and subtask-observation relationships, including movement direction selection, trace-description matching, subtask/primitive discrimination, and execution stage identification.
- **Temporal VQA (Generation)**: Tasks requiring trace generation and multi-step planning, conditioned on varying levels of prior information (e.g., past subtasks, overall instructions).
- **Scale**: RoboInter-VQA includes approximately 1M spatial generation, 172k spatial understanding, 131k temporal generation, and 935k temporal understanding entries.

#### 3.3. RoboInter-VLA: Plan-Then-Execute Framework for Robotic Manipulation

RoboInter-VLA provides a family of models that follow the plan-then-execute paradigm, composed of a Planner and an Executor.
- **VLMs as Planner**: The Planner uses VLM architectures (e.g., Qwen-VL, LLaVA-One-Vision) trained on RoboInter-VQA data. It acquires embodied capabilities through a VQA formulation and co-training strategy to generate intermediate representations autoregressively.
- **VLAs as Executor**: The Executor is built on a VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) with a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) action head. It consumes multi-view visual observations, language instructions, and intermediate representations from the Planner, producing multi-step action chunks. An information aggregator compresses hidden states and intermediate representations into conditioning features.
- **Plan-Then-Execute Paradigms**: Three variants are supported:
 - **RoboInter-IC-E2E (Implicitly-Conditioned End-to-End)**: The pretrained VLM Planner is directly injected into the end-to-end Executor, serving as a stronger vision-language feature extractor for robust embodied perception.
 - **RoboInter-EC-E2E (Explicitly-Conditioned End-to-End)**: The Executor is initialized with the Planner's VLM and jointly optimizes intermediate-representation reasoning and action generation. The Planner explicitly generates intermediate representations as part of the model's output.
 - **RoboInter-Modular (Modular Planner-to-Executor)**: A non-end-to-end hierarchical design where Planner and Executor are independent modules. The Executor is conditioned on ground-truth (during training) or Planner-predicted (during inference) intermediate representations to generate actions.
- **Flexible Chain-of-Thought (F-CoT)**: This is a key component, allowing multiple intermediate representations (subtasks, skills, bounding boxes, traces, etc.) to be composed. F-CoT acts as VQA supervision for the Planner and action-aligned guidance for the Executor, available in textual (RoboInter-Te-Modular) or visual-prompted (RoboInter-Im-Modular) forms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the significant advantages of the RoboInter suite in enhancing VLM capabilities for robotic manipulation.

#### 4.1. Benchmarking the VLM Planner

- **Enhanced Grounding and Embodied Capability**: RoboInter-trained VLMs (RoboInter-Qwen, RoboInter-LLaVAOV) substantially outperformed their base models and competitive embodied VLMs like RoboBrain2.0 on third-party benchmarks (Where2Place, RoboRef It, RoboVQA). For instance, RoboInter-Qwen-3B showed a 49.1% improvement over RoboBrain2.0 on RoboRef It and 12.7% on RoboVQA at the 3B scale.
- **RoboInter-VQA Benchmark Performance**: On the curated RoboInter-VQA tasks, models trained with RoboInter-Data showed marked improvements, especially in generation tasks where general VLMs often struggled (typically below 40% accuracy for spatial generation). This highlights the critical role of specific intermediate representation annotations for embodied reasoning. For temporal tasks, RoboInter-trained models significantly surpassed baselines in trace generation and task planning. The general capabilities of the VLMs were largely maintained.

#### 4.2. Open-Loop Evaluation of the Executor

- **Planner Improves Executor**: The incorporation of a pretrained VLM Planner consistently improved the Executor's action generation performance. RoboInter-IC-E2E achieved a higher mean Open-Loop Score (mOLS) than the Vanilla baseline (0.3218 vs. 0.3086).
- **Explicit vs. Implicit Intermediate Representations**: Explicit intermediate representations proved more helpful. RoboInter-EC-E2E outperformed RoboInter-IC-E2E (mOLS 0.3340 vs. 0.3218), indicating that direct, explicit guidance benefits action generation more.
- **Modular Design Benefits**: The modular architecture (RoboInter-Te-Modular) achieved the best performance among learning-based models (mOLS 0.3543), surpassing EC-E2E. This suggests that decoupling planning and execution allows for dedicated optimization of each component and mitigates mode conflicts.
- **Informativeness of Annotations**: The Oracle+Executor, guided by ground-truth intermediate representations, achieved the highest scores (mOLS 0.3861), validating the high quality and informativeness of RoboInter's annotations.
- **Ablation of Intermediate Representations**: Finer-grained intermediate representations (e.g., Object Box, Gripper Box, Affordance, and especially Trace) provided substantially larger performance gains than coarse-grained ones (Subtask, Primitive Skill). Trace, providing dense temporal grounding, yielded the most significant improvement.
- **Data Scaling and Cross-Platform**: Experiments on RoboInter-VQA and F-CoT data confirmed a scaling law where performance generally improves with more data. Cross-platform open-loop evaluations showed consistent outperformance of RoboInter variants over baselines across five different robot embodiments.

#### 4.3. Closed-Loop Real-World Evaluation of the Executor

- **Superior Generalization**: In real-world closed-loop tasks, RoboInter-IC-E2E consistently outperformed the Vanilla baseline, achieving a higher average success rate (77.3% vs. 65.0% for in-distribution, 58.3% vs. 38.3% for out-of-distribution, OOD). This highlights the benefit of the pretrained VLM Planner's stronger perceptual priors.
- **Robustness to Distribution Shift**: RoboInter-EC-E2E demonstrated superior OOD generalization compared to IC-E2E, with a significantly smaller ID-to-OOD performance drop (8.3% vs. 19%). This indicates that explicitly modeling intermediate representations fosters greater robustness to novel scenarios, despite potentially lower in-distribution accuracy due to modality interference during joint training.
- **Competitive Modular Performance**: The modular variant also showed strong real-world performance and competitive OOD generalization, further supporting the benefits of intermediate representations.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer**: RoboInter-IC-E2E, even without specific training data for WidowX or Google robots, surpassed baseline models on the SimplerEnv benchmark, validating the transferability of the learned embodied reasoning abilities across different robot platforms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboInter Manipulation Suite represents a substantial contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, offering a unified and comprehensive resource that addresses several critical gaps in current research. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Generalizable Robotic Learning**: By providing the first large-scale, real-world manipulation dataset with dense, per-frame annotations across diverse intermediate representations, RoboInter-Data creates an unprecedented resource for training VLA models. This rich, fine-grained supervision is crucial for teaching robots to reason, plan, and execute actions with greater precision and generality, moving beyond embodiment-specific and task-specific solutions.
2. **Advancing Embodied AI Capabilities of VLMs**: RoboInter-VQA systematically benchmarks and enhances the embodied generation and understanding capabilities of VLMs. This is vital for grounding general-purpose VLMs in the physical world, allowing them to translate broad language and visual understanding into actionable robotic intelligence. The improved performance on both spatial and temporal reasoning tasks demonstrates that the suite effectively trains VLMs to act as powerful "Planners" for robotic tasks.
3. **Facilitating Plan-Then-Execute Paradigms**: RoboInter-VLA offers a principled framework for integrating intermediate representations into both modular and end-to-end VLA models. This allows researchers to systematically study the trade-offs and benefits of different architectural designs, providing a clear path forward for developing robust manipulation policies. The empirical evidence of superior generalization and robustness (especially in OOD scenarios for EC-E2E) highlights the practical value of explicit intermediate reasoning.
4. **Supporting Broader AI Research Directions**: Beyond direct VLA training, RoboInter-Data has several broader applications:
 - **Expert Generative Models**: The large-scale, high-quality annotations for individual intermediate representations can be used to pre-train specialized generative models for specific tasks like object segmentation, grasp pose estimation, or trajectory prediction.
 - **Human-Robot Interaction**: Intermediate representations naturally bridge high-level human instructions with low-level robot control, paving the way for more intuitive and precise shared-autonomy systems. Humans could provide guidance in terms of subtasks, target objects, or desired motion traces.
 - **Embodied World Model Learning**: The temporal alignment of annotations with raw videos and robot states provides strong supervision for learning structured embodied world models. These models could generate controllable video sequences, enhancing a robot's predictive and simulated reasoning capabilities.
 - **Video Action Model Learning**: The combination of action annotations, multi-view videos, and diverse intermediate representations makes RoboInter-Data an ideal resource for training video action models with rich, multi-level supervision, leading to more robust and versatile action policies.

By open-sourcing the entire suite—data, benchmarks, and models—the authors aim to foster community-driven research and accelerate the development of a new generation of robots capable of complex, general-purpose manipulation. RoboInter establishes a critical bridge between advanced vision-language reasoning and practical robotic action.
