# ERA: Transforming VLMs into Embodied Agents via Embodied Prior Learning and Online Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> ERA introduces a two-stage framework that transforms compact 3B-parameter Vision Language Models into embodied agents by combining structured prior knowledge injection with robust online reinforcement learning. This approach enables the model to achieve an average success rate of 65.2% on EB-ALFRED and 48.3% on EB-Manipulation, surpassing larger proprietary models like GPT-4o by 8.4 to 19.4 percentage points and demonstrating strong generalization to unseen tasks.

## Problem

- Compact Vision Language Models (VLMs) lack the specialized embodied knowledge and robust reasoning required to act as effective agents in complex, interactive environments.
- Reliance on large, computationally expensive proprietary VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) for top performance creates scalability and deployment bottlenecks for embodied AI.
- Existing embodied training paradigms suffer from fragmented approaches and data scarcity, particularly lacking detailed reasoning traces for learning planning and control.

## Method

- Implements a two-stage training framework: ==Embodied Prior Learning (EPL)== and ==Online Reinforcement Learning (RL)==, designed to equip compact VLMs with embodied agency.
- EPL involves ==supervised fine-tuning== on a principled taxonomy of curated datasets, enriching trajectories with structured reasoning, grounding spatial understanding, and transferring general external knowledge.
- Online RL refines agent behavior using ==self-summarization== for efficient context management, a ==dense multi-component reward function==, and ==turn-level policy optimization== for stable learning in long-horizon tasks.

## Results

- ERA-**3B** achieved an average success rate of **65.2%** on EB-ALFRED and **48.3%** on EB-Manipulation, outperforming GPT-4o by 8.4 to 19.4 percentage points and competitive with Claude-3.5-Sonnet.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization to unseen tasks, surpassing other training-based baselines by up to 38 percentage points on specific unseen subsets.
- Self-summarization improved success rates by 8-**10%** with higher token efficiency, and turn-level policy optimization increased success rates by 8.4 to 12 points compared to token-level approaches, confirming their efficacy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A systematic approach to embodied prior learning (EPL) is essential for equipping compact VLMs with the foundational knowledge needed for effective online reinforcement learning.
> - Augmenting existing embodied trajectories with structured reasoning traces (visual descriptions, reflections, plans) significantly improves the model's generalization to unseen tasks.
> - Innovations in online reinforcement learning, such as self-summarization for context, dense multi-component reward functions, and turn-level policy optimization, are critical for stable and efficient training of VLM-based agents in long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of embodied AI: how to leverage the impressive capabilities of Vision Language Models (VLMs) to create effective and deployable agents for complex, interactive environments.

**Current Landscape:**
Recent advancements have demonstrated VLMs' potential in instruction following, visual understanding, and various forms of reasoning. Researchers are actively working to transition these models from static text-and-image understanding to dynamic perception, reasoning, and action in physical or simulated environments. High-performing embodied systems often rely on massive, proprietary VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet) that are computationally expensive for training and inference, hindering real-world deployment. Smaller, more efficient VLMs, while desirable, typically exhibit a significant performance gap due to a lack of embodied knowledge, robust reasoning, and seamless integration of high-level planning with low-level grounding.

**Existing Gaps and Limitations:**
- **Cost and Scalability:** The reliance on large, costly models for top performance creates a bottleneck for widespread application.
- **Knowledge & Skills Deficit in Smaller Models:** Smaller VLMs often lack the specialized "embodied knowledge" (e.g., spatial reasoning, object interaction dynamics) required for successful agency.
- **Fragmented Approaches:** Previous efforts tend to focus on either high-level planning or low-level control in isolation, or rely on static QA-style datasets for reinforcement learning (RL) without comprehensive online interaction. There has been a lack of unified frameworks that systematically combine supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with online RL for VLM-based agents across diverse embodied tasks.
- **Data Scarcity & Quality:** Collecting high-quality, richly annotated embodied trajectory data is expensive and time-consuming, and existing datasets often lack detailed reasoning traces.

**ERA's Contribution to the Landscape:**
ERA directly tackles these limitations by providing a practical and scalable framework designed to empower *compact* VLMs. It unifies two key learning paradigms:
1. **Systematic Embodied Prior Learning (EPL):** ERA proposes a principled taxonomy and methodology for curating and leveraging diverse data sources to inject foundational embodied knowledge into smaller VLMs *before* online interaction. This addresses the "knowledge deficit" and "data scarcity" problems by enriching existing data and incorporating external knowledge.
2. **Robust Online Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Building on the strong priors, ERA introduces an improved online RL pipeline specifically tailored for VLM agents. This addresses challenges like long-horizon tasks, sparse rewards, and training instability, which are common in embodied RL. The novel designs (self-summarization, dense reward shaping, turn-level policy optimization) improve efficiency and effectiveness of online learning.

By combining these two stages, ERA bridges the performance gap, allowing a 3B-parameter model to surpass much larger proprietary models and existing training-based baselines. This work offers a methodological "recipe" for building generalizable embodied agents with practical deployment considerations, pushing the frontier towards more capable and scalable embodied intelligence. The comprehensive evaluation on both high-level planning (EB-ALFRED) and low-level control (EB-Manipulation) further distinguishes ERA from approaches focused on a single task granularity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the ERA framework are multifaceted:

1. **Bridge the Performance Gap for Compact VLMs:** The core motivation is to enable smaller, more computationally efficient Vision Language Models (VLMs) to achieve performance comparable to, or even superior to, much larger and more expensive proprietary models (like GPT-4o or Claude-3.5-Sonnet) in complex embodied tasks. This directly addresses the scalability and deployment challenges currently facing embodied AI.
2. **Develop Generalizable Embodied Skills:** The research aims to equip VLMs with robust, transferable embodied skills, allowing them to succeed not only on seen tasks but also to generalize effectively to unseen and novel scenarios requiring commonsense and spatial reasoning.
3. **Address Limitations of Existing Training Paradigms:**
 - **Data Scarcity and Limited Reasoning Supervision:** Traditional embodied datasets often lack detailed intermediate reasoning traces, making it difficult for models to learn effective planning. ERA seeks to overcome this by augmenting data with structured reasoning.
 - **Challenges in Embodied RL:** Online reinforcement learning for embodied agents is difficult due to long task horizons, sparse reward signals, and training instability, especially when dealing with VLM-based policy networks. ERA aims to introduce specific designs to make RL more efficient and stable.
4. **Provide a Practical and Principled Framework:** The authors intend to offer a systematic, two-stage training framework (Embodied Prior Learning + Online Reinforcement Learning) complemented by a clear taxonomy of prior knowledge types. This aims to serve as a practical guide for future research and development of embodied AI systems.

In essence, ERA is motivated by the desire to make sophisticated embodied intelligence accessible and deployable by improving the efficiency and generalization capabilities of smaller foundation models through carefully designed data curation and robust online learning mechanisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ERA employs a novel two-stage training framework: **Embodied Prior Learning (EPL)** followed by **Online Reinforcement Learning (RL)**. This approach aims to first inject foundational embodied knowledge into compact VLMs and then refine these skills through interactive, trial-and-error learning.

#### 3.1. Embodied Prior Learning (EPL)

EPL is designed to address the inherent gap between general VLM pretraining and the specific requirements of embodied agent tasks. It involves supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on carefully curated datasets categorized into three types:

1. **Trajectory-Augmented Priors:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing embodied trajectories often lack explicit reasoning steps, making it hard for agents to learn robust planning and error correction. Large VLMs like GPT-4o often produce inaccurate visual descriptions for low-level tasks.
 - **Approach:** For high-level tasks (EB-ALFRED), each step of an existing trajectory is enriched with a structured reasoning trace `z_t = {z_vis_t, z_ref_t, z_plan_t}`. `z_vis_t` is a visual description, `z_ref_t` is a reflection on history and errors, and `z_plan_t` is a step-level plan. GPT-4o is prompted with current context and action to generate this "inner monologue."
 - For low-level tasks (EB-Manipulation), rule-based methods are used to generate ground-truth visual descriptions from simulator backend information, ensuring accuracy where LLM-generated descriptions fail. This precise visual grounding is crucial for fine-grained control.

2. **Environment-Anchored Priors:**
 - **Motivation:** Trajectory data is expensive and limited. This category aims to leverage additional environment-specific knowledge beyond direct agent interaction.
 - **Approach:**
 - **For EB-ALFRED (High-level planning):**
 - **Masked Action Modeling:** Given an instruction and an action sequence, a random action is masked. The model learns to predict the missing action along with a GPT-4o generated reasoning trace explaining its correctness. This enhances task comprehension and action feasibility.
 - **Action Sequence Reordering:** A shuffled action sequence and instruction are provided, and the model learns to output the correct temporal order with a reasoning trace. This improves understanding of action dependencies and planning.
 - **For EB-Manipulation (Low-level control):** Focuses on spatial understanding using visual-QA pairs from VLMbench with ground-truth 3D coordinates.
 - **Absolute Coordinate Grounding:** Direct mapping between object descriptions/coordinates and vice-versa (e.g., "What is the coordinate of the gray moon?" or "What object is at [X, Y, Z]?").
 - **Relative Coordinate Grounding:** Captures spatial relations (e.g., "What is the 3D location of the leftmost object?").
 - **Combined Grounding:** Integrates both absolute and relative information (e.g., "Is the object at [X,Y,Z] the leftmost?").

3. **External Knowledge Priors:**
 - **Motivation:** To transfer general reasoning and cross-domain grounding capabilities from large-scale, out-of-environment datasets, complementing environment-specific priors.
 - **Approach:**
 - **For EB-ALFRED (High-level planning):** Uses a sampled subset of the OpenO1-SFT dataset, a text-based corpus designed to activate chain-of-thought reasoning.
 - **For EB-Manipulation (Low-level control):** Utilizes the SpaceThinker dataset, a synthesized multimodal spatial reasoning corpus.

EPL training follows a curriculum: general skills (Env-Anchored/External) are learned first, followed by task-specific trajectory-level learning (Raw/Trajectory-Augmented).

#### 3.2. Online Reinforcement Learning (RL)

The second stage refines the learned priors through interactive online training, addressing challenges like long horizons, sparse rewards, and training instability.

1. **Agent Framework:**
 - **Structured Reasoning and Action:** The agent generates responses combining a reasoning trace `z_t` (visual description, reflection, language plan) and an executable action, using special tokens `<|think start|>...<|think end|>` and `<|action start|>...<|action end|>`. This ReAct-style format elicits reasoning and ensures action parsability.
 - **Self-Summarization Context Management:** To combat context explosion in long-horizon tasks, the agent's prior reflection (`z_t-1`) is used to compress the entire past history into a one-step context `h_t = (z_{t-1}, a_{t-1}, e_{t-1})`. This maintains crucial context efficiently (O(1) context size).

2. **Reward Design:** A dense, process-level reward function `r_t` is used at each turn: `r_t = r_success_t + r_subgoal_t + r_behavior_t`.
 - **Success-based Reward (`r_success_t`):** A sparse, high-magnitude reward for task completion (e.g., +4.0 for EB-ALFRED, +3.0 for EB-Manipulation).
 - **Subgoal-based Reward (`r_subgoal_t`):** Rewards for achieving intermediate milestones. For EB-ALFRED, based on PDDL subgoal conditions (+1.0 per new subgoal). For EB-Manipulation, based on end-effector proximity to target objects (+1.0 per new object approached).
 - **Behavior-Shaping Reward (`r_behavior_t`):** Rewards/penalizes specific actions. For EB-ALFRED, penalties for invalid actions (-0.5). For EB-Manipulation, rewards/penalties based on the accuracy of the agent's visual grounding description (+0.5 for >75% accuracy, -0.5 for <25%).

3. **Turn-level Policy Optimization:**
 - **Motivation:** Token-level value functions are unstable and less meaningful for multi-turn embodied tasks.
 - **Approach:** The agent's entire response in a turn is treated as a single "action." A single value estimate `V_ϕ(x_t)` is learned for the state `x_t` at the beginning of each turn. The Generalized Advantage Estimate (GAE) `A_t` is computed at the turn level and shared across all tokens within that turn's response. This stabilizes credit assignment and PPO training.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating sophisticated data curation with tailored RL techniques, forms the backbone of ERA's success.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ERA-3B demonstrates significant advancements in embodied AI, particularly for compact VLMs, outperforming both larger prompting-based models and previous training-based baselines.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Generalization (Q1)

- **State-of-the-Art for Training-based Agents:** ERA-3B achieves an average success rate of 65.2% on EB-ALFRED (high-level planning) and 48.3% on EB-Manipulation (low-level control).
- **Outperforms Large Proprietary Models:** ERA-3B surpasses GPT-4o by 8.4 percentage points on EB-ALFRED and 19.4 points on EB-Manipulation, despite being a much smaller model. It is also competitive with Claude-3.5-Sonnet (66.4% on EB-ALFRED).
- **Strong Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** ERA significantly outperforms other training-based baselines (e.g., RL4VLM, VAGEN) on unseen subsets. For instance, on the "Spatial" subset of EB-ALFRED, ERA achieves 66%, 38 points higher than VAGEN (28%). This indicates that ERA learns robust and transferable skills.
- **Complementary Roles of EPL and RL:** EPL alone provides a strong foundation (56.0% on EB-ALFRED, 40.0% on EB-Manipulation). The subsequent RL stage adds substantial gains (9.2 and 8.3 points, respectively), with even larger improvements on unseen tasks (13.0 points on EB-ALFRED, 11.5 points on EB-Manipulation).

#### 4.2. Role of Different Prior Datasets (Q2)

Ablation studies reveal the distinct contributions of various prior datasets:

- **Failure without Priors:** Direct RL training from the base 3B model completely fails without any embodied prior learning.
- **Trajectory-Augmented Priors (Traj-Aug) for Generalization:** This category yields the largest individual gains in generalization. After RL, Traj-Aug improves unseen EB-ALFRED performance by +13.0% and EB-Manipulation by +13.6% over the raw-trajectory baseline. This highlights the importance of structured reasoning for transferability.
- **Environment-Anchored Priors (Env-Anc) for Balanced Improvement:** Env-Anc priors provide consistent gains across both seen and unseen subsets (+6% for EB-ALFRED unseen, +1% for EB-Manipulation unseen after RL). This suggests they instill useful environment-level knowledge.
- **External Knowledge Priors (Ext-Know) for Unseen Tasks:** Ext-Know priors primarily improve performance on unseen tasks (+10.0% for EB-ALFRED unseen, +5.2% for EB-Manipulation unseen after RL), indicating their role in transferring general reasoning skills.
- **Best Combination: Trajectory-Augmented + Environment-Anchored Priors:** The most significant overall improvements are achieved by combining Traj-Aug and Env-Anc priors, leading to 60% success on unseen EB-ALFRED (+24% over baseline) and 43.8% on unseen EB-Manipulation (+21.9% over baseline). This indicates strong synergy between explicit reasoning and environment-specific grounding.
- **EPL Performance Predicts RL Success:** A near-linear correlation (Pearson coefficients 0.88-0.97) between EPL-only and EPL+RL performance shows that stronger prior learning directly translates to better final RL outcomes.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Self-Summarization for Context Management (Q3)

- **Critical Role of History:** Removing all interaction history drastically reduces success rates (to 2% on EB-ALFRED, 3.1% on EB-Manipulation).
- **Self-Summarization Improves Performance & Efficiency:** Incorporating self-summarization yields an 8–10% increase in success rates. Crucially, a one-step history with self-summarization outperforms 3-5 step histories without it, while using fewer input tokens. This validates self-summarization as an efficient and effective mechanism for retaining relevant context in long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.4. Impact of Reward Design and Turn-Level Value (Q4)

- **Synergistic Dense Rewards:**
 - For long-horizon tasks (EB-ALFRED), combining success, subgoal, and behavior-shaping rewards dramatically improves performance, raising the average success rate from 46% (outcome-only) to 60% (+14%).
 - For shorter-horizon tasks (EB-Manipulation), the impact is more modest (+2.1%), suggesting that dense rewards are particularly vital for credit assignment in extended tasks.
- **Turn-Level Value Estimation for Stable Learning:**
 - Turn-level GAE significantly outperforms token-level and bi-level GAE. It improves average success rates by 12 points on EB-ALFRED (48% to 60%) and 8.4 points on EB-Manipulation (35.4% to 43.8%) over token-level GAE.
 - This confirms that aligning credit assignment with the natural unit of environment interaction (the entire turn's response) leads to more stable and effective policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ERA framework offers a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI, particularly in making advanced agent capabilities more practical and accessible. Its main significance and potential impacts are:

1. **Enabling Scalable Embodied Intelligence with Compact Models:** ERA demonstrates a viable path for developing highly capable embodied agents using smaller, more parameter-efficient VLMs. This is crucial for real-world deployment where computational resources are often constrained, reducing the reliance on massive, costly, and energy-intensive models. This directly addresses a major bottleneck in the current embodied AI landscape.
2. **Principled Data Curation for Embodied Priors:** The introduction of a clear taxonomy for embodied prior knowledge (Trajectory-Augmented, Environment-Anchored, External) provides a structured and actionable guide for researchers. This systematic approach to data curation allows for the injection of foundational knowledge—from detailed reasoning traces to precise spatial grounding—thereby boosting the learning efficiency and generalization capabilities of VLMs before any online interaction.
3. **Advancements in Online Reinforcement Learning for VLMs:** ERA’s innovations in the RL stage—specifically self-summarization for efficient context management, a multi-component dense reward function, and turn-level policy optimization—tackle critical challenges inherent in training VLM-based agents in dynamic environments. These methodological insights can be adopted by future embodied AI systems to enhance learning stability, efficiency, and effectiveness in long-horizon, sparse-reward settings.
4. **Strong Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** The ability of ERA-3B to significantly outperform baselines on unseen tasks (e.g., commonsense and spatial reasoning challenges) highlights its capacity to learn robust and transferable skills, rather than merely overfitting to training environments. This is a crucial step towards building truly intelligent and adaptable agents.
5. **Methodological "Recipe" for Future Systems:** By rigorously evaluating the individual and synergistic contributions of each component (different prior types, context management, reward designs, and GAE schemes), ERA provides a clear, practical roadmap for designing and implementing effective training pipelines for VLM-based embodied agents.
6. **Bridging the Perception-Reasoning-Action Gap:** The framework’s emphasis on integrating structured reasoning, accurate visual descriptions (especially for low-level tasks), and refined action policies helps to more seamlessly connect the perceptual, cognitive, and motor aspects of embodied intelligence in VLMs.

**Limitations:**
It is important to note the acknowledged limitation that all evaluations were conducted in simulated environments. While simulations offer control and reproducibility, real-world deployment often introduces unforeseen complexities, physics discrepancies, and sensory noise that can challenge policies trained solely in simulation. Future work would need to validate ERA's approach in real-world robotic systems.

In conclusion, ERA represents a significant stride towards practical and scalable embodied intelligence. By offering a comprehensive and empirically validated framework that carefully integrates data-driven prior learning with robust online reinforcement learning, it lays important groundwork for the development of next-generation, general-purpose embodied agents.
