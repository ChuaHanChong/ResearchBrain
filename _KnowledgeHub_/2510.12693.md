# ERA: Transforming VLMs into Embodied Agents via Embodied Prior Learning and Online Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> ERA introduces a two-stage framework that transforms compact 3B-parameter Vision Language Models into embodied agents by combining structured prior knowledge injection with robust online reinforcement learning. This approach enables the model to achieve an average success rate of 65.2% on EB-ALFRED and 48.3% on EB-Manipulation, surpassing larger proprietary models like GPT-4o by 8.4 to 19.4 percentage points and demonstrating strong generalization to unseen tasks.

## Problem

- Compact Vision Language Models (VLMs) lack the specialized embodied knowledge and robust reasoning required to act as effective agents in complex, interactive environments.
- Reliance on large, computationally expensive proprietary VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) for top performance creates scalability and deployment bottlenecks for embodied AI.
- Existing embodied training paradigms suffer from fragmented approaches and data scarcity, particularly lacking detailed reasoning traces for learning planning and control.

## Method

- Implements a two-stage training framework: ==Embodied Prior Learning (EPL)== and ==Online Reinforcement Learning (RL)==, designed to equip compact VLMs with embodied agency.
- EPL involves ==supervised fine-tuning== on a principled taxonomy of curated datasets, enriching trajectories with structured reasoning, grounding spatial understanding, and transferring general external knowledge.
- Online RL refines agent behavior using ==self-summarization== for efficient context management, a ==dense multi-component reward function==, and ==turn-level policy optimization== for stable learning in long-horizon tasks.

## Results

- ERA-**3B** achieved an average success rate of **65.2%** on EB-ALFRED and **48.3%** on EB-Manipulation, outperforming GPT-4o by 8.4 to 19.4 percentage points and competitive with Claude-3.5-Sonnet.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization to unseen tasks, surpassing other training-based baselines by up to 38 percentage points on specific unseen subsets.
- Self-summarization improved success rates by 8-**10%** with higher token efficiency, and turn-level policy optimization increased success rates by 8.4 to 12 points compared to token-level approaches, confirming their efficacy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A systematic approach to embodied prior learning (EPL) is essential for equipping compact VLMs with the foundational knowledge needed for effective online reinforcement learning.
> - Augmenting existing embodied trajectories with structured reasoning traces (visual descriptions, reflections, plans) significantly improves the model's generalization to unseen tasks.
> - Innovations in online reinforcement learning, such as self-summarization for context, dense multi-component reward functions, and turn-level policy optimization, are critical for stable and efficient training of VLM-based agents in long-horizon tasks.
