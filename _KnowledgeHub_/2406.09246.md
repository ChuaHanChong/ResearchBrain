# OpenVLA: An Open-Source Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenVLA introduces a fully open-source, 7B-parameter Vision-Language-Action model that sets a new state of the art for generalist robot manipulation, outperforming larger closed-source models by 16.5% absolute success rate. The model also demonstrates effective and efficient fine-tuning strategies for adapting to new robot setups and tasks on commodity hardware.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic manipulation policies were task-specific and struggled with generalization to novel objects, scenes, or unseen instructions.
- Existing state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models were largely closed-source, limiting accessibility, transparency, and collaborative community development.
- There was a lack of established, efficient methods for fine-tuning large VLA models to new robot embodiments and tasks, particularly for deployment on more accessible, commodity hardware.

## Method

- Develops and releases **OpenVLA**, a 7B-parameter, fully open-source ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== model based on a ==Llama 2== LLM combined with a fused ==DINOv2== and ==SigLIP== visual encoder.
- Trains OpenVLA on **970k** real-world robot demonstrations from the ==Open X-Embodiment dataset==, formulating robot action prediction as a ==next-token prediction== task by tokenizing continuous actions.
- Investigates and validates efficient fine-tuning methodologies, including ==parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA)== and ==4-bit quantization==, to enable practical adaptation and inference on commodity hardware.

## Results

- **OpenVLA**, a **7B**-parameter model, established a new state of the art for generalist robot manipulation, outperforming the **55B**-parameter RT-2-X by **16.5%** absolute success rate across **29** tasks on WidowX and Google Robot embodiments.
- The model demonstrated effective and data-efficient adaptation to new real-world robot setups with small datasets (**10-150** demonstrations), outperforming from-scratch imitation learning methods by **20.4%** on diverse multi-instruction tasks.
- LoRA fine-tuning achieved performance comparable to full fine-tuning while training only **1.4%** of parameters, reducing compute by **8x**, and 4-bit quantization maintained performance while halving GPU memory footprint to **7.0 GB** for inference at ~**6Hz** on consumer GPUs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-tuning the vision encoder is crucial for achieving high performance in robot control, suggesting pretrained vision backbones need adaptation for precise spatial details.
> - Extensive training over many epochs (27) with a fixed learning rate is essential for maximizing action token accuracy and real-world robot performance.
> - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) and 4-bit quantization enable significant reductions in computational requirements and memory footprint, making VLA model adaptation and deployment practical on commodity hardware without substantial performance degradation, provided vision encoder adaptation is included.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is a significant contribution to the rapidly evolving field of generalist robot policies, specifically within the emerging domain of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

Historically, robotic manipulation policies have often been task-specific, trained for individual skills or restricted environments. While these policies could generalize to new initial conditions, they struggled with scene distractors, novel objects, or unseen task instructions. In parallel, the field of artificial intelligence has seen a revolution with "foundation models" in vision and language, such as CLIP, SigLIP, Llama 2, and PaLI. These models, trained on Internet-scale datasets, exhibit impressive generalization capabilities across diverse visual and linguistic tasks.

The broader research landscape in robotics has been actively seeking to leverage these advances in foundation models to overcome the generalization limitations of traditional robot policies. Early efforts involved integrating pretrained language and vision-language models for representation learning or as components in modular planning systems. More recently, the focus has shifted towards directly learning end-to-end VLAs, which fine-tune visually-conditioned language models (VLMs) to output robot control actions. Models like Google DeepMind's RT-2 and RT-2-X represent the state-of-the-art in this direction, demonstrating impressive robustness and generalization by transferring "web knowledge" to robotic control.

However, despite their promising capabilities, existing VLAs face two critical barriers to widespread adoption:
1. **Accessibility:** Most state-of-the-art VLA models, including RT-2 and RT-2-X, are largely closed-source, with limited transparency regarding their architecture, training procedures, and data mixture. This proprietary nature hinders open research, replication, and further development by the broader robotics community.
2. **Adaptation:** Prior work has not adequately explored efficient methods for fine-tuning these large VLA models for new robots, environments, and tasks, especially on more accessible, commodity hardware. This lack of practical adaptation strategies limits their real-world utility and adoption.

OpenVLA directly addresses these critical gaps. By releasing a fully open-source VLA model, code, and training pipeline, the authors aim to democratize access to these powerful models, fostering an open ecosystem similar to that seen with large language models (LLMs). Furthermore, the paper explicitly investigates and demonstrates efficient fine-tuning strategies, making VLA models more practical for researchers and practitioners without access to massive computational resources. This work positions OpenVLA as a foundational step towards building a truly open and adaptable generalist robotics AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the OpenVLA project are multifaceted, aiming to advance the field of generalist robot manipulation through open-source innovation and practical deployment strategies:

1. **Develop and Release an Open-Source Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Model:** The primary objective is to introduce OpenVLA, a 7B-parameter, fully open-source VLA. This directly counters the closed nature of existing state-of-the-art VLAs (e.g., RT-2-X), which have limited accessibility and transparency. The motivation here is to democratize VLA research, enabling the broader academic and industry community to inspect, reproduce, build upon, and contribute to these models, fostering a collaborative ecosystem akin to the success seen in open-source LLMs.

2. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance in Generalist Robot Manipulation:** A key objective is to demonstrate that an open-source VLA can match or even surpass the performance of larger, closed models. The authors specifically aim to set a new state of the art for generalist robot manipulation policies, challenging the notion that superior performance requires proprietary models or significantly larger parameter counts. This motivates proving the efficacy of their architectural choices and training methodology.

3. **Demonstrate Multi-Robot Control Capabilities Out-of-the-Box:** OpenVLA is designed to be a "generalist" policy capable of controlling multiple robot embodiments without specific per-robot fine-tuning. This objective aims to showcase the cross-embodiment generalization inherent in the model, making it a versatile foundation for diverse robotic platforms.

4. **Explore and Validate Efficient Fine-Tuning Methodologies:** A significant motivation stems from the lack of established best practices for adapting VLAs to new tasks and robot setups. The authors aim to investigate and demonstrate methods for efficiently fine-tuning OpenVLA, particularly focusing on parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and quantization techniques, to enable adaptation on commodity hardware (e.g., consumer-grade GPUs). This objective is crucial for the widespread practical adoption and deployment of VLA models in varied real-world scenarios.

5. **Leverage Pretrained Vision and Language Foundation Models Effectively:** The motivation is to fully capitalize on the powerful priors learned from Internet-scale vision-language data by existing foundation models. By building OpenVLA on a Llama 2 language model combined with fused DINOv2 and SigLIP visual encoders, the authors aim to transfer and adapt this vast knowledge to robotic control, thereby improving generalization to novel objects, scenes, and tasks beyond the robot's specific training data.

In essence, the project is driven by a vision of accessible, high-performing, and adaptable generalist robot AI, breaking down barriers to entry and accelerating progress in robotic manipulation research and application.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OpenVLA's methodology centers on fine-tuning a powerful, pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) for the task of robot action prediction, leveraging a large and diverse dataset of real-world robot demonstrations.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The OpenVLA model, a 7B-parameter architecture, is built upon the **Prismatic-7B VLM** as its backbone. This VLM design follows a common pattern:
- **Visual Encoder:** A 600M-parameter visual encoder is responsible for processing image inputs. A key design choice here is the **fusion of two pretrained vision models: DINOv2** for low-level spatial information and **SigLIP** for higher-level semantics. Input image patches are passed through both encoders, and the resulting feature vectors are concatenated channel-wise. This multi-resolution approach is hypothesized to improve spatial reasoning, crucial for precise robot control.
- **Projector:** A small 2-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) projector maps the visual features from the encoder into the input embedding space of the language model.
- **Large Language Model (LLM) Backbone:** The core of the model is a **Llama 2 7B-parameter** LLM.

#### 3.2. Training Procedure

The training of OpenVLA involves fine-tuning the pretrained Prismatic-7B VLM for robot action prediction, formulated as a "vision-language" task:
- **Action Representation:** Continuous robot actions (7-dimensional, likely 6DoF end-effector pose plus gripper state) are discretized into tokens. Each dimension is discretized into 256 uniform bins, spanning the 1st to 99th percentile of action values in the training data (a refinement over min-max bounds to ignore outliers).
- **Tokenization:** These discrete action values are represented as tokens. Since the Llama tokenizer has limited "special tokens" for new vocabulary, the authors overwrite the 256 least-used tokens in Llama's vocabulary with these action tokens.
- **Objective Function:** OpenVLA is trained with a standard **next-token prediction objective**, specifically minimizing the cross-entropy loss only on the predicted action tokens. This allows the VLM's language modeling capabilities to be repurposed for action generation.
- **Data Curations:** The model is trained on **970k real-world robot demonstrations from the Open X-Embodiment dataset**. This large and diverse dataset spans a wide range of robot embodiments, tasks, and scenes. To ensure training practicality and balance, the authors applied several curation steps:
 - Restriction to manipulation datasets with at least one 3rd-person camera and single-arm end-effector control.
 - Balanced mixture of embodiments, tasks, and scenes, leveraging heuristic data mixture weights from the Octo project.
 - Crucially, they filtered out "all-zero" (no-op) actions from certain datasets (e.g., BridgeData V2's first timestep actions) to prevent the policy from freezing.
- **Training Scale:** The final OpenVLA model was trained for **27 epochs** (significantly more than typical LLM/VLM training) on a cluster of 64 A100 GPUs for 14 days, accumulating approximately **21,500 A100-hours** of compute. A fixed learning rate of 2e-5 was used.

#### 3.3. Key Design Decisions and Learnings (from smaller-scale ablations)

The authors conducted preliminary experiments on smaller datasets (BridgeData V2) to inform key design choices:
- **VLM Backbone Choice:** Prismatic-7B was chosen over IDEFICS-1 and LLaVA due to its superior language grounding and spatial reasoning abilities, attributed to its fused SigLIP-DinoV2 visual encoder, and its modular codebase.
- **Image Resolution:** 224x224px was selected as it showed no performance difference compared to 384x384px but significantly reduced training compute (3x faster).
- **Fine-tuning Vision Encoder:** Contrary to common VLM practices where freezing the vision encoder often helps preserve pretraining knowledge, the authors found that **fine-tuning the vision encoder during VLA training was crucial** for good performance. They hypothesize this is because the pretrained vision backbone might lack the fine-grained spatial details needed for precise robotic control.
- **Training Epochs and Learning Rate:** Extensive experimentation led to the conclusion that a high number of epochs (27) and a fixed learning rate (2e-5) were optimal for achieving high action token accuracy and real robot performance.

#### 3.4. Infrastructure for Training and Inference

The OpenVLA codebase is built on PyTorch, supporting scalable training techniques like Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP), FlashAttention, and Fully Sharded Data Parallelism (FSDP). For inference, the model requires 15GB GPU memory in `bfloat16` precision and runs at ~6Hz on an NVIDIA RTX 4090. They also implemented a remote VLA inference server for real-time action streaming to robots.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OpenVLA's experimental evaluation demonstrates its strong performance as a generalist robot policy and its adaptability to new tasks and robot setups.

#### 4.1. Direct Evaluations on Multiple Robot Platforms (Out-of-the-Box Performance)

- **Superior Generalist Performance:** OpenVLA (7B parameters) set a new state of the art for generalist robot manipulation, outperforming the much larger, closed-source RT-2-X (55B parameters) by **16.5% absolute success rate** across 29 tasks on WidowX and Google Robot embodiments. This translates to significantly better performance with 7x fewer parameters.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** OpenVLA (and RT-2-X) exhibited markedly more robust behaviors than RT-1-X and Octo, including approaching correct objects with distractors, proper end-effector orientation, and recovery from insecure grasps.
- **Specific Strengths:** OpenVLA performed comparably to or better than RT-2-X in most task categories (visual, motion, physical generalization, and language grounding), particularly on the BridgeData V2 evaluations. While RT-2-X showed slightly higher performance in *semantic generalization* tasks (attributed to its larger Internet pretraining data and co-fine-tuning), OpenVLA's overall strong performance is attributed to its larger and more diverse robot training dataset (970k vs. 350k for RT-2-X), careful data cleaning (filtering all-zero actions in BridgeData V2), and the novel fused SigLIP-DinoV2 vision encoder.

#### 4.2. Data-Efficient Adaptation to New Robot Setups (Fine-Tuning)

- **Effective Adaptation:** OpenVLA can be effectively fine-tuned on new real-world robot setups (Franka-Tabletop and Franka-DROID) using relatively small datasets (10-150 demonstrations).
- **Outperforming Imitation Learning on Diverse Tasks:** Fine-tuned OpenVLA policies clearly outperformed from-scratch imitation learning methods like Diffusion Policy by **20.4%** on diverse multi-instruction tasks involving multiple objects and language grounding.
- **Task-Specific Performance:** While Diffusion Policy remained competitive or superior on *narrow single-instruction tasks* (e.g., "Put Carrot in Bowl"), OpenVLA excelled in more complex scenarios where language grounding and handling distractors were crucial.
- **Consistent Reliability:** OpenVLA was the only approach to achieve at least 50% success rate across all tested fine-tuning tasks, suggesting it is a robust default option for learning new skills, especially those with diverse language instructions.
- **Benefit of Large-Scale Robot Pretraining:** Ablation studies showed that the large-scale OpenX pretraining was crucial for OpenVLA's adaptation capabilities; direct fine-tuning of the base Prismatic VLM (OpenVLA (scratch)) yielded significantly lower performance.

#### 4.3. Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)

- **LoRA's Effectiveness:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) proved highly effective, matching full fine-tuning performance (68.2% vs. 69.7% success rate) while training only **1.4% of the model parameters**.
- **Compute Reduction:** This enabled fine-tuning OpenVLA on a *single A100 GPU* in 10-15 hours, an 8x reduction in compute compared to full fine-tuning (which required 8 A100 GPUs).
- **Vision Encoder Adaptation is Key:** Ablations showed that fine-tuning only the last layer or freezing the vision encoder led to poor performance, underscoring the importance of adapting the visual features.

#### 4.4. Memory-Efficient Inference via Quantization

- **4-bit Quantization's Success:** 4-bit quantization matched the performance of `bfloat16` half-precision inference (71.9% vs. 71.3% success) while reducing the GPU memory footprint by more than half (7.0 GB vs. 16.8 GB).
- **Performance vs. Speed Trade-offs:** 8-bit quantization showed a performance decrease in non-blocking control due to lower inference speed. However, under blocking control (where inference speed is decoupled from task dynamics), 8-bit performance was comparable to `bfloat16` and 4-bit, confirming that inference speed was the bottleneck, not intrinsic accuracy loss.
- **Consumer GPU Compatibility:** OpenVLA can be served efficiently on consumer-grade GPUs like the NVIDIA RTX 4090 at approximately 6Hz, demonstrating practical deployment capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OpenVLA project carries significant implications for the future of robotics research and practical applications:

1. **Democratization of Robotics AI:** By fully open-sourcing the 7B-parameter OpenVLA model, its training code, and fine-tuning notebooks, the authors are directly addressing the critical barrier of accessibility in advanced robotics AI. This move is akin to the impact of open-source large language models (LLMs) on NLP research. It allows a much broader community of researchers, startups, and developers, even those without access to multi-billion dollar compute infrastructure, to experiment with, modify, and build upon state-of-the-art VLA capabilities. This democratizes innovation and accelerates progress in ways that closed-source models cannot.

2. **Establishment of an Open VLA Ecosystem:** The release of OpenVLA, alongside its comprehensive codebase and HuggingFace integration, is poised to catalyze the creation of a vibrant open-source ecosystem for VLA models. This could lead to collaborative efforts on data collection, model improvements, novel applications, and standardized benchmarks, similar to how the open-source LLM community has flourished.

3. **New Benchmark for Generalist Robot Policies:** OpenVLA's ability to outperform a much larger, closed-source model like RT-2-X (55B parameters) with 7x fewer parameters fundamentally shifts the landscape of what's achievable with more accessible models. This sets a new, more attainable benchmark for generalist robot manipulation policies and highlights the effectiveness of sophisticated architectural choices and careful data curation.

4. **Enabling Practical Robot Deployment:** The thorough investigation into efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) and memory-efficient inference (4-bit quantization) is crucial for translating theoretical advancements into practical applications. The demonstrated ability to fine-tune on consumer-grade GPUs and deploy models with significantly reduced memory footprints makes VLA models viable for a wider range of real-world robotic systems, including those with limited onboard compute or budget constraints. This paves the way for more widespread adoption in industrial, service, and even domestic robotics.

5. **Accelerating Future Research:** The authors explicitly state their hope that OpenVLA will enable the community to jointly investigate underexplored VLA design questions, such as the optimal size of the base VLM, the benefits of co-training with Internet-scale data, and the best visual features for robotics. The open-source nature facilitates ablation studies, comparative analyses, and the rapid iteration necessary for such fundamental research.

While acknowledging limitations such as reliance on single-image observations and current inference throughput not yet suitable for very high-frequency control (e.g., 50Hz), OpenVLA provides a robust foundation. These identified limitations also clearly delineate promising avenues for future research, which the open-source release is specifically designed to facilitate. In summary, OpenVLA represents a pivotal step towards accessible, high-performance, and adaptable generalist robots, fostering an open collaborative future for robotics AI.
