# Wow, wo, val! A Comprehensive Embodied World Model Evaluation Turing Test

> [!summary] Summary
> WoW-World-Eval introduces a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating embodied world models across perception, planning, prediction, generalization, and execution. It reveals that current video generation models, despite visual fidelity, significantly underperform in physical plausibility and real-world actionability when tested with an Inverse Dynamics Model.

## Problem

- Existing video generation benchmarks for world models focus primarily on visual fidelity or coarse task success, often overlooking the specific requirements for robotic applications.
- Current evaluation frameworks lack explicit assessment of crucial embodied dimensions such as physical plausibility, long-horizon planning, and direct actionability in real-world contexts.
- A gap exists in determining if video foundation models are robust and expressive enough to serve as universal priors for real-world embodied agents beyond just visual appeal.

## Method

![[2601.04137-method.png]]
*WoW-World-Eval overview: multi-faceted metrics suite across five dimensions (Video Quality, Instruction Understanding, Planning Reasoning, Physical Law, Execution Accuracy) aligned with five core embodied-world-model abilities (Perception, Planning, Prediction, Execution, Generalization).*

- Researchers developed **WoW-World-Eval**, a benchmark comprehensively assessing an embodied world model's capabilities across ==five core dimensions==: Perception, Planning, Prediction, Generalization, and Execution.
- The framework introduces a protocol of ==22 specific metrics==, designed to capture nuanced demands like physical consistency, causal reasoning, and planning rationality beyond conventional video quality scores.
- The methodology includes both a ==Human Turing Test== using a ==two-alternative forced-choice (2AFC)== method and an ==Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) Turing Test== to evaluate real-world action executability.

## Results

- Top models like **Hailuo (52.55)** and **WoW-cosmos2 (50.74)** achieved relatively balanced overall scores, but all models showed **planning reasoning** as their weakest area, with very low scores.
- A strong correlation (**Pearson r=0.93, Spearman ρ=0.91**) was found between WoW-World-Eval overall scores and human preference judgments, validating the benchmark's alignment with human perception.
- The IDM Turing Test revealed most high-scoring models (e.g., **Kling 9.88%, Hailuo 2.47%**) largely failed to produce executable actions, while models trained with real-robot data (e.g., **WoW-wan 40.74%, WoW-cosmos2 18.52%**) performed significantly better, exposing a critical gap in real-world actionability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Planning Reasoning emerged as a significant bottleneck for all evaluated world models, exhibiting consistently low scores and limited capabilities in long-horizon planning and structured task decomposition.
> - The Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) Turing Test demonstrated a substantial gap: visually realistic generated videos often fail when translated into real-world robotic actions, highlighting insufficient physical grounding.
> - Dense prompts consistently improved video quality, instruction understanding, and physical law adherence for models, yet provided minimal impact on fundamental long-horizon planning capabilities.
