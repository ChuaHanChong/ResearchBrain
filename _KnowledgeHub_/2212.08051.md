# Objaverse: A Universe of Annotated 3D Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> Objaverse introduces an unprecedented collection of 818,000 diverse, annotated 3D objects, which is 16 times larger than previous general-purpose 3D datasets, addressing the critical data gap in 3D AI. The dataset's scale and rich metadata enable advancements in 3D generative modeling, 2D instance segmentation through data augmentation, and open-vocabulary object navigation for embodied AI.

## Problem

- The development of advanced 3D AI models was hindered by a scarcity of large-scale, diverse 3D datasets, especially when compared to 2D vision and NLP.
- Existing 3D datasets like ShapeNet were limited in size (51,000 objects), diversity (55 categories), and often lacked rich annotations such as text descriptions.
- Specialized 3D datasets were typically small and domain-specific, impeding progress in general-purpose 3D generative modeling, robotics, and embodied AI.

## Method

- Researchers introduced **Objaverse**, a dataset comprising 818,000 ==annotated 3D objects==, representing a 16x increase in size compared to ShapeNet.
- Data was sourced primarily from Sketchfab under Creative Commons licenses, and enriched with comprehensive ==metadata== including text descriptions, hierarchical categories, and technical attributes.
- A subset, **Objaverse-LVIS**, was created by mapping 47,000 objects to 1,156 LVIS categories using ==CLIP-based classification== and human verification to bridge 3D and 2D computer vision.

## Results

- Training **GET3D** on Objaverse produced 3D objects with greater diversity and realism compared to models trained on ShapeNet, confirmed by crowdsourced human evaluations.
- The **3D Copy-Paste (3DCP)** data augmentation, utilizing Objaverse objects, improved **Mask R-CNN** performance on rare object categories in 2D instance segmentation.
- Objaverse facilitated the creation of procedurally generated environments for open-vocabulary object navigation, enabling agents to find objects specified by arbitrary text descriptions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, diverse 3D datasets can significantly improve the performance and diversity of 3D generative models and enhance 2D instance segmentation, particularly for rare categories.
> - Integrating 3D data enables open-vocabulary object navigation for embodied AI agents and facilitates comprehensive robustness analysis of vision models against viewpoint variations.
> - The power-law distribution of semantic tags in the dataset reflects the long-tail nature of real-world object categories, underscoring the need for large-scale, diverse data to cover this spectrum.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

The field of artificial intelligence has seen remarkable progress in domains like natural language processing and 2D computer vision, fueled by the availability of massive datasets such as WebText and ImageNet. However, 3D vision and modeling have lagged behind largely due to a scarcity of large-scale, diverse 3D datasets. To address this gap, researchers have introduced Objaverse, an unprecedented collection of 818,000 annotated 3D objects that represents a significant leap forward in scale and diversity compared to existing datasets.

Objaverse stands as the largest annotated 3D object dataset available, containing over 818,000 objects spanning 21,000 categories. This represents a 16× increase in size compared to ShapeNet, previously the largest general-purpose 3D dataset with approximately 51,000 objects. Beyond raw numbers, Objaverse offers remarkable diversity in object categories, visual styles, and complexity, making it a rich resource for training robust AI models that can understand and interact with the three-dimensional world.

### 2. The Data Gap in 3D AI

The development of advanced AI models is heavily dependent on the availability of large-scale, diverse datasets. While domains like natural language processing and 2D computer vision have benefited from massive datasets containing billions of examples, 3D datasets have remained comparatively small and limited in scope.

Prior to Objaverse, the landscape of 3D datasets was fragmented and limited:

- **ShapeNet**: With 51,000 objects across 55 categories, ShapeNet was previously the largest general-purpose 3D dataset but limited in diversity.
- **Smaller specialized datasets**: Many datasets focused on specific domains (e.g., YCB for robotics with just 77 objects, IKEA with 219 objects) or contained primarily synthetic objects lacking realism.
- **Limited annotations**: Few datasets paired 3D objects with rich textual descriptions or categorical information.

This scarcity of comprehensive 3D data has impeded progress in various applications, including 3D generative modeling, robotics, augmented reality, and embodied AI. Objaverse directly addresses this challenge by providing a dataset of unprecedented scale and diversity.

### 3. Dataset Construction

The creation of Objaverse involved a systematic approach to data collection, processing, and annotation:

#### 3.1. Data Sourcing and Licensing

The researchers sourced 3D models primarily from Sketchfab, a popular online platform hosting millions of 3D models. They focused exclusively on models released under Creative Commons licenses to ensure legal redistributability while respecting creators' rights. Models with inappropriate or adult content were excluded, resulting in a clean, redistributable dataset suitable for research purposes.

#### 3.2. Metadata Extraction and Organization

Each 3D model in Objaverse comes with rich metadata:

- **Text descriptions**: Natural language descriptions of the objects, helpful for language-based retrieval and generation tasks.
- **Categories and tags**: Hierarchical categorization and keyword tags that enable efficient organization and filtering.
- **Technical attributes**: Information about model complexity (vertex count, face count), material properties, and animation capabilities.

The distribution of tags follows a characteristic power law, with some common tags (like "furniture," "car," and "art") appearing thousands of times, while many specialized tags appear only a few times, reflecting the long-tail nature of real-world objects.

#### 3.3. Objaverse-LVIS Construction

A key contribution is the Objaverse-LVIS subset, which maps 47,000 Objaverse objects to 1,156 categories from the LVIS (Large Vocabulary Instance Segmentation) dataset. This mapping was achieved through a two-step process:

1. **CLIP-based initial classification**: Using CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) to match rendered views of 3D objects with LVIS category names.
2. **Human verification**: Crowdworkers verified and refined these classifications through a specialized interface.

This careful mapping enables Objaverse to serve as a bridge between 3D data and the rich ecosystem of 2D computer vision research built around datasets like LVIS and COCO.

### 4. Dataset Characteristics

Objaverse stands out not just for its scale but also for its remarkable diversity and quality of content:

#### 4.1. Diversity of Content

The dataset encompasses objects across numerous domains:
- 77,000 architectural models
- 62,000 characters and creatures
- 52,000 furniture and home items
- 51,000 art pieces
- 35,000 weapons and military items
- 29,000 electronic devices
- 28,000 nature and plant models
- And many more categories

#### 4.2. Diversity of Model Types and Styles

Objaverse contains a wide range of model types:
- **Photorealistic scans**: Highly detailed models created through photogrammetry
- **Stylized models**: Cartoon, abstract, and stylistically diverse representations
- **Animated objects**: Models with animation capabilities
- **Rigged characters**: Characters with skeletons that can be posed
- **Multi-part assemblies**: Complex objects with separable components
- **Interior and exterior scenes**: Complete environments rather than just individual objects

This diversity makes Objaverse valuable not just for training general 3D understanding models but also for specialized applications in gaming, animation, virtual reality, and architectural visualization.

#### 4.3. Quality and Technical Characteristics

The models in Objaverse exhibit varying levels of complexity and technical characteristics:
- **Geometric complexity**: From simple objects with few hundred vertices to intricate models with millions of vertices
- **Material properties**: Many models include PBR (Physically Based Rendering) materials that realistically interact with light
- **Textures**: High-resolution textures providing detailed surface appearance
- **UV mapping**: Professional-quality UV unwrapping for texture application

### 5. Applications and Demonstrations

The paper demonstrates Objaverse's utility across four application areas:

#### 5.1. 3D Generative Modeling

The researchers showed that training GET3D (a state-of-the-art 3D generative model) on Objaverse data produces more diverse and realistic 3D objects compared to training on ShapeNet. This was verified through both qualitative assessments and crowdsourced human evaluations.

The greater diversity and realism of Objaverse-trained models illustrate how larger, more diverse training data directly translates to better generative capabilities.

#### 5.2. Instance Segmentation

The researchers introduced 3D Copy-Paste (3DCP), a novel data augmentation technique that uses Objaverse objects to improve 2D instance segmentation models. By rendering Objaverse objects and compositing them into LVIS training images, they improved the performance of Mask R-CNN models on rare object categories.

This technique demonstrated how 3D data can enhance 2D computer vision tasks, particularly for rare object categories where collecting real-world examples is challenging.

#### 5.3. Open-Vocabulary Object Navigation

Objaverse enables more realistic and diverse training environments for embodied AI agents. The researchers created procedurally generated rooms populated with Objaverse objects and trained agents to navigate to objects specified by text descriptions.

Using CLIP-based visual and textual encodings, the system allowed for open-vocabulary navigation, where agents could seek objects specified by arbitrary text descriptions rather than just predefined categories.

#### 5.4. Robustness Analysis

Objaverse enabled the creation of benchmarks for evaluating the robustness of vision models to changes in object viewpoint. The researchers found that standard vision models show significant performance drops when classifying objects from non-canonical viewpoints.

This application highlights how 3D data can reveal and potentially address robustness issues in current computer vision systems.

### 6. Impact and Future Directions

Objaverse represents a significant advancement for AI research in several ways:

#### 6.1. Democratizing 3D AI Research

By providing free access to a large, diverse 3D dataset, Objaverse democratizes research in 3D AI, allowing smaller research groups and independent researchers to work on problems that previously required proprietary or difficult-to-access data.

#### 6.2. Enabling New Research Directions

The scale and diversity of Objaverse opens up new research directions:
- **Large-scale 3D foundation models**: Similar to how large datasets enabled foundation models in NLP and 2D vision
- **3D-2D cross-modal learning**: Leveraging 3D data to improve 2D vision and vice versa
- **Domain adaptation**: Studying how to transfer knowledge between synthetic and real-world domains
- **Long-tail learning**: Developing methods that can handle rare object categories and unusual instances

#### 6.3. Ethical Considerations and Limitations

The researchers acknowledge certain limitations and ethical considerations:
- **Representation biases**: The dataset may reflect biases in what content creators choose to model
- **Content gaps**: Some domains may be underrepresented due to the source platform's user demographics
- **Quality variation**: Not all models meet the same quality standards, with varying levels of detail and realism

#### 6.4. Data Collection Interface

The dataset creation process included custom annotation interfaces for human verification and categorization of objects.

### 7. Conclusion

Objaverse represents a significant milestone in 3D dataset development, providing the AI research community with an unprecedented resource of 818,000 diverse, annotated 3D objects. By addressing the critical data gap in 3D AI, it opens the door to rapid advances in 3D vision, generative modeling, robotics, and embodied AI.

The dataset's scale, diversity, and rich annotations make it valuable for a wide range of applications, from training generative models to improving instance segmentation and enabling more realistic embodied AI environments. The demonstrated applications only scratch the surface of what's possible with this resource.

As AI continues to extend its capabilities into the three-dimensional world, datasets like Objaverse will play an increasingly important role in bridging the gap between digital systems and physical reality. The dataset's release under open licenses ensures that the broader research community can build upon this foundation, potentially accelerating progress toward more capable and generalized artificial intelligence systems that can understand and interact with our three-dimensional world.
