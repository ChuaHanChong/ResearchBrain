# A Survey of Self-Evolving Agents: What, When, How, and Where to Evolve on the Path to Artificial Super Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper from a global collaboration of institutions introduces a comprehensive framework for self-evolving agents, unifying research around the "What, When, How, and Where to Evolve" dimensions. It identifies critical evaluation gaps in adaptivity, retention, generalization, efficiency, and safety, while also outlining emergent risks and mitigation strategies for these dynamic AI systems.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) are static and cannot adapt their internal parameters or reasoning processes in real-time to novel tasks, evolving knowledge, or dynamic environments.
- The lack of a unified theoretical framework for self-evolving agents leads to fragmented research and inconsistent terminology.
- Existing evaluation benchmarks and metrics are insufficient for assessing continuous learning, long-term adaptivity, and safety in dynamic AI systems.

## Method

- The paper provides the first systematic and comprehensive review of self-evolving agents, establishing a structured framework around four core questions: =="What to Evolve?"==, =="When to Evolve?"==, =="How to Evolve?"==, and =="Where to Evolve?"==.
- It critically analyzes existing ==evaluation methodologies==, proposing five core evaluation goals (==Adaptivity==, ==Retention==, ==Generalization==, ==Efficiency==, ==Safety==) and advocating for ==standardized, longitudinal protocols==.
- The survey identifies and categorizes ==emergent risks== unique to self-evolving agents, such as ==uncontrolled behavior drift== and ==deployment-time reward hacking==, along with prescriptive guardrails and mitigation strategies.

## Results

- The survey presents a unified taxonomy for self-evolving agents, categorizing evolutionary mechanisms by *what* (models, context, tools, architecture), *when* (intra-test-time, inter-test-time), and *how* (reward-based, imitation, population-based methods) they evolve.
- It highlights significant gaps in current evaluation, particularly regarding retention and long-horizon safety, and proposes new evaluation goals and standardized protocols for measuring adaptivity, generalization, and efficiency.
- The paper outlines critical future research directions, including personalization in open-ended environments, achieving robust generalization while avoiding catastrophic forgetting, and ensuring safety, control, and governance in increasingly autonomous multi-agent ecosystems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-evolution encompasses changes across an agent's entire system, including models, context (memory/prompts), tools (acquisition/mastery), and even architectural designs (single/multi-agent).
> - Evolutionary processes occur both intra-test-time (real-time, task-specific adaptation) and inter-test-time (retrospective, generalized learning between tasks), leveraging various learning paradigms.
> - The development of truly adaptive and safe self-evolving agents requires a fundamental shift in evaluation, moving beyond episodic metrics to assess long-term adaptivity, catastrophic forgetting, generalizability under distribution shifts, resource efficiency, and evolving safety alignment.
