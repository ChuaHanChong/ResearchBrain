# A Survey of Self-Evolving Agents: What, When, How, and Where to Evolve on the Path to Artificial Super Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper from a global collaboration of institutions introduces a comprehensive framework for self-evolving agents, unifying research around the "What, When, How, and Where to Evolve" dimensions. It identifies critical evaluation gaps in adaptivity, retention, generalization, efficiency, and safety, while also outlining emergent risks and mitigation strategies for these dynamic AI systems.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) are static and cannot adapt their internal parameters or reasoning processes in real-time to novel tasks, evolving knowledge, or dynamic environments.
- The lack of a unified theoretical framework for self-evolving agents leads to fragmented research and inconsistent terminology.
- Existing evaluation benchmarks and metrics are insufficient for assessing continuous learning, long-term adaptivity, and safety in dynamic AI systems.

## Method

- The paper provides the first systematic and comprehensive review of self-evolving agents, establishing a structured framework around four core questions: =="What to Evolve?"==, =="When to Evolve?"==, =="How to Evolve?"==, and =="Where to Evolve?"==.
- It critically analyzes existing ==evaluation methodologies==, proposing five core evaluation goals (==Adaptivity==, ==Retention==, ==Generalization==, ==Efficiency==, ==Safety==) and advocating for ==standardized, longitudinal protocols==.
- The survey identifies and categorizes ==emergent risks== unique to self-evolving agents, such as ==uncontrolled behavior drift== and ==deployment-time reward hacking==, along with prescriptive guardrails and mitigation strategies.

## Results

- The survey presents a unified taxonomy for self-evolving agents, categorizing evolutionary mechanisms by *what* (models, context, tools, architecture), *when* (intra-test-time, inter-test-time), and *how* (reward-based, imitation, population-based methods) they evolve.
- It highlights significant gaps in current evaluation, particularly regarding retention and long-horizon safety, and proposes new evaluation goals and standardized protocols for measuring adaptivity, generalization, and efficiency.
- The paper outlines critical future research directions, including personalization in open-ended environments, achieving robust generalization while avoiding catastrophic forgetting, and ensuring safety, control, and governance in increasingly autonomous multi-agent ecosystems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-evolution encompasses changes across an agent's entire system, including models, context (memory/prompts), tools (acquisition/mastery), and even architectural designs (single/multi-agent).
> - Evolutionary processes occur both intra-test-time (real-time, task-specific adaptation) and inter-test-time (retrospective, generalized learning between tasks), leveraging various learning paradigms.
> - The development of truly adaptive and safe self-evolving agents requires a fundamental shift in evaluation, moving beyond episodic metrics to assess long-term adaptivity, catastrophic forgetting, generalizability under distribution shifts, resource efficiency, and evolving safety alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey positions itself at a critical juncture in the evolution of artificial intelligence, marking a paradigm shift from static large language models (LLMs) to dynamic, self-evolving agents. While LLMs have demonstrated "remarkable capabilities across diverse tasks," they fundamentally remain static, unable to adapt their internal parameters or reasoning processes in real-time to novel tasks, evolving knowledge domains, or dynamic interaction contexts. This static nature is identified as a "critical bottleneck" as LLMs are increasingly deployed in open-ended, interactive environments.

The paper argues for a conceptual shift: "from scaling static models to developing self-evolving agents." This shift is presented not merely as an incremental improvement but as a transformative path toward Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI). The authors explicitly illustrate this trajectory as a progression from "LLMs" to "Foundation Agents," then to "Self-evolving Agents," and ultimately towards the hypothetical "ASI" (Figure 1).

A key contribution of this survey is to fill a significant gap in the existing literature. The authors note that previous surveys predominantly addressed agent evolution as a subsidiary component within broader agent taxonomies, offering limited coverage of dedicated self-evolving mechanisms across the entire agent system. Specifically, they distinguish their work from related learning paradigms:
- **Curriculum Learning:** This paradigm, focused on training data ordering, operates on static datasets and updates only model parameters, contrasting with self-evolving agents' dynamic environments and ability to adjust non-parametric components like memory and tools.
- **Lifelong Learning (Continual/Incremental Learning):** While sharing a sequential task setting, lifelong learning primarily uses memory for training-time parameter optimization and often involves passive knowledge acquisition. Self-evolving agents, conversely, leverage runtime context for test-time state adaptation and actively explore, reflect, and self-initiate learning. They encompass "system-wide evolution including tool acquisition, architectural reconfiguration, and environmental exploration."
- **Model Editing and Unlearning:** These focus on targeted, localized parameter modifications. They cannot modify non-parametric components and rely on pre-defined pipelines, unlike the spontaneous and flexible strategies of self-evolving agents.

By providing the "first systematic and comprehensive review of self-evolving agents" as a "first-class research paradigm," this work aims to unify the heterogeneous mechanisms emerging in the community into a coherent framework. It addresses fundamental questions about *what*, *when*, *how*, and *where* evolution should occur, thereby establishing a foundational roadmap for a field that is still rapidly forming and negotiating its conceptual boundaries.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the inherent limitations of static Large Language Models (LLMs) when deployed in complex, dynamic, and open-ended environments. Despite their advanced capabilities, LLMs are fundamentally unable to adapt their internal parameters, knowledge, or strategies in real-time, leading to a "critical bottleneck" for truly autonomous and intelligent systems. The paper argues for a "paradigm shift" toward developing agents that can "adaptively reason, act, and evolve in real time." This capability is deemed essential for creating systems that are "more robust, versatile, and capable of tackling complex, dynamic real-world problems," and is seen as a crucial step towards the realization of Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI).

The key objectives of this comprehensive survey are articulated through its contributions:

1. **Establish a Unified Theoretical Framework:** The core objective is to provide a structured and systematic understanding of self-evolutionary processes in agent systems. This is achieved by organizing the field around three fundamental, underexplored questions: "what to evolve" (agent components like models, memory, tools, architecture), "when to evolve" (temporal stages of adaptation like intra-test-time and inter-test-time), and "how to evolve" (algorithmic designs and feedback mechanisms such as reward-based, imitation, and population-based methods). This framework aims to offer clear design guidance for future self-evolving agentic systems.
2. **Investigate Evaluation Benchmarks and Metrics:** The survey aims to analyze existing and emerging evaluation methodologies tailored for self-evolving agents. This includes highlighting metrics and benchmarks that can adequately capture aspects such as adaptivity, robustness, generalization, efficiency, and safety, which are critical for systems capable of continuous learning and evolution.
3. **Showcase Real-World Applications:** Another objective is to illustrate the practical potential of self-evolving agents by examining their emerging applications across various domains. Examples include autonomous software engineering, personalized education, healthcare, and intelligent virtual assistance, demonstrating where continual adaptation is essential.
4. **Identify Critical Open Challenges and Future Research Directions:** The paper seeks to provide a roadmap for future research by outlining persistent challenges and promising avenues. This includes emphasizing crucial aspects like safety, personalization, multi-agent co-evolution, and scalability, guiding the community toward impactful future work.

In essence, the survey is motivated by the recognized need for truly adaptive AI and aims to provide a structured, comprehensive, and forward-looking synthesis of the nascent field of self-evolving agents, thereby accelerating its theoretical and practical advancement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey, the methodology of this paper is primarily based on an extensive and systematic review, synthesis, and categorization of existing literature in the rapidly evolving field of self-evolving agents. The authors do not present new experimental results but rather consolidate the fragmented research landscape into a coherent, structured framework.

The core of their approach is built around addressing four foundational questions, which also serve as the primary organizing dimensions of the survey: "What to Evolve?", "When to Evolve?", "How to Evolve?", and "Where to Evolve?".

1. **What to Evolve?** This dimension systematically examines the specific components within an agent system that can undergo autonomous modification. The authors decompose agents into four major pillars:
 - **Models:** Investigating how agent parameters can be refined through self-generated supervision, environmental interaction, and feedback (e.g., policy, experience).
 - **Context:** Focusing on how an agent's working memory and prompts can evolve through reflection, storage, retrieval, and optimization.
 - **Tools:** Analyzing how agents autonomously discover, create, master, and manage their executable skills and capabilities.
 - **Architecture:** Exploring how the agent's internal logic and collaborative structures (for single or multi-agent systems) can be optimized over time.

2. **When to Evolve?** This dimension categorizes evolutionary mechanisms based on their temporal relationship to task execution:
 - **Intra-test-time self-evolution:** Adaptation that occurs in real-time during task execution, often driven by immediate feedback and leveraging in-context learning (ICL), supervised fine-tuning (SFT), or reinforcement learning (RL) for rapid, task-specific improvements.
 - **Inter-test-time self-evolution:** Learning processes that happen between task completions, using accumulated experiences and various learning paradigms (SFT, RL) to improve future performance more generally.

3. **How to Evolve?** This dimension delves into the algorithmic and architectural designs that guide evolutionary adaptation, identifying three major paradigms:
 - **Reward-based Self-Evolution:** This encompasses methods where adaptation is guided by various forms of feedback, including textual critiques, internal confidence scores, explicit external rewards from the environment, or implicit signals.
 - **Imitation and Demonstration Learning:** This involves agents learning by mimicking high-quality behavioral exemplars, which can be self-generated (e.g., successful past trajectories) or provided by other agents.
 - **Population-based and Evolutionary Methods:** Drawing inspiration from biological evolution, this approach involves maintaining and evolving populations of agent variants or collaborating agents through mechanisms like selection, mutation, and crossover.
 The authors also discuss cross-cutting dimensions such as online vs. offline learning, on-policy vs. off-policy learning, and reward granularity (outcome-based vs. process-based vs. hybrid).

4. **Where to Evolve?** This section maps the applications of self-evolving agents across various domains:
 - **General Domain Evolution:** Focusing on broad capability expansion for versatile digital assistants (e.g., through memory mechanisms, model-agent co-evolution, curriculum-driven training).
 - **Specialized Domain Evolution:** Concentrating on enhancing proficiency within specific task areas (e.g., coding, Graphical User Interfaces (GUI), financial tasks, medical applications, education).

Beyond these core dimensions, the methodology includes a dedicated section on **Evaluation of Self-Evolving Agents**. Here, the authors categorize evaluation goals into Adaptivity, Retention, Generalization, Efficiency, and Safety, discussing relevant metrics and analyzing the coverage gaps in existing benchmarks. They also propose **Standardized Evaluation Protocols** for both short-horizon and long-horizon assessments, emphasizing the need for longitudinal, cost-aware, and transparent reporting. Finally, the paper concludes by outlining **Future Directions**, addressing personalization, generalization, safety, and the development of multi-agent ecosystems.

The authors extensively use figures (e.g., comprehensive taxonomies, evolutionary landscapes) and tables (e.g., comparison of paradigms, benchmark mapping, compliance checklists) to visually structure the vast amount of information and facilitate understanding. This systematic approach allows the survey to provide a cohesive understanding of a complex and rapidly developing research area.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a comprehensive survey, the paper does not present new empirical findings but rather synthesizes the current state of research, identifies key trends, conceptual gaps, and outlines future directions for the field of self-evolving agents. The main "findings" are therefore in the form of a structured understanding of this nascent field and the identification of its critical challenges and opportunities.

The core results of the survey can be summarized as follows:

1. **Unified Framework for Self-Evolution:** The paper's most significant "finding" is the establishment of a novel, comprehensive, and systematic framework for understanding self-evolving agents, organized around the dimensions of "What to evolve," "When to evolve," "How to evolve," and "Where to evolve." This framework unifies fragmented research efforts and provides a common language for analyzing and designing such systems.
 - **What:** The survey reveals that evolution is not limited to model parameters but extends across an agent's entire system, including its internal **models** (policy, experience), **context** (memory, prompts), **tools** (creation, mastery, selection), and even its overarching **architecture** (single-agent, multi-agent designs).
 - **When:** Adaptation occurs through both **intra-test-time** (real-time, immediate, task-specific) and **inter-test-time** (retrospective, between tasks, generalized improvement) mechanisms, leveraging in-context learning (ICL), supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms.
 - **How:** Self-evolution is driven by three main methodological families: **Reward-based** learning (using textual, internal, external, or implicit signals), **Imitation & Demonstration Learning** (from self-generated or cross-agent exemplars), and **Population-based & Evolutionary Methods** (for exploring diverse agent variants or multi-agent ecosystems).
 - **Where:** Self-evolving agents are applicable across **general domains** (e.g., through memory optimization, model-agent co-evolution, curriculum learning) and a wide array of **specialized domains** (e.g., coding, GUI automation, finance, medicine, education).

2. **Critical Evaluation Gaps and Proposed Metrics:** The survey identifies significant deficiencies in current evaluation practices for self-evolving agents. It proposes five core evaluation goals—**Adaptivity, Retention, Generalization, Efficiency, and Safety**—and highlights that existing benchmarks often fall short in comprehensively assessing these dimensions, particularly for long-term, continuous learning scenarios.
 - **Adaptivity:** While many benchmarks track short-term improvement, they lack robust assessment of autonomous adaptation strategies.
 - **Retention:** This is the most underserved area, as most benchmarks use episodic evaluation that resets agent states, precluding the measurement of catastrophic forgetting or knowledge accumulation over long periods.
 - **Generalization:** Evaluations are often static, failing to track how cross-domain transfer capability changes during extended evolution or under distribution shifts.
 - **Efficiency:** There is inconsistent reporting of evolution costs (tokens, time, memory, human oversight), and benchmarks rarely enforce realistic resource budgets.
 - **Safety:** Current safety evaluations are typically episodic, failing to track long-horizon safety drift or the emergence of unsafe behaviors through autonomous evolution and multi-agent interactions.
 The paper advocates for **standardized evaluation protocols** for both short-horizon and long-horizon assessments, emphasizing the need for longitudinal, cost-aware trajectory views, persistent state tracking, and explicit reporting of self-directedness.

3. **Identification of Emergent Risks and Mitigation Strategies:** The survey explicitly details emergent risks unique to self-evolving systems, distinct from static AI systems. These include:
 - **Uncontrolled behavior drift (misevolution):** Where an agent's goals or safety alignment can degrade over time, leading to unexpected and potentially harmful actions.
 - **Deployment-time reward hacking:** Agents exploiting loopholes in self-defined or internal reward signals, leading to undesirable behaviors.
 - **Safety of self-created and ingested tools:** The risk of agents generating or integrating vulnerable or malicious tools.
 The paper also provides **prescriptive guardrails** and mitigation strategies, such as strict sandboxing, automated security verification, immutable audit trails, tested rollback mechanisms, continuous monitoring, automated red-teaming, approval gates for high-stakes actions, and robust privacy-protection measures.

4. **Challenges in Personalization, Generalization, and Multi-Agent Ecosystems:** The survey outlines critical future research directions, pointing to the inherent difficulties in achieving:
 - **Personalization:** Tackling the cold-start problem, effective long-term memory management, and personalized planning while ensuring fairness and data governance.
 - **Robust Generalization:** Addressing the specialization-vs-adaptability trade-off, cross-domain adaptation without retraining, mitigating catastrophic forgetting, and improving knowledge transferability across agents.
 - **Scalable Multi-Agent Ecosystems:** Balancing individual and collective reasoning, developing efficient collaboration frameworks, and evolving dynamic roles and knowledge within agent teams.

Overall, the main "findings" are a comprehensive categorization of the current landscape, a critical assessment of existing methods and evaluation practices, and a clear articulation of the unique challenges and opportunities for advancing self-evolving agents towards ASI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of "A Survey of Self-Evolving Agents" are substantial, particularly given the rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and the burgeoning interest in agentic AI systems. This work serves as a foundational synthesis for a nascent yet profoundly impactful field, offering both immediate and long-term implications for research, development, and societal integration of advanced AI.

1. **Establishing a Foundational Roadmap:** By providing the "first systematic and comprehensive review" of self-evolving agents and organizing the field around the "What, When, How, and Where" dimensions, the survey offers an indispensable roadmap. For researchers entering this complex domain, it clarifies terminology, categorizes diverse methodologies, and identifies key components and processes involved in self-evolution. This structured framework will undoubtedly accelerate theoretical inquiry and practical deployment by providing a common language and conceptual anchor, preventing fragmented efforts and fostering more coherent research directions.

2. **Driving Next-Generation AI Development:** The paper explicitly frames self-evolving agents as a "paradigm shift" essential for overcoming the limitations of static LLMs. This highlights the urgent need for AI systems that can adapt in real-time to dynamic, open-ended environments. By delineating the mechanisms for continuous learning, self-correction, and autonomous adaptation, the survey directly informs the design of more robust, versatile, and capable agentic systems. This will be critical for developing AI that can truly operate autonomously in the real world, moving closer to the vision of Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI).

3. **Enhancing Evaluation Standards:** A major contribution is the critical analysis of current evaluation practices and the proposal of a comprehensive framework centered on **Adaptivity, Retention, Generalization, Efficiency, and Safety**. This push for more rigorous, longitudinal, and cost-aware evaluation metrics is vital. It will enable more meaningful comparisons between different self-evolving agent architectures and learning strategies, ensuring that progress is measured against the true capabilities required for continuous, real-world deployment, rather than just isolated task success.

4. **Enabling Broad Real-World Applications:** The survey effectively showcases the wide applicability of self-evolving agents across diverse domains such as coding, education, healthcare, GUI automation, and finance. This demonstration of practical potential can inspire further innovation and investment in these areas, leading to highly customized and adaptive AI solutions that can significantly improve human productivity, personalized services, and decision-making in critical sectors. For example, self-evolving coding agents could revolutionize software development, while adaptive medical agents could personalize diagnostics and treatment.

5. **Prioritizing Safety and Trustworthiness:** Critically, the paper addresses the emergent risks unique to self-evolving systems, such as "misevolution," reward hacking, and the safety of autonomously generated tools. By delineating these amplified risks and proposing "prescriptive guardrails" and mitigation strategies (e.g., sandboxing, audit trails, continuous monitoring), the survey underscores the paramount importance of safety and control. This proactive approach is essential for building trustworthy AI that remains aligned with human values and intentions as it continuously learns and adapts, thereby addressing growing societal concerns about AI autonomy.

6. **Fostering Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The extensive list of authors from a multitude of prestigious institutions globally reflects the inherently interdisciplinary nature of this field. The survey's comprehensive scope, integrating concepts from machine learning, reinforcement learning, cognitive science, and systems design, will further encourage cross-domain collaboration, which is crucial for tackling the multifaceted challenges of self-evolving AI.

In conclusion, "A Survey of Self-Evolving Agents" is a seminal work that not only maps the current landscape of a rapidly evolving field but also provides a visionary blueprint for its future. By establishing a unified framework, highlighting critical research gaps, and emphasizing responsible development, it stands to significantly influence the trajectory of AI research and its eventual impact on society, pushing the boundaries towards truly intelligent and adaptive autonomous systems.
