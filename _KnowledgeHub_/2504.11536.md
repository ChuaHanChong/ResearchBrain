# ReTool: Reinforcement Learning for Strategic Tool Use in LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> ReTool, developed by ByteDance Seed, introduces a reinforcement learning framework that enables Large Language Models to strategically use code interpreters for complex problem-solving. It achieved 67.0% accuracy on the AIME 2024 benchmark, significantly improving performance over text-based reasoning and demonstrating emergent self-correction capabilities in code generation.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle with tasks requiring high precision, complex numerical computation, or symbolic manipulation (e.g., advanced mathematics) due to the inherent 'fuzziness' of textual reasoning.
- Existing tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) methods, primarily based on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), train LLMs to imitate pre-defined tool invocation patterns, limiting their ability to adaptively decide when and how to strategically use tools.
- Pure text-based reasoning can lead to compounding errors and lack of verifiable accuracy in domains requiring exact solutions.

## Method

- ==ReTool== employs a two-stage training framework: an initial cold-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase, followed by a reinforcement learning (RL) phase using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- The SFT phase utilizes automatically constructed ==code-integrated data (D_CI)== to teach foundational tool invocation and output interpretation.
- The core RL phase features ==dynamic, interleaved code execution==, where the LLM generates code that is immediately run in an external sandbox, and the interpreter's output (success or error) is fed back to the model, guiding subsequent reasoning based on a simple accuracy reward.

## Results

- ReTool achieved **67.0%** accuracy on AIME 2024 using Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, significantly outperforming the text-based RL baseline (**40.0%**) and other competitive baselines.
- The approach demonstrated high training efficiency, reaching superior performance in only **400** training steps, compared to over **1000** steps for the text-based RL baseline.
- Cognitive analysis revealed a **40%** reduction in average response length (from **10k** to **6k** tokens), a near **fivefold** increase in code lines per response, and a high code pass rate (approaching **100%**) in correct responses after RL training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning enables LLMs to develop strategic tool-use behaviors, such as increasing code invocation frequency and learning to invoke tools earlier in the reasoning process.
> - The model exhibits emergent meta-cognitive abilities, including autonomously self-correcting non-executable code based on real-time interpreter error feedback.
> - RL training promotes more diverse code utilization, moving beyond basic calculations to encompass a wider range of problem-solving purposes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: Large Language Models (LLMs), advanced reasoning, and tool-augmented AI.

**Evolution of LLM Reasoning:** The paper acknowledges the significant strides made in LLM reasoning capabilities, particularly through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. Early works by Wei et al. (2022, 2023) demonstrated how explicit step-by-step natural language reasoning can enhance LLM performance on complex tasks. More recent advancements, exemplified by models like OpenAI o1 (2024) and DeepSeek R1 (DeepSeek-AI, 2025), have leveraged Reinforcement Learning (RL) to further refine these reasoning processes, enabling models to self-correct and engage in more deliberate, analytical thinking, suggesting early signs of metacognitive control.

**Limitations of Text-Based Reasoning:** Despite these advances, the authors identify a critical limitation: pure text-based LLMs often falter in tasks requiring precise numerical computation, symbolic manipulation, or structured problem-solving, such as geometric reasoning or complex equation solving. These are domains where the inherent "fuzziness" of language models leads to compounding errors and lack of verifiable accuracy.

**Emergence of Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR):** To address this, the research landscape has seen a growing interest in tool-integrated reasoning. Early concepts like Program-of-Thought (PoT) by Chen et al. (2023) and Gao et al. (2023) pioneered the integration of external computational tools, such as Python interpreters, to provide LLMs with symbolic computation capabilities. Subsequent works have explored prompting and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods to equip LLMs with tool-use capabilities (e.g., Chen et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2023). These approaches enable models to write and execute code, verifying intermediate steps and expanding their problem-solving search space.

**The "Reinforcement Learning for Tool Use" Niche:** ReTool specifically targets a critical gap in existing TIR methods: the inability of SFT-based approaches to adaptively decide *when* and *how* to invoke tools. SFT relies on imitating pre-curated data, which limits generalization and the discovery of robust, flexible tool-use strategies. This is where RL offers a principled solution. By providing outcome-based feedback, RL allows models to explore diverse reasoning trajectories, learn from mistakes (including tool execution errors), and autonomously discover optimal invocation patterns, rather than just mimicking them. The paper acknowledges concurrent work like "ToRL: Scaling tool-integrated RL" (Li et al., 2025), which also applies RL to tool usage but at smaller model scales, further highlighting ReTool's contribution in scaling this approach to larger, more capable models and demonstrating superior performance.

In essence, ReTool represents a significant step towards more robust and intelligent hybrid neuro-symbolic AI systems, pushing beyond mere tool *application* to strategic tool *management* through the power of reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ReTool is to enhance the long-form reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) by strategically integrating computational tools, specifically code interpreters (CI), into their reasoning processes. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridge the Performance Gap:** Text-based reasoning models, while advanced in textual logic, inherently struggle with tasks demanding high precision, such as complex mathematical computations, geometric reasoning, or equation solving. Computational tools, like code interpreters, excel in these areas. ReTool aims to bridge this gap by enabling LLMs to leverage the strengths of both symbolic computation and natural language reasoning.

2. **Move Beyond Imitation to Strategic Tool Use:** Existing methods for tool integration often rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which trains models to imitate pre-defined patterns of tool invocation from curated datasets. This approach limits a model's ability to generalize, adapt to novel situations, or autonomously decide the optimal *timing* and *nature* of tool invocation. ReTool's core motivation is to move beyond mere imitation, allowing the model to *learn* a strategic policy for tool use through outcome-based feedback.

3. **Enable Autonomous Discovery of Optimal Strategies:** The authors explicitly aim for an "automated RL paradigm" that allows policy rollouts with multi-turn, real-time code execution. This setup is designed to teach the model not just *how* to invoke tools, but *when* to invoke them, *what kind* of code to write, and *how to respond* to execution outcomes (including errors), all based on direct feedback regarding the final task outcome. This fosters autonomous discovery of optimal tool invocation patterns without relying on explicit human priors for every decision point.

4. **Improve Reasoning Efficiency and Robustness:** By offloading precise computations to a code interpreter, ReTool seeks to make the reasoning process more accurate and efficient. It aims to reduce the "ambiguity and compounding error often seen in textual reasoning" and potentially reduce the verbosity of textual thought processes. The ability to receive real-time feedback (including error messages) from the interpreter also motivates the model to self-correct, leading to more robust and reliable problem-solving.

5. **Advance Hybrid Neuro-Symbolic AI:** The project fundamentally aims to contribute to the understanding and development of hybrid neuro-symbolic systems. By demonstrating how LLMs can effectively integrate and learn to manage symbolic tools, ReTool offers new insights into building AI systems that combine the pattern recognition and generative capabilities of neural networks with the precision and verifiability of symbolic computation.

In summary, ReTool's objective is to create LLMs that are not just *tool-aware* but *tool-strategic*, capable of dynamic, adaptive, and efficient problem-solving in complex computational domains by intelligently leveraging external symbolic tools through reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReTool employs a systematic, two-stage training framework designed to instill and refine strategic tool-use capabilities in LLMs: **cold-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT)** followed by **reinforcement learning (RL) with interleaved code execution**.

#### 3.1. Cold-Start for Tool-Integrated Reasoning Foundation

The first stage focuses on building a foundational understanding of tool invocation and execution analysis.

- **Data Collection and Curation:**
 - **Initial Text-Based Data (`D_init`):** The process begins by gathering existing high-quality mathematical reasoning data from diverse sources, including open-source datasets like Open-Thoughts. This raw data contains textual reasoning traces.
 - **Dual-Verification for `D_init`:** To ensure quality, `D_init` undergoes a two-step verification: human expert curation and evaluation by a powerful LLM (DeepSeek-R1) to filter out invalid or low-quality data.
 - **Automated Code-Integrated Data Construction (`D_CI`):** The crucial step involves automatically transforming `D_init` into `D_CI`. This is achieved using a structured prompt template (detailed in Figure 8 of the appendix). This template guides an LLM to modify the original textual reasoning by identifying steps that would benefit from code execution (e.g., manual calculations) and replacing them with corresponding code snippets and their interpreter execution results.
 - **Two-Stage Verification for `D_CI`:** The newly generated `D_CI` undergoes further rigorous verification:
 - **Format Verification:** Ensures readability and consistent syntax, crucial for detecting tool invocation triggers during RL.
 - **Answer Verification:** Confirms that the final outputs in the code-augmented traces align with the correct solutions to the problems.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The base LLM (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct in this work) is then fine-tuned on the `D_CI` dataset. This SFT phase teaches the model an initial competency in *when* and *how* to invoke the code interpreter and interpret its outputs, providing a robust initialization for the subsequent RL phase.

#### 3.2. ReTool: Reinforcement Learning for Strategic Tool Use

The second and core stage leverages RL to enable the model to autonomously discover and optimize tool-use strategies.

- **Training Algorithm:** ReTool is trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm (Schulman et al., 2017), a popular on-policy algorithm known for its stability and performance in RL settings. The PPO objective function is modified to account for the interleaved code execution and feedback from the interpreter.

- **Reward Design:** A simple yet effective rule-based *accuracy reward* is implemented:
 - `R(a, ˆa) = 1` if the predicted answer (`ˆa`) is equivalent to the ground-truth answer (`a`).
 - `R(a, ˆa) = -1` otherwise.
 The final answer must be presented in a specified format (e.g., `\boxed{}` ) for reliable verification. This simplified reward design focuses solely on the *outcome*, encouraging the model to explore diverse problem-solving behaviors without being overly constrained by intermediate code executability, thus preventing "reward hacking" from optimizing trivial code.

- **Rollout with Interleaved Code Execution:** This is the most distinctive feature of ReTool's RL phase. Unlike traditional RL where LLMs generate pure text (Figure 2a), ReTool's rollout dynamically integrates real-time code execution (Figure 2b).
 - The policy LLM generates natural language reasoning (`t1`).
 - When the model decides to invoke a tool, it writes a code block marked by specific tags (e.g., `<code>...</code>`).
 - Upon detecting these tags, the generation process pauses. The code snippet (`c1`) is parsed and sent to an external **code sandbox environment** for execution.
 - The sandbox executes the code and returns its output, which can be a successful result or an error message (`f1`).
 - This feedback (`f1`), wrapped in `<interpreter>...</interpreter>` tags, is then fed back to the LLM.
 - The LLM continues its generation, using the interpreter's feedback to guide its subsequent reasoning or generate further code snippets. This iterative process continues until a final answer or a predefined maximum length is reached.
 - **Crucially, both successful execution results and error messages are returned.** This allows the model to learn from its mistakes and iteratively refine its code and reasoning.

- **Training Details and Optimizations:**
 - **Framework:** VeRL framework is used for training.
 - **Backbone Model:** Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (Yang et al., 2024) serves as the primary base model.
 - **Hyperparameters:** AdamW optimizer, initial learning rate of 1e-6, maximum sequence length of 16384 tokens, mini-batch size of 512, KL coefficient of 0.0.
 - **Interpreter Feedback Masking:** The tokens within the `<interpreter>...</interpreter>` tags (the sandbox output) are masked out from the loss computation. This ensures training stability by preventing external tokens from interfering with the model's internal loss calculation and preserving the coherence of its generated reasoning sequences.
 - **KV-Cache Reuse:** To optimize memory usage during rollouts, the Key-Value (KV) cache accumulated *before* code execution is cached. Only the KV-cache from the interpreter feedback is calculated and appended, significantly reducing memory overhead.
 - **Asynchronous Code Sandbox:** A pool of sandbox pods functions as workers, pulling tasks independently. This distributed asynchronous approach accelerates RL training by enabling parallel environment interactions and preventing bottlenecks.

This comprehensive methodology allows ReTool to not only equip LLMs with tool-use capabilities but also to strategically optimize *when* and *how* to apply these tools, leading to superior problem-solving performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ReTool demonstrates significant advancements in tool-integrated reasoning, particularly for complex mathematical problems, as evidenced by its performance on the challenging MATH Olympiad benchmark AIME.

- **Superior Performance and Efficiency:**
 - **AIME 2024:** ReTool (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) achieved 67.0% accuracy, dramatically outperforming the text-based RL baseline (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct), which only managed 40.0%.
 - **AIME 2025:** ReTool achieved 49.3% accuracy, compared to the text-based RL baseline's 36.7%.
 - **Training Efficiency:** ReTool attained its superior performance with only 400 training steps, whereas the text-based RL baseline required over 1000 steps to achieve a much lower accuracy. This highlights that tool-integrated learning not only boosts reasoning capabilities but also significantly enhances training efficiency.
 - **Competitive Baseline Comparison:** ReTool (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) surpassed other strong baselines:
 - 10.3% higher than s1-32B (56.7%) on AIME2024.
 - 11.4% higher than OpenAI o1-preview (37.9%) on AIME2025.

- **Impact of Advanced Backbone:**
 - When trained on a more advanced backbone, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, ReTool further improved, reaching 72.5% accuracy on AIME2024 and 54.3% on AIME2025. This indicates that the RL training process enables the discovery of even more effective problem-solving strategies when built upon a stronger base model.

- **Effectiveness of Cold-Start Training:**
 - The cold-start model (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, trained only with SFT) achieved 40.9% accuracy on AIME2024. This performance is comparable to the text-based RL baseline (40.0%) and significantly surpasses the untrained base model (26.7%). This validates the efficacy of the curated `D_CI` dataset in establishing a solid foundation for tool usage patterns.

- **Cognitive Analysis (Emergent Behaviors from RL Training):**
 The paper provides a detailed "cognitive analysis" of the model's behavior during RL training, revealing several key emergent properties:

 - **Response Length:** The average response length decreased by approximately 40% (from 10k to 6k tokens) after RL training. This suggests that replacing verbose textual computation with concise code significantly improves token efficiency in reasoning.
 - **Code Ratio:** The proportion of responses containing code steadily increased throughout RL training, eventually reaching nearly 98% of all questions, indicating enhanced proficiency in strategic tool usage.
 - **Code Lines:** The average number of code lines generated per response increased almost fivefold by the end of training. This points to the model learning to implement more complex and sophisticated code strategies.
 - **Total Test Set Correct Code Counts:** The total count of correctly executed code snippets on the test set showed an overall upward trend (from 1k to 5k), further demonstrating improved code utilization proficiency.
 - **Code Pass Rate:** The pass rate for code in *correct* responses remained consistently high (approaching 100%). In contrast, the pass rate for code in *incorrect* responses exhibited a declining trend, suggesting that code executability is a critical factor influencing the final reasoning outcome.
 - **Code Invocation Timing:** The analysis revealed that the model learned to invoke code earlier in its reasoning process during RL training. This indicates a strategic development in determining the optimal timing for tool integration.
 - **"Aha Moment" of Code Self-Correction:** A remarkable emergent behavior was the model's ability to self-correct non-executable code. When the interpreter returned an error (e.g., `NameError: name 'greedy' is not defined`), the model autonomously reflected on the error ("Oops, the functions need to be defined in the same scope. Let's correct that.") and generated a revised, executable version of the code. This metacognitive capability developed without explicit self-correction training data.
 - **Code Purpose Analysis:** A qualitative analysis using word clouds showed that while "calculation" and "verification" remained dominant code purposes, RL training led to a *more diverse* set of code purposes. This suggests the development of adaptive tool selection, enhancing the model's generalizability.
 - **CI-powered vs. Text-based Reasoning:** A case study visually demonstrated the qualitative difference: text-based reasoning involved lengthy, error-prone manual calculations, while CI-powered reasoning replaced these with concise, accurate code snippets, allowing the model to focus on higher-level strategic reasoning.

In summary, ReTool's findings not only establish a new state-of-the-art in mathematical reasoning for LLMs by effectively integrating code interpreters via RL but also provide profound insights into the emergent cognitive behaviors that arise from such outcome-driven training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "ReTool: Reinforcement Learning for Strategic Tool Use in LLMs" carry significant implications for the future of large language models and artificial intelligence as a whole.

- **Advancement in LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** ReTool pushes the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve in complex, structured problem-solving, especially in domains requiring high numerical precision and symbolic manipulation (e.g., mathematics, physics, engineering). The substantial performance gains on MATH Olympiad benchmarks demonstrate that LLMs can go beyond mere textual fluency to become highly capable computational problem-solvers.

- **Bridging the Neural-Symbolic Gap:** The work provides a compelling demonstration of how neural networks (LLMs) can effectively integrate and learn to leverage symbolic systems (code interpreters). This is a crucial step towards developing robust, hybrid neuro-symbolic AI systems that combine the strengths of both paradigms – the pattern recognition and generalization of neural models with the logical consistency and verifiability of symbolic computation.

- **Efficiency in Training and Inference:** ReTool's ability to achieve superior performance with significantly fewer training steps compared to text-based RL baselines, along with its reduced response length (token efficiency), highlights a major practical impact. More efficient training means faster development cycles and lower computational costs, while concise outputs make the models more practical for real-world deployment.

- **Emergence of Higher-Order Cognitive Skills:** The observed emergent behaviors, particularly code self-correction and adaptive tool selection, are profoundly significant. The fact that the model can autonomously identify and rectify errors in its own generated code based on interpreter feedback, without explicit training for this specific behavior, indicates the development of meta-cognitive capabilities. This is a critical step towards more autonomous and robust AI agents that can monitor and revise their own internal thought processes.

- **Foundation for Generalizable Tool Use:** By enabling LLMs to learn *when* and *how* to invoke tools strategically based on outcomes, ReTool lays a foundation for more generalizable tool-use capabilities across diverse domains. Future work could extend this framework to integrate other specialized tools (e.g., databases, APIs, specific domain-expert systems), transforming LLMs into powerful, versatile agents capable of navigating and solving problems in complex real-world environments.

- **Implications for AI Safety and Interpretability:** The explicit use of code interpreters can potentially enhance the interpretability and verifiability of LLM reasoning. By externalizing complex computations into executable code, it becomes easier to audit the intermediate steps and identify the source of errors, which could be beneficial for developing safer and more reliable AI systems.

- **Commercial and Research Impact for ByteDance:** For ByteDance, this research positions "ByteDance Seed" as a leading player in advanced AI development, particularly in the critical area of reasoning and tool-augmented LLMs. The success of ReTool could translate into new capabilities for ByteDance's products, from intelligent assistants to advanced data analysis tools.

In conclusion, ReTool represents a pivotal advancement in LLM capabilities, showcasing a robust and efficient method for integrating symbolic computation through reinforcement learning. Its findings illuminate the potential for emergent cognitive behaviors and pave the way for a new generation of intelligent agents capable of sophisticated, verifiable, and adaptive problem-solving.
