# DenseGRPO: From Sparse to Dense Reward for Flow Matching Model Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> DenseGRPO enhances the alignment of text-to-image flow matching models with human preferences by introducing a method for estimating step-wise dense rewards and adaptively calibrating the exploration space during reinforcement learning. This approach yielded improved scores, including a PickScore of 24.64 for human preference alignment and an OCR Accuracy of 0.95 for visual text rendering.

## Problem

- Existing GRPO-based methods for text-to-image (T2I) alignment suffer from a "sparse reward problem," applying a single, terminal reward from the final image to all intermediate denoising steps.
- This creates a mismatch between global feedback and fine-grained, step-wise contributions, hindering efficient policy optimization in flow matching models.
- Current SDE samplers for exploration use a uniform noise injection strategy, which is suboptimal for the time-varying denoising process, leading to unbalanced exploration.

## Method

- **DenseGRPO** defines a ==step-wise dense reward== as the reward gain (ΔR_i^t = R_i^{t-1} - R_i^t), quantifying each denoising step's contribution.
- Estimates the reward R_i^t for intermediate latents x_i^t by performing a ==multi-step ODE denoising== process to obtain a clean latent x_i^{t,0} and then applying an existing ==reward model==.
- Introduces a ==reward-aware adaptive noise scheme== that adjusts a timestep-specific noise level ψ(t) in the SDE sampler, calibrating the exploration space for balanced positive/negative dense rewards.

## Results

- Achieved superior performance across T2I tasks, including a **GenEval score of 0.97** for compositional image generation and **0.95 OCR Accuracy** for visual text rendering.
- Demonstrated substantial improvements in human preference alignment, attaining a **PickScore of 24.64**, significantly higher than Flow-GRPO (**23.31**).
- Qualitative evaluations showed higher visual and semantic quality, with better color accuracy, text fidelity, and alignment to complex positional relationships in prompts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing step-wise dense rewards, particularly defined as reward gain, leads to more effective and efficient policy optimization for flow matching model alignment.
> - Calibrating the exploration space adaptively based on reward feedback significantly improves alignment performance compared to uniform noise injection.
> - The accuracy of dense reward estimation is crucial; performing more ODE denoising steps to derive intermediate clean images (n=t) results in better performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image (T2I) generation has witnessed significant advancements, particularly with the emergence of flow matching models. A critical challenge within this domain is the alignment of these generative models with human preferences, ensuring that the generated images not only are coherent with the text prompt but also meet aesthetic and quality criteria as perceived by humans.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a promising methodology for this post-training alignment phase. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), in particular, has garnered attention for its effectiveness in enhancing human preference alignment for T2I generation. Prior works, such as Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO, have integrated the GRPO framework with flow matching models, enabling exploration through the conversion of deterministic ODE samplers into stochastic SDE samplers. These approaches have shown improvements in alignment performance.

However, existing GRPO-based methods face a limitation known as the "sparse reward problem." In these frameworks, a single, terminal reward, derived from the final generated image of an entire denoising trajectory, is applied uniformly to optimize all intermediate denoising steps. This creates a mismatch: the global feedback signal (the terminal reward) does not accurately reflect the fine-grained, step-wise contributions made at each intermediate denoising stage. This discrepancy can misguide the policy optimization process, potentially limiting the overall performance and efficiency of alignment.

The concept of "dense reward" has been explored in various sequential generation tasks to mitigate the sparse reward issue. In text generation, this has involved techniques like per-step KL penalties or token-level entropy weighting. For T2I generation, some DPO-style methods have fined per-step reward signals or trained step-aware performance models. PPO-style approaches have utilized temporal critic functions to assign temporal rewards. While some GRPO-based methods, like TempFlow-GRPO and CoCA, have attempted to provide per-timestep signals (e.g., through trajectory branching or latent similarity), they often still rely on a trajectory-wise signal for optimization or introduce other forms of mismatch.

DenseGRPO addresses this specific gap by proposing a method for estimating and utilizing truly step-wise dense rewards within the GRPO framework for flow matching models. It aims to provide a more precise credit assignment at each denoising step, thereby improving the effectiveness of human preference alignment. Furthermore, it identifies and mitigates an issue related to the exploration space in SDE samplers, where a uniform noise injection strategy can be suboptimal for the time-varying denoising process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified "sparse reward problem" in current GRPO-based methods for aligning text-to-image flow matching models with human preferences. Existing approaches assign a single, global reward, derived from the final generated image, to all intermediate denoising steps within a trajectory. This practice overlooks the distinct contributions of individual steps. The authors argue that this global feedback signal creates a fundamental mismatch with the exact, fine-grained contributions of each intermediate denoising step, which can lead to inefficient or misdirected policy optimization.

Against this background, the key objectives of the DenseGRPO framework are detailed as follows:

1. **To introduce a novel method for estimating step-wise dense rewards:** The core objective is to move beyond trajectory-level sparse rewards by proposing a mechanism to predict the reward gain at each denoising step. This is achieved by applying a reward model to intermediate clean images, derived through an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE)-based approach. This method is designed to provide feedback signals that align precisely with the fine-grained contributions of individual denoising steps, thereby facilitating more effective training for human preference alignment.

2. **To identify and rectify the mismatch in exploration space calibration:** Leveraging the newly estimated dense rewards, the research aims to reveal a drawback in existing GRPO-based methods: the use of a uniform exploration setting despite the time-varying nature of noise intensity in the denoising process. This uniform setting can result in either excessive or insufficient stochasticity at different timesteps, leading to an inappropriate exploration space that hinders learning. The objective is to propose a reward-aware scheme that adaptively adjusts a timestep-specific stochasticity injection in the SDE sampler. This calibration is intended to ensure a more suitable and balanced exploration space across all timesteps, optimizing the conditions for GRPO learning.

3. **To empirically demonstrate the effectiveness and significance of DenseGRPO:** A crucial objective is to validate the proposed framework through extensive experimentation on multiple standard benchmarks. This includes comparing DenseGRPO's performance against existing GRPO-based methods across various text-to-image tasks, thereby highlighting the critical role of accurate dense rewards and calibrated exploration in achieving superior flow matching model alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DenseGRPO introduces two main components to address the sparse reward problem and exploration space limitations in GRPO-based flow matching models. The methodology builds upon the existing GRPO framework for flow matching models and their use of SDE samplers for stochastic exploration.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Concepts

The paper first reviews the formulation of reinforcement learning (RL) on flow matching models, where the iterative denoising process is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). A state `s_t` comprises a prompt `c`, timestep `t`, and latent `x_t`. An action `a_t` corresponds to the predicted latent `x_{t-1}`. Critically, the standard reward function `R(s_t, a_t)` provides a non-zero reward `R(x_0, c)` only at the terminal step (`t=0`), remaining zero for all intermediate steps. This highlights the sparse reward issue that DenseGRPO aims to resolve.

The GRPO framework optimizes a policy by maximizing an objective function that incorporates an advantage estimate, `A_i^t`. In existing methods, this advantage `A_i^t` is derived solely from the terminal reward `R(x_i^0, c)` of the entire trajectory, rendering it constant across all timesteps.

For exploration, flow matching models, which typically use deterministic ODE samplers, are converted to SDE samplers by injecting additional noise. The noise level `σ_t` is defined as `a * sqrt(t/(1-t))`, where `a` is a scalar hyper-parameter controlling uniform noise injection across timesteps.

#### 3.2. Step-Wise Dense Reward

DenseGRPO addresses the feedback-contribution mismatch by defining the step-wise dense reward `ΔR_i^t` for a given timestep `t` as the reward gain between the subsequent and current latent states: `ΔR_i^t = R_i^{t-1} - R_i^t`. This definition aims to quantify the contribution of the one-step denoising process `x_i^t → x_i^{t-1}`.

To compute `ΔR_i^t`, the method first needs to estimate the reward `R_i^t` for any intermediate latent `x_i^t`. Rather than training an additional critic function (which would increase training overhead), DenseGRPO leverages the deterministic nature of ODE samplers. Given an intermediate latent `x_i^t` at timestep `t`, a multi-step ODE denoising process, `ODE_n(x_i^t, c)`, is performed to obtain its corresponding clean latent `x_i^{t,0}`. The reward of this clean latent `x_i^{t,0}` is then predicted by an established reward model `R` (e.g., PickScore, GenEval) and assigned as the latent reward `R_i^t` for `x_i^t`. The paper notes that `n=t` (full ODE rollout) is used for improved performance.

Once the set of latent rewards `{R_i^t}` is obtained for a trajectory, the dense reward `{ΔR_i^t}` for each step is calculated. This step-wise `ΔR_i^t` then replaces the sparse `R(x_i^0, c)` in the GRPO advantage calculation, ensuring that the policy optimization receives fine-grained feedback aligned with each step's contribution.

#### 3.3. Exploration Space Calibration

The second component of DenseGRPO addresses the issue of inappropriate exploration space arising from the uniform noise injection in existing SDE samplers. The uniform hyper-parameter `a` in `σ_t` does not adapt to the time-varying nature of the denoising process, leading to imbalanced dense reward distributions (e.g., too many negative rewards at certain timesteps).

DenseGRPO proposes a reward-aware scheme to calibrate the exploration space by adaptively adjusting a timestep-specific noise level, denoted `ψ(t)`. This replaces the fixed `a` in `σ_t`, so `σ_t = ψ(t)`. The calibration process is iterative and described in Algorithm 1:
1. **Sampling and Reward Calculation:** The algorithm samples multiple trajectories using the current `ψ(t)` and calculates their dense rewards `{ΔR_i^t}`.
2. **Adaptive Adjustment:** For each timestep `t`, the algorithm assesses the balance between positive and negative dense rewards.
 - If the number of positive and negative dense rewards are approximately balanced (i.e., their difference is below a threshold `ε1`), `ψ(t)` is slightly increased by `ε2` to encourage more diverse exploration.
 - If the rewards are imbalanced, `ψ(t)` is slightly decreased by `ε2` to temper the stochasticity and bring the exploration space back to a suitable range.
3. **Iteration:** This process is repeated iteratively until a stable and suitable `ψ(t)` function is obtained, which then ensures a balanced exploration space across all timesteps during GRPO training.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

DenseGRPO was evaluated on three T2I tasks: Compositional Image Generation (using GenEval as reward model), Human Preference Alignment (using PickScore), and Visual Text Rendering (using OCR accuracy). The experimental setup largely followed Flow-GRPO, using a sampling timestep `T=10`, evaluation timestep `T=40`, group size `G=24`, and an image resolution of 512x512. LoRA with `α=64` and `r=32` was employed. Training utilized the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 3e-4, global batch size of 144, and a gradient accumulation step of 8. The KL ratio `β` was set to 0.04 for compositional image generation and visual text rendering, and 0.01 for human preference alignment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of DenseGRPO across various benchmarks and highlight the role of dense rewards in flow matching model alignment.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

DenseGRPO achieved superior performance compared to Flow-GRPO and an adapted Flow-GRPO+CoCA (which integrates CoCA's core idea of latent similarity into flow matching models).

- **Compositional Image Generation:** DenseGRPO attained a GenEval score of 0.97, surpassing Flow-GRPO (0.95) and Flow-GRPO+CoCA (0.96). While other quality and preference metrics were competitive, the primary task metric showed improvement.
- **Visual Text Rendering:** DenseGRPO achieved an OCR Accuracy of 0.95, outperforming Flow-GRPO (0.92) and Flow-GRPO+CoCA (0.93).
- **Human Preference Alignment:** This task showed the most significant gains, with DenseGRPO achieving a PickScore of 24.64, substantially higher than Flow-GRPO (23.31) and Flow-GRPO+CoCA (23.63). The Aesthetic score also improved by 0.43 over Flow-GRPO.

Qualitative evaluations indicated that DenseGRPO generated images with higher visual and semantic quality, exhibiting better color accuracy, text fidelity, and alignment with complex positional relationships specified in prompts (e.g., "ladybug on top of a toadstool"). Learning curves for all tasks demonstrated that DenseGRPO converged to higher performance levels more efficiently than the baseline methods.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Key Components

**Effect of Dense Reward:**
An ablation study comparing "Dense Reward (Baseline)" (using `R_i^{t-1}` directly as reward) against Flow-GRPO and DenseGRPO revealed that "Dense Reward (Baseline)" offered greater benefits than Flow-GRPO, indicating the general effectiveness of dense rewards. DenseGRPO, which utilizes the reward gain `ΔR_i^t`, further surpassed the "Dense Reward (Baseline)," validating the specific approach of defining step-wise contribution as reward gain. These findings suggest that aligning feedback signals more closely with step-wise contributions facilitates policy optimization.

**Effect of Exploration Space Calibration:**
Comparing DenseGRPO with its calibrated, time-specific noise level `ψ(t)` against a version of DenseGRPO using a uniform noise level (`a=0.7`) demonstrated the advantage of the reward-aware calibration scheme. The time-specific `ψ(t)` led to improved alignment, indicating a more suitable exploration space for all timesteps. Even with a uniform noise setting, DenseGRPO still outperformed Flow-GRPO, reinforcing the contribution of the dense reward mechanism itself.

**Effect of Different ODE Denoising Steps (n):**
Ablation studies on the number of ODE denoising steps (`n`) used to estimate `R_i^t` showed that increasing `n` generally improved performance (n=t > n=2 > n=1). A single-step ODE (`n=1`) yielded suboptimal results, performing worse than Flow-GRPO. This suggests that more accurate dense rewards, obtained by performing more ODE steps to bring the intermediate latent closer to a clean image (for which reward models are designed), provide greater benefits. While larger `n` incurs higher computational costs in terms of GPU hours per training step, it translated to better performance for the same total GPU training time, emphasizing the critical role of dense reward accuracy.

#### 4.3. Generalizability and Reward Hacking

**Generalizability:**
The research demonstrated DenseGRPO's scalability to higher resolutions (1024x1024 on SD 3.5-M) and its robustness when applied to other flow matching models (FLUX.1-dev). Furthermore, the core principle of DenseGRPO—estimating `R_i^t` via a deterministic sampler to derive `ΔR_i^t`—was successfully applied to a standard diffusion model (SD 1.5) as well, indicating its potential generalizability across different generative model families that employ deterministic denoising.

**Reward Hacking:**
While DenseGRPO showed significant improvements across metrics, the authors acknowledged instances of "reward hacking" in specific tasks (e.g., occasional decline in general image quality when optimizing for compositional accuracy). They attribute this to the precise, step-wise feedback potentially making the model more susceptible to overfitting the reward model. However, the overall strong performance across multiple metrics, including Aesthetic score, suggests a robust alignment capability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DenseGRPO makes several contributions to the field of text-to-image generation and reinforcement learning for generative models.

Firstly, its primary significance lies in effectively addressing the **sparse reward problem** within GRPO-based flow matching models. By introducing an ODE-based approach to estimate fine-grained, step-wise dense rewards, the framework ensures a more accurate credit assignment for each denoising operation. This direct alignment between feedback signals and individual step contributions can lead to more efficient and targeted policy optimization, which is a fundamental improvement over existing trajectory-level reward systems.

Secondly, the introduction of the **reward-aware exploration space calibration scheme** is significant. Recognizing that uniform noise injection in SDE samplers can be suboptimal, DenseGRPO adaptively adjusts timestep-specific stochasticity. This calibration ensures a balanced and suitable exploration space across all denoising timesteps, which is crucial for stable and effective reinforcement learning, preventing scenarios of either excessive or insufficient exploration.

The **empirical results** demonstrate that these methodological advancements translate into tangible performance gains. DenseGRPO consistently outperforms baseline methods across diverse text-to-image tasks, including compositional image generation, visual text rendering, and general human preference alignment. The substantial improvement in human preference scores, in particular, suggests that DenseGRPO can help generative models produce outputs that are more aesthetically pleasing and better aligned with subjective human judgments.

Moreover, the **generalizability** of the dense reward estimation approach, which relies on the deterministic nature of ODE samplers (and can be extended to diffusion models via deterministic samplers), indicates a broader potential impact. This suggests that the principles of fine-grained reward allocation could be applied to a wider range of generative models and tasks beyond flow matching, fostering advancements in various areas of conditional generation.

Finally, while the paper acknowledges instances of reward hacking, its overall robustness across multiple evaluation metrics points to a more effective and stable alignment process. The insights gained regarding the importance of reward accuracy and balanced exploration could stimulate further research into more sophisticated reward modeling techniques, advanced exploration strategies, and methods to mitigate overfitting to reward models in complex generative tasks. DenseGRPO provides a foundational step towards more sophisticated and human-aligned generative AI systems by refining how feedback is processed and utilized during the learning phase.
