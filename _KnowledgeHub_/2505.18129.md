# One RL to See Them All: Visual Triple Unified Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A Visual Triple Unified Reinforcement Learning system, V-Triune, enables Vision-Language Models to simultaneously improve across visual reasoning and perception tasks within a single training pipeline. Models trained using V-Triune, named Orsta, show performance gains of up to +14.1% on the MEGA-Bench Core, along with specific improvements like +7.81 mAP for COCO Detection and over 5% on the MathVista benchmark.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) applications for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have primarily focused on visual reasoning tasks, neglecting perception-intensive tasks like object detection and grounding.
- The absence of a unified RL framework makes it challenging to integrate diverse input formats, reward mechanisms, and evaluation metrics needed for jointly training VLMs on both reasoning and perception.
- Current RL setups for VLMs lack specialized reward designs and stability measures for perception tasks, leading to issues such as reward ambiguity with fixed IoU thresholds or cold-start problems.

## Method

- Developed ==V-Triune==, a unified RL system with a three-component architecture for Sample-Level Data Formatting, Verifier-Level Reward Computation via an asynchronous client-server, and Source-Level Metric Monitoring.
- Introduced a ==Dynamic IoU reward mechanism== for visual perception tasks, which adaptively adjusts the Intersection over Union threshold during training to facilitate stable learning towards high-precision outcomes.
- Implemented a comprehensive training recipe featuring ==frozen Visual Transformer (ViT) parameters==, filtering of spurious special tokens, a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompt pool, and optimized memory management to enhance training stability.

## Results

- Orsta models achieved up to a **+14.1%** performance increase on the MEGA-Bench Core benchmark compared to their Qwen2.5-VL backbones, with substantial improvements in mathematical and perception-related tasks.
- Visual perception capabilities improved significantly, including **+7.81 mAP** for single-object detection and **+3.77 mAP** for multi-object detection on COCO, along with **+5.3 mAP** on OVDEval and new state-of-the-art results on CountBench.
- Visual reasoning saw notable gains, with Orsta-7B improving by **4%** on MMMU and all Orsta variants achieving over **5%** gains on the MathVista benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning in VLMs primarily functions as an alignment strategy, refining decision-making and unlocking latent capabilities of pre-trained models rather than introducing entirely new knowledge.
> - Freezing the Visual Transformer (ViT) parameters and employing conservative learning rates are critical engineering optimizations for achieving stable and peak performance during VLM RL training, preventing instability.
> - Training VLMs on a combined corpus of reasoning and perception tasks leads to additive performance gains and transferable alignment signals, indicating synergy between these diverse learning objectives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) is an expanding area for post-training foundation models, including vision-language models (VLMs). While RL post-training for text-only models has seen significant development, public methodologies for unified multimodal RL, particularly for tasks involving both heterogeneous reasoning and perception, are less mature. Existing VLM-RL research often concentrates on specific task categories. One line of work focuses on multimodal reasoning tasks, such as mathematics and science, typically employing rule-based rewards. Another line addresses perception-heavy tasks like detection, grounding, OCR, and counting, frequently using task-specific rewards such as Intersection over Union (IoU) or mean Average Precision (mAP).

A gap exists in stable RL frameworks that can jointly improve a single VLM across both high-level reasoning and fine-grained perception tasks. The challenge lies in accommodating both capability heterogeneity (covering diverse tasks) and verification heterogeneity (handling different reward structures and outcome verification regimes, from symbolic checking to continuous spatial feedback) within a unified pipeline. Previous efforts toward broader multimodal RL have explored mixed answer types or heterogeneous image-video training, but have not comprehensively addressed the joint training of reasoning-heavy and perception-heavy tasks with their distinct verification and reward shaping requirements within a single VLM RL framework. This paper aims to address this specific gap by proposing a unified methodology for heterogeneous visual tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a stable and scalable methodology for unified RL post-training of vision-language models on heterogeneous visual tasks, specifically those encompassing both reasoning-heavy and perception-heavy capabilities. The authors identify three practical issues that impede existing unified multimodal RL pipelines:

1. **Rigid Reward Interfaces:** Different tasks and even individual samples often require distinct reward compositions and verifiers. Hard-coding this logic into the trainer component can lead to brittle and unscalable systems, hindering the addition of new task families.
2. **Ambiguity-versus-Sparsity Trade-off in Localization Rewards:** For localization tasks (e.g., object detection, grounding), fixed IoU thresholds present a dilemma. Loose thresholds (e.g., IoU@50) can lead to high reward density but also high reward ambiguity, as multiple coarse predictions may receive similar rewards, reducing pressure for precise localization. Conversely, strict thresholds (e.g., IoU@99) offer clearer objectives but result in sparse rewards, particularly in early training stages, leading to a cold-start problem where the model receives minimal positive feedback. This challenge is amplified when localization rewards must coexist with the typically sharper rule-based rewards from reasoning tasks.
3. **Lack of Source-Level Observability:** In joint training across multiple tasks, aggregated performance metrics can mask underlying issues specific to individual data sources, such as reward degradation, format drift, or optimization instability. This lack of granular insight makes it difficult to diagnose and address problems in a timely manner.

To mitigate these issues, the authors propose V-Triune, a methodology designed to support heterogeneous reward structures and verification regimes within a single optimization loop. This aims to enable joint improvement of VLMs on both high-level reasoning and fine-grained perception.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The V-Triune methodology organizes unified multimodal RL training around three coordinated abstractions: Sample-Level Reward Routing, Verifier-Level Outcome Verification, and Source-Level Diagnostics. These abstractions are designed to manage heterogeneous rewards, verification, and failure signals in a unified pipeline.

#### 3.1. Sample-Level Reward Routing

This abstraction defines a boundary between the RL trainer and task-specific reward logic. Each training sample includes metadata that specifies the required reward components, their weights, and the verifier to be used for evaluating the model's rollout. The trainer interacts with a unified routing interface, allowing task-specific verification logic to reside outside the core update loop. This design enables extensibility, as adding new tasks primarily requires updating data routing or registering a new verifier, rather than modifying the trainer's core code. The routing metadata also includes a source identifier for later diagnostic purposes.

#### 3.2. Verifier-Level Outcome Verification

This component provides a common interface for heterogeneous outcome evaluation. The trainer sends model predictions and ground-truth references to a designated verifier, which then performs parsing, verification, and reward computation. The paper primarily instantiates two types of verifiers:

- **MathVerifyVerifier (Rule-verifiable outcomes):** This verifier handles tasks where outputs can be parsed and deterministically checked, such as mathematics, puzzles, science, chart reasoning, OCR, and counting. It uses a 0–1 rule-based accuracy reward, where `R_acc` is 1 if the parsed predicted answer matches the parsed ground-truth answer, and 0 otherwise. Model responses are instructed to enclose final answers within `\boxed{}`.
- **DetectionVerifier (Localization-centric outcomes):** This verifier addresses tasks like object detection and grounding, requiring both structural format adherence and spatial accuracy. It computes a composite reward:
 - **Format Reward:** `R_format` is calculated based on the presence of four specific format tags (`<think>`, `</think>`, `<answer>`, `</answer>`). It assigns a partial reward for each correctly used tag.
 - **IoU-based Accuracy Reward:** `R_acc` is the Intersection over Union (IoU) between the predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes, provided the IoU exceeds a specified threshold `𝜖`. If the IoU is below `𝜖`, the reward is 0.
 The final reward combines `R_acc` and `R_format` using weights (`𝛼_acc`, `𝛼_format`) specified by the sample-level routing metadata.

 **Dynamic IoU for Precision Enhancement:** Within the DetectionVerifier, Dynamic IoU addresses the ambiguity-versus-sparsity trade-off of fixed IoU thresholds. Instead of a single threshold, it employs a three-stage schedule that progressively increases the IoU requirement over training. This starts with a looser threshold for denser feedback in early stages, gradually moving to stricter thresholds to encourage high-precision localization in later stages. The main schedule used thresholds of 0.85, 0.95, and 0.99 over the first 10%, next 15%, and remaining training steps, respectively.

#### 3.3. Source-Level Diagnostics

To counter the limitations of aggregate metrics, V-Triune incorporates source-level diagnostics, tracking verified samples based on their `data_source` key. These diagnostics are categorized into four groups:
- **Training Reward:** Breakdowns of rule-based accuracy, format reward, and IoU-based reward per source.
- **Response Behavior:** Metrics like average response length, lengths of correct/incorrect responses, and truncation rates to detect verbosity or collapse.
- **Reflection-related Metrics:** Online proxies for response strategy, tracking the presence and correctness of reflective cues in responses.
- **Auxiliary Diagnostics:** Includes fixed-threshold IoU, Vision Transformer (ViT)/Large Language Model (LLM) gradient norms, and qualitative response examples.
These diagnostics are integrated into the methodology to enable actionable insights and timely detection of source-specific failures, leading to training adjustments (e.g., freezing the vision encoder, filtering leaked image special tokens).

#### 3.4. Model and Training Setup

The methodology is instantiated in the Orsta (One RL to See Them All) model family, using Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 32B as backbone models. Unified RL post-training is performed on a 47.7K-sample VQA dataset with verifiable answers, compiled from 18 sources covering four reasoning tasks (mathematics, puzzle, science, chart) and four perception tasks (detection, grounding, counting, OCR). The training process uses GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization) with a rollout batch size of 1024 and 8 sampled responses per prompt, built upon the Verl framework. The vision encoder and connector layers are frozen during training, with only the LLM layers updated, based on insights from source-level diagnostics showing vision encoder instability. Rollouts are generated using vLLM with temperature 1.0, top-p 1.0, and max_length 2048. Data curation involves a two-stage filtering process (rule-based and difficulty-based) to ensure high-quality, verifiable samples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several findings regarding the efficacy of the V-Triune methodology and the Orsta models.

#### 4.1. Unified Training Under Fair Budgets

Under a fixed training budget (7B off-policy, 60 optimization steps), unified training on both reasoning and perception tasks demonstrates performance comparable to or surpassing specialist mixtures (Reason-only and Perception-only). On MEGA-Bench task-composition curves, the "Reason+Perception" mixture maintains a strong or tied-strongest trajectory. Across a 10-benchmark suite covering both reasoning (MMMU, MathVista, MathVision, MME-R, CharXiv (RQ)) and perception (HrBench4K, VStar, COCO, OCRBenchV2, ScreenSpot-Pro), the unified model matches or exceeds specialist baselines on 5/10 benchmarks and remains competitive on others. This indicates improved budget efficiency and suggests a lack of a clear reasoning-versus-perception trade-off on MEGA-Bench, potentially due to cross-task regularization.

#### 4.2. General Performance on MEGA-Bench

Orsta models, trained for 3 epochs, consistently improve the MEGA-Bench performance of their base Qwen2.5-VL backbones at both 7B and 32B scales. For instance, Orsta-7B improved its backbone score by +3.24 points, and Orsta-32B-0321 showed a substantial improvement of +14.07 points. Even for a stronger backbone like Qwen2.5-VL-32B-0326, Orsta-32B-0326 achieved a gain of +2.10 points, indicating stable and notable improvements.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Multi-task RL-VLM Baselines

Orsta-7B was compared against strong multi-task RL-VLM baselines (MM-Eureka-7B, VL-Rethinker-7B, VisionReasoner-7B) on the same 10-benchmark suite. Orsta-7B achieved the highest score on 7/10 benchmarks. It performed strongly on reasoning tasks (winning MMMU, MME-Reasoning, and CharXiv (RQ)) and perception tasks (top score on 4/5 benchmarks, with a notable margin on COCO multi-object). This suggests that the benefits of unified training translate into improved final model performance across diverse task families.

#### 4.4. Efficacy of Dynamic IoU for Localization Tasks

Ablations conducted using only detection and grounding training subsets demonstrated the necessity of Dynamic IoU. Fixed IoU@50 learned quickly but experienced performance degradation later in training, attributed to reward ambiguity allowing multiple coarse boxes to be similarly rewarded. Fixed IoU@99, while precise, exhibited slower early learning due to reward sparsity. The three-stage Dynamic IoU schedule (0.85 → 0.95 → 0.99) provided a balance, maintaining learnability early on while enforcing higher precision later. Alternative staged schedules either plateaued too early (loose variant) or slowed initial learning (strict variant). The same pattern was observed on the OVDEval negation subset. An adaptive IoU scheduler did not show a clear advantage over the fixed three-stage schedule.

#### 4.5. Insights from Source-Level Diagnostics

Source-level diagnostics revealed key training dynamics and issues.
- **Response Behavior:** Reasoning-heavy tasks (e.g., Puzzle) showed increasing response length and reflection ratio over training, while localization-centric tasks (e.g., Detection) maintained short, direct, and nearly reflection-free responses. This indicates that unified training preserves task-appropriate response patterns rather than collapsing them into a uniform style.
- **Training Stability:** Diagnostics exposed instability when the vision encoder was updated. Updating both ViT and LLM led to a performance drop on COCO and increased gradient norms, particularly in the ViT layers (gradient explosion). Freezing the vision encoder and connector, and updating only the LLM, resulted in more stable training and better performance.
- **Data Issues:** Source-level logs also identified "leaked image special tokens" in model responses, which could disrupt the matching of visual features to input sequences and cause training failures. These tokens were filtered before reward recomputation.

#### 4.6. Extension to New Task Domains

V-Triune was tested for extensibility by adding approximately 3K GUI grounding samples from the ShowUI dataset, a domain not present in the original training mix. Adding this data substantially improved performance on ScreenSpot-Pro, a relevant benchmark. The "Unified + GUI" model still outperformed "Perception + GUI," suggesting that reasoning-heavy data did not impede learning in the new GUI domain, and the benefits of unified training persisted, showing positive transfer. This demonstrates the framework's scalability when new tasks can reuse existing verifier regimes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The V-Triune methodology offers a practical and structured approach to address the complexities of unified multimodal RL for VLMs. By systematically tackling rigid reward interfaces, the IoU ambiguity-sparsity trade-off, and the lack of source-level observability, it provides a framework for stable and scalable training across heterogeneous reasoning and perception tasks.

The key contributions and potential impacts include:
- **Improved Training Methodology:** V-Triune provides a clear separation of concerns (reward routing, outcome verification, diagnostics), making multimodal RL more manageable and extensible compared to ad-hoc, task-specific approaches.
- **Enhanced VLM Capabilities:** The Orsta models, developed using V-Triune, demonstrate that unified RL post-training can simultaneously improve performance on diverse reasoning and perception benchmarks, often matching or exceeding specialist models under comparable budgets. This suggests that a single VLM can be effectively optimized for a broader range of capabilities.
- **Addressing Localization Challenges:** Dynamic IoU provides a solution to a specific practical problem in localization tasks, enabling both learnability in early training and high-precision refinement later, which is critical for fine-grained perception.
- **Actionable Diagnostics:** The emphasis on source-level diagnostics transforms a traditionally opaque joint training process into a more transparent and diagnosable one, allowing for timely detection and correction of issues like model instability or undesirable response patterns.
- **Scalability and Extensibility:** The demonstrated ability to integrate new task domains (e.g., GUI grounding) by reusing existing verifier regimes suggests that V-Triune can facilitate the expansion of VLM capabilities without requiring extensive re-engineering of the core RL pipeline.

The findings indicate that this methodology is effective for joint RL post-training of reasoning and perception in open VLMs, leading to more robust and capable models. Future work may explore extending this approach to multimodal agentic tool-use tasks and generalizing the training recipe to other modalities beyond vision, such as speech, video, and text.
