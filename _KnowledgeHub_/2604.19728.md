# VLA Foundry: A Unified Framework for Training Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA Foundry is an open-source framework unifying the training of large language, vision-language, and vision-language-action models within a single codebase, enabling end-to-end control from backbone pretraining to action fine-tuning. The framework demonstrates efficient scaling and produces models like FOUNDRY-QWEN3VLA-2.1B-MT, which improved aggregate success rate by over 20 percentage points compared to a baseline on bimanual manipulation tasks by leveraging a strong pretrained VLM backbone.

## Problem

- The infrastructure and tooling for robotics foundation models have not kept pace with algorithmic advancements, leading to tight coupling with specific algorithms and limited research flexibility.
- Data scarcity in robotics is a fundamental bottleneck, and current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) frameworks often neglect upstream data processing for Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which directly impacts robotics performance.
- Existing open-source VLA efforts are fragmented, requiring researchers to 'stitch together' incompatible pretraining pipelines for LLM and VLM components, hindering exploration of the full design space.

## Method

- **VLA Foundry** provides a ==unified open-source framework== that integrates LLM, VLM, and VLA training within a single codebase, offering a shared data-loading and training stack.
- The framework employs a ==modular, configuration-driven architecture== enabling end-to-end control over the entire training pipeline, supporting both from-scratch training and efficient integration of pretrained backbones.
- It natively supports ==probabilistic dataset mixing== and robust dataloaders to effectively incorporate diverse data types, addressing robotics data scarcity.

## Results

- VLA Foundry demonstrated efficient distributed-training scaling for LLM, VLM, and VLA stages, achieving near-linear scaling with DDP for LLM training.
- The **FOUNDRY-QWEN3VLA-2.1B-MT** model, built on a pretrained Qwen3-VL backbone, surpassed the LBM-MT baseline by over **+20 percentage points** in aggregate success rate on closed-source bimanual manipulation tasks.
- The framework successfully trained **FOUNDRY-VLA-1.7B** entirely from scratch, exhibiting functional language (HellaSwag **66.7**, ARC-e **71.7**, PIQA **77.5**) and image captioning (BLEU-4 **15.57**, CIDEr **55.14**), though its VLA performance was weaker than models with strong pretrained backbones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified framework offers comprehensive control over the entire VLA model development lifecycle, from foundational pretraining to action-specific fine-tuning, fostering greater research flexibility and allowing for in-depth investigation of component interactions.
> - Leveraging powerful, pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones significantly enhances the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, leading to substantial performance improvements in complex robotic manipulation tasks.
> - Rigorous and accessible evaluation environments, including standardized simulators and statistical analysis tools, are critical for validating and comparing robotic policy performance accurately, especially given distribution shifts between evaluation platforms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics foundation models is characterized by rapid advancements, with numerous systems demonstrating increasingly sophisticated capabilities in recent years. Despite this progress, two primary challenges persist in the broader research landscape:

Firstly, the supporting infrastructure and tooling for rigorous research have not kept pace with algorithmic developments. Many existing codebases either have not been extensively tested at scale or are primarily designed for model releases, which often results in tight coupling to specific algorithmic decisions and limits research flexibility. Key research questions regarding data scaling, backbone pretraining, and the interaction between robotics and non-robotics data require both substantial computational resources and modular algorithmic infrastructure that provides comprehensive control over different components of the model and training pipeline.

Secondly, data scarcity remains a fundamental bottleneck in robotics. Robot interaction data is limited in scale, diversity, and signal density compared to data available for language and vision models. As robot policies continue to scale, the importance of incorporating non-robotics data grows. However, most open-source Vision-Language-Action (VLA) frameworks primarily focus on the action training stage, often treating upstream data processing for Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as a fixed or out-of-scope component. This separation is problematic because decisions made during LLM and VLM pretraining directly influence downstream robotics performance. A framework that integrates the entire pipeline, from pretraining to policy learning, as a single, controllable system is necessary to effectively explore the full design space.

VLA Foundry addresses these challenges by introducing a unified, open-source framework. Unlike many existing LLM/VLM training frameworks or VLA frameworks that often treat capabilities separately, VLA Foundry offers a shared data-loading and training stack that encompasses LLM, VLM, and VLA training within a single codebase. This approach aims to provide practitioners with end-to-end control, from backbone pretraining to action-expert fine-tuning, thereby enabling co-training across modalities, flexible dataset mixing, and the prototyping of new architectures without the need to integrate disparate tools. The work positions itself as a comprehensive solution for developing and scaling VLA systems while facilitating experimentation with diverse training recipes, architectures, and data mixtures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the VLA Foundry project is to establish an open-source framework that unifies the training of LLMs, VLMs, and VLAs within a singular codebase. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Addressing Fragmentation in Robotics Foundation Model Development:** Current open-source efforts in VLA model development frequently focus exclusively on the action training stage. This often necessitates "stitching together" pre-existing and potentially incompatible pretraining pipelines for LLM and VLM components. The motivation here is to eliminate this fragmentation by offering a single, integrated environment that supports all stages of an embodied model's training lifecycle.

2. **Enabling End-to-End Control and Research Flexibility:** The existing fragmented approach limits researchers' ability to thoroughly investigate the impact of decisions made at earlier stages (e.g., LLM/VLM pretraining data and architectures) on downstream robotics performance. VLA Foundry is motivated by the need to provide end-to-end control, allowing researchers to intervene at any stage of the data and training recipe—from backbone pretraining to action expert fine-tuning—through a consistent, shared, and configuration-driven interface.

3. **Facilitating Multi-Modal Training and Data Exploration:** Robotics data scarcity is a persistent issue. The growing importance of non-robotics data in scaling robot policies necessitates flexible mechanisms for mixing and co-training across various data modalities (text, image-caption, robotics). A key objective is to provide infrastructure that natively supports flexible multi-modal training with probabilistic dataset mixing and dataloaders that handle diverse data types, enabling precise control over the training mixture at every stage.

4. **Supporting Both From-Scratch Training and Pretrained Backbones:** To cater to diverse research needs, the framework is motivated to support both training models entirely from scratch (demonstrating full pipeline control) and efficiently integrating powerful off-the-shelf pretrained backbones (e.g., from Hugging Face). This dual capability allows for benchmarking new architectures and leveraging state-of-the-art components within a consistent training and evaluation pipeline.

5. **Ensuring Scalability and Performance:** Developing large foundation models requires substantial computational resources. An objective is to build a scalable distributed training system based on established technologies like FSDP2 and cloud-native tooling (AWS SageMaker, S3), designed to support multi-node, multi-GPU runs with features like automatic gradient accumulation, mixed precision, and checkpoint synchronization.

6. **Promoting Reproducibility and Rigorous Evaluation:** Robust evaluation is crucial for advancing research. The project aims to contribute usability improvements to existing benchmarks and analysis tools, such as the LBM Eval simulator and STEP statistical analysis tools. The objective is to make these tools more accessible for public use and facilitate rigorous, statistically sound comparisons of policy performance.

7. **Demonstrating Framework Utility with Concrete Models:** To validate the framework's capabilities, a specific objective is to train and release two types of VLA models: one fully from scratch through the LLM→VLM→VLA pipeline and another leveraging a strong pretrained backbone (Qwen3-VL). These models serve as concrete demonstrations and baselines for the research community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA Foundry is designed as an open-source framework structured around four core principles: modularity and composability, hackability and interoperability, performance, and reproducibility.

**Framework Architecture and Core Components:**
The architecture of VLA Foundry comprises four layers: a YAML-based configuration system, a registry for pluggable models and data pipelines, modality-specific preprocessing and dataloading, and a model-agnostic training loop.

- **Modular Configuration System:** The system is built on Draccus, which uses frozen dataclasses for parameter declaration. Parameters can be overridden hierarchically via YAML presets or command-line arguments, with CLI arguments taking highest precedence. YAML files support nested includes, allowing for composable and reusable configuration blocks. A `–resolve_configs` flag assists in verifying the merged configuration.
- **Extending the Framework:** New models are integrated by defining a frozen dataclass for hyperparameters and a factory function decorated with `@register_model`. This approach avoids central configuration file modifications. Each model selects a `BatchHandler` (e.g., for LLM, VLM, DP-VLA) that encapsulates batching, loss construction, and output reduction, maintaining a model-agnostic training loop. New training paradigms can register additional handlers via `@register_batch_handler`.
- **Dataloading:** The framework utilizes WebDatasets to store data in tar shards. The data processing pipeline is defined as a sequential composition of stages, supporting both WebDataset built-in functions and user-defined functions. This modularity allows for flexible extension, particularly for robotics-specific processing.
- **Dataset Mixing:** VLA Foundry natively supports mixing multiple datasets. Dataset-related arguments are lists, and a `dataset_weighting` parameter controls batch balancing ratios, enabling probabilistic mixing.
- **Preprocessing and Manifests:** Custom scripts are provided to convert raw datasets into WebDataset tar shards for text, image-caption, and robotics modalities. These scripts are parallelized using Ray. Robotics data preprocessing involves staging data into `frames`, `episodes`, and `shards` folders. Each dataset's `shards` folder contains a `manifest.json` and a `stats.json` file. The `stats.json` file stores precomputed statistics for normalization.

**Robotics Data Handling:**
- **Normalization:** The `RoboticsNormalizer` class handles action and proprioceptive state normalization, supporting standard deviation, min-max, and percentile-based methods (e.g., percentile\_1\_99, percentile\_5\_95). Normalization can be applied globally or per-timestep. Statistics are precomputed during preprocessing. For multi-dataset training, `stats.json` files can be merged; means are weighted by sample count, standard deviations use the law of total variance, min/max are element-wise, and percentiles use t-digest sketches.
- **Absolute vs. Relative Actions:** The framework supports both absolute (world-frame poses) and relative (computed with respect to the robot’s actual pose at the anchor timestep) action representations. Rotations are handled in the 6D continuous format.
- **Past/Future Action Window:** Training samples are constructed around an anchor timestep `t`, spanning `[t − N_past, t + N_future]` timesteps. This allows conditioning on past history and predicting future action chunks. Padding strategies (copy, zero, reflect) are configurable for episode boundaries.
- **Proprioception:** Proprioceptive state fields are processed separately, typically including joint positions, velocities, and end-effector poses. Crucially, proprioception is causally restricted to past and current timesteps, distinguishing it from actions which include future predictions.

**Training Performance:**
The training loop is model-agnostic and supports standard distributed training techniques: FSDP (with optional CPU offloading), mixed precision, gradient checkpointing, `torch.compile`, and gradient accumulation. Training budgets are expressed in samples for consistent comparison across different batch sizes or GPU counts. Throughput scaling tests were conducted on LLM, VLM, and VLA stages using up to 128 GPUs, demonstrating performance scaling, though for the specific model scales tested, FSDP did not consistently outperform DDP.

**Evaluation:**
- VLA Foundry supports evaluation on `lbm_eval_oss`, the open-source release of the LBM simulation benchmark. This benchmark uses the Drake physics engine for high-fidelity modeling of 49 bimanual manipulation tasks.
- The simulator is provided as a Docker image for ease of use. A dashboard enables management of evaluation experiments, viewing rollout videos, and plotting results.
- Rigorous statistical analysis is performed using STEP, which offers Bayesian estimates of success rates and Compact Letter Display (CLD) for identifying statistically significant differences (5% family-wise error rate). This framework allows for adaptive evaluation budgets (stopping early or collecting more data) without compromising statistical validity, by ensuring unbiased aggregation of per-task sample sizes.

**Demonstration Models:**
The framework’s utility is demonstrated through two VLA model types:
- **FOUNDRY-VLA-1.7B:** Trained entirely from scratch following an LLM→VLM→VLA pipeline.
 - **LLM Training:** A 1.2 billion-parameter transformer was trained on 1 trillion tokens from the DCLM dataset.
 - **VLM Training:** An 86 million-parameter randomly initialized Vision Transformer (ViT) was added to the LLM, and the VLM was trained on 200 million samples of DataCompDR-1B.
 - **VLA Training:** A 325 million-parameter flow transformer action head was added to the VLM, conditioned by the VLM’s hidden features corresponding to an observation token.
- **FOUNDRY-QWEN3VLA-2.1B-MT:** This model leverages a pretrained Qwen3-VL 2B backbone and uses the same action flow transformer architecture as FOUNDRY-VLA-1.7B.
Both VLA models were trained on a multi-task mixture of real and simulated teleoperated demonstrations from bimanual manipulation stations, encompassing 42 simulation tasks and 361 real-world tasks, with 39 tasks replicated across both domains. Ablation studies included training `FOUNDRY-VLA-1.7B` on simulation-only and real-only data subsets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings related to both the VLA Foundry framework itself and the performance of the models trained using it.

**Framework Capabilities and Performance:**
- **Unification and Controllability:** VLA Foundry successfully unifies LLM, VLM, and VLA training within a single codebase, providing end-to-end control over the entire pipeline. This enables researchers to intervene at any stage, from language pretraining to action fine-tuning.
- **Modularity and Flexibility:** The framework's modular, configuration-driven design allows for the seamless swapping of architectural components, data pipelines, and training recipes. It supports both training models entirely from scratch and integrating pretrained backbones from Hugging Face.
- **Scalability:** Benchmarking demonstrated the framework's capability for scalable distributed training, achieving throughput scaling across LLM, VLM, and VLA stages. For the 1.2B parameter LLM, 86M parameter ViT, and 325M parameter action head models, near-linear scaling was observed with DDP for LLM training.
- **Reproducibility and Evaluation:** The integration of `lbm_eval_oss` (a Dockerized open-source simulator) and STEP (statistical analysis tools) provides a robust and accessible evaluation pipeline, facilitating rigorous comparison of robot policies with features like unbiased aggregation and statistical significance testing.

**Model Performance:**

1. **FOUNDRY-VLA-1.7B (From-Scratch Pipeline):**
 - **LLM Performance:** The `Foundry-LLM-1.2B` model, trained on 1 trillion tokens from DCLM, achieved a HellaSwag score of 66.7, an ARC-e score of 71.7, and a PIQA score of 77.5, demonstrating language understanding capabilities. Performance on more challenging benchmarks like MMLU (26.6%) and ARC-c (39.3%) indicated room for improvement, consistent with models not undergoing instruction tuning or being larger in scale.
 - **VLM Performance:** The `Foundry-VLM-1.3B` model showed functional image captioning capabilities on COCO_VAL, achieving a BLEU-4 score of 15.57 and a CIDEr score of 55.14.
 - **VLA Performance on `lbm_eval_cs` (Closed-Source Seen Tasks):**
 - The `Foundry-VLA-1.7B-MT-sim` variant (trained on simulation-only data) performed statistically on par with the prior closed-source `LBM-MT` model in aggregate.
 - The `Foundry-VLA-1.7B` model (trained on a mixture of real and simulated data) exhibited weaker performance compared to `LBM-MT` and `Foundry-VLA-1.7B-MT-sim`.
 - **VLA Performance on `lbm_eval_oss` (Open-Source Seen Tasks):** For the `Foundry-VLA-1.7B` series, multi-task (MT) training and subsequent finetuning (FT) generally resulted in statistically worse aggregate performance compared to single-task (ST) training.
 - **VLA Performance on `lbm_eval_oss` (Open-Source Unseen Tasks):** The `Foundry-VLA-1.7B` series demonstrated non-zero success rates in zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks, but finetuning did not consistently improve performance over single-task training.
 - **Data Subset Ablations:** Training `Foundry-VLA-1.7B` solely on real data (`Foundry-VLA-1.7B-MT-real`) resulted in near 0% success rates on the simulation benchmark, as expected due to the distribution shift. The simulation-only trained variant (`Foundry-VLA-1.7B-MT-sim`) performed best among the from-scratch variants.

2. **FOUNDRY-QWEN3VLA-2.1B-MT (Pretrained Backbone Pipeline):**
 - **VLA Performance on `lbm_eval_cs` (Closed-Source Seen Tasks):** This model demonstrated superior performance, significantly outperforming the `LBM-MT` baseline by over 20 percentage points in aggregate success rate, establishing it as a capable multi-task table-top manipulation policy.
 - **VLA Performance on `lbm_eval_oss` (Open-Source Seen Tasks):** The `Foundry-Qwen3VLA-2.1B-MT` model series showed a clear progression, with aggregate performance increasing from single-task training to multi-task training, and further improving with finetuning on specific tasks.
 - **VLA Performance on `lbm_eval_oss` (Open-Source Unseen Tasks):** This model achieved non-zero success rates in zero-shot generalization. Additionally, finetuning the multi-task model on unseen tasks resulted in improved aggregate performance compared to the single-task variant, supporting the hypothesis that stronger backbones can lead to better policy outcomes.
 - **Efficiency of Integration:** The results confirmed that the traditional VLM→VLA recipe using pretrained backbones can be efficiently reproduced within VLA Foundry, leveraging its unified training loop, dataloader, and preprocessing pipeline.

**Evaluation Environment Differences:**
Comparison of model performance on the `lbm_eval_cs` (closed-source) and `lbm_eval_oss` (open-source) benchmarks indicated substantial variation. Models generally exhibited weaker performance on `lbm_eval_oss`, suggesting it acts as a distribution-shifted version of the closed-source environment, likely due to updates between the two versions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLA Foundry framework and its accompanying models hold several significant implications and potential impacts for the robotics and AI research communities:

1. **Advancing Robotics Foundation Model Research:** By providing a unified, open-source framework for LLM, VLM, and VLA training, VLA Foundry offers a critical tool for systematic investigation into the fundamental questions facing robotics foundation models. This includes exploring optimal data scaling strategies, the impact of different backbone pretraining approaches, and the complex interplay between robotics and non-robotics data modalities. The end-to-end control offered by the framework enables researchers to design and execute experiments that were previously challenging due to fragmented tooling.

2. **Lowering Barriers to Entry and Enhancing Reproducibility:** The release of the VLA Foundry codebase, alongside multi-task model weights and a standardized evaluation pipeline (Dockerized `lbm_eval_oss` with STEP statistical analysis), significantly lowers the barrier for researchers to engage with and contribute to advanced robotics research. The emphasis on configuration-driven design, deterministic seeding, and checkpointing promotes reproducibility of experiments, a crucial factor for scientific progress in fast-moving fields.

3. **Demonstrating the Value of Strong Backbones:** The empirical results, particularly the substantial performance improvement demonstrated by `FOUNDRY-QWEN3VLA-2.1B-MT` over prior baselines and the from-scratch model, highlight a key insight: leveraging powerful, pre-trained VLM backbones can translate directly into significantly more capable VLA policies. This finding encourages further research into the selection, adaptation, and co-training of state-of-the-art LLM/VLM components for robotics applications.

4. **Enabling Flexible Architectural and Data Experimentation:** The framework's modularity and support for probabilistic dataset mixing empower researchers to rapidly prototype and evaluate new architectures, fusion strategies, and data recipes. This flexibility is essential for iterating on embodied AI systems, where optimal configurations for diverse tasks and environments are still being discovered.

5. **Foundation for Future Research Directions:** VLA Foundry is positioned as a foundation for addressing several open research questions outlined by the authors. This includes characterizing optimal multi-modal data recipes, integrating additional simulation suites (e.g., LIBERO, SimplerEnv, RoboCasa), supporting new robot embodiments, and exploring different action head architectures (e.g., autoregressive discrete action tokenizations). Furthermore, the framework provides an environment to investigate critical, yet underexplored, areas like safety, alignment, and failure-mode detection for embodied agents.

In summary, VLA Foundry has the potential to accelerate the development and understanding of robotics foundation models by providing a comprehensive, flexible, and open-source platform that bridges the gap between language, vision, and action learning, while promoting rigorous evaluation practices within the research community.
