# ArtHOI: Articulated Human-Object Interaction Synthesis by 4D Reconstruction from Video Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> ArtHOI synthesizes articulated human-object interactions zero-shot by treating a text-generated diffusion video as supervision for a 4D inverse-rendering problem. Optical-flow part segmentation splits object Gaussians into moving and static regions, then a decoupled pipeline first recovers object articulation and afterwards refines SMPL-X human motion under that fixed geometry, cutting mean rotation error to 6.71 degrees and raising hand-object contact to 75.64%.

## Problem

- Zero-shot video-diffusion interaction methods treat manipulated objects as single rigid bodies, so everyday part-wise articulation like doors, drawers and cabinets stays unmodeled.
- Prior pipelines generate interactions end-to-end from 2D priors without explicit 4D geometric reconstruction, yielding penetration, floating hands and geometrically inconsistent contact.
- Articulated-reconstruction work needs category templates, known kinematic chains or multi-view input, while unsupervised part discovery ignores the contact cues human interaction provides.

## Method

- ==Flow-based part segmentation==: ==point-tracker== trajectories thresholded at 5 and 2 pixels seed ==SAM== prompts, back-projected onto ==3D Gaussians==, with ==quasi-static binding pairs== linking dynamic and static parts at hinges.
- Stage I optimizes per-frame ==SE(3) part transforms== under ==reconstruction==, ==tracking==, ==smoothness== and ==articulation-regularization losses== that preserve binding-pair distances, producing a fixed geometrically consistent 4D object scaffold.
- Stage II refines ==SMPL-X== pose with ==kinematic==, ==collision==, ==foot-sliding== and ==prior losses==, lifting 3D contact targets from human-occluded object silhouette regions plus nearest dynamic-Gaussian depth.

## Results

- On monocular videos rendered from ArtGS scenes, mean rotation error is **6.71 degrees**, a **73.3%** cut versus D3D-HOI at **25.13** and **68.3%** versus 3DADN at **21.17**.
- Interaction quality leads all baselines: X-CLIP **0.244**, contact **75.64%** versus ZeroHSI's **61.95%**, penetration **0.08%** versus **1.49%**; **51** participants preferred it **89.42%** over ZeroHSI.
- Ablations: one-stage joint optimization drops contact to **61.45%** and nearly doubles rotation error to **12.34 degrees**; removing articulation regularization raises rotation error **133%** to **15.67**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling optimization by motion modality beats joint optimization when monocular supervision cannot disambiguate whether image motion came from the human or the object.
> - Optical flow is a stronger part-boundary cue than appearance, and human occlusion of an object silhouette is a free, usable 3D contact signal.
> - Smoothness metrics mislead when contact is rare; low-texture surfaces, multi-DOF joints and moving cameras still break flow-driven articulation recovery.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of 3D and 4D content generation, particularly in Human-Object Interaction (HOI) synthesis. This field is critical for applications in computer graphics, virtual/augmented reality (VR/AR), embodied AI, and robotics.

Traditionally, HOI synthesis relied heavily on motion capture (mocap) datasets, which are expensive to acquire and limited in generalization to novel objects or interaction types. Recent advancements have shifted towards learning-based methods that leverage language prompts or sparse waypoints, but these often require extensive training on interaction-specific data and known object kinematics.

A significant trend has been the emergence of "zero-shot" methods, which utilize large pre-trained foundation models, especially video diffusion models (VDMs), as powerful priors to generate 4D human-object interactions without explicit 3D/4D ground truth supervision. Prior zero-shot approaches, such as GenZi and ZeroHSI, have shown promise but are largely confined to rigid-object manipulation. They treat dynamic objects as single rigid bodies, failing to model complex part-wise articulation (e.g., opening a door, drawer, or cabinet). This limitation is a major gap, as many real-world interactions involve articulated objects. Furthermore, these methods often generate interactions end-to-end from 2D priors without explicit 4D geometric reconstruction, leading to physically implausible or geometrically inconsistent results, especially regarding contact and penetration.

Concurrently, the field of 4D scene reconstruction has seen advances in reconstructing temporally coherent 4D representations from monocular videos, often leveraging neural radiance fields or 3D Gaussian splatting. However, these typically focus on rigid scenes or simple object dynamics, leaving articulated human-object interactions largely unaddressed in a zero-shot, physically plausible manner. Similarly, research into articulated object reconstruction often relies on category-level templates or multi-view inputs, or focuses purely on object-centric motion without leveraging human interaction cues.

ArtHOI directly addresses these gaps by:
1. Extending zero-shot HOI synthesis to *articulated objects*.
2. Introducing *explicit 4D geometric reasoning* through a reconstruction-based approach, ensuring physical plausibility.
3. Achieving this from *monocular video priors* without any 3D supervision, thus improving generalization and reducing data dependency.

It bridges the gap between video-based generation (using VDMs for initial 2D motion priors) and geometry-aware reconstruction (inverse rendering to recover 4D structure), moving beyond rigid manipulation to genuinely articulated interaction synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ArtHOI is to synthesize physically plausible articulated human-object interactions (HOI) without requiring 3D/4D supervision. This is framed as a 4D reconstruction problem from monocular video priors.

The key motivations driving this research are:
- **Addressing limitations of existing zero-shot HOI methods:** Current zero-shot approaches using video diffusion models are restricted to rigid objects, failing to model complex, everyday interactions with articulated objects (e.g., opening a fridge, cabinet, or microwave). This significantly limits their real-world applicability.
- **Ensuring physical plausibility and geometric consistency:** End-to-end 2D prior-driven generation methods often lack explicit 4D geometric reasoning, leading to physically implausible results like object penetration or unnatural contact. ArtHOI aims to produce interactions that naturally respect contact, articulation, and temporal coherence.
- **Overcoming monocular ambiguity in joint optimization:** Jointly optimizing human motion and object articulation under monocular observation is highly ambiguous. It's challenging to disentangle whether observed 2D motion comes from human movement, object articulation, or both. This often leads to unstable convergence and conflicting gradients in a single-stage approach. ArtHOI seeks a stable and robust solution.
- **Leveraging video diffusion models as effective 2D priors:** Recognizing the power of pretrained video diffusion models to generate diverse semantic actions, the motivation is to effectively "ground" these 2D priors into coherent and physically consistent 4D scenes, rather than just generating 2D videos.
- **Enabling zero-shot generalization:** The goal is to synthesize novel interactions with novel articulated objects without requiring expensive 3D interaction data or category-specific object templates, thereby promoting broader applicability in various domains.

In essence, ArtHOI aims to enable machines to "understand" and generate complex, real-world articulated human-object interactions by reconstructing their underlying 4D geometry from readily available 2D video information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ArtHOI formulates articulated HOI synthesis as a 4D reconstruction problem from monocular video priors. Instead of directly generating 3D interactions, it first leverages a video diffusion model to generate a 2D video from a text prompt, and then reconstructs a full 4D articulated scene through inverse rendering using this 2D video as supervision. The human is parameterized by SMPL-X, and articulated object parts are represented by rigid SE(3) transformations. Both are represented using 3D Gaussians for end-to-end optimization.

The core of ArtHOI is a decoupled two-stage reconstruction framework:

#### 3.1. Flow-based Part Segmentation

This initial stage is crucial for identifying which parts of an object are articulated (dynamic) versus static, a key challenge under monocular observation. Motion is leveraged as the primary cue.
- **Point Tracking and Motion Classification:** A pre-trained point tracking network (CoTracker) generates dense 2D trajectories. Points on the object mask are tracked, and their 2D displacement across frames determines if they are dynamic (articulated) or static.
- **SAM-guided Dense Mask:** Sparse point-level labels from tracking are refined into dense, accurate masks using Segment Anything Model (SAM). Clustered dynamic and static points serve as positive and negative prompts to SAM, providing precise part boundaries. The object is rendered from a canonical pose to avoid human occlusion during SAM processing.
- **Back Projection to 3D Gaussians:** The 2D SAM mask information is transferred to the 3D Gaussian representation. Each pixel in the dynamic mask influences nearby 3D Gaussians in 2D image space, weighted by distance and opacity. Gaussians are then assigned to either dynamic (G_d) or static (G_s) groups. Connectivity refinement is applied in 3D to ensure spatially coherent partitions.
- **Quasi-static Binding:** To enforce rigid-body constraints at articulation points (like hinges), quasi-static points (dynamic points with low motion magnitude) are identified near the articulation boundary. Corresponding 3D Gaussians from the dynamic set are bound to their nearest static Gaussians within a defined radius, forming binding constraints.

#### 3.2. Decoupled Two-Stage Reconstruction

This strategy addresses the monocular ambiguity and instability of jointly optimizing human and object dynamics by separating them.

**Stage I: Object Articulation Reconstruction**
In this stage, the focus is solely on reconstructing articulated object motion. This is achieved by optimizing per-frame SE(3) transformations (rotation R_d(t) and translation t_d(t)) for the dynamic parts, while static parts remain fixed. Object Gaussians are driven by articulation parameters and weights determined from flow-based segmentation.
The optimization objective combines four losses:
- **Reconstruction Loss (L_o^r):** Ensures the rendered object (RGB and silhouette) matches the video prior, driven by 3D Gaussian Splatting.
- **Articulation Loss (L_a):** Maintains the distance between quasi-static binding pairs (from the segmentation stage) over time, enforcing rigid-body kinematic constraints at the hinge.
- **Tracking Loss (L_tr):** Aligns the 2D projection of dynamic Gaussians with the dense 2D trajectories obtained from the point tracker, providing direct 2D motion supervision for articulated parts.
- **Smoothness Loss (L_s):** Penalizes abrupt changes in object articulation transformations across frames to ensure temporal coherence.
This stage is optimized frame-by-frame, with subsequent frames warm-started from previous solutions to maintain temporal consistency.

**Stage II: Human Motion Refinement**
With the object articulation fixed from Stage I, this stage refines human motion, parameterized by SMPL-X, under the reconstructed 4D object geometry. The main challenge here is deriving accurate 3D contact targets without multi-view input.
- **3D Contact Keypoint Derivation:** This is a crucial step.
 1. **Contact Frame Selection:** Frames where the object is actively articulating (indicating human interaction) are identified by monitoring changes in the object's SE(3) transformations.
 2. **2D Contact Region:** In these frames, the overlap between the human mask and the object's rendered silhouette (where the object's SAM mask is absent) identifies 2D contact regions where a human limb is occluding the object.
 3. **Joint Assignment:** 2D keypoints from an initial human pose estimate (e.g., GVHMR) falling within these 2D contact regions are selected. Hand and fingertip joints are prioritized.
 4. **3D Lifting:** For each selected 2D keypoint, the nearest dynamic object Gaussians in image space are identified. The Gaussian closest to the camera is chosen as the 3D contact target for that keypoint, optionally offset to prevent penetration.
The optimization objective for human motion includes:
- **Reconstruction Loss (L_h^r):** Aligns the rendered human (RGB and silhouette) with the video.
- **Kinematic Loss (L_k):** Pulls the human's hand joints towards the derived 3D contact keypoints, ensuring proper hand-object interaction.
- **Prior Loss (L_p):** Regularizes the human motion towards an initial VDM-estimated pose, preventing unnatural movements.
- **Foot Sliding Loss (L_fs):** Penalizes unrealistic horizontal foot movement during ground contact, promoting stability.
- **Collision Loss (L_c):** A hinge loss that penalizes penetration between human mesh vertices (especially hands) and object Gaussians.
- **Smoothness Loss (L_s):** Ensures temporal coherence of human pose parameters.
Stage II optimizes all human motion parameters jointly across all frames, conditioned on the now-fixed object dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ArtHOI's performance is evaluated through quantitative metrics, qualitative comparisons, a user study, and ablation studies, demonstrating significant advancements in articulated human-object interaction synthesis.

#### 4.1. Interaction Quality Results

ArtHOI demonstrates superior performance in synthesizing articulated human-object interactions:
- **Semantic Alignment (X-CLIP Score):** Achieves the highest X-CLIP score (0.244), indicating better alignment between synthesized interactions and textual descriptions compared to all baselines (e.g., ZeroHSI: 0.204, LINGO: 0.205).
- **Foot Sliding:** Achieves the lowest foot sliding score (0.31), implying more realistic and stable foot contact during interactions.
- **Contact Accuracy (Contact%):** Attains the highest contact percentage (75.64%), significantly outperforming ZeroHSI (61.95%) and other baselines, indicating consistent and proper human-object contact.
- **Physical Plausibility (Penetration%):** Records the lowest penetration errors (0.08), showing superior physical realism by effectively preventing human-object interpenetration, especially compared to ZeroHSI (1.49).
- **Motion Smoothness:** While having a slightly higher smoothness score than some non-zero-shot methods (e.g., LINGO 0.30), the paper clarifies that these lower scores often stem from minimal contact with articulated objects, which simplifies the motion. ArtHOI maintains competitive smoothness (0.87) even with high contact rates (75.64%), indicating a better balance between naturalness and interaction complexity.

Qualitative comparisons (Figure 4) visually confirm that ArtHOI recovers geometrically consistent and physically plausible 4D scenes with diverse articulated objects, successfully modeling part-wise articulation unlike rigid-object baselines.

#### 4.2. Articulated Object Dynamics Results

Compared to specialized articulated object estimation methods (D3D-HOI, 3DADN) under monocular settings, ArtHOI exhibits dramatically superior accuracy in recovering object articulation:
- **Rotation Errors:** Achieves a mean rotation error of 6.71 degrees, a 73.3% reduction compared to D3D-HOI (25.13 degrees) and 68.3% reduction compared to 3DADN (21.17 degrees). It also shows lower maximum and minimum rotation errors. This validates the effectiveness of ArtHOI's approach to deriving accurate articulated object dynamics from 2D video priors without 3D supervision.

#### 4.3. Rigid Object Results

Although designed for articulated HOI, ArtHOI generalizes well to rigid objects. Compared to SAM3D+FP and ZeroHSI on rigid object interactions, ArtHOI achieves the best Foot Sliding (0.28), Contact% (76.18%), and Penetration% (0.06%), demonstrating its robustness and superior physical plausibility even for simpler object types.

#### 4.4. User Study

A comprehensive user study with 51 participants showed a consistent preference for ArtHOI over baselines across realism, contact quality, motion smoothness, and overall preference. For instance, ArtHOI was preferred over TRUMANS in 98.04% of cases overall, and over ZeroHSI in 89.42% of cases overall. This underscores the perceived quality and naturalness of the interactions generated by ArtHOI.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments confirm the critical contribution of each key component:
- **Two-stage decoupling vs. joint optimization:** Joint optimization severely degrades interaction metrics (X-CLIP 0.187 vs. 0.244, Contact% 61.45% vs. 75.64%) and increases articulation errors (Rot mean 12.34° vs. 6.71°). This confirms the necessity of decoupling to avoid conflicting gradients and ensure stable convergence.
- **Articulation regularization (L_a):** Removing L_a (distance preservation for quasi-static binding pairs) leads to a significant increase in articulation error (Rot mean 15.67° vs. 6.71°, a 133% increase), causing parts to drift and degrading contact quality (Contact% 68.75% vs. 75.64%).
- **Kinematic loss (L_k):** Eliminating L_k (pulling hand joints to 3D contact keypoints) drastically reduces contact (Contact% 59.82% vs. 75.64%) and worsens X-CLIP and Foot Sliding, highlighting its essential role in resolving monocular depth ambiguity and ensuring realistic hand-object alignment.
- **Smoothness loss (L_s):** Removing L_s results in degraded articulation and interaction metrics (e.g., Contact% 65.43% vs. 75.64%), indicating its importance for temporal coherence and stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Conclusion

ArtHOI marks a significant advancement in the field of 4D content generation by introducing the first zero-shot framework capable of synthesizing articulated human-object interactions from monocular video priors. By framing the problem as 4D reconstruction through inverse rendering, ArtHOI effectively integrates explicit geometric and kinematic priors into monocular dynamics. The key innovations, including flow-guided part segmentation and a decoupled two-stage optimization pipeline, successfully address the challenges of articulation modeling, monocular ambiguity, and physical plausibility. The resulting interactions are both semantically aligned with user intent and physically grounded, outperforming previous zero-shot methods that were limited to rigid objects.

#### 5.2. Limitations

Despite its strengths, ArtHOI has several limitations:
- **Single-part Articulated Objects:** The current framework primarily focuses on objects with a single degree of articulation (e.g., a single door or drawer). It struggles with objects having multiple degrees of freedom or complex hierarchical articulated structures.
- **Optical Flow Tracking Failures:** The reliance on optical flow (e.g., CoTracker) can lead to issues with low-texture, reflective, or ambiguous regions on object surfaces, causing distortions that propagate into articulation prediction.
- **Non-rigid Articulations:** The method assumes rigid-body articulation. It is not designed for objects with non-rigid parts, soft-body joints, or elastic connections.
- **Long-term Temporal Consistency:** For very long interaction sequences, cumulative errors in articulation extraction and human motion refinement might lead to gradual deviations from physical plausibility or increased jitter.
- **Fixed Camera Assumption:** The current approach assumes a fixed camera. Interactions with moving cameras introduce complex ego-motion challenges that would make kinematic recovery significantly harder.

#### 5.3. Broader Impact

ArtHOI has substantial practical implications across several domains:
- **Robotics:** It can generate vast amounts of realistic training data for manipulation policies involving articulated objects. This capability bypasses the need for expensive motion capture or laborious manual annotation, facilitating scalable simulation-to-real transfer for robotic systems.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality (VR/AR):** The framework enables the creation of highly realistic human-object interactions for games, virtual environments, and telepresence applications. This reduces the reliance on handcrafted animations, allowing for more dynamic and diverse virtual experiences.
- **Embodied AI and Data Synthesis:** The zero-shot pipeline allows for rapid and diverse generation of physically plausible 4D interaction datasets from simple text prompts. Such datasets are invaluable for research in scene understanding, action recognition, and contact-aware motion prediction, accelerating progress in embodied AI.
- **Content Creation:** Its efficiency (approximately 30 minutes per scene on a single GPU) makes it suitable for rapid prototyping and iterative content creation workflows in animation and multimedia production.

By extending zero-shot interaction synthesis to articulated objects and grounding it in 4D geometric reconstruction, ArtHOI significantly pushes the boundaries of generative AI, offering a versatile tool for future research and applications involving complex human-environment interactions.
