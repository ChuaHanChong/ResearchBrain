# NuWa: Deriving Lightweight Task-Specific Vision Transformers for Edge Devices

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Australian universities introduce NuWa, a framework that derives lightweight task-specific Vision Transformers from pre-trained models through dimension-specific structured pruning, achieving superior accuracy while enabling deployment on edge devices with significant inference speedups on both GPU and CPU platforms.

## Problem

- Standard Vision Transformers are too computationally expensive and memory-intensive for edge devices
- Pre-trained ViTs contain redundant knowledge irrelevant to specific edge tasks, reducing efficiency and accuracy
- Deriving numerous customized models for heterogeneous edge devices with different class requirements and resource constraints is computationally expensive and time-consuming, largely due to repeated retraining

## Method

- Two-stage ==pruning== approach combining one-shot pruning for depth, classifier size, and attention heads
- Adaptive pruning strategy for query-key size, value size, expansion size, and embedding dimensions using SVD-based techniques
- Iterative fine-tuning process to recover performance after pruning

## Results

- Outperforms baseline pruning methods (Random Pruning, X-Pruner, NViT) on various sub-tasks while maintaining high accuracy despite reducing model size through structured pruning
- Achieves significant inference speedups on both GPU and CPU platforms
- Demonstrates improved attention focus on task-relevant features through visualization

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dimension-specific pruning strategies are more effective than uniform compression
> - SVD-based decomposition helps preserve important knowledge during pruning
> - Task-specific derivation enables better resource utilization than generic compression

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have demonstrated efficacy across a range of visual tasks, including image recognition, object detection, and instance segmentation. Their effectiveness, however, typically necessitates substantial computational and storage resources. This requirement poses a challenge for their deployment on resource-constrained edge devices, such as drones and smart vehicles, where real-time processing and limited energy budgets are critical.

The broader research landscape addressing this challenge focuses on model compression techniques, including low-bit quantization, knowledge distillation, and various forms of pruning (structured and unstructured). Structured pruning, which maintains regular model shapes, is particularly compatible with diverse hardware and offers scalability for edge deployment.

Existing structured pruning methods generally aim to reduce overall model size and computational cost while preserving general task performance. However, these methods often operate under a class-agnostic paradigm, meaning they do not consider that many edge applications only require recognition or processing for a specific subset of classes. Consequently, models derived using these approaches may retain knowledge redundant or even detrimental to the target classes, leading to suboptimal performance and inefficient resource utilization for class-specific tasks. Furthermore, the derivation of numerous customized models for heterogeneous edge devices with varying class requirements and resource constraints remains a computationally expensive and time-consuming process, largely due to the iterative search for pruning configurations and the necessity of extensive retraining to recover accuracy.

NuWa addresses these identified limitations by introducing a method specifically designed for deriving class-specific ViTs for edge devices. It differentiates itself by recognizing and actively addressing the issue of "class-detrimental weights" and by providing an efficient, retraining-free solution for model derivation, thus fitting into the landscape as an advancement in targeted, efficient ViT deployment on the edge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the NuWa research are to develop a cost-efficient method capable of deriving accurate and lightweight class-specific Vision Transformers from pre-trained base ViTs for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices, without requiring post-pruning retraining. This is motivated by two fundamental limitations observed in existing model compression approaches:

#### 2.1. Neglect of Class-Detrimental Weights

Current model compression methods, including structured pruning, primarily focus on identifying and removing "unimportant" weights to minimize the loss of useful knowledge. The implicit assumption is that any pruning will degrade model performance, and the goal is to mitigate this degradation. However, the authors observed that in a class-specific derivation setting, certain weights, termed "class-detrimental weights," can actively interfere with a model's specialization on target classes. Their removal can, counter-intuitively, improve the model's performance on these specific classes.

This observation is evidenced by experiments showing that randomly pruning a subset of neurons from the Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) modules of a DeiT-Base model can unexpectedly increase its accuracy on specific ImageNet classes. Traditional importance metrics (e.g., magnitude, activation, gradient, Taylor expansion approximation) fail to identify these detrimental weights, even when calculated using class-specific data. Consequently, existing pruning methods, by not identifying and removing such detrimental knowledge, produce derived edge ViTs that are suboptimal for their intended class-specific applications. The motivation here is to enable the model to autonomously identify and prune these class-detrimental weights to enhance class-specific performance.

#### 2.2. High Time and Computation Cost for Customization

Edge device applications are characterized by diverse requirements concerning target classes and available computational resources. This necessitates the derivation of a large number of customized models, each tailored to specific operational contexts and hardware constraints. Existing pruning methods, especially training-dependent ones, involve extensive search for optimal pruning configurations and often rely on computationally expensive retraining to restore model accuracy after pruning. This process incurs significant computational and time overheads, which are exacerbated when custom models need to be derived from scratch for every new combination of target classes or pruning rates, due to a lack of reusable intermediate results.

For instance, deriving a single model can take several hours and incur substantial cloud computing costs. This inefficiency renders large-scale deployment of customized ViTs impractical for diverse edge environments. The motivation is to overcome this scalability issue by developing a method that can efficiently derive compact ViTs without retraining, significantly reducing the computational and temporal overhead for customization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NuWa, a cost-efficient method, addresses the identified challenges through a two-step process: Self-Knowledge Purification (SKP) and Optimization-based Fast Pruning (OFP).

#### 3.1. Self-Knowledge Purification (SKP)

The objective of SKP is to identify and prune class-detrimental weights within the base ViT without prior knowledge of their location or quantity.

- **Constructing Pruning Space:** NuWa freezes the parameters of a pre-trained base ViT ($V_B$). It then embeds learnable mask vectors ($M^{(l)} \in \mathbb{R}^{e_l}$) and control factors ($\beta^{(l)} \in \mathbb{R}^1$) into the MLP modules of each transformer block (where $e_l$ is the intermediate dimension of the $l$-th MLP). A binarization operation, defined by a threshold based on the $k$-th largest value in $M^{(l)}$ and influenced by $\sigma(\beta^{(l)})$, is applied to $M^{(l)}$ to create a structured pruning space. A mask value of 0 corresponds to pruning the associated neuron. $\beta^{(l)}$ is initialized to a high value (5.0) to initially retain all neurons. SKP is intentionally applied only to MLP modules, as empirical observations suggested better results compared to applying it to Multi-Head Attention (MHA) modules or both.
- **Knowledge Purification:** To enable the model to learn and prune detrimental weights, a Straight-Through Estimator (STE) is used to handle the non-differentiability of the binarization operation during training. The learnable masks ($M$) and control factors ($B$) are updated using class-specific data ($D_S$) under the supervision of the original visual task loss ($L_T$). The base ViT's weights remain frozen. This training process guides the model to autonomously identify and filter out class-detrimental knowledge. After this process, the MLP modules of $V_B$ are pruned based on the learned binarized masks, resulting in a refined "anchor model" ($V_A$). This $V_A$ is designed to be smaller yet more accurate on the target classes, having removed knowledge detrimental to them.

#### 3.2. Optimization-based Fast Pruning (OFP)

The anchor model ($V_A$) may still require further compression to satisfy strict edge device resource constraints. OFP efficiently achieves this by formulating pruning tasks as optimization problems with closed-form solutions, eliminating the need for retraining.

- **Pruning MHA Modules:**
 - **Target:** NuWa prunes the query-key (QK) and value-output (VO) dimensions of the MHA modules. This fine-grained pruning offers high compression potential and allows pruning results for these dimensions to be shared across all sub-tasks, as they are independent of input features.
 - **Formulation:** The pruning of MHA modules is formulated as minimizing the Frobenius norm of the difference between the original composite weight matrix ($W_{QK} = W_Q^T W_K$) and its pruned approximation ($W_{QK}' = W_Q'^T W_K'$).
 - **Solution:** This is a low-rank matrix approximation problem, optimally solved by Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) according to the Eckart-Young theorem. NuWa performs SVD on $W_{QK}$ and $W_{VO}$ matrices.
 - **Control:** A hyperparameter $\rho$, representing the ratio of retained singular value energy to total energy, adaptively determines the pruned dimensions ($q_l'$ and $v_l'$), allowing a balance between MHA and MLP pruning intensity.
- **Pruning MLP Modules:**
 - **Target:** Further compress the intermediate dimensions ($e_l$) of the MLP modules in $V_A$ to achieve the desired overall pruning rate ($\alpha$).
 - **Dimension Determination:** To promote inference efficiency and avoid over-pruning in specific blocks, NuWa aims to prune all MLP modules to similar sizes using an iterative "block-uniform" strategy (Algorithm 1 in supplementary material).
 - **Neuron Selection:** NuWa performs one forward propagation of $V_A$ on $D_S$ to collect the mean activation value ($a_i^{(l)}$) of each neuron and block-wise activation features ($H^{(l)}$). The top $e_l'$ neurons with the highest activation values are retained.
 - **Formulation:** The pruning is formulated as minimizing the Frobenius norm of the difference between the original MLP output ($H^{(l)} W_2^{T(l)}$) and the output with pruned weights ($H^{(l)}[I_r^{(l)}] W_2'^{T(l)}$), where $I_r^{(l)}$ are the indices of retained neurons.
 - **Solution:** A closed-form solution is derived (Eq. 12) using a least-squares approach, specifically through the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse, to minimize knowledge loss.
- **Outcome:** Based on these closed-form solutions, NuWa efficiently prunes $V_A$ to derive the final class-specific edge ViT ($V_E$) without requiring further training or fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate NuWa involved six different ViT models (DeiT-B/S/T, ViT-L/16, Fast/Mask R-CNN Swin-T) across four datasets (ImageNet-1K, CIFAR-100, CIFAR-10, COCO2017) and various class-specific sub-tasks.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Training-Free Baselines

NuWa was compared against Magnitude Pruning, Wanda-sp, and Numerical Pruning on nine ImageNet sub-tasks (S1/10–S9/50) with varying pruning rates.
- **Improved Accuracy:** NuWa-derived edge ViTs consistently surpassed the base ViT in class-specific accuracy, a feat not achieved by existing training-free methods. For instance, at a 0.60 pruning rate, NuWa achieved an accuracy increase of up to 20.60% over the base ViT and up to 29.00% over the best-performing Numerical Pruning.
- **Anchor Model Performance:** The Self-Knowledge Purification (SKP) stage alone, which produces the anchor model, achieved an average pruning rate of 22.61% while improving average class-specific accuracy by 15.84%. This indicates the effectiveness of SKP in identifying and removing class-detrimental knowledge.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Training-Dependent Baselines

NuWa's performance was evaluated against X-Pruner, DC-ViT, RECAP, and MDP, which typically involve extensive retraining.
- **Competitive Accuracy Without Retraining:** NuWa achieved average class-specific accuracy improvements of 15.37% and 10.04% at pruning rates of 0.40 and 0.60, respectively, without any post-pruning retraining. It reached 99.35%, 100.03%, and 100.23% of the average accuracy of the best-performing X-Pruner, RECAP, and MDP, respectively.
- **Enhanced Accuracy with Fine-tuning:** With light fine-tuning (10 epochs), NuWa further surpassed these baselines by an average of 2.00%, 2.64%, and 2.82%.
- **Superior Efficiency:** In a simulated large-scale deployment scenario (50 sub-tasks, 10 devices/sub-task), NuWa demonstrated an average speedup of 33.69× over X-Pruner (the best-performing baseline in terms of accuracy) for deriving a single model, with only a 0.61% average accuracy loss. The derivation cost was reduced by up to 99.83% compared to X-Pruner, and by 99.70%–99.93% compared to other baselines. This efficiency is attributed to reusable pruning results and calibration features.

#### 4.3. Generality Across Models and Datasets

NuWa's generality was confirmed across various models and datasets:
- **Recognition Tasks:** For DeiT-Tiny, DeiT-Small, and ViT-Large on CIFAR-10/100, NuWa-derived models consistently outperformed their base counterparts on target classes up to pruning rates of 0.40, 0.50, and 0.60, respectively. Larger models tended to have a higher proportion of class-detrimental weights, leading to higher anchor model pruning rates.
- **Detection and Segmentation Tasks:** For Fast/Mask R-CNN (Swin-T) on COCO, NuWa-derived models also surpassed the base ViT in mean Average Precision (mAP) on target classes when the backbone pruning rate was below 0.40.

#### 4.4. Inference Speedups

Edge ViTs derived by NuWa demonstrated notable inference speedups:
- **Jetson Orin NX (batch size 1):** Achieved 1.31×–2.07× speedup and 26.47%–58.82% memory reduction compared to the base ViT.
- **NVIDIA RTX 4090 (batch size 256):** Achieved 1.30×–1.92× speedup and 12.67%–41.63% memory reduction.

#### 4.5. Interpretability and Ablation Studies

- **SKP Interpretation:** SKP functions by blurring class boundaries among non-target classes and suppressing their output probabilities, thereby enhancing the model's focus on target classes and reducing misclassifications.
- **Derivation Breakdown:** SKP contributes an average of 22.61% to the pruning rate, with MLP modules being the primary target for further OFP (two-thirds of total parameters). The SVD-based MHA pruning (P1) is data-free and reusable, while SKP calibration (P2) is reusable across pruning rates, and MLP pruning (P3) is computationally negligible.
- **Ablation Study:** Removing SKP resulted in an 18.75% accuracy drop, highlighting its criticality. Skipping MHA or MLP pruning reduced accuracy by 15.39% and 85.95%, respectively. Using random neuron selection instead of activation-based selection caused a 4.99% drop. Replacing the closed-form optimization with direct removal led to a 14.75% accuracy reduction. These results confirm the necessity of each component in NuWa.
- **Hyperparameter Analysis:** Smaller batch sizes during SKP enabled more thorough exploration of the pruning space. The retained energy ratio ($\rho$) for MHA pruning was found to adapt to the overall pruning rate ($\alpha$). 128 calibration samples were sufficient for optimal MLP pruning.

#### 4.6. Performance under Complex Settings (Supplementary Material)

- **Class Co-occurrence:** NuWa maintained stable performance in multi-label classification tasks on COCO2017, achieving 97.13% relative average precision to the base ViT at a 0.40 pruning rate.
- **Long-tailed Distributions:** Models derived by NuWa maintained stable accuracy across varying imbalance factors on ImageNet sub-tasks with 10 classes at a 0.40 pruning rate, demonstrating robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NuWa represents an advancement in the deployment of Vision Transformers on resource-constrained edge devices by specifically addressing the challenges of class-specific tasks. The work introduces two notable contributions:

- **Identification and Pruning of Class-Detrimental Weights:** The concept of "class-detrimental weights" and the proposed Self-Knowledge Purification (SKP) mechanism to identify and remove them is a novel approach. This enables the derivation of models that not only are smaller but also intrinsically perform better on their target classes, surpassing the original, larger base models without requiring additional training or fine-tuning for performance recovery. This 'less is more' outcome challenges conventional assumptions in model compression.
- **Efficient Model Derivation via Closed-Form Optimization:** By formulating pruning tasks for Multi-Head Attention and Multi-Layer Perceptron modules as optimization problems with closed-form solutions, NuWa eliminates the need for computationally intensive post-pruning retraining. This dramatically reduces the time and cost associated with deriving customized models, making the process significantly more scalable and practical for real-world heterogeneous edge deployments.

The potential impact of NuWa is substantial:

- **Enhanced Edge AI Performance:** It enables edge devices to execute ViT-based visual tasks with higher accuracy and efficiency for specific applications (e.g., recognizing specific objects for autonomous vehicles or drones), optimizing the use of limited computational and energy resources.
- **Accelerated Deployment and Customization:** The considerable speedup and cost reduction in model derivation can accelerate the development and deployment cycles of AI applications on the edge. This allows for rapid customization of models for diverse scenarios and device capabilities, which was previously impractical due to computational overheads.
- **Broad Applicability:** The demonstrated generality across various ViT architectures (DeiT, ViT-L, Swin-T) and visual tasks (recognition, detection, segmentation) and its robustness to complex data settings (co-occurrence, long-tailed distributions) suggest that NuWa could be widely adopted in different edge AI domains.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The insights gained regarding class-detrimental knowledge and the efficiency of retraining-free pruning methods could inspire further research into more targeted and efficient model compression techniques, potentially leading to hybrid approaches that combine NuWa's strengths with lightweight retraining or hypernetworks for even higher pruning rates or larger sub-tasks.

Overall, NuWa provides a principled and practical solution for generating lightweight, class-specialized Vision Transformers, potentially paving the way for more widespread and effective integration of advanced AI models into edge computing environments.
