# RL-ViGen: A Reinforcement Learning Benchmark for Visual Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> RL-ViGen introduces a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating out-of-distribution generalization in visual reinforcement learning, integrating diverse tasks and an expanded set of generalization categories. The benchmark reveals that no single existing algorithm achieves universally strong performance, with all methods demonstrating significant limitations in generalizing to novel scene structures and cross-embodiments.

## Problem

- Existing visual reinforcement learning (RL) benchmarks for generalization are limited in task diversity, realism, and the range of generalization categories they address (e.g., Procgen for non-realistic images, DMC-GB for narrow scope).
- These limitations risk promoting algorithmic overfitting to specific benchmark characteristics, hindering the development of truly robust agents capable of real-world out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization.
- A lack of a unified evaluation framework across various algorithms makes fair and reliable comparisons difficult, leading to potentially misleading conclusions about algorithmic progress.

## Method

- **RL-ViGen** constructs a novel benchmark integrating 5 diverse task categories, including dexterous manipulation, autonomous driving, indoor navigation, table-top manipulation, and locomotion, using high-fidelity simulators.
- The benchmark incorporates 5 distinct generalization categories: visual appearances, lighting conditions, camera views, scene structures, and cross embodiments, thoroughly testing OOD shifts.
- A unified algorithmic framework is established to evaluate 8 state-of-the-art visual RL algorithms under consistent optimization schemes and hyperparameters, ensuring fair and reliable comparisons.

## Results

- PIE-G, which leverages ImageNet pre-trained models, showed superior generalization for visual appearances and lighting changes in Indoor Navigation and Dexterous Hand Manipulation tasks.
- All evaluated algorithms demonstrated unsatisfactory performance when tested with novel 'scene structures' (e.g., in Autonomous Driving), indicating a significant unaddressed challenge for current methods.
- SGQN, utilizing saliency maps, exhibited advantageous generalization abilities against varying 'camera views' (in Dexterous Hand Manipulation), attributed to its enhanced awareness of object geometry.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No single existing visual generalization algorithm demonstrates universally strong performance; their efficacy varies significantly depending on the specific task and generalization category.
> - Algorithms that perform well in terms of sample efficiency or asymptotic performance during training do not necessarily guarantee good OOD generalization without additional inductive biases.
> - Current visual RL algorithms show substantial weaknesses in generalizing to changes in 'scene structures' and 'cross embodiments', categories that impact not just observations but also action space interpretation and transition dynamics.
