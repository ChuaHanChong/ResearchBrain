# Distribution-Free Risk-Aware Planning and Control Under Uncertainty Using Conformal Spectral Risk Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Michigan developed Conformal Spectral Risk Control (CSRC) and integrated it into Model Predictive Control (CSRC-MPC) to provide distribution-free statistical safety guarantees for autonomous systems operating under uncertainty. This framework achieved a 100% success rate in simulated vehicle obstacle avoidance, significantly outperforming a baseline by reducing constraint violations from 52.9% to 6.0% and halving solve times, even when the underlying uncertainty models were misspecified.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems require robust planning and control under uncertainty, but precise characterization of uncertainty distributions is often difficult in real-world dynamic environments.
- Traditional Risk-Aware Model Predictive Control (RA-MPC) relies on accurate uncertainty distribution models, leading to potentially unsafe operations if these models are misspecified.
- Existing distribution-free conformal methods provide statistical guarantees but do not directly extend to controlling general spectral risk measures, which offer flexible and nuanced risk assessment.

## Method

- Developed Conformal Spectral Risk Control (CSRC) to extend existing conformal methods to general spectral risk measures by reformulating them as weighted expectations, enabling distribution-free risk quantification.
- Introduced offline calibration and conservative approximations (e.g., truncating unbounded weighting functions, bounding online weights) to address computational challenges and unknown loss function CDFs, making the method practical for online applications.
- Integrated CSRC-generated prediction sets into a ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework (CSRC-MPC), reformulating spectral risk constraints using Lipschitz continuity to provide statistical safety guarantees with respect to true, unknown uncertainty.

## Results

- The CSRC framework empirically controlled the risk of loss functions below user-specified thresholds for CVaR and Wang risk measures in both stationary and dynamic obstacle avoidance scenarios, confirming its statistical guarantees.
- CSRC-MPC achieved a **100%** success rate in obstacle avoidance and reduced constraint violation rates from **52.9%** (SAA-MPC baseline) to **6.0%** under uncertainty misspecification, demonstrating superior robustness.
- The proposed CSRC-MPC framework exhibited improved computational efficiency, with an average solve time of **49.9** ms per control step, nearly halving the **104.9** ms of the SAA-MPC baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spectral risk measures can be reformulated as weighted expectations, allowing their integration into weighted conformal risk control frameworks for distribution-free risk quantification.
> - Conservative approximations and offline calibration enable practical, computationally efficient application of conformal methods to control general spectral risk measures in real-time systems.
> - Leveraging the Lipschitz continuity of constraint functions, distribution-free prediction sets can be integrated into MPC to provide robust statistical safety guarantees, even when the true uncertainty distribution is unknown or misspecified.
