# MATH-Beyond: A Benchmark for RL to Expand Beyond the Base Model

> [!summary] Summary
> MATH-Beyond (MATH-B) is a new benchmark designed to assess the genuine expansion of reasoning capabilities in large language models for mathematics, moving beyond mere refinement of existing skills. The benchmark reveals that current open-source reinforcement learning fine-tuning methods achieve only marginal improvements in expanding reasoning boundaries on genuinely challenging problems, whereas knowledge distillation from stronger models yields substantially higher expansion.

## Problem

- Existing mathematical reasoning benchmarks for LLMs are becoming saturated, making it difficult to measure genuine progress in expanding model capabilities.
- Current reinforcement learning (RL) methods for LLMs often only "sharpen" existing solution modes rather than fostering "expansion" by discovering new reasoning pathways.
- A need exists for a diagnostic tool to distinguish between incremental performance gains and true, out-of-distribution reasoning skill acquisition in LLMs.
- Common pitfalls in rule-based verification pipelines can lead to inaccurate evaluations of mathematical reasoning performance.

## Method

- Developed **MATH-Beyond** (MATH-B), a benchmark of 184 ==mathematical problems== rigorously filtered to be unsolvable by common open-source LLMs (up to 8B parameters) even with a large sampling budget (pass@1024).
- Established a formal evaluation framework that defines "Expansion Rate" to specifically quantify the discovery of new reasoning capabilities beyond a base model's reach.
- Employed a multi-stage problem construction pipeline, including sourcing from difficult datasets, extensive quality and ground-truth verification using frontier models, and deduplication against standard benchmarks.
- Incorporated robust verification logic to address seven identified pitfalls in rule-based mathematical answer checking, ensuring accurate evaluation.

## Results

- RL-finetuned models showed limited expansion rates on MATH-B: Nemotron-v1 achieved **7.83%**, Nemotron-v2 reached **9.57%**, and DeepScaleR-**1.5B** showed **5.22%** expansion at pass@1024.
- Skywork-OR1-**7B**, an RL-tuned model utilizing adaptive entropy control, demonstrated a higher expansion rate of **21.21%** on a specific subset of problems.
- Distillation-finetuned models achieved substantially higher expansion: Qwen3-**4B** reached **58.93%** and Qwen3-**8B** achieved **66.38%** expansion.
- The median human-perceived difficulty of MATH-B problems was 4 out of 10, indicating that current LLMs struggle with problems not deemed exceptionally hard by humans.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing open-source RL fine-tuning methods for LLMs largely fail to achieve genuine expansion of reasoning capabilities on truly novel and challenging problems, remaining constrained by the base model's initial skills.
> - While increasing RL compute yields only marginal gains, knowledge distillation from more capable teacher models can lead to substantial expansion by exposing models to the "correct distribution of reasoning steps."
> - Problems considered moderately difficult for humans can be fundamentally beyond the reach of state-of-the-art LLMs, highlighting a significant gap in their reasoning abilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself directly within the rapidly evolving landscape of large language models (LLMs) and their application to complex reasoning tasks, particularly in mathematics. The advent of models like DeepSeek-R1, which leverage RL methods, has sparked excitement about unlocking stronger mathematical reasoning. However, the authors argue that this progress, especially within the open-source ecosystem, faces a critical limitation: existing benchmarks like MATH-500 and AIME 2024 are becoming *saturated*. With sufficiently many attempts (e.g., pass@1024), many base models already solve nearly all problems, implying that current RL fine-tuning largely *sharpens existing solution modes* rather than *discovering entirely new ones*.

This observation creates a stark contrast with the historical successes of deep reinforcement learning in other domains, such as Atari games (Mnih et al., 2013) or board games like Go and Chess (AlphaGo, AlphaZero), where RL agents started from scratch or inept policies and achieved superhuman mastery by *exploring and acquiring genuinely new skills*. The authors contend that current LLM-focused RL for reasoning falls short of this "exploratory promise," an issue referred to as the "invisible leash" problem by Wu et al. (2025).

MATH-Beyond directly addresses this gap by creating a benchmark specifically designed to be unsolvable by common open-source base models even with extensive sampling budgets. By doing so, it aims to shift the research focus from incremental improvements on saturated datasets to the development of RL methods that can truly *expand* the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. This work aligns with a growing academic concern about the adequacy of current evaluation metrics and benchmarks, especially as models become more powerful and datasets risk contamination or saturation. It contributes to the broader effort to create diagnostic tools that reveal fundamental limitations and drive progress in genuine AI capabilities. The paper also draws parallels to adversarial and out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluations in computer vision, suggesting MATH-B functions as an OOD test for reasoning, exposing brittleness in LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop and introduce MATH-Beyond (MATH-B)**, a novel benchmark suite specifically constructed to defeat common open-source LLMs (up to 8B parameters) even under large sampling budgets (pass@1024).

The core motivations driving this objective are:
- **Address Benchmark Saturation**: Existing popular math benchmarks are increasingly solvable by base LLMs given enough attempts, making it impossible to measure genuine progress in *expanding* reasoning capabilities.
- **Distinguish Sharpening from Expansion**: Current RL methods often only refine or "sharpen" existing solution pathways. The authors aim to create a testbed where success unequivocally requires "expansion"—the discovery of entirely new reasoning modes or capabilities beyond the base model's initial reach.
- **Catalyze Exploration-Driven RL**: By creating a "zero-baseline" benchmark where base models fail comprehensively, MATH-B intends to incentivize the development of RL approaches that foster true exploration and skill acquisition in LLMs, akin to the historical successes of deep RL in other domains.
- **Provide a Diagnostic Tool**: MATH-B is designed as a diagnostic tool to empirically quantify the "discovery vs. sharpening" debate and provide a clear signal for when a model has genuinely expanded its reasoning boundaries.
- **Document Verification Pitfalls**: The authors also aim to highlight and address common failure modes in rule-based verification pipelines for mathematical answers, which can mask true model performance and lead to misleading evaluations.

In essence, the authors seek to provide the LLM research community with a robust, challenging, and uncontaminated benchmark that accurately reflects and encourages advancement in the fundamental reasoning abilities of models, moving beyond superficial performance gains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a meticulous, multi-stage pipeline to construct MATH-Beyond, ensuring problem quality, novelty, and, crucially, difficulty for existing open-source LLMs. Their evaluation framework is also specifically tailored to measure "expansion" in a "zero-baseline" setting.

#### 3.1. Evaluation Framework and Metrics

The paper formalizes an evaluation framework, adapting concepts from Wu et al. (2025), to isolate the phenomenon of "boundary expansion."
- **Empirical pass@k**: This is the core metric. For a problem `x` and policy `p`, `pass@k(p; x)` is 1 if at least one of `k` samples from `p` correctly solves `x`, and 0 otherwise. The overall `pass@k(p)` is the average across a dataset.
- **Reachable Set `R_k(p, D)`**: The set of problems policy `p` solves in dataset `D` within `k` attempts.
- **Expansion Rate (Primary Metric)**: Defined as `|R_k(π, D) \ R_k(q, D)| / |D|`, where `π` is the post-trained model and `q` is the base model. This measures problems `π` solves that `q` could not.
- **Special Case: MATH-B as a "Zero-Baseline" Test**: A critical aspect of MATH-B is its construction such that `R_k(q, D) = ∅` (the base model empirically fails on all problems at `pass@k`). In this scenario, Shrinkage and Preservation metrics become inapplicable, and the Expansion Rate simplifies to `|R_k(π, D)| / |D| = pass@k(π)`. Any positive `pass@k` on MATH-B directly signifies genuine expansion. The authors deliberately chose `k=1024` for its ability to probe true reasoning boundaries while providing stable and computationally feasible measurements, arguing that marginal gains diminish significantly beyond this budget.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction Pipeline

The construction of MATH-B involves a multi-stage filtering process:

1. **Base Dataset Sourcing**:
 - **Candidate Pool**: Problems were sourced from DAPO-Math-17K (Yu et al., 2025a) and DeepScaleR (Luo et al., 2025). These were chosen for their difficulty and to minimize data contamination with pre-training data of common open-source models. The initial pool comprised 53,682 problems.
2. **Quality Filters**:
 - **Question and Answer-Type Filtering**: To mitigate verification pitfalls (e.g., multiple valid answers, unordered tuples, MCQ parsing issues), problems were filtered to have integer-based ground truths, remove multiple-choice questions, eliminate Chinese characters, and ensure self-contained questions (no external figures). This reduced the pool to 34,515.
 - **Pre-screening and Random Sampling**: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen2.5-7B was evaluated on all problems at a `pass@16` budget, keeping only unsolved problems to reduce the dataset size for further intensive processing. A portion of this was then randomly sampled.
 - **Ground-Truth Answer Verification**: To ensure intrinsic challenge rather than incorrect ground truths, frontier models (o4-mini-high and GPT-5-Mini) were used (`pass@2`) to verify that the ground truth answers were reproducible by highly capable models. Only problems where at least one frontier model matched the ground truth were retained.
 - **Deduplication Against Standard Benchmarks**: Exact string-match deduplication was performed against benchmarks like MATH-500, MinervaMath, OlympiadBench, AMC, and AIME to ensure the novelty of the problems. After these steps, 184 problems remained.
3. **Final Benchmark Construction (pass@1024 Filtering)**:
 - The remaining 184 problems were evaluated against a diverse "gauntlet" of 21 open-source models (including Qwen, DeepSeek-R1, OLMo, Llama-3.1, and their instruct/RL-finetuned variants) with a budget of `1024` samples per problem.
 - **Robust Verification**: The evaluation incorporated robust verification logic to account for 7 identified "pitfalls" in rule-based math answer verification (e.g., handling multiple valid answers, late/early correct answers, corrections, unordered tuples, missing anchors, MCQ partial matches).
 - **Benchmark Subsets**: This extensive evaluation (requiring over 20,000 A100 GPU hours) led to the definition of MATH-B:
 - **MATH-Beyond-Union Set (MATH-B-U)**: 181 problems, unsolved by at least one model from the entire suite.
 - **MATH-Beyond-Intersection Set (MATH-B-I)**: 41 problems, unsolved by *all* considered base models, representing the most challenging subset.
 - **Model-Specific Sets**: Problems unsolved by specific models, for targeted analysis.
4. **Data Characteristics**: Problems are annotated for mathematical domain (standard high-school math) and human-perceived difficulty using GPT-5. The median human difficulty is 4/10, highlighting a disconnect between human and model perceived difficulty.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core findings revolve around the empirical evaluation of existing RL-finetuned models on the newly created MATH-Beyond benchmark, showcasing their limited ability to genuinely expand reasoning boundaries.

- **Limited Expansion by Current RL Models**: The paper reveals that RL-finetuned models, such as Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B (v1 and v2) and DeepScaleR-1.5B, achieve only modest Expansion Rates on MATH-B. For Nemotron-v1, the expansion was 7.83%, and for Nemotron-v2, it was 9.57% (on 115 problems where the base model failed). DeepScaleR-1.5B showed an even lower 5.22% expansion. These figures represent the `pass@1024` success rate, indicating that even with a very large sampling budget, these models struggle significantly with problems just beyond their base capabilities.
- **Marginal Gains from Prolonged RL Training**: The slight improvement from Nemotron-v1 to Nemotron-v2 (a 1.5% increase) suggests that simply increasing RL compute or training duration with current methods yields only marginal gains, underscoring the need for more efficient and effective exploration strategies.
- **Higher Expansion from Specific RL Approaches**: Skywork-OR1-7B, another RL-tuned model, achieved a more promising 21.21% expansion (on 99 problems). The authors attribute this relatively better performance to its training involving adaptive entropy control and a higher temperature, which likely encourages greater exploration. This hints at the potential for exploration-driven RL methods to achieve higher expansion rates.
- **Significant Expansion via Distillation/SFT**: In contrast to RL, models fine-tuned through distillation of long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories from more capable teacher models, such as Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B, demonstrated substantially higher Expansion Rates of 58.93% and 66.38% respectively. While not a direct apples-to-apples comparison, this finding is highly informative: it suggests that exposing models to the "correct distribution of reasoning steps" (as provided by a teacher model during distillation) can lead to significant capability expansion.
- **Disconnect between Human and Model Difficulty**: The analysis of MATH-B's characteristics showed that its problems, while robustly unsolvable by current models, only had a median human-perceived difficulty of 4 out of 10 (with a maximum of 6.5). This highlights that problems considered "not exceptionally hard" for humans can still be fundamentally beyond the reach of state-of-the-art LLMs, indicating a significant gap in their reasoning abilities.
- **Justification of k=1024**: Empirical analysis showed that while `pass@k` increases with `k`, marginal gains diminish considerably as `k` approaches 1024. This supports the choice of a high sampling budget as a practical and stable point for evaluating genuine boundary expansion.

Overall, the main finding is that current open-source RL fine-tuning methods largely fail to expand LLM reasoning boundaries on genuinely challenging problems, operating more in a "sharpening" mode. Significant expansion is possible, but currently seems more reliably achieved through knowledge distillation from stronger models, emphasizing the need for RL to develop better exploratory capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MATH-Beyond carries significant implications for the future direction of LLM research, particularly in the domain of mathematical reasoning and reinforcement learning.

- **Drives Genuine Capability Expansion**: MATH-B directly addresses the stagnation caused by saturated benchmarks. By creating a "zero-baseline" test, it provides an unambiguous signal for genuine reasoning expansion, pushing researchers to develop methods that teach LLMs to acquire *new skills* rather than merely refine existing ones. This is crucial for moving towards more generally intelligent AI.
- **Catalyzes Exploration-Driven RL**: The benchmark is specifically designed to highlight the limitations of current RL fine-tuning in fostering exploration. Its existence is intended to catalyze research into novel RL algorithms and exploration strategies that can discover previously unreachable reasoning pathways, fulfilling the broader promise of RL. This could lead to breakthroughs in how LLMs learn and reason independently.
- **Diagnostic Tool for LLM Brittleness**: MATH-B acts as a powerful diagnostic tool, akin to adversarial or out-of-distribution benchmarks in computer vision. It exposes the brittleness of current LLM reasoning, showing that models can fail robustly on problems that are topically in-distribution but whose solutions are effectively out-of-distribution for their base capabilities. This helps in understanding fundamental weaknesses of LLMs.
- **Informs Benchmark Design and Verification**: The meticulous process of MATH-B's construction, including the detailed documentation of seven common verification pitfalls, serves as a valuable guide for the community. It encourages more robust and reliable evaluation practices, preventing misleading performance metrics due to artifacts in the verification pipeline.
- **Shifts Research Focus**: By providing a quantifiable metric for "Expansion Rate" on a challenging dataset, MATH-B aims to shift the community's focus from incremental `pass@k` improvements on already solvable problems to the more fundamental challenge of expanding reasoning boundaries. This reorientation is vital for fostering truly innovative research in LLM capabilities.
- **Unveils the "Invisible Leash"**: The paper empirically supports the "invisible leash" hypothesis (Wu et al., 2025), demonstrating that existing RL methods for LLMs remain constrained by their base models. By making this limitation explicit and providing a means to measure escape, MATH-B offers a path towards breaking this "leash" and achieving more autonomous skill acquisition.

In conclusion, MATH-Beyond is more than just another dataset; it is a strategically designed scientific instrument that challenges the prevailing paradigms in LLM reasoning research. Its rigorous construction and clear objective provide a critical foundation for evaluating and driving the next generation of LLM capabilities, focusing on genuine intellectual expansion rather than mere performance sharpening.
