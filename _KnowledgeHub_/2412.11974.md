# Emma-X: An Embodied Multimodal Action Model with Grounded Chain of Thought and Look-ahead Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Singapore University of Technology and Design developed EMMA-X, an embodied multimodal action model that enhances robot policies with visually grounded chain-of-thought and look-ahead spatial reasoning. This approach leads to a 24.17% increase in task success rate over the OpenVLA baseline on real-world robot manipulation tasks, particularly improving performance in scenarios requiring complex spatial understanding and generalization to new instructions.

## Problem

- Current Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack long-horizon spatial reasoning, exhibiting "muscle memory" without foresight for multi-step movements or future states.
- Task planning in VLAs, particularly with chain-of-thought reasoning, frequently suffers from hallucination due to insufficient visual grounding, leading to incoherent or physically impossible plans.
- Existing robotic control policies, including traditional reinforcement learning and prior VLA models, struggle with generalization to diverse environments, unseen objects, or varied instructions.

## Method

- EMMA-X constructs a ==hierarchical embodiment dataset== by auto-annotating existing robot trajectories with grounded task reasoning and look-ahead spatial guidance.
- It employs a novel ==trajectory segmentation strategy== using HDBSCAN with a custom distance metric and gripper state changes to define coherent action segments.
- The model integrates ==future 2D gripper position prediction==, natural language 3D movement plans, and ==visually grounded chain-of-thought reasoning== into a fine-tuned 7B-parameter OpenVLA model.

## Results

- EMMA-X achieved a **24.17%** increase in task success rate and a **26.25%** increase in half-success rate compared to OpenVLA across 12 real-world WidowX-250 robot manipulation tasks.
- The model showed the most pronounced improvements (**35%** over OpenVLA in half-success rate) on tasks requiring complex spatial relations and demonstrated substantial gains on out-of-domain instruction tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of each core component, with removing grounded chain-of-thought reasoning leading to a **4**3%**-55%** performance drop, and removing spatial reasoning components causing **2**5%**-45%** declines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visually grounding chain-of-thought reasoning with segmented image sequences significantly mitigates hallucination and enhances the coherence of task planning in VLA models.
> - Integrating explicit look-ahead predictions of future 2D gripper positions and high-level 3D movement plans enables robots to achieve superior long-horizon spatial reasoning and more precise actions.
> - A novel trajectory segmentation strategy, combining motion clustering and gripper state changes, is crucial for constructing high-quality hierarchical training data necessary for embodied reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control is rapidly evolving, driven by advancements in artificial intelligence, particularly in machine learning and large foundation models. Traditionally, robotic policies relied on reinforcement learning (RL), which, while effective for specific tasks in controlled environments, struggles with generalization across diverse environments, unseen objects, or varied instructions. This limitation stems from their task-specific nature and heavy reliance on extensive, targeted training data.

The emergence of Visual Language Models (VLMs) has offered a promising avenue. VLMs, such as those based on large language models (LLMs) combined with vision encoders, excel at scene understanding, abstract reasoning, and high-level task planning (e.g., decomposing complex tasks into simpler steps via chain-of-thought reasoning). However, a significant gap remains: VLMs are not inherently designed to output low-level, actionable policies tailored for specific robotic embodiments (e.g., precise joint movements or end-effector velocities).

To bridge this gap, Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a new paradigm. VLAs aim to integrate multimodal inputs (vision and language) to directly generate adaptive and generalized robotic actions. Existing VLA models, such as RT-2, RT-2-X, and OpenVLA (which EMMA-X builds upon), have demonstrated impressive capabilities in transferring web knowledge to robotic control and handling multi-task scenarios. However, the authors identify two key limitations in current VLAs:
1. **Lack of long-horizon spatial reasoning**: Many VLAs exhibit "muscle memory" response patterns, struggling to predict future states or plan multi-step movements in complex environments. They often lack the foresight to anticipate the gripper's future position or the precise 3D movements required to achieve a subtask.
2. **Insufficient grounding in task planning**: While some VLAs attempt task reasoning (e.g., ECoT, which uses text-only prompts for chain-of-thought), they often suffer from hallucination due to a lack of visual grounding. Relying solely on textual descriptions can lead to incoherent or physically impossible reasoning, especially in out-of-domain scenarios.

EMMA-X directly addresses these two critical limitations. By incorporating "Grounded Chain of Thought" (GCoT) and "Look-ahead Spatial Reasoning" into a VLA framework, EMMA-X seeks to enhance both the reasoning and execution capabilities of robots. It positions itself as an evolution of VLA models, moving beyond reactive policies to more proactive, spatially aware, and contextually grounded robotic intelligence. This work contributes to the broader goal of developing truly generalist robots capable of performing a wide array of complex tasks in unstructured, real-world environments with human-like understanding and foresight.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of EMMA-X are to:

1. **Enhance long-horizon spatial reasoning in VLA models**: To enable robots to predict future gripper positions (2D) and plan detailed 3D spatial movements required to reach those positions, thereby allowing for more purposeful and adaptive task completion.
2. **Improve the grounding of task planning and reasoning**: To mitigate hallucination in existing chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning for VLAs by incorporating strong visual grounding, ensuring that the generated subtasks and justifications are consistent with the actual visual environment.
3. **Develop a robust and generalizable robotic policy**: To create a VLA model capable of superior performance across diverse real-world manipulation tasks, including those requiring complex spatial understanding and generalization to unseen objects and instructions.
4. **Construct a high-quality hierarchical embodiment dataset**: To provide the necessary training data for achieving the above objectives, meticulously auto-annotating robot trajectories with grounded task reasoning and spatial guidance.

The motivation for EMMA-X stems from the shortcomings of current robotic control methods. Traditional RL-based approaches are too narrow, failing to generalize. While VLMs offer planning capabilities, they lack actionable policy generation. Existing VLAs, though a step forward, often react impulsively ("muscle memory") without adequate foresight or contextual understanding. The authors hypothesize that integrating look-ahead spatial reasoning and visually grounded chain-of-thought will enable VLAs to plan more effectively, execute more precisely, and generalize more robustly, akin to how humans use both a high-level plan and immediate feedback to navigate and accomplish tasks. The goal is to move towards more intelligent, adaptive, and less hallucination-prone embodied AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EMMA-X's methodology revolves around three core components: a novel data construction pipeline, a sophisticated trajectory segmentation strategy, and the fine-tuning of a large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Embodiment Dataset Construction

The foundation of EMMA-X is a newly constructed hierarchical embodiment dataset based on the existing BridgeV2 dataset. BridgeV2 contains 60,000 robot manipulation trajectories paired with task instructions. EMMA-X augments this dataset by auto-annotating each state of these trajectories with detailed spatial reasoning and grounded task reasoning. This hierarchical structure includes:
- **States (s_t)**: Individual image frames of the environment.
- **Segments (σ)**: Sequences of consecutive states where the robot performs semantically similar actions, contributing to a subgoal.
- **Subtasks (S)**: Collections of segments that achieve a specific subgoal.
- **Tasks (T)**: A series of subtasks to complete the overall objective.

#### 3.2. Trajectory Segmentation

A crucial step for enabling grounded reasoning and look-ahead planning is to accurately segment long robot trajectories into meaningful sub-sequences. The authors propose a novel trajectory segmentation strategy that integrates both motion trajectory and gripper states:
- **Why Segmentation?** Direct frame-by-frame annotation for Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning with large language models (LLMs) like Gemini often results in noisy and contextually inconsistent labels. Segmenting provides richer context, improving the quality of generated subtask labels and justifications. It also naturally delineates periods where look-ahead spatial reasoning can predict future states (e.g., the end of a segment).
- **Method:** The Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (HDBSCAN) algorithm is used. HDBSCAN is robust to noise and can discover diverse movement patterns.
 - **Custom Distance Metric:** A novel distance metric `d(i, j)` is defined to cluster end-effector trajectories, incorporating 3D position (`||p_i - p_j||_2`), 3D orientation (`||r_i - r_j||_2`), and temporal information (`|t_i - t_j|`). This ensures that segments capture both spatial movement and temporal progression.
 - **Gripper State Integration:** In addition to HDBSCAN clusters, a segmentation breakpoint is introduced whenever the gripper state changes (from open to close or vice-versa). This dual approach ensures segments reflect both motion patterns and manipulation actions.

#### 3.3. Data Generation for Hierarchical Planning

After segmentation, detailed hierarchical planning data is generated for each demonstration:
- **Look-ahead Gripper Position Generation (g_t):** For each state `s_t`, the model is trained to predict the 2D gripper position for the *first state of the next segment*. This contrasts with prior work that primarily identifies the gripper's current position. OWLv2 and SAM are used to detect 2D gripper positions. This enables the model to foresee key "checkpoints" in the task.
- **Look-ahead Movement Plan Generation (m_t):** Based on the current state and the end state of the current segment, the 3D spatial positions are inferred. The displacement between these positions is encoded into a high-level natural language "motion plan" using a standardized template (e.g., "move forward X steps, move right Y steps"). This provides the robot with a coarse, interpretable route to its immediate subgoal.
- **Grounded Chain-of-Thought Reasoning (GR_t):** Gemini 1.5-pro-latest is utilized to generate subtasks and detailed justifications for each *segment*. Critically, unlike ECoT which relies solely on textual descriptions, EMMA-X provides Gemini with *sequences of segmented images* alongside task descriptions. This visual grounding significantly reduces hallucination and ensures the generated reasoning is coherent with the observed environment. The output includes the subtask label and a comprehensive reasoning chain explaining the robot's actions for that segment.

The final dataset for training comprises tuples of `(current_state_image, task_instruction, current_2D_gripper_position)` as input, paired with `(predicted_movement_plan, predicted_future_2D_gripper_position, grounded_reasoning, next_7D_robot_action)` as output labels.

#### 3.4. EMMA-X Architecture and Training

- **Base Model:** EMMA-X is a 7B-parameter VLA model, fine-tuned from OpenVLA. OpenVLA itself is built upon the Prismatic vision-language framework and pretrained on the Open X-Embodiment dataset.
- **Fine-tuning:** The OpenVLA backbone is fine-tuned for 3 epochs on the newly constructed hierarchical embodiment dataset. The 7-dimensional robot actions (Cartesian velocity x, y, z; orientation roll, pitch, yaw; and gripper close/open action) are tokenized into discrete policy tokens, consistent with OpenVLA's methodology for autoregressive training.
- **Inference:** During real-world deployment, EMMA-X takes the task description, current observation image, and real-time 2D gripper position (detected by OWLv2/SAM) as input. It then autoregressively generates the subtask, scene description, spatial relationship information, target 2D/3D gripper positions for the future state, and finally, the 7D robot action policy for downstream closed-loop control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EMMA-X demonstrates significant performance improvements over competitive baselines, particularly in tasks requiring complex spatial reasoning and generalization.

- **Overall Performance (Table 1):** EMMA-X substantially outperforms OpenVLA and ECoT across 12 real-world WidowX-250 robot manipulation tasks.
 - Compared to OpenVLA, EMMA-X achieves a **24.17% increase in task success rate** and a **26.25% increase in half-success rate**.
 - Compared to ECoT, EMMA-X shows even more significant gains, indicating its superiority in handling noisy training data, out-of-domain instructions, and unfamiliar objects due to its grounded task reasoning.
- **Performance Across Task Categories (Figure 4):**
 - **Spatial Relation tasks:** EMMA-X shows the most pronounced improvement, outperforming OpenVLA by 35% and ECoT by 29% in half-success rate. This strongly validates the effectiveness of the predicted 3D spatial movements.
 - **OOD Instruction tasks:** Demonstrates substantial performance gains, highlighting the efficacy of its grounded task reasoning for generalization to novel instructions.
- **Ablation Studies (Table 2):** These experiments isolate the impact of EMMA-X's core components:
 - **Segmentation (w/o HDBSCAN):** Removing HDBSCAN (i.e., segmenting only by gripper state) leads to a general performance drop of 10% to 50%, with spatial reasoning tasks experiencing the most significant decline (50%). This confirms the critical role of the custom distance metric and comprehensive segmentation.
 - **Look-ahead Spatial Reasoning (w/o m_t and w/o g_t):**
 - `w/o m_t` (without 3D movement plan): Performance drops by 25%-40%.
 - `w/o g_t` (without future 2D gripper position): Performance drops by 30%-45%.
 - The decline is more pronounced in spatial reasoning tasks. The results suggest predicting the future gripper position (`g_t`) is slightly more critical than the 3D movement plan (`m_t`), possibly due to OpenVLA's inherent spatial capabilities simplifying movement transitions.
 - **Grounded CoT Reasoning (w/o GR_t):** Removing grounded reasoning results in a marked performance drop of 43%-55%, indicating that spatial reasoning alone is insufficient. This ablation shows that improving broader reasoning capabilities (object recognition, commonsense, etc.) is essential for generalized VLA policies.
 - **Fine-tuning OpenVLA (OpenVLA w/ FT):** Fine-tuning OpenVLA directly on BridgeV2 without EMMA-X's data augmentation *degrades* performance by 5%-30%. This suggests overfitting, as BridgeV2 is already part of OpenVLA's pre-training data, underscoring the necessity of EMMA-X's novel data augmentation approach.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 5):** Successful trajectories demonstrate EMMA-X's ability to align predicted gripper positions and 3D movements with the subtask goal. Failed cases highlight that errors in fundamental capabilities like object recognition (e.g., misidentifying "hotdog" as "pineapple") can propagate and lead to downstream policy failures, even with sophisticated reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EMMA-X represents a significant step forward in developing more intelligent and capable generalist robot policies. Its contributions have several key implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By effectively integrating look-ahead spatial reasoning and visually grounded chain-of-thought, EMMA-X allows robots to move beyond "muscle memory" and react intelligently to novel situations, unseen objects, and out-of-domain instructions. This is crucial for deploying robots in complex, unstructured real-world environments where variations are common.
- **Improved Task Planning and Execution:** The ability to predict future gripper positions and plan 3D movements enables more precise and purposeful actions, leading to higher task success rates. The hierarchical breakdown of tasks into subtasks with grounded reasoning provides a more interpretable and robust planning mechanism.
- **Reduced Hallucination in Reasoning:** The novel trajectory segmentation strategy combined with visual grounding of Chain-of-Thought reasoning (using images in addition to text for Gemini prompting) is a critical advancement. This directly addresses the common problem of hallucination in LLM-based reasoning for robotics, making the robot's "thought process" more reliable and physically consistent.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The public release of codes, models, and datasets is a substantial contribution to the robotics and AI community. This fosters reproducibility, allows other researchers to build upon EMMA-X, and accelerates the development of more advanced embodied AI systems.
- **Practical Applicability:** The demonstrated superior performance on real-world robot manipulation tasks, especially those requiring nuanced spatial understanding, indicates EMMA-X's potential for practical applications in manufacturing, logistics, domestic assistance, and other fields where flexible and adaptive robotic manipulation is required.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
1. **Latency:** EMMA-X's increased inference time due to generating approximately 10 times more reasoning tokens than OpenVLA is a practical concern. A proposed mitigation is to predict policies for an entire segment and only regenerate if deviations occur.
2. **Generalization Scope:** While improved, EMMA-X's generalization capabilities could still benefit from scaling the training process to a larger subset of the Open X-Embodiment dataset.
3. **Perception Accuracy:** Reliance on SAM for gripper detection can lead to inaccuracies when the gripper is occluded or out of frame. Employing more robust hand detection and segmentation models could enhance reliability.

Addressing these limitations would further solidify EMMA-X's position as a leading model for embodied AI. The work paves the way for future research into more efficient reasoning token generation, broader generalization through larger datasets, and the integration of even more robust perception systems for real-world deployment.
