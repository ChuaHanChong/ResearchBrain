# Safe-SAGE: Social-Semantic Adaptive Guidance for Safe Engagement through Laplace-Modulated Poisson Safety Functions

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for legged robots integrates semantic understanding and social norms into real-time safety-critical control using Laplace-modulated Poisson safety functions. This approach allows robots to maintain context-dependent safety margins and social compliance, demonstrating a wider human-robot margin of 0.318 meters and enabling social passing behavior in dynamic environments.

## Problem

- Traditional safety-critical control methods treat all obstacles geometrically, leading to "semantically blind" behaviors that are either universally conservative or aggressive.
- There is a "modality mismatch" between high-latency semantic reasoning models and the low-latency requirements of real-time safety filters.
- Current social navigation approaches often lack formal safety guarantees or are too computationally demanding for real-time deployment on resource-constrained robots.

## Method

- A pipeline integrating multi-sensor point cloud fusion, vision-based instance segmentation, and an object-level tracker to gather and maintain semantic scene understanding.
- Synthesis of Social-Semantic Laplace Guidance Fields (LGFs) with class-aware boundary conditions and tangent biasing, from which Poisson Safety Functions (PSFs) are derived.
- Implementation of a dual-layer safety filter, combining an ==MPC== for proactive trajectory planning with a real-time analytical safety filter for immediate, semantics-aware Control Barrier Function (CBF) enforcement.

## Results

- Robots operating with the proposed method maintained a positive human-robot safety margin of **0.318** meters (compared to -**0.008** meters for baseline) due to class-aware boundary conditions.
- Tangent biasing enabled social compliance, with robots achieving a maximum lateral offset of **0.75** meters (compared to -**0.1** meters for baseline), indicating a preference for socially appropriate passing sides.
- The framework was successfully deployed on both quadruped (Unitree Go2) and humanoid (Unitree G1) robots in complex, dynamic environments, including a crowded cafeteria, demonstrating platform-agnostic generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating semantic knowledge into safety functions through Laplace-modulated Poisson fields enables context-dependent conservatism, moving beyond purely geometric obstacle avoidance.
> - Social biases and directional avoidance norms can be directly embedded into low-level safety guidance fields, ensuring both formal safety guarantees and social compliance in real time.
> - A dual-layer safety filter combining MPC with an analytical CBF filter can effectively manage both proactive trajectory planning and reactive, semantics-aware collision avoidance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of safety-critical control, semantic reasoning for robotics, and social navigation. It addresses limitations observed in traditional control methods when applied to complex, human-centered environments.

**Traditional Safety-Critical Control:** Established techniques such as Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), Model Predictive Control (MPC), and Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability provide formal safety guarantees by enforcing forward invariance of a "safe set." However, these methods are often "semantically blind," meaning they treat all obstacles identically based on their geometric properties alone. For instance, a human and a static object occupying the same volume would elicit the same avoidance behavior. This limitation necessitates controllers to be either universally conservative, potentially hindering performance in cluttered spaces, or universally aggressive, increasing the risk of safety failures. While some prior work introduced state-dependent relaxations for conservatism, these typically require manual design rather than autonomous contextual adaptation. This work aims to overcome this semantic blindness by integrating contextual understanding into the safety set construction process.

**Safety from Semantics:** Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have enabled robots to engage in high-level reasoning about semantics and social norms. These models can generate high-level plans or barrier conditions based on visual-language queries. However, a "modality mismatch" exists: these large models often operate at low frequencies with high latency, which is incompatible with the near-real-time latency requirements of execution-level safety filters and underlying controllers. This paper proposes a framework to bridge this "neuro-symbolic gap," enabling semantic reasoning to inform dynamic safety enforcement at operational frequencies.

**Poisson Safety Functions (PSFs) and Laplace Guidance Fields (LGFs):** The work builds upon existing theoretical foundations for PSFs and LGFs. PSFs, introduced in prior work, provide a framework for characterizing safety with respect to spatial environments by solving Poisson's equation. LGFs, further expanded in other research, enable spatially varying conservatism around obstacle boundaries. This paper extends these concepts by being the first to introduce semantic flux modulation into this architecture, allowing for semantic-aware social and safety compliance, and by embedding social biases directly into the Laplace guidance field.

**Social Navigation:** Robots operating in human environments are expected to adhere to social norms. Earlier approaches utilized models like the social force model or deep reinforcement learning and topological invariants to learn cooperative behaviors. However, many of these methods either lack rigorous safety guarantees (like those provided by CBFs) or demand substantial computational resources, rendering them unsuitable for real-time operation on resource-constrained platforms. This research integrates social behaviors directly into a real-time safety filter through a flux-modulated guidance field, aiming to ensure both social compliance and rigorous safety guarantees.

In summary, Safe-SAGE attempts to unify high-level semantic understanding with low-level safety-critical control to enable legged robots to navigate dynamic, human-centric environments while maintaining both formal safety guarantees and adherence to social norms, addressing the limitations of prior methods in both semantic integration and real-time social compliance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing deployment of legged robots into "semantically rich and dynamic real-world environments," particularly those involving human interaction. While advanced locomotion capabilities exist, the critical challenge becomes ensuring safety in these complex settings.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed below:

- **Addressing Semantic Blindness in Traditional Safety Control:** Current safety-critical control methods (CBFs, MPC, HJ reachability) operate based on geometric representations of the environment. This leads to them being "semantically blind," meaning they cannot differentiate between obstacles with different contextual significance. For example, a human and a static object like a chair, if occupying similar geometric volumes, would trigger identical avoidance responses. This forces controllers to adopt either a universally conservative strategy, which can degrade performance in cluttered or dynamic environments, or a universally aggressive one, which may compromise safety. The motivation is to overcome this limitation by enabling robots to perceive and react to obstacles in a context-dependent manner, assigning varying levels of "conservatism" based on the semantic meaning of objects.

- **Bridging the Neuro-Symbolic Gap for Real-time Safety:** The development of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) has provided robots with sophisticated capabilities for semantic reasoning and understanding social norms. However, these models typically operate at low frequencies and high latencies. This creates a "modality mismatch" with the high-frequency, near-real-time latency requirements of underlying safety filters and controllers responsible for immediate execution. A key objective is to propose a unified framework that bridges this gap, enabling semantic understanding to directly inform and modulate real-time safety-critical control without incurring prohibitive latency.

- **Enabling Context-Dependent Safety Margins:** The goal is to allow robots to adjust their safety margins based on the semantic identity of environmental entities. For instance, a robot in a hospital setting should maintain a wider berth around a patient compared to a wall. This requires a mechanism to embed semantic information directly into the safety function and guidance fields that dictate avoidance behavior.

- **Integrating Social Norms into Safety Control:** Beyond mere collision avoidance, robots in human environments are expected to navigate in a socially compliant manner, adhering to conventions like passing on a specific side (e.g., pass-on-the-right/left). Existing social navigation methods often lack rigorous safety guarantees or are computationally intensive. A specific objective is to embed these social biases and directional avoidance norms directly into the low-level safety guidance field, ensuring both social compliance and formal safety guarantees simultaneously and in real time.

- **Developing a Robust and Reactive Safety Framework:** The system must be capable of both proactive trajectory planning over a finite horizon and immediate, reactive collision avoidance in dynamic environments. This necessitates a layered safety architecture that combines the predictive capabilities of MPC with the immediate responsiveness of a real-time safety filter, both informed by semantic understanding.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for legged robots to transition from controlled laboratory settings to complex, human-centric real-world environments, requiring a safety framework that is not only formally guaranteed but also contextually aware, socially compliant, and capable of real-time operation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Safe-SAGE framework is structured as a layered safety architecture that integrates semantic understanding into safety-critical control. It comprises three main components: Semantic Scene Understanding, Social-Semantic Field Synthesis, and a Dual-layer Safety Filter.

**A. Semantic Scene Understanding (Perception & Tracking)**
The system perceives its environment and maintains semantic information through a multi-sensor fusion and object tracking pipeline:
1. **Multi-sensor Point Cloud Fusion and Occupancy Grid Generation:** Point clouds from multiple sensors (e.g., LiDAR) are fused into a robot-centric occupancy grid. The robot's own geometry is masked out, and cell occupancy is maintained using exponential decay, smoothed by a Gaussian kernel for spatial consistency.
2. **Vision-Based Instance Segmentation:** An instance segmentation network (YOLOv11n) processes RGB camera streams to identify semantic labels for objects, which are then projected into the robot's body frame to create a sparse class map.
3. **Object-Level Tracker:** To overcome the limited field of view of the camera and enable persistent tracking, an object-level tracker is employed. This tracker uses connected component analysis to extract spatial clusters from the occupancy grid. These clusters are then matched to existing semantic detections via a greedy nearest-neighbor association. Positions and velocity estimates of labeled clusters are updated using exponential smoothing and decay. This mechanism allows the safety filters to maintain up-to-date semantic information even when objects move outside the camera's view.

**B. Social-Semantic Field Synthesis**
This module converts the semantic occupancy grid into a guidance field and a safety function that incorporate semantic and social information.

1. **Laplace Guidance Field (LGF) Construction:**
 - The robot's orientation `ψ` is integrated by parameterizing the free space domain as `Ω(ψ)`.
 - A vector Laplace equation is solved over the free space `Ω(ψ) \ ∂Ω_r(ψ)` to construct the social-semantic guidance field `v_sem = (v_x, v_y)`.
 - **Class-Aware Boundary Conditions (Repulsion):** On the primary obstacle boundaries `∂Ω(ψ)`, outward-normal conditions are prescribed, where the magnitude `b(q, ψ)` of repulsion is class-dependent (e.g., larger for humans, smaller for walls).
 - **Social Navigation Bias Integration (Tangential Flow):** An internal Dirichlet interface `∂Ω_r(ψ)` is defined by buffering obstacle boundaries (`Ω_r(ψ) = Ω(ψ) ⊖ B_r`). On this buffered boundary, tangential boundary conditions are applied, encoding desired social flow directions (e.g., "pass-on-the-right") with a magnitude `λ(q, ψ)`. This tangential component allows `v_sem` to be non-conservative, which is necessary to encode rotational social flow patterns.

2. **Poisson Safety Function (PSF) Construction:**
 - Utilizing the constructed guidance field `v_sem`, a scalar safety function `h_full` is generated by solving Poisson's equation over a 3D configuration space encompassing position `q=(x,y)` and yaw orientation `ψ`.
 - The forcing function `ˆf(q, ψ)` for Poisson's equation is derived from the divergence of the semantic guidance field (`∇ ⋅ v_sem`).
 - The resulting `h_full` is smooth, strictly positive in free space, and zero on obstacle boundaries. Critically, the class-aware boundary conditions of `v_sem` propagate into `h_full`, leading to faster changes in `h_full` near semantically critical boundaries.

3. **Temporal Safety Variation:**
 - To account for dynamic obstacles and perception latency, a temporal derivative `∂h_full/∂t` is estimated via motion-compensated finite differences between consecutive safety function solutions.
 - This derivative is smoothed using a low-pass filter and scaled by a factor `σ` to prevent over-conservatism far from obstacles, ensuring its full effect near boundaries (`h_full ≈ 0`) but diminishing in open space.

**C. Dual-layer Safety Filter**
The framework employs a two-layer safety filter to ensure both optimal planning and immediate reactivity.

1. **Model Predictive Control (MPC) Safety Filter:**
 - An MPC optimization problem is formulated to plan safe trajectories over a finite horizon `N`.
 - The objective is to minimize the deviation of control inputs `u_k` from a nominal command `u_nom`.
 - Constraints include system dynamics (`ζ_k+1 = ζ_k + ∆t u_k`), control input limits, and linearized CBF constraints derived from the semantic LGF (`h_full(ζ_k+1) ≥ e^(-γ∆t) h_full(ζ_k)`).
 - The CBF constraint is evaluated using 2D slices of `h_full` at corresponding yaw directions.
 - A Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) loop is used to solve the optimization, updating the linearization point until convergence.
 - The time-varying safety function `h_full(t_k)` is proactively propagated as `h_full,0 + σ(∂h_full/∂t)t_k` to anticipate closing obstacles.

2. **Real-time Analytical Safety Filter:**
 - For immediate safety enforcement, a closed-form analytical safety filter is applied at the system's control update rate.
 - It takes the MPC solution `u_mpc` as its nominal input.
 - The safety function `h_full(q, ψ)` and guidance field `v_sem(q, ψ)` are evaluated via trilinear interpolation.
 - The filter projects `u_mpc` onto the safe control set using a semantics-aware CBF constraint:
 `⟨v_sem(q, ψ), ∂h_full/∂ψ⟩ · u + (∥v_sem(q, ψ)∥ / (∥∇h∥ + ϵ)) · σ(h) · ∂h/∂t ≥ -γh_full(q, ψ)`.
 - This guarantees real-time adherence to safety requirements by minimally modifying the control input.

**D. Hardware Implementation and Software:**
Experiments were conducted on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot and a Unitree G1 humanoid robot. Sensor suites included a front UTLidar, a back-mounted Livox Mid360, and a gimbal-mounted RealSense D435 camera. YOLOv11n was used for semantic segmentation, running on an onboard Jetson Orin NX. Robot odometry was estimated using FastLIO2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both quantitative assessments and demonstrations of real-world applicability for the Safe-SAGE framework.

**A. Safety and Social Compliance Analysis (Quantitative)**
Experiments were conducted to quantitatively evaluate the impact of the social-semantic guidance field modulation on robot performance, particularly regarding human-robot interaction.

- **Importance Differentiation:** A benchmark scenario involved the robot navigating a gap between a human and a static obstacle, with the human pushing the robot toward a wall in a U-shaped corridor. In this scenario, the robot was forced to stabilize in a closed safe set.
 - With the proposed method, using class-aware boundary conditions (`b_human(q) = -1.7`, `b_objects(q) = -0.5`), the measured human-robot margin (deviation from the center of free space) was 0.318 ± 0.0774 meters.
 - In contrast, a baseline method with a nominal guidance field (uniform `b_human(q) = -1.0`, `b_objects(q) = -1.0`) resulted in a human-robot margin of -0.008 ± 0.0625 meters.
 - This indicates that the proposed method enabled the robot to maintain a positive and wider margin from humans, demonstrating its ability to differentiate obstacles semantically and apply context-dependent conservatism.

- **Tangent Biasing (Social Norms):** The same benchmark was used to assess the effect of tangent biasing on social navigation.
 - The proposed method achieved a maximum lateral offset of 0.75 meters, indicating the robot would choose to pass on the socially compliant side of the human with a substantial clearance.
 - The baseline method resulted in a maximum lateral offset of -0.1 meters, suggesting a lack of social compliance or even choosing the non-socially preferred side.
 - These results demonstrate the framework's capability to embed and enforce directional avoidance norms for social navigation.

- **Visual Representation:** Figure 3 visually illustrates this effect, showing the robot maintaining a visibly wider margin to a human than to walls when the class-aware modulation is active, compared to a uniform margin in the baseline case. Figure 4 provides simulation benchmarks showing the robot's social compliance and differentiated clearance from humans versus static obstacles. Figure 5 demonstrates similar behavior on the Unitree Go2 quadruped in hardware experiments.

**B. Real-World Deployment Scenarios (Qualitative)**
The effectiveness and generalization of Safe-SAGE were further evaluated through deployment in several real-world scenarios:

- **Hallway Navigation:** The robot successfully navigated a hallway with pedestrians moving both towards and away from it, demonstrating adherence to social norms during dynamic interactions.
- **Open Area Navigation:** The system exhibited robustness in an open, dynamic environment with sensory noise, consistently maintaining both safety and social compliance.
- **Crowded Cafeteria Environment:** The framework was deployed in a complex, crowded cafeteria setting. This scenario highlighted the approach's adaptability and ease of deployment on a different robot setup (a modified sensor configuration with only a forward-facing D435 camera in addition to two LiDARs). The robot effectively maneuvered through the cluttered environment.
- **Platform-Agnostic Generalization:** The methodology's reliance on a reduced-order model and a general control interface allowed for its successful deployment on different robotic platforms, including the Unitree Go2 quadruped and the Unitree G1 humanoid robot (Figure 6), with minimal modifications. This indicates the framework's versatility across various legged robot architectures.

The findings collectively indicate that Safe-SAGE enables robots to integrate semantic understanding and social norms directly into their real-time safety-critical control, resulting in behavior that is both formally safe and contextually appropriate in dynamic human environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Safe-SAGE framework presents several points of significance and offers potential impacts on the field of robotics, particularly in the domain of human-robot interaction and autonomous navigation.

- **Bridging the Neuro-Symbolic Gap:** A primary significance of this work is its successful integration of high-level semantic understanding, typically associated with large language and vision models, with low-level, high-frequency safety-critical control. By translating semantic information into dynamically modulated Poisson safety functions and Laplace guidance fields, the framework addresses the "modality mismatch" that has hindered the direct application of semantic reasoning to real-time safety enforcement. This provides a mechanism for robots to execute safe actions informed by complex contextual knowledge without incurring prohibitive latency.

- **Enhanced Safety and Social Compliance:** The framework moves beyond "semantically blind" collision avoidance by enabling robots to perceive and react to obstacles with context-dependent safety margins and adherence to social norms. This is crucial for robot deployment in human-centered environments. By differentiating between semantically distinct obstacles (e.g., humans vs. static objects) and encoding directional avoidance preferences, robots can navigate more safely and predictably while also improving their social acceptability and fluency in shared spaces. The quantitative results demonstrate a measurable improvement in human-robot margin and social passing behavior compared to baseline methods.

- **Improved Robot Autonomy and Performance:** By allowing for variable conservatism based on semantic context, Safe-SAGE can enable robots to operate more efficiently in cluttered environments. Instead of maintaining a universally wide and potentially unnecessary safety margin, robots can adopt tighter, yet still safe, clearances where appropriate. This can lead to less conservative and more agile navigation, improving overall task performance without compromising safety.

- **Formal Safety Guarantees with Semantic Awareness:** The integration of Laplace guidance fields and Poisson safety functions within a CBF-constrained MPC and a real-time analytical safety filter provides formal guarantees for safety. This combination is significant because it imbues traditionally rigorous safety methods with semantic intelligence, offering a robust approach to context-aware safe navigation, which often remains a challenge in learning-based or heuristic approaches to social navigation.

- **Generalizability and Platform Agnosticism:** The methodology's reliance on a reduced-order model and its demonstration across different legged robotic platforms (quadruped and humanoid) with minimal modifications highlight its generalizability. This suggests that Safe-SAGE could be widely applicable to various robotic systems designed for operation in complex, dynamic environments, reducing the effort required for system adaptation.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a theoretical and practical foundation for future advancements. The stated plans to incorporate better occupancy grid memory, semantic graph construction, and the reasoning capabilities of large language models directly indicate a path toward even more sophisticated and adaptive social-semantic navigation. The framework provides a structured way to integrate these advanced cognitive abilities into the fundamental safety layer of robotic control.

In conclusion, Safe-SAGE represents a significant step towards creating intelligent autonomous robots that can not only operate safely but also interact appropriately and efficiently within complex human environments, thereby expanding the potential applications and societal integration of robotic technology.
