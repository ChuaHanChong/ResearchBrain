# Visual Generation in the New Era: An Evolution from Atomic Mapping to Agentic World Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> A roadmap outlines the evolution of visual generation, presenting a five-level taxonomy from atomic mapping to agentic world modeling. It analyzes current generative paradigms, training advancements, and evaluates frontier models, revealing limitations in spatial reasoning and causal understanding through stress testing while charting future research directions.

## Problem

- Current visual generative models exhibit limitations in robust spatial reasoning, long-horizon consistency, and causal understanding, despite producing high-fidelity images.
- Existing evaluation metrics primarily focus on perceptual quality, which can overstate model capabilities and fail to diagnose underlying logical and physical inconsistencies.
- The field lacked a structured framework to categorize and understand the progression of visual intelligence from basic rendering to complex, interactive world modeling.

## Method

- Introduces a ==five-level taxonomy== for visual intelligence, from atomic generation to ==agentic world modeling==.
- Systematically reviews generative paradigms, architectural components, and ==multi-stage training pipelines== (pre-training, SFT, RL).
- Uses ==in-the-wild stress tests== to diagnose frontier models on spatial logic, physical reasoning, and multi-turn consistency.

## Results

- The proposed five-level taxonomy frames the progression toward enhanced controllability and causal grounding.
- Stress tests revealed critical gaps in frontier models: precise spatial reasoning, physical law understanding, and multi-turn editing degradation.
- Leading closed-source systems employ an **L4 agentic generation** stack with VLMs and external control loops for planning and verification.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual generation is evolving from aesthetic synthesis to intelligent visual agents, demanding greater controllability, persistent coherence, and causal understanding.
> - Progress in visual models is increasingly attributed to advanced data construction, VLM-based annotation, and structured multi-stage training pipelines, including reinforcement learning for alignment.
> - Traditional evaluation metrics are insufficient for assessing higher-level visual intelligence, necessitating diagnostic stress tests to reveal limitations in spatial, physical, and causal reasoning.
