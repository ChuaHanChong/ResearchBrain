# MMFactory: A Universal Solution Search Engine for Vision-Language Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> MMFactory, developed by researchers from the University of British Columbia and the Vector Institute for AI, introduces a universal solution search engine for vision-language tasks that automatically generates programmatic solutions by combining available models based on user-defined tasks, constraints, and performance requirements. The framework demonstrated improved performance on benchmarks like BLINK and Seedbench while also achieving reduced API calling costs.

## Problem

- No single vision-language model excels at all tasks, making it difficult for users to identify and implement optimal solutions for specific needs.
- Current methods often rely on single tools, disregard user-specific computational and performance constraints.
- Existing approaches frequently generate sample-specific solutions, hindering generalization and deployment.

## Method

- MMFactory introduces a universal solution search engine with two main components: a ==Solution Router== and a ==Metric Router==.
- The Solution Router employs a ==multi-agent system== to iteratively generate and refine programmatic solutions by combining visual, LLM, and VLM tools based on user-defined tasks and examples.
- The Metric Router selects appropriate evaluation metrics, benchmarks solution performance, and presents resource utilization tradeoffs to users, enabling informed decision-making.

## Results

- MMFactory demonstrated improved performance in specific tasks like function correspondence, jigsaw solving, and spatial understanding on BLINK and Seedbench benchmarks.
- The framework achieved comparable or better performance than existing augmentation frameworks for MLLMs (e.g., SoM, Visprog, Visual Sketchpad) while significantly reducing API calling costs.
- The system generates general, executable solutions applicable to all instances of a task type, in contrast to previous methods that create sample-specific solutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-agent system can effectively generate robust and high-quality programmatic solutions for vision-language tasks by mimicking human team problem-solving dynamics.
> - Integrating user-defined constraints (performance, computational resources) into the solution generation process allows for adaptable and efficient AI tool development.
> - Automating the search and combination of existing vision-language models can overcome the limitations of single-tool approaches and sample-specific solutions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of vision-language tasks has seen remarkable progress in recent years with the development of increasingly powerful Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). However, a persistent challenge remains: no single model excels at all tasks, and users often struggle to identify and implement the optimal model for their specific needs, especially when facing constraints related to computational resources or performance requirements.

MMFactory addresses this challenge by introducing a universal solution search engine for vision-language tasks. Developed by researchers from the University of British Columbia and the Vector Institute for AI, this innovative framework automatically generates programmatic solutions by searching and combining available models based on user-defined tasks, constraints, and performance requirements.

#### 1.1. Research Context

MMFactory builds upon several active research areas:

1. **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**: Recent years have seen an explosion of models like GPT-4o and LLaVA that combine visual and textual processing capabilities.

2. **Visual Programming**: Researchers have explored approaches where LLMs generate code that leverages external tools or APIs to solve visual tasks.

3. **Model Selection and Routing**: A growing research area focuses on dynamically selecting the optimal model for a given task based on performance, cost, and resource constraints.

4. **Multi-Agent Systems**: Leveraging multiple AI agents to collaborate and communicate has shown promise in improving reasoning, factuality, and divergent thinking in LLMs.

While these research directions have made significant progress individually, MMFactory's innovation lies in its comprehensive approach that addresses multiple limitations of existing systems, providing a universal framework that considers user-specific needs when generating solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper identifies three critical limitations in current approaches to vision-language tasks:

1. **Over-reliance on Single Tools**: Existing methods often assume a single tool for each sub-task, ignoring the variety of available tools and their differing performance characteristics.

2. **Disregard for User Constraints**: Most approaches fail to account for user-specific computational needs, hardware limitations, or deployment costs.

3. **Sample-Specific Solutions**: Many current methods generate solutions tailored to individual examples, making deployment and generalization challenging.

MMFactory addresses these limitations by offering a flexible framework that:
- Considers multiple potential tools for each task
- Incorporates user constraints into solution generation
- Creates general solutions applicable to all instances of a given task

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MMFactory consists of two primary components that work together to deliver a comprehensive solution:

1. **Solution Router**: Generates a pool of programmatic solutions by combining visual, LLM, and VLM tools.
2. **Metric Router**: Proposes appropriate metrics and benchmarks the performance/resource characteristics of generated solutions.

This architecture enables users to specify their task through natural language descriptions and example input-output pairs, along with any constraints they might have regarding performance or computational resources. The system then autonomously discovers and combines available models to create efficient, executable solutions.

#### 3.1. The Solution Router

The Solution Router is the core component responsible for generating programmatic solutions. It employs a multi-agent system with two teams:

1. **Solution Proposing Team**: Generates ideas and code by leveraging available models and tools.
2. **Solution Committee Team**: Evaluates proposals for correctness, redundancy, and alignment with requirements.

This collaborative approach ensures that the final solutions are robust, executable, and meet the user's specific needs. The Solution Router iteratively refines its proposals based on feedback from the committee, resulting in high-quality solutions.

```python
def sol_0(prompt, image_1, image_2, image_3):
    valid_styles = ["Realism", "Impressionism", "Expressionism", "Pop Art", "Cubism"]
    prompt_style = "Some most common art painting styles include Realism, Impressionism, Expressionism, Pop Art, and Cubism. What is the painting style of this image? Please return only the style name without any additional comments."
    style_reference = gpt4o_model(prompt_style, [image_1])
    style_second = gpt4o_model(prompt_style, [image_2])
    style_third = gpt4o_model(prompt_style, [image_3])
    # Validate the returned styles
    styles = [style_reference, style_second, style_third]
    if all(style in valid_styles for style in styles):
        if style_reference == style_second:
            response = "(A)"
        elif style_reference == style_third:
            response = "(B)"
        else:
            response = "Neither matches the reference style."
    else:
        response = "Unable to determine a valid style for comparison."

    return response
```

#### 3.2. The Metric Router

The Metric Router is responsible for selecting appropriate evaluation metrics based on the user's task and the format of ground truth and predictions. This component:

1. Analyzes task characteristics to determine suitable metrics (e.g., accuracy for classification, IOU for detection)
2. Benchmarks the performance of different solutions against these metrics
3. Presents performance vs. resource utilization tradeoffs to help users make informed decisions

This approach allows users to select the solution that best aligns with their specific requirements, whether they prioritize accuracy, speed, or computational efficiency.

#### 3.3. Multi-Agent System and Collaborative Approach

A key innovation in MMFactory is its use of a multi-agent system for solution generation and evaluation. This approach leverages the strengths of collaborative AI systems:

1. **Diverse Perspective Integration**: Multiple agents with different roles contribute varied perspectives to solution development.
2. **Self-Critical Evaluation**: Solutions undergo rigorous evaluation by committee agents before being presented to users.
3. **Iterative Refinement**: The collaborative process includes cycles of proposal, critique, and refinement to improve solution quality.

This collaborative framework mimics human team problem-solving dynamics and results in more robust, creative solutions than single-agent approaches could achieve.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MMFactory was evaluated on two benchmarks: BLINK and Seedbench, with impressive results:

1. **Performance Improvements**: The framework demonstrated significant performance boosts in specific tasks such as function correspondence, jigsaw solving, and spatial understanding.

2. **Comparable or Better Performance**: MMFactory achieved results comparable to or better than existing augmentation frameworks for MLLMs, such as SoM, Visprog, and Visual Sketchpad.

3. **Cost Efficiency**: The approach significantly reduced API calling costs compared to previous methods, as the generated solutions are general and applicable to all instances of a task.

4. **Solution Generalization**: Unlike methods that generate task-specific solutions, MMFactory produces general solutions that work across different instances of the same task type.

These results validate the effectiveness of MMFactory's approach to solution generation and selection, demonstrating its potential to address complex vision-language tasks efficiently.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MMFactory has numerous potential applications across various domains:

1. **Custom AI Tool Development**: Organizations can use MMFactory to build specialized AI tools tailored to their specific visual analysis needs without requiring deep AI expertise.

2. **Resource-Constrained Environments**: The framework is particularly valuable in scenarios with limited computational resources, such as edge devices or mobile applications.

3. **Research Acceleration**: Researchers can leverage MMFactory to quickly prototype and test different approaches to vision-language tasks without manually implementing each solution.

4. **Educational Applications**: MMFactory can serve as an educational tool, helping students understand how different models and approaches can be combined to solve complex vision-language problems.

By automating the process of solution generation and selection, MMFactory makes advanced AI capabilities more accessible to a wider audience.

The introduction of MMFactory has several important implications for the future of AI development:

1. **Democratization of AI**: By simplifying the process of building and deploying AI solutions, MMFactory could accelerate AI adoption across industries and reduce the expertise barrier.

2. **Customizable AI Agents**: The framework enables the creation of custom AI agents tailored to specific user needs and constraints, potentially leading to more diverse and specialized AI applications.

3. **Resource Optimization**: MMFactory's consideration of resource limitations could drive more efficient use of computational resources in AI applications.

4. **Model Interoperability**: The approach encourages greater interoperability between different AI models and tools, potentially fostering a more collaborative AI development ecosystem.

These implications suggest that frameworks like MMFactory could play a significant role in shaping how AI solutions are developed and deployed in the future.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

MMFactory represents a significant advancement in the field of vision-language tasks by providing a universal framework for automatically generating programmatic solutions based on user-defined specifications and constraints. By leveraging a multi-agent system and considering both performance and resource requirements, it addresses key limitations in existing approaches.

The framework's ability to generate general, executable solutions that can be easily deployed and reused makes it a valuable tool for researchers, developers, and organizations seeking to leverage AI for vision-language tasks. As AI continues to evolve, approaches like MMFactory that focus on accessibility, customization, and resource efficiency will be crucial in ensuring that the benefits of AI can be realized across diverse applications and contexts.

By bridging the gap between powerful AI models and practical, deployable solutions, MMFactory takes an important step toward making advanced AI capabilities more accessible and useful for real-world applications.
