# VideoAgent: All-in-One Framework for Video Understanding and Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoAgent is an all-in-one agentic framework for video understanding and editing. A shot planning agent writes storyboards from captioned footage, and cross-modal retrieval plus VLM trimming fetches matching clips. Over thirty tool agents are composed into workflow graphs by intent parsing and textual-gradient graph optimization. It reaches 87-95% orchestration success, cuts API cost by 60%, and rates within 4% of human-made videos.

## Problem

- Existing video systems handle only specific understanding or editing tasks and cannot cover the diverse operations real editing workflows need, such as audio, rhythm, voice and style tools.
- Prior methods lack long-video understanding and top-down planning, so they process isolated segments and fail to build coherent multi-shot narratives from large material libraries.
- Agent orchestration relies on predefined workflows or single-pass LLM graph generation with no systematic quality assessment, so complex, interdependent multimodal pipelines are brittle and expensive.

## Method

- ==Global-aware shot creation==: a ==shot planning agent== turns compressed keyframe captions plus the instruction into storyboards, then ==ImageBind/CLIP cosine retrieval== and a ==VLM trimming agent== select and cut clips to target durations.
- ==Multi-agent library== of over thirty tool agents, covering foundational processing (beat extraction, voice synthesis, lip-sync, transcription) and expert creation prompts. Shot creation is also wrapped as an agent.
- ==Textual-gradient graph optimization==: an LLM iteratively edits a ==DAG of agents==, guided by ==structural loss== (cycles, disconnected components) and intent-coverage and edge-compatibility signals, after ==intent parsing== filters the relevant tools.

## Results

- On the new VideoEdit benchmark, VideoAgent reaches **0.87-0.95** orchestration success across four LLM backbones, beating the best baseline (GraphCounselor, **0.81-0.86**) by **2-25%**.
- On Shot2Story, wrapping GPT-4o with VideoAgent lifts Recall@1 from **31.22%** to **48.85%** and Gemini-2.5-flash from **30.04%** to **44.93%**, while API cost falls about **60%** (**0.253** to **0.099** for GPT-4o).
- Ablations: removing gradient optimization drops success from over **90%** to roughly **50-70%**, and removing intent parsing raises cost. Human raters (**26** participants) score its videos only **4%** below human-created ones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Framing agent-workflow construction as iterative optimization with explicit structural and semantic critiques beats single-pass LLM planning, and more optimization rounds shrink the gap between backbone LLMs.
> - Planning with a compressed global summary before retrieval gives coherent, cheaper long-video handling than feeding all candidate footage into context, improving accuracy and cost together.
> - Pre-filtering tools by parsed intent barely changes accuracy but cuts cost, and ablating a highly central agent collapses success, showing dependency-aware orchestration matters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The work presented in "VideoAgent" is situated within the evolving field of automated digital media creation, particularly video editing, which has seen substantial growth driven by platforms such as YouTube and TikTok. This demand for content creation has highlighted a gap between the complexity of professional video editing tools and the technical accessibility for general users, leading to a need for automated systems capable of interpreting natural language instructions for sophisticated editing tasks.

Prior research has made advancements in leveraging artificial intelligence for multimodal data understanding and editing. Examples include Deep Video Discovery (DVD) for autonomous video exploration using LLM-coordinated tool ensembles, VideoRAG for complex reasoning over long videos via a dual-channel architecture, and mPLUG-2 for unified text, image, and video understanding. These efforts demonstrate foundational capabilities in multimodal processing and understanding.

However, the authors identify two limitations in existing approaches concerning automated video editing:
1. **Task Specificity**: Current systems often concentrate on particular understanding or editing tasks, lacking the versatility to handle the diverse range of operations required in real-world video production scenarios. This limits their applicability to general-purpose video creation.
2. **Long-Video Comprehension**: Existing methods generally exhibit insufficient capabilities for understanding and planning over long video durations. This deficiency hinders the creation of videos with substantial length and coherent narrative structures, a requirement for professional-quality content.

VideoAgent aims to address these identified limitations by proposing an integrated agentic framework designed for both comprehensive video understanding and versatile editing, with a particular focus on long-form video planning and multi-agent workflow orchestration. This positions VideoAgent as an attempt to bridge the gap between specialized AI tools and a holistic, human-level automated video creation pipeline. It extends multi-agent systems, predominantly used in text-centric applications, to multimodal video editing workflows.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an all-in-one agentic framework, named VideoAgent, that can achieve human-level automated video creation capabilities. This objective is driven by the identified challenges in current automated video editing systems, specifically their inability to handle diverse operations and their limitations in long-video understanding for coherent narrative generation.

The key motivations and specific challenges VideoAgent aims to address are detailed as follows:

- **Coherent Long-Form Video Planning:** Professional video production necessitates narratives that are coherent across multiple shots and extended durations. This requires sophisticated, top-down shot planning that involves multi-step decomposition and strong reasoning capabilities. For instance, creating a movie trailer requires understanding character arcs, identifying climactic moments, and sequencing emotional beats to build narrative tension. Such planning must consider available visual materials, demanding global understanding and precise retrieval from extensive content libraries. The current limitation is that existing approaches primarily focus on isolated segment understanding and editing, lacking the holistic story creation capabilities crucial for professional-quality output. VideoAgent seeks to provide a solution for generating coherent narrative structures and retrieving semantically aligned visual content from extensive material libraries.

- **Multi-Agent Workflow Orchestration:** Video editing tasks involve orchestrating a diverse array of tools, ranging from foundational utilities (e.g., audio extraction, rhythm detection) to more complex creative agents (e.g., dialogue creation, narrative structuring). These tools often operate across multiple modalities and have interdependencies. Effective production requires systems that can interpret natural language instructions, identify creative patterns, and dynamically compose non-linear workflow networks that coordinate multi-modal processing. An example is creating a music video synchronized to beat drops, which requires integrating audio analysis, rhythm detection, visual retrieval, and temporal alignment seamlessly. Existing methods are often restricted to specific domains or predefined workflows, lacking the comprehensive tool libraries and adaptive orchestration required for general-purpose content creation. VideoAgent aims to design a framework that integrates numerous specialized editing agents through dynamic workflow composition, employing intent parsing and textual-gradient graph optimization for assembling complex editing pipelines.

In summary, the core motivation is to overcome the limitations of current automated video editing systems by enabling comprehensive video understanding, coherent long-form video planning, and flexible, adaptive multi-agent workflow orchestration, thereby making product-level automated video creation accessible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoAgent proposes an all-in-one agentic framework built upon two primary technical innovations: automated video shot creation and multi-agent graph orchestration.

#### 3.1. Video Editing Task Formalization

The video editing task is defined as a function `f` that takes a natural language instruction `I` and a collection of multimodal content resources `M` as input, and produces a temporally ordered sequence of video shots `V` as output. Each shot `v_i` in `V` is a coherent video segment comprising synchronized visual and audio components.

#### 3.2. Automated Video Shot Creation

This component focuses on generating coherent video narratives and retrieving appropriate visual content.

- **Shot Planning Agent**: To generate the sequence of video shots `V`, a shot planning agent produces structured, shot-level storyboards in natural language. Each storyboard provides a textual description of the visual content for a specific shot, ensuring narrative coherence. This agent operates with global awareness by:
 1. Processing keyframe captions from all available visual materials to generate a compressed visual summary (`C`).
 2. Combining this visual summary with the user instruction (`I`) to generate the required number of shot descriptions (`S`) that form the overall narrative structure. The process is guided by specific prompts (`p_s`, `p_c`) for shot planning and visual context generation.

- **Shot Visual Content Retrieval**: Given the storyboards (`S`), VideoAgent uses paired video indexing and retrieval modules.
 1. **Indexing**: Keyframes are extracted from each video segment, and cross-modal encoders (ImageBind, CLIP) generate embeddings for these keyframes within a unified text-visual representation space.
 2. **Retrieval**: For each storyboard `s_i`, its embedding is generated using the same cross-modal encoder. Visual content (keyframes) with the highest cosine similarity to the storyboard embedding is then selected, retrieving semantically aligned materials for each shot.

- **Fine-grained Video Trimming**: Raw retrieved materials typically have uniform lengths. To align shot lengths with narrative rhythm (e.g., music beats, dialogue duration), a fine-grained video trimming agent is introduced. This agent employs Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to select the most suitable sub-segment from the retrieved material `m_r_i` based on the shot's storyboard text and target duration. This ensures precise temporal alignment of visual content with narrative requirements, yielding the final trimmed video segment `v_i`.

#### 3.3. Multi-Agent Graph Orchestration

This component enables sophisticated video editing operations beyond simple concatenation, through a system of specialized agents and dynamic workflow composition.

- **Multimodal Editing Agent Library**: VideoAgent integrates over thirty specialized editing agents categorized into:
 - **Foundational multimodal processing tools**: These perform essential technical operations such as music beat extraction, multi-emotional voice synthesis, face swapping, lip-sync generation, audio transcription, and visual style transfer.
 - **Creation prompts with human expertise**: These offer guidance for content generation, including style-specific scriptwriting, lyrical composition, and meme creation. Examples include transforming stand-up routines into crosstalk or synchronizing video cuts with music rhythms. The shot creation functionalities (Section 4.2) are also encapsulated as tool agents within this library.

- **Textual-Gradient Graph Optimization**: Instead of single-pass graph generation, VideoAgent reformulates graph construction as an iterative optimization problem over graph topology space, progressively correcting structural deficiencies.
 1. **Optimization Objective**: The goal is to find an optimal agent graph `G*` that minimizes a loss function comprising `L_struct` (measuring structural validity violations like cyclic dependencies or disconnected components) and `L_align` (quantifying instruction-graph alignment error).
 2. **Graph Quality Assessment**: At each iteration `t`, the graph `G^(t)` is evaluated using multi-faceted quality metrics:
 - **Topological properties**: Acyclicity validation (`τ`) ensures valid execution orderings.
 - **Intent parsing coverage**: Compares extracted intents (`T`) from the user instruction against the capabilities (`Ψ`) of agents in the graph.
 - **Edge consistency**: Scores compatibility between connected agents.
 These metrics (`χ`, `κ`, `τ`) form a composite quality signal `Q^(t)`.
 3. **Textual Gradient for Graph Topology**: To refine discrete graph structures using LLMs, textual gradients are introduced. The LLM synthesizes gradient directions by reasoning over the current graph `G^(t)`, quality metrics `Q^(t)`, user instruction `I`, intent decomposition `T`, and the agent library `A`. This process, guided by a prompt `p_topo`, generates graph modifications by:
 - Removing edges causing cycles or invalid dependencies.
 - Inserting missing agents to cover unaddressed intents.
 - Reordering execution sequences to satisfy data flow constraints.
 This approximates discrete gradient descent through symbolic reasoning over graph structures using natural language transformations.
 4. **Convergence Criterion**: The optimization terminates when both structural and alignment losses become zero, or when a maximum iteration count (`T_max`) is reached.

This methodology combines global narrative planning with a robust, adaptive multi-agent orchestration system, enabling flexible and coherent video creation from natural language instructions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VideoAgent involved experiments on a newly proposed VideoEdit benchmark, the Shot2Story dataset, and VideoRepurpose benchmark, as well as human evaluations. The findings address five research questions:

- **RQ1: Overall Performance Comparison**
 - **Workflow Orchestration**: VideoAgent consistently outperformed baseline agentic systems across various LLM backbones (Claude-Sonnet-4, Claude-Sonnet-3.7, GPT-4o, Deepseek-v3). It achieved success rates ranging from 0.87 to 0.95, surpassing the best baseline by 2% to 25%. This indicates its ability to handle multimodal complexity through its flexible agent graph and gradient-based orchestration.
 - **Video Understanding and Retrieval**: VideoAgent substantially improved retrieval performance compared to foundational models. For example, GPT-4o's Recall@1 increased from 31.22% to 48.85%, and Gemini-2.5-flash from 30.04% to 44.93%. This improvement is attributed to the shot planning agent's global perception, which generates coherent storyboards for accurate retrieval. It also outperformed RAG and agent frameworks in visual-language alignment metrics such as Embedding Matching (EM) and Intersection over Union (IoU).
 - **Cost-Efficiency**: VideoAgent demonstrated a 60% reduction in API costs while maintaining superior performance. This is achieved through strategic shot planning and targeted retrieval, which minimize redundant processing compared to baselines that process all candidate shots.
 - **Generalization (VideoRepurpose)**: On the VideoRepurpose benchmark, VideoAgent consistently achieved superior Recall and IoU scores compared to commercial models and specialized video understanding models, further validating its effectiveness across diverse datasets.
 - **Computational Efficiency**: While achieving superior task completion quality, VideoAgent maintained competitive latency in workflow orchestration compared to other approaches, demonstrating a balance between effectiveness and efficiency.

- **Human Evaluation on Video Quality**: Human ratings from 26 participants across 18 trending video categories indicated that VideoAgent generally outperformed four baseline video generation agents. VideoAgent achieved significantly higher scores in categories where existing methods partially failed. Its quality ratings were reported to be only 4% below human-created videos, approaching or exceeding human-level performance in several categories, while human creators exhibited more variable skill levels. The evaluation considered consistency, audio quality, and scene diversity.

- **RQ2: Ablation Study**
 - **Shot Planning Agent**: Removing the shot planning agent and directly using raw queries resulted in significant drops in retrieval metrics (Recall, EM Score, IoU), confirming the importance of global shot planning for coherent storyboards.
 - **Cross-Modal Representation (CM Rep.)**: Replacing the proposed cross-modal representation with caption-then-encoding via a text embedding model yielded inferior results, validating that the CM Rep. paradigm better preserves visual fidelity.
 - **Agent Graph Orchestration (-G)**: Removing the gradient-based optimization significantly reduced workflow orchestration success rates from over 90% to below 55%, highlighting its critical role in handling complex video editing workflows.
 - **Intent Parsing (-I)**: While its removal had limited impact on accuracy, it notably increased API costs, confirming its role in cost-efficiency.
 - **Key Agent Dependencies**: Ablating individual agents (e.g., LoudnessNormalizer, AudioExtractor, StandUpSynth) led to proportional performance drops correlated with their centrality in the agent graph, demonstrating effective multi-agent orchestration.

- **RQ3: Hyperparameter Study**
 - **Frames per Segment**: Using only 1 frame per segment in shot planning significantly decreased recall, indicating insufficient visual information. Excessively increasing frame count led to slight performance declines due to potential noise from over-sampling.
 - **Optimization Rounds**: Increasing optimization rounds consistently improved the success rate of workflow orchestration, demonstrating performance scaling during inference. Sufficient gradient-based graph iterations were shown to reduce performance gaps between different underlying LLM models.

- **RQ4: LLM-Human Judge Consistency**: Claude-Sonnet-3.7 achieved the strongest alignment with human judgment, scoring 0.85–1.0 across accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 metrics for evaluating agent graph success. Across model-modality pairs, recall generally exceeded precision, suggesting that LLM evaluators were more prone to false positives than false negatives, indicating that the framework rarely overlooked genuinely successful orchestrations.

- **RQ5: Real-World Case Study**: A case study of creating a rhythm-synced Spiderman montage demonstrated VideoAgent's practical capabilities. The system dynamically composed agents based on user intent, synchronized visual cuts with musical beats, and retrieved target scenes from shot-level descriptions, illustrating seamless multimodal integration within a unified framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VideoAgent represents an advancement in the field of automated video understanding and editing by introducing a comprehensive agentic framework designed to address existing limitations in versatility and long-form narrative coherence.

**Significance:**
- **Integrated Framework:** VideoAgent unifies video understanding and editing tasks within a single framework, facilitating product-level automated video creation across diverse genres. This is a significant step beyond existing systems that often focus on isolated tasks or predefined workflows.
- **Novel Technical Solutions:** The introduction of global-aware shot creation and textual-gradient graph optimization addresses two fundamental challenges previously hindering fully automated video editing: coherent long-video planning and dynamic multi-agent orchestration. The iterative graph optimization via textual gradients provides a principled method for refining complex workflows.
- **Demonstrated Performance:** The system exhibits high orchestration success rates (87-95%) and substantial API cost reductions (60%), demonstrating both effectiveness and efficiency.
- **Human-Comparable Quality:** Human evaluation results, indicating quality ratings only 4% below professionally human-created videos, suggest that VideoAgent can produce content suitable for real-world application, bridging the accessibility gap for content creators.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Democratization of Video Editing:** By automating complex, multi-step workflows, VideoAgent has the potential to lower the barrier to entry for video content creation. This could empower individuals and small businesses without specialized skills or access to professional editors to produce high-quality videos for platforms like YouTube and TikTok.
- **Increased Content Production Efficiency:** For professional creators and media companies, VideoAgent could significantly reduce the time and resources required for video production, particularly for tasks involving extensive content libraries, long-form narratives, and multimodal synchronization.
- **Foundation for Future AI-driven Creativity:** The agentic framework and orchestration mechanisms could serve as a foundation for more sophisticated AI-driven creative tools, potentially extending to other multimodal content generation tasks beyond video editing.
- **Research Direction in Agentic AI:** The textual-gradient graph optimization method presents a novel approach to discrete combinatorial optimization using LLMs, which could influence future research in agentic systems and their application in complex, multi-step tasks across various domains.

**Limitations and Ethical Considerations:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, including VideoAgent's reliance on retrieval-based shot creation, which is constrained by the quality of available source footage. They suggest integrating generative video models as a future direction. Additionally, the potential for misuse of automated video editing capabilities to create misleading or manipulative content (e.g., misinformation) is acknowledged. The authors encourage future work on content moderation and responsible usage practices to mitigate these risks. Ethical considerations regarding the use of publicly available materials and avoidance of personal/sensitive data collection are also highlighted.
