# FlowBot++: Learning Generalized Articulated Objects Manipulation via Articulation Projection

> [!summary] Summary
> FlowBot++ predicts, from a single point cloud, both dense per-point Articulation Flow (instantaneous motion direction) and a new Articulation Projection (per-point displacement onto the articulation axis), fusing them via Gram-Schmidt to analytically compute a full multi-step manipulation trajectory. It generalizes zero-shot to unseen articulated objects, beating prior baselines on PartNet-Mobility and real-world Sawyer trials while cutting execution time from 17.1s to 1.2s per object.

## Problem

- Modular pipelines that segment, estimate connectivity, then fit articulation parameters are brittle since a single-stage failure propagates and breaks downstream manipulation on unseen objects.
- Per-point instantaneous-motion methods like FlowBot3D must re-predict at every timestep, producing jerky back-and-forth trajectories, costly repeated inference, and poor robustness under heavy self-occlusion.
- Directly regressing global articulation parameters (axis, origin) loses the per-point local-frame generalization advantage that dense flow-based predictions have shown across diverse object geometries and kinematics.

## Method

- ==Articulation Projection==: a novel dense per-point 3D representation giving the displacement vector from each point to its perpendicular projection on the object's articulation axis, complementing Articulation Flow.
- **FlowProjNet**: a single PointNet++-based network trained with joint L2 loss to regress both Articulation Flow and Articulation Projection per point from one point cloud across all object categories.
- ==Gram-Schmidt fusion== and MPC-style trajectory planning: orthogonalize the projection estimate against the flow via Gram-Schmidt, derive axis/origin via cross product, and interpolate a smooth K-step Rodrigues-formula trajectory replanned only every H steps.

## Results

- On PartNet-Mobility, **FlowBot++** achieves the lowest normalized distance (**0.09** avg train, **0.18-0.20** avg test) versus FlowBot3D (**0.29/0.73**), UMP-Net, Screw Parameters, and DAgger E2E baselines.
- Success rate (distance under 0.1 threshold) reaches **0.91** avg on train and **0.45-0.82** on test categories, exceeding FlowBot3D's **0.75/0.14-0.69** and all other baselines.
- Ablations show optimal replanning horizon H=7 and removing Gram-Schmidt correction or segmentation-mask aggregation both degrade performance, while wall-clock execution drops from **17.1s** to **1.2s** per object versus FlowBot3D.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining two complementary per-point representations, an instantaneous local signal and a projected global-structure signal, can outperform either one alone without sacrificing per-point generalization.
> - Lower-frequency replanning over longer analytically-derived trajectories can be both faster and more robust to occlusion than closed-loop per-step re-prediction.
> - Local per-point reference frames, rather than predictions relative to a global coordinate system, are a reusable mechanism for generalizing 3D manipulation policies to novel object geometries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of articulated objects (e.g., doors, drawers, cabinets) is a fundamental capability for robots aiming to operate effectively and autonomously in human-centric environments. However, equipping robots with this skill is challenging due to the immense diversity in object geometries, kinematic structures, and the need for sophisticated three-dimensional reasoning. The broader research landscape in this area is broadly categorized into methods that attempt to explicitly model object kinematics and those that learn manipulation policies more implicitly.

Traditionally, approaches have often relied on modular pipelines to estimate an object's kinematic tree, involving steps like part segmentation, connectivity estimation, and articulation parameter estimation. While structured, these methods often suffer from brittleness; a failure in any single module can lead to cascading errors and poor generalization to unseen or novel objects. Conversely, some end-to-end learning methods aim to directly map visual input to actions but frequently struggle with generalization across the wide spectrum of articulated object variations.

More recent advancements have explored learning-based methods, leveraging large-scale datasets and simulation. A particularly relevant predecessor to FlowBot++ is FlowBot3D (Eisner et al.), which introduced the concept of "Articulation Flow" – a dense per-point prediction of how each point on an object would move under instantaneous articulation. FlowBot3D demonstrated superior generalization to unseen articulated objects because its per-point prediction avoids the need for a global, object-specific coordinate system. However, a significant limitation of FlowBot3D was its inability to predict a full, multi-step trajectory. It required re-estimating the instantaneous motion at each time step, leading to jerky robot motions, high computational costs, and susceptibility to occlusions that change the observed flow.

FlowBot++ directly addresses these limitations. It positions itself as a synergistic approach that seeks to combine the best aspects of previous methods: the strong generalization capabilities of dense per-point predictions (like FlowBot3D) with the ability to infer stable, global articulation parameters for smooth, multi-step trajectory planning, all without requiring prior interaction with novel objects. It builds upon the "flow" paradigm, extending it with a novel representation that bridges the gap between instantaneous motion and full kinematic understanding, thereby pushing the boundaries of zero-shot generalization in articulated object manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable robotic system capable of manipulating novel articulated objects, transferring knowledge gained from training on a diverse set of other articulated objects. The authors explicitly aim for "zero-shot manipulation," meaning the robot should be able to articulate an object it has never encountered before, without any prior interaction or fine-tuning.

The core motivations for FlowBot++ stem from the identified shortcomings of existing approaches:

- **Bridging the Generalization Gap:** While humans intuitively manipulate novel articulated objects, robots struggle due to the complex 3D reasoning required. The paper seeks to enable similar generalization capabilities in robotic agents.
- **Addressing Brittleness of Modular Methods:** The inherent fragility of multi-stage pipelines for kinematic estimation motivates a more robust, integrated approach.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Instantaneous Motion Prediction:** FlowBot3D, despite its strong generalization, suffered from jerky motions, computational inefficiency, and susceptibility to occlusions because it could only predict instantaneous movements. The authors explicitly motivated the need for a system that could plan longer, smoother trajectories from an initial observation.
- **Enabling Multi-Step Trajectory Planning:** The lack of a mechanism in previous dense per-point methods to infer stable articulation parameters prevented the generation of smooth, pre-planned multi-step trajectories. FlowBot++ is motivated to fill this gap.
- **Reducing Computational Overhead:** Frequent re-prediction of instantaneous motion is computationally expensive. By inferring stable parameters, the system can reduce the replanning frequency, thus speeding up execution.

In essence, the motivation is to create a robot that can perceive articulated objects, understand their articulation mechanisms, and then execute smooth, efficient, and generalizable manipulation actions, moving beyond mere instantaneous responses to a more predictive and robust control strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FlowBot++ introduces a novel methodology that significantly advances articulated object manipulation by combining dense per-point motion estimation with a new representation for articulation parameters. The core of the approach is the joint prediction and integration of "Articulation Flow" (AF) and "Articulation Projection" (AP).

#### 3.1. Novel Representations: Articulation Flow and Articulation Projection

- **Articulation Flow (AF):** As introduced in FlowBot3D, AF (`f_p`) is a 3D vector for each point `p` on the object, representing its instantaneous motion direction when the joint articulates by an infinitesimal displacement in the "opening" direction. It is normalized by the maximum displacement on the part. AF is highly generalizable as it is defined relative to each point's local frame.
- **Articulation Projection (AP):** This is FlowBot++'s key novel representation (`r_p`). For each point `p` on the articulated part, AP is a vector representing the displacement from `p` to its orthogonal projection onto the part's articulation axis `v(t)`. Mathematically, for an axis defined by direction `ω` and origin `v`, `r_p = (ωω^T - I)(p - v)`. The significance of AP lies in its ability to encode the object's articulation parameters (`ω` and `v`) in a dense, per-point manner, thereby retaining the generalization advantages of per-point predictions while enabling the inference of global kinematic properties.

#### 3.2. Joint Learning and Articulation Parameter Inference

- **FlowProjNet:** A deep neural network, `FlowProjNet (f_θ)`, based on a PointNet++ backbone, is trained to jointly predict both `f_p` and `r_p` directly from a partial 3D point cloud observation (`O_t`). The network is trained with a supervised `L2` loss on the concatenated ground-truth `f_t` and `r_t`. Ground-truth labels are generated analytically from the PartNet-Mobility dataset's kinematic information.
- **Articulation Type Classifier:** A separate PointNet++ classifier (`f_ψ`) determines if the object has a prismatic (translational) or revolute (rotational) joint. This guides the subsequent trajectory planning.
- **Gram-Schmidt Correction:** To enhance the accuracy of the inferred articulation axis, a crucial step involves using Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization. The predicted Articulation Projection `r_p` is corrected to `~r_p = r_p - proj_fp r_p`, ensuring it is perpendicular to the Articulation Flow `f_p`. This leverages the complementary information from both predictions to yield a more robust and accurate axis estimate (`ω̂`).
- **Parameter Estimation:**
 - For **revolute** joints, the rotation axis `ω̂` is computed as the cross product of the corrected `~r_p` and `f_p` (`ω̂ = (~r_p × f_p) / ||~r_p × f_p||`). The origin `v̂` is estimated as `p + ~r_p`, which projects `p` onto the axis.
 - For **prismatic** joints, the axis `ω̂` is simply the normalized `f_p`, as the motion direction aligns with the axis.
- **Mean Aggregation:** For robustness, the per-point `ω̂` and `v̂` estimates are averaged over all points belonging to the segmented articulated part.

#### 3.3. Manipulation Policy and Trajectory Generation

- **Multi-Step Trajectory Planning:** Once `ω̂` and `v̂` are inferred, FlowBot++ can analytically compute a smooth, multi-step trajectory for the contact point.
 - For **revolute** joints, Rodrigues' Formula is used to generate a sequence of rotations (`R(ϕ)`) about the inferred `ω̂` and `v̂` for a desired goal angle `ϕ_g`.
 - For **prismatic** joints, a simple translation along `ω̂` for a desired goal distance `l_g` is computed.
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC)-style Execution:** Unlike FlowBot3D, which replans every step, FlowBot++ employs an MPC-style controller. It plans a longer trajectory (e.g., K steps) but executes only a subset (`H` steps, typically `H=7`) before re-observing the environment and re-planning. This significantly reduces replanning frequency, mitigates the effects of occlusions, and ensures smoother execution.
- **Robot Control:** A suction gripper is used for contact, and the planned contact point trajectory is translated into robot end-effector poses. For revolute joints, the end-effector's orientation is also iteratively updated based on the rotation matrix difference to maintain a consistent relative pose with the articulated part, enabling full 6D pose control.

#### 3.4. Training Details

- The system is trained via supervised learning on synthetic data generated from the PartNet-Mobility dataset. This dataset provides ground-truth kinematics for various articulated objects, allowing for the analytical computation of `f_p` and `r_p` labels.
- A binary part mask is provided as an additional input channel to the network, indicating the part of interest, allowing the network to condition its predictions.

This comprehensive methodology allows FlowBot++ to leverage the local generalization power of dense predictions while gaining the global consistency and multi-step planning benefits of inferred articulation parameters, integrated in a robust manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FlowBot++ demonstrates significant improvements over prior art through extensive simulation and real-world experiments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results (PartNet-Mobility Dataset)

- **Superior Manipulation Performance:** FlowBot++ consistently outperforms all tested baselines, including UMP-Net, Normal Direction, Screw Parameters, DAgger End2End, and the predecessor FlowBot3D (AF Only). This superiority is evident across both novel instances of seen categories and entirely unseen object categories, as measured by the "Normalized Distance" metric (Table 1), where lower values indicate better performance (closer to the target articulation joint angle).
- **Impact of Articulation Projection (AP) and Gram-Schmidt Correction:**
 - The "AP Only" version (our model without Gram-Schmidt correction) shows improved performance over FlowBot3D, highlighting the inherent advantage of the new Articulation Projection representation in deriving multi-step plans.
 - Crucially, the "FlowBot++ (Combined)" method, which integrates AF and AP via Gram-Schmidt correction, achieves the best results, validating the efficacy of combining both representations for enhanced accuracy.
- **Smoother and More Effective Trajectories:** Qualitative analysis (Fig. 5a) and quantitative metrics (Fig. 5b) demonstrate that FlowBot++ generates significantly smoother and more consistent trajectories. Unlike FlowBot3D, which often exhibits jerky motions and struggles with occlusions, FlowBot++'s ability to plan longer trajectories leads to a higher average angular acceleration and a larger opening fraction for the same number of steps. This is attributed to the reduced replanning frequency and the robustness against immediate occlusions.
- **Reduced Execution Time:** A major finding is the drastic reduction in execution time. In simulation, FlowBot++ reduced the average wall-clock time to open an object from 17.1 seconds (FlowBot3D) to a mere 1.2 seconds. This efficiency gain is attributed to the MPC-style controller with lower replanning frequency (H=7 steps), which makes the system less prone to occlusion-induced errors and back-and-forth movements.
- **High Success Rate:** FlowBot++ also achieves higher success rates (defined as articulating a part for >90% of its range of motion) compared to baselines (Table 4), corroborating its overall improved performance.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - The optimal lookahead horizon `H` for the MPC-style controller was found to be `H=7`, balancing between too frequent replanning (like FlowBot3D) and relying too heavily on initial predictions (Table 2).
 - Mean aggregation of per-point predictions using segmentation masks significantly contributes to robustness, especially for revolute objects (Table 3).

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Strong Sim2Real Transfer:** FlowBot++ successfully transfers its learned capabilities from simulation to real-world objects without any fine-tuning or retraining. Tested on 6 diverse real-world objects (Drawer, Fridge, Oven, Toilet, Microwave, Trashcan), the system was able to predict and execute feasible full opening trajectories (Fig. 6, Fig. 8).
- **Enhanced Stability and Reduced Unwanted Motion:** Real-world trials confirmed the observations from simulation: FlowBot++ produced visibly smoother trajectories and minimized undesired movements of the entire object body (Fig. 9). By controlling the full 6D pose of the gripper through the planned trajectory, the relative pose between the gripper and the articulated part remained consistent, preventing the gripper from yanking or misaligning the object. In contrast, FlowBot3D's step-by-step re-prediction often led to unstable motions and even failure (e.g., tipping over a fridge or failing to open an oven).
- **Feasibility in Challenging Scenarios:** FlowBot++ successfully opened a real-world oven door in a single planning step, a task on which FlowBot3D reportedly failed completely.

#### 4.3. Limitations

The authors acknowledge two primary limitations:
1. **Compound Failure:** If both Articulation Flow and Articulation Projection predictions are simultaneously incorrect, the system's ability to correct the trajectory is compromised, potentially leading to failure.
2. **Segmentation Mask Dependency:** The current system relies on an externally provided (hand-labeled in real-world experiments) segmentation mask to filter and aggregate per-point predictions. This adds another potential point of failure if the mask is inaccurate or not available.

Overall, the findings strongly validate FlowBot++'s design, demonstrating a robust, efficient, and highly generalizable solution for articulated object manipulation, surpassing prior methods in both simulated and real-world performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FlowBot++ represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation of articulated objects, particularly in its emphasis on zero-shot generalization and robust, smooth execution. Its contributions have several key implications and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Scientific Significance

- **Novel Representation:** The introduction of "Articulation Projection" as a dense, per-point representation for articulation parameters is a core scientific contribution. It elegantly bridges the gap between instantaneous motion (Articulation Flow) and global kinematic understanding, enabling full trajectory planning while retaining the strong generalization properties of per-point predictions.
- **Synergistic Integration:** The method for jointly learning and integrating Articulation Flow and Articulation Projection, especially through the Gram-Schmidt correction, demonstrates a powerful approach to leverage complementary information from different visual cues for enhanced accuracy and robustness.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to novel objects, including those from unseen categories, highlights a crucial step towards truly adaptive and intelligent robotic systems.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact and Applications

- **Robots in Human Environments:** By enabling robots to robustly and smoothly interact with common articulated objects like doors and drawers, FlowBot++ moves closer to deploying robots in unstructured human environments (homes, offices, warehouses). This could include assistive robotics, logistics, and service robotics.
- **Efficiency and Speed:** The drastic reduction in execution time (from 17.1s to 1.2s per object in simulation) makes robot manipulation tasks much more efficient, which is vital for real-world applications where task completion speed is critical.
- **Robustness to Occlusions:** The MPC-style control with lower replanning frequency significantly enhances robustness against self-occlusions and dynamic changes during manipulation, a common challenge in real-world robotics.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The method provides a strong foundation for future research in several directions:
 - **Joint Learning of Segmentation:** Eliminating the dependency on external segmentation masks by integrating a learned segmentation module directly into the FlowProjNet pipeline.
 - **Handling More Complex Articulations:** Extending the framework to more complex joint types (e.g., screw joints, multiple interacting joints) or non-rigid object manipulation.
 - **Uncertainty Quantification:** Developing methods to quantify prediction uncertainty, enabling the robot to identify and potentially recover from situations where both AF and AP predictions are highly inaccurate.
 - **Active Perception:** Integrating active perception strategies where the robot purposefully moves to gain better views for more accurate predictions, especially in challenging occlusion scenarios.

#### 5.3. Broader Implications

FlowBot++ represents a step forward in endowing robots with a deeper understanding of object functionality beyond static geometry. By learning to predict not just *how* an object can move, but *how* its parts are kinematically linked, the work contributes to the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic agents. The success in sim2real transfer without fine-tuning also reinforces the potential of simulation-based training for complex manipulation tasks, a critical area for scaling robotic capabilities.

In conclusion, FlowBot++ is an impactful contribution that advances the state-of-the-art in articulated object manipulation, addressing critical limitations of previous methods and paving the way for more capable and autonomously operating robots in diverse, real-world settings.
