# 100 Days After DeepSeek-R1: A Survey on Replication Studies and More Directions for Reasoning Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey by MiroMind and affiliated universities synthesizes open-source replication studies of DeepSeek-R1, detailing methodologies for reasoning language models (RLMs) through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR). It highlights the critical role of high-quality data and adapted RL algorithms in achieving strong reasoning capabilities, also identifying emerging research directions and challenges.

## Problem

- The emergence of powerful Reasoning Language Models (RLMs) like DeepSeek-R1, which demonstrate advanced step-by-step reasoning capabilities.
- DeepSeek-R1's proprietary nature and lack of full open-sourcing (training data, configurations) created a knowledge gap for the open-source community.
- A fragmented landscape of uncoordinated open-source replication efforts made it difficult for researchers to stay updated and identify best practices for RLM development.

## Method

- The paper provides a comprehensive survey of open-source replication studies related to DeepSeek-R1's reasoning capabilities.
- It systematically categorizes and analyzes ==training methodologies==, focusing on ==Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)== and ==Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)==.
- The survey synthesizes findings regarding data curation, algorithm adaptations, and training configurations, providing a structured overview and identifying future research directions.

## Results

- SFT replication studies primarily concentrate on math and coding datasets, emphasizing rigorous data cleaning, verification, and often benefiting from fine-tuning already capable instruction-tuned models.
- RLVR success relies on large, diverse datasets of moderately difficult problems, with numerous algorithm modifications (e.g., DAPO, Dr. GRPO, VC-PPO) proposed to enhance stability and efficiency.
- The role of KL loss in RLVR is debated among studies, and increased context lengths (up to **32k** tokens) and curriculum learning are effective strategies for improving performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, verified Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data is essential for effective Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) of reasoning language models (RLMs).
> - Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), particularly with rule-based outcome rewards and adapted algorithms, is crucial for refining and enhancing RLM reasoning.
> - RL-trained models demonstrate improved out-of-domain generalization across tasks and modalities, though safety concerns like reward hacking and overthinking are emerging.
