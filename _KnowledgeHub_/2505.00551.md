# 100 Days After DeepSeek-R1: A Survey on Replication Studies and More Directions for Reasoning Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey by MiroMind and affiliated universities synthesizes open-source replication studies of DeepSeek-R1, detailing methodologies for reasoning language models (RLMs) through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR). It highlights the critical role of high-quality data and adapted RL algorithms in achieving strong reasoning capabilities, also identifying emerging research directions and challenges.

## Problem

- The emergence of powerful Reasoning Language Models (RLMs) like DeepSeek-R1, which demonstrate advanced step-by-step reasoning capabilities.
- DeepSeek-R1's proprietary nature and lack of full open-sourcing (training data, configurations) created a knowledge gap for the open-source community.
- A fragmented landscape of uncoordinated open-source replication efforts made it difficult for researchers to stay updated and identify best practices for RLM development.

## Method

- The paper provides a comprehensive survey of open-source replication studies related to DeepSeek-R1's reasoning capabilities.
- It systematically categorizes and analyzes ==training methodologies==, focusing on ==Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)== and ==Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)==.
- The survey synthesizes findings regarding data curation, algorithm adaptations, and training configurations, providing a structured overview and identifying future research directions.

## Results

- SFT replication studies primarily concentrate on math and coding datasets, emphasizing rigorous data cleaning, verification, and often benefiting from fine-tuning already capable instruction-tuned models.
- RLVR success relies on large, diverse datasets of moderately difficult problems, with numerous algorithm modifications (e.g., DAPO, Dr. GRPO, VC-PPO) proposed to enhance stability and efficiency.
- The role of KL loss in RLVR is debated among studies, and increased context lengths (up to **32k** tokens) and curriculum learning are effective strategies for improving performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, verified Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data is essential for effective Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) of reasoning language models (RLMs).
> - Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), particularly with rule-based outcome rewards and adapted algorithms, is crucial for refining and enhancing RLM reasoning.
> - RL-trained models demonstrate improved out-of-domain generalization across tasks and modalities, though safety concerns like reward hacking and overthinking are emerging.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work directly addresses a significant trend in the rapidly evolving field of large language models (LLMs): the emergence and growing importance of Reasoning Language Models (RLMs). Traditional LLMs are adept at generating text, but RLMs aim to mimic human cognitive processes, providing explicit step-by-step reasoning (Chain-of-Thought, CoT) for complex tasks like mathematical problem-solving, code generation, and logical inference. This shift is crucial for enhancing explainability, accuracy, and trustworthiness in AI systems.

The paper is specifically positioned as a direct response to the recent release of DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025), a proprietary RLM that demonstrated powerful reasoning capabilities through innovative training techniques, notably Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). However, DeepSeek's lack of full open-sourcing of its training data and configurations prompted a wave of "replication studies" across the open-source community. This survey steps into this gap by comprehensively summarizing these replication efforts, making it a critical contribution to the open science movement within AI.

By providing a structured overview of the methodologies, datasets, and findings from these diverse replication works, the survey aids reproducibility and transparency. It serves as a benchmark and a guide for researchers attempting to build or improve their own RLMs, fostering further innovation in a field where proprietary models often guard their "secret sauce." Furthermore, by discussing "More Directions," the paper extends beyond mere replication analysis, actively shaping the future research agenda for RLMs, including addressing generalizability, safety, and multimodal/multilingual applications. It underscores the community's collective effort to demystify advanced LLM capabilities and push towards more transparent, robust, and accessible AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this survey are explicitly articulated in its abstract and introduction:

1. **Summarize Recent Replication Studies:** The core motivation is to provide a comprehensive summary of open-source replication efforts that emerged in response to the DeepSeek-R1 model. DeepSeek-R1's impressive performance, particularly its explicit reasoning paradigm and novel training techniques (SFT, RLVR), generated significant interest. However, the lack of full open-sourcing of its implementation details (e.g., training data, specific configurations) created a knowledge gap. This survey aims to fill that gap by aggregating insights from various attempts to reproduce DeepSeek-R1's capabilities.
2. **Inspire Future Research:** By synthesizing the findings, methodologies, and challenges encountered by replication studies, the authors aim to identify best practices and underexplored areas. This consolidated knowledge is intended to "inspire new ideas to further enhance RLMs" and guide the community towards more fruitful research directions.
3. **Help Researchers and Developers Stay Updated:** The rapid pace of RLM development can make it challenging for researchers and developers to keep abreast of the latest advancements. This survey serves as a timely resource, offering a structured overview of the current state-of-the-art in open-source RLM training, enabling practitioners to optimize their models effectively.
4. **Promote Deeper Understanding of RLM Training:** The survey delves into the intricate details of data construction, method design, and training procedures for both SFT and RLVR. By analyzing common practices and variations, it seeks to provide a deeper understanding of the factors contributing to strong RLM performance, particularly those related to self-verification and reflection behaviors.
5. **Identify Challenges and Opportunities:** Beyond merely summarizing, the paper also highlights the strengths and weaknesses of current RLMs (e.g., powerful generalization vs. occasional overthinking) and discusses the challenges in their development, particularly in areas like safety, generalizability, and extending to multimodal/multilingual contexts. This critical analysis helps pinpoint crucial areas for future investigation.

In essence, the survey is motivated by a desire to bring clarity and coherence to a fragmented field, accelerate open-source RLM development, and foster a more informed approach to building intelligent reasoning systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper is a survey, and its methodology involves a systematic review and synthesis of existing open-source research efforts related to Reasoning Language Models (RLMs), with a particular focus on those attempting to replicate or advance the capabilities observed in DeepSeek-R1. The approach can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Categorization of Training Methodologies:** The survey organizes its analysis around the two primary training paradigms identified as crucial for DeepSeek-R1's success:
 - **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** This section reviews studies that leverage high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets to imbue base models with reasoning capabilities.
 - **Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** This section examines works that employ reinforcement learning, guided by objective, verifiable rewards, to further refine and enhance reasoning.

2. **Detailed Review of SFT Aspects:**
 - **SFT Datasets:** The authors analyze data collection and curation pipelines, describing how questions from diverse domains (math, science, coding, puzzles) are gathered, filtered (deduplication, rejection sampling), and verified (Math Verify, code execution, LLM judges). Specific public datasets (e.g., OpenThoughts, OpenR1-Math-220k, Light-R1-SFT) are detailed, including their size, categories, and distillation sources (primarily DeepSeek-R1).
 - **Analysis and Discussions on SFT Data:** The survey provides insights into token length distributions, discusses data contamination risks against benchmarks, and illustrates cross-referencing among popular math reasoning datasets to highlight dependencies and shared sources.
 - **Training & Performance Comparison:** It formalizes the SFT objective and compares the performance of various SFT models on common benchmarks (AIME24/25, MATH500), noting initial checkpoints and training details (e.g., learning rates, batch sizes, context length configurations).

3. **In-depth Examination of RLVR Components:**
 - **RL Datasets:** Similar to SFT, it details datasets specifically curated for RLVR, focusing on verifiable math and coding problems (e.g., DeepScaleR, Skywork-OR1, Open-Reasoner-Zero), emphasizing data cleaning, de-duplication, and difficulty assessment.
 - **RL Components (Algorithms, Rewards, Sampling):**
 - **Algorithms:** The survey presents a unified theoretical framework for prevalent RL algorithms (REINFORCE, PPO, GRPO) and their various modifications (e.g., REINFORCE++, DAPO, Dr. GRPO, CPPO, GPG, VC-PPO, VAPO), elucidating their motivations for addressing issues like variance, instability, and bias.
 - **Rewards:** It discusses the design of rule-based outcome reward systems, primarily categorized into accuracy, format, and length rewards, and their implications for model behavior and potential for reward hacking.
 - **Sampling Strategies:** Techniques like curriculum learning based on difficulty, dynamic/rejection sampling, and epoch-level history resampling are explored for their role in improving sample efficiency and training stability.

4. **Synthesis and Discussion of RLVR Findings:** The authors draw conclusions from the implementation details and experimental results across replication studies, analyzing the "recipes" for training data, RL algorithm design choices, impact of model size and type, context length considerations, reward modeling nuances, and the role of KL loss.

5. **Exploration of "More Directions":** The survey extends its scope beyond direct replication to identify broader research avenues:
 - **Alternative Approaches:** It discusses advanced reward modeling (Process-level Reward Modeling, PRM) and efficient preference optimization techniques (Direct Preference Optimization, DPO) as alternatives or complements to traditional RLVR.
 - **Generalizability:** It investigates how SFT and RL contribute to generalization, particularly for out-of-distribution tasks, and notes the trade-offs.
 - **Safety:** It addresses emerging safety concerns specific to RLMs, such as reward hacking, jailbreaking attacks, and overthinking, along with proposed mitigation strategies.
 - **Multimodal and Multilingual Extensions:** It briefly discusses the challenges and opportunities in extending RLMs to handle diverse modalities (visual, audio) and languages.

Overall, the methodology is characterized by a structured, comparative analysis of recent open-source advancements, aiming to extract actionable insights and identify future research priorities in the domain of reasoning language models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey distills numerous insights and key findings from the collective efforts of the open-source community to replicate and advance DeepSeek-R1's reasoning capabilities. These findings span data curation, training methodologies, and broader RLM characteristics.

#### 4.1. Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) Findings

- **Data Quality is Paramount:** High-quality, verified Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data is crucial for effective SFT. Datasets are typically curated by collecting questions from diverse domains (math, coding, science) and generating/verifying CoTs using powerful LLMs like DeepSeek-R1, often with domain-specific verifiers (e.g., Math Verify, code execution).
- **Focus on Math and Coding:** The majority of replication datasets for SFT concentrate on math and coding problems, with broader reasoning tasks still relatively underrepresented. This specialization allows for targeted improvement in areas requiring explicit, step-by-step logic.
- **Data Curation Pipelines:** Common practices include rigorous deduplication, rejection sampling, and ground-truth verification. Some studies use intermediate model pass rates to select challenging problems, ensuring the data is neither too easy nor too hard.
- **Data Contamination:** A significant concern highlighted is data contamination, where popular benchmarks (e.g., MATH500) are partially compromised across multiple open-source datasets, posing challenges for fair evaluation.
- **Starting Checkpoints:** While DeepSeek-R1 uses base models, many replication studies achieve strong results by fine-tuning from already capable instruction-tuned models like Qwen2.5-Instruct, which are observed to have higher learning efficiency.
- **Non-Reasoning Data:** DeepSeek-R1 uniquely incorporates non-reasoning data during SFT, a practice less explored by replication studies, suggesting an open question about its interplay with reasoning capabilities.
- **Training Configuration:** Common SFT practices involve adjusting RoPE scaling factors and maximum context lengths for long-context reasoning, with typical learning rates between 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ and 5.0 × 10⁻⁵, and packing for efficiency.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) Findings

- **Verifiable Outcome Rewards are Key:** Rule-based outcome rewards (accuracy, format, length penalties) are favored due to their simplicity and ability to minimize reward hacking compared to learned reward models. While accuracy is fundamental, format rewards can quickly saturate or even incentivize undesirable behaviors.
- **RL Datasets – Quantity, Diversity, Difficulty:** RL training benefits from large, diverse datasets. Crucially, successful RL datasets often consist of problems where models are likely to make mistakes (moderate pass rates), creating learning opportunities. Strict data cleaning (removing unsolvable/unverifiable samples, hyperlinks, multi-part questions) is vital.
- **Algorithm Adaptations:** While PPO and GRPO are foundational, replication studies introduce numerous modifications to address stability and efficiency:
 - **GRPO Variants:** DAPO (Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization) tackles entropy collapse, length bias, and gradient decreasing by using decoupled clipping, dynamic sampling (filtering zero-advantage samples), and token-level loss. Dr. GRPO removes length and advantage normalization terms to improve token efficiency. CPPO (Completions Pruning Policy Optimization) accelerates training by skipping low-impact completions. GPG (Group Policy Gradient) simplifies the objective by directly optimizing the true objective without surrogate losses.
 - **PPO Variants:** VC-PPO (Value-Calibrated PPO) and VAPO (Value-based Augmented Proximal Policy Optimization) focus on mitigating value initialization bias and reward signal decay in long CoT reasoning, often through value pretraining and decoupled GAE trace decay rates.
- **KL Loss is Debatable:** Some studies (Open-Reasoner-Zero, DAPO, MiMo, Skywork-OR1) successfully omit KL loss, arguing it can restrict exploration and response length. Others (Light-R1, Logic-RL) retain it and report improvements, suggesting its effectiveness might be context-dependent.
- **Context Length and Curriculum Learning:** Increased maximum response lengths (up to 32k tokens) are common to accommodate longer CoTs. Curriculum learning, progressively increasing the maximum response length or task difficulty, has proven effective in improving performance.
- **Model Size and Type:** RLVR has shown effectiveness across a wide range of model sizes (1.5B to 32B parameters) and types (both base and R1-distilled instruction models), with strong gains observed on various scales.

#### 4.3. Emerging Directions and Challenges

- **Alternative Reward Modeling:** Process-level Reward Modeling (PRM) is emerging as a more expressive approach, providing step-level feedback to guide complex reasoning, often through Process Preference Models (PPM) or implicit PRM trained on outcome labels.
- **Preference Optimization (DPO):** DPO is gaining traction as a computationally efficient alternative to online RL (PPO/GRPO), showing promising results in improving reasoning with less overhead, though sometimes slightly lagging behind PPO.
- **Generalizability:** RL, especially with diverse on- and off-policy data, can significantly enhance out-of-domain generalization across tasks, languages, and modalities, often outperforming SFT in this regard. However, SFT remains crucial for stabilizing output format and bootstrapping initial reasoning. There are also observations that RL might sometimes narrow reasoning scope for smaller models.
- **Safety:** RLMs introduce new safety concerns like reward hacking (exploiting flaws in reward functions), jailbreaking attacks (triggering unsafe outputs), and "overthinking" (excessive computation). Mitigating these requires robust safety alignment and decoding strategies, which can sometimes trade-off with reasoning capability.
- **Multimodality and Multilinguality:** Extending RLMs to multimodal (visual, audio) and multilingual contexts remains challenging. While reasoning capabilities can transfer across modalities, base model weaknesses and resource limitations (especially for low-resource languages) hinder performance, suggesting a need for specialized training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the field of reasoning language models (RLMs) and the broader AI research community, particularly in fostering open science and accelerating innovation.

**1. Consolidating Fragmented Knowledge and Best Practices:** The paper serves as a vital resource by meticulously aggregating, categorizing, and analyzing a multitude of open-source replication studies of DeepSeek-R1. This consolidation is crucial in a rapidly moving field where information is often dispersed across various pre-print servers, Hugging Face repositories, and blog posts. By summarizing common practices in data curation, algorithm design, and training configurations for both SFT and RLVR, the survey provides a structured compendium of "recipes" for building and enhancing RLMs. This helps researchers avoid redundant efforts, learn from collective experiences, and adopt effective strategies, thus streamlining future R&D.

**2. Promoting Reproducibility and Transparency:** In an era where many state-of-the-art LLMs remain proprietary with undisclosed training details, this survey champions reproducibility. By highlighting the successful replication efforts and the methodologies employed, it contributes to demystifying the complex capabilities of models like DeepSeek-R1. This transparency is essential for scientific progress, allowing the community to understand *how* these models acquire reasoning abilities, rather than just *that* they do.

**3. Guiding Future Research Directions:** Beyond synthesizing current knowledge, the survey critically evaluates existing approaches and identifies open questions and challenges. Its "More Directions" section explicitly points towards promising avenues such as advanced reward modeling techniques (e.g., Process-level Reward Modeling), more efficient alignment strategies (e.g., Direct Preference Optimization), and crucial considerations like generalizability, safety, multimodality, and multilinguality. This foresight helps to shape the research agenda, directing efforts towards addressing fundamental limitations and expanding RLM capabilities.

**4. Informing Model Development and Deployment:** For developers, the practical insights on data construction (quantity, diversity, difficulty, cleaning), algorithm selection and modification, reward system design, and training hyperparameters are invaluable. Understanding the trade-offs (e.g., KL loss impact on exploration, safety vs. helpfulness) empowers better decision-making in building robust and reliable RLMs. The discussions on safety, in particular, are critical for the responsible deployment of increasingly capable AI systems, mitigating risks like reward hacking and jailbreaking.

**5. Accelerating Progress Towards AGI:** The emphasis on explicit reasoning, self-verification, and reflection in RLMs is a step towards more human-like intelligence. By thoroughly analyzing how these capabilities are acquired and enhanced, the survey contributes to the foundational understanding necessary for developing more generally intelligent AI systems. The observation of strong out-of-domain generalization in RL-trained models, in particular, offers tantalizing hints at the path toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

In conclusion, "100 DAYS AFTER DEEPSEEK-R1" is not merely a summary; it is a strategic document that maps the current landscape of RLM research, highlights critical successes and failures, and provides a clear roadmap for future innovation. Its impact will likely be felt through accelerated open-source development, more robust and transparent RLM deployments, and a more focused pursuit of advanced AI reasoning capabilities.
