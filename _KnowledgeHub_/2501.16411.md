# PhysBench: Benchmarking and Enhancing Vision-Language Models for Physical World Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> A new comprehensive benchmark, PhysBench, evaluates Vision-Language Models' (VLMs) understanding of the physical world, revealing an average accuracy of approximately 40% across 75 models. The proposed PhysAgent framework enhances VLMs' physical reasoning by 18.4% for GPT-4o on this benchmark, directly improving robotic manipulation task performance.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with understanding real-world physical interactions and dynamics, leading to operational errors in embodied AI agents.
- Existing benchmarks for physical comprehension are limited, focusing on vision-only, simplistic scenarios, or common-sense VQA rather than deep physical perception and dynamic understanding.
- There is a lack of comprehensive tools to diagnose the specific factors (e.g., perception, knowledge, reasoning) contributing to VLMs' limitations in physical world understanding.

## Method

- Introduction of **PhysBench**, a comprehensive human-annotated benchmark with 10,002 interleaved video-image-text entries covering object properties, relationships, scene understanding, and physics-based dynamics, for evaluating 75 diverse VLMs.
- Development of PhysAgent, a novel framework that enhances VLMs' physical understanding by integrating specialized Vision Foundation Models (e.g., Depth Anything, SAM) and explicit physical knowledge memory into a three-step reasoning process.
- Validation of PhysAgent's impact through application in embodied AI, demonstrating improved performance of a MOKA-based robotic manipulation system across five fundamental tasks.

## Results

- 75 representative VLMs achieved an average accuracy of approximately **40%** on PhysBench, with the best model (GPT-4o) reaching only **49.49%**, highlighting a substantial gap from human performance.
- PhysAgent significantly boosted zero-shot performance, improving GPT-4o's accuracy by **18.4%** overall on PhysBench and by **49.5%** in the challenging 'Scene' category, outperforming standard prompting methods and neuro-symbolic baselines.
- Integrating PhysAgent or fine-tuning with PhysBench data led to improved success rates and reduced reasoning and execution errors in five fundamental robotic manipulation tasks, directly validating enhanced physical understanding in embodied agents.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs possess a profoundly limited understanding of the physical world, achieving an average accuracy of approximately 40% on PhysBench, significantly below human performance (95.87%).
> - VLM struggles are primarily due to perceptual inaccuracies and a lack of explicit physical knowledge in their training data, rather than solely reasoning deficiencies.
> - Scaling VLM parameters, increasing general training data, or providing more video frames does not consistently improve physical comprehension, indicating fundamental limitations in current VLM architectures for this domain.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Understanding the physical world is a cornerstone challenge in embodied AI, crucial for developing intelligent agents capable of complex tasks and safe operation in real-world environments. This paper directly addresses a critical gap in the capabilities of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) within this landscape.

Historically, the field of Vision-Language Models has seen remarkable progress in reasoning and task planning, driven by training on vast amounts of human knowledge encoded in image-text pairs. Models like GPT-4V, Gemini, and open-source counterparts such as LLaVA have demonstrated strong capabilities in visual question answering (VQA), image captioning, and multi-modal dialogue. However, as embodied AI agents transition from simulated environments to the real physical world, their reliance solely on common-sense reasoning and superficial visual understanding proves insufficient. Previous studies have highlighted that VLMs frequently exhibit "operational errors" in physical interactions, such as mishandling fragile objects or failing to recognize appropriate grasping affordances, primarily due to a lack of genuine physical world understanding.

Existing benchmarks designed to assess physical comprehension have largely focused on either vision-only models or simplistic visual primitives (e.g., spheres, cubes, rigid collisions in limited simulated scenarios). More recent VQA datasets evaluate common-sense reasoning rather than deep physical perception, and spatial VQA benchmarks, while addressing geometric relationships, capture only a subset of broader physical understanding. These prior approaches suffer from limitations such as being restricted to narrow tasks, relying on predefined rules, or failing to generalize to open-ended physical problems.

"PHYSBENCH" positions itself as the *first comprehensive benchmark* explicitly designed to evaluate VLMs' perception and reasoning capabilities across a wide array of physical phenomena, far beyond geometric relationships or common-sense knowledge. By encompassing diverse categories like object properties, relationships, scene understanding, and physics-based dynamics, this work aims to move VLM evaluation closer to the demands of real-world embodied intelligence. The introduction of "PhysAgent" further contributes to this landscape by proposing a novel framework to enhance VLMs' physical understanding, combining their generalization strengths with specialized perceptual expertise, thereby overcoming the rigidity and limitations of previous physics-specialized or oracle models. This work thus represents a significant step towards bridging the gap between sophisticated VLM reasoning abilities and the fundamental need for intuitive physics in embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper is driven by two fundamental questions that highlight the current limitations of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in the context of embodied AI:

1. **Do VLMs possess an understanding of the physical world, and if not, what factors contribute to this limitation?** This objective seeks to empirically evaluate the current state of VLMs regarding their comprehension of physical phenomena, properties, and dynamics. The motivation here stems from the observation that despite their advanced reasoning and planning capabilities, embodied agents powered by VLMs often fail in real-world physical interactions. The authors want to pinpoint if this is a capability gap and, if so, diagnose its root causes.
2. **How can we enhance VLMs’ physical world understanding capabilities and facilitate the effective deployment of embodied agents like MOKA?** This objective focuses on developing solutions once the existing limitations are identified. The motivation is to bridge the identified gap, enabling VLMs to acquire a robust understanding of the physical world. This is crucial for equipping embodied agents to operate safely and efficiently in complex, unstructured real-world environments, preventing operational errors such as mishandling objects or misjudging dynamic interactions.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to advance embodied AI towards human-level capabilities by endowing VLMs with a comprehensive, intuitive understanding of physics, a domain critically underexplored in current VLM research. The authors recognize that strong common-sense reasoning alone is insufficient for agents that must perceive, interpret, and predict physical interactions in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research paper introduces a two-pronged approach: first, a new benchmark called **PhysBench** to rigorously evaluate existing VLMs, and second, a novel framework named **PhysAgent** to enhance their physical world understanding capabilities. This is then validated through **embodied AI applications**.

#### 3.1. PhysBench: A Comprehensive Benchmark

- **Dataset Construction**: PhysBench is a large-scale, human-annotated dataset comprising 10,002 entries of interleaved video-image-text data. The data collection was a laborious, five-step process involving 4,000 hours of annotation, sourcing content from the internet, real-world captures, and simulations. This mixed-format approach (integrating text, images, and videos) is crucial for capturing dynamic and complex physical phenomena that single images often miss (e.g., elasticity, mass, temperature changes).
- **Categorization**: The benchmark is meticulously structured into four major domains of physical world understanding:
 1. **Physical Object Properties**: Assessing attributes like mass, size, density, stiffness, elasticity.
 2. **Physical Object Relationships**: Evaluating spatial relations, relative positions, and motions.
 3. **Physical Scene Understanding**: Interpreting environmental factors such as light sources, viewpoints, and temperature.
 4. **Physics-based Dynamics**: Understanding events like collisions, throwing, fluid dynamics, and basic chemical reactions.
 These domains are further broken down into 19 subclasses and 8 distinct capability dimensions (e.g., perception, prediction, judgment, reasoning).
- **Format**: Questions are posed in a multiple-choice format (four options, one correct answer), which simplifies evaluation while still presenting significant challenges. Visual prompts (e.g., red circles, text options) are used in some tasks to guide models.
- **Evaluation Protocol**: 75 representative VLMs, categorized into Image VLMs, Video VLMs, and General VLMs, were evaluated. The evaluation setup accounted for different input modalities (single image, video sequences, interleaved image-text). For models not natively supporting video, frames were either concatenated into a single image ("merge" method) or input sequentially ("seq" method).
- **Error Analysis**: A qualitative error analysis was conducted on 500 random questions from three top-performing models (GPT-4o, Phi-3V, Gemini-1.5-flash). Errors were categorized into perception errors, reasoning errors, lack of knowledge, refusal to answer, failure to follow instructions, and annotation errors.

#### 3.2. PhysAgent: An Enhancement Framework

- **Motivation**: Directly addresses the primary error sources identified in the PhysBench evaluation: perceptual inaccuracies and insufficient knowledge.
- **Architecture**: PhysAgent is a unified framework that combines the generalization strengths of VLMs with specialized external modules:
 - **Knowledge Memory**: Stores prior physical world knowledge and rules, selectively invoked based on the task.
 - **Vision Foundation Models (VFMs)**: Integrates specialized models like Depth Anything (for depth estimation), SAM (Segment Anything Model, for object segmentation), and GroundingDINO (for grounded object detection) to enhance perceptual capabilities, especially for tasks VLMs struggle with (e.g., numerical distance calculation).
- **Three-Step Reasoning Process**:
 1. **Task-specific Prompt Activation**: Classifies the question (manually or automatically) and activates VLM prompts augmented with relevant physical knowledge from the memory (e.g., shadow direction knowledge for light-related questions).
 2. **Foundation Models Integration**: Processes outputs from VFMs (e.g., GroundingDINO identifies objects, SAM segments them), leveraging VLM's reasoning to interpret this enhanced visual information.
 3. **Chain-of-Thoughts Reasoning**: Engages the VLM in step-by-step reasoning, including a self-verification step, before generating the final answer.
- **Key Design Principle**: Unlike previous physical reasoning models that are rigid and task-specific, PhysAgent aims to retain the VLM's strong generalization and open-ended problem-solving abilities.
- **Baselines**: PhysAgent's performance was compared against standard prompting methods (Chain of Thought, Desp-CoT, Pure Language Reasoning) and a neuro-symbolic oracle model (ContPhy, re-implemented using ViperGPT).

#### 3.3. Embodied AI Application (MOKA)

- **Platform**: Utilizes MuJoCo and the 7-DoF Franka Emika robotic arm from Menagerie, built upon the MOKA embodied agent framework. MOKA converts affordance reasoning into VQA problems that VLMs can solve, leveraging mark-based visual prompting with GroundedSAM and farthest point sampling.
- **Tasks**: Five fundamental robotic manipulation tasks were designed: Affordance (grasping various objects), Force (grasping fragile, soft, rigid objects appropriately), Color (picking specific colored objects), Location (grasping objects at specific spatial positions), and Tool (selecting the correct tool for a task). These are basic tasks not requiring complex hierarchical decomposition.
- **Enhancement Methods**: MOKA's underlying VLM (GPT-4o) was enhanced in two ways: (1) fine-tuning with PhysBench data, and (2) integrating PhysAgent for zero-shot inference to assist in affordance reasoning.
- **Evaluation**: Success rates, reasoning error reductions, and execution error reductions were reported over 10 trials for each task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings regarding the capabilities and limitations of VLMs in physical world understanding:

#### 4.1. VLM Performance on PhysBench

- **Limited Physical Understanding**: Across 75 representative VLMs, the average accuracy on PhysBench was approximately 40%, significantly below human performance (95.87%). Even the best-performing model, GPT-4o, achieved only 49.49% accuracy, highlighting a substantial gap.
- **Domain-Specific Struggles**: Models particularly struggled with tasks related to "physical scene understanding" and "physics-based dynamics," indicating deeper deficiencies in interpreting environmental changes and predicting event outcomes.
- **Closed-Source Advantage**: Proprietary models like those from the GPT series and Gemini-1.5 consistently and significantly outperformed open-source models. For instance, GPT-4o surpassed the best open-source model (LLaVA-interleave) by 20.7%, suggesting that current open-source VLMs lag considerably in this domain. Claude models, however, did not show a clear advantage.

#### 4.2. Reasons for VLM Struggles

- **Distinct from Common VQA**: PhysBench tasks exhibited low correlation with traditional VLM benchmarks, suggesting that succeeding in general VQA does not necessarily translate to physical world understanding. It aligns more closely with tasks like hallucination detection (POPE), indicating its unique challenge.
- **Poor Scalability with Size, Data, and Frames**:
 - **Model Size**: Increasing model parameters (e.g., VILA-1.5 from 3B to 7B) often led to minimal or even negative performance gains on PhysBench, contrasting sharply with significant improvements on common QA tasks.
 - **Training Data**: Scaling up datasets (e.g., PLLaVA and VILA-1.5, which are larger-data variants of LLaVA-1.5) provided limited benefits or even declines in physical comprehension. Analysis revealed that most VLM training data is descriptive and lacks physically grounded knowledge.
 - **Multi-frame Information**: Open-source models showed insensitivity to the number of video frames, performing similarly to single-frame inputs, and sometimes even worse with more frames. This suggests an inability to effectively utilize temporal information for physical reasoning.
- **Primary Error Sources: Perception and Knowledge Gaps**: A detailed error analysis of GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-flash, and Phi-3V revealed that "perceptual errors" (37-45%) and "lack of knowledge" (23-35%) constituted the majority of mistakes. This indicates that models can extract visual and textual information but lack the embedded physical priors and rigorous reasoning capabilities for the physical world.
- **Limited Knowledge Transfer (but possible)**: When models were fine-tuned or provided with in-context learning examples containing physical world knowledge, their performance improved. This suggests that while current training data is deficient, VLMs do possess some capacity to absorb and transfer physical knowledge.

#### 4.3. PhysAgent Effectiveness

- **Significant Performance Boost**: PhysAgent consistently improved zero-shot performance, achieving an 18.4% overall improvement for GPT-4o on PhysBench. Notably, it showed a 49.5% improvement in the challenging "Scene" category for GPT-4o.
- **Superior to Baselines**: PhysAgent outperformed standard prompting strategies (CoT, Desp-CoT, PLR), which often showed minimal or negative impacts. It also surpassed the performance of the neuro-symbolic approach ContPhy, which struggled with diverse scenarios due to its predefined logical templates and reliance on external visual modules.

#### 4.4. Impact on Embodied Applications

- **Enhanced Robotic Manipulation**: Both fine-tuning MOKA's VLM with PhysBench and using PhysAgent for zero-shot inference significantly improved performance across five fundamental robotic manipulation tasks (Affordance, Force, Color, Location, Tool). This directly validates that improved physical world understanding in VLMs translates into more reliable and capable embodied agents. The "force" task saw particularly significant progress.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "PHYSBENCH" paper makes several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact across embodied AI, VLM development, and robotics.

#### 5.1. Pioneering a New Evaluation Frontier

- **Addressing a Critical Gap**: PhysBench is the first comprehensive benchmark to systematically evaluate Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for physical world understanding. This directly fills a crucial void, moving beyond common-sense reasoning, descriptive tasks, or limited geometric understanding found in existing benchmarks. It forces VLMs to grapple with complex concepts such as object properties, spatial relationships, scene dynamics, and physics-based interactions.
- **Revealing Fundamental Limitations**: The extensive evaluation of 75 VLMs unequivocally demonstrates that current models, including state-of-the-art proprietary ones, possess a profoundly limited understanding of the physical world. This finding challenges the prevailing narrative of VLM omnipotence and provides concrete evidence of where foundational improvements are needed, particularly in areas like physical scene understanding and dynamics prediction.
- **Guiding Future Research**: By identifying perceptual inaccuracies and the lack of physical knowledge in training data as primary error sources, PhysBench offers clear directions for future VLM development. It underscores the necessity of incorporating more physically grounded data and designing architectures that can either inherently learn or explicitly integrate physical priors. The scalability analysis, showing that performance does not consistently improve with model size or data scale on physical tasks, further highlights that simply "scaling up" current VLM paradigms might not be sufficient for achieving true physical intelligence.

#### 5.2. Advancing VLM Capabilities for Physical Reasoning

- **A Novel Enhancement Framework**: PhysAgent introduces an effective, generalizable framework to inject specialized perceptual capabilities (via vision foundation models like Depth Anything, SAM, GroundingDINO) and explicit physical knowledge (via a knowledge memory) into VLMs. This hybrid approach leverages the best of both worlds: the broad reasoning and generalization of VLMs and the precise, specialized expertise of perception models.
- **Practical Improvements**: The demonstrated 18.4% improvement for GPT-4o on PhysBench (and even 49.5% in the challenging "Scene" category) proves the efficacy of PhysAgent. Its ability to outperform standard prompting methods and more rigid neuro-symbolic systems highlights its practical utility and flexible design, which avoids reliance on manually predefined processing logic.

#### 5.3. Direct Impact on Embodied AI and Robotics

- **Bridging the Physical-Cognitive Gap**: The most impactful finding is the direct validation that enhanced VLM physical understanding translates into improved performance in downstream robotic manipulation tasks. By demonstrating reduced reasoning errors, fewer execution failures, and increased success rates in tasks performed by embodied agents like MOKA, the paper establishes a clear link between VLM cognitive capabilities and real-world physical dexterity.
- **Enabling Safer and More Capable Robots**: The ability of robots to accurately perceive and reason about the physical world—understanding how objects move, interact, and respond to forces—is paramount for safe and effective operation in human environments. PhysBench and PhysAgent pave the way for developing more robust, reliable, and "physically intuitive" embodied agents. This is critical for applications ranging from assistive robotics and manufacturing to autonomous navigation and exploration.
- **Towards Human-Level Physical Intuition**: Ultimately, this work contributes significantly to the long-term goal of building AI systems with human-like intuitive physics. By providing the tools for both rigorous evaluation and effective enhancement, PhysBench and PhysAgent are accelerating the progress towards VLMs and embodied agents that can truly comprehend and interact intelligently within the complexities of our physical world.
