# SpatioLM: Towards General Physical Spatial Intelligence in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Xiaomi EV and Tongji University introduce SpatioLM, a parameter-efficient framework that integrates a plug-and-play Spatio-Vision Module into Vision-Language Models to enhance visual spatial reasoning. The model significantly improved metric depth estimation, achieving 83.5 on MD-S, and advanced spatial understanding, scoring 71.6 on VSI-Bench, while largely preserving general VLM capabilities.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate insufficient performance in fine-grained visual spatial reasoning, particularly 3D metric and physical spatial comprehension, due to architectures emphasizing semantic over intrinsic spatial understanding.
- Existing spatial intelligence enhancement methods rely on explicit 3D prior inputs or invasive architectural modifications, limiting scalability, practical applicability in RGB-only scenarios, and potentially degrading general VLM capabilities.
- Tool-use and agentic frameworks for spatial reasoning treat VLMs as high-level planners dependent on external modules, which introduces latency, potential cascading errors, and does not fundamentally improve the VLM's intrinsic spatial reasoning ability.

## Method

- **SpatioLM** employs a ==Frozen Backbone + Plug-and-Play Spatio-Vision Module (SV-Module)== design, keeping pretrained VLM parameters fixed to preserve general capabilities while enhancing spatial reasoning.
- The SV-Module processes intermediate vision tokens from the VLM's vision encoder, utilizing stacked Spatio-Vision Blocks with an ==Alternating-Attention== mechanism to explicitly elicit geometry-aware features.
- A ==Dual Dense Prediction Transformer (DPT) Head== is supervised by pseudo depth and camera ray maps to enforce explicit geometric learning, and these geometry-aware features are non-invasively injected into the VLM's language model blocks.

## Results

- SpatioLMSenseNovaSI−8B_{SenseNovaSI-8B}SenseNovaSI−8B achieved top performance on spatial perception benchmarks, scoring **83.5** on Metric Depth Estimation-Small (MD-S) and **69.0** on Metric Depth Estimation-Medium (MD-M), significantly improving over its base model (e.g., from 52.6 to **83.5** on MD-S).
- On high-level spatial understanding tasks, SpatioLMSenseNovaSI−8B_{SenseNovaSI-8B}SenseNovaSI−8B attained **71.6** average accuracy on VSI-Bench, becoming the first model to exceed 70, and consistently outperformed baselines on ScanQA (**101.8** CIDEr) and SQA3D (**56.5%** E1).
- The framework maintained general VLM capabilities, showing a smaller performance degradation on general benchmarks (average drop within **4%**) compared to other spatial VLM enhancements, and demonstrated improved embodied manipulation success rates on LIBERO (e.g., **91.0%** for discrete actions).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intermediate layers of Vision Transformers (ViTs) contain richer latent spatial information, which can be effectively extracted and leveraged for geometry-aware feature generation.
> - A non-invasive, zero-initialized projection layer allows the seamless fusion of geometry-aware tokens into the frozen language model, enhancing spatial reasoning without disrupting pretrained feature distributions or causing catastrophic forgetting.
> - The combination of Vision Token Supervision (VTS) for feature alignment and Dense Geometric Supervision (DGS) for explicit geometric predictions provides complementary and synergistic signals crucial for robust spatial learning and maintaining general capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in semantic alignment and generalization across various vision-language tasks, such as visual question answering. However, their performance on tasks requiring fine-grained visual spatial reasoning, including 3D metric reasoning and spatial understanding, has been noted as insufficient. This limitation arises primarily from the architectures of current multimodal large models and their large-scale image-text pretraining paradigms, which tend to emphasize semantic representations over intrinsic spatial understanding. The inability of VLMs to robustly perform spatial reasoning poses a challenge for their application in real-world scenarios such as embodied manipulation and autonomous driving.

Existing efforts to enhance spatial intelligence in VLMs typically follow several paradigms:
- **Explicit geometric augmentation**: Methods introduce 3D prior information (e.g., depth maps, point clouds, camera parameters) obtained from sensors or auxiliary estimation models. While these methods can be effective in controlled environments, they often depend on specialized hardware or multi-stage preprocessing pipelines, which limits their scalability and robustness in general, RGB-only scenarios.
- **Architectural-level modification**: This approach involves integrating additional spatial encoders into the VLM architecture to process 3D information. Such methods externalize spatial reasoning rather than eliciting inherent spatial knowledge from the VLM. This can lead to difficulties in adapting to diverse spatial tasks and may degrade the VLM's general-purpose capabilities due to extensive fine-tuning on large-scale 3D data, which can disrupt pretrained feature distributions.
- **Tool-use and agentic frameworks**: This paradigm leverages large language models (LLMs) to interact with external tools or APIs (e.g., depth estimators, 3D detectors) or employs reinforcement learning with multimodal verifiers. While these methods offer flexibility and can circumvent invasive fine-tuning, they treat the VLM as a high-level planner reliant on external modules. This can introduce multi-step execution latency, cascading errors from API failures, and does not fundamentally improve the intrinsic spatial reasoning capabilities of the underlying visual backbone.

SpatioLM is positioned to address these limitations by proposing a parameter-efficient framework that aims to enhance VLM spatial intelligence without requiring extra 3D prior inputs or third-party spatial encoders, while simultaneously preserving the models' general-purpose capabilities. The work seeks to elicit latent spatial structure from pretrained VLM vision tokens directly, end-to-end, and on demand.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that despite significant advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for semantic understanding and commonsense reasoning, these models consistently exhibit limitations in visual spatial reasoning, particularly concerning 3D metric and physical spatial comprehension. This deficiency restricts their deployment in applications requiring a precise understanding of physical space, such as embodied AI and autonomous systems.

The core challenge identified by the authors is to develop a method that can enhance the spatial intelligence of VLMs without introducing additional complexity or compromising their existing general-purpose capabilities. Specifically, the authors sought to overcome the following problems associated with previous approaches:
- The reliance on explicit 3D prior inputs (e.g., depth maps, camera parameters) from sensors or external estimation models, which limits practical applicability in real-world, RGB-only scenarios.
- The integration of external spatial encoders through architectural modifications, which often leads to complex dual-encoder architectures and invasive fine-tuning that can disrupt pretrained representations and degrade general semantic and reasoning abilities (catastrophic forgetting).
- The use of tool-use or agentic frameworks, which treat the VLM as a high-level planner, introducing latency, potential error propagation, and failing to improve the VLM’s intrinsic spatial reasoning.

To address this core challenge, the key objectives of SpatioLM are:
1. **Develop a parameter-efficient framework**: Introduce SpatioLM as a novel architecture that can effectively improve spatial intelligence in VLMs with minimal additional parameters.
2. **Design a non-invasive, plug-and-play module**: Create a "Spatio-Vision Module" (SV-Module) that can be easily integrated into existing pretrained VLM backbones without modifying their core structure or parameters, thereby preserving their general capabilities.
3. **Elicit inherent spatial knowledge**: Enable the SV-Module to extract and utilize implicit geometric representations present in the VLM's intermediate vision tokens, rather than relying on external 3D data inputs.
4. **Incorporate effective supervision**: Leverage pseudo depth and camera ray information as supervision signals to guide the model in learning physically coherent and geometry-aware representations.
5. **Achieve performance improvements**: Demonstrate significant enhancements across a range of spatial perception (e.g., metric depth estimation) and spatial understanding (e.g., object relations, route planning) tasks.
6. **Maintain general capabilities**: Ensure that the improvements in spatial reasoning do not lead to a substantial degradation of the VLM's performance on general vision-language tasks.
7. **Evaluate transferability**: Assess the model's ability to transfer its enhanced spatial intelligence to downstream tasks, particularly in embodied manipulation contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpatioLM is built upon a core paradigm described as "Frozen Backbone + Plug-and-Play Spatio-Vision Module." This design strategy ensures that all parameters of the pretrained VLM backbone, comprising a vision encoder and an LLM, remain fixed throughout the training process. This is intended to preserve the VLM's general capabilities while a new, non-invasive Spatio-Vision Module (SV-Module) is introduced to specifically enhance spatial reasoning.

#### 3.1. Visual Condition

The model utilizes intermediate vision tokens from the VLM's vision encoder as input to the SV-Module. Empirical analysis has indicated that intermediate layers of Vision Transformers (ViTs) often contain richer spatial information relevant for geometric tasks than the final output layer. Specifically, tokens from the 16th layer of a 24-layer ViT are selected.

#### 3.2. Spatio-Vision Module (SV-Module)

The SV-Module is the core component responsible for extracting and processing geometry-aware features. It is composed of stacked Spatio-Vision Blocks.
- **Spatial Feature Elicitation**: The SV-Module processes vision tokens from the (j-1)-th LM block, denoted as $H_v^{j-1}$, to generate geometry-aware tokens $H_g^i$. These $H_g^i$ tokens are then injected into the j-th LM block's output via a zero-initialized projection layer ($P_z$). This zero-initialization strategy is employed to fuse the geometry-aware tokens without disrupting the pretrained distribution of the frozen language model. This process occurs at regular intervals, mapping each Spatio-Vision Block to a specific LM Block.
- **Spatio-Vision Block**: Each Spatio-Vision Block is designed to explicitly capture 3D perception. It employs an Alternating-Attention mechanism, which alternates between:
 - **Frame Attention**: Processes visual tokens independently within each video frame to preserve fine-grained geometric details.
 - **Global Attention**: Attends jointly to all tokens across frames to aggregate multi-view or temporal geometric consistency.
 The default configuration for the SV-Module includes K=6 SV-Blocks, each with approximately 50 million parameters. The alternating attention mechanism is applied internally within the SV-Block and does not modify the causal self-attention of the frozen LLM decoder.

#### 3.3. Dual DPT Head

To enforce explicit geometric learning, the output features $H_g$ from the SV-Module are fed into a dual-branch Dense Prediction Transformer (DPT) head, adapted from Depth Anything V3. This head predicts two types of dense geometric outputs:
- A Dense Depth Map ($\hat{D}$), which can represent either metric or relative depth depending on the supervision.
- A Camera Ray Map ($\hat{R}$), which encodes per-pixel viewing directions based on camera intrinsics and extrinsics.
Supervising these predictions encourages the injected features to encode camera-aware 3D priors critical for downstream spatial tasks.

#### 3.4. Loss Design

The overall training objective is a weighted sum of three distinct loss components:
- **Language Modeling Loss ($L_L$)**: This is the standard cross-entropy loss applied to the generated text sequence.
- **Distillation Loss ($L_d$)**: Referred to as Vision Token Supervision (VTS), this loss aligns the SV-Module's features ($H_s$) with the latent features of a pretrained 3D foundation model (Depth Anything V3, $H_t$) by matching their token-structure similarity using a Gram-matrix.
- **Dense Geometric Loss ($L_g$)**: Known as Dense Geometric Supervision (DGS), this loss supervises the Dual DPT Head's predictions ($\hat{D}$ and $\hat{R}$) using pseudo-labels ($\bar{D}$ and $\bar{R}$) and the depth and ray losses defined in Depth Anything V3.
The final training loss is given by $L = \alpha L_L + \beta L_d + \gamma L_g$, with empirical weights $\alpha=0.6$, $\beta=0.2$, and $\gamma=0.2$. During training, only the SV-Module and the projection layers are updated, while the VLM backbone remains frozen.

#### 3.5. Unified Task Formulation

SpatioLM frames diverse spatial tasks as conditional language modeling problems. Task specification is achieved through prompt design, and responses are generated via next-token prediction within the model's vocabulary space.
- **Spatial Perception**: Tasks are depth-centric, primarily metric depth estimation, formulated as question-answering. Visual markers on input images query the distance at marked locations.
- **Spatial Understanding**: Encompasses various spatial reasoning tasks such as object counting, relative size estimation, spatial relations, and route planning, also framed as visual question answering.

#### 3.6. Training Data and Setting

A comprehensive training corpus was constructed, integrating low-level spatial perception with high-level spatial understanding tasks from various indoor and outdoor, real-world and synthetic 3D datasets (e.g., ScanNet, ARKitScenes, Waymo). Pseudo-labels for depth and camera rays were generated by a teacher model (Depth Anything V3).
Two main configurations were trained: SpatioLM$_{SenseNovaSI-8B}$ and SpatioLM$_{InternVL3.5-8B}$, built upon SenseNovaSI-8B and InternVL3.5-8B architectures, respectively. Training utilized 64 NVIDIA H200 GPUs with AdamW optimizer and a cosine learning rate schedule.

#### 3.7. Evaluation Setting

During inference, the Dual DPT Head can be optionally removed. The SV-Module is activated once during the prefilling phase to encode spatial context. Subsequent token generation proceeds with standard VLM decoding, utilizing key-value caching, which results in no additional inference overhead. This design aims to provide enhanced physical spatial reasoning with efficient and scalable inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SpatioLM was evaluated across spatial perception, spatial understanding, general capabilities, and embodied manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Visual Spatial Reasoning

- **Spatial Perception**: SpatioLM demonstrated performance enhancements on low-level spatial perception benchmarks:
 - **Metric Depth Estimation (MD-S, MD-M)**: SpatioLM$_{SenseNovaSI-8B}$ achieved the highest overall performance with scores of 83.5 on MD-S and 69.0 on MD-M, outperforming proprietary, open-source, and specialized models. Improvements over its base model, SenseNovaSI-8B, were substantial (e.g., from 52.6 to 83.5 on MD-S).
 - **Relative Depth Estimation (DA-2K)**: SpatioLM$_{SenseNovaSI-8B}$ attained 83.8 accuracy, showing competitive performance and significant improvement over its base model (71.8).
 - **Depth-Related Multi-Tasks (DR)**: SpatioLM$_{SenseNovaSI-8B}$ scored 41.8, placing it first among all baselines and representing a substantial gain over its base model (17.5).

- **Spatial Understanding**: On high-level spatial understanding benchmarks, SpatioLM$_{SenseNovaSI-8B}$ achieved top performance:
 - **VSI-Bench**: It scored 71.6 (average accuracy), becoming the first model to exceed 70 on this benchmark and outperforming all proprietary, open-source, and specialized spatial models.
 - **ScanQA**: SpatioLM consistently outperformed all baselines across metrics like BLEU-1, BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, and CIDEr (achieving 101.8 on CIDEr), indicating improved understanding of spatial relations in 3D scenes.
 - **SQA3D**: It achieved 56.5% E1 and 58.6% ER1 accuracy, surpassing baselines like SenseNovaSI-8B (47.8% E1 / 50.1% ER1) and demonstrating stronger spatial reasoning under pose-conditioned question answering.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on General Capabilities

To assess the preservation of general-purpose VLM capabilities, SpatioLM was evaluated on benchmarks including VideoMME, VideoMMMU, MVBench, and MMMU (val).
- SpatioLM exhibited a smaller performance degradation on general tasks compared to baseline models like Spatial-MLLM. The average performance drop for SpatioLM variants was within 4% of their base models, whereas Spatial-MLLM showed reductions ranging from 23% to 67%.
- In some cases, SpatioLM even improved performance on certain general benchmarks (e.g., +12% on VideoMMMU), suggesting effective mitigation of catastrophic forgetting.

#### 4.3. Downstream Task on Embodied Manipulation

SpatioLM was adapted to vision-language-action (VLA) models for embodied manipulation tasks on the LIBERO benchmark.
- **Discrete Action (SpatioLM-VLA0)**: Improved manipulation success from 79.2% (SenseNovaSI-VLA0) to 91.0%.
- **Continuous Action (SpatioLM-OFT)**: Improved manipulation success from 95.3% (SenseNovaSI-OFT) to 96.3%.
- SpatioLM-OFT, with 2B parameters, achieved competitive performance among VLA methods without large-scale robot manipulation pretraining, indicating parameter efficiency and spatial generalization.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted to analyze the contributions of key components:
- **Spatio-Vision Module (SV-Module)**: Integrating the SV-Module alone yielded substantial gains (e.g., +22.1 on MD-S, +12.2 on DA-2K, +0.4 on VSI-Bench over the baseline without SV-Module).
- **Vision Token Supervision (VTS) and Dense Geometric Supervision (DGS)**: Both provided complementary supervision, with their combined application achieving the best overall performance (83.5 on MD-S, 83.8 on DA-2K, 71.6 on VSI-Bench), indicating synergistic contributions.
- **Sensitivity to Loss Weights**: SpatioLM exhibited robustness to variations in loss weights, with VSI-Bench scores changing minimally (within 0.5 points), and ScanQA/SQA3D scores remaining strong.
- **Attention Mechanism**: The proposed Alternating-Attention in the SV-Block consistently outperformed a self-attention baseline (e.g., +1.2 on VSI-Bench, +3.1 on ScanQA CIDEr, +1.8 on SQA3D ER1), supporting the benefit of decoupling intra-frame and inter-frame attention.
- **Number of SV-Blocks**: Increasing SV-Blocks from 4 to 6 yielded clear performance gains, especially on MD-S and DA-2K. Performance saturated beyond 6 blocks, indicating that 6 SV-Blocks offers an optimal performance-efficiency trade-off, requiring fine-tuning of approximately 0.3B parameters.
- **Comparison with LoRA**: SpatioLM outperformed a LoRA baseline with a comparable parameter budget on spatial understanding benchmarks, suggesting the effectiveness of its non-invasive side-path design.
- **MVBench Degradation Analysis**: Performance degradation on MVBench was concentrated in motion/dynamics-related subtasks, while scene-level reasoning was preserved or improved. This was attributed to geometry-oriented supervision emphasizing static spatial structure, potentially competing with fine-grained temporal cues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SpatioLM addresses a critical limitation in current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by enhancing their intrinsic visual spatial reasoning capabilities. The research contributes to the field by proposing a parameter-efficient framework that integrates a plug-and-play, non-invasive Spatio-Vision Module. This approach allows VLMs to perform more robust spatial reasoning, ranging from low-level perception to high-level understanding, without relying on external 3D prior inputs or third-party spatial encoders.

The significance of this work includes:
- **Advancement in Spatial Intelligence**: SpatioLM demonstrates improvements over state-of-the-art models on diverse spatial perception and understanding tasks, including metric depth estimation, relative depth estimation, and complex 3D visual question answering benchmarks.
- **Preservation of General Capabilities**: A key contribution is the development of a non-invasive module design that mitigates catastrophic forgetting, a common issue when fine-tuning VLMs for specific tasks. This ensures that the VLM's general knowledge and semantic understanding are largely preserved while spatial intelligence is enhanced.
- **Efficiency and Scalability**: The parameter-efficient nature of SpatioLM, particularly with its plug-and-play module and minimal inference overhead after initial prefilling, suggests a scalable solution for integrating spatial reasoning into VLMs. The model achieves competitive performance with a relatively small number of fine-tuned parameters.
- **Broader Applicability**: The demonstrated transferability to downstream embodied manipulation tasks highlights the practical utility of SpatioLM, indicating its potential impact on domains such as robotics, autonomous driving, and general embodied AI, where precise physical spatial intelligence is crucial.
- **Synergy with Agentic Systems**: The work posits that a spatially-aware foundation model like SpatioLM can serve as a more reliable backbone for agentic AI systems, complementing tool-use frameworks by providing stronger intrinsic spatial understanding.

**Limitations acknowledged by the authors include:**
- **Pseudo-label Dependence**: The model relies on pseudo-labels generated by teacher models (e.g., Depth Anything V3). The inherent noise and potential biases in these pseudo-labels could impose an upper bound on the model's overall 3D comprehension, as errors may propagate.
- **Motion-dynamics Trade-off**: Geometric supervision, by emphasizing static spatial structures, may partially compete with temporal-dynamic cues. This can lead to a degradation in performance on motion-intensive subtasks (e.g., action counting, moving direction) while scene-level reasoning is maintained. Further research could explore augmenting geometric supervision with explicit temporal signals.
- **Safety-Critical Deployment**: Given the aforementioned constraints, SpatioLM is not yet considered reliable for safety-critical applications, and rigorous additional verification would be required for such scenarios.

Overall, SpatioLM offers a novel perspective and an effective solution for enhancing physical spatial intelligence in VLMs. Its contributions are aimed at facilitating further research in multimodal learning and enabling more capable AI systems for real-world applications.
