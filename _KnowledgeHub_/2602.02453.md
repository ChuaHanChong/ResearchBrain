# Thinking with Comics: Enhancing Multimodal Reasoning through Structured Visual Storytelling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Harbin Institute of Technology introduced 'Thinking with Comics' (TwC), a visual reasoning paradigm utilizing multi-panel comics as intermediate representations to enhance multimodal reasoning. The approach demonstrated improved accuracy across diverse benchmarks, including 85.8% on MathVista, and reduced media generation costs by 86.6% compared to video-based methods for dynamic reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Thinking with Images (TWI) using static images struggles to represent temporal dynamics and causal relationships, complicating cross-modal alignment without explicit textual cues.
- Thinking with Video introduces substantial redundancy and high computational costs for generation and processing, limiting efficiency for dynamic reasoning.
- Existing visual reasoning paradigms lack a balanced approach that combines temporal structure and narrative coherence with information density and computational efficiency.

## Method

- Introduced =='Thinking with Comics' (TwC)==, a paradigm externalizing intermediate reasoning processes into structured comic panels to explicitly incorporate temporal and causal structures.
- Implemented two paths: ==End-to-End Visualized Reasoning (Path I)== where Gemini-3 Pro Image directly generates comics depicting the entire reasoning, and ==Comic as Conditioning Context (Path II)== where a generated comic conditions an MLLM for joint inference.
- Utilized advanced generative models capable of producing coherent sequential comic panels with embedded text, allowing for practical instantiation of the TwC paradigm.

## Results

- TwC (Path II) achieved **85.8%** accuracy on MathVista, outperforming 'Thinking with Video' (Sora 2 V-o-T at **67.6%**) and remaining competitive on text-based mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- The cost analysis revealed TwC reduced media generation costs by **86.6%** compared to a 10-second video for dynamic reasoning tasks, costing **$0.134** vs. **$1.00**.
- A detective-style narrative within comics yielded an average of **28.5** absolute percentage points higher accuracy in logical reasoning tasks compared to a standard documentary style.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Comics offer a Pareto optimal state between information density and computational overhead, efficiently capturing dynamic logic with a minimal set of information-dense panels (typically 4-6 panels optimal).
> - Embedded textual elements within comics (speech bubbles, narration) serve as 'Semantic Anchors,' significantly reducing visual ambiguity and enhancing cross-modal semantic comprehension.
> - Specific narrative styles (e.g., detective vs. documentary) in comics can function as effective 'Visual System Prompts' to activate distinct reasoning paths in MLLMs, influencing performance in logical tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has led to significant advancements in complex reasoning tasks, often facilitated by explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, where models perform step-by-step textual reasoning. This paradigm, referred to as "Thinking with Text," has been extended to the visual domain with the emergence of multimodal large language models (MLLMs).

The "Thinking with Images" (TWI) paradigm involves models using images as input or generating intermediate visual representations to supplement reasoning, thereby enhancing the performance of vision-language models (VLMs). Building upon this, "Thinking with Video" further incorporates temporal structure by generating short video sequences to enable dynamic reasoning.

Despite these advancements, existing visual reasoning paradigms exhibit limitations. Static images, inherent to TWI, struggle to represent temporal structure or dynamic processes, and the absence of explicit textual cues can complicate cross-modal alignment. Conversely, videos, used in "Thinking with Video," provide temporal information but introduce substantial redundancy and incur significantly higher computational costs, limiting their practical efficiency for reasoning.

This work introduces "Thinking with Comics" (TwC) as a novel visual reasoning paradigm positioned to address these identified limitations. Comics are presented as a high information-density medium located between static images and videos. The research explores comics' ability to preserve temporal structure, embed text, and maintain narrative coherence while requiring lower reasoning costs compared to video. The advent of advanced generative models, such as Gemini-3 Pro Image, which can convert text into coherent sequential comic panels with embedded text, is noted as a key enabling factor for this paradigm. The paper aims to systematically investigate comics as an intermediate visual representation to improve multimodal reasoning, offering a new direction for visual reasoning research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to introduce and systematically evaluate "Thinking with Comics" (TwC) as a novel paradigm for enhancing multimodal reasoning. This paradigm leverages multi-panel comics as an intermediate visual representation to facilitate complex reasoning processes.

The motivation for proposing TwC stems from a critical analysis of the limitations inherent in existing visual reasoning paradigms:

- **Limitations of Thinking with Images (TWI):** While TWI has improved reasoning in MLLMs by incorporating visual inputs, it predominantly relies on static images. Static images are inherently constrained in their ability to represent temporal dynamics, causal relationships, or multi-step processes effectively. This static nature can impede reasoning tasks that require understanding sequences of events or changes over time. Additionally, the absence of explicit textual cues directly embedded within purely visual representations can complicate the alignment between visual and textual modalities, potentially leading to ambiguities in reasoning.

- **Limitations of Thinking with Video:** To overcome the temporal limitations of static images, the "Thinking with Video" paradigm integrates video generation to capture dynamic information. However, this approach introduces substantial redundancy. Video sequences often contain highly correlated frames, meaning much of the information between consecutive frames is repetitive. This redundancy translates into significantly higher computational overhead for both generation and processing, rendering video-based reasoning less efficient and economically costly for practical applications.

To address these limitations, the researchers propose comics as a natural and efficient reasoning medium. The motivation for choosing comics is based on their unique properties:

- **Temporal and Causal Structure:** Comics inherently convey temporal logic and causal sequences through their panel-based structure, similar to videos, but in a more abstracted and information-dense format. Each panel can represent a distinct step or state in a reasoning process.
- **Embedded Text and Narrative Coherence:** Comics naturally integrate embedded text (e.g., dialogue, narration, onomatopoeia) alongside visual information. This explicit textual anchoring can reduce visual ambiguity and provide precise semantic cues, facilitating cross-modal alignment and enhancing narrative coherence, which is crucial for complex problem-solving.
- **Information Density and Efficiency:** Compared to videos, individual comic panels are designed to be highly information-dense, depicting key moments or states without the temporal redundancy of continuous video frames. This characteristic is expected to lead to significantly lower reasoning and generation costs while retaining essential dynamic information.
- **Leveraging Generative Models:** The advancement of generative models capable of producing coherent sequential comic panels from text, including embedded text, makes the practical instantiation of the TwC paradigm feasible.

Through these motivations, the study aims to demonstrate that comics can serve as an effective, efficient, and interpretable intermediate visual representation that systematically enhances multimodal reasoning across diverse tasks, offering a balanced approach between the expressiveness of video and the efficiency of static images.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces "Thinking with Comics" (TwC) as a paradigm where intermediate reasoning processes are externalized into a sequence of comic panels, explicitly incorporating temporal and causal structures. The methodology explores two distinct paths for implementing TwC.

#### 3.1. Two Instantiation Paths for Thinking with Comics

- **Path I: End-to-End Visualized Reasoning:**
 - **Mechanism:** In this path, an image generation model is directly employed to produce a multi-panel comic based on the input question. The sequence of comic panels is designed to visually depict the entire reasoning process from the problem statement to the final solution. The generation of the comic itself is considered the reasoning process.
 - **Implementation:** Gemini-3 Pro Image is utilized as the generative model. It takes the input question (`q`) and generates a sequence of comic panels (`C = c_1, c_2..., c_T`), where each panel `c_t` illustrates an intermediate reasoning step. The model implicitly learns a latent state transition process (`h_t = f(h_{t-1}, q)`) and maps these states to visual panels (`c_t = g(h_t)`).
 - **Answer Extraction:** The final answer (`a_hat`) is obtained by extracting information from the last panel of the generated comic (`a_hat = R(c_T)`). An external answer reader, GPT-5.2, is used for this purpose, given the comic panels and the original question.
 - **Characteristics:** This path offers an end-to-end framework with relatively low computational cost, providing interpretable intermediate representations through the sequential and causally coherent nature of the comic panels. The reasoning trajectory is directly visualized. However, the overall reasoning capability is constrained by the implicit capabilities of the generation model.

- **Path II: Comic as Conditioning Context for VLM:**
 - **Mechanism:** This path treats the generated comic as an explicit intermediate reasoning representation that then conditions a multimodal large language model (MLLM) for downstream inference.
 - **Implementation:** First, Gemini-3 Pro Image generates a comic (`C = G_theta(q)`) based on the input question. Subsequently, this generated comic `C`, along with the original question `q`, is provided as input to an MLLM (specifically, Gemini-3 Pro) for joint reasoning. The comic acts as an explicit intermediate variable (`z = C`).
 - **Answer Generation:** The MLLM then processes this joint input (`q, C`) to predict the final answer (`a_hat = F_phi(q, C)`).
 - **Characteristics:** This approach allows for a richer and more structured multimodal reasoning context compared to traditional textual Chain-of-Thought. The comic representation jointly encodes spatial structure, object relationships, and temporal evolution, providing enhanced context for the MLLM.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Framework

- **Evaluation Datasets:** The effectiveness of TwC is assessed across a diverse set of benchmarks categorized into two types:
 - **Reasoning Tasks:** MATH500, GSM8K (symbolic/numerical reasoning, textual), and MathVista (visually grounded mathematical problems requiring joint visual perception and logical reasoning).
 - **(Long) Context Understanding Tasks:** DocVQA (document-level information aggregation), eBDtheque (document-level multilingual understanding and visual-text alignment across panels), and CulturalBench (contextualized cultural understanding, text-only with Easy/Hard subsets).
- **Baselines:** The performance of TwC is compared against four groups of models:
 - **Text-only MLLMs:** GPT-5.2, Gemini-3 Pro, Claude-Sonnet 4.5 (performing direct reasoning without explicit intermediate steps).
 - **Reasoning-oriented LLMs:** DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-235B-A22B (designed for multi-step reasoning via CoT mechanisms).
 - **Thinking with Images (TWI) models:** G-IMG (prompt-based) and DREAMLLM (training-based, end-to-end).
 - **Thinking with Video models:** Sora 2 (temporal video generation implicitly encoding reasoning).
- **Metrics:** Accuracy is the primary evaluation metric across all benchmarks, measuring the correctness of the final predicted answers.
- **Experimental Setup:** All models are evaluated in a zero-shot setting. Prompt templates are designed to ensure fair comparison and avoid task-specific tuning. Specific model versions are detailed in the paper.

#### 3.3. Analysis Experiments

The paper includes several ablation and analysis studies to further understand the properties and contributions of TwC:

- **Role-playing Narrative Alignment:** Investigates the impact of different comic narrative styles (documentary, detective, slice-of-life) on reasoning performance, particularly for logical deduction tasks.
- **Scaling the Panels:** Examines how varying the number of generated panels (N) in Path I affects reasoning accuracy and token consumption, to understand the scaling law and information compression efficiency.
- **Panel Distribution Across Task Difficulties:** Analyzes the model's adaptive mechanism for allocating the number of panels based on problem complexity across different benchmarks.
- **The Role of Temporal Sequence in Reasoning:** Assesses the model's reliance on temporal relationships by perturbing the order (Complete Shuffle) or deleting intermediate panels (Random Intermediate Deletion) in sequences generated via Path II.
- **Ablation on Textual Anchoring:** Quantifies the contribution of embedded textual elements (speech bubbles, narration) in eliminating visual ambiguity and enhancing semantic comprehension.
- **Cross-Model Generalization:** Evaluates the transferability and stability of TwC (Path II) across diverse MLLM architectures (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Qwen-VL-72B, GPT-4o, GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro).
- **Efficiency Analysis of TwC and Think with Video:** Compares the computational and economic costs of TwC versus "Thinking with Video" using established industrial pricing models for generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings demonstrating the efficacy and characteristics of the "Thinking with Comics" (TwC) paradigm.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance on Reasoning and Context Understanding Benchmarks

TwC exhibited strong performance across diverse multimodal reasoning and context understanding tasks. On multi-modal reasoning benchmarks such as MathVista, TwC (Img & Txt G-t-R, Path II) achieved 85.8% accuracy, outperforming "Thinking with Video" (Sora 2 V-o-T at 67.6%). On purely text-based mathematical reasoning, TwC remained competitive with strong proprietary models. For context understanding tasks, TwC achieved 99.4% accuracy on DocVQA and demonstrated leading performance on CulturalBench, particularly on its harder subset. These results indicate that incorporating comic-style reasoning enhances both textual and visual reasoning abilities and generalizes across varied tasks.

#### 4.2. Role-playing Narrative Alignment

Different role-playing narrative structures within comics were found to influence reasoning performance. Specifically, a detective-style narrative significantly outperformed a standard documentary-style comic in logical reasoning tasks, showing an average increase of 28.5 absolute percentage points in accuracy across MathVista and GSM8K benchmarks. This suggests that specific narrative styles can function as effective "Visual System Prompts" to activate specific reasoning paths in MLLMs.

#### 4.3. Scaling the Panels

An analysis of reasoning capability as a function of the number of generated panels (N) revealed a scaling law. Reasoning accuracy plateaued between 4 and 6 panels, with marginal gains diminishing rapidly beyond this range. This suggests that comics capture dynamic logic with efficiency, abstracting continuous temporality into a minimal set of information-dense panels, thus representing a Pareto optimal state between information density and computational overhead.

#### 4.4. Panel Distribution Across Task Difficulties

The distribution of generated panels varied adaptively with task difficulty. For simpler tasks like GSM8K, a bimodal distribution was observed, with 33.28% of samples solved with a single panel and 62.82% utilizing four panels. In contrast, for harder reasoning tasks like MathVista, while four panels remained common, a notable 30.41% of samples required six panels. This indicates TwC's ability to dynamically allocate visual resources and reasoning steps based on task complexity.

#### 4.5. The Role of Temporal Sequence in Reasoning

Controlled experiments perturbing the temporal structure of comic panel sequences demonstrated the model's reliance on temporal logic. Both complete shuffling and random intermediate deletion of panels led to a decline in reasoning accuracy (from 75.0% to 71.5%). Notably, missing temporal sequence information (deletion) had a more detrimental effect on the reasoning process than disordered inputs (shuffling), confirming that the model processes panels not as isolated images but as a sequence with dependencies.

#### 4.6. Ablation on Textual Anchoring

Embedded textual elements within comics, such as speech bubbles and narration, were found to significantly reduce visual ambiguity and enhance semantic comprehension. Comics with embedded text consistently outperformed pure visual panels across CulturalBench and MathVista, yielding accuracy gains of 8.3 to 18.1 points. This highlights the "Semantic Anchoring" role of text in comic contexts, reducing the search space for correct solutions within the cross-modal domain.

#### 4.7. Cross-Model Generalization

Path II of the TwC paradigm demonstrated a degree of cross-model generalization. When the same TwC-generated comic was used as input for various MLLM architectures (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Qwen-VL-72B, GPT-4o, GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro), consistent performance trends were observed across tasks. For instance, all models maintained high accuracy (above 99.4%) on DocVQA. This suggests that comic panels function as a stable, reusable intermediate representation that delivers robust performance gains, positioning TwC as a potentially model-agnostic visual reasoning plug-in.

#### 4.8. Efficiency Analysis of TwC and Think with Video

A cost comparison revealed that TwC offers substantial economic advantages over "Thinking with Video." Based on standard industrial pricing, a typical 10-second dynamic reasoning task, which would cost $1.00 via video generation, costs only $0.134 with TwC, representing an 86.6% reduction in media generation cost. A break-even point was calculated at approximately 1.34 seconds, beyond which video-based reasoning becomes strictly more expensive. This indicates that TwC achieves computational efficiency without compromising reasoning accuracy.

#### 4.9. Empirical Analysis from Appendices

Further empirical analyses supported the advantages of comics. Comic prompts consistently led to higher success rates in generating complete multi-panel layouts (90-95%) and better reasoning accuracy (70-75%) compared to non-comic "realistic style" storyboards (65-70% layout success, 55-60% accuracy). Additionally, global comic generation (creating all panels in a single pass) yielded significantly stronger cross-panel coherence, more stable entity representations, and smoother reasoning progression than incremental image chaining, which suffered from error accumulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of "Thinking with Comics" (TwC) represents a significant advancement in multimodal reasoning research, offering a novel and efficient paradigm that addresses critical limitations of existing approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging Modality Gaps:** TwC effectively bridges the gap between the static nature of "Thinking with Images" and the computational overhead of "Thinking with Video." By leveraging comics, the paradigm provides temporal structure and narrative coherence that static images lack, while avoiding the redundancy and high cost associated with video generation. This positions comics as a valuable, high-information-density intermediate representation for multimodal reasoning.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** The research demonstrates that TwC systematically improves performance on multi-step temporal, causal, and complex context understanding tasks. The observed gains across various benchmarks, including MathVista, DocVQA, and CulturalBench, highlight its ability to facilitate more accurate and robust reasoning in MLLMs.
- **Computational Efficiency:** A crucial impact of TwC is its economic feasibility. The comparative analysis shows a substantial reduction in media generation cost (e.g., 86.6% reduction compared to a 10-second video), making complex visual reasoning more accessible and practical for real-world applications, especially where computational resources are a constraint. This efficiency is critical for scaling multimodal AI.
- **Interpretability and Structured Reasoning:** By externalizing intermediate reasoning steps into structured comic panels, TwC offers a more interpretable and structurally grounded reasoning trajectory. The explicit visual and textual cues within comics can make the model's thought process more transparent, which is beneficial for development, debugging, and user understanding.
- **Insights into Visual Reasoning Mechanisms:** The detailed analysis experiments provide valuable insights into the underlying mechanisms of visual reasoning. Findings regarding the impact of narrative structures, optimal panel scaling, the importance of temporal sequence, and the role of embedded textual anchoring contribute to a deeper understanding of how MLLMs process and leverage multimodal information for complex problem-solving.
- **Model-Agnostic and Generalizable Approach:** The demonstration of TwC's consistent performance across diverse MLLM architectures suggests its potential as a robust, model-agnostic visual reasoning plug-in. This indicates that the paradigm is broadly applicable and can enhance a wide range of existing and future multimodal models without requiring specific architectural modifications.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work opens new avenues for exploring "Thinking with" paradigms and developing unified visual reasoning frameworks. Future research could focus on further enhancing the controllability and faithfulness of comic generation, exploring more sophisticated narrative structures, and developing comprehensive evaluation metrics for comic-based reasoning.
- **Practical Applications:** The ability to handle long-context and temporal reasoning efficiently makes TwC potentially impactful for applications in education (explaining complex concepts step-by-step), intelligent tutoring systems, document analysis, cultural education tools, and other domains requiring structured, visual explanations of dynamic processes.

The impact statement acknowledges the absence of immediate harmful applications but emphasizes the importance of considering the influence of narrative styles and cultural conventions in comics to ensure fair and robust deployment across diverse settings.
