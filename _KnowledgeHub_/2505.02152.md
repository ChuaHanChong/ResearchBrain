# Interleave-VLA: Enhancing Robot Manipulation with Interleaved Image-Text Instructions

> [!summary] Summary
> Interleave-VLA introduces a new paradigm for robot manipulation that processes interleaved image-text instructions, enabling superior out-of-domain generalization and mitigating attentional hallucination in robot policies. The method also allows for flexible, zero-shot instruction following from varied visual inputs and comes with a large-scale, real-world interleaved embodied dataset.

## Problem

- Generalist robot policies primarily use text-only instructions, resulting in limited generalization to novel objects and environments.
- Linguistic commands can be ambiguous and lead to "attentional hallucination," where robots misinterpret visually specific goals.
- A scarcity of large-scale, real-world datasets with interleaved image-text instructions hinders the development of robust multimodal robot policies.

## Method

- Introduce **Interleave-VLA**, a paradigm that adapts existing VLA models to process ==interleaved image-text instructions== via a lightweight module and special tokens.
- Create the Open Interleaved X-Embodiment Dataset, an automatically generated large-scale, real-world dataset by converting text-only robot data using LLMs and VLMs.
- Implement a versatile inference interface for zero-shot instruction following using various visual input modalities, including web images and sketches.

## Results

- Interleave-VLA demonstrates **2x** stronger out-of-domain generalization in simulation and 2-**3x** higher performance on real robots compared to text-only VLA models.
- The approach effectively reduces "attentional hallucination" by providing explicit visual grounding, consistently directing the robot's attention to target objects.
- Robots using Interleave-VLA exhibit zero-shot instruction following with diverse visual inputs like cropped images, web photos, and hand-drawn sketches, and also improves OpenVLA performance by **2x** on VIMA-Bench tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interleaved image-text instructions offer richer, less biased context, leading to improved generalization and reduced attentional hallucination compared to text-only instructions.
> - Training with cross-modal, interleaved image-text instructions develops robust multimodal representations, enhancing robot understanding even for text-only evaluations.
> - Incorporating diverse instruction image types, including Internet images, alongside task-specific cropped images during training yields optimal performance across various generalization scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by Fan et al. is positioned at the intersection of foundation models, large language models (LLMs), vision-language models (VLMs), and robotic control. The recent success of foundation models in the digital domain, capable of generalizing across diverse tasks, has inspired the robotics community to develop "robotic foundation models" to achieve similar generalizability in the physical world. However, a critical limitation in current generalist robot policies, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, is their predominant reliance on *text-only instructions*.

This reliance often leads to ambiguity and difficulties in generalizing to unseen scenarios. For example, a text instruction like "pick up an object like this" is inherently vague without visual context. The paper argues that current VLA models, while extending VLMs by incorporating action understanding, are largely restricted to a "Text-VLA" paradigm, thereby missing the richer semantics and improved grounding offered by multimodal instruction signals.

The paper directly addresses this gap by extending the concept of *interleaved vision-language models* (which have shown success in the digital domain by processing mixed sequences of images and text from web corpora) to the *action modality* for real-world robotics. While some prior works, like VIMA, explored interleaved instructions for high-level 2D action planning in simulation, they did not fully investigate the broader benefits for real-world low-level robotic actions or demonstrate advantages over text-only instructions due to a lack of suitable datasets and policies. Interleave-VLA thus represents a significant step forward in bridging this gap, enabling VLA models to leverage interleaved image-text inputs for enhanced real-world robotic manipulation. It aims to make robotic policies more intuitive, flexible, and robust, moving beyond the constraints of purely linguistic commands.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalization capabilities of robot manipulation policies, particularly in unseen scenarios, by moving beyond traditional text-only instructions. The authors are motivated by several key challenges in the current landscape of robotic foundation models:

1. **Limited Generalization of Text-only VLA Models:** Existing VLA models, heavily reliant on text instructions, struggle to generalize effectively to novel objects, environments, or tasks not explicitly seen during training. Text often lacks the specificity required for complex visual distinctions.
2. **Ambiguity in Textual Instructions:** Linguistic commands can be inherently ambiguous or cumbersome when users need to specify visually unique goals (e.g., "pick up an object like this"). This ambiguity can lead to "attentional hallucination" where the robot misinterprets the target or task.
3. **Lack of Real-world Interleaved Datasets:** A significant barrier to developing robust multimodal robot policies is the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality real-world datasets containing interleaved image-text instructions for robot manipulation.
4. **Desire for Flexible Human-Robot Interaction:** The ultimate goal is to enable more intuitive and natural human-robot interaction, where users can provide instructions through a combination of visual cues (like pointing to an object in an image, showing a photo, or even sketching) and text.

To address these, the paper proposes Interleave-VLA, aiming to:
- Develop a novel paradigm for robot learning that comprehends interleaved image-text instructions and directly generates continuous action sequences.
- Demonstrate that interleaved inputs offer richer, less biased context, enabling superior generalization to unseen tasks and mitigating issues like attentional hallucination.
- Create a large-scale, real-world interleaved embodied dataset to facilitate the training of such multimodal robot policies.
- Provide a model-agnostic and lightweight adaptation framework that can extend existing state-of-the-art VLA models with minimal modifications.
- Unlock zero-shot generalization capabilities, allowing robots to follow instructions from diverse visual modalities (e.g., web images, hand-drawn sketches) without specific fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Interleave-VLA paradigm is built upon three core components and is underpinned by a novel dataset construction pipeline:

#### 3.1. Interleave-VLA Paradigm

1. **Lightweight Adaptation Module:** This module enables existing VLA models to process interleaved image-text inputs. The core idea is to extend the tokenizer vocabulary of the base VLA model (e.g., π0 or OpenVLA) by introducing special separator tokens, `<BOI>` (beginning of image) and `<EOI>` (end of image). These tokens delineate image embeddings within the instruction sequence, allowing the model to switch between processing visual patches and text tokens. Crucially, the core VLA architecture remains largely unchanged, making the adaptation model-agnostic and easily transferable.
2. **Scalable Training Pipeline:** Interleave-VLA is trained on a large-scale interleaved embodied dataset (detailed below), without modifying the original objectives or hyperparameters of the base VLA model. The training process is designed to efficiently scale with dataset size and cross-modal instruction diversity, leveraging the benefits of multimodal inputs to learn richer representations.
3. **Versatile Inference Interface:** The paradigm supports both text-only and interleaved instructions during inference. A key feature is its flexibility in accepting diverse image sources for instructions, including real-world camera crops, web images, or even hand-drawn sketches, demonstrating zero-shot generalization capabilities to novel input modalities. A user-friendly GUI is also designed to simplify interaction.

#### 3.2. Construction of Open Interleaved X-Embodiment Dataset

Recognizing the critical need for a large-scale, high-quality real-world dataset, the authors developed an automated pipeline to convert existing text-only robot datasets into interleaved image-text formats. This pipeline comprises three main steps:
1. **Instruction Parsing:** An LLM (Qwen2.5) is used to extract key objects from raw language instructions. This is more robust than rule-based NLP tools, handling diverse instruction formats and summarizing lengthy commands.
2. **Open-Vocabulary Detection:** The OWLv2 open-vocabulary detector is employed to locate and crop target objects from trajectory frames based on the parsed keywords. This achieves 82.6% accuracy.
3. **Data Quality Verification:** For cases where OWLv2 might fail, a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) verifies the detected objects. If necessary, it provides keypoints for more precise segmentation using Segment Anything (SAM), boosting accuracy to 95.6%. This collaborative approach leverages the strengths of different models.

The resulting dataset, "Open Interleaved X-Embodiment Dataset," integrates 11 diverse datasets from Open X-Embodiment (e.g., RT-1, Bridge), totaling over 210k episodes and 13 million frames, featuring 3,500 unique objects. To further enhance instruction diversity, the dataset is augmented by randomly integrating Internet-sourced images alongside original text instructions. The action space across all datasets is standardized to a 7D pose format (xyz position, Euler orientation, and gripper state) for coherent training and cross-embodiment transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted in both simulation (SimplerEnv) and on real robots (FANUC LRMate 200iD/7L), reveal several significant findings:

1. **Superior Out-of-Domain Generalization:** Interleave-VLA consistently outperforms text-only VLA models (e.g., π0, RT-1-X, Octo) in out-of-domain tasks. In simulation, it achieves **2x stronger out-of-domain generalization** on semantically new objects and categories. On real robots, it demonstrates **2-3x higher out-of-domain performance** compared to text-VLA when pretrained, especially in low-data regimes.
2. **Mitigation of Attentional Hallucination:** A key qualitative insight is that interleaved image-text instructions effectively mitigate "attentional hallucination" observed in Text-VLA models. The authors categorize three forms:
 - **Attentional Bias:** Incorrect focus on salient distractors.
 - **Diffused Attention:** Lack of a focal point, indicating uncertainty.
 - **Attention Leakage:** Correct target identification but scattered focus.
 Interleave-VLA, by providing explicit visual grounding, consistently directs attention to target objects, overcoming linguistic ambiguity and distributional biases in training data.
3. **Flexible and Zero-Shot Instruction Following:** Interleave-VLA supports highly versatile instruction formats in a zero-shot manner, without additional fine-tuning. This includes:
 - **Cropped Image Instructions:** Users can crop regions from a camera feed.
 - **Internet Image Instructions:** Any web photo can be used to specify objects.
 - **Hand-Drawn Sketch Instructions:** The model can interpret user sketches, demonstrating an emergent capability to handle completely novel input modalities.
 This flexibility makes human-robot interaction more intuitive and practical.
4. **Importance of Modality and Instruction Diversity in Training:**
 - **Modality Diversity:** Training with cross-modal, interleaved image-text instructions leads to significant absolute improvements (e.g., +5.7% on novel object tasks) even when evaluated with text-only instructions (Interleave-VLA (Partial) vs. Text-VLA), indicating the development of richer multimodal representations.
 - **Instruction Image Diversity:** Combining task-specific cropped images with diverse Internet images during training yields the best overall performance, demonstrating complementary advantages for both in-domain accuracy and out-of-domain generalization.
5. **Scalability and Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The large-scale Open Interleaved X-Embodiment Dataset is crucial for scaling. Pretraining on this diverse dataset significantly boosts performance in low-data real-robot regimes, enabling strong cross-embodiment transfer even to a robot (FANUC) not included in the pretraining data. Co-training with additional datasets further improves semantic generalization.
6. **Model-Agnostic Adaptation:** The Interleave-VLA paradigm is shown to be easily adaptable and effective across different VLA architectures. When extended to OpenVLA, it achieves a **2x performance improvement** on VIMA-Bench tasks across various generalization levels (L1-L4), confirming its general applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Interleave-VLA paradigm and the accompanying Open Interleaved X-Embodiment Dataset represent a substantial advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, with several significant implications:

1. **Enhanced Generalization for Robotics:** By effectively addressing the limitations of text-only instructions, Interleave-VLA pushes the boundaries of out-of-domain generalization for robot policies. This is critical for deploying robots in real-world, unstructured environments where they will inevitably encounter novel objects, scenes, and tasks.
2. **More Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** The ability to accept diverse visual instruction types (cropped images, web photos, sketches) in a zero-shot manner makes HRI far more natural and user-friendly. Users no longer need to rely solely on precise, unambiguous textual descriptions, which can be challenging for complex visual goals. This flexibility can significantly lower the barrier for non-expert users to interact with and program robots.
3. **Scalable and Data-Efficient Robot Learning:** The automated pipeline for generating large-scale, high-quality interleaved datasets is a major contribution. It addresses a critical data bottleneck and enables more efficient training of robust multimodal robot policies, reducing the reliance on laborious manual data collection. The demonstrated cross-embodiment transfer also suggests that learned policies can be more readily adapted to new robot platforms.
4. **Deeper Understanding of VLA Failures:** The detailed analysis of "attentional hallucination" provides valuable insights into why text-only VLA models fail. By explicitly showing how interleaved instructions mitigate these issues, the research offers a clearer path for developing more reliable and interpretable robot learning models.
5. **Foundation for Future Generalist Robots:** This work contributes to the vision of generalist robot policies that can operate flexibly and robustly in open-world settings. By integrating multimodal understanding directly into action generation, it lays a stronger foundation for robots that can truly perceive, reason, and act based on rich, human-like instructions.
6. **Open Science Contribution:** The open-sourcing of the large-scale, real-world interleaved embodied dataset is a significant contribution to the research community, providing a valuable resource for future work in multimodal robot learning and accelerating progress in this area.

While acknowledging computational demands of longer image token sequences as a limitation, the paper’s contributions in generalization, HRI, and data creation pave the way for a new generation of more capable and adaptable robotic systems.
