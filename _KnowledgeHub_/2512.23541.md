# Act2Goal: From World Model To General Goal-conditioned Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Act2Goal proposes a general goal-conditioned robotic policy integrating a vision-based world model for multi-scale planning and a reward-free online adaptation mechanism. The system exhibited strong zero-shot generalization in both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, with autonomous improvements boosting success rates up to 8x in some simulations and from 0.30 to 0.90 in real-world unseen scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing goal-conditioned policies struggle with long-horizon manipulation due to a lack of explicit task progress modeling and understanding of intermediate steps.
- Robot policies often exhibit limited generalization to novel objects, environments, or task variations when trained solely on offline demonstration data.
- Online adaptation for robotic policies typically requires extensive human supervision, complex reward engineering, or frequent retraining of large models.

## Method

- A goal-conditioned visual world model generates multi-scale visual plans, forecasting intermediate states between the current observation and the desired goal.
- Multi-Scale Temporal Hashing (MSTH) balances long-horizon consistency with local reactivity by structuring imagined trajectories into dense proximal and sparse distal segments.
- A reward-free online autonomous improvement mechanism, utilizing Hindsight Experience Replay and LoRA-based fine-tuning, enables efficient self-supervised adaptation.

## Results

- Act2Goal achieved superior zero-shot generalization, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art baselines in both simulation (e.g., 0.43 vs 0.06 success rate on Robotwin 2.0 Hard mode Pick Bottles) and real-world manipulation tasks.
- The online autonomous improvement mechanism led to up to an 8x increase in success rate in some simulated scenarios and boosted real-world out-of-domain task performance (e.g., Plug-In operation from 0.30 to 0.90).
- Multi-Scale Temporal Hashing (MSTH) was critical for long-horizon tasks, demonstrating substantially higher success rates (e.g., 0.90-0.95 vs 0.10-0.35) for complex real-world word writing compared to fixed-horizon baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a goal-conditioned visual world model with multi-scale temporal control enables robust long-horizon manipulation by effectively balancing global task consistency and local reactivity.
> - Policies trained on large-scale demonstration data, when combined with a structured visual planning mechanism, can achieve strong zero-shot generalization to unseen manipulation tasks and environments.
> - Reward-free, lightweight online adaptation through HER and LoRA fine-tuning provides a practical path for autonomous skill improvement in real-world scenarios without explicit supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in Act2Goal addresses a critical challenge in robotic manipulation: enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks with high precision and adaptability using visual goal specifications. It integrates and advances three major lines of research:

**A. Goal-Conditioned Policies (GCPs):**
Traditional GCPs map current observations and visual goals directly to actions. While effective for short-horizon tasks and offering some generalization, they struggle with long-horizon manipulation because they lack an explicit understanding of task progress or intermediate feasibility. This limitation is particularly evident in out-of-distribution (OOD) settings where policies, often trained on narrow demonstration data, tend to overfit and fail to maintain long-term coherence. Act2Goal aims to overcome this by introducing an explicit model of visual dynamics, enabling the policy to reason about progress towards a distant goal rather than just local state-action correlations. It improves upon methods like GoalGAIL (Ding et al., 2019) and CoA (Zhang et al., 2025) by providing structured visual trajectories and a multi-scale temporal planning mechanism.

**B. World Models for Robotic Control:**
World models have emerged as powerful tools for simulating environmental dynamics, generating synthetic data, and guiding policy learning. Recent works have combined world models with action experts to form planning systems, where the world model predicts future states (e.g., visual features) and the action expert predicts corresponding actions. Examples include GE-Act (Liao et al., 2025), which uses a vision-language world model, and WorldVLA (Cen et al., 2025), which jointly predicts vision and action. Act2Goal differentiates itself by leveraging a **purely vision-based goal-conditioned world model** specifically designed to generate intermediate visual states *between* a current observation and a target visual goal. This is a novel integration, focusing the world model's generative power directly on goal-directed visual planning for robotic control.

**C. Online Autonomous Improvement:**
Enhancing policy adaptability during deployment is crucial. Existing methods include DAgger-style interactive imitation learning (Ross et al., 2011), which requires frequent human intervention, and in-context learning (ICL), which doesn't update model weights and has limited performance on complex tasks. Another prominent approach is Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) (Andrychowicz et al., 2017), which relabels failed trajectories with achieved goals. While HER reduces the need for explicit rewards, it often still relies on complex reward relabeling or external reward signals. Act2Goal takes a significant step towards full autonomy by combining HER-style goal relabeling with **LoRA-based finetuning** for efficient on-device adaptation. This creates a lightweight, fully self-supervised update mechanism, eliminating the need for task rewards or human annotations during online improvement, which is a major advancement for real-world deployment.

In summary, Act2Goal represents a synthesis of these areas, addressing the limitations of existing GCPs by introducing a structured, visually-grounded planning mechanism (world model + multi-scale control) and ensuring practical deployability through efficient, reward-free online adaptation. It pushes the boundaries of generalist robotic policies, moving towards systems that can reason over long horizons and autonomously improve in novel, real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind Act2Goal stems from the persistent challenge of specifying robotic manipulation tasks expressively and precisely, particularly for long-horizon scenarios. While visual goals offer unambiguous task specification, existing goal-conditioned policies often fall short due to fundamental limitations:
1. **Lack of Task Progress Modeling:** Standard GCPs typically perform single-step action prediction, failing to explicitly model the intermediate steps, feasibility, or long-horizon consistency required to reach a distant visual goal. This leads to brittle performance, especially in complex, multi-stage tasks or when encountering out-of-distribution scenarios.
2. **Brittleness to Deviations:** Full-trajectory planning can be fragile under perturbations, while short-horizon control often loses long-term goal alignment. There's a need to balance global coherence with local reactivity.
3. **Limited Generalization from Offline Data:** Policies trained on narrowly scoped demonstration data tend to overfit, struggling with novel objects, spatial layouts, or environments without explicit modeling of visual transitions.
4. **Reliance on Supervision for Adaptation:** Online adaptation methods often require frequent expert intervention, complex reward engineering, or re-training large models, hindering autonomous deployment.

To address these motivations, Act2Goal sets out to achieve the following key objectives:
- **Integrate a Goal-Conditioned Visual World Model:** Develop a model that generates plausible sequences of intermediate visual states (a "visual plan") between a current observation and a target visual goal, thereby introducing an explicit representation of task progress.
- **Implement Multi-Scale Temporal Control:** Introduce a mechanism (Multi-Scale Temporal Hashing, MSTH) to decompose imagined visual trajectories into dense proximal frames (for fine-grained, reactive control) and sparse distal frames (for anchoring global task consistency). This aims to balance long-horizon planning with robust closed-loop execution.
- **Achieve Robust Zero-Shot Generalization:** Enable the policy, initialized from large-scale human demonstration data, to generalize effectively to novel objects, spatial layouts, and environments without task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Enable Reward-Free Online Autonomous Improvement:** Develop a lightweight, self-supervised adaptation mechanism based on Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) and LoRA-based finetuning, allowing the policy to rapidly improve its performance on challenging out-of-distribution tasks through autonomous interaction without external supervision.

Ultimately, Act2Goal aims to provide the structured guidance necessary for robust, generalizable, and adaptable long-horizon manipulation, moving towards truly general-purpose robotic controllers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Act2Goal proposes a general goal-conditioned manipulation policy that integrates a goal-conditioned visual world model with multi-scale temporal control, coupled with a novel reward-free online adaptation mechanism.

**A. Goal-Conditioned World Model (GCWM)-Guided Policy:**
- **Architecture:** The core is a goal-conditioned world model built upon a modified "Genie Envisioner" architecture. It removes language conditioning and introduces a goal visual condition, concatenated with the current observation, to generate purely vision-based intermediate visual states.
- **Generative Process:** The GCWM uses a continuous flow matching approach. It learns a transformation `fθ` from random noise `ϵ` to structured visual sequences, conditioned on VAE-compressed latents of the current observation (`zt`) and goal (`zg`). During inference, it progressively refines noisy latents through a deterministic flow process, `z(n+1) = z(n) + (1/N) * vθ(z(n), zt, zg)`, where `vθ` is a learned vector field. These latents are then decoded into visual states.
- **Action Expert:** An isomorphic network architecture, with the same number of DiT blocks but reduced width, acts as the action expert. It predicts action trajectories using a flow matching process, `apred = gϕ(cw, cp, ζ)`, conditioned on proprioceptive state (`cp`) and multi-scale features (`cw`) from the world model. `uϕ` is the learned vector field for action generation during iterative refinement: `a(n+1) = a(n) + (1/N) * uϕ(a(n), cw, cp)`.

**B. Multi-Scale Temporal Hashing (MSTH):**
- **Purpose:** MSTH is crucial for balancing global consistency (long-term goals) and local reactivity (short-term control) by decomposing the imagined trajectory across different temporal scales.
- **Mechanism:** Given a total imagined trajectory length `K`, a proximal horizon `P`, and a vision sampling stride `r`, MSTH partitions the future into:
 - **Proximal Segment:** High-frequency visual states `{st+kr}` for `k=1...P/r`, capturing fine-grained local dynamics.
 - **Distal Segment:** `M` sparsely sampled visual states `{st+dm}` for `m=1...M`, where indices `dm` are determined by logarithmic spacing (e.g., `dm = P + ((K-P)/log(M+1)) * log(m+1)`). This ensures increasing temporal intervals further into the future, providing coarse, goal-aligned long-term guidance.
- **Actions:** Predicted actions follow the same multi-scale structure. Proximal actions are dense (every timestep `at+1...at+P`), while distal actions `{at+dm}` align with distal visual states for long-horizon guidance. Only proximal actions are executed during deployment.

**C. Two-Stage Offline Training:**
1. **Stage 1: Joint Training of GCWM and Action Expert:** A pre-trained world model (e.g., Genie Envisioner, 1.6B parameters) is fine-tuned to predict multi-view video frames following the MSTH distribution between initial observation and goal. To align visual prediction with action planning, both the visual generation loss (`Lv`) and action generation loss (`La`) are jointly optimized using flow matching: `Lstage1 = Lv + λ * La` (with `λ = 0.1`). This ensures the world model generates actionable visual trajectories.
2. **Stage 2: Action Adaptation via Behavioral Cloning:** The entire model is fine-tuned end-to-end using only the action flow matching loss (`Lstage2 = La`). Gradients propagate through the action generation components and the GCWM, optimizing visual representations specifically for action planning based on expert demonstrations.

**D. Online Autonomous Improvement:**
- **Reward-Free HER-style Relabeling:** During deployment, the policy executes actions based on an expected goal. At each step, the transition (observation, proprio state, action, resulting observation) is collected in a replay buffer. Crucially, the achieved visual state (the resulting observation `o'`) is automatically relabeled as the *new goal* (`g'`) for that transition, regardless of whether the original intended goal was achieved. This eliminates the need for manual labeling or external reward signals.
- **LoRA-based Finetuning:** When the replay buffer reaches a threshold, a fixed number of end-to-end training iterations are performed. To ensure efficient on-device training, only the lightweight LoRA layers (Low-Rank Adaptation) added to the Act2Goal model are updated, keeping the base model parameters frozen.
- **Continuous Learning Loop:** After training, the replay buffer is cleared, and the system continues collecting new data through rollouts, repeating the cycle until performance converges. The robot automatically resets after a maximum inference count, minimizing human intervention to only physically resetting the environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Act2Goal demonstrates strong performance across diverse tasks and generalization settings, validated through extensive simulation and real-world experiments.

**A. Generalization Capability Evaluation:**
- **Robotwin 2.0 Simulation Benchmark:** Act2Goal significantly outperformed state-of-the-art baselines (DP-GC, HyperGoalNet, π0.5-GC) in all "Easy" mode tasks and 3 out of 4 "Hard" mode tasks (Move can pot, Pick dual bottles, Place empty cup, Place shoe). Notably, it showed a substantial advantage in the challenging "Hard" mode (e.g., 0.43 success rate for Pick Bottles vs. 0.06 for π0.5-GC), indicating superior generalization to unseen scenarios.
- **Real-World Manipulation Tasks (AgiBot Genie-01):**
 - **In-Domain (ID) Tasks:** Achieved high success rates: 0.93 for Whiteboard Word Writing, 0.75 for Dessert Plating, and 0.45 for Plug-In Operation. This consistently outperformed all baselines, which often scored 0.00-0.23.
 - **Out-of-Domain (OOD) Tasks:** Maintained strong performance: 0.90 for Whiteboard Word Writing (novel character combinations), 0.48 for Dessert Plating (significant visual variations), and 0.30 for Plug-In Operation (unseen task like bottle insertion). Baselines mostly failed (0.00-0.20 success rates). These results underscore Act2Goal's remarkable zero-shot generalization capabilities in realistic and visually complex environments.

**B. Analysis of Online Autonomous Improvement:**
- **Robotwin 2.0 Simulation:**
 - The model's performance consistently improved over approximately three rounds of online training, with some scenarios showing up to an **8x increase** in success rate compared to the pre-trained baseline.
 - Evaluation of data selection strategies revealed that using **all rollouts** (successful and failed) yielded the best performance. Importantly, even using only *failed* rollouts still led to noticeable improvement, validating the effectiveness of the HER-based strategy in extracting valuable experience from failures for goal-conditioned manipulation.
- **Real-World Unseen Scenarios:**
 - On a novel whiteboard drawing task (different from word writing), the model initially performed poorly but showed **consistent improvements in drawing quality within 15 minutes** of online fine-tuning.
 - For the OOD Plug-In task, the success rate notably increased **from 0.30 to 0.90** through online training, demonstrating practical and rapid adaptation in real-world settings.

**C. Effectiveness of MSTH (Multi-Scale Temporal Hashing):**
- **Qualitative Analysis:** The goal-conditioned world model effectively generated both fine-grained proximal frames (for detailed short-term guidance) and sparser distal frames (for capturing long-horizon task structure). This hierarchical visual forecasting enabled reasoning across multiple temporal scales.
- **Ablation Study (Real-World Whiteboard Word Writing):**
 - MSTH showed comparable performance to a fixed-horizon baseline for short words (0.95 success rate for ID).
 - However, for medium and long words, MSTH demonstrated **substantially higher success rates** (e.90-0.95 vs. 0.10-0.35 for ID; 0.88-0.93 vs. 0.00-0.60 for OOD) compared to the fixed-horizon baseline.
 - This highlights that MSTH's dynamic temporal abstraction is critical for maintaining goal consistency and robustness over extended sequences, especially in OOD scenarios where compounding goal misalignment would otherwise lead to drastic performance drops.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Act2Goal makes significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation, addressing key limitations of current approaches and paving the way for more capable and autonomous robots in real-world settings. Its primary significance lies in the synergistic integration of three core innovations:

1. **Unified Goal-Conditioned World Model with Multi-Scale Control:** The paper demonstrates that by combining a purely vision-based goal-conditioned world model (which generates actionable visual plans) with the novel Multi-Scale Temporal Hashing (MSTH) mechanism, robots can achieve robust long-horizon manipulation. This approach effectively bridges the gap between high-level visual goals and low-level motor control, enabling a balance between global task coherence and local reactivity. The MSTH mechanism is particularly impactful, showing how dynamic temporal abstraction can prevent performance degradation in complex, extended tasks, a common pitfall for fixed-horizon methods.
2. **Strong Zero-Shot Generalization:** The extensive experimental results in both simulation and real-world scenarios validate Act2Goal's impressive zero-shot generalization capabilities to novel objects, spatial layouts, and environments. This is a crucial step towards deploying robots in open-ended settings where every scenario cannot be exhaustively covered by training data. The ability to generalize from offline imitation learning alone signifies a robust foundation model for robotic skills.
3. **Reward-Free Online Autonomous Improvement:** Perhaps one of the most practical and impactful contributions is the lightweight, reward-free online adaptation mechanism. By leveraging Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) with efficient LoRA-based finetuning, Act2Goal can autonomously learn and improve its performance on challenging out-of-distribution tasks within minutes, without any external supervision or explicit reward engineering. This self-improving capability significantly lowers the deployment barrier for complex robotic systems, making them more adaptable and resilient to real-world variability and unseen challenges.

The potential impact of Act2Goal is profound:
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** The ability to plan over long horizons, generalize to novel situations, and autonomously adapt will enable robots to tackle more complex and unstructured tasks, reducing human intervention.
- **Accelerated Development and Deployment:** The self-supervised online improvement loop can dramatically accelerate the iterative development and refinement of robotic policies in real-world environments, making robot learning more efficient and scalable.
- **Foundational Model for Embodied AI:** By integrating a general visual world model with robust action generation and adaptation, Act2Goal contributes to the development of more generalist robot policies, akin to foundational models in other AI domains. This could lead to a new generation of visuomotor policies capable of open-ended, real-world manipulation.
- **Reduced Data Dependency and Cost:** The reward-free and efficient online adaptation strategy mitigates the need for vast amounts of meticulously labeled data or complex reward function designs for every new task, lowering the cost and effort of robot deployment.

In conclusion, Act2Goal represents a significant stride towards creating truly general-purpose robotic agents that are not only skilled and adaptable but can also autonomously improve their capabilities in the face of novel challenges, making it a pivotal work in advancing the frontier of embodied artificial intelligence.
