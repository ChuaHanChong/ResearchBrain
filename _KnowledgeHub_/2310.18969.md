# Analyzing Vision Transformers for Image Classification in Class Embedding Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Goethe University Frankfurt developed a framework for Vision Transformers (ViTs) that projects internal representations onto a learned class embedding space, revealing how class-specific information is built across layers. This method provides an efficient and task-relevant tool for mechanistic interpretability and explainability, offering insights into the distinct contributions of self-attention and MLP layers.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks, particularly Vision Transformers, often operate as 'black boxes', making it challenging to mechanistically understand why they make specific predictions.
- Existing interpretability methods for ViTs, such as linear probing, suffer from limitations in computational efficiency and direct relevance to the model's actual decision-making process.
- The internal representational dynamics of ViTs are less understood compared to traditional CNNs or NLP transformers, hindering trust, debugging, and targeted improvements.

## Method

- The framework projects hidden states of image and `[CLS]` tokens, as well as parameter matrices (e.g., MLP `W_out`), onto the learned ==class embedding space== to quantify ==class identifiability==.
- It utilizes a ==gradient-based== method to generate fine-grained, block- and attention-head-specific feature importance maps, identifying image regions critical for class representation.
- The approach draws on NLP interpretability by treating transformer layers as ==key-value memory systems==, analyzing how self-attention and MLP layers contribute to categorical updates via their value vectors.

## Results

- Class identifiability scores consistently increased across ViT blocks for both image and `[CLS]` tokens in all **7** tested ViT variants.
- MLP layers promoted stronger categorical updates, with their value vectors manually inspected to cluster semantically similar concepts like 'racket, tennis ball, ping-pong ball'.
- The gradient-based explainability tool generates global relevancy maps comparable in quality to established methods while being significantly more time-efficient, requiring only a **single forward pass** over validation images.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Image tokens progressively encode class-specific information across ViT layers, not solely relying on the `[CLS]` token, with distinct patterns for class-labeled versus context-labeled regions.
> - Self-attention layers provide weaker, more compositional, and broadly distributed categorical updates, primarily in earlier blocks, while MLP layers yield stronger, more direct, and semantically clustered updates, peaking in penultimate blocks.
> - The proposed method offers a more task-relevant and efficient measure of feature importance compared to linear probing, which often fails to predict actual token relevance to the network's classification decision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of deep neural networks, especially transformer-based architectures, has led to remarkable performance across various domains, including computer vision. However, these models often operate as "black boxes," making it challenging to understand *why* they make particular predictions. This lack of mechanistic understanding hinders trust, debugging, and further scientific discovery. The present work positions itself squarely within the burgeoning field of **mechanistic interpretability and explainability** for deep learning models.

The paper specifically addresses **Vision Transformers (ViTs)**, which have emerged as powerful alternatives to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for image classification. While their performance is impressive, the internal representational dynamics of ViTs are less understood compared to their NLP counterparts or traditional CNNs.

The authors draw significant inspiration from prior research in **Natural Language Processing (NLP)**. Several foundational studies (e.g., Geva et al., Belrose et al.) have successfully reverse-engineered large language transformers by projecting their hidden states and parameters onto a *human-interpretable vocabulary space*. This approach has provided profound insights into how these models build next-word predictions. The key novelty of this paper is to *adapt and extend this powerful NLP interpretability paradigm to Vision Transformers* by projecting their internal representations onto a *learned class embedding space*.

Within the ViT interpretability landscape, previous work has explored:
- **Linear Probing:** Studies like Raghu et al. used linear classifiers to decode class representations from the hidden states of the `[CLS]` token, especially in early layers. However, linear probing has been critiqued for sometimes highlighting features not actually relevant to the model's task.
- **Spatial Representation Analysis:** Ghiasi et al. and Raghu et al. investigated how ViT tokens preserve input spatial information across layers.
- **Component-Specific Roles:** Researchers have examined the differential roles of self-attention and MLP layers through perturbation studies or statistical analyses.
- **General Explainability:** Methods for generating human-interpretable explanations (e.g., saliency maps) for specific predictions have also been developed.

This paper significantly advances the field by:
1. **Bridging the NLP-CV interpretability gap:** It demonstrates that the highly successful "embedding space projection" method from NLP can be effectively translated to ViTs, opening a new avenue for mechanistic understanding in computer vision.
2. **Going beyond existing ViT methods:** Unlike linear probing, which is shown to be less task-relevant, this method directly quantifies alignment with the model's actual class prototypes. It also complements spatial analyses by focusing on categorical information flow and expands on component role analyses by dissecting *how* these components build class representations via key-value memories.
3. **Providing a novel explainability tool:** The proposed gradient-based method offers fine-grained, block- and head-specific insights into feature importance, which differs from traditional global saliency maps.

In summary, this work provides a new, intuitive, and powerful methodological framework for deep mechanistic interpretability and explainability in Vision Transformers, drawing on successful precedents and addressing limitations of existing approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the increasing adoption of transformer models in computer vision and the concurrent lack of a comprehensive mechanistic understanding of their internal workings. Despite their remarkable performance, these models remain largely opaque, making it difficult to fully trust, debug, or even improve them systematically.

The key objectives of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Develop a Novel Interpretability Framework for ViTs:** The overarching goal is to introduce a robust method to reverse-engineer Vision Transformers trained for image classification. This method is inspired by successful approaches in NLP that project internal representations onto a meaningful semantic space.
2. **Uncover Categorical Representation Building:** Specifically, the authors aim to demonstrate how the inner representations at any hierarchical level within a ViT can be projected onto the *learned class embedding space*. This projection is intended to reveal *how* these networks construct the categorical representations that ultimately lead to their predictions.
3. **Investigate Class-Specific Representation Development in Image Tokens:** A core objective is to show how individual image tokens, which represent portions of the input image, gradually develop class-specific representations. This includes understanding the role of attention mechanisms and contextual information in this process.
4. **Delineate the Roles of Self-Attention and MLP Layers:** The research seeks to provide insights into how the fundamental building blocks of transformers—Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) and Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) layers—differentially contribute to the categorical composition of representations. This involves examining their contribution through the lens of key-value memory pair mechanisms.
5. **Establish a New Explainability Tool:** Beyond mechanistic understanding, the authors aim to demonstrate that their framework can be effectively used to identify the specific parts of an image that are most critical for detecting a class of interest, thereby serving as a valuable explainability method.
6. **Highlight Advantages over Traditional Linear Probing:** A significant objective is to showcase that the proposed method offers substantial advantages over conventional linear probing approaches in terms of efficiency, accuracy, and relevance to the model's actual decision-making process.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond simply observing what a ViT does, towards a deeper understanding of *how* it does it, and to provide tools that enable both mechanistic understanding and practical explainability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology introduced by Vilas, Schaumlöffel, and Roig revolves around projecting the internal representations of Vision Transformers into the learned class embedding space. This approach is conceptually derived from successful interpretability techniques in NLP.

#### 3.1. Vision Transformer Architecture Overview

The study focuses on vanilla ViT architectures, as introduced by Dosovitskiy et al.. These models process an input image by first dividing it into a sequence of fixed-size patches, linearly projecting them into "image tokens," and adding position embeddings. A special "class token" (`[CLS]`) is prepended to this sequence. This combined sequence is then fed through a series of transformer blocks, each consisting of alternating Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) and Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) layers, augmented with residual connections. For image classification, the final hidden state of the `[CLS]` token is projected onto the class embedding space using a learned output embedding matrix **E** ∈ R^(|C|×d), where |C| is the number of classes and d is the token dimension. Applying a softmax yields the predicted class probabilities. The rows of **E** are interpreted as "class prototypes."

#### 3.2. Activation Space Projection

To understand how class representations emerge, the method projects the hidden states of *any* token (image tokens or `[CLS]`) at *any* block `b` onto the class embedding space.
- **Projection:** For a token's hidden state `x_i^b` ∈ R^d, the class distribution `p_i^b` ∈ R^|C| is computed as `p_i^b = E ⋅ x_i^b`.
- **Quantification of Alignment (Class Identifiability):** To measure how well the projected distribution aligns with the correct class `c_j` for the `j`-th image, the authors define a "class identifiability" score `r_j^i`. This score is derived from the rank of the correct class logit in the sorted list of all class logits, normalized to range from 0 (correct class has lowest logit) to 1 (correct class has highest logit).

#### 3.3. Parameter Space Projection and Key-Value Memory Pairs

Building on NLP research that views transformer layers as key-value memory systems, the framework extends this concept to ViTs.
- **MLP Layers:** An MLP layer `MLP(X) = GELU(XW_inp)W_out` is conceptualized where the columns of `W_inp` act as "keys" (detecting patterns in input `X`), and the rows of `W_out` act as "values" (modifying `X` based on detected patterns). The core idea is to project `W_out` onto the class embedding space (`E ⋅ W_out^T`) to see which class prototypes the value vectors promote.
- **Self-Attention Layers:** MHSA layers, `MHSA(X) = hconcat(...A_h X W_V_attn^h...)W_out`, also implement key-value mechanisms. Here, `W_V_attn^h` (for each head `h`) represents the key vectors, which detect patterns within the token sequence. The resulting output is then multiplied by `W_out`. The paper focuses on projecting the `W_out` matrix of the MHSA layer onto the class embedding space.
- **Distinction from NLP:** Unlike some NLP models where input and output embeddings are tied, ViTs have distinct input patch embeddings and class output embeddings. This implies that ViT "keys" might represent image-space patterns, while "values" represent updates in the class embedding space. The authors prioritize analyzing the value vectors due to their direct relevance to class representation updates.

#### 3.4. Experimental Design

The proposed framework was validated across a diverse set of **7 ViT variants** differing in patch size (16 vs. 32), layer depth (12 vs. 24), pre-training/fine-tuning datasets (ImageNet-21k, ImageNet-1k, CIFAR100), and architectural modifications (e.g., Refinement module, Global Average Pooling (GAP) instead of `[CLS]` token).

The **ImageNet-S dataset**, a subset of ImageNet with semantic segmentation annotations, was used. This dataset is crucial for experiments requiring fine-grained analysis of image regions (e.g., class-labeled vs. context-labeled tokens).

The experimental approach involved:
- **Measuring Class Identifiability:** Tracking the evolution of class identifiability scores for both image and `[CLS]` tokens across all blocks.
- **Perturbation Studies:**
 - **Attention Mechanisms:** Disrupting attention weights between image tokens, or between image tokens and the `[CLS]` token, to assess their impact on class representation formation.
 - **Contextual Information:** Comparing identifiability in class-labeled vs. context-labeled image regions (using semantic segmentation) and conducting token removal experiments.
- **Mechanistic Analysis of SA and MLP Layers:**
 - **Class Similarity Change Rate:** Measuring how much SA and MLP layers increase the correct class logits.
 - **Influence on Residual Stream:** Quantifying how the predictions of SA and MLP layers integrate into the residual stream.
 - **Categorical Updates (Class-Value Agreement Score):** Projecting `W_out` matrices into class space to assess the strength of class prototype promotion.
 - **Key-Value Agreement Rate:** Measuring how frequently highly activated keys correspond to value vectors that promote the correct class at inference time.
 - **Compositionality:** Analyzing whether layer outputs are composite predictions or direct matches to individual memory predictions.
- **Explainability Application:** A gradient-based method was developed to generate feature importance maps. It computes the gradient of the `[CLS]` token's cross-entropy loss (for a class of interest) with respect to the attention weights assigned to image tokens in a specific block and head. These maps can be aggregated for global relevancy.
- **Comparison with Linear Probing:** The framework's ability to predict task relevance was compared against traditional 10-shot linear probing by performing perturbation tests (removing tokens based on their "importance" as identified by each method) and comparing resulting model accuracies (AUC).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, providing novel insights into the internal workings of Vision Transformers:

#### 4.1. Class Representations in Image Tokens Across the Hierarchy

- **Encoding of Class Information:** The study affirmatively demonstrated that image tokens, not just the `[CLS]` token, encode class-specific information. This information is recoverable by projecting their hidden states onto the class embedding space.
- **Evolution of Identifiability:** Class identifiability scores for both image and `[CLS]` tokens consistently increased across the ViT hierarchy, from early to deeper blocks, for all tested variants. Image tokens showed greater variability in scores, suggesting heterogeneous development of class representations across different image patches.
- **Resilience of Information:** Even in misclassified images, a high percentage contained at least one image token with a perfect class identifiability score for the correct class, implying that the underlying class information might be present but not effectively aggregated by the `[CLS]` token.

#### 4.2. Impact of Attention Mechanisms and Context on Class Representations

- **Critical Role of Intra-Image Attention:** Perturbing attention weights *between* image tokens dramatically reduced their class identifiability in the last block (from ~60% to ~0.12%), indicating that image tokens critically rely on integrating information from other image tokens to form class-specific representations.
- **Independence from `[CLS]` Token Attention:** Conversely, removing attention between image tokens and the `[CLS]` token did not affect image token class identifiability, suggesting image tokens do not need to extract information from `[CLS]` for their own class representation building.
- **Contextual Influence:** Class-labeled image tokens (regions depicting the object of interest) exhibited earlier and stronger class representations than context-labeled tokens. However, in deeper layers, context tokens also achieved high identifiability, suggesting they either extract class-relevant information from class tokens or that class prototypes incorporate contextual information.
- **Mutual Dependence:** Perturbation studies (removing either class- or context-labeled tokens) showed that both types of tokens contribute to and benefit from each other in building robust class representations. This implies that ViTs, similar to CNNs, may incorporate contextual information into their class prototypes, which can lead to robustness issues in out-of-distribution scenarios.

#### 4.3. Differential Contributions of Self-Attention and MLP Layers

- **Class Similarity Change Rate:** Self-attention layers showed a higher-than-chance class similarity change rate, especially for `[CLS]` tokens. MLP layers exhibited a peak in class similarity change in penultimate blocks, otherwise often performing below chance.
- **Influence on Residual Stream:** In early blocks, the residual stream's predictions were highly compositional. In later blocks, sub-modules' predictions were more directly incorporated, with SA peaking in the last block and MLP in penultimate blocks (before the final `[CLS]` aggregation).
- **Categorical Updates via Key-Value Memory Pairs (Class-Value Agreement):** Deeper SA and MLP layers demonstrated significantly higher class-value agreement scores (i.e., their value vectors strongly resembled class prototypes) compared to random models. MLP layers promoted *stronger* categorical updates than SA layers. Manual inspection revealed that MLP value vectors could cluster semantically similar and human-interpretable concepts (e.g., "racket, tennis ball, ping-pong ball").
- **Key-Value Agreement at Inference Time:** SA layers exhibited higher key-value agreement rates (activated keys matched value vectors promoting the correct class) in earlier blocks, while MLP layers peaked in penultimate blocks. SA updates, though weaker, were more broadly distributed across image tokens. Crucially, higher agreement rates in penultimate MLP layers (and deeper SA layers) were found for *correctly classified* samples, demonstrating a direct link between these mechanisms and model performance.
- **Compositionality:** Penultimate MLP layers showed lower compositionality, meaning their final predictions often directly matched the prediction of one of their most activated memories. SA layers maintained higher compositionality, indicating more integrative predictions.

#### 4.4. Explainability Application

- **Gradient-Based Importance Mapping:** The framework was successfully adapted to generate gradient-based feature importance maps. By computing the gradient of the `[CLS]` token's cross-entropy loss with respect to attention weights, it identifies image tokens crucial for forming a categorical representation.
- **Fine-Grained and Class-Generalizable:** The method provides block- and attention-head-specific visualizations, offering a hierarchical understanding of information building. It is also class-generalizable, allowing identification of features contributing to correct, incorrect, or alternative class predictions.
- **Validation:** Global relevancy maps derived by aggregating these gradients were found to be comparable in quality to those from established explainability methods (e.g., Chefer et al.), validated through perturbation studies.

#### 4.5. Advantages over Linear Probing

- **Efficiency:** The proposed framework is significantly more time-efficient, requiring only a one-forward pass over validation images, compared to linear probing which involves an additional forward pass over training images and fitting a separate linear classifier for every token and layer.
- **Task Relevance:** Critically, while linear probes could sometimes decode class information with higher accuracy from internal layers, their identified "importance" did *not* significantly predict the actual relevance of image tokens to the network's categorical decision (as measured by perturbation tests). In contrast, the proposed method directly quantifies alignment with the class embedding space, offering a more faithful representation of what the model *actually* uses for classification.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes significant contributions to the fields of mechanistic interpretability and explainability for deep learning models, particularly Vision Transformers. Its potential impact is substantial and far-reaching:

#### 5.1. Advancement in Mechanistic Interpretability

The most significant contribution is the introduction of an intuitive, powerful, and efficient framework for reverse-engineering ViTs by projecting their internal representations onto the class embedding space. This method moves beyond descriptive analyses to provide a *mechanistic understanding* of how ViTs construct categorical representations. It offers a novel lens for analyzing the "black box" of ViTs, providing human-interpretable insights that were previously elusive. By demonstrating the successful adaptation of NLP interpretability paradigms to computer vision, it paves the way for cross-domain advancements in understanding complex neural architectures.

#### 5.2. Deeper Understanding of ViT Functioning

The findings provide concrete, data-driven insights into the internal dynamics of ViTs:
- **Class Information Flow:** The demonstration that image tokens themselves contain class-specific information from early layers, and how this information is refined hierarchically, is fundamental.
- **Roles of Components:** The distinct roles elucidated for self-attention (weak, compositional, disseminated, earlier updates) and MLP layers (strong, direct, semantic clustering, later updates) through key-value memory mechanisms offer a nuanced understanding of their complementary contributions to class representation building. This could inform future architectural designs, guiding where to place emphasis for specific representational tasks.
- **Attention and Context:** The precise roles of intra-image attention and contextual information underscore critical aspects of how ViTs process visual data, revealing both strengths (context utilization) and potential weaknesses (reliance on context that could lead to OOD generalization issues).

#### 5.3. Enhanced Explainability Tool

The framework introduces a novel explainability method that provides fine-grained, block- and attention-head-specific insights into feature importance. This level of detail surpasses many existing global saliency maps and offers a powerful way to:
- **Debug Models:** Identify problematic feature reliance or misattribution of importance.
- **Improve Trust:** Provide clear, localized justifications for predictions, helping users understand why a model makes a specific classification.
- **Facilitate Scientific Discovery:** Uncover how different parts of an image contribute to abstract class concepts at various stages of processing. The class-generalizable nature allows for flexible analysis of various prediction scenarios, including incorrect classifications.

#### 5.4. Methodological Efficiency and Relevance

The study rigorously demonstrates the advantages of its proposed method over widely used linear probing techniques. By being more computationally efficient (requiring no additional model training) and, crucially, by providing a measure of interpretability that is more directly aligned with the model's actual task relevance, it offers a superior alternative for certain interpretability goals. This finding challenges the sole reliance on linear probing for assessing task-relevant representations.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The work explicitly opens doors for future research:
- **Model Editing and Improvement:** The mechanistic insights gained could be leveraged for targeted model editing or for designing more robust and efficient ViT architectures. For example, understanding how specific value vectors promote certain semantic concepts could lead to ways of injecting or modifying knowledge within the network.
- **Extension to Other Tasks:** While currently focused on image classification, the framework could potentially be adapted to other vision tasks (e.g., segmentation, object detection) by defining appropriate output embedding spaces, broadening its applicability.
- **Further Dissection of Keys:** The authors acknowledge that interpreting the "keys" in ViTs remains an open challenge, suggesting this as a fruitful area for future exploration to further complete the understanding of the key-value memory systems.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust, insightful, and practical framework for understanding the internal "thinking" of Vision Transformers. Its blend of mechanistic interpretability, validated explainability, and comparative methodological rigor positions it as a foundational contribution with significant potential to advance the responsible development and application of powerful AI models.
