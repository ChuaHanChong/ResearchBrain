# BehaviorWorldGen: Closing the Loop between Action Models and World Simulators via Controllable Behavior-Aware Structured World Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> BehaviorWorldGen closes the self-improvement loop between driving action models and world simulators. Its core BehaviorFlow is a meta-action-conditioned autoregressive traffic-flow model controlling agent lifecycles and frame-level behaviors, producing interaction-consistent multi-agent rollouts that a DiT world model or 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline renders into multi-view video plus LiDAR. Diagnosed policy failures become targeted training data, raising NAVSIM PDMS for VLA and end-to-end planners.

## Problem

- Existing world simulators are forward-dynamics models conditioned on ego action alone, so surrounding agents are replayed or emitted as by-products with futures fixed regardless of ego behavior.
- Because surrounding agents never truly react, interaction feedback is distorted: an unsafe plan can look perfectly safe inside the simulator, and refined policies inherit that distortion.
- Self-improvement loops are undirected, reproducing routine driving and rarely surfacing coordinated long-tail interactions, leaving generated data imbalanced and rare interactive scenarios hard to learn.

## Method

- **BehaviorFlow**: ==autoregressive traffic model== factorizing rollouts into lifecycle (ENTER/ALIVE/EXIT), frame-level meta-actions from a ten-verb vocabulary, and map-relative agent states, with injectable constraints regenerating unconstrained agents jointly.
- ==DiT==-based ==action-conditioned world model== jointly generating multi-view RGB and LiDAR rangemaps from HD-map, text, and reference latents, trained in four stages: LiDAR VAE, ==rectified-flow== bidirectional, ==DMD2 distillation==, causal ==diffusion-forcing== plus ==self-forcing==.
- Outer closed loop: score action-model trajectories, convert failing scenarios into meta-action prompts, roll out **BehaviorFlow**, render via world model or extended ==OmniRe== ==3D Gaussian Splatting==, then append paired interaction-aware trajectories to training data.

## Results

- The distilled causal world model reaches FID **4.36** and FVD **43.27** versus **6.72** and **61.29** for the bidirectional teacher, with roughly **70x** speedup (**0.3s** versus **20s** per frame).
- Augmenting **85K** NAVSIM samples with **16K** synthetic ones lifts DiffusionDrive PDMS **87.7** to **88.6** and ReCogDrive **86.5** to **87.3**, with comfort unchanged; ChainFlow-VLA reaches **93.3**.
- On low-scoring scenarios gains are far larger: in the **[0, 0.15)** bucket PDMS moves from **0.0** to **34.8**, and **[0.3, 0.45)** from **38.2** to **60.0**; LiDAR extrapolation removes floaters that image-only reconstruction leaves.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured trajectories make a good module interface: keeping behavior control upstream of rendering lets one traffic model serve both generative and reconstruction-based simulators unchanged.
> - Aggregate benchmark scores hide the effect of targeted data; sub-scoring by baseline difficulty exposes gains an order of magnitude larger than headline improvements.
> - Geometric supervision matters for extrapolation: image-only generative augmentation extends coverage but leaves occluded regions underconstrained until synthetic LiDAR anchors the primitives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the advancing field of autonomous driving, specifically focusing on the improvement of action models through self-improvement loops with learned world simulators. Current end-to-end autonomous driving systems increasingly rely on action models, such as end-to-end planners or vision-language-action (VLA) models, which map multimodal observations and navigation intent to ego behavior. To enhance these models beyond passive data collection, a growing trend involves a self-improvement loop where a world simulator generates future observations based on candidate ego actions, and the resulting data is used to refine the action model.

The paper identifies a bottleneck in this existing loop: current world simulators, often forward-dynamics models conditioned solely on ego actions, struggle to generate behaviorally plausible and interactive responses from surrounding agents. This limitation means surrounding agents in simulations typically replay historical behaviors or are generated as static by-products, rather than dynamically reacting to the ego vehicle. Consequently, the simulated interaction feedback can be distorted, potentially leading to the refinement of action models that are unsafe in real-world interactive scenarios. Furthermore, the undirected nature of current loops tends to reproduce routine driving situations, failing to adequately cover critical "long-tail" interactive scenarios (e.g., cut-ins, merges, yielding) that are essential for robust and safe autonomous driving.

BehaviorWorldGen addresses this gap by introducing controllable, behavior-aware structured world generation, thereby enhancing the realism and utility of simulated data for action model refinement. It aims to transform passive simulators into sources of targeted, interactive training data, particularly for difficult interactive scenarios that are underrepresented in naturalistic driving logs. The framework’s use of structured trajectories as an interface makes it compatible with diverse action models (e.g., VLA models, end-to-end planners) and world simulators (e.g., generative world models, 3D Gaussian Splatting).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of BehaviorWorldGen is to enhance the self-improvement loop for autonomous driving action models by addressing the limitations of current world simulators in generating behaviorally plausible and interactive responses from surrounding agents. This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming Unrealistic Interaction Feedback:** Current world simulators, by primarily conditioning on ego actions, often fail to model realistic reactions from surrounding agents. This leads to scenarios where a plan that would provoke a significant response in the real world might appear safe within the simulator. This distortion can result in action models being refined on unreliable feedback, potentially inheriting a propensity for unsafe or unrealistic behaviors in interactive situations. The goal is to provide interaction-consistent feedback where surrounding agents react realistically to the ego and to each other.

2. **Addressing Imbalanced Data Distribution and Long-Tail Scenarios:** The self-improvement loop, when undirected, tends to generate data predominantly reflecting routine driving conditions. Critical long-tail interactive scenarios (e.g., aggressive cut-ins, complex yielding situations, emergency braking scenarios) are rare in real-world data and are thus rarely synthesized by passive simulators. These scenarios, however, are crucial for training robust and safe autonomous driving policies. A key motivation is to enable the targeted generation of such underrepresented, safety-critical interactions to create a more balanced and informative training dataset.

3. **Introducing Controllable Behavior Generation:** To address the issues of unrealistic feedback and imbalanced data, the framework aims to introduce explicit, interpretable control over the behaviors of surrounding agents. This control, termed "meta-actions," allows for the programmatic injection of specific behaviors (e.g., lane change, turn, stationary) for individual agents at specific times. This capability is motivated by the need to orchestrate diverse and challenging interactions on demand, focusing on areas where the current action model exhibits weaknesses or failures.

4. **Ensuring Compatibility and Modularity:** The development of BehaviorWorldGen is also motivated by the desire for a flexible and modular framework. By using structured trajectories as the interface between its core components (action model, BehaviorFlow, world simulator), the system aims to be compatible with a variety of existing and future action models (e.g., VLA models, end-to-end planners) and world simulators (e.g., generative models, reconstruction-based systems like 3D Gaussian Splatting). This modularity reduces development friction and allows different components to be improved independently.

In summary, BehaviorWorldGen seeks to close a critical loop in autonomous driving development by empowering simulators to generate more realistic, diverse, and controllable multi-agent interactions. This capability is intended to yield more robust, safer, and generalizable action models by providing targeted training data that specifically addresses the shortcomings of current approaches in interactive and long-tail scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BehaviorWorldGen operates as a closed-loop data generation process that integrates an action model, a BehaviorFlow traffic-flow model, and a world simulator. The framework’s central innovation lies in BehaviorFlow, which acts as an outer loop component, injecting controllable, behavior-aware elements into the simulation.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The self-improvement loop proceeds as follows:
1. **Action Model Prediction:** An action model (π) predicts an initial ego trajectory ($\hat{Y}_{act}$) given an observation (O).
2. **Scenario Diagnosis:** This predicted trajectory is evaluated against scenario-level criteria to identify failure cases (e.g., collisions, unsafe maneuvers).
3. **Meta-Action Prompt Generation:** For identified failure cases, target meta-actions are derived and provided as prompts to BehaviorFlow.
4. **BehaviorFlow Rollout Generation:** Conditioned on these injected meta-actions, BehaviorFlow jointly evolves the ego vehicle and surrounding agents into an "interaction-consistent rollout." This rollout realizes the specified behaviors while allowing unconstrained agents to react to the ego and one another.
5. **World Simulator Rendering:** The structured rollout from BehaviorFlow is rasterized into a multi-view layout, which the world simulator then renders into realistic multi-view RGB videos and synchronized LiDAR sequences.
6. **Data Augmentation and Refinement:** These generated observations are paired with corrected, interaction-aware trajectories and appended to the action model’s training data for subsequent refinement (via reinforcement learning or supervised fine-tuning).

The framework uses structured trajectories as the interface, allowing compatibility with various action models upstream and world simulators downstream.

#### 3.2. World Simulator

The world simulator consumes behavior-aware rollouts and generates realistic observations. Two instantiations are explored:

- **4.2.1. Action-Conditioned World Model:** This model generates future multi-view RGB videos and LiDAR rangemaps from an HD-map layout, offering broad controllability and the ability to imagine novel scenes.
 - **Architecture:** It is based on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT). Multi-view images, LiDAR rangemaps, HD maps, and text descriptions are encoded into latents. HD-map latents are injected into RGB latents. RGB and LiDAR latents are concatenated and processed by cascaded DiT blocks, employing full self-attention (for consistency and alignment) and cross-attention with text latents (for semantic alignment). VAE decoders convert latents back to pixel space.
 - **Four-Stage Training Scheme:**
 1. **LiDAR VAE Training:** A separate VAE is trained for LiDAR rangemaps, complementing a pretrained video VAE.
 2. **Bidirectional Model Training:** A bidirectional DiT-based model is trained using rectified flow for joint RGB video and LiDAR rangemap generation, conditioned on HD maps and text.
 3. **Bidirectional Model Few-step Distillation:** DMD2 is used to distill the multi-step bidirectional model into a few-step student model, improving inference efficiency.
 4. **Causal Model Training:** The distilled model is further trained for real-time autoregressive inference and long-horizon generation using diffusion forcing (with causal masks) and self-forcing (distilling into a few-step causal model via DMD).

- **4.2.2. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** This approach reconstructs observed scenes for high-fidelity novel-view rendering, with extensions for scene extrapolation using BehaviorFlow and the action-conditioned world model.
 - **Scene Reconstruction:** Builds upon OmniRe, using separate Gaussian representations for static environments and dynamic actors. Adaptations include:
 - **RoGS-based Road Modeling:** Dense 2D Gaussian primitives on a bird's-eye-view grid for stable road geometry under lateral viewpoint shifts.
 - **Object-level Reconstruction and Refinement:** Each vehicle is reconstructed as an independent object-centric 3D Gaussian model from high-quality observations, followed by a three-stage coarse-to-fine refinement.
 - **Rear-View Geometry Completion with MVSA:** A Multi-View Stereo-based Visual Depth Completion (MVSA) strategy is used to provide dense geometric initialization and supervision for rear-view regions where LiDAR data might be sparse.
 - **Scene Extrapolation:** BehaviorFlow predicts future traffic evolution. The action-conditioned world model synthesizes multimodal observations (camera, LiDAR) along these extrapolated trajectories. These generated observations are then appended to the original log for joint 3DGS reconstruction, extending the scene's spatial coverage.

#### 3.3. Action Model

BehaviorWorldGen is validated with two types of action models:

- **4.3.1. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Model:**
 - **ChainFlow-VLA:** Decomposes planning into autoregressive proposal generation (producing multimodal trajectory proposals) and VLM-guided residual refinement (using semantic representations from a VLM to guide local refinement of proposals via a diffusion model).
 - **ReCogDrive:** An existing VLA model that adapts a pretrained VLM for driving scene understanding and trajectory prediction, employing a diffusion-based planner to translate VLM latent cognitive representations into smooth trajectories.

- **4.3.2. End-to-End Planner:**
 - **DiffusionDrive:** Maps raw sensor observations directly to a distribution over future ego trajectories, formulating planning as conditional trajectory generation using a truncated diffusion policy. It uses prior trajectory anchors to shorten the denoising process and a cascaded diffusion decoder for refinement.

#### 3.4. BehaviorFlow

This core module generates structured traffic, connecting the action model and world simulator.
- **Modeling:** Models the traffic scene as an autoregressive sequence over agent lifecycles, frame-level meta-actions, and agent physical states, conditioned on static scene context (HD map, traffic lights).
- **Identity-aware Lifecycle Generation:** Defines lifecycle variables (ENTER, ALIVE, EXIT) for each agent, preserving temporal identity while allowing the traffic composition to evolve.
- **Meta-action-Conditioned Agent Generation:** Attaches frame-level meta-actions (e.g., LaneChangeLeft, KeepStraight) to active agents. These meta-actions are generated jointly, and then physical states (category, dimensions, map-segment index, relative pose/heading/velocity) are generated, allowing unconstrained agents to respond to controlled ones.
- **Training Objective:** Supervised loss components for lifecycle variables, meta-actions, and agent states, with ground-truth teacher-forcing during training.
- **Controllable Traffic-Flow Editing:** At inference, lifecycle and meta-action variables can be specified for arbitrary agent-timestep pairs, allowing targeted interventions to produce diverse, interaction-consistent rollouts that transform action-model failures into specific training examples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of BehaviorWorldGen across world generation, scene extrapolation, and policy refinement tasks.

#### 4.1. World Simulator Results

- **5.1.1. Action-Conditioned World Model:**
 - **Generation Quality and Speed:** The causal model (Stage 4) achieved better FID (4.36 vs. 6.72) and FVD (43.27 vs. 61.29) scores compared to the bidirectional model (Stage 2) for short-time video generation (93 frames, 10 FPS), with a nearly 70x acceleration (0.3s/frame vs. 20s/frame). Generated videos maintained temporal coherence and followed HD-map conditions.
 - **Long-Time Video Generation:** The causal model maintained stable visual quality and plausible scene evolution over 30-second autoregressive rollouts, supporting closed-loop simulation.
 - **LiDAR Generation:** The model successfully generated temporally consistent LiDAR point clouds with clear structures for backgrounds and vehicles, suitable for LiDAR-dependent applications.
 - **Controllable Scene-Level Editing:**
 - **Animal Scene Generation:** Successfully inserted animals (dog, sheep, duck, bear) that maintained consistent appearances and plausible motion without disrupting scene coherence.
 - **Fog Density Control:** Demonstrated smooth, continuous control over fog density, progressively decreasing visibility while maintaining scene content.
 - **Weather Style Transfer:** Generated diverse weather conditions (cloudy, night, rainy, foggy, snowy) for the same scene, preserving underlying road geometry and object identities.
 - **Trajectory Editing:** Showed the ability to modify input trajectories (lane change, overtaking, straight-to-turn, turn-to-straight) and generate plausible counterfactual observations consistent with the specified maneuvers and scene context.

- **5.1.2. 3DGS Scene Extrapolation:**
 - **Extended Spatial Coverage:** Comparing "Original Reconstruction," "Image Extrapolation Reconstruction," and "Image + LiDAR Extrapolation Reconstruction," the extrapolation-augmented variants maintained substantially higher visual quality in regions beyond the original observed log.
 - **Geometric Reliability:** While image-only extrapolation improved appearance, adding generated LiDAR observations provided direct geometric constraints, leading to more stable scene geometry, suppressed ground-level floaters, and improved visibility of dynamic objects. This indicated LiDAR's crucial role in maintaining geometric reliability in extrapolated regions.

#### 4.2. Action Model Results

Evaluation on the NAVSIM benchmark used 85K real samples augmented with 16K synthetic samples generated by BehaviorWorldGen.

- **5.2.1. Vision-Language-Action Model:**
 - **ChainFlow-VLA:** Achieved the highest PDMS (93.3) among compared VLA approaches, showing improvements in safety-related metrics (NC, DAC, TTC) and fewer catastrophic zero-score outcomes.
 - **ReCogDrive:** Saw an absolute PDMS improvement of 0.8 points (to 87.3). Significant gains were noted in TTC (0.7 points), DAC (0.6 points), and EP (0.6 points), with NC also improving. Comfort score remained unchanged. This suggests the benefits generalize across VLA architectures.

- **5.2.2. End-to-End Planner (DiffusionDrive):**
 - **Overall Performance:** Achieved an absolute PDMS improvement of 0.9 points (to 88.6) over the baseline (87.7). Consistent gains were observed across all component metrics, with EP showing the largest improvement (0.7 points), followed by DAC and TTC (0.6 points each). NC also improved, and comfort remained unchanged.
 - **Performance on Low-Score Scenarios:** BehaviorWorldGen significantly improved planning performance on scenarios where the original DiffusionDrive performed poorly (normalized PDMS below 0.45). The most pronounced recovery was in the [0, 0.15) interval, where the baseline nearly collapsed to zero PDMS, but the augmented model showed meaningful recovery (PDMS 34.8). Gains in DAC and EP were strong in more difficult intervals, while TTC saw improvement in less severe low-score scenarios. This demonstrates concentrated benefit on difficult failure cases.

#### 4.3. BehaviorFlow Results

Qualitative evaluation demonstrated BehaviorFlow's controllability and interaction consistency.
- **Straight-Driving Scenario:** Generated distinct following and emergency-avoidance behaviors (e.g., ego changing lane or avoiding a suddenly stopped vehicle after a cut-in) from the same initial scene by controlling the front-left vehicle's meta-actions.
- **Intersection Scenario:** Generated two variants with opposite yielding orders between a left-turning ego vehicle and an oncoming vehicle, demonstrating successful orchestration of different interaction outcomes while avoiding collisions.
These results indicate that BehaviorFlow can produce diverse and interaction-consistent rollouts, providing controllable training scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BehaviorWorldGen presents a significant step towards improving the robustness and safety of autonomous driving systems by addressing critical limitations in current simulation-based policy refinement loops. Its core contribution, BehaviorFlow, offers a mechanism for controllable, behavior-aware structured world generation, which has several key impacts:

1. **Enhanced Realism in Interactive Simulation:** By enabling surrounding agents to react plausibly to the ego vehicle and to each other through meta-action-conditioned control, BehaviorWorldGen provides more realistic and interaction-consistent feedback. This is crucial for training action models that can operate safely and effectively in complex, dynamic traffic environments, where anticipating and responding to other agents' behaviors is paramount.

2. **Targeted Learning for Long-Tail Scenarios:** The ability to explicitly orchestrate diverse and challenging interactive scenarios (e.g., cut-ins, yielding, emergency maneuvers) on demand allows for focused data generation. This helps overcome the inherent sparsity of safety-critical "long-tail" events in real-world driving logs, enabling action models to learn from a more balanced and informative dataset. This targeted approach is expected to lead to policies that are more robust to rare but critical situations.

3. **Improved Action Model Performance:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate that training with behavior-aware data generated by BehaviorWorldGen leads to tangible improvements in diverse action models (VLA and end-to-end planners). The gains are particularly notable in safety-related metrics (e.g., reduced collisions, improved drivable area compliance, safer time-to-collision) and for scenarios where baseline models previously struggled. This direct impact on policy performance underscores the practical utility of the framework.

4. **Extending Simulation Capabilities:** The integration of BehaviorFlow with both generative action-conditioned world models and reconstruction-based 3D Gaussian Splatting simulators broadens the scope and reliability of autonomous driving simulation. Specifically, the multimodal image and LiDAR extrapolation for 3DGS-based simulators extends their operational range beyond originally observed boundaries while maintaining geometric accuracy, which is vital for closed-loop evaluation.

5. **Modularity and Compatibility:** The structured trajectory interface ensures that BehaviorWorldGen can be seamlessly integrated with a wide range of existing and future action models and world simulators. This modular design promotes broader adoption and allows for independent advancements in each component, fostering further research and development in the autonomous driving ecosystem.

In conclusion, BehaviorWorldGen addresses a fundamental challenge in autonomous driving development by closing the loop between action models and world simulators with controllable, behavior-aware interaction generation. Its potential impact lies in accelerating the development of safer, more robust, and more generally capable autonomous driving systems by providing a scalable and targeted method for generating high-quality training data for interactive scenarios.
