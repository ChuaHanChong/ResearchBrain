# Learning a High-quality Robotic Wiping Policy Using Systematic Reward Analysis and Visual-Language Model Based Curriculum

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Institute of Technology and ETRI developed a method for learning high-quality robotic wiping policies by combining a theoretically grounded bounded reward formulation with an automated visual-language model (VLM) based curriculum. Their approach achieved a 98% navigation success rate and an Integral Absolute Error (IAE) of 243 for force control, consistently outperforming baseline methods on diverse surfaces.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning for quality-critical robotic tasks, like surface wiping, struggles with designing effective reward functions that balance quality and task completion.
- Naive reward formulations often lead to suboptimal behaviors, such as fast but low-quality task completion or perpetual high-quality execution without ever reaching the goal (the 'forever' strategy).
- Manual reward engineering and hyperparameter tuning by human experts are laborious and hinder the scalability and autonomy of RL for real-world applications.

## Method

- Formal analysis of quality-critical ==Markov Decision Processes (MDPs)== revealed the inherent infeasibility of naive reward formulations due to the 'forever' strategy.
- A novel 'bounded reward' design was introduced, incorporating concentric circular checkpoint regions between waypoints to limit cumulative quality rewards and ensure the feasibility of optimal behavior.
- An automated visual-language model (VLM) based curriculum system was developed to monitor training progress, provide semantic feedback from visual replays, and intelligently adjust reward hyperparameters, mimicking human reward engineering.

## Results

- The `bounded-llm-curr` method achieved a **98%** navigation success rate and an average Integral Absolute Error (IAE) of 243 for normal force control, demonstrating high-quality wiping policies.
- This performance significantly surpassed the naive baseline (**58%** success rate) and the `bounded-reward` method without the VLM curriculum (**92%** success rate), which also had higher IAEs.
- The VLM-based curriculum adaptively tuned reward weights during training, improving both task completion and force control and showing robustness to poorly initialized reward parameters by recovering from near-zero success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive reward functions for quality-critical robotic tasks are fundamentally infeasible, as agents can learn to endlessly accrue quality rewards without ever completing the task.
> - Bounding positive quality rewards within specific task segments effectively prevents pathological 'forever' behaviors and makes the learning problem feasible for balancing quality and completion.
> - Visual-language models can automate reward engineering by interpreting visual policy replays to diagnose failure modes and guide the intelligent tuning of reward parameters, reducing human intervention.
