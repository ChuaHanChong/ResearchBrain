# Learning a High-quality Robotic Wiping Policy Using Systematic Reward Analysis and Visual-Language Model Based Curriculum

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Institute of Technology and ETRI developed a method for learning high-quality robotic wiping policies by combining a theoretically grounded bounded reward formulation with an automated visual-language model (VLM) based curriculum. Their approach achieved a 98% navigation success rate and an Integral Absolute Error (IAE) of 243 for force control, consistently outperforming baseline methods on diverse surfaces.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning for quality-critical robotic tasks, like surface wiping, struggles with designing effective reward functions that balance quality and task completion.
- Naive reward formulations often lead to suboptimal behaviors, such as fast but low-quality task completion or perpetual high-quality execution without ever reaching the goal (the 'forever' strategy).
- Manual reward engineering and hyperparameter tuning by human experts are laborious and hinder the scalability and autonomy of RL for real-world applications.

## Method

- Formal analysis of quality-critical ==Markov Decision Processes (MDPs)== revealed the inherent infeasibility of naive reward formulations due to the 'forever' strategy.
- A novel 'bounded reward' design was introduced, incorporating concentric circular checkpoint regions between waypoints to limit cumulative quality rewards and ensure the feasibility of optimal behavior.
- An automated visual-language model (VLM) based curriculum system was developed to monitor training progress, provide semantic feedback from visual replays, and intelligently adjust reward hyperparameters, mimicking human reward engineering.

## Results

- The `bounded-llm-curr` method achieved a **98%** navigation success rate and an average Integral Absolute Error (IAE) of 243 for normal force control, demonstrating high-quality wiping policies.
- This performance significantly surpassed the naive baseline (**58%** success rate) and the `bounded-reward` method without the VLM curriculum (**92%** success rate), which also had higher IAEs.
- The VLM-based curriculum adaptively tuned reward weights during training, improving both task completion and force control and showing robustness to poorly initialized reward parameters by recovering from near-zero success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive reward functions for quality-critical robotic tasks are fundamentally infeasible, as agents can learn to endlessly accrue quality rewards without ever completing the task.
> - Bounding positive quality rewards within specific task segments effectively prevents pathological 'forever' behaviors and makes the learning problem feasible for balancing quality and completion.
> - Visual-language models can automate reward engineering by interpreting visual policy replays to diagnose failure modes and guide the intelligent tuning of reward parameters, reducing human intervention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses critical challenges at the intersection of robotic manipulation, deep reinforcement learning (RL), and the burgeoning field of visual-language models (VLMs) in robotics.

**Robotic Surface Wiping:** Traditionally, robotic surface wiping, an important task across industries from manufacturing to healthcare, has been tackled using classical model-based approaches such as operational space control and impedance control. While effective on simple, flat surfaces, these methods struggle with generalization to diverse surfaces exhibiting varying curvatures and friction parameters, due to the difficulty in designing robust controllers for uncertain environments. This paper positions itself by advocating for learning-based algorithms, specifically deep RL, which can leverage large amounts of simulation data to achieve robust performance and adapt to complex environmental variables without explicit models. The authors distinguish their work from previous learning-based approaches by focusing on integrating sophisticated force control within navigational tasks, rather than just tangential angle estimation or crumb collection on fixed surfaces, and by achieving adaptive force control through learning across varied training environments.

**Deep Reinforcement Learning for Robotics Manipulation:** Deep RL has become a pivotal paradigm for robotic tasks, often complemented by learning from demonstration (LfD). The paper acknowledges RL's role in autonomously enhancing simulated demonstrations and refining policies for adaptation. However, a key issue in applying RL to real-world robotic tasks, especially "quality-critical" ones, is the inherent difficulty in designing effective reward functions. Naive reward formulations often lead to suboptimal behaviors, where agents either prioritize fast task completion at the expense of quality or perpetually pursue quality without ever completing the task. This problem highlights the sensitivity of RL training to hyperparameter tuning, which typically requires extensive, labor-intensive manual trial-and-error by human experts. The paper directly tackles this foundational problem in RL for quality-critical tasks.

**Language to Reward and Visual-Language Models (VLMs):** The paper situates its VLM-based curriculum within the "Language to RL Reward" initiative, a nascent but rapidly evolving area aiming to automate the generation and tuning of reward functions using large language models (LLMs). It draws parallels with recent works like EUREKA, which uses LLMs for evolutionary optimization of reward code, and TEXT2REWARD, which incorporates human feedback. The authors differentiate their approach by starting with a structured RL reward formula (to avoid known undesirable behaviors) and primarily updating reward *weights* during training, rather than evolving the entire reward function or retraining from scratch. Furthermore, their novel use of a separate VLM agent to provide visual policy replay feedback, mimicking human intuition and monitoring, represents a significant advancement in making automated reward engineering more robust and insightful, especially for diagnosing visual failure modes. This integration of VLMs allows the system to semantically understand failure reasons, reducing the need for extensive logging and human-intensive monitoring.

In summary, this work contributes to bridging the gap between the theoretical promise of deep RL and its practical application in complex, quality-critical robotic tasks by addressing the reward engineering bottleneck through systematic analysis and intelligent automation powered by VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the significant challenges faced when applying deep reinforcement learning (RL) to "quality-critical" robotic manipulation tasks, such as surface wiping. These tasks demand a delicate balance between achieving a high standard of quality in execution (e.g., maintaining consistent contact force and pose) and completing the task efficiently (e.g., wiping all designated waypoints). The authors observed that traditional, naive reward formulations in RL often fail to achieve this balance, leading to hyperparameter-sensitive learning processes. This sensitivity results in two undesirable outcomes:
1. **Degrading Work Quality:** The agent might learn to complete the task quickly but with poor quality (e.g., by skipping contact or applying incorrect force).
2. **Avoidance of Task Completion:** The agent might get stuck in a loop of high-quality, but perpetual, execution without ever reaching the terminal goal.

Such issues necessitate laborious, manual reward engineering and hyperparameter tuning by human experts, hindering the scalability and autonomy of RL for real-world applications.

Given this context, the paper sets out to achieve several key objectives:

1. **Formally Analyze Convergence:** To rigorously demonstrate the inherent infeasibility and poor convergence properties of naive reward formulations for quality-critical robotic wiping tasks. This involves understanding why agents can get trapped in suboptimal local optima or fail to converge to desired behaviors.
2. **Propose a Feasible Reward Formulation:** To design and theoretically ground a new "bounded reward" formulation that inherently guides the agent towards optimal behaviors by making the problem feasible. This formulation aims to prevent the aforementioned pathologies (like perpetual wiping) and ensure that desired actions lead to maximal rewards.
3. **Develop an Automated Curriculum for Reward Tuning:** To introduce a novel, automated "visual-language model (VLM)-based curriculum" system. This system is designed to actively monitor the RL training progress, leverage semantic understanding (both textual and visual), and intelligently suggest adjustments to reward hyperparameters, thereby simulating and automating the role of a human reward engineer.
4. **Demonstrate Comprehensive Effectiveness:** To empirically validate that the combination of the proposed bounded reward design and the VLM-based curriculum can effectively learn high-quality robotic wiping policies across diverse environments, encompassing varying surface curvatures, friction parameters, and waypoint configurations, where baseline methods fail.

Ultimately, the goal is to make deep RL a more robust, efficient, and autonomous tool for learning complex, quality-critical robotic manipulation skills, significantly reducing the human effort currently required for reward engineering.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes a two-pronged approach to tackle the challenges of learning high-quality robotic wiping policies: a systematic reward analysis leading to a bounded reward formulation, and a VLM-based curriculum for automated hyperparameter tuning.

#### 3.1. Initial Problem Formulation: Quality-critical MDP

The authors frame robotic wiping as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP).
- **State ($s$):** Internal state of a physics-based simulation.
- **Observation ($o$):** A 46-dimensional vector including waypoint information, joint positions/velocities (encoded as sine/cosine), end-effector (EE) position/orientation, and force/torque sensor values.
- **Action ($a$):** A 6-dimensional pose control command directly adjusting the EE's position and orientation, indirectly controlling forces.
- **Naive Reward Function ($r$):** Initially defined as a weighted sum of five terms:
 - $r_{col}$: Negative scalar penalty for collisions, terminating the episode.
 - $r_{con}$: Positive binary reward for contact with the table.
 - $r_{force}$: Gaussian-shaped reward centered around a target normal force (e.g., 60N), weighted by an alignment flag to ensure movement towards the waypoint.
 - $r_{way}$: Positive sparse episodic reward for wiping each waypoint, with an extra reward for the final waypoint.
 - $r_{ac}$: Penalty for excessive end-effector accelerations.
The weights for these terms are critical and determine the balance between quality (contact, force, low acceleration) and completion (waypoints).

#### 3.2. Convergence Analysis of Quality-critical MDP and its Infeasibility

The authors formally analyze the convergence behavior of this naive reward formulation. They identify three potential strategies an agent might learn:
1. **Optimal:** Achieves best quality wiping and terminates in minimum required time.
2. **Lazy:** Suboptimal, finishes the episode as early as possible without maintaining wiping quality (e.g., high accelerations, no constant contact).
3. **Forever:** Suboptimal, continuously receives quality rewards without ever completing the task, leading to infinite episodic returns in undiscounted settings, or a very high return in discounted settings if the quality reward is sufficiently large.
Through mathematical analysis (Equations 3, 4, and 5), they demonstrate that finding a feasible range for the terminal reward ($W_T$) relative to the maximum quality reward ($W_q^{max}$) is extremely challenging, particularly for the lower bound. This makes the problem prone to the "forever" strategy, where the agent maximizes its cumulative quality reward by never finishing the task. This analysis highlights the fundamental infeasibility of the naive formulation for reliably learning quality-critical tasks.

#### 3.3. Bounded Reward Design for Improved Feasibility

To address the "forever" problem and improve feasibility, the authors propose a novel **bounded reward design** incorporating concentric circular checkpoint regions between waypoints (Fig. 2).
- Instead of granting $r_{con}$ and $r_{force}$ at every step, these positive quality rewards are now controlled by an indicator function, $I_{check}$, meaning they are only awarded *once per checkpoint region*.
- After all checkpoint regions between two waypoints are visited, further quality rewards for contact and force are withheld until the next waypoint, effectively introducing a negative quality reward (only acceleration penalties apply).
This mechanism directly bounds the cumulative quality reward an agent can obtain by lingering indefinitely. By limiting the number of times an agent can accrue positive quality rewards within a segment, the "forever" strategy becomes less attractive, making the optimal strategy (high quality + completion) the most rewarding option. The new formulation ensures that the lower bound for $W_T$ becomes very low or negative, creating a wide and stable feasible range for balancing $W_T$ and $W_q^{max}$.

#### 3.4. Visual-Language Model (VLM) Based Curriculum

Even with the bounded reward, the learning process remains hyperparameter-sensitive. To further refine learning and reduce human effort, a **VLM-based curriculum learning system** is introduced. This system automates the monitoring of training metrics and adjusts reward weights, mimicking a human expert's tuning process.
- **Cycle:** The curriculum module is invoked every $K$ training steps after an initial $M$ steps.
- **Step 1: Inspection:** The system collects performance metrics over $N$ rollout episodes, including navigation success rates, landing pressure profiles, and navigation pressure statistics. It compares current performance with historical data and uses pre-defined predicates (e.g., improvement in force variance without significant drop in navigation success) to decide if reward tuning is necessary.
- **Step 2: Update:** This step involves two large model agents:
 - **LLM Agent (gpt-4):** Receives current and historical metrics. It can request additional information based on training progress. For low completion rates, it requests visual feedback. For force-related issues, it requests detailed force percentiles. The LLM then analyzes the logs, identifies focus-learning areas, and outputs Python code to update the reward parameters (weights).
 - **VLM Agent (gpt-4-vision-preview):** Provides semantic understanding of failure reasons from visual policy replays (e.g., "no contact," "close to endpoint without finishing wiping"). This visual feedback is crucial for diagnosing complex failure modes that are difficult to infer from numerical logs alone.
- **Implementation Details:** The module starts after 300k steps, evaluates 50 episodes every 100k steps. Initial reward weights are set ($W_T=1000, W_{max}^q=29$) but are adjusted by the LLM. Safeguards (e.g., clipping reward weights) are optionally applied.

#### 3.5. Experiment Setup

Experiments are conducted in a MuJoCo-based simulation environment using a 7-DoF Panda robot and a robosuite pose controller. To promote Sim2Real transfer and robust policy learning, **domain randomization** is applied across various parameters at the start of each episode:
- **Curvature:** Six distinct tabletop curvatures (one flat, five curved).
- **Textures:** Sliding, torsional, and rolling frictions sampled from Gaussian distributions.
- **Waypoints:** Randomized 2-point navigation tasks.
Three methods are compared:
1. **non-bounded-reward (Baseline):** The naive reward formulation without checkpoint regions.
2. **bounded-reward:** Incorporates the checkpoint regions but uses fixed reward scales from the baseline.
3. **bounded-llm-curr (Ours):** Extends `bounded-reward` with the VLM-based curriculum for dynamic reward weight adjustment.

This comprehensive methodology combines theoretical rigor in reward design with an innovative, automated curriculum learning system, aiming to solve the long-standing problem of reward engineering in quality-critical reinforcement learning tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates the significant effectiveness of the proposed bounded reward design and the VLM-based curriculum in learning high-quality robotic wiping policies.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance of the Proposed Method (bounded-llm-curr)

The `bounded-llm-curr` method successfully trains a wiping policy capable of navigating between waypoints on surfaces with diverse curvatures and friction properties, while consistently maintaining the target normal force of 60N. After 800,000 training steps, the policy achieved:
- An impressive **98% navigation success rate**, significantly outperforming baselines.
- An average **Integral Absolute Error (IAE) of 243** for navigational forces, indicating precise force control.
- An average of **25 completion steps**, demonstrating efficient task execution.
Visualizations (Fig. 1) confirm that the learned trajectories maintain contact and apply the desired pressure.

#### 4.2. Comparative Analysis of Methods (Table I and Fig. 4)

The comparison across the three methods highlights the contributions of each component:

- **non-bounded-reward (Baseline):** This method struggled significantly, achieving only a **58% navigation completion rate**. The primary reason identified was the "persistent wiping behavior" (the "forever" strategy discussed in Section III-B) in four out of five random seeds, where agents optimized for continuous quality rewards without completing the task. While its force control quality (IAE=267) was decent, it came at the cost of task completion. It also had a higher average of 38 completion steps.

- **bounded-reward:** The introduction of the bounded reward design, incorporating checkpoint regions, dramatically improved the situation. It boosted the navigation success rate to **92%**, effectively preventing the "persistent wiping" pathology. The average completion steps decreased to 29. However, simply bounding the reward without active tuning led to a slight increase in IAE to 333, suggesting that while task completion improved, the nuance of force control could be compromised without further guidance. This confirms the theoretical analysis that bounding makes the problem feasible but doesn't guarantee optimal quality.

- **bounded-llm-curr (Ours):** This method, combining the bounded reward with the VLM-based curriculum, achieved the best overall performance. It further increased the navigation success rate to **98%** and refined task efficiency, reducing average completion steps to **25**. Crucially, it also optimized force control, yielding the lowest IAE of **243**, demonstrating that it could achieve high-quality force control without sacrificing completion rates. This outcome confirms that the VLM-based curriculum effectively tunes reward weights to balance both quality and speed objectives.

#### 4.3. Benefits and Updates from VLM-Based Curriculum

- **Efficient Fine-tuning with Reasoning (Fig. 5):** The curriculum system demonstrated intelligent adaptation based on training progress:
 - **Early Training (Scenario 1):** When the navigation completion rate was low, the LLM agent increased navigation rewards. This initially boosted completion at the expense of slightly increased landing forces, allowing the RL agent to first learn task completion before refining force control.
 - **Later Training (Scenario 2):** As training progressed, and force control became the bottleneck (especially for sparse events like landing force), the LLM adjusted penalties for landing forces, leading to significant reductions without negatively impacting other metrics.
 - **Robustness to Poor Initialization:** Even when initialized with highly imbalanced reward weights (navigation reward only 10% of wiping quality), `bounded-llm-curr` successfully corrected this during early exploration, leading to a 40% success rate by 500k steps, whereas `bounded-reward` remained near zero. This highlights the curriculum's ability to recover from suboptimal initial conditions.

- **Visual Monitoring over Failed Behaviors (Fig. 6):** The VLM component provided crucial semantic feedback on failure reasons. For instance, in one case, it identified "failure occurred early, before contact with the table," enabling the LLM to infer the need for increased intermediate rewards for reaching the first waypoint. This capability significantly reduces the need for human domain expertise, iterative monitoring, and extensive logging to diagnose and address complex failure modes, enhancing the autonomy and efficiency of the reward engineering process.

In summary, the results clearly validate that the systematic reward analysis leading to a bounded formulation makes the quality-critical wiping problem feasible, and the VLM-based curriculum further refines the learning process by automating the intricate balancing act of quality and completion objectives, leading to superior and more robust robotic policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents significant contributions to the fields of robotics and reinforcement learning, with broad potential impact on the development of autonomous systems for quality-critical tasks.

#### 5.1. Significance of Contributions

1. **Formal Analysis of Quality-Critical MDPs:** The paper's formal analysis demonstrating the inherent infeasibility of naive reward formulations for quality-critical tasks is a fundamental contribution. By identifying and mathematically proving the existence of pathological behaviors (like "perpetual wiping"), it highlights a core challenge in applying RL to real-world problems with multiple, potentially conflicting, objectives. This analysis provides a theoretical foundation for understanding why traditional reward engineering often fails.
2. **Theoretically Grounded Bounded Reward Design:** The proposed bounded reward function, incorporating checkpoint regions, offers a theoretically sound solution to the identified infeasibility. By limiting the cumulative positive quality reward for lingering, it inherently guides the agent towards a strategy that balances quality with timely task completion. This design principle is generalizable and could prevent similar "forever" pathologies in other complex RL tasks.
3. **Novel VLM-Based Curriculum for Automated Reward Tuning:** This is a pioneering contribution in automated reward engineering. By leveraging the semantic understanding capabilities of Visual-Language Models (VLMs), the system automates a highly labor-intensive and expert-dependent process—the iterative tuning of reward weights. The ability to monitor progress, reason about learning deficiencies, and even provide visual diagnoses of failure modes (mimicking human intuition) represents a significant step towards more autonomous and robust RL training.
4. **Demonstrated Effectiveness on a Complex Robotic Task:** The empirical validation on robotic wiping across varied surfaces (curvatures, frictions) and waypoints is crucial. It shows that the combined framework not only theoretically addresses the problem but also practically achieves superior performance in terms of high completion rates, precise force control, and efficient task execution, where baseline methods fail or underperform.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancing Robotic Autonomy for Quality-Critical Tasks:** The work provides a robust framework for developing autonomous robotic skills that require both precision and efficiency. This extends beyond wiping to a vast array of industrial applications such as polishing, grinding, assembly, inspection, and delicate material handling, where maintaining specific contact forces or trajectories is critical. By reducing the burden of manual reward engineering, this research can accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots in manufacturing, healthcare, and service industries.
- **Improving the Generalizability and Robustness of RL:** The insights into quality-critical MDPs and the bounded reward design can inform future RL algorithm and reward function design, making RL more resilient to suboptimal behaviors and hyperparameter sensitivity. This could lead to more stable and reproducible RL training across diverse domains.
- **Enhancing Human-Robot Collaboration through Automated Learning:** The VLM-based curriculum exemplifies a new paradigm in automated machine learning, where advanced AI models (LLMs and VLMs) act as intelligent tutors or engineers, overseeing and adapting the learning process of other AI agents (RL policies). This form of meta-learning or AI-assisted AI development can significantly reduce the need for constant human oversight, freeing researchers to focus on higher-level problems.
- **Broader Impact on AI Research:** The innovative use of VLMs for interpreting visual policy replays and providing semantic feedback on failures opens new avenues for integrating multimodal AI into robot learning. This could pave the way for more intuitive debugging, faster problem identification, and more human-understandable explanations of robot behavior.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge several limitations and outline promising directions for future research:
1. **VLM Generalizability:** Enhancing the VLM system's applicability and understanding in more complex, open-ended scenarios beyond robotic wiping.
2. **Hardware Deployment:** Validating the learned policies on physical robot hardware to confirm real-world performance and address potential Sim2Real gaps.
3. **Autonomous Waypoint Generation:** Eliminating the current assumption of pre-defined waypoints by enabling the system to autonomously generate waypoints from high-level observations, moving towards more fully autonomous task planning.

In conclusion, this paper presents a compelling solution to a long-standing problem in reinforcement learning for robotics. By rigorously analyzing reward design challenges and introducing an innovative VLM-powered curriculum, it paves the way for more autonomous, efficient, and robust learning of quality-critical robotic manipulation skills, with significant implications for both academic research and industrial applications.
