# Vision-as-Inverse-Graphics Agent via Interleaved Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> VIGA is a training-free agent that reconstructs and edits 2D, 3D and 4D scenes as executable code from a reference image. It runs a code-render-inspect loop, where a VLM writes a program, renders it, inspects the discrepancies and edits again. A semantic skill library and a sliding-window multimodal memory support this loop. It improves on one-shot baselines by 35.32% on BlenderGym, 117.17% on SlideBench and 124.70% on the new BlenderBench.

## Problem

- Differentiable inverse graphics gives pixel-level grounding but relies on local gradients, so it gets stuck in local minima when the initial state is far from the target.
- One-shot VLM scene-code generation escapes those minima with semantic priors but is open-loop, so it cannot verify or refine camera extrinsics, object poses or lighting.
- Prior LLM scene-program methods depend on synthetic datasets, domain-specific verification pipelines or predefined workflows, which limits scalability and adaptation to diverse visual feedback and tasks.

## Method

- ==Interleaved code-render-inspect loop==: the agent plans, synthesizes a program, executes it in a graphics engine, explores the render, and turns the observed discrepancy into actionable feedback for the next edit.
- ==Evolving multimodal memory== archives plans, code diffs and renders, and a fixed-size ==sliding window== keeps only the latest L iterations to prevent context bloat and rot.
- ==Semantic skill library== splits read-only observation tools (set_camera, investigate, get_scene_info) from modification tools (execute_code, undo_action), compressing the action space of raw Blender scripting.

## Results

- On BlenderGym, VIGA averages **+35.32%** improvement over one-shot and beats the memory-less BlenderAlchemy; with GPT-4o best-of-4 it reaches **48.86%** versus **27.63%** for BlenderAlchemy.
- On BlenderBench, VIGA gains up to **+187.50%** VLM score over BlenderAlchemy (GPT-4o, best-of-4), and Qwen3-VL-8B gains **+312.20%** at best-of-1, narrowing the gap to closed models.
- On SlideBench, the average overall score improves by **117.17%**; Qwen3-8B execution success rises from **0.10** to **0.54** and overall score from **6.0** to **32.8**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pair semantic reasoning with grounded visual verification: a closed loop that renders and inspects its own output fixes what open-loop VLM generation and gradient-only optimization each miss.
> - Bound the memory. The latest program is a self-contained state, so a sliding window drops old traces without loss, while unbounded history collapses on long horizons.
> - Wrapping fragile low-level APIs in high-level, read-only versus modifying skills lets weak open-source VLMs execute reliably, so the scaffold matters more than raw model scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the long-standing problem of "vision-as-inverse-graphics," which involves reconstructing images into editable programs, a concept dating back to Larry Roberts' 1963 thesis on the blocks world. This area seeks to create compositional scene representations that are easily manipulable for various applications, such as robot training or digital twins.

Existing approaches to inverse graphics generally fall into two categories:

- **Differentiable Inverse Graphics:** These methods offer fine-grained visual grounding through pixel-level optimization. However, they are susceptible to local minima, particularly when large discrepancies exist between the initial and target states. Their reliance on local gradients limits their capacity for high-level semantic reasoning and active exploration, which prevents them from escaping non-convex traps.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** VLMs leverage broad semantic priors to generate scene code in a single pass, which can bypass the local minima issues of gradient-based methods. However, these are often open-loop, meaning they lack fine-grained visual grounding. This limitation hinders their ability to precisely verify and refine complex scene details such as camera extrinsics, object poses, and lighting. Consequently, one-shot VLM-based code generation often struggles with accurate complex scene reconstruction.

This paper proposes an approach that integrates the complementary strengths of these two paradigms. It aims to overcome the limitations of both one-shot VLMs and differentiable inverse graphics by coupling discrete symbolic generation with continuous visual perception. The work positions itself within the emerging field of LLM-based agents, specifically addressing their shortcomings in processing and reasoning within visual environments (2D/3D/4D spaces) beyond text-centric interactions. It unifies visual analysis (generating code to understand visuals) and visual synthesis (generating content from instructions) by introducing an iterative analysis-by-synthesis loop, which is a departure from single-pass code generation or rule-based procedural content generation. The research also extends the concept of inverse graphics by focusing on the recovery of editable graphics programs rather than just structured markup code, while aiming for a task-agnostic, training-free framework that does not rely on domain-specific verification pipelines or predefined workflows.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework that can reconstruct and edit visual scenes by translating images into editable programs. The core motivation stems from the observed limitations of current approaches to inverse graphics:

- **Addressing Spatial Grounding Deficiencies in VLMs:** Modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs), despite their semantic capabilities, often lack the fine-grained spatial grounding necessary for accurate scene reconstruction and editing from a single input image. They struggle with precise verification and refinement of details like camera viewpoints, object positions, and lighting.
- **Overcoming Local Minima in Differentiable Inverse Graphics:** Differentiable inverse graphics methods, while providing pixel-level precision, can get trapped in local minima during optimization, especially when initial states significantly diverge from target states. They lack the high-level semantic reasoning to make "leaps" out of these traps.
- **Bridging the Gap between Symbolic and Perceptual Reasoning:** The paper argues that solving inverse graphics for complex scenes requires continuous observation, active information-seeking, and precise geometric refinement, which are not effectively addressed by either one-shot generation or purely gradient-based optimization. The motivation is to create an agent that can seamlessly interleave abstract symbolic program generation with concrete visual perception.
- **Enabling Compositional and Editable Scene Representations:** The ultimate goal of inverse graphics is to produce compositional scene representations (programs) that can be easily manipulated and adapted for various downstream applications (e.g., robot training, digital twins, content creation). Current methods often fall short of generating such robust and editable representations.
- **Developing a Training-Free and Task-Agnostic Framework:** A key motivation is to create a system that does not require extensive task-specific training or auxiliary predictors, allowing it to generalize across diverse visual domains (2D documents, 3D scenes, 4D physics simulations) with a unified approach. This increases adaptability and reduces development overhead.
- **Facilitating Long-Horizon Reasoning and Refinement:** Complex inverse graphics tasks often involve multiple steps of modification and refinement. The existing open-loop nature of many VLM-based approaches makes sustained, evidence-based modifications challenging. The researchers aim to enable long-horizon, self-correcting reasoning trajectories.
- **Establishing a Robust Benchmark for Agentic Inverse Graphics:** Recognizing the limitations of existing benchmarks that primarily assess static, single-turn outputs, the motivation also includes introducing a new benchmark, BlenderBench, to rigorously evaluate multi-step, dynamic agentic behaviors in inverse graphics.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to advance the state of inverse graphics by presenting VIGA as a system capable of complex, iterative scene reconstruction and editing through an active, cross-modal reasoning loop.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VIGA (Vision-as-Inverse-Graphics Agent) framework operates through an interleaved multimodal reasoning process, tightly coupling symbolic code generation with visual perception. This process is structured around a continuous **code-render-inspect loop**, sustained by an evolving multimodal memory and a high-level semantic skill library.

#### 3.1. Interleaved Multimodal Reasoning Framework

The framework formalizes inverse graphics as an iterative analysis-by-synthesis process. Given a target visual specification ($s^*_g$, e.g., a reference image) and an initial state ($s_0$), the agent's objective is to synthesize a final executable program ($p_T$) that renders a state ($s_T$) visually consistent with the target. This is achieved through a dynamic execution loop that transitions between different multimodal action spaces:

- **Symbolic Reasoning from Visual Input (Phase 1):** At each iteration $t$, the agent ($A$) generates a high-level strategic plan ($\tau^{plan}_t$) by evaluating the target $s^*_g$ and its current context memory $M_t$. This plan dictates the subsequent programmatic actions. The agent then synthesizes an executable program ($p_t$), which constitutes a set of precise symbolic actions within a discrete symbolic space. This phase involves "forward anticipation," where the agent mentally maps abstract symbolic parameters to their continuous visual consequences. If the plan includes an `end_process` command, the loop terminates.

- **Cross-Modal Execution (Phase 2):** The synthesized program ($p_t$) is not a terminal output but is immediately executed within a 3D graphics engine. This execution renders a continuous visual state ($s_t = \text{exec}(p_t)$), serving as the critical cross-modal bridge. This step transforms abstract symbolic instructions into concrete visual observations, making spatial discrepancies apparent and directly informing the next iteration.

- **Visual-to-Symbolic Feedback (Phase 3):** Following execution, the agent transitions into an active observation phase within the continuous visual space. It utilizes interactive tools (e.g., adjusting camera viewpoints) to explore the newly rendered scene ($s_t$), generating an observation trajectory ($\tau^{obs}_t$). The agent then rigorously compares the rendered scene $s_t$ against the target $s^*_g$, translating any identified visual discrepancies into actionable natural language feedback ($f_t$). This feedback acts as the bridge back to the symbolic domain, guiding the agent's subsequent programmatic edits.

- **Context Memory Update (Phase 4):** The grounded visual feedback ($f_t$) and the synthesized program ($p_t$) are assimilated to dynamically update the context memory ($M_{t+1}$), providing the foundation for the next reasoning cycle.

This design ensures a robust, self-correcting reasoning cycle where code logic and visual evidence actively cross-verify each other.

#### 3.2. Evolving Multimodal Memory for Long-Horizon Reasoning

Unlike standard text-based chat logs, VIGA's memory ($M_t$) is structured as a multimodal trajectory. It systematically archives the interleaved sequence of reasoning traces (plans and feedback $f_t$), symbolic actions (executable programs $p_t$), and grounded observations (rendered states $s_t$). This structured memory is crucial for:

- **Progressive Refinement:** It enables the agent to perform complex, progressive spatial refinements by explicitly referencing past programmatic attempts and their visual outcomes. This allows for systematic convergence towards the target state, for example, by adjusting parameters based on previous errors.
- **Mitigating Context Bloat and Rot:** To prevent unbounded memory expansion, which can lead to increased computational overhead and degraded reasoning precision, VIGA employs a fixed-size **sliding window** mechanism. This window retains only the most recent $L$ iterations. The rationale is that the latest synthesized program ($p_t$) inherently encapsulates all prior successful geometric edits, rendering older intermediate reasoning traces redundant. This truncation is described as lossless, maintaining stable generation quality over long horizons.

#### 3.3. Skill Library as a Semantic Interface for Graphics

To manage the high-dimensional and often brittle action space of raw graphics APIs (e.g., Blender Python scripts), VIGA introduces a high-level semantic skill library:

- **Action Space Compression:** Instead of exposing the agent to low-level syntax, the skill library encapsulates atomic operations into reliable, macro-level skills. This significantly compresses the action space, reducing context bloat and preventing syntactic hallucinations or non-executable loops. The abstraction insulates the agent from programming trivialities, freeing its cognitive capacity for high-level multimodal reasoning.
- **Perception, Reasoning, and Action Decoupling:** The library is bifurcated into two categories mirroring the dual-space reasoning framework:
 - **State Observation Skills:** These are inherently read-only and non-destructive (e.g., `set_camera`, `investigate`, `get_scene_info`, `set_keyframe`). The agent uses these to conduct exhaustive, multi-turn spatial queries, actively adjust viewpoints, gather multi-angle visual evidence, and distill raw visual phenomena into a semantic understanding of the scene's physical, geometric, and spatial properties.
 - **State Modification Skills:** These execute persistent changes (e.g., `execute_code`, `init_scene`, `get_better_assets`). The agent triggers these only after a rigorous transition from surface-level visual grounding to abstract semantic reasoning. `execute_code` is central, translating semantic understanding into precise parametric adjustments by injecting the synthesized program into the engine. Other tools include `get_better_object` for generating and downloading high-quality 3D assets.

This decoupling ensures that scene mutations are deliberate and conditioned on a comprehensive series of prior observations, fostering a more cautious and effective reasoning process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VIGA demonstrates its capabilities across diverse visual generation and editing tasks, including 2D, 3D, and 4D domains, and its performance on new and established benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results across Diverse Tasks

VIGA exhibits broad adaptability and visual fidelity across different visual domains, operating under a unified interleaved reasoning loop without domain-specific architectures.

- **3D Generation and Editing:** VIGA reconstructs diverse 3D scenes from sparse single-view inputs, including photorealistic images and hand-drawn sketches. It dynamically uses tools like `get_better_assets` to retrieve and position high-quality external meshes, iteratively optimizing spatial layout and material properties to match lighting and textures. The system demonstrates sketch-to-3D generalization, converting line drawings into complete 3D geometries.
- **4D Dynamic Scene Simulation:** Beyond static reconstruction, VIGA simulates 4D physical dynamics based on a single image and a text prompt. It infers and configures physical parameters (e.g., mass, collision shapes) to create interactive environments, such as a ball scattering fruit or a mirror shattering. The framework enforces physical accuracy and temporal consistency by executing programmatic commands within a physics engine, avoiding issues like flickering or physical hallucinations common in pixel-based generative models.
- **2D Document Design:** VIGA's framework is applicable to 2D document generation. By adapting the underlying execution engine, it synthesizes professional PowerPoint slides from text instructions, managing layout planning, text formatting, and image placement. This flexibility suggests its generalizability to any visual domain governed by a programmatic interface.

#### 4.2. BlenderBench: Evaluating Agentic Behaviors

To rigorously test interleaved multimodal reasoning, a new benchmark, BlenderBench, was introduced, comprising three progressive tracks:

- **Task 1 (Camera Adjustment):** Evaluates active spatial exploration for viewpoint alignment.
- **Task 2 (Fixed-View Editing):** Assesses iterative scene reasoning and attribute modification under a fixed camera pose.
- **Task 3 (Exploratory Editing):** Unifies the challenges, requiring active spatial exploration alongside multi-step programmatic edits.

**Metrics:** Photometric Loss (PL), Negative-CLIP Score (N-CLIP), and a VLM Score (assessing task completion, spatial accuracy, detail accuracy, and visual quality, prioritizing structural layout over stylistic artifacts).

**Results:** VIGA consistently surpassed existing baselines, including one-shot methods and the memory-less BlenderAlchemy.
- VIGA achieved an average relative improvement of +124.70% across all evaluation tracks and model settings.
- In Task 1 (Camera Adjustment), one-shot methods failed due to limited spatial perception, while VIGA dynamically updated its viewpoint. Scaling the search budget (best-of-N) from N=1 to N=4 with GPT-4o boosted the VLM Score from 1.44 to 3.25, indicating an ability to overcome local minima during viewpoint alignment.
- In Task 3 (Exploratory Editing), BlenderAlchemy suffered from cross-step inconsistency. VIGA, by explicitly tracking states, maintained sequential consistency, achieving a +187.50% VLM Score improvement (GPT-4o, best-of-4).
- The iterative reasoning loop provided a capability scaffold, with VIGA (instantiated with Qwen) delivering a +312.20% relative gain over BlenderAlchemy (best-of-1), bridging performance gaps between models.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on Existing Benchmarks

VIGA demonstrated strong task-agnostic generalization on established single-step programmatic generation and editing tasks:

- **3D Editing (BlenderGym):** On BlenderGym, VIGA consistently outperformed both the one-shot baseline and BlenderAlchemy, yielding an average improvement of +35.32% across all settings. This indicates its precision in localizing and modifying fine-grained object attributes (e.g., blend shapes, placement, lighting) while preserving unedited scene elements.
- **2D Design (SlideBench):** On SlideBench for programmatic PowerPoint generation, VIGA achieved an average Overall score improvement of +117.17% across various foundation models (Qwen3-VL-8B, GPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet-4, Gemini-2.5-Pro). Its interleaved reasoning loop acted as an error-recovery mechanism, improving execution success rates (Exec.) by iteratively rectifying faulty code, which is a significant advantage over one-shot methods that often halt on errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VIGA framework represents a contribution to the field of inverse graphics and agentic AI, offering several significant implications:

- **Reframing Inverse Graphics as an Agent Problem:** By integrating code synthesis, rendering, and visual verification within a closed-loop agentic framework, the work redefines inverse graphics beyond traditional optimization or one-shot generation. This perspective emphasizes active, iterative reasoning in a rendered world.
- **Enhanced Spatial Grounding and Precision:** VIGA's interleaved multimodal reasoning directly addresses the limitations of existing VLMs regarding fine-grained spatial grounding. By allowing visual perception to actively verify and guide symbolic program edits, the framework enables more precise reconstruction and manipulation of scene elements, including object geometry, pose, and lighting.
- **Robustness to Complex and Long-Horizon Tasks:** The proposed architecture, which combines a high-level semantic skill library with an evolving multimodal context memory, allows VIGA to sustain evidence-based modifications over long horizons. This contributes to handling complex tasks like multi-step 3D editing and 4D physical interactions, where prior methods often fail due to context loss or inconsistency.
- **Training-Free and Task-Agnostic Generalization:** VIGA's design as a zero-shot, training-free framework is significant. Its reliance on interpreting discrepancies between renders and reference images, without auxiliary predictors or differentiable renderers, renders it exceptionally flexible and adaptable across diverse visual domains (2D documents, 3D scenes, 4D physics simulations) and different foundation VLMs. This reduces the need for extensive domain-specific training.
- **Establishment of a New Benchmark:** The introduction of BlenderBench provides a standardized and challenging suite of tasks specifically designed to evaluate dynamic, multi-step agentic behaviors in inverse graphics. This benchmark serves as a crucial tool for stress-testing and advancing future research in this area, particularly for assessing capabilities like active spatial exploration and iterative scene reasoning.
- **Bridging the Performance Gap Across VLMs:** The empirical results demonstrate that VIGA's iterative reasoning loop acts as a capability scaffold, allowing even less powerful open-source models (like Qwen3-VL-8B) to achieve substantial performance gains, effectively bridging the gap with larger, proprietary VLMs. This suggests that the agentic framework can leverage and amplify the reasoning capabilities of various underlying foundation models.
- **Potential for Automated Content Creation and Digital Twins:** The ability to reconstruct images into editable programs and simulate physical dynamics has potential applications in automated content creation for various industries (e.g., gaming, film), as well as in creating interpretable digital twins for robotics, urban planning, or engineering simulations.

While acknowledging limitations such as dependence on underlying VLM spatial perception and context window constraints for extremely long sequences, the framework is presented as inherently extensible, capable of integrating advancements in foundation models and external generative tools. VIGA represents a foundational step towards achieving more fully automated and intelligently guided scene generation.
