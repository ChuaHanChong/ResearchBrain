# MemoryVLA++: Temporal Modeling via Memory and Imagination in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MemoryVLA++ introduces a comprehensive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that integrates explicit past memory and latent-space future imagination to enhance temporal reasoning in robotic manipulation. The model achieved an average success rate of 98.4% on Libero and a 16.6 percentage point improvement over CogACT on SimplerEnv, alongside substantial gains on long-horizon memory-dependent (+26%) and imagination-dependent (+28%) real-robot tasks.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models primarily rely on current observations, hindering performance on tasks requiring understanding of past interactions or anticipation of future events.
- Naive temporal expansion, such as concatenating multiple frames or predicting pixels, is computationally expensive and suffers from redundancy and error propagation.
- Prior temporal methods often focus on short-horizon history or lack the joint preservation of fine-grained perceptual details and high-level semantic information.

## Method

- **MemoryVLA++** integrates a Vision-Language-Cognition (VLC) module for ==working memory==, a Perceptual-Cognitive Memory Bank (PCMB) for long-term past memory, and a World Model-Based Imagination Module for future state anticipation.
- The PCMB uses redundancy-aware consolidation to compress historical perceptual and cognitive tokens, and memory gate fusion to adaptively integrate retrieved past information.
- A pre-trained Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model is adapted to generate manipulation-oriented future latent tokens, which are integrated via memory-guided attention to efficiently anticipate future states without full pixel-level prediction.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **98.4%** across Libero suites and **73.9%** on SimplerEnv, representing improvements of +5.2 and +16.6 percentage points respectively over the CogACT baseline.
- Demonstrated significant improvements in real-robot long-horizon tasks, with +26 percentage points on memory-dependent tasks and +28 percentage points on imagination-dependent tasks compared to CogACT.
- Exhibited enhanced robustness and generalization, achieving **73.1%** success (zero-shot) and **82.7%** (fine-tuned) on Libero-Plus, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT by +5.2 and +3.1 percentage points under diverse out-of-distribution conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A comprehensive temporal modeling approach, inspired by human cognition and integrating both past memory and future imagination, significantly improves VLA performance on long-horizon and temporally complex robotic tasks.
> - Efficient temporal representation can be achieved by using compact perceptual and cognitive memory tokens and latent-space imagination, avoiding the high computational cost of explicit full video prediction.
> - Adaptive gating mechanisms for memory fusion and memory-guided imagination integration are crucial for selectively incorporating relevant temporal cues while filtering out noise and redundancies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has advanced significantly with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, such as OpenVLA and π0, leverage large-scale cross-embodiment robotic datasets and pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to enable robots to perform various tasks based on visual observations and language instructions. However, a common limitation among many existing VLA models is their primary reliance on the current observation, which can hinder performance on tasks requiring an understanding of temporal dependencies, particularly those involving long horizons.

Prior attempts to address temporal modeling in robotic manipulation include expanding VLA inputs with recent history frames or predicted future video frames. However, these methods often face challenges such as high computational costs due to quadratic self-attention, redundancy in consecutive frames, limited context length, and the propagation of visual prediction errors when directly using predicted future frames for action generation. Some methods encode history into latent representations or use abstract temporal cues, but these often focus on short-horizon local history or lack the joint preservation of perceptual details and high-level semantics. Similarly, while generative video models show promise for future modeling by explicitly predicting visual states or modeling dynamics in a shared latent space, they can be computationally expensive or may introduce decision-irrelevant noise without explicit memory integration.

MemoryVLA++ positions itself in this landscape by proposing a comprehensive temporal modeling framework that integrates both memory of past interactions and imagination of future states, addressing the limitations of existing VLA models that typically focus on a single aspect or rely on inefficient mechanisms. It extends previous work, particularly MemoryVLA, by moving from past-only memory to a full temporal modeling approach that incorporates future imagination, aiming to provide a more complete and efficient representation for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a comprehensive temporal modeling framework that enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to effectively handle long-horizon, temporally dependent robotic manipulation tasks. The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations in current VLA models and insights drawn from cognitive science:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Current VLA Models:** Typical VLA models primarily process the current observation, which makes them less effective for tasks where crucial information lies in past interactions or requires anticipation of future events. For example, "Button Pressing" tasks illustrate the need for memory, as the visual state before and after a button press can be nearly identical, making it difficult to discern if the action has already occurred. Conversely, "Dynamic-Conveyor Grasping" tasks highlight the necessity of imagination, as predicting the movement of objects on a conveyor belt allows the robot to execute a grasp at an opportune moment. The lack of explicit temporal modeling leads to suboptimal performance in such scenarios.

2. **Overcoming Inefficiencies of Naive Temporal Expansion:** A straightforward approach to incorporate temporal context involves concatenating multiple past frames or integrating predicted future frames. However, this method is computationally expensive due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention with increasing sequence length, leading to substantial temporal redundancy. This limits the usable context length, particularly problematic for tasks with slow motions where relevant interactions span longer durations. Furthermore, explicit future video prediction is computationally intensive and often prioritizes pixel-level fidelity over control-relevant dynamics, potentially introducing visual prediction errors that can propagate to action generation. The motivation is to find a more efficient and decision-relevant way to represent temporal information.

3. **Drawing Inspiration from Cognitive Science:** The authors are motivated by human cognitive mechanisms for handling manipulation tasks. Human cognition relies on:
 - **Working Memory:** To temporarily buffer short-lived context and current multimodal sensory information (perceptual and cognitive representations) for immediate decision-making.
 - **Episodic Memory (Hippocampal System):** A long-term memory system that preserves past experiences, storing both verbatim details and abstract gist representations.
 - **Internal Models:** To anticipate possible future states and outcomes before executing actions.
 During execution, humans integrate current representations with retrieved past experiences and future anticipations to guide motor actions. This tripartite cognitive architecture—present perception, past memory, and future imagination—serves as the conceptual foundation for MemoryVLA++.

In summary, the key objectives are to imbue VLA models with a robust capacity for both memory and imagination, addressing the shortcomings of current models and inefficient temporal augmentation strategies, and drawing inspiration from biological cognitive systems to achieve more effective and temporally consistent robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MemoryVLA++ is an end-to-end temporal modeling framework that integrates current perception, past memory, and future imagination into VLA models. Its architecture comprises several interconnected modules: a Vision-Language-Cognition (VLC) Module, a Perceptual-Cognitive Memory Bank (PCMB), a World Model-Based Imagination Module, and a Full Temporal-Aware Action Expert.

#### 3.1. Vision-Language-Cognition Module

This module is responsible for processing the current visual observations and language instructions into a compact representation, forming the "working memory." It is built upon a 7B-parameter Prismatic VLM, pre-trained on the Open-X Embodiment dataset.
- **Visual Encoding:** RGB observations from multiple camera views are processed by parallel DINOv2 and SigLIP encoders, generating raw visual tokens.
- **Perceptual Branch:** A SE-bottleneck-based compression module reduces the channel dimension of raw visual tokens to produce `perceptual tokens` ($p \in R^{N_p \times d_p}$), retaining fine-grained visual details.
- **Cognitive Branch:** Raw visual tokens are projected into the language embedding space and concatenated with the tokenized instruction. This sequence is fed into LLaMA-7B, and the output at the End-Of-Sentence (EOS) position becomes the `cognitive token` ($c \in R^{1 \times d_c}$), capturing high-level semantics.
These $p$ and $c$ tokens collectively form the current `working memory`.

#### 3.2. Perceptual-Cognitive Memory Modeling

This module manages long-term memory, inspired by the human hippocampus, storing historical context.
- **Perceptual-Cognitive Memory Bank (PCMB):** Stores up to $L$ temporally ordered entries, each comprising `perceptual memory entries` ($m_p_i$) for low-level details and `cognitive memory entries` ($m_c_i$) for high-level semantics.
- **Memory Retrieval:** The current working memory ($p, c$) acts as a query to retrieve relevant historical information from the PCMB.
 - Each memory entry is augmented with a sinusoidal `timestep embedding` (TE) to preserve temporal order.
 - Cross-attention is applied, where current tokens ($q_p = p, q_c = c$) query stacked historical keys ($K_p, K_c$) and values ($V_p, V_c$), incorporating timestep embeddings.
 - Two Transformer layers process the attention outputs to yield retrieved embeddings ($H_p, H_c$).
- **Memory Gate Fusion:** The retrieved historical embeddings are adaptively fused with the current representations.
 - For each stream (perceptual/cognitive), a `gating vector` ($g_x$) is computed from the concatenation of current ($x$) and retrieved ($H_x$) representations using an MLP and sigmoid activation.
 - The memory-augmented representation ($\tilde{x}$) is obtained by a weighted sum: $\tilde{x} = g_x \odot H_x + (1 - g_x) \odot x$.
- **Memory Consolidation:** When the PCMB reaches its capacity ($L$), redundancy-aware consolidation is performed to maintain compactness.
 - Cosine similarities are computed between temporally adjacent memory entries for both perceptual and cognitive streams.
 - The most similar adjacent pair is merged by averaging their representations, and one of the merged entries is removed, effectively compressing redundant information.

#### 3.3. World Model-Based Imagination Modeling

This module anticipates future state evolution, addressing the "imagination" aspect. It avoids explicit pixel-level video prediction for efficiency.
- **Manipulation-Oriented World Model Adaptation:** A pre-trained Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model is adapted for robotic manipulation.
 - SVD is conditioned on the current observation and instruction (encoded by CLIP).
 - It is fine-tuned on a dataset ($D_{wm}$) of manipulation videos using a denoising diffusion objective, training it to reconstruct future video sequences from noisy versions.
- **Imagination Generation:** During VLA training, the adapted world model is frozen.
 - It performs partial denoising of future latents, extracting multi-scale intermediate UNet features ($\{U_s\}$).
 - An FPN aggregates these features into `latent tokens` ($z$), capturing future evolution over $K$ steps.
 - A learnable temporal embedding is added to $z$.
 - An "imagination former" (query-based spatial attention followed by temporal attention and FFN) compresses these tokens into `imagined future tokens` ($z_{img}$).
- **Memory-Guided Imagination Integration:** To filter out irrelevant predictions from $z_{img}$ and integrate relevant future cues.
 - Memory-augmented perceptual tokens ($\tilde{p}$) query $z_{img}$ via cross-attention.
 - The cross-attention output ($h$) is then adaptively fused with $\tilde{p}$ using a gating mechanism, similar to memory gate fusion.
 - The final outputs are the `full temporal-aware tokens` ($F_{temp} = \{\bar{p}, \tilde{c}\}$), combining present perception, past memory, and future imagination.

#### 3.4. Full Temporal-Aware Action Expert

This module utilizes the `full temporal-aware tokens` to predict actions.
- **Diffusion-based Transformer (DiT):** A DiT, implemented with Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM), is used to generate continuous 7-DoF action sequences.
- **Conditional Denoising:** At each denoising timestep, the sinusoidal timestep embedding is added to the cognitive token ($\tilde{c}$).
 - A `cognition-attention layer` performs self-attention over the concatenated cognitive token and noisy action sequence, providing high-level semantic guidance.
 - The cognition-aware features then interact with perceptual tokens ($\bar{p}$) via a `perception-attention layer` to inject fine-grained visual details.
 - An FFN and MLP project the denoised representations to predict the target action sequence.
The model is trained using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between predicted and target action sequences, employing classifier-free guidance during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MemoryVLA++ was evaluated through extensive experiments across five simulation benchmarks and three categories of real-robot tasks, spanning three different robotic platforms. The findings highlight the effectiveness of its full temporal modeling with memory and imagination.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation

- **General Robotic Manipulation (Libero & SimplerEnv):**
 - **Libero:** MemoryVLA++ achieved an average success rate of **98.4%** across five suites, outperforming the CogACT baseline by **+5.2 percentage points**. It recorded 99.8% on Spatial, 100.0% on Object, 98.2% on Goal, 96.0% on Long-10, and 97.8% on Long-90, showing consistent gains across both general and long-horizon tasks.
 - **SimplerEnv:** An average success rate of **73.9%** was achieved, surpassing CogACT by **+16.6 percentage points**. Notable performance includes 83.3% on Spoon on Towel, 66.7% on Carrot on Plate, 45.8% on Stack Cube, and 100.0% on Eggplant in Basket.
- **Long-Horizon Temporal Tasks (Mikasa-Robo & Calvin):**
 - **Mikasa-Robo:** MemoryVLA++ achieved an average success rate of **44.4%**, improving over the previous best single-frame VLA by **+15.0 percentage points**. It notably improved ShellGameTouch by +50.0 points.
 - **Calvin (ABC→D zero-shot):** The model achieved an average completed task length of **4.29**, a **+1.04 improvement** over the CogACT baseline. It showed higher success rates across all five task steps, with larger gains at longer horizons (e.g., 76.1% at task 5).
- **Robustness and Generalization (Libero-Plus):**
 - **Zero-shot setting:** MemoryVLA++ achieved an average success rate of **73.1%**, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT by **+5.2 percentage points** under diverse out-of-distribution variations (camera, robot, language, lighting, background, noise, layout).
 - **Supervised fine-tuning setting:** Performance further improved to **82.7%**, exceeding OpenVLA-OFT by **+3.1 percentage points**.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Evaluation

Experiments were conducted on Franka, WidowX, and Dual-ARX5 robots.
- **General Manipulation Tasks:** MemoryVLA achieved an average success score of **85%**, surpassing CogACT by **+9 percentage points**.
- **Long-Horizon Memory-Dependent Tasks:** MemoryVLA achieved an average success score of **83%**, exceeding CogACT by **+26 percentage points**. Significant improvements were observed on tasks like Seq. Push Buttons (+43) and Change Food (+38).
- **Long-Horizon Imagination-Dependent Tasks:** MemoryVLA++ achieved an average success score of **77%**, outperforming CogACT by **+28 percentage points** and MemoryVLA by +12 points. Gains were substantial on Bag Pack & Zip (+36) and Conveyor Scan-Pick (+33).
- **Robustness and Generalization (Real-World):** The model demonstrated minor performance drops under variations in backgrounds, distractors, lighting, objects, containers, and occlusions for tasks like Pick Place Order and Clean Restaurant Table, indicating robustness to real-world distribution shifts.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analytical Results

- **Memory Modeling:** Ablations confirmed the benefits of default memory length, gated fusion over addition, redundancy-aware "Token Merge" consolidation over FIFO, timestep positional encoding for retrieval, and combining both perceptual and cognitive memory streams.
- **Imagination Modeling:** Ablations indicated that 1 denoising step for imagination generation provided competitive performance. Longer imagination horizons generally improved results. Freezing the world model during policy training outperformed updating it. Memory-guided integration significantly outperformed direct addition, highlighting the importance of selectively using imagined cues.
- **Analysis of Mechanisms:** Visualizations showed the memory mechanism consistently attended to decision-relevant past frames that resolved ambiguities in current observations. Imagination visualizations demonstrated that the world model captured meaningful future semantic dynamics even with partial denoising.
- **Inference Efficiency:** MemoryVLA introduced a minor overhead (~4% latency increase). MemoryVLA++ with latent-space imagination showed a moderate overhead (latency increased to 0.241s on RTX 4090, throughput 66.4 Hz) but remained practical for real-robot deployment.
- **Stronger VLA Pretraining:** Replacing the LLaMA2-based VLA with a Qwen2.5-based VLA and Dexbotic pretraining improved SimplerEnv performance from 71.9% to 84.4%, suggesting potential for further gains with enhanced VLMs.

These findings collectively indicate that MemoryVLA++'s comprehensive temporal modeling, integrating explicit memory and imagination, contributes to improved performance, robustness, and generalization across various robotic manipulation tasks, especially those requiring long-horizon temporal reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MemoryVLA++ represents a significant step in enhancing the temporal reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. By equipping VLA models with explicit mechanisms for both past memory and future imagination, inspired by cognitive science, this work addresses a fundamental limitation in existing VLA models that predominantly rely on current observations.

The demonstrated improvements across a diverse set of simulation benchmarks and real-robot tasks, particularly on long-horizon and temporally dependent tasks, indicate that a holistic approach to temporal modeling is beneficial for complex robotic control. The substantial gains in success rates on memory-dependent tasks (e.g., +26% on real-robot memory tasks) and imagination-dependent tasks (e.g., +28% on real-robot imagination tasks) validate the importance of these cognitive functions for robust and adaptive robotic behavior.

The potential impact of MemoryVLA++ extends to several areas:

1. **Advancing Generalist Robot Policies:** By improving a robot's ability to understand and act within a temporal context, MemoryVLA++ contributes to the development of more capable and generalist robot policies. Robots equipped with such temporal intelligence could perform a wider range of tasks, adapt to changing environments, and recover from transient perception ambiguities, moving closer to human-like versatility.
2. **Robustness and Generalization:** The framework's demonstrated robustness to various out-of-distribution conditions in Libero-Plus and real-world settings suggests improved reliability for practical deployment. The ability to leverage both precise low-level perceptual details and high-level cognitive semantics from memory, combined with decision-relevant future imagination, makes the policies less susceptible to immediate sensory noise or partial occlusions.
3. **Efficiency in Temporal Modeling:** The methodology highlights an efficient way to integrate temporal cues, avoiding computationally expensive approaches like full pixel-level video prediction. Latent-space imagination and redundancy-aware memory consolidation offer a balance between comprehensive temporal coverage and computational feasibility, which is crucial for real-time robotic applications.
4. **Inspiration for Future Embodied AI:** The cognitive science inspiration provides a biologically plausible blueprint for designing more intelligent embodied AI systems. Future research could further explore and refine these cognitive parallels, potentially leading to more sophisticated memory architectures, imagination mechanisms, and their interplay within larger AI frameworks.
5. **Benchmarking and Development:** The extensive experimental evaluation, including new benchmarks like Calvin and Libero-Plus, and the comparison against numerous strong baselines, provides a valuable reference for the community. The analytical insights into memory attention and imagination quality contribute to a better understanding of how these temporal mechanisms function and can be optimized.

In conclusion, MemoryVLA++ represents a significant step towards developing robot policies that can reason over time with both memory and foresight, contributing to the realization of more intelligent, autonomous, and adaptable robotic systems.
