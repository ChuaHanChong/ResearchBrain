# LLMPhy: Complex Physical Reasoning Using Large Language Models and World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Mitsubishi Electric Research Labs (MERL) developed LLMPHY, a framework integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) with physics engines to perform complex physical reasoning. It uses LLMs for zero-shot black-box optimization, iteratively refining physical parameters and scene layouts based on simulator feedback, and achieved 62.0% mIoU accuracy on the new TraySim dataset.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with precise, dynamic physical reasoning due to their inability to directly interact with the physical world or measure unobservable attributes like friction.
- Existing LLM-based methods often rely on 'intuitive physics' which is insufficient for exact predictions, while physics-informed models require extensive data or struggle with complex multi-body interactions.
- Traditional black-box optimization methods lack semantic understanding of physical parameters, making exploration less efficient in complex, non-differentiable physical systems.

## Method

- **LLMPHY** introduces a zero-shot black-box optimization framework that synergizes an LLM's program synthesis capabilities with a high-fidelity physics engine (**MuJoCo**).
- In Phase 1, the LLM iteratively infers unobservable physical parameters (e.g., friction, damping) by generating code for the simulator, receiving trajectory error feedback, and refining its parameter proposals.
- In Phase 2, the LLM reconstructs the task scene layout from images and then uses the inferred physical parameters to simulate object dynamics and answer physical reasoning questions about stability.

## Results

- LLMPHY achieved a mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) accuracy of 62.0% on the `TraySim` QA task, outperforming a pure LLM baseline (32.1% mIoU) and traditional black-box optimization methods (59.6-59.7% mIoU).
- LLM-based optimization showed faster initial convergence and produced more accurate physical parameter estimations compared to Bayesian Optimization and CMA-ES when minimizing trajectory errors.
- Providing the full optimization trace (history of past estimations and errors) to the LLM improved performance by 5% Avg. IoU, indicating the importance of historical context for effective iterative refinement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of LLMs with physics engines effectively provides LLMs with an interactive 'world model,' enabling them to perform grounded, dynamic physical reasoning beyond purely linguistic understanding.
> - LLMs can function as intelligent optimizers in black-box settings, leveraging their semantic understanding of physical attributes to achieve faster initial convergence and more accurate parameter estimation than purely numerical optimization methods.
> - The framework successfully handles both continuous optimization (for physical parameters) and discrete optimization (for scene layout and object properties), demonstrating versatility in complex physical reasoning tasks.
