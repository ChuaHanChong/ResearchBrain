# OneVL: One-Step Latent Reasoning and Planning with Vision-Language Explanation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Xiaomi Embodied Intelligence Team developed OneVL, a framework integrating a Vision-Language-Action model with a world model auxiliary for autonomous driving, which is the first latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) method to surpass explicit autoregressive CoT in trajectory prediction performance while maintaining answer-only inference latency. OneVL achieved an 88.84 PDM-score on NAVSIM, outperforming prior 8B models by up to 2.64 points, with inference latency comparable to answer-only prediction.

## Problem

- Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for VLA models improves interpretability and performance but introduces prohibitive inference latency for real-time autonomous driving applications.
- Prior latent CoT methods, primarily designed for language-only tasks, underperform explicit CoT in multimodal VLA driving because their purely linguistic latents fail to capture causal spatiotemporal dynamics and often still generate latents autoregressively.
- Existing latent representations lacked a direct mechanism to internalize the physical, causal dynamics of the driving environment, instead compressing only symbolic abstractions.

## Method

- The **OneVL** framework integrates a pretrained ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== with specialized ==language and visual latent tokens==, supervised by ==dual auxiliary decoders==.
- ==Dual-modal latent supervision== uses a ==visual auxiliary decoder== to predict future frames (acting as a world model) and a ==language auxiliary decoder== to reconstruct human-readable ==CoT== text.
- A ==three-stage training pipeline== progressively aligns the VLM and auxiliary decoders, while a ==prefill inference== mechanism processes all latent tokens in a single parallel pass to achieve answer-only latency at inference time.

## Results

- OneVL achieved an **88.84 PDM-score** on NAVSIM, surpassing previous state-of-the-art **8B** models by up to **2.64 points**, and became the first latent CoT method to outperform explicit autoregressive CoT across all tested autonomous driving benchmarks.
- The prefill inference mechanism enabled OneVL to achieve latency comparable to answer-only prediction (e.g., **4.46s** on NAVSIM vs **6.58s** for AR CoT+Answer), with a real-time deployment variant achieving **0.24s** latency at **86.83 PDM-score**.
- OneVL provides interpretable dual-modal explanations, with its language auxiliary decoder achieving **71.00** Meta Action Accuracy and **78.26** STS Score on NAVSIM, and its visual decoder generating plausible future-frame predictions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Guiding the VLM's compact latent space with dual-modal supervision (language and visual world model) distills trajectory-relevant reasoning, leading to more effective and generalizable representations that surpass verbose explicit CoT.
> - Explicitly forcing visual latents to encode and predict future frames, as a world model auxiliary, significantly grounds the reasoning in causal spatiotemporal dynamics, which is crucial for improved trajectory prediction.
> - A progressive three-stage training pipeline is essential for stable optimization when jointly training a complex VLM with disparate language and visual auxiliary decoding objectives, preventing catastrophic performance drops.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a critical challenge in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving, specifically concerning the trade-off between interpretability, achieved through Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, and the real-time efficiency required for practical deployment.

Recent advancements in VLAs have leveraged CoT reasoning to enhance trajectory prediction accuracy and provide interpretable explanations. Explicit CoT involves the model articulating intermediate reasoning steps in natural language before producing an action, which can significantly reduce planning errors and bind predictions into coherent causal chains. However, this autoregressive generation process incurs substantial inference latency, rendering it unsuitable for safety-critical real-time driving applications.

To mitigate this latency, a line of research has explored latent CoT methods, which compress reasoning into continuous hidden states. Existing latent CoT approaches, such as COCONUT, CODI, and SIM-CoT, were primarily developed for language-only tasks. When applied to VLA-based autonomous driving, these methods have generally underperformed their explicit CoT counterparts. The paper posits that this limitation stems from purely linguistic latent representations encoding a symbolic abstraction of the world rather than the causal spatio-temporal dynamics essential for driving. Linguistic descriptions, while semantically rich, may not directly capture the geometric and physical interactions that determine future outcomes. Furthermore, prior latent CoT methods often retain some sequentiality in hidden state generation, thus not fully addressing the inference speed issue.

OneVL aims to address these identified gaps by proposing a unified VLA and World Model framework. It seeks to integrate the benefits of CoT reasoning—improved accuracy and interpretability—with the efficiency of latent representations, specifically by grounding these latents in the causal dynamics of the driving environment. This is achieved through multimodal supervision and a novel inference mechanism, positioning OneVL as an effort to overcome the inherent limitations of language-only latent reasoning in complex, multimodal, and real-time decision-making domains like autonomous driving.

The work also relates to world modeling in autonomous driving, where models predict future environmental evolution. Previous world models are often used for data generation, closed-loop evaluation, or as separate representation stacks. OneVL integrates a short-horizon visual prediction as a training-only world model auxiliary, directly supervising the compressed latent CoT within a single VLA, which distinguishes its approach in the broader context of VLA and world model research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of OneVL are multifaceted, addressing critical shortcomings in existing approaches to autonomous driving planning with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

**Motivation:**

1. **High Latency of Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** While CoT reasoning has proven effective in improving the accuracy and interpretability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving, its autoregressive nature mandates the sequential generation of every reasoning token. This process significantly increases inference latency, making real-time deployment in safety-critical autonomous driving systems prohibitive. The time required to articulate detailed reasoning steps is a bottleneck that prevents the benefits of explicit CoT from being fully realized in practical applications.

2. **Limitations of Purely Linguistic Latent CoT Representations:** Previous latent CoT methods attempt to compress reasoning into continuous hidden states to reduce latency. However, these methods, predominantly designed for language-only tasks, have consistently underperformed explicit CoT in the multimodal and spatio-temporal domain of autonomous driving. The researchers hypothesize that this performance gap arises because purely linguistic latent representations primarily encode symbolic abstractions of the world. Such abstractions may lack the direct encoding of causal dynamics—such as road geometry, agent motion, and environmental changes—that are fundamental to accurate trajectory prediction and planning in a physical environment. Compressing linguistic descriptions alone may not force the model to internalize the truly relevant causal structures.

3. **Sequential Nature of Prior Latent CoT Inference:** Even in some existing latent CoT methods, the generation of latent hidden states can still be sequential, which means they do not fully eliminate the iterative overhead of autoregressive processes. This further limits their ability to achieve truly real-time inference speeds.

### 3. Key Objectives

Based on these motivations, OneVL was developed with the following objectives:

1. **Achieve Superior Accuracy with Latent Reasoning:** Design a latent CoT method that not only matches but surpasses the trajectory prediction accuracy of explicit autoregressive CoT, demonstrating that compression can lead to more generalizable and effective reasoning when properly guided.

2. **Attain Answer-Only Prediction Latency:** Implement an inference mechanism that allows for the generation of all latent tokens in a single parallel pass, effectively matching the speed of models that only predict the final answer without intermediate reasoning steps. This aims to resolve the latency issue inherent in explicit CoT.

3. **Internalize Causal Scene Dynamics into Latent Space:** Introduce a mechanism, specifically a visual world model decoder, to supervise compact latent tokens. This decoder forces the latent space to encode the causal dynamics of the driving environment (e.g., future-frame tokens representing road geometry, agent motion, and environmental changes), thereby moving beyond abstract linguistic representations.

4. **Provide Multimodal Interpretability:** Ensure that the compressed latent tokens can be decoded into both coherent natural language CoT explanations and plausible future-frame visual previews, thereby offering human-interpretable reasoning in both linguistic and visual modalities.

5. **Ensure Stable and Effective Training:** Develop a robust multi-stage training pipeline that progressively aligns the latent representations with trajectory prediction, language explanation, and visual prediction objectives, ensuring stable joint optimization and preventing performance degradation observed in direct end-to-end training of complex multimodal systems.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

OneVL integrates a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) with a compact latent token interface and dual auxiliary decoders for multimodal explanation. The approach is structured around a novel architecture and a three-stage training pipeline.

#### 4.1. Model Architecture

1. **Main Vision-Language Model (VLM):** The backbone is Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, comprising a Vision Encoder (ViT), a Visual Projector (MLP Aligner), and a Large Language Model (LLM). All components are initialized from the pretrained checkpoint and are trainable throughout the relevant training stages. The VLM is primarily optimized using a standard next-token prediction objective with a cross-entropy loss ($L_c$) applied to both trajectory answers and latent reasoning tokens.

2. **Latent Token Design:** OneVL introduces two types of specialized latent tokens to carry implicit reasoning:
 - **Language Latent Tokens ($\langle|$latent|$\rangle$):** A fixed sequence of two tokens ($C_t=2$), delimited by start and end markers. These are placed in the assistant response where explicit CoT reasoning would typically appear. Their hidden states, post-LLM processing, encode implicit language-grounded reasoning.
 - **Visual Latent Tokens ($\langle|$latent-vis|$\rangle$):** A fixed sequence of four tokens ($C_v=4$), also delimited. These are positioned before the language latent tokens in the response and are designed to encode spatio-temporal visual reasoning about future scene states.
 To avoid performance degradation, these special tokens are realized using existing vocabulary tokens rather than adding new ones.

3. **Language Auxiliary Decoder ($D_l$):**
 - **Purpose:** To reconstruct human-readable CoT reasoning text from the compact language latent hidden states.
 - **Input:** The hidden states ($H_l$) from the language latent tokens (after LLM processing) are concatenated with the current-frame ViT patch embeddings ($V$). Both are mapped into the decoder's embedding space via an MLP layer ($W_l$).
 - **Training Objective:** A cross-entropy loss ($L_l$) is used to train $D_l$ to predict the ground-truth CoT reasoning text ($T_y_t$), encouraging the main model's language latents to encode rich semantic information.

4. **Visual Auxiliary Decoder ($D_v$):**
 - **Purpose:** To predict anticipated future-frame visual tokens, acting as a world model auxiliary.
 - **Motivation:** Autonomous driving requires spatio-temporal prediction. Predicting future scene states from compact latents forces the main model to encode causal scene dynamics (agent trajectories, road geometry, hazards).
 - **Input:** The hidden states ($H_v$) from the visual latent tokens (after LLM processing) are concatenated with the current-frame ViT embeddings ($V$). Both are mapped via an MLP layer ($W_v$).
 - **Visual Tokenizer:** Images are converted into discrete token sequences using the IBQ (Index Backpropagation Quantization) visual tokenizer (Emu3.5 tokenizer) with a codebook of 131,072 discrete visual codes. The Qwen3-VL-4B vocabulary is extended with these visual token IDs.
 - **Training Objective:** A cross-entropy loss ($L_v$) trains $D_v$ to predict discrete visual token sequences ($T_y_v$) for two future time steps (+0.5s and +1.0s).

5. **Combined Training Objective:** The total training loss is a weighted sum: $L = L_c + \lambda_l L_l + \lambda_v L_v$, where $L_c$ is the main VLM's cross-entropy loss for trajectory and latent tokens, $\lambda_l = 1.0$ for the language loss, and $\lambda_v = 0.1$ for the visual loss (a lower weight reflects the harder nature of visual token reconstruction).

6. **Prefill Inference:** During inference, both auxiliary decoders are discarded to maintain efficiency. The key innovation is that all latent tokens (visual and language) are prefilled into the model's context as fixed token sequences. Modern transformers process the entire prefill in parallel, meaning these additional tokens add negligible overhead compared to sequential autoregressive generation. The model then autoregressively generates only the trajectory tokens. This mechanism aims to achieve inference latency nearly identical to answer-only autoregressive prediction. Optional language and visual explanations can be generated post-hoc using the auxiliary decoders.

#### 4.2. Three-Stage Training Pipeline

To manage the complexity of optimizing multiple components with different objectives, a three-stage training pipeline is employed:

1. **Preliminary: Visual Auxiliary Decoder Self-Supervised Pretraining:** The visual auxiliary decoder ($D_v$) is pretrained independently to predict future-frame visual tokens solely from current-frame ViT embeddings ($V$), without latent token conditioning. This establishes a robust prior for visual dynamics, learning to generate plausible future frames unconditionally.

2. **Stage 0: Main Model Warmup:** The main VLM (ViT, LLM, aligner) is trained end-to-end on the trajectory prediction task. Latent tokens are included in the assistant response during this stage. This warms up the VLM to predict accurate trajectories and, importantly, encourages the latent token positions to encode meaningful intermediate representations useful for prediction, establishing information routing pathways.

3. **Stage 1: Auxiliary Decoder Warmup:** The main VLM is frozen. The language auxiliary decoder ($D_l$) is trained with $L_l$ to decode ground-truth CoT text from the language latent hidden states. Concurrently, the visual auxiliary decoder ($D_v$) is further trained with $L_v$ to predict future frames, now conditioned on both current-frame ViT embeddings and the visual latent token hidden states, effectively becoming an action-conditioned world model. This stage aligns the decoders with the already stable latent representations.

4. **Stage 2: Joint End-to-End Fine-tuning:** All components of OneVL are jointly fine-tuned using the combined loss $L$. Gradients from both $L_l$ and $L_v$ flow back to the main VLM, refining the latent representations to simultaneously serve trajectory prediction, language explanation, and visual prediction. This stage enables a virtuous cycle where richer latents improve prediction, and decoders adapt to the evolving representations.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of OneVL demonstrated its performance across four benchmarks, alongside analyses of explanation quality, ablation studies, and real-world deployment considerations.

#### 5.1. Performance Comparisons

OneVL consistently outperformed various baselines, including previous state-of-the-art methods, AR-based models, and existing latent CoT approaches, in terms of trajectory prediction accuracy while maintaining low inference latency.

- **NAVSIM Benchmark:** OneVL achieved a PDM-score of 88.84, surpassing the previous supervised fine-tuned state-of-the-art methods like AdaThinkDrive (86.20) and LaST-VLA (87.30). Notably, it also outperformed the AR CoT+Answer baseline (88.29).
- **ROADWork Benchmark:** OneVL recorded an ADE of 12.49 pixels and an FDE of 28.80 pixels, significantly outperforming the previous state-of-the-art YNet (22.68 ADE / 80.78 FDE) and the AR CoT+Answer baseline (13.18 ADE / 29.98 FDE).
- **Impromptu Benchmark:** OneVL demonstrated an ADE of 1.34 meters and an FDE of 3.70 meters, outperforming the Impromptu VLA (1.60 ADE / 4.28 FDE) and the AR CoT+Answer baseline (1.42 ADE / 3.96 FDE). It also showed superior L2 trajectory prediction errors across 1s, 2s, 3s, and 4s horizons.
- **Alpamayo-R1 Benchmark:** OneVL achieved an ADE of 2.62 meters, outperforming Cosmos-Reason (2.86 ADE). Its FDE of 7.53 meters was slightly higher than Cosmos-Reason (7.42 FDE), which benefits from additional reinforcement learning.

#### 5.2. Efficiency of Prefill Inference

The prefill inference mechanism proved effective in significantly reducing latency.
- On NAVSIM, OneVL achieved an inference latency of 4.46 seconds, which is comparable to the AR Answer baseline (4.49s) and substantially faster than the AR CoT+Answer baseline (6.58s).
- Similar latency reductions were observed across ROADWork (4.71s vs 10.74s for AR CoT+Answer), Impromptu (4.02s vs 6.84s), and Alpamayo-R1 (3.23s vs 3.51s). This validates the claim that parallel prefilling of latent tokens allows for efficient inference without the overhead of sequential autoregressive generation.

#### 5.3. Limitations of Existing Latent CoT Methods

The study highlighted the shortcomings of adapting existing latent CoT methods (COCONUT, CODI, SIM-CoT) to VLA-based autonomous driving.
- Across all benchmarks, these methods generally performed worse than the AR Answer baseline, with SIM-CoT showing particularly poor performance on Impromptu. This indicates that purely linguistic latent representations are insufficient for the multimodal, spatio-temporal reasoning required for accurate trajectory prediction in driving.

#### 5.4. Explanation Quality

OneVL provides human-interpretable explanations through its dual auxiliary decoders.
- **Text CoT Quality (NAVSIM):** Quantitatively, the language auxiliary decoder achieved a Meta Action Accuracy of 71.00, an STS score of 78.26, and an LLM Judge score of 79.13. These scores surpass SIM-CoT's performance and approach that of the AR CoT+Answer baseline, demonstrating the ability of compressed latents to generate semantically rich and accurate driving rationales.
- **Visual CoT Quality:** The visual auxiliary decoder successfully generated spatially coherent future-frame previews, offering a visual interpretation of the model's anticipation of scene evolution.

#### 5.5. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments confirmed the contribution of each core component to OneVL's performance:
- **Visual Auxiliary Decoder:** Removing the visual auxiliary decoder (OneVL w/o visual dec.) resulted in a 0.87 PDM-score drop on NAVSIM (87.97 vs 88.84 for full OneVL). This indicates that supervision grounding latents in future scene dynamics provides a significant performance boost.
- **Language Auxiliary Decoder:** Removing the language auxiliary decoder (OneVL w/o language dec.) led to a 0.31 PDM-score drop (88.53 vs 88.84). This confirms the positive, albeit smaller, contribution of explicit language-based reasoning compression.
- **Three-Stage Training Pipeline:** This pipeline was identified as essential. Directly training OneVL end-to-end without the staged approach (OneVL w/o staged train) resulted in a catastrophic performance drop of 21.71 PDM-score (67.13 vs 88.84). This failure was attributed to gradient shock and task interference during initial optimization, highlighting the necessity of gradual alignment for stable learning. Visual analysis also showed that the visual decoder in the unstaged setup produced irrelevant, memorized future frames, indicating a failure to generalize.

#### 5.6. Towards Real-World Deployment

A deployment-oriented variant with a compact MLP head for trajectory regression (instead of autoregressive generation by the LLM) demonstrated practical potential:
- This MLP variant achieved an 86.83 PDM-score with an inference latency of 0.24 seconds (approximately 4.16 Hz). While this represents a slight performance trade-off compared to the full autoregressive OneVL (88.84 PDM-score at 4.46s latency), it meets typical on-vehicle frequency requirements and remains competitive with strong prior models, suggesting its viability for real-world application.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The OneVL framework demonstrates a notable advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving, with several implications for research and deployment.

**Bridging Efficiency and Performance in Explainable AI:** OneVL addresses the fundamental tension between the interpretability offered by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and the low-latency requirements of real-time autonomous driving. By becoming the first latent CoT method to surpass explicit autoregressive CoT in accuracy while matching answer-only prediction latency, OneVL provides a solution that combines both high performance and efficiency. This suggests that explicit, verbose reasoning is not a prerequisite for optimal performance and that effective compression can lead to more robust and generalizable representations.

**Advancement in Latent Representation Learning:** The core contribution of OneVL lies in identifying and rectifying a critical limitation of prior latent CoT methods for planning tasks: their reliance on purely linguistic representations, which abstract the world symbolically rather than capturing its causal dynamics. By introducing a visual world model auxiliary (future-frame prediction), OneVL grounds the latent representations in the physical, spatio-temporal dynamics of the driving environment. This multimodal supervision ensures that the compressed latents encode genuinely causal information, rather than superficial correlations or abstract linguistic summaries, thereby enhancing the intelligence and transferability of these representations.

**Robust Training Methodologies:** The proposed three-stage training pipeline is shown to be crucial for successful joint optimization of the complex OneVL architecture. Its ability to progressively align the main VLM and the dual auxiliary decoders prevents catastrophic performance drops observed in direct end-to-end training. This methodology offers a template for training other complex multimodal systems where different learning objectives and component states need careful orchestration.

**Enhanced Interpretability for Safety-Critical Systems:** OneVL's ability to provide both linguistic (CoT text) and visual (future-frame previews) explanations from compact latent tokens is significant for safety-critical applications. This multimodal interpretability can aid in debugging, safety auditing, and building trust in autonomous systems by allowing humans to understand the vehicle's reasoning and anticipated actions.

**Potential for Real-World Deployment:** The demonstration of a deployment-oriented variant with an MLP regression head, achieving high performance at very low latency (0.24 seconds), indicates a pathway for practical integration into autonomous vehicles. This suggests that OneVL's principles could lead to robust, efficient, and explainable driving systems.

**Future Research Directions:** The work opens several avenues for future research, including:
- **Optimizing Trajectory Decoding:** Exploring parallel or non-autoregressive methods for trajectory decoding to further reduce overall latency, as current limitations are still tied to autoregressive waypoint generation.
- **Advanced World Modeling:** Extending the visual world model decoder to multi-camera inputs for comprehensive 360-degree future-scene prediction, enhancing the causal grounding of latents.
- **Human-Machine Interaction:** Leveraging the dual-modal explanation framework to design novel human-machine interfaces for real-time justification of planning decisions, potentially aiding in reinforcement learning by providing richer training signals.

In summary, OneVL represents a significant step towards developing autonomous driving systems that are simultaneously accurate, efficient, and interpretable, by effectively integrating world model supervision into compact latent reasoning.
