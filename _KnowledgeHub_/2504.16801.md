# Decoupled Global-Local Alignment for Improving Compositional Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Decoupled Global-Local Alignment (DeGLA), enhances compositional understanding in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by leveraging LLM-driven hard negative samples and a self-distillation mechanism. This approach improves compositional reasoning on benchmarks like SugarCrepe by up to 6.9% over prior state-of-the-art, while preserving general capabilities with only a minor reduction in zero-shot classification accuracy compared to original CLIP and outperforming CE-CLIP by 13.0%.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP, relying on global contrastive learning, struggle to interpret compositional concepts (e.g., relations, attributes, element order) in image-text pairs.
- Prior methods to improve compositional understanding often cause catastrophic forgetting, leading to a significant degradation of the model's general capabilities.
- There is a critical trade-off between achieving fine-grained compositional reasoning and maintaining broad general comprehension in VLMs.

## Method

- ==DeGLA== introduces an LLM-driven pipeline to generate diverse, high-quality hard negative captions for fine-grained compositional training, categorized by intra-sentence reshuffling and semantic substitution.
- A ==decoupled alignment strategy== combines a global hard negative-aware contrastive loss with local ==Image-Grounded Contrast (IGC)== and ==Text-Grounded Contrast (TGC)== losses, enhancing specific compositional understanding.
- A ==self-distillation mechanism==, using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher, regularizes the student model's training to preserve foundational pre-trained knowledge and mitigate catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- DeGLA achieved state-of-the-art compositional performance on VALSE, SugarCrepe (e.g., **6.9%** improvement on SugarCrepe Swap over CE-CLIP), and ARO benchmarks.
- The framework successfully preserved general capabilities, with zero-shot classification accuracy of **58.7%** (compared to CLIP's **61.0%**), marking a **13.0%** improvement over CE-CLIP.
- DeGLA outperformed other compositional methods in zero-shot image-text retrieval tasks on MSCOCO and Flickr30k datasets, indicating no compromise on general retrieval accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models are effective for generating high-quality and diverse hard negative samples that are crucial for learning fine-grained compositional distinctions.
> - Achieving a balance between compositional understanding and general capabilities requires a decoupled approach that combines global and local alignment with a self-distillation mechanism.
> - The use of a frozen EMA teacher embedding in a text-grounded contrastive loss helps enhance the text encoder's discriminative power against compositional negatives while maintaining overall semantic alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the rapidly evolving field of **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, specifically building upon the foundational work of **Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP)**. CLIP has revolutionized multimodal understanding by effectively aligning image and text representations through global contrastive learning on vast internet-scale datasets. This has enabled impressive zero-shot generalization capabilities across a wide array of downstream tasks, from image classification to object detection and segmentation.

However, the authors correctly identify a crucial limitation of CLIP: its inherent **global contrastive learning** approach often falls short in comprehending **compositional concepts**, such as complex relations between objects, specific attributes, or the precise order of elements within a sentence. For instance, CLIP might struggle to differentiate between "a cat *on* a mat" and "a mat *on* a cat," or "a *red* apple" and a "green apple" if the global features are too similar. This limitation restricts VLMs from achieving truly fine-grained semantic understanding.

Previous research efforts, such as NegCLIP, Structure-CLIP, and CE-CLIP, have attempted to address this compositional gap primarily by incorporating **hard negative samples** during fine-tuning. These hard negatives are designed to be semantically similar to positive samples but with subtle compositional differences, forcing the model to learn finer distinctions. While these methods have shown success in improving compositional understanding, the authors highlight a critical trade-off: these improvements often come at the significant cost of **compromising the model's inherent general capabilities**, leading to "catastrophic forgetting" of the broad knowledge acquired during initial pre-training. This loss in generalizability is a major drawback, as it limits the model's versatility across diverse tasks.

DeGLA positions itself as a direct response to this trade-off. It aims to develop a framework that not only enhances compositional understanding but also **substantially mitigates the loss of general capabilities**. By doing so, this work pushes the boundaries of VLM development towards models that are both compositionally aware and generally robust, offering a more balanced and versatile solution than previous approaches. It also leverages the recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) for data generation, integrating state-of-the-art NLP capabilities into multimodal training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are twofold:

1. **To significantly improve the compositional understanding of Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) models.** This involves enabling VLMs to better grasp fine-grained semantic nuances such as attributes (e.g., "red," "fluffy"), relations (e.g., "on," "under," "chasing"), and the order of elements within visual-linguistic descriptions.
2. **To achieve this improvement while simultaneously mitigating the detrimental effects of catastrophic forgetting on the model's inherent general capabilities.** The authors specifically aim to prevent the degradation of performance on standard vision-language tasks (like zero-shot classification and retrieval) that has plagued prior methods focused solely on compositional improvements.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the observed limitations of existing VLMs and the shortcomings of current compositional improvement techniques:

- **CLIP's "Bag-of-Words" Problem:** CLIP, due to its global contrastive nature, tends to treat image-text pairs as "bags of words" or global concepts, overlooking the intricate interplay of objects, attributes, and actions. This prevents it from accurately interpreting complex descriptions, as illustrated by its inability to distinguish subtle changes in captions (e.g., swapping subjects/objects).
- **Unacceptable Trade-off in Prior Work:** While methods like NegCLIP and CE-CLIP have advanced compositional understanding, their approaches often lead to a considerable decline in the model's general performance. This trade-off renders such models less practical for broad applications where both specific compositional understanding and general domain knowledge are required.
- **Demand for Robust and Versatile VLMs:** The increasing complexity of real-world multimodal tasks necessitates VLMs that are not only capable of detailed scene understanding but also maintain strong generalization abilities across a wide range of common visual and linguistic phenomena.

Therefore, the core motivation is to overcome the fundamental challenge of achieving a **harmonious balance** between enhancing specific compositional reasoning and preserving broad general comprehension in VLMs, thereby developing more intelligent and versatile multimodal AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Decoupled Global-Local Alignment (DeGLA)** framework introduces a multi-faceted approach to simultaneously enhance compositional understanding and preserve general capabilities. It consists of three main components: an LLM-driven negative caption generation pipeline, a global alignment process with self-distillation, and a local alignment mechanism.

#### 3.1. LLM-Driven Negative Caption Generation

To improve compositional understanding, DeGLA first addresses the need for high-quality, diverse hard negative samples. Unlike previous rule-based or unmasking-based methods, which suffered from limited complexity or propensity to generate hard positives, DeGLA leverages the in-context learning capabilities of **Large Language Models (LLMs)**.
- **Process:** The authors first use ChatGPT4-Turbo to generate 200 high-quality rewritten examples for each of five predefined negative types. These examples are then manually filtered to select the 50 best ones. Subsequently, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is employed to generate a large-scale dataset of approximately 2 million negative captions based on these high-quality examples and specific rewrite rules.
- **Negative Types:** The negative captions are categorized into two primary types to challenge different aspects of compositional understanding:
 - **Intra-sentence Reshuffling:** Includes full sentence reordering, noun swapping, and adjective swapping. This forces the model to be sensitive to syntactic structure and element order.
 - **Minimal Semantic Substitution:** Involves adjective replacement and noun replacement. This pushes the model to discern subtle semantic variations in descriptions.
- **Quality Control:** Crucially, prompts for the LLMs include explicit filtering constraints (e.g., "generated sentences must exhibit distinct semantics") to ensure the generated negatives are truly "hard negatives" rather than "hard positives" (i.e., semantically different enough to be negative, but visually similar enough to be challenging).

#### 3.2. Global Alignment with Self-Distillation

To mitigate catastrophic forgetting and retain the model's general capabilities, DeGLA integrates a **self-distillation mechanism** within the global alignment process.
- **Base Loss (L_base):** This is the standard hard negative-aware image-text contrastive loss (similar to CLIP's InfoNCE loss, but augmented with the generated hard negative samples). It aims to align positive image-text pairs while pushing away hard negative text samples from images in the global embedding space.
- **EMA Teacher Model:** An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) strategy is used to maintain a "teacher" version of the image and text encoders (E∗_I, E∗_T). These teacher models are updated slowly from the learnable "student" encoders (E_I, E_T) with a hyperparameter α (set to 0.9996). The EMA teacher remains largely stable and reflects the pre-trained knowledge.
- **Distillation Loss (L_Distill):** This loss minimizes the L2 squared distance between the embeddings produced by the learnable student encoders and the frozen EMA teacher encoders for images, positive texts, and negative texts. By constraining the student's embeddings to stay close to the teacher's, L_Distill effectively regularizes the training, ensuring that the model refines its understanding without drastically deviating from its foundational pre-trained knowledge.

#### 3.3. Local Alignment

Beyond global alignment, DeGLA introduces two local alignment losses to specifically enhance fine-grained compositional understanding.
- **Image-Grounded Contrast (IGC) Loss (L_IGC):** This loss operates from the perspective of an image anchor. It attracts the image embedding (v_i) towards its corresponding positive text embedding (t_i) and repels it from its hard negative text embeddings (t_i,k) in the feature space. This directly forces the image encoder to learn to differentiate images based on subtle textual differences.
- **Text-Grounded Contrast (TGC) Loss (L_TGC):** This loss focuses solely on the text modality. It uses the *frozen* EMA text embedding (t*i) as an anchor to attract the learnable text embedding (t_i) and repel hard negative text embeddings (t_i,k). This component enhances the text encoder's ability to discriminate between compositionally similar but semantically different texts. Using the frozen EMA embedding as an anchor prevents the text encoder from overfitting to the fine-tuning data and ensures its compositional reasoning aligns with the robust, pre-trained semantic space.

#### 3.4. Overall Loss Function

The final objective function combines all these components:
L_all = L_Base + λ1*L_IGC + λ2*L_TGC + λ3*L_Distill, where λ1, λ2, and λ3 are hyperparameters used to balance the influence of each loss term.

**Training Details:** The model used is CLIP-ViT/B-32, fine-tuned on the MSCOCO dataset with a batch size of 256 for 5 epochs. AdamW optimizer is used, and hyperparameters for loss weights (λ1=0.1, λ2=0.1, λ3=0.005) were optimized.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results comprehensively demonstrate the effectiveness of the DeGLA framework across both compositional reasoning and general understanding tasks, validating its central claim of improving compositionality without significant general capability loss.

#### 4.1. Compositional Reasoning Performance

DeGLA consistently achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance on standard compositional benchmarks:
- **VALSE:** DeGLA outperforms prior models in categories like "existence," "counting," and "spatial relations," and ranks second in others. It shows an average performance improvement of **8.8% over original CLIP** and **1.9% over CE-CLIP**, the previous state-of-the-art, highlighting its enhanced ability to leverage hard negatives and fine-grained alignment.
- **SugarCrepe:** This benchmark, designed to reduce language bias, sees DeGLA setting new state-of-the-art records across all metrics (Replace, Swap, Add). DeGLA shows improvements of **3.5% on Replace, 6.9% on Swap, and 1.9% on Add over CE-CLIP**. These substantial gains are attributed to the high-quality, LLM-generated negative captions and the effective image-grounded and text-grounded contrast mechanisms.
- **ARO:** DeGLA achieves a remarkable average performance increase of **28.7% over CLIP** and **4.9% over CE-CLIP**. While slightly trailing in "relations" and "attributes," its overall superior performance is significant, which the authors attribute to the diversity of their negative samples.

#### 4.2. General Understanding Performance

Crucially, DeGLA succeeds in preserving general capabilities, directly addressing the trade-off identified in prior work:
- **Zero-shot Classification (11 datasets):** While previous compositional methods like Structure-CLIP and CE-CLIP led to significant drops in average accuracy (17.2% and 15.3% reduction respectively compared to CLIP), DeGLA achieves an average accuracy of **58.7%**, which is only slightly lower than the original CLIP's 61.0% and a substantial **13.0% improvement over CE-CLIP**. This demonstrates the effectiveness of the self-distillation mechanism in retaining pre-trained knowledge.
- **Linear Probe (11 datasets):** Similar to zero-shot classification, DeGLA shows superior retention of general capabilities, achieving a **2.3% average performance improvement over CE-CLIP**, further confirming its robustness.
- **Zero-shot Image-Text Retrieval (MSCOCO, Flickr30k):** DeGLA surpasses Structure-CLIP and CE-CLIP on both datasets in image-to-text and text-to-image retrieval tasks, indicating that its compositional enhancements do not come at the expense of general retrieval accuracy.

#### 4.3. Trade-off Analysis

A dedicated analysis (Figure 5) visually confirms that DeGLA achieves a much better balance between compositional reasoning and general comprehension compared to other methods, particularly CE-CLIP. It significantly improves compositional scores while minimizing the reduction in zero-shot classification performance.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirm the individual contributions of each component:
- Integrating LLM-generated high-quality negative samples drastically boosts ARO performance but also causes a notable drop in zero-shot classification if not properly managed.
- The IGC and TGC losses incrementally improve ARO performance.
- The **self-distillation mechanism is pivotal** for general capability retention, improving zero-shot classification by 1.8% with only a negligible 0.1% loss in ARO performance.
- The combination of all modules yields the most balanced and highest overall performance.
- Ablation on negative sample types validates that combining all five LLM-generated subtypes leads to optimal compositional understanding.

#### 4.5. Case Studies

Qualitative examples (Figure 7) illustrate DeGLA's ability to overcome CLIP's "bag-of-words" problem. DeGLA correctly differentiates images based on subtle textual changes like word order swaps or noun substitutions (e.g., distinguishing "two ducks swim... with green water" from "two swans swim... with green ducks"), where CLIP fails.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Decoupled Global-Local Alignment" framework presented in this paper represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models, with broad implications for future research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical Trade-off

The most profound significance of DeGLA lies in its successful resolution of the long-standing trade-off between enhancing compositional understanding and preserving general capabilities in VLMs. By demonstrating that substantial improvements in fine-grained reasoning can be achieved without catastrophic forgetting, the authors pave the way for the development of more versatile and robust multimodal AI systems. This is a crucial step towards models that can handle both specific, detailed queries and broad, open-ended tasks simultaneously.

#### 5.2. Novel Methodological Contributions

The paper introduces several innovative methodological elements:
- **LLM-driven Negative Caption Generation:** This pipeline is a powerful and flexible method for creating high-quality, diverse, and challenging hard negative samples. By leveraging the advanced semantic understanding of LLMs and incorporating careful filtering, it overcomes the limitations of previous rule-based or unmasking approaches. This technique could be adopted in other multimodal learning scenarios requiring fine-grained data augmentation.
- **Decoupled Global-Local Alignment:** The idea of explicitly separating and optimizing global (general) and local (compositional) alignments, combined with self-distillation, provides a principled framework for managing complex training objectives.
- **Text-Grounded Contrast with EMA Anchor:** The novel application of TGC loss, particularly with a frozen EMA text embedding as an anchor, offers an elegant solution to enhance the text encoder's discriminative power against compositional negatives while preventing overfitting and maintaining alignment with the pre-trained semantic space.

#### 5.3. Enhanced VLM Utility

The improved compositional understanding offered by DeGLA directly translates to more intelligent and reliable VLMs for various applications. Models trained with DeGLA can:
- **Better interpret complex image descriptions:** Crucial for applications like visual question answering, detailed image search, and human-computer interaction where precise language comprehension is vital.
- **More accurately identify fine-grained visual details:** Such as specific attributes ("blue car" vs. "red car"), spatial relationships ("dog *under* the table" vs. "dog *on* the table"), and actions ("person *eating* food" vs. "person *cooking* food").
- **Support more robust content moderation and generation:** By understanding the nuanced meanings of image-text pairs, AI systems can better identify problematic content or generate more contextually appropriate descriptions.

#### 5.4. Foundation for Future Research

DeGLA provides a robust foundation for future research in multimodal learning. It encourages further exploration into:
- **Hybrid training strategies:** Combining pre-training objectives with specialized fine-tuning techniques to achieve optimal balance.
- **Advanced negative sampling:** Developing even more sophisticated methods for generating hard negatives, potentially incorporating multimodal feedback.
- **Scaling DeGLA to larger models and datasets:** Investigating how this framework performs with increasingly powerful VLMs and diverse data sources.
- **Application to other multimodal tasks:** Exploring its benefits for video-language understanding, multimodal reasoning, and embodied AI.

In conclusion, "Decoupled Global-Local Alignment for Improving Compositional Understanding" makes a crucial contribution by offering a highly effective and balanced approach to VLM training. By strategically combining LLM-driven data augmentation, self-distillation for knowledge preservation, and decoupled alignment losses, DeGLA pushes the capabilities of VLMs towards truly intelligent and versatile multimodal understanding.
