# Progressive End-to-End Object Detection in Crowded Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from MEGVII Technology, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and the University of Hong Kong developed a progressive prediction method for end-to-end object detection in crowded scenes. This approach enhances query-based detectors by mitigating duplicate predictions and overcoming performance saturation, yielding significant improvements in detection accuracy across various crowded datasets.

## Problem

- Existing query-based object detectors frequently produce multiple bounding box predictions for a single object instance in crowded scenes, leading to redundant detections and false positives.
- Increasing the depth of decoding stages in current query-based detectors often leads to performance saturation or degradation in crowded environments, rather than consistent improvements.
- Standard object detection methods struggle with high-density scenes due to heavy object overlap, which complicates accurate instance differentiation and label assignment.

## Method

- A ==progressive prediction method== integrated into the last decoding stage of query-based detectors, which divides predictions into 'accepted' (high confidence) and 'noisy' (low confidence) sets.
- A ==relation information extractor== that enables noisy queries to 'perceive' confidently detected objects by generating geometry relation features from accepted neighbors.
- A ==Local Multi-head Self-Attention (LMSA)== mechanism for noisy queries, fostering local interactions, combined with a ==two-step progressive label assignment== that prioritizes high-confidence detections.

## Results

- On the heavily crowded CrowdHuman dataset, the method achieved **9**2.0%** AP**, **4**1.4%** MR -2**, and **8**3.2%** JI** with a ResNet-50 Sparse R-CNN, representing a **+**1.3%** AP**, **-**3.3%** MR -2**, and **+**1.8%** JI** gain over the baseline.
- The approach is generic, demonstrating improvements when applied to Deformable DETR, achieving **9**2.1%** AP** and **8**4.0%** JI** on CrowdHuman, and consistent gains on moderately (CityPersons) and slightly crowded (COCO) datasets.
- Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of the relation information extractor and local self-attention in reducing duplicate errors, improving localization accuracy, and decreasing background errors compared to the baseline Sparse R-CNN.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-confidence predictions from query-based detectors are reliable true positives, even in crowded settings, which can be leveraged to guide the refinement of lower-confidence predictions.
> - Enabling noisy queries to understand whether their target objects have already been confidently detected by other queries can effectively reduce redundant predictions.
> - Local self-attention among neighboring predictions is more effective than global attention for resolving ambiguities and reducing duplicate detections in crowded scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper positions itself within the highly dynamic field of object detection, with a specific focus on the challenging domain of crowded scenes. The broader landscape of object detection has seen a significant evolution, moving from early methods reliant on hand-crafted features and extensive post-processing steps (like Non-Maximum Suppression, NMS) to modern end-to-end learning paradigms.

Historically, object detection models were largely categorized into two-stage (e.g., Faster R-CNN, FPN) and one-stage detectors (e.g., RetinaNet, YOLO, ATSS). While these methods achieved impressive performance, they often relied on pre-defined anchors and subsequent NMS, which is particularly problematic in crowded scenarios where objects heavily overlap. The NMS heuristic, designed to suppress redundant bounding boxes, can inadvertently remove legitimate detections or struggle to differentiate between tightly packed instances. Several works attempted to address this, including methods like Soft-NMS, Softer-NMS, Adaptive NMS, and specialized approaches like Multiple Instance Prediction (MIP), which focused on refining the post-processing or generating multiple predictions per proposal to handle occlusion.

A significant paradigm shift occurred with the introduction of **DETR (DEtection TRansformer)**, which utilized a transformer architecture to achieve truly end-to-end object detection, eliminating the need for hand-crafted anchors and post-processing like NMS. DETR frames object detection as a set prediction problem, directly predicting a fixed-size set of unique object detections. This "query-based" approach, where learnable object queries directly predict object bounding boxes, represented a new frontier. However, DETR had its own limitations, including slow convergence and high computational costs.

Subsequent research rapidly improved upon DETR. **Deformable DETR** addressed the convergence and computational issues by introducing deformable attention, focusing on a sparse set of sampling points instead of global attention. **Sparse R-CNN** also adopted learnable queries but integrated them with ROI (Region of Interest) features, drawing inspiration from R-CNN style detectors to ensure queries interact with local features. These query-based methods have achieved competitive performance on standard datasets like COCO.

However, as highlighted by the authors, even these advanced query-based detectors still face unresolved challenges specifically in **crowded scenes**: they tend to infer multiple predictions for a single object (introducing false positives) and their performance can saturate or even degrade as the decoding stage deepens. This paper directly addresses these two critical shortcomings. It builds upon the foundation of query-based detectors like Sparse R-CNN and Deformable DETR, aiming to enhance their robustness and accuracy in highly cluttered environments without sacrificing their end-to-end nature.

Furthermore, the work touches upon previous attempts at **relation modeling** in object detection (e.g., RelationNet, GossipNet) and **progressive/iterative detection** (e.g., PS-RCNN, IterDet). The paper differentiates its approach by offering a novel progressive strategy integrated directly into the decoding process of query-based detectors, aiming for a more robust and end-to-end solution compared to previous iterative methods that often still required post-processing or introduced noise. By tackling the fundamental limitations of query-based detectors in crowded scenes, this research significantly contributes to making end-to-end detection more reliable and applicable in complex real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a more sophisticated and robust end-to-end object detection framework specifically designed to excel in **crowded scenes**, leveraging the strengths of recent query-based detection methodologies.

The authors meticulously motivate their work by identifying two critical limitations of existing query-based detectors (such as Sparse R-CNN and Deformable DETR) when confronted with high-density, occluded environments:

1. **Redundant Predictions and False Positives:** In crowded scenes, query-based detectors frequently infer multiple bounding box predictions for a single object instance. This redundancy leads to a proliferation of false positives, cluttering the detection results and significantly degrading overall performance. Figure 1a in the paper visually illustrates this common failure mode, where a single person is erroneously detected multiple times. This issue is particularly detrimental in crowded settings where precise instance differentiation is crucial.

2. **Performance Saturation or Degradation with Deeper Decoding Stages:** Counterintuitively, the authors' initial investigations revealed that simply increasing the depth of the decoding stage in query-based detectors does not consistently improve performance in crowded scenes; instead, it often leads to saturation or even a decline. This suggests that the detectors struggle to effectively refine predictions or resolve ambiguities when presented with more processing stages in such challenging contexts (as further detailed in Appendix B).

Driven by these observations, the authors conducted further investigations into Sparse R-CNN's behavior in crowded scenes. They discovered an intriguing pattern: predictions with high confidence scores (e.g., above 0.7) are highly likely to be true positives and contain very few false positives. These "accepted predictions" accurately identify a large percentage of target objects. Conversely, predictions with lower confidence scores ("noisy predictions") constitute a mix of both true positives and a substantial number of false positives.

This insight forms the core motivation for their proposed **progressive prediction method**. The central idea is to empower the "noisy queries" to "perceive" whether their target objects have already been reliably detected by "accepted queries." If an object has been confidently identified, there's no need for the noisy queries to attempt to detect it again. This discriminatory capability would allow noisy queries to reduce their confidence scores for already-detected objects, thereby filtering out redundant detections and improving overall accuracy. The overarching goal is to achieve a scenario where "each object can be detected only once," significantly reducing false positives and increasing true positives, as illustrated in Figure 1b and Figure 2b, which demonstrate consistent performance improvements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Progressive End-to-End Object Detection** framework is designed as an enhancement module that integrates into the *last decoding stage* of existing query-based object detectors, such as Sparse R-CNN and Deformable DETR. This strategic integration allows the method to refine predictions only after several initial decoding stages have processed the scene.

The methodology comprises four key components:

1. **Prediction Selector:**
 - **Function:** At the beginning of the last decoding stage, this component divides the predictions generated from the *previous stage* (t-1) into two sets: "accepted predictions" ($D^h_{t-1}$) and "noisy predictions" ($D^l_{t-1}$).
 - **Mechanism:** Predictions with confidence scores greater than or equal to a predefined threshold `s` (defaulting to 0.7) are classified as accepted. The remaining predictions are designated as noisy. This segmentation directly leverages the observation that high-confidence predictions are typically true positives.

2. **Relation Information Extractor:**
 - **Motivation:** To enable noisy queries to "perceive" if their targets have already been detected by accepted queries.
 - **Process:** For each noisy prediction $b_i$ (from $D^l_{t-1}$), the extractor identifies its "accepted neighbors" $N(b_i)$ from the set of accepted predictions $D^h_{t-1}$. This neighborhood is defined by an Intersection-over-Union (IoU) overlap greater than a threshold $\theta$ (defaulting to 0.4).
 - **Feature Generation:** The spatially-related pairs $(b_i, N(b_i))$ are then encoded using sine and cosine spatial positional encoding, similar to existing relation modeling techniques. These encoded features, along with their IoU values, are fed into a compact neural network (two FC layers with ReLU) to generate "geometry relation features" $H(b_i)$.
 - **Aggregation and Fusion:** Since the number of accepted neighbors can vary, an aggregation function (max pooling) is applied to $H(b_i)$ to produce a fixed-dimension representation. This pooled geometry feature is then fused via element-wise summation with a transformed feature of the noisy query itself, activated by another non-linear function, to produce the final "relation features" $R(b_i)$. Crucially, gradients for $q_i$ are detached from propagating backward to previous stages, ensuring stability.

3. **Query Updater:**
 - **Function:** This module refines the features of the noisy queries based on the relation information.
 - **Mechanism:** Each noisy query $q_i$ is updated by summing its corresponding relation feature $R(b_i)$ with a new, learnable embedding $e_i$. These "complemented noisy queries" then serve as the input to a modified self-attention mechanism.
 - **Local Self-Attention (LMSA):** Instead of the global Multi-head Self-Attention (MSA) used in standard query-based detectors, a **Local Multi-head Self-Attention (LMSA)** is employed. LMSA ensures that each query only interacts with its *local neighbors* (defined by IoU overlap > 0 among predictions) rather than all queries in the image. This local interaction is more computationally efficient and more relevant for resolving ambiguities in crowded contexts, preventing redundant information flow from distant, unrelated objects. The LMSA replaces the MSA in the standard decoding block (e.g., Figure 4b).

4. **Label Assignment (Progressive One-to-One Rule):**
 - **Motivation:** Standard one-to-one label assignment (e.g., Hungarian algorithm) can struggle in crowded scenes by not prioritizing reliable detections. The progressive approach aims to ensure each ground truth object is matched optimally.
 - **Two-step Matching:**
 1. First, the "accepted predictions" ($D^h_t$) from the current (last) stage are matched with the full set of ground truth objects using Hungarian algorithm.
 2. Next, the *refined noisy predictions* ($D^l_t$) are matched only with the *remaining* ground truth objects that have not yet been assigned. This prioritizes the high-confidence detections and then allocates the refined noisy predictions to previously un-detected targets.
 - **Matching Cost:** The matching cost calculation for the Hungarian algorithm includes a spatial prior: the center of a predicted bounding box must fall within its corresponding target box, in addition to the standard cost components (e.g., classification, L1, GIoU loss).
 - **Box Prediction and Loss:** For the last stage, the box prediction directly uses identity mapping from the bounding boxes predicted at the previous stage (t-1), which is observed to be stable in later layers. The recognition branch, however, remains active. The training loss uses the standard set prediction loss but with modifications to mitigate class imbalance by removing samples from accepted predictions and applying negative sample filtering.

By implementing these components within the last decoding stage, the method progressively refines noisy predictions by leveraging the context of confidently detected objects, effectively mitigating false positives and improving detection accuracy in crowded scenes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments across heavily, moderately, and slightly crowded datasets to validate the effectiveness and generality of their proposed progressive prediction method.

#### 4.1. Experiments on CrowdHuman (Heavily Crowded)

- **Superior Performance:** The proposed method significantly outperforms state-of-the-art box-based, point-based, and existing query-based detectors. Equipped with the progressive approach, Sparse R-CNN (S-RCNN) with a ResNet-50 backbone and 500 queries achieves 92.0% AP, 41.4% MR$^{-2}$ (Miss Rate at 10$^{-2}$ FPPI), and 83.2% JI (Jaccard Index).
- **Significant Gains over Baselines:** This represents a substantial improvement over the original Sparse R-CNN which achieved 90.7% AP, 44.7% MR$^{-2}$, and 81.4% JI. Specifically, the method yields +1.3% AP, -3.3% MR$^{-2}$, and +1.8% JI. The reduction in MR$^{-2}$ is particularly indicative of improved recall in crowded scenarios.
- **Outperforming Specialized Methods:** The approach surpasses MIP (Multiple Instance Prediction), a box-based method specifically designed for crowded scenes, which achieved 90.7% AP and 82.4% JI. This highlights the effectiveness of the end-to-end progressive strategy.
- **Generalizability:** The method is shown to be generic; when applied to Deformable DETR, it also yields notable improvements, achieving 92.1% AP, 41.5% MR$^{-2}$, and 84.0% JI, demonstrating its robustness across different query-based architectures.
- **Scaling with Queries:** Increasing the number of queries for S-RCNN from 500 to 750 further boosts performance to 92.5% AP and 83.3% JI, indicating that the method can leverage more query capacity effectively.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (on CrowdHuman)

- **Effectiveness of Components:**
 - **Relation Information Extractor (R):** The inclusion of 'R' alone improves AP by 0.8%, MR$^{-2}$ by 1.7%, and JI by 1.6% over the baseline Sparse R-CNN. This confirms its role in reducing false positives and improving recall.
 - **Local Self-Attention (LMSA):** Adding LMSA further boosts performance, achieving 92.0% AP, 42.0% MR$^{-2}$, and 83.5% JI. This shows its effectiveness in reducing duplicate detections by enabling local interactions.
 - **Newly Initialized Embeddings (E):** Provides a slight additional improvement in MR$^{-2}$, indicating better approximation of noisy prediction data distribution.
- **Hyper-parameter Robustness:**
 - **Confidence Score Threshold `s`:** The method is largely robust to changes in `s` (default 0.7), showing consistent performance across a range of values (0.5 to 0.9), with slight improvements as `s` increases.
 - **IoU Threshold `θ` for Neighbors:** The approach demonstrates robustness to variations in `θ` (default 0.4), suggesting that the designed components effectively handle different definitions of "neighborhood."
- **Comparison with Previous Relation Modeling:** Our approach significantly outperforms older relation modeling methods like RelationNet, GossipNet, and IterDet, which showed performance degradation in crowded scenes, attributed by the authors to sub-optimal label assignment and handling of coordinate predictions in those methods.
- **Error Analysis (TIDE):** Using TIDE analysis, the method significantly reduces errors related to **duplicates**, improves **localization accuracy**, and decreases **background errors** (false positives from background), and **missing objects** (false negatives), compared to Sparse R-CNN. This directly supports the claims of reducing false positives and increasing true positives in crowded scenes.

#### 4.3. Experiments on CityPersons (Moderately Crowded)

- The method demonstrates consistent gains on the CityPersons dataset, which is moderately crowded. Sparse R-CNN + Ours achieves 7.8% MR$^{-2}$ and 97.6% AP, outperforming both baseline S-RCNN (10.0% MR$^{-2}$, 96.8% AP) and Deformable DETR (9.4% MR$^{-2}$, 96.6% AP). This confirms its robustness beyond extremely dense scenarios.

#### 4.4. Experiments on COCO (Slightly Crowded/General)

- Despite COCO being a multi-class dataset with low crowdedness, the progressive method still yields moderate but consistent improvements. Sparse R-CNN + Ours achieves 46.1% AP, which is 1.1% higher than baseline S-RCNN (45.0% AP). Deformable DETR + Ours achieves 46.7% AP, a 0.9% gain over baseline D-DETR (45.8% AP). These results prove the method's generality, its ability to handle multi-class detection, and its effectiveness in mitigating the performance saturation problem of query-based detectors even in less crowded settings.

#### 4.5. Performance with Large Models (Appendix C)

- When equipped with a stronger backbone like Swin-Large, the method further pushes the state-of-the-art on both CrowdHuman and CityPersons, achieving 93.8% AP and 37.4% MR$^{-2}$ on CrowdHuman with S-RCNN, and 98.4% AP and 4.9% MR$^{-2}$ on CityPersons with S-RCNN. This suggests that the proposed progressive strategy effectively scales with more powerful base detectors.

In summary, the experimental results unequivocally demonstrate that the progressive prediction method is highly effective in boosting the performance of query-based detectors across diverse crowdedness levels, primarily by tackling the issues of duplicate predictions and performance saturation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Progressive End-to-End Object Detection in Crowded Scenes" paper introduces a highly significant and impactful contribution to the field of computer vision, particularly within object detection.

#### 5.1. Advancement in End-to-End Object Detection

The most profound significance lies in its direct and effective resolution of critical limitations inherent in state-of-the-art query-based object detectors (like DETR, Sparse R-CNN, Deformable DETR) when applied to challenging crowded scenes. By mitigating the tendency to produce multiple predictions for a single object and overcoming performance saturation with deeper decoding stages, this work brings the promise of truly end-to-end detection closer to practical reality in complex environments. It enhances the robustness and reliability of these modern architectures in scenarios where traditional methods struggle due to NMS limitations.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Detection Accuracy and Robustness in Crowded Environments

The consistent and substantial performance gains observed across heavily (CrowdHuman), moderately (CityPersons), and even slightly crowded (COCO) datasets underscore the method's versatility and robustness. The significant reduction in Miss Rate (MR$^{-2}$) and improvement in Average Precision (AP) and Jaccard Index (JI) on CrowdHuman highlight its ability to precisely localize and distinguish individual instances within dense groups. This is a crucial step towards more accurate and reliable detection systems, especially where objects are occluded or tightly packed.

#### 5.3. Generic and Modular Enhancement

The proposed method is designed as a modular enhancement that can be seamlessly integrated into the last decoding stage of various existing query-based detectors. This "plug-and-play" nature makes it a highly valuable and easily adoptable contribution for researchers and practitioners working with transformer-based or query-based detection models. It avoids the need for a complete architectural overhaul, promoting efficient innovation.

#### 5.4. Practical Implications for Real-World Applications

The ability to accurately detect objects in crowded scenes has immense practical value for numerous applications:
- **Autonomous Driving:** Improved pedestrian and vehicle detection in urban, high-traffic scenarios.
- **Surveillance and Security:** More reliable identification and tracking of individuals in crowded public spaces for safety and anomaly detection.
- **Crowd Analysis and Management:** Enhanced tools for monitoring crowd density, flow, and potential safety hazards.
- **Retail Analytics:** Better understanding of customer behavior in busy stores.

By making object detection more robust in these challenging settings, the work directly contributes to building safer and smarter AI systems.

#### 5.5. Insights into Transformer-based Models

The findings related to performance saturation with deeper decoders offer valuable insights into the behavior of transformer-based architectures in specific scenarios. The progressive prediction method's success in overcoming this suggests that targeted, context-aware refinement strategies are more beneficial than simply adding more layers, guiding future architectural designs for similar models.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The paper also constructively identifies areas for future exploration, such as improving computational efficiency of query-based detectors and refining the decision boundary for noisy queries through better feature engineering or loss functions. These explicit acknowledgments of remaining challenges will likely stimulate further research in these critical areas.

#### 5.7. Open-Source Contribution

The commitment to making the code publicly available will significantly accelerate research and development in this area, allowing other researchers to build upon and extend this work.

In conclusion, this research provides a powerful and practical solution to a long-standing challenge in object detection. By enabling query-based detectors to perform effectively in crowded environments, it not only pushes the boundaries of computer vision research but also holds substantial promise for real-world deployment across a wide array of critical applications.
