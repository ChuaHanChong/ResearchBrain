# FRoM-W1: Towards General Humanoid Whole-Body Control with Language Instructions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and Shanghai Innovation Institute developed FRoM-W1, an open-source framework enabling humanoid robots to execute diverse natural language instructions with whole-body and hand movements. The system achieved a Fréchet Inception Distance of 0.229 for motion generation and demonstrated up to 15% improvement in motion tracking accuracy on physical robots.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots are limited by hard-coded or task-specific control policies, hindering versatility and natural interaction.
- A semantic gap exists between abstract natural language instructions and the concrete, low-level actions required for robot control.
- Reliably generating and executing stable, expressive whole-body motions, including intricate hand movements, on physical humanoid platforms is difficult.

## Method

- A two-stage framework, **FRoM-W1**, comprising H-GPT for language-driven motion generation and H-ACT for robot execution.
- H-GPT utilizes a LLaMA-3.1-based model, enriched with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and a VQ-VAE motion tokenizer, to generate hand-inclusive human motions from text.
- H-ACT employs an RL-based motion imitator with a novel ==Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RFT)== step and a modular deployment framework (RoboJudo) for stable and accurate physical execution on various humanoid robots.

## Results

- H-GPT demonstrated superior human motion generation with an FID of **0.229** on HumanML3D-X, and its Chain-of-Thought mechanism improved performance for rephrased and noisy instructions.
- The Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning within H-ACT consistently enhanced motion tracking accuracy by up to **15%** and increased success rates for Unitree H1 and G1 robots in simulation.
- The full FRoM-W1 framework successfully enabled physical Unitree H1 and G1 robots to execute complex, abstract language instructions with integrated whole-body and hand movements, showcasing its real-world applicability and modular deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Chain-of-Thought reasoning significantly improves the generalization and semantic consistency of language-driven motion generation, especially for complex or rephrased instructions.
> - A novel Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RFT) strategy during inference effectively adapts pre-trained robot control policies, leading to improved tracking accuracy and stability for specific generated motions.
> - Open-sourcing a modular, two-stage framework for language-to-motion generation and robot execution fosters reproducibility and accelerates research in embodied AI and humanoid control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in FRoM-W1 addresses a critical and rapidly evolving frontier in robotics: enabling humanoid robots to understand and execute diverse, natural language instructions for whole-body control in the physical world. This field lies at the intersection of Natural Language Processing, computer vision (specifically human motion analysis), and advanced robot control.

Historically, humanoid robot capabilities, such as greeting, dancing, or performing complex maneuvers like backflips, have largely been achieved through hard-coded trajectories or task-specific reinforcement learning (RL) policies. These approaches are inherently limited in their versatility and scalability, hindering natural human-robot interaction. The advent of large language models (LLMs) has revolutionized natural language understanding and generation, prompting researchers to explore how these powerful models can be leveraged for robotic control.

Within the broader landscape, FRoM-W1 differentiates itself from existing research in several key areas:

- **Text-to-Human Motion Generation:** Prior work in generating 3D human motions from text (e.g., T2M, MotionDiffuse, MLD, T2M-GPT, MotionGPT) often suffered from small dataset scales (like HumanML3D, which also lacked hand modeling) or limited integration with state-of-the-art LLMs. More recent efforts like Motion-X and MotionMillion have expanded data scale, and some works (e.g., Motion-R1) incorporated Chain-of-Thought (CoT). FRoM-W1 builds upon these advancements by training a 9B model (H-GPT) on an extended, hand-inclusive dataset (HumanML3D-X and Motion-X) and integrating CoT directly into a LLaMA-3.1-based architecture. This allows for more expressive, diverse, and semantically aligned human motion generation, critically including detailed hand movements (SMPL-X format) essential for humanoid expressiveness and manipulation tasks.
- **Humanoid Whole-Body Motion Tracking:** The field of humanoid control has seen significant progress in motion mimicking and stable locomotion (e.g., H2O, OmniH2O, TWIST, HugWBC). These methods typically focus on robustly executing pre-defined or teleoperated human motions on a robot. FRoM-W1 integrates a sophisticated RL-based motion imitator (H-ACT) that leverages a teacher-student paradigm for pre-training and, uniquely, introduces **reinforcement learning fine-tuning (RFT)** during inference for a specific motion. This RFT step adapts the policy to the nuances of the *generated* motion, significantly enhancing tracking accuracy and stability, addressing a common challenge where generated motions might be noisy or dynamically unstable for direct robot execution.
- **Language-Driven Humanoid Whole-Body Control:** This is a very new area. Early attempts (e.g., Robot-MDM for small robots, Harmon using off-the-shelf components, UH-1 training models from scratch) have demonstrated preliminary capabilities. However, these often face limitations such as lack of hand modeling, inability to leverage large LLMs for deep language understanding, robot-specific motion generation modules that are not scalable, or proprietary nature. FRoM-W1 stands out by unifying state-of-the-art LLM capabilities with robust motion control, providing a **general framework** that explicitly includes hand movements, utilizes CoT for enhanced language generalization, and is designed for **cross-platform deployment** on different full-size humanoid robots (Unitree H1 and G1). Its fully open-source nature further distinguishes it, aiming to lower the barrier for other researchers.

In essence, FRoM-W1 positions itself as a comprehensive, open-source solution that systematically addresses the challenges of integrating natural language understanding with dynamic, whole-body control for physical humanoid robots, particularly focusing on robustness, generality, and the often-overlooked expressiveness of hand movements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of FRoM-W1 is to enable humanoid robots to **comprehend diverse natural language instructions and execute corresponding whole-body motions (including hand movements) in the real physical world**. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives and motivations driven by current limitations in humanoid robotics and advancements in AI:

1. **Overcome Versatility Limitations of Current Humanoid Control:** Existing humanoid robots, despite their impressive acrobatic feats, largely rely on hard-coded or task-specific training. This severely limits their ability to adapt to novel situations or interpret open-ended human commands. FRoM-W1 seeks to unlock broader utility by enabling "general" control based on language.
2. **Bridge the Semantic Gap between Language and Robot Motion:** A significant challenge is the disparity between abstract human language and the concrete, low-level actions required for robot control. The project aims to develop a mechanism that can universally understand natural language instructions and translate them into plausible, diverse human motions.
3. **Ensure Stable and Accurate Physical Execution:** Even if a motion can be generated, executing it stably on a bipedal humanoid robot in the physical world, considering gravity and dynamic uncertainties, is a formidable task. A key objective is to develop robust control policies that can accurately track generated motions while maintaining dynamic stability on various robot platforms.
4. **Leverage Large-Scale Data and Advanced AI Models:** Motivated by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) in other domains, FRoM-W1 aims to utilize massive human motion data and powerful LLMs (like LLaMA-3.1) to train a motion generation model (H-GPT) with strong generalization capabilities for language understanding. The analogy to the human cerebrum (semantic comprehension) and cerebellum (stable motor execution) provides a conceptual framework for this two-stage approach.
5. **Incorporate Hand Movements for Enhanced Expressiveness and Functionality:** Most prior work on text-to-motion generation for robotics neglected hand movements. FRoM-W1 recognizes that hands are crucial for the expressiveness of whole-body motions and for future manipulation tasks. Therefore, a core objective is to generate hand-inclusive (SMPL-X format) motions.
6. **Foster Open Science and Community Advancement:** A strong motivation is to "open-source the entire FRoM-W1 framework." By releasing the code, models, and benchmarks, the authors intend to accelerate research and development in language-guided whole-body control, promote reproducibility, and facilitate collaborative efforts within the robotics and AI communities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FRoM-W1 framework tackles the complex problem of language-guided humanoid whole-body control through a novel two-stage pipeline: **H-GPT** for language-driven human motion generation and **H-ACT** for stable human-to-humanoid motion execution. This modular design addresses the inherent challenges of language understanding, motion synthesis, and physical robot control.

**Stage 1: H-GPT (Language to Whole-Body Human Motion Generation)**

The H-GPT module, inspired by large language models, aims to generate diverse and semantically consistent whole-body human motions (including hands) from natural language instructions.

1. **Dataset Augmentation and Enrichment:** Recognizing the scarcity of large-scale, paired robot datasets, the authors leverage existing large human motion datasets (HumanML3D and Motion-X). To bridge the semantic gap between simple text labels and complex instructions, they use a powerful LLM (GPT-4o) to enrich each data sample. For every instruction (I) and motion (M_h) pair, a **Chain-of-Thought (CoT)** sequence (CoT_i) is generated. This CoT provides a fine-grained, step-by-step textual description of motion primitives and their temporal structure, enabling the model to convert abstract commands into a more actionable format. The resulting enriched dataset comprises (I, CoT, M_h) triplets.
2. **Whole-Body Human Motion Tokenizer:** To integrate continuous human motion data with LLMs, a **VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder)** based tokenizer is trained. This tokenizer converts raw SMPL-X format human motion sequences (which include body, face, and hand poses) into discrete motion tokens. An encoder maps motion sequences to latent vectors, a quantizer discretizes these vectors by mapping them to nearest entries in a learnable codebook, and a decoder reconstructs the motion from the quantized tokens. This process effectively transforms continuous motion into a discrete token sequence compatible with language modeling.
3. **Whole-Body Human Motion Generator:** The core of H-GPT is an auto-regressive generative model based on the **LLaMA-3.1 LLM**. Its vocabulary is expanded to include both original text tokens and the newly created motion tokens. During training, the model learns to predict CoT and motion tokens given an instruction, processing sequences like "[Instruction] I [Chain-of-Thought] CoT [Motion Tokens] ˆC." Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of the pre-trained LLaMA-3.1 backbone. During inference, for a new instruction, the model first generates a CoT and then the corresponding motion tokens, which are finally decoded back into a continuous human motion sequence.

**Stage 2: H-ACT (Human Motion to Humanoid Execution)**

The H-ACT module is responsible for enabling humanoid robots to stably and accurately execute the human motion sequences generated by H-GPT.

1. **Human-to-Humanoid Whole-Body Motion Retargeter:** This module bridges the morphological differences between the human SMPL-X model and specific robot structures (e.g., Unitree H1, G1). It's a two-step process:
 - **SMPL-X Pose Reconstruction:** 3D keypoint positions from the generated human motion are used to reconstruct SMPL-X pose sequences (joint angles) via an inverse kinematics approach (HybrIK).
 - **Robot Pose Optimization:** An optimization problem is solved for each frame to determine robot-specific joint angles that best align with the reconstructed human SMPL-X pose. This involves minimizing positional and orientational discrepancies of selected robot joints relative to corresponding human joints, while respecting robot-specific joint limits and constraints. Body and hand retargeting are handled with specific optimization objectives.
2. **RL-based Motion Imitator: Pre-Training and Fine-Tuning (RPT & RFT):** A robust whole-body control policy (𝜋𝜃) is learned using a two-stage reinforcement learning framework within the IsaacGym simulator.
 - **RL Pre-Training (RPT):** A generalist policy is initially trained on a large dataset of retargeted human motions (e.g., from AMASS). This uses a **teacher-student distillation paradigm**, where a "teacher" policy with access to privileged information is trained to achieve high-quality motion tracking and stability, and a "student" policy is then trained via supervised learning to mimic the teacher's actions using only realistic, onboard observations.
 - **RL Fine-Tuning (RFT):** A novel aspect is the fine-tuning of the pre-trained student policy *during inference for a specific target motion*. This process, conducted in simulation, further adapts the policy to the unique kinematic and dynamic characteristics of the particular motion, leading to significantly improved tracking fidelity and stability. PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) is used for optimizing teacher policies with domain randomizations.
3. **Modular Deployment Framework (RoboJudo):** This framework is designed for robust real-world execution and flexible integration. It unifies a motion Controller (C), an environment abstraction layer (E), and the Policy module (Π𝜃) behind a consistent interface. This modularity allows for:
 - **Cross-Platform Compatibility:** Supports deployment on different robots (Unitree H1, G1) without hardware modifications.
 - **Policy Agnosticism:** Accommodates diverse whole-body control policies (e.g., upper-lower decoupled, teleoperation-based, full-body tracking) by converting canonical retargeted motions into policy-specific command formats.
 - **Efficient Sim2Real Transfer:** The environment layer abstracts platform communication, ensuring functional parity between simulation and real-robot execution.
 - **Runtime Flexibility:** Enables efficient policy switching and composition.

The overall approach combines sophisticated language understanding and motion generation with robust, adaptive robot control, aiming for a general, stable, and expressive humanoid system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on FRoM-W1 provided comprehensive insights into its capabilities across motion generation, simulation control, and real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Instruction to Whole-Body Human Motion Generation (H-GPT)

To evaluate H-GPT, the authors introduced two new benchmarks, HumanML3D-X (extending HumanML3D with hand-inclusive SMPL-X motions) and δHumanML3D-X (for generalization to rephrased and noisy instructions).

- **Quantitative Performance on HumanML3D-X:** H-GPT (with and without CoT) demonstrated **superior performance** on the primary metric, Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), achieving 0.229 and 0.255, respectively. This is a significant improvement over baseline models like T2M-GPT (0.677), indicating that H-GPT generates human whole-body motions that are more faithful to textual descriptions. H-GPT also achieved competitive motion diversity (DIV). The authors noted a trade-off where improved FID sometimes correlated with slight degradation in R Top-1 and MM Dist, suggesting the primary training objective prioritizes motion distribution fit over explicit text-motion correlation.
- **Generalization on δHumanML3D-X:** The Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mechanism proved crucial for generalization. H-GPT with CoT showed better performance (lower FID) than H-GPT without CoT under instruction rephrasing (0.355 vs. 0.455) and instruction noising (0.602 vs. 0.830). This validates CoT's effectiveness in enhancing language comprehension for complex or varied instructions.
- **Data Scaling with Motion-X:** While Motion-X is a much larger dataset, training H-GPT++ models on it did not yield better performance than training on HumanML3D-X alone (e.g., FID 0.312 for H-GPT++ vs. 0.255 for H-GPT). The authors attributed this to potential quality issues and distributional differences in video-estimated motion data and VLM-labeled text within Motion-X, suggesting that raw data scale doesn't automatically equate to improved performance without rigorous quality control. However, even with Motion-X, CoT still improved the overall performance.
- **Qualitative CoT Generation:** Manual comparative evaluation on 50 complex and 50 abstract instructions confirmed that H-GPT models incorporating CoT produced better and more semantically appropriate motions than those without CoT, highlighting its value for open-ended and nuanced commands.

#### 4.2. Humanoid Whole-Body Control in Simulation Environments (H-ACT)

Evaluations focused on the Unitree H1 and G1 robots in IsaacGym and MuJoCo simulators.

- **RL Pre-Training (RPT):** Policies trained on *filtered* AMASS data consistently improved motion tracking metrics (MPJPE, VEL, ACCEL) and success rates (SR) compared to raw data. The G1 robot consistently showed higher success rates (80-90%) than the H1 (40-50%), suggesting G1's design inherently supports more stable and diverse actions. On *generated* motions, results were mixed, with filtered data not always outperforming raw, possibly due to increased noise in H-GPT-generated motions.
- **RL Fine-Tuning (RFT):** The novel RFT strategy significantly enhanced controller performance. After fine-tuning, both H1 and G1 robots showed consistent improvements in MPJPE (up to 15%) and motion tracking success rates compared to training from scratch or pre-training alone. Significant improvements were observed within approximately 500 tuning steps, demonstrating the efficiency of this approach for adapting to specific motions.

#### 4.3. Performance of the Entire Framework in the Real World

FRoM-W1 was successfully deployed on Unitree H1 and G1 robots using an edge-side workstation for H-GPT and onboard compute for H-ACT.

- **Demonstrated Capabilities:** The framework successfully enabled robots to execute complex and abstract language instructions (e.g., "play the role of an elephant," "play a violin," "answer a phone call") with coherent whole-body and hand movements. Stable locomotion was also demonstrated.
- **Cross-Policy Compatibility:** The modular RoboJudo deployment framework proved effective, demonstrating compatibility with external control policies like HugWBC and TWIST, enabling their efficient and stable real-world deployment on generated motions.
- **Identified Limitations:** Despite successes, real-world deployment highlighted challenges:
 - **Misalignment:** Occasional semantic mismatch or instability in H-GPT-generated actions.
 - **Self-Collisions:** Generated motions sometimes led to self-collisions within the robot's body.
 - **Sim2Real Gap:** For extreme movements (e.g., deep squats, rapid running), policies stable in simulation often failed in the real world, leading to instability and falls, underscoring the persistent "Sim2Real gap."
 - **Low-Inertia Joints:** The control of wrist and head joints (low-inertia) posed challenges, requiring specific handling (e.g., freezing during training) to maintain stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FRoM-W1 represents a substantial contribution to the fields of robotics, natural language processing, and artificial intelligence, offering a comprehensive and open-source framework for language-guided whole-body control of humanoid robots. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Advancement Towards General Humanoid Intelligence:** By enabling humanoid robots to interpret and execute diverse natural language instructions for whole-body actions, FRoM-W1 takes a crucial step towards more versatile, intelligent, and autonomous humanoids. This moves beyond task-specific or pre-programmed behaviors, paving the way for robots that can adaptively respond to human commands in unstructured environments.
2. **Bridging Language and Embodied AI:** The framework effectively bridges the gap between sophisticated large language models and real-world robot embodiment. The integration of LLaMA-3.1 with custom motion tokenizers and Chain-of-Thought reasoning demonstrates a powerful paradigm for grounding abstract language into physical action, a key challenge in embodied AI.
3. **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability for humanoids to understand and respond to natural language instructions, including nuanced and abstract commands, will profoundly change how humans interact with robots. It promises more intuitive, natural, and accessible control interfaces, reducing the need for specialized programming or teleoperation.
4. **Novel Contributions in Motion Generation and Control:**
 - **Hand-Inclusive Motion Generation:** The use of SMPL-X and focus on hand movements significantly expands the expressive capabilities of generated motions, which is crucial for manipulation and detailed communication.
 - **Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RFT):** The introduction of RFT during inference is a significant methodological advance for robust robot control. It addresses the practical challenge of making general policies more accurate and stable for specific, potentially noisy, generated motions. This adaptive control mechanism improves reliability and fidelity.
 - **Modular and Universal Deployment (RoboJudo):** The open-source RoboJudo framework for sim2real transfer and policy deployment is a valuable contribution. Its compatibility with various control policies and robot platforms promotes standardization and reduces the engineering overhead for future research.
5. **Open Science and Accelerating Research:** The full open-sourcing of the entire framework—including H-GPT and H-ACT training code, model checkpoints, evaluation benchmarks (HumanML3D-X and δHumanML3D-X), and the lightweight deployment module—is a tremendous contribution to the research community. This initiative will foster reproducibility, enable direct comparisons, and accelerate innovation by providing a solid foundation for future work in language-guided humanoid control.
6. **Identification of Future Research Directions:** Beyond its successes, the paper candidly highlights current limitations, such as challenges with data quality for large motion datasets, residual misalignment between language and generated actions, and the persistent sim2real gap for extreme movements. These identified shortcomings serve as clear and valuable directions for the community's future efforts.

In conclusion, FRoM-W1 establishes a robust and openly accessible foundation for the next generation of humanoid robots capable of understanding and executing complex human commands. Its impact will likely be seen in the proliferation of more intelligent, versatile, and user-friendly humanoid systems in various applications, from assistance and entertainment to complex industrial tasks.
