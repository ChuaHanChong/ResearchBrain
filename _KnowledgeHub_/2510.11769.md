# GAR: Generative Adversarial Reinforcement Learning for Formal Theorem Proving

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and NVIDIA developed the Generative Adversarial Reinforcement Learning (GAR) framework, which co-evolves a formal theorem prover and a problem composer through an adversarial loop. This method dynamically adjusts problem difficulty, yielding an average relative pass@32 improvement of 4.20% on MiniF2F-Test and raising DeepSeek-Prover-V2's pass@32 from 22.58% to 25.81% on ProofNet-Test.

## Problem

- Current reinforcement learning (RL) methods for formal theorem proving rely on static problem sets, leading to inefficient training and wasted computation on trivial or unsolvable tasks.
- Most approaches optimize only the prover, neglecting the co-evolution of problem generation and often requiring costly human annotation for new challenges.
- Existing systems lack an adaptive curriculum, hindering the prover's ability to explore advanced reasoning strategies and make sustained progress on complex theorems.

## Method

- The ==Generative Adversarial== Reinforcement Learning (**GAR**) framework jointly optimizes a formal theorem prover and a problem composer (statement fuser) in an adversarial training loop.
- The statement fuser synthesizes novel, more challenging theorem statements by combining existing natural language statements, which are then autoformalized.
- An implicit curriculum learning mechanism dynamically adjusts problem difficulty; the fuser is rewarded for generating problems slightly beyond the prover's current capability, while the prover is rewarded for solving them correctly with minimal statement modification.

## Results

- GAR training improved Goedel-Prover-V2-**8B**'s pass@32 rate to **80.33%** (a **3.16%** relative gain) and DeepSeek-Prover-V2-**7B**'s pass@32 rate to **74.18%** (a **5.23%** relative gain) on the MiniF2F-Test benchmark.
- On the more advanced ProofNet-Test, GAR training raised DeepSeek-Prover-V2's pass@32 rate from **22.58%** to **25.81%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the co-evolution of the prover and statement fuser, along with the statement modification penalty, was crucial for these performance gains, significantly outperforming static-dataset RL and single-problem enhancement methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial co-training between a problem composer and a solver creates an implicit curriculum, dynamically escalating problem difficulty to match the solver's evolving capabilities.
> - Synthesizing new problems by fusing concepts from multiple natural language statements, rather than modifying a single problem, generates a more effective and challenging learning gradient.
> - Implementing a penalty for statement modification during proof attempts mitigates reward hacking, ensuring the prover focuses on solving the original problem rather than simplifying it.
