# Intelligence from Learnable Novelty

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tufts University and Harvard University propose 'learnable novelty' as a unifying principle for intelligence, and introduce a differentiable estimator for it. This objective successfully drives complex dynamics in cellular automata, enables unsupervised representation learning for MNIST with 0.89 accuracy, and provides a robust intrinsic reward for reinforcement learning agents across various tasks.

## Problem

- Existing intrinsic motivation approaches, such as novelty search, are susceptible to being distracted by unlearnable random noise (the 'noisy television' problem).
- Surprise minimization (e.g., free-energy principle) can lead agents to seek overly predictable, uninformative environments (the 'dark room' problem).
- Both traditional objectives conflate genuine, learnable surprise with irreducible noise, hindering robust learning and exploration.

## Method

- Defined **learnable novelty** as the program length of the optimal model a computationally bounded observer can fit to data, extending the prior ==epiplexity== concept.
- Developed a closed-form, differentiable estimator for **learnable novelty** using a ==reservoir computer== as the bounded observer, leveraging ==spectral description length== and ==ridge regression==.
- Applied this estimator as an optimizable objective across dynamical systems, unsupervised representation learning, and reinforcement learning domains.

## Results

- The estimator accurately classified Elementary Cellular Automata (ECA) complexity, ranking Turing-complete Rule 110 highest, and drove Neural Cellular Automata (NCA) to generate complex soliton-like dynamics.
- An unsupervised encoder, trained with the learnable novelty objective, spontaneously organized MNIST digits into distinct clusters, achieving **0.89 accuracy** in recovering labels.
- As an intrinsic reward, learnable novelty improved reinforcement learning performance and stability in **9 out of 10** diverse environments, effectively guiding exploration and resolving common pitfalls of prior methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complexity generation, abstraction, and exploration can emerge from optimizing a single objective: the pursuit of learnable novelty.
> - Focusing on the *learnable* component of surprise, rather than total surprise, explicitly resolves the 'noisy television' and 'dark room' pathologies in intrinsic motivation.
> - Intelligence is framed from an observer-centric perspective, where a bounded learner's computational capacity determines what constitutes 'learnable structure' and drives adaptive behavior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The paper positions its work within the context of varying definitions of intelligence across multiple scientific disciplines. It notes that intelligence is conceptualized as data compression in statistics and machine learning, universal computation in dynamical systems, and adaptive behavior in agent-environment interactions. The authors observe that these conceptualizations often lead to disparate theoretical frameworks and objective functions, which rarely converge.

Existing approaches to intrinsic motivation and learning objectives are discussed in relation to their limitations. "Novelty search," which promotes the discovery of previously unseen behaviors, is described as prone to being "transfixed by a noisy television screen." This indicates that such an objective can be perpetually engaged by unlearnable random noise, failing to extract meaningful structure. Conversely, the "free-energy principle," which aims to minimize cumulative surprise, is characterized as seeking a "dark room," where predictable, uninformative states offer the lowest surprise. Both of these frameworks are identified as conflating two distinct types of surprise: that which a bounded learner can convert into knowledge, and that which it cannot.

The authors propose "learnable novelty" as a unified principle that addresses these shortcomings. This concept extends the notion of "epiplexity," previously introduced by Finzi et al. (2026), by transforming it from a static measure of existing data into an optimizable objective. This modification is intended to provide a common quantitative foundation for understanding and generating intelligent behaviors across different domains, thereby integrating previously incompatible theoretical perspectives on complexity generation, abstraction, and exploration. The work relates to principles such as Minimum Description Length (MDL) by focusing on the learnable component of data compression, while also distinguishing itself from related concepts like Schmidhuber’s compression progress by explicitly avoiding the pitfalls associated with overly simple or unlearnable data.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and operationalize "learnable novelty" as a fundamental, unifying principle for various manifestations of intelligence. The authors hypothesize that seemingly disparate intelligent phenomena—such as the generation of complexity in dynamical systems, the formation of abstract representations, and open-ended exploration in agents—can all emerge from the pursuit of this single quantity.

A central motivation stems from the observed limitations of established intrinsic motivation objectives:
- **Novelty Search:** While effective at escaping local optima in goal-directed tasks, it can be perpetually distracted by unlearnable randomness (e.g., a noisy television screen), which offers continuous but uninstructive surprise. The objective fails to differentiate between meaningful, learnable novelty and irreducible noise.
- **Free-Energy Principle / Surprise Minimization:** This framework seeks to minimize cumulative surprise, leading agents to models that fit their inputs. However, it can motivate agents to seek environments devoid of activity or structure (e.g., a dark room) because such environments are maximally predictable and minimize surprise, offering no opportunities for rich learning.

The authors identify a common root cause for these failures: both objectives treat the total surprise as a monolithic quantity, failing to distinguish between the surprise that a learner can absorb and convert into knowledge (i.e., learnable structure) and the surprise that remains irreducible noise.

To overcome these issues, the paper introduces "learnable novelty," which is formally defined as the program length of the optimal model a *computationally bounded observer* can fit to a given data stream. This is precisely the "epiplexity" concept from prior work by Finzi et al. (2026). However, a key motivation for the current study is to move beyond simply *measuring* epiplexity to making it a *directly optimizable* quantity. The original epiplexity calculation involves a costly search over bounded-compute models and training a neural network for every system scored, making it computationally prohibitive and opaque to gradient-based optimization. The authors aim to develop a cheap, differentiable estimator for learnable novelty that can be integrated into optimization loops.

This differentiable estimator is then intended to test specific predictions:
- A dynamical system optimized for learnable novelty should tend towards complex, general-purpose computation, operating at the boundary of order and chaos to continuously produce learnable structure.
- A representation optimized for learnable novelty should shed redundancy and organize data into meaningful, distinguishable categories without explicit supervision.
- An agent whose policy maximizes the learnable novelty of its future states should be driven to explore rich, diverse environments, avoid termination or stagnation, and preserve its capacity for action, aligning with and sharpening causal-entropic and empowerment accounts of intelligent behavior.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The core of the methodology is the development of a closed-form, differentiable estimator for learnable novelty (epiplexity) and its application as both a measure and an optimization objective across various domains.

**Formalization of Learnable Novelty:**
The authors begin by defining cumulative surprise as the prequential description length, $L = \sum_i \ell_i = -\log_2 p(Y|X)$, representing the total bits required to transmit a sequence $Y$ given $X$. Drawing on the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle, this total description length $L$ is decomposed into two parts: the program length of the best model $M$ from a class $\mathcal{M}$ (i.e., $|M|$), and the residual information the model cannot account for.
To make this computable, the concept is restricted to a "bounded observer" $\phi$ with finite computational resources. The learnable novelty, $S_\phi(Y|X)$, is then defined as the program length of the optimal model $M^*_\phi$ within this bounded model space $\mathcal{M}_\phi$: $S_\phi(Y|X) = |M^*_\phi(Y|X)|$. This corresponds to the epiplexity introduced by Finzi et al. (2026).

**Closed-Form Estimator via Reservoir Computing:**
The critical innovation is to use a reservoir computer as the bounded observer $\phi$. A reservoir computer consists of a fixed, randomly initialized nonlinear feature map (the reservoir) followed by a single, trainable linear readout layer.
1. **Feature Mapping:** An input $X$ is transformed by the fixed reservoir $\phi$ into a feature matrix $H = \phi(X)$.
2. **Linear Readout:** All learnable capacity is concentrated in a linear readout matrix $W$, which maps $H$ to the target $Y$. The model space $\mathcal{M}_\phi$ is defined as $\phi$ followed by all possible linear readouts.
3. **Spectral Description Length:** The program length of the linear operator $W$ is quantified using a spectral description length: $C_{\text{spec}}(W) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_i \log_2(1 + \eta s_i(W)^2)$, where $s_i(W)$ are the singular values of $W$, and $\eta$ is a resolution parameter. This measure penalizes model complexity by the number and magnitude of independent directions learned, consistent with algorithmic information theory.
4. **Ridge Regression Approximation:** Instead of directly minimizing the combined objective (residual prediction error + $C_{\text{spec}}(W)$), which lacks a closed-form solution, the authors approximate it using ridge regression. A Taylor expansion shows that for small $W$, $C_{\text{spec}}(W)$ is approximately proportional to the squared Frobenius norm, $||W||^2_F$, which is the penalty term in ridge regression. The optimal readout $W_\lambda$ is found in closed form: $W_\lambda = (\tilde{H}^\top \tilde{H} + \lambda I_m)^{-1} \tilde{H}^\top \tilde{Y}$, where $\tilde{H}$ and $\tilde{Y}$ are standardized versions of the features and targets, and $\lambda$ is the ridge parameter.
5. **Final Estimator:** The closed-form estimator for learnable novelty is then $S_\phi(Y|X) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_i \log_2(1 + \eta s_i(W_\lambda)^2)$. This formulation makes $S_\phi$ cheap, deterministic, and crucially, differentiable with respect to $X$ and $Y$.
6. **Numerical Stability:** To maintain numerical stability and differentiability, the authors employ a QR factorization-based solution for the ridge regression, avoiding the computationally less stable normal equations.
7. **Reservoir Criticality:** The random reservoirs are initialized using a pre-activation normalization strategy to ensure they operate at the "edge of chaos," a state empirically found to maximize their expressivity and responsiveness.

**Experimental Applications:**
The estimator is applied in three distinct contexts:

1. **Dynamical Systems:**
 - **Measurement (Elementary Cellular Automata - ECA):** The estimator is used to score the complexity of all 88 locally unique ECA rules. For each rule, 512 initial states are evolved for a burn-in period, and the subsequent 32 time steps are used as the target $Y$ given the initial state $X$. A convolutional reservoir is used as the observer.
 - **Optimization (Neural Cellular Automata - NCA):** The parameters $\theta$ of a 1D, two-channel NCA are trained via gradient ascent to maximize $S_\phi(Y_\theta(X, \tau)|X)$, where $Y_\theta$ represents a stacked window of future states generated by the NCA.

2. **Representation Learning (MNIST):**
 - An encoder $E_\theta$ maps MNIST images $x$ to a latent code $z = E_\theta(x)$. The encoder is trained to maximize $S_\phi(Z = E_\theta(X)|X)$ without any class labels. An MLP reservoir is used as the observer.

3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - The increment of $S_\phi$ along an agent's trajectory ($\Delta S_\phi$) is used as an intrinsic reward bonus, added to the environment's task reward. The agent's policy is trained using PPO. A small MLP reservoir is used, and the $S_\phi$ calculation considers a window of future observations. A control experiment with a state-magnitude bonus is also performed.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents findings across three experimental domains, demonstrating the utility of learnable novelty as both a measure and an optimizable objective.

**Dynamical Systems:**
- **Elementary Cellular Automata (ECA) Classification:** When used as a pure measurement tool, the learnable novelty estimator successfully reproduced decades of complexity classification for ECAs. The Turing-complete Rule 110 was consistently ranked highest among all 88 locally unique rules. Near-trivial periodic rules (e.g., Rule 1, Rule 2) received the lowest scores, while chaotic rules (e.g., Rule 30) were ranked intermediately, below complex rules like Rule 54. This ranking was found to be robust across a range of estimator hyperparameters, with Rule 110 maintaining its top position unless the observer's capacity or the target window length was significantly reduced (Appendix B).
- **Neural Cellular Automata (NCA) Optimization:** Gradient ascent on the learnable novelty objective successfully drove continuous, two-channel NCA rules from simple dynamics into a regime characterized by complex solitons. Solitons are localized, coherent traveling structures that interact upon collision, a hallmark of systems capable of information transmission and computation, such as Rule 110. This emergence of complex behavior was consistent across multiple random seeds and robust to variations in the specific form of the local update rule (direct vs. residual).

**Representation Learning:**
- **Unsupervised MNIST Encoder:** An image encoder trained solely to maximize the learnable novelty of its output representations, without any supervisory labels, spontaneously organized the MNIST dataset. Two-dimensional t-SNE projections showed that initially entangled representations progressively converged into distinct clusters, largely separated by digit class. Quantitative analysis confirmed this, with a linear probe and a 5-nearest-neighbor classifier achieving an accuracy of 0.89 in recovering digit labels from the learned codes. This indicates that the objective implicitly drove the encoder to perform data compression, shedding redundant information and retaining discriminative features, which on MNIST corresponded to the digit identity itself. The results were stable to moderate changes in training and estimator settings (Appendix G.2).

**Reinforcement Learning:**
- **Intrinsic Reward for Exploration:** When provided as an intrinsic reward bonus to a PPO agent, the learnable novelty increment significantly improved performance across 9 out of 10 diverse reinforcement learning environments, encompassing classic control, MuJoCo locomotion, and sparse maze navigation.
 - **Reliability:** On tasks with sparse or deceptive rewards (e.g., Acrobot, MountainCarContinuous), the learnable novelty bonus drastically reduced the variance in returns across multiple seeds, transforming unreliable exploration into consistent task solving.
 - **Stability:** A control experiment using a "state magnitude" bonus (squared norm of input-normalized state) demonstrated instability, with performance collapsing below the baseline on two tasks (Hopper, LunarLander) and falling short on another (Walker2d). In contrast, the learnable novelty bonus never resulted in performance collapse, remaining at or above the task reward baseline on all but one task (Walker2d, where it fell 4% short). This indicates that the bonus effectively rewards learnable structure rather than raw state magnitude, which can be easily hijacked by degenerate coordinates or environmental noise.
 - **Exploration vs. Task Solving:** An agent rewarded solely by learnable novelty typically learned to avoid termination and sustain rich interactions, rather than solving specific tasks (e.g., avoiding the goal in Acrobot). This confirms its role as an exploration drive, complementing external task rewards. However, on tasks like Hopper and Walker2d, where continuous locomotion is inherently rich, the epiplexity-only agent learned to locomote effectively, even surpassing the task reward baseline on Walker2d. The learnable novelty bonus was most effective where exploration was a bottleneck.
 - **Immunity to Noise:** The learnable novelty reward inherited the epiplexity framework's immunity to the "noisy-television" problem. Pure noise inflates prediction error but adds no learnable structure, preventing the agent from being drawn to uninstructive chaotic environments.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research introduces "learnable novelty" as a candidate unifying principle for various forms of intelligence, proposing that complexity generation, abstraction, and exploration are interconnected manifestations of a single underlying drive. The development of a cheap and differentiable estimator for this quantity, built upon reservoir computing, provides a practical tool for empirical investigation across diverse domains.

The work's significance lies in several areas:
- **Unified Theoretical Framework:** By demonstrating that a single objective can drive phenomena ordinarily pursued with distinct, field-specific objectives, the paper suggests a common quantitative foundation for understanding intelligence. This contributes to bridging disciplinary divides in research on complex systems, machine learning, and cognitive science.
- **Resolution of Motivational Pathologies:** Learnable novelty explicitly addresses and resolves the "noisy television" and "dark room" problems that plague traditional novelty search and surprise minimization approaches, respectively. By focusing on the *learnable* component of surprise, the framework prevents learners from being distracted by uninstructive noise or retreating to overly predictable, structure-poor environments.
- **Advancement in Unsupervised Learning:** The ability of learnable novelty to drive unsupervised abstraction, as demonstrated by the spontaneous clustering of MNIST digits without labels, indicates a potential for developing more robust and biologically plausible methods for representation learning. It suggests that intrinsic motivations rooted in data compressibility can lead to the discovery of meaningful latent structures.
- **Robust Intrinsic Motivation for Reinforcement Learning:** The consistent improvement and stability of learnable novelty as an intrinsic reward for exploration in reinforcement learning tasks represent a practical advancement. Unlike more brittle curiosity-driven methods or naive magnitude-based bonuses, learnable novelty offers a principled, noise-resistant mechanism to enhance exploration, particularly in sparse-reward or deceptive environments. This could lead to more reliable and generalizable AI agents.
- **Observer-Centric View of Intelligence:** The research explicitly places the "bounded observer" at the center of its definition of intelligence and complexity. This relational perspective aligns with philosophical traditions that emphasize the role of interpretation and measurement in defining system properties, moving away from absolute characterizations.

Potential future impacts and directions for research highlighted by the authors include:
- **Co-evolution of Observer and Observed:** Exploring scenarios where the bounded observer itself can learn and grow alongside the system it observes. This could lead to open-ended learning and the discovery of increasingly deep and complex structures, as the boundary of what is "learnable" dynamically expands. Large language models are suggested as a potential substrate for investigating this symmetric co-evolution.
- **Refined Role of Task Rewards:** Investigating how task rewards could be integrated as modulators that shape the landscape of novelty-seeking, rather than acting as external anchors. This would position the pursuit of learnable novelty as the primary, fundamental drive, with external goals merely directing its focus.
- **Application to Infinite Games:** Extending the framework to "infinite games" lacking terminal states, where the agent's drive for sustained learnable novelty might more directly align with maintaining long-term adaptive capacity without the survival incentives present in finite environments.

In summary, this work provides a quantitative and operational framework for a unified theory of intelligence, with implications for the design of more intrinsically motivated, robust, and general-purpose artificial intelligence systems.
