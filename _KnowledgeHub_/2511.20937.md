# ENACT: Evaluating Embodied Cognition with World Modeling of Egocentric Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> ENACT introduces a benchmark for evaluating embodied cognition in Vision-Language Models through two VQA-based sequence reordering tasks: forward and inverse world modeling. The work reveals that current VLMs struggle with long-horizon interactions and exhibit human-like biases, performing significantly below human levels in predicting and inferring action-observation sequences in complex environments.

## Problem

- Modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are predominantly trained on static, disembodied data, raising questions about their genuine embodied cognition.
- A critical gap exists in objective, unified benchmarks that tightly couple egocentric perception with multi-step, dynamic interactions in everyday activities.
- Existing evaluations often focus on isolated cognitive slices or subjective taxonomies, failing to provide a holistic measure of world understanding through interaction.

## Method

- **ENACT** formalizes embodied cognition evaluation as a ==world modeling== problem from egocentric interaction, using two Visual Question Answering (VQA) sequence reordering tasks.
- It introduces Forward World Modeling (predicting future observations from actions) and Inverse World Modeling (inferring actions from observations) to assess prospective and retrospective reasoning.
- A scalable data generation pipeline is developed, leveraging the BEHAVIOR robotics simulator to synthesize high-quality, long-horizon QA pairs from realistic everyday activities.

## Results

- GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro are the top-performing proprietary VLMs, but all models lag far behind human performance (over **90%** PA) across interaction horizons, achieving human-comparable performance only at the shortest step (L=3).
- Evaluation on real-world videos validates the simulator's efficacy, showing similar trends and performance, while rendering fidelity variations have no statistically significant impact on VLM performance.
- Error analysis reveals that 81-**84%** of VLM errors are due to omission (failing to predict a ground-truth change) and hallucination (predicting a non-existent change), particularly in reasoning about spatial relations and agent interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs struggle significantly with long-horizon embodied world modeling, with performance degrading sharply as the number of interaction steps increases, indicating a lack of robust interactive memory and state tracking.
> - VLMs demonstrate an asymmetry in reasoning, performing better on inverse world modeling (retrospective action inference) than on forward world modeling (prospective visual simulation).
> - VLMs exhibit anthropocentric biases, performing worse when camera parameters (FOV, height) deviate from human-like vision, and showing a strong right-handed bias in understanding actions.
