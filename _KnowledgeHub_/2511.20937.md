# ENACT: Evaluating Embodied Cognition with World Modeling of Egocentric Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> ENACT introduces a benchmark for evaluating embodied cognition in Vision-Language Models through two VQA-based sequence reordering tasks: forward and inverse world modeling. The work reveals that current VLMs struggle with long-horizon interactions and exhibit human-like biases, performing significantly below human levels in predicting and inferring action-observation sequences in complex environments.

## Problem

- Modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are predominantly trained on static, disembodied data, raising questions about their genuine embodied cognition.
- A critical gap exists in objective, unified benchmarks that tightly couple egocentric perception with multi-step, dynamic interactions in everyday activities.
- Existing evaluations often focus on isolated cognitive slices or subjective taxonomies, failing to provide a holistic measure of world understanding through interaction.

## Method

- **ENACT** formalizes embodied cognition evaluation as a ==world modeling== problem from egocentric interaction, using two Visual Question Answering (VQA) sequence reordering tasks.
- It introduces Forward World Modeling (predicting future observations from actions) and Inverse World Modeling (inferring actions from observations) to assess prospective and retrospective reasoning.
- A scalable data generation pipeline is developed, leveraging the BEHAVIOR robotics simulator to synthesize high-quality, long-horizon QA pairs from realistic everyday activities.

## Results

- GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro are the top-performing proprietary VLMs, but all models lag far behind human performance (over **90%** PA) across interaction horizons, achieving human-comparable performance only at the shortest step (L=3).
- Evaluation on real-world videos validates the simulator's efficacy, showing similar trends and performance, while rendering fidelity variations have no statistically significant impact on VLM performance.
- Error analysis reveals that 81-**84%** of VLM errors are due to omission (failing to predict a ground-truth change) and hallucination (predicting a non-existent change), particularly in reasoning about spatial relations and agent interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs struggle significantly with long-horizon embodied world modeling, with performance degrading sharply as the number of interaction steps increases, indicating a lack of robust interactive memory and state tracking.
> - VLMs demonstrate an asymmetry in reasoning, performing better on inverse world modeling (retrospective action inference) than on forward world modeling (prospective visual simulation).
> - VLMs exhibit anthropocentric biases, performing worse when camera parameters (FOV, height) deviate from human-like vision, and showing a strong right-handed bias in understanding actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in ENACT addresses a fundamental question at the forefront of AI: whether modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs), predominantly trained on static, disembodied datasets, exhibit genuine signs of embodied cognition. Embodied cognition theory posits that intelligence fundamentally arises from an agent's continuous sensorimotor interaction with its environment, grounding abstract knowledge in perception and action. This contrasts with the disembodied nature of most VLM training, which relies on passive observation of images and text.

The broader research landscape for embodied AI and VLMs is rapidly evolving. Previous work has explored various facets of embodied cognition, such as spatial perception in static scenes (Ramakrishnan et al., 2024), linguistic reasoning for planning (Li et al., 2024b), and reasoning about primitive object interactions (Yi et al., 2019; Gao et al., 2025). Taxonomies like Yang et al. (2025) attempt to categorize embodied capabilities but often rely on subjective criteria. The authors identify a critical gap: the absence of a unified, objective benchmark that tightly couples egocentric perception with embodied interactions in complex, everyday activities. Existing benchmarks for embodied agents often focus on instruction following or goal-conditioned control, rather than the multi-step, consequence-aware reasoning essential for long-horizon interaction.

ENACT distinguishes itself by framing the evaluation of embodied cognition as a *world modeling problem* through *egocentric interaction* in a *visual question answering (VQA)* format. This approach directly assesses an agent's understanding of environmental dynamics and the causal consequences of its actions. Unlike many world models that prioritize generative fidelity for pixel-level prediction, ENACT's sequence-reordering VQA tasks implicitly demand higher-level reasoning about state changes and actions, abstracting away the confounding factors of low-level image synthesis.

The paper differentiates ENACT from other related benchmarks:
- **Aurora-Bench** (Qiu et al., 2025) focuses on short-horizon, general-purpose video world modeling. ENACT, in contrast, emphasizes *long-horizon* interactions with an *explicitly defined action space* represented by scene graphs.
- **GVL** (Ma et al., 2024) casts value estimation as observation sequence reordering. ENACT, however, focuses on *transition modeling over interleaved observation-action sequences* with explicit, semantically rich scene graph actions.

By providing a scalable, objective, and reproducible evaluation grounded in robotics simulation (BEHAVIOR), ENACT carves out a unique and crucial niche. It moves beyond isolated capability tests or subjective assessments to provide a holistic measure of a VLM's capacity to internalize and reason about the dynamics of an interactive physical world from an agent's perspective. This positions ENACT as a pivotal tool for charting the progress of VLMs towards truly embodied intelligence and informs the design of future foundational models for embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for introducing ENACT stems from an intriguing, yet unanswered, question in modern AI: "Do modern vision-language models (VLMs), trained largely in a disembodied manner, exhibit signs of embodied cognition?" While VLMs have shown remarkable progress in various domains, their capacity for intelligence grounded in sensorimotor interaction, as proposed by the theory of embodied cognition, remains largely unquantified and often implicitly assumed rather than directly measured.

The authors identify a critical gap in existing evaluation methodologies. Prior work typically examines isolated "slices" of embodied cognition, such as spatial perception or linguistic planning, or relies on subjective taxonomies that lack objective, unified metrics. A comprehensive, objective benchmark that tightly couples egocentric perception with dynamic, multi-step interactions in everyday environments was conspicuously absent. This lack of a unified evaluation framework makes it challenging to ascertain the true extent to which embodied understanding emerges in current VLMs.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the ENACT research are threefold:

1. **To introduce ENACT as a novel benchmark for evaluating embodied cognition:** The primary objective is to create a robust and objective framework that can effectively assess whether VLMs possess capabilities central to embodied intelligence.
2. **To formalize embodied cognition evaluation as world modeling from egocentric interaction:** ENACT frames this complex problem as two distinct, yet complementary, sequence reordering tasks within a Visual Question Answering (VQA) format:
 - **Forward World Modeling:** Requires models to predict an ordered sequence of future visual observations given an initial state and a sequence of actions. This implicitly tests prospective reasoning, affordance recognition, and action-effect prediction.
 - **Inverse World Modeling:** Challenges models to infer an ordered sequence of actions given an initial state and a progression of visual observations. This implicitly assesses retrospective reasoning, causal inference, and understanding of how observations relate to actions.
 These tasks are designed to demand capabilities such as affordance recognition, action-effect reasoning, embodied awareness, and interactive, long-horizon memory from partially observable egocentric input, crucially avoiding confounding evaluations with low-level image synthesis.
3. **To provide a scalable data generation pipeline and evaluate state-of-the-art VLMs:** A practical objective is to develop an automated and scalable pipeline that synthesizes high-quality QA pairs from realistic robotics simulations (specifically, the BEHAVIOR simulator). This enables the generation of a large, diverse dataset suitable for rigorous evaluation. The subsequent evaluation of frontier VLMs against this benchmark aims to systematically identify their strengths, weaknesses, and inherent biases, and to compare their performance against human baselines across varying interaction horizons and experimental conditions. Ultimately, the work seeks to generate insights that can guide the development of future, more genuinely embodied VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ENACT’s methodology is carefully constructed to provide a rigorous and scalable evaluation of embodied cognition. It frames the problem within a *Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP)* framework, focusing on world modeling through egocentric interaction, evaluated via a visual question answering (VQA) paradigm.

**Problem Formulation:**
The benchmark defines a state space (**S**) as symbolic scene graphs (structured representations of objects and their relationships), an observation space (**O**) as egocentric RGB images, and an action space (**A**) as scene-graph differences (deltas) between states. These scene-graph deltas abstract continuous robot motions into semantic changes. The evaluation is broken down into two core sequence reordering tasks:

- **Forward World Modeling:** Given an initial observation (𝑜₀), an ordered sequence of symbolic actions (𝑎₀..., 𝑎L-2), and a shuffled list of future observations (𝑂′ = (𝑜′₁..., 𝑜′L-1)), the VLM must output a permutation that correctly orders the future observations. This task assesses the model’s ability for *prospective visual simulation* and *action-effect reasoning*.
- **Inverse World Modeling:** Given an initial observation (𝑜₀), an ordered sequence of observations (𝑜₁..., 𝑜L-1), and a shuffled list of actions (𝐴′ = (𝑎′₀..., 𝑎′L-2)), the VLM must output a permutation that correctly orders the actions. This task evaluates the model’s capacity for *retrospective textual reasoning* and *causal inference*.

This VQA-based sequence reordering approach is crucial because it isolates higher-level interactive reasoning from the complexities of photorealistic video generation, which could otherwise confound the evaluation.

**Data Generation Pipeline:**
A key strength of ENACT is its scalable data generation pipeline, which leverages the BEHAVIOR robotics simulator. The process involves:

1. **Segmented Frames with Abstract State Changes:** Raw robot manipulation trajectories (e.g., from BEHAVIOR) are filtered to identify "key-frames" – timestamps where minimal, abstract scene graph state changes occur (e.g., `RightGrasping` a `drill`). This segmentation uses a temporal stability filter (changes must persist for >1.3s) and a cosine similarity threshold (0.97) for state change signatures to avoid near-duplicate or trivial frames.
2. **Key-Frame Trajectories Synthesis:** From these segmented frames, trajectories of varying lengths (L = 3 to 10 steps) are sampled. This is an efficient process converting trajectory construction into a "seat selection" combinatorics problem on a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) and employing dynamic programming with weighted backtracking to sample valid trajectories (where each step has a visible state change). This allows for generating millions of QA pairs from a single episode.
3. **QA Curation:** The synthesized key-frame trajectories are then formatted into the forward and inverse world modeling VQA pairs.

The resulting dataset for ENACT comprises 8,972 QA pairs, covering diverse, long-horizon home-scale activities and common predicate classes (e.g., `Open`, `Cooked`, `Grasping`).

**Evaluation Design:**
The evaluation employs two metrics to provide both strict and nuanced assessments:

- **Task Accuracy (TA):** A binary metric (0 or 1) that measures whether the VLM correctly orders the *entire* sequence. An online verifier accepts predictions if they are either exactly correct or semantically consistent (e.g., predicted changes are a subset of ground-truth visible changes for forward modeling).
- **Pairwise Accuracy (PA):** Grants partial credit by measuring the fraction of adjacent pairs in the sequence that are correctly ordered and semantically consistent. This metric is micro-averaged across the dataset and provides a more informative signal, especially for longer, more challenging horizons where exact ordering is rare.

**Experimental Setup:**
The study rigorously evaluates 7 proprietary VLMs (from OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Anthropic) and 23 open-weight models, using a unified prompt template, standardized image resizing (512x512), and a decoding temperature of 0 for deterministic outputs. A human baseline is established through trained annotators following the same instructions, with a high Krippendorff's 𝛼 of 0.83 confirming strong inter-annotator agreement.

**Ablation Studies and Error Analysis:**
The methodology further includes a series of controlled ablation studies to investigate specific aspects of VLM performance:

- **Sensitivity to Image Realism:** Assessed by comparing performance on manually annotated real-world videos and simulator-generated images rendered with varying fidelity (Realistic, Path Tracing, Ray Tracing Only).
- **Anthropocentric Biases on Human Vision:** Examined by manipulating camera parameters like Field of View (FOV: Aperture 30, 60, 80, Fisheye vs. default 40) and Camera Height (High, Low vs. default Mid).
- **Anthropocentric Biases on Embodiment:** Explored through variations in robot appearance (White, Random, Skin Color vs. default) and a detailed predicate-level error analysis of handedness (LeftGrasping vs. RightGrasping).
- **Error Analysis Framework:** A systematic framework is developed to categorize errors (Entity Substitution, Polarity Inversion, Predicate Substitution, Omission, Hallucination) and analyze their semantic nature (Spatial Relations, Functional States, Material States, Agent Interactions) by comparing ground-truth and predicted scene graph deltas.

This comprehensive approach allows ENACT to not only quantify VLM performance but also to diagnose the underlying reasons for success and failure, providing valuable insights for future research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with ENACT reveal critical insights into the capabilities and limitations of state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models in embodied cognition tasks, particularly in contrast to human performance.

1. **Significant Performance Gap Between VLMs and Humans:**
 - Human performance consistently remains high (over 90% Pairwise Accuracy) across all interaction horizons and task types.
 - In stark contrast, even the frontier VLMs like GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro are comparable to humans only at the shortest step length (L=3), and their performance degrades sharply as the interaction horizon lengthens. This vast gap confirms that current VLMs are still far from robustly performing embodied world modeling.

2. **Long-Horizon Degradation:**
 - Accuracy for nearly every VLM, proprietary and open-weight alike, decreases monotonically and significantly with an increasing number of steps (L). While shorter tasks (L ≤ 4) are somewhat manageable, longer tasks (L ≥ 8) prove exceedingly challenging, with many models collapsing to near-zero task accuracy. This indicates a fundamental struggle for VLMs to track evolving physical states and maintain interactive, long-term memory under partial observability.

3. **Asymmetry in World Modeling Tasks (Inverse vs. Forward):**
 - A consistent finding across all VLM families and step lengths is that models perform *better on inverse world modeling* than on forward world modeling. This margin often widens with increasing L. This suggests that VLMs are stronger at retrospective textual reasoning (inferring actions from observed state changes) than at prospective visual simulation (predicting future observations based on given actions).

4. **Leading VLM Performance:**
 - GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro emerge as the strongest proprietary models. Among open-weight models, InternVL3.5-241B-A28B, GLM-4.5V, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Ins demonstrate competitive performance, sometimes surpassing other proprietary models like Claude Sonnet 4. Cosmos-Reason1, a model trained on embodied data, exhibits more stable performance at longer interaction horizons compared to other similarly sized models, hinting at the benefits of embodied training data.

5. **Robustness to Image Realism, Minimal Sim-to-Real Gap:**
 - **Real-World Validation:** Evaluation on manually annotated real-world videos (across kitchen, dinner table, workspace scenes) shows that findings from the simulator largely transfer, with similar absolute accuracies and the same qualitative trends (inverse > forward, long-horizon degradation). This validates the BEHAVIOR simulator as a faithful proxy for real-world embodied cognition evaluation.
 - **Rendering Fidelity:** VLMs are found to be robust to variations in rendering realism (Realistic, Path Tracing, Ray Tracing Only). No statistically significant performance degradation or improvement was observed, suggesting that current bottlenecks are primarily in multi-step interaction reasoning rather than in discerning low-level visual fidelity.

6. **Anthropocentric Biases in VLMs:**
 - **Human-like Vision Preference:** VLMs exhibit significant performance degradation when camera intrinsics (e.g., FOV) or viewpoints deviate from typical human vision. Larger aperture values (60, 80) and fisheye lenses consistently and significantly hurt performance compared to the human-like Aperture 40 baseline. Similarly, an elevated camera height (High) significantly reduces performance in forward tasks. This suggests a strong bias towards human-centric visual priors.
 - **Robustness to Robot Appearance:** Conversely, altering the robot's visual appearance (White, Random, Skin Color) has no statistically significant impact on VLM performance. This indicates that the models' understanding of interaction is not tied to a specific visual representation of the agent's body.
 - **Right-Handed Bias:** A strong and consistent handedness asymmetry is observed. VLMs show a clear preference for understanding and processing right-handed actions. Precision and recall for predicates like `RightGrasping` are substantially higher than for `LeftGrasping`, and there is a significantly higher rate of misattributing ground-truth left-hand actions as right-hand actions (left-to-right mixing) than vice-versa. This mirrors human handedness distribution.

7. **Dominance of Omission and Hallucination Errors:**
 - A detailed error analysis for GPT-5 reveals that the vast majority of errors (81-84%) are due to **Omission** (failing to predict a ground-truth state change) and **Hallucination** (predicting a state change that did not occur). This indicates that VLMs primarily struggle with correctly identifying *which* changes happened or will happen, rather than merely misinterpreting the specifics of a known change.
 - In forward tasks, hallucination (43.9%) is more prevalent than omission (37.1%), while in inverse tasks, they are nearly balanced (around 41.8% each). Other error types like Entity Substitution, Predicate Substitution, and Polarity Inversion are far less frequent.
 - **Semantic Error Analysis** further localizes these issues to *Spatial Relations* and *Agent Interaction*, indicating particular difficulties in reasoning about object positions and the consequences of agent actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ENACT represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and Vision-Language Models, offering a novel framework that brings clarity and objectivity to the evaluation of embodied cognition. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

1. **Foundational Benchmark for Embodied Cognition:** By formally casting embodied cognition as a world modeling problem within a VQA framework, ENACT provides a unified, objective, and scalable benchmark. This addresses a critical gap identified in previous research, which often relied on fragmented evaluations or subjective criteria. ENACT's design, specifically isolating reasoning from low-level generative tasks, ensures a cleaner signal for assessing core cognitive capabilities.

2. **Deep Insights into VLM Capabilities and Limitations:** The study yields fundamental insights into current VLMs:
 - **The Embodiment Gap:** The stark and widening performance gap between VLMs and humans, particularly in long-horizon interactive tasks, definitively highlights that despite impressive progress, current models are far from exhibiting human-like embodied intelligence. This clarifies where significant research effort is still needed.
 - **Asymmetric Reasoning:** The consistent observation that models perform better on inverse than forward world modeling suggests a stronger capacity for retrospective causal inference from observations compared to prospective action-conditioned visual simulation. This indicates a key area for VLM architectural and training data improvements.
 - **Anthropocentric Biases:** The discovery of strong biases towards human-like camera perspectives (FOV, height) and handedness in actions reveals critical limitations in VLMs' generalization to diverse robotic embodiments. This is a crucial finding for developing versatile embodied agents that operate in non-human-centric ways or with varied sensor configurations.
 - **Nature of Reasoning Failures:** The dominance of omission and hallucination errors, particularly in spatial relations and agent interactions, suggests that VLMs frequently construct inconsistent or incomplete mental models of the world. This points to a reliance on learned textual priors that are not sufficiently grounded in faithful visual-physical understanding and a deficit in maintaining object permanence and tracking complex state transitions under partial observability.

3. **Guidance for Future VLM and Embodied AI Development:** The findings from ENACT offer concrete directions for advancing research:
 - **Curating Training Data:** The identified biases suggest the need for VLM training datasets that are more diverse in camera viewpoints, balanced in terms of handedness, and explicitly encode action-effect consistency and object persistence across complex state changes.
 - **Developing Robust World Models:** The performance limitations underscore the necessity for architectural innovations that enable VLMs to build and utilize more robust internal world models, capable of accurate long-horizon simulation and consequence-aware reasoning.
 - **Bridging Simulation to Reality:** The validation that simulation trends largely mirror real-world performance is significant, confirming the utility of high-fidelity simulators like BEHAVIOR as reproducible testbeds for foundational embodied AI research.

4. **Broader Implications for General AI:** By rigorously evaluating the emergence of embodied cognition, ENACT contributes to a deeper understanding of intelligence itself. The insights gained can inform not only the development of more capable embodied agents and robots but also advance the broader quest for more general, adaptable, and robust artificial intelligence systems. The ethical considerations outlined in the paper further ensure that this powerful evaluative tool is applied responsibly.

In conclusion, ENACT is more than just a benchmark; it is a diagnostic tool that provides a critical lens for understanding the embodied intelligence of modern VLMs, offering clear pathways for future research to develop truly interactive and world-aware AI agents.
