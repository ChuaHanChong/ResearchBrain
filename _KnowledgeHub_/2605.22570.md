# VGenST-Bench: A Benchmark for Spatio-Temporal Reasoning via Active Video Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> VGenST-Bench introduces a novel video benchmark for spatio-temporal reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), constructed by actively synthesizing photorealistic videos with generative models. Evaluations reveal top MLLMs are over 13 percentage points below human performance on complex reasoning tasks, showing significant degradation from basic perception to higher-order inference.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM benchmarks for spatio-temporal reasoning are susceptible to data contamination and allow models to exploit shortcuts, leading to inflated performance metrics.
- Passively curated video datasets for evaluation offer limited scalability and diversity, and often fail to truly isolate dynamic spatio-temporal relationships.
- Synthetic data benchmarks often suffer from a 'visual realism gap,' limiting their applicability for evaluating modern MLLMs trained on photorealistic data.

## Method

- **VGenST-Bench** utilizes an active synthesis paradigm, employing video generative models to create a wide array of precisely controlled, photorealistic evaluation scenarios.
- The benchmark is structured around a 3x2x2 taxonomy (spatial scale, perspective, scene dynamics) to systematically cover diverse spatio-temporal contexts, with a dedicated task for each category.
- A hierarchical Question-Answering (QA) design (L1: Visual Perception, L2: Scene Understanding, L3: Spatio-Temporal Reasoning) coupled with question reformulation variants (e.g., 'None-of-these' options, open-ended) is used to enable fine-grained diagnosis and mitigate shortcut exploitation.

## Results

- Human annotators achieved near-ceiling **99.0%** accuracy, while the highest-performing MLLM (Gemini 3 Flash) reached only **85.9%** - a gap of over **13** percentage points, with most open-source models performing below **70%**.
- A consistent performance degradation was observed across MLLMs from L1 (Visual Perception) to L3 (Spatio-Temporal Reasoning), with some models dropping from over **90%** on L1 to below **40%** on L3.
- MLLMs showed dramatic performance drops when faced with 'None-of-these' correct answers or open-ended questions, revealing a vulnerability to question reformulation and a reliance on reasoning shortcuts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The active synthesis approach effectively mitigates data contamination and shortcut exploitation, providing a more reliable and diagnostic testbed for MLLM spatio-temporal reasoning.
> - MLLMs exhibit a consistent and substantial performance degradation as cognitive tasks move from basic visual perception (L1) to higher-order spatio-temporal reasoning (L3), highlighting a systematic bottleneck in complex inference.
> - MLLMs struggle significantly with 'None-of-these' answer options and open-ended questions, indicating a reliance on choice priors and positional biases rather than robust reasoning in standard multiple-choice formats.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are increasingly being applied in physically grounded domains such as robotics and autonomous driving. A foundational capability for these applications is spatio-temporal reasoning, which involves perceiving and inferring the positions, orientations, and attributes of objects across time and varying viewpoints. Evaluating this capability precisely is a current challenge in MLLM research.

Prior work on MLLM evaluation has largely fallen into several categories:
- **Static Image-Based Benchmarks**: These benchmarks primarily assess spatial understanding from 2D images, focusing on tasks like object localization, relative positioning, and compositional spatial relations (e.g., MME, 3DSRBench, SpatialViz-Bench, Spatial457). A limitation of these benchmarks is their inability to capture dynamic spatio-temporal relationships or state transitions over time.
- **Passively Curated Video-Based Benchmarks**: More recent efforts have incorporated video datasets to address temporal aspects (e.g., VSI-Bench, EgoExoBench, STI-Bench, OST-Bench). However, these benchmarks, which often rely on collecting clips from the web or repurposing existing datasets, exhibit several limitations:
 - **Data Contamination**: MLLMs are extensively pretrained on vast volumes of public data, making evaluations on passively curated benchmarks susceptible to train-test overlap. This can inflate reported performance metrics.
 - **Shortcut Exploitation**: These benchmarks may contain distributional regularities that allow models to achieve high scores by utilizing linguistic priors, single-frame cues, or static scene context, rather than genuinely performing spatio-temporal reasoning. For example, some benchmarks have been shown to fail in isolating true temporal understanding.
 - **Limited Scalability and Coverage**: Manually curating and annotating video data from web sources is labor-intensive. While some benchmarks repurpose 3D scene datasets, these often offer a narrow range of environments, limiting the diversity of spatial scales, perspectives, or scene dynamics that can be evaluated.
- **Synthetic Data-Based Benchmarks**: Some benchmarks utilize synthetic data for evaluation (e.g., CLEVR, CLEVRER, Super-CLEVR). These offer precise control over ground truth but often suffer from a "visual realism gap," which can limit their applicability for evaluating modern MLLMs trained on photorealistic data.

The work presented in this paper introduces VGenST-Bench, a benchmark that aims to overcome the limitations of existing approaches by leveraging recent advancements in video generative models. By actively synthesizing precisely controlled evaluation scenarios, it seeks to provide a more reliable and diagnostic testbed for spatio-temporal reasoning in MLLMs, distinguishing itself as the first video spatio-temporal reasoning benchmark constructed entirely from photorealistic videos synthesized by video generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop VGenST-Bench, a novel video benchmark designed for the fine-grained evaluation and diagnosis of spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This objective is driven by several motivations, primarily addressing the shortcomings observed in existing benchmarks:

- **Addressing Data Contamination and Shortcut Exploitation**: Current MLLMs are trained on extensive datasets, including publicly available video and image data. This exposes passively curated benchmarks to data contamination, where models may have encountered similar data during training, leading to inflated performance metrics. Furthermore, such benchmarks can allow models to exploit "shortcuts," such as linguistic priors or single-frame visual cues, rather than engaging in genuine spatio-temporal reasoning. VGenST-Bench aims to mitigate these issues by generating novel, highly controlled scenarios, making it more difficult for models to rely on prior exposure or superficial cues.
- **Enabling Fine-Grained Diagnostic Evaluation**: The researchers identified a need for a benchmark that can offer a detailed understanding of where MLLMs succeed or fail in their reasoning processes. This led to the development of a structured video taxonomy and a hierarchical question design. The taxonomy systematically covers diverse spatio-temporal scenarios by varying spatial scale, perspective, and scene dynamics. The question hierarchy (L1: Visual Perception, L2: Scene Understanding, L3: Spatio-Temporal Reasoning) is designed to decouple basic perception from higher-order reasoning, facilitating a more precise diagnosis of capabilities.
- **Enhancing Scalability and Diversity of Scenarios**: Traditional methods of benchmark creation, particularly with video data, are often limited by the labor-intensive process of collecting, filtering, and annotating real-world footage. Existing 3D scene datasets, while useful, may not offer sufficient diversity across various spatio-temporal dimensions. The motivation behind VGenST-Bench is to leverage the capabilities of modern video generative models to actively synthesize a wide array of scenarios at scale. This allows for the systematic creation of specific combinations of spatial scales, perspectives, and scene dynamics that might be scarce in natural datasets, ensuring comprehensive coverage for evaluation.
- **Establishing a Controllable and Reliable Testbed**: By shifting from passive data curation to active video synthesis, the research aims to establish a benchmark where the precise ground truth for spatio-temporal events can be programmatically defined and controlled. This ensures that the evaluation scenarios accurately reflect the intended reasoning tasks and that the ground truth answers are unambiguous, thereby increasing the reliability of the benchmark. The inclusion of a human quality control stage further validates the generated videos and question-answer pairs for visual fidelity and scenario adherence.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to move beyond simply measuring MLLM performance to providing a diagnostic tool that can pinpoint the specific strengths and weaknesses of models in complex spatio-temporal reasoning tasks, unhindered by data-related biases of previous benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VGenST-Bench adopts an active synthesis paradigm, using video generative models to create evaluation scenarios, a departure from passively curated video datasets. The methodology involves a multi-agent pipeline integrated with a human quality control stage.

#### 3.1. Video Taxonomy and Task Design

The benchmark is structured around a 3 × 2 × 2 taxonomy, grounded in cognitive studies of spatial cognition and event perception, to systematically cover spatio-temporal reasoning scenarios:
- **Spatial Scale (3 categories)**:
 - **Figural**: Local object configurations apprehended from a single view.
 - **Vista**: Larger scene layouts visible from a local viewpoint.
 - **Environmental**: Extended spaces requiring integration across multiple views, landmarks, or navigation.
- **Perspective (2 categories)**:
 - **Egocentric**: Spatial relations defined relative to the observer/camera viewpoint.
 - **Exocentric**: Spatial relations defined from an external or scene-centered viewpoint.
- **Scene Dynamics (2 categories)**:
 - **Static**: Reasoning over stable spatial configurations.
 - **Dynamic**: Reasoning involving object motion, agent-object interactions, causal changes, and event progression over time.

This taxonomy results in 12 distinct video categories, with one dedicated reasoning task designed for each cell (e.g., "Multi-Container Attribute Mapping" for Figural, Egocentric, Static scenes; "Relative Velocity Identification" for Environmental, Egocentric, Dynamic scenes).

#### 3.2. QA Design

Each video is paired with Question-Answering (QA) pairs organized along a three-level cognitive hierarchy:
- **Level 1 (L1) – Visual Perception**: Probes object recognition and visual attributes from individual frames (e.g., Object Existence, Object Attribute Recognition, 2D Frame Localization).
- **Level 2 (L2) – Scene Understanding**: Assesses the integration of perceptual cues across multiple frames into coherent spatial and temporal structures (e.g., Identity Tracking, Action Recognition, Object Counting, Temporal Ordering, Camera Motion Recognition, Spatial Layout Understanding).
- **Level 3 (L3) – Spatio-Temporal Reasoning**: Evaluates higher-order inference beyond direct observation, such as novel viewpoints, hypothetical alterations, and future events (e.g., Perspective-Taking, Counterfactual Reasoning, Predictive Reasoning).

A "task–QA applicability matrix" specifies which QA types are relevant for each of the 12 tasks, ensuring valid and balanced coverage. To mitigate shortcut exploitation, each base Multiple-Choice Question (MCQ) is expanded into three variants:
- **None-of-these distractor (V1)**: Adds "None of these" as an additional incorrect option to test commitment to the correct choice.
- **None-of-these answer (V2)**: Replaces the correct option with "None of these" (making "None of these" the correct answer) to test rejection of false options.
- **Open-ended (V3)**: Removes all options to assess reasoning without choice priors, with answers judged by an LLM-as-judge protocol.

#### 3.3. Dataset Construction (Multi-Agent Pipeline)

The benchmark is constructed via a multi-agent pipeline with four sequential agent modules and a human quality control stage:
1. **Structuring Inputs to a Video Generator**: The pipeline conditions video generation on three structured representations:
 - **Theme**: Provides visual and semantic context (e.g., "Cyberpunk Hacker's Neon Desk").
 - **Scene Graph**: Specifies static spatial configuration (objects, attributes, relations).
 - **Scenario**: Lifts the scene graph into the temporal domain (reasoning goal, camera setup, event timeline).
2. **Scene Graph Agent**: Takes a (theme, task) pair and generates a structured JSON scene graph. A Scene Graph Validator iteratively checks for compliance with task rules, object requirements, and consistency, providing feedback for regeneration until validated.
3. **Scenario Agent**: Translates a validated scene graph into a temporal scenario with a phase-by-phase timeline. A Scenario Validator iteratively verifies object/attribute fidelity, first-frame compliance, temporal flow accuracy, and ground-truth determinacy.
4. **Video Agent**: Renders the scenario into a video. This involves an Image Prompt Translator creating a first-frame prompt for an Image Generator (producing an anchor frame), followed by a Video Prompt Translator creating a video prompt for a Video Generator (producing the final clip, conditioned on the anchor frame). A diverse pool of image and video generative models is employed. For specific tasks (e.g., Behavioral Trigger Identification), direct text-to-video generation is used.
5. **QA Agent**: Generates base MCQs based on the scene graph and scenario, consulting the task-QA applicability matrix. A Reformatter then expands each base MCQ into the three variants (V1, V2, V3).
6. **Human Quality Control**: All generated videos and base QA pairs undergo a two-stage human verification protocol by trained validators.
 - **Stage 1 (Video QC)**: Validators review videos for visual fidelity, scenario adherence, and absence of generation artifacts.
 - **Stage 2 (QA QC)**: Validators review base MCQs on passed videos for correct ground truth, lack of ambiguity, and absence of single-frame or language-prior shortcuts.
 An intersection rule is applied, requiring both validators to mark an item as valid for retention, with author resolution for disagreements.

This systematic methodology is designed to create a benchmark with high control over scenarios, unambiguous ground truths, and diagnostic capabilities, while leveraging the visual richness offered by generative models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VGenST-Bench involved 15 MLLMs (proprietary and open-source) and human annotators, with accuracy as the primary metric. Multiple-choice questions were subjected to circular evaluation to control for position bias, and open-ended questions were assessed using an LLM-as-judge protocol.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Human Performance Benchmark**: Human annotators achieved an average accuracy of 99.0%, demonstrating near-ceiling performance across all 12 tasks and confirming the clarity and unambiguous nature of the benchmark tasks.
- **MLLM Performance Gap**: Even the highest-performing MLLM, Gemini 3 Flash, reached an average accuracy of 85.9%, exhibiting a gap of over 13 percentage points (pp) below human performance. Smaller and compact models (e.g., GPT-5.4 nano) performed below 60%.
- **Open-Source MLLMs**: Most open-source models performed below 70%, indicating limitations in their spatio-temporal reasoning. Kimi-K2.6 (71.0%) and Gemma-4-31B-it (72.5%) showed more competitive performance, narrowing the gap with top proprietary models.

#### 4.2. Hierarchical Analysis

- **Consistent Degradation from L1 to L3**: A substantial and consistent performance degradation was observed across all MLLMs as the cognitive level increased from L1 (Visual Perception) to L3 (Spatio-Temporal Reasoning). For example, GPT-5.4 mini scored 90.2% on L1, dropped to 65.3% on L2, and further to 36.4% on L3.
- **Human Robustness**: In contrast, human annotators showed only a marginal decline (99.4% on L1 to 97.9% on L3).
- **Diagnostic Utility**: This significant performance gap between MLLMs and humans, particularly at higher reasoning levels, validates the effectiveness of the hierarchical design as a diagnostic tool. It indicates that current MLLMs are proficient at single-frame visual perception (L1) but face systematic bottlenecks in higher-order reasoning that necessitates integrating complex spatial and temporal cues (L2 and L3).

#### 4.3. Robustness to Question Reformulation

- **Position Bias and Choice Priors**: Vanilla accuracy (standard MCQ) consistently exceeded circular accuracy across all models. This finding suggests that MLLMs exploit position bias and choice priors, and that standard single-attempt evaluations may overestimate genuine reasoning.
- **"None-of-these" Variants**:
 - **V1 (None-of-these distractor)**: MLLM accuracy generally maintained or slightly exceeded vanilla accuracy.
 - **V2 (None-of-these answer)**: Introduced dramatic drops in MLLM performance. This indicates that models struggle to reject all listed options when the correct answer is explicitly absent, often selecting the most plausible distractor instead of recognizing "None of these" as the correct choice.
- **Open-Ended Variant (V3)**: The open-ended format further accentuated the L1→L3 performance degradation compared to closed-form MCQs.
- **Interpretation**: These results collectively suggest that high accuracy on standard multiple-choice benchmarks may not fully reflect MLLMs' true spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities. The reformulation protocol is effective in exposing reasoning shortcuts and encouraging more robust reasoning.

#### 4.4. Failure Analysis

- Analysis of specific L3 failures, such as on the "Direction Estimation" task, revealed that MLLMs can exhibit accurate visual perception of events (e.g., recognizing a camera turn) but then make errors in higher-level reasoning processes, such as inverting egocentric transformations (e.g., mistaking back-right for back-left when calculating relative direction). This confirms that models struggle with logical composition and inference even when visual cues are correctly processed.

These findings highlight that despite progress, current MLLMs possess substantial limitations in spatio-temporal reasoning, particularly when tasks require integrating information over time, across viewpoints, or involve counterfactual and predictive inference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VGenST-Bench represents a notable contribution to the evaluation of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in the domain of spatio-temporal reasoning. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

#### 5.1. Novel Benchmark Paradigm

The benchmark establishes a new paradigm for constructing evaluation datasets by leveraging active video synthesis through generative models. This approach directly addresses critical limitations of prior benchmarks:
- **Mitigation of Data Contamination:** By synthesizing novel, controlled scenarios, VGenST-Bench reduces the risk of models exploiting train-test overlap, leading to more reliable and uninflated performance evaluations.
- **Reduced Shortcut Exploitation:** The carefully designed scenarios aim to necessitate genuine spatio-temporal reasoning, making it more challenging for models to rely on superficial cues, linguistic priors, or single-frame understanding.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Diversity:** The generative approach enables the creation of a wide array of specific and diverse spatio-temporal scenarios that are difficult to curate from real-world footage. This includes systematically covering different spatial scales, perspectives, and scene dynamics, which allows for a more comprehensive evaluation than previously possible.

#### 5.2. Advanced Diagnostic Capabilities

The multi-dimensional design of VGenST-Bench provides sophisticated diagnostic tools:
- **Comprehensive Taxonomy:** The 3 × 2 × 2 video taxonomy (Spatial Scale × Perspective × Scene Dynamics) allows for detailed assessment of MLLMs' performance across a broad spectrum of spatio-temporal contexts.
- **Hierarchical QA Design:** The three-level cognitive hierarchy (L1: Visual Perception, L2: Scene Understanding, L3: Spatio-Temporal Reasoning) serves as a diagnostic instrument, clearly separating basic perceptual abilities from higher-order reasoning. The observed performance degradation from L1 to L3 across MLLMs highlights specific bottlenecks in their reasoning capabilities, indicating areas for future model improvement.
- **Robustness Evaluation:** The inclusion of question reformulation variants (e.g., "None-of-these" distractors and answers, open-ended questions) provides a rigorous method to uncover reasoning shortcuts and confirm that models are performing genuine inference rather than relying on positional biases or choice priors.

#### 5.3. Guidance for Future MLLM Development

The findings from VGenST-Bench offer clear directions for improving MLLMs:
- The significant performance gap between MLLMs and humans, especially at higher reasoning levels, indicates that current models still have considerable room for improvement in complex spatio-temporal inference.
- The diagnostic insights, such as models struggling with perspective-taking or counterfactual reasoning, can guide researchers in developing more robust architectures and training methodologies that prioritize these challenging cognitive skills.

#### 5.4. Broader Applicability and Future Work

VGenST-Bench is positioned as more than a single dataset; it proposes a scalable framework for benchmark construction:
- **Adaptability to Evolving Generative Models:** As video generative models continue to advance in fidelity, length, and control, the VGenST-Bench pipeline can be adapted to create even more complex and realistic scenarios, enabling the evaluation of extended temporal reasoning, fine-grained physical interactions, and multi-agent dynamics.
- **Expansion of Taxonomy and Hierarchy:** The existing taxonomy and QA hierarchy can be expanded to include additional evaluation criteria or more granular task breakdowns as generative capabilities mature.
- **Domain Transfer:** The methodology can be applied to create benchmarks in other challenging domains where real-world data collection is difficult or constrained, such as autonomous driving edge cases, robotics failure modes, or surgical simulations, enabling evaluation of rare or safety-critical scenarios.

By releasing the dataset, the generation pipeline, and the full evaluation suite, the authors aim to catalyze further research into spatio-temporal reasoning for MLLMs, fostering the development of more capable and robust AI systems.
