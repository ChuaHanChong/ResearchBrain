# CPF: Learning a Contact Potential Field to Model the Hand-Object Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called MIHO integrates a Contact Potential Field (CPF) and an anatomically constrained hand model (A-MANO) to improve 3D hand-object interaction modeling from single RGB images. This approach achieved more physically plausible grasps, evidenced by significant reductions in interpenetration and disjointedness on FHB and HO3D datasets compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing hand-object (HO) pose estimation methods often treat contact implicitly, leading to issues like interpenetration or disjointedness between hand and object meshes.
- Parametric hand models, while flexible, frequently produce anatomically implausible hand postures when optimized with high degrees of freedom, lacking specific anatomical constraints.
- Achieving physically plausible HO poses from potentially noisy ground-truth data, ensuring stable grasps and avoiding self-intersections, remains a complex challenge.

## Method

- The **Contact Potential Field (CPF)** is introduced to explicitly model multi-point hand-object contact as a ==spring-mass system==, featuring attractive springs for contact affinity and repulsive springs to prevent interpenetration.
- **A-MANO**, an anatomically constrained hand model based on ==MANO==, is developed to prevent abnormal poses by restricting joint rotations within a novel ==twist-splay-bend coordinate frame== with axial and angular constraints.
- A hybrid learning-fitting framework named **MIHO** is proposed, integrating **HoNet** for coarse HO pose estimation, **PiCR** for learning CPF (contact semantics and elasticities), and **GeO** for refining poses by minimizing CPF elastic energy and applying anatomical constraints.

## Results

- On the FHB dataset, MIHO reduced Penetration Depth by **18%** (**16.92 mm** vs. **20.63 mm**) and Solid Intersection Volume by **44%** (**11.76 cm³** vs. **21.10 cm³**) compared to a baseline, indicating improved physical plausibility.
- MIHO achieved lower Hand MPVPE (**23.99 mm** vs. **24.80 mm**) and significantly reduced interpenetration (PD **11.42 mm** vs. **18.57 mm**; SIV **3.46 cm³** vs. **9.62 cm³**) on HO3Dv1 compared to a reproduced baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the repulsive springs within CPF are effective in mitigating interpenetration, and A-MANO's anatomical constraints successfully prevent abnormal hand postures, contributing to more natural grasp configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling hand-object contact through a potential field with learned per-vertex elasticities can effectively balance the competing goals of preventing interpenetration and ensuring stable, multi-point contact.
> - Integrating anatomical constraints directly into a flexible parametric hand model like MANO, via a tailored coordinate system, allows for realistic and anatomically plausible hand poses without sacrificing modeling adaptability.
> - Combining deep learning for initial estimation and semantic contact recovery with non-linear optimization for physical and anatomical refinement leads to robust and physically coherent 3D hand-object interaction modeling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modeling hand-object (HO) interaction from a single image is a problem area within computer vision and graphics that is relevant for understanding human activities and for applications such as virtual/augmented reality (VR/AR), teleoperation, and robotic grasping. Significant advancements have been made in separately estimating 3D hand pose and shape or 3D object pose and shape using deep learning methods. However, the simultaneous estimation of HO pose, coupled with the accurate modeling of contact between the hand and the object, presents a more complex challenge that has received less comprehensive exploration.

Prior approaches to joint HO estimation have often treated contact as a byproduct of accurate pose estimation. This assumes that if hand and object poses are perfectly recovered, contact will naturally be satisfied. In practice, achieving such perfection is difficult, leading to issues like interpenetration (hand and object occupying the same space) or disjointedness (hand and object appearing unnaturally separated). Some methods have attempted to mitigate these issues by employing distance-based attraction and repulsion heuristics or by refining predicted poses using physics simulators. However, these solutions have limitations; distance-based heuristics often lack specific contact semantics (i.e., which specific parts of the hand contact which specific parts of the object), and physics simulators, while offering physical realism, can be inflexible regarding hand pose and shape, often relying on rigid robotic hand models.

Recent research has begun to emphasize explicit contact modeling and representation, with new datasets specifically designed to capture HO contact information. Despite these developments, a method for effectively integrating comprehensive contact modeling into existing HO pose estimation pipelines remained an open area of research.

This paper positions its work as an advancement in this landscape by introducing an explicit contact representation and an anatomically constrained hand model within a hybrid learning-fitting framework. This aims to overcome the limitations of previous methods by providing a semantic understanding of contact and ensuring anatomically plausible hand configurations, thereby enabling more robust and physically accurate HO interaction modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust method for modeling hand-object interaction from a single RGB image. This involves not only estimating the 3D pose and shape of both the hand and the object but also accurately recovering a natural and physically plausible grasp configuration, specifically focusing on the contact between the two entities.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges identified in the existing literature:

- **Lack of Explicit Contact Representation:** Current HO pose estimation methods frequently address contact implicitly through post-hoc refinement or simple heuristic loss functions (e.g., proximity metrics, signed distance functions). These approaches often lack a granular, semantic understanding of contact, failing to identify specific hand-object vertex pairings that are in contact. This can lead to difficulties in simultaneously preventing interpenetration and ensuring sufficient contact (avoiding disjointedness) in a balanced manner. The authors are motivated to develop an explicit representation that captures the "contact semantics"—the affinities between specific hand and object vertices that come into contact.

- **Anatomical Plausibility of Hand Models:** Many state-of-the-art methods utilize parametric hand models like MANO, which offer high flexibility through pose and shape parameters. However, optimizing these high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) parameters can result in anatomically abnormal or implausible hand postures. While robotics-focused methods sometimes use dexterous hand models within physics simulators to guarantee valid poses, these models are often rigid and less suitable for the dynamic and deformable nature of human hands in computer vision and graphics applications. There is a motivation to develop a hand model that retains MANO's flexibility while incorporating anatomical constraints to prevent unnatural poses.

- **Achieving Physically Plausible HO Poses:** Even when ground-truth (GT) data is available, it can be noisy and exhibit issues like interpenetration, as observed in datasets like FHB. Existing methods, if they closely replicate such GT, might inadvertently produce physically implausible results. The motivation here is to develop a framework that can not only achieve high reconstruction accuracy but also inherently produce physically plausible interactions by actively avoiding interpenetration and ensuring stable contact, even in the presence of noisy GT.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the paper are:
1. To propose **Contact Potential Field (CPF)**, an explicit contact representation that models hand-object interaction based on multi-point contact affinities, treating each contacting HO vertex pair as a spring-mass system.
2. To introduce **A-MANO**, an anatomically constrained hand model based on MANO, which restricts joint rotations within a novel twist-splay-bend coordinate frame to mitigate anatomical abnormalities.
3. To present **MIHO (Modeling the Interaction of Hand and Object)**, a novel learning-fitting hybrid framework that integrates HoNet (for coarse pose estimation), PiCR (for learning CPF and contact semantics), and GeO (for refining poses by minimizing CPF elastic energy and applying anatomical constraints).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a learning-fitting hybrid framework termed MIHO (Modeling the Interaction of Hand and Object), which operates in three stages: HoNet, PiCR, and GeO. The core of the interaction modeling lies in the Contact Potential Field (CPF) and an anatomically constrained hand model, A-MANO.

#### 3.1. Anatomically Constrained A-MANO

The A-MANO model is built upon the parametric skinning hand model, MANO, which represents hand shape and pose via shape parameters $\beta \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$ and 15 joint rotations $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{15 \times 3}$. A-MANO introduces two main modifications:
- **Twist-splay-bend Frame:** To address the issue of anatomically implausible hand poses resulting from optimizing high-DoF MANO parameters, each joint along the hand's kinematic tree is assigned a twist-splay-bend Cartesian coordinate frame. This frame's axes are coaxial to three revolute directions (twist, splay, bend) based on hand anatomy. Constraints are then imposed: axial constraints on twist and splay axes (penalizing rotations that cause finger twisting or splaying where inappropriate) and angular constraints on the bend angle (e.g., maximum bend of $\pi/2$). This guides the optimization towards anatomically valid poses.
- **Anchors:** To simplify the multi-point contact modeling, the hand mesh is divided into 17 subregions (e.g., phalanges, metacarpals, carpals). Instead of considering all hand vertices, a limited number of "anchors" (up to 4 per subregion) are interpolated on the hand surface. These anchors are chosen to be uniformly distributed within their subregions and are derivable from hand vertices in a differentiable manner, allowing backpropagation during optimization. This reduces the complexity of associating hand contact points with object vertices.

#### 3.2. Contact Potential Field (CPF)

CPF provides an explicit representation of hand-object contact by modeling interactions as a system of springs.
- **Spring-Mass System:** Each potential contacting HO vertex pair is treated as a spring-mass system. The total interaction is represented by a potential field whose elastic energy is minimized at the natural grasp position.
- **Attractive Springs:** These springs model the affinity between specific hand anchors ($a_i$) and object vertices ($v_{o_j}$). Their rest length is defined as 0, implying perfect contact. The potential energy $E_{atr_{ij}} = \frac{1}{2} k_{atr_{ij}} \|a_i - v_{o_j}\|_2^2$ is based on the Euclidean distance. The elasticity $k_{atr_{ij}}$ is a predicted value that indicates the strength of the affinity.
- **Repulsive Springs:** These springs prevent interpenetration. When a hand vertex $v_{h_i}$ is in the vicinity or penetrating an object vertex $v_{o_j}$, a repulsive force is applied. The potential energy $E_{rpl_{ij}} = \frac{1}{2} k_{rpl_{ij}} (e^{-| \Delta l_{rpl_{ij}}|})^2$, where $|\Delta l_{rpl_{ij}}|$ is the projection of $(v_{h_i} - v_{o_j})$ onto the object normal $n_{o_j}$. Repulsion is active only when the hand vertex is within a certain distance threshold (e.g., 20 mm) of the object surface. The elasticity $k_{rpl_{ij}}$ is empirically set.
- **Total Elastic Energy:** The total elastic energy $E_{elast} = \sum_i \sum_j (E_{atr_{ij}} + E_{rpl_{ij}})$ quantifies the plausibility of the HO interaction, with minimal energy representing a stable grasp.
- **Annotation of $k_{atr}$:** For training the network to predict attractive spring elasticities, ground-truth $k_{atr}$ values are automatically generated. For HO anchor-vertex pairs with a ground-truth distance below a threshold (e.g., 20 mm), $k_{atr}$ is assigned a value inversely proportional to this distance (using a cosine function for smoothness), bounded between 0 and 1. Pairs beyond the threshold are considered non-contacting with $k_{atr}=0$.

#### 3.3. Hybrid Framework – MIHO

MIHO integrates learning and optimization in three stages:
1. **HoNet (Hand-object Pose Estimation Network):** This initial stage takes an RGB image as input and predicts coarse 6D poses for the hand (wrist pose, PCA components of MANO pose, and shape) and the object using a baseline MeshRegNet. This provides initial meshes $V_h$ and $V_o$.
2. **PiCR (Pixel-wise Contact Recovery Module):** This module learns to construct the CPF from the image and the coarse HO meshes. It predicts three cascaded outcomes for each object vertex $v_{o_j}$:
 - **Vertex Contact (VC) $\in \mathbb{R}^{N_o}$:** A probability indicating if an object vertex is in contact with the hand. Trained with a binary focal loss.
 - **Contact Region (CR) $\in \mathbb{R}^{N_o \times 17}$:** For contact-likely object vertices, probabilities indicating affinity to the 17 hand subregions. Trained with a multi-class focal loss.
 - **Anchor Elasticity (AE) $\in \mathbb{R}^{N_o}$:** The elasticity $k_{atr_{ij}}$ for the attractive spring connecting $v_{o_j}$ to its most probable hand anchor (from CR prediction). Trained with a binary cross-entropy loss against annotated $k_{atr}$.
 PiCR utilizes an Hourglass Network backbone, a PointNet encoder for object vertex features, and multi-layer perceptron heads for VC, CR, and AE predictions. These predictions, along with the coarse HO meshes, are used to recover the $E_{elast}$ (Algorithm 1 in the paper).
3. **GeO (Grasping Energy Optimizer):** This is a non-linear optimization stage that refines the HO pose parameters to minimize a combined objective function:
 $ \min_{P_o, P_w, R_j} (E_{elast} + L_{anat} + L_{offset}) $
 - $P_o$: Object 6D pose.
 - $P_w$: Hand wrist 6D pose.
 - $R_j$: 15 joint rotations of the A-MANO model.
 - $E_{elast}$: The elastic energy from the CPF, recovered by PiCR.
 - $L_{anat}$: An anatomical cost function that penalizes unwanted axial components (twist, splay) and excessive angular values (bend) in the A-MANO joint rotations, ensuring anatomical plausibility.
 - $L_{offset}$: An L2 distance loss that penalizes large deviations of the refined HO vertices from their initial HoNet predictions, stabilizing the optimization.
 GeO is implemented in PyTorch, leveraging its auto-differentiation capabilities, and uses an Adam solver.

#### 3.4. Datasets and Metrics

The method is evaluated on two real-world datasets: FHB and HO3D, with specific subsets and augmentation procedures used (e.g., HO3Dv2$^{-}$ for HO3D v2, and HO3Dv1$^{+}$ for HO3D v1 training augmentation).
Evaluation metrics cover both reconstruction quality and physical plausibility:
- **MPVPE (Mean Per Vertex Position Error):** Measures 3D hand and object reconstruction accuracy.
- **Penetration Depth (PD):** Maximum depth of hand vertices penetrating the object.
- **Solid Intersection Volume (SIV):** Volume of intersection between hand and object meshes.
- **Disjointedness Distance (DD):** Average distance of hand fingertips to the object surface, encouraging stable contact.
- **Simulation Displacement (SD):** Measures grasp stability by simulating object displacement under gravity in a physics simulator (PyBullet) while holding the hand fixed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented quantitative and qualitative results, including ablation studies, to assess the performance of the proposed MIHO framework.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art

The MIHO framework was compared against established baselines, including and an adapted version of ObMan (termed ObMan$^*$) which incorporated contact losses into's MeshRegNet.

- **FHB Dataset:**
 - MIHO (hand-object setting) demonstrated improvements in physical plausibility metrics. It reduced Penetration Depth (16.92 mm vs. 20.63 mm for and 19.76 mm for ObMan$^*$), Solid Intersection Volume (11.76 cm$^3$ vs. 21.10 cm$^3$ for and 16.16 cm$^3$ for ObMan$^*$), and Disjointedness Distance (22.41 mm vs. 37.40 mm for and 27.95 mm for ObMan$^*$). These reductions indicated a more physically plausible interaction compared to baselines and even the FHB ground truth, which itself suffered from interpenetration (GT PD 19.55mm, SIV 20.41cm$^3$, DD 37.28mm).
 - Hand MPVPE (19.54 mm) and Object MPVPE (21.57 mm) for MIHO were comparable to or slightly higher than's (17.51 mm and 21.06 mm respectively), suggesting a trade-off where minor increases in vertex error lead to substantial improvements in physical realism.
 - Simulation Displacement was also improved (58.02 mm vs. 65.48 mm for), indicating a more stable grasp.
- **HO3D Dataset (v1 and v2$^{-}$):**
 - On HO3Dv1, MIHO (hand-object setting) outperformed the reproduced$^+$ across most metrics. For instance, it achieved lower Hand MPVPE (23.99 mm vs. 24.80 mm), lower PD (11.42 mm vs. 18.57 mm), lower SIV (3.46 cm$^3$ vs. 9.62 cm$^3$), and lower DD (11.83 mm vs. 18.62 mm).
 - $^+$ showed a slightly lower Simulation Displacement (25.68 mm vs. 27.66 mm), which the authors attributed to the stability derived from interpenetration forces balancing each other in the baseline.
 - On the selected HO3Dv2$^{-}$ subset, MIHO showed lower wrist-relative object vertex error compared to.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons demonstrated that MIHO generated hand-object poses that were more visually appealing and physically coherent, particularly in avoiding interpenetration and maintaining natural contact, even when ground truth exhibited inconsistencies.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

- **Comparison with Simple Distance-based Contact Heuristics:**
 - MIHO, utilizing CPF, was compared against "Vanilla Contact" (simple fingertip-to-object attraction) and "ObMan Contact" (ObMan's interaction losses in GeO).
 - MIHO achieved better overall performance on reconstruction (Hand and Object MPVPE) and physical quality (PD, SIV) compared to both baselines. While Vanilla and ObMan Contact showed slightly lower Disjointedness Distance (due to direct optimization for attraction), they suffered from higher interpenetration and lower reconstruction accuracy.
 - MIHO demonstrated computational efficiency, requiring an average of 55.77 ms per iteration, compared to 103.41 ms for ObMan Contact, suggesting a more efficient optimization landscape.
- **Effectiveness of Repulsive Springs ($E_{rpl}$):**
 - Removing the repulsive energy term from CPF led to increased Penetration Depth (17.79 mm vs. 16.92 mm) and Solid Intersection Volume (13.76 cm$^3$ vs. 11.76 cm$^3$). This confirms the contribution of repulsive springs in actively mitigating interpenetration. The remaining reduction in interpenetration compared to FHB GT (19.55mm, 20.41cm$^3$) was attributed to the implicit repulsive behavior of attractive springs when the hand penetrates the object.
 - The study also explored varying the empirical elasticity ($k_{rpl}$) of repulsive springs. Higher $k_{rpl}$ values further reduced interpenetration but could lead to increased disjointedness and slightly higher vertex errors, indicating a need for balanced parameter selection.
- **Effectiveness of Anatomical Constraints ($L_{anat}$):**
 - An experiment comparing optimization with and without $L_{anat}$ demonstrated that the anatomical constraints effectively prevented abnormal hand postures (e.g., unnatural joint rotations or hyper-extension/flexion) during the pose refinement process, resulting in more anatomically plausible hand configurations.
 - A specific example highlighted A-MANO's ability to correct unwanted thumb twists present in some ground-truth data, leading to a more visually pleasing and natural pose.
- **A-MANO with PCA Pose vs. Full MANO:**
 - Comparing A-MANO driven by full 15 relative joint rotations (so(3)) versus 15 PCA components of rotations showed that the full MANO, when combined with anatomical constraints, achieved a notable decrease in hand MPVPE. This indicated that full joint rotations offer greater flexibility and accuracy, which can be leveraged without sacrificing anatomical plausibility when constraints are applied.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of 3D hand-object interaction modeling by introducing methodological advancements that enhance both the physical plausibility and anatomical correctness of estimated poses.

The **Contact Potential Field (CPF)** represents a significant contribution as an explicit contact representation. By modeling hand-object contact as a system of attractive and repulsive springs, CPF provides a semantic understanding of multi-point contact, moving beyond simple distance heuristics. This unified energy minimization framework can effectively manage the competing objectives of avoiding interpenetration and ensuring stable contact (preventing disjointedness). The ability to assign per-vertex contact semantics to object meshes and learn spring elasticities allows for more nuanced and context-aware interaction modeling.

The introduction of **A-MANO**, an anatomically constrained hand model, addresses a key limitation of optimizing flexible parametric hand models like MANO. By embedding anatomical rules within a twist-splay-bend coordinate frame, the framework can consistently produce hand poses that are anatomically plausible, preventing the abnormal joint configurations often seen in high-DoF regression or optimization. This is crucial for applications where realistic human hand motion is paramount.

The **MIHO hybrid framework** integrates these innovations within a practical learning-fitting pipeline. The initial learning stage (HoNet and PiCR) leverages deep learning for coarse estimation and semantic contact recovery, while the subsequent optimization stage (GeO) refines these estimations using physical and anatomical principles. This combination yields robust performance, demonstrating state-of-the-art results on standard benchmarks, particularly in producing physically plausible HO poses even from noisy ground-truth data.

The potential impact of this work is broad. In **VR/AR**, physically plausible and anatomically correct hand-object interactions are fundamental for immersive and believable user experiences. For **teleoperation** and **robotic grasping**, the explicit contact representation and stable grasp configurations derived from MIHO could inform better grasp planning and execution. In the broader context of **human activity understanding**, accurately modeled hand-object interactions provide richer cues for analyzing and interpreting human actions involving manipulation. The proposed CPF could serve as a foundational contact representation for future research in these domains, potentially extending to object-agnostic interaction modeling.
