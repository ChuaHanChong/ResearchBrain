# StaMo: Unsupervised Learning of Generalizable Robot Motion from Compact State Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> An unsupervised framework, StaMo, learns a compact two-token state representation from static images, allowing robot motion to emerge as a simple difference between states. This approach enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, improving task success rates on benchmarks like LIBERO by up to 11.1% and enabling strong zero-shot generalization to new environments while maintaining high inference speeds.

## Problem

- Robotic state representation learning faces a trade-off between compactness (efficient) and expressiveness (semantically rich), hindering efficient world modeling and action generation.
- Learning robot motion from video data is computationally expensive, often yields ambiguous action representations, and struggles with temporal complexities.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are often reactive, lack explicit world modeling capabilities, and rely on redundant state representations, which limits generalization and predictive reasoning.

## Method

- A ==Diffusion Autoencoder== with a frozen DINOv2 encoder and a trainable Diffusion Transformer decoder is employed to compress high-dimensional images into a compact two-token latent state.
- Robot motion is represented as the direct vector difference between consecutive compact latent state tokens, defining ==latent actions== from static observations.
- The compact state representations are integrated into existing VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA) as an auxiliary task for future state prediction, and used to generate pseudo-actions for policy co-training from unlabeled video data.

## Results

- StaMo reconstructs high-quality images from its compact two-token latent state and enables plausible, continuous motion interpolation, demonstrating strong generalization across diverse robotic datasets.
- Integration into VLA baselines like OpenVLA and OpenVLA-OFT improved task success rates on the LIBERO benchmark by up to **11.1%** and **4.6%** respectively, with minimal inference overhead.
- The method shows superior zero-shot transfer performance to unseen environments and enhances real-world manipulation, achieving an average success rate of **0.56** for OpenVLA + StaMo state compared to **0.25** for the OpenVLA baseline on real-world tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - It is possible to learn a highly compact (e.g., two 1024-dimensional tokens) yet semantically expressive state representation from static images alone, capturing critical information like robot poses and object interactions.
> - Robot motion can naturally emerge as a simple vector difference between consecutive compact state representations, challenging the necessity of complex temporal modeling from video data.
> - Integrating compact state representations into VLA models enables efficient world modeling and future state prediction, which regularizes policies and enhances primary action prediction without significant computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses foundational challenges in embodied intelligence, particularly concerning the learning of state representations for robotic systems. Current approaches often present a trade-off between the compactness and expressiveness of visual representations. High-dimensional visual embeddings, while rich in perceptual detail, can be redundant and computationally intensive for world modeling and action generation. Conversely, compact representations like optical flow or trajectories may lack the semantic richness required to encode complex states, goal information, or intricate interaction dynamics.

A significant aspect of the broader landscape this work challenges is the dominant paradigm of learning latent actions from video data. While intuitively aligned with the temporal nature of motion, video-based methods often necessitate complex, computationally expensive temporal models and can yield ambiguous or coarse-grained action representations due to high variance within video clips. This approach often leads to inefficient per-frame learning and flawed representations.

In the context of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, existing frameworks, often benefiting from large-scale pre-training on internet data, primarily operate as reactive policies, directly mapping visuolinguistic context to low-level actions. While effective, this setup typically lacks explicit world modeling capabilities, limiting the agent's ability to reason about future states or the physical consequences of its actions. Attempts to integrate world modeling often involve decoding full images or relying on overly redundant state representations, which can hinder generalization and inference efficiency.

StaMo aims to bridge these gaps by introducing an unsupervised method for learning a compact (e.g., two-token) yet expressive state representation from static images. By demonstrating that robot motion can emerge naturally as a simple difference between these compact state representations, StaMo offers an alternative to complex video-based motion learning. Furthermore, it integrates these representations into existing VLA architectures to enhance world modeling and policy performance without significant computational overhead, thereby advancing the field towards more efficient, generalizable, and scalable robotic learning systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an unsupervised learning framework, named StaMo, capable of extracting a compact and expressive state representation from static visual observations. This representation is intended to be efficient for world modeling and decision-making in embodied intelligence contexts.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Learning Compact and Expressive State Representations:** A core motivation is to overcome the prevalent trade-off in robot representation learning, where methods are either compact but lack semantic richness (e.g., flow fields) or expressive but high-dimensional and redundant (e.g., raw image features). The objective is to learn a representation that is both highly compressed (e.g., as few as two 1024-dimensional tokens) and sufficiently expressive to capture task-critical information, such as robot poses, object interactions, and goal states. This compactness is motivated by the need for efficient future prediction and real-time execution in robotics.
- **Emergence of Motion from Static State Representations:** The research is motivated by a fundamental question: whether a sufficiently expressive state representation learned from individual static frames can inherently encapsulate meaningful latent actions through simple differences between states. This challenges the conventional reliance on complex temporal modeling and video data for learning robotic motions, which often entails computational expense, ambiguous action representations, and sensitivity to temporal parameters. The objective is to demonstrate that robot motion can naturally emerge from a compact state representation space derived from static images.
- **Enhancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** The work aims to integrate the proposed compact state representation seamlessly into existing VLM-based architectures. The motivation is to improve VLA model performance by endowing them with efficient world modeling capabilities, allowing them to anticipate future visual states and reason about action consequences. This auxiliary task is hypothesized to regularize policies and enhance primary action prediction, addressing the limitations of purely reactive policies and redundant state representations in current VLA frameworks.
- **Achieving Generalizability and Scalability:** A further objective is to develop a representation that exhibits strong generalization capabilities across diverse robotic tasks and environments, including zero-shot transfer to unseen scenes. The method is also motivated by the need for scalability, enabling effective learning from varied data sources, including real-world robot data, simulation data, and action-free human egocentric videos, thereby facilitating unsupervised skill discovery.
- **Improving Interpretability and Efficiency:** The research aims to produce latent actions that are more interpretable and less ambiguous than those learned from video sequences. Additionally, the approach seeks to ensure minimal inference overhead, making it practical for real-world robotic deployments where efficiency is critical.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

StaMo proposes a framework for learning generalizable robotic motion from compact state representations encoded from static images. The methodology encompasses three main components: compressing images into compact states, interpolating motion from these states, and utilizing these representations for efficient world modeling and policy co-training.

#### 3.1. Compress Image into Compact State

The core of the state representation learning is a Diffusion Autoencoder architecture.
- **Encoder (E):** The encoder comprises a frozen DINOv2 model, which serves as a feature extractor, followed by a trainable transformer-based compressor. This compressor maps high-dimensional input observations into a compact sequence of tokens. The implementation utilizes two 1024-dimensional tokens as the compact latent state.
- **Decoder (D):** The decoder is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) initialized from a pre-trained DiT model (specifically, Stable Diffusion 3). It is conditioned on the compact tokens generated by the encoder to reconstruct the original input image observation.
- **Training Objective:** During training, only the transformer-based compressor and the DiT decoder are optimized. The DINOv2 encoder remains frozen. The model is trained using the Flow Matching objective, which minimizes the squared error between the velocity predicted by the decoder and a target velocity. This objective is applied in the latent space of a pre-trained VAE encoder, which transforms the input image `x_0` into `z_0`. The training aims to reconstruct `z_0` accurately, forcing the compact tokens to encapsulate essential state information.

#### 3.2. Interpolating Motion from States

A key insight of StaMo is that robot motion can be represented as the difference between consecutive compact state tokens.
- **Motion Definition:** Motion `a_t` is explicitly defined as the vector difference between the latent encoding of the next state `s_{t+1}` and the current state `s_t`, i.e., `a_t = s_{t+1} - s_t`.
- **Rationale:** This approach bridges the gap between traditional representations that are either compact (for dynamics) but not expressive (for rich context) or expressive (for state) but not compact (for dynamics). By learning a compact and expressive state, the simple vector difference inherently captures the "how the world changes" aspect while the state tokens themselves describe "what the world looks like."

#### 3.3. StaMo for Efficient World Modeling

The compact state representations are integrated into existing VLA models to imbue them with predictive world modeling capabilities.
- **Integration:** StaMo's compact representations are incorporated into VLA architectures, specifically OpenVLA and its successor OpenVLA-OFT. The StaMo encoder `E` replaces the original vision encoders.
- **Auxiliary Prediction Task:** A lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) head is attached to the VLA's autoregressive backbone. This head is tasked with predicting the subsequent state representation `s_pred`.
- **Loss Function:** The model is optimized using a composite loss function `L_total`. This combines the standard cross-entropy loss for action generation (`L_action`) with a world model objective. The world model objective consists of both Mean Squared Error (MSE) and L1 losses between the predicted future state `s_pred` and the ground-truth future state `s_gt`. In experiments, `λ_action` and `λ_future` were set equally, reflecting the hypothesis that learning to predict future states is as crucial as learning to act.

#### 3.4. StaMo for Latent Motions Co-Training

To evaluate the utility and quality of the emergent latent motion, a policy co-training strategy is employed.
- **Pseudo-Action Generation:** For a large dataset of action-less video data, latent motions `m_t` are inferred for each pair of consecutive video frames. This is achieved by computing `m_t = E(o_{t+1}) - E(o_t)`, where `E(·)` is the frozen StaMo encoder and `o_t` and `o_{t+1}` are consecutive observations.
- **Unified Dataset:** These pseudo-action labels are combined with a small set of action-labeled robot demonstration data.
- **Policy Training:** A single policy model is then jointly trained on this unified dataset, learning from both ground-truth robot actions and StaMo-generated pseudo-actions. This method allows leveraging vast amounts of unlabeled visual data to enhance policy learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted validate StaMo's capabilities across various metrics, including reconstruction quality, world modeling performance, inference efficiency, generalization, and its utility in co-training and planning.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Analysis of StaMo (Section 4.1)

StaMo demonstrates high-quality image reconstructions from its compact two-token (1024-dimensional) latent state, as evidenced by PSNR and SSIM metrics (Table 1). The model exhibits strong generalization, performing well on both in-domain (LIBERO, DROID) and out-of-domain (ManiSkill) data. Qualitative results (Figure 3) illustrate plausible and continuous motion interpolation by linearly interpolating tokens in the latent space. Furthermore, the learned latent motion proves highly transferable across sim-to-sim, sim-to-real, and real-to-sim scenarios, suggesting scene-independent motion generalization.

#### 4.2. World Modeling Results (Section 4.2)

Integrating StaMo into VLA baselines (OpenVLA and OpenVLA-OFT) significantly improves task success rates on the LIBERO benchmark (Table 2). OpenVLA* + StaMo state achieves an average success rate of 87.6%, an 11.1% improvement over the original OpenVLA (76.5%). Similarly, OpenVLA-OFT* + StaMo state reaches 96.8% average success, an increase of 4.6% over OpenVLA-OFT (92.2%). The optimal choice between predicting StaMo state and StaMo motion depends on the policy's action horizon: motion is effective for short, single-step predictions, while state serves as a stable goal-conditioning signal for longer horizons.

#### 4.3. Inference Speed Comparison (Section 4.3)

StaMo introduces minimal inference overhead. When integrated into OpenVLA and OpenVLA-OFT, the inference frequencies are 4.02 Hz and 17.82 Hz, respectively, which are comparable to the original baselines (4.16 Hz for OpenVLA, 18.24 Hz for OpenVLA-OFT) (Table 3). This performance is higher than other world model-based VLA approaches like WorldVLA (2.27 Hz) and UniVLA (2.65 Hz).

#### 4.4. Zero-Shot Transfer to New Environment (Section 4.4)

StaMo exhibits superior zero-shot generalization. A model trained on LIBERO, when directly applied to the ManiSkill environment without fine-tuning, significantly outperforms competing methods (OpenVLA, UniVLA, WorldVLA) across various tasks (Table 4). For example, OpenVLA + StaMo state achieves 41% success on "Pick Cube" compared to 28% for OpenVLA.

#### 4.5. Policy Co-training Results (Section 4.5)

In policy co-training experiments using the RDT architecture, StaMo-generated pseudo-actions lead to substantial performance gains (Table 5). Training RDT with 1 real trajectory and 4 StaMo-generated video trajectories achieves an average success rate of 84.6%, outperforming RDT with only 1 real trajectory (62.9%) and co-training with LAPA (74.2%) and ATM (73.4%).

#### 4.6. Action Linear Probing Results (Section 4.6)

Linear probing quantitatively validates the quality of emergent latent actions. StaMo consistently achieves the lowest Mean Squared Error (MSE) when predicting ground-truth action sequences from the vector difference between latent states, across prediction horizons of 1, 2, 4, and 8 steps (Figure 4). This indicates that the latent action representation is informative and linearly separable.

#### 4.7. Goal-Conditioned Task Planning (Section 4.7)

For goal-conditioned task planning on ManiSkill, StaMo outperforms DINO-WM, with StaMo achieving a 32% success rate on "Push Cube" compared to DINO-WM's 8% (Table 6).

#### 4.8. Real-World Experiments (Section 4.8)

On a benchmark of six real-world manipulation tasks, StaMo significantly enhances VLA performance (Table 7). OpenVLA + StaMo state achieves an average success rate of 0.56, and OpenVLA-OFT + StaMo state achieves 0.64, both substantially higher than the OpenVLA baseline of 0.25.

#### 4.9. Ablation Study (Section 4.9)

- **Token Dimension (4.9.1):** Varying the hidden dimension of the two-token latent state (256, 512, 1024) has limited impact on reconstruction performance, suggesting that the pre-trained DiT decoder's generative prior offsets potential information loss in lower dimensions (Table 8).
- **Additional Training Data (4.9.2):** StaMo's performance scales positively with increased volume and diversity of training data, including simulation, DROID, OXE, and human egocentric video data (Figure 6).
- **Pretrained Diffusion Decoder (4.9.3):** The use of a pre-trained diffusion decoder is critical. Training a decoder from scratch required longer convergence times and did not match the performance of the pre-trained counterpart, highlighting the importance of the strong generative prior from large-scale natural image datasets (Table 9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

StaMo introduces a novel approach to learning state representations and emergent motion in robotics, challenging established paradigms and offering several areas of potential impact:

- **Rethinking Motion Learning:** By demonstrating that generalizable robot motion can naturally emerge as a simple vector difference within a compact state representation space learned from static images, StaMo offers an alternative to the prevailing reliance on complex temporal modeling and video data. This simplification could lead to more efficient and less ambiguous action representations.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Generalization for VLAs:** The learned compact state representation significantly improves the performance of existing VLA models with minimal inference overhead. Its ability to generalize across diverse data sources and achieve strong zero-shot transfer to new environments suggests a pathway towards more robust and adaptable robotic policies. This efficiency and generalization are critical for deploying intelligent agents in varied real-world scenarios.
- **Scalable Unsupervised Skill Discovery:** The methodology supports policy co-training by generating pseudo-action labels from unlabeled video data, allowing robotic systems to leverage vast and heterogeneous visual datasets. This scalability is crucial for advancing unsupervised skill discovery and alleviating the need for extensive, manually-labeled robot demonstration data.
- **Improved World Modeling Capabilities:** StaMo's integration into VLA architectures enables explicit world modeling by predicting future states. This predictive capability can regularize policies, improve future planning, and enhance the agent's understanding of action consequences, moving beyond purely reactive control.
- **Implications for Embodied AI Foundations:** The finding that large-scale visual models can implicitly learn a linearized dynamics manifold from static observations suggests a deeper underlying capacity within these models. This could simplify the design of future embodied AI systems by reducing the explicit complexity required for motion extraction and temporal reasoning.
- **Practical Applicability:** The approach's strong performance in both simulation and real-world experiments, combined with its computational efficiency and generalizability, indicates its potential for direct application in developing more capable and reliable robot manipulation systems for various industrial and service tasks.
