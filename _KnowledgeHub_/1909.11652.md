# Deep Dynamics Models for Learning Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> An approach called Online Planning with Deep Dynamics Models (PDDM) enables dexterous multi-fingered robots to efficiently learn complex manipulation skills. This method combines deep neural network dynamics models with advanced online model-predictive control, achieving up to 100% success for 90-degree Baoding ball rotations on a real 24-DoF Shadow Hand with just under 2 hours of real-world training.

## Problem

- Dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered hands is challenging due to complex contact dynamics, redundant degrees of freedom, and controlling unactuated objects.
- Model-free reinforcement learning methods are data-intensive for real-world robotics and often yield task-specific policies that lack flexibility.
- Prior model-based reinforcement learning approaches have generally not scaled effectively to high-dimensional manipulators and complex contact-rich environments.

## Method

- **PDDM** learns an ==ensemble of deep neural network dynamics models== to capture complex system dynamics and quantify ==epistemic uncertainty==, using supervised learning on collected state transitions.
- It employs an improved online ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== with a novel optimizer that utilizes ==reward-weighted refinement== and ==action smoothing== through temporal filtering of noise samples.
- The approach operates in a closed-loop fashion, iteratively refining the learned dynamics model with collected real-world or simulated data for continuous control.

## Results

- PDDM successfully learned diverse complex dexterous manipulation tasks in simulation (**1-2 hours**) and real-world (**2-4 hours**), including Baoding balls on a **24-DoF** Shadow Hand, achieving **100%** success for 90-degree rotations in under **2 hours**.
- It consistently outperformed state-of-the-art model-based and model-free RL algorithms in sample efficiency and generalization on challenging tasks like arbitrary handwriting and Baoding ball manipulation.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of a sufficiently large model architecture, model ensembles, intermediate planning horizons, and PDDM's refined MPC controller for robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining deep dynamics models with an ensemble approach effectively captures complex, contact-rich interactions and manages model uncertainty in high-dimensional dexterous manipulation.
> - An advanced online planning optimizer, incorporating reward-weighted refinement and action smoothing, is critical for achieving precision and scaling in complex control tasks.
> - Learning a general dynamics model provides inherent flexibility, enabling adaptation to arbitrary task specifications and generalization to variations without extensive retraining, unlike task-specific model-free policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered robotic hands represents a significant challenge in the field of robotics, aiming to bridge the gap between human hand versatility and robotic capabilities. While simple parallel-jaw grippers suffice for some tasks, many complex operations necessitate the redundant degrees of freedom inherent in dexterous hands for object-centric control, precise force application, and dynamic object manipulation. Tasks such as in-hand object reorientation, finger gaits, or fine motor skills like writing require intricate balancing of contact forces, repeated contact breaking and re-establishment, and control over unactuated objects.

Traditional analytical approaches to manipulation struggle with these complexities, particularly due to the prevalence of contact dynamics, which require highly accurate system models and face computation times that scale exponentially with the number of contacts. This has often limited their application to simpler manipulators and more constrained tasks.

In response to these challenges, learning-based techniques have emerged as a data-driven alternative. Model-free reinforcement learning (RL) methods have achieved success in complex tasks but typically demand vast amounts of data, rendering them difficult to apply in real-world scenarios, and often yield task-specific policies that lack flexibility for varied goals. Conversely, model-based RL methods offer the potential for greater sample efficiency by learning system dynamics, but historically have not scaled effectively to tasks involving high-dimensional manipulators and complex contact-rich environments.

Prior work in hardware development for multi-fingered hands has advanced significantly, but corresponding advancements in manipulation capabilities have not kept pace. While some research has explored geometric planning, multi-phase planners, and push-grasping, these methods often require precise system characterization and have struggled to scale to more sophisticated manipulators or complex tasks. Recent deep RL efforts have aimed to leverage data for control, with some even incorporating expert demonstrations to improve sample efficiency, but general sample efficiency and flexibility remain challenges.

This paper addresses the limitations of both model-free and existing model-based RL by presenting a method that combines advances in learned dynamics models with improved online model-predictive control. It positions itself by demonstrating that model-based RL, specifically with deep dynamics models, can indeed be scaled to complex, high-dimensional dexterous manipulation tasks in both simulation and the real world, doing so with significantly reduced data requirements and offering greater flexibility compared to previous methods. The work aims to challenge the perception that learned models are difficult or insufficient for impressive control results, pushing the empirical boundaries of model-based RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robotic systems equipped with dexterous multi-fingered hands to autonomously acquire and perform a wide range of complex manipulation skills efficiently and flexibly. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges and limitations identified in the current state of robotic manipulation:

1. **Complexity of Dexterous Tasks:** Many advanced manipulation behaviors, such as in-hand object reorientation, finger gaits, or fine motor skills like handwriting, require finely balancing contact forces, repeatedly establishing and breaking contacts, and maintaining control of unactuated objects. These tasks are notoriously difficult to control using traditional analytical methods due to the need for precise system characterization and the computational burden of contact modeling.
2. **Limitations of Existing Learning Approaches:**
 - **Model-Free Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While powerful for complex tasks, model-free methods are highly data-intensive, making their application in real-world robotics prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. Furthermore, they often learn task-specific policies, limiting their flexibility and generalization to variations in task goals or new environments.
 - **Prior Model-Based RL:** Although more sample-efficient, previous model-based RL approaches have generally not demonstrated the ability to scale up to the level of complexity required for fine motor skills with high-dimensional dexterous manipulators.
3. **Need for Sample Efficiency:** Real-world robotic learning is constrained by the cost and time associated with collecting interaction data. A method that can learn complex skills from modest amounts of real-world experience is highly desirable.
4. **Desire for Flexibility and Generalization:** Robotic systems should ideally be capable of adapting to varying task specifications or novel scenarios without requiring extensive retraining. Learned policies that are rigid and task-specific limit the utility of robots in dynamic environments.
5. **Empirical Validation of Deep Models for Control:** The research is motivated by a desire to empirically demonstrate that deep neural network dynamics models, when properly integrated with online planning, can indeed enable impressive control results on complex manipulation tasks, challenging the perception that such models are inherently difficult to learn or inadequate for high-performance control.

To address these motivations, the key objective is to develop and validate an approach, termed Online Planning with Deep Dynamics Models (PDDM), that leverages improvements in learned dynamics models and online model-predictive control to efficiently and effectively learn flexible, contact-rich dexterous manipulation skills. Specifically, the authors aim to:
- Show that PDDM can autonomously accomplish a variety of complex dexterous manipulation tasks, including those involving high-dimensional manipulators (up to 24-DoF hands) and free-floating objects.
- Demonstrate the sample efficiency of PDDM, particularly in real-world scenarios, by learning complex tasks with limited real-world interaction data (e.g., approximately 4 hours).
- Illustrate the versatility and generalization capabilities of the learned models, enabling performance on arbitrary task specifications (e.g., following arbitrary writing patterns) without additional training.
- Analyze the impact of different design decisions within the PDDM framework, such as model architecture, ensemble use, planning horizon, and controller type, to identify crucial components for success.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Online Planning with Deep Dynamics Models (PDDM), integrates deep model-based reinforcement learning with advanced online planning to acquire dexterous manipulation skills. The overall approach operates through an iterative cycle of collecting data by executing planned actions in the real world or simulation, and then using this data to refine the learned dynamics model.

#### 3.1. Model-Based Reinforcement Learning Framework

PDDM frames the control problem within a Markov Decision Process (MDP). Its core principle involves learning an approximate state transition distribution, denoted `p_hat_theta(s'|s, a)`, which models the system dynamics. This learned model is subsequently used for online planning to determine optimal actions that maximize expected cumulative rewards.

#### 3.2. Learning the Dynamics Model

A deep neural network serves as the function approximator for the dynamics model, `f_hat_theta(s, a)`, representing the mean of a conditional Gaussian distribution `N(f_hat_theta(s, a), Sigma)`. This high-capacity network is intended to capture the complex, non-linear interactions inherent in dexterous manipulation.

- **Epistemic Uncertainty Estimation:** To address potential overfitting and erroneous extrapolation by high-capacity models, PDDM employs a bootstrap ensemble of `E` deep dynamics models. Each model `theta_i` in the ensemble is initialized with different random weights and trained using distinct mini-batches of data, approximating the posterior distribution `p(theta|D)`. This ensemble-based approach helps quantify and manage model uncertainty.
- **Data Efficiency:** The dynamics model is trained using a supervised learning setup, where each observed state transition `(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})` provides a dense training signal. This allows for efficient utilization of all collected data, including off-policy data, for model improvement. The model is trained using the Adam optimizer with a mean squared error (MSE) loss.

#### 3.3. Online Planning for Closed-Loop Control (Model Predictive Control)

At each time step, PDDM utilizes Model Predictive Control (MPC) to select actions. This involves performing a short-horizon trajectory optimization using predictions from the learned dynamics model ensemble. The method evaluates several gradient-free optimizers, with PDDM ultimately employing a refined approach:

- **Evolution of Optimizers:**
 - **Random Shooting:** Generates `N` random action sequences over a planning horizon `H` and selects the sequence with the highest predicted reward. This method scales poorly with higher action space and horizon dimensions.
 - **Iterative Random-Shooting with Refinement (Cross-Entropy Method - CEM):** Improves upon random shooting by iteratively refining a Gaussian sampling distribution for actions. In each iteration, the top `J` highest-scoring action sequences are used to update the mean and variance of the sampling distribution for the next iteration. While better, it still faces challenges with high dimensionality and tasks requiring both coordination and precision.
 - **PDDM's Optimizer (Filtering and Reward-Weighted Refinement):** This is the advanced optimizer used by PDDM, designed to enhance scaling and precision.
 - **Reward-Weighted Update:** Instead of a hard selection of elites, it uses a soft update rule to refine the mean action distribution. This rule weights each action sequence's contribution to the mean based on the exponential of its predicted reward, governed by a reward-weighting factor `gamma` (Equation 2). This allows for a smoother integration of a larger number of samples.
 - **Action Smoothing via Filtering:** Candidate action sequences are generated by applying a filtering technique. Noise samples `u_t` (from a Gaussian distribution) are filtered over time steps using a coefficient `beta` to produce smoother noise `n_t`, which is then added to the current mean action `mu_t` (Equations 3 and 4). This temporal coupling effectively reduces the dimensionality of the search space, improving optimization performance.
- **Ensemble-based Reward Calculation:** For each candidate action sequence, the total reward `R_k` is calculated as the mean predicted reward across all models in the dynamics ensemble. This mechanism allows model disagreement to influence action selection, promoting conservative and robust behavior.
- **Closed-Loop Execution:** After optimizing for an `H`-step action sequence, only the first action `a_t` is executed in the environment. The robot then observes the new state `s_{t+1}` and replans at the subsequent time step. This closed-loop control strategy helps to mitigate the accumulation of model prediction errors.

#### 3.4. Real-World Implementation Details

For real-world experiments, specifically the Baoding balls task on a 24-DoF Shadow Hand, the methodology includes:
- **Object Tracking:** A dilated CNN model, based on KeypointNet, is used with a stereo RGB image pair to provide low-latency, robust, and accurate 3D position estimates of the Baoding balls. The model is trained in simulation and fine-tuned with real-world data.
- **Automated Reset Mechanism:** To facilitate continuous real-world data collection, a pre-programmed 7-DoF Franka-Emika arm with a parallel jaw gripper automatically resets dropped Baoding balls back into the Shadow Hand's palm.
- **Control Frequency:** The planner commands the hand at 10Hz, which is communicated to a low-level 1KHz position controller via a 0-order hold.

The entire PDDM process involves continuous interaction with the environment, data collection, and periodic model training to iteratively improve the learned dynamics and, consequently, the planning capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to assess PDDM's capabilities across various complex dexterous manipulation tasks in both simulation and the real world, comparing its performance against established model-based and model-free RL algorithms.

#### 4.1. Task Suite and Sample Efficiency

PDDM demonstrated successful learning across a suite of challenging dexterous manipulation tasks:
- **Valve Turning (9-DoF D'Claw hand):** Rotating a valve to arbitrary target locations.
- **In-hand Reorientation (24-DoF Shadow Hand):** Reorienting a free-floating cube to goal poses.
- **Handwriting (24-DoF Shadow Hand):** Precisely controlling a pencil to write user-specified strokes.
- **Baoding Balls (24-DoF Shadow Hand):** Rotating two free-floating balls in the palm.

Simulated tasks were learned efficiently, typically requiring 1-2 hours worth of data, while real-world experiments were successful with 2-4 hours of real-world interaction. For instance, the simulated Baoding balls task was solved with 100,000 data points (approximately 2.7 hours).

#### 4.2. Ablations and Analysis of Design Decisions (Baoding Balls Task)

Ablation studies on the Baoding balls task revealed the critical impact of specific PDDM design choices:
- **Model Architecture:** A sufficiently large neural network architecture (2x500 neurons) was crucial for representing complex dynamics. Smaller architectures (e.g., 2x64) exhibited significantly lower performance.
- **Ensemble Size:** The use of model ensembles (e.g., 3 or 5 models) was beneficial, particularly in the early stages of training, to mitigate overfitting and prevent overconfident, detrimental behaviors from single high-capacity models.
- **Warmstarting Model Weights:** No substantial difference was observed between randomly reinitializing model weights at each training iteration and warmstarting them from previous values.
- **Planning Horizon:** An intermediate planning horizon (e.g., 7 steps) yielded optimal performance. Short horizons led to greedy planning, while excessively long horizons suffered from compounding prediction errors.
- **Controller Type:** PDDM's refined controller, incorporating action smoothing via filtering and reward-weighted refinement, significantly outperformed simpler gradient-free optimizers like CEM and random shooting.
- **Reward-Weighting Factor (gamma):** Medium values of `gamma` provided the best balance, effectively reducing search space dimensionality while retaining sufficient control authority. Values that were too low resulted in minimal hand movement, whereas excessively high values led to aggressive behaviors and frequent task failures.

#### 4.3. Comparisons with State-of-the-Art Algorithms

PDDM consistently demonstrated superior performance and sample efficiency compared to various state-of-the-art model-based (Nagabandi et al., PETS) and model-free (NPG, SAC, MBPO) RL algorithms:
- **Valve Turning:** While most methods eventually succeeded on this simpler task, PDDM exhibited the fastest learning speed. Model-free methods like NPG required substantially more data.
- **In-hand Reorientation:** On a task with two goal angles, PDDM performed comparably to SAC. However, when the number of goal angles increased to eight, PDDM maintained high performance, whereas model-free approaches (SAC, NPG, MBPO) struggled, getting stuck in local optima and failing to achieve previously attainable goals. This highlights PDDM's advantage in multi-goal, flexible tasks due to its model learning object interactions rather than specific trajectories.
- **Handwriting:** For a fixed writing trajectory, PDDM learned substantially faster than SAC and NPG. Critically, when required to follow *arbitrary* trajectories, only PDDM succeeded. Model-free methods failed to generalize, indicating an inability to extract the underlying general skill due to the narrow success manifold. Prior model-based methods (PETS, Nagabandi et al.) did not solve this task.
- **Baoding Balls:** This task, characterized by complex inter-object interactions and high failure rates, was only successfully solved by PDDM. Other model-based and model-free methods were unable to achieve success. The model learned for this task was also shown to be reusable for related variations (e.g., single ball manipulation, different rotation directions) without additional training.

#### 4.4. Real-World Manipulation of Baoding Balls

PDDM was successfully applied to a real-world 24-DoF Shadow Hand manipulating Baoding balls, training entirely from real-world experience without simulation or prior dynamics knowledge.
- **Performance:** The method achieved approximately 100% success rate for 90-degree rotations of two Baoding balls in under 2 hours of real-world training. For the more challenging 180-degree rotation task, a success rate of about 54% was achieved using approximately 4 hours of real-world data.
- **Learning Progression:** Qualitative observations indicated that significant performance improvements occurred during the initial 1.5 hours of training, followed by learning more complex maneuvers like transferring control between fingers.

These results collectively indicate that PDDM is capable of efficiently learning complex, high-dimensional dexterous manipulation skills, demonstrating superior sample efficiency, flexibility, and generalization capabilities compared to existing learning algorithms, particularly on challenging real-world tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of robotic dexterous manipulation, demonstrating a methodology that addresses several long-standing challenges. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Complex Dexterous Manipulation:** The work showcases that deep dynamics models, when combined with sophisticated online planning, can effectively learn highly complex, contact-rich manipulation skills that were previously difficult or impossible for learning-based methods to acquire efficiently. This includes tasks requiring delicate balance, repeated contact changes, and control of multiple unactuated objects, moving beyond simpler grasping or pick-and-place operations.
2. **Validation of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The paper provides strong empirical evidence that model-based RL can achieve high-performance control results on par with, or exceeding, state-of-the-art model-free methods for complex manipulation. It counters the notion that deep models are too difficult or unreliable for real-world control, pushing the empirical boundaries of what model-based RL can achieve in high-dimensional, dynamic environments.
3. **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** A critical impact of PDDM is its significantly improved sample efficiency compared to model-free counterparts. By learning a predictive model of the environment, the method requires substantially less real-world interaction data (demonstrated with just 4 hours for a complex real-world task). This reduction in data requirements is vital for making advanced robotic skill learning practical and scalable for deployment in real-world scenarios, where data collection is often costly and time-consuming.
4. **Increased Flexibility and Generalization:** Unlike many model-free approaches that yield task-specific policies, the learned dynamics model in PDDM is inherently more general. This allows for flexible task execution, such as adapting to arbitrary writing patterns or repurposing the model for variations of a task (e.g., different Baoding ball movements) without needing extensive retraining. This flexibility is a key step towards robots that can adapt to changing environments and user specifications.
5. **Autonomous Real-World Skill Acquisition:** The successful demonstration of learning complex fine motor skills on a 24-DoF anthropomorphic robotic hand purely from real-world experience, without reliance on simulation or prior system knowledge, is a substantial achievement. This capability paves the way for robots to autonomously discover and refine skills directly in their operational environments.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The PDDM framework provides a robust foundation for future research directions. These include exploring planning at different levels of abstraction to tackle sparse-reward or long-horizon tasks, as well as integrating additional sensing modalities like vision and touch into the dynamics models to further enhance environmental understanding and control robustness.

In summary, this work contributes to making dexterous manipulation a more tangible reality for autonomous robots by demonstrating an efficient, flexible, and robust learning approach that scales to complex real-world challenges.
