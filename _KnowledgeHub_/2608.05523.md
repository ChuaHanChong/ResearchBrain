# HERA: Historical Evidence Routing Adapter for Physical Prediction in Latent World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Chongqing University, Tsinghua University, Fudan University, and Everwise-Tech Co., Ltd. developed HERA (Historical Evidence Routing Adapter), a parameter-efficient method to improve physical prediction in latent world models, particularly when crucial evidence is occluded. HERA integrates a structured memory bank with distinct register types into frozen pre-trained predictors, achieving a 1.78 percentage point absolute improvement in pairwise AvgSurprise accuracy on the IntPhys2 Main split, with gains up to 17.31% in specific challenging scenarios.

## Problem

- Latent world models struggle with physical prediction when key evidence for future events is occluded or no longer directly visible, leading to 'physical memory' failures.
- Existing temporal reasoning strategies (e.g., longer input windows, generic feature caches, object-centric memory) often fail to selectively retrieve and integrate relevant historical evidence without disrupting frozen latent predictors.
- Models frequently over-rely on recent context, resulting in diluted or lost early evidence critical for anticipating consequences like object permanence or attribute constancy.

## Method

- Introduced **HERA** (Historical Evidence Routing Adapter), a parameter-efficient adapter framework designed to augment frozen latent video predictors without altering their core architecture or original prediction objective.
- Implemented HERA with ==Register-Routed Patch Memory (RRPM)==, which features a ==Structured Memory Bank== to store compressed patch evidence from different temporal regions (anchor, middle, recent, global context).
- Utilized distinct ==Memory Registers== for explicit, gated retrieval of relevant evidence from the Structured Memory Bank, and ==Workspace Registers== to integrate this retrieved information into the predictor's native self-attention pathway as trainable carriers.

## Results

- HERA with RRPM improved the pairwise AvgSurprise accuracy on the IntPhys2 Main split to **54.35%**, an absolute gain of **1.78 percentage points** over the V-JEPA 2-G baseline (52.57%).
- Demonstrated substantial performance gains in IntPhys2 subgroups highly reliant on historical evidence, including an **8.93%** increase for 'Hard Examples', an **11.54%** increase for 'Continuity (Fixed Camera)', and a **17.31%** increase for 'Immutability (Fixed Camera)'.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of both Memory Registers and Workspace Registers, with the removal of either group leading to a consistent decrease in accuracy by **0.39 percentage points**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Selective routing and integration of historical evidence are more effective for physical memory than merely increasing the amount or organization of stored history.
> - A parameter-efficient adapter framework can substantially enhance the physical prediction capabilities of pre-trained latent world models without extensive fine-tuning of the entire backbone.
> - The functional separation of Memory Registers (for retrieving explicit memory) and Workspace Registers (for integrating it into the frozen predictor's computation) is crucial for optimal performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of video representation learning and the development of predictive world models. Contemporary models, particularly those leveraging masked video autoencoders and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA-style objectives like V-JEPA 2), have demonstrated capabilities in learning latent visual dynamics from extensive video datasets. These models are designed to anticipate missing or future visual content and support tasks such as understanding, prediction, and planning in physically grounded environments.

However, a recognized limitation within this landscape is the challenge of physical prediction under conditions of occlusion or when predictions depend on historical evidence that is no longer directly observable. Existing strategies to enhance temporal reasoning in these models typically involve extending the input window, caching generic video features, or imposing explicit object-centric states. While these methods may increase the capacity or structure of retained historical information, they do not consistently resolve the issue of *selectively retrieving* and *integrating* relevant historical evidence into a *pretrained predictor's native latent workspace* without interference.

This research addresses this specific bottleneck by proposing a mechanism for routing historical evidence. It uses violation-of-expectation benchmarks, such as IntPhys2, which directly probe intuitive physics understanding by presenting paired possible and impossible events. This approach contrasts with methods primarily focused on generative rollouts or those requiring stable object discovery and temporal association, which might not be reliably supported by frozen video features. By focusing on an adapter framework that operates on frozen latent predictors, the paper contributes to the development of more robust physical memory capabilities in existing world models, aiming to enable them to maintain consistent physical reasoning even when evidence is transient or occluded.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the physical prediction capabilities of latent world models, particularly when confronted with scenarios where crucial evidence for future events is no longer directly visible. The motivation stems from a recognized gap in current predictive video models: despite their ability to learn useful physical regularities, they often fail to maintain a persistent representation of physical state through occlusion or disappearance of objects. This limitation is termed "physical memory" and manifests as difficulties in anticipating consequences when information about existence, attributes, motion, or interaction must persist through time.

The specific problems that motivate this work include:
- **Occlusion Handling**: Models struggle to predict the behavior of objects that become hidden, such as a ball continuing its trajectory behind an occluder or a block retaining its color after disappearing from view. This indicates an inability to preserve object identity and attributes over time.
- **Dependence on Historical Evidence**: Future physical events (e.g., collisions, interactions) can be constrained by objects or conditions observed earlier but not currently visible. Current models often over-rely on recent context, leading to diluted or lost early evidence.
- **Limitations of Existing Temporal Reasoning Strategies**:
 - **Longer Input Windows**: While retaining more observations, this approach can dilute sparse early evidence amidst a larger set of recent tokens, making it harder to access specific historical cues.
 - **Generic Feature Caches**: These store history but lack a mechanism to determine which part of that history is relevant for a specific future prediction.
 - **Object-Centric Memory**: This approach provides a stronger state abstraction but relies on robust object discovery, identity binding, and temporal matching, properties that are not guaranteed to be exposed by a frozen video representation.

Given these limitations, the key objectives of the research are:
1. **Formulate a Behavioral Probe**: To establish pairwise AvgSurprise as a diagnostic tool for physical memory failures in settings where prediction relies on historical, unobservable evidence.
2. **Develop a Routing Framework**: To introduce HERA (Historical Evidence Routing Adapter), a framework designed to make retained historical evidence broadly accessible to a *frozen latent predictor*. This framework is intended to route relevant information without disrupting the predictor's native latent workspace.
3. **Instantiate with a Lightweight Adapter**: To instantiate HERA with Register-Routed Patch Memory (RRPM), a parameter-efficient adapter. RRPM is designed to store structured patch history, selectively retrieve it using dedicated Memory Registers, and integrate this retrieved evidence into the predictor via separate Workspace Registers.
4. **Demonstrate Efficacy and Distinguish Contributions**: To demonstrate that selective evidence routing, as implemented by HERA with RRPM, provides benefits beyond simple historical storage and that the architectural separation of register roles is functionally important. This is evaluated through quantitative performance gains on physical prediction benchmarks and ablation studies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on augmenting a pre-trained latent video predictor with a mechanism for selectively routing historical evidence. The approach avoids modifications to the original prediction objective or the backbone model's core architecture.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Diagnostic Protocol

The study leverages the IntPhys2 benchmark, which comprises matched possible and impossible video pairs that share scene content but differ in physical validity. The objective is to determine if a predictive model assigns a larger "future surprise" (prediction error) to physically impossible continuations compared to their possible counterparts.
- **Context and Prediction**: A pre-trained V-JEPA 2 model observes a context prefix `v_w,1:c` and predicts latent representations for future masked tubelets `v_w,c+1:c+h`.
- **Encoder and Predictor**: `E_ϕ` (frozen context encoder) generates patch tokens `X_w,c`. `G_ψ` (frozen predictor) receives projected tokens `X_p,w,c` and future mask tokens `Y_mask`, outputting predicted target latents `ˆZ_w,c`. `E_¯ϕ` (frozen target encoder) provides normalized target latents `¯Z_w,c`.
- **Surprise Metric**: The per-window surprise `ℓ_v,w,c` is defined as the mean L1 error between predicted and target latent features across target tokens and feature dimensions.
- **Video-level Score**: `AvgSurprise(v, c)` is the mean `ℓ` over all valid, non-padded sliding windows for a given context length `c`.
- **Pairwise Surprise Margin**: For a matched pair, `m_s,k,c = AvgSurprise(v_imp_s,k, c) − AvgSurprise(v_pos_s,k, c)`. A pair is considered "correct" if `m > 0`.
- **Accuracy**: `Acc(c)` is the proportion of correct pairs. `Acc_best(C)` selects the maximum accuracy over a predefined set of context lengths.
- **Training and Evaluation**: The memory module is trained with the same future-latent prediction objective as V-JEPA 2, without physical labels. Its physical value is judged solely by whether it alters pairwise violation sensitivity, maintaining a direct tie to the prediction pathway.

#### 3.2. HERA with Register-Routed Patch Memory (RRPM)

HERA extends a frozen V-JEPA 2 predictor without requiring object annotations or altering the original latent prediction objective. It addresses historical information retention, selective retrieval, and evidence integration through three RRPM modules:

1. **Structured Memory Bank**:
 - **Purpose**: To store selected or compressed patch evidence from different temporal regions of the context in a compact form. This avoids storing all raw tokens, which is redundant, and object-centric representations, which rely on stable object decomposition from frozen features.
 - **Mechanism**: The projected context tokens `X_p` are viewed as temporally ordered tubelets `U`. These are divided into four conceptual memory groups based on temporal role:
 - `M_anchor`: Stores earliest context evidence (e.g., initial object identity, color, position).
 - `M_middle`: Compresses intermediate motion and interaction history.
 - `M_recent`: Preserves the state immediately preceding prediction.
 - `M_global`: Summarizes the full context at a coarse level, generated from the concatenated anchor, middle, and recent memories.
 - **Implementation**: Lightweight token selection, projection, or compression operators (`ρ`) are applied, and learned role embeddings (`e`) are added. If the context mask is irregular, a global-only fallback mechanism is used. The total memory `M` is a concatenation of these structured memory groups, residing in the predictor's hidden dimension.

2. **Memory Registers (`R_mem`)**:
 - **Purpose**: To explicitly retrieve relevant evidence from the Structured Memory Bank.
 - **Mechanism**: These registers attend to `M` through gated cross-attention (`CrossAttn`) at predefined "read layers" (e.g., after the 4th and 8th predictor blocks). Only the Memory Registers are directly updated by this read operation. The gate `g` is initialized conservatively to minimize initial disruption.

3. **Workspace Registers (`R_w`)**:
 - **Purpose**: To integrate the retrieved evidence into the frozen predictor's native self-attention pathway without direct modification.
 - **Mechanism**: `R_w` are concatenated with the projected context tokens `X_p` and future mask tokens `Y_mask` to form the initial input `H_0` to the predictor. Workspace Registers participate only in predictor self-attention. The information retrieved by `R_mem` propagates to the context and future mask tokens indirectly through the frozen predictor's subsequent self-attention layers. This separation prevents direct modification of the context tokens and allows memory to enter via a small set of trainable carriers.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference Details

- **Training**: Only the Structured Memory Bank, cross-attention readers and gates, Memory Registers, and Workspace Registers are trainable. The V-JEPA 2 encoders and main predictor blocks remain frozen. Optimization uses the base model's future-latent prediction L1 objective.
- **Inference**: The same procedure is applied independently to each video window to construct `M`, inject memory via `R_mem`, predict future target latents, and compute the video-level AvgSurprise. Pairwise margin and accuracy are then calculated using the IntPhys2 protocol.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Backbone**: V-JEPA 2-G.
- **Trainable Parameters**: 3.00M for HERA with RRPM.
- **Registers**: 12 Memory Registers, 8 Workspace Registers.
- **Structured Memory Bank Configuration**: 96 anchor tokens, 28 middle tokens, 64 recent tokens, and 8 global tokens (196 total memory tokens).
- **Datasets**: Trained on 14,000-video Physion training split; evaluated on IntPhys2 Main split (506 matched possible–impossible pairs), with no IntPhys2 data used during training.
- **Evaluation Protocol**: 48-frame clips (384x384), temporal sampling step of 10, sliding-window stride of 2, batch size of 1, bfloat16 inference. AvgSurprise averaged over valid sliding windows.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results indicate that HERA with RRPM improves physical prediction in latent world models, particularly for scenarios requiring access to historical evidence.

- **Overall Accuracy Improvement**: HERA with RRPM achieved a pairwise AvgSurprise accuracy of **54.35%** on the IntPhys2 Main split. This represents an absolute improvement of **1.78 percentage points** over the baseline V-JEPA 2-G model, which scored 52.57%.

- **Comparison with Alternative Memory Mechanisms**:
 - HERA with RRPM outperformed five alternative memory designs: Clip-Local Patch Memory (52.77%), Prefix Cross-Window Memory (52.77%), Hierarchical Patch Memory (52.96%), Shared-Register Memory (52.96%), and Direct-to-Target Memory (53.56%).
 - Notably, HERA (3.00M trainable parameters) also surpassed Direct-to-Target Memory, which required tuning 5.96M parameters by unfreezing additional predictor blocks. This suggests that selective routing and integration, as implemented by HERA, offers a parameter-efficient approach.
 - The smaller gains from clip-local, prefix, and hierarchical patch memories indicate that merely increasing the amount, temporal range, or organization of stored history is not as effective without a dedicated retrieval and integration pathway. Shared-Register Memory's lower performance compared to HERA suggests that separating the roles of memory access and predictor-side computation is beneficial.

- **Register-Role Ablation**:
 - Ablation studies on the register groups, starting from a checkpoint with 53.95% accuracy (HERA + low-LR continuation), demonstrated the importance of both Memory Registers and Workspace Registers.
 - Removing Memory Registers led to a decrease in accuracy to 53.56%.
 - Removing Workspace Registers also resulted in an accuracy of 53.56%.
 - The consistent reduction in performance (0.39 percentage points each) when either register group was removed supports their complementary roles in retrieving explicit memory and integrating it into the frozen predictor.

- **Subgroup Analysis**: HERA demonstrated particularly strong improvements in IntPhys2 subgroups where physical prediction is more reliant on earlier, often occluded, evidence:
 - **Hard Examples**: Accuracy improved from 48.81% to 57.74%, a gain of **8.93%**.
 - **Fixed Camera Scenarios**: Accuracy increased from 48.56% to 56.73%, an **8.17%** gain.
 - **Continuity (Fixed Camera)**: Accuracy showed a substantial increase from 46.15% to 57.69%, a **11.54%** gain. This category often involves objects moving behind occluders, requiring persistent object knowledge.
 - **Immutability (Fixed Camera)**: Accuracy improved from 46.15% to 63.46%, a **17.31%** gain. This category involves objects retaining attributes (e.g., color, material) through occlusion.
 - **Solidity**: Accuracy increased from 47.26% to 55.48%, an **8.22%** gain, suggesting improved access to historical spatial and interaction evidence.

These results collectively support the hypothesis that selective evidence routing, implemented through a temporally structured memory bank and distinct register types, is an effective strategy for enhancing physical memory in latent world models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces HERA (Historical Evidence Routing Adapter) and its instantiation, RRPM, as a strategy for improving physical prediction in latent world models. This work demonstrates a method for addressing the challenge of physical events under occlusion, where predictions depend on historical evidence that is no longer immediately available.

One key aspect of this work is its demonstration of a parameter-efficient adapter framework that operates on a frozen pre-trained predictor. By adding only 3.00M trainable parameters to a V-JEPA 2-G backbone, HERA achieves an improvement in pairwise AvgSurprise accuracy from 52.57% to 54.35% on the IntPhys2 Main split. This indicates that significant gains in physical reasoning can be obtained through a targeted, modular intervention rather than extensive fine-tuning of the entire model.

The architectural design, featuring a Structured Memory Bank, Memory Registers for selective retrieval, and Workspace Registers for integration, is supported by ablation studies. The consistent reduction in performance upon removal of either register group suggests that the separation of roles between memory access and predictor-side computation is functionally important. This modularity offers a practical approach for enhancing the capabilities of existing large-scale models without requiring their full retraining or complex modifications to their internal mechanisms.

The most substantial gains observed in subgroups like "fixed-camera continuity" (11.54% increase) and "fixed-camera immutability" (17.31% increase) highlight HERA's effectiveness in scenarios where physical prediction heavily relies on recovering past, unobservable evidence. This suggests that HERA enhances the model's ability to maintain object permanence, attribute constancy, and motion continuity.

The approach does not rely on object supervision, making it applicable to broader contexts where such annotations might be unavailable. The findings contribute to the development of more robust and generalizable world models, which could have implications for applications in reinforcement learning, robotics, and physical AI, where models need to anticipate consequences from incomplete or transient observations. The work provides a foundation for future research into more sophisticated structured states and their integration into complex predictive systems.
