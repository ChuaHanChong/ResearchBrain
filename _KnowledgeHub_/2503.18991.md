# Inverse Reinforcement Learning with Dynamic Reward Scaling for LLM Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beijing Electronic Science and Technology Institute, Alibaba Group, and collaborators introduced DR-IRL, an approach that enhances large language model safety by combining category-specific inverse reinforcement learning with dynamic reward scaling. This method achieved top performance in harmlessness benchmarks and maintained utility across various LLMs, demonstrating improved resistance to jailbreak attacks.

## Problem

- Existing LLM safety datasets are imbalanced, overrepresenting common hazards and neglecting 'long-tail' threats, leading to incomplete safety coverage.
- Traditional reward models in RLHF assign static rewards irrespective of task difficulty, hindering optimization efficiency and limiting alignment gains for nuanced safety scenarios.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) are prone to generating harmful content when subjected to malicious queries or jailbreak attacks, requiring more robust intrinsic alignment mechanisms.

## Method

- **DR-IRL** integrates a balanced, category-specific safety dataset (constructed using Chain of Draft refusal responses) with ==Inverse Reinforcement Learning== (IRL) to train specialized 'shadow reward models' for seven distinct harmful categories.
- The method incorporates dynamic reward scaling within a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework, where a combined hardness coefficient (from data-level hardness and model-level responsiveness) adjusts rewards based on task difficulty.
- The final LLM alignment iteratively updates the policy by optimizing a dynamically scaled advantage function, emphasizing challenging samples and preventing over-optimization on trivial cases.

## Results

- DR-IRL achieved state-of-the-art harmlessness, with StrongReject scores of **0.9361** for Llama-3.1-**8B** and **0.8798** for Qwen-2-**7B**, while maintaining high utility scores on benchmarks like AdvGLUE (**70.71%**) and GSM8k (**88.10%**).
- The approach demonstrated superior robustness against jailbreak attacks, achieving refusal rates of **96.98%** on GCG and **59.00%** on AutoDAN for Llama-3.1-**8B**, outperforming existing baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both data-level and model-level hardness coefficients are critical, with their combined multiplicative effect preventing degradation in harmlessness (StrongReject dropping by 4 percentage points without them) and stabilizing general capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training category-specific shadow reward models via IRL from balanced single-response demonstrations effectively captures fine-grained safety values and reduces reward interference compared to generic models.
> - Dynamically scaling rewards based on both data-level hardness (semantic similarity) and model-level responsiveness (reward gap) significantly improves optimization efficiency and robustness across diverse safety tasks.
> - The multiplicative combination of hardness coefficients acts as an effective 'AND-gate,' focusing learning on samples that are both semantically distinct from expert responses and still uncertain for the current model, leading to more stable and robust alignment.
