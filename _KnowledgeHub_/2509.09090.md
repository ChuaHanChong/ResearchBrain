# SQAP-VLA: A Synergistic Quantization-Aware Pruning Framework for High-Performance Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The SQAP-VLA framework introduces a training-free method for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, synergistically combining 4-bit quantization and token pruning to resolve their inherent incompatibility. This approach improves task success rate by up to 4.5% over the full-precision baseline, while achieving a 1.93x speedup and reducing GPU memory by over 46%.

## Problem

- High computational and memory demands of large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models hinder their practical deployment on resource-constrained edge devices for robotics applications.
- Existing compression techniques like quantization and token pruning, while effective individually, exhibit an intrinsic incompatibility when combined, leading to severe performance degradation.
- Quantization distorts attention scores crucial for token pruning, while token pruning makes the model more sensitive to quantization noise.

## Method

- Developed **SQAP-VLA**, a ==training-free, co-designed== framework that synergistically combines ==quantization== and ==token pruning== for VLA models to resolve their intrinsic incompatibility.
- Introduced ==quantization-aware pruning== strategies including quantization-insensitive token preservation (==top-k attention==), ==robot-aware token protection==, and spatially-aware token sampling using ==Farthest Point Sampling==.
- Enhanced the quantizer with ==channel-wise quantization== and ==Hadamard transformation== on query/key layers to mitigate activation outliers and improve the reliability of attention maps under quantization.

## Results

- Achieved an average task success rate of **79.3%** on the Visual Matching scenario, surpassing the full-precision CogACT baseline (**74.8%**) by **4.5%**.
- Delivered a **1.93x** end-to-end system speedup and reduced peak GPU memory usage by over **46%** (from **14.3 GB** to **7.6 GB**) compared to the full-precision baseline.
- Reduced Basic Operations (BOPs) to **26.3%** of the baseline model, representing a **~73.7%** reduction in computational load.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The fundamental incompatibility between quantization and token pruning in VLA models arises from quantization distorting critical attention distributions and pruning increasing model sensitivity to quantization noise.
> - Even under aggressive quantization, task-critical information can be preserved by leveraging stable high-magnitude attention tokens and incorporating explicit robot-aware spatial priors.
> - Applying a Hadamard transformation to activations effectively smooths their distributions and suppresses outliers, leading to more reliable attention maps and improved pruning effectiveness in quantized models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical and rapidly growing area within artificial intelligence: **embodied intelligence** and **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**. VLA models, exemplified by architectures like CogACT, π0, RT-2, and PALM-E, represent a significant leap forward, enabling robots to interpret human language instructions, perceive complex visual environments, and generate real-world actions. They are pivotal for developing truly autonomous and intelligent robotic systems.

However, the immense computational and memory demands of these large models pose a formidable barrier to their practical deployment, especially on resource-constrained edge devices where robotic applications often reside. Bridging this "reality gap" between powerful models and limited hardware is a central challenge in current AI research.

Existing model compression techniques, primarily **quantization** and **token pruning**, have shown individual success in reducing model size and computational costs. Quantization reduces the precision of model weights and activations (e.g., to 4-bit, W4A4), offering significant memory and computation savings. Token pruning, on the other hand, reduces the number of tokens processed by the model, thereby reducing the operational count and latency.

The broader research landscape has seen numerous advancements in both areas:
- **Quantization:** Techniques like QAIL (for VLA), GPTQ, AWQ, QUIP#, FlatQuant, and SpinQuant (for LLMs, which are a major component of VLA models) aim to quantize models with minimal performance degradation.
- **Token Pruning:** Methods like FastV, SP-VLA, EfficientVLA, VLA-Cache, Mole-VLA, and Token Merging have been developed to identify and remove redundant or less important tokens, especially in vision-language models (VLMs) and VLA models.

The critical insight and novel contribution of SQAP-VLA stem from identifying and overcoming a fundamental, previously overlooked **incompatibility** between these two powerful compression techniques. Prior research often treated quantization and pruning as orthogonal, assuming their combination would yield additive benefits. However, SQAP-VLA reveals that a straightforward combination leads to severe performance degradation. This is because:
1. Quantization profoundly alters the statistical distribution of features (e.g., attention scores) that token pruning relies upon.
2. Token pruning, by reducing information, makes the model more sensitive to the noise introduced by quantization.

By explicitly addressing this incompatibility through a **principled co-design**, SQAP-VLA positions itself as a pioneering work. It moves beyond *ad-hoc* application of compression methods to a *synergistic* framework, offering a more holistic and effective solution for VLA model acceleration. This work directly pushes the boundaries of efficient embodied AI by enabling high-performance VLA models to operate within the stringent constraints of real-world robotic systems, thereby accelerating the deployment and impact of advanced AI in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the SQAP-VLA framework arises from the significant discrepancy between the burgeoning capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and their practical deployability in real-world robotic applications. While VLA models have demonstrated unprecedented performance in tasks requiring integrated perception, language understanding, and physical interaction, their inherent computational and memory costs are prohibitive for low-latency, energy-efficient operation on resource-constrained edge devices.

The paper identifies two state-of-the-art model compression techniques: quantization and token pruning, as promising avenues for addressing this challenge. However, a crucial insight driving this work is the **observed intrinsic incompatibility** between these two methods. Specifically:
- **Quantization's Impact on Pruning:** Low-precision quantization introduces noise and dramatically alters the statistical distribution of internal features, such as attention scores, which are traditionally used to determine token importance for pruning. This distortion renders pruning strategies developed for full-precision models ineffective or even invalid when applied to quantized networks (as vividly illustrated in Figure 2, where attention maps become scattered and shifted).
- **Pruning's Impact on Quantization Sensitivity:** By removing tokens, pruning leaves the model with less information to process, making it more susceptible to the adverse effects of quantization noise.

This fundamental coupling and incompatibility represent a major barrier to realizing the full efficiency potential of VLA models through combined compression strategies. Prior research has largely overlooked this challenge, treating quantization and token pruning as independent, additive solutions.

Given this motivation, the **key objectives** of the SQAP-VLA framework are:
1. **Develop a Synergistic Compression Framework:** To introduce the first structured, training-free VLA inference acceleration framework that *simultaneously* and *compatibly* enables state-of-the-art quantization and token pruning.
2. **Overcome Incompatibility through Co-design:** To resolve the intrinsic conflict between quantization and token pruning by proposing a principled co-design approach. This involves adapting token pruning criteria to function reliably on aggressively quantized models and, conversely, enhancing the quantizer design to improve pruning effectiveness.
3. **Preserve and Enhance Model Performance:** To ensure that aggressive compression (e.g., W4A4 quantization combined with significant pruning) does not lead to severe performance degradation. Ideally, the framework should maintain or even improve core model performance (e.g., task success rate) compared to the original, uncompressed model.
4. **Achieve Significant Efficiency Gains:** To deliver substantial improvements in computational efficiency (e.g., speedup, reduction in Basic Operations or BOPs) and memory footprint, thereby facilitating practical deployment on edge devices.
5. **Maintain Training-Free Operation:** To achieve these objectives without requiring costly retraining or fine-tuning, allowing the direct application of the framework to pre-trained VLA model checkpoints.

In essence, the paper aims to provide a robust and practical solution that bridges the gap between the high performance of VLA models and the stringent resource constraints of real-world embodied AI applications by tackling the most critical challenge in combined model compression.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SQAP-VLA framework introduces a novel, training-free, and co-designed approach to overcome the incompatibility between quantization and token pruning for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The methodology focuses on two interconnected fronts: developing quantization-aware pruning strategies and enhancing the quantizer to be more pruning-friendly.

#### 3.1. Core Philosophy: Co-design for Incompatibility Resolution

The central tenet is that a naive combination of quantization and pruning fails due to attention score distortion (scattered and shifted attention maps in quantized models, as shown in Figure 2). SQAP-VLA addresses this by explicitly co-designing the two processes.

#### 3.2. Quantization-aware Pruning Strategies

To counteract the distortion of attention scores caused by quantization, SQAP-VLA proposes three synergistic pruning strategies:

1. **Quantization-insensitive Token Preservation:**
 - **Problem:** Attention scores, critical for identifying important tokens, are perturbed by quantization, especially mid-range values.
 - **Solution:** Empirical analysis reveals that the relative ordering of a small number of "top-k" tokens with the highest-magnitude attention scores remains remarkably stable despite quantization noise. These tokens typically correspond to the most task-critical visual elements (e.g., target objects, robot end-effector).
 - **Mechanism:** The framework directly preserves these `Top_k` tokens based on the attention weight vector (`a_q`), ensuring that vital information, whose importance is robust to quantization, is retained.

2. **Robot-aware Token Protection:**
 - **Problem:** Relying solely on (even stable) attention scores might miss crucial context, especially when `k` must be small for stability. Task-critical features like the robot arm itself are always important.
 - **Solution:** Leverage explicit task-specific priors. The robot's known 3D world coordinates (`x_w`, `y_w`, `z_w`) are projected into 2D pixel coordinates (`u`, `v`) using camera parameters. These pixel coordinates are then mapped to discrete token coordinates (`t_u`, `t_v`) based on patch size.
 - **Mechanism:** A "protected ring" of tokens (`K_ring`) is defined around the robot's central token (e.g., end-effector) using Chebyshev distance. This physically-grounded approach ensures the preservation of visuomotor grounding, making it fundamentally invariant to quantization errors.

3. **Spatially-aware Token Sampling:**
 - **Problem:** After preserving high-importance and robot-centric tokens, the remaining tokens might still contain redundancy, or their distribution might be unbalanced, leading to information collapse in peripheral regions.
 - **Solution:** Mimic human selective attention, maintaining high fidelity for critical points while representing peripheral regions with reduced, but spatially diverse, detail.
 - **Mechanism:** Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) is applied to the remaining tokens (`T_remain`) to select a spatially diverse subset of `m` tokens (`K_fps`). This efficiently prunes redundant information while maximizing spatial coverage.
 - **Final Pruned Set:** The ultimate set of retained tokens (`K_final`) is the union of these three sets: `K_attn ∪ K_ring ∪ K_fps`. The size of `K_attn` is adjusted proportionally to the target pruning rate, and `K_fps` fills the remaining quota for spatial balance.

#### 3.3. Pruning-Targeted Quantizer Enhancement

Beyond adapting pruning to quantization, SQAP-VLA also enhances the quantizer itself to improve the interpretability and reliability of attention maps under quantization:

1. **Addressing Activation Outliers:**
 - **Problem:** VLA model activations (especially for query and key vectors) exhibit highly skewed and asymmetric distributions, with outlier channels having values several magnitudes larger than others (Figure 3a). Traditional token-wise quantization is highly susceptible to these outliers, leading to significant quantization errors and degraded attention maps.
 - **Initial Step:** The approach uses **channel-wise quantization for activations** to achieve finer granularity and better fidelity, particularly for smaller activation values.
 - **Further Mitigation: Hadamard Transformation:** Inspired by QUIP# for LLM quantization, a **Hadamard transformation** is applied to the weights and activations of the query and key layers.
 - **Mechanism:** The Hadamard transform (`H`) effectively redistributes activation energy more uniformly across channels, suppressing outlier effects (Figure 3b). This transformation maintains mathematical equivalence (`(W^T H^T)(HX) = W^T X`) while "smoothing" the activation landscape, resulting in more reliable attention maps and consequently, more effective token pruning.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Model:** State-of-the-art VLA model, **CogAct** (composed of Prism-DinoSigLIP-224px vision encoder, LLM, and a diffusion model for action generation).
- **Dataset:** Pre-trained on the large-scale Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset.
- **Training-Free:** All quantization and pruning techniques are applied post-training to publicly available model checkpoints.
- **Baselines:** Full-precision (FP16) CogAct, Random Dropping, FastV, VLA-Cache, EfficientVLA (all FP16).
- **Evaluation:** Robotics simulation benchmark, four challenging manipulation tasks (Pick Coke Can, Move Near, Open/Close Drawer, Place Apple in Top Drawer).
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR), Speed-up, Basic Operations (BOPs), Peak GPU Memory Usage.
- **Hardware:** NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU.
- **Quantization Precision:** W4A4 (4-bit weights and 4-bit activations).
- **Pruning Ratio:** Optimal ratio of 0.4 based on ablation studies.

This comprehensive methodology, combining task-aware pruning with quantization-friendly quantizer enhancements, represents a significant step towards practical VLA model deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the CogAct VLA model demonstrate that SQAP-VLA effectively addresses the incompatibility between quantization and token pruning, yielding significant efficiency gains while preserving, and often enhancing, task performance.

#### 4.1. Validation of Incompatibility and Effectiveness of Co-design

- **Problem Confirmation:** Initial observations (Figure 2) vividly illustrate the degradation of attention heatmaps in a naively quantized model, where attention becomes "scattered and shifted" compared to the sharply focused attention in full-precision models. This confirms the fundamental challenge SQAP-VLA aims to resolve.
- **Overall Superiority:** SQAP-VLA consistently achieves superior average success rates and speedup ratios compared to various state-of-the-art FP16 pruning methods (Random Dropping, FastV, VLA-Cache, EfficientVLA), even when operating under aggressive W4A4 quantization. This highlights the effectiveness of the synergistic co-design.

#### 4.2. Performance Gains (Success Rate)

- **Visual Matching Scenario (Table 1):**
 - SQAP-VLA achieves an average success rate of **79.3%**, outperforming the full-precision CogACT baseline (74.8%) by a significant **4.5%**.
 - It also surpasses the best FP16 pruning baseline, EfficientVLA (76.4%), by **2.9%**. This is a remarkable achievement, as SQAP-VLA operates at W4A4 precision, which typically entails performance degradation.
- **Variant Aggregation Scenario (Table 2):**
 - SQAP-VLA achieves an average success rate of **64.4%**, a **3.1%** improvement over the full-precision CogACT baseline (61.3%).
- **Aggressive Quantization with Performance Enhancement:** The results demonstrate that the proposed synergistic approach not only mitigates the performance loss typically associated with aggressive W4A4 quantization but also enables the compressed model to *outperform* its full-precision counterpart. This suggests that the pruning strategies act as a form of task-focused regularization or noise reduction.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Gains (Speed-up, BOPs, and Memory)

- **System Speed-up (Figure 4a, Table 1 & 2):**
 - SQAP-VLA achieves a notable **1.93× end-to-end system speedup** relative to the FP16 CogACT baseline.
 - This system-level gain is primarily driven by a **2.56× acceleration within the Large Language Model (LLM) backbone**, which is decomposed into 2.09× from W4A4 quantization and 1.21× from token pruning. This confirms the additive and synergistic nature of the co-designed components.
 - It also achieves a **36% improvement in speedup** over EfficientVLA (1.93x vs 1.59x).
- **Reduced Basic Operations (BOPs) (Table 1 & 2):**
 - SQAP-VLA significantly reduces computational load, achieving only **26.3%** of the baseline BOPs, indicating a ~73.7% reduction in operations. This is the lowest BOPs percentage among all methods, including FP16 pruning methods.
- **GPU Memory Reduction (Figure 4b):**
 - The peak GPU memory usage is dramatically reduced from **14.3 GB** for the baseline model to just **7.6 GB** for SQAP-VLA. This represents a reduction of over 46% (or 73% if looking at the difference relative to the baseline's usage), which is crucial for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Pruning Ratios (Table 3):**
 - The study confirmed that W4A4 quantization alone causes a performance drop (74.8% -> 71.78% for Visual Matching).
 - However, applying SQAP-VLA's pruning methods *recovers and surpasses* this performance. An optimal pruning ratio of **0.4** was identified, yielding the highest success rates (79.30% for Visual Matching and 64.40% for Variant Aggregation), indicating a sweet spot between efficiency and information retention.
- **Compression Strategies (Table 4):**
 - This detailed study systematically validates the contribution of each proposed strategy:
 - Baseline (FP16): 74.80% (Visual Matching).
 - `+ Quantization` (W4A4): Performance degrades to 71.78%.
 - `+ Quant-Insensitive` preservation: Partial recovery to 70.90% (still below baseline).
 - `+ Robot-Aware` protection: Significant boost to 73.98%, nearly recovering baseline performance.
 - `+ Spatially-Aware` sampling: Final success rate of **79.30%**, decisively outperforming the full-precision baseline.
 - This sequential improvement strongly supports the core hypothesis that co-designing quantization with intelligent, task-centric pruning (especially robot-aware and spatially-aware strategies) is essential for superior performance and efficiency.
- **Hadamard Transform Impact (Figure 3):** Visualization of activation distributions clearly shows that the Hadamard transform effectively smooths the activation landscape by redistributing energy from outliers, thereby making attention maps more reliable for pruning.

In summary, SQAP-VLA delivers a robust, efficient, and high-performing solution for VLA model compression, definitively resolving the long-standing incompatibility between quantization and token pruning through a novel co-design framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SQAP-VLA framework represents a significant advancement in the field of efficient artificial intelligence, particularly for embodied intelligence and robotics. Its impact extends across several crucial dimensions:

#### 5.1. Resolving a Critical Bottleneck in VLA Deployment

The most immediate and impactful contribution of SQAP-VLA is its elegant solution to the previously overlooked "intrinsic incompatibility" between quantization and token pruning in VLA models. This incompatibility was a fundamental barrier preventing researchers and developers from synergistically leveraging the full potential of both compression techniques. By presenting the *first structured, training-free framework* that enables their simultaneous application without performance degradation, SQAP-VLA opens new pathways for deploying powerful VLA models.

#### 5.2. Enabling Practical On-Device Deployment

The ability to significantly reduce computational and memory costs is paramount for moving VLA models from high-performance computing clusters to real-world, resource-constrained robotic platforms.
- The **1.93× speedup** in end-to-end inference translates directly to lower latency, enabling faster decision-making and more responsive robotic systems.
- The dramatic **reduction in GPU memory usage (from 14.3 GB to 7.6 GB)** directly addresses the memory footprint constraint of edge devices, making advanced VLA models feasible for deployment on hardware with limited resources. This is a game-changer for autonomous robotics, where real-time performance and energy efficiency are critical.

#### 5.3. Training-Free and Cost-Effective Compression

A key strength of SQAP-VLA is its training-free nature. This eliminates the need for costly and time-consuming retraining or fine-tuning processes, which are typically required for many quantization-aware training or pruning methods. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developers, allowing them to apply SQAP-VLA directly to existing pre-trained VLA models, thereby accelerating the development and iteration cycles for robotic applications.

#### 5.4. Unprecedented Performance Retention and Enhancement

Perhaps most remarkably, SQAP-VLA not only mitigates the performance degradation typically associated with aggressive compression (W4A4 quantization and significant pruning) but often *surpasses* the performance of the full-precision baseline model. Achieving an average success rate enhancement of up to 4.5% compared to the original model, while simultaneously delivering substantial speedup and memory reduction, is a testament to the efficacy of its co-designed, context-aware strategies. This suggests that the selective pruning and targeted quantization techniques act not just as compression, but potentially as a form of noise reduction or regularization, focusing the model on the most critical, quantization-robust information.

#### 5.5. Establishing a New Paradigm for Efficient AI

By demonstrating that intelligent co-design can lead to superior outcomes than independent application of compression techniques, SQAP-VLA establishes a new standard for efficient, high-performance VLA model deployment. This work highlights the importance of deeply understanding the interactions between different model optimization techniques rather than treating them in isolation.

#### 5.6. Broader Impact on Embodied AI and Robotics

The practical deployability of VLA models fostered by SQAP-VLA will have a profound impact on embodied AI. It will enable more sophisticated and capable robotic systems in various domains, including manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and exploration. Robots will be able to interpret more complex commands, operate more efficiently in dynamic environments, and perform intricate manipulation tasks with greater precision and responsiveness.

#### 5.7. Future Research Directions

The paper also lays the groundwork for future research. The authors suggest extending this synergistic framework to diverse VLA architectures and exploring dynamic, task-adaptive pruning policies. This indicates that SQAP-VLA is not just a solution for a specific model but a foundational approach that can inspire further innovations in efficient embodied AI.

In conclusion, SQAP-VLA offers a principled, effective, and practical solution that bridges the critical gap between the theoretical capabilities of VLA models and their real-world deployment challenges. Its innovative co-design approach, leading to substantial efficiency gains alongside performance enhancement, marks a significant step forward for both AI model compression and the broader field of embodied intelligence.
