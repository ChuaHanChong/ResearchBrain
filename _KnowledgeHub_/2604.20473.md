# Video-ToC: Video Tree-of-Cue Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Video-ToC, developed at Harbin Institute of Technology, enhances complex video understanding in Video Large Language Models through a tree-guided visual cue localization mechanism and a reasoning-demand reward for reinforcement learning. This framework improves reasoning accuracy and reduces hallucination, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple video benchmarks.

## Problem

- Video Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with complex video reasoning tasks beyond simple perception, often due to limited analytical capabilities.
- Existing Video LLMs exhibit a propensity for hallucination, generating information not supported by video content, often stemming from over-reliance on pre-existing language knowledge.
- Current Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methods for Video LLMs produce generic reasoning rationales that are not optimally tailored for smaller base models, hindering effective visual cue extraction.

## Method

- **Video-ToC** uses a two-stage process: ==SFT== with a novel ==Video-ToC-SFT-1k== dataset generated through a ==tree-guided visual cue localization== mechanism.
- ==Reinforcement Learning== using ==GRPO==, guided by a novel ==Reasoning-demand Reward==.
- The ==reasoning-demand reward== adaptively incentivizes reasoning based on the intrinsic difficulty of a question, promoting efficient and context-appropriate reasoning strategies.

## Results

- Video-ToC achieved **+2.9% to +7.0%** average accuracy gains over Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline across six video understanding benchmarks.
- On VideoHallucer: **51.9%** average accuracy vs **50.5%** baseline and **45.4%** for Video-R1.
- Ablations confirmed both tree-guided visual cue localization (SFT) and reasoning-demand reward (RL) are essential for synergistic gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical, tree-guided localization of spatio-temporal visual cues is crucial for grounding LLMs in specific video evidence, enabling more fine-grained and perception-aware reasoning.
> - Tailoring reinforcement learning incentives to the varying reasoning demands of different questions leads to more effective and generalizable reasoning strategies.
> - Explicitly guiding models to progressively locate visual cues significantly mitigates hallucination by reducing reliance on potentially misleading prior language knowledge.
