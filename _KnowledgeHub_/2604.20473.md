# Video-ToC: Video Tree-of-Cue Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Video-ToC, developed at Harbin Institute of Technology, enhances complex video understanding in Video Large Language Models through a tree-guided visual cue localization mechanism and a reasoning-demand reward for reinforcement learning. This framework improves reasoning accuracy and reduces hallucination, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple video benchmarks.

## Problem

- Video Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with complex video reasoning tasks beyond simple perception, often due to limited analytical capabilities.
- Existing Video LLMs exhibit a propensity for hallucination, generating information not supported by video content, often stemming from over-reliance on pre-existing language knowledge.
- Current Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methods for Video LLMs produce generic reasoning rationales that are not optimally tailored for smaller base models, hindering effective visual cue extraction.

## Method

- **Video-ToC** uses a two-stage process: ==SFT== with a novel ==Video-ToC-SFT-1k== dataset generated through a ==tree-guided visual cue localization== mechanism.
- ==Reinforcement Learning== using ==GRPO==, guided by a novel ==Reasoning-demand Reward==.
- The ==reasoning-demand reward== adaptively incentivizes reasoning based on the intrinsic difficulty of a question, promoting efficient and context-appropriate reasoning strategies.

## Results

- Video-ToC achieved **+2.9% to +7.0%** average accuracy gains over Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline across six video understanding benchmarks.
- On VideoHallucer: **51.9%** average accuracy vs **50.5%** baseline and **45.4%** for Video-R1.
- Ablations confirmed both tree-guided visual cue localization (SFT) and reasoning-demand reward (RL) are essential for synergistic gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical, tree-guided localization of spatio-temporal visual cues is crucial for grounding LLMs in specific video evidence, enabling more fine-grained and perception-aware reasoning.
> - Tailoring reinforcement learning incentives to the varying reasoning demands of different questions leads to more effective and generalizable reasoning strategies.
> - Explicitly guiding models to progressively locate visual cues significantly mitigates hallucination by reducing reliance on potentially misleading prior language knowledge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Video Large Language Models (Video LLMs) has demonstrated capabilities in perception-based video understanding, encompassing tasks such as object recognition and activity detection. However, these models frequently encounter difficulties with complex video reasoning, often exhibiting limitations in analytical capabilities and a propensity for generating information not present in the video content, known as hallucination. Current approaches to enhancing video reasoning in LLMs, often inspired by text-based reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1, commonly involve a two-stage training process: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) followed by reinforcement learning (RL).

A recognized limitation in existing SFT methods for Video LLMs is the generation of reasoning rationales. These rationales are typically created by larger, more powerful models, and may not be optimally tailored for smaller base models. This mismatch can lead to smaller models over-relying on pre-existing language knowledge rather than on specific visual cues within the video, exacerbating hallucination and hindering effective reasoning for tasks requiring fine-grained visual perception. While reinforcement learning has shown promise in improving the generalizability of reasoning in text-based and some multimodal LLMs, its effectiveness for complex video understanding tasks has remained comparatively limited.

Video-ToC addresses these issues by introducing a structured, perception-aware reasoning paradigm. It attempts to guide the model towards progressively locating relevant spatio-temporal visual cues, aiming to improve reasoning accuracy and mitigate hallucinations. This work integrates a novel data generation pipeline for SFT and a refined reward mechanism for RL, distinguishing itself from prior work that relies on generic reasoning patterns or uniform reward structures. By focusing on explicit visual cue localization within a hierarchical framework, Video-ToC seeks to establish a more robust foundation for video reasoning, particularly for tasks demanding intricate spatio-temporal understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the complex video understanding and reasoning capabilities of Video Large Language Models (Video LLMs), specifically by addressing their limitations in reasoning and mitigating the occurrence of hallucinations. The authors identify that existing Video LLMs struggle with tasks requiring intricate analysis beyond simple perception, often due to two main issues:

1. **Limited Perception-Aware Reasoning:** Current Video LLMs tend to rely on pre-trained, inherent reasoning rationales that are not adaptively informed by the specific content of the input video. This can lead to generic reasoning patterns that fail when fine-grained visual cues are necessary for an accurate answer. For instance, when a question demands precise spatio-temporal details, models may default to language-based prior knowledge rather than diligently extracting information from the video.
2. **Propensity for Hallucinations:** The reliance on prior language knowledge, especially when visual perception is inadequate, increases the risk of hallucination, where the model generates plausible but factually incorrect information not supported by the video content. This is particularly problematic for smaller Video LLMs that may not possess the same inherent reasoning or perceptual capabilities as the larger models used to generate their training rationales.

To overcome these challenges, the authors are motivated to develop a framework that:
- **Enhances fine-grained perceptual capabilities:** By providing the model with a structured approach to progressively locate key spatio-temporal visual cues within a video. This aims to ensure that reasoning is directly anchored in the visual evidence.
- **Improves reasoning strategy adaptation:** By enabling on-demand incentives for effective reasoning strategies, allowing the model to determine when and how much reasoning is required based on the task's complexity, rather than applying a uniform approach.
- **Mitigates hallucination:** By encouraging the model to primarily derive answers from video semantics, thereby reducing reliance on potentially misleading prior language knowledge.

These motivations lead to the development of Video-ToC, which introduces specific mechanisms—tree-guided visual cue localization and reasoning-demand reward—designed to foster robust, perception-grounded reasoning and reduce hallucination risk in Video LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Video-ToC framework employs a two-stage training process: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL).

#### 3.1. Data Construction Pipeline for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The SFT stage utilizes the novel "Video-ToC-SFT-1k" dataset, built upon a tree-guided visual cue localization mechanism. This dataset comprises 1,000 high-quality training samples. The construction pipeline involves three key steps:

1. **Leaf Node Construction:**
 - An input video is segmented into multiple clips using the Panda-70M method, which involves shot boundary detection and stitching of semantically similar adjacent clips.
 - A Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM), specifically Qwen2.5-VL-7B, generates a comprehensive description for each video clip.
 - An LLM (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) analyzes the given question-answer pair and identifies the key clips essential for answering the question, based on the generated clip captions.
 - A Segment Tree is constructed where each leaf node represents a distinct video clip.

2. **Reasoning Trajectory Generation:**
 - A reasoning trajectory is derived by backtracking from the identified key leaf nodes (target clips) up to the root (the entire video). This forms a subtree that implicitly encodes a hierarchical, coarse-to-fine localization process of spatio-temporal cues.
 - To make this trajectory interpretable by an LLM, it is preprocessed into "visual cue descriptions." This involves concatenating all clips associated with the constituent nodes at each layer of the subtree, forming "video compilations."
 - Duplicate compilations, resulting from identical clip sets across different layers, are removed to yield a concise representation.

3. **SFT Data Construction:**
 - An MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) describes these deduplicated "video compilations," with each description detailing specific visual cues to focus on for each step of the localization process.
 - An LLM (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) filters out samples where the visual cues from the final step are deemed insufficient to derive the correct answer.
 - Finally, an LLM (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) generates a natural language narrative, termed the "Video-ToC rationale," by summarizing these visual cue descriptions along with the question-answer pair. This rationale follows a step-by-step localization pattern, as exemplified in Figure 2 and 5. The prompt structure ensures a first-person tone, detailing how to locate the most important clip. The rigid "Step k:" structure is later removed for naturalness.
 - The Video-ToC rationale, combined with the video and question-answer pair, forms a single training sample for SFT. The total cost for building Video-ToC-SFT-1k is estimated at approximately 1 GPU day on A6000 GPUs.

#### 3.2. Reasoning-demand Reward for Reinforcement Learning (RL)

After the SFT cold start, the model undergoes RL using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, guided by a novel "Reasoning-demand Reward." This stage uses the "Video-ToC-RL-2k" dataset, comprising 2,000 multiple-choice QA samples with reasoning demand annotations.

1. **Vanilla Accuracy Reward (Baseline):** The standard GRPO reward is a binary (0 or 1) function, indicating only whether the prediction is correct. The authors argue this is suboptimal as reasoning demands vary across questions.

2. **Reasoning-demand Reward:**
 - For each question, an MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) attempts to answer directly, without reasoning, across M (set to 8) independent trials.
 - Let α be the number of correct predictions in these M trials.
 - The "reasoning demand" for the question is quantified as *e^(-α/M)*.
 - If the model correctly answers the question during training, it receives a reward equal to this reasoning demand (R_rd = e^(-α/M)); otherwise, the reward is 0.
 - This design incentivizes reasoning more for questions where the model struggles to answer directly (low α, high R_rd) and less for easy questions (high α, low R_rd). The exponential function modulates reward magnitudes.
 - The Video-ToC-RL-2k dataset is constructed from LLaVA-Video-178K multiple-choice items, by calculating this reasoning demand for each sample. Questions with difficulty scores (1 - α/M) below 0.2 or above 0.8 are discarded to focus on a balanced range of complexity. The cost for building this dataset is estimated at 14 GPU hours on A6000 GPUs.

3. **GRPO Training Objective:**
 - The model generates a group of G candidate responses for each input.
 - Reasoning-demand rewards are calculated for each response.
 - Instead of directly using these rewards as advantages, GRPO calculates relative advantages (A_i) within the group by normalizing rewards with respect to the group's mean and standard deviation (Equation 4).
 - Crucially, the authors modify GRPO by multiplying these relative advantages (A_i) by the question's specific reasoning demand (γ) to derive the final advantage (Â_i = A_i × γ). This ensures that the magnitude of the advantage signal is tailored to the actual reasoning complexity of the question, not just its correctness.
 - The model is then optimized to maximize the GRPO objective, which includes a clipping term and a KL-divergence regularization term to constrain policy deviation from a reference model.
 - Format reward is omitted during GRPO training, as the SFT stage and prompt instructions are sufficient for format adherence.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B is chosen as the baseline.
- **Training Details:** SFT is performed for one epoch on Video-ToC-SFT-1k, followed by RL for one epoch on Video-ToC-RL-2k. Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 5e-7 is used.
- **Input Frames:** 16 frames are uniformly sampled for training. For inference, models are evaluated with 16, 32, and 64 input frames at a resolution of 256x28x28.
- **Prompting:** A unified prompt is used for both training and inference, explicitly instructing the model to "progressively locate video clips... and then provide your final answer," enclosed within `<locate>` and `<answer>` tags, respectively. Task-specific instructions are appended for multiple-choice or numerical answers.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluations are conducted on three video reasoning benchmarks (VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, MMVU), three video general benchmarks (MVBench, TempCompass, VideoMME), and one video hallucination benchmark (VideoHallucer).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides comprehensive evaluations on various benchmarks, demonstrating the efficacy of Video-ToC.

#### 4.1. Overall Video Understanding Performance

As presented in Table I, Video-ToC consistently outperforms the baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and other recent methods, including Video-R1, across six video understanding benchmarks (VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, MMVU, MVBench, TempCompass, VideoMME) under all tested input frame settings (16, 32, and 64 frames).

- **SFT Stage Effectiveness:** The SFT model, Video-ToC-SFT, which is trained on only 1,000 samples, already shows substantial performance gains over the baseline. It also significantly outperforms Video-R1-SFT (the SFT stage model of Video-R1) and exhibits performance comparable to the fully trained Video-R1 model. This indicates the effectiveness of the tree-guided visual cue localization rationales in the Video-ToC-SFT-1k dataset.
- **RL Stage Enhancement:** The subsequent reinforcement learning stage, utilizing the proposed reasoning-demand reward, further boosts the performance of the SFT model. The final Video-ToC model achieves the highest average accuracy across all benchmarks, with improvements ranging from 2.9% to 7.0% compared to the baseline, and 1.3% to 3.0% compared to Video-R1, depending on the input frame setting. This demonstrates the generalizability of the proposed training strategies and datasets.

#### 4.2. Hallucination Mitigation

Table II illustrates the hallucination risks on the VideoHallucer benchmark.

- Compared to the baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), Video-R1 exhibits more severe hallucination across most task categories, particularly in object-relation, temporal, and semantic detail tasks. This suggests that rationales freely generated by more powerful models may not be suitable for smaller base models, leading to reliance on language knowledge over visual cues and increased hallucination.
- Video-ToC demonstrates a higher average accuracy (51.9%) compared to both the baseline (50.5%) and Video-R1 (45.4%), indicating its effectiveness in mitigating hallucination. Specifically, Video-ToC shows improvements in Object-Relation, Temporal, and Semantic Detail categories, while having a slightly lower score in the Factual category. This suggests that guiding the model to localize visual cues progressively reduces the generation of unsupported information.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Necessity of SFT Cold Start (Table III):** Directly applying GRPO training to the baseline model without SFT ('Baseline + GRPO') yields only marginal performance gains. In contrast, SFT using the Video-ToC-SFT-1k dataset ('Baseline + SFT') results in significantly better performance, demonstrating that the proposed reasoning paradigm of progressively identifying critical visual cues is crucial for effective video understanding. The combination of SFT and GRPO ('Baseline + SFT + GRPO') achieves the best performance, indicating that RL further refines the SFT-introduced reasoning strategies.
- **Effect of Tree-Guided Visual Cue Localization (Table IV):** Training with Video-ToC-SFT-1k (which employs tree-guided localization) significantly outperforms training with Video-SingleCue-SFT-1k (which uses a single step of cue localization). This confirms the advantage of the hierarchical, tree-guided approach in annotating rationales, enabling a more structured and effective localization pattern.
- **Effect of Reasoning-Demand Reward (Table V):** The proposed reasoning-demand reward mechanism ('SFT + GRPO w/ RD Reward') consistently improves accuracy on all evaluated benchmarks compared to conventional GRPO training with a vanilla accuracy reward ('SFT + GRPO w/ Vanilla Reward'). This highlights the benefit of tailoring incentive levels to the varying reasoning demands of different questions.
- **Discussion on Format Reward (Table VI):** The study found that including a format reward during GRPO training had negligible effect on performance, with both models achieving 100% adherence to the specified output format. This indicates that the detailed formatting guidelines in the prompts and the implicit enforcement through the reasoning-demand reward are sufficient.

#### 4.4. Visualization Analysis

- **Quantitative Analysis (Figure 4):** On the VideoMME benchmark, Video-ToC demonstrates improvements across all task categories compared to the baseline. It also outperforms Video-R1 on most categories, notably 'Temporal Perception,' 'Counting Problems,' and 'Object Reasoning,' suggesting enhanced perceptual and reasoning capabilities.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 5 & 6):** Examples of Video-ToC rationales illustrate a progressive process of locating increasingly informative video clips. Model outputs show that Video-ToC effectively acquires and generalizes this step-by-step reasoning paradigm, adapting flexibly to both reasoning-based and perception-based questions by either deducing and searching for cues or meticulously scanning for information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Video-ToC framework introduces several advancements that hold significance for the field of multimodal large language models and video understanding:

1. **Enhanced Reasoning and Perception for Video LLMs:** By integrating a tree-guided visual cue localization mechanism, Video-ToC enables LLMs to perform more fine-grained, perception-aware reasoning. This is crucial for complex video understanding tasks that demand a meticulous examination of spatio-temporal details, moving beyond superficial interpretations. The ability to progressively locate relevant visual evidence anchors the reasoning process in concrete video content, making it more robust.

2. **Mitigation of Hallucination:** The structured approach to grounding reasoning in specific visual cues directly addresses the problem of hallucination. By reducing the model's reliance on prior linguistic knowledge when visual evidence is paramount, Video-ToC contributes to more factual and reliable responses, which is a critical step towards trustworthy AI systems in multimodal domains.

3. **Novel Data Annotation and Reward Mechanisms:** The automatic data generation pipeline for creating the Video-ToC-SFT-1k and Video-ToC-RL-2k datasets provides a scalable method for producing high-quality training data. The "reasoning-demand reward" mechanism is a significant contribution to reinforcement learning for multimodal reasoning. By adaptively incentivizing reasoning based on the actual complexity of the question, it guides the model to develop more efficient and context-appropriate reasoning strategies, avoiding both overthinking on simple tasks and insufficient reasoning on complex ones.

4. **Improved Generalizability and Robustness:** The consistent outperformance across a diverse set of six video understanding benchmarks (both reasoning-focused and general) and a hallucination benchmark indicates that Video-ToC offers improved generalizability and robustness compared to prior methods. This suggests a more adaptable and reliable framework for handling various video-related queries.

The potential impact of Video-ToC extends to various applications requiring advanced video comprehension, such as video surveillance, content analysis, educational tools, and human-robot interaction, where accurate and grounded reasoning from visual information is essential. The methodology presents a blueprint for developing more sophisticated and less error-prone video-language models.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge current limitations, including the use of uniform frame sampling (16, 32, or 64 input frames) and the exclusive focus on video training data. Future work aims to explore the effects of increasing input frame numbers, investigate alternative frame sampling strategies, and devise methodologies for leveraging image-video hybrid reasoning data to further enhance video reasoning capabilities. These directions suggest continued efforts to refine the perceptual and reasoning robustness of Video LLMs.
