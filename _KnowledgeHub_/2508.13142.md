# Holistic Evaluation of Multimodal LLMs on Spatial Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> SenseTime Research and NTU introduce EASI, a framework for holistically evaluating spatial intelligence in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), revealing that while models like GPT-5 achieve new benchmarks, a substantial performance gap of over 30 percentage points generally persists compared to human capabilities, particularly in complex reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit a surprising inability to perform basic spatial tasks, despite advanced capabilities in other multimodal domains.
- Spatial intelligence, crucial for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and embodied agents interacting with the physical world, remains a fundamentally underexplored and fragmented area in MLLM evaluation.
- Existing benchmarks for spatial understanding are inconsistent and lack a unified framework for systematic assessment, hindering comparable progress across models.

## Method

- ==EASI== (Evaluation of Multimodal LLMs on Spatial Intelligence) is proposed as a comprehensive framework to systematically assess MLLMs' spatial intelligence.
- A ==unified taxonomy of six fundamental spatial capabilities== (Metric Measurement, Mental Reconstruction, Spatial Relations, Perspective-Taking, Deformation and Assembly, Comprehensive Reasoning) is developed to categorize and integrate diverse spatial tasks.
- Eight representative benchmarks are incorporated and evaluated under ==standardized EASI protocols==, including a unified zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) system prompt, to ensure consistent and comparable assessment of leading MLLMs.

## Results

- GPT-5 establishes a new state of the art in spatial intelligence, consistently ranking as the best performer across evaluated benchmarks, yet lags human performance by over **30** percentage points on average in complex SI tasks.
- Models show relative proficiency in Metric Measurement and Spatial Relations, achieving human-level or superior performance in some object/room size estimations, but fail significantly in areas like Perspective-Taking and Mental Reconstruction.
- Qualitative analysis confirms that even advanced models like GPT-5 struggle with complex visual illusions, misinterpretations of transformations, and failure to infer hidden objects, highlighting a lack of fundamental spatial representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial intelligence tasks expose greater deficiencies in MLLM capabilities compared to non-spatial tasks, with performance gaps often exceeding 76 percentage points against human baselines on SI-specific benchmarks.
> - The advantage of proprietary models over open-source counterparts diminishes significantly on the most challenging spatial intelligence tasks, suggesting that breakthroughs in these hard areas may not solely depend on proprietary scaling.
> - Despite progress, fundamental weaknesses persist in MLLMs, particularly in Perspective-Taking, Mental Reconstruction, Deformation and Assembly, and Comprehensive Reasoning, which often require integrated capabilities and multi-stage logical deduction.
