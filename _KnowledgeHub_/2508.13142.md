# Holistic Evaluation of Multimodal LLMs on Spatial Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> SenseTime Research and NTU introduce EASI, a framework for holistically evaluating spatial intelligence in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), revealing that while models like GPT-5 achieve new benchmarks, a substantial performance gap of over 30 percentage points generally persists compared to human capabilities, particularly in complex reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit a surprising inability to perform basic spatial tasks, despite advanced capabilities in other multimodal domains.
- Spatial intelligence, crucial for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and embodied agents interacting with the physical world, remains a fundamentally underexplored and fragmented area in MLLM evaluation.
- Existing benchmarks for spatial understanding are inconsistent and lack a unified framework for systematic assessment, hindering comparable progress across models.

## Method

- ==EASI== (Evaluation of Multimodal LLMs on Spatial Intelligence) is proposed as a comprehensive framework to systematically assess MLLMs' spatial intelligence.
- A ==unified taxonomy of six fundamental spatial capabilities== (Metric Measurement, Mental Reconstruction, Spatial Relations, Perspective-Taking, Deformation and Assembly, Comprehensive Reasoning) is developed to categorize and integrate diverse spatial tasks.
- Eight representative benchmarks are incorporated and evaluated under ==standardized EASI protocols==, including a unified zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) system prompt, to ensure consistent and comparable assessment of leading MLLMs.

## Results

- GPT-5 establishes a new state of the art in spatial intelligence, consistently ranking as the best performer across evaluated benchmarks, yet lags human performance by over **30** percentage points on average in complex SI tasks.
- Models show relative proficiency in Metric Measurement and Spatial Relations, achieving human-level or superior performance in some object/room size estimations, but fail significantly in areas like Perspective-Taking and Mental Reconstruction.
- Qualitative analysis confirms that even advanced models like GPT-5 struggle with complex visual illusions, misinterpretations of transformations, and failure to infer hidden objects, highlighting a lack of fundamental spatial representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial intelligence tasks expose greater deficiencies in MLLM capabilities compared to non-spatial tasks, with performance gaps often exceeding 76 percentage points against human baselines on SI-specific benchmarks.
> - The advantage of proprietary models over open-source counterparts diminishes significantly on the most challenging spatial intelligence tasks, suggesting that breakthroughs in these hard areas may not solely depend on proprietary scaling.
> - Despite progress, fundamental weaknesses persist in MLLMs, particularly in Perspective-Taking, Mental Reconstruction, Deformation and Assembly, and Comprehensive Reasoning, which often require integrated capabilities and multi-stage logical deduction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have revolutionized many areas of AI, enabling models to process and reason across various modalities, particularly vision and language. These models, exemplified by architectures like GPT, Gemini, and Qwen, have demonstrated impressive capabilities in tasks such as image captioning, visual question answering, and even complex logical reasoning. However, as MLLMs become more sophisticated and move towards broader application in embodied agents (e.g., robotics), a critical limitation has emerged: their struggle with spatial understanding and reasoning.

Spatial intelligence (SI), defined as the ability to perceive, understand, and reason about objects and their relationships in physical space, is acknowledged as an "indispensable" capability for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and general embodied agents operating in the physical world. Despite impressive progress in other multimodal capabilities, the paper highlights that even state-of-the-art MLLMs often fail at spatial tasks that are trivially easy for humans, or even children. Previous work has identified SI as a distinct and "fundamentally underexplored frontier" compared to capabilities measured by mainstream benchmarks.

This research directly addresses this critical gap by providing a holistic framework for evaluating SI. The timing of the paper, coinciding with the release of powerful new models like GPT-5, underscores the pressing need for systematic evaluation in this domain. By consolidating existing and newly curated benchmarks, proposing a unified taxonomy, and rigorously testing leading proprietary and open-source models, EASI aims to provide a clear picture of the current state of SI in MLLMs. This work contributes to the broader landscape by:
- **Highlighting a crucial bottleneck:** Drawing attention to spatial intelligence as a key challenge for advancing MLLMs towards AGI.
- **Providing a standardized evaluation:** Offering a unified framework that overcomes the fragmentation of existing benchmarks, enabling consistent and comparable assessment of SI.
- **Driving future research:** By open-sourcing its codebase and launching a leaderboard, EASI fosters community engagement and accelerates progress in developing MLLMs with more robust spatial reasoning capabilities, which are essential for applications ranging from robotics and autonomous navigation to augmented reality and human-computer interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a recognized deficiency in state-of-the-art MLLMs: their surprising inability to perform basic spatial tasks that humans find effortless, despite their impressive achievements in other complex multimodal challenges. This limitation is considered a significant barrier to achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), particularly for embodied agents that must interact with and adapt to the physical world.

Against this backdrop, the paper's key objectives are clearly articulated:

1. **Propose EASI for Holistic Evaluation of Spatial Intelligence:** Introduce a comprehensive framework named EASI (Evaluation of Multimodal LLMs on Spatial Intelligence) to systematically assess the SI capabilities of MLLMs.
2. **Develop a Comprehensive Taxonomy of Spatial Tasks:** Conceptualize a unified taxonomy of six fundamental spatial capabilities (Metric Measurement, Mental Reconstruction, Spatial Relations, Perspective-Taking, Deformation and Assembly, Comprehensive Reasoning) that integrates existing benchmarks and new task collections. This taxonomy aims to provide a structured way to understand and categorize the diverse facets of spatial intelligence.
3. **Systematically Evaluate State-of-the-Art MLLMs:** Conduct a rigorous empirical study across eight key benchmarks, evaluating leading proprietary models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-pro, Grok-4, Seed-1.6) and open-source counterparts (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, InternVL3.5, Qwen3-8B). The goal is to establish "where the leading models stand on the path toward spatial intelligence."
4. **Identify Strengths and Weaknesses of Current MLLMs:** Through quantitative and qualitative analysis, pinpoint specific areas where models excel and, more importantly, where significant performance gaps persist relative to human capabilities.
5. **Accelerate Community Progress:** Open-source the EASI codebase, including standardized interfaces, protocols, and prompts, and launch an accompanying EASI leaderboard. These resources are intended to provide a reproducible, one-stop solution for SI assessment, reduce friction for researchers, and foster collaborative advancement toward robust spatial intelligence.

In essence, the research is driven by the need to bridge the gap between current MLLM capabilities and the human-level spatial understanding required for truly intelligent, physically grounded agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EASI framework adopts a rigorous and multifaceted approach to evaluating spatial intelligence in MLLMs. Its methodology is built upon a novel taxonomy, a comprehensive set of benchmarks, and standardized evaluation protocols.

#### 3.1. Six Fundamental Capabilities

To unify diverse spatial tasks, EASI distills spatial intelligence into six fundamental capabilities:
- **Metric Measurement (MM):** Assessing the ability to infer 3D dimensions (e.g., depth, length, object size, room size) from 2D observations, reflecting an understanding of physical scale.
- **Mental Reconstruction (MR):** Evaluating the ability to infer complete 3D structures from limited 2D views and perform mental manipulation, akin to mental rotation or reconstructing 3D objects from drawings.
- **Spatial Relations (SR):** Understanding the relative positions and orientations of multiple objects within a view, often requiring the application of a virtual coordinate system.
- **Perspective-Taking (PT):** Reasoning across distinct viewpoints, involving the construction of mental 3D representations, scene-level reasoning over multiple objects, and explicit reasoning under changing camera perspectives.
- **Deformation and Assembly (DA):** Focusing on understanding and reasoning about shape changes, structural transformations, and part-based compositionality (e.g., knot tying, box unfolding, object assembly).
- **Comprehensive Reasoning (CR):** Requiring the coordinated use of various spatial capabilities with extended memory and multi-stage logical deduction, such as navigation, puzzle-solving, or simulating physical interactions.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Benchmarks

The study incorporates eight representative and recently released benchmarks that together provide comprehensive coverage of the six capabilities:
- VSI-Bench
- SITE
- MMSI
- OmniSpatial
- MindCube (using MindCube-Tiny for balanced evaluation)
- STARE
- CoreCognition
- SpatialViz

These benchmarks collectively involve approximately 31,000 images, 4,500 videos, and 24,000 question-answer pairs, highlighting the extensive scale of the evaluation.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocols

EASI addresses inconsistencies in existing evaluations by defining clear protocols:
- **Official Protocol:** For direct comparison with prior work, models are evaluated using each benchmark's native system prompt (Direct QA, Zero-shot CoT, or Manual CoT) and official metrics (Accuracy, F1, Chance-Adjusted Accuracy (CAA), Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA)).
- **EASI Protocol:** To enable meaningful cross-benchmark comparison, a unified protocol is introduced. This uses a standardized zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) system prompt (Figure 4 in the appendix) with structured answer templates and standardized metrics (CAA for MCQs, MRA for numerical answers).
- **Answer-Matching Methods:** A robust two-step rule-based matching process (initial tag-based, then extended pattern-based) is employed to ensure accuracy and consistency in extracting answers.
- **Circular Evaluation:** The study investigates circular evaluation (rotating answer options) to mitigate option-position bias. While a standard (non-circular) strategy is primarily adopted for efficiency, Soft-circular scoring is used for CoreCognition to align with its original paper, and comparative analysis shows consistent rankings across strategies.

#### 3.4. Models Evaluated

The study evaluates a wide array of high-performing MLLMs, encompassing both proprietary and open-source models:
- **Proprietary Models:** GPT-5 (and its nano/mini variants), Gemini-2.5-pro, Grok-4, Seed-1.6.
- **Open-source Models:** Qwen2.5-VL (3B, 7B, 72B), InternVL3 (8B, 78B), InternVL3.5-8B, Qwen3-8B.

#### 3.5. Token Consumption Analysis

The methodology also includes an analysis of token consumption, distinguishing between "internal tokens" (hidden reasoning) and "external tokens" (user-visible output) for proprietary models. This provides insights into models' reasoning strategies and efficiency, informing optimal inference configurations (e.g., setting `max_tokens` appropriately).

#### 3.6. Qualitative Case Study

A detailed case study is conducted, particularly focusing on GPT-5 (including its "thinking mode") and its predecessor GPT-o3. This qualitative analysis examines representative failure cases from benchmarks and in-the-wild examples to gain deeper insights into the models' strengths and limitations across the six fundamental capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical study, conducted at a cost exceeding ten billion total tokens, yielded several significant findings:

1. **GPT-5 Establishes New State of the Art but Lags Human Performance:** GPT-5 demonstrates unprecedented strength in spatial intelligence, consistently ranking as the overall best performer across the evaluated benchmarks, surpassing strong baselines like Gemini-2.5-pro and InternVL3. It achieves human-level or even surpasses human performance in certain Metric Measurement (MM) and Spatial Relations (SR) tasks (e.g., object and room size estimation, 3D information understanding in SITE). However, despite this progress, a substantial performance gap remains between GPT-5 and human capabilities across a broad spectrum of SI tasks, often exceeding 30 percentage points, particularly in more complex reasoning categories.

2. **Spatial Intelligence Tasks Expose Greater Model Capability Deficiencies:** MLLMs perform significantly worse on fundamental spatial intelligence tasks compared to non-spatial tasks. This is evident in benchmarks like MMSI, where GPT-5 scores more than 76 percentage points below human performance on average, despite the benchmark being entirely SI-related. In contrast, models achieve human-level accuracy on many non-SI tasks within benchmarks like CoreCognition and SpatialViz. Comparing analogous 2D vs. 3D tasks (e.g., Mental Rotation in SpatialViz, Transformation in STARE) further highlights the increased difficulty of 3D spatial reasoning for models.

3. **Proprietary Models' Advantage Narrows on Most Difficult SI Tasks:** While proprietary models generally outperform open-source counterparts, this advantage diminishes considerably when facing the most challenging spatial intelligence tasks. In subtasks where model performance is more than 60 percentage points below human levels (e.g., in MMSI), the performance gap between open- and closed-source models is often small (around or less than 15 points). In some categories (e.g., Geometric Reasoning and Hypothetical Reasoning in OmniSpatial), the strongest open-source models can even match or surpass leading proprietary models. This suggests that further breakthroughs in hard SI tasks might not exclusively rely on proprietary scaling.

4. **Specific Strengths and Persistent Weaknesses:**
 - **Strengths:** Models show relative proficiency in Metric Measurement (MM) and Spatial Relations (SR), especially for basic estimations and straightforward spatial deductions from single views.
 - **Weaknesses:** Significant gaps persist in:
 - **Perspective-Taking (PT):** The most fundamental challenge, requiring reasoning from multiple viewpoints. Large discrepancies (e.g., 80 points in MMSI, 50 points in MindCube) persist, particularly for complex viewpoint transformations and minimal view overlap.
 - **Mental Reconstruction (MR):** Reconstructing 3D structures from limited observations remains difficult. GPT-5 sometimes performs worse than random guessing on certain MR subtasks (e.g., Attribute (Appearance) in MMSI, 3D Mental Rotation in SpatialViz), failing tasks trivial for humans (Figure 1, MR4 case study).
 - **Deformation and Assembly (DA):** Reasoning about complex shape transformations and part-based compositionality (e.g., mental folding, object assembly) continues to be a critical weakness.
 - **Comprehensive Reasoning (CR):** Tasks demanding multi-step inference, sustained spatial memory, and logical deduction (e.g., navigation, counting occluded objects) show significant performance gaps.

**Qualitative Case Study Insights (Section 4):**
The qualitative analysis largely confirms the quantitative findings. GPT-5 demonstrates principled reasoning for MM but struggles with complex scenarios in SR involving visual illusions. Its MR capabilities show improvements (e.g., reconstructing from multiple views, novel view generation with "thinking mode"), yet it fails on simple tasks. PT, DA, and CR remain particularly challenging due to reliance on integrated capabilities, multi-stage reasoning, and a lack of fundamental spatial representations, leading to models misinterpreting transformations or failing to infer hidden objects. The "thinking mode" in GPT-5 shows accuracy improvements from Minimal to Medium effort, but further increases can lead to time-outs or token limits, suggesting diminishing marginal returns or robustness issues at very high effort levels.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Holistic Evaluation of Multimodal LLMs on Spatial Intelligence" paper makes several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the field of artificial intelligence:

1. **Demarcation of a Critical Research Frontier:** By systematically highlighting the profound limitations of even the most advanced MLLMs in spatial intelligence, the work clearly delineates this area as a critical, yet underexplored, frontier for AI research. This clear articulation of a "core knowledge deficit" is crucial for re-orienting research efforts and investment towards foundational capabilities necessary for true intelligence.

2. **Standardized and Holistic Evaluation Framework (EASI):** The introduction of EASI provides a much-needed unified and comprehensive framework for evaluating spatial intelligence. By proposing a common taxonomy of six fundamental capabilities and integrating various disparate benchmarks under standardized protocols, EASI significantly reduces evaluation friction and enhances comparability across different models. This standardization is vital for ensuring meaningful progress, preventing fragmented efforts, and offering a clear yardstick for future advancements.

3. **Reproducible and Community-Driven Resources:** The open-sourcing of the EASI codebase, including standardized interfaces, integrated protocols, and prompts, is a major contribution to the research community. This ensures reproducibility of results and lowers the barrier for other researchers to conduct rigorous spatial intelligence evaluations. Coupled with the accompanying EASI leaderboard, these resources foster a collaborative environment, enabling continuous tracking of model performance and accelerating collective progress.

4. **Actionable Insights for Model Development:** The detailed empirical findings provide concrete insights into the strengths and weaknesses of current MLLMs. Knowing that models excel in certain Metric Measurement and Spatial Relations tasks but struggle significantly with Perspective-Taking, Mental Reconstruction, Deformation and Assembly, and Comprehensive Reasoning offers a clear roadmap for targeted architectural innovations, training data curation, and algorithmic improvements. The observation that proprietary models' advantage narrows on the most difficult SI tasks is particularly impactful, suggesting that open-source foundations can effectively drive breakthroughs in these challenging areas.

5. **Accelerating Progress Towards Embodied AGI:** Robust spatial reasoning is a cornerstone for developing truly intelligent embodied agents that can perceive, navigate, and interact effectively within the physical world. By identifying and quantifying the current limitations, this research directly contributes to overcoming a major hurdle on the path toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Future MLLMs, informed by EASI's insights, will be better equipped to handle complex real-world scenarios in robotics, autonomous systems, virtual reality, and other applications requiring deep spatial understanding.

In conclusion, the EASI framework and the comprehensive evaluation presented in this paper serve as a foundational resource for the multimodal AI community. It not only provides a precise snapshot of the current state of spatial intelligence in MLLMs but also lays the groundwork for guiding methodological innovations and fostering cumulative progress towards models capable of robustly understanding and reasoning about our 3D world.
