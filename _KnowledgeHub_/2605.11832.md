# Learning Action Manifold with Multi-view Latent Priors for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework enhances robotic manipulation by improving monocular spatial perception through a Geometry-Guided Gated Transformer and boosting action learning efficiency via Action Manifold Learning. This approach achieved an average success rate of 98.6% on LIBERO and 85.7% on LIBERO-Plus, outperforming prior methods in robustness and generalization.

## Problem

- Monocular Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with 3D spatial perception due to inherent depth ambiguity and geometric inconsistency from single RGB camera inputs, limiting manipulation precision.
- Current generative action prediction methods for VLA models rely on indirect, high-dimensional statistical targets (noise or velocity), imposing a heavy optimization burden and leading to inefficient and less robust policy learning.
- Alternative approaches that incorporate explicit 3D inputs like RGB-D or point clouds require additional depth-sensing hardware, restricting the scalability and practical deployment of VLA systems.

## Method

- Introduced a Geometry-Guided Gated Transformer (G
3T) that synthesizes multi-view latent features and adaptively fuses them with monocular geometric priors to enhance spatial perception.
- Developed Action Manifold Learning (AML), an action expert that directly predicts clean action chunks on a hypothesized low-dimensional action manifold, using a velocity-consistent loss.
- Integrated these components into a modular VLA framework that leverages a powerful Vision-Language Model (Qwen3-VL) for semantic feature extraction and an AML-based action expert.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of 98.6% on the LIBERO benchmark and 85.7% on LIBERO-Plus under zero-shot perturbations, significantly outperforming existing state-of-the-art VLA methods.
- Demonstrated improved depth estimation accuracy with G
3T, showing lower Absolute Relative Error and Root Mean Squared Error compared to monocular VGGT features.
- Showcased AML's efficiency, maintaining robust performance with minimal denoising steps and improved graceful degradation with increasing action chunk sizes, alongside higher success rates in real-world bi-manual manipulation on RoboTwin 2.0 (86.06%).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing synthesized multi-view latent representations with monocular geometric priors through an adaptive gating mechanism can effectively resolve monocular depth ambiguity and improve 3D spatial awareness.
> - Directly predicting clean action chunks, rather than abstract noise or velocity targets, by aligning with an "Action Manifold" simplifies the optimization landscape and enhances learning efficiency for high-dimensional actions.
> - A synergistic integration of enhanced spatial perception and efficient action learning significantly improves the robustness, precision, and generalization capabilities of VLA models in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, an area experiencing significant advancements due to the integration of large-scale pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs). VLA frameworks aim to enable autonomous agents to interpret natural language instructions and translate them into physical actions, facilitating complex and long-horizon tasks.

Existing VLA models have demonstrated capabilities in zero-shot generalization and semantic reasoning. However, the paper identifies two fundamental limitations within the current landscape. Firstly, these models often exhibit limited spatial perception under monocular conditions. While some approaches incorporate explicit 3D inputs (e.g., RGB-D) or leverage features from 3D foundation models, the former requires additional hardware, and the latter often struggles with inherent depth ambiguity from single-camera input, leading to noisy and geometrically inconsistent spatial features. This compromises precision in manipulation tasks.

Secondly, current action generation mechanisms, particularly those based on generative models like diffusion or flow matching, typically rely on indirect targets such as noise (ϵ-prediction) or velocity (v-prediction). These targets are high-dimensional, unstructured statistical fields that lack direct physical semantics. Regressing such abstract signals imposes a substantial optimization burden on the VLA network, requiring it to disentangle complex patterns rather than learn meaningful action structures. This challenge intensifies with increasing action dimensionality, hindering the efficient learning of complex robotic policies.

This work contributes to the VLA landscape by proposing a synergistic framework that directly addresses these two bottlenecks. It introduces novel designs to enhance spatial perception from monocular inputs by leveraging multi-view latent priors and to improve action learning efficiency by shifting to a direct action prediction mechanism. By tackling these issues, the research aims to enable more robust and precise robotic manipulation in complex environments without relying on specialized hardware, thus improving the scalability and practicality of VLA systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to improve the spatial perception ability of VLA models under monocular conditions and to enhance the efficiency of action learning in high-dimensional action spaces. These objectives are motivated by specific limitations observed in current VLA frameworks:

**Detailed Motivation for Enhancing Spatial Perception:**
Current VLA models, when operating with monocular (single RGB camera) input, face an inherent challenge in acquiring accurate 3D spatial awareness. The projection from a 3D physical scene to a 2D image plane results in an irreversible loss of depth information, leading to what is termed "monocular depth ambiguity." This limitation is critical because precise robotic manipulation, especially for tasks requiring fine motor control or interaction with closely spaced objects, fundamentally relies on accurate 3D understanding of the environment.

Existing attempts to address this problem have their own drawbacks:
- **Explicit 3D Inputs (e.g., RGB-D, point clouds):** While providing richer spatial information, these approaches necessitate additional, often costly, hardware (e.g., depth sensors). This requirement restricts the scalability and broad deployment of VLA systems, making them less accessible for setups with standard RGB cameras.
- **Leveraging Features from Pre-trained 3D Foundation Models:** Models like VGGT can extract implicit geometric priors from 2D images. However, even these advanced models are fundamentally limited by the ill-posed nature of recovering full scene geometry from a single view. The spatial features derived are frequently noisy, geometrically inconsistent, and susceptible to occlusions, which can lead to unreliable environmental perception and subsequent manipulation failures. The motivation here is to overcome this fundamental depth ambiguity using only monocular input, without requiring additional hardware.

**Detailed Motivation for Improving Action Learning Efficiency:**
The prevailing generative approaches for action prediction in VLA models, such as those based on diffusion (ϵ-prediction) or flow matching (v-prediction), rely on predicting abstract, high-dimensional statistical fields (noise or velocity vectors) rather than directly predicting the actions themselves. The paper argues that this indirect prediction paradigm imposes a significant optimization burden for several reasons:
- **Unstructured Targets:** Noise and velocity fields are often unstructured and lack direct physical semantics. The VLA network is forced to learn complex mappings from multimodal observations to these abstract targets, which can be computationally inefficient.
- **Increased Optimization Difficulty:** Disentangling these complex patterns rather than learning meaningful action structures diverts model capacity. This learning difficulty is exacerbated as the dimensionality of the action space increases (e.g., for multi-arm or whole-body control), making it challenging to converge to robust and precise policies within a practical training budget.
- **Off-Manifold Predictions:** Based on the "Action Manifold Hypothesis," successful actions are posited to reside on a low-dimensional, smooth manifold defined by physics, task goals, and environmental constraints. Indirect targets like noise or velocity, however, often lie off this intrinsic manifold. Training a model to predict these off-manifold targets can lead to less stable and potentially physically implausible actions.

The motivation for Action Manifold Learning is to directly align the policy output with the underlying structure of valid actions, thereby simplifying the optimization landscape and improving the efficiency, robustness, and interpretability of the learned robotic policies, particularly in high-dimensional and complex manipulation scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed VLA framework integrates two novel designs: a Geometry-Guided Gated Transformer (G³T) for enhanced spatial perception and Action Manifold Learning (AML) for efficient action generation. The overall architecture is modular, separating high-level semantic reasoning from low-level action execution.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The framework comprises a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for perception and an Action Expert based on AML for decision-making. It takes multimodal inputs: a primary third-person view image ($I_{main}$), an optional wrist-mounted camera view ($I_{wrist}$), a natural language instruction ($L$), and the current robot state ($q$). The output is a sequence of continuous action commands ($\hat{A}_t$) over a horizon H.

#### 3.2. Semantic Feature Extraction

Qwen3-VL is employed as the backbone VLM due to its capabilities in image-text alignment and long-context modeling. The main image, optional wrist image, and instruction are tokenized and processed by the VLM encoder ($E_{vlm}$) to produce high-level semantic features ($\phi_{sem}$). These features capture semantic intent and object relationships but lack metric spatial details.

#### 3.3. Geometry-Enhanced Spatial Perception

To provide robust spatial awareness, a geometry module complements the semantic features.
- **Monocular 3D Structural Prior:** Dense 3D structural information from $I_{main}$ is extracted using the spatial encoder of VGGT, yielding geometric embeddings ($\phi_{vggt}$). VGGT, trained on large 3D datasets, implicitly encodes global spatial context.
- **Multi-view Latent Synthesis:** To address monocular depth ambiguity, complementary multi-view information is synthesized using the 6B LongCat-Image-Edit model. This synthesis occurs entirely in the latent space:
 1. The main view $I_{main}$ is encoded into a latent representation ($z_{main}$) by a pre-trained Variational Autoencoder (VAE) encoder ($E_{vae}$).
 2. A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) denoiser ($D_{dit}$), conditioned on $z_{main}$ and target view prompts (e.g., "left view," "right view"), generates latent representations for two novel virtual views ($z_l, z_r$).
 This latent-space operation improves computational efficiency (reduced resolution, fewer denoising steps) and robustness (avoids pixel-level artifacts of image generation). These latent features are pre-computed and cached.
- **Geometry-Guided Gated Transformer (G³T):** This module fuses $\phi_{vggt}$ and the synthesized multi-view latents ($z_l, z_r$) into a unified, geometrically consistent spatial embedding ($\phi_{geo}$).
 - **Cross-view Alignment:** $\phi_{vggt}$, $z_l$, and $z_r$ are projected into a shared latent space using linear projectors ($W_v, W_z$). These projected features are concatenated and processed by a Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) layer. This step enables interaction between monocular and multi-view features, resolving depth ambiguity and suppressing noise. The aligned features are denoted as $\phi'_{vggt}$, $z'_l$, $z'_r$.
 - **Adaptive Gated Fusion:** To handle occlusions, an adaptive gating mechanism is introduced. The aligned left and right features ($[z'_l; z'_r]$) are passed through an MLP and a Sigmoid activation to predict a scalar gate value ($G$) for each token. $G$ represents the confidence for the left view, and $(1-G)$ for the right. The fused multi-view feature ($z_{fused}$) is computed as an element-wise weighted sum: $z_{fused} = G \odot z'_l + (1-G) \odot z'_r$. This mechanism selectively aggregates information from reliable views.
 - **Geometric Consistency Refinement:** Finally, $\phi'_{vggt}$ and $z_{fused}$ are concatenated and passed through another MHSA layer to allow global interaction, producing the final geometry-enhanced spatial embedding ($\phi_{geo}$).

#### 3.4. Feature Fusion and Action Prediction

- **Feature Fusion:** The semantic features ($\phi_{sem}$) and the geometry-enhanced spatial embeddings ($\phi_{geo}$) are fused via a cross-attention mechanism. $\phi_{sem}$ forms the query, while $\phi_{geo}$ provides keys and values. This allows semantic context to dynamically attend to relevant geometric structures, creating a comprehensive multimodal representation ($\phi$).
- **Action Manifold Learning (AML):** The action expert, a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) denoted as $V_\theta$, directly predicts the clean action chunk ($\hat{A}_t$) instead of noise or velocity. This approach is based on the "Action Manifold Hypothesis."
 - **Input:** The multimodal feature $\phi_t$, the current robot state $q_t$, and a noisy action input $A^\tau_t = \tau A_t + (1-\tau)\epsilon$ (where $A_t$ is the ground-truth action chunk, $\tau$ is diffusion timestep, and $\epsilon$ is noise).
 - **Prediction:** $\hat{A}_t = V_\theta(\phi_t, A^\tau_t, q_t)$.
 - **Loss Function:** The network is optimized using a velocity-consistent loss, which is equivalent to minimizing a reweighted MSE between predicted and ground-truth actions: $L(\theta) = E_{\tau,\epsilon}[w(\tau)\|V_\theta(\phi_t, A^\tau_t, q_t) - A_t\|^2]$, where $w(\tau) = \frac{1}{(1-\tau)^2}$. This weighting adjusts learning signal strength across noise levels.
 - **Inference:** Actions are generated by solving an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) defined by the learned velocity field. Starting from pure noise $A^0_t \sim N(0, I)$, iterative denoising over N steps predicts $\hat{A}_t$, computes instantaneous flow velocity $\hat{v}$, and updates the action state using a numerical integrator (e.g., Euler method).

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The model is built on StarVLA codebase and fine-tuned from. AdamW optimizer is used. The VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL 4B) and DiT action expert have different learning rates. Training is conducted on 4 NVIDIA H20 GPUs for 30K steps (approx. 27 hours) with a batch size of 16 per GPU (bfloat16). Input images are resized to 224x224. Inference uses 4-step action denoising.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive evaluations across simulation and real-world environments to validate the proposed framework.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

The method was benchmarked on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and RoboTwin 2.0.
- **LIBERO:** Achieved an average success rate of 98.6%, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods in policy generalization across spatial, object, goal, and long-horizon suites (Table 2).
- **LIBERO-Plus:** Demonstrated superior zero-shot robustness with an 85.7% success rate under diverse environmental perturbations (camera viewpoints, lighting, background, noise). This performance surpassed all baselines, indicating resilience to unseen conditions (Table 1).
- **RoboTwin 2.0:** Attained an average success rate of 85.18% in clean settings and 86.06% in randomized settings for complex bi-manual manipulation tasks, significantly outperforming baselines like X-VLA and π⁰.⁵ (Table 3). This validates its capability in complex, multi-arm coordination under domain randomization.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **VLM Feature Interaction:** Experiments on LIBERO-Plus indicated that using features from the last layer of the VLM with direct conditioning yielded the best performance compared to intermediate layers or learnable action queries (Table 4).
- **Feature Fusion Strategy:** Direct cross-attention for fusing semantic VLM embeddings with spatial features was found to be more effective than simple concatenation or Q-Former mechanisms, demonstrating better alignment of cross-modal representations (Table 5).
- **G³T Fusion Strategy:** The proposed G³T module, incorporating adaptive gating based on geometric consistency, achieved the highest success rate (77.9%) on LIBERO-Plus compared to static fusion methods (e.g., concatenation, standard cross-attention), affirming the necessity of dynamic feature aggregation for handling occlusion and view ambiguity (Table 6).
- **Impact of Key Components:** A stepwise ablation on LIBERO-Plus confirmed the individual contribution of each novel component (Table 7). The baseline (monocular features, GR00T-N1 action head) improved sequentially with the addition of:
 1. VGGT geometric prior (from 66.4% to 71.1%).
 2. Multi-view features from LongCat-Image-Edit (from 71.1% to 77.9%).
 3. Action Manifold Learning (AML) module (from 77.9% to 85.7%).
 The full framework, integrating G³T and AML, achieved the highest success rate.

#### 4.3. Analysis of G³T

- **Depth Estimation:** A controlled experiment comparing standard monocular VGGT features against G³T's fused features (with a DPT head) on LIBERO demonstrated that G³T significantly improved depth estimation accuracy. It yielded lower Absolute Relative Error (AbsRel), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and Log10 RMSE, and higher accuracy thresholds (e.g., δ < 1.25 increased from 0.0265 to 0.0791), indicating more robust depth estimation and resolution of monocular ambiguity (Table 8, Figure 5).
- **Adaptive Gating Visualization:** Visualizations of the G³T gate maps (Figure 6) confirmed that the gating mechanism adaptively highlights reliable geometric structures (e.g., object boundaries, distinct textures) and suppresses regions with severe occlusion, motion blur, or synthesis artifacts, thereby constructing robust geometric representations.

#### 4.4. Robustness to Environmental Perturbations

The method exhibited minimal performance degradation on LIBERO-Plus when subjected to various input perturbations. Compared to baselines, it achieved the highest original performance with the lowest average degradation (12.9%), particularly in Spatial tasks (8.0% degradation), suggesting that the geometric-aware architecture effectively anchors policy decisions to stable 3D structures (Table 9).

#### 4.5. Efficiency of Action Manifold Learning (AML)

Compared to the GR00T baseline on LIBERO-Plus (under identical VLM and action expert configurations), AML consistently outperformed GR00T. AML maintained robust performance with minimal denoising steps (e.g., 2 steps) and showed more graceful degradation with increasing action chunk sizes, indicating a more structured optimization landscape and superior efficiency for high-dimensional temporal predictions (Table 10).

#### 4.6. Real-world Experiment

Using a Franka Emika Panda arm, the method was evaluated on four manipulation tasks (stack block, insert cube, place cylinder, place cup). A single unified model trained on 20 demonstration episodes per task achieved higher success rates across all tasks compared to fine-tuned OpenVLA-OFT and π⁰ baselines (e.g., 70% vs. 60% for stack block, 60% vs. 40% for insert cube) (Table 11, Figure 8). This demonstrated its effectiveness in real-world deployment.

#### 4.7. Zero-shot Generalization

The model was tested on unseen tasks by modifying object attributes (colors) in language instructions, evaluated in both "Clean Context" (only target objects) and "Cluttered Context" (with distractors).
- **Clean Context:** Achieved higher success rates compared to baselines (Table 12), indicating compositional reasoning and generalization to new semantic concepts.
- **Cluttered Context:** Also outperformed baselines (Table 13), demonstrating robustness against visual interference and the ability to attend to correct objects amidst clutter (Figure 9, Figure 10).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for VLA models.

Firstly, the development of the **Geometry-Guided Gated Transformer (G³T)** offers a method for enhancing 3D spatial perception from monocular RGB input. By synthesizing multi-view latent representations and adaptively fusing them with monocular geometric priors, G³T addresses the inherent depth ambiguity and occlusion issues without requiring additional depth sensing hardware. This capability can improve the precision and reliability of VLA systems in environments where multiple views or depth sensors are not feasible, thus expanding the practical deployment scenarios for such robots.

Secondly, the introduction of **Action Manifold Learning (AML)** represents a shift in generative action prediction paradigms. By directly predicting clean action chunks on an hypothesized low-dimensional action manifold, AML aims to reduce the optimization burden associated with regressing abstract noise or velocity targets. This approach is demonstrated to lead to more efficient and robust action learning, especially in high-dimensional action spaces. Its ability to maintain performance with fewer denoising steps and show graceful degradation with larger action chunks suggests potential for more stable and responsive control in complex multi-joint or multi-arm robotic systems.

Collectively, these innovations contribute to the robustness and generalization capabilities of VLA models. The extensive evaluations on challenging benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world experiments indicate that the proposed framework consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of success rate and resilience to environmental perturbations and unseen task variations. This level of performance, particularly in zero-shot generalization and cluttered environments, suggests that the method can enable robotic agents to operate more effectively in complex and dynamic real-world settings.

The potential impact of this work includes facilitating the development of more capable and adaptable embodied agents that can perform precise manipulation tasks based on high-level language instructions. By reducing reliance on specialized hardware for 3D perception and improving the efficiency of policy learning, this research can contribute to broader accessibility and scalability of advanced robotic manipulation systems.

A noted limitation is the computational overhead of the multi-view diffusion model during inference, which currently hinders real-time responsiveness for high-frequency control. Future work on distilling the geometric reasoning capabilities directly into the VLA backbone could address this, potentially leading to even more practical applications.
