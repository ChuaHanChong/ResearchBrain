# TRACE: A Self-Improving Framework for Robot Behavior Forecasting with Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> TRACE introduces a self-improving framework for robot behavior forecasting, leveraging Vision-Language Models, a counterfactual critic, and a world model to iteratively generate a comprehensive set of plausible future trajectories from sparse observations. This method significantly expands the hypothesis space, identifying critical edge-case behaviors, and enables VLMs to implicitly self-improve during inference, increasing valid hypothesis generation by 31.8%.

## Problem

- Accurately forecasting robot behavior with sparse or intermittent observations is challenging, making it difficult to infer intentions and actions.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often provide conservative predictions, overlooking crucial "edge cases" or low-probability but physically plausible maneuvers.
- Current VLM applications in robotics lack mechanisms for iterative refinement and self-improvement during inference, hindering their adaptability to dynamic environments.

## Method

- An iterative framework (**TRACE**) integrates a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for initial hypothesis generation via ==Tree-of-Thought== reasoning.
- A Counterfactual Critic systematically perturbs VLM-generated trajectories to explore overlooked "edge cases," ensuring both feasibility and meaningful divergence from original predictions.
- A World Model enforces physical, kinematic, and domain-specific rules, while feedback from identified feasible and infeasible trajectories drives implicit VLM self-improvement during inference.

## Results

- TRACE achieved an average coverage ratio of 83-**93%** for ground-truth feasible trajectories across diverse scenarios, significantly outperforming baseline methods like GIoT (57-**64%**).
- The framework successfully identified crucial edge-case trajectories, such as complex maritime overtaking maneuvers and an unexpected "continue straight" option in a road turning scenario, which baselines failed to predict.
- The VLM component demonstrated inference-time self-improvement, with its autonomous generation of valid trajectories increasing by **31.8%** and its generation of invalid trajectories decreasing from approximately 25-**26%** to 7-**9%** over successive updates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematic counterfactual exploration significantly expands the space of plausible future behaviors, successfully identifying rare but critical "edge-case" maneuvers overlooked by conservative VLM predictions.
> - Vision-Language Models exhibit emergent self-improvement during inference, implicitly learning from feedback on feasible counterfactuals and world model rejections without requiring explicit parameter updates.
> - Integrating a domain-aware World Model provides crucial physical grounding, allowing the VLM to internalize kinematic and regulatory constraints, thereby reducing the generation of infeasible trajectories over time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the critical and long-standing problem of **robot behavior forecasting**, a cornerstone for safe and effective autonomous system operation. Predicting the near-future actions of co-located agents is essential for an observer robot to plan its own movements, adapt to dynamic environments, and intervene when necessary.

Historically, behavior hypothesis generation methods have fallen into several categories:

- **Classical Motion Models:** These rely on pre-defined dynamics or carefully hand-tuned rules (e.g.,). While effective in well-defined, static scenarios, they struggle with variability, complex agent intentions, and situations where behaviors deviate from simplistic assumptions, especially under sparse observations.
- **Data-Driven Approaches:** Leveraging large, task-specific datasets, these methods (e.g.,) use machine learning, including generative adversarial networks (GANs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and graph neural networks (GNNs), to learn patterns from observed behaviors. Their limitation lies in their reliance on extensive data and potential failure in scenarios underrepresented in training or when observations are minimal.
- **Model-Based Approaches:** These explicitly model agent decision-making through techniques like Reinforcement Learning (RL) (e.g.,), Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) (e.g.,), or probabilistic graphical models (e.g.,). While offering deeper insight into agent intentions, they demand precise dynamic models, can be computationally intensive, and may still struggle with unexpected maneuvers or when objectives are unknown, especially with sparse observations.
- **Formal Methods (e.g., Reachability Analysis):** These provide strong theoretical guarantees (e.g.,) but are typically computationally expensive, require precise dynamic models, and are difficult to apply to systems with complex, rule-based behaviors.

More recently, **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** have emerged as a promising avenue (e.g.,). VLMs excel at integrating textual domain knowledge (like navigation rules or common-sense reasoning) with visual cues, allowing for a richer contextual understanding. However, the paper identifies a crucial gap in current VLM applications for robotics:

1. **One-shot, Conservative Predictions:** VLMs typically generate hypotheses in a single inference step, often converging towards "typical" or "most likely" outcomes (e.g.,). This conservative nature means they tend to overlook crucial "edge cases" or unusual but physically plausible maneuvers that are vital for robust planning in safety-critical domains.
2. **Lack of Iterative Refinement:** Existing VLM-based approaches often operate in an open-loop fashion, lacking mechanisms for systematic iterative refinement or explicit feedback to improve their predictions over time (e.g.,).

**TRACE** positions itself directly at the intersection of VLMs, reasoning, and counterfactual analysis, specifically to bridge this gap. It represents a significant advancement by moving beyond one-shot VLM predictions and rigid model-based approaches. By introducing an iterative, self-improving framework, TRACE aims to leverage the strengths of VLMs (contextual understanding, multimodal reasoning) while systematically addressing their limitations (conservatism, lack of iterative refinement) in the challenging context of sparse observations. This work contributes to the growing trend of integrating large foundation models into robotics, not as static predictors, but as dynamic, reasoning components within self-correcting systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for TRACE stems from the critical need for robust behavior forecasting in robotic systems, particularly when confronted with **sparse or intermittent observations** of other agents. This real-world challenge—arising from sensor occlusions, range limits, communication bandwidth constraints, or stealth requirements—makes accurate prediction incredibly difficult.

The paper outlines two primary challenges that TRACE aims to address:

1. **Uncertainty from Sparse, Partial Observations:** How to generate accurate and comprehensive behavior hypotheses when sensor data about a target agent is minimal and discontinuous. In such settings, inferring the target's intentions and potential actions requires drawing heavily on contextual cues, domain rules, and environmental constraints.
2. **Extending VLM Predictions Beyond Conservative Outcomes:** While Vision-Language Models show promise for their contextual understanding, their tendency to produce "typical" or "most likely" behaviors means they often miss important "edge cases"—physically plausible but rare or unusual maneuvers. Overlooking these edge cases can have severe consequences for the safety and effectiveness of an autonomous system's planning.

Based on these challenges, the **key objectives** of TRACE are:

- To develop an inference framework that can systematically generate a **comprehensive set of plausible future trajectories** (behavior hypotheses) for a target agent, even with minimal sensor data.
- To ensure these generated trajectories **respect feasibility** (physically plausible according to a world model) and **maintain consistency** (align with sparse observations and domain knowledge).
- Crucially, to **capture heterogeneity**, meaning the framework must include multiple plausible trajectories, encompassing not just the most common paths but also **rare, low-probability, or borderline maneuvers** that an autonomous system must still account for.
- To enable **self-improvement** in VLMs during inference, allowing them to progressively internalize domain constraints and identify edge cases without requiring explicit fine-tuning or parameter updates.

The overarching question guiding this research is: "Can systematically stress-testing VLM outputs through counterfactual 'what-if' variants uncover low-probability yet feasible behavior hypotheses that would otherwise be overlooked?" TRACE is motivated to answer this in the affirmative, aiming to create a more robust and adaptable forecasting system for real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TRACE (Tree-of-thought Reasoning And Counterfactual Exploration) is an iterative inference framework designed to enhance VLM-based behavior forecasting. It operates through a self-improving cycle involving three core components: a Vision-Language Model, a Counterfactual Critic, and a World Model.

**A. Vision-Language Tree-of-Thought Inference:**
The **Vision-Language Model (VLM)** forms the core of hypothesis generation. When a new, sparse observation Ω(t) of the target agent arrives, it is transformed into an approximate position bs(t) on an environment map (env). This annotated map, along with textual domain constraints (C) (e.g., navigation rules), serves as contextual side-information for the VLM.

- **Tree-of-Thought Generation:** Instead of a single prediction, the VLM is prompted to generate a branching "tree-of-thought." It proposes a set of short-horizon next states (es₁..., esₖ) for the target agent, spanning several future time steps (e.g., t+1 to t+Δ). This process is repeated to a certain depth, creating a tree of partial trajectories. This approach naturally retains multiple lines of progression, moving beyond single-path predictions. The VLM leverages its learned representations to infer basic feasibility from the map layout, avoiding obvious obstacles or restricted zones. The paper uses **GPT-4 Turbo with Vision** via the OpenAI API, highlighting the framework's compatibility with off-the-shelf VLMs.

**B. Counterfactual Critic:**
A key insight of TRACE is that even tree-of-thought generation by a VLM can be conservative. The **Counterfactual Critic (Nϕ)** addresses this by systematically exploring variations around the VLM's initial hypotheses to uncover overlooked edge cases.

- **Mechanism:** For each partial trajectory (Ξ) proposed by the VLM, the critic generates a set of incremental state offsets (δ₁..., δΔ). These perturbations create a "counterfactual trajectory" (Ξ*) by slightly altering the VLM's proposed path.
- **Input Representation:** The critic takes two inputs: (i) an embedded representation of the VLM's proposed trajectory (Local Trajectory Context), and (ii) an embedding of the local environment around the trajectory, combined with a latent representation of the textual constraints (Environment Embedding). These are integrated via cross-attention to generate contextually relevant offsets.
- **Training Criterion:** The critic network Nϕ is trained with a loss function that balances two objectives:
 - **Feasibility (L_feas):** Penalizes counterfactual trajectories (Ξ*) that are physically impossible or contradict sensor data, as verified by the world model.
 - **Divergence (L_div):** Rewards meaningful differences between the counterfactual (Ξ*) and the VLM's original trajectory (Ξ). This ensures the critic generates novel, non-trivial alternatives.
- **Integration:** Feasible counterfactual trajectories are incorporated into the existing tree structure, effectively expanding the hypothesis space.

**C. Integration into the Iterative Reasoning Loop (Self-Improvement):**
The core of TRACE's self-improvement lies in systematically feeding back information from the critic and world model into the VLM's subsequent reasoning steps.

- **Feedback Mechanism:** After world model verification, each trajectory node (original VLM hypothesis or counterfactual) is categorized: feasible hypothesis, physically implausible path, or overlooked edge case. These examples, particularly the identified edge cases and rejected infeasible paths, become part of the VLM's context window for subsequent prompts.
- **Inference-Time Learning:** Unlike traditional fine-tuning, the VLM's "learning" happens during inference without parameter updates. By being explicitly exposed to both successful hypotheses (including novel edge cases) and failure modes (infeasible paths), the VLM implicitly internalizes domain constraints and a broader range of behavioral patterns. This allows it to generate more comprehensive and valid hypotheses in later iterations.
- **Dynamic Cycle:** This self-improving cycle continues until new sensor data arrives, at which point inconsistent branches are pruned, and the process restarts with updated observations. The VLM's growing capability leads to a compounding benefit: later iterations require fewer counterfactual corrections as the model learns to anticipate common and previously identified edge-case behaviors.

**D. World Model Implementation:**
The **World Model** acts as a domain-aware filter, enforcing physical, kinematic, and regulatory constraints. It consists of two components:

- **Kinematic Feasibility Module:** Verifies if proposed state transitions adhere to the agent's motion limits (e.g., max velocity, turning radius, acceleration bounds).
- **Domain Compliance Validator:** Checks for violations of domain-specific rules (C), such as collisions with obstacles, restricted zones, or navigation regulations (e.g., COLREGs for marine scenarios, traffic rules for ground vehicles).

These modules collectively define a binary feasibility function F (1 for valid, 0 for invalid), pruning infeasible branches and ensuring that only physically and legally plausible trajectories are considered.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of TRACE was conducted across diverse robotic navigation settings, including real-world marine autonomous surface vehicles (ASVs) and ground-vehicle simulations. The goal was to predict target agent trajectories under sparse observation.

**Key Finding 1: Counterfactual Exploration Significantly Expands the Behavioral Hypothesis Space.**
TRACE consistently and substantially outperformed all baseline methods (Chain-of-Thought (CoT) VLM, Guided Iteration of Thought (GIoT) VLM, and Tree-of-Thought (ToT) VLM) in terms of **coverage ratio**—the proportion of ground-truth feasible trajectories correctly identified.

- TRACE achieved an average coverage of **83-93%** across all scenarios.
- The next-best baseline, GIoT, managed **57-64%** coverage.
- This significant improvement (e.g., 32.2 percentage points over GIoT in the complex T3 maritime crossing scenario) demonstrates the effectiveness of TRACE's systematic exploration through counterfactual reasoning. The structured nature of road environments (T4, T5) led to slightly higher coverage for all methods compared to the less-constrained maritime environments (T1, T2, T3), highlighting the increased difficulty of prediction in broader action spaces.

**Key Finding 2: Iterative Tree-of-Thought Expansion Reveals Rare but Critical Maneuvers.**
Beyond quantitative coverage, TRACE demonstrated a superior ability to identify **edge-case trajectories**—valid maneuvers that deviate from typical expectations but remain consistent with environmental constraints and sparse observations.

- **Qualitative Examples:** In a maritime overtaking scenario (T2), TRACE identified unusual but legal passing trajectories, such as momentary accelerations before yielding or S-curve maneuvers, which were missed by baselines.
- **Safety-Critical Insight:** In a road right-turn decision point scenario (T5), where the target vehicle's positioning suggested an imminent right turn, TRACE uniquely identified that the target could legitimately continue straight by making a minor heading adjustment. Baselines, including GIoT and CoT, predominantly predicted only right-turn variations. This capability is crucial for safety-critical planning, as it prevents over-reliance on seemingly obvious intentions.

**Key Finding 3: VLM's Outputs Self-Improve Through Iterative Counterfactual Exposure.**
A remarkable discovery was the **emergent self-improvement of the VLM component** within TRACE, even without explicit fine-tuning or parameter updates.

- **Increased Diversity:** Analyzing the VLM's Tree-of-Thought output *before* counterfactual expansions, the number of valid trajectories generated solely by the VLM showed a consistent upward trend over successive measurement updates. By the fifth measurement update (M5), the VLM's autonomous generation capacity increased by **31.8%** across maritime scenarios.
- **Implicit Learning:** This indicates that the VLM implicitly "learns" from the counterfactual feedback from previous iterations. When the critic identifies and validates edge cases, these become part of the VLM's context for subsequent reasoning, leading it to independently propose similar diverse behaviors over time.

**Key Finding 4: Iterative World Model Feedback Teaches VLMs to Reduce Invalid Trajectories.**
Complementing the increase in valid trajectories, TRACE also demonstrated a significant **reduction in the generation of invalid trajectories** by the VLM over successive measurement updates.

- **Reduced Infeasibility:** The percentage of generated trajectories rejected by the world model (due to constraint violations or physical infeasibility) consistently decreased. For instance, in maritime tasks, the invalid rate dropped from an average of ~25-26% at M1 to ~7-9% by M5.
- **Internalized Constraints:** This shows that the negative feedback from the world model (rejecting impossible maneuvers) is also implicitly internalized by the VLM. The VLM gradually learns the domain's physical and regulatory boundaries, leading it to avoid proposing maneuvers that violate kinematic or environmental rules. This enhances computational efficiency by reducing wasted effort on infeasible predictions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TRACE framework represents a significant advancement in robot behavior forecasting and the broader application of foundation models in robotics, offering several critical implications and potential impacts:

1. **Transforming VLMs into Dynamic Reasoning Systems:** TRACE shifts the paradigm of integrating Vision-Language Models into robotics. Instead of treating VLMs as static, one-shot prediction engines, it demonstrates their potential as dynamic, self-correcting reasoning components. This iterative feedback mechanism allows VLMs to evolve their capabilities during inference, which is particularly valuable in dynamic, uncertain real-world environments.

2. **Enhanced Robustness for Safety-Critical Applications:** The ability to systematically uncover edge cases and rare but feasible maneuvers is paramount for safety-critical domains like autonomous driving, marine navigation, and aerial robotics. Traditional VLM approaches' conservative predictions can be dangerous if they fail to anticipate an unexpected but lawful action by another agent. TRACE's comprehensive hypothesis generation significantly improves the robustness and reliability of an autonomous system's understanding of its environment.

3. **Inference-Time Self-Improvement: A Novel Learning Paradigm:** The demonstration that VLMs can implicitly "learn" and adapt their output quality through iterative counterfactual exposure, without requiring explicit model fine-tuning or parameter updates, is a profound contribution. This "inference-time learning" is highly advantageous in operational settings where continuous model retraining is impractical or resource-intensive. It suggests a future where foundation models can adapt to specific scenarios and agent behaviors over extended periods, enhancing their domain awareness on the fly.

4. **Addressing Foundation Model Limitations in Robotics:** Foundation models, including VLMs, often struggle with grounding in physical reality, avoiding "hallucinations," and adapting to novel, unseen scenarios without retraining. TRACE directly addresses these limitations by integrating a robust world model for feasibility checks and using counterfactuals to expose the VLM to the boundaries of physical possibility and regulatory compliance. This makes VLMs more reliable and trustworthy for robotic applications.

5. **Platform-Agnostic and Generalizable Framework:** By demonstrating its utility across diverse robotic navigation settings—from marine ASVs operating under complex COLREGs to ground vehicles in urban simulations—TRACE showcases its platform-agnostic nature. The core framework can be applied to various robotic systems, requiring only domain-specific world model implementations, thereby increasing its broad applicability.

6. **Improved Efficiency and Computational Resource Management:** The significant reduction in the generation of invalid trajectories over successive iterations directly translates to improved computational efficiency. By learning to avoid impossible maneuvers, TRACE allocates more processing power towards exploring the valid and critical hypothesis space, making real-time prediction more viable in resource-constrained environments.

In conclusion, TRACE represents a substantial step forward in developing intelligent, adaptable, and safe autonomous systems. By enabling VLMs to reason more comprehensively and self-improve through structured counterfactual exploration, it lays a foundation for future robotics that can reliably navigate complex, dynamic environments with minimal and intermittent information, ultimately leading to safer and more capable autonomous operations.
