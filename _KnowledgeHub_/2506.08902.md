# Intention-Conditioned Flow Occupancy Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Intention-Conditioned Flow Occupancy Models (InFOM) enables pre-training of RL agents to learn long-term temporal dynamics and diverse user intentions from offline data. InFOM achieves a 1.8x median improvement in returns and a 36% increase in success rates on various benchmarks by using flow matching for future state prediction and implicit generalized policy improvement for fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Applying large-scale pre-training paradigms to Reinforcement Learning (RL) is challenging due to long-term temporal dependencies and compounding prediction errors in existing world models.
- Large RL datasets are often heterogeneous, containing data from multiple users/tasks with diverse "intentions," which traditional pre-training methods typically do not model explicitly.
- Existing occupancy models, while effective for future event prediction, face training complexity and typically do not account for underlying user intentions, limiting adaptability.

## Method

- **InFOM** introduces a probabilistic model that predicts future states (an ==occupancy measure==) while explicitly conditioning on a latent variable representing user intention, inferred via a variational encoder.
- It leverages flow matching, a generative AI technique, to learn a time-dependent vector field for future state prediction, combined with a SARSA-variant Temporal Difference (TD) flow matching loss.
- During fine-tuning, InFOM estimates intention-conditioned Q-values and uses an implicit Generalized Policy Improvement (GPI) via an upper expectile loss to distill a scalar Q-function for robust policy optimization.

## Results

- InFOM achieved a **1.8x** median improvement in returns and a **36%** increase in success rates compared to eight state-of-the-art baselines across 40 RL tasks, including both state-based and image-based environments.
- The method demonstrated superior performance on challenging tasks with high-dimensional state spaces and sparse rewards (e.g., **20x** improvement on the `jaco` domain) and exhibited faster policy learning during fine-tuning.
- Visualizations confirmed that InFOM successfully infers distinct, clustered latent intentions aligned with underlying ground-truth behaviors, indicating a more effective mechanism for disentangling intentions compared to alternatives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning future state predictions on inferred latent intentions allows for effective disentanglement of diverse behaviors within heterogeneous offline datasets, enhancing adaptability to new tasks.
> - Combining flow matching with a SARSA-style temporal difference loss provides an expressive and stable way to model multimodal future state distributions and long-horizon temporal dependencies, improving temporal reasoning.
> - An implicit Generalized Policy Improvement (GPI) strategy, implemented with an expectile loss, offers a stable and computationally efficient method for policy extraction from continuous intention spaces, outperforming explicit GPI.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a core challenge in applying large-scale pre-training paradigms to Reinforcement Learning (RL), a methodology that has transformed fields such as computer vision and natural language processing. In these other domains, large foundation models are pre-trained on vast datasets and subsequently adapted or fine-tuned for specific downstream tasks. Applying this framework to RL is attractive due to its potential to enhance sample efficiency and robustness. However, RL introduces complexities primarily due to the long-term temporal dependencies of actions and the often heterogeneous nature of large-scale datasets, which are typically collected by multiple users performing distinct tasks.

Current attempts to build foundation models for RL often focus on predicting actions within the pre-training dataset, potentially overlooking the importance of temporal reasoning and user intentions. For instance, approaches based on world models, while effective for sample efficiency and generalization, exhibit limitations in long-horizon reasoning due to compounding prediction errors. Another class of methods, occupancy models (also known as successor representations), demonstrates strong performance in predicting future events but traditionally faces challenges in training complexity and typically does not account for underlying user intentions.

Recent advancements in generative AI, including flow matching and diffusion models, have provided new tools for modeling complex distributions. This paper integrates these generative modeling capabilities into the RL pre-training framework. Specifically, it builds upon previous work on variational inference and successor representations, but introduces a novel probabilistic model that predicts future states (an occupancy measure) while explicitly conditioning on a latent variable representing user intention. This approach differentiates itself from existing flow-matching-based occupancy models, such as those by Farebrother et al. (2025), by employing a latent variable model for intentions and utilizing an expectile loss for implicit Generalized Policy Improvement (GPI), rather than relying on forward-backward representations and explicit GPI over a finite set of intentions. This integration aims to create a more expressive model capable of handling the temporal and intentional complexities inherent in large, diverse RL datasets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to adapt the successful pre-training and fine-tuning paradigm, prevalent in other machine learning domains, to the field of Reinforcement Learning. This overarching goal is motivated by the potential to enhance sample efficiency and robustness in RL by leveraging large, diverse datasets.

The authors identify two fundamental challenges that hinder the direct application of foundation models in RL:
1. **Long-term Temporal Dependencies:** Effective RL agents must reason about the distant, long-term consequences of current actions. Existing world models, while powerful, often struggle with accurately predicting outcomes over extended horizons due to the accumulation of prediction errors. This limitation restricts their capacity for long-horizon planning and decision-making.
2. **Heterogeneity of Data and User Intentions:** Large datasets for RL are frequently amassed from diverse sources, such as multiple users or agents performing various tasks. These datasets inherently encode a spectrum of "intentions" or task objectives. Traditional occupancy models and other pre-training approaches in RL typically do not explicitly model or infer these latent intentions, limiting their adaptability and transferability to new, unseen tasks.

To address these challenges, the paper sets forth several specific objectives for its proposed method, InFOM:
- **Develop a Probabilistic Model for Future State Prediction:** The method aims to construct a probabilistic model capable of predicting which states an agent will visit in the temporally distant future (i.e., an occupancy measure). This model is designed to facilitate robust long-term temporal reasoning.
- **Incorporate Latent Variables for User Intention:** A key objective is to integrate a latent variable into the model to capture and infer the implicit user intentions embedded within heterogeneous, reward-free offline datasets. This latent variable is hypothesized to augment the model's expressivity and enable more effective adaptation to specific downstream tasks.
- **Leverage Flow Matching for Complex Distribution Modeling:** The work aims to employ flow matching, a recent advancement in generative AI, to train these occupancy models. Flow matching is selected for its capacity to model highly complex and potentially multimodal distributions of future states, while offering stable learning objectives and potentially faster inference compared to alternative generative models like diffusion models.
- **Enable Efficient Policy Adaptation:** The ultimate goal is to allow for efficient policy extraction and fine-tuning for specific downstream tasks. This involves utilizing the pre-trained intention-conditioned flow occupancy models for generative value estimation and then deriving a policy through a robust generalized policy improvement procedure.

By simultaneously modeling both temporal dependencies through occupancy measures and diverse user intentions via latent variables, InFOM is motivated to overcome the limitations of current RL foundation model research, thereby creating more adaptable and high-performing agents that can learn from unstructured, real-world data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Intention-Conditioned Flow Occupancy Models (InFOM), is structured into two main phases: pre-training and fine-tuning, aiming to simultaneously learn temporal dynamics and user intentions from offline datasets.

**Problem Setting:**
The framework operates with an unlabeled (reward-free) dataset `D` for pre-training, which is assumed to be collected by a behavioral policy `β` composed of a mixture of policies from different users or tasks. During fine-tuning, a separate reward-labeled dataset `D_reward` is provided for a specific downstream task. A crucial assumption, termed "Consistency," states that consecutive transitions `(s, a)` and `(s', a')` within the dataset share the same latent intention `z`. This simplifies the inference of intentions and enables dynamic programming over trajectory segments.

**Pre-training Phase:**

1. **Variational Intention Inference:**
 The objective is to jointly learn an intention encoder `p_e(z | s', a')` and a generative occupancy model `q_d(s_f | s, a, z)`. The intention encoder infers a latent intention `z` from a subsequent state-action pair `(s', a')`, which aligns with the Consistency Assumption. The learning process maximizes an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) of the data likelihood, which can be interpreted as an information bottleneck where `(S', A')` predicts `Z` to predict `(S, A, S_f)`.
 The ELBO objective takes the form:
 `max_{p_e, q_d} E_{p_β(s,a,s_f,s',a')} [E_{p_e(z|s',a')} [log q_d(s_f | s, a, z)] - λD_KL(p_e(z | s', a') || p(z))]`
 Here, `p(z) = N(0, I)` is a standard Gaussian prior for the latent intention, and `λ ≥ 0` controls the strength of the KL divergence regularization. In practice, this optimization is achieved by minimizing a surrogate objective that incorporates a flow matching loss.

2. **Predicting the Future via SARSA Flows:**
 The generative occupancy model `q_d(s_f | s, a, z)` is learned using a SARSA variant of the Temporal Difference (TD) flow matching loss. This approach leverages the expressivity of flow matching for modeling multimodal distributions of future states and the combinatorial generalization capabilities of TD methods. The model represents the occupancy measure using a time-dependent vector field `v_d`.
 The SARSA flow loss is defined as:
 `L_SARSA flow(v_d, p_e) = (1 - γ)L_SARSA current flow(v_d, p_e) + γL_SARSA future flow(v_d, p_e)`
 - `L_SARSA current flow` aims to reconstruct the current state `s` by mapping a noise vector `ϵ` to `s`.
 - `L_SARSA future flow` bootstraps the vector field from the next state-action pair `(s', a')` and the inferred intention `z` to a target future state `s_f_bar`, using a Polyak-averaged target vector field `v_bar_d`. This loss propagates temporal information.
 The samples from `q_d` (i.e., `s_f`) are generated by numerically solving an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) defined by the learned vector field `v_d`, typically using the Euler method.

**Fine-tuning Phase:**

1. **Generative Value Estimation:**
 For a given downstream task with a reward function `r(·)`, InFOM estimates intention-conditioned Q-values, `Q_z(s, a)`. This is done by sampling `N` future states `s_f^(i)` from the pre-trained `q_d(s_f | s, a, z)` for a given state-action pair `(s, a)` and a sampled latent intention `z` (drawn from the prior `p(z)`). The Q-value is then computed as the average discounted reward of these sampled future states:
 `Q_z(s, a) = (1 / ((1 - γ)N)) * Σ_{i=1 to N} r(s_f^(i))`

2. **Implicit Generalized Policy Improvement (GPI):**
 To overcome the challenges of traditional GPI (e.g., instability of maximizing over continuous `z`, computational cost of backpropagating through ODEs), InFOM employs an "implicit" approach:
 - **Critic Learning:** An explicit scalar Q-function `Q(s, a)` is learned to distill the information from the intention-conditioned `Q_z(s, a)` estimates. This is achieved using an upper expectile loss `L_μ^2`, where `μ ∈ [0.5, 1)`. This loss implicitly performs a relaxed maximization over the infinite intention space, approaching the maximum Q-value as `μ` approaches 1.
 `L(Q) = E_{(s,a)~p_~β(s,a), z~p(z)} [L_μ^2(Q_z(s, a) - Q(s, a))]`
 - **Policy Optimization:** The policy `π(a | s)` is then optimized to maximize this distilled Q-function, regularized by a behavioral cloning term to mitigate out-of-distribution (OOD) action errors and value overestimation:
 `L(π) = -E_{(s,a)~p_~β(s,a), a_π~π(a_π|s)} [Q(s, a_π) + α log π(a | s)]`
 where `α` controls the regularization strength.

The entire framework utilizes neural networks (MLPs) to parameterize the intention encoder, vector field, reward predictor, critic, and policy. Asynchronous policy training and Polyak averaging for target networks are employed to stabilize learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate InFOM focused on its ability to leverage pre-training for downstream RL tasks, the efficacy of its intention inference mechanism, and the robustness of its policy extraction strategy.

#### 4.1. Comparison to Prior Pre-training and Fine-tuning Methods

InFOM was evaluated against eight baselines, including state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms (IQL, ReBRAC), representation learning methods (DINO + ReBRAC), model-based RL (MBPO + ReBRAC), contrastive RL (CRL + IS, TD InfoNCE + IS), and unsupervised skill learning approaches (FB + IQL, HILP + IQL). The evaluation covered 36 state-based and 4 image-based tasks from the ExORL and OGBench benchmarks.

- **Overall Performance:** InFOM generally matched or surpassed all baselines across a majority of the domains. It achieved a 1.8x median improvement in returns and a 36% increase in success rates compared to alternative methods.
- **ExORL Benchmarks:** On easier domains like cheetah, walker, and quadruped, InFOM performed comparably to baselines. However, on the more challenging `jaco` domain, characterized by a high-dimensional state space and sparse rewards, InFOM achieved a 20x improvement over the baselines. Ablation studies (Appendix F.6) indicated that providing dense rewards significantly improved baseline performance on `jaco` tasks, supporting the hypothesis that reward sparsity is a key challenge addressed by InFOM's approach.
- **OGBench Benchmarks:** InFOM demonstrated a 36% higher success rate on state-based manipulation tasks and outperformed the best baseline by 31% on image-based visual tasks. This was attributed to its ability to explore diverse regions of the state space via different intentions, which helps in tasks with semi-sparse reward functions.
- **Real Robotics Datasets:** Experiments on the RT-1 dataset for a `google robot pick coke can` task showed InFOM achieving a 34% improvement over the best baseline when trained on fixed image embeddings (Appendix F.1).
- **Convergence Speed (Appendix F.3):** InFOM exhibited faster policy learning during fine-tuning compared to baselines that primarily pre-train behavioral cloning policies or self-supervised representations. This suggests the effectiveness of extracting long-horizon temporal information using expressive generative models.

#### 4.2. Visualization of Latent Intentions

To assess whether InFOM successfully infers distinct user intentions, latent intentions were visualized using t-SNE on the `cube double task 1`. This was compared against intentions inferred by HILP + FOM and FB + FOM (which is equivalent to TD flows with GPI from Farebrother et al. (2025)).

- **Clustering:** InFOM produced clearly clustered latent intentions that aligned with underlying ground-truth behaviors (e.g., "pick" and "place" actions). In contrast, the comparison methods often showed mixed or less distinct clusters.
- **Predictive Accuracy and Downstream Performance (Appendix F.2):** InFOM achieved lower pairwise mean squared errors in future state prediction after pre-training and outperformed these alternative intention encoding mechanisms on 3 out of 4 fine-tuning tasks. This indicates that InFOM's variational intention encoder provides a more effective and simpler mechanism for disentangling intentions compared to methods relying on full unsupervised RL objectives.

#### 4.3. Importance of the Implicit Generalized Policy Improvement

Ablation studies investigated the impact of InFOM's implicit GPI strategy compared to alternative policy extraction methods.

- **Implicit GPI vs. Standard GPI:** InFOM, utilizing the implicit GPI with expectile loss, achieved 44% higher performance and 8x smaller variance compared to a variant using standard GPI (maximizing over a finite set of intentions). This highlights the robustness and efficiency of the implicit approach in handling a continuous intention space.
- **Implicit GPI vs. One-Step Policy Improvement:** InFOM significantly outperformed a variant (FOM + one-step PI) that removed intention conditioning from the flow occupancy models and used one-step policy improvement. This finding underscores the importance of inferring and conditioning on user intentions for successful policy optimization.

#### 4.4. Additional Findings from Appendix Experiments

- **Continuous vs. Discrete Intentions (Appendix F.4):** Using a continuous latent space for intentions generally led to better performance; a discrete intention space (VQ-VAE style) reduced performance significantly on some tasks.
- **Fine-tuning with Posterior Intentions (Appendix F.5):** Sampling latent intentions from the posterior distribution `q_d(z | s', a')` during fine-tuning did not substantially alter performance compared to sampling from the prior `p(z)`. This suggests InFOM's robustness to unseen latents and the sufficiency of the prior for providing learning signals.
- **Behavioral Cloning Regularization (Appendix F.7):** The behavioral cloning regularization in the policy loss was identified as an important component for InFOM's performance.
- **Dataset Size Effects (Appendix F.9):** Larger pre-training datasets generally yielded higher returns, indicating the benefit of diverse, reward-free data. Similarly, increasing the fine-tuning dataset size consistently improved success rates, likely due to enhanced reward prediction accuracy.
- **Suboptimal Datasets (Appendix F.10):** Fine-tuning on highly suboptimal datasets significantly degraded performance, emphasizing the importance of data quality for effective fine-tuning.
- **Number of Future States (N) (Appendix F.11):** Increasing the number of sampled future states (`N`) for Q-value estimation improved accuracy and reduced variance, leading to better final performance. `N=16` was selected as a balance between accuracy and computational cost.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity (Appendix F.12):** InFOM's performance was sensitive to the latent intention dimension `d`. The number of Euler steps `T` saturated at a certain threshold. The expectile `μ`, KL divergence coefficient `λ`, and behavioral cloning regularization `α` were identified as crucial hyperparameters, requiring domain-specific tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "INTENTION-CONDITIONED FLOW OCCUPANCY MODELS" contributes to the field of Reinforcement Learning by offering a novel framework for pre-training and fine-tuning that explicitly addresses two long-standing challenges: effective long-term temporal reasoning and the utilization of heterogeneous, multi-intent offline datasets.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Advancement in RL Foundation Models:** By integrating intention-conditioning with flow-based occupancy models, InFOM pushes the boundary for building more generalist RL agents. It bridges the gap between the successful pre-training paradigms in other machine learning fields and the unique complexities of RL, moving closer to the vision of "foundation models" for control.
- **Enhanced Data Utilization:** The ability to extract actionable information, including diverse user intentions and their corresponding long-term behaviors, from unstructured, reward-free offline datasets significantly enhances data utilization. This can reduce the reliance on costly and time-consuming online data collection and reward engineering.
- **Improved Performance and Robustness:** The demonstrated performance improvements (1.8x median return, 36% success rate increase) and increased robustness across a wide range of tasks, particularly in environments with high-dimensional state spaces or sparse rewards, underscore the practical utility of InFOM. The method's effectiveness on visual and real-robotics tasks further supports its applicability to complex, real-world scenarios.
- **Effective Latent Intention Discovery:** The capacity of InFOM to infer and disentangle latent user intentions from diverse data, as evidenced by clear clustering in visualizations, represents a substantial step toward more interpretable and adaptable policy learning. This can facilitate a deeper understanding of learned behaviors and potentially enable more intuitive task specification.
- **Leveraging Modern Generative AI:** The successful application of flow matching, a state-of-the-art generative modeling technique, for learning discounted state occupancy measures highlights the potential of such powerful tools in tackling complex RL problems. This approach enables flexible modeling of multimodal future state distributions while benefiting from stable learning and efficient sampling.
- **Robust Policy Extraction:** The introduction of implicit Generalized Policy Improvement via expectile distillation provides a stable and efficient mechanism for extracting policies from intention-conditioned models, overcoming common difficulties associated with explicit maximization over continuous latent spaces.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Generalist Robotics:** InFOM could significantly accelerate the development of generalist robots capable of learning from vast quantities of human demonstration data or diverse exploratory behaviors. Such robots could adapt more rapidly to new tasks with minimal additional task-specific supervision.
- **Efficient Transfer Learning:** The learned intention-conditioned occupancy models could serve as powerful building blocks for zero-shot or few-shot transfer learning. By inferring or adapting relevant intentions, agents might quickly generalize to novel tasks or environments.
- **Improved Exploration:** The explicit modeling of diverse intentions could inform intrinsically motivated exploration strategies, allowing agents to systematically cover a broader range of behaviors and environments.
- **Reduced Reward Engineering:** By learning rich, intention-aware representations of future dynamics, InFOM could potentially mitigate the arduous process of hand-crafting reward functions for new tasks, relying more on the inferred task structures from data.

#### 5.3. Limitations (as stated by authors)

The primary limitation noted by the authors is that reducing trajectories to consecutive state-action pairs for intention capture might not always perfectly represent the original, long-term intentions embedded in complex trajectories. They suggest that alternative, more sophisticated trajectory encoding techniques could be explored in future work to address this.
