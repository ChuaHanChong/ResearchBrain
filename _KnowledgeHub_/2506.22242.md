# 4D-VLA: Spatiotemporal Vision-Language-Action Pretraining with Cross-Scene Calibration

> [!summary] Summary
> The 4D-VLA framework enhances vision-language-action (VLA) pretraining by integrating 3D coordinate embeddings and multi-frame historical context to resolve issues of ambiguous robot positioning and latent state. This approach achieves a 12.1% higher average success rate compared to OpenVLA on LIBERO benchmarks and demonstrates robust generalization across diverse simulated and real-world robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Existing VLA pretraining paradigms often suffer from "coordinate system chaos," where single-image inputs lack sufficient spatial context to infer a robot's precise position and orientation, leading to dispersed action distributions.
- Models experience "state chaos" when single frames provide insufficient temporal or contextual cues, resulting in ambiguity for actions, such as symmetric trajectories or visually similar observations requiring different movements.
- The limitations of incomplete inputs hinder the efficient pretraining and broad generalization of VLA models across diverse robotic tasks and viewpoints.

## Method

- Integrates ==3D coordinate information== derived from depth data and camera poses into "==spatial vision tokens==," explicitly grounding visual features in a 3D space to resolve coordinate system ambiguity.
- Incorporates temporal dynamics by using an adaptive "==memory bank sampling (MBS)==" strategy to select informative historical frames, augmented with ==learnable temporal positional encodings==, to mitigate state chaos.
- Utilizes a large pretrained vision-language model (==InternVL-4B==) as a backbone, fine-tuned with a ==composite loss function== including translation, rotation, grip, and directional terms for robust action prediction.

## Results

- **4D-VLA** achieves an average **12.1%** higher success rate than OpenVLA on LIBERO simulation benchmarks, including a **25.4%** improvement on challenging long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-LONG).
- On the novel MV-Bench, 4D-VLA demonstrates superior spatial understanding, achieving an **81.0%** success rate in in-view settings (**28.8%** higher than OpenVLA) and strong generalization with **73.8%** in cross-view settings.
- Real-world evaluations using a Franka arm show the full 4D-VLA model achieving an **85.63%** average success rate, significantly outperforming OpenVLA (**27.70%**) in tasks requiring precise placement, instruction following, and robustness to distractors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly fusing 3D spatial coordinates with visual features, rather than relying solely on 2D images, is critical for achieving precise spatial grounding and mitigating coordinate system chaos in robot control.
> - Adaptive sampling of historical frames provides a more efficient and effective way to capture temporal context and disambiguate actions compared to uniform sampling or single-frame inputs.
> - Combining both 3D spatial information and multi-frame temporal context (4D representation) in a VLA pretraining framework leads to significantly improved generalization and robustness in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence, particularly in robotics, has seen rapid advancements driven by the integration of vision-language models (VLMs) and the availability of large, diverse robotic datasets. This paper positions itself within the broader context of developing generalizable robot policies through large-scale pretraining.

Historically, vision-language models like Flamingo, LLaVA, and BLIP-2 have focused on aligning textual instructions with static visual data, primarily images. More recently, the research community has expanded these models to handle multi-image inputs to capture temporal dynamics, and some have begun incorporating 3D information to bolster spatial reasoning (e.g., 3D-LLM, Scene-LLM). These developments aim to move beyond static understanding towards dynamic interaction and spatial awareness, crucial for robotic tasks.

In parallel, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a significant advancement, enabling robots to perform complex, interactive tasks by translating language instructions into physical actions. Early VLA approaches often predict trajectories directly (e.g., DiffusionPolicy) or infer current actions for closed-loop control. The open-sourcing of diverse robotic datasets like DROID and LIBERO has further accelerated data-driven robot control, allowing for pretraining on a scale previously unfeasible.

Despite these advancements, a critical challenge remains: efficiently extracting useful information from these diverse datasets to achieve broad generalization. Prior VLA pretraining paradigms, such as OpenVLA, commonly use limited inputs (e.g., a single RGB image and textual instruction). The authors argue that this incompleteness leads to two primary issues:
1. **Coordinate system chaos**: When robot actions are defined in the robot's local coordinate frame, a single image often lacks sufficient spatial context (e.g., the robot's base might be occluded), making it difficult to infer the robot's precise position and orientation. This results in a dispersed and high-variance conditional action distribution.
2. **State chaos**: A single frame often fails to provide necessary temporal or contextual cues, leading to ambiguity in actions. This can manifest in symmetric trajectories (unclear direction of motion) or visually similar observations requiring entirely different actions, hindering the learning of smooth and robust action distributions.

Existing methods have attempted to address parts of this problem. Octo incorporates historical context, acknowledging the importance of temporal information. HPT addresses heterogeneity across datasets by introducing dataset-specific parameters. However, 4D-VLA distinguishes itself by explicitly tackling the fundamental problem of *insufficient input context* through the integration of *4D spatiotemporal information*. It builds upon the idea of multi-image inputs and 3D-aware models but specifically unifies them within an action-centric pretraining framework to resolve these "chaos" issues, aiming for enhanced efficiency and generalization that prior models have overlooked or partially addressed. This positions 4D-VLA as a significant step towards more robust and generalizable embodied AI systems capable of operating in diverse and complex real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the 4D-VLA research is to develop a more efficient and generalizable pretraining framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling them to learn robust control policies from diverse robotic datasets. The core motivation stems from the inherent limitations of existing VLA pretraining paradigms, which often rely on incomplete or under-informative inputs, thereby hindering effective learning and generalization.

The authors identify two critical issues that result from these limited inputs:
1. **Coordinate System Chaos**: When robot actions are defined within the robot’s coordinate frame, but the visual input (e.g., a single RGB image) lacks sufficient spatial context (e.g., the robot’s base is occluded, as observed in 67% of DROID dataset samples), the model struggles to accurately infer the robot’s position and orientation relative to the scene. This ambiguity leads to a highly dispersed or non-smooth conditional action distribution, making it challenging for the model to learn reliable actions. The inability to align the robot's coordinate system with the scene's spatial context fundamentally undermines precise action inference.
2. **State Chaos**: A single snapshot often provides insufficient temporal or contextual cues to disambiguate actions. For instance, visually similar frames might correspond to vastly different actions, or symmetric trajectories might lack the necessary information to infer the correct direction of motion. This temporal and contextual ambiguity results in a conditional action distribution that is multimodal or exhibits high variance, significantly impeding the efficiency and effectiveness of pretraining.

To address these profound challenges, the key objectives of the 4D-VLA project are:
- **Mitigate Coordinate System Chaos**: By integrating 3D spatial information derived from depth data and camera poses, the model aims to explicitly align the robot’s coordinate system with the scene. This involves generating "spatial vision tokens" that are inherently 3D-aware, providing precise spatial grounding for action prediction.
- **Resolve State Chaos**: By incorporating temporal information through sequences of historical frames, the model seeks to provide richer contextual cues that disambiguate actions. This involves processing multi-frame inputs to enhance temporal reasoning and resolve ambiguities arising from single-frame observations.
- **Enhance Pretraining Efficiency and Generalization**: To make the integration of multi-frame information efficient, the authors aim to develop an adaptive frame sampling strategy that captures crucial historical context without introducing redundancy or excessive computational overhead.
- **Develop a Robust VLA Model**: The ultimate goal is to create a VLA model that exhibits strong spatiotemporal reasoning capabilities, leading to superior performance in both simulated and real-world robotic tasks, with improved generalization to novel scenarios and viewpoints.
- **Introduce a New Benchmark**: To thoroughly evaluate the spatial understanding and generalization capabilities of VLA models, the paper aims to introduce "MV-Bench," a multi-view simulation dataset designed to test performance across diverse viewpoints.

These objectives are motivated by the fundamental belief that robust action inference in diverse and complex robotic environments necessitates a richer, more complete input representation that accounts for both spatial and temporal dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The 4D-VLA framework is meticulously designed to integrate 4D spatiotemporal information into the input of a large vision-language model, thereby mitigating the identified challenges of coordinate system and state chaos. The methodology involves several innovative components:

#### 3.1. Vision-Language Model Backbone and Problem Definition

The model leverages a pretrained large vision-language model (VLM) as its backbone, specifically InternVL-4B. This backbone comprises a text tokenizer (T), a vision encoder (E), an MLP projector (P), and a Transformer decoder (D). The VLM backbone provides a robust foundation for aligning visual and linguistic features.
The VLA task is defined as a low-level control policy problem: given a language instruction and current observations (sequential RGB-D images), the model predicts an action for the robot. The action consists of three components: ∆x ∈ R^3 (translation offset), ∆θ ∈ R^3 (rotation offset), and g ∈ (gripper open/close state).

#### 3.2. Spatial-Aware Visual Tokens

To address coordinate system chaos and enhance spatial perception, 4D-VLA fuses 3D coordinate information derived from depth with traditional vision tokens.
1. **Feature Extraction**: An input RGB image $I \in R^{3 \times h \times w}$ is processed by the vision encoder $E$ to produce a feature map $f_v \in R^{k \times \frac{h}{c} \times \frac{w}{c}}$.
2. **3D Coordinate Generation**: A corresponding downsampled depth map $D \in R^{\frac{h}{c} \times \frac{w}{c}}$ provides depth values for each visual feature patch. Using the camera's extrinsic ([R|T]) and intrinsic (K) parameters, each depth value is back-projected into 3D world coordinates $P_w \in R^{3 \times \frac{h}{c} \times \frac{w}{c}}$. This step explicitly grounds the visual features in a 3D space, relative to the robot's coordinate system.
3. **Spatial Embedding**: A learnable positional embedding $E_S$ is applied to encode these 3D coordinates.
4. **Fusion**: The spatial embedding $E_S(P_w)$ is integrated with the original visual feature map $E(I)$ via element-wise addition.
5. **Projection**: The combined spatial vision features are then processed by the MLP projector $P$ to generate "spatial vision tokens" $e_{ST}$. These tokens are 3D-aware and resolve the ambiguity of the robot's position within the scene.

#### 3.3. 4D Representation with Multi-Frame Encoding

To address state chaos and incorporate temporal dynamics, the model extends the spatial vision tokens to include multi-frame information.
1. **Memory Bank Sampling (MBS)**: A key innovation is the adaptive historical frame sampling strategy called "memory bank sampling" (Algorithm 1). Instead of uniform sampling, which can be redundant and inefficient, MBS intelligently selects $k$ informative historical frames from a temporal window of $n$ available frames. It works by maintaining a similarity queue, ensuring that newly added frames are sufficiently distinct from previously selected ones. This method effectively captures rich historical information with a minimal number of frames, boosting efficiency and effectiveness.
2. **Temporal Positional Encoding (TPE)**: Since MBS produces a non-uniform sequence of frames, explicit temporal encoding is crucial. A learnable time encoding token $e_T = E_T(t-j)$ is introduced, capturing the relative temporal offset between a historical frame (timestamp $j$) and the current frame (timestamp $t$).
3. **Input Token Assembly**: The final input token set $X$ to the VLM Transformer is constructed by concatenating the temporal encoding token with its corresponding spatial vision token for each sampled historical frame, along with the text instruction tokens: $X = \bigcup_{i \in H} [e_{T_i} | e_{ST_i}] \cup \{e_{text}\}$. This design ensures that both spatial and temporal relationships are explicitly conveyed to the model.

#### 3.4. Loss Functions

The model's action head, consisting of two MLP layers, predicts the action components ($[\Delta \hat{x}, \Delta \hat{\theta}, \hat{g}]$) from the VLM Transformer's last token's hidden features. The total training loss $L$ is a composite of several terms:
- **Translation Loss ($L_t$)**: Mean Squared Error ($L_2$ norm) for translation offset, $L_t = ||\Delta \hat{x} - \Delta x||^2$.
- **Rotation Loss ($L_r$)**: Mean Squared Error ($L_2$ norm) for rotation offset, $L_r = ||\Delta \hat{\theta} - \Delta \theta||^2$.
- **Grip Loss ($L_g$)**: Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) for the gripper open/close state, $L_g = BCE(\hat{g}, g)$.
- **Directional Loss ($L_d$)**: To emphasize directional accuracy for small translations, a directional loss $L_d = ||d(\Delta \hat{x}) - d(\Delta x)||^2$ is introduced, where $d(x) = \frac{x}{||x||_2+\epsilon}$ normalizes the vector. A weighting factor $\lambda_d$ (set to 1 during pretraining) controls its contribution.

#### 3.5. MV-Bench: Multi-View Benchmark

To rigorously evaluate spatial understanding and generalization across diverse viewpoints, the authors introduce MV-Bench. This benchmark is built upon LIBERO-SPATIAL and includes:
- **Dataset Generation**: For each trajectory, 6 training and 6 testing viewpoints are uniformly sampled within a 270° front-facing range.
- **Evaluation Settings**:
 - **In-View**: Training and testing are conducted using the same set of viewpoints.
 - **Cross-View**: Testing is performed on novel, unseen viewpoints, assessing generalization to new perspectives.

This comprehensive methodological approach, combining 3D-aware spatial token generation, efficient temporal sampling, and a robust VLM backbone, aims to create a highly effective VLA model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that 4D-VLA consistently outperforms existing baselines across various simulated and real-world robotic tasks, robustly addressing the identified challenges of coordinate system and state chaos.

#### 4.1. Pretraining Setup and Data Processing

4D-VLA is pretrained on the DROID dataset, comprising 76,000 real-world robotic demonstration trajectories with RGB-D data from multiple cameras. RGB-D frames are resized, and trajectories are downsampled. Actions are defined as end-effector state differences. The InternVL-4B model is used as the backbone. During pretraining, a temporal window of 20 frames is used, from which memory bank sampling (MBS) selects 5 past frames along with the current frame. 3D positional encodings are incorporated into the vision encoder, with a mechanism to handle sparse depth data (mean depth per patch, skipping 3D features if >90% masked). The vision encoder is frozen, and other parameters are trained for one epoch on 8 NVIDIA A6000 GPUs.

#### 4.2. LIBERO Simulation Evaluation

Fine-tuned on LIBERO (LIBERO-SPATIAL, LIBERO-OBJECT, LIBERO-GOAL, LIBERO-LONG), 4D-VLA shows substantial performance gains:
- **Overall Performance**: 4D-VLA significantly surpasses OpenVLA and other leading methods, achieving an average success rate 12.1% higher than OpenVLA (e.g., 88.6% vs 76.5%).
- **Task-Specific Improvements**:
 - On LIBERO-SPATIAL, 4D-VLA improves by 5.2% over OpenVLA.
 - On LIBERO-OBJECT, it is 2.7% higher than DiffusionPolicy.
 - On LIBERO-GOAL, it outperforms Octo by 6.3%.
 - Crucially, for the challenging LIBERO-LONG tasks (long-horizon multi-step tasks), 4D-VLA achieves a remarkable 25.4% higher success rate than OpenVLA (79.1% vs 53.7%). This highlights its strong temporal and spatiotemporal reasoning capabilities in complex settings.

#### 4.3. MV-Bench Evaluation

The newly introduced MV-Bench benchmark, designed to evaluate spatial understanding and generalization across viewpoints, further validates 4D-VLA's robustness:
- **In-View Performance**: 4D-VLA achieves an 81.0% success rate, significantly outperforming OpenVLA (52.2%) by 28.8%. This demonstrates its ability to effectively handle diverse training views and reconcile potentially conflicting perspectives through integrated spatiotemporal information.
- **Cross-View Generalization**: In the more challenging Cross-View setting (testing on unseen viewpoints), 4D-VLA maintains superior performance with a 73.8% success rate, showcasing strong generalization to novel perspectives and robust spatial understanding.

#### 4.4. Real-World Evaluation (Franka Robotic Arm)

Experiments with a Franka robotic arm across four representative tasks (Spatial generalization, Robustness to distractors, Precise placement, Instruction following) provide strong real-world validation:
- **Impact of Pretraining**: Adding DROID pretraining to the base VLA model (InternVL-4B with MLP action head) drastically improves average success rate from 15.67% to 47.00%, underscoring the benefit of large-scale pretraining.
- **Contribution of Coordinate Encoding**: Incorporating 3D coordinate information further boosts performance, particularly for tasks requiring precise spatial alignment (e.g., Task 3: Precise placement, jumping from 40% to 85% success). This confirms that coordinate encoding enhances spatial grounding and action accuracy.
- **Contribution of Historical Information**: Adding historical frames via memory bank sampling (MBS) significantly improves performance in long-horizon tasks (Task 2: Robustness to distractors, Task 4: Instruction following) by alleviating latent state ambiguity. For example, Task 4's success rate jumps from 34.67% to 80% with history.
- **Full Model Superiority**: The full 4D-VLA model (Pretraining + Coordinate Encoding + Historical Frames) achieves an average success rate of 85.63%, vastly outperforming OpenVLA (27.70%) and demonstrating superior robustness and reasoning in real-world scenarios.
- **Multi-view Real-world Evaluation**: In even more challenging real-world multi-view tasks (Out-of-distribution generalization, Novel-view generalization), 4D-VLA significantly outperforms OpenVLA in both in-view (55% vs 18% avg) and cross-view settings (50% vs 18% avg), demonstrating robustness to spatial perturbations and strong generalization to viewpoint shifts and unseen camera angles.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies and Discussion

- **Historical Information Utilization**: Analysis shows that the historical window size ($n$) is more critical than the number of sampled frames ($k$), implying uniform sampling can be redundant. MBS effectively reduces redundancy and improves performance with smaller $k$.
- **Temporal Encoding Method**: Learnable relative concatenation for temporal positional encoding yields the best performance, effectively integrating temporal context with spatial vision tokens.
- **Architectural Components**: The MLP action head provides the best balance of inference speed and success rate. Proprioceptive tokens were found to be detrimental, likely encouraging overfitting to motion patterns rather than generalizable visuomotor features.
- **Memory Bank Sampling (MBS) Efficiency**: MBS achieves the highest success (0.866 on Libero-Spatial) with competitive latency and memory usage compared to other video-style samplers (Adaptive Pooling, Grid Pooling, Q-Former), demonstrating its effectiveness for streaming, causal control.
- **Coordinate System Chaos Impact**: A controlled experiment confirmed that deliberately introduced coordinate system chaos severely degrades the performance of models without 3D information. Conversely, the 3D-aware 4D-VLA model maintains relatively high success rates even under high levels of chaos, explicitly demonstrating the value of 3D cues in mitigating this issue.

In summary, the main findings strongly support that explicitly integrating 4D (spatial coordinates + temporal sequence) information, coupled with efficient sampling and a robust VLM backbone, significantly enhances VLA models' pretraining efficiency, generalization, and performance in complex manipulation tasks across diverse simulated and real-world environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The 4D-VLA framework represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotic learning, addressing fundamental limitations that have hindered the development of truly generalizable robotic policies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Resolution of Core Challenges in VLA Pretraining

The paper's primary contribution lies in effectively identifying and mitigating "coordinate system chaos" and "state chaos"—two critical issues arising from insufficient input context in current VLA pretraining paradigms. By introducing depth-derived 3D coordinate embeddings and multi-frame historical information via memory bank sampling, 4D-VLA provides a robust solution for grounding robot actions in both space and time. This directly leads to more stable, smoother, and less dispersed conditional action distributions, making pretraining more efficient and effective.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Generalization and Robustness

The superior performance of 4D-VLA across diverse benchmarks, including LIBERO, the novel MV-Bench, and real-world experiments, highlights its enhanced generalization capabilities.
- **Spatial Generalization**: The integration of 3D information allows the model to better understand the spatial relationships within a scene, leading to improved performance when generalizing to novel object layouts, varied backgrounds, and unseen camera viewpoints (as demonstrated by MV-Bench and real-world multi-view evaluations). This is crucial for robots operating in dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Temporal Robustness**: By incorporating relevant historical context through memory bank sampling, the model gains a better understanding of sequential actions and latent state ambiguities, leading to more robust performance in long-horizon, multi-step tasks and scenarios with temporal complexities.
- **Real-world Applicability**: The significant performance gains in physical robot experiments (using a Franka arm) underscore the framework's practical utility and its potential for deployment in real-world robotic applications. The ability to handle distractors, achieve precise placements, and follow complex instructions from varying views is highly impactful for industrial, service, and domestic robotics.

#### 5.3. Methodological Innovations

4D-VLA introduces several notable methodological innovations:
- **Spatial Vision Tokens**: A novel way to fuse 3D coordinate information directly into visual features, creating inherently 3D-aware tokens that align robot and scene coordinate systems.
- **Memory Bank Sampling (MBS)**: An efficient and adaptive frame sampling strategy that extracts informative historical context, overcoming the redundancy and inefficiency of uniform sampling.
- **MV-Bench Benchmark**: A valuable contribution to the research community, providing a standardized and challenging benchmark for evaluating spatial perception and multi-view generalization in VLA models.

#### 5.4. Implications for Future Embodied AI

The success of 4D-VLA suggests a blueprint for building more capable and general-purpose robotic agents. By establishing a robust framework for spatiotemporal vision-language-action pretraining, it opens avenues for:
- **Developing Foundation Models for Robotics**: This work contributes towards the vision of large, foundation models that can learn diverse robot behaviors from vast datasets and generalize to new tasks and environments with minimal fine-tuning.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap**: By enhancing spatial and temporal understanding, the model's ability to transfer learned policies from simulation to the real world is likely improved.
- **More Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction**: A robot with a deeper spatial and temporal understanding can interpret human instructions more accurately and perform tasks more reliably, leading to more seamless human-robot collaboration.

#### 5.5. Limitations and Future Work

The primary limitation acknowledged by the authors is the reliance on RGB-D input, which introduces hardware restrictions. This points to potential future research directions such as exploring robust depth estimation techniques from monocular RGB input or investigating alternative 3D representations that do not strictly require depth sensors, thus increasing the model's applicability across a wider range of robotic platforms.

In conclusion, 4D-VLA represents a crucial step forward in addressing long-standing challenges in robot learning by providing a comprehensive and empirically validated solution for integrating meaningful 4D information. Its impact will likely be seen in the development of more intelligent, adaptable, and deployable robotic systems in diverse real-world settings.
