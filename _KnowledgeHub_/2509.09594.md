# ObjectReact: Learning Object-Relative Control for Visual Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> ObjectReact presents an object-relative control framework for visual navigation, employing a Relative 3D Scene Graph and a WayObject Costmap to condition a local controller. This method achieves improved generalization across challenging tasks like finding shortcuts and navigating in reverse, and shows remarkable invariance to robot embodiment changes, outperforming image-relative baselines by up to 48% absolute SPL in cross-height scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing image-relative visual navigation methods struggle with generalization across varying robot embodiments, sensor heights, or previously unseen routes.
- Local controllers tied to specific subgoal images are highly sensitive to the agent's exact pose and visual appearance, limiting their ability to navigate in reverse or find shortcuts.
- Achieving cross-embodiment generalization with image-relative approaches demands prohibitively large, specialized datasets covering all potential configurations.

## Method

- An ==object-relative control== paradigm is introduced, leveraging object-level information for local control rather than image-level appearance.
- A ==Relative 3D Scene Graph (3DSG)== is constructed for topometric mapping, using open-set object segmentation and relative 3D distances for intra-image object connections.
- The local controller, **ObjectReact**, is conditioned on a novel ==WayObject Costmap== which encodes object segmentation masks with their normalized path lengths to the goal, abstracting away direct RGB input.

## Results

- **ObjectReact** significantly outperforms image-relative baselines across challenging tasks, achieving an SPL of **21.74%** on Alt Goal (vs. **2.17%** for GNM) and **23.08%** on Shortcut (vs. **7.69%**).
- The system exhibits strong invariance to robot embodiment changes, maintaining consistent performance (only **3.04%** SPL difference) when executing at varying sensor heights (0.4m vs. 1.3m).
- Ablation studies confirm that the object-relative continuous action controller, topometric maps using relative 3D information, and the use of the WayObject Costmap without direct RGB input are key to its improved generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-level representations provide a more stable, embodiment-, and trajectory-invariant basis for visual navigation compared to image-level cues.
> - Decoupling the local controller from explicit image matching and training it on an abstract "WayObject Costmap" significantly improves generalization across tasks and embodiments.
> - Integrating relative 3D distances into the scene graph for intra-image object connections yields more geometrically informed and superior global path planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual navigation for autonomous agents has traditionally relied on complex systems involving dense 3D maps, often constructed using 3D sensors like LiDAR or depth cameras. While effective, these methods can be resource-intensive, require significant computational power, and often struggle with semantic understanding or generalization across different embodiments.

In contrast, "visual topological navigation" has emerged as a promising alternative, inspired by human navigation abilities. These approaches typically use a single camera and a topological map, representing the environment as a graph of interconnected locations or images. Early topological methods were often limited to "teach-and-repeat" scenarios, where a robot simply re-traces a previously observed path using image-based visual servoing. More recent advancements, such as SPTM and GNM, have introduced learning-based controllers that predict control signals from a current observation and a subgoal image, allowing for more flexible navigation within a known topological map. These are broadly categorized as "image-relative" approaches.

ObjectReact critically challenges the limitations inherent in these image-relative paradigms. The core issue with image-relative control is its strong dependence on the agent's precise pose and embodiment, and the specific visual appearance captured in the subgoal image. This makes it difficult for agents to traverse new routes, generalize across different robot platforms (embodiments), or navigate in reverse directions.

The broader research landscape has increasingly recognized the value of object-level information for navigation. Works like those utilizing 3D Scene Graphs (3DSG) or object-centric topological maps like RoboHop, PixNav, and TANGO have demonstrated that objects provide a more stable, embodiment- and trajectory-invariant representation of the world compared to raw images. Objects offer semantic meaning and spatial relationships that are beneficial for global path planning and instruction following. However, many existing object-level methods either rely on zero-shot, often rudimentary, controllers (e.g., RoboHop's proportional yaw controller), or employ discrete action spaces (e.g., PixNav), or still link graph nodes to agent poses, thereby partially reintroducing the pose-specificity problem.

ObjectReact positions itself as a significant advancement in this landscape by introducing a novel "object-relative control" paradigm. It builds upon object-level topological mapping but fundamentally changes how the local controller is learned and conditioned. By introducing the **Relative 3D Scene Graph** and the **WayObject Costmap**, ObjectReact aims to:
1. **Decouple control from image matching:** Unlike image-relative methods that need to solve an image matching problem between current and subgoal images, ObjectReact conditions its controller directly on object-level costs derived from the map.
2. **Enhance invariance:** Objects, being properties of the map rather than agent-specific observations, naturally offer better invariance to agent pose, embodiment, and trajectory variations.
3. **Improve planning and traversal:** The use of relative 3D information in the scene graph for intra-image edges provides more geometrically grounded path planning than purely 2D object connectivity.

Thus, ObjectReact represents a pivotal step towards more robust, generalized, and practical visual navigation systems by leveraging the strengths of object-centric representations and addressing the inherent limitations of image-relative control in topological navigation. It synthesizes insights from scene graphs, topological mapping, and learning-based control, pushing the boundary for cross-embodiment and task-agnostic navigation in seen environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of "ObjectReact" is to develop a more robust, generalized, and efficient visual navigation system for known environments, moving beyond the limitations of existing image-relative approaches. The authors are motivated by the inherent constraints of relying on image-level representations for control, which are tightly coupled with the agent's specific pose and embodiment.

The key motivations and objectives can be articulated as follows:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Image-Relative Control:**
 - **Pose and Embodiment Specificity:** Traditional image-relative methods (e.g., GNM) use subgoal images that are strictly tied to the robot's pose and camera properties. This makes generalization difficult when the robot's sensor height changes, or when navigating previously unseen routes that deviate from the "teach" trajectory.
 - **Image Matching Challenge:** Control prediction in image-relative approaches often implicitly or explicitly involves solving an image matching problem between the current observation and the subgoal image. This is a complex task prone to errors, especially in environments with changing appearances or viewpoints.
 - **Data Requirements:** Achieving cross-embodiment generalization for image-relative methods would necessitate quadratically larger datasets to cover all combinations of embodiments for current and subgoal observations, making data collection and annotation prohibitively expensive.

2. **Leverage Object-Level World Representation:**
 - **Invariance:** Objects, unlike images, are properties of the environment itself, offering an embodiment- and trajectory-invariant representation. This motivates conditioning control on objects rather than specific image views.
 - **Semantic Richness:** Objects naturally provide semantic information that can enrich global path planning and make navigation more intuitive and interpretable.

3. **Introduce a Novel Paradigm of Object-Relative Control with Specific Desirable Characteristics:**
 - **New Route Traversal:** Enable navigation along routes not strictly requiring imitation of prior experience. This includes tasks like finding shortcuts or reaching alternative goals not directly on the mapping path (Alt Goal, Shortcut tasks).
 - **Decoupled Control and Image Matching:** Separate the problem of predicting control signals from the intricate challenge of matching current and subgoal images. This simplifies the learning problem for the local controller.
 - **High Invariance in Cross-Embodiment Deployment:** Achieve robust performance despite variations across both training-testing settings (e.g., different robot heights) and mapping-execution settings (e.g., map created by one robot, executed by another).

4. **Develop a More Informative Map Representation:**
 - **Relative 3D Scene Graph (3DSG):** Improve upon existing 2D object-connectivity maps (like RoboHop) by incorporating relative 3D information to define intra-image object connections. This aims to provide more geometrically accurate costs for global path planning.
 - **Topometric Map:** Combine topological connectivity (inter-image object associations) with relative metric information (intra-image 3D distances) to form a robust and meaningful graph.

5. **Propose a Novel Local Controller (ObjectReact) Conditioned on Object-Level Costs:**
 - **"WayObject Costmap":** Create an interpretable and learnable representation that encodes object segmentation masks with their corresponding path lengths to the goal. This costmap serves as the direct input to the local controller, eliminating the need for explicit RGB input and providing a visual distribution of "attractiveness" or "repelling" regions.
 - **Continuous Action Prediction:** Learn to predict a continuous trajectory rollout, offering more nuanced control than discrete action spaces or simple zero-shot controllers.

By addressing these motivations and pursuing these objectives, ObjectReact aims to make significant strides towards highly generalizable and robust visual navigation systems, bridging the gap between perception and action in complex real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ObjectReact introduces a comprehensive object-relative navigation pipeline structured into three main phases: mapping, execution, and training. This modular design allows for independent development and improvement of each component, with a core focus on object-level representations.

### 4. Mapping Phase: Relative 3D Scene Graph (3DSG) Construction

The first phase involves creating a topometric map, represented as a Relative 3D Scene Graph, from a prior image sequence.
- **Image Segments as Object Nodes:** For each image in the sequence, open-set, semantically meaningful segmentation masks are extracted using foundational models like SAM2 or FastSAM. Each segment then forms an object node in the graph. To capture relative 3D information, each object node is associated with a 3D coordinate (p_i), defined as the farthest point on the object in a local frame of reference. This relative 3D projection is achieved using monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything) and a fixed focal length, avoiding the need for global camera poses or absolute depth.
- **Intra-image Edges:** Unlike previous methods that used 2D pixel-center connectivity (e.g., Delaunay triangulation in RoboHop), ObjectReact establishes intra-image edges by calculating the Euclidean distance between the 3D coordinates of all object pairs within an image. This "relative 3D connectivity" provides more informative geometric context for planning, which is shown to be superior to purely 2D connections.
- **Inter-image Edges:** To connect object nodes across different images, object tracking is performed using local feature matching. SuperPoint keypoints and descriptors are extracted and matched across consecutive frames using LightGlue. Segment-level correspondences are then established based on the number of pixel-level matches falling into a segment pair. These inter-image edges are assigned a weight of 0, effectively merging nodes for global path planning purposes and enabling traversal without incurring a cost for image-to-image transitions. CLIP is used to filter out segments corresponding to 'floor' or 'ceiling' to prevent erroneous shortcuts in planning.

### 5. Execution Phase: Object Localizer, Global Planner, and Local Controller

During task execution, the robot leverages the constructed 3DSG to navigate towards a given object goal.
- **Object Localizer:** When a query image is captured by the robot, its visible objects are segmented (using FastSAM). These query objects are then localized within the map by matching them against map object nodes, similar to the inter-image edge creation process (SuperPoint-LightGlue). A submap centered at the closest reference map image is used for efficient localization.
- **Global Planner:** Path lengths from the localized map object nodes to the long-horizon goal node are computed using Dijkstra's algorithm on the 3DSG. If a query object matches multiple map nodes, the match with the least path length is selected. Unlocalized or untracked segments are assigned a fixed high "outlier" cost.
- **Local Controller (ObjectReact):** This is the core of the proposed method. Instead of directly predicting actions from RGB images, ObjectReact uses a novel **"WayObject Costmap"** representation as its input.

### 6. Training Phase: The ObjectReact Controller

The ObjectReact controller learns to predict continuous trajectory rollouts based on the WayObject Costmap.
- **WayObject Costmap Representation:** This is a multi-channel image (W_H×W×D) designed to be an interpretable representation of object-level costs. For each visible object in the current RGB image, its segmentation mask is combined with its normalized path length to the goal. The path lengths are converted into a D-dimensional sine-cosine embedding (similar to positional encoding in Transformers), ensuring local cost distribution invariance and accommodating a variable number of objects. This representation eliminates the need for an explicit RGB input to the controller, making it more generalized and less prone to overfitting on visual appearance. (D=8, Z=10000, L=100 are used).
- **Trajectory Prediction:** The authors adapt the training pipeline of GNM, an existing image-relative controller, to enable direct ablative comparisons. The GNM architecture is modified to use a custom goal encoder that handles the multi-channel WayObject Costmap as input. The controller predicts a rollout of 10 future 2D waypoints in the robot's Bird's-Eye View (BEV) space, rather than just a single control signal.
- **Training Data:** Training data is generated using the Habitat-Matterport 3D dataset (HM3Dv0.2), specifically the IIN-HM3D-v3 challenge set. Shortest paths are computed using the simulator's geodesic distance estimator to generate ground-truth trajectories. During training, WayObject Costmaps are created using simulator-provided object instances and depth. To enhance generalization to real-world deployment (where segmentation, matching, and depth are inferred), a data augmentation strategy is employed: 30% of segment costs are randomly replaced with the "outlier" cost.
- **Control Execution:** The trained controller uses the last predicted waypoint from the trajectory rollout to compute linear and angular velocities. These velocities are smoothed over a moving window of past predictions to ensure stable control.

The methodology highlights a sophisticated integration of state-of-the-art perception models (SAM, FastSAM, SuperPoint, LightGlue, Depth Anything) with a novel graph representation and a learning-based control strategy. By focusing on object-relative information and decoupling the perception and control problems, ObjectReact aims for unprecedented generalization capabilities in visual navigation.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the significant advantages of their object-relative control paradigm over traditional image-relative approaches, particularly in challenging navigation scenarios and across embodiment variations.

### 8. Image-Relative vs. Object-Relative Comparison (Table 1)

This core comparison highlights ObjectReact's superior performance:
- **Imitate Task:** Both the image-relative GNM baseline and ObjectReact perform similarly (SPL ~57-59%, SSPL ~64-66%). This suggests that for simple teach-and-repeat scenarios, both approaches are effective.
- **Challenging Tasks (Alt Goal, Shortcut, Reverse):** ObjectReact vastly outperforms the image-relative baseline on all three challenging tasks:
 - **Alt Goal:** ObjectReact achieves an SPL of 21.74% (vs. 2.17% for GNM) and SSPL of 27.40% (vs. 13.54%). This demonstrates its ability to reach previously unvisited objects, leveraging its trajectory-invariant, object-level representation.
 - **Shortcut:** ObjectReact records an SPL of 23.08% (vs. 7.69% for GNM) and SSPL of 39.56% (vs. 18.40%). This indicates its capacity to identify and take shortcuts by grounding object-to-goal distances geometrically, contrasting with GNM's reliance on temporal distance prediction which can miss optimal paths.
 - **Reverse:** ObjectReact achieves an SPL of 26.67% (vs. 11.60% for GNM) and SSPL of 36.69% (vs. 23.59%). This shows greater robustness to viewpoint changes, benefiting from its "partial matching" ability where even a few matched objects can guide the agent correctly.

These results unequivocally establish that the object-relative navigation pipeline effectively addresses multiple weaknesses of its image-relative counterpart, leading to inherent capabilities for solving a broader range of navigation tasks. The authors note, however, that absolute performance on challenging tasks remains lower than on the Imitate task, largely attributed to the limitations of current robust perception techniques rather than the controller itself.

### 9. Embodiment Variations based on Robot Height (Table 2)

This experiment investigates generalization across different sensor heights during execution while keeping the map height fixed (1.3m).
- **Image-Relative:** GNM performs very well when the execution height matches the map height (1.3m SPL = 81.82%). However, its performance significantly drops by an absolute 48.49% (SPL = 33.33%) when the execution height is lowered to 0.4m. This highlights its strong dependence on pose and embodiment.
- **Object-Relative:** ObjectReact exhibits remarkable invariance. The performance difference (∆SPL) between 0.4m and 1.3m execution heights is only 3.04% (SPL 60.60% vs. 57.56%). In fact, it slightly benefits from a lower height, possibly due to better visibility of traversable regions. This demonstrates ObjectReact's ability to maintain high performance across varying embodiments without requiring quadratically larger, specialized training datasets.

### 10. Ablation Studies (Table 3)

Ablation studies provide insights into the contributions of different design choices:
- **Controller Types:** When provided with ground-truth WayObject Costmaps (perfect perception), ObjectReact significantly outperforms other object-level controllers: PixNav (discrete action, pixel tracking) and RoboHop (zero-shot, proportional yaw). ObjectReact achieves much higher SPL and SSPL scores across all tasks, validating its learned, continuous action prediction approach.
- **Topological (2D) vs. Topometric (3D) Maps:** Using relative 3D Euclidean distances for intra-image object connectivity (topometric) proves superior to 2D Delaunay triangulation (topological, as in RoboHop). ObjectReact with 3D edges (SPL 71.20% on Imitate) outperforms ObjectReact with 2D edges (SPL 59.08%), indicating that geometric information, even relative 3D, is crucial for effective global path planning.
- **Subgoal Conditioning with and without Current RGB:** A surprising finding is that learning control *solely* from the WayObject Costmap, without an explicit RGB input, yields better generalization. ObjectReact without RGB (SPL 71.20% on Imitate) outperforms ObjectReact with RGB (SPL 63.61%). The authors speculate that including RGB can lead to overfitting on specific visual appearances, whereas the WayObject Costmap provides a more abstract and invariant representation of goals.

### 11. Real-World Generalization (Qualitative Results in Appendix C.2, Demo Videos)

Despite being trained *only* in simulation, ObjectReact demonstrates promising qualitative generalization to real-world indoor environments.
- **New Obstacles:** The robot successfully re-routes around new obstacles not present in the original map, highlighting its adaptability.
- **Cross-Embodiment:** Successful navigation using maps generated from a phone camera, deployed on a quadruped robot (Unitree Go1). This confirms the mapping-execution cross-embodiment invariance.
- **Alternative Tasks:** Successful demonstrations of Alt Goal (reaching an object only visible in map periphery) and Shortcut (taking a direct path instead of a longer mapped path).
- **Environmental Changes:** Robustness to varying lighting conditions (LowLight, DayNight) and the presence of dynamic obstacles, attributed to the WayObject Costmap's invariance.

### 12. Identified Limitations

The paper also candidly discusses limitations:
- **Perception Quality:** The absolute performance on challenging tasks is often bottlenecked by the accuracy and recall of current state-of-the-art segmentation (SAM/FastSAM) and matching (SuperPoint-LightGlue) techniques, which still face challenges like low recall for certain objects, overlapping masks, and accurate data association.
- **Mapping and Planning:** Issues such as shortcut paths through large instance categories like "floor" and "ceiling" can still occur, though mitigated by filtering.
- **Learned Controller:** The lack of history context for the controller can lead to instability if the costmap varies significantly frame-to-frame due to perception failures.
- **Dynamic Objects/Map Changes:** While minor changes are handled, significant scene rearrangements or dynamic objects can still cause failures if they lead to misleading object-level connectivity.

In summary, the main findings strongly support the efficacy of object-relative control, particularly with the Relative 3D Scene Graph and WayObject Costmap, for achieving robust generalization across challenging navigation tasks and varying robot embodiments. While current perception techniques pose a bottleneck, the core approach shows significant promise.

### 13. Significance and Potential Impact

ObjectReact introduces a significant paradigm shift in visual navigation, proposing "object-relative control" as a powerful alternative to the prevalent "image-relative control." Its contributions hold substantial significance and promise a wide-ranging impact on the field of embodied AI and robotics.

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The most profound impact of ObjectReact lies in its ability to achieve unprecedented levels of generalization.
 - **Cross-Embodiment Invariance:** By decoupling control from specific image appearances and relying on embodiment-invariant object properties, ObjectReact allows for maps created by one robot (or even a phone camera) to be used by a completely different robot (e.g., a quadruped). This drastically reduces the need for collecting expensive, robot-specific datasets and makes navigation systems more versatile and deployable.
 - **Trajectory and Viewpoint Invariance:** The method's success in navigating new routes, finding shortcuts, and traveling in reverse directions highlights its robustness to deviations from the original mapping trajectory and changes in viewpoint. This moves beyond the limitations of "teach-and-repeat" to more intelligent, adaptive navigation.
 - **Decoupling Perception and Control:** By abstracting the subgoal information into the WayObject Costmap, the control learning problem is simplified. The controller no longer needs to solve an implicit image matching task, allowing it to focus purely on reacting to generalized object-level costs. This modularity makes the system more robust to varying visual conditions.

2. **Novel Technical Contributions:**
 - **Relative 3D Scene Graph:** This novel map representation leverages relative 3D information for intra-image object connectivity, providing more geometrically accurate planning than prior 2D-based methods. This improved spatial understanding is crucial for efficient and safe navigation.
 - **WayObject Costmap:** This innovative input representation for the local controller is a key enabler of ObjectReact's generalization. By encoding normalized, D-dimensional path lengths onto segmentation masks, it provides an interpretable, dense, and embodiment-agnostic representation of goals, eliminating the need for explicit RGB inputs.
 - **Challenging Benchmark Tasks:** The introduction of Alt Goal, Shortcut, and Reverse tasks provides a rigorous benchmark for evaluating navigation agents beyond simple teach-and-repeat, pushing the community to develop more capable and intelligent systems.

3. **Implications for Practical Robotics:**
 - **Wider Deployability:** Robots can be deployed in environments where a map already exists (e.g., from human recordings or other robots) without extensive recalibration or retraining.
 - **Reduced Development Cost:** Less need for large, diverse datasets covering every possible robot embodiment or scene variation, thereby lowering the cost and effort of developing and deploying navigation solutions.
 - **Foundation for Future AI:** The generic nature of the WayObject Costmap makes it a suitable conditioning variable for integration with other advanced AI capabilities. It could be generated from language instructions (e.g., "go to the red chair"), exploration objectives, cross-sensor modalities, or directly by large vision-language models (LLMs/VLMs). This opens exciting avenues for more intelligent and interactive robotic agents.

4. **Alignment with Human Cognition:** The paper notes that object-relative navigation strategies are closer to "landmark-based navigation strategies observed in human trials." This suggests that ObjectReact's approach might be a step towards bridging the gap between human and robot navigation, potentially leading to more intuitive and understandable robot behaviors.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, particularly in the current state of perception modules, its modular design ensures that improvements in segmentation, object detection, and feature matching can be directly integrated to further enhance ObjectReact's performance. The work strongly encourages future research in these foundational perception areas.

In conclusion, ObjectReact offers a compelling vision for the future of visual navigation. By shifting the control paradigm from image-relative to object-relative, it addresses fundamental challenges of generalization, robustness, and practicality, setting a new benchmark for developing versatile and intelligent autonomous agents.
