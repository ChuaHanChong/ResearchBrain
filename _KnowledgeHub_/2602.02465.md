# MentisOculi: Revealing the Limits of Reasoning with Mental Imagery

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce MENTISOCULI, a benchmark with five procedurally generated visual reasoning tasks to evaluate how frontier multimodal models leverage self-generated visual information. The study reveals that current models struggle with consistent visual generation and interpretation of these images, often failing to effectively use visual aids for multi-step reasoning despite possessing underlying textual reasoning capabilities.

## Problem

- The effectiveness of "machine mental imagery" for reasoning in multimodal models remained ambiguous, with inconsistent results in existing literature.
- It was unclear if failures in using visual aids stemmed from fundamental reasoning deficits, poor image generation quality, or an inability to interpret self-generated visual cues.
- A rigorous, standardized evaluation framework was lacking to comprehensively assess and disentangle these potential failure modes across different model architectures.

## Method

- Developed ==MENTISOCULI==, a novel benchmark comprising ==five multi-step visual reasoning tasks== designed with high information density, sequential manipulation, and procedural generation.
- The benchmark includes tasks like FORM BOARD, HINGE FOLDING, PAPER FOLD, RUSH HOUR, and SLIDING PUZZLE, each with five stratified difficulty levels.
- Evaluated a spectrum of models including MLLMs, latent visual reasoning models, Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs), and video models, using automated scoring, human reference data, and ablation studies to isolate failure sources.

## Results

- MENTISOCULI tasks consistently challenge state-of-the-art models, with performance degrading to or below chance levels by Level **5** for most models across all tasks.
- Providing UMMs with ground-truth intermediate images did not consistently enable them to reach human performance or fully leverage the correct visual information for decision-making on planning tasks.
- Common reasoning enhancements (in-context learning, prompt optimization, increased reasoning budget, tool use) failed to provide systematic or reliable performance gains on MENTISOCULI tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit visual intervention strategies, such as interleaved generated images from UMMs or latent visual tokens, do not consistently improve or even match text-only MLLM performance in visual reasoning.
> - Frontier MLLMs demonstrate strong underlying reasoning competence when provided with verbose symbolic textual descriptions of visual tasks, indicating that core reasoning capacity exists.
> - UMMs exhibit pervasive issues with visual generation consistency (e.g., object identity, geometry maintenance, hallucinations) and struggle to effectively interpret even oracle (ground-truth) visual aids for planning-intensive tasks.
