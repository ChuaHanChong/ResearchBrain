# Real-World Point Tracking with Verifier-Guided Pseudo-Labeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced a verifier-guided pseudo-labeling framework for long-term point tracking, which learns to dynamically assess the reliability of multiple teacher tracker predictions. This approach generates higher-quality supervision for fine-tuning a student model on unlabeled real-world videos, leading to improved performance on various real-world benchmarks.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art point trackers, primarily trained on synthetic data, suffer performance degradation in real-world videos due to the sim-to-real domain gap.
- Generating dense, long-term ground-truth annotations for real-world videos is extremely costly and labor-intensive.
- Existing pseudo-labeling methods are limited by the inconsistent reliability of teacher tracker predictions, which often vary across frames and scenarios.

## Method

- A "verifier" module is introduced, learning to estimate the per-frame reliability of candidate trajectories generated by multiple pretrained teacher trackers.
- The verifier is trained on synthetic data, exposed to ground truth and stochastically perturbed trajectories to identify various error patterns.
- High-quality pseudo-labels are generated for unlabeled real-world videos by selecting the verifier's most reliable prediction from the teacher ensemble.

## Results

- The verifier, operating as an inference-time ensemble, consistently surpassed individual teacher models and random selection baselines across various real-world datasets.
- The fine-tuned student model, Track-On-R, achieved new state-of-the-art performance across four real-world benchmarks: EgoPoints, RoboTAP, TAP-Vid Kinetics, and TAP-Vid DAVIS.
- Track-On-R showed a significant increase in δxavg from 61.7 to 67.3 on EgoPoints and an Average Jaccard improvement of +2.8 on RoboTAP, demonstrating strong generalization despite fine-tuning on diverse real-world data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial "oracle gap" indicates that dynamically selecting the best teacher prediction at each frame could significantly improve point tracking performance.
> - Pretrained teacher trackers demonstrate high inconsistency and varying error profiles across real-world video frames, making static selection or averaging ineffective.
> - A learned, adaptive "verifier" can effectively identify and leverage complementary strengths of different trackers, outperforming rigid heuristic-based ensemble methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Point tracking, which involves reliably following physical points across extended video sequences, is a fundamental task in computer vision. It underpins applications such as video editing, robot manipulation, 4D scene understanding, and medical analysis. Recent advancements, particularly with transformer-based models, have improved the state of the art in short-term and long-term correspondence tasks. However, these models are typically trained on large synthetic datasets due to the prohibitively high cost of obtaining dense, frame-accurate long-term trajectory annotations in real-world videos.

This reliance on synthetic data introduces a "sim-to-real discrepancy," where differences in appearance statistics, nonrigid motion, occlusion patterns, lighting changes, and sensor artifacts in natural footage cause performance degradation. Bridging this domain gap is a critical challenge. Self-training approaches, which use predictions from a pretrained model (or an ensemble of models) as "pseudo-labels" to adapt to unlabeled real-world data, have emerged as a practical strategy.

The current work positions itself within this landscape by addressing a limitation of naive pseudo-labeling. Prior methods often struggle because teacher model predictions are not uniformly reliable across frames or scenes. Different trackers exhibit varying error profiles (e.g., resistance to fast motion vs. drift under low texture, handling occlusion vs. identity switches). Fixed heuristics or global confidence thresholds fail to reconcile these heterogeneous error patterns, potentially propagating systematic errors during the adaptation process.

This research introduces a novel mechanism for reliability estimation within the self-training paradigm. Instead of relying on a single, potentially inconsistent teacher or simple ensemble averaging, the proposed "verifier" learns to dynamically assess the trustworthiness of multiple candidate trajectories per frame. This approach aims to provide higher-quality supervision for fine-tuning on real-world data, thereby enhancing model robustness and accuracy in diverse real-world scenarios. It builds upon prior work in point tracking (e.g., PIPs, TAP-Vid, CoTracker, Track-On) and pseudo-labeling/ensemble learning, extending the latter by proposing a learnable, adaptive ensemble rather than fixed combinations or random selection of teachers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the performance of long-term point tracking models in real-world videos by developing a robust and data-efficient fine-tuning framework that mitigates the sim-to-real domain gap. This is achieved without requiring extensive real-world ground-truth annotations.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current point tracking methodologies:

1. **Sim-to-Real Discrepancy:** State-of-the-art point trackers are predominantly trained on synthetic datasets like TAP-Vid Kubric. While effective in synthetic environments, these models often exhibit degraded performance when applied to real-world videos. This is attributed to inherent differences in visual characteristics such as texture, illumination, nonrigid motion, and occlusion patterns, which are difficult to synthesize accurately and comprehensively.
2. **Prohibitive Cost of Real-World Annotation:** Obtaining dense, frame-accurate, long-term ground-truth trajectories for real-world videos is an extremely labor-intensive and costly process. This makes large-scale, supervised training on real data impractical for many applications and leads to the reliance on synthetic data.
3. **Limitations of Naive Pseudo-Labeling:** Self-training on unlabeled real-world videos using pseudo-labels generated by pretrained "teacher" trackers has been explored as a solution to the annotation problem. However, this approach is often brittle because the reliability of teacher predictions varies significantly across different frames, scenes, and motion patterns. A single teacher rarely provides consistently accurate predictions throughout an entire video. Furthermore, different trackers exhibit complementary strengths and weaknesses; one might handle fast motion better, while another excels under occlusion or low texture. Naive strategies like randomly selecting a teacher or simply averaging predictions can propagate errors, amplify noise, and undermine the benefits of self-training.
4. **Identified Potential for Improvement (Oracle Gap):** An "oracle" experiment, which selects the *perfect* prediction (closest to ground truth) from an ensemble of teacher models at each frame, demonstrates a substantial performance gap compared to individual teachers or random selection. This gap indicates significant headroom for improvement if a mechanism could reliably identify and select the most accurate prediction at each time step. This observation motivates the need for an adaptive, learned approach to assess reliability.

To address these issues, the key objective is to introduce a "verifier"—a meta-model designed to explicitly estimate the per-frame reliability of candidate trajectories from multiple pretrained trackers. This verifier is intended to guide the generation of high-quality pseudo-labels for subsequent fine-tuning of a student tracker on unlabeled real-world videos. The motivation is to create a unified, data-efficient framework that turns the diversity of existing tracking models into a strength, leading to improved long-term coherence and robustness in real-world tracking scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the introduction of a "verifier" module designed to assess the reliability of point tracker predictions. This verifier facilitates real-world adaptation of tracking models without requiring dense manual annotations.

#### 3.1. Verifier Design and Functionality

The verifier is a learned meta-model that does not directly predict trajectories. Instead, it learns to evaluate which of several candidate tracker predictions is most reliable for each frame.

- **Input:** For a given query point in a video and a tensor of `M` candidate trajectories (from multiple trackers), the verifier aims to assign a reliability score to each candidate at every time step.
- **Localized Feature Extraction:**
 - Dense visual features for all video frames are first extracted using a frozen CNN encoder (e.g., from CoTracker3).
 - A stable visual reference for the target point is created by bilinearly sampling the feature map at the query point's initial visible location.
 - For each frame and each candidate location, deformable attention is used to aggregate local contextual descriptors, comparing the local appearance around the candidate to the initial query reference.
 - Positional context (sinusoidal embedding of displacement vectors) and learned identity embeddings (query vs. candidate) are concatenated with these visual features and projected to a consistent model dimension.
 - The output consists of a time-replicated query feature tensor (representing the target's reference appearance) and a tensor of candidate feature descriptors across all frames.
- **Candidate Transformer:**
 - These extracted features are fed into a transformer-based decoder.
 - The transformer employs localized cross-attention, where the query embedding for each frame attends *only* to its corresponding candidate features for that frame. This allows per-frame assessment of candidate consistency.
 - Temporal self-attention then processes the resulting per-frame query embeddings across time, enabling information flow between frames to maintain consistency and resolve ambiguities using contextual evidence.
 - A feed-forward network follows each attention stage.
- **Verifier Output:** The candidate transformer outputs a reliability distribution over the `M` candidates at each time step. This is computed as a temperature-scaled softmax over cosine similarities between the decoded query feature and the candidate features for that frame.

#### 3.2. Verifier Training

The verifier is trained exclusively on labeled synthetic videos (e.g., K-EPIC) with ground-truth trajectories. This ensures that it learns to distinguish reliable from unreliable predictions under known conditions.

- **Candidate Trajectory Generation (Training):** Instead of using actual tracker outputs during training, candidate trajectories (`C`) are generated by applying various stochastic perturbations to the ground-truth trajectory (`p`). These perturbations simulate common tracker errors such as:
 - Light Gaussian noise.
 - Stable noise (small, smoothed displacements).
 - Gradual drift (progressive displacement or soft identity blending).
 - Long-term drift (steadily increasing offset).
 - Spiky noise (short, high-magnitude spikes).
 - Abrupt jumps or complete identity switches.
 - This strategy exposes the verifier to a diverse range of realistic error patterns, enabling it to learn robust reliability estimation.
- **Loss Function:** A soft contrastive objective is used. For each frame, a target reliability distribution (`s_t`) is defined as a softmax over the negative Euclidean distances between all candidate predictions and the ground-truth point. The predicted reliability scores (`s_hat_t`) are supervised using a cross-entropy loss, with occluded frames masked out.

#### 3.3. Real-World Fine-tuning with Verifier-Guided Pseudo-Labels

After training, the verifier is applied to unlabeled real-world videos to generate high-quality pseudo-labels for fine-tuning a base point tracking model (e.g., Track-On2).

- **Inference-time Candidate Trajectories:** During real-world fine-tuning and inference, `C` consists of trajectories predicted by an ensemble of `M` pretrained, off-the-shelf teacher trackers (e.g., Track-On2, BootsTAPIR, BootsTAPNext, Anthro-LocoTrack, AllTracker, CoTracker3).
- **Pseudo-Label Generation:** For each query point in an unlabeled real-world video, the verifier evaluates the `M` candidate trajectories from the teacher ensemble. At every frame, the candidate with the highest reliability score is selected as the pseudo-label. Visibility is estimated via majority voting among teacher predictions.
- **Student Model Adaptation:** The base point tracking model (Track-On2) is fine-tuned on a collection of real-world videos (TAO, OVIS, VSPW) using these verifier-guided pseudo-labels.
- **Training Schedule:** A hybrid training approach is employed, combining synthetic data (with ground truth) and real-world pseudo-labeled data. The loss weight for real-world samples is gradually increased, while that for synthetic samples is reduced over the course of training. This schedule aims to gradually shift the model's focus towards real-world characteristics while retaining some benefits of synthetic ground truth.

#### 3.4. Verifier as an Inference-time Ensemble

Beyond pseudo-labeling for fine-tuning, the trained verifier can also function as a plug-and-play inference-time ensemble module. It dynamically combines predictions from complementary trackers based on learned reliability cues, offering improved robustness without requiring further training of the base tracker.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the verifier's effectiveness as both an inference-time ensemble and a guide for real-world adaptation.

#### 4.1. Oracle Performance and Teacher Inconsistency

An initial oracle experiment demonstrated that a hypothetical selector, choosing the most accurate teacher prediction at each frame, significantly outperforms any individual teacher model and a random teacher selection baseline across four real-world datasets (DAVIS, Kinetics, RoboTAP, EgoPoints). The substantial gap between the oracle and current models highlighted the potential for an adaptive per-frame selection mechanism. Furthermore, analysis showed that teacher model accuracy fluctuates considerably across frames and scenes, confirming their inconsistency and the limitations of static selection.

#### 4.2. Verifier as an Inference-time Ensemble

When deployed as an inference-time ensemble, the verifier demonstrated consistent improvements:
- **Superior Performance:** The verifier consistently outperformed individual teacher models and the random-teacher baseline across all four real-world datasets (DAVIS, Kinetics, RoboTAP, EgoPoints).
- **Adaptive Selection:** This indicates the verifier's ability to adaptively select the most reliable prediction from the ensemble at each time step.
- **Challenging Scenarios:** The gains were particularly notable on challenging datasets such as EgoPoints, where model reliability exhibits significant variability.
- **Teacher Composition Robustness:** Ablation studies showed that the verifier remained robust and effective even when including weaker teachers or varying the teacher set, consistently outperforming random selection. This suggests the verifier effectively leverages complementary strengths rather than being diluted by individual model weaknesses.

#### 4.3. Verifier-Guided Adaptation (Track-On-R)

The student model, "Track-On-R" (fine-tuned Track-On2 with verifier-guided pseudo-labels), achieved notable performance improvements:
- **State-of-the-Art on Real-World Benchmarks:** Track-On-R established new state-of-the-art performance across all four real-world datasets (EgoPoints, RoboTAP, TAP-Vid Kinetics, and TAP-Vid DAVIS), surpassing both synthetic baselines and prior real-world fine-tuning methods.
- **EgoPoints:** Achieved a significant increase in δxavg from 61.7 (synthetic baseline) to 67.3, outperforming the closest competitor, AllTracker, by +5.3 in δxavg. This demonstrates effective transfer to complex egocentric motion.
- **RoboTAP:** Showed consistent gains across all metrics, with a +2.8 improvement in Average Jaccard (AJ) over the synthetic baseline. This was achieved despite the fine-tuning dataset containing no robotic sequences, indicating strong generalization.
- **Kinetics and DAVIS:** Improved AJ by +2.5 on Kinetics and +1.1 on DAVIS compared to the synthetic baseline, also achieving higher Occlusion Accuracy (OA) on Kinetics.
- **Data Efficiency:** The fine-tuning process demonstrated data efficiency, with most adaptation benefits achievable with fewer than 3K real videos and short fine-tuning.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Further Analysis

- **Synthetic vs. Real Data for Fine-tuning:** While a scheduled mixture of synthetic and real data provided the best overall performance, training solely on real-world videos with verifier-guided pseudo-labels also yielded competitive results, sometimes slightly better in localization (δxavg). Mixing data generally improved visibility prediction (OA) due to synthetic ground truth.
- **Non-Learning Baselines:** The learned verifier consistently outperformed fixed, non-learning ensemble heuristics (e.g., geometric median, agreement-based, Kalman-style, minimum acceleration). This indicates that rigid heuristics struggle to capture the diverse failure modes, whereas the verifier's learned approach adapts to varying error patterns more effectively.
- **Synthetic Benchmarks Post-Adaptation:** Track-On-R, despite being fine-tuned on real-world data, also showed improved performance on synthetic long-term tracking benchmarks like PointOdyssey (e.g., +8.3 in δxavg and +5.4% in survival rate). This suggests that the real-world adaptation enhances general long-term tracking robustness without degrading synthetic domain performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of computer vision by addressing a critical challenge in point tracking: the performance degradation of models when transitioning from synthetic training environments to real-world scenarios, particularly in the absence of dense, manually annotated ground truth.

The introduction of the "verifier" module represents a notable methodological advancement. By learning to assess the reliability of tracker predictions on a per-frame basis, the verifier provides a principled and adaptive mechanism for handling the inherent inconsistencies and diverse error profiles of multiple pretrained tracking models. This moves beyond naive pseudo-labeling strategies that often propagate errors and amplify noise.

The key significance and potential impacts include:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The proposed verifier-guided pseudo-labeling framework effectively bridges the domain gap between synthetic training data and real-world videos without requiring costly manual annotations for real data. This enables more practical deployment of advanced point tracking models.
- **Enhanced Supervision Quality:** The verifier improves the quality of pseudo-labels, leading to more robust and accurate fine-tuning of student trackers on unlabeled real-world videos. This high-quality supervision reduces error accumulation and prevents performance collapse when individual teacher models fail.
- **Data-Efficient Adaptation:** The approach facilitates data-efficient adaptation to new, unlabeled real-world videos. The ablation studies suggest that significant performance gains can be achieved with a moderate amount of unlabeled real data, making the framework accessible for various applications.
- **Versatile Tool:** The verifier's dual functionality—both as a training-time supervision selector for pseudo-labeling and as an optional inference-time ensemble mechanism—provides a flexible and general framework for model coordination and uncertainty estimation in video correspondence tasks. This allows it to improve existing trackers and enable future model development.
- **Improved Long-Term Robustness:** The demonstrated performance improvements on challenging real-world benchmarks, including long egocentric videos and robotic manipulation sequences, indicate enhanced long-term tracking coherence and robustness to diverse motion patterns, occlusions, and distribution shifts.
- **Broad Applicability:** Point tracking is a fundamental primitive for various high-level visual understanding and control tasks. Improvements in its reliability and adaptability can positively impact fields such as:
 - **Video Editing:** More accurate motion trajectories for effects and stabilization.
 - **Robot Manipulation and Perception:** Reliable tracking of objects for precise control and interaction.
 - **4D Scene Understanding:** Enhanced reconstruction and understanding of dynamic scenes.
 - **Medical Analysis:** Improved tracking of anatomical structures or medical instruments.

The research also implicitly highlights the ongoing challenge of teacher model quality, noting that the verifier's upper bound is constrained by the performance of its constituent teacher trackers. This suggests future work could focus on developing even stronger and more robust base models to further amplify the benefits of verifier-guided learning.
