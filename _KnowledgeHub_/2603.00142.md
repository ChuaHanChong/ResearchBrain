# Evaluating Theory of Mind and Internal Beliefs in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> An evaluation of multi-agent systems augmented with Theory of Mind and internal beliefs, verified by symbolic logic, reveals that the efficacy of these cognitive enhancements is highly conditional on the underlying LLM's capacity. Top-performing LLMs like ChatGPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet maintain robust performance, while less capable models may benefit or be hindered by the added cognitive load.

## Problem

- Large Language Model (LLM) agents often exhibit inconsistent and variable performance in complex multi-agent systems, particularly when dealing with partial or conflicting information.
- Existing multi-agent systems frequently rely on rigid rules, limiting their adaptability and scalability in dynamic, real-world environments.
- A lack of comprehensive, synergistically integrated architectures that combine Theory of Mind (ToM), internal belief systems, and logical verification within LLM-based multi-agent systems.

## Method

- A novel agent architecture was proposed, integrating ==Theory of Mind (ToM)== capabilities, ==internal belief states (IB)==, and logical verification using an ==Answer Set Programming (ASP) solver==.
- The agents were deployed in an interactive city resource allocation simulation, designed to be dynamic and require effective coordination under resource consumption scenarios.
- A modular agent design allowed for systematic evaluation of four configurations (Base, ToM Only, IB Only, ToM + IB) across diverse LLMs, with iterative logical consistency checking for internal beliefs.

## Results

- ChatGPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet consistently achieved the highest median final health scores (e.g., around **70%** for ChatGPT-4o), showing high reliability in collaborative resource management.
- Less capable LLMs, such as Meta Llama 3.1 8B, showed varied impacts, with performance sometimes improving (e.g., ToM Only at **38.33%** vs. Base **35.00%**) and sometimes declining (e.g., ToM+IB at **30.02%**) when cognitive mechanisms were added.
- For models with restricted reasoning abilities, the additional cognitive load from simultaneously processing ToM and internal beliefs, coupled with iterative logical verification, could lead to miscoordination or decreased overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The choice of the underlying LLM is a dominant factor, with models like ChatGPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet demonstrating superior and more consistent performance regardless of added cognitive mechanisms.
> - The effectiveness of integrating explicit cognitive mechanisms (ToM, internal beliefs with ASP verification) is not universally beneficial and depends critically on the LLM's inherent capacity to process complex information and manage cognitive load.
> - While logical verification of internal beliefs can promote more organized thinking and prevent errors, its ultimate utility is contingent on the LLM's ability to produce consistent and interpretable natural language inputs for the solver.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving and critical fields within Artificial Intelligence: Large Language Models (LLMs), Multi-Agent Systems (MAS), cognitive architectures (specifically Theory of Mind and Belief-Desire-Intention models), and symbolic reasoning.

The advent of powerful LLMs has revolutionized AI, offering unprecedented capabilities in natural language understanding, generation, and complex reasoning. Concurrently, Multi-Agent Systems have long been a robust paradigm for solving distributed and complex problems through coordination and collaboration among independent agents. The synergy between these two fields, leading to LLM-based MAS, is a burgeoning area of research, promising enhanced autonomy, cooperation, and scalability in dynamic environments. Applications range from decentralized decision-making in task and resource allocation to simulating complex human interactions.

Despite the immense potential, the integration of LLMs into MAS presents significant challenges. LLM performance in multi-agent settings can be highly variable and inconsistent, leading to suboptimal decisions, especially under conditions of partial or conflicting information. Most traditional MAS rely on rigid rules and formal communication protocols, limiting their adaptability in real-world, dynamic scenarios.

This paper critically addresses these limitations by focusing on integrating advanced cognitive mechanisms into LLM-based MAS. It draws inspiration from:
- **Theory of Mind (ToM):** The ability to infer and act upon the intentions and beliefs of other agents, shown to significantly enhance collaborative intelligence. Recent research even suggests emergent ToM capabilities in advanced LLMs, leading to the development of benchmarks like OpenToM.
- **Belief-Desire-Intention (BDI) Models:** A well-established framework for formalizing agents' internal belief processes, enabling rational action based on internal representations of the world. While ToM focuses on *others'* mental states, BDI models govern the *agent's own* internal reasoning.
- **LLMs with Symbolic Solvers:** The combination of LLMs' natural language prowess with the systematic and verifiable reasoning of symbolic logic tools (like Answer Set Programming - ASP) is a promising avenue for improving reasoning robustness and consistency.

The existing literature often explores these components in isolation or in limited combinations. For instance, much ToM research in MAS does not fully integrate with internal belief systems and logical verification. Similarly, while LLM-symbolic integration is gaining traction, its application within a multi-agent cognitive architecture, considering internal beliefs and ToM, remains largely underexplored. This work identifies a critical research gap: the lack of a comprehensive, synergistically integrated multi-agent system that combines ToM, BDI-based internal beliefs, and logical verification. By addressing this gap, the paper contributes to the broader effort of making LLM-based MAS more robust, consistent, and genuinely intelligent in collaborative problem-solving. It builds upon foundational work in LLM-based agent frameworks (e.g., MegaAgent, MetaGPT) by adding a layer of sophisticated cognitive processing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the inherent inconsistencies and performance variability observed when deploying powerful Large Language Models in complex, dynamic multi-agent environments. While LLMs excel in standalone reasoning tasks, their application in multi-agent collaboration, especially with partial or conflicting information, often falls short of optimal performance. The authors contend that simply introducing advanced cognitive mechanisms like Theory of Mind (ToM) or internal beliefs does not automatically guarantee improved coordination or decision-making. The precise interplay between these mechanisms, particularly when combined with formal logic verification, remains largely an open question across different LLMs.

To address this challenge, the paper articulates two fundamental research questions:
1. **"How do internal cognitive processes involving symbolic solvers and the Theory of Mind influence collective decision-making in LLM-based multi-agent systems?"** This question aims to investigate the individual and combined effects of these cognitive enhancements on an agent's ability to contribute to collective goals.
2. **"And how does the interaction among those processes influence the system’s accuracy?"** This delves into the synergistic or potentially antagonistic effects when ToM, internal beliefs, and logical verification are integrated, and how these interactions manifest in measurable system performance.

Based on these questions, the paper outlines two main contributions:
- **Proposal of a novel agent architecture:** This architecture integrates Theory of Mind, internal belief states, and logical verification using Answer Set Programming (ASP) to enhance collective intelligence in multi-agent systems. The goal is to provide agents with more sophisticated reasoning capabilities beyond raw LLM inference.
- **Detailed analysis of impact:** To empirically understand the efficacy of the proposed architecture, the authors conduct a thorough evaluation of how different combinations of ToM and internal belief mechanisms influence overall system performance and accuracy, specifically across a range of commercially available and open-source LLMs.

Ultimately, the objective is to move beyond simple LLM-driven agents towards a more cognitively enhanced, robust, and verifiable multi-agent system capable of more reliable and intelligent collaboration in complex, dynamic scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a novel multi-agent architecture and evaluates it within a simulated resource allocation problem. The methodology is structured to systematically assess the impact of integrated cognitive mechanisms across various LLMs.

#### 3.1. Task Environment Description

The chosen task environment is an **interactive city resource allocation simulation**. This environment is designed to be dynamic and challenging, requiring agents to coordinate effectively under resource consumption and potential health decline scenarios.
- **Agents and Resources:** Three agents are assigned specific roles: FOOD_AGENT, MEDICAL_AGENT, and SECURITY_AGENT. Each is responsible for supplying one type of resource.
- **City Structure:** The simulation features four districts (D), with `d1` serving as the supply node and `d2`, `d3`, `d4` as regular districts. These districts form a graph, dictating agent movement.
- **Dynamics:** Resources (`R(d, r, t)`) are consumed each round. District health (`H(d, t)`) starts at 100 units and decreases if resource levels fall below critical thresholds, with the `Health_Decrease` function quantifying this.
- **Actions:** Agents can `MOVE(d)` to adjacent districts or `SUPPLY_RESOURCE(x)` in their current district. They have a carrying capacity (`Cmax`) and start at the supply node.
- **Feedback and Communication:** Agents receive real-time updates on district health, available resources, and their inventory. The system provides feedback for illegal actions. Crucially, agents share information sequentially via a **shared memory** for coordination, while individual agents maintain **private memory** (Beliefs about Others, Internal Beliefs, Action) for individual reasoning.

#### 3.2. Agent Architecture

Each agent's design is modular, facilitating the selective activation of cognitive enhancements.
- **Roles and Initial Prompt:** Agents are initialized with a specific role and a prompt that defines their task, environment rules, objectives (e.g., cooperation, preventing shortages), and the required output format (e.g., "Response" and "Action" sections). The prompt emphasizes communication for non-observable information.
- **Modular Response Components:** Agents generate responses with four key components:
 - **Internal Beliefs (IB):** A summary of the agent's internal justification about the world, represented in a logic-friendly format. This is unshared and forms the basis for individual decisions.
 - **Beliefs on Others (ToM):** The agent's expectation of other agents' likely plans and actions, derived from their roles, observed activity, and inferred intentions. This module supports alignment with team actions.
 - **Response:** A shared natural language message containing observations, resource levels, planned actions, and coordination requests.
 - **Action:** The specific action (MOVE or SUPPLY_RESOURCE) the agent intends to execute.
- **Dynamic Interaction:** The ToM module informs the creation and updating of the "Internal Beliefs" by incorporating expectations about other agents. These internal beliefs are then subjected to consistency checking.

#### 3.3. Logical Tool Integration

A critical aspect of the methodology is the integration of formal logic for verifying internal beliefs.
- **Answer Set Programming (ASP):** The Clingo solver, a tool for Answer Set Programming, is used for logical verification of the agent's "Internal Beliefs." ASP is chosen for its capability in non-monotonic reasoning and its declarative nature, suitable for encoding knowledge and environmental constraints.
- **Verification Pipeline:**
 1. The agent's natural language "Internal Beliefs" are translated into an ASP model.
 2. The Clingo solver checks this model for consistency against pre-established constraints and the agent's knowledge base.
 3. If inconsistencies are detected (e.g., logical contradictions, unsafe variables), the system generates an explanation. This involves finding the minimal subset of conflicting statements using a binary search, then translating these into natural language feedback for the agent.
 4. The agent receives this feedback and has up to three attempts to reformulate its "Internal Beliefs" to achieve logical coherence before executing an action. This iterative process aims to refine the agent's internal reasoning.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

To evaluate the proposed architecture, simulations were run under controlled conditions:
- **Configurations:** Four distinct configurations were tested for each LLM:
 - **Base Case:** Agents with no ToM capabilities ("Beliefs on Others" section disabled) and no Internal Belief mechanism ("Internal Beliefs" section disabled).
 - **ToM Only:** Agents had access to "Beliefs on Others" for reasoning about peers.
 - **IB Only:** Agents used "Internal Beliefs" (subject to ASP verification).
 - **ToM + IB:** Agents had access to both "Beliefs on Others" and "Internal Beliefs" (with ASP verification).
- **LLMs Tested:** A diverse set of LLMs were used: ChatGPT-3.5-Turbo, ChatGPT-4o, ChatGPT-4o-mini, Meta Llama 3.1 8B, and Claude 3.5 Sonnet.
- **Trials:** Multiple trials were conducted for each model and configuration combination, with each trial running for up to 7 turns per agent (21 agent actions).
- **Performance Metric:** The primary metric for evaluating system performance was the **average health across all non-supply districts at the end of the simulation**. This quantifies the agents' success in collaborative resource management.

This comprehensive methodology allows for a robust investigation into how different cognitive enhancements interact with the inherent capabilities of various LLMs in a dynamic, collaborative setting, providing empirical data to answer the central research questions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation revealed a complex and nuanced interaction between the inherent capabilities of the Large Language Models, the integration of Theory of Mind (ToM) and Internal Belief (IB) mechanisms, and the overall system performance in the collaborative resource allocation task.

- **Significant Performance Variability Across LLMs:** The results clearly demonstrated that LLM choice is a dominant factor in system performance.
 - **Top Performers:** **ChatGPT-4o** and **Claude 3.5 Sonnet** consistently achieved the highest median final health scores across all configurations. Their narrow 95% confidence intervals indicated high reliability and robust performance, suggesting these models possess superior inherent reasoning and processing capabilities, allowing them to effectively integrate or manage the cognitive overhead introduced by ToM and IB. ChatGPT-4o, for instance, maintained high scores (around 70%) regardless of the cognitive mechanisms enabled.
 - **Lower Performers:** **ChatGPT-3.5-Turbo** and **Meta Llama 3.1 8B** generally exhibited lower median health scores. ChatGPT-3.5-Turbo consistently scored in the 27-33% range, while Meta Llama 3.1 8B's performance varied significantly, from a high of 38.33% (ToM Only) to a low of 26.34% (ToM + IB).
 - **Intermediate Performer:** **ChatGPT-4o-mini** showed intermediate performance, generally falling between ChatGPT-4o/Claude 3.5 Sonnet and ChatGPT-3.5-Turbo, with scores in the 52-59% range.

- **Inconsistent Impact of ToM and IB Mechanisms:** The integration of ToM and IB was not a universal performance booster; its effectiveness depended heavily on the underlying LLM's capacity.
 - **Potential for Enhancement:** For some models, particularly those with less native reasoning capacity, the cognitive mechanisms *did* show signs of improvement. ChatGPT-3.5-Turbo, for example, saw a modest increase in median health score when ToM and IB were added (from 27.50% in the Base Case to 33.34% with ToM+IB). Similarly, Meta Llama 3.1 8B performed best with ToM Only (38.33%) compared to its Base Case (35.00%). This indicates that when the LLM's core capabilities are insufficient for complex coordination, these explicit cognitive modules can provide beneficial structure and guidance, leading to better decision-making and coordination.
 - **Cognitive Load and Detriment:** Conversely, for other models, or in certain combinations, these mechanisms could either have limited effect or even lead to a decline in performance or increased variability.
 - Meta Llama 3.1 8B's performance *declined* when both ToM and IB were integrated (30.02%) compared to ToM Only (38.33%) or even its Base Case (35.00%). This suggests that for models with more restricted reasoning abilities, the additional cognitive load imposed by simultaneously processing ToM and IB, and the iterative logical verification, can overwhelm the LLM, leading to miscoordination or inconsistent internal states.
 - ChatGPT-4o-mini also exhibited fluctuations, with its ToM Only performance (59.00%) being slightly higher than ToM+IB (55.67%).
 - Even for top performers like ChatGPT-4o, while performance remained high, the inclusion of ToM and IB did not always translate to a significant *improvement* over configurations without them, suggesting its inherent capabilities might already implicitly handle some of these cognitive functions.

- **Role of Information Exchange and ASP Verification:**
 - **Sequential Communication:** The sequential communication protocol influenced agent behavior, requiring agents to infer intentions and plans of others based on shared memory and expected actions.
 - **ToM's Dual Nature:** The "Beliefs on Others" (ToM) module could lead to better coordination through accurate prediction of peers' actions in more advanced models. However, less capable models often made false predictions, leading to miscoordination and sometimes compromising overall performance due to increased cognitive load.
 - **IB and ASP's Benefits:** The "Internal Beliefs" module, supported by the ASP solver for logical verification, generally promoted more organized thinking and helped prevent logical errors. However, its ultimate effectiveness was contingent on the LLM's ability to produce consistent and interpretable natural language inputs for the solver. More refined LLMs were better at leveraging this formal verification process.

In summary, the results highlight that the effectiveness of integrating cognitive mechanisms like ToM and internal beliefs into LLM-based multi-agent systems is highly conditional. It depends critically on the underlying LLM's inherent capacity to process complex information, manage cognitive load, and generate coherent internal representations, rather than being a universally beneficial addition.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of multi-agent systems and the broader landscape of Artificial Intelligence, with substantial potential impact on the design and application of collaborative AI.

#### 5.1. Novel Integrated Architecture for Collaborative AI

The most prominent contribution is the proposal of a **novel agent architecture** that synergistically integrates Theory of Mind (ToM), BDI-style internal beliefs, and symbolic solvers (via Answer Set Programming) into Large Language Model-based multi-agent systems. This addresses a critical gap in current research, where these advanced cognitive mechanisms are often studied in isolation or in less comprehensive combinations. By providing a structured framework for agents to reason about others' intentions, maintain consistent internal world models, and verify their logic, the work lays a robust foundation for building more sophisticated and cognitively capable AI agents. This integrated approach represents a step forward from purely reactive or rule-based agents towards more reflective and truly intelligent collaborative entities.

#### 5.2. Empirical Insights into LLM-Cognitive Mechanism Interaction

The study provides invaluable **empirical insights** into the complex interplay between the innate reasoning capabilities of different LLMs and the explicit cognitive enhancements of ToM and internal beliefs. The finding that these mechanisms are not universally performance-boosting, but rather have a highly conditional impact based on the specific LLM's capacity, is crucial. It challenges the simplistic assumption that "more cognitive features equal better performance." This nuanced understanding is vital for researchers and developers, as it guides them to consider LLM selection, inherent model strengths, and potential cognitive load when designing multi-agent systems. It indicates that the effectiveness of these enhancements may be greatest for models with moderate capabilities, providing structure they might otherwise lack, while potentially overwhelming smaller models or being redundant for highly capable ones.

#### 5.3. Guidance for Robust MAS Design

The research offers practical **guidance for the design of robust and resilient multi-agent systems**. It underscores the importance of:
- **LLM Selection:** The choice of LLM is paramount, as different models demonstrate varying aptitudes for integrating complex cognitive processes without incurring debilitating cognitive load.
- **Careful Mechanism Design:** The implementation of ToM and IB mechanisms needs to be tailored, possibly with adaptive strategies, to the specific LLM and task complexity.
- **Structured Communication:** The role of structured communication protocols and dual-memory systems (shared and private) is highlighted as essential for facilitating reflective and coordinated agent interactions.
- **Logic Verification:** The integration of symbolic solvers like ASP provides a crucial layer of logical consistency checking, moving towards more verifiable and reliable AI decision-making, which is particularly important in high-stakes applications.

#### 5.4. Paving the Way for Future Research

The paper explicitly identifies several promising **directions for future research**, effectively charting a course for advancing collaborative AI:
- **Sophisticated ToM Models:** Moving beyond simple inference to learned models of other agents' goals and behaviors, potentially through continuous learning.
- **Advanced LLM-Symbolic Integration:** Exploring hybrid neural-symbolic architectures that combine the strengths of LLMs for natural language processing and pattern recognition with the precision and verifiability of symbolic reasoning, beyond just ASP.
- **Optimized Communication Protocols:** Addressing the inefficiencies of sequential communication by exploring more dynamic, adaptive, or parallel communication strategies to enhance information flow and coordination efficiency.
- **Scalability and Cognitive Overload:** Investigating methods to optimize system architecture and communication protocols for larger agent populations and more complex tasks, mitigating cognitive overload.
- **Enhanced Explainability:** Incorporating more sophisticated behavioral analysis and formal statistical testing to provide deeper insights into agent decision-making and foster greater transparency.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Exploring lightweight logic engines or distributed computing to balance reasoning depth with computational cost, enabling real-world deployment.

#### 5.5. Broad Potential Applications

The advancements proposed in this work have the potential to significantly impact numerous fields requiring sophisticated coordination and decision-making among autonomous entities:
- **Logistics and Resource Management:** The resource allocation scenario evaluated directly translates to applications in supply chain optimization, disaster relief efforts, and urban planning, where autonomous agents could efficiently manage and distribute critical resources.
- **Healthcare:** Improved multi-agent coordination can enhance clinical decision support systems, optimize resource allocation in hospitals, or manage complex treatment plans.
- **Software Development and Project Management:** LLM-based agents with enhanced cognitive abilities could form collaborative teams for agile software engineering or complex project management, coordinating tasks and resolving conflicts more effectively.
- **Finance:** Applications in financial market analysis and prediction could benefit from agents that can better anticipate market dynamics and coordinate investment strategies.
- **Simulations and Training:** More cognitively realistic LLM-based agents can improve simulations for training purposes, from military strategy to social interaction models.

In conclusion, this research is a foundational step towards building more intelligent, robust, and truly collaborative LLM-based multi-agent systems. By meticulously evaluating the integration of cognitive mechanisms and providing empirical evidence for their conditional effectiveness, it offers critical insights and a roadmap for the ongoing evolution of collaborative AI. The work emphasizes that successful integration requires a multi-faceted approach, balancing model selection, task complexity, and interaction mechanism design to harness LLMs' strengths effectively while mitigating their inherent limitations.
