# AD-WM: Action-Discriminative World Models for Counterfactual Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> AD-WM is a joint-embedding world model built for counterfactual model-predictive control. It predicts latent residual increments and adds predictor-level action-recovery regularization, via an inverse-dynamics head plus a normalized mutual-information objective, so predicted transitions retain action-dependent differences. Auxiliary heads are dropped at test time, leaving CEM planning untouched. Hard-start cube success rises from 3.7% to 52.0%, and zero-shot Franka pick-and-place from 42.2% to 71.1%.

## Problem

- Latent world models are trained to predict factual recorded transitions, but planning requires comparing alternative actions from the same state, a different demand.
- When visual features are dominated by persistent scene content, a predictor can reach low error by copying the current representation, hiding action-dependent changes.
- Prior JEPA planners judge models by factual prediction accuracy and whole-bank ranking, metrics that do not reveal whether the elites retained by CEM are good.

## Method

- **Residual latent prediction**: an action embedding feeds a predictor that estimates the latent increment added to the current state, biasing learning toward local action-driven change.
- ==Inverse-dynamics head== recovers the action embedding from the current and predicted next latents, with a ==stop-gradient== target so gradients shape model-generated transitions, not just encoded ones.
- **Normalized action recovery**: a Gaussian head on batch-standardized action embeddings, a ==Barber-Agakov style conditional-mutual-information bound== with KL shrinkage toward a unit prior.

## Results

- On OGBench-Cube, **AD-WM** beats matched LeWM on every protocol, **90.7%** vs **73.3%** original and **52.0%** vs **3.7%** hard-start, and reaches **25.3%** on the hardest perturbation where rivals hit **10%**.
- Ablations isolate contributions: residual alone gives **34.7%** hard-start, residual plus mutual information **54.7%**, while inverse dynamics adds little at its default weight **0.1**.
- Hard-start success correlates with negative elite regret at **0.863** and **0.810**, but only **-0.399** with whole-bank ranking; the lowest-MSE model, LeWM, has the worst control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimize world models for the comparison the planner actually makes, not factual prediction fidelity, since low reconstruction error can coexist with action-blind dynamics.
> - Evaluation metrics should mirror the planner's selection step; elite-set regret predicts closed-loop success where prediction error and global ranking mislead.
> - Auxiliary objectives that only shape training can be discarded at deployment, giving control gains without changing planner architecture or inference cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot arm must grasp a yellow cube, and the goal is given as an image. The planner has to compare several possible actions from the same starting state. Its world model, however, learns only from the action that actually happened. If the model predicts mostly unchanged scenery, it can score well on factual prediction while giving the planner little basis for choosing between those possibilities.

AD-WM, by Jiabin Qiu, Zixuan Chen, and colleagues, trains the model to predict what happened and also to retain clues about which action produced its predicted change. On specified difficult Cube starts, this raised planning success substantially.

The paper's illustration of the problem, not a measured rollout. Upper, "Prior JEPA world models": candidate-action paths converge on similar predicted states ("Low prediction error can hide differences between actions!"). Lower, AD-WM: action-dependent paths differ and guide selection toward the goal.

### 2. Train the changes that matter

A latent world model encodes images into compact representations and predicts how those representations change under actions. In AD-WM, residual dynamics predicts an increment to the current representation and adds it to that representation to form the next prediction.

Two auxiliary training heads encourage recovery of the action from the current and predicted-next representations:

- Inv (inverse dynamics) predicts the action embedding.
- MI (normalized action recovery) predicts normalized action embeddings.

Gradients from recovery shape model-generated transitions, while factual prediction still anchors them to observed successors.

The key distinction from prior action-recovery work such as the [Delta-JEPA paper](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.31232) is where the signal acts. Delta-JEPA recovers actions from the displacement between *observed* consecutive latent states. AD-WM applies recovery to the model's *predicted* transition, which is what model predictive control (MPC) rolls out.

At test time, both auxiliary heads are discarded. The planner, CEM, still samples candidate action sequences and scores predicted terminal latent distance to the image goal. It then refits to its elite (lowest-cost) sequences, executes the first action block, and replans. The architecture and CEM procedure remain unchanged.

Training (left) and planning (right) with AD-WM. A residual dynamics predictor estimates a latent increment that is added to the current latent. Training-only Inv and MI heads link to the current and predicted next representations and are discarded at deployment. At test time, CEM rolls out candidate action sequences, evaluates terminal planning cost, and updates toward elites.

### 3. Prediction error versus selection

The evidence comes from Cube hard starts. Hard-start success (HS) is the average success rate over five tabletop-start protocols, P00–P04. Each protocol uses 50 episodes per checkpoint, and results cover three training seeds. P02–P04 apply 2–4 cm cube-position perturbations.

Against a matched LeWM reproduction, trained and run under the same conditions, Cube HS is **52.0% ± 3.1 for AD-WM versus 3.7% ± 1.4 for LeWM**. This is an improvement on these specified hard starts, not a general guarantee of robustness to arbitrary distribution shift.

The surprising part is what this gain does *not* track. LeWM ([[2603.19312|LeWorldModel paper]]) has lower one-step factual latent MSE than AD-WM on Cube: 2.72 ± 0.06 versus 4.27 ± 0.10, in units of $10^{-3}$. MSE depends on each model's latent representation and scale.

The authors also measure how the models rank candidate action sequences:

- **CAD** is Spearman rank agreement between model-predicted and environment-realized costs across the entire shared candidate bank. LeWM scores 0.460 ± 0.010 and AD-WM 0.448 ± 0.011.
- **Best-in-elite regret** is the normalized realized cost penalty of the best-realized candidate among the 30 candidates CEM would keep; lower is better. LeWM scores 0.074 ± 0.006 and AD-WM 0.029 ± 0.001.

Across 15 observations (five variants × three seeds), HS correlates with CAD at Spearman ρ = −0.399 and with *lower* best-in-elite regret at ρ = 0.863. Elite regret therefore tracks the observed success ordering more closely in this Cube analysis. The diagnostic uses fixed candidate banks, not adaptive CEM trajectories.

### 4. Prediction for choosing

A model used for planning is valuable when its predicted futures help a planner keep useful alternatives, beyond predicting the recorded future. AD-WM improves mean success over matched LeWM in four of five simulation environments; PushT is the exception. Its clearest support is the Cube contrast between factual error, elite selection, and closed-loop success. That diagnostic is evidence about fixed candidate banks in Cube, not proof that elite regret predicts performance in every planner or environment.
