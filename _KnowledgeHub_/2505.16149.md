# When VLMs Meet Image Classification: Test Sets Renovation via Missing Label Identification

> [!summary] Summary
> REVEAL, a new framework, renovates image classification test sets by identifying and correcting both noisy and missing labels using an ensemble of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and other curation methods. This approach generates soft-labeled outputs with likelihoods, demonstrating high alignment with human annotations, such as a 0.976 agreement rate on CIFAR-10.

## Problem

- Image classification benchmark datasets are compromised by pervasive noisy labels and, critically, overlooked missing labels, leading to misleading model evaluations.
- Existing label cleaning methods primarily focus on correcting incorrectly assigned labels (noisy labels), largely neglecting instances where an image contains multiple valid classes but is only partially annotated.
- These label errors, particularly within test sets, result in unfair comparisons and an unreliable assessment of model performance.

## Method

- The ==REVEAL framework== integrates diverse Vision-Language Models (BLIP, Janus, Qwen, LLaVA) with established label curation methods (Cleanlab, Docta) to identify label errors.
- It employs a ==weighted aggregation strategy== that estimates the expertise of each method against a pseudo ground-truth to build a robust consensus.
- A ==post-aggregation filtering and softmax normalization== step generates soft-labeled outputs with likelihoods, providing a comprehensive and probabilistic representation of image content.

## Results

- REVEAL identified substantial label errors across datasets; for instance, CIFAR-100 contained **552** noisy labels and **6083** missing labels in its test set.
- The framework demonstrated high alignment with human annotations, achieving agreement rates of **0.976** on CIFAR-10, **0.880** on CIFAR-100, and **0.946** on Caltech256.
- The findings provide a systematic analysis of VLM capabilities for data curation, highlighting their distinct failure modes and biases while demonstrating their utility when combined intelligently.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Missing labels are a pervasive and often more extensive problem than noisy labels in benchmark test sets, challenging the assumption of single-label ground truth.
> - While powerful, individual VLMs exhibit specific limitations, including struggles with fine-grained class prediction, repetitive label generation, hallucinations, refusal of null responses, and class-specific biases.
> - REVEAL's ensembling and refinement strategy effectively mitigates the limitations of individual VLMs, consistently achieving superior alignment with human judgments compared to any single VLM.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Image classification is a cornerstone of computer vision, with its progress heavily reliant on high-quality benchmark datasets like CIFAR, MNIST, and ImageNet. However, a well-acknowledged challenge in the machine learning community is the pervasive presence of label errors in these datasets. Previous research, notably by Northcutt et al. and Wei et al., has highlighted that even "clean" test sets can harbor noisy labels, compromising the reliability of model evaluations and comparisons.

This paper builds upon this understanding but identifies a crucial, often overlooked, dimension of label errors: **missing labels**. While much of the existing literature on label noise detection and correction [e.g., 31, 27, 42, 56, 41] focuses on identifying and fixing *incorrectly assigned* labels (noisy labels), the problem of *omitted* labels—where an image contains multiple valid objects, but only a subset (often just one) is annotated as ground truth—has received less attention, particularly in the context of *test sets*. This omission can significantly bias model evaluations, especially for models capable of multi-label recognition.

The advent of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP, BLIP, LLaVA, Qwen-VL, and Janus represents a significant paradigm shift in computer vision. These models, pre-trained on vast quantities of image-text pairs, exhibit remarkable zero-shot and few-shot classification capabilities, enabling them to understand visual content in an open-vocabulary manner. This work strategically leverages the powerful semantic understanding and recognition capabilities of these state-of-the-art VLMs. Unlike traditional label cleaning methods that rely on model feedback or statistical analyses of confusion matrices, VLMs can directly interpret image content and generate descriptive labels, offering a new avenue for data curation.

Thus, this research is situated at the intersection of several critical areas:
1. **Data Quality and Robust Learning:** Continuing the line of work that emphasizes the importance of accurate labels for reliable ML, particularly in test sets.
2. **Multi-Label Classification:** Addressing a fundamental challenge in real-world image understanding where single-label annotations are often insufficient.
3. **Vision-Language Models:** Exploring and pushing the boundaries of VLMs for practical, data-centric tasks beyond their typical application in zero-shot classification or image captioning.
4. **Data-centric AI:** Aligning with the growing movement that prioritizes improving data quality as much as (or more than) refining model architectures, to achieve more robust and trustworthy AI systems.

By focusing on both noisy and, critically, missing labels in test sets and employing cutting-edge VLMs, the paper addresses a significant gap, promising to enhance the foundational benchmarks upon which much of computer vision research is built.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the recognized deficiencies in existing image classification benchmark datasets. Despite rigorous initial cleaning efforts, these datasets, including widely-used ones like CIFAR, MNIST, and ImageNet, still suffer from:
1. **Pervasive Noisy Labels:** Cases where an image is assigned an incorrect label.
2. **Overlooked Missing Labels:** Instances where an image contains multiple valid object classes, but only a single (or incomplete) label is provided as ground truth. This is particularly problematic in "co-exist" image patterns.

These label errors, especially within critical test sets, lead to **misleading model comparisons and unfair evaluations**, as models might be penalized for correctly identifying objects not specified in the original ground truth or rewarded for overfitting to erroneous labels. While the issue of noisy labels has received considerable attention, the challenge of missing labels has been largely overlooked by existing label cleaning methods.

Against this backdrop, the paper's **key objectives** are to:
- **Highlight the critical yet underexplored issue of missing labels** in widely-used image classification test sets, underscoring that images often contain multiple valid classes beyond their single ground-truth annotation.
- **Propose a comprehensive framework, REVEAL,** to systematically address *both* noisy labels and missing label detection in these benchmark test sets.
- **Leverage state-of-the-art pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** as a primary tool for image understanding and label identification, given their robust recognition capabilities and prior knowledge.
- **Provide a thorough systematic analysis of VLMs** (e.g., BLIP, Janus, Qwen, LLaVA) in the context of dataset renovation, highlighting their strengths, limitations, and potential failure modes (e.g., hallucinations, biases) through empirical observations.
- **Enhance label accuracy through a robust ensembling strategy** that aggregates predictions from multiple VLMs and complementary machine/human label curation methods, ensuring high alignment with human judgments.
- **Generate soft-labeled outputs with likelihoods**, providing a more nuanced and informative representation of image content.
- **Demonstrate the effectiveness of REVEAL** in significantly improving the quality of widely-used benchmark test sets across diverse characteristics (e.g., CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Caltech256, ImageNet, QuickDraw, MNIST).

In essence, the paper aims to "renovate" existing test sets to provide a more accurate and comprehensive representation of their visual content, thereby enabling more reliable and meaningful evaluations of image classification models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce **REVEAL (Renovation via Missing Label Identification)**, a comprehensive framework designed to systematically identify and correct both noisy and missing labels in image classification test sets. REVEAL integrates a multi-pronged approach combining advanced Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with established label curation techniques, orchestrated through an intelligent aggregation and refinement pipeline.

**A. Core Components and Workflow (Figure 3):**

1. **Initial Label Assignment by Diverse Methods:**
 - **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The framework leverages four state-of-the-art pre-trained VLMs: BLIP, Janus-Pro-7B, Qwen-VL-Plus, and LLaVA-13B. These models are tasked with independently predicting labels for each image.
 - **Prompting Strategies:** A critical aspect of VLM utilization is effective prompting. The authors systematically evaluate three strategies: Binary Questioning, Direct Multi-Label Selection, and Batched Multi-Label Selection. The latter, where the full label set is partitioned into batches, is chosen as optimal, balancing recall and inference time. Key enhancements to prompting include incorporating reasoning, image descriptions, and label shuffling to mitigate positional bias and leverage VLMs' captioning capabilities.
 - **VLM-Specific Settings:** For BLIP, Image-Text Matching (ITM) confidence scores are used with a threshold to filter low-confidence labels. For all VLMs, a "top-t" filtering strategy limits the maximum number of predicted labels per image to improve precision.
 - **Label Curation Methods:** Two established methods are incorporated to complement the VLMs:
 - **Cleanlab:** A model-agnostic framework based on Confident Learning, designed to identify and correct noisy labels by estimating the joint distribution of noisy and true labels.
 - **Docta:** A framework for auditing dataset credibility by estimating label noise and ranking instances based on alignment between observed and inferred labels, without requiring ground-truth annotations. (Docta's application is limited to datasets with smaller label candidate pools due to computational constraints).

2. **Weighted Aggregation and Consensus Building:**
 - **Pseudo Ground-Truth Generation:** For a small subset of images (e.g., the first 100), preliminary labels from all methods are collected. A voting strategy is then applied, where a label is included in the pseudo ground-truth if it receives support from a minimum number of methods (defined by a threshold *k*). This initial pseudo ground-truth provides a robust reference for evaluating individual method performance.
 - **Model Expertise (Accuracy) Estimation:** Each VLM and curation method's estimated accuracy (`est_acc`) is calculated against the generated pseudo ground-truth. Crucially, a regularization term is introduced to penalize models that produce excessively broad predictions, promoting more precise outputs. This `est_acc` serves as a weight for subsequent aggregation.
 - **Weighted Aggregation:** For each image, a weighted support score is computed for every candidate label. This score is the sum of `est_acc` values of all methods that predicted that specific label, reflecting the aggregate confidence and expertise.

3. **Post-Aggregation Filtering and Normalization:**
 - **Thresholding and Top-N Selection:** The aggregated labels are refined by retaining only those with a support score above a predefined threshold (`τ`) and those ranking among the top *n* highest-scoring labels for that image. This ensures both quality and conciseness.
 - **Softmax Normalization:** The final scores of the filtered labels are normalized using a softmax function, converting them into likelihoods (soft labels). This provides a probabilistic representation of the image's content, allowing for nuanced multi-label assignments.

**B. Experimental Setup:**

- **Datasets:** REVEAL is applied to renovate six widely-used image classification test sets: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Caltech256, ImageNet-1K, QuickDraw, and MNIST.
- **Computational Resources:** VLMs are deployed on NVIDIA A800 GPUs (80GB memory) or accessed via API (for Qwen). Docta and Cleanlab are run on NVIDIA L20 GPU (48GB memory).
- **Hyperparameters:** Specific thresholds and top-K values are tuned for each dataset (e.g., BLIP threshold, labels/prompt for other VLMs, Top-K for final selection) to optimize performance.
- **Human Verification:** The renovation results are critically compared against human annotations collected via MTurk, following a protocol similar to previous work. An "Agreement Rate" metric is defined to quantify consistency between REVEAL's outputs and human judgments across different MTurk categories (given, guessed, both, neither).

By meticulously combining the semantic understanding prowess of VLMs with robust statistical label curation techniques and an intelligent ensemble strategy, REVEAL aims to provide a comprehensive and reliable solution for renovating image classification test sets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields significant insights into the nature of label errors in benchmark datasets and the capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in addressing them. The findings are summarized across ten key observations:

**A. Pervasiveness of Label Errors:**
- REVEAL identifies a substantial number of noisy and, more notably, **missing labels** across all six benchmark test sets (Table 2). For example, CIFAR-100 is found to have 552 noisy labels and 6083 missing labels in its 10,000-image test set, while Caltech256 has 766 noisy and 30,187 missing labels in its 30,607-image test set. This quantitatively confirms that missing labels are a widespread and critical issue, often exceeding the number of noisy labels.

**B. VLM Capabilities and Limitations (Observations 1-7):**
- **Observation 1: Failure in Fine-grained Class Prediction:** VLMs struggle with highly specific or fine-grained labels, often defaulting to more general terms (e.g., "snake" instead of "sea snake"). This issue exacerbates with larger label sets, indicating insufficient grounding in very specific label spaces.
- **Observation 2: Label Repetition and Stalling:** VLMs can get trapped in repetitive loops, generating the same labels multiple times within a single prediction, particularly with large label batches. This suggests a form of generation mode collapse.
- **Observation 3: Irrelevant Associative Reasoning (Hallucination):** Models sometimes generate labels based on weak or imaginative associations rather than direct visual evidence (e.g., predicting "apple" for an image of an "oak tree"), a common VLM hallucination.
- **Observation 4: Refusal of Null Response:** VLMs often fail to produce a "None" response even when no candidate labels are relevant, possibly due to training as cooperative assistants biased against negative outputs.
- **VLM Biases (Figure 6):** An analysis of label distribution on CIFAR-10 reveals distinct prediction biases among VLMs. Qwen strongly overpredicts "bird" and "cat", Janus overpredicts "horse", while LLaVA and BLIP show more balanced distributions. These biases are attributed to prior knowledge or imbalanced training data.
- **Observation 5: Missing Labels as Uncertainty (Small Label Sets):** In datasets with fewer classes (e.g., CIFAR-10, MNIST), missing labels often reflect probabilistic ambiguity or uncertainty in a single true label, rather than actual multiple objects (e.g., distinguishing between "4" and "9" in MNIST).
- **Observation 6: Missing Labels as True Multi-Object Presence (Large Label Sets):** In datasets with many classes (e.g., CIFAR-100, Caltech256), missing labels are more likely to indicate genuine multi-object scenarios where multiple valid classes are present but unannotated (e.g., "forest" also containing "man" and "boy").
- **Observation 7: Semantic Relatedness in Large Label Sets:** For datasets with a very large number of candidate labels (e.g., ImageNet, Caltech256), VLMs tend to suggest semantically similar labels (e.g., "baseball bat" vs. "bat"), indicating their continuous semantic understanding of labels, which differs from discrete one-hot encodings.

**C. REVEAL's Performance and Human Alignment (Observations 8-10):**
- **Estimated Accuracy (Table 1):** The estimated accuracy of different methods varies by dataset. VLMs (BLIP, LLaVA, Qwen) generally perform well on higher-resolution, complex datasets (Caltech256, ImageNet), while traditional methods like Cleanlab and Docta excel on structured or simpler data (MNIST, CIFAR-10). This highlights a complementary relationship.
- **Observation 8: High Alignment with Human Annotations:** REVEAL demonstrates a strong degree of consistency with MTurk human annotations across most datasets, particularly CIFAR-10 (0.976 agreement rate), CIFAR-100 (0.880), and Caltech256 (0.946) (Table 3).
- **Observation 9: Discrepancies Due to MTurk Constraints:** Lower agreement rates on ImageNet (0.569) and QuickDraw (0.630) are attributed to limitations in the MTurk evaluation protocol, which typically validates only two candidate labels. This constraint fails to adequately capture the extensive missing label issues prevalent in these datasets, making the MTurk annotations less reliable for comprehensive multi-label assessment.
- **Observation 10: REVEAL Outperforms Individual VLMs:** Consistently across most datasets, the aggregated REVEAL framework achieves superior alignment with human judgment compared to any individual VLM (Table 3), underscoring the effectiveness of its ensembling and refinement strategy.

In summary, the findings reveal that label errors, especially missing labels, are more widespread than commonly acknowledged. While VLMs offer powerful tools for semantic understanding and label prediction, they exhibit specific failure modes and biases. REVEAL, by intelligently combining these VLMs with other curation methods and a robust aggregation pipeline, effectively mitigates these limitations, yielding highly accurate and human-aligned soft labels for renovated test sets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The REVEAL framework and the accompanying comprehensive analysis offer several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of machine learning and computer vision:

1. **Elevating Dataset Quality and Evaluation Reliability:** By systematically identifying and correcting both noisy and, critically, *missing labels* in benchmark test sets, REVEAL directly addresses a fundamental flaw in current evaluation practices. More accurate test sets lead to more reliable model comparisons, fostering fair competition and ensuring that progress in model architectures genuinely reflects improved performance rather than overfitting to flawed data. This is essential for the integrity of machine learning research.

2. **Addressing an Overlooked Problem in Data Curation:** The paper compellingly highlights the pervasive nature and distinct impact of missing labels, a problem often overshadowed by noisy labels. By providing a robust solution for multi-label imputation, REVEAL paves the way for a more comprehensive understanding and mitigation of data quality issues, pushing the boundaries of data-centric AI.

3. **Harnessing the Power of Vision-Language Models for Data Curation:** This work effectively demonstrates the utility of advanced VLMs not just for classification or captioning, but as powerful tools for automated data auditing and renovation. By dissecting their strengths and limitations (the "10 observations"), the paper offers valuable insights for future development and application of VLMs in data-centric tasks.

4. **Enabling Nuanced Soft-Labeled Benchmarks:** The generation of soft-labeled outputs with likelihoods provides a richer, more semantically informative representation of image content than traditional one-hot encodings. This nuanced labeling can facilitate:
 - **Improved Model Training:** Soft labels can be used for training, allowing models to learn from ambiguity and multi-object presence more effectively.
 - **Enhanced Explainability:** Likelihoods offer a degree of interpretability, showing how strongly different labels are associated with an image.
 - **Broader Task Generalization:** Soft labels are naturally applicable to complex tasks like object detection, scene understanding, and sentinel analysis, where multiple labels with varying confidence are inherent.

5. **Advancing Data-Centric AI and Human-AI Alignment:** REVEAL embodies the principles of data-centric AI by focusing on improving data quality as a primary driver of AI progress. Furthermore, the comparison with human annotations provides a valuable lens for understanding the perceptual gaps between AI models and human judgment. This opens promising avenues for research into building more transparent, trustworthy, and human-compatible AI systems by iteratively aligning model reasoning with human perception.

6. **Extensibility to Other Multimodal Tasks:** The core weighted voting ensemble approach used in REVEAL is not confined to image classification. It can be extended to other multimodal tasks, such as image captioning, to generate more accurate, abundant, and fine-grained image descriptions, thereby improving benchmarks for training and evaluating various multimodal models.

7. **Mitigating Biases through Ensembling:** The aggregation design, which estimates and weights the expertise of different methods, inherently helps correct for biases present in individual VLMs or original datasets. This contributes to more robust and less biased data curation outcomes.

In conclusion, "When VLMs Meet Image Classification" delivers a significant methodological contribution with REVEAL, a framework that provides a scalable and robust solution for renovating critical machine learning benchmarks. By revealing and addressing the pervasive issue of missing labels, the work ensures fairer model evaluations, leverages cutting-edge VLM capabilities, and offers a blueprint for creating more accurate and semantically rich datasets, ultimately fostering more reliable and trustworthy AI development.
