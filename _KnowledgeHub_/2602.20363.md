# Aesthetic Camera Viewpoint Suggestion with 3D Aesthetic Field

> [!summary] Summary
> A method for aesthetic camera viewpoint suggestion in 3D environments learns a 3D aesthetic field by distilling knowledge from 2D aesthetic models into a feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting representation. This approach enables geometry-grounded aesthetic reasoning and efficient viewpoint discovery from sparse input views, demonstrating improved aesthetic prediction stability and higher aesthetic scores for suggested viewpoints compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Single-view aesthetic composition methods are restricted to minor 2D adjustments and cannot reason about significant 3D viewpoint changes crucial for optimal composition.
- Existing 3D viewpoint exploration methods often rely on dense 3D scene data or computationally expensive iterative search algorithms like reinforcement learning.
- Developing a geometry-grounded aesthetic reasoning capability in 3D from sparse input views, allowing for efficient and substantial camera pose adjustments, remains a challenge.

## Method

- Introducing a 3D ==aesthetic field== that encodes spatially varying aesthetic cues directly into a 3D representation, built upon a feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting framework.
- Distilling aesthetic knowledge by training lightweight modules to predict per-Gaussian aesthetic embeddings, using features from a pretrained 2D aesthetic model as the distillation target.
- Employing a two-stage viewpoint search pipeline that first performs coarse sampling of candidate viewpoints along an interpolated trajectory, then refines these candidates using gradient ascent on the differentiable 3D aesthetic field.

## Results

- Achieved higher Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient (PLCC) and Spearman Rank-Order Correlation Coefficient (SRCC) for aesthetic prediction at novel views, reaching PLCC of **0.836** and SRCC of **0.794** on the RE10k dataset with 6 input views.
- Generated camera viewpoints consistently received higher aesthetic scores from external models (e.g., VEN score of 2.20 and SAMPNet score of 2.49 on RE10k with 6 views) compared to RGB-scoring baselines and single-view adjustment methods.
- Demonstrated stable and consistent aesthetic score increases (e.g., 								VEN of **0.46** on RE10k) through gradient-based optimization on the learned 3D aesthetic field, outperforming unstable RGB-scoring gradient ascent.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling aesthetic features from a 2D aesthetic model into a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation provides more stable and consistent aesthetic predictions at novel viewpoints compared to directly scoring rendered RGB images.
> - A 3D aesthetic field, encoded by per-Gaussian aesthetic embeddings, can effectively bridge 2D aesthetic perception with 3D geometric understanding, enabling geometry-grounded reasoning from sparse input.
> - An efficient two-stage search combining coarse sampling along interpolated trajectories with gradient-based refinement on the differentiable 3D aesthetic field allows for effective discovery of aesthetically pleasing camera viewpoints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computational aesthetics and computer vision has seen various attempts to automate or assist in the photographic process, particularly in optimizing image composition. This research primarily addresses the challenge of suggesting aesthetically pleasing camera viewpoints in 3D environments, a task that remains complex due to the inherent 3D dependency of composition and framing.

Existing approaches in this domain generally fall into two categories:

1. **Single-view Adjustment Methods:** These techniques typically operate on a single input image and aim to predict limited camera adjustments, such as cropping, in-plane shifts, or minor rotations, to enhance the existing composition. While they can locally refine framing, their reasoning is confined to a narrow neighborhood around the original viewpoint. They generally lack awareness of the underlying 3D scene geometry, which restricts their ability to discover entirely new and potentially more aesthetic viewpoints that require significant changes in camera position or orientation. Some recent works attempt to incorporate broader viewpoint changes using generative models, but these often rely on hallucinated content, leading to potential geometric inconsistencies with real scenes.

2. **3D Exploration Methods:** This category of research explicitly operates within 3D spatial environments. These methods often employ reinforcement learning (RL) or genetic algorithms to search for optimal viewpoints. While they address the 3D nature of the problem, they typically necessitate access to dense, high-quality visual inputs or prebuilt 3D environments, which are resource-intensive to construct or acquire. Furthermore, RL-based solutions involve iterative, step-by-step exploration, which can be computationally costly and impractical for real-time applications or scenarios with sparse initial data.

This paper positions itself as a solution to the limitations of both categories by introducing a method that enables geometry-grounded aesthetic reasoning in 3D from *sparse* scene captures, aiming for efficient viewpoint suggestions without the need for dense data or extensive RL searches. It leverages advancements in 3D Gaussian Splatting and feature distillation to bridge the gap between 2D aesthetic perception and 3D geometric understanding, an area largely unexplored in the context of viewpoint-dependent aesthetic information. The work aims to establish a new direction toward 3D-aware aesthetic modeling that is robust to sparse input and offers efficient inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that can identify aesthetically pleasing camera viewpoints for a given 3D scene, even when observed from only a sparse set of input views. This is formulated as finding the camera pose that maximizes a predicted aesthetic score across a continuous view space.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and opportunities:

- **Inherent 3D-Dependency of Aesthetics:** The aesthetic quality of an image is strongly influenced by the camera's viewpoint, as framing, composition, and spatial relationships change significantly with viewing position. Experienced photographers intuitively reason about 3D spatial layout from multiple angles to anticipate how visual appeal varies. The goal is to enable machines to emulate this capability.
- **Limitations of Current Approaches:**
 - **Single-view methods** are constrained by their 2D-only understanding and lack the ability to reason about significant 3D viewpoint changes that might be necessary for optimal composition. They cannot reliably introduce or exclude scene elements by moving the camera in 3D space.
 - **3D exploration methods**, while operating in 3D, are often impractical due to their reliance on dense 3D captures (e.g., from NeRFs or simulations) or costly iterative search procedures (e.g., reinforcement learning). This makes them less suitable for scenarios where only sparse input data is available or efficient, non-iterative suggestions are required.
- **Need for Geometry-Grounded Aesthetic Reasoning:** There is a requirement for a solution that grounds aesthetic reasoning in scene geometry, allowing for reliable predictions of aesthetic quality beyond the observed views and ensuring geometric consistency.
- **Demand for Efficient Viewpoint Discovery:** The process of finding optimal viewpoints needs to be efficient, avoiding the computational overhead associated with dense data reconstruction or extensive exploration algorithms.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives are:

1. **Introduce the concept of a 3D aesthetic field:** This field is intended to encode spatially varying aesthetic cues directly within a 3D representation, enabling geometry-grounded aesthetic reasoning.
2. **Develop a mechanism to learn this 3D aesthetic field from sparse observations:** This involves distilling high-level aesthetic knowledge from a pretrained 2D aesthetic model into a 3D scene representation, specifically using a feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting network. This aims to predict per-Gaussian aesthetic features, allowing aesthetic quality evaluation for novel viewpoints.
3. **Propose an efficient viewpoint search pipeline:** This pipeline is designed to leverage the differentiable nature of the learned 3D aesthetic field, combining a coarse search for promising candidates with gradient-based refinement to identify appealing viewpoints effectively.

The overarching motivation is to advance automated aesthetic viewpoint suggestion, benefiting applications such as personal photography, view selection and planning in virtual/augmented reality (VR/AR), and autonomous systems that require intelligent camera placement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on learning a "3D aesthetic field" from sparse input views and subsequently using this field within a two-stage search pipeline to suggest aesthetically pleasing camera viewpoints.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task is formulated as finding the optimal camera pose P* that maximizes a predicted aesthetic score, score(P), over a continuous 3D view space. Directly optimizing this objective is challenging due to the need for 2D projection and evaluation. The authors propose approximating this by learning a 3D aesthetic field that provides a continuous, geometry-grounded mapping from camera pose to aesthetic quality.

#### 3.1. Distilling the 3D Aesthetic Field

The core of the method involves distilling aesthetic knowledge into a 3D representation based on a feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting framework.

- **Feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting Backbone:** The system builds upon DepthSplat, a feedforward Gaussian Splatting model. Given sparse input views (images) and their corresponding camera poses, a multi-view transformer extracts features. These features are then aggregated across neighboring views using plane-sweep techniques and enhanced with monocular depth cues to predict per-pixel depth. This depth information is used to determine the center (μ) of each 3D Gaussian. A DPT head then regresses the remaining per-Gaussian parameters: covariance (Σ), opacity (α), and color (c). This backbone provides an efficient and differentiable 3D representation.

- **Addressing Direct RGB Scoring Instability:** A naive approach would be to render novel views from the 3D Gaussians and directly score them using a pretrained 2D aesthetic model. However, the authors identify two issues:
 1. **Sensitivity to Pixel Variations:** 2D aesthetic models are often sensitive to minor pixel-level changes, leading to fluctuating scores even for geometrically similar, adjacent views.
 2. **Rendering Artifacts:** Artifacts in novel view renderings (e.g., noise, blurriness) can bias aesthetic predictions and mislead optimization.
 To mitigate these, the method shifts from pixel-level scoring to feature-level reasoning.

- **Aesthetic Feature Distillation:** This is a key contribution designed to achieve stable and consistent aesthetic predictions.
 1. **Teacher Model:** A pretrained 2D aesthetic teacher model, VEN, is used. An intermediate layer's features (e.g., 14 × 14 × 512) are selected as the distillation target.
 2. **Aesthetic Modules:** Lightweight modules are added on top of the Gaussian Splatting backbone:
 - **CNN Aesthetic Encoder:** This encoder, derived from the teacher's feature extraction layers, processes input images to produce multi-scale aesthetic features.
 - **Aesthetic DPT Head:** This head fuses the aesthetic features with the multi-view features from the backbone and regresses a compact (e.g., 32-dimensional) per-Gaussian aesthetic embedding (f_aes).
 - **Transformer Downsampler:** This module aligns the rendered aesthetic feature maps with the teacher's feature maps, which are typically smaller and deeper.
 3. **Rendering Aesthetic Features:** The predicted per-Gaussian aesthetic embeddings, along with other Gaussian attributes, are rendered into aesthetic feature maps (F_pred) at novel viewpoints using the same rasterization pipeline as RGB rendering. These rendered features are then fed into the remaining layers of the pretrained 2D aesthetic teacher model (aesthetic decoder) to obtain an aesthetic score.
 4. **View-Conditioning:** The model is conditioned on camera poses at both input and novel views to capture the inherent viewpoint-dependency of aesthetic representations.

- **Training Objective:** The backbone multi-view transformer, DPT head, and the aesthetic encoder are kept frozen to maintain consistent geometry predictions and 2D aesthetic perception. The additional aesthetic modules are trained end-to-end using a mean-squared error (MSE) loss between the rendered aesthetic feature maps (F_pred) and the ground-truth aesthetic feature maps (F_gt), which are obtained by feeding ground-truth images into the pretrained aesthetic teacher model on hold-out views.

#### 3.2. Viewpoint Search Pipeline

The learned 3D aesthetic field, being continuous and differentiable, enables an efficient two-stage search for optimal viewpoints.

- **Stage 1: Coarse Sampling:**
 1. **Trajectory Generation:** The input views are connected to form a continuous camera trajectory by interpolating their positions and orientations.
 2. **Candidate Generation:** Viewpoints are linearly sampled along this interpolated trajectory. For each sampled viewpoint, a local neighborhood of perturbed cameras is generated, incorporating small in-plane shifts and directional jitters to explore the immediate vicinity while maintaining focus on the scene.
 3. **Scoring and Selection:** Each candidate viewpoint is evaluated using the distilled aesthetic field (rendering aesthetic features and decoding to a score). The top-K highest-scoring candidates are selected for the next stage. A distance-based duplication check is applied to enhance diversity among selected candidates.

- **Stage 2: Gradient-based Refinement:**
 1. **Initialization:** The top-K candidates from Stage 1 serve as initial camera poses.
 2. **Gradient Ascent:** For each candidate, local optimization is performed directly on its camera pose to further maximize the aesthetic score. The camera pose is parameterized by 3D translation and two rotation variables (yaw and pitch, as roll is typically less critical). Gradient ascent is applied: `P_t+1 = P_t + η∇_P score(P_t)`, where η is the step size.
 3. **Optimization:** The Adam optimizer is used for a fixed number of iterations. The final refined viewpoints are returned as aesthetic suggestions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate the proposed method on its ability to predict aesthetics at novel views and to suggest aesthetically appealing camera viewpoints.

#### 4.1. Aesthetic Prediction at Novel Views

- **Quantitative Correlation:** The method was tested on RE10k and DL3DV datasets, using 2, 4, or 6 input views to build the aesthetic field and predicting scores for remaining frames. Compared to an RGB-scoring baseline (which directly scores rendered images), the proposed method achieved significantly higher Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient (PLCC) and Spearman Rank-Order Correlation Coefficient (SRCC) with the teacher aesthetic model's scores across all settings. For instance, with 6 input views on RE10k, the method yielded PLCC of 0.836 and SRCC of 0.794, compared to the baseline's 0.745 and 0.701, respectively. This indicates that the distilled field provides more stable and faithful aesthetic predictions.
- **Qualitative Stability:** Visualizations demonstrate that the RGB-scoring baseline produces highly fluctuating scores for consecutive, nearly identical frames due to sensitivity to rendering artifacts and minor pixel variations. In contrast, the proposed aesthetic field generates smoother and more consistent score predictions that align more closely with the overall trend of the teacher scores, implying a more robust understanding of viewpoint-dependent aesthetics. This stability is attributed to learning in a latent feature space, which implicitly regularizes the score landscape.

#### 4.2. Aesthetic Viewpoint Suggestion

- **Quantitative Superiority:** The method was evaluated by comparing the aesthetic scores of suggested viewpoints using two external aesthetic models (VEN and SAMPNet). Across various numbers of input views (2, 4, 6) and both datasets, the proposed method consistently recommended viewpoints with higher aesthetic scores than the direct RGB-scoring baseline and adapted single-view adjustment methods (UNIC, Uchida et al., and approximations of in-plane shift and rotation methods). For example, with 6 input views on RE10k, the method achieved a VEN score of 2.20 and SAMPNet of 2.49, outperforming the best single-view approximation (Rotation) which scored 2.13 and 2.45, respectively.
- **Robustness to Sparsity:** The method demonstrated strong performance even with minimal input (two views), and its performance progressively improved as the number of input views increased, indicating effective utilization of broader scene coverage.
- **Qualitative Composition:** Qualitative examples illustrate that the proposed framework identifies visually balanced and well-composed views, aligning with human aesthetic preferences. In contrast, single-view methods often failed to address broader compositional issues, such as removing distracting objects or maintaining structural continuity, due to their limited adjustment range. The proposed method enables the camera to be freely placed to enhance composition.
- **3D Visualization:** Visualizations of sampled viewpoints, colored by their predicted aesthetic scores, show how aesthetic quality varies across 3D space, with higher scores corresponding to renderings that exhibit strong alignment with human perceptual preferences.

#### 4.3. Aesthetic Field Gradient Optimization Analysis

- **Effective Gradient Ascent:** A comparison of gradient ascent using the proposed aesthetic field versus the RGB-scoring baseline showed that the proposed method achieved consistent and significant aesthetic score increases (e.g., ∆VEN of 0.46 on RE10k) after 25 optimization steps. The RGB-scoring baseline, however, often suffered from unstable updates and sometimes led to degraded results. This indicates that the distilled aesthetic field provides a well-behaved and optimizable landscape.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **View-Conditioning:** Ablation experiments confirmed that explicitly conditioning the model on camera poses significantly improved aesthetic prediction accuracy (higher PLCC and SRCC), underscoring the importance of modeling viewpoint dependence for aesthetic cues.
- **Search Configurations:** Analysis of the number of candidates in coarse sampling and refinement steps showed that performance gains saturated around 2 candidates and 25 gradient ascent steps, justifying the chosen default parameters.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel framework for 3D-aware aesthetic viewpoint suggestion that addresses several limitations of existing approaches. By learning a 3D aesthetic field through feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting and distilling aesthetic knowledge from 2D models, the method enables geometry-grounded aesthetic reasoning and efficient viewpoint discovery from sparse input views.

**Key advancements include:**
- **Bridging 2D Aesthetics and 3D Geometry:** The proposed 3D aesthetic field effectively unifies 2D aesthetic perception with 3D geometric understanding. This allows for reasoning about aesthetic variations across viewpoints in a spatially consistent manner, which was a challenge for prior single-view methods.
- **Efficiency and Sparsity Robustness:** Unlike 3D exploration methods that require dense scene captures or costly reinforcement learning, this framework operates efficiently from sparse observations. This significantly broadens its applicability to real-world scenarios where dense 3D data might not be readily available.
- **Stable Aesthetic Prediction and Optimization:** By distilling aesthetic features rather than directly scoring rendered RGB images, the method achieves more stable and consistent aesthetic predictions, leading to a well-behaved, differentiable optimization landscape for viewpoint refinement. This overcomes the sensitivity and artifact-induced errors observed in direct RGB scoring.

**Potential Impact and Applications:**
The framework has implications across various domains:
- **Personal Photography:** It can assist users in capturing more aesthetically pleasing photographs by suggesting optimal camera poses, moving beyond simple cropping or minor adjustments.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality (VR/AR):** The ability to efficiently suggest appealing viewpoints can enhance view selection and planning in immersive environments, improving user experience and narrative presentation.
- **Autonomous Systems:** Robots and drones equipped with cameras could utilize this technology for intelligent view planning, object inspection, or aesthetic scene capture, moving towards more sophisticated visual decision-making.

**Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors identify several areas for future improvement:
- **Reliance on Camera Poses:** The current method relies on known camera poses. Developing a pose-free variant, potentially by integrating recent advances in pose-free 3D Gaussian Splatting, would expand its applicability.
- **Reconstructed Geometry Accuracy:** The quality of the aesthetic field is influenced by the accuracy of the reconstructed geometry. Future work could explore stronger geometry backbones or active perception strategies to ensure sufficient scene coverage and improve reconstruction quality.
- **Search Space Expansion:** The current viewpoint search is constrained to regions supported by initial observations. Incorporating an active-perception loop to acquire additional views in promising directions could dynamically expand the aesthetic field and the feasible search space.

Overall, this research establishes a foundation for 3D-aware aesthetic modeling with sparse input, offering a pathway toward more intelligent and efficient viewpoint suggestion systems.
