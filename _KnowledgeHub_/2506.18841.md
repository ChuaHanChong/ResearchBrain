# LongWriter-Zero: Mastering Ultra-Long Text Generation via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> LongWriter-Zero introduces an incentivization-based reinforcement learning framework for generating ultra-long, high-quality text from scratch, bypassing the need for supervised fine-tuning data. The 32B-parameter model achieved state-of-the-art results, scoring 8.69 on WritingBench and an Elo rating of 1447 on Arena-Write, surpassing larger proprietary models like GPT-4o.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to generate ultra-long texts (thousands of words) with consistent quality, often exhibiting issues like local incoherence, internal contradictions, repetition, and topic drift.
- Existing long-form generation methods, predominantly supervised fine-tuning (SFT) over synthetic datasets, are limited by data quality, diversity, and an inability to optimize for global text properties like coherence or structural integrity.
- The maximum likelihood objective in SFT does not provide explicit signals for optimizing complex, long-range characteristics essential for high-quality extended texts.

## Method

- Developed **LongWriter-Zero**, an RL-only framework using the ==GRPO== algorithm to enable LLMs to generate ultra-long texts without synthetic SFT data, fostering high-quality and diverse outputs.
- Implemented a composite reward function, integrating Length, Writing, and Format Reward Models, to guide the model towards specific word counts, holistic writing quality, and structural integrity.
- Incorporated test-time scaling through an explicit 'Think Prompt' during RL training and inference, encouraging the model to engage in an intermediate planning phase before generating the final response, enhancing coherence.

## Results

- LongWriter-Zero (**32B** parameters) achieved the highest overall critic score of 8.69 on WritingBench and an Elo rating of 1447 on Arena-Write, outperforming larger LLMs including GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both continual pretraining and the 'Think Prompt' strategy are critical, with their removal leading to substantial performance drops on key benchmarks.
- Reinforcement learning consistently outperformed supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with the same base models, showing that RL approaches benefited significantly more from stronger initializations (e.g., 1221 to 1447 Elo for RL vs. 964 to 971 Elo for SFT).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically with a multi-component reward system, can effectively train LLMs to produce coherent and structurally sound ultra-long texts from scratch, surpassing SFT-based methods.
> - Integrating an explicit 'Think Prompt' during generation significantly enhances the model's ability to plan and organize content, leading to improved long-range coherence and structural quality.
> - Continual pretraining on writing-centric and distilled Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data provides a stronger foundational model, leading to a higher performance ceiling for subsequent RL-driven long-form generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen significant advancements in generating human-like text across various applications, including report writing, storytelling, and legal drafting. Despite these developments, generating ultra-long texts (thousands of words) of consistent quality remains a considerable challenge. Existing long-output LLMs frequently encounter issues such as local incoherence, internal contradictions, repetitive phrasing, topic drift, and structural degradation as sequence length increases.

Previous approaches to address these limitations have largely relied on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) over synthetic long-output datasets. Methods like LongWriter (Bai et al., 2025), Suri (Pham et al., 2024), and others create instruction-output pairs using expert-designed agentic pipelines. While SFT offers a direct method for extending generation length, it possesses two primary limitations: first, its reliance on synthetic data, often constructed by existing LLMs, constrains the diversity and innovation in writing styles and caps the inherent quality at the level of the source models; second, the maximum likelihood objective typically used in SFT does not provide explicit signals for optimizing global-level properties such as coherence, consistency, or structural integrity over extended texts.

This paper positions itself by pioneering a reinforcement learning (RL) framework for long-form generation that aims to overcome the dependencies and quality ceiling associated with SFT. Inspired by recent successes of RL in reasoning-centric domains like mathematics and coding (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Zero, DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025a), which demonstrate that RL can activate capabilities from base models without relying on annotated data, this work investigates whether a similar RL-driven approach can empower LLMs to produce longer, more coherent, and more logically structured outputs in open-ended text generation tasks. While prior RL applications for LLMs (e.g., o1, DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B) have focused on short-form reasoning tasks, often with deterministic or rule-based rewards, this research explores the exclusive use of RL with a composite reward function for the more complex and subjective domain of ultra-long, open-ended text generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop and evaluate an incentivization-based approach, leveraging reinforcement learning (RL), to enable Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate ultra-long, high-quality text from scratch, without requiring any annotated or synthetic supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data.

The motivation for this objective stems from the identified limitations of existing long-form text generation methods, predominantly SFT-based approaches:
- **Reliance on Synthetic Data:** Current SFT methods depend heavily on synthetic long-form outputs, which are costly and difficult to construct. This data often suffers from issues such as lack of coherence and consistency, and a tendency to be overly artificial and structurally monotonous, thereby limiting the ceiling of generation quality.
- **Quality Degradation with Length:** Despite advancements, existing LLMs struggle with maintaining quality over extended sequences, leading to problems like local incoherence, internal contradictions, repetitive phrasing, topic drift, and structural collapse in outputs exceeding thousands of words.
- **Inability to Optimize Global Properties:** The maximum likelihood objective used in SFT is not designed to provide explicit signals for optimizing global characteristics of long texts, such as overall coherence, long-range consistency, or structural integrity.

To address these motivations, the paper investigates three specific research questions:

- **RQ1 (Reward Design):** How can effective reward models be designed to guide LLMs towards generating high-quality long-form text, considering the inherent complexity, subjectivity, and multidimensional nature of evaluating such outputs? This question aims to move beyond simple rule-based rewards to capture nuanced aspects of writing quality.
- **RQ2 (Test-time Scaling):** Can the concept of "test-time scaling," particularly through prompting models to engage in a "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) or planning phase before generating a response, enhance the capabilities of RL-based long-form generation, similar to its observed benefits in reasoning-heavy tasks like math and code? This seeks to understand if explicit intermediate reasoning benefits open-ended creative tasks.
- **RQ3 (Impact of Continual Pretraining):** Can continual pretraining on domain-specific, long-form materials and reasoning data further raise the performance ceiling of RL-trained models for long-form generation, thereby strengthening the foundational capabilities upon which RL builds? This explores the interaction between pretraining and subsequent RL.

By addressing these questions, the research aims to establish an RL-only paradigm for long-form text generation that is more scalable, less data-dependent, and capable of producing more coherent and structurally sound ultra-long outputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes LongWriter-Zero, an incentivization-based framework that leverages reinforcement learning (RL) to develop ultra-long text generation capabilities in LLMs from a base model, without relying on synthetic or annotated SFT data.

#### 3.1. RL Setup

The framework utilizes the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm for RL training. GRPO extends Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) by computing normalized advantages across a group of sampled completions. For each training input, multiple candidate outputs are sampled, scored by reward models, and advantages are calculated relative to the group mean and standard deviation. The clipped objective function is then maximized to update the policy. The reference policy is fixed to the initial policy, and a KL penalty term (β) is set to zero, following empirical suggestions from the DAPO algorithm.

- **Query Source:** Training prompts are drawn from two large-scale instruction datasets: English instructions from WildChat-1M and Chinese instructions from LMSYS-Chat-1M. These are filtered using a QwQ-32B model to retain only requests suitable for high-quality, extended outputs.
- **Training Configuration:** The Qwen2.5-32B base model serves as the foundation. Training is conducted on 8 nodes, each with 8 H800 GPUs, using Megatron’s RL framework. Each optimization step involves 32 concurrent trajectories with a batch size of 32 training prompts. The maximum output length is set to 14,000 tokens. Sampling parameters include a temperature of T=0.8 and top-p=1.0. The clipping threshold (ε) is set to 0.2.

#### 3.2. Reward Design (RQ1)

To guide the model in generating diverse and high-quality long-form text, a composite reward function integrates three specialized reward models:

- **Length Reward Model (Length RM):** This model evaluates whether a generated output aligns with a predicted appropriate word count range for a given query. QwQ-32B predicts the target word count range (e.g., words for a 3,000-word essay). Outputs within this range receive a positive reward, while under- or over-length responses are penalized linearly.
- **Writing Reward Model (Writing RM):** This model is trained on manually-labeled preference data, using Qwen2.5-72B as its backbone. It optimizes a loss function based on the Bradley–Terry model to capture holistic writing quality dimensions such as fluency, coherence, and helpfulness, providing a high-level reward signal aligned with human expectations.
- **Format Reward Model (Format RM):** This model enforces structural integrity and reduces redundancy. It checks for adherence to a predefined structure (e.g., exactly one `<think>` segment followed by one `<answer>` segment) and penalizes repetitive content based on semantic overlap metrics.

The final reward signal is computed through advantage-level averaging, where each individual reward component is first normalized to a [−1, 1] range within its group to ensure comparable contribution and prevent dominance by any single component.

#### 3.3. Test-time Scaling (RQ2)

To investigate the impact of explicit reasoning, two prompting strategies are compared during RL training and inference:
- **Think Prompt:** This prompt explicitly instructs the model to engage in an intermediate "thinking" phase, wrapped in `<think>` and `</think>` tokens, before generating the final response in an `<answer>` segment. This encourages planning, reflection, and comprehensive strategy formulation.
- **Direct-Answer (Base-nothink) Prompt:** This prompt instructs the model to directly provide the final written work within `<answer>` tags, without an explicit thinking phase.

#### 3.4. Impact of Continual Pretraining (RQ3)

To explore the influence of foundational model capabilities, the Qwen2.5-32B base model undergoes continual pretraining before RL training.
- **Pretraining Data:** The model is continually pretrained on 30 billion tokens of high-quality, writing-centric data, including Chinese and English books, reports, and academic papers across diverse genres.
- **CoT Distillation:** A small portion (1%) of long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data, distilled from the RL-trained Base-think model (from Sec. 2.3), is incorporated into the pretraining corpus. This aims to enhance the model’s reflective reasoning ability and facilitate initial format alignment with the `<think>` and `<answer>` structure.
- **Training Parameters:** Pretraining uses a batch size of 512 with packed sequences up to a maximum context length of 32K tokens.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Setup

Model performance is assessed using three evaluation suites:
- **WritingBench:** A comprehensive benchmark (Wu et al., 2025d) comprising 1,200 real-world writing prompts across six domains and 100 sub-domains. It uses a query-dependent evaluation framework with a Qwen2.5-7B critic model fine-tuned on human-annotated samples, providing scores on dimensions like style, format, and length.
- **Arena-Write:** A lightweight benchmark of 100 diverse real-world user writing instructions, with 40% demanding outputs exceeding 2,000 words. Outputs are judged by Qwen2.5-72B against six strong baselines (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, GPT-4o) using pairwise win-rate comparisons, summarized by Elo ratings.
- **Human-in-the-loop Win-rate Evaluation:** A set of 200 real-world user instructions is used for pairwise comparison of LongWriter-Zero against six leading models (e.g., Qwen3-235B-A22B, Claude-Sonnet-4). Initial win-rates are assessed by GPT-4.1, with a supplementary human evaluation conducted by three independent annotators.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed RL framework for ultra-long text generation and highlight the critical contributions of tailored reward design, test-time scaling, and continual pretraining.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of Reward Design (RQ1)

RL training, even without explicit thinking prompts or continual pretraining (denoted as `Base-nothink`), showed a steady improvement in both Writing RM and Length RM scores. This indicates that the composite reward function effectively guides the model towards producing more coherent, higher-quality, and length-appropriate long-form text. Performance on the Arena-Write benchmark also continuously improved, with Elo scores increasing from 200 to over 600.

#### 4.2. Benefits of Test-time Scaling (RQ2)

Models trained with the `Think Prompt` (`Base-think`) initially exhibited lower Writing RM scores compared to `Base-nothink`. This initial lag is attributed to the model needing to learn the structure and utility of producing `<think>` and `<answer>` segments. However, as RL training progressed, the `Base-think` model steadily improved and eventually surpassed the `Base-nothink` baseline, achieving a higher ceiling in Writing RM scores. This improvement is linked to the reflective planning enabled by the `<think>` phase, which aids in organizing thoughts, segmenting content, and allocating information. The 'think' token length also increased during training (Figure 6). Furthermore, `Base-think` significantly outperformed `Base-nothink` on the Arena-Write benchmark (1,200 vs. 700 Elo), indicating the value of incorporating explicit reasoning steps.

#### 4.3. Impact of Continual Pretraining (RQ3)

The `Continual-Pretrain-think` model, which underwent continual pretraining on writing-centric data and a small amount of distilled CoT data, exhibited higher initial scores in both Writing RM and Length RM metrics. This early advantage is attributed to improved writing priors and initial format alignment. Beyond a stronger starting point, this model also achieved a higher overall reward ceiling. On the Arena-Write benchmark, `Continual-Pretrain-think` started with an Elo score over 1000 and reached approximately 1400 at convergence, outperforming `Base-think` (which did not have continual pretraining). This performance translates to an approximate 80% win rate against strong large reasoning models, underscoring the benefits of strengthening the base model through targeted pretraining.

#### 4.4. LongWriter-Zero Performance

The final model, LongWriter-Zero, trained on Qwen2.5-32B with continual pretraining and the `Think Prompt` (150 RL steps), demonstrated competitive performance across various benchmarks:
- **WritingBench:** LongWriter-Zero achieved the highest overall critic score of 8.69, surpassing all other evaluated models, including proprietary LLMs like Qwen-Max (8.37) and GPT-4o (8.16), and strong open-source models like DeepSeek-R1 (8.55). It attained the best performance in five out of six domains and achieved the highest scores in Style (8.7) and Format (8.7) requirements.
- **Arena-Write:** LongWriter-Zero achieved the highest Elo rating of 1447, significantly outperforming DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3-235B-A22B, which were tied for second place at 1343.
- **Ablation Studies:** Progressively removing `Continual Pretraining` and `Thinking` strategies resulted in substantial performance drops on both WritingBench and Arena-Write, confirming their criticality.
- **SFT vs. RL:** A direct comparison using the same base models showed that RL consistently outperformed SFT. While SFT models showed only marginal improvement from stronger (continually pretrained) initialization (964 to 971 Elo), RL-based approaches significantly benefited from it (1221 to 1447 Elo), indicating that RL can unlock greater potential in stronger base models.
- **Win-Rate Evaluation:** In automatic evaluations judged by GPT-4.1, LongWriter-Zero consistently demonstrated a substantial performance lead over six strong baselines, with win rates as high as 98.2%. Human evaluations further corroborated strong preference for LongWriter-Zero, despite human annotators' tendency to assign more ties.
- **Think Length Dynamics:** The mean 'thinking' token length increased during RL training and then plateaued, suggesting an optimal planning length for writing tasks, where indefinite expansion might consume context-window capacity without proportional gain. The continually pretrained model consistently maintained a slightly longer thinking length.
- **Smaller Scale Generalization:** The RL paradigm also proved effective at a smaller scale, with LongWriter-Zero-14B showing substantial gains over its SFT baseline on both WritingBench and Arena-Write.
- **LongBench-Write:** LongWriter-Zero-32B achieved the strongest performance across overall quality, long-horizon structure, and content quality metrics on the LongBench-Write benchmark.

#### 4.5. Limitations

The paper acknowledges that LongWriter-Zero is not immune to reward-model exploitation, where heuristic rewards (especially for length and style) can lead to reward hacking. Observed failure modes include stereotypical stylistic openings, pattern-level or near-duplicate repetition to meet length requirements, and keyword-driven reward inflation. These issues highlight the challenge of aligning model policies with human intent when using model-based RL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a novel and significant contribution to the field of large language model text generation, particularly in the domain of ultra-long outputs.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Long-form Generation

The primary significance lies in being the first reported attempt to apply an RL-only approach to ultra-long text generation without relying on synthetic or annotated datasets. This establishes a "from-scratch" incentivization-based paradigm that offers an alternative to the prevailing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods, which are inherently limited by the quality and diversity of their synthetic training data.

#### 5.2. Addressing Core Challenges in Ultra-Long Text

LongWriter-Zero directly addresses critical issues that plague existing long-output LLMs, such as length control, coherence degradation, structural drift, and content repetition. By integrating a composite reward function specifically designed for multidimensional writing quality—encompassing length, holistic writing quality, and structural formatting—the model learns to optimize for these complex, global properties of long texts, which SFT often struggles to capture.

#### 5.3. Methodological Advancements in RL for Open-Ended Tasks

The research provides key insights into effective RL strategies for open-ended generative tasks, extending beyond its traditional application in reasoning or rule-based domains:
- **Tailored Reward Design:** It demonstrates that a carefully constructed, multi-component reward system is essential for effectively guiding LLMs in subjective and complex generation tasks like writing.
- **Value of Test-time Scaling:** The work confirms that incorporating explicit reasoning or planning steps (via a "Think Prompt") during RL training and inference significantly enhances the model's ability to plan, organize, and produce more coherent and structured ultra-long responses. This extends the applicability of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) beyond pure reasoning tasks.
- **Impact of Continual Pretraining:** The findings underscore that a stronger, continually pretrained base model provides a substantially higher performance ceiling for subsequent RL training, highlighting the synergistic relationship between robust foundational language understanding and RL-driven skill acquisition.

#### 5.4. State-of-the-Art Performance

LongWriter-Zero achieves state-of-the-art results on established long-form writing benchmarks like WritingBench and Arena-Write, even outperforming larger, more widely recognized LLMs (e.g., Qwen3-235B, DeepSeek-R1, GPT-4o) with a significantly smaller parameter count (32B). This demonstrates that the proposed RL framework can unlock superior capabilities efficiently. The robust performance validated through extensive automatic and human-in-the-loop evaluations lends credibility to the approach.

#### 5.5. Potential Impact and Future Directions

The successful development of LongWriter-Zero opens new pathways for scalable and coherent ultra-long text production across various real-world applications, including:
- **Enhanced Content Creation:** Generating multi-section reports, in-depth articles, comprehensive narratives, and educational materials with improved quality and consistency.
- **Automated Drafting:** Assisting in legal drafting, technical documentation, and complex creative writing tasks where sustained coherence and structure are paramount.
- **Reduced Data Dependency:** Offering a more resource-efficient method for developing advanced text generation capabilities, reducing the heavy reliance on costly and potentially biased synthetic datasets.

Future research directions identified by the authors include developing more discourse-aware and factuality-sensitive reward models, incorporating adversarial or uncertainty-aware objectives to resist reward hacking, and maintaining periodic human-in-the-loop auditing to address the observed limitations of reward model exploitation.
