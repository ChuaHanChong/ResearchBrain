# QuaDreamer: Controllable Panoramic Video Generation for Quadruped Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> QuaDreamer is a panoramic video generation engine specifically designed for quadruped robots, producing controllable and realistic videos that mimic the robots' unique motion and vibration patterns. The synthetic data generated by this engine improved multi-object tracking performance for downstream tasks, boosting HOTA by 10.1% and MOTA by 14.8% compared to training with real data alone.

## Problem

- A notable scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and photorealistic panoramic training datasets from the perspective of quadruped robots.
- Challenges in real-world data collection due to kinematic constraints, ego-agent instability causing vibrations, and complex sensor calibration for panoramic cameras.
- Existing video generation models fail to capture the subtle, high-frequency spatial jitters characteristic of quadruped robot locomotion.

## Method

- **QuaDreamer**, a panoramic motion generation engine built on a ==Latent Diffusion Model (LDM)== framework, integrates control signals to generate panoramic videos with quadruped robot characteristics.
- A ==Vertical Jitter Encoding (VJE)== module extracts and encodes unique vertical vibration characteristics into Plücker embeddings, which a ==Scene-Object Controller (SOC)== integrates alongside geometric features into the LDM's U-Net to manage background and object motion fields.
- A ==Panoramic Enhancer (PE)== module uses a dual-stream approach with ==State Space Models (SSMs)== for spatial-structure correction and ==Fast Fourier Convolution (FFC)== for frequency-texture refinement to address panoramic distortions.

## Results

- **QuaDreamer** demonstrated improved video quality metrics compared to the TrackDiffusion baseline, with LPIPS decreasing by **3.68%**, SSIM increasing by **3.46%**, and PSNR improving by **1.68%**.
- The model achieved a **43.86%** reduction in the PTrack metric compared to the baseline, indicating enhanced control over high-frequency jitter in generated videos.
- Using QuaDreamer's synthetic data for augmentation improved multi-object tracking (OmniTrack) performance by **10.1%** for HOTA and **14.8%** for MOTA, demonstrating the functional benefit for downstream tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly encoding and leveraging vertical jitter through VJE is critical for accurately simulating the unique motion paradigm of quadruped robots in generated videos.
> - The Scene-Object Controller's fusion of encoded jitter features with object bounding box positions is essential for maintaining precise jitter control without confusing it with object motion.
> - The dual-stream Panoramic Enhancer, integrating SSMs and FFC, effectively mitigates panoramic distortions and preserves both global consistency and local details in wide-FoV video generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the intersection of embodied artificial intelligence, robotics, computer vision, and generative models. Quadruped robots are recognized as a component in advanced embodied intelligence, demonstrating capabilities in diverse applications such as industrial inspection, search and rescue operations, and security. Enhancing the environmental perception of these robotic agents is a critical area of focus. Panoramic cameras offer advantages for quadruped robots due to their 360-degree Field-of-View (FoV) and compatibility with sensors like LiDAR, providing comprehensive situational awareness essential for navigating complex terrains and adapting to dynamic body movements.

However, the development of robust perception systems for quadruped robots, particularly those utilizing panoramic vision, is constrained by the scarcity of high-quality, accurately annotated training data. Challenges in data collection arise from factors such as the kinematic constraints of quadruped platforms, limited robot endurance, inherent ego-agent instability causing vibrations, difficulties in complex sensor calibration (e.g., image stitching and time synchronization), and the resulting homogeneous scene distribution in existing datasets. These issues render the data acquisition process labor-intensive and costly.

In parallel, advancements in generative models, specifically diffusion models, have enabled the synthesis of high-quality images and videos. Recent works have explored conditional generation, incorporating control signals such as camera pose, object trajectories, and scene layouts to produce diverse and controllable synthetic content. Furthermore, generated data has been demonstrated as an effective means of data augmentation to improve performance in downstream tasks like object detection and tracking. Despite these advances, existing video generation models typically focus on global style and viewpoint transformations, often failing to capture the subtle, high-frequency spatial jitters that are characteristic of quadruped robot locomotion.

QuaDreamer addresses this gap by developing a panoramic video generation engine specifically designed to simulate the unique motion paradigm and vibration characteristics of quadruped robots. By focusing on generating data that accurately reflects these robot-specific dynamics, this work aims to provide a targeted solution to the data scarcity problem, thereby supporting the development of more robust and specialized perception models for quadruped robots in complex environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop QuaDreamer, the first panoramic data generation engine specifically engineered for quadruped robots. This engine aims to generate highly controllable and realistic panoramic videos that accurately mimic the distinct motion and vibration patterns observed during quadruped locomotion. A central goal is for these generated videos to serve as a high-quality data source, thereby enhancing the performance of downstream perception tasks for quadruped robots.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in the field:

1. **Data Scarcity for Quadruped Robot Perception:** The effective training of robust perception systems for quadruped robots, especially those leveraging panoramic cameras for comprehensive environmental understanding, is fundamentally a data-driven process. There is a notable scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and photorealistic panoramic training datasets captured from the perspective of quadruped robots.
2. **Challenges in Real-World Data Collection:** Acquiring such high-quality panoramic data in real-world environments is difficult and labor-intensive due to:
 - **Kinematic Constraints and Instability:** Quadruped robots exhibit inherent kinematic constraints and dynamic body movements, leading to significant vibrations and instability of the ego-agent during locomotion. These factors make stable data capture challenging.
 - **Sensor Calibration Complexities:** Panoramic cameras require intricate calibration, including accurate image stitching and time synchronization, which further complicates data collection and annotation.
 - **Limited Endurance and Homogeneous Scenes:** Quadruped platforms have limited operational endurance, contributing to a lack of diverse scene distributions in existing datasets.
3. **Limitations of Existing Generative Models:** While diffusion models have advanced video generation, they often do not explicitly account for or precisely control the subtle, high-frequency spatial jitters and motion characteristics specific to quadruped robots. Generating general videos without these tailored dynamics may not adequately address the needs of robot perception systems.
4. **Enhancing Downstream Tasks:** The lack of realistic and diverse training data directly limits the robustness and generalization capabilities of perception models for quadruped robots, particularly for critical tasks like multi-object tracking in dynamic 360-degree scenes. Generating synthetic data that incorporates realistic robot motion patterns offers a pathway to overcome these performance plateaus.

By addressing these motivations, QuaDreamer intends to efficiently and affordably mass-produce high-quality, controllable panoramic data, thereby bridging the data gap and supporting the advancement of environmental understanding capabilities for embodied quadruped agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

QuaDreamer is designed as a panoramic motion generation engine that operates as a world model, integrating control signals into a Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) framework to generate controllable, realistic panoramic videos from a quadruped robot's perspective. The system takes a single panoramic image, object motion trajectories, and robot vibration signals as input. Its architecture comprises three main components: Vertical Jitter Encoding (VJE), Scene-Object Controller (SOC), and Panoramic Enhancer (PE).

**Preliminary: Latent Diffusion Models (LDM)**
QuaDreamer utilizes LDMs, which perform the diffusion process in a latent space to reduce computational cost while maintaining generation quality. A U-Net is trained to predict the denoising process, optimized using an MSE loss function, with conditional information incorporated to guide generation.

**Vertical Jitter Encoding (VJE)**
VJE is designed to extract and encode the unique vertical vibration characteristics of quadruped locomotion.
1. **Signal Decomposition:** The vertical coordinates of the bounding box of the data collector ($y_{human}(t)$), which is consistently present in panoramic images, are spectrally analyzed. This decomposes the signal into low-frequency components (representing stable relative displacement) and high-frequency components (reflecting vertical jitter from gait).
2. **Jitter Extraction:** A first-order Butterworth high-pass filter with a cutoff frequency of 0.3 Hz is applied to $y_{human}(t)$ to isolate the high-frequency component, denoted as $y_w(t)$, which represents the vertical jitter data.
3. **Coordinate Transformation and Encoding:** The extracted vertical jitter data is converted into world coordinates ($P^i_w$) by fixing x and z values and setting $y^i_w = y_w(i)$. These world coordinates are then mapped into camera coordinates ($C_i$) using an estimated camera intrinsic matrix K. To achieve precise control over visual details, these camera poses are further converted into Plücker embeddings ($P^i_u$). A frozen camera encoder processes these embeddings to compute per-frame background features ($f^i_{bg}$), allowing the generation model to explicitly learn and leverage vibration patterns in both geometric and latent spaces.

**Scene-Object Controller (SOC)**
SOC manages object motion and enhances background jitter control by integrating jitter and geometric features into the LDM's U-Net via an attention mechanism. The scene is conceptualized as having two interrelated motion fields:
1. **Background Motion Field:** The background features ($f^i_{bg}$) from VJE are embedded into a temporal attention module to capture temporal dependencies of the camera trajectory. These features are added to the image latent features at a pixel level to control background jitter. Additionally, 3D convolutions process $f^i_{bg}$ to extract spatiotemporal features ($A^i_{bg}$), modeling global motion patterns and camera jitter.
2. **Object Motion Field:** Object bounding box coordinates ($b_t$) are encoded using Fourier Embedding, mapping them into a high-dimensional frequency space to capture multi-scale motion. A binary switching mechanism (visibility mask $m_t$) is incorporated to handle visible or occluded states, using a learnable zero embedding ($\varphi_{null}$) for missing detection frames.
3. **Feature Fusion and Integration:** The encoded object motion field ($B$) is fused with the background features ($A_{bg}$) using MLPs to create a combined representation ($F_{sum}$). This $F_{sum}$ is then passed through a gated self-attention mechanism, integrating it with the input visual features within the U-Net architecture.

**Panoramic Enhancer (PE)**
PE is designed as a dual-stream module within an encoder-decoder architecture to address panoramic distortions and maintain global consistency and local detail in wide-FoV video generation.
1. **Spatial-Structure Correction:** State Space Model (SSM) modules are strategically injected into the architecture: before the first downsampling layer of the encoder and after the final upsampling layer of the decoder. These SSMs utilize multi-directional scanning mechanisms to model long-range geometric dependencies, suppressing edge distortions inherent in panoramic unwrapping and enforcing structural continuity across distorted regions.
2. **Frequency-Texture Refinement:** After three successive downsampling stages in the encoder, intermediate layers employ residual blocks with Fast Fourier Convolution (FFC). FFC uses frequency-domain convolutions with global receptive fields to mitigate resolution-sensitive artifacts and preserve high-frequency details, enabling the recovery of periodic structures while avoiding typical grid artifacts.
3. **Hierarchical Collaboration:** This dual-stream design allows for a phased collaboration. Geometric constraints are propagated bidirectionally through SSM-enhanced encoder-decoder layers, while FFC-driven intermediate optimization focuses on local detail enhancement and frequency-domain representations.

**Dataset and Training:**
The model is trained on the QuadTrack dataset, comprising 19,200 panoramic frames with 189,876 manually annotated object bounding boxes. Training involves 320 epochs (200,000 steps) on an NVIDIA A6000 GPU, using mixed-precision training (FP16), a batch size of 2, and a linear warm-up learning rate schedule. Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) serves as the base generative model, and the CameraCtrl camera encoder is utilized with frozen parameters to encode jitter, reducing the number of trainable parameters. Video generation employs the DPM-Solver scheduler for 30 steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of QuaDreamer on the QuadTrack dataset focused on assessing its video generation quality and controllability, as well as its utility for downstream tasks.

**Quantitative Performance (Video Quality and Controllability):**

- **Video Quality Comparison:**
 - Compared to the baseline TrackDiffusion, QuaDreamer demonstrated improvements in image quality metrics: LPIPS (perceptual similarity) decreased by 3.68%, SSIM (structural similarity) increased by 3.46%, and PSNR (peak signal-to-noise ratio) improved by 1.68%.
 - A version of QuaDreamer without control modules (QuaDreamer*) also outperformed SVD, indicating the effectiveness of the Panoramic Enhancer in improving video quality.
- **Controllability Assessment:**
 - QuaDreamer achieved a significant 43.86% reduction in the PTrack metric compared to the baseline, which quantifies point-tracking consistency and jitter control performance. This indicates an enhancement in the model's ability to synthesize videos with controlled high-frequency jitter resembling real quadruped robot motion.
 - While FVD (Fréchet Video Distance) slightly increased, this was attributed to the expanded control dimensions introduced by jitter control.
- **Ablation Study on QuaDreamer Framework:**
 - **Scene-Object Controller (SOC):** Introducing SOC alone resulted in improved MOTA by 6.74% and a PTrack decrease of 36.76%, indicating enhanced control effectiveness.
 - **Panoramic Enhancer (PE):** Incorporating PE (without control modules) led to a FVD decrease of 6.36%, confirming its role in video quality improvement.
 - The full QuaDreamer model, integrating both SOC and PE, achieved the best balance of controllability and video quality.
- **Ablation Study on VJE Filtering:**
 - Experiments demonstrated that applying the high-pass filter within VJE for jitter extraction was crucial. When filtering was omitted, the PTrack metric was higher (17.4361 vs. 14.1744), and other control metrics (HOTA, AssA) showed reduced performance. This indicated a loss of jitter control without proper signal filtering, despite a slight improvement in MOTA due to the fusion of unfiltered object bounding box coordinates.
- **Impact of Module Design on Control:**
 - The temporal attention blocks within the SOC module were identified as key to improving the model's control effectiveness.
 - Fusing the encoded jitter features ($A_{bg}$) with the object bounding box positions ($B$) in the gated attention mechanism was found to be essential. Without this fusion, the model would confuse object motion with jitter features, leading to a loss of jitter control.
- **6-DoF Control and Generalization:**
 - The SOC framework was shown to support full 6-DoF control, including vertical jitter suppression and simulated yaw-axis variations, demonstrating its robustness beyond purely vertical control.
 - The model exhibited generalization ability across different quadruped robot platforms, including Unitree Robotics Go2 and DEEP Robotics Lite3, successfully supporting controllable generation for robots with varying motion characteristics.

**Analysis of Benefits for Downstream Tasks (Multi-Object Tracking):**

- The generated video frames from QuaDreamer were utilized as data augmentation to train OmniTrack, a multi-object tracking model designed for panoramic views from quadruped robots.
- Training OmniTrack with additional frames generated by QuaDreamer resulted in a notable performance gain compared to training with real data only. Specifically, HOTA improved by 10.1% and MOTA improved by 14.8%.
- In contrast, other baseline models (SVD, TrackDiffusion) showed slight declines in MOTA when their generated data was used for augmentation. This indicates that QuaDreamer's synthetic data, by more realistically simulating jitter and providing higher-quality images, enhances the authenticity and diversity of the training data, enabling downstream models to adapt better to complex real-world environments.

**Qualitative Results:**

- Visual comparisons (Figures 4, 6, 7, 9) illustrated that the rainbow-colored jitter trajectories generated by QuaDreamer closely matched the ground truth videos, outperforming SVD and TrackDiffusion in mimicking realistic robot motion.
- QuaDreamer's generation effectively avoided blur artifacts present in ground truth panoramic images captured with long exposure times during high-frequency vertical jitter, providing clearer data for downstream tasks (Figure 8).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents QuaDreamer, a novel framework for controllable panoramic video generation tailored for quadruped robots, which holds significant theoretical and practical implications for embodied artificial intelligence and robotics.

**Addressing Data Scarcity and Bottlenecks:**
The most direct impact of QuaDreamer is its ability to mitigate the critical issue of data scarcity for quadruped robot perception. By providing an efficient and affordable method to generate high-quality, diverse, and photorealistic panoramic training data, the framework removes a significant bottleneck that currently limits the development and deployment of robust perception systems for these robots in complex, unconstrained environments (e.g., inspection, search and rescue, security). This accelerates the training and validation cycles for perception models.

**Enabling Controllable Data Generation:**
QuaDreamer introduces a capability for fine-grained control over generated panoramic videos, specifically by mimicking the unique vertical vibration (jitter) and general motion characteristics of quadruped robots. The Vertical Jitter Encoding (VJE) and Scene-Object Controller (SOC) modules allow for explicit regulation of both robot-centric motion and object interactions. This controllability enables researchers to generate targeted synthetic scenarios, which can be invaluable for stress-testing perception algorithms under specific challenging conditions or for synthesizing data for rare events that are difficult to capture in the real world.

**Enhancing Downstream Perception Tasks:**
A crucial finding is that the synthetic data generated by QuaDreamer demonstrably enhances the performance of downstream tasks, such as multi-object tracking for quadruped robots. The significant improvements observed in HOTA and MOTA metrics for the OmniTrack model underscore the practical value of QuaDreamer's output as an effective data augmentation strategy. This indicates that QuaDreamer-generated data is not merely visually plausible but also functionally beneficial for improving the robustness and generalization capabilities of robot vision systems, enabling them to better adapt to the complexities of real-world dynamic environments.

**Technological Innovation:**
The proposed architectural components—VJE, SOC, and Panoramic Enhancer (PE)—represent specific technical advancements. VJE's method for decoupling and encoding jitter signals, SOC's multimodal fusion for combined jitter and object control, and PE's dual-stream approach integrating frequency-domain refinement with spatial-structure correction for distortion handling, all contribute to advancing the state-of-the-art in controllable panoramic video generation. These innovations can serve as a foundation for future generative models in embodied AI.

**Foundation for Future Robotics Research and Development:**
QuaDreamer establishes a technical framework for simulating realistic robot-environment interactions. This can facilitate the development of more advanced robotic simulation systems, accelerate iterative design processes for robot behaviors and perception algorithms, and expand the utility of generative AI in embodied agent research. The planned open-source release of the code and model weights is expected to foster collaborative innovation within the quadruped robot vision community, promoting further research and application development.

**Future Research Avenues:**
The work also highlights potential future directions, such as extending the framework to RGB-X generation (incorporating other sensor modalities like depth, infrared, or event cameras), leveraging IMU data for more comprehensive 6-DoF robot posture control, and integrating large language models to enable generation from coarsely annotated or abstract conditions, thereby broadening the accessibility and applicability of such data generation tools.
