# MMCD: Multi-Modal Collaborative Decision-Making for Connected Autonomy with Knowledge Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Maryland and North Carolina State University developed MMCD, a framework for multi-modal collaborative decision-making in connected autonomous systems, which leverages cross-modal knowledge distillation to maintain robust performance despite missing sensor data. The system improved accident detection rates by up to 20.7% in ground vehicle scenarios and 7.0% in aerial-ground collaboration compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- Autonomous vehicles face limitations from restricted sensor range and occluded views, increasing accident risk in complex environments.
- Existing multi-vehicle and multi-modal systems often assume consistent availability of all sensor data and connected vehicles, which is impractical in real-world deployment due to potential sensor malfunctions or communication loss.
- The high cost of expensive sensors like LiDAR hinders the widespread and cost-effective deployment of advanced autonomous driving capabilities.

## Method

- A Multi-Modal Collaborative Decision-making (**MMCD**) framework was developed to fuse RGB image and LiDAR point cloud data from an ego vehicle and multiple collaborative vehicles, utilizing attention mechanisms for feature extraction and aggregation.
- A cross-modal knowledge distillation approach trains a 'teacher' model with all available multi-modal and multi-vehicle data, then distills this knowledge to a 'student' model capable of robust operation with reduced modalities (e.g., RGB-only) at test time.
- The decision-making model uses a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict braking probabilities based on the fused features, dynamically adapting its input based on the availability of RGB, LiDAR, or both modalities.

## Results

- The MMCD framework (RGB+LiDAR) achieved the best overall performance, up to a **20.7%** improvement in Accident Detection Rate (ADR) in ground vehicle scenarios and **7.0%** in aerial-ground collaboration vs baselines, and its knowledge-distilled RGB-only student model consistently outperformed RGB-only baselines, validating cross-modal distillation robustness with missing modalities.
- Collaborative settings universally surpassed non-collaborative ones, underscoring the benefits of inter-vehicle information sharing in enhancing safety and situational awareness in complex, accident-prone environments.
- Ablation studies confirmed that self-attention mechanisms in the RGB encoder and cross-attention for collaborative feature aggregation were critical components contributing to the improved decision-making capability of the framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Collaborative sensing significantly enhances driving safety by providing a broader field of view and mitigating issues caused by occlusions, consistently outperforming non-collaborative approaches.
> - Fusing multi-modal RGB-LiDAR data through self-attention encoders and cross-attention aggregation across connected vehicles produces more comprehensive, context-aware, robust decision-making than single-modality approaches.
> - Cross-modal knowledge distillation effectively enables a student model with reduced sensor inputs (e.g., RGB-only) to achieve robust performance by leveraging the richer 'dark knowledge' learned from a multi-modal teacher model during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous systems have experienced substantial development across areas such as perception, decision-making, and control. However, challenges persist, particularly in accident-prone environments where decision-making robustness is critical. A single autonomous vehicle faces inherent limitations due to restricted sensor range and occluded views, which elevate accident risks.

To address these limitations, two prominent research directions have emerged:
1. **Multi-vehicle connected systems**: These systems enable vehicles to share information, thereby expanding their collective field of view and reducing accident probabilities. Prior work in this area includes decentralized cooperative lane-changing, game-theoretic approaches, end-to-end LiDAR-based systems, and spatio-temporal graph neural networks, alongside communication mechanisms like Who2com, When2com, and Where2com.
2. **Multi-modal data approaches**: Leveraging diverse sensor data, such as RGB images and LiDAR point clouds, can enhance the perception and decision-making capabilities of individual autonomous systems. Previous studies have explored RGB-to-LiDAR fusion for 3D semantic segmentation, object classification, and end-to-end autonomous driving systems.

Recent research has begun to integrate these two paradigms, developing multi-vehicle, multi-modal systems to further improve autonomous driving performance by combining connectivity with diverse sensor data. However, a common assumption in many existing multi-vehicle and multi-modal methods is the consistent availability of all sensor data modalities and connected vehicles throughout both training and testing phases. This assumption is often impractical in real-world deployment due to potential sensor malfunctions, temporary data unavailability, or the absence of expected connected vehicles. Furthermore, the cost efficiency of sensor suites, particularly the higher expense of LiDAR compared to RGB cameras, is a practical consideration.

This paper addresses the gap concerning the robustness of multi-modal, multi-vehicle systems when faced with partial data unavailability or sensor failures during real-time operation. It aims to develop a framework that can maintain effective performance even when specific data modalities are missing at test time, and to offer a more cost-effective deployment strategy by reducing the exclusive reliance on expensive sensors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of current autonomous driving systems, particularly their lack of robustness when faced with real-world complexities such as sensor failures, communication disruptions, or varying sensor capabilities among connected vehicles. Specifically, the paper aims to address two main challenges:

1. **Ensuring robust decision-making despite missing modalities**: Existing multi-modal systems often assume the constant availability of all sensor data (e.g., both RGB images and LiDAR point clouds) during both training and testing. This is unrealistic, as LiDAR sensors can malfunction, communication with certain connected vehicles might be temporarily lost, or some vehicles might only be equipped with cameras. The absence of a specific modality during testing, especially if the model was trained with it, can degrade performance and compromise safety. The objective is to enable the ego vehicle to make informed decisions even when some of its own sensors or data from collaborative vehicles are partially or completely unavailable during operation.
2. **Improving cost-effectiveness of deployment**: LiDAR sensors are generally more expensive than RGB cameras. A system that relies heavily on LiDAR for robust performance can significantly increase the cost of autonomous vehicles. A key motivation is to explore methods that allow autonomous systems to leverage the rich information provided by expensive sensors like LiDAR during training, but then operate effectively using only more affordable sensors, such as RGB cameras, during testing. This would facilitate broader and more economical deployment of autonomous driving technology.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to answer two specific research questions:
- How can observations from connected vehicles, regardless of whether they are equipped with the same or different sensing modalities, be effectively utilized for multi-modal collaborative decision-making?
- How can a model be trained using multi-vehicle, multi-modal observations to ensure robust performance when only single-vehicle or single-modal observations are available during testing?

The overarching goal is to enhance driving safety and reliability in complex, accident-prone environments by developing a flexible and robust decision-making framework for connected autonomous systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce a novel framework named MMCD (Multi-Modal Collaborative Decision-making) that fuses multi-modal observations from ego and collaborative vehicles. To ensure robust performance when certain data modalities are unavailable during testing, MMCD incorporates a cross-modal knowledge distillation approach using a teacher-student model structure.

**A. Multi-Modal Collaborative Decision Making (MMCD Framework)**
The MMCD framework consists of three primary components: an RGB model, a LiDAR model, and a decision-making model.

1. **RGB Model**: This model processes RGB image data.
 - **RGB Encoder**: Each vehicle locally processes its front-view RGB images. A ResNet-18 backbone is used to extract feature maps from resized 224x224 images. Self-attention mechanisms are applied to the feature map to emphasize relevant visual features (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians, traffic signals). The processed features then pass through three convolutional layers and a fully connected layer to produce a 256-dimensional feature representation.
 - **RGB Aggregator**: Features from the ego vehicle ($f_{ego} \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$) and $N$ collaborative vehicles ($F_{collab} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 256}$) are aggregated using a cross-attention mechanism. The ego vehicle's features generate a query (Q), while collaborative vehicles' features generate keys (K) and values (V). Attention weights are computed as $A = \text{softmax}(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d}})$, determining the relevance of each collaborator's features. The aggregated feature ($f_{agg}$) is a weighted sum of the value vectors. The final RGB embedding ($f_{rgb}$) combines the ego vehicle's features and the aggregated collaborative features using learnable transformation matrices.
2. **LiDAR Model**: This model processes LiDAR point cloud data. The COOPERNAUT model, which uses a Point Transformer backbone, is adopted. Raw 3D point clouds from each vehicle are encoded into keypoints, with representations learned by Point Transformer blocks. These representations are then merged with the ego vehicle's representations to yield a final LiDAR embedding ($f_{lidar}$).
3. **Decision-making Model**: This model takes the fused features to determine actions for the ego vehicle, specifically whether to brake in accident-prone scenarios.
 - If both RGB and LiDAR observations are available, the final RGB and LiDAR embeddings are concatenated ($f_{rgb}||f_{lidar}$) and fed into a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict the braking probability ($p$).
 - If only one modality (RGB or LiDAR) is available, the decision-making model uses the corresponding available final embedding ($f_{rgb}$ or $f_{lidar}$) as input to the MLP to output the braking probability.

**B. Cross-Modal Knowledge Distillation**
To address scenarios with missing data modalities during testing, a cross-modal knowledge distillation (KD) approach is employed.
1. **Teacher Model Training**: A teacher decision-making model ($T$) is trained offline using *all* available data: both RGB and LiDAR data from multiple vehicles. This training uses a binary cross-entropy loss ($L_{BCE}$) against ground truth braking labels.
2. **Student Model Training**: A student model ($S$) is designed to operate with reduced modalities (e.g., only RGB data from the ego vehicle). The student model learns to mimic the behavior of the teacher model. The student's loss function ($L_S$) is a weighted sum of two terms:
 - **Distillation Loss ($L_{KD}$)**: This measures the KL divergence between the soft targets (probabilities with temperature scaling) generated by the teacher model and the student model. This term enables the student to learn from the teacher's "dark knowledge," including class similarities and decision boundaries.
 - **Student Task Loss**: This is a standard binary cross-entropy loss between the student's output and the true (hard) labels.
 The combined loss is $L_S = (1 - \alpha)L_{BCE}(y, S) + \alpha t^2 L_{KD}(S, T)$, where $\alpha=0.5$ balances the two losses and $t=3.0$ is the temperature for soft targets.
After training, the student model, which uses only RGB data, benefits from the insights provided by the teacher model trained with both RGB and LiDAR data. This allows the student to maintain robust performance even when LiDAR data is absent during deployment.

**C. Experimental Setup**
Experiments were conducted on two main collaboration scenarios:
1. **Connected Autonomous Driving with Ground Vehicles**: This used CARLA simulator integrated with AutoCast for three challenging traffic scenarios: Overtaking, Left Turn, and Red Light Violation. Data included RGB images and LiDAR point clouds from up to four connected vehicles with a communication range of 150m.
2. **Aerial-Ground Vehicles Collaboration**: This used the AirSim simulator for similar accident-prone scenarios with an ego vehicle, a ground collaborator, and an aerial collaborator.

Four data modality availability cases were evaluated:
- Case A: Only RGB data for both training and testing.
- Case B: Only LiDAR data for both training and testing.
- Case C (MMCD, teacher): Both RGB and LiDAR data for both training and testing.
- Case D (MMCD, student): Both RGB and LiDAR data for training, but only RGB data for testing.

Evaluation metrics included Package Size (PS) for communication efficiency, Accident Detection Rate (ADR) for driving safety, and Imitation Rate (IR) for expert behavior reproduction. Baselines included COOPERNAUT (LiDAR-based collaborative), STG (RGBD-based collaborative), AML (RGB+LiDAR training, RGB-only testing, single-agent), and non-collaborative settings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both connected autonomous driving with ground vehicles and aerial-ground vehicles collaboration, evaluating MMCD against several baselines and different data modality availability cases.

**A. Connected Autonomous Driving with Ground Vehicles**
In scenarios like Overtaking, Left Turn, and Red Light Violation, MMCD demonstrated performance improvements.

- **Non-Collaborative Settings**: In scenarios where the ego vehicle operated independently, Case C (both RGB and LiDAR) achieved the highest performance across all metrics, indicating the benefit of multi-modal data. AML, which uses LiDAR during training but only RGB during testing in a single-agent context, outperformed Case A (RGB-only training and testing), suggesting that incorporating auxiliary LiDAR data during training improved RGB-only performance at test time.

- **Collaborative Settings**:
 - All collaborative settings consistently outperformed non-collaborative settings, underscoring the value of information sharing from connected vehicles in overcoming sensing limitations and occlusions.
 - MMCD (Case C), utilizing both RGB and LiDAR data from multiple vehicles during training and testing, achieved the best overall performance. For instance, in the "Left Turn" scenario, it showed an Accident Detection Rate (ADR) of 0.7329 and an Imitation Rate (IR) of 0.8122, and in "Overtaking", an ADR of 0.9604.
 - Compared to the best-existing baseline, STG, MMCD achieved significant improvements in accident detection. For example, in the "Left Turn" scenario, MMCD improved ADR by 20.7% (from 0.6070 for STG to 0.7329 for MMCD). Overall, improvements ranged from 3.7% to 20.7% in ADR and 4.1% to 9.3% in IR across scenarios.
 - Case D (MMCD student model), which was trained with both RGB and LiDAR but tested with only RGB, performed better than Case A (RGB-only for training and testing). This finding specifically supports the effectiveness of cross-modal knowledge distillation, where the student model, despite having fewer modalities at test time, benefits from the richer training data provided by the teacher model.

- **Ablation Studies**:
 - **Self-Attention in RGB Encoder**: Removing self-attention from the RGB encoder resulted in a decrease in decision-making capability (e.g., ADR dropped from 0.8522 to 0.8319 in Overtaking for Collaborative Case A). This indicated that self-attention aids the RGB encoder in focusing on relevant image features.
 - **Cross-Attention vs. Concatenation**: Using a cross-attention mechanism for RGB feature aggregation outperformed simple concatenation (e.g., ADR of 0.8522 vs. 0.8367 in Overtaking for Collaborative Case A). This suggests that cross-attention is more effective in extracting important features from collaborative vehicles and building a context-aware representation.

**B. Aerial-Ground Vehicles Collaboration**
Experiments involving an ego vehicle, a ground collaborator, and an aerial collaborator mirrored the findings from ground vehicle collaboration.

- **Performance Trends**: Collaborative settings consistently outperformed non-collaborative ones, emphasizing the utility of aerial perspectives in enhancing situational awareness, especially for obstructed views.
- **MMCD Performance**: MMCD (Case C) again demonstrated superior performance across all scenarios. It achieved up to a 7.0% improvement in accident detection (ADR) compared to the state-of-the-art STG in the "Scenario 2" (Left Turn) context (from 0.8410 for STG to 0.9000 for MMCD). Overall, improvements ranged from 3.9% to 7.0% in ADR and 4.4% to 7.4% in IR across scenarios relative to STG.
- **Knowledge Distillation Efficacy**: Similar to ground vehicle results, Case D (student model) showed improved performance over Case A, further validating the benefit of cross-modal knowledge distillation in robustly handling missing modalities at test time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MMCD framework and its integrated cross-modal knowledge distillation approach present several significant implications and potential impacts for the field of autonomous driving:

1. **Enhanced Robustness to Sensor Failures and Data Unavailability**: A key contribution is the demonstrated ability of MMCD to maintain robust decision-making performance even when certain data modalities (e.g., LiDAR data) are unavailable during testing. This addresses a critical practical challenge in autonomous system deployment, where sensor malfunctions, temporary communication loss, or varied sensor suites among connected vehicles are realistic possibilities. By learning from a multi-modal teacher, the RGB-only student model can generalize effectively, ensuring continued safe operation in degraded sensor environments.

2. **Improved Driving Safety in Complex Scenarios**: The experimental results consistently show that MMCD improves driving safety, measured by Accident Detection Rate (ADR), by up to 20.7% compared to existing baselines in ground vehicle scenarios, and up to 7.0% in aerial-ground collaboration. This improvement is particularly pronounced in accident-prone situations characterized by occlusions, limited sensor range, or complex traffic interactions, where collaborative sensing and multi-modal data fusion provide crucial context.

3. **Cost-Effectiveness for Deployment**: The knowledge distillation strategy offers a pathway to more cost-effective deployment of autonomous vehicles. Instead of equipping every vehicle with expensive LiDAR sensors, the framework allows for a scenario where models are trained with the full range of multi-modal data (including LiDAR) but deployed on vehicles primarily equipped with more affordable RGB cameras. This can reduce the hardware cost per vehicle while retaining a high level of performance.

4. **Flexible and Adaptable Collaboration**: MMCD supports a wide range of collaboration scenarios, from ground-to-ground vehicle communication to aerial-ground partnerships, and can handle both single-modal and multi-modal inputs from collaborators. This flexibility is valuable for heterogeneous fleets and evolving smart city infrastructures.

5. **Advancement in Collaborative AI for Autonomy**: The work contributes to the advancement of collaborative AI by demonstrating an effective method for leveraging distributed, multi-modal sensor information across connected entities. The use of cross-attention for feature aggregation highlights a mechanism for intelligent data fusion, where the ego vehicle can prioritize relevant information from its collaborators.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
The paper acknowledges certain limitations, specifically the assumption of accurate vehicle position detection for collaborative decision-making. Future research could focus on incorporating state estimation strategies that account for noise and uncertainty in vehicle positions. Additionally, exploring the integration of other sensor modalities, such as radar or infrared, is proposed as a direction to further enhance decision-making capabilities.
