# A Cognitive Paradigm Approach to Probe the Perception-Reasoning Interface in VLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces a cognitively-inspired framework to evaluate Vision-Language Models, specifically dissecting how they integrate visual perception with abstract reasoning. It demonstrates that decoupling perception into rich textual descriptions significantly boosts performance on visual reasoning tasks like Bongard Problems and Winoground, achieving new state-of-the-art results and revealing a primary "perception bottleneck" in many current VLMs.

## Problem

- While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) excel at many tasks, their underlying cognitive mechanisms for complex visual reasoning, especially those requiring abstract concept formation across multiple images, remain unclear.
- Current VLM evaluations often do not sufficiently isolate abstract visual concept formation and relational reasoning from linguistic context or perceptual noise.
- There's a lack of diagnostic tools to understand how VLMs bridge the gap between pixels and abstract concepts, hindering targeted improvements.

## Method

- The authors propose a cognitively-inspired evaluation framework with three paradigms: DVRL, DRL, and ==Componential Analysis (CA)==.
- ==Componential Analysis (CA)== is introduced to systematically ==decouple visual perception from abstract reasoning== by generating task-agnostic textual descriptions for images and then performing reasoning solely on these descriptions.
- The framework uses challenging visual reasoning datasets like Bongard-OpenWorld, Bongard-HOI, and Winoground to probe VLM capabilities and diagnose limitations.

## Results

- The Componential Analysis (CA) paradigm consistently yielded the highest accuracies for top models on Bongard-OpenWorld (e.g., Gemini 2.0 at **93.6%**, GPT-4o at **92.8%**), surpassing human average.
- CA achieved new state-of-the-art results on the Winoground benchmark (e.g., Group score of **52.0%** with GPT-4o as reasoning engine), demonstrating its effectiveness on fine-grained compositional reasoning.
- Ablation studies revealed that providing high-quality, externally generated descriptions dramatically improved the reasoning accuracy of weaker VLMs (e.g., Llama-Vision-11B from **53.4%** to **84.17%**) and allowed text-only LLMs to exceed **90%** accuracy on Bongard-OpenWorld.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling visual perception from reasoning, especially through rich, task-agnostic textual descriptions, significantly enhances VLM performance on complex visual reasoning tasks.
> - Many contemporary VLMs, particularly open-source ones, are primarily limited by a "perception bottleneck"—their inability to consistently and accurately translate visual information into high-fidelity, usable internal representations.
> - Text-only Large Language Models (LLMs) can effectively perform complex visual reasoning when provided with high-quality textual descriptions of visual input, demonstrating their robust symbolic reasoning capabilities and enabling modality transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the critical intersection of artificial intelligence, computer vision, natural language processing, and cognitive science. The broader landscape of AI research has seen remarkable progress in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which are increasingly capable of understanding and generating content across visual and textual modalities. While foundational tasks like Visual Question Answering (VQA) have been benchmarks, the frontier has shifted towards more complex challenges demanding abstract reasoning, multi-modal, and multi-image understanding.

However, the authors identify a significant gap: despite impressive performance, the *underlying cognitive mechanisms* enabling complex visual reasoning in VLMs remain largely opaque. Many existing evaluations, while useful for performance benchmarking, often rely heavily on linguistic context or do not specifically isolate the abstract visual concept formation and relational reasoning that are central to purely visual intelligence, as demonstrated by tasks like Bongard Problems (BPs) or Raven's Progressive Matrices.

This paper directly addresses this transparency gap by introducing a *cognitively-inspired evaluation framework*. By structuring VLM tasks in ways that mirror human problem-solving strategies (holistic processing, explicit deduction, analytical decomposition), the work aims to move beyond simple accuracy metrics to diagnose *how* VLMs process information. It connects to the growing interest in making AI models more interpretable and understanding their limitations beyond just overall performance. Furthermore, it differentiates itself from related Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting methods by emphasizing *task-independent* description generation in its Componential Analysis paradigm, which is a crucial distinction for robust and generalizable reasoning, rather than context-dependent prompting aimed solely at performance boosting. The success of the proposed Componential Analysis in achieving new state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like Bongard-OpenWorld, Bongard-HOI, and Winoground also positions this work as a significant advancement in pushing the capabilities of visual reasoning in VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the aspiration to imbue machines with visual intelligence akin to human cognition, which adeptly integrates visual perception with abstract reasoning to understand the world. Despite the remarkable progress of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), the authors note that the underlying mechanisms enabling complex visual reasoning – particularly tasks requiring abstraction across multiple images or fine-grained compositional understanding – remain elusive. They pose fundamental questions: How do VLMs bridge the gap between pixels and abstract concepts? How do their processing strategies compare to human cognition?

To address these questions, the paper sets out several key objectives:

1. **Introduce a Novel, Cognitively-Inspired Evaluation Framework:** To create a structured methodology for diagnostically evaluating the cognitive processes underlying VLM performance on challenging visual reasoning tasks.
2. **Systematically Analyze VLM Behavior:** To probe VLM responses under varying cognitive demands by structuring the Bongard Problem task in three distinct ways, mirroring human problem-solving strategies. This aims to provide insights into their internal processing characteristics.
3. **Disentangle Perception from Reasoning:** To develop a method (Componential Analysis) that allows for the isolation and evaluation of reasoning capabilities largely independent of perceptual errors, including the ability to evaluate text-only Large Language Models (LLMs) on visual reasoning tasks.
4. **Identify Specific Bottlenecks:** Through systematic evaluation and ablation studies, to pinpoint where VLMs struggle (e.g., in visual perception, rule extraction, or rule application).
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** To demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed framework, particularly the Componential Analysis paradigm, by achieving new state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks.
6. **Provide a Valuable Diagnostic Tool:** To offer a framework that serves as a diagnostic instrument for VLM developers, highlighting areas for improvement and suggesting promising architectural directions (e.g., decoupling perception and reasoning).

In essence, the research aims to shed light on the "black box" of VLM visual reasoning, providing both diagnostic insights and a pathway for developing more robust and general visual intelligence in AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is a structured evaluation framework using cognitively-inspired paradigms applied to challenging visual reasoning tasks.

#### 3.1. Datasets and Tasks

The primary testbed is the **Bongard-OpenWorld** dataset, which features natural, real-world images requiring few-shot abstract reasoning based on commonsense visual concepts. Each task involves distinguishing 6 positive examples from 6 negative examples based on an abstract rule, and then classifying a query image. For generalizability, the framework is also applied to:
- **Bongard-HOI (Human-Object Interactions):** Natural images focusing on rules derived from human-object interactions.
- **Winoground:** A dataset designed to assess fine-grained compositional visio-linguistic reasoning, requiring models to differentiate between minimally contrastive image-caption pairs.

#### 3.2. Cognitively-Inspired Evaluation Paradigms

Three distinct paradigms are proposed, designed to probe different facets of visual reasoning, drawing inspiration from human cognitive strategies (Figure 2 in the paper):

1. **Direct Visual Rule Learning (DVRL):**
 - **Concept:** Simulates holistic or "gist-based" processing, where all information is processed concurrently.
 - **Approach:** All 13 images (6 positive, 6 negative, 1 query) are presented simultaneously to the VLM. The model must identify the rule and classify the query in a single step.
 - **Applicability:** Only high-capacity VLMs capable of processing multiple images concurrently (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0) were tested.

2. **Deductive Rule Learning (DRL):**
 - **Concept:** Mimics explicit, rule-based deduction, separating the process of rule discovery from its application.
 - **Approach:** A two-stage process:
 - **Stage 1 (Rule Extraction):** The VLM analyzes the 12 context images (positive/negative sets) to identify and concisely summarize the distinguishing rule.
 - **Stage 2 (Rule Application):** The VLM receives the *generated rule summary* and the *query image*, classifying the query based solely on the provided rule.

3. **Componential Analysis (CA):**
 - **Concept:** Parallels analytical decomposition, breaking down problems into constituent features for systematic reasoning, and crucially, aiming to decouple perception from reasoning.
 - **Approach:** A multi-stage process based on textual representations:
 - **Stage 1 (Image Description):** The VLM (or an external high-quality VLM/source) generates a detailed, structured JSON description for each of the 13 images *individually*. These descriptions are designed to be comprehensive and *task-agnostic*.
 - **Stage 2 (Text-Based Reasoning):** The VLM or a text-only Large Language Model (LLM) receives the collection of 13 JSON descriptions (labeled positive/negative/query) and performs rule extraction and query classification *based only on this textual input*.
 - **Key Advantage:** This paradigm allows evaluating models lacking direct multi-image input, enables assessing reasoning independent of *perceptual errors* (by potentially using externally generated high-quality descriptions), and facilitates evaluation of text-only LLMs on visual reasoning tasks.

#### 3.3. Models Evaluated

A diverse suite of contemporary VLMs was selected, including leading closed-source systems (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0) and widely used open-source models of varying scales (Pixtral-12B, Llama-Vision-3.2 (11B, 90B), LLaVA (7B, 13B, 34B), and LLaVA-Llama3-8B). For ablation studies, text-only LLMs (e.g., Phi-4, Qwen2.5, Deepseek-r1, Gemma2) were also included.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

All evaluations used few-shot prompting with zero temperature for deterministic output. No model fine-tuning was performed. Models were accessed via official APIs or the Ollama framework.

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies

Two key ablation studies were conducted to isolate the impact of perception and reasoning:
- **Rule Application Fidelity:** Models were provided with high-quality, externally generated rule summaries and a query image to test their ability to *apply* a symbolic rule to visual input, isolating this step from rule extraction.
- **Impact of Description Quality on Reasoning:** Weaker VLMs and text-only LLMs were tested in the CA paradigm's Stage 2, but using image descriptions *generated by GPT-4o* (a high-fidelity source) instead of their own internal descriptions. This directly assessed their reasoning capability when perceptual challenges were mitigated.

#### 3.6. Metrics

Primary metrics included classification accuracy. Semantic similarity (cosine similarity of OpenAI embeddings) was used to assess the alignment of derived rules with query descriptions. Winoground has its own specific text, image, and group scores.

The methodology provides a robust, multi-faceted approach to diagnose VLM capabilities, allowing for a deeper understanding of their strengths and weaknesses in integrating vision and language for complex reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, particularly highlighting a critical "perception bottleneck" in many current VLMs and demonstrating the power of a decoupled perception-reasoning approach.

#### 4.1. Performance Across Cognitively-Inspired Paradigms (Bongard-OpenWorld)

- **Direct Visual Rule Learning (DVRL):** Applicable only to multi-image capable models (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0), performance was strong (Gemini 82.2%, GPT-4o 80%) but suggested limitations in purely holistic, simultaneous multi-image reasoning for complex tasks.
- **Deductive Rule Learning (DRL):** Performance improved markedly for both GPT-4o (88.0%) and Gemini 2.0 (86.8%) compared to DVRL. This indicates that explicitly separating rule extraction from rule application is beneficial, aligning with human strategies of breaking down complex tasks.
- **Componential Analysis (CA):** This paradigm, which relies on reasoning over textual descriptions, consistently yielded the highest accuracies for the top models (Gemini 2.0: 93.6%, GPT-4o: 92.8%). This performance surpassed the reported human average for Bongard-OpenWorld, underscoring the powerful text-based reasoning ability of these models when visual input is effectively encoded into language. Pixtral-12B also showed strong CA performance (87.2%).

#### 4.2. Evidence of a Perception Bottleneck

- A significant performance gap was observed among open-source models using the CA paradigm. Models like Llama-Vision and LLaVA variants exhibited much lower CA accuracy (e.g., Llama-Vision-11B: 53.4%; LLaVA-Llama3-8B: 53.2%), often with dramatic imbalances between positive and negative sample performance. This pattern strongly suggested that the primary limitation for these models was not their abstract reasoning capacity but rather the fidelity of their *internal visual perception* and subsequent translation into usable representations (i.e., the initial text descriptions they generated).

#### 4.3. Generalizability (Bongard-HOI)

- On the Bongard-HOI dataset, the trends from Bongard-OpenWorld were replicated. Performance systematically improved with increased paradigm structure (DVRL < DRL < CA) for both GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0. CA consistently yielded the highest accuracies (65-83% range), validating the framework's applicability across different complex natural image reasoning datasets, though a gap remained compared to human performance on HOI.

#### 4.4. State-of-the-Art on Winoground

- Applying the CA paradigm to Winoground, using Gemini 2.0 for task-agnostic image descriptions and various LLMs (including GPT-4o) as reasoning engines, achieved *new state-of-the-art results* across all three standard metrics (Text: 75.5%, Image: 58.5%, Group: 52.0% with GPT-4o as reasoning engine). This success on a distinct compositional reasoning task further demonstrates the robustness and generalizability of decoupling rich perception (into text) from robust linguistic reasoning.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies: Isolating Perception and Reasoning

- **Rule Application Fidelity (Section 7.1):** When models were provided with externally generated, high-quality rule summaries and tasked only with query classification, performance generally improved compared to end-to-end CA (where they generate their own descriptions). However, some open-source models (e.g., Llama-Vision-11B) still showed a "confirmation bias" (high accuracy on positive queries, poor on negatives), suggesting difficulty in reliably identifying rule violations even with an explicit rule. This implies that while rule application is less challenging than initial perception/description, it's not without its nuances.
- **Impact of Description Quality on Reasoning (Section 7.2):** This was a highly revealing study. Providing *GPT-4o generated high-quality descriptions* dramatically improved the reasoning accuracy of VLMs that struggled using their own descriptions (e.g., Llama-Vision-11B improved from 53.4% to 84.17%; Llama-Vision-90B from 55.1% to 90.98%).
 - Remarkably, this approach also enabled *text-only LLMs* (e.g., Phi-4, Qwen models) to perform the visual reasoning task effectively, with several achieving over 90% accuracy, surpassing human baselines on Bongard-OpenWorld. This provides strong evidence that: (1) high-quality textual descriptions can serve as effective surrogates for visual input, enabling modality transfer; and (2) the core symbolic reasoning abilities of many VLMs (and LLMs) are significantly stronger than suggested by end-to-end evaluations, with *perceptual representation generation* being the primary bottleneck.

In summary, the results consistently point to the effectiveness of structured, multi-stage processing, particularly when perception is effectively decoupled into rich textual representations. The pervasive "perception bottleneck" in many open-source models is a key diagnostic insight, highlighting that improving visual encoding into usable representations is paramount for advancing visual reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the development and understanding of advanced AI systems, particularly Vision-Language Models.

1. **A Novel Diagnostic Tool for VLM Development:** The cognitively-inspired evaluation framework (DVRL, DRL, CA) provides a powerful diagnostic lens, moving beyond simple accuracy metrics to pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses in VLM processing. This allows developers to understand *how* models fail, not just *that* they fail. This level of diagnosis is crucial for targeted improvements and for designing more robust AI.

2. **Highlighting the "Perception Bottleneck":** A central and critical finding is the pervasive "perception bottleneck" in many current VLMs, especially open-source ones. The dramatic performance improvement observed when these models are provided with high-fidelity, externally generated descriptions unequivocally demonstrates that their *reasoning capabilities* are often much stronger than their ability to *reliably extract and represent visual information*. This insight shifts the focus of future research and development: rather than solely optimizing reasoning architectures, significant effort must be directed towards enhancing the fidelity, richness, and consistency of the visual perception front-end in VLMs.

3. **Validation of Decoupled Architectures:** The consistent success of the Componential Analysis (CA) paradigm, achieving new State-of-the-Art (SOTA) results across diverse benchmarks (Bongard-OpenWorld, Bongard-HOI, Winoground), strongly supports the hypothesis that decoupling perception from reasoning is a highly promising direction. By converting complex visual inputs into rich, task-agnostic textual descriptions, the CA approach allows powerful language models to apply their sophisticated reasoning abilities effectively. This suggests a modular architectural paradigm where specialized, high-fidelity perception modules feed symbolic representations to general-purpose reasoning engines could lead to more robust and generalizable visual intelligence.

4. **Enabling Multi-Modality Transfer and LLM Capabilities:** The demonstration that text-only LLMs can perform complex visual reasoning tasks effectively when provided with high-quality textual descriptions is a profound insight. It shows that rich symbolic representations derived from vision can serve as an effective interface for general reasoning engines, essentially bridging the modality gap. This opens avenues for leveraging the advanced reasoning power of LLMs even for tasks that are inherently visual, by focusing on robust visual-to-textual conversion.

5. **Informing Cognitive AI Research:** By systematically structuring VLM evaluations along lines inspired by human cognitive strategies, the work contributes to our understanding of how artificial intelligence processes information compared to human cognition. The observed benefits of structured processing (e.g., DRL over DVRL, and CA's analytical decomposition) resonate with cognitive science findings on the advantages of breaking down complex problems.

6. **Towards More Reliable and Human-Like AI:** Ultimately, by providing tools and insights that help build VLMs with a more robust and diagnosable understanding of the visual world, this research contributes to the broader goal of developing AI systems that exhibit more human-like visual intelligence—systems that can not only "see" but also "understand" and "reason" about complex visual concepts in a reliable and generalizable manner.
