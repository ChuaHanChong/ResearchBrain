# Adapt3R: Adaptive 3D Scene Representation for Domain Transfer in Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Adapt3R introduces an adaptive 3D scene representation for robotic imitation learning, leveraging 2D foundation models for semantic reasoning and 3D information for end-effector relative localization. This approach enables robust zero-shot policy transfer to novel robot embodiments and unseen camera viewpoints, enhancing generalization capabilities.

## Problem

- Robot policies learned through imitation often exhibit brittleness and limited generalization capacity when faced with out-of-distribution observations like varied camera poses, lighting, or novel robot embodiments.
- Traditional 2D visual inputs struggle with explicit spatial reasoning and are highly sensitive to viewpoint changes, making them inadequate for broad generalization in robotics.
- Existing 3D scene representation methods for robot learning frequently overfit to specific 3D geometries, are computationally intensive, or neglect rich semantic information, hindering performance in multitask or high-precision settings.

## Method

- **Adapt3R** employs a 3D observation encoder that explicitly decouples semantic reasoning (using 2D ==CLIP ResNet== features) from 3D localization, focusing 3D information solely on end-effector relative positions.
- It canonicalizes 3D point clouds to the end-effector's coordinate frame and applies Fourier basis positional encoding for enhanced spatial learning, alongside feature-based downsampling and attention pooling for efficient representation.
- The architecture is designed as a modular observation encoder that produces a compact conditioning vector, readily integrable with various downstream imitation learning algorithms such as ACT, Diffusion Policy, and BAKU.

## Results

- Achieved robust zero-shot transfer for unseen camera poses, demonstrating a performance drop of less than **6%** on real-world tasks compared to **44.4%** for RGB and **55.6%** for 3D Diffuser Actor.
- Outperformed baseline methods like RGB, RGBD, and DP3 in zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer tasks in simulation, successfully transferring policies to novel robot platforms (UR5e, Kinova3, Kuka IIWA).
- Maintained strong in-distribution performance on 93 multitask simulated tasks (LIBERO-90) and high-precision insertion tasks (MimicGen), all while operating at a practical inference speed of **44.1** Hz.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Offloading semantic reasoning entirely to a 2D foundation model and utilizing 3D information specifically for localization relative to the end-effector is crucial for superior generalization across embodiments and viewpoints.
> - Canonicalizing 3D scene representations to the end-effector's coordinate frame and applying selective cropping of points significantly improves cross-embodiment transfer and robustness to changing camera perspectives.
> - Feature-based downsampling, positional encoding, and attention pooling collectively enable the model to retain diverse task-relevant semantic information and dynamically focus on critical scene regions, preventing overfitting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning, particularly imitation learning (IL), has made significant strides in enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks. However, a major bottleneck remains: the **brittleness and limited generalization capacity** of learned policies when faced with observations outside their training distribution. This includes variations in camera pose, lighting conditions, background clutter, and especially, novel robot embodiments.

Traditionally, robot learning has heavily relied on 2D visual inputs (RGB images). While powerful, 2D representations struggle with explicit spatial reasoning and are highly sensitive to viewpoint changes, making them poor candidates for broad generalization. The computer vision and natural language processing communities have achieved remarkable success with generalist models (e.g., large language models, vision transformers) due to massive, diverse datasets, but similar scale in robotics data collection is prohibitively expensive and often leads to sparse data distributions.

To address these generalization limitations, researchers have increasingly explored **3D scene representations**. Prior work in this area can be broadly categorized:
- **Keypose Prediction with Voxels/Simulated Views (e.g., PerAct, RVT):** These methods predict discrete end-effector keyposes, assuming tasks can be decomposed sequentially. This limits their applicability to tasks requiring continuous, high-frequency control or non-linear trajectories.
- **Implicit Scene Representations:** Embedding foundation model outputs into implicit scene representations (e.g., NeRF-based approaches) can be powerful but often require expensive inference-time training, hindering real-time control.
- **Point Cloud-based Methods:** Point clouds offer a relatively efficient 3D representation. Approaches like DP3 (3D Diffusion Policy) leverage colorless point clouds, showing some generalization but struggling in multitask or high-precision settings due to the omission of rich semantic information. Other methods, like 3D Diffuser Actor (3DDA), incorporate semantic features and self-attention over scene points, but this can be computationally intensive and prone to overfitting to 3D geometry, limiting transfer to unseen camera poses. Finally, approaches like GenDP use DINOv2 features lifted into 3D, but rely on hand-selecting task-relevant reference features, which is not scalable for multitask learning.

**Adapt3R carves out a unique niche** by focusing on a general-purpose 3D observation encoder that *explicitly separates semantic reasoning from 3D localization*. It acknowledges the strengths of large 2D foundation models (like CLIP) for semantic feature extraction and uses 3D information *primarily* to localize these semantics relative to the robot's end-effector. This design choice aims to mitigate the overfitting to specific 3D geometries or viewpoint distributions that plague many existing 3D-based methods, thereby offering superior zero-shot transfer capabilities. The modular nature of Adapt3R, allowing it to serve as a conditioning vector for various downstream imitation learning algorithms (e.g., ACT, Diffusion Policy, BAKU), further positions it as a flexible and broadly applicable solution in the evolving landscape of generalist robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable observation representation for imitation learning that can overcome the limitations of existing methods in handling out-of-distribution scenarios. Specifically, the paper aims to:

1. **Enable Zero-Shot Transfer to Novel Embodiments:** Design an architecture that allows policies trained on one robot embodiment to effectively operate on different, unseen robot embodiments without requiring any fine-tuning.
2. **Facilitate Zero-Shot Transfer to Unseen Camera Viewpoints:** Create a representation that is robust to significant changes in camera pose, allowing a policy to perform tasks effectively even when observed from novel perspectives.
3. **Maintain High Performance on In-Distribution and Complex Tasks:** Ensure that the proposed representation does not sacrifice learning capacity or precision on standard multitask learning benchmarks and high-precision insertion tasks.
4. **Develop a General-Purpose and Modular Encoder:** Create an observation encoder that can seamlessly integrate with and condition various existing imitation learning algorithms, making it a versatile tool for the broader robotics community.
5. **Achieve Efficient Inference:** Provide a solution that operates at a practical inference speed for real-world robotic deployment.

The primary motivation stems from the recognition that current imitation learning policies, even those trained on large datasets, are notoriously brittle to changes in environmental factors like camera pose and robot embodiment. Collecting enough data to cover the full spectrum of deployment variations is infeasible. The authors hypothesize that by intelligently combining the semantic power of 2D foundation models with targeted 3D localization information, they can create a more resilient scene representation that generalizes better to unseen conditions, moving closer to the goal of training truly generalist robot agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Adapt3R's methodology is built upon a "key insight": **offloading semantic reasoning entirely to a 2D backbone and using 3D information solely to localize that semantic information relative to the end-effector.** This allows the system to leverage pre-trained visual knowledge while gaining robust spatial understanding without needing to learn complex 3D semantics from scratch, which is data-intensive and prone to overfitting.

The architecture comprises three main stages: 3D Scene Representation, Point Cloud Reduction, and Policy Learning Integration.

#### 3.1. 3D Scene Representation

1. **Feature Extraction and Projection:** For each RGBD image from calibrated cameras, an intermediate feature volume is extracted using a pretrained **CLIP ResNet backbone**. These 2D semantic features (F) are then deprojected into a 3D point cloud using the depth information and camera calibration matrices. The points are transformed into the robot's base frame (X_robot).
2. **End Effector Coordinate Frames (EECF):** Crucially, the point cloud (X_robot) is then transformed into the **end-effector's coordinate frame (X_EE)** using proprioceptive information. This canonicalization helps the policy reason about relative positions, which is particularly beneficial for cross-embodiment learning by making the scene representation independent of the robot's specific base position or arm configuration.
3. **Positional Encoding:** To enable neural networks to better learn from high-frequency spatial variations (critical for precise manipulation), each 3D point in X_EE is encoded using a **Fourier basis (sin/cos functions of increasing frequencies)**. This lifts the raw 3D coordinates into a higher-dimensional space (ˆX_EE), making spatial relationships more amenable to learning.

#### 3.2. Point Cloud Reduction

The goal here is to compress the rich 3D scene representation into a compact conditioning vector (`z`) without losing critical information or overfitting.

1. **Cropping:** An intermediate cropping strategy is employed. Instead of tight, task-specific bounds or no cropping, Adapt3R crops scenes to ensure the entire table is visible while background points are excluded. Furthermore, points with negative z-values (relative to the end-effector) are filtered out, effectively removing most of the robot arm and further aiding cross-embodiment transfer.
2. **Feature-Based Downsampling:** Unlike methods that use Cartesian coordinates for farthest point sampling (FPS), Adapt3R downsamples the point cloud based on the **ℓ2 distance between the semantic features (F)**. This strategy prioritizes retaining points with diverse semantic information, making the resulting downsampled cloud (F_ds, ˆX_EE,ds) more concentrated around task-relevant objects and more robust to camera pose changes that might alter spatial point distribution.
3. **Language Embeddings:** Projected **CLIP language embeddings (φ(L))** corresponding to the natural language task instruction are concatenated to the features of each point in the downsampled cloud. This provides semantic guidance for multitask settings, enabling the policy to differentiate and reason about specific task goals. The final point cloud (P) combines positional encodings, semantic features, and language embeddings.
4. **Attention Pooling:** The processed point cloud (P) is reduced to a single conditioning vector (`z`) using a learned **attention pooling operation**. This involves learning key and value MLPs (K_θ, V_θ) for each point and a fixed query (q). The attention mechanism dynamically weighs the importance of different points in the scene, allowing the model to focus on task-relevant regions and ignore out-of-distribution points, which is crucial for generalization to novel viewpoints.

#### 3.3. Policy Learning Integration

Adapt3R is designed as a **modular encoder** that outputs a conditioning vector `z`. This vector can then be used as input for a variety of downstream action decoders, trained end-to-end with Adapt3R. The paper demonstrates this compatibility by integrating Adapt3R with three popular imitation learning algorithms:
- **Action Chunking Transformer (ACT):** A transformer-based policy that predicts chunks of actions.
- **Diffusion Policy (DP):** A policy that samples action chunks through a learned reverse Langevin dynamics process.
- **BAKU:** An efficient transformer for multi-task policy learning.

This modularity allows Adapt3R to leverage the strengths of different action decoders while providing a generalizable observation backbone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experimental validation in both simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating Adapt3R's effectiveness in multitask learning and zero-shot domain transfer.

#### 4.1. Simulated Experiments

The evaluations were conducted across 93 simulated tasks from LIBERO-90 (multitask) and 3 high-precision insertion tasks from MimicGen (Coffee, Square, Threading).

- **In-Distribution Behavior Cloning:** Adapt3R achieves strong in-distribution performance, comparable to or exceeding the best baselines (RGB, RGBD, DP3, 3D Diffuser Actor). Notably, it significantly outperforms DP3 (which lacks semantic features) on MimicGen tasks and achieves the strongest overall success rate for MimicGen Threading, highlighting the importance of semantic features for precision and complex tasks. RGBD often performs worse than RGB, suggesting that raw depth information can be an overfitting risk when wrist cameras already provide spatial context.
- **Unseen Camera Poses (Zero-Shot Transfer):** Adapt3R consistently provides a substantial boost in performance with unseen camera viewpoints. On LIBERO-90, it maintains over 80% success rate even with large rotations, and is the only method to achieve non-zero success rates on MimicGen tasks after significant camera rotations (≥ 0.6 radians). This demonstrates Adapt3R's robustness to viewpoint changes, contrasting with DP3 and 3DDA which struggle, likely due to their reliance on learning 3D geometry from scene point distributions that change with camera shifts.
- **Cross Embodiment (Zero-Shot Transfer):** When trained on a Franka Panda robot and evaluated zero-shot on UR5e, Kinova3, and Kuka IIWA, Adapt3R consistently outperforms RGB, RGBD, and DP3. Its performance is similar to or superior to 3DDA, particularly in MimicGen tasks, confirming that semantic-aligned point clouds are effective for cross-embodiment transfer.
- **Inference Speed:** Adapt3R achieves high performance at 44.1 Hz, which is significantly faster than 3D Diffuser Actor (2.6 Hz) and practical for real-world deployment (faster than typical RGBD camera input rates of ~30 Hz). While RGB and DP3 are faster, their generalization performance is poor.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **End-Effector Coordinate Frame (EECF) & EE Cropping:** Both features lead to substantial improvements in generalizability by helping the robot reason about relative positions and ignore irrelevant arm parts.
 - **Image Features:** Removing semantic image features (replacing with nothing or raw RGB) leads to a significant performance drop, especially for new camera poses, underscoring their critical role.
 - **Language Features:** Including language embeddings provides a modest but consistent improvement across tasks.
 - **Positional Encoding:** Lifting 3D points with Fourier basis positional encoding is crucial for generalization to new views, yielding an average 11.2% improvement.
 - **Feature-Based Farthest Point Sampling (FPS):** Downsampling based on feature similarity (rather than Cartesian coordinates) results in a respectable 6.6% average improvement, particularly beneficial for novel camera views by focusing on task-relevant objects.
 - **Attention Pooling:** Using attention pooling instead of max pooling is especially helpful for new viewpoints, as it allows the model to selectively attend to relevant points and disregard out-of-distribution regions.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

Adapt3R's capabilities were validated on a real-world multitask IL benchmark involving 6 pick-and-place tasks using a Universal Robots UR5.

- **In-Distribution Performance:** Adapt3R shows strong in-distribution performance, similar to 3DDA and superior to RGB and DP3. DP3's poor performance in the real world highlights the unreliability of noisy depth estimates from real sensors without rich semantic information.
- **Unseen Camera Poses (Zero-Shot Transfer):** This is where Adapt3R truly shines. While RGB and 3DDA performance drops by 44.4% and 55.6% respectively with an unseen camera viewpoint, Adapt3R's performance drops by less than 6%. It achieves a higher average task completion out-of-distribution than RGB and DP3 manage in-distribution. This confirms Adapt3R's robust design, explicitly encouraging the learner *not* to over-condition on the relative locations of points, thereby generalizing effectively to new viewpoints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Adapt3R represents a significant step forward in developing generalizable robotic policies for manipulation tasks. Its key contributions and potential impact are:

- **Enhanced Generalization:** By intelligently decoupling semantic reasoning (via 2D foundation models) from 3D localization (relative to the end-effector), Adapt3R provides unprecedented zero-shot transfer capabilities to novel robot embodiments and vastly different camera viewpoints. This directly addresses one of the most pressing challenges in robot learning: the brittleness of policies to out-of-distribution observations.
- **Practical Applicability:** The method is validated on both extensive simulated benchmarks and a real-world robot, demonstrating its deployability and effectiveness in physical systems. Its favorable inference speed makes it suitable for real-time control.
- **Modular and Flexible Design:** Adapt3R's design as a general-purpose observation encoder compatible with various action decoders (ACT, DP, BAKU) makes it a versatile tool. Researchers can integrate Adapt3R into their preferred IL frameworks, potentially boosting the generalization of existing and future algorithms.
- **Catalyst for Generalist Robots:** This work moves the field closer to the vision of "generalist robots" that can operate reliably in diverse, unstructured environments without constant retraining or fine-tuning. By reducing the data requirements for covering varied deployment conditions, Adapt3R makes large-scale robot learning more efficient and practical.
- **Informing Future 3D Representation Design:** The ablation studies provide crucial insights into the importance of specific architectural choices (EECF, feature-based downsampling, positional encoding, attention pooling). These findings can guide future research in designing robust 3D perception systems for robotics, emphasizing the strategic use of 3D information over simply learning complex 3D geometry.

#### 5.1. Limitations

The authors candidly acknowledge several limitations:
- **Reliance on Depth Information and Camera Calibration:** Adapt3R requires calibrated RGBD cameras, and its performance can be affected by noisy or missing depth data, a common issue in large-scale datasets or with certain object materials (reflective, translucent).
- **Bimanual Settings:** The transformation to the end-effector's coordinate frame raises questions about its direct applicability to bimanual tasks, although conditioning on two Adapt3R vectors (one per hand) or using a robot torso frame are suggested as potential solutions.
- **Shared Action Space for Cross-Embodiment:** The current cross-embodiment transfer relies on robots having shared action spaces (6D pose prediction). While Adapt3R helps bridge the *perception gap* between embodiments, truly general cross-embodiment learning across diverse robots might require more complex architectures (e.g., separate heads for different action spaces).

Despite these limitations, Adapt3R provides a robust and practical solution for improving generalization in robotic imitation learning, paving the way for more capable and adaptable autonomous systems.
