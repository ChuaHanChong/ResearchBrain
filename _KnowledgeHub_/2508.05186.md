# Learning to See and Act: Task-Aware Virtual View Exploration for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Task-aware Virtual View Exploration (TVVE) framework improves multi-task robotic manipulation by dynamically selecting informative camera viewpoints and employing a task-aware visual encoder to address occlusions and task interference. It attained an 86.6% average success rate on RLBench and exhibited increased robustness across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems often rely on fixed-view cameras, leading to occlusions of target objects or the end-effector in cluttered or dynamic scenes.
- Shared visual encoders in multi-task Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from task interference when handling visually and semantically diverse tasks, limiting generalization.
- Prior multi-view perception systems attempted to address occlusion but typically lacked dynamic, task-driven viewpoint adaptation during manipulation.

## Method

- A ==Task-aware Virtual View Exploration== (**TVVE**) framework combines dynamic viewpoint selection with task-specific visual processing.
- The Task-aware Mixture-of-Experts (==TaskMoE==) uses context-aware routing and a decoupled gating strategy to specialize visual representations and action policies.
- A Multi-Viewpoint Exploration Policy (MVEP) dynamically selects optimal camera poses to maximize visibility of manipulation targets.

## Results

- TVVE achieved an **86.6%** average success rate across 18 RLBench tasks, outperforming ARP by **5%**, and an **83.2%** success rate in single-view RLBench, surpassing RVT2 and ARP.
- On the RLBench-OG benchmark, TVVE attained a **67.0%** average success rate, demonstrating higher robustness to occlusions, background texture shifts, and camera pose changes compared to baselines.
- In real-world tests, TVVE achieved an **88.0%** success rate on Dobot Nova 2 and **78.0%** on Franka Research 3, showing superior performance and adaptability under unseen instances and heavy occlusion compared to Diffusion Policy and ARP.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically exploring and re-rendering virtual viewpoints significantly enhances scene understanding by mitigating occlusions and providing richer contextual information for action prediction.
> - Task-aware expert routing in visual encoders, leveraging both language instructions and visual cues, effectively reduces task interference and improves generalization in multi-task robotic manipulation.
> - A decoupled gating strategy in MoE allows for scalable parameter sharing among similar tasks while routing distinct tasks to different experts, optimizing resource utilization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

General-purpose robotic systems are increasingly expected to execute a multitude of manipulation tasks across various environments. Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and transformer-based architectures have enabled end-to-end robotic manipulation frameworks to achieve results on complex tasks. These models typically rely on visual observations from a single fixed view or a small number of predefined viewpoints to inform action predictions.

This research addresses two primary limitations within this landscape:
1. **Fixed-Viewpoint Limitations:** The reliance on fixed cameras often leads to occlusions of target objects or the robot's end-effector, particularly in cluttered or dynamic scenes where objects are repositioned during task execution. This can result in incomplete scene understanding and inaccurate action predictions, hindering task success. Previous work in 3D perception and multi-view systems has attempted to overcome this, but often without dynamic viewpoint adaptation.
2. **Multi-task Learning Challenges:** While large VLA models and vision-language models (VLMs) have improved multi-task capabilities, their shared visual encoders can suffer from task interference when handling visually and semantically diverse tasks. This entanglement can limit generalization and scalability, a problem that Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models have begun to address in broader machine learning contexts, but less directly for the specific challenges of robotic manipulation's visual feature distribution and motion patterns.

This paper positions itself by proposing a framework that integrates dynamic viewpoint exploration with a task-aware visual encoder. This approach aims to enhance spatial understanding and reduce task interference, thereby improving the robustness and generalization of multi-task robotic manipulation systems. It builds upon existing VLA models, specifically adapting components from models like RVT-2 and ARP, while introducing novel mechanisms for visual perception and task-specific feature extraction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to enhance the robustness and generalization capabilities of multi-task robotic manipulation systems by addressing limitations in visual perception and task-specific feature extraction.

The motivations for these objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Occlusion and Incomplete Visual Information:**
 - **Problem:** Current VLA models for robotic manipulation frequently rely on observations from fixed cameras. In complex environments, particularly when objects or the end-effector are moved, these fixed viewpoints often result in occlusions. This means critical parts of the scene, such as the target object or the robot's gripper, may not be fully visible.
 - **Consequence:** Incomplete visual information impedes the robot's ability to accurately interpret the scene and predict appropriate actions. For instance, if a target object is obscured, the robot might fail to grasp it correctly or place it in the designated location, leading to task failure (as illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper).
 - **Motivation:** To overcome this, the authors aim to develop a system that can actively "see" by dynamically selecting informative viewpoints, thereby maximizing the visibility of task-relevant elements and enabling a more comprehensive 3D understanding of the scene.

- **Mitigating Task Interference in Multi-Task Learning:**
 - **Problem:** While transformer-based and VLA models have shown promise in multi-task robotic control, they typically use shared visual encoders across all tasks. When tasks are visually and semantically diverse (e.g., picking a delicate object versus tightening a screw), a shared encoder can experience "task interference." This occurs because different tasks require distinct visual representations and processing pathways, and forcing a single set of parameters to handle all tasks can lead to conflicts and suboptimal learning.
 - **Consequence:** Task interference limits the model's ability to generalize across a broad range of manipulation behaviors and can hinder its scalability as the number and diversity of tasks increase.
 - **Motivation:** To improve scalability and generalization, the authors are motivated to design a visual encoder that can adaptively specialize its processing based on the specific task at hand. This involves routing visual features to task-specialized components to mitigate interference and ensure that appropriate representations are learned for each distinct manipulation behavior.

In summary, the authors aim to develop a robotic system that can both intelligently explore its visual environment to gather complete information and process that information in a task-specific manner, thereby improving manipulation accuracy, robustness to environmental shifts, and generalization across diverse tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Task-aware Virtual View Exploration (TVVE), integrates dynamic viewpoint selection with task-specific visual feature extraction. The methodology is structured around three main components: a Task-aware Mixture-of-Experts (TaskMoE), a Multi-Viewpoint Exploration Policy (MVEP), and a multi-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Overview of TVVE Framework

The TVVE framework processes a language instruction, RGB-D camera observations, and the current gripper state. Initially, input RGB-D images from fixed viewpoints are used to reconstruct a 3D point cloud of the scene. This global point cloud is then refined by a coarse grounding module (adapted from RVT-2) to identify an area of interest, which narrows the search space for view selection. Unlike RVT-2, TVVE incorporates a TaskMoE module within its Multi-View Transformer (MVT) for visual feature extraction, allowing for task-aligned feature processing. The MVEP component then leverages this refined point cloud to search for optimal camera poses that maximize visibility of manipulation targets. These selected viewpoints are re-rendered into new image observations, which are subsequently processed by another TaskMoE-based MVT before being fed to an autoregressive action prediction model (an upgrade of ARP's policy, also incorporating TaskMoE).

#### 3.2. TaskMoE (Task-aware Mixture-of-Experts)

TaskMoE is designed to manage the heterogeneity of complex manipulation tasks in multi-task learning by specializing visual representation and action policy. It introduces two main innovations compared to prior MoE approaches:
- **Context-Aware Expert Routing:** Instead of relying solely on task identifiers, TaskMoE incorporates fused instruction and scene-related visual cues to guide expert selection. A cross-modality module uses a cross-attention mechanism to model interaction between instruction and visual information. The resulting context-aware features are then fused with a task identifier via a Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) layer, enabling adaptive expert selection.
- **Decoupled Gating Strategy:** To improve scalability and generalization, the number of routing gates (N_G) is decoupled from the total number of tasks (N_J), with N_G < N_J. This allows parameter sharing among semantically similar tasks through the same gate while routing semantically diverse tasks through different gates. All tasks access a shared pool of N_E experts, and for each input, only the top-k experts (based on gating scores) are activated. Regularization terms (balance, entropy, semantic regularizers) are used to prevent gate/expert collapse and encourage diverse routing.

#### 3.3. Multi-Viewpoint Exploration Policy (MVEP)

The MVEP is designed to select K viewpoints that maximize the capture of informative regions for manipulation.
- **Input:** The MVEP takes a concatenated representation of the reconstructed 3D point cloud (P) and its associated RGB features (F_img).
- **Camera Pose Parameterization:** Each camera pose is represented by a 5-dimensional vector (θ, ϕ, r, θ_up, ϕ_up) using a look-at model, which specifies the camera center in spherical coordinates and the orientation of its up-vector, always looking towards the coordinate origin.
- **Policy Output and Sampling:** The MVEP outputs the mean and log-standard deviation of a diagonal Gaussian distribution for these 5-dimensional parameters for each of the K viewpoints. Camera poses are then sampled using the reparameterization trick (μ + σ ⊙ ϵ). A sigmoid function is applied to constrain the sampled pose parameters within valid spherical coordinate ranges.

#### 3.4. Training Strategy

The training of TVVE proceeds in three stages:
1. **Stage 1: Fixed-View Pre-training:** A fixed-view variant of TVVE is initially trained using three default camera perspectives (front, left, top). The training objective comprises cross-entropy losses for coarse and fine grounding heatmaps (L_hc, L_hf), cross-entropy loss for end-effector rotation (L_rot), and binary classification losses for gripper state (L_gri) and collision indicator (L_col).
2. **Stage 2: MVEP Refinement via PPO:** The MVEP is refined using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. To avoid high time costs of physical interaction, a pseudo-environment interaction mechanism is introduced. The fixed-view TVVE from Stage 1 serves as a reference model, providing baseline losses (L_ref). MVEP generates dynamic viewpoints, leading to new losses (L_TVVE). The reward function for PPO is composed of three terms:
 - Improvement over reference: (L_ref - L_TVVE)
 - Confidence-based reward: Negative average entropy across fine grounding heatmaps.
 - Viewpoint diversity: Average pairwise cosine distance between camera positions.
 The reward components are adaptively normalized and aggregated. In this stage, only MVEP is trainable, while other TVVE components remain frozen.
3. **Stage 3: Full TVVE Fine-tuning:** To enhance the adaptation between MVEP and the action generation policy, the entire TVVE model (excluding the MVEP itself) is fine-tuned using the same loss functions as in Stage 1.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy and generalization capabilities of the TVVE framework were evaluated across simulated and real-world environments through extensive experimentation and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Comparisons on RLBench

- **Multi-View Setup:** TVVE achieved an average success rate of 86.6% across 18 tasks, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art method ARP (81.6%) by approximately 5%. The method exhibited an average rank of 2.17, indicating superior overall performance. Notable improvements were observed in tasks such as "Insert Peg" (from 65.6% to 98.0%) and "Sort Shape" (from 36.0% to 62.0%).
- **Single-View Setup:** In the single-view configuration across 10 tasks, TVVE attained an average success rate of 83.2%, surpassing RVT2 (82.7%) and ARP (81.6%). This demonstrates the system's ability to enhance performance even with restricted initial visual input.
- **Qualitative Observations:** The dynamic re-rendering enabled by TVVE was observed to mitigate occlusions and collisions more effectively than fixed-viewpoint methods, providing richer context for action prediction.

#### 4.2. Comparisons on RLBench-OG

- TVVE achieved an overall average success rate of 67.0% (±6.2) and the best average rank of 1.4 on the RLBench-OG benchmark, which includes occluded scenarios and various out-of-distribution (OOD) perturbations. This surpassed ARP (63.7% ± 6.1, rank 2.5) and Diffusion Policy (23.8% ± 1.9, rank 4.0).
- **Robustness to Perturbations:** TVVE showed higher resilience across different types of disturbances:
 - **Occlusion:** Achieved absolute gains of +2.0% and +5.4% over ARP for Occlusion 1 and 2, respectively.
 - **Background Texture Shifts:** Maintained a success rate of 74.3% (±0.9) compared to ARP's 68.1% (±1.6).
 - **Camera Pose Changes:** Preserved reliable execution with 73.2% (±0.9) success rate versus ARP's 69.7% (±1.4).
These results indicate that task-aware viewpoint exploration and decoupled visual representations contribute to robust manipulation under OOD disturbances.

#### 4.3. Comparisons on Real-World Tasks

- **Dobot Nova 2:** TVVE achieved an average success rate of 88.0% across five tasks, significantly outperforming Diffusion Policy (68.0%). This included substantial gains in tasks like "Push Buttons" (+30%) and "Stack Bowls" (+20%).
- **Franka Research 3:** TVVE achieved an average success rate of 78.0% across five tasks, outperforming Diffusion Policy (58.0%) and ARP (72.0%). When evaluated on ten additional tasks, TVVE maintained a higher average success rate of 70.0% compared to ARP's 62.0%.
- **Real-World Robustness:** In robustness tests on the "Pick Grape" task with the Dobot robot, TVVE demonstrated greater adaptability than Diffusion Policy under unseen instances, backgrounds, objects, heavy occlusion, and illumination variations. While both performed similarly in seen scenarios, TVVE significantly outperformed DP under unseen backgrounds, objects, and heavy occlusion.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of TaskMoE:** Removing the TaskMoE module resulted in a performance drop from 86.6% to 85.6% on the RLBench multi-view setup. For generalization to unseen tasks, the model with TaskMoE achieved 72.0% on "Open Drawer" compared to 60.0% without it, and 80.8% on trained tasks vs. 72.0% without, indicating its contribution to multi-task representation learning and generalization.
- **Effectiveness of MVEP:** Replacing MVEP's explored viewpoints with fixed viewpoints reduced the success rate from 86.6% to 83.3%. Using randomly selected viewpoints drastically reduced it to 8.9%. This underscores the MVEP's role in identifying informative perspectives.
- **Setting of K and r:**
 - **Radial Constraint (r):** A tighter radial constraint (0.90-1.04m) improved average success rate to 56.0% from 49.6% (baseline 0.75-1.3m), while a looser constraint (0.60-1.56m) slightly reduced it to 48.8%.
 - **Number of Views (K):** Increasing K from 2 to 4 (under baseline r) consistently improved success rates from 27.2% to 49.6% and 55.2%, respectively. K=3 was chosen for balancing performance and computational cost.

#### 4.5. TaskMoE Analysis

- **Instruction-level Routing:** t-SNE visualizations showed that increasing the number of experts (N_E) from 4 or 8 to 16 improved semantic disentanglement, creating more compact and specialized clusters for instructions. This also led to more balanced expert utilization and reduced reliance on "catch-all" fallback experts.
- **Task-level Routing:** Task-wise gate and expert usage visualization for N_E=8 and N_E=16 demonstrated clear task-dependent routing. With N_E=16, the system achieved finer skill specialization; tasks that shared a single expert at N_E=8 were routed to distinct experts (e.g., "meat off grill" dispersed over E1/E7, "place shape in shape sorter" shifted to E8/E10). This supported the effectiveness of the two-stage gate-expert design in providing capacity and achieving fine-grained routing.

**Data Efficiency:** TVVE consistently outperformed ARP across varying demonstration sizes (20, 40, 80, 100 demonstrations), suggesting superior sample efficiency. At 100 demonstrations, TVVE achieved 52.0% success rate compared to ARP's 45.3%.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TVVE framework presents contributions to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for multi-task learning in complex environments.

The key aspects of its significance include:
- **Enhanced Spatial Understanding and Occlusion Handling:** The Multi-Viewpoint Exploration Policy (MVEP) enables robots to dynamically select and re-render informative virtual camera viewpoints. This capability directly addresses the limitations of fixed-camera systems, which are susceptible to occlusions and incomplete scene observations. By actively optimizing viewpoints, the system can acquire more comprehensive 3D perception, which is critical for accurate and robust manipulation in cluttered or dynamic scenes.
- **Improved Multi-Task Generalization and Robustness:** The Task-aware Mixture-of-Experts (TaskMoE) module contributes to more effective multi-task learning by dynamically routing visual features to specialized expert encoders. This mechanism mitigates task interference, a common challenge in models that rely on shared encoders for diverse manipulation behaviors. The decoupled gating strategy further promotes parameter sharing among similar tasks while isolating processing for distinct ones, enhancing the model's scalability and ability to generalize to unseen tasks and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.
- **Introduction of a Robustness Benchmark:** The creation of RLBench-OG provides a standardized benchmark for evaluating robotic manipulation models under various visual perturbations (occlusions, lighting, texture, camera pose changes). This resource facilitates rigorous testing of model robustness and generalization, which is crucial for advancing the practical deployment of robotic systems in uncontrolled environments.
- **Demonstrated Performance in Simulation and Real-World:** The consistent outperformance of TVVE against multiple state-of-the-art baselines across RLBench, RLBench-OG, and real-robot tasks (Franka Research 3 and Dobot Nova 2) suggests the practical utility of its design principles. The real-world experiments further confirm its robustness to visual disturbances and unseen instructions, indicating its potential for application beyond simulation.

Potential impacts of this work include:
- **Advancement of General-Purpose Robotics:** By addressing fundamental challenges in perception and multi-task learning, TVVE contributes to the development of more versatile and autonomous robotic systems capable of performing a wide array of manipulation tasks in real-world settings.
- **Improved Reliability and Safety:** The enhanced ability to handle occlusions and adapt to environmental variations can lead to more reliable task execution, which is important for applications where robot failure has significant consequences.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The framework's success may encourage further exploration into active perception, adaptive learning architectures, and the development of more sophisticated reward mechanisms for reinforcement learning in robotics.

**Limitations:**
The framework does have some limitations. The dynamic multi-view re-rendering process can introduce a slight increase in inference latency. Additionally, the reliance on accurate global point cloud reconstruction makes the system potentially vulnerable to challenges posed by reflective or transparent objects, which are difficult to model accurately with current depth sensing technologies.

**Future Directions:**
Future research could focus on integrating multi-sensor data to enhance scene reconstruction robustness, particularly for challenging materials. Further exploration into domain adaptation techniques could also improve real-world generalization and bridge the simulation-to-reality gap more effectively.
