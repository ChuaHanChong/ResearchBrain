# Reasoning over Boundaries: Enhancing Specification Alignment via Test-time Deliberation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce "specification alignment" to address large language models' adherence to dynamic, scenario-specific behavioral and safety requirements. They propose ALIGN3, a lightweight test-time deliberation method, and SPECBENCH, a unified evaluation benchmark, demonstrating ALIGN3 improves Specification Alignment Rate (SAR) by up to 11.89% for models like Qwen3-14B with minimal computational cost.

## Problem

- Current LLM alignment methods often fail to adhere to dynamic, fine-grained, and scenario-specific behavioral and safety requirements in real-world applications.
- Prior instruction-following research focuses on isolated or single-constraint instructions, lacking the complexity required for real-world, scenario-level tasks.
- Existing safety alignment approaches utilize uniform standards across contexts, proving inflexible and costly to adapt to evolving, scenario-dependent safety boundaries.

## Method

- Formalized =="specification alignment"== as the ability of LLMs to satisfy dynamic, scenario-specific safety and behavioral specifications, highlighting the inherent safety-behavior trade-off.
- Developed ==ALIGN3==, a lightweight, three-step ==Test-Time Deliberation (TTD)== method, which integrates specifications into the LLM's reasoning process during inference to enhance alignment.
- Created ==SPECBENCH==, a unified benchmark encompassing five diverse real-world scenarios with 103 specifications and attack-enhanced prompts, for comprehensive evaluation of specification alignment.

## Results

- The SPECBENCH benchmark revealed substantial alignment gaps across current LLMs, with most models scoring below **65%** in Specification Alignment Rate (SAR), while GPT-5-chat achieved the highest SAR at **82.14%**.
- ALIGN3 significantly boosted SAR by up to **11.89%** (e.g., Qwen3-14B from **51.03%** to **62.92%**) across various LLMs, achieving superior performance among TTD methods with minimal token overhead (< **2,000** tokens).
- Reasoning-capable LLMs consistently outperformed their instruct-tuned counterparts in SAR, and models trained for safety often showed high safety scores but lower behavioral scores, demonstrating the safety-behavior trade-off.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-Time Deliberation (TTD) methods can be effectively extended beyond cognitive tasks to significantly enhance LLM adherence to complex, multi-faceted behavioral and safety specifications during inference.
> - An inherent trade-off exists between maximizing an LLM's helpfulness and ensuring its harmlessness, where optimizing one dimension often impacts the other, revealing over-refusal or safety violations.
> - Models with explicit internal reasoning capabilities (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) consistently outperform instruct-tuned models in understanding and adhering to complex alignment specifications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of large language models (LLMs) across various real-world applications has necessitated their adherence to specific behavioral and safety guidelines. The existing research landscape for LLMs involves several related areas:

- **Instruction-Following:** Prior work in instruction-following has focused on enabling LLMs to comply with given instructions, often emphasizing single semantic or format constraints. More recent efforts have introduced structured evaluations for complex instructions. However, these often overlook the dynamic and varied nature of real-world scenarios and the complexity of scenario-level specifications.
- **Safety Alignment:** A central focus in LLM research has been safety alignment, aiming to mitigate the generation of toxic or harmful content. This has led to the development of frameworks, policies, and benchmarks from major foundation model providers (e.g., OpenAI, Google, Meta, Anthropic) and research communities. These efforts often involve training-based or inference-time methods to enhance safety. While extensive, many safety benchmarks apply uniform standards, potentially overlooking that diverse applications and scenarios may demand distinct and adaptable safety boundaries.
- **Test-Time Scaling (TTS):** This area of research aims to improve LLM performance by increasing computational resources during inference. TTS methods include multi-pass techniques that refine outputs through iterative feedback or parallel sampling, and single-pass methods that enhance reasoning within a single generation by adjusting verbosity or introducing interventions. These methods are typically applied to improve performance in tasks like mathematical reasoning and code generation.

This paper addresses a gap in these areas by introducing "specification alignment." It argues that existing approaches are often insufficient because they either: 1) apply uniform safety standards across diverse contexts, failing to capture scenario-specific nuances; 2) focus on question-level instructions without considering systematic, scenario-level requirements; or 3) are costly to adapt when specifications evolve. The work positions itself as a unified approach to evaluate and improve LLMs' ability to follow dynamic, fine-grained, and scenario-specific specifications, encompassing both behavioral helpfulness and safety considerations. It integrates concepts from instruction-following and safety alignment within a test-time deliberation framework, aiming for a more flexible and adaptable solution to real-world deployment challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable large language models (LLMs) to effectively follow dynamic, fine-grained, and scenario-specific behavioral and safety specifications, a challenge termed "specification alignment." The work is motivated by the increasing deployment of LLMs in diverse real-world applications, each requiring bespoke and evolving guidelines tailored by users or organizations.

The key objectives and their underlying motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Formalize the Challenge of Specification Alignment:**
 - **Motivation:** Real-world applications of LLMs are governed by varying specifications, from defining what constitutes "helpful" behavior (e.g., coding assistants requiring specific formats, storytelling needing educational content) to establishing "safe" boundaries (e.g., mental health chatbots strictly prohibiting harmful advice). Current evaluations often treat these aspects separately or apply generalized standards.
 - **Objective:** To formally define "specification alignment" as the joint optimization of satisfying scenario-specific behavioral specifications (behavioral-spec) to maximize helpfulness while remaining within the bounds of scenario-specific safety specifications (safety-spec) to ensure harmlessness. This formalization also highlights the inherent "safety-behavior trade-off," where enhancing one dimension can inadvertently weaken the other.

- **Develop a Unified Benchmark for Specification Alignment (SPECBENCH):**
 - **Motivation:** A lack of comprehensive and unified benchmarks exists for systematically evaluating LLMs against dynamic, fine-grained, and scenario-specific specifications that reflect real-world user intent and encompass both behavioral and safety requirements simultaneously. Existing benchmarks may focus on general safety or specific instruction formats but do not combine the nuanced and adaptable nature of real-world application guidelines.
 - **Objective:** To create SPECBENCH, a benchmark that quantifies LLMs' alignment with both behavioral and safety specifications. This benchmark covers five diverse real-world scenarios, includes 103 distinct specifications, and features 1,500 prompts. Each specification is derived from domain-specific resources and policies to ensure realism, and the dataset is constructed to be of moderate difficulty through rigorous filtering and attack enhancement.

- **Evaluate LLMs and Test-Time Deliberation (TTD) Methods on SPECBENCH:**
 - **Motivation:** Understanding the current capabilities of various LLMs (both instruct and reasoning models) in achieving specification alignment is crucial. Furthermore, identifying flexible and efficient strategies to improve this alignment, particularly in dynamic environments where specifications evolve frequently, is important. Training-based alignment methods are often costly and inflexible for rapidly changing requirements.
 - **Objective:** To conduct extensive experiments on 18 instruct and 15 reasoning models, including open-source and closed-source families, using SPECBENCH. The evaluation aims to reveal existing alignment gaps and assess the effectiveness of various Test-Time Deliberation (TTD) methods (such as Self-Refine, TPO, and MoreThink) in enhancing specification alignment without retraining.

- **Propose a Lightweight Test-Time Deliberation Method (ALIGN3):**
 - **Motivation:** While existing TTD methods show promise, there is a need for a lightweight and effective strategy that can specifically address the safety-behavior trade-off in specification alignment with minimal computational overhead.
 - **Objective:** To propose ALIGN3, a three-stage TTD method that utilizes hierarchical reflection and revision. This method is designed to progressively optimize behavior while ensuring safety, providing an efficient way for LLMs to reason over specification boundaries at inference time.

In essence, the research is motivated by the practical necessity of making LLMs dependable and controllable in diverse real-world deployments, where adherence to both helpfulness and safety often involves navigating complex and dynamic constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research establishes a framework for "specification alignment," introducing a benchmark and a novel method for enhancing LLMs' adherence to dynamic, scenario-specific behavioral and safety guidelines.

#### 3.1. Formalization of Specification Alignment

The study defines a **Scenario** as a specific application context with a task description and a set of operational specifications reflecting user preferences. **Specifications (spec)** are formalized criteria comprising **safety-spec** (defining safety boundaries, adaptable to scenario objectives) and **behavioral-spec** (guiding helpful content preferences, goal orientation, and format constraints). **Specification Alignment** is proposed as the challenge of simultaneously satisfying both dimensions: maximizing helpfulness within safety boundaries. This highlights an inherent **safety-behavior trade-off**, where strengthening one dimension may weaken the other.

#### 3.2. Test-Time Specification Alignment (TTD) Formulation

The problem is formalized as maximizing an expected behavioral score `r_beh(x, z)` (proportion of behavioral-spec satisfied) over responses `z` from prompts `x`, subject to a safety risk `Risk_safety(x, z)` (likelihood/severity of safety-spec violations) remaining below a budget `ϵ`. This inference-time optimization, denoted as Test-Time Deliberation (TTD), aims to refine the reasoning trace `y` of a fixed model `θ` to balance these objectives.

#### 3.3. SPECBENCH Benchmark Construction

SPECBENCH is a unified benchmark designed to evaluate specification alignment.

- **Scenario Construction:** Five diverse, mutually independent scenarios are defined:
 - Biochemical Procedure Instruction (Biochem)
 - Child-Oriented Storytelling Generation (Child)
 - Code Development & Secure Operation (Code)
 - Personal Health Education Instruction (Health)
 - Travel Itinerary Planning (Travel)
 Each scenario comes with a task description and a set of operational specifications.
- **Specification Construction:** A total of 103 specifications (approximately 10 safety-spec and 10 behavioral-spec per scenario) are developed. These are derived from domain resources, model specifications (e.g., OpenAI model spec), and safety taxonomies. Specifications are designed to be concise policy-style statements, covering criteria for content preferences, goal orientation, format constraints (behavioral), and risk boundaries (safety). They undergo iterative refinement with LLM assistance and human involvement to ensure clarity, consistency, and appropriate difficulty.
- **Data Collection:** 1,500 prompts are collected: 1,000 unsafe and 500 safe, distributed across the five scenarios (300 prompts per scenario: 200 unsafe, 100 safe).
 - **Synthetic Generation:** GPT-4.1 is used to generate unsafe prompts by seeding it with each safety-spec to intentionally induce violations. Hand-crafted, real-world seed questions are incorporated for realism.
 - **Curation from Existing Datasets:** Prompts are also drawn from diverse existing benchmarks and datasets, including WMDP, WildJailbreak, BioprotocolBench, ChildSafe DPO, Code Vulnerability, Mitre Attack, Arena Hard, BigCodeBench, Fitness QA, Medical Dialogue, and Traveler Trip. Data not in QA format are rewritten by an LLM to align with scenario consistency.
- **Data Filtering and Quality Control:**
 - **Filtering:** Semantic-based filtering (using LLM) ensures scenario relevance and removes low-quality items. Sentence embedding-based filtering (using `text-embedding-3-large` and cosine distance) removes semantically redundant entries to ensure diversity, reducing each scenario to around 600 items. Random sampling then balances each scenario to 300 prompts.
 - **Human-in-the-loop Quality Control:** Three human experts review prompts for scenario relevance, safety categorization (ensuring unsafe prompts are sufficiently harmful and safe prompts are truly safe), factual accuracy, grammatical clarity, and structural quality.
- **Attack Enhancement:** The WildTeaming framework is applied to all 1,000 unsafe prompts. This process rewrites raw unsafe prompts into adversarial variants that disguise malicious intent, making them harder for LLMs to detect. A verification step (Qwen3-32B-thinking and human review) ensures semantic preservation.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol

- **Metric:** The **Specification Alignment Rate (SAR)** is introduced. It quantifies alignment by prioritizing safety: unsafe responses receive a score of 0, while safe responses receive `α + (1 - α) * r_beh(x, z)`. Here, `α` is a constant offset (set to 0.3 in experiments) providing a baseline for safe responses, and `r_beh` is the behavioral score.
- **Procedure:** GPT-4.1 (and Qwen3-32B-thinking for development) serves as the LLM evaluator. For each prompt-response pair, the evaluator assesses compliance with each individual spec, labeling it YES (compliant), NO (violation), or NA (not applicable). `Risk_safety` is 1 if any safety-spec is NO, and 0 otherwise. `r_beh` is the proportion of behavioral-spec labeled YES (excluding NA). Reference answers are included where available to guide content helpfulness judgments. A human evaluation study (Cohen's Kappa of 0.86 with GPT-4.1) validated the evaluator's reliability.

#### 3.5. Test-Time Deliberation (TTD) Methods

- **Baselines:**
 - **Multi-pass TTD (for instruct models):**
 - **Best-of-N:** Samples N responses (N=15) and selects the best based on a reward model (FsfairX-LLaMA3-RM-v0.1).
 - **Self-Refine:** Iteratively refines a response based on feedback, with 15 iterations. Specifications are incorporated into the feedback.
 - **TPO (Test-time Preference Optimization):** Combines parallel sampling (sample size 5) and iterative refinement (2 iterations), using a reward model and textual update instructions.
 - **Single-pass TTD (for reasoning models):**
 - **ZeroThink:** Suppresses internal reasoning by using `<think></think>`.
 - **MoreThink:** Encourages longer reasoning traces by replacing the end-of-thinking delimiter.
- **ALIGN3 (Proposed Method):** A lightweight, three-stage single-pass thinking intervention method for reasoning models:
 1. **Behavior Optimization:** Integrates behavioral-spec into the LLM's initial reasoning to maximize helpfulness.
 2. **Safety-Guided Refinement:** Near the end of the thinking stage, applies safety-spec to adjust the reasoning chain and remove safety risks.
 3. **Holistic Specification Audit:** Before generating the final answer, conducts a comprehensive audit using all specifications for final revision and gap-filling. This progressive enforcement aims to reduce safety violations and improve alignment with minimal token overhead.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on SPECBENCH provide insights into LLM capabilities regarding specification alignment and the effectiveness of test-time deliberation strategies.

- **Performance Gaps and Moderate Difficulty:** SPECBENCH reveals significant performance gaps among models. Most models scored below 65% SAR, indicating that achieving robust specification alignment is challenging. GPT-5-chat achieved the highest SAR at 82.14%, outperforming GPT-4.1 by 12.94%, suggesting that frontier models are advancing in this area. This indicates that the benchmark is sufficiently difficult to differentiate models.
- **Safety-Behavior Trade-off Evidence:** The evaluation consistently demonstrated the "safety-behavior trade-off." Models designed for strict safety, such as Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, RealSafe-R1-8B, and STAIR-Llama-3.1-8B-DPO-3, achieved high safety scores but often resulted in lower behavioral scores due to frequent refusals of risky prompts (over-refusal). Conversely, models prioritizing helpfulness without explicit safety alignment (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and its DeepSeek-R1-Distill variant) achieved higher behavioral scores but lower safety scores. This pattern validates the SAR metric's design, which emphasizes the need for a balance between helpfulness and harmlessness.
- **Reasoning Models Outperform Instruct Models:** LLMs explicitly designed for reasoning (e.g., "thinking" variants) generally exhibited better specification alignment than their instruct counterparts. For example, Qwen3-32B-thinking achieved a 7.65% higher SAR than Qwen3-32B. Similar improvements were observed for Gemini-2.5-flash-lite (14.87%↑) and Gemini-2.5-flash (12.74%↑), and DeepSeek-R1 (9.47%↑). This indicates that the ability to engage in multi-step reasoning processes contributes to better adherence to complex specifications.
- **Test-Time Deliberation (TTD) Enhances Alignment:** TTD methods generally improved specification alignment.
 - Multi-pass TTD methods like Self-Refine and TPO demonstrated larger SAR gains on models like Qwen3-14B (e.g., TPO increased SAR by 7.73% to 58.76%), but incurred substantial token overhead.
 - The proposed **ALIGN3** method yielded the strongest overall results for single-pass TTD, boosting Qwen3-14B's SAR by 11.89% (from 51.03% to 62.92%) over the non-thinking baseline, and 6.29% over the vanilla thinking baseline, with minimal token overhead (<2k tokens). This highlights ALIGN3's efficiency and effectiveness in simultaneously improving safety and behavioral adherence.
- **Scenario-Specific Performance Variation:** Performance varied across the five scenarios. Code and Biochemical Procedure Instruction scenarios consistently showed lower average SAR scores, attributed to their stricter safety requirements (e.g., vulnerability constraints in code, dual-use concerns in biochemistry) and more ambiguous intentions. The Personal Health Education Instruction scenario generally yielded higher SAR, suggesting its specifications might be relatively easier to navigate.
- **Impact of Attack Enhancement:** The attack enhancement applied to unsafe prompts using the WildTeaming framework successfully increased task difficulty. Safety scores dropped noticeably (approximately 10% reduction) on the unsafe subset for evaluated models, confirming that the adversarial prompts are more challenging for LLMs to handle safely.
- **Evaluation Reliability:** An analysis of evaluator consistency showed high agreement between GPT-4.1 and Qwen3-32B-thinking, with Kendall's coefficient of concordance for SAR rankings at W = 0.995. A human evaluation study also reported a Cohen's Kappa of 0.86 between human annotators and GPT-4.1, confirming the reliability of the LLM-as-a-judge approach for this benchmark.
- **Correctness and Evolving Boundaries Analysis:** ALIGN3 demonstrated comparable or slightly better correctness in specific scenarios (Biochem and Health), suggesting it does not compromise factual accuracy. Furthermore, evaluating models against an updated set of specifications showed that overall ranking trends remained consistent, indicating the framework's robustness to evolving requirements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of large language models by addressing a critical aspect of their real-world deployment: ensuring alignment with dynamic, scenario-specific behavioral and safety specifications.

- **Addressing a Critical Gap in LLM Alignment:** The work formalizes "specification alignment" as a key challenge, moving beyond generalized safety or instruction-following paradigms. By emphasizing the need for LLMs to reason over fine-grained, dynamic, and scenario-specific boundaries for both helpfulness and safety, it highlights a crucial area for future development. This is particularly significant as LLMs are integrated into diverse, domain-specific applications where one-size-fits-all solutions are inadequate.
- **Introducing a Comprehensive Evaluation Tool:** The release of SPECBENCH provides a novel, unified benchmark. Its design, encompassing five diverse scenarios, 103 specifications, and 1,500 prompts (including attack-enhanced unsafe queries), offers a robust platform for systematic evaluation. SPECBENCH can serve as a valuable resource for organizations and researchers to define, quantify, and track LLM compliance with their customized policies, thereby facilitating more responsible and effective LLM deployment. The modular and extensible nature of SPECBENCH also allows for the incorporation of new scenarios and evolving requirements.
- **Advancing Test-Time Deliberation Strategies:** The findings demonstrate that test-time deliberation (TTD) methods can effectively enhance specification alignment without costly retraining. The proposed ALIGN3 method stands out as a lightweight, efficient, and effective single-pass TTD strategy. Its hierarchical reflection and revision approach provides a practical solution for improving the safety-helpfulness trade-off with minimal computational overhead, offering a flexible mechanism for LLMs to adapt to changing specifications in real-time.
- **Providing Insights into LLM Capabilities and Limitations:** The extensive experimental evaluation across a wide range of LLMs (both instruct and reasoning models) reveals significant alignment gaps and consistently demonstrates the inherent "safety-behavior trade-off." The superior performance of reasoning models underscores the value of explicit deliberation capabilities in achieving better alignment. These insights can guide future research in model architecture, training methodologies, and alignment techniques.
- **Promoting More Responsible LLM Development:** By providing a framework to systematically evaluate adherence to both behavioral and safety specifications, the research supports the development of LLMs that are not only helpful but also reliably harmless in specific contexts. This aligns with the broader goal of building trustworthy AI systems that adhere to ethical guidelines and user expectations, potentially mitigating risks associated with misaligned LLM behavior in sensitive applications.
