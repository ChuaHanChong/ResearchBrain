# Chain-of-Visual-Thought: Teaching VLMs to See and Think Better with Continuous Visual Tokens

> [!summary] Summary
> The Chain-of-Visual-Thought (COVT) framework equips Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with continuous visual tokens, distilled from lightweight vision experts, to improve reasoning in dense perceptual space. This approach yields performance gains across vision-centric benchmarks and enhances interpretability.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with perception-intensive tasks (e.g., precise counting, depth estimation) due to the loss of rich perceptual cues when continuous visual information is compressed into discrete text tokens.
- Existing VLM reasoning is predominantly language-bound, limiting their ability to reason about fine-grained spatial and geometric details, with text-only Chain-of-Thought often degrading visual reasoning performance.
- External tool-augmented methods for VLMs introduce computational overhead and still often project tool outputs back into the text space, reintroducing information loss and delegating core perception.

## Method

- Introduce ==Chain-of-Visual-Thought (COVT)==, a framework enabling VLMs to generate and process =="continuous visual tokens"==—compact latent representations encoding fine-grained perceptual cues—directly within their autoregressive sequence.
- Distill knowledge from specialized, ==lightweight vision experts== (e.g., SAM for segmentation, DepthAnything v2 for depth, PIDINet for edges, DINOv2 for semantics) into these visual tokens using tailored, task-oriented alignment strategies.
- Employ a ==four-stage training pipeline== (comprehension, generation, reasoning, efficient reasoning) to progressively teach the VLM to effectively utilize and reason with visual tokens, complementing its text-space abilities.

## Results

- COVT consistently improved performance across diverse vision-centric benchmarks (e.g., CV-Bench, BLINK, RealWorldQA), achieving a **5.5%** overall gain on CV-Bench and a **14.0%** improvement on its Depth sub-task with Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- Demonstrated strong generalization by outperforming prior latent reasoning methods (e.g., Aurora) on LLaVA-v1.5-13B, with gains such as **12.9%** on BLINK relative-depth and **26.6%** on BLINK count.
- Enabled enhanced interpretability through optional decoding of visual tokens into human-readable outputs (segmentation masks, depth maps, edge maps), providing a visual window into the model's intermediate reasoning process.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating continuous visual tokens allows VLMs to "think visually," directly addressing the limitations of language-bound reasoning and providing a more faithful bridge between continuous visual perception and higher-level cognition.
> - Tailored alignment strategies, particularly projecting visual tokens into the decoder prompt space of vision experts, are crucial for effectively distilling dense perceptual information into the compact token representations.
> - Unlike text-only Chain-of-Thought, which can degrade performance on vision-centric tasks, COVT's continuous visual tokens consistently enhance performance, empirically validating the necessity of direct visual grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the rapidly evolving landscape of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and advanced AI reasoning paradigms, primarily addressing a critical limitation in how current VLMs process and reason about visual information.

**Emergence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs):**
VLMs have become foundational for multimodal intelligence, demonstrating remarkable capabilities in understanding and generating content across both text and images. By mapping visual inputs into a language-centric token space, VLMs leverage the powerful compositional and logical reasoning abilities of large language models (LLMs). Recent advancements, particularly in text-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, have further amplified LLMs' performance on complex tasks involving logic, mathematics, and knowledge grounding. VLMs have inherited this CoT paradigm, applying it to multimodal scenarios.

**Limitations of Current VLM Reasoning:**
Despite these successes, the paper identifies a fundamental bottleneck: existing VLM reasoning remains predominantly **language-bound**. When continuous, high-dimensional visual information (such as object boundaries, spatial layouts, depth, and geometric structures) is compressed into discrete text tokens, rich perceptual cues are inevitably lost. This loss makes current VLMs struggle with perception-intensive tasks like precise counting, fine-grained spatial correspondence, or relative depth estimation, even when equipped with powerful vision encoders. The authors cite examples where text-only CoT can even degrade visual reasoning performance, such as Qwen3-VL-Thinking performing worse than its instruction-tuned counterpart on spatial understanding benchmarks. This exposes the core problem: visual reality is continuous, while traditional VLM reasoning forces it through a discrete linguistic "bottleneck."

**Existing Solutions and Their Shortcomings:**
The research landscape has seen several approaches to augment VLM capabilities:
- **Tool-Augmented Reasoning:** Methods like Visual ChatGPT augment VLMs with external vision tools (e.g., specialized image segmentation or object detection models). While these tools can restore some spatial and geometric information, they introduce significant computational overhead, delegate core perception capabilities to external modules, and are ultimately bounded by the performance of these tools. Crucially, they still often project the tool outputs back into the text space, reintroducing the information loss.
- **Text-Space Reasoning Extensions:** Other approaches aim to improve visual reasoning by generating dense captions and then reasoning within the language space. However, this remains an inherently lossy process, as detailed visual nuances are hard to capture entirely in words. Related methods like Visual CoT (VCoT) also rely on textual interpretations, limiting reasoning to discrete text. MCoT attempts continuous visual reasoning by editing/generating images, but it is computationally intensive and lacks flexibility. VChain interleaves images and text, but still projects images into a text-compatible space, leading to information loss.
- **Latent Space Reasoning:** Concurrent research has explored reasoning directly within latent spaces, showing benefits for complex multi-step tasks in LLMs (e.g., Coconut, CCoT). Some VLM work, like Aurora, uses VQ-VAE latents of depth and detection signals, or Mirage employs latent imagination. These methods are closer to COVT's philosophy.

**The Gap COVT Fills:**
COVT directly addresses the limitations of language-bound reasoning and the drawbacks of external tools. It posits that VLMs should learn to "think visually" by directly injecting fine-grained visual signals into their reasoning process, allowing them to "see" and "think" simultaneously in a self-contained and efficient manner. Unlike previous methods, COVT uniquely aims to satisfy all desired properties for advanced multimodal reasoning: operating without external tools, reasoning in continuous visual space, leveraging dense visual information, and maintaining 3D-aware perception (as summarized in Table 1 of the paper). It extends latent space reasoning by treating continuous visual tokens as implicit "tools" embedded directly within the VLM's reasoning chain, tied to specific perceptual experts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for Chain-of-Visual-Thought (COVT) stems from the recognition that current Vision-Language Models (VLMs), despite their advanced linguistic reasoning capabilities, fundamentally struggle with tasks requiring dense visual perception, spatial reasoning, and geometric awareness. This struggle is rooted in the inherent mismatch between the continuous nature of visual information and the discrete, language-centric token space through which VLMs typically operate.

The key objectives and motivations driving COVT are:

- **Overcoming the Language-Bound Bottleneck:** The primary objective is to enable VLMs to reason beyond the limitations of discrete linguistic space. Current VLMs lose rich perceptual cues (e.g., boundaries, layout, depth, geometry) when projecting continuous visual information into text, leading to errors and degradation in perception-intensive tasks. COVT aims to circumvent this bottleneck.

- **Enabling Continuous Visual Thinking:** The core objective is to introduce a mechanism for VLMs to "think" in a continuous visual space. This involves generating and reasoning with "continuous visual tokens" – compact latent representations that directly encode rich, fine-grained perceptual cues. The authors hypothesize that integrating these tokens will allow VLMs to mirror human visual reasoning more closely, where visual perception and cognitive processing are tightly coupled.

- **Injecting Dense Perceptual Grounding:** VLMs often lack mechanisms to capture dense visual information across spatial dimensions. COVT seeks to imbue VLMs with this capability by distilling knowledge from lightweight vision experts (e.g., for segmentation, depth, edges, and semantic features). These experts provide complementary properties, ensuring the VLM gains a holistic perceptual understanding.

- **Achieving Self-Contained Reasoning:** A key motivation is to avoid the drawbacks associated with external vision tools. While tools can provide specialized perception, they introduce computational overhead, delegate core reasoning, and bound performance by their individual capabilities. COVT aims for a self-contained framework where the VLM internalizes fine-grained perceptual knowledge, allowing for more flexible and efficient reasoning without external dependencies.

- **Enhancing Interpretability:** The framework is designed to support interpretable multimodal intelligence. By optionally decoding the continuous visual tokens into dense predictions (like segmentation masks or depth maps), COVT provides a visual window into the model's intermediate reasoning process, fostering greater transparency and trust in VLM outputs.

- **Improving Precision and Accuracy on Vision-Centric Tasks:** Ultimately, the motivation is to achieve more precise, grounded, and accurate multimodal intelligence, particularly on tasks that demand fine-grained visual understanding (e.g., counting, relative depth estimation, scene understanding, and spatial correspondence). The paper cites instances where current text-only Chain-of-Thought (CoT) even degrades performance on such tasks, highlighting the critical need for a new approach.

In essence, COVT aims to equip VLMs with an internal "visual thought chain" that bridges language and vision more faithfully, leading to a new generation of multimodal reasoning systems capable of fluidly thinking across both modalities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Chain-of-Visual-Thought (COVT) introduces a novel framework that augments Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with the ability to reason through continuous visual tokens, thereby grounding multimodal understanding in rich perceptual space.

**Core Concept: Continuous Visual Tokens:**
Instead of restricting reasoning to discrete language tokens, COVT enables VLMs to generate and process "continuous visual tokens." These are compact latent representations (approximately 20 tokens in total, across different types) that encode various perceptual cues directly within the VLM's autoregressive sequence. The VLM, therefore, functions as a dense visual encoder capable of generating these tokens as part of its thought process.

**Integration with VLM Architecture:**
COVT operates within the standard next-token prediction paradigm of VLMs. When predicting a sequence of outputs, each element (`y_i`) can be either a text token or a continuous visual token. These visual tokens are integrated into the VLM's reasoning chain, often within a `<think>` block, allowing the model to use perceptual information to condition subsequent predictions and derive final answers.

**Knowledge Distillation from Lightweight Vision Experts:**
To endow these continuous visual tokens with semantic and perceptual meaning, COVT distills knowledge from specialized, lightweight vision experts. These experts are chosen to capture complementary visual properties:

1. **Segmentation Tokens (8 tokens):**
 - **Purpose:** Provide instance-level position, shape information, and 2D spatial perception.
 - **Expert:** Segment Anything Model (SAM).
 - **Alignment Strategy:** Task-oriented and fine-grained. The 8 predicted segmentation tokens are projected into SAM's decoder prompt space. Each token serves as a prompt for the SAM decoder to reconstruct a mask. This is supervised using Dice Loss and Focal Loss, with Hungarian matching to align predicted masks with ground-truth SAM masks (quality-filtered).

2. **Depth Tokens (4 tokens):**
 - **Purpose:** Provide pixel-level depth information and enable 3D spatial relationship understanding.
 - **Expert:** DepthAnything v2.
 - **Alignment Strategy:** Task-oriented and fine-grained. The 4 predicted depth tokens are projected into DepthAnything's decoder prompt space. Each token interacts with intermediate-layer features from the DepthAnything encoder via batch matrix multiplication (BMM) to reconstruct a depth map. The averaged reconstructed depth maps are supervised with an L1 reconstruction loss against DepthAnything's ground-truth depth map.

3. **Edge Tokens (4 tokens):**
 - **Purpose:** Provide geometry-level details, structural cues, and fine-grained 2D spatial information.
 - **Expert:** PIDINet.
 - **Alignment Strategy:** Task-oriented. The 4 predicted edge tokens are used as 1x1 convolutional kernels applied to dense features from the PIDINet encoder to reconstruct edge maps. These are then averaged and sigmoid-normalized, supervised with an L1 loss against PIDINet's ground-truth edge map.

4. **DINO Tokens (4 tokens):**
 - **Purpose:** Provide patch-level semantic representations and rich semantic information.
 - **Expert:** DINOv2 (self-supervised representation learning).
 - **Alignment Strategy:** Representation-based and less fine-grained. The 4 DINO tokens are mapped into the same shape as DINOv2 features using a projection layer and aligned directly in feature space via an MSE objective.

**Projection Layer:**
A common "Projection Layer" (detailed in Sec. A.1 of supplementary material) is used to project the VLM latent features into the appropriate prompt space (for decoders like SAM/DepthAnything) or feature space (for DINO). This layer consists of a linear transformation followed by a learnable query and a cross-attention mechanism.

**Four-Stage Training Pipeline:**
To progressively teach the VLM to effectively use and reason with visual tokens without degrading text-space abilities, COVT employs a four-stage data formatting and training pipeline (illustrated in Figure 4):
1. **Comprehension Stage:** Visual tokens are inserted after `<image>` to introduce their basic semantics to the VLM.
2. **Generation Stage:** Questions and answers are modified to explicitly guide the VLM in generating precise visual tokens.
3. **Reasoning Stage:** The "chain-of-visual-thought" format is introduced, where visual tokens are used within the `<think>` block to leverage perceptual cues for deriving final answers.
4. **Efficient Reasoning Stage:** Randomly drops out a subset of visual token types, forcing the model to learn to dynamically select and utilize available visual thoughts, rather than relying on a fixed output pattern.

**Training Loss:**
The overall training loss (`L_total`) is a joint function comprising the standard cross-entropy loss (`L_ce`) for VLM token prediction and a weighted sum of the reconstruction/distillation losses (`L_seg_visual`, `L_depth_visual`, `L_edge_visual`, `L_dino_visual`) for each visual token type.

**Inference:**
During inference, COVT operates efficiently by reasoning directly within the continuous visual token space. The dense predictions from decoders are optional and generated only when interpretability (visualizing the thought process) is desired, thus preserving efficiency for most applications.

**Base Models and Implementation:**
The main experiments use **Qwen2.5-VL-7B** as the baseline VLM, fine-tuned with LoRA. Additional experiments validate COVT's generalization on **LLaVA-v1.5-13B**. The training data combines vision-centric subsets of LLaVA-OneVision with filtered TallyQA and ADE20K-Depth datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Chain-of-Visual-Thought (COVT) significantly enhances the perceptual reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly in tasks requiring fine-grained visual understanding.

**Consistent Performance Gains on Vision-Centric Benchmarks:**
- **Overall Improvement:** COVT consistently improves performance across a diverse set of vision-centric benchmarks (CV-Bench, BLINK, RealWorldQA, MMT-Bench, MMStar-P, MMVP, MME-RealWorld, V*Bench, HRBench4K, HRBench8K).
- **Qwen2.5-VL-7B Baseline (Table 2):** When integrated into Qwen2.5-VL-7B, COVT (using Segmentation, Depth, and DINO tokens) achieves substantial gains. For example:
 - **CV-Bench:** A 5.5% overall gain.
 - **CV-Bench Depth Sub-task:** A remarkable 14.0% improvement, highlighting its effectiveness in 3D spatial understanding.
 - **HRBench8K:** A 4.5% improvement.
- **Impact of Edge Tokens:** Adding Edge tokens (total of 4 visual token types) further boosts performance, particularly on CV-Bench Depth, where it achieves an even larger 16.4% gain, demonstrating the complementary nature of different visual cues.

**Generalization Across VLM Baselines:**
- **LLaVA-v1.5-13B Integration (Table 3):** COVT's effectiveness is not limited to Qwen. When implemented on LLaVA-v1.5-13B, it significantly outperforms Aurora—a prior latent reasoning method—on specific sub-tasks:
 - **BLINK relative-depth:** COVT with Depth tokens surpasses Aurora-depth by 12.9%.
 - **BLINK count:** COVT with Segmentation tokens outperforms Aurora-count by 26.6%. These results underscore COVT's strong generalization capabilities across different VLM architectures.

**Enhanced Interpretability through Visualizations:**
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 5):** The ability to decode COVT tokens into human-readable visual outputs (segmentation masks, depth maps, edge maps) provides crucial interpretability. The qualitative examples clearly illustrate that different visual tokens provide complementary information, aiding in complex perceptual reasoning. For instance, segmentation tokens localize objects in 2D, while depth tokens provide 3D spatial relations, enabling the model to correctly answer questions like "Which point is closer to the camera?"

**Superiority over Text-Only Chain-of-Thought:**
- **Ablation Study (Figure 6):** A key finding is the direct comparison between text-only CoT and COVT. Text-only CoT not only fails to improve performance on vision-centric tasks but often *degrades* it. In stark contrast, COVT consistently enhances performance, empirically validating the necessity of continuous visual tokens for effective visual reasoning. This highlights the inherent limitation of forcing visual reasoning through a discrete textual bottleneck.

**Optimal Token Number and Visual Alignment:**
- **Token Number Ablation (Table 4, Figure 12):** Experiments on the number of segmentation tokens reveal an optimal configuration. Using too few tokens (e.g., 1) degrades performance, while too many (e.g., 32) also harms it, possibly due to increased alignment difficulty. Eight segmentation tokens (along with 4 depth and 4 DINO) achieve the best balance between performance and computational efficiency. Furthermore, replacing visual tokens with "empty" ordinary tokens ("16 empty" setting) results in poor performance, underscoring the critical role of visual alignment.
- **Alignment Strategy Ablation (Table 5):** The tailored alignment strategies, particularly aligning task-oriented visual tokens with decoders (prompt-level alignment), consistently outperform direct feature-level alignment (MSE loss). This suggests that aligning tokens with the expressive capacity of task-specific decoders is crucial for capturing rich, fine-grained perceptual cues.

**Maintenance of Performance on Non-Vision-Centric Tasks:**
- **Generalization (Figure 7):** Beyond its gains on vision-centric tasks, COVT maintains competitive performance and even achieves a slight average improvement (1.2%) across a range of non-vision-centric benchmarks (e.g., OCRBench, MME-translate, A-OKVQA). This demonstrates that integrating continuous visual thinking does not lead to a degradation in the model's general multimodal or linguistic capabilities.

In summary, the main findings conclusively show that COVT significantly improves VLMs' ability to perform fine-grained visual reasoning by introducing and effectively aligning continuous visual tokens, offering both enhanced performance and greater interpretability, all while maintaining efficiency and generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Chain-of-Visual-Thought (COVT) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and holds substantial potential impact across various dimensions of AI research and application.

**1. A Paradigm Shift in VLM Reasoning:**
COVT fundamentally challenges the prevailing language-centric reasoning paradigm in VLMs. By enabling models to "think visually" through continuous visual tokens, it offers a more faithful bridge between language and vision. This shift allows VLMs to process and reason about visual information in a manner that more closely resembles human cognition, where visual perception is inherently continuous and integrated with higher-level reasoning. This could lay the groundwork for next-generation multimodal AI systems that are less constrained by symbolic linguistic bottlenecks.

**2. Enhanced Fine-Grained Perceptual Understanding:**
The most immediate and demonstrable impact of COVT is its ability to significantly improve VLMs' performance on tasks requiring dense, fine-grained visual perception. Tasks like precise counting, spatial localization, relative depth estimation, and geometric awareness—where current VLMs typically struggle—show substantial gains. This breakthrough unlocks more sophisticated applications in areas such as robotics (better scene understanding for navigation and manipulation), autonomous driving (more accurate perception of object distances and layouts), and medical imaging (finer detail interpretation).

**3. Towards Self-Contained and Efficient Multimodal Intelligence:**
By internalizing perceptual expertise through continuous visual tokens, COVT moves away from the reliance on external, specialized vision tools. This leads to a more self-contained and unified multimodal intelligence system, reducing computational overhead and increasing flexibility. During inference, the ability to operate directly in the latent visual token space without always decoding dense predictions means that these enhanced capabilities come with maintained efficiency, making them practical for real-world deployments.

**4. Increased Interpretability and Trust:**
The optional decoding of continuous visual tokens into human-readable visual outputs (like segmentation masks, depth maps, and edge maps) is a crucial contribution to AI interpretability. This feature provides a "window" into the model's internal visual reasoning process, making VLM decisions more transparent and understandable. In high-stakes applications like healthcare or defense, where trust and accountability are paramount, this interpretability can be invaluable for debugging, verification, and user acceptance.

**5. A Generalizable Framework for Multimodal Intelligence:**
The modular design of COVT, allowing for flexible integration of different visual token types and alignment strategies, suggests it can serve as a general framework. Researchers can easily incorporate new lightweight vision experts to imbue VLMs with a broader spectrum of perceptual capabilities (e.g., material properties, optical flow, texture). This adaptability ensures COVT can evolve with future advancements in specialized vision models.

**6. Paving the Way for Truly Interleaved Multimodal Reasoning:**
While COVT currently integrates visual thoughts within a reasoning chain, it opens exciting avenues for future work, particularly in achieving fully interleaved multimodal reasoning. A system that seamlessly blends free-form textual thoughts with continuous visual thoughts, allowing for dynamic shifts between modalities during complex problem-solving, would represent a major leap towards more human-like intelligence.

In conclusion, COVT's introduction of continuous visual tokens not only significantly boosts the perceptual abilities of VLMs but also offers a principled, efficient, and interpretable pathway for integrating complex visual understanding directly into their reasoning processes. This work has the potential to redefine how VLMs "see and think," pushing the boundaries of multimodal AI towards more grounded, precise, and human-understandable intelligence.
