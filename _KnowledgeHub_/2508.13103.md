# Grounding Actions in Camera Space: Observation-Centric Vision-Language-Action Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Observation-Centric VLA (OC-VLA), addresses spatial misalignment in Vision-Language-Action models by grounding action predictions directly in the camera observation space. This approach leads to improved cross-view generalization, increased robustness to camera viewpoint changes, and higher task success rates in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from spatial misalignment, as visual perception is camera-centric while robot control signals are defined in the robot's base coordinate system.
- This misalignment creates "learning conflicts" and hinders cross-view generalization, making VLA models struggle with diverse camera viewpoints and varied hardware configurations.
- Limited robotic data combined with this spatial inconsistency constrains the ability of VLA policies to generalize effectively to real-world environments.

## Method

- Introduced the ==Observation-Centric VLA (OC-VLA)== framework, which redefines the action prediction target to be in the camera observation space instead of the robot base frame.
- Utilizes camera extrinsic calibration matrices to transform robot-centric actions into camera-centric actions for training and then back for execution.
- The framework is a lightweight, plug-and-play strategy compatible with existing VLA architectures, evaluated on both continuous and discrete action spaces.

## Results

- OC-VLA achieved significantly higher task success rates in simulation, with approximately a **14%** increase for discrete actions and an **8%** increase for continuous actions compared to baselines.
- On a real Franka robot, OC-VLA surpassed baselines with a **77.5%** success rate in 10-shot learning, and demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization to novel camera viewpoints, experiencing only a **14%** performance drop.
- The framework consistently outperformed robot-base coordinate baselines under slight camera perturbations and in a novel zero-shot view, validating its robustness for real-world deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying the coordinate frames for perception and action prediction simplifies the learning task for VLA models by removing the need to implicitly learn complex spatial transformations.
> - Grounding actions in the camera space naturally enhances cross-view generalization and robustness, making policies more resilient to varying camera viewpoints.
> - A simple, non-architectural modification to action representation can yield substantial improvements in VLA model performance and data efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the rapidly evolving domain of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This field seeks to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and visual observations to generate appropriate physical actions, drawing heavily from advancements in large multimodal models (LMMs), Large Language Models (LLMs), and Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

The broader research landscape for VLA models is characterized by ambitious efforts to leverage large-scale datasets and pre-trained foundation models (like CLIP, DINOv2, LLaMA2) to achieve generalist robot policies capable of performing diverse tasks in varied environments. Recent works such as RT-1, OpenVLA, Octo, and Dita have made significant strides by adapting these powerful models for robotic control.

However, despite impressive progress, VLA models face several critical limitations that this paper directly addresses:
1. **Data Scarcity and Cost:** Unlike web-scale datasets used for VLM pre-training, robotic data collection is inherently expensive and limited in scale, constraining generalization capabilities.
2. **Spatial Discrepancy (Perception-Action Misalignment):** Vision models are primarily trained and supervised in the image or camera coordinate system, producing camera-centric latent representations. In contrast, most robotic control signals (end-effector poses, joint commands) are defined in the robot's base coordinate system. This fundamental misalignment between perception and action spaces hinders effective policy learning.
3. **Cross-View Generalization:** Robotic datasets often feature diverse camera viewpoints and hardware configurations (e.g., the Droid dataset with 1417 views), where the robot base may not always be visible. When models are forced to predict actions in the robot base frame from varied camera observations, they implicitly struggle with consistent 3D action reasoning from limited 2D views. This leads to "learning conflicts" where images capturing the same robot action from different angles are forced to share a single robot-space supervision signal, impeding generalization across views.

This work specifically identifies and tackles the "spatial inconsistency" problem, positioning itself as a lightweight yet impactful enhancement to existing VLA paradigms. Instead of proposing a new VLA architecture, it introduces a crucial change in the *representation* of the action target, thereby improving the robustness and generalizability of current VLA models without introducing significant computational or architectural overhead. This represents a significant step towards more robust and universally applicable robot learning systems, particularly important for real-world deployment where camera setups can vary unpredictably.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly their struggles with generalization to real-world environments due to inherent spatial discrepancies between visual observations and predicted actions.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Address Spatial Misalignment:** The core motivation stems from the fundamental conflict that visual perception pathways in VLA models are optimized for camera-centric representations (aligned with image coordinates), while robot control signals are typically defined in the robot's base coordinate system. This "misalignment between the perception and action spaces" is identified as a significant impediment to effective policy learning.
- **Enhance Cross-View Generalization:** Existing VLA models struggle when dealing with diverse camera viewpoints. When the same robot action is observed from different angles, but the model is supervised with a single, robot-centric action, it creates ambiguity and "learning conflicts." The goal is to make VLA models more resilient to camera viewpoint variations and improve their ability to generalize to unseen camera perspectives.
- **Improve Model Robustness:** By grounding action predictions directly in the camera observation space, the authors aim to achieve a more consistent and robust alignment between visual input and control output, thereby enhancing the overall resilience of the robotic policy.
- **Accelerate Convergence and Increase Task Success Rates:** A more natural alignment between perception and action spaces is hypothesized to simplify the learning task, leading to faster training convergence and higher task success rates in both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.
- **Lightweight and Compatible Solution:** A crucial objective is to achieve these improvements through a "lightweight, plug-and-play strategy." This means the proposed solution should not require substantial architectural modifications to existing VLA models, making it easily adoptable and minimizing computational overhead, thus promoting practical utility.

In essence, the research aims to bridge the gap between how a robot "sees" and how it "acts" by making the action representation directly dependent on the observation viewpoint, thereby enabling more intuitive and generalizable robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology introduced in this paper is the **Observation-Centric VLA (OC-VLA)** framework, which fundamentally redefines how VLA models predict robotic actions. Instead of predicting end-effector poses in the robot base coordinate frame, OC-VLA grounds these predictions directly in the camera observation space.

#### 3.1. Core Idea and Transformation

The central tenet of OC-VLA is to align the action prediction target with the visual observation space. This is achieved through a straightforward yet effective transformation process utilizing the camera's extrinsic calibration matrix:
- **Action Definition:** Given two nearby end-effector poses in the world coordinate frame, $P_{world\_1}$ and $P_{world\_2}$ (represented as 4x4 matrices combining 3D rotation and translation), the action in the world frame is derived as $A_{world} = P_{world\_2} P^{-1}_{world\_1}$.
- **Coordinate Transformation:** The camera's extrinsic calibration matrix, $T \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$ (representing the world-to-camera transformation), is used to convert world-frame poses into camera-frame poses: $P_{cam} = T P_{world}$.
- **Camera-Space Action:** The action in the camera space, $A_{cam}$, is then derived using the camera-space poses: $A_{cam} = P_{cam\_2} P^{-1}_{cam\_1}$. Critically, this can be expressed as $A_{cam} = T A_{world} T^{-1}$, highlighting how a robot-centric action is re-expressed from the perspective of the camera.
- **Prediction Target:** The $A_{cam}$ matrix is finally converted into a 7-dimensional action vector $\langle x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw, gripper \rangle$ to serve as the direct prediction target for the VLA model.

#### 3.2. Training and Inference Process

- **During Training:** All robot-space action annotations in the dataset are pre-processed. Using the known extrinsic parameters of the corresponding camera view, these actions are transformed into the camera coordinate system. The VLA model is then trained to predict these camera-centric ground truth actions.
- **During Inference:** The VLA model outputs an action prediction in the camera coordinate frame. This predicted action is then transformed back into the robot base coordinate system using the inverse of the camera's extrinsic matrix. The robot then executes this robot-centric action.

#### 3.3. VLA Model Architecture

The authors adopt a "lightweight 300M VLA model" for their evaluations, specifically following the architecture of Dita. This model uses:
- A **CLIP text encoder** for language instructions.
- A **DINOv2 vision encoder** for third-person RGB images.
- A **Q-Former** with FiLM conditioning layers to reduce image tokens and modulate visual features with language embeddings.
- A **LLaMA2-style Transformer** (12 layers, 768 hidden size) for action prediction, operating under a causal masking scheme.

The OC-VLA framework is applied to both **continuous** and **discrete** action spaces:
- **Continuous Action Space:** The Transformer acts as a Diffusion Transformer (DiT). It is trained to predict noise added to the camera-space actions using a DDPM scheduler and an MSE loss. During inference, DDIM with 10 timesteps is used for denoising.
- **Discrete Action Space:** The Transformer functions as a non-autoregressive predictor. Camera-space actions are normalized and quantized into discrete bins, and the model predicts the entire action sequence in a single pass using a cross-entropy loss.

#### 3.4. Optimization Details

The models are optimized using AdamW for 30,000 steps (or 20,000 steps for finetuning) with specific learning rates for different components and trained on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

#### 3.5. Datasets for Evaluation

- **Pretraining:** The **Droid dataset** is used, known for its large scale (1417 distinct camera viewpoints) and availability of camera extrinsic parameters, which are crucial for OC-VLA.
- **Simulation Evaluation:** A custom dataset generated from **ManiSkill2** is used. This dataset includes five diverse manipulation tasks (PickCube, StackCube, PickSingleYCB, PickClutterYCB, PickSingleEGAD) and features over 40,000 unique trajectories rendered from 300,000 randomly configured third-person camera viewpoints.
- **Real Robot Evaluation:** Experiments are conducted on a **Franka Emika Panda robot** equipped with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper and three RealSense D435i RGB-D cameras. A privately collected dataset of 15 tasks (Camera 1) and 8 tasks with slight camera perturbations (Camera 2) is used for few-shot finetuning and evaluation, including a novel camera viewpoint for zero-shot testing.

This comprehensive methodology, ranging from theoretical formulation to extensive empirical validation in both simulation and real-world settings, ensures a thorough assessment of the OC-VLA framework's effectiveness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the Observation-Centric VLA (OC-VLA) framework yielded consistent and significant improvements across various experimental settings, both in simulation and on a real robot platform.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation (ManiSkill2)

- **Improved Task Success Rates:** In comparative analyses on five representative ManiSkill2 tasks, models employing camera-space end-effector actions (OC-VLA) as prediction targets consistently achieved higher task success rates than baselines predicting actions in the robot base coordinate frame.
- **Impact of Action Space:** The improvement was particularly pronounced for models utilizing a discrete action space, where OC-VLA demonstrated an increase in success rate of approximately 14% (from 38.6% to 52.4% average success rate across tasks). For continuous action spaces, an increase of about 8% was observed (from 45.2% to 53.2%).
- **Qualitative Improvements:** Qualitative comparisons highlighted that OC-VLA enabled more accurate grasp poses and better end-effector alignment, indicating superior understanding of spatial relationships for manipulation.
- **Ablation Study Robustness:** Ablation studies confirmed OC-VLA's consistent performance gains across different training conditions, including variations in observation sequence length, trajectory length, and whether the ViT encoder was frozen during optimization.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Evaluation (Franka Emika Panda)

Experiments on a physical Franka robot validated OC-VLA's practical utility across three challenging scenarios:

- **Fixed Camera Viewpoint (10-shot learning):** Under a fixed camera setup with 10-shot demonstrations per task, OC-VLA (Camera Base) achieved an average success rate of 77.5% across 15 tasks. This significantly surpassed the robot base coordinate baseline (66.3%) and the best-performing existing baseline, OpenVLA-OFT (63.3%), suggesting enhanced data efficiency even with limited demonstrations.

- **Novel Camera Viewpoint (Zero-Shot Generalization):** This was a critical test of cross-view generalization. When evaluated with a novel, previously unseen camera viewpoint without any additional fine-tuning, all models showed expected performance degradation. However, OC-VLA exhibited remarkable resilience, experiencing only a 14% performance drop, compared to OpenVLA-OFT's drop of over 20%. OC-VLA ultimately outperformed all baselines in this challenging zero-shot setting (54.0% vs. OpenVLA-OFT (var) 42.0%, $\pi_0$ (var) 34.7%, Robot Base (var) 41.3%). This is a strong indicator of its robustness to camera viewpoint changes.

- **Slight Camera Perturbations (in Training Data):** When fine-tuned on data where the training camera viewpoints had slight variations, OC-VLA again demonstrated superiority. It achieved an average success rate of 73.8% across 8 tasks, outperforming the robot base coordinate baseline (61.3%). This further underscored OC-VLA's generalizability in more realistic and variable data collection scenarios.

- **Qualitative Analysis on Real Robot:** Qualitative observations confirmed that OC-VLA consistently enabled more precise grasp localization and overall fine-grained manipulation. Baseline methods often failed due to inaccurate grasping, especially under camera perturbations, whereas OC-VLA maintained resilience and task completion success.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that by transforming action prediction targets into the camera observation space, OC-VLA effectively addresses the perception-action misalignment. This leads to substantial gains in learning efficiency, task success rates, and, most importantly, significantly improved generalization capabilities and robustness to varying camera viewpoints, which are crucial for real-world robotic deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Observation-Centric VLA (OC-VLA) framework represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with several layers of impact:

- **Addresses a Fundamental Discrepancy:** The most significant aspect of OC-VLA is its elegant solution to a core, yet often overlooked, problem in VLA research: the spatial misalignment between vision-centric observations and robot-centric action predictions. By explicitly unifying the coordinate frames of perception and action prediction, OC-VLA removes an implicit burden on the VLA model to learn complex, varying transformations between camera and robot spaces. This conceptual clarity simplifies the learning problem for the model.

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The demonstrated improvements in cross-view generalization and resilience to camera viewpoint variations are paramount. In real-world robotic deployments, camera positions are rarely perfectly static or uniform across different environments and tasks. OC-VLA's ability to maintain performance under novel or perturbed camera views drastically increases the practical applicability and reliability of VLA policies, pushing them closer to being "generalist" in truly dynamic settings. This directly tackles one of the major hurdles for VLA models moving from controlled lab environments to unstructured real-world scenarios.

- **Lightweight and Universally Applicable:** The "plug-and-play" nature of OC-VLA, requiring no architectural overhead or substantial modifications to existing VLA models, is a critical advantage. This means the proposed strategy can be easily integrated into current and future VLA architectures (like those based on Diffusion Transformers or other large multimodal backbones) without incurring additional computational costs or design complexities. This ease of adoption is likely to accelerate its widespread use and contribute to a new standard in VLA training.

- **Improved Data Efficiency:** The results indicate that OC-VLA can "partially compensate for the limited pretraining data and model size by improving data efficiency." In robotics, where data collection is expensive and time-consuming, any method that allows models to learn effectively from fewer demonstrations or generalize better from diverse but sparse data is highly impactful. This could lower the barrier to entry for developing and deploying sophisticated robotic skills.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** By demonstrating the critical importance of observation-centric action representations, OC-VLA opens new avenues for research. Future work could explore more advanced ways to leverage camera intrinsics and extrinsics, integrate other observation-centric cues (e.g., depth, 3D point clouds), or develop adaptive mechanisms for cases where camera calibration might be uncertain or dynamic.

In conclusion, OC-VLA represents a pragmatic yet profoundly impactful step towards building more capable, reliable, and adaptable generalist robot policies. Its simplicity, effectiveness, and compatibility make it a strong candidate for wide adoption, potentially accelerating the deployment of intelligent robots in diverse real-world applications, from manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics and beyond. The public release of the code further underscores its potential to foster community collaboration and drive innovation in the field.
