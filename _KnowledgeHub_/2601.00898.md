# Dichotomous Diffusion Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> DIPOLE introduces a reinforcement learning algorithm for optimizing diffusion policies that formulates a "greedified" KL-regularized objective, decomposing the optimal policy into positive and negative components with bounded sigmoid weighting. This approach resolves the optimality-stability trade-off, improves learning efficiency, and enables fine-grained control over action generation.

## Problem

- Existing KL-regularized RL methods for diffusion policies face an optimality-stability trade-off, where high greediness leads to unstable training and exploding loss due to unbounded exponential weighting.
- Learning in prior weighted regression schemes for diffusion policies is often inefficient, dominated by a few high-return samples, with poor-quality samples still receiving positive weights.
- Other RL integration methods for diffusion models, such as direct gradient backpropagation or Gaussian approximations for policy gradients, suffer from unstable training, high computational costs, or accumulation of approximation errors.

## Method

- **DIPOLE** re-formulates the ==KL-regularized== RL objective, replacing the unstable exponential weighting term with a bounded and smooth sigmoid function.
- The optimal policy is theoretically decomposed into two dichotomous policies: a positive policy (π+) aiming to maximize return and a negative policy (π−) aiming to minimize return, both learned by separate diffusion models with bounded weights.
- During inference, actions are sampled by linearly combining the score functions of the positive and negative policies, introducing a 'greediness factor' (ω) to control the optimality level, similar to classifier-free guidance.

## Results

- DIPOLE demonstrated superior performance over existing Gaussian and diffusion/flow policy baselines on various offline RL benchmarks, including ExORL and OGBench tasks.
- The method successfully transitioned to offline-to-online fine-tuning on OGBench, showing further performance improvements after online updates and achieving competitive performance with methods using direct value maximization.
- Applied to a 1-billion parameter vision-language-action model for autonomous driving, DIPOLE fine-tuning improved the PDM score on the NAVSIM benchmark by up to **6.5** points (from **88.3** to **94.8**), enhancing safety and progress metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using a bounded sigmoid function for weighting in KL-regularized RL objectives effectively resolves the optimality-stability trade-off, allowing for greedy policy improvement without loss explosion.
> - Decomposing the optimal policy into positive and negative components enables more efficient learning by leveraging both high- and low-return samples, preventing dominance by a small subset of high-reward data.
> - The linear combination of dichotomous policy scores provides a mechanism for explicit and controllable tuning of the generated actions' greediness during the diffusion reverse process.
