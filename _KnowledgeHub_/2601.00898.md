# Dichotomous Diffusion Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> DIPOLE introduces a reinforcement learning algorithm for optimizing diffusion policies that formulates a "greedified" KL-regularized objective, decomposing the optimal policy into positive and negative components with bounded sigmoid weighting. This approach resolves the optimality-stability trade-off, improves learning efficiency, and enables fine-grained control over action generation.

## Problem

- Existing KL-regularized RL methods for diffusion policies face an optimality-stability trade-off, where high greediness leads to unstable training and exploding loss due to unbounded exponential weighting.
- Learning in prior weighted regression schemes for diffusion policies is often inefficient, dominated by a few high-return samples, with poor-quality samples still receiving positive weights.
- Other RL integration methods for diffusion models, such as direct gradient backpropagation or Gaussian approximations for policy gradients, suffer from unstable training, high computational costs, or accumulation of approximation errors.

## Method

- **DIPOLE** re-formulates the ==KL-regularized== RL objective, replacing the unstable exponential weighting term with a bounded and smooth sigmoid function.
- The optimal policy is theoretically decomposed into two dichotomous policies: a positive policy (π+) aiming to maximize return and a negative policy (π−) aiming to minimize return, both learned by separate diffusion models with bounded weights.
- During inference, actions are sampled by linearly combining the score functions of the positive and negative policies, introducing a 'greediness factor' (ω) to control the optimality level, similar to classifier-free guidance.

## Results

- DIPOLE demonstrated superior performance over existing Gaussian and diffusion/flow policy baselines on various offline RL benchmarks, including ExORL and OGBench tasks.
- The method successfully transitioned to offline-to-online fine-tuning on OGBench, showing further performance improvements after online updates and achieving competitive performance with methods using direct value maximization.
- Applied to a 1-billion parameter vision-language-action model for autonomous driving, DIPOLE fine-tuning improved the PDM score on the NAVSIM benchmark by up to **6.5** points (from **88.3** to **94.8**), enhancing safety and progress metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using a bounded sigmoid function for weighting in KL-regularized RL objectives effectively resolves the optimality-stability trade-off, allowing for greedy policy improvement without loss explosion.
> - Decomposing the optimal policy into positive and negative components enables more efficient learning by leveraging both high- and low-return samples, preventing dominance by a small subset of high-reward data.
> - The linear combination of dichotomous policy scores provides a mechanism for explicit and controllable tuning of the generated actions' greediness during the diffusion reverse process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models have demonstrated capabilities in modeling multi-modal action distributions and facilitating controllable generation during inference. Consequently, their application in decision-making tasks, such as embodied robotics and autonomous driving, has gained traction, particularly in imitation learning settings. However, training large diffusion policies effectively with reinforcement learning (RL) presents several challenges.

Existing methods for integrating diffusion models with RL can be broadly categorized. One approach involves directly optimizing reward or value objectives through gradient backpropagation across the multi-step denoising process. This method, exemplified by works like ReFL and DRaFT, is often characterized by unstable training due to noisy gradient updates and high computational costs. Another strategy, known as inference-time scaling, attempts to mitigate these issues by freezing the diffusion model and searching for optimal noise during inference. While this avoids training instability, it limits performance to the upper bound of the pre-trained diffusion policies.

Policy gradient methods, such as those adapted from PPO, represent another direction. These methods treat the denoising process as a multi-step Markov Decision Process (MDP) and employ Gaussian approximations to compute log-likelihoods of intermediate denoising steps (e.g., DDPO, DPPO). A significant limitation here is the reliance on crude Gaussian approximations, which necessitate a large number of sufficiently small denoising steps to provide accurate likelihood information. This leads to large exploration spaces, prolonged training times, and accumulation of approximation errors.

A third line of research leverages KL-regularized RL objectives, which provide a weighted regression objective for policy extraction. Methods like those by Kang et al. and Zheng et al. integrate an exponential reward- or value-based weighting term into the standard diffusion regression loss. While conceptually simple and scalable, this approach faces its own set of limitations. The exponential weighting term can lead to loss explosion and training instability when the temperature parameter (β) is set high to achieve greedy reward maximization. Conversely, reducing β or clipping the weights compromises the optimality of the extracted policy. Additionally, the learning process can become inefficient as it becomes dominated by a small number of high-reward samples, and poor-quality samples still retain positive, albeit small, weights.

This paper positions itself within the landscape of KL-regularized RL, specifically aiming to overcome the optimality-stability trade-off and inefficient learning observed in prior weighted regression schemes for diffusion policies. It seeks to develop a stable and controllable RL method for diffusion policy optimization that addresses the shortcomings of existing approaches without sacrificing scalability or greediness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a novel reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm, named DIPOLE (Dichotomous diffusion Policy improvement), designed for stable and controllable optimization of diffusion-based policies. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations and challenges within the current landscape of RL for diffusion models.

The key motivations and specific problems this research aims to address are:

1. **Addressing Training Instability in Direct Value Optimization:** Existing RL methods that directly maximize value objectives by backpropagating gradients through the multi-step denoising process of diffusion models often encounter unstable training. This instability arises from noisy gradient updates and the inherent complexity of optimizing a multi-step generative process. DIPOLE aims to provide a more stable training paradigm that avoids these direct gradient issues.

2. **Mitigating Computational Issues from Gaussian Likelihood Approximations:** Policy gradient methods, when applied to diffusion models, typically approximate the denoising process with Gaussian distributions to compute log-likelihoods. This approximation requires a large number of very small denoising steps to be accurate, which in turn leads to computationally intensive training, large exploration spaces, and an accumulation of approximation errors. The research is motivated by the need for a method that is computationally more efficient and less prone to approximation errors.

3. **Overcoming the Optimality-Stability Trade-off in KL-Regularized RL:** KL-regularized RL objectives offer a weighted regression objective for policy extraction, where an exponential weighting term (exp(βG(s,a))) scales actions based on their value or reward. This approach, while promising for its simplicity and scalability, presents a fundamental trade-off:
 - **Loss Explosion and Instability:** To achieve greedy reward maximization, the temperature parameter (β) must be set to a large value. However, the exponential function's rapid growth can cause the weighting term to become excessively large for high-value actions, leading to exploding loss and destabilizing the training process.
 - **Compromised Optimality:** Practical solutions to this instability, such as using a smaller β or clipping the weighting term, compromise the theoretical optimality of the extracted policy by limiting its greediness.
 - **Inefficient Learning:** The training loss becomes dominated by a small number of high-return samples, which can make learning inefficient. Moreover, poor-quality samples still receive positive, albeit small, weights, potentially hindering policy improvement.

The overall motivation is to construct an RL framework that can effectively train diffusion policies to surpass data-level performance, offering both stability during learning and fine-grained control over the greediness of action generation, thereby enabling scalability to complex, real-world decision-making tasks like autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, DIPOLE (Dichotomous diffusion Policy improvement), introduces a novel RL framework to address the stability and efficiency limitations of diffusion policy optimization, particularly within the context of KL-regularized objectives.

#### 3.1. Revisiting KL-Regularized Objective

The work begins by examining the KL-regularized RL objective (Eq. 2), which constrains policy optimization to remain close to a reference policy μ while maximizing expected return G(s,a). This framework yields a closed-form optimal policy (Eq. 3): π*(a|s) ∝ μ(a|s) ⋅ exp(βG(s,a)). When applied to diffusion policies, this translates to a weighted diffusion loss (Eq. 4), where the standard diffusion regression loss is weighted by exp(βG(s,a)).

The paper reiterates the limitations of this traditional approach:
- **Optimality-Stability Trade-off:** A large β for greedy reward maximization leads to rapidly growing exponential weights, causing loss explosion and training instability. Solutions like small β or weight clipping compromise optimality.
- **Inefficient Learning:** High-return samples dominate the loss, making learning inefficient. Poor-quality samples still contribute positively, potentially hindering policy improvement.

#### 3.2. Dichotomous Diffusion Policy Improvement (DIPOLE)

To overcome these limitations, DIPOLE formulates a "greedified" KL-regularized RL objective (Eq. 5):
`max_π E_{s~d_π(s)} [E_{a~π(a|s)} [G(s,a)] - (1/ωβ) D_KL(π(·|s) || μ(·|s) ⋅ σ(βG(s,a))/Z(s))]`

This objective regularizes the policy towards a value-aware reference policy, where the weighting function is the bounded and smooth sigmoid function, σ(x) = 1/(1 + exp(−x)). A new hyperparameter, ω (greediness factor), is introduced to adjust the policy extraction's greediness.

**Key Derivations:**

1. **Optimal Solution Derivation:** The paper proves (Theorem 1 in Appendix B) that the optimal solution for this greedified objective is:
 `π*(a|s) ∝ μ(a|s) ⋅ σ(βG(s,a)) ⋅ exp(ω ⋅ βG(s,a))` (Eq. 6)

2. **Dichotomous Policy Extraction:** Leveraging properties of the sigmoid function, the optimal policy's weighting term is further decomposed:
 `π*(a|s) ∝ [μ(a|s) ⋅ σ(βG(s,a))]^(1+ω) / [μ(a|s) ⋅ (1 - σ(βG(s,a)))]^ω` (Eq. 7)

 This decomposition naturally leads to the definition of two **dichotomous policies**:
 - **Positive Policy (π+):** `π+(a|s) ∝ μ(a|s) ⋅ σ(βG(s,a))` – aims to maximize return.
 - **Negative Policy (π−):** `π−(a|s) ∝ μ(a|s) ⋅ (1 - σ(βG(s,a)))` – aims to minimize return.

 Crucially, both π+ and π− are weighted by strictly bounded sigmoid functions, replacing the unstable and unbounded exponential term of the original KL-regularized objective. This design promotes stable training and resolves the issue of loss explosion. Additionally, by learning from both high-return (π+) and low-return (π−) samples, the method improves learning efficiency, addressing the problem of dominance by a few high-return samples.

**Training Dichotomous Policies:**
Two separate diffusion models, parameterized as `ϵ+_{θ1}` and `ϵ-_{θ2}`, are trained to learn these dichotomous policies using the following weighted diffusion losses (Eq. 9):
`L_{ϵ+_{θ1}} = E[σ(βG(s,a)) ⋅ ||ϵ - ϵ+_{θ1}(a_t, s, t)||^2]`
`L_{ϵ-_{θ2}} = E[(1 - σ(βG(s,a))) ⋅ ||ϵ - ϵ-_{θ2}(a_t, s, t)||^2]`

**Controllable Generation:**
During inference, the score function of the optimal policy π* can be expressed as a linear combination of the scores of the dichotomous policies (Eq. 10):
`∇_a log π*(a|s) = (1 + ω)∇_a log π+(a|s) - ω∇_a log π−(a|s)`

This allows action sampling through the diffusion reverse process using a combined noise predictor:
`~ϵ(a_t, s, t) = (1 + ω)ϵ+_{θ1}(a_t, s, t) - ωϵ-_{θ2}(a_t, s, t)`

This formulation is noted for its similarity to classifier-free guidance (CFG), providing a mechanism to control the optimality level (greediness) of generated actions via the hyperparameter ω.

#### 3.3. Practical Implementations

- **Offline and Offline-to-Online RL:** For standard multi-step RL, G(s,a) is set to the advantage function A(s,a). In offline RL, μ is the behavior policy of the dataset. For offline-to-online settings, μ is the previously updated policy. Rejection sampling is used during inference to select the action with the highest Q-value from N sampled actions.
- **End-to-End Autonomous Driving:** DIPOLE is applied to a large vision-language-action (VLA) model (DP-VLA) for autonomous driving. A non-reactive pseudo-closed-loop simulation based on real-world datasets defines G(s,a) using a reward function that evaluates trajectory quality (safety, progress, comfort). A Florence-2 vision-language model acts as the encoder, and a diffusion action head as the decoder. Two separate Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) modules are applied to the decoder to form the positive and negative policies, leveraging the offline-to-online RL setting for fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DIPOLE was conducted across various RL benchmarks and a large-scale autonomous driving platform, demonstrating its effectiveness in both offline and offline-to-online settings.

#### 4.1. Experiments on RL Benchmarks

**Experimental Setup:**
DIPOLE was evaluated on the ExORL (Yarats et al., 2022) and OGBench (Park et al., 2025a) benchmarks, covering 39 locomotion and manipulation tasks (ExORL: 9 tasks across 4 domains; OGBench: 30 tasks across 6 domains). Offline performance was assessed, and a subset of 4 OGBench tasks were used for offline-to-online validation. Baselines included Gaussian policy methods (IQL, ReBRAC) and diffusion/flow policy methods (IDQL, IFQL, FQL, CFGRL). Performance metrics were average return for ExORL and success rate for OGBench, averaged over 8 random seeds.

**Evaluation Results:**
- **Offline RL Performance (ExORL & OGBench):**
 - On ExORL (Table 1), DIPOLE generally outperformed IQL, ReBRAC, CFGRL, IFQL, and FQL across most domains, indicating its capability to leverage valuable data within datasets. The variant DIPOLE w/o rs (without rejection sampling) also demonstrated superior performance compared to CFGRL, suggesting the effectiveness of the core dichotomous policy design.
 - On OGBench (Table 2), DIPOLE achieved the best or near-best aggregate scores across 6 challenging task categories, including complex humanoid maze navigation and object manipulation, indicating its strong capability in long-horizon tasks.

- **Offline-to-Online Fine-tuning Performance (OGBench):**
 - Table 3 shows that DIPOLE successfully transitioned from offline pre-training to online fine-tuning, demonstrating further performance improvements after 1 million online updates. It achieved a higher performance upper bound compared to IFQL and competitive performance with FQL, which uses direct value maximization, thereby highlighting DIPOLE's balance of greediness and stability.

- **Ablation Studies (Appendix D.4):**
 - Ablations on hyperparameters such as beta (β), shift factor (k), expectile factor (τ), and CFG scale (ω) were conducted. The results indicated that β and ω, which control the greediness, exhibited an easy-tuning property, suggesting robustness of the method to parameter choices within reasonable ranges.

#### 4.2. Experiments on Autonomous Driving Benchmark

**Experimental Setup:**
The scalability of DIPOLE was demonstrated by training a large (1-billion parameter) vision-language-action (VLA) model, DP-VLA, on the NAVSIM (Dauner et al., 2024) real-world autonomous driving benchmark. The model uses a Florence-2 encoder and a diffusion action head. Fine-tuning with DIPOLE involved applying LoRA modules for positive and negative policies. Performance was measured using the PDM score, which aggregates metrics like no-collision rate (NC), drivable area compliance (DAC), time-to-collision safety (TTC), comfort, and ego progress (EP). Closed-loop assessment was performed on the official simulator, with results averaged over the public test split.

**Evaluation Results:**
- **Baseline Performance:** The imitation-based pre-trained DP-VLA model already showed strong performance, significantly outperforming other baselines like UniAD, PARA-Drive, Transfuser, and Hydra-MDP (Table 4).
- **DIPOLE Fine-tuning Improvements:**
 - Fine-tuning DP-VLA with DIPOLE on the `navtrain` dataset improved the PDMS score by 1.4 points (from 88.3 to 89.7), with gains across safety and progress metrics.
 - Further fine-tuning on `navtest` scenarios yielded a substantial 6.5-point PDMS improvement (from 88.3 to 94.8). This demonstrates DIPOLE's capacity for significant performance enhancement in complex real-world applications, even for models exceeding 1 billion parameters.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 2, Appendix Figure 7):** Visualizations illustrated that DIPOLE fine-tuning enabled DP-VLA to correct trajectories, prevent collisions, mitigate compounding errors, and reduce erratic driving behavior in scenarios where the pre-trained model failed.

In summary, the results indicate that DIPOLE consistently provides stable and greedy policy optimization across various RL tasks and complex real-world applications like autonomous driving, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods in several configurations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DIPOLE offers a significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of training diffusion-based policies. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to address several long-standing challenges:

1. **Enhanced Stability and Greediness in Diffusion Policy Optimization:** DIPOLE resolves the optimality-stability trade-off inherent in prior KL-regularized RL methods for diffusion policies. By decomposing the optimal policy into dichotomous components with bounded sigmoid weighting, it eliminates the issue of exploding loss due to unbounded exponential terms. This allows for achieving greedy policy improvement without sacrificing training stability, a critical factor for applying RL to complex, high-dimensional action spaces.

2. **Improved Learning Efficiency:** The dichotomous policy framework allows for the simultaneous utilization of both high-return (positive policy) and low-return (negative policy) samples during training. This approach mitigates the problem of learning being dominated by a small subset of high-reward data, leading to more efficient policy optimization and potentially better generalization across the action distribution.

3. **Controllable Action Generation:** The explicit formulation of the optimal policy's score function as a linear combination of the dichotomous policies' scores introduces a mechanism akin to classifier-free guidance. This "greediness factor" (ω) provides practitioners with flexible control over the level of optimality or risk-aversion in generated actions, which is valuable in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving where fine-grained control over behavior is desired.

4. **Scalability to Complex, Real-World Applications:** The successful application of DIPOLE to a 1-billion parameter vision-language-action (VLA) model for end-to-end autonomous driving on the NAVSIM benchmark highlights its scalability and practical applicability. The significant performance improvements observed in metrics like the PDM score, particularly in safety and progress, demonstrate its potential to enhance advanced robotic and autonomous systems in complex, real-world scenarios.

5. **Broad Applicability Across RL Settings:** The method's effectiveness across diverse robotic locomotion and manipulation tasks in both offline and offline-to-online RL settings (ExORL, OGBench) suggests its generalizability. This indicates that DIPOLE could be a versatile tool for fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion policies in various domains where reward signals are available for improvement.

In summary, DIPOLE advances the state-of-the-art in diffusion-based reinforcement learning by providing a robust, stable, and controllable optimization framework. Its theoretical underpinnings address fundamental limitations, while its empirical performance on challenging benchmarks, including a real-world autonomous driving setting, underscore its practical significance and potential to enable more capable and reliable AI systems in decision-making tasks.
