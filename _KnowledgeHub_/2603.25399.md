# LaMP: Learning Vision-Language-Action Policies with 3D Scene Flow as Latent Motion Prior

> [!summary] Summary
> LaMP (Latent Motion Prior), a framework from Southeast University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and others, integrates dense 3D scene flow as a latent motion prior into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to improve robotic manipulation. This approach bridges the gap between 2D semantic features and 3D physical interactions, achieving a 98.3% average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and demonstrating superior zero-shot generalization in out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face a representational mismatch between 2D semantic features from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the explicit 3D physical interactions required for robotic manipulation.
- Current VLA policies struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios and tasks demanding precise depth control or contact-rich manipulation, as they learn 3D interactions implicitly from action labels.
- Prior methods utilizing 2D optical flow or static 3D inputs often lack the explicit depth information or continuous dynamic scene understanding necessary for robust physical foresight.

## Method

- LaMP introduces a ==dual-expert VLA framework== with a Motion Expert that learns a dense ==3D scene flow== as a latent motion prior and an Action Expert that leverages this prior for policy learning.
- The latent motion is represented as dense 3D scene flow, tracking keypoint displacements in (u, v, d) over future timesteps, and the Motion Expert is trained using ==conditional flow matching==.
- A ==gated cross-attention== mechanism adaptively fuses partially denoised, one-step 3D motion features into the last-layer VLM features, conditioning the Action Expert while preserving VLM's semantic understanding.

## Results

- LaMP achieved a **98.3%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, including **96.7%** on LIBERO-Long tasks, outperforming existing baselines.
- The model demonstrated improved zero-shot robustness to out-of-distribution conditions on LIBERO-Plus, achieving an average success rate of **79.3%**, which is **9.7** percentage points higher than OpenVLA-OFT.
- Real-world experiments showed LaMP consistently outperformed baselines across in-domain and OOD tasks, achieving **80%** task progress on pick-and-place and **62.5%** average task progress under OOD conditions, confirming its resilience to visual distribution shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating dense 3D scene flow as a latent motion prior effectively bridges the 2D-to-3D representational gap in VLA models, providing geometric grounding for complex physical interactions.
> - Using partially denoised, one-step 3D motion states for conditioning is sufficient to provide actionable geometric foresight while maintaining computational efficiency for online robotic deployment.
> - An adaptive fusion strategy, such as gated cross-attention, is crucial for selectively integrating geometric cues from the motion prior, complementing rather than overriding the rich semantic understanding from pre-trained VLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is increasingly focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to enable robots to execute natural language instructions in complex visual environments. Traditional VLA models typically regress actions directly from 2D semantic visual features extracted by large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs). While these models have demonstrated generalization capabilities across various tasks and robot embodiments, they often encounter limitations when precise 3D physical interactions are required. This is primarily due to a representational mismatch: VLMs provide 2D-centric semantic features, whereas physical manipulation necessitates explicit reasoning about 3D dynamics, including depth, contact, and object relationships. Consequently, existing VLA models must implicitly infer these complex 3D interactions solely from action supervision, which can lead to brittle execution, especially in novel or out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios such as variations in camera viewpoints, object positions, or kinematic configurations.

Prior research has explored various intermediate representations to bridge this 2D-to-3D gap. Some approaches, such as FlowVLA and TraceVLA, utilize 2D optical flow or visual traces to enhance temporal reasoning, but these methods generally lack explicit depth information critical for contact-rich tasks. Other works, like F1 and FLARE, inject visual foresight by predicting pixel-space futures; however, these predictions can conflate appearance changes with actionable kinematics. Point-cloud diffusion policies operate directly in 3D but often treat geometric features as static inputs rather than modeling continuous temporal dynamics. Furthermore, methods that investigate 3D traces or flows for world modeling (e.g., 3DFlowAction, TraceGen) often function as standalone pipelines, requiring task-specific training and lacking the ability to leverage pretrained VLMs for broader semantic understanding and cross-task transfer.

LaMP positions itself within this landscape by addressing the fundamental representational mismatch. It proposes to explicitly incorporate dense 3D scene flow as a "latent motion prior" within a unified, language-conditioned VLA framework. This contrasts with previous attempts by integrating a future, chunk-level dense 3D scene-flow prior directly into a pretrained VLA, rather than treating 3D flow prediction as a standalone world model or injecting only current-frame 3D geometry. The core contribution is the explicit modeling of continuous, scene-level spatial geometry and temporal dynamics, intended to provide physically grounded guidance for policy learning, thereby enhancing robustness and generalization compared to purely 2D-centric or implicitly 3D-aware VLA policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that integrates explicit 3D dynamic understanding to enhance robotic manipulation capabilities, particularly in tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning and robustness to out-of-distribution conditions.

The motivation for this objective stems from a identified limitation in current VLA models:
1. **Representational Mismatch:** Existing VLA models typically rely on 2D semantic visual features provided by pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs). However, robot manipulation tasks inherently require reasoning about explicit 3D dynamics—such as depth, object interactions, and continuous motion—for precise control. This disparity forces VLA models to implicitly learn complex 3D physical interactions from action labels alone, an inductive bias that is insufficient for robust and generalizable policies.
2. **Brittle Execution under Unfamiliar Dynamics:** The implicit learning of 3D dynamics often leads to degraded performance when robots encounter spatial dynamics or visual conditions that differ from the training data. Examples include tasks requiring precise depth control (e.g., peg insertion, object stacking) where policies fail under varying camera viewpoints or object configurations.
3. **Lack of Explicit Geometric Foresight:** Unlike humans who implicitly form a mental model of necessary motion dynamics before executing a task, current VLA models lack an explicit intermediate representation that captures these 3D dynamic relationships. This absence limits their ability to anticipate future geometric states and plan contact-aware motions.
4. **Limitations of Prior Intermediate Representations:** While previous works have attempted to bridge the 2D-to-3D gap using intermediate modalities like 2D optical flow or pixel-space visual foresight, these often lack explicit depth information, conflate appearance changes with kinematics, or function as standalone models without leveraging the semantic understanding of pretrained VLMs.

To address these motivations, the key objective is to investigate whether dense 3D scene flow can serve as a latent motion prior that explicitly grounds VLA policy learning in physical geometry. The specific objectives include:
- **Developing a dual-expert VLA framework:** This involves creating a "Motion Expert" that generates a 3D scene flow prior and an "Action Expert" that predicts robot actions, with both experts interacting.
- **Efficiently integrating 3D scene flow:** Designing a mechanism to inject 3D scene flow information into the VLM features without incurring prohibitive latency or causing representational collapse of the pretrained VLM features. This specifically involves using one-step partially denoised hidden motion states and a gated cross-attention mechanism.
- **Evaluating performance and robustness:** Assessing whether this explicit 3D motion prior improves performance on tasks requiring long-horizon planning and fine-grained spatial reasoning, and whether it enhances zero-shot generalization under visual perturbations and cross-domain transfer compared to existing VLA baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LaMP (Latent Motion Prior) is proposed as a dual-expert Vision-Language-Action framework that integrates dense 3D scene flow as a latent motion prior. The methodology is structured in a two-stage training process, leveraging pre-trained VLMs.

**Problem Formulation:**
The framework models the joint distribution of actions (A) and latent motion (M), conditioned on visual observations (O) and language instructions (l). Instead of directly modeling P(A | z_t) where z_t is the VLM latent representation, LaMP factorizes this as P(A | z_t, M_t) * P(M_t | z_t). This means an "Action Expert" models P(A | z_t, M_t), predicting actions conditioned on both VLM features and the motion prior, while a "Motion Expert" models P(M_t | z_t), generating the 3D scene flow prior.

**Motion Expert: Learning a Latent 3D Motion Prior:**
1. **3D Scene Flow Representation:** The latent motion M_t is represented as dense 3D scene flow over a uniform spatial grid. Keypoints are sampled on the current image plane (K_h x K_w grid, K=400) and tracked across T future timesteps (T=32). For each keypoint at each timestep, the predicted displacement increment is ∆p = (∆u, ∆v, ∆d), representing 2D displacement in image coordinates and change in depth. This KxTx3 representation is designed to be scene-level, 3D, and embodiment-agnostic. All trajectories are transformed into a unified reference camera frame (cam_ref) to compensate for camera motion, and the (u,v,d) representation is screen-aligned. Ground truth dense correspondences and depth are produced offline by the TraceForge labeler.
2. **Architecture and Training:** The Motion Expert uses a CogVideoX-style 3D transformer that operates in motion-token space (10x10 spatial tokens per timestep from 2x2 patchification). It is conditioned on the last-layer VLM hidden features z_t via Adaptive LayerNorm. The expert is trained using conditional flow matching, where a velocity field v_θ(M_τ, τ, z_t) is learned to predict the direction from noisy motion M_τ to the ground-truth motion M_t. The objective L_motion minimizes the squared difference between the predicted velocity and (M_t - ϵ).
3. **One-step Denoising Features:** To avoid the computational cost of full multi-step 3D scene flow reconstruction during inference, LaMP extracts intermediate motion hidden states (z_m) after a single denoising step (τ=0.1 out of N=10 total steps) from a purely noisy input M_0. These partially denoised features are intended to retain task-relevant dynamics efficiently.

**Motion Guidance and Action Expert:**
1. **Single-layer Motion Guidance:** A gated cross-attention mechanism is employed to fuse the one-step partially denoised motion features (z_m) into the last-layer VLM features (z). The guided features z_guided are computed as z + σ(g) * CA(Q=LN(z), K=V=LN(W_proj z_m)). Here, CA is multi-head cross-attention, and 'g' is a learnable scalar gate initialized at zero, constrained to (0,1) by a sigmoid activation σ(·). This adaptive gating strategy is designed to inject 3D dynamics cues only when beneficial, preventing representational collapse of pretrained VLM features.
2. **Action Expert:** The Action Expert receives the z_guided features, along with the robot's proprioceptive state, and predicts continuous actions (6-DoF end-effector pose and 1-DoF gripper state) using flow matching. Similar to the Motion Expert, it learns a velocity field v_ϕ(a_τ, τ, z_guided) to predict the action velocity, minimizing L_action. This ensures that action denoising is conditioned on explicit 3D dynamics.

**Two-Stage Training and Inference:**
1. **Stage 1: Motion-Prior Pretraining:** The Motion Expert is pretrained on L_motion using 1.6M observation-language-motion triplets. These triplets are constructed from datasets like LIBERO, BridgeV2, DROID, and InternData-A1, with 3D scene-flow supervision generated by the TraceForge pipeline.
2. **Stage 2: Motion-Guided Policy Learning:** The pretrained Motion Expert and the VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct) are frozen. Only the Motion Guidance module (gated cross-attention) and the Action Expert are trained using L_action. During this stage, guidance features are obtained by applying the one-step partial denoising process from the frozen Motion Expert.
3. **Inference:**
 - Encode current observation and instruction using the VLM to get z.
 - Execute the frozen Motion Expert for one denoising step (τ=0 to τ=0.1) from Gaussian noise to extract z_m.
 - Fuse z_m into z via the trained gated cross-attention to get z_guided.
 - Denoise in the action space from τ=0 to τ=1 using the Action Expert to sample the final action.
 This online policy does not require full motion reconstruction during inference, maintaining computational efficiency.

**Data Curation:**
The training dataset for the Motion Expert consists of 1.6M observation-language-motion triplets derived from various robotics datasets, including LIBERO, BridgeV2, DROID, and InternData-A1. The motion labels (dense 3D scene flow) are generated offline using the TraceForge labeler.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research systematically evaluated LaMP across multiple simulation benchmarks and real-world experiments, addressing four specific research questions (Q1-Q4).

**Q1: Geometric Foresight (LIBERO Benchmark):**
- LaMP achieved an average success rate of 98.3% across the LIBERO benchmark (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long suites), which was the highest among all evaluated VLA baselines.
- Specifically, on LIBERO-Long tasks, LaMP reached 96.7% success, outperforming the next strongest baseline (π0.5 at 92.4%). This suggests that explicit 3D motion modeling contributes to reducing error accumulation in long-horizon manipulation sequences.
- Ablation studies comparing LaMP with a "No-Motion" variant (Motion Expert removed) showed a significant performance degradation. On LIBERO, removing the Motion Expert reduced the average success rate from 98.3% to 92.4% (a 5.9% drop), with the most substantial decrease on LIBERO-Long tasks (from 96.7% to 78.2%, an 18.5% drop). On SimplerEnv-WidowX, the "No-Motion" variant saw an average drop from 79.2% to 56.3% (22.9%), including a 50.0% reduction on the challenging Stack Block task (75.0% to 25.0%). These results indicate that explicit visual foresight, via the 3D motion prior, is critical for long-horizon planning and effective skill transfer.

**Q2: 3D Geometry vs. 2D Pixels (SimplerEnv-WidowX Ablation):**
- Comparing LaMP (3D scene flow prior) with a "2D-Flow Prior" variant (where depth dimension was masked) on SimplerEnv-WidowX, the 3D prior consistently yielded performance gains.
- For example, on Stack Block, the 3D prior improved success by 16.7% (from 58.3% for 2D-Flow to 75.0% for LaMP), and on Put Spoon, it improved by 8.3% (from 70.8% to 79.1%).
- Tasks requiring less fine-grained 3D reasoning, such as Put Eggplant, showed comparable performance between 2D and 3D flow (91.7% vs. 95.8%). This suggests that while 2D flow provides temporal cues, the explicit depth information in 3D scene flow is particularly advantageous for contact-rich manipulation tasks with stringent spatial constraints.

**Q3: Out-of-Distribution Robustness (LIBERO-Plus and SimplerEnv-WidowX):**
- **LIBERO-Plus (Zero-Shot OOD):** LaMP achieved the highest average success rate of 79.3% when evaluated zero-shot on seven perturbation dimensions (Camera, Robot, Language, Light, Background, Noise, Layout) after training only on original LIBERO demonstrations. This surpassed the strongest prior baseline, OpenVLA-OFT (69.6%), by 9.7 percentage points.
 - Notable gains were observed on perturbations altering visual appearance or spatial layout: LaMP reached 97.4% on Background and 95.3% on Light.
 - On Robot perturbation, LaMP achieved 69.6%, significantly higher than the next best (π0-Fast at 21.6%). This robustness is attributed to the camera-frame motion prior, allowing dynamics to transfer across visual and kinematic variations.
 - The ablation study showed that removing the Motion Expert reduced the average LIBERO-Plus success from 79.3% to 71.6%, with large drops on Camera (−17.8%) and Robot (−13.6%), confirming the motion prior's role in OOD robustness.
- **SimplerEnv-WidowX (Cross-Domain Transfer):** LaMP achieved a 79.2% average success rate when policies trained on BridgeV2 real-world data were evaluated on the simulated WidowX embodiment without fine-tuning. This outperformed FlowVLA (74.0%) by 5.2% and π0.5 (57.1%) by 22.1%.
 - On Stack Block, a challenging task, LaMP achieved 75.0% compared to FlowVLA's 62.5%.
 - This strong sim-to-sim transfer, despite visual domain gaps, suggests that reasoning in 3D camera coordinate space provides a geometrically grounded representation robust to appearance changes.

**Q4: Fusion Strategy (SimplerEnv-WidowX Ablation):**
- The gated cross-attention mechanism used in LaMP was compared against direct addition ("Add") and concatenation with an MLP ("Concat+MLP") for integrating the motion prior.
- Gated cross-attention consistently outperformed both alternatives across all four SimplerEnv-WidowX tasks. On Stack Block, the "Add" variant achieved only 33.3% and "Concat+MLP" 45.8%, significantly lower than LaMP's 75.0%.
- This indicates that unweighted or non-adaptive motion injection can be detrimental, particularly when spatial precision is critical or when predicted flow might conflict with visual observations. The gated mechanism's ability to adaptively increase guidance when beneficial was shown to be essential.

**Real-World Experiments:**
- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted using a 7-DoF Flexiv Rizon 4 arm with a Robotiq gripper and Intel D415 RGB-D camera, with actions consisting of 6-DoF end-effector pose and 1-DoF gripper state.
- **Tasks:** Three in-domain tasks were evaluated: Pick-and-Place (Stack Cup), Fold Towel (deformable manipulation), and Making Bread (long-horizon multi-step). Three Out-of-Distribution (OOD) conditions were tested: Unseen Layout, Unseen Object, and Unseen Background.
- **Baselines:** LaMP was compared against π0 (flow-matching VLA) and a re-implemented 3D Flow Diffusion Policy (3D FDP).
- **Results:** LaMP consistently outperformed both baselines across all task categories.
 - On Pick-and-Place, LaMP achieved 80% average task progress (π0: 70%, 3D FDP: 53%).
 - On Deformable manipulation, LaMP reached 50% (π0: 40%, 3D FDP: 40%), suggesting a stronger inductive bias for deformable dynamics.
 - On Long-Horizon tasks, LaMP maintained 80% progress (π0: 70%, 3D FDP: 60%).
 - Under OOD conditions, LaMP achieved 62.5% (π0: 52.5%, 3D FDP: 26.8%). The significant margin indicates that camera-frame geometric reasoning is more resilient to visual distribution shifts. 3D FDP showed a substantial performance drop under OOD.
- **Motion Foresight Visualizations:** Visualizations demonstrated that the Motion Expert anticipates the arm's approach and manipulation, showing physically meaningful 3D trajectories for various tasks.

**Runtime and Efficiency (Appendix D):**
- The Motion Expert, performing one partial-denoising step, added 46.3 ms latency (1.35x) and 1.4 GB memory (1.15x) compared to a motion-free variant on a Flexiv platform. This allows for a chunk rate of approximately 5.6 Hz (around 90 Hz effective action rate), meeting real-time control requirements.

**Bimanual Evaluation (Appendix E):**
- LaMP achieved superior average success rates on 10 RoboTwin 2.0 bimanual tasks under both clean (83.9%) and randomized (81.8%) settings compared to π0.5 and X-VLA. This indicates the embodiment-agnostic nature of the (u,v,d) representation and its transferability to bimanual manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper introduces LaMP, a VLA framework that integrates dense 3D scene flow as a latent motion prior, and provides evidence that this approach can contribute to advancing generalist robotic manipulation policies.

The significance of this work can be summarized as follows:
1. **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap:** LaMP directly addresses a fundamental challenge in current VLA models: the mismatch between 2D semantic VLM features and the explicit 3D physical dynamics required for robot manipulation. By introducing an explicit 3D scene flow prior, the framework provides a structured geometric foresight that is typically inferred implicitly by other methods.
2. **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** The consistent outperformance of LaMP against various VLA baselines across multiple benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, SimplerEnv-WidowX) and real-world tasks indicates the utility of the 3D motion prior. Specifically, its improved performance on long-horizon tasks and under diverse out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions (camera, robot, layout, background perturbations) suggests a higher degree of physical grounding and generalization capabilities. The robustness to visual appearance changes and kinematic variations, particularly in cross-domain transfer scenarios, is a notable aspect.
3. **Efficient Integration Strategy:** The proposed one-step partial denoising of motion states and the gated cross-attention mechanism offer an efficient method for integrating 3D dynamic cues. This design avoids the computational burden of full 3D scene flow reconstruction during inference while adaptively injecting geometric information, which contributes to its practical deployability for real-time robotic control.
4. **Embodiment-Agnostic Representation:** The use of a camera-frame, screen-aligned (u,v,d) representation for 3D scene flow contributes to its embodiment-agnostic nature. This allows the motion prior learned from diverse data to transfer effectively to unseen robot embodiments, including bimanual systems, without requiring extensive embodiment-specific robotic action data for pretraining. This property supports the development of more generalist robot policies.

The potential impact of this research includes:
- **Advancing Generalist Robot Policies:** By improving robustness and generalization across various tasks, embodiments, and environmental conditions, LaMP contributes to the development of more capable and broadly applicable robot control policies.
- **Informing Future VLA Architectures:** The findings underscore the importance of explicit 3D dynamic representations in VLA models. This could guide future architectural designs to incorporate similar physically-grounded priors, moving beyond purely 2D-centric perception.
- **Applications in Complex Manipulation:** The demonstrated capabilities in deformable object manipulation and long-horizon tasks suggest potential applications in complex real-world scenarios where precise contact, sequential planning, and adaptability to object properties are crucial.

The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the fixed spatial resolution and temporal horizon of the Motion Expert, its dependence on TraceForge-generated supervision, and the single-layer motion injection. Future work is proposed to address these, including adaptive-resolution motion representations, learning motion priors directly from unlabeled in-the-wild video, and exploring multi-layer fusion strategies, which could further broaden the applicability and capabilities of such frameworks.
