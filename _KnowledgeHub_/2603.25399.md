# LaMP: Learning Vision-Language-Action Policies with 3D Scene Flow as Latent Motion Prior

> [!summary] Summary
> LaMP (Latent Motion Prior), a framework from Southeast University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and others, integrates dense 3D scene flow as a latent motion prior into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to improve robotic manipulation. This approach bridges the gap between 2D semantic features and 3D physical interactions, achieving a 98.3% average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and demonstrating superior zero-shot generalization in out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face a representational mismatch between 2D semantic features from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the explicit 3D physical interactions required for robotic manipulation.
- Current VLA policies struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios and tasks demanding precise depth control or contact-rich manipulation, as they learn 3D interactions implicitly from action labels.
- Prior methods utilizing 2D optical flow or static 3D inputs often lack the explicit depth information or continuous dynamic scene understanding necessary for robust physical foresight.

## Method

- LaMP introduces a ==dual-expert VLA framework== with a Motion Expert that learns a dense ==3D scene flow== as a latent motion prior and an Action Expert that leverages this prior for policy learning.
- The latent motion is represented as dense 3D scene flow, tracking keypoint displacements in (u, v, d) over future timesteps, and the Motion Expert is trained using ==conditional flow matching==.
- A ==gated cross-attention== mechanism adaptively fuses partially denoised, one-step 3D motion features into the last-layer VLM features, conditioning the Action Expert while preserving VLM's semantic understanding.

## Results

- LaMP achieved a **98.3%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, including **96.7%** on LIBERO-Long tasks, outperforming existing baselines.
- The model demonstrated improved zero-shot robustness to out-of-distribution conditions on LIBERO-Plus, achieving an average success rate of **79.3%**, which is **9.7** percentage points higher than OpenVLA-OFT.
- Real-world experiments showed LaMP consistently outperformed baselines across in-domain and OOD tasks, achieving **80%** task progress on pick-and-place and **62.5%** average task progress under OOD conditions, confirming its resilience to visual distribution shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating dense 3D scene flow as a latent motion prior effectively bridges the 2D-to-3D representational gap in VLA models, providing geometric grounding for complex physical interactions.
> - Using partially denoised, one-step 3D motion states for conditioning is sufficient to provide actionable geometric foresight while maintaining computational efficiency for online robotic deployment.
> - An adaptive fusion strategy, such as gated cross-attention, is crucial for selectively integrating geometric cues from the motion prior, complementing rather than overriding the rich semantic understanding from pre-trained VLMs.
