# VisualScratchpad: Inference-time Visual Concepts Analysis in Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VisualScratchpad introduces an interactive framework that uses Sparse Autoencoders to analyze and causally test visual concepts within Vision Language Models during inference. It enables researchers to pinpoint specific visual concepts influencing VLM outputs and diagnose various failure modes through concept ablation.

## Problem

- Vision Language Models (VLMs) produce errors, but the underlying causes often lack clear explanations, hindering systematic debugging and trust.
- Current VLMs are largely black-box, making it difficult to determine if failures stem from visual perception or reliance on misleading cues.
- Existing interpretability tools for VLMs lack integrated interfaces for comprehensive concept exploration, inference-time debugging, and causal analysis.

## Method

- Applies ==Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)== to the vision encoder of VLMs (e.g., CLIP-ViT-large) to extract granular ==visual concepts== from image token representations.
- Links identified visual concepts to the language model's utilization by re-ranking them based on ==text-to-image cross-attention maps== for specific output tokens.
- Provides ==VisualScratchpad==, an interactive interface enabling causal influence testing through ==latent ablation== (setting activations to zero) or amplification to observe output changes.

## Results

- Diagnosed VLM failures in LLaVA-Next-8B due to poor cross-modal alignment, where rephrasing a question to align with internal visual concepts (e.g., 'mitten' instead of 'hand') corrected the output.
- Identified and mitigated model reliance on misleading cues (e.g., 'wheelchair' concepts for a 'standing' person with a walker) by ablating specific latents, causing the model to output the correct 'standing' prediction.
- Demonstrated that 'hidden' visual concepts (e.g., 'rabbit' in a duck-rabbit illusion) could be 'unlocked' by ablating dominant concepts and amplifying latent cues, shifting the VLM's description. The approach was validated on VLMs with up to **8B** parameters using CLIP-ViT-large as the vision encoder.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs can exhibit limited cross-modal alignment, where the vision encoder perceives correct visual cues (e.g., 'gloves'), but the language model fails to link them to the linguistic prompt (e.g., 'hand').
> - VLMs may ground their responses on misleading visual concepts or spurious correlations (e.g., associating a 'walker' with 'sitting' via 'wheelchair' concepts).
> - VLMs can possess and activate 'hidden' or unused visual cues (e.g., both 'duck' and 'rabbit' concepts in an illusion) that are not expressed in the initial output, but can be elicited through intervention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of high-performing Vision Language Models (VLMs) has advanced capabilities in multimodal AI. However, these models still produce errors, and the reasons for these failures often lack clear explanations, posing challenges for systematic debugging and fostering trust. This paper contributes to the field of **mechanistic interpretability**, which aims to understand the internal workings of AI models by analyzing their weights and activation patterns to reverse-engineer the functional roles of internal components.

A significant challenge in mechanistic interpretability is the **polysemanticity** of individual neurons, where a single neuron can activate for multiple unrelated concepts. Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) have emerged as a method to address this by transforming dense model representations into a higher-dimensional, sparse basis of "latents" or "features" that are designed to be more interpretable and ideally monosemantic (representing a single concept). SAEs have demonstrated potential across various AI architectures, including large language models, vision encoders, diffusion models, and domain-specific foundation models.

While SAEs have shown promise for applications such as AI safety, scientific discovery, and dataset analysis, practical interfaces that support systematic analysis pipelines for leveraging SAE latents remain an area of ongoing development. Existing tools like Neuronpedia and ConceptViz are primarily for language models, and PatchSAE offers vision model concept inspection but lacks steering capabilities or VLM inference integration.

This work specifically positions itself by applying SAEs to **vision encoders within VLMs** and providing an **interactive interface** for inference-time analysis. It distinguishes itself from prior VLM-SAE work (e.g., SAE-V, VL-SAE) that applies SAEs to intermediate language model layers or aims for joint vision-language concept alignment. Instead, VisualScratchpad focuses on analyzing *pure visual concepts* from the vision encoder and then linking them to language model behavior *post hoc* via text-to-image attention, offering direct control and insights into the visual component's contribution to VLM outputs. By doing so, it addresses a gap in the interpretability landscape for systematically debugging VLM failure modes that originate from the visual perception stage or cross-modal integration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the interpretability and debuggability of Vision Language Models (VLMs) by providing a systematic method for analyzing their inference-time behavior, specifically focusing on the visual concepts they process and utilize. The core motivations stem from several observations:

1. **Explaining VLM Failures:** Despite high performance, VLMs still produce incorrect answers. The underlying causes of these errors, such as misinterpreting visual cues or failing to utilize relevant information, are often opaque. Understanding "why" a VLM makes an error is crucial for improving its reliability and trustworthiness.
2. **Addressing Limited Interpretability:** Current VLMs are largely black-box models, making it difficult to rigorously determine if failures arise from insufficient visual perception or reliance on misleading visual cues. This opacity hinders effective debugging and model refinement.
3. **Leveraging Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) for Granular Concepts:** SAEs have demonstrated the ability to decompose dense model representations into more granular, interpretable units (latents or concepts). The motivation is to apply this capability to the vision encoder of VLMs to extract meaningful visual concepts.
4. **Connecting Visual Concepts to Language Model Utilization:** A key challenge is not only identifying visual concepts but also determining which of these concepts are *actually used* by the language model component of a VLM when generating an output. The paper aims to bridge this gap by linking visual concepts with specific text tokens via attention mechanisms.
5. **Enabling Causal Analysis and Debugging:** Beyond observation, the motivation extends to enabling *causal testing*. This involves verifying whether identified visual concepts truly influence the model's output and providing a mechanism for "steering" the model by modifying these concepts. This is critical for debugging, as it allows researchers to test hypotheses about specific concept influences.
6. **Developing a Practical and Unified Interface:** The current landscape lacks an integrated interface that supports a comprehensive pipeline for SAE-based concept exploration, inference-time debugging, and causal analysis specifically for VLMs. The motivation is to provide such a tool, called VisualScratchpad, to make these advanced interpretability techniques accessible and actionable for researchers.
7. **Identifying Underexplored Failure Modes:** Through the application of this interpretability framework, the research is motivated to uncover and characterize specific, underexplored failure modes in VLMs, such as issues with cross-modal alignment, reliance on misleading associations, or underutilization of available visual information.

In essence, the work aims to move beyond simply observing model activations to interactively diagnosing and potentially correcting VLM errors by understanding how they perceive and use visual information at a conceptual level.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the VisualScratchpad framework, which integrates Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) with VLM inference to analyze and causally test visual concepts.

#### 3.1. Extracting Visual Concepts with Sparse Autoencoders

- **SAE Application:** Vanilla SAEs are applied to the frozen CLIP-ViT-large vision encoder (Radford et al., 2021), specifically to the image token representations $z \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}$.
- **Architecture:** The SAEs expand the 1024-dimensional hidden representations into a higher-dimensional sparse basis of 32,768 latents $h \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{SAE}}}$. The process is defined as $h = \text{ReLU}(W_{\text{enc}}^{\top} z)$ and $\hat{z} = W_{\text{dec}}^{\top} h$.
- **Training:** SAEs are trained on ImageNet-1K for 2 million steps using a mean-squared error reconstruction loss and an L1 sparsity penalty of $8 \times 10^{-5}$.
- **Layer Selection:** Activations are extracted from multiple layers of the CLIP-ViT backbone (layers 2, 6, 10, 14, 18, and 22). The paper notes a progression in learned concepts across layers, from simple features in early layers to object and scene-level semantics in deeper layers. Layer 18 is primarily used for the case studies due to its strong alignment with object-centric concepts.
- **Concept Characterization:** After training, images are passed through the SAEs to collect maximally activating images for each latent. This allows for computing activation frequency and mean activation strength, which aid in understanding the semantic meaning of each latent.

#### 3.2. Linking Visual Concepts via Text-to-Image Attention

- **VLM Architecture:** The analysis focuses on common VLM architectures where a vision encoder produces image tokens, which are then concatenated with text tokens. The language model component uses cross-attention to allow text tokens to attend to these image tokens. Models like LLaVA, LLaMA, Qwen, InternVL, and Gemma utilize this architecture.
- **Attention Map Generation:** For each output text token, the attention weights from that token to all image tokens (patches) are averaged across all layers and attention heads, forming an attention map $attn \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{\text{patches}}}$. This map quantifies the model's focus on different image regions for a given text token.
- **Attention-Weighted Concept Re-ranking:** To link vision encoder concepts with the language model's utilization, the attention map is multiplied with the patch-wise SAE latent activations $h \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{\text{patches}} \times d_{\text{SAE}}}$. This patch-wise multiplication, represented as $h^{\top}attn$, creates an attention-weighted average. This process re-ranks activated visual concepts, giving higher prominence to latents from image regions that the language model explicitly attends to for generating a specific text token, and down-ranking concepts from less attended regions.

#### 3.3. Testing Causal Influence through Latent Ablation

- **Challenge of Granularity:** SAE latents can vary in semantic granularity and may be correlated, making it challenging to identify a complete set of latents for a single conceptual ablation.
- **Token–Latent Activation Heatmap:** To address this, a token–latent activation heatmap is constructed.
 - **Filtering Noisy Latents:** Noisy latents (e.g., those with very high mean activation or activation frequency) are initially removed based on activation statistics.
 - **Top-k Latent Selection:** For each text token, the top-k (k=20) latents are selected based on their attention-weighted activation strength, creating a manageable set of activated and relevant latents.
 - **Clustering:** These selected latents are then clustered based on their activation similarity across tokens (using correlation-based hierarchical clustering). This grouping helps identify sets of latents that collectively represent a coherent concept influencing the generation of specific text tokens.
 - **Normalization:** The heatmap columns are normalized for clearer visualization, highlighting relative activation strengths.
- **Causal Intervention:** To test causal influence, the activations of selected latent clusters are modified (typically by setting them to zero, i.e., ablation) and the resulting model output is compared to the original. This allows verifying if specific conceptual groups drive particular parts of the VLM's generated response. Users can also increase activation values to emphasize concepts.

#### 3.4. VisualScratchpad Interface

The system is presented as an interactive interface with four main components:
- **SAE Latent Exploration:** Provides an overview of SAE latent statistics (mean activation, sparsity, label entropy) and concept clusters (e.g., UMAP projection of decoder weights).
- **Model Inference:** Supports vision-question answering (VQA) using VLMs (e.g., LLaVA-Next-8B) and CLIP-style image classification tasks.
- **Internal Observation:** Displays attention maps, token-wise latent activations, the token–latent heatmap, and spatial attribution masks for selected latents with reference images.
- **Internal Modification (Steering):** Enables users to modify latent activations (ablate or amplify) and observe changes in the model's output, thus performing causal analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper demonstrates the utility of VisualScratchpad through three case studies, each revealing distinct failure modes in VLMs using LLaVA-Next-8B. The analysis relies on ablating (zeroing out) or amplifying specific SAE latent activations identified via the token–latent heatmap.

#### 4.1. Case 1: Limited Cross-Modal Alignment

- **Observation:** The VLM was asked "Is the cup placed on a surface or on a hand?" and incorrectly responded "on a surface." VisualScratchpad revealed that the model's attention map correctly focused on the hand, and the vision encoder activated concepts related to "(knitted) gloves."
- **Diagnosis:** The misalignment was hypothesized to be between the linguistic concept "hand" in the prompt and the visual concept of "gloves" captured by the vision encoder. The model possessed the correct visual information but failed to connect it to the corresponding textual query effectively.
- **Intervention & Result:** By rephrasing the question to "Is the cup placed on a surface or on a hand with a mitten?", the model produced the correct answer.
- **Implication:** This suggests that VLMs can fail due to poor alignment between rich visual concepts internally represented by the vision encoder and their linguistic counterparts in the prompt, even when the relevant visual cues are perceived.

#### 4.2. Case 2: Grounding on Misleading Cues

- **Observation:** When asked "Is the elderly person in the picture sitting or standing?", the model incorrectly predicted "sitting." The attention map indicated focus on the "walker" in the image.
- **Diagnosis:** VisualScratchpad showed that despite attending to the walker, concepts related to "(wheel)chair" and "sitting" were activated. This indicated the model was relying on associative but semantically inappropriate visual cues, associating the presence of a walker with sitting in a wheelchair.
- **Intervention & Result:** Removing the activated concepts related to "wheelchair" and "sitting" through latent ablation caused the model to flip its prediction to "standing." This process involved an iterative steering, as detailed in the appendix, to fully remove the influence of sitting-related concepts.
- **Implication:** This case illustrates a failure mode where VLMs are misled by spurious correlations or over-reliance on associative cues that are not directly relevant to the specific question asked, even if those cues are visually present.

#### 4.3. Case 3: Unused Hidden Cues

- **Observation:** Presented with an optical illusion image (e.g., "duck-rabbit" illusion), the model initially described a "duck." However, VisualScratchpad revealed that concepts related to "rabbit" were also activating within the vision encoder.
- **Diagnosis:** This suggested that the VLM's vision encoder internally captured multiple plausible visual interpretations, but the language model component only utilized the most dominant one in its final output. The "rabbit" concepts were "hidden" or underutilized.
- **Intervention & Result:** By ablating the "duck-related" latents and simultaneously amplifying the "rabbit-related" latents, the model's output shifted to describing a "rabbit."
- **Implication:** This finding demonstrates that VLMs may internally encode richer and more diverse visual information than what is expressed in their final generated output, leading to inconsistent behaviors or an inability to report alternative interpretations. It highlights the potential for "unlocking" hidden knowledge within the model.

**Summary of Findings:**
The case studies collectively demonstrate that VisualScratchpad is effective in:
- Pinpointing the specific visual concepts contributing to VLM outputs.
- Diagnosing different categories of VLM failures: those related to cross-modal understanding, those from spurious visual associations, and those from underutilizing available visual information.
- Enabling targeted interventions (ablation/amplification) to causally test and correct model behavior, providing a debugging workflow.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VisualScratchpad framework and its associated findings contribute to the field of AI interpretability and trustworthy AI in several ways:

#### 5.1. Advancement in VLM Interpretability

- **Granular Visual Concept Analysis:** By applying SAEs directly to the vision encoder and linking these concepts to language model outputs via attention, the work enables a more granular understanding of *which specific visual features* influence *which specific parts of the generated text*. This offers a level of detail beyond general attention maps or feature visualizations.
- **Causal Debugging Framework:** The introduction of the token–latent heatmap and the capability for latent ablation provides a structured approach for causal analysis. This moves beyond mere observation to actively testing hypotheses about concept influence, which is crucial for systematic debugging.
- **Uncovering VLM Failure Modes:** The identified failure modes—limited cross-modal alignment, misleading visual concepts, and unused hidden cues—provide concrete insights into the current limitations of VLMs. These categories can inform future research directions for building more robust and reliable multimodal models.

#### 5.2. Practical Tool for Researchers and Developers

- **Interactive Debugging Interface:** VisualScratchpad offers a unified, interactive interface that integrates concept exploration, VLM inference, internal observation, and causal steering. This practical tool can empower researchers and developers to debug VLM errors more efficiently, understand model behavior, and potentially improve model performance.
- **Accelerating Model Development:** By pinpointing the root causes of errors (e.g., whether it's a visual perception issue, a cross-modal linking problem, or a language generation issue), developers can make more informed decisions about model architecture changes, training data curation, or prompt engineering strategies.

#### 5.3. Broader Impact on Trustworthy AI

- **Increased Transparency:** By making VLM internals more accessible, VisualScratchpad contributes to increasing the transparency of these complex models. Understanding *why* a VLM makes a decision is a foundational step towards building trust in AI systems.
- **AI Safety and Reliability:** The ability to diagnose failures like reliance on misleading cues can be critical for AI safety, especially in high-stakes applications. By providing mechanisms to detect and potentially mitigate such issues, the work supports the development of more reliable AI.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Directions

The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also suggest avenues for future research:
- **Cross-modal Processing:** The current approach focuses on visual concepts from the vision encoder and links them post hoc. It does not explicitly capture how image-token representations are transformed *within* the language model during cross-modal processing layers. Future work could integrate deeper cross-modal tracing.
- **Scalability for Automated Experiments:** The interface is designed for interactive analysis, not large-scale, fully automated experiments. Future extensions could focus on automating aspects of causal analysis for broader studies.
- **Broader Architectures:** The techniques could be extended to analyze other multimodal architectures beyond the current VLM focus.

Overall, VisualScratchpad represents a step towards a more interpretable and debuggable future for Vision Language Models, offering both conceptual insights and a practical tool to advance the field of trustworthy AI.
