# You Don't Need Strong Assumptions: Visual Representation Learning via Temporal Differences

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UIUC and NYU propose Temporal Difference in Vision (TDV), a self-supervised method for learning visual representations from video by predicting future frame embeddings from past frames and learned motion. This approach, which avoids strong inductive biases, demonstrates improved performance on dense spatial tasks like optical flow and stereo depth compared to existing techniques like DINO and iBOT.

## Problem

- Existing self-supervised visual representation learning methods depend on strong inductive biases, such as image augmentations, masking, or contrastive assumptions.
- These strong biases can limit performance scaling as data volume increases, with empirical evidence suggesting weaker biases are more effective at larger scales.
- Many current approaches, by enforcing invariances or focusing on semantic tasks, may inadvertently discard fine-grained spatial and temporal information relevant for dense prediction tasks.

## Method

- Introduces ==Temporal Difference in Vision== (**TDV**), a self-supervised framework that leverages the causal principle of "past predicts future" from video streams.
- Employs a student frame encoder and a motion encoder, conditioned on the current frame, to additively compose a prediction for the next frame's embedding.
- Integrates a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss for temporal prediction with a DINO-like self-distillation loss to prevent representation collapse, without relying on traditional image augmentations or masking.

## Results

- TDV surpasses DINO and iBOT in optical flow estimation, achieving **9.84** EPE (clean) on MPI-Sintel for ViT-S models, outperforming iBOT's **11.31** EPE.
- The method also achieves lower bad pixel rates for stereo depth, with **39.70** bad@1px on SceneFlow (ViT-S) compared to iBOT's **44.91** bad@1px, indicating fewer large correspondence errors.
- TDV provides competitive performance on semantic segmentation tasks, such as **10.54** mIoU on ADE20K (ViT-S), while showing lower performance on strictly semantic classification benchmarks due to its design focus on spatio-temporal properties rather than semantic invariance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The optimal strength of inductive biases in visual representation learning empirically decreases as the dataset size expands, favoring approaches with fewer hard-coded assumptions.
> - The fundamental causal principle of predicting the immediate future from the past offers a weak yet universally valid inductive bias for learning visual representations from video.
> - Factorizing video learning into frame content and motion in the latent space allows for explicit modeling of temporal dynamics, which is crucial for tasks requiring fine-grained spatial and temporal understanding.
