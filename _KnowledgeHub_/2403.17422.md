# InterHandGen: Two-Hand Interaction Generation via Cascaded Reverse Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> InterHandGen learns a generative prior over interacting two-hand shapes by factoring the joint two-hand distribution into an unconditional single-hand distribution and a conditional one, trained as a single MANO-space diffusion network via conditioning dropout, then sampled cascaded with classifier-free and anti-penetration guidance. It cuts FHID from 3.48 to 1.00 and sets state-of-the-art monocular two-hand reconstruction.

## Problem

- Interacting two-hand reconstruction is well studied, but two-hand generation is under-explored, with no generative prior and no established synthesis benchmark or feature backbone.
- Hand-object generators lean on contact or physics priors assuming dense grasps, so they bias toward heavy contact and fail on loosely contacting bimanual interaction.
- Two-human diffusion priors model the joint distribution directly, which is combinatorially complex for two fully 6-DOF articulated hands and yields low-fidelity samples.

## Method

- ==Factored formulation== replaces joint modeling with an unconditional anchor-hand distribution times a partner-hand distribution conditioned on it; an object point-cloud branch extends it to hand-object generation.
- One shared ==transformer diffusion== network trained with **MANO** mirroring into a common right-hand parameter space plus ==conditioning dropout==, learning conditional and unconditional single-hand scores together.
- **Cascaded reverse diffusion** samples the anchor hand, then the partner hand under ==classifier-free guidance== plus ==anti-penetration guidance== descending a nearest-vertex penetration loss each denoising step.

## Results

- On InterHand2.6M, FHID **1.00** and KHID **0.15** beat BUDDI* at **3.48** and **4.10** and VAE at **8.18** and **6.23**, with diversity **3.59**, recall **0.85**.
- Object-conditioned ARCTIC synthesis reaches FHID **12.91** against **17.00** without the shared network, **19.84** without decomposition, **22.51** for BUDDI*, and **22.56** for ContactGen*.
- Adding the prior to InterWild improves MRRPE **10%** on InterHand2.6M and **18%** on HIC; anti-penetration guidance cuts penetration volume from **6.58** to **0.76** at proximity ratio **0.97**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factoring a joint distribution over interacting instances into unconditional plus conditional single-instance models cuts learning complexity and beats modeling the joint directly.
> - Symmetry normalization plus conditioning dropout lets one network serve both roles, outperforming two separate networks on fidelity, diversity, and penetration simultaneously.
> - Contact-centric grasp priors do not transfer to loose bimanual interaction; test-time gradient guidance restores physical plausibility without retraining or sacrificing closeness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Generating realistic interactions between two human hands remains a challenging problem in computer graphics and computer vision. While significant progress has been made in single hand pose estimation and hand-object interaction, the generation of plausible two-hand interactions has received less attention despite its importance for applications in augmented reality, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction.

InterHandGen, developed by researchers from KAIST, Meta's Codec Avatars Lab, and Imperial College London, addresses this gap by introducing a novel generative model for creating plausible and diverse 3D poses for two interacting hands. The approach introduces a cascaded reverse diffusion process that intelligently decomposes the complex joint distribution of two interacting hands into simpler conditional distributions, leading to more realistic generation results.

### 2. Background and Related Work

The research on hand pose generation exists within several related domains:

**Hand Pose Estimation and Reconstruction**: Most existing work focuses on reconstructing hand poses from images or depth data, with significant progress due to datasets like InterHand2.6M. However, the generation aspect has been less explored.

**Hand-Object Interaction Generation**: Methods for generating hand poses that interact with objects typically rely on physical priors, which are challenging to adapt to two-hand interactions due to the additional degrees of freedom.

**Two-Human Interaction Synthesis**: Recent approaches have addressed full-body human-human interactions, but these methods don't directly translate to the specific challenges of hand-hand interactions.

**Generative Models for Human Motion**: Advances in diffusion models have shown impressive results in human motion synthesis, providing the foundation for InterHandGen's approach.

The fundamental challenge with two-hand interaction generation is the high complexity of joint articulation and contact between two highly articulated structures. Unlike human-human interaction that typically occurs on a shared ground plane, interacting hands have six degrees of freedom that allow for more diverse interactions, making the problem significantly more challenging.

### 3. Methodology

InterHandGen's key innovation lies in its decomposition of the joint distribution of two interacting hands. Rather than directly modeling the complex joint distribution p(x_l, x_r) where x_l and x_r represent the left and right hands, the approach models:

1. The unconditional distribution of a single hand p(x_l)
2. The conditional distribution of the other hand given the first p(x_r|x_l)

This decomposition simplifies the learning problem and leads to more realistic generation results. The method leverages the MANO hand model, a parametric representation that provides a compact encoding of hand shape and pose.

### 4. Cascaded Reverse Diffusion Approach

The core of InterHandGen is a novel cascaded reverse diffusion process that follows these steps:

1. **First Stage**: Generate one hand (e.g., the left hand) from the unconditional distribution using a diffusion model.
2. **Second Stage**: Generate the other hand (e.g., the right hand) conditioned on the first hand using a conditional diffusion model.

This cascaded approach has several advantages:
- It allows conditioning on a clean, non-noisy sample from the first stage
- It simplifies the learning problem by breaking it into more manageable pieces
- It provides better control over the generation process

The method also incorporates physical plausibility through an anti-penetration loss that penalizes interpenetration between the hands during the reverse diffusion process. Additionally, classifier-free guidance is used during conditional sampling to balance fidelity and diversity.

### 5. Network Architecture

InterHandGen uses a transformer-based architecture consisting of:

1. **Encoders**: Separate encoders process the diffused hand, conditional hand, diffusion time, and optional conditioning object (for object-conditioned generation).
2. **Transformer Encoder**: Processes the combined embeddings from all encoders.
3. **Decoder**: Predicts the denoised hand parameters.

The network is trained to handle both unconditional and conditional generation through a unified framework. By sharing network parameters between these tasks, the model leverages the symmetric nature of hands and improves learning efficiency.

The mathematical formulation of the diffusion process involves gradually adding noise to the data and then learning to reverse this process. For a hand pose x₀, the forward diffusion process adds Gaussian noise according to a schedule, while the reverse process learns to denoise gradually:

q(xₜ|x₀) = N(xₜ; √ᾱₜx₀, (1-ᾱₜ)I)

Where ᾱₜ represents the cumulative product of noise schedule parameters.

### 6. Evaluation Protocol

Since no standard benchmark exists for evaluating two-hand interaction synthesis, the authors establish a new evaluation protocol. This includes:

1. **Fidelity**: How closely the generated samples match the real data distribution
2. **Diversity**: The variety in the generated samples
3. **Physical Plausibility**: The absence of unnatural penetrations between hands

To quantify these aspects, they extend existing generative model metrics (FID, KID, Diversity) to the two-hand setting by training a tailored feature backbone network. For physical plausibility, they measure hand penetration volume.

### 7. Results and Analysis

InterHandGen demonstrates superior performance compared to baseline methods in several aspects:

1. **Generation Quality**: The model produces more realistic and diverse hand interactions compared to VAE-based approaches, BUDDI (a concurrent work), and ablated versions of the proposed method.

2. **Component Effectiveness**: Ablation studies confirm that both the distribution decomposition approach and the shared network design contribute significantly to the performance. Without decomposition, the model struggles with generating physically plausible interactions; without the shared network, the diversity of generated samples decreases.

3. **Object-Conditioned Generation**: InterHandGen effectively generates two-hand interactions with objects, as shown in Figure 2, demonstrating the versatility of the approach.

A comparative analysis (Figure 10) shows that InterHandGen produces significantly fewer interpenetrations (highlighted in red boxes) compared to baseline methods.

### 8. Applications

InterHandGen demonstrates practical value in several applications:

1. **Pose Estimation from In-the-Wild Images**: By incorporating the InterHandGen prior into existing hand pose estimation frameworks (like InterWild), the accuracy of reconstruction significantly improves, particularly in reducing interpenetration artifacts.

2. **Hand Pose Prior for Optimization**: The model can serve as a regularizer for various optimization problems involving hand pose, such as tracking or reconstruction.

3. **Data Augmentation**: The diverse and realistic samples generated by InterHandGen can be used to augment training data for downstream tasks like gesture recognition.

### 9. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its strengths, InterHandGen has several limitations:

1. **Static Nature**: The current model generates static poses and doesn't account for temporal coherence in hand motion. Extending to sequential data is a promising direction for future work.

2. **Limited by Training Data**: The model's performance is bounded by the quality and diversity of the training data, which may not cover all possible hand interactions.

3. **One-Way Conditioning**: The current cascaded approach conditions one hand on the other in a one-way fashion. A bidirectional conditioning approach might yield even better results.

Future research directions include:
- Extending to temporal hand motion generation
- Incorporating more explicit physical constraints
- Developing a universal hand prior that works across different tasks and domains
- Exploring applications in robotics for teaching dexterous manipulation

### 10. Conclusion

InterHandGen represents a significant advancement in the field of two-hand interaction generation. By introducing a cascaded reverse diffusion approach that decomposes the complex joint distribution of two interacting hands, the method achieves superior results in generating plausible, diverse, and physically realistic hand interactions.

The model demonstrates versatility in handling both unconditional and object-conditioned generation, and provides valuable contributions as a prior for downstream tasks like hand pose estimation from in-the-wild images. The proposed evaluation protocol establishes a foundation for benchmarking future work in this area.

As AR/VR applications continue to grow in importance, the ability to generate realistic hand interactions becomes increasingly valuable for creating immersive and natural user experiences. InterHandGen provides a powerful tool for addressing this need, with potential applications spanning entertainment, communication, training, and human-computer interaction.
