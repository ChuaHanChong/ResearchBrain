# PostAlign: Multimodal Grounding as a Corrective Lens for MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The PostAlign framework enhances Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) visual understanding and reduces hallucinations by incorporating multimodal grounding as a corrective lens. This approach employs explicit visual and textual grounding mechanisms, enabling MLLMs to anchor responses more faithfully to visual evidence.

## Problem

- MLLMs frequently over-rely on spurious correlations and linguistic priors, leading to generated content inconsistent with actual visual information.
- A direct consequence of linguistic biases is multimodal hallucination, where MLLMs generate objects, attributes, or actions not present in the input image.
- Current MLLMs often struggle with fine-grained visual understanding and complex reasoning, tending to prioritize text-driven generalizations over specific visual evidence.

## Method

- Introduced ==MMGrounded-PostAlign==, a post-multimodal alignment framework that integrates an MLLM backbone, a visual grounding encoder, and a multi-task decoder.
- Developed a ==negative rejection mechanism== where the MLLM predicts a special token for non-existent objects, preventing visual hallucinations by explicitly signaling absence.
- Implemented a ==selective reasoning mechanism==, training the MLLM to dynamically assess query complexity and generate textual rationales only for complex queries, optimizing efficiency and accuracy.

## Results

- The negative rejection mechanism provided the most significant improvement in mitigating hallucinations on the HaloQuest benchmark, particularly in anti-concept scenarios.
- MMGrounded-PostAlign outperformed bounding-box-as-language token methods on the POPE benchmark, achieving superior reduction of object and attribute hallucinations.
- The framework preserved or improved the general reasoning abilities of the underlying MLLM backbone on datasets like VQAv2, MME, and MMBench (e.g., MMBench score of **1514.3** for 7B model vs. baseline **1504.6**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Linguistic priors can progressively override visual information during MLLM decoding, leading to hallucinations that persist even when visual evidence contradicts them.
> - Explicit visual grounding, particularly the negative rejection mechanism, significantly mitigates hallucinations by enforcing stronger multimodal alignment between text and image.
> - The framework allows MLLMs to retain or enhance general reasoning capabilities, suggesting a balanced approach that avoids the degradation seen when over-biasing models towards specific visual tokens.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advent of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), exemplified by models like GPT-4, Gemini, and LLaVA, has revolutionized vision-language tasks such as image captioning, visual question answering, and visually grounded dialogue. These models aim to achieve a robust "multimodal alignment" between visual and textual representations, bridging the semantic gap between modalities. However, despite their impressive capabilities, MLLMs are not without their limitations, particularly as tasks demand increasingly fine-grained visual understanding and complex reasoning.

The paper identifies a critical vulnerability in current MLLMs: their tendency to suffer from "over-reliance on spurious correlations" and "linguistic priors." This means MLLMs often generate outputs based on statistical associations found in their massive training data (e.g., objects frequently co-occurring) or common textual patterns, rather than truly interpreting the visual content. A significant consequence of this bias is "hallucination," where the model generates content that does not exist or is inconsistent with the input image. This manifests as incorrect object attributes, inaccurate spatial locations, or entirely fabricated objects, severely impacting the reliability and trustworthiness of MLLM outputs. Furthermore, this over-reliance on linguistic priors hinders their ability to perform precise visual understanding and complex reasoning, as they may prioritize text-driven generalizations over actual visual evidence.

In the broader research landscape, efforts to address these MLLM limitations fall into several categories:
- **Vision-Language Grounding Models:** Early work focused on establishing correspondences between textual descriptions and image regions, often using object detection or segmentation pipelines. More recently, MLLMs themselves have been adapted for grounding tasks (e.g., Kosmos-2, Shikra), or integrated with separate grounding modules (e.g., LLaVA-Grounding, LISA). The current paper critically distinguishes itself by flipping this paradigm: instead of using MLLMs *for* grounding, it leverages multimodal grounding *to enhance* MLLMs' core visual understanding and mitigate hallucinations. This represents a shift towards using grounding as an *internal corrective mechanism* rather than an output task.
- **Hallucination Mitigation in MLLMs:** Existing strategies include:
 - **Post-processing approaches:** Techniques like self-revising mechanisms (e.g., Volcano) or specialized decoding strategies (e.g., visual contrastive decoding, classifier-free guidance) attempt to correct or prevent hallucinations after initial generation. However, these often incur increased inference time or require specific decoding modifications.
 - **Training-based methods:** These focus on data-level improvements (e.g., negative samples, counterfactual data) or reinforcement learning (RL) to guide models towards hallucination-free outputs. While effective, they often require extensive data augmentation or complex reward engineering.

"PostAlign" positions itself at the intersection of these two areas by proposing a novel "post-multimodal alignment framework." It acknowledges the limitations of current MLLMs and offers a holistic solution by integrating explicit visual and textual grounding. Unlike previous works that either treat grounding as a downstream task or rely on post-hoc corrections or specific data augmentation for hallucination, PostAlign proposes to fundamentally strengthen the multimodal alignment *within* the MLLM's inference process. By making grounding an intrinsic part of how MLLMs process and generate information, the paper aims to create more reliable and visually faithful models, representing a significant advancement in building robust MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the visual understanding capabilities and mitigate the hallucinations of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**. The authors propose to achieve this by introducing a "post-multimodal alignment framework" called MMGrounded-PostAlign, which incorporates multimodal grounding as a "corrective lens" for MLLMs.

The core motivations driving this work stem from the observed shortcomings of existing MLLMs:

- **Addressing Over-reliance on Spurious Correlations and Linguistic Priors:** MLLMs frequently make predictions based on superficial statistical patterns in training data (spurious correlations) or common linguistic associations (linguistic priors) rather than genuine visual evidence. This leads to outputs that are inconsistent with the image.
- **Combating Multimodal Hallucinations:** A direct consequence of the above is hallucination, where MLLMs generate objects, attributes, or actions that are not present in the image. This undermines the credibility and reliability of MLLM-generated content, especially in critical applications. The paper highlights "co-occurrence hallucinations" as a specific manifestation.
- **Improving Fine-Grained Visual Understanding:** Current MLLMs often struggle with precise visual details, tending to focus on high-level visual cues rather than specific object attributes or subtle visual interactions. The goal is to enable models to "perceive and understand visual information more accurately."
- **Enhancing Complex Reasoning Abilities:** When confronted with queries requiring complex reasoning, object interactions, or causal inferences, MLLMs may default to text patterns, leading to illogical or ungrounded responses. The framework aims to ensure "logical consistency" and "well-grounded multimodal responses."
- **Ensuring Outputs are Anchored in Evidence:** The overarching motivation is to make MLLM outputs "firmly anchored in both real visual and textual evidence," thereby making them more trustworthy and interpretable. By establishing reliable connections between language and relevant sources of information, the model should produce outputs that are faithful to the visual input.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move MLLMs beyond merely "seeing" to truly "understanding" by providing them with explicit mechanisms to verify their linguistic generation against the visual reality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **MMGrounded-PostAlign**, is a post-multimodal alignment system designed to reduce MLLMs' reliance on linguistic priors and enhance visual understanding by integrating explicit visual and textual grounding.

The framework comprises three key components:
1. **A MLLM Backbone:** This serves as the primary generative model, processing image and text inputs. It is responsible for predicting tokens related to visual grounding, textual grounding, and the final answer. The authors utilize LLaVA-1.5 (7B and 13B versions) as their MLLM backbone, benefiting from its strong pre-trained vision-language alignment.
2. **A Visual Grounding Encoder:** This module extracts dense visual features from the input image. The authors employ a pre-trained ViT-H SAM (Segment Anything Model) as the backbone for its robust object segmentation capabilities.
3. **A Multi-Task Decoder:** This component performs the actual visual grounding, generating segmentation masks and bounding boxes for referred objects.

**Core Mechanism:**
The MLLM generates a structured output `A = {V, T, F}`, where `V` is the visual grounding token (`<LOC>`), `T` is the textual grounding token for rationale, and `F` is the final answer token. The key idea is that the generated visual grounding outputs and textual rationales act as implicit constraints, guiding the MLLM's final answer generation.

**Key Mechanisms for Enhanced Grounding:**

1. **Rejecting Non-existent Objects (Negative Rejection Mechanism):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** MLLMs frequently hallucinate non-existent objects due to strong linguistic priors and co-occurrence biases.
 - **Approach:** If the referred object is absent from the image, the MLLM is explicitly trained to predict a special `<REJ>` token instead of the standard `<LOC>` token.
 - **Decoder Action:** When the multi-task decoder receives an `<REJ>` token, it is designed to bypass the decoding process and directly assign empty masks and bounding boxes.
 - **Loss Function:** A `Negative Rejection Loss (L_rej)` (binary cross-entropy) is introduced to penalize incorrect grounding predictions when a referent is absent, strengthening the model's ability to distinguish genuine visual referents from linguistic biases. This helps prevent the model from assigning visual regions to hallucinated entities.

2. **Selective Reasoning in Textual Grounding:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Not all queries require explicit rationale generation. Over-generating rationales for simple queries can be inefficient and even degrade performance due to "overthinking."
 - **Approach:** The MLLM is trained to dynamically assess the complexity of a query and adjust its reasoning strategy. Queries are categorized as `<SIMPLE>` or `<COMPLEX>`.
 - **During Training:**
 - For `<SIMPLE>` queries, the MLLM outputs the `<LOC>` token and the final answer directly, skipping rationale generation.
 - For `<COMPLEX>` queries, the MLLM's output includes the `<LOC>` token, a contextual rationale, and the final answer.
 - **During Inference:** A "self-reflection prompting" mechanism is incorporated into user instructions, guiding the MLLM to classify query complexity (`<SIMPLE>` or `<COMPLEX>`) before generating its response.
 - **Loss Function:** A `Selective Reasoning Loss (L_reason)` (binary cross-entropy) supervises the model in correctly identifying whether a query requires complex reasoning.

**Training Objective:**
The MLLM is fine-tuned using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) to preserve its pre-trained knowledge and achieve parameter-efficient fine-tuning. The visual grounding encoder (ViT-H SAM) is frozen, while the multi-task decoder, LLM token embeddings, LLM head, and projection layer are fully fine-tuned.
The overall loss function for optimization is a combination of:
- `L_rej`: Negative rejection loss.
- `L_reason`: Selective reasoning loss.
- `L_ground`: Visual grounding loss, which jointly optimizes detection (Smooth-L1, GIoU) and segmentation (BCE, DICE) losses.
- `L_text`: Cross-entropy language modeling loss for the MLLM's textual output.

This multi-objective training ensures that the model not only generates accurate text and visual groundings but also learns to reject hallucinations and adapt its reasoning process efficiently based on query complexity. The use of a post-alignment framework allows for integrating powerful off-the-shelf grounding models without heavily modifying or retraining the large MLLM backbone, making the approach practical and scalable.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive evaluations across various benchmarks, demonstrating the effectiveness of MMGrounded-PostAlign in mitigating hallucinations, enhancing visual understanding, and preserving general reasoning capabilities.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Linguistic Priors Override Visual Information in MLLMs:**
 - **Evidence:** Analysis of token probability distributions during decoding showed that while non-hallucinated tokens exhibited high probabilities early on, hallucinated tokens only gained prominence later in the decoding process, often surpassing ground-truth tokens. This suggests a progressive suppression of visual information by linguistic priors.
 - **Further Validation:** A statistical analysis removing image input revealed an 89.2% overlap in hallucinated tokens, indicating that a significant portion of hallucinations persists regardless of visual evidence, strongly confirming that linguistic biases are the primary driver of hallucinations.

2. **Explicit Visual Grounding Mitigates Hallucinations:**
 - **HaloQuest Benchmark (Table 1):** Ablation studies on HaloQuest, a dataset designed to evaluate visual hallucinations, showed substantial improvements.
 - Introducing mask grounding (`<SEG>`) and bounding box grounding (`<DET>`) significantly improved performance over the baseline (no grounding).
 - Combining both (`<SEG>` + `<DET>`) yielded even better results.
 - Crucially, the **negative rejection mechanism (`<REJ>`) provided the most significant boost**, particularly in "False Premise" and "Insufficient Context" categories, which contain anti-concept scenarios. This mechanism allows the model to explicitly reject non-existent objects, effectively suppressing hallucinations.
 - **POPE Benchmark (Table 2):** MMGrounded-PostAlign significantly outperformed methods that treat bounding boxes as language tokens (BTL-Generation, BTL-Caption), which showed only moderate or no gains. This highlights the superiority of the explicit visual grounding module and negative rejection in enforcing stronger multimodal alignment and reducing object/attribute hallucinations.

3. **MLLM Retains Strong Reasoning and Generalization Abilities:**
 - **Generalization & Reasoning Datasets (VQAv2, MME, MMBench) (Table 2):** The evaluations demonstrated that MMGrounded-PostAlign not only preserves but often *improves* the general reasoning abilities of the underlying MLLM backbone (LLaVA-1.5). For instance, MMGrounded-PostAlign-7B scored 1514.3 on MMBench compared to Baseline-7B's 1504.6.
 - **Contrast with BTL:** In stark contrast, the BTL-Generation training paradigm often led to a *decline* in the MLLM's reasoning ability, suggesting it might inadvertently bias the model towards visual bounding box information at the expense of abstract reasoning and generalization.

4. **Selective Reasoning in Textual Grounding Optimizes Efficiency and Accuracy:**
 - **ReasonSeg Benchmark (Table 3):** Comparison of textual grounding strategies revealed:
 - "Pre-Reasoning" (separate MLLM for rationale) achieved good performance but was inefficient due to multiple inference rounds.
 - "Inter-Reasoning" (main MLLM generates rationale and answer simultaneously) was less effective.
 - The proposed **"Selective Reasoning" mechanism performed best**, adapting to query complexity. It improved performance on complex queries by providing necessary rationales while avoiding performance degradation (due to "overthinking") on simple queries, all within a single inference round.

5. **Our Architecture Provides a Potential Approach for Visual Grounding with MLLMs:**
 - **REC and RES Tasks (RefCOCO series, ReasonSeg) (Table 4):** While not the primary goal, MMGrounded-PostAlign achieved competitive results on referring expression comprehension (REC) and referring expression segmentation (RES) tasks compared to specialized MLLM-based grounding models. This indicates a surprising mutual benefit: the visual grounding module not only helps the MLLM but also benefits from the MLLM's linguistic capabilities, enhancing its zero-shot grounding abilities.
 - **Emphasis:** The authors clarify that their focus was on leveraging grounding for MLLM improvement, not optimizing grounding tasks directly, hence no additional grounding-specific training data was used.

**Qualitative Analysis (Figure 4):**
Visualization of attention maps showed that grounding significantly influences the model's attention to image features. Without grounding, attention was sparse and scattered. With grounding, attention became **significantly more concentrated on relevant image areas**, indicating that the framework effectively guides the MLLM to align textual outputs with pertinent visual evidence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MMGrounded-PostAlign framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), addressing critical limitations that have hindered their robustness and real-world applicability.

**Key Significance Points:**

- **Robustness against Hallucinations:** The most profound impact lies in its effective mitigation of hallucinations, a pervasive and detrimental problem in MLLMs. By introducing an explicit negative rejection mechanism and anchoring outputs in visual evidence, the framework makes MLLMs significantly more reliable and factually consistent. This is crucial for building trust in AI systems.
- **Enhanced Visual Understanding:** The framework moves beyond superficial vision-language alignment, enabling MLLMs to achieve a more fine-grained and accurate understanding of visual content. This is achieved by forcing the model to refer to specific visual regions and objects, reducing reliance on general patterns or co-occurrence biases.
- **Improved Reasoning Capabilities:** By integrating selective textual grounding, the model not only retains but often enhances its capacity for complex reasoning. The ability to generate rationales only when necessary optimizes efficiency and ensures that complex queries receive the thoughtful, step-by-step reasoning required.
- **Novel Paradigm of Grounding for MLLMs:** Unlike prior work that often treats grounding as a downstream task *for* MLLMs, PostAlign proposes a novel approach where grounding acts as an *internal corrective lens* *for* MLLMs. This conceptual shift could inspire new architectural designs and training methodologies for future multimodal models.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** The use of LoRA for MLLM fine-tuning and a post-alignment framework allows the integration of powerful, pre-trained components (like SAM) without extensive re-training of the entire large model. The selective reasoning mechanism further optimizes inference by avoiding unnecessary rationale generation for simple queries.

**Potential Impact:**

The implications of MMGrounded-PostAlign are far-reaching across various domains:

- **Real-World Applications:** MLLMs are being deployed in diverse applications, from intelligent assistants to medical imaging analysis and autonomous systems. The improved reliability and reduced hallucinations offered by this framework will make these applications safer, more accurate, and more trustworthy. For instance, in healthcare, preventing hallucinations in image reports is paramount.
- **Human-AI Interaction:** More factually grounded and coherent responses from MLLMs will lead to more natural, helpful, and satisfying human-AI interactions, reducing user frustration caused by incorrect or nonsensical outputs.
- **Foundation Model Development:** This work provides valuable insights into the fundamental challenges of MLLM design, particularly concerning the interplay between linguistic priors and visual evidence. It could guide the development of future foundation models that are inherently more robust and less prone to generating spurious content.
- **Interpretability and Explainability:** By generating explicit visual groundings (bounding boxes, masks) and textual rationales, the framework also contributes to greater interpretability of MLLM decisions. Users can "see" what the model is referring to and "understand" its reasoning, fostering greater transparency.
- **Future Research Directions:** The demonstrated mutual benefit between MLLMs and grounding modules (where MLLMs assist grounding and vice versa) opens new avenues for research into jointly optimizing these components or designing truly unified, general-purpose vision-language models that inherently understand and represent visual information with high fidelity. Further exploration of different types of grounding (e.g., temporal, causal) or more sophisticated query complexity assessment could also emerge from this work.

In conclusion, "PostAlign" offers a compelling solution to a critical problem in MLLMs, making them more robust, reliable, and intelligent, thus accelerating their practical deployment and pushing the boundaries of multimodal AI.
