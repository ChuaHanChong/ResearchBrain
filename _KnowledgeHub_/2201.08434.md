# DROPO: Sim-to-Real Transfer with Offline Domain Randomization

> [!summary] Summary
> DROPO estimates domain randomization distributions offline from a small precollected real trajectory dataset. It replays real state-action pairs in simulation under sampled dynamics, models the next-state spread as a Gaussian, and maximizes the likelihood of real next states via CMA-ES, optimizing both means and variances. Zero-shot policies transfer to Kuka hockey-puck sliding and Panda box pushing, beating DROID, BayesSim, UDR.

## Problem

- Online adaptive domain randomization methods need repeated real-hardware rollouts of unconverged policies during optimization, costing supervision time and risking hardware damage in contact-rich tasks.
- Offline L2 trajectory-matching objectives collapse to point-estimate dynamics, discarding the variance that domain randomization needs to cover unmodeled phenomena and parameter uncertainty.
- Likelihood-based inference approaches assume the data-collection policy is known and train inference networks only on simulated rollouts, generalizing poorly to human demonstrations and unmodeled real effects.

## Method

- **DROPO** fits a ==truncated-normal== dynamics distribution by replaying each real state-action segment under K sampled parameter vectors, then maximizing the ==Gaussian log-likelihood== of the real next state.
- An epsilon term added to the next-state covariance diagonal trades domain variance against observation noise; it is tuned by an ==elbow rule==, raising epsilon until sim-real MSE stops improving.
- Optimization is gradient-free ==CMA-ES== over a 2d-dimensional vector of means and variances; a multi-step horizon lambda replays several actions before scoring, raising signal-to-noise. Policies then train with ==PPO== or ==SAC==.

## Results

- On Hopper, DROPO recovered ground-truth masses exactly (e.g. **3.534**, **3.927 kg**) and recovered the generating **0.25** standard deviation, while DROID collapsed to sigma near **1e-12** and BayesSim overestimated variance.
- With a **1 kg** misspecified torso mass excluded from optimization, DROPO policies nearly matched the oracle trained in the target environment, while DROID, BayesSim and UDR returns dropped sharply.
- Zero-shot Panda pushing from a single **13-second** kinesthetic demonstration landed the box about **2 cm** from target, beating DROID, BayesSim and UDR; hockey-puck sliding tied DROID but beat UDR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Squared-error trajectory matching on fixed offline data provably converges to point estimates; any offline randomization method needing spread must use a probabilistic objective or explicit variance constraints.
> - Fitted parameter variance can absorb unmodeled physics: distributions widen away from true values to explain transitions a single simulator setting cannot reproduce.
> - Distributional dynamics help only in closed-loop sequential tasks; in feedforward bandit-style settings point-estimate identification performs equally, so match the inference method to the control structure.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics has seen remarkable advancements through reinforcement learning (RL), but its direct application to real-world physical systems is hampered by a significant challenge known as the "reality gap." This gap arises from discrepancies between simulated and real environments (e.g., inaccuracies in physics parameters, unmodeled phenomena), leading to policies trained in simulation performing poorly when deployed on real hardware.

**Domain Randomization (DR)** has emerged as a prominent technique to address the reality gap. DR involves training an RL agent across a wide distribution of randomized environments in simulation, forcing the policy to learn robust behaviors that generalize to unseen variations in the real world. While successful, a major challenge in DR is determining the optimal randomization distributions for physical parameters. Too narrow a distribution may lead to overfitting to simulation, while too wide a distribution can make learning a generalizable policy difficult.

Prior work to automate DR can be broadly categorized into:
- **Online-adaptive DR:** These methods iteratively collect data from the real world, evaluate policy performance, and then update the randomization distribution. Examples include SimOpt, EPOpt, and methods using Bayesian optimization. While effective, they require extensive and potentially risky real-world interaction during the optimization process, which can be data-inefficient and unsafe for delicate robotic hardware.
- **Offline DR:** To mitigate the risks and data demands of online methods, approaches like DROID and BayesSim proposed optimizing DR distributions using a fixed, pre-collected offline dataset from the real world. This reduces real-world interaction to a single data collection step.

DROPO positions itself firmly within the **offline DR** paradigm, aiming to advance it further. It addresses specific limitations of existing offline methods:
- **Comparison with DROID:** DROID primarily minimizes an L2-based discrepancy between simulated and real trajectories. The authors argue (and demonstrate in Appendix A) that L2-based objectives often lead to optimization converging to point-estimate dynamics parameters rather than distributions, thereby hindering generalization, especially in the presence of unmodeled phenomena. DROPO, in contrast, uses a *likelihood-based objective* and explicitly optimizes *both the mean and variance* of the dynamics parameter distribution.
- **Comparison with BayesSim:** BayesSim learns a neural network to predict dynamics parameter posteriors. However, it assumes the data collection policy used in the real world is known and available during optimization, which limits its applicability for general offline datasets or human demonstrations. DROPO's maximum likelihood framework does not require knowledge of the data collection policy, making it more flexible for use with diverse offline data sources, including human demonstrations.

In essence, DROPO introduces a novel, principled approach to offline DR that leverages probabilistic modeling to learn robust parameter distributions, overcoming the limitations of previous methods in achieving non-degenerate, generalizable distributions without requiring extensive online interaction or specific knowledge of the data collection process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable **safe and effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer** of reinforcement learning policies for robotic manipulation tasks. The authors are specifically motivated by the challenges associated with finding appropriate domain randomization (DR) distributions, which are crucial for this transfer.

The core motivations driving the development of DROPO are:
- **Overcoming the Reality Gap:** Despite the successes of RL in simulated environments, the policies often fail to transfer to real robots due to the reality gap, stemming from simulator inaccuracies (e.g., uncertain physical parameters, unmodeled phenomena).
- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Safety in Real Robotics:** Training RL agents directly on real robots is highly sample-inefficient, poses risks of hardware damage during random exploration, and is time-consuming. Simulation offers a safe and fast alternative for data collection, but effective sim-to-real transfer is paramount.
- **Improving upon Existing Domain Randomization (DR) Methods:**
 - **Difficulty of Manual Tuning:** Manually finding effective DR distributions is a difficult and tedious process, requiring expert intuition and significant trial and error.
 - **Limitations of Online Adaptive DR:** While automating DR, online methods require iterative interaction with the real system, which is costly, slow, and unsafe.
 - **Limitations of Current Offline DR Methods:**
 - **Point-Estimate Collapse:** Methods like DROID, relying on L2-based loss functions, tend to collapse to point-estimate dynamics parameters. This can lead to policies that are not robust enough to handle the inherent uncertainty and unmodeled aspects of the real world. The authors explicitly motivate DROPO by demonstrating that an L2-based objective for optimizing distributions inevitably converges to a point-estimate (Appendix A).
 - **Policy Dependence:** Methods like BayesSim require knowledge of the real-world data collection policy, which restricts their use, particularly with human demonstrations.
- **Explicitly Modeling Uncertainty:** The authors hypothesize that explicitly modeling the variance in dynamics parameters, rather than just their means, is crucial for developing policies that can compensate for unmodeled phenomena and generalize effectively.

Based on these motivations, the paper outlines four key contributions, which also serve as its specific objectives:
1. **Introduce DROPO:** A novel method for offline optimization of domain randomization distributions using safely collected data.
2. **Validate Distribution Recovery:** Experimentally demonstrate DROPO's capability to accurately recover ground truth dynamics distributions in simulated environments.
3. **Demonstrate Compensation for Unmodeled Phenomena:** Show that DROPO-derived DR distributions can be used to train robust policies even when the simulation model is incomplete.
4. **Achieve Successful Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Validate DROPO's effectiveness on real robotic setups, demonstrating that policies trained with its distributions can be deployed directly to the real world with improved performance over baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DROPO (Domain Randomization Off-Policy Optimization) is a three-stage methodology designed for sim-to-real transfer. Its core innovation lies in the second stage: a likelihood-based approach to fit dynamics parameter distributions to an offline dataset.

**1. Data Collection on Real Hardware:**
- **Purpose:** To gather a limited, pre-collected dataset `D` of real-world state-action trajectories `(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})`. This is the *only* stage requiring interaction with physical hardware, ensuring safety and data efficiency.
- **Methods:** Data can be collected via:
 - Running any pre-existing, safe policy (e.g., for a different task).
 - Human demonstrations, such as kinesthetic teaching.
- **Requirements:** The data must be sufficiently "exciting" to allow identification of relevant dynamics parameters (e.g., objects must move to infer friction).
- **Action Inference:** If human demonstrations are used, the true actions `a_t` might need to be inferred from observed state sequences using an inverse dynamics model. The authors note this can be simplified for position/velocity-controlled robots, and DROPO can tolerate some discrepancies.
- **Preprocessing:** Raw sensor data (e.g., joint positions, object poses from motion capture) is synchronized (accounting for delays) and resampled to match the simulator's timestep frequency using Akima splines for smooth interpolation, preventing overshooting artifacts.

**2. Dynamics Distribution Fitting (Core of DROPO):**
- **Goal:** Optimize the parameters `φ` of a domain randomization distribution `p_φ(ξ)` over dynamics parameters `ξ` to maximize the likelihood of observing the collected real-world dataset `D` in simulation.
- **Modeling `p_φ(ξ)`:** The distribution is modeled as an uncorrelated truncated normal distribution. Crucially, `φ` encapsulates both the *means (μ)* and *variances (Σ)* for each univariate dynamics parameter. This contrasts with DROID, which primarily optimizes only the means.
- **Likelihood Maximization using Gradient-Free Optimization (CMA-ES):**
 1. **Initialization:** `φ` is initialized with an arbitrary guess, potentially informed by a rough simulation replay.
 2. **Sampling Dynamics:** For each iteration of optimization, `K` different dynamics parameter vectors `ξ_k` are sampled from the current `p_φ(ξ)`.
 3. **Simulator Execution & Next-State Distribution Estimation:** For each `(s_t, a_t..t+λ-1, s_{t+λ})` tuple from the real dataset `D`:
 - The simulator state is reset to `s_t`.
 - The action sequence `a_t..t+λ-1` is executed in the simulator `K` times, once for each sampled `ξ_k`.
 - This yields `K` different next-states `s_ξk_t+λ`. These `K` states are used to estimate the conditional next-state distribution `p_sim(s_sim_t+λ | s_t, a_t..t+λ-1, φ)` as a Gaussian distribution.
 - The mean `¯s_φ_t+λ` is computed as the average of the `K` simulated next-states.
 - The covariance matrix `Σ_φ_t+λ` is computed as the unbiased sample covariance of the `K` simulated next-states, *plus* a diagonal regularization term `diag(ε)`.
 - The hyperparameter `ε` accounts for observation noise and regularizes the covariance, preventing singularities. It is tuned by increasing it until the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between real and simulated trajectories no longer significantly decreases (similar to the "elbow method"). This balances attributing next-state variance to dynamics vs. observation noise.
 4. **Log-Likelihood Calculation:** The log-likelihood `L_t` of the actual real-world `s_{t+λ}` is computed under the estimated Gaussian `N(¯s_φ_t+λ, Σ_φ_t+λ)`.
 5. **Total Likelihood and Parameter Update:** The individual `L_t` values are summed to get the total log-likelihood `L`. `φ` is then updated to maximize `L` using a gradient-free optimizer (CMA-ES is used). Parameters are normalized for stable optimization.
 6. **λ Parameter:** An optional hyperparameter `λ` allows stepping the simulator more than once (`λ` > 1) before evaluating likelihood, increasing signal-to-noise ratio.
- **Quaternion Handling:** For orientations (e.g., of a box), DROPO uses quaternion representations. Likelihood is based on the angle `α` between the real and simulated quaternions, creating a symmetric distribution of angles.

**3. Policy Training in Simulation:**
- **Goal:** Train an RL policy for the target task using the learned robust dynamics distribution.
- **Process:** The converged `p_φ*(ξ)` from the previous step is used for Domain Randomization during standard RL training. At the start of *each* training episode, a new `ξ` is sampled from `p_φ*(ξ)`.
- **RL Algorithm:** Any standard RL algorithm can be used (PPO and SAC are mentioned).
- **Independence:** The initial offline dataset `D` is *not* used during policy training; thus, the demonstrations do not need to be specific to the ultimate task, as long as they provide enough information for parameter inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to assess DROPO's ability to recover dynamics distributions, compensate for unmodeled phenomena, and achieve successful sim-to-real transfer, benchmarked against DROID, BayesSim, and Uniform Domain Randomization (UDR).

**1. Recovery of Dynamics Parameters and Distributions (Hopper Environment - Sim-to-Sim):**
- **Point-Dynamics System Identification:** When the offline dataset was generated from a *single* Hopper environment instance, DROPO successfully identified the ground truth physical parameters, converging to point-estimate dynamics (very low standard deviations ≈ 10⁻⁵). This performance was comparable to DROID. BayesSim, however, overestimated the distribution variance and was less accurate in recovering mean parameter values.
- **Dynamics Distribution Recovery:** When the dataset was generated from a *distribution* of Hopper environments (masses randomized), DROPO successfully learned both the original means and *variances* of the randomized masses. In contrast, DROID's L2-based objective function largely failed to provide wide distribution ranges, collapsing to point estimates. BayesSim, while preserving some variance, was less accurate than DROPO.

**2. Compensation for Unmodeled Phenomena (Hopper - Sim-to-Sim):**
- **Scenario:** An unmodeled effect was introduced by misspecifying the Hopper torso mass by 1kg in the source simulator, excluding it from optimization. Methods could only adjust other masses.
- **Results:** Policies trained with DROPO's randomization distributions significantly **outperformed** all baseline methods (DROID, BayesSim, UDR), almost matching a policy trained directly in the ground-truth environment.
- **Analysis:** DROPO converged to a *distribution* over the remaining masses, widening it to compensate for the unmodeled phenomenon, maximizing the likelihood of observing target data. DROID, however, still converged to point-estimate parameters, leading to poor generalization. BayesSim produced wide distributions, but policies were overly conservative and slower, possibly due to poor generalization of its inference network to out-of-distribution real-world data (with unmodeled effects). This result highlights DROPO's crucial ability to model uncertainty for robustness.

**3. Impact of the `ε` Hyperparameter:**
- **Role:** `ε` adds a diagonal term to the covariance matrix, regularizing likelihood computation and compensating for observation noise.
- **Sensitivity Analysis:**
 - **Low `ε`:** DROPO might overestimate standard deviations in noisy settings, attempting to explain observation noise through dynamics variance.
 - **`ε` matching noise variance:** Leads to accurate distribution recovery and significant drops in MSE, indicating optimal balance.
 - **High `ε`:** Leads to over-regularization, attributing more variance to observation noise, causing estimated dynamics distributions to collapse towards point estimates.
- **Tuning Strategy:** The proposed tuning procedure (increasing `ε` until MSE no longer meaningfully decreases) was validated as effective, resembling the "elbow method."

**4. Real-World Robotics Experiments (HockeyPuck and Pushing Tasks):**
- **HockeyPuck (Contextual Multi-Armed Bandit):**
 - **Task:** Kuka robot hits a puck to a target. Actions are pre-defined hitting trajectories (latent space).
 - **Results:** Both DROPO and DROID significantly outperformed UDR, with no significant difference between DROPO and DROID.
 - **Analysis:** The feed-forward nature of this bandit task (no online feedback for policy adjustment) diminished the benefits of learning a distribution over dynamics. Policies primarily focused on minimizing expected final distance, similar to DROID's L2 objective.
- **Pushing (Franka Panda, Sequential Decision-Making):**
 - **Task:** Franka Panda robot pushes a box with an offset center of mass.
 - **Sim-to-Sim:** DROPO policies performed close to ground truth, outperforming DROID (overfitting to point estimates) and UDR.
 - **Sim-to-Real:** DROPO **consistently outperformed** DROID, BayesSim, and UDR, successfully pushing the box to within 2cm of the target. This demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer.
 - **Analysis of Baselines:**
 - DROID again converged to point estimates, leading to worse performance.
 - BayesSim struggled significantly with real-world data (out-of-distribution for its inference network), producing inconsistent, highly varied, and sometimes physically unfeasible parameter estimates (e.g., negative masses, center of mass outside the object), severely hindering policy performance. This confirmed the hypothesis that BayesSim generalizes poorly to real-world data with unmodeled effects.
- **Overall:** The pushing task, a sequential decision-making problem with an unmodeled center of mass offset, clearly demonstrated DROPO's superiority in balancing system identification and domain randomization for complex real-world transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DROPO represents a significant advancement in the field of sim-to-real transfer for reinforcement learning in robotics, offering a principled and practical solution to a critical bottleneck: automatically finding robust domain randomization distributions. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Safety and Data Efficiency:** By leveraging limited, pre-collected *offline* data (including human demonstrations) for parameter inference, DROPO drastically reduces the need for costly, time-consuming, and potentially hazardous online interaction with real robotic hardware during the learning process. This is a crucial practical advantage for deploying RL in real-world applications.
- **Improved Robustness to Reality Gap:** Unlike prior methods that tend to converge to point-estimate dynamics, DROPO's likelihood-based approach explicitly models and optimizes for the *variance* of dynamics parameters. This enables the learned policies to inherently account for uncertainty, noise, and crucially, **unmodeled phenomena** present in the real world that are not fully captured by the simulator. This robust generalization capability is paramount for reliable real-world deployment.
- **Broader Applicability and Accessibility:** DROPO's independence from the data collection policy makes it more versatile than methods like BayesSim. It can effectively utilize various sources of offline data, including readily available human demonstrations, making advanced sim-to-real techniques accessible to a wider range of robotics problems and practitioners.
- **Principled Approach to Uncertainty Quantification:** By treating the simulator as a stochastic forward model and maximizing the likelihood of real-world observations, DROPO offers a statistically sound method for quantifying the uncertainty in physical parameters. The careful tuning of the `ε` hyperparameter provides a systematic way to balance this parametric uncertainty with observation noise.
- **Facilitating Complex Robotic Tasks:** The demonstrated success in complex manipulation tasks like the box pushing with an offset center of mass highlights DROPO's capability to handle subtle yet impactful discrepancies between simulation and reality. This opens doors for more sophisticated RL applications in areas such as industrial automation, logistics, and assistive robotics, where robustness and reliability are non-negotiable.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays out several promising avenues for future work:
 - **Advanced Data Collection Strategies:** Investigating how different offline data collection strategies impact the accuracy of parameter inference.
 - **Scaling to Higher Dimensions:** Extending DROPO to higher-dimensional parametric distributions (e.g., for more complex contact dynamics) by exploring more efficient non-differentiable or gradient-based optimization methods.
 - **Vision-Based Sim-to-Real:** Applying the core idea to high-dimensional state spaces, like images, to optimize not only physics but also visual domain randomization parameters (e.g., for procedural texture generation).
 - **Automated Parameter Selection and Simulation Model Design:** Moving towards automating the selection of parameters to randomize and even the construction of simulation models from real-world observations, reducing the significant manual effort currently required.

In conclusion, DROPO represents a significant step towards closing the reality gap in a practical and robust manner. Its ability to learn comprehensive dynamics distributions from minimal offline data offers a valuable tool for accelerating the development and deployment of advanced reinforcement learning policies in real-world robotic systems.
