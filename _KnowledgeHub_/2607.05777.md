# Observation Quality Matters: Robust Multi-Fisheye Calibration via Failure-Oriented Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology found that multi-fisheye camera calibration failures stem from ill-conditioned intrinsic initialization, where focal scale and projection-shape parameters are poorly separable without broad radial observation coverage. Their CO-Calib framework, which includes a robust learning-based detector and an observability-aware frame selector, increased synthetic calibration success rates from 68.1% to 99.3% and enabled successful calibration of complex real-world systems where other methods failed.

## Problem

- Accurate calibration of multi-fisheye camera systems, crucial for robotics, becomes challenging and less robust with increasing rig size, diverse camera arrangements, and wider fields of view.
- Existing calibration methods are highly sensitive to the quality and distribution of input observations, often leading to inconsistent results or failures in real-world applications.
- A systematic understanding of how observation conditions fundamentally contribute to calibration failures was lacking, leading to reliance on empirical tuning and specialized capture procedures.

## Method

- A systematic ==failure-oriented analysis== was conducted using controlled synthetic datasets to pinpoint the exact stage and observational characteristics contributing to calibration failures.
- **CO-Calib**, a plug-in framework, was developed featuring a robust ==learning-based calibration target detector== capable of accurate detection under severe fisheye distortion.
- An ==error-analysis-guided frame selector== was designed to construct an optimization-ready sequence of frames based on ==Point-based Projective Isotropy== and ==Directed Radial Span== metrics, ensuring well-conditioned intrinsic initialization and strong multi-camera constraints.

## Results

- CO-Calib significantly improved the overall synthetic calibration success rate from **68.1%** to **99.3%** and reduced extrinsic calibration errors (e.g., translation from **0.54 mm** to **0.18 mm**).
- The learning-based detector demonstrated superior performance on synthetic data, achieving detection recall of **0.9255-0.9344** and a mean error of **0.7841-0.8277 px**, outperforming geometry-based methods.
- On a challenging real-world Hex-Fisheye system, CO-Calib achieved a **100%** success rate across **10** trials, maintaining high extrinsic consistency, while baseline methods like Kalibr failed in all trials and Basalt only succeeded **40%** of the time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Calibration failures in multi-fisheye systems primarily originate from the intrinsic initialization stage, especially in configurations with wide fields of view.
> - Simply providing more observations, particularly in peripheral regions, does not guarantee calibration success and can, in fact, exacerbate underlying optimization instabilities if observations are ill-conditioned.
> - The dominant cause of failure is ill-conditioned intrinsic initialization, where a strong coupling between focal scale and fisheye projection-shape parameters arises when observations lack sufficient radial span, making these parameters difficult to disentangle.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-fisheye camera systems, characterized by their large fields of view (FoVs), are increasingly utilized in mobile robotics and data collection platforms to enhance perception capabilities for tasks such as state estimation and depth prediction. Accurate calibration of these systems is a prerequisite for their effective operation. However, robust calibration becomes more challenging with increasing rig size, diversity in camera arrangements, and expanding FoVs. This complexity exacerbates the underlying bundle adjustment (BA) problem, making it larger and more tightly coupled.

Existing multi-fisheye calibration methods, exemplified by toolboxes like Kalibr, typically employ a staged pipeline that progressively initializes and refines camera intrinsics, per-view target poses, and inter-camera extrinsics. Subsequent research has aimed to improve specific aspects, such as enhancing intrinsic estimation under severe distortion (e.g., TartanCalib) or strengthening geometric constraints through richer calibration targets (e.g., MC-Calib). These prior works indicate that calibration performance is critically dependent on the quality and distribution of observations supplied to the solver, rather than solely on the nonlinear optimization algorithm itself.

Despite these advancements, the role of observation quality often remains implicitly understood and empirically managed. Current industrial workflows frequently rely on tailored procedures, specific capture trajectories, and sampling requirements, highlighting an underlying sensitivity that leads to varied outcomes or failures with minor process changes. This paper addresses a gap in the understanding of the fundamental observational conditions necessary for a multi-fisheye calibration problem to be well-conditioned and robustly solvable. It departs from merely improving optimization algorithms or adding more data by systematically investigating *why* calibration fails under certain observational conditions, aiming to provide a principled framework for observation construction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized challenges in reliably calibrating multi-fisheye camera systems, particularly as their complexity (number of cameras, arrangement diversity, and FoV) increases. Current calibration pipelines, while effective under ideal conditions, exhibit sensitivity to the quality and distribution of input observations, leading to inconsistent outcomes or failures in real-world applications. This sensitivity often necessitates extensive empirical tuning and specialized capture procedures, indicating a lack of fundamental understanding regarding the properties of "good" observations.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **To conduct a systematic failure-oriented analysis** of existing multi-fisheye calibration pipelines to identify the dominant factors contributing to calibration failures. This involves evaluating hypotheses beyond common assumptions like detector recall loss or global image-plane distribution imbalance.
2. **To identify specific observation-dependent failure mechanisms**, particularly focusing on how observations influence the conditionality of the intrinsic initialization process in fisheye camera models. The research aims to understand if and how observations with limited radial span affect the separability of focal scale and fisheye projection-shape parameters.
3. **To propose a principled, analysis-guided solution** that explicitly improves observation quality throughout the calibration process without altering the underlying bundle adjustment solver or camera model. This solution, termed CO-Calib, is designed to construct optimization-ready calibration data.
4. **To develop a robust learning-based calibration target detector** capable of accurate and reliable detection under severe fisheye distortion and varied appearance conditions, especially in peripheral regions where traditional geometry-based methods often struggle.
5. **To design an error-analysis-guided frame selector** that organizes detected observations into a sequence conducive to stable and progressive parameter estimation, specifically by prioritizing frames that support well-conditioned intrinsic initialization and strong multi-camera constraints.
6. **To validate the proposed analysis and framework** through extensive experiments on both controlled synthetic and challenging real-world multi-fisheye systems, demonstrating improvements in calibration success rate, accuracy, and stability.

The overarching motivation is to transform multi-fisheye camera calibration from an empirical, often fragile process into a more robust and reliable procedure by understanding and addressing the root causes of failure related to observation quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research consists of two main components: a systematic failure-oriented analysis to identify critical issues in multi-fisheye calibration and the development of CO-Calib, a plug-in framework for robust calibration data construction.

**Failure Mechanism Analysis:**
The analysis was conducted using controlled synthetic datasets designed to systematically evaluate multi-fisheye calibration performance. This involved 16 stereo camera configurations, combining four FoVs (180°, 200°, 220°, 240°) with four relative yaw angles (0°, 60°, 90°, 120°). For each configuration, 100 calibration sequences, each with 480 synchronized frames, were generated. A trial was deemed successful if the pipeline terminated normally with valid parameters and bounded reprojection errors.

The analysis followed a staged approach:
1. **Localization of Calibration Failures:** The study first localized failures within the standard three-stage pipeline (Intrinsic Initialization, Intrinsic Refinement, Full Calibration). It was found that nearly all failures originated from the intrinsic initialization stage, particularly for wide FoV settings.
2. **Hypothesis Testing:** Three observation-related hypotheses were tested:
 - **Insufficient Peripheral Constraints:** This hypothesis posited that severe fisheye distortion reduces target recall near image boundaries, leading to weakly constrained peripheral regions. This was tested by comparing calibration success rates using geometry-based detections against full ground-truth (GT) observations.
 - **Imbalanced Spatial Distribution:** This hypothesis suggested that non-parallel camera arrangements could skew image-plane observations and bias intrinsic estimation. This was evaluated by comparing observation distributions across different stereo configurations and between successful and failed trials within the same configuration using metrics like total observation count difference ($D_{qty}$) and normalized spatial distribution distance ($D_{sp}$), benchmarked against a random-label baseline ($D_{rand}$).
 - **Ill-Conditioned Intrinsic Initialization:** This hypothesis proposed that the strong nonlinearity of fisheye models makes focal scale and projection-shape parameters difficult to disentangle during initialization, leading to ill-conditioned optimization. This was investigated by analyzing the conditioning score ($\gamma_k$) of the normalized Schur complement ($ \tilde{S}_c^{(k)} $) of the camera-parameter block along the intrinsic-initialization path. Additionally, focal–projection coupling ($C_{f\eta}$) was quantified for different fisheye models (Omni, EUCM, Double-Sphere) under various radial profiles (central, middle-radius, edge, radially covered).

**CO-Calib Framework:**
Guided by the insights from the failure analysis, CO-Calib was developed as a plug-in framework with two main components:

1. **Robust Learning-Based Calibration Target Detector:**
 - **Architecture:** A multi-stage architecture utilizing a U-Net-style encoder for multi-level features and a shallow side branch for original-resolution details. It generates rough corner predictions from a 1/4 heatmap, updates them with 1/8 and 1/16 features, and refines them using the shallow branch.
 - **Supervision:** Incorporates auxiliary supervision for visibility estimation, prediction reliability, and cross-slot identity consistency.
 - **Data Generation:** Trained using an online, physically grounded data generation pipeline that synthesizes calibration targets with randomized camera parameters and poses, compositing them with varying backgrounds. This provides scalable and diverse supervision.
 - **Loss Function:** A multi-task objective function combines weighted regression losses for coarse-to-fine localization (rough, updated, refined coordinates), heatmap supervision (positive and negative terms), exclusion loss to reduce cross-slot confusion, and weighted losses for auxiliary heads (visibility, learnability, confidence).

2. **Coverage- and Observability-Aware Selector:**
 This component constructs an optimization-ready frame sequence based on two geometry-aware criteria for each candidate frame:
 - **Point-based Projective Isotropy ($s_{iso}$):** Measures the stability of target pose estimation by computing the ratio of the smallest to largest singular values of the homography's local projective Jacobian at the board center. A higher score indicates less degenerate target projection.
 - **Directed Radial Span ($s_{drs}$):** Quantifies how broadly the target observation covers the radial direction from the image center. It is calculated as the diameter-normalized signed span of valid corners projected onto the primary radial direction of the target. A larger span helps decouple focal scale and projection-shape parameters.

 The selector organizes frames into three stages:
 - **Anchor Selection:** Frames with high $s_{iso}$ and $s_{drs}$ are chosen to provide stable, decoupled observations for intrinsic initialization.
 - **Co-visible Selection:** Timestamp-matched frames that pass per-camera gates (relaxed $s_{iso}$ and $s_{drs}$ thresholds) in both cameras are selected to provide reliable multi-camera constraints for stereo extrinsic estimation.
 - **Mono-fill:** Additional monocular frames are added to cover weakly observed image regions, further relaxing the selection criteria, to complete coverage for refinement without overwhelming initialization with low-quality data.

This approach ensures that observations supplied to the existing calibration workflow are geometrically sound and provide well-conditioned updates during the optimization process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding calibration failure mechanisms and demonstrated the performance of the proposed CO-Calib framework.

**Findings from Failure Mechanism Analysis:**
- **Localization of Failures:** A systematic analysis revealed that nearly all calibration failures, particularly those occurring in wide FoV configurations (e.g., 67% failure rate at 220° FoV with 0° stereo, 88% at 240° FoV with 0° stereo), originated from the intrinsic initialization stage of the pipeline. Failures were rare at narrower FoVs but increased sharply for wider FoV settings.
- **Role of Peripheral Constraints and Detector Recall:** The hypothesis that insufficient peripheral constraints due to detector recall loss is the primary cause of failure was not supported. Replacing detected points with full ground-truth observations actually *decreased* the overall success rate from 68.1% to 53.7%, and made most wide-FoV settings less stable. This indicated that simply having more observations, especially in peripheral regions, does not guarantee success and can expose underlying optimization instabilities.
- **Role of Spatial Distribution:** The analysis also disproved that imbalanced spatial distribution of observations is the dominant failure factor. Differences in observation patterns across stereo configurations or between successful and failed trials within the same configuration did not correlate with failure rates. Quantitative metrics ($D_{qty}$ for point count, $D_{sp}$ for spatial distribution) showed negligible differences between successful and failed cases, suggesting that neither observation count nor image-plane distribution alone explains initialization failures.
- **Dominant Failure Factor: Ill-Conditioned Intrinsic Initialization:** The study identified that failed cases consistently exhibited higher conditioning scores ($\gamma_k$) of the normalized pose-free camera-parameter block. This indicated weaker and more ambiguous intrinsic constraints, pointing to poor local parameter separability. Specifically, a strong coupling between focal scale and fisheye projection-shape parameters was identified. This coupling becomes pronounced when observations occupy only a narrow radial range, causing similar residual changes for different parameter updates and leading to ill-conditioned optimization. Analyses across various fisheye models (Omni, EUCM, Double-Sphere) confirmed that observations with broad radial coverage significantly reduce this focal–projection coupling, improving parameter separability.

**Performance of CO-Calib:**
- **Robust Calibration Target Detection:** The proposed learning-based detector demonstrated superior performance compared to geometry-based methods. On synthetic data, it consistently improved detection recall (e.g., 0.9255-0.9344 vs. 0.6838-0.7085 for geometry-based) and achieved lower mean detection error (e.g., 0.7841-0.8277 px vs. 0.8304-0.8553 px). This indicates its robustness in distorted fisheye images.
- **Synthetic Calibration Performance:** CO-Calib significantly improved calibration robustness and accuracy on the synthetic benchmark across 16 configurations.
 - **Success Rate:** The overall success rate increased from 68.1% (with Kalibr) to 99.3% (with CO-Calib). For challenging wide-FoV configurations (e.g., 240° FoV), Kalibr's success rate was as low as 12-41%, while CO-Calib achieved 98-100%.
 - **Extrinsic Accuracy:** CO-Calib reduced the overall extrinsic calibration error from 0.54 mm / 0.029 deg (Kalibr) to 0.18 mm / 0.021 deg (CO-Calib) in translation and rotation, respectively. While CO-Calib showed slightly higher Average Trajectory Error (ATE) for some configurations (e.g., FoV180), this was attributed to using fewer frames for optimization in the selection process, prioritizing geometric constraint quality over raw frame count.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Selector Effectiveness:** Replacing CO-Calib's selection strategy with random subsets of the same size resulted in a drastic drop in overall success rate from 99.3% to 30.9%. This confirmed that the improvement is due to the *quality* and *structure* of selected frames, not merely a reduction in frame count.
 - **Initialization Importance:** Bypassing the intrinsic initialization stage (using a BA-only two-stage pipeline) severely reduced the overall success rate to 13.5%. This underscores the critical role of a reliable, well-conditioned initialization path for robust calibration.
- **Real-World Calibration Stability:** On real-world multi-fisheye systems, CO-Calib demonstrated robust and consistent performance.
 - For standard stereo configurations, it achieved extrinsic consistency (standard deviation of baseline length and relative rotation) comparable to Kalibr.
 - Crucially, for a challenging Hex-Fisheye system, Kalibr failed on all 10 trials, while CO-Calib succeeded on all 10 trials, maintaining high extrinsic consistency (0.97 mm / 0.048 deg). Basalt, another baseline, also showed poor performance on this system (4/10 success rate, high extrinsic std. dev.).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of multi-camera system calibration by shifting the focus from simply increasing observation quantity or refining optimization algorithms to a deeper understanding of observation quality in terms of parameter separability. The systematic failure-oriented analysis provides a foundational insight: calibration failures in multi-fisheye systems are predominantly caused by ill-conditioned intrinsic initialization, specifically the coupling between focal scale and projection-shape parameters when observations lack sufficient radial span. This redefines "observation quality" to include the capacity of observations to provide separable and stable parameter-update directions, rather than just high recall or uniform spatial coverage.

The proposed CO-Calib framework offers a practical and immediate impact. As a plug-in solution, it enhances existing calibration pipelines without requiring modifications to camera models or bundle adjustment backends. Its key components—a robust learning-based target detector and an error-analysis-guided frame selector—address the identified failure mechanisms by providing reliable detections and constructing optimization-ready observation sets.

The significance and potential impact include:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** By dramatically increasing the calibration success rate (from 68.1% to 99.3% in synthetic tests) and improving extrinsic accuracy, CO-Calib can make multi-fisheye system deployment more reliable across diverse configurations and FoVs. This reduces the likelihood of calibration failures that currently plague complex systems.
2. **Reduced Empirical Effort:** The framework's ability to automatically construct high-quality observation sets, guided by geometric principles, can minimize the need for manual, empirical capture rules and tailored procedures. This simplifies the calibration workflow and reduces repeated engineering effort in industrial and research settings.
3. **Improved Performance in Challenging Scenarios:** The demonstrated success on challenging real-world systems, such as the Hex-Fisheye rig where existing methods failed, indicates that CO-Calib can enable the reliable calibration of more complex and diverse multi-camera platforms, which are critical for advanced robotic navigation, autonomous driving, and 3D mapping applications.
4. **Guidance for Data Collection Strategies:** The insights derived from the failure analysis provide clear guidance for future data collection strategies. Calibrators can prioritize collecting frames that exhibit broad radial coverage and projective isotropy, rather than merely maximizing the number of detected points or attempting uniform image-plane coverage.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a quantitative framework for analyzing the conditionality of calibration problems based on observation properties. This could serve as a basis for developing more sophisticated, adaptive calibration techniques that dynamically assess observation quality and guide data acquisition in real-time.

In summary, this research provides both a theoretical explanation for critical calibration failures and a practical, validated solution, thereby contributing to the advancement and broader applicability of multi-fisheye camera systems.
