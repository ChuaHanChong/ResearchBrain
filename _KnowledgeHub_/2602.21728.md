# Explore-on-Graph: Incentivizing Autonomous Exploration of Large Language Models on Knowledge Graphs with Path-refined Reward Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> A research collaboration introduces Explore-on-Graph (EoG), a framework that enables large language models (LLMs) to autonomously navigate and reason over knowledge graphs (KGs). The method employs a two-stage training process, incorporating path-refined reinforcement learning to achieve state-of-the-art performance and improved generalizability on complex KG question-answering tasks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently generate factual inaccuracies and hallucinations when applied to knowledge-intensive tasks, undermining their reliability.
- Existing Knowledge Graph (KG)-enhanced LLM reasoning methods struggle to adapt and generalize to novel or out-of-distribution reasoning patterns within complex graph structures.
- Current approaches are predominantly passive, confining LLMs to either predefined rules or previously observed reasoning pathways, limiting their exploratory capacity.

## Method

- The **Explore-on-Graph** (EoG) framework implements a two-stage training regimen: ==Supervised Fine-Tuning== (SFT) to establish foundational reasoning and Reinforcement Learning (RL) to cultivate autonomous exploration.
- The RL stage utilizes Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and introduces a 'path-refined reward modeling' strategy, which combines an outcome-based F1 score for answers with a path-based reward derived from ground-truth reasoning triplets.
- A 'Search-and-Verify' pipeline constructs high-quality ground-truth reasoning paths for reward calculation, employing Breadth-First Search (BFS) combined with LLM-based semantic verification to filter out irrelevant paths.

## Results

- EoG achieved state-of-the-art performance across five diverse KGQA benchmarks (WebQSP, CWQ, GrailQA, QALD10-en, 2WikiMultihop), surpassing existing open-source methods and powerful closed-source models like GPT-5 and Gemini **2.5** Pro.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Reinforcement Learning stage and the path-refined reward modeling are crucial for EoG's robust exploration capabilities and superior performance.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced reasoning quality, superior performance on complex reasoning scenarios (e.g., multi-hop queries), and strong generalizability in out-of-distribution settings, validating its reinforced exploration strategy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous exploration, driven by a reinforcement learning paradigm, is essential for LLMs to effectively generalize their reasoning capabilities to unseen and out-of-distribution graph structures.
> - Integrating a path-specific reward alongside an outcome-based reward within the RL process significantly enhances the efficiency and semantic coherence of the LLM's exploratory search for reasoning paths.
> - A foundational Supervised Fine-Tuning stage is critical to equip LLMs with structured reasoning abilities, serving as an effective 'cold start' for the subsequent and more complex reinforcement learning for exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements of Large Language Models (LLMs) have brought impressive capabilities across various Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks. However, a significant and persistent challenge remains their susceptibility to hallucinations and factual inaccuracies, particularly in knowledge-intensive tasks like question answering (QA). This limitation severely impacts their reliability in real-world applications.

To address these issues, the research community has widely adopted the strategy of grounding LLMs in external, verifiable knowledge sources. Among these, Knowledge Graphs (KGs) have emerged as an ideal candidate, providing structured factual information to guide and validate LLM reasoning. Existing KG-enhanced reasoning methods generally fall into two broad paradigms:
1. **Rule-based methods:** These approaches enforce pre-defined rules or structural constraints during LLM generation to ensure logical consistency and faithfulness to the KG (e.g., ToG by Sun et al., 2023; DoG by Li et al., 2025). While effective in maintaining factuality, they are inherently limited by the scope of these pre-defined rules and often struggle to generalize to novel reasoning patterns not explicitly covered.
2. **Imitation-based methods:** These methods involve fine-tuning LLMs on extensive datasets of demonstrations to mimic established reasoning patterns and heuristics (e.g., KG-Agent by Jiang et al., 2025; RoG by Luo et al., 2024). While enhancing intrinsic reasoning, their performance is typically confined to the distribution of the training data, limiting their generalizability to out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) graph reasoning problems.

The critical gap identified by this paper is the failure of these prevailing methods to generalize effectively to complex graphs, especially when faced with reasoning patterns that deviate from the training distribution or pre-defined rules. The current approaches are largely passive, restricting LLMs to previously seen or explicitly defined pathways. The "EXPLORE-ON-GRAPH" (EoG) framework directly tackles this limitation by shifting the paradigm from passive constraint or imitation to active, **autonomous exploration** of the knowledge graph. This conceptual leap is crucial for enhancing generalizability.

The work leverages recent advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) applied to LLMs for reasoning, drawing inspiration from models like Deepseek-R1 (Shao etal., 2024). By framing KG reasoning as an exploration problem, EoG positions itself at the forefront of developing more adaptive and robust LLM-based reasoning systems, addressing a core challenge in making LLMs truly reliable and intelligent agents in knowledge-rich environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the Explore-on-Graph (EoG) framework stems from a critical limitation observed in current Knowledge Graph (KG)-enhanced Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning methods: their inability to generalize effectively to novel or out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) reasoning patterns. Existing rule-based or imitation-based approaches, by design, constrain LLMs to either follow explicit rules or replicate patterns found in their training data. This inherent confinement severely limits their adaptability when confronted with complex, unfamiliar structures or relationships within KGs, leading to hallucinations and missing facts in question-answering tasks.

The authors highlight that for LLMs to become truly robust reasoners, they must possess the complementary capability of autonomously venturing into unexplored regions of a graph to discover novel, yet correct, reasoning paths. This capability is essential for overcoming the "knowledge gap" and "hallucination" issues that plague LLMs when applied to real-world, dynamic KGs.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the EoG framework are:
- **To incentivize autonomous exploration:** Develop a mechanism that encourages LLMs to actively navigate and search for diverse reasoning paths within Knowledge Graphs, rather than passively adhering to pre-defined rules or imitating fixed demonstrations. This is intended to broaden the LLM's reasoning space.
- **To introduce reinforcement learning for exploration:** Integrate reinforcement learning (RL) into the LLM training process, using the correctness of the reasoning paths' final answers as a reward signal. This feedback mechanism is designed to stimulate and refine the LLM's exploratory capabilities.
- **To enhance exploration efficiency and meaningfulness:** Propose a novel "path-refined reward modeling" approach. This involves incorporating rich path information as additional reward signals to guide the RL process, aiming to reduce futile exploration efforts and steer the LLM towards semantically meaningful and logically coherent paths.
- **To improve generalizability:** Enable LLMs to better generalize to out-of-distribution graph reasoning problems, thereby improving their robustness and accuracy on complex, unseen questions and graph structures.
- **To achieve state-of-the-art performance:** Empirically demonstrate that the EoG framework, by fostering autonomous and efficient exploration, can significantly outperform existing KG-enhanced methods, including powerful closed-source LLMs, on standard KGQA benchmark datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Explore-on-Graph (EoG) framework introduces a novel two-stage training approach designed to imbue Large Language Models (LLMs) with autonomous exploration capabilities on Knowledge Graphs (KGs). The two stages are Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

**1. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Stage:**
The SFT stage serves as a "cold start" mechanism, providing the LLM with foundational knowledge and structured reasoning abilities crucial for subsequent exploration. Direct RL on KGs is challenging due to large action spaces and sparse rewards.
- **Long CoT Dataset Construction:** The authors construct a high-quality "long Chain-of-Thought (CoT)" reasoning dataset. This involves:
 - Designing a specific prompt (Figure 9 in Appendix F) that instructs a powerful LLM (Gemini 2.5 Flash) to generate structured, step-by-step reasoning processes (`<think>` tags) and final answers (`<answer>` tags) based on a given question, starting entity, and knowledge graph.
 - Implementing additional rules to filter these generated reasoning processes, retaining only those that are both structurally sound and factually correct according to the KG.
- **SFT Process:** Standard language modeling is applied, optimizing the LLM's parameters (θ) to predict tokens in the generated reasoning processes (`L_SFT`, Eq. 1). This phase establishes a strong baseline for structured reasoning, preparing the model to effectively leverage environmental feedback in the next stage.

**2. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Stage:**
The RL stage is designed to transcend the limitations of SFT data, fostering autonomous exploration and discovery of novel reasoning paths. This stage comprises two phases, progressively refining the reward signal.
- **Phase 1: Reinforcement Learning with Outcome Reward:**
 - **Objective:** Optimize the LLM's policy (`π_θ`) to improve the discovery of correct answers through exploration.
 - **Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which is a variant of policy optimization methods inspired by recent advancements in LLM-RL like Deepseek-R1. GRPO optimizes the policy by comparing the likelihood of generating sampled paths under the new policy versus an older policy, weighted by a normalized advantage (Eq. 2).
 - **Reward Function (`R_outcome(p)`):** The primary reward is the entity-level F1 score (Eq. 3) calculated on the predicted final answers within the `<answer>` tags of a generated exploration sequence (`p`), against the ground-truth answers. This reward implicitly encourages correct formatting and accuracy of the final answer.
- **Phase 2: Reinforcement Learning with Path-refined Reward:**
 - **Motivation:** To improve the efficiency and semantic meaningfulness of exploration, the authors introduce path-specific information as an auxiliary reward.
 - **Ground-truth Path Construction:** For datasets that do not provide explicit reasoning paths, a "Search-and-Verify" pipeline is employed:
 1. **Search:** Breadth-First Search (BFS) is performed on the KG to find all potential paths between topic entities (from the question) and ground-truth answer entities within a maximum hop constraint.
 2. **Verify:** An LLM (Gemini-2.5-Flash) semantically verifies these BFS-generated paths to filter out topologically connected but logically irrelevant "spurious paths."
 - **Path-based Reward (`R_path(p)`):** This reward (Eq. 4) quantifies the proportion of ground-truth reasoning triplets successfully identified and articulated within the LLM's generated `think` process. It directly encourages the model to follow semantically correct and coherent reasoning trajectories.
 - **Joint Reward (`R_joint(p)`):** The final reward for RL combines both outcome and path rewards (Eq. 5): `R_outcome(p) + α * R_path(p)`. The hyperparameter `α` controls the relative influence of the path reward. This joint reward is designed to incentivize the LLM to generate both accurate final answers and high-quality, efficient reasoning paths.

**Implementation Details:**
- EoG is applied to open-source LLMs: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.
- Experiments are conducted on five diverse KGQA benchmarks: CWQ, WebQSP, GrailQA (Freebase-based), and QALD10-en, 2WikiMultihop (Wikidata-based).
- Evaluation metrics are Hit@1 and F1 score.

This two-stage approach effectively addresses the LLM cold-start problem and leverages sophisticated reward modeling to guide autonomous exploration, making the reasoning process on KGs both more generalized and efficient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation of Explore-on-Graph (EoG) across five diverse Knowledge Graph Question Answering (KGQA) benchmark datasets yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the framework's effectiveness and superiority.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** EoG consistently achieved state-of-the-art results on all five benchmark datasets (WebQSP, CWQ, GrailQA, QALD10-en, 2WikiMultihop). Crucially, EoG not only outperformed existing open-source KG-enhanced reasoning methods (e.g., GCR, DoG, KG-Agent) but also surpassed the performance of powerful closed-source models such as Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT-5, as evidenced in Table 1. This highlights the remarkable efficacy of EoG's autonomous exploration capabilities.

- **Critical Role of Reinforcement Learning:** An ablation study comparing the full EoG model with `EoG_SFT` (the model trained only with Supervised Fine-Tuning) showed a significant performance drop for `EoG_SFT` across all datasets (Table 1). This observation strongly indicates that the robust exploration capabilities of EoG are primarily attributable to the Reinforcement Learning (RL) stage, rather than solely to knowledge distillation from the teacher LLM during SFT.

- **Contribution of Path-Refined Reward Modeling:** The ablation study (Table 2) further validated the importance of the individual components of the reward function:
 - **Path Reward:** Removing the path reward (`w/o path reward`) led to a notable decrease in performance (e.g., F1 dropped from 73.9 to 70.8 on CWQ). This confirms that the path-refined reward effectively enhances exploration by promoting semantically meaningful reasoning paths.
 - **Outcome Reward:** Eliminating the outcome reward (`w/o outcome reward`) resulted in a drastic performance reduction, emphasizing the necessity of accurately evaluating final answers (via F1 score) to guide policy optimization.
 - **SFT Necessity:** Training EoG solely with RL (`w/o SFT`) yielded suboptimal performance, even with In-Context Learning (ICL). This confirms that the SFT stage is indispensable for providing a foundational, structured reasoning capability, addressing the "cold start" problem for subsequent RL.

- **Optimal Reward Balance:** Experiments on the ratio (α) between outcome and path rewards (Figure 3) demonstrated that there is an optimal balance. Both excessively low or high `α` values led to performance degradation. This suggests that a balanced incentive—encouraging both correct final answers and coherent reasoning paths—is crucial for effective exploration.

- **Efficiency and Meaningfulness of Exploration:** Analysis of the impact of path reward (Table 3) showed that its inclusion led to more efficient and meaningful outputs. EoG generated shorter average output lengths while maintaining higher scores for comprehensiveness, relevance, and overall exploration behavior compared to the model without path reward.

- **Enhanced Reasoning Quality:** A multi-dimensional evaluation using GPT-4o-mini as a judge (Section 4.4, Figure 4) revealed that EoG significantly improved reasoning quality across six dimensions, particularly in reasoning depth, exploration, and comprehensiveness. This indicates that the two-stage RL method substantially enhances the model's exploratory policy.

- **Superiority in Complex Reasoning:** EoG demonstrated superior performance on complex reasoning scenarios, including questions involving conjunctions, comparatives, superlatives, and multiple hops (≥ 3 hops), as detailed in Table 4. This strongly supports the hypothesis that autonomous exploration enables the model to effectively understand and navigate diverse and deeper logical patterns.

- **Robust Out-of-Distribution (O.O.D.) Generalization:** Performance evaluations in O.O.D. settings (Figure 5) showed that EoG consistently outperformed `EoG_SFT` and achieved higher O.O.D.-to-I.I.D. ratios. This illustrates EoG's strong robustness and cross-domain generalizability, a direct outcome of its reinforced exploration strategy.

- **Case Study Validation:** A detailed case study (Figure 6) effectively illustrated how EoG could discover novel and previously unseen reasoning patterns by actively exploring unknown entities, a capability that traditional methods like GCR lacked.

These findings collectively underscore the effectiveness and innovative nature of the EoG framework in addressing the critical challenge of generalizability for LLMs in KGQA tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Explore-on-Graph (EoG) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Knowledge Graph Question Answering (KGQA) and, more broadly, in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Breakthrough in LLM Generalizability:** The most profound impact of EoG is its ability to enable LLMs to generalize to out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) reasoning patterns on KGs. This directly addresses a fundamental limitation of previous rule-based and imitation-based methods, which are inherently confined by prior experience. By empowering LLMs with autonomous exploration, EoG allows them to discover novel, correct reasoning paths beyond their initial training distribution, making them far more robust and adaptable to evolving and complex knowledge environments.

- **Reduced Hallucinations and Enhanced Factuality:** Grounding LLM reasoning in verifiable external knowledge sources like KGs is crucial for mitigating hallucinations. EoG takes this a step further by actively searching for and verifying paths within the KG, significantly boosting the factuality and trustworthiness of LLM-generated answers. This is a critical step towards building more reliable AI systems.

- **Efficient and Interpretable Reasoning:** The proposed path-refined reward modeling is a clever innovation that guides the LLM's exploration process. By incentivizing the discovery of semantically meaningful and logically coherent reasoning paths, EoG not only improves exploration efficiency but also enhances the interpretability of the LLM's decision-making. Users can better understand *how* an LLM arrived at an answer by examining the discovered path, fostering greater trust in AI-driven insights.

- **New State-of-the-Art in KGQA:** EoG's achievement of state-of-the-art performance, surpassing even powerful closed-source LLMs like GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro, firmly establishes it as a leading approach in KGQA. This benchmark achievement sets a new standard for future research in this domain.

- **Broad Applicability and Scalability:** The framework's demonstrated effectiveness across diverse KGQA datasets (Freebase and Wikidata-based) and its compatibility with different open-source LLM architectures (Qwen, Llama) suggest wide applicability. The efficient Reinforcement Learning (RL) training strategy, highlighted by relatively low computational costs (Appendix I), indicates that EoG is not just theoretically sound but also practically scalable.

- **Catalyst for Future Research:** EoG opens up several promising avenues for future research:
 - **More Sophisticated Reward Strategies:** Exploring advanced graph-structure-oriented reward functions (while acknowledging the current paper's findings on GED complexity) could further refine exploration.
 - **Dynamic Knowledge Graph Interaction:** The autonomous exploration paradigm could be extended to dynamic KGs where information is constantly updated, enabling LLMs to adapt in real-time.
 - **Knowledge Discovery and Completion:** The core mechanism of path discovery could be adapted for automated knowledge graph completion, identifying missing links or inferring new relations.
 - **Beyond QA:** The principles of autonomous, RL-driven exploration on structured knowledge could be transferred to other knowledge-intensive tasks, such as complex planning, scientific discovery, or even creative problem-solving, where navigating vast information spaces is key.

In conclusion, Explore-on-Graph represents a significant leap forward in empowering LLMs with more intelligent, autonomous, and verifiable reasoning capabilities over structured knowledge. Its potential impact extends from improving the reliability of factual question-answering systems to laying a foundational stone for truly adaptive and explorative AI agents.
