# SCOPED: Score-Curvature Out-of-distribution Proximity Evaluator for Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper, SCOPED, introduces a computationally efficient method for out-of-distribution (OOD) detection in diffusion models by leveraging the information-theoretic relationship between the score function's squared norm and the local curvature of the log-probability density. It achieves competitive detection accuracy across vision and reinforcement learning tasks with an order of magnitude fewer model evaluations than prior diffusion-based approaches.

## Problem

- Modern neural networks often exhibit high confidence in predictions for inputs outside their training distribution, posing safety and reliability risks in real-world applications.
- Existing generative likelihood-based and reconstruction-based OOD detection methods suffer from reliability issues (e.g., assigning higher likelihood to OOD data) or sensitivity to specific architectural choices.
- Prior diffusion-based OOD detection methods, while promising, are computationally expensive, requiring many model evaluations (10-1000 forward passes) which limits their practicality for real-time or resource-constrained applications.

## Method

- **SCOPED** introduces a "==score-curvature ratio==" test statistic, derived from information geometry, relating the squared norm of the score function to the local curvature of the log-probability density.
- It leverages pre-trained score-based diffusion models to estimate both the score function and its Jacobian trace (curvature) using a single-point evaluation at specific noise levels, employing Hutchinson's stochastic trace estimator for efficiency.
- An unsupervised calibration procedure fits a Kernel Density Estimate (KDE) to the ID distribution of this statistic, transforming it into a robust anomaly score that requires only in-distribution data.

## Results

- SCOPED achieved competitive or state-of-the-art AUROC scores across various vision benchmarks (e.g., average AUROC **0.892** for two-step variant) and perfect separation (AUROC 1.0) on DeepMind Control Suite shifts in RL tasks.
- It demonstrated an order of magnitude reduction in computational cost, requiring only 2-4 effective forward passes (equivalent to 1-2 score network evaluations plus Jacobian-vector products), compared to 10-10,000 passes for prior diffusion-based methods.
- The method proved robust to noise level selection, with a two-step approach (combining early and mid-level timesteps) consistently yielding high AUROC without requiring specific OOD data for tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The ratio of the squared score norm to local curvature provides a theoretically grounded and reliable signal for OOD detection, expected to be near 1 for in-distribution samples and diverge for OOD inputs.
> - Diffusion models, by directly learning the score function, enable efficient computation of both the score and its curvature, facilitating OOD detection with minimal additional computational overhead.
> - Evaluating the diffusion model at just a few strategic noise levels, rather than along full denoising paths, drastically reduces computational cost while retaining sufficient information for accurate OOD detection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the critical challenge of Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection in machine learning systems. OOD detection is fundamental for the reliable and safe deployment of models in various domains, including computer vision, robotics, and reinforcement learning, as modern neural networks can exhibit high confidence in predictions for abnormal or irrelevant inputs.

The broader research landscape for OOD detection encompasses various approaches:
- **Confidence score-based methods**: These assess the confidence of a model's prediction, with low confidence often indicating an OOD input.
- **Generative likelihood-based methods**: These models attempt to learn the distribution of in-distribution (ID) data and detect OOD inputs as those with low likelihood. However, this category suffers from documented pathologies, such as sometimes assigning higher likelihoods to OOD datasets than to training data, and the issue of typicality where the highest density points may be atypical in high dimensions.
- **Reconstruction-based methods**: Approaches using autoencoders or diffusion reconstructions identify OOD inputs based on their poor reconstruction quality. These methods often depend on specific architectural choices and can be brittle.
- **Diffusion-based methods**: More recently, diffusion models have been employed for OOD detection, leveraging their ability to model complex data distributions. Existing diffusion-based OOD methods, such as those relying on denoising paths or multiple model evaluations (e.g., DiffPath, MSMA, DDPM-OOD, LMD), often incur high computational costs due to the necessity of evaluating the model along entire denoising trajectories or performing a large number of forward passes. This computational burden presents a significant barrier to real-time and compute-limited applications.

SCOPED positions itself within the diffusion-based OOD detection paradigm but aims to overcome the computational limitations of prior methods by introducing a more efficient and theoretically grounded approach. It leverages the inherent properties of score-based diffusion models to extract OOD signals with significantly fewer model evaluations, addressing a key practical challenge in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a fast, general-purpose, and computationally efficient OOD detection method for diffusion models. The authors aim to introduce a method that requires substantially fewer model evaluations compared to existing diffusion-based approaches while maintaining or improving detection accuracy.

The motivation for SCOPED stems from several observations regarding current OOD detection techniques:
1. **Reliability and Safety Concerns**: Machine learning systems, especially deep neural networks, are prone to making high-confidence predictions on inputs that deviate significantly from their training distribution. This lack of robustness poses safety and reliability risks in real-world applications across various domains, necessitating effective OOD detection.
2. **Limitations of Existing Generative Approaches**:
 - **Likelihood-based methods**: As noted by prior work (e.g., Nalisnick et al., 2018), generative models can assign higher likelihoods to OOD data than to ID data. Furthermore, the concept of "typicality" in high dimensions suggests that points of highest density might not be typical, leading to misinterpretations of likelihoods.
 - **Reconstruction-based methods**: These are often sensitive to design choices, such as the information bottleneck in autoencoders, and can be unreliable in practice.
3. **Computational Inefficiency of Diffusion-based Methods**: Recent diffusion-based OOD detection methods, while showing promise, often demand extensive computational resources. They typically require numerous evaluations of the diffusion model along denoising paths (e.g., 10-1000 forward passes) to compute OOD statistics. This serial nature of trajectory integration makes them impractical for real-time applications or environments with limited computational budgets.
4. **Theoretical Gaps**: The authors observe a connection between the local curvature of the log-probability density and the norm of the score function in information geometry, particularly near the typical set of a distribution. They hypothesize that this relationship, captured by a "score-curvature ratio," could serve as a reliable OOD signal, given that diffusion models directly learn the score function.

To address these motivations, SCOPED aims to:
- Exploit information-theoretic principles (typicality, score-curvature relationship) to derive a novel OOD statistic.
- Dramatically reduce the computational cost of diffusion-based OOD detection by requiring fewer, and parallelizable, model evaluations.
- Provide a general-purpose method applicable to both vision and reinforcement learning domains, validating its utility beyond traditional image benchmarks.
- Develop an unsupervised calibration procedure that only requires in-distribution data, enhancing applicability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SCOPED, the Score–Curvature Out-of-distribution Proximity Evaluator for Diffusion, is built upon a foundation of information geometry and the inherent properties of score-based generative models.

#### 3.1. Core Theoretical Foundation: Score–Curvature Ratio

The central idea of SCOPED is based on the relationship between the squared norm of the score function and the local curvature of the log-probability density.
- The **score function** is defined as $s(x) := -\nabla_x \log p(x)$.
- The **local curvature** is defined as $\kappa(x) := \text{Tr}(\nabla_x s(x)) = -\text{Tr}(\nabla_x^2 \log p(x))$.
For in-distribution samples drawn from $p(x)$, classical results from information theory state that the expected score magnitude (measured by the squared norm) matches the expected curvature.
The method defines a test statistic $T(x) = \frac{\lVert s(x) \rVert^2}{\kappa(x)}$ (with a small $\epsilon$ added to the denominator for numerical stability). The intuition is that for high-dimensional distributions, in-distribution samples will lie on a "typical set" where this ratio $T(x)$ is expected to be close to 1. Samples deviating significantly from this typical set, i.e., OOD samples, are hypothesized to yield $T(x)$ values that substantially diverge from 1. A global sign factor, derived from the sum of elements of the score function, is applied to $T(x)$ to correct for potential sign ambiguities, as the numerator (squared norm) discards orientation while the denominator (Jacobian trace) retains sign information.

#### 3.2. Leveraging Diffusion Models

Diffusion models (DDPMs and EDMs) are designed to learn the score function $s_\theta(x_t, t) \approx \nabla_x \log p_t(x_t)$ of progressively noised data $x_t$. This direct access to the score function is critical for SCOPED, as it allows for the computation of both the score $s(x)$ and its Jacobian trace $\kappa(x)$ from a single trained diffusion model at a chosen noise level $t$.

#### 3.3. Computational Efficiency

SCOPED introduces several mechanisms to achieve computational efficiency:
1. **Single-Point Evaluation**: Unlike path-based diffusion methods (e.g., DiffPath) that require propagating samples through the denoising ODE for many steps, SCOPED probes the diffusion model at specific, chosen noise levels without reconstructing the full denoising path. Each statistic is computed from a single forward evaluation of the score network and one Jacobian-vector product (JVP) at a chosen timestep. This reduces the number of function evaluations (NFEs) by an order of magnitude compared to prior methods.
2. **Parallelization**: Because SCOPED evaluations at different timesteps or for different samples are independent, they can be fully parallelized on modern hardware, leading to significant wall-clock time reductions beyond what NFE counts alone suggest.
3. **Efficient Curvature Estimation**: Naively computing the Jacobian trace $\text{Tr}(\nabla_x s(x))$ is computationally prohibitive in high dimensions. SCOPED employs Hutchinson's stochastic trace estimator, which provides an unbiased estimate of the trace with a cost linear in the dimension. This estimator requires only a few JVPs with the score network, avoiding explicit Jacobian matrix formation and keeping the cost comparable to one additional forward pass of the score network per probe.

#### 3.4. OOD Detection with Kernel Density Estimation (KDE)

The raw $T(x)$ values are not universally calibrated across datasets or models. To transform $T(x)$ into a reliable OOD detector, an unsupervised calibration procedure is used:
- **ID Data Calibration**: During the offline phase, SCOPED computes $T(x)$ for a set of in-distribution (ID) samples.
- **KDE Fitting**: A Kernel Density Estimate (KDE) is then fitted to these ID $T(x)$ values, creating a smooth, non-parametric estimate of the ID distribution of the test statistic.
- **Anomaly Score**: For any new test input, its computed $T(x)$ value is evaluated under this fitted KDE. The negative log-likelihood ($-\log h(T(x))$, where $h$ is the KDE) serves as the anomaly score, with higher values indicating a higher likelihood of being OOD.

#### 3.5. Offline Selection of Noise Levels

The choice of noise level $t$ is crucial for balancing signal retention and noise.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Tasks**: For proprioceptive RL, a single noise level is chosen corresponding to the mode of the log-normal prior over noise scales. This point is typically in the early-to-middle part of the schedule, suppressing fine detail while preserving coarse structure.
- **Vision Tasks**: For vision experiments, SCOPED evaluates at two specific timesteps: an early step ($t=1$) to capture fine details, and a mid-level step ($t=300$) where approximately 95% of the signal remains and coarse structure is preserved. These points are selected based on an offline analysis of the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) curve of the in-distribution data. The maximum of the negative log-likelihoods from these two timesteps is taken as the final anomaly score. This multi-timestep approach enhances robustness without tuning on OOD data.
The paper also defines:
- **SCOPED (Single)**: Uses a single noise level ($t=300$).
- **SCOPED (Oracle)**: An upper bound, assuming knowledge of the best-performing timestep for each ID/OOD pair.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Reinforcement Learning Case Studies

The research first validates SCOPED on reinforcement learning (RL) tasks, which present a unique challenge due to the ambiguous definition of "in-distribution."
- **DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) Tasks**: SCOPED was evaluated on scenarios involving shifts in reward functions (e.g., `reacher-easy` vs. `reacher-hard`, `humanoid-stand` vs. `humanoid-walk`) and independently trained policies (e.g., `reacher-hard` seed A vs. seed B). The method achieved perfect separation with an AUROC score of 1.0 across all tested DMC shifts. These results serve as a sanity check, confirming SCOPED's ability to capture distributional divergences even in cases with overlapping state-action spaces or subtle differences.
- **D4RL Gym Benchmarks**: For the D4RL benchmarks, which feature datasets collected from different behavior policies (random, medium, expert, medium-replay), SCOPED showed nuanced performance:
 - In `hopper-v2` and `walker2d-v2`, SCOPED achieved near-perfect AUROC for most ID/OOD pairs.
 - In `ant-v2`, the task was more challenging, especially when distinguishing `medium` vs. `expert` or `medium-replay` policies.
 - A key observation was that training the diffusion model on the most diverse dataset (`medium-replay`) did not consistently lead to the strongest detector. It underperformed in `Hopper` and `Walker` and only conditionally helped in `Ant`. This finding suggests that while diversity might seem beneficial, the specific nature of RL datasets (e.g., termination conditions, noisy replay mixtures) can lead to entangled distributions that are less separable. Expert policies, generating broad but coherent coverage, often resulted in sharper boundaries and stronger detectors.

#### 4.2. Main Vision-Based Results

For vision tasks, SCOPED utilized an unconditional DDPM trained on CelebA as the backbone model, ensuring direct comparability with strong baselines. The performance was benchmarked against various generative and diffusion-based OOD methods across CIFAR-10, SVHN, CelebA, and CIFAR-100 datasets, using AUROC as the primary metric.

- **Competitive or State-of-the-Art Accuracy**: SCOPED achieved competitive or state-of-the-art AUROC scores across the vision benchmarks. For example, for "CelebA vs C100," SCOPED achieved 0.962, compared to 0.998 for DiffPath, while for "SVHN vs C100," SCOPED achieved 0.971 with SCOPED (Single) and SCOPED (Oracle), matching DiffPath's 0.998 in that specific setup. The average AUROC for SCOPED (Two-step) was 0.892, and for SCOPED (Single) was 0.884, closely approaching the strongest baseline DiffPath (0.918) and exceeding many others.
- **Significant Computational Efficiency**: A central finding is SCOPED's drastic reduction in computational cost.
 - SCOPED (Two-step) required 2F+2J (2 forward passes + 2 Jacobian-vector products), approximately equivalent to 4 effective forward passes.
 - SCOPED (Single) required 1F+1J (approximately 2 effective forward passes).
 This represents an order of magnitude fewer effective function evaluations compared to many prior diffusion-based methods, some of which required 10F (MSMA, DiffPath), 350F (DDPM-OOD), 1000F (NLL, IC), or even $10^4$F (LMD). The authors emphasize that SCOPED's independence of evaluations allows for full parallelization, further reducing wall-clock time.
- **Robustness of Noise Level Selection**:
 - **Ablation over Single Step Choices**: Experiments showed that while AUROC varied across individual timesteps, mid-level timesteps generally yielded higher average AUROC. This supports the strategy of combining early and mid-level evaluations for a more robust detector.
 - **Ablation over Two Step Choices**: Combining one early and one mid-level timestep consistently resulted in high AUROC across different pairs, demonstrating that the two-step variant is robust and does not require fine-tuning of specific timesteps.
- **Importance of Sign Correction**: An ablation study confirmed that the global sign correction applied to the typicality ratio is necessary for stable OOD performance in vision tasks, as its omission led to a drop in average AUROC from 0.84 to 0.81.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SCOPED contributes to the field of OOD detection by presenting a method that combines theoretical grounding with practical efficiency.

- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency**: The most significant impact is the substantial reduction in computational cost, achieving competitive OOD detection performance with an order of magnitude fewer model evaluations. The method's ability to parallelize computations further reduces wall-clock time, making it suitable for real-time and resource-constrained environments where previous diffusion-based methods were impractical.
- **Theoretical Foundation**: By leveraging the information-theoretic connection between score norm and curvature, SCOPED grounds OOD detection in fundamental geometric properties of the generative model. This approach offers an alternative to expensive likelihood or path-based estimators, suggesting a broader design principle for OOD detection.
- **Generalizability Across Domains**: SCOPED demonstrates consistent performance across diverse domains, including both high-dimensional vision tasks and complex proprioceptive reinforcement learning environments. Its success in distinguishing subtle distribution shifts in RL (e.g., across reward functions or policy realizations) expands the applicability of diffusion-based OOD methods.
- **Robust and Unsupervised Operation**: The method's calibration relies solely on in-distribution data, eliminating the need for OOD samples during training or hyperparameter tuning. This unsupervised nature enhances its practical applicability in scenarios where OOD data is unavailable or ill-defined.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The success of SCOPED opens avenues for further exploration. It could serve as a practical tool for:
 - Detecting perceptual artifacts in vision.
 - Outlier detection in autoregressive models.
 - Informing exploration strategies in reinforcement learning by identifying novel or atypical states.
 - Curating datasets for unsupervised training by filtering out atypical samples.

In summary, SCOPED offers a practical and robust solution for fast OOD detection, bridging information-theoretic concepts with the capabilities of generative diffusion models, and thus has the potential to contribute to the reliable deployment of machine learning systems.
