# ECO: Energy-Constrained Optimization with Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Walking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from BIGAI and other Chinese universities introduced ECO, an Energy-Constrained Optimization framework using constrained reinforcement learning, to achieve stable and energy-efficient humanoid walking. The method demonstrates a 6-fold reduction in energy consumption compared to MPC and 2.3-fold compared to standard PPO on a physical humanoid robot, BRUCE, by reformulating energy and reference motion as explicit inequality constraints.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots exhibit high energy consumption, limiting operational range and endurance for real-world applications.
- Existing locomotion methods (Model Predictive Control and standard Reinforcement Learning) struggle to balance energy efficiency, stability, and robustness, often requiring extensive and non-intuitive hyperparameter tuning for multi-objective reward functions.
- Model Predictive Control faces real-time computation challenges with complex energy constraints, while standard Reinforcement Learning can yield suboptimal or unstable policies when balancing conflicting objectives.

## Method

- ECO leverages a Constrained Reinforcement Learning (CRL) approach, specifically PPO-Lagrangian, to maximize task-related reward while strictly adhering to energy and motion symmetry constraints.
- Energy consumption is formulated as an explicit inequality constraint, integrated via the absolute power of motors (|τ * q_dot|), simplifying optimization and offering clear physical interpretability.
- A mirror-symmetry loss is included as a second inequality constraint to encourage natural, stable, and balanced walking behaviors, avoiding the need for manual trajectory design or complex reward shaping.

## Results

- ECO achieved approximately 6 times lower energy consumption than Model Predictive Control and 2.3 times lower than standard PPO on the physical BRUCE humanoid robot, maintaining comparable walking speeds (approx. 0.12 m/s).
- The framework demonstrated superior training stability and faster convergence compared to other constrained RL algorithms (CRPO, IPO, P3O), effectively enforcing constraints while maintaining high episode lengths.
- Policies learned with ECO showed strong sim-to-real transferability, enhanced robustness to external disturbances, and consistent performance across multiple simulation environments (IsaacGym, MuJoCo, Gazebo).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling energy optimization from the reward function through explicit inequality constraints significantly simplifies hyperparameter tuning and leads to more robust and intuitive learning for humanoid locomotion.
> - For humanoid robots, judicious selection of a small number of critical constraints (energy and motion symmetry) is more effective for stable convergence than incorporating many, due to their inherently smaller feasible control domain.
> - Constrained RL can implicitly generate bio-inspired, energy-efficient walking behaviors, such as more extended knees and lighter steps, without requiring explicit biomechanical priors in the policy design.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has seen remarkable advancements, enabling robots to perform complex tasks like walking, running, and even intricate loco-manipulation. However, a persistent challenge remains: achieving stable and energy-efficient locomotion comparable to biological systems. Humanoid robots currently consume substantially more energy than their biological counterparts, severely limiting their operational range, endurance, and payload capacity in real-world applications such as surveillance, disaster response, and long-duration autonomous operations.

Existing approaches to humanoid locomotion generally fall into two categories: Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC)** methods, while capable of dynamic locomotion, often struggle with real-time computation due to the high-dimensional and non-convex nature of optimal control problems, especially when incorporating complex energy-related constraints. These methods typically approximate energy constraints through metrics like joint torques or accelerations, which can lead to implicit trade-offs and suboptimal energy performance when balanced against other task requirements like stability.
- **Standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches**, particularly those based on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), integrate energy-related metrics (e.g., minimizing joint torques, accelerations, or contact forces) directly into the reward function. While effective for learning complex gaits, these multi-objective reward functions necessitate extensive and non-intuitive hyperparameter tuning. The weights assigned to energy cost, task completion, and stability terms often conflict, leading to suboptimal policies, unstable walking, or prolonged training periods to find a suitable balance. The trial-and-error nature of tuning reward weights lacks clear physical interpretability, making the process arduous and time-consuming.

This paper positions itself directly in this critical gap by proposing **Energy-Constrained Optimization (ECO)**, a novel application of constrained reinforcement learning (CRL) to humanoid locomotion. CRL, also known as safe RL, offers a principled framework to decouple conflicting objectives by treating critical requirements as explicit constraints rather than reward terms. This separation allows the robot to maximize its primary task reward while strictly adhering to safety or performance boundaries. While CRL has been successfully applied to quadruped robots, its application to humanoid robots, especially for energy optimization, introduces unique challenges due to the humanoid's inherently smaller feasible control domain and balance dynamics.

ECO addresses the limitations of both MPC and standard RL by:
1. **Reconceptualizing Energy as a Constraint:** Instead of a reward component, energy consumption is defined as an explicit inequality constraint, providing a clear, physically interpretable threshold. This simplifies hyperparameter tuning, making it more intuitive and efficient.
2. **Integrating Reference Motion as a Constraint:** A mirror-symmetry loss is also included as a constraint to encourage natural, stable, and balanced walking behaviors, avoiding the need for manual trajectory design.
3. **Leveraging PPO-Lagrangian:** The work empirically investigates and selects PPO-Lagrangian, a primal-dual optimization method, which dynamically updates Lagrange multipliers to adaptively enforce constraints and achieve stable convergence, outperforming other CRL algorithms (CRPO, IPO, P3O) that struggle with the complexity or sensitivity of multiple constraints in the humanoid domain.

By focusing on these two core constraints, ECO avoids the complexities of juggling multiple conflicting reward terms or constraints that can hinder convergence in humanoids. This work represents a significant advancement in the landscape of humanoid locomotion control by demonstrating a robust and energy-efficient solution, validated through extensive simulations and, crucially, real-world deployment on a kid-sized humanoid robot, BRUCE. This is noted as the first work to achieve energy-efficient humanoid walking using constrained RL on a physical humanoid robot, setting a new benchmark for practical applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to achieve **stable and energy-efficient locomotion for humanoid robots** to enable continuous operation in real-world applications. The authors are specifically motivated to overcome the limitations of current control paradigms, namely Model Predictive Control (MPC) and standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods, in effectively balancing energy consumption with walking stability and robustness.

The key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

1. **Address the Energy Bottleneck:** Humanoid robots are significantly less energy-efficient than their biological counterparts. This high energy consumption is a major bottleneck, limiting their operational range, endurance, and maximum payload. The primary objective is to develop a method that drastically reduces the energy footprint of humanoid walking.

2. **Simplify Hyperparameter Tuning:** Traditional MPC and RL approaches often rely on complex multi-objective optimization frameworks where energy-related metrics are embedded within reward functions. Balancing these energy terms with other objectives (like velocity tracking, motion tracking, or stability) requires extensive, non-intuitive, and time-consuming hyperparameter tuning (e.g., reward weights). The authors are motivated to eliminate this arduous tuning process.

3. **Reformulate Energy as an Explicit Constraint:** To simplify tuning and provide clearer physical meaning, a core objective is to move energy-related metrics from the reward function into explicit inequality constraints. This allows for direct, interpretable physical representation of energy costs, enabling more efficient and intuitive tuning focused on energy efficiency.

4. **Ensure Stable and Robust Walking:** While minimizing energy, it is crucial not to compromise walking stability or robustness. The method must ensure the humanoid robot can walk stably and adapt to disturbances, even with reduced energy consumption. This includes encouraging natural and symmetric movements to enhance stability.

5. **Develop a Robust Constrained RL Framework:** The authors aim to investigate and develop a constrained RL framework that can effectively handle the unique challenges of humanoid locomotion, particularly the potentially smaller feasible domain compared to quadrupedal robots. This involves comparing and selecting the most suitable constrained RL algorithm and constraint configuration.

6. **Validate on Real-World Hardware:** A critical objective is to demonstrate the practical viability and significant advancements of the proposed method through real-world validation on a physical humanoid robot, going beyond simulation results.

In summary, the motivation stems from the critical need for energy-efficient humanoids coupled with the practical difficulties of achieving this through conventional multi-objective optimization. ECO aims to provide a more principled, intuitive, and effective solution by leveraging constrained reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **ECO (Energy-Constrained Optimization)** framework introduces a constrained Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach to achieve stable and energy-efficient humanoid walking. The core idea is to reformulate energy consumption as an explicit inequality constraint rather than incorporating it as a term in a multi-objective reward function.

**A. Problem Formulation and Optimization Algorithm:**
The problem is framed as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP), extending the standard MDP by incorporating a set of cost functions with associated thresholds. The objective is to maximize the task-related reward while strictly satisfying these constraints.
The authors compare four state-of-the-art constrained RL algorithms: PPO-Lagrangian (PPO-Lag), CRPO, IPO, and P3O. Through empirical evaluation, PPO-Lagrangian is selected as the optimization approach for ECO due to its superior training stability, faster convergence, and effective constraint enforcement in the humanoid context. PPO-Lagrangian transforms the CMDP into an unconstrained optimization problem by introducing Lagrange multipliers, which are dynamically updated alongside the policy parameters using the Adam optimizer. This primal-dual approach allows adaptive scaling of the impact of constraints during training, improving stability.

**B. Control Policy and Architecture:**
- **Policy Network:** Both the control policy (actor) and the reward critic are implemented as fully connected neural networks.
- **Input (State Space):** The policy network receives an observation history of 15 consecutive frames, including:
 - Velocity commands (linear x, y, angular yaw).
 - Clock inputs (sin/cos of phase).
 - Joint positions and velocities of leg DoFs.
 - Body angular velocities.
 - Last action taken.
 - XY components of body Euler angles (yaw excluded due to real-world drift).
- **Output (Action Space):** The policy outputs desired joint positions for the leg joints (arm joints are fixed). These actions are deviations from nominal joint positions and are fed to a low-level PD controller, which converts them into torque commands.
- **Reward Critic:** Trained with privileged observations, including all policy inputs plus additional data like body linear velocity, body yaw angle, external forces/torques, friction coefficients, body mass, phase identifiers, and contact indicators. The cost returns for constraints are estimated using a Monte Carlo approach, simplifying the architecture by avoiding a separate cost critic network.

**C. Constraint Design:**
The methodology strategically focuses on two primary constraints, based on empirical findings that a larger number of constraints can hinder convergence in humanoid robots with smaller feasible domains.
1. **Energy Constraint (J_C1):**
 - **Metric:** Motor energy consumption, calculated by integrating the absolute power of the motors (|τ * q_dot| for each joint). This is the dominant part of total energy.
 - **Formulation:** Discounted sum constraint (Eq. 15), ensuring the cumulative energy over an episode remains below a threshold (b1).
 - **Threshold (b1) Tuning:** A linear search strategy is used to determine b1, incrementally adjusted based on direct energy criteria and desired speed range.
2. **Reference Motion Constraint (J_C2):**
 - **Metric:** Mirror loss (Eq. 16), which encourages symmetric and natural movements. This ensures that a mirrored observation input leads to a mirrored action output.
 - **Formulation:** Average sum constraint (Eq. 17), ensuring the average mirror loss per timestep remains below a threshold (b2).
 - **Threshold (b2) Tuning:** Empirically set to 0.05, based on the mirror-symmetry loss observed in a stable PPO baseline.

**D. Reward Function:**
The reward function is simplified by moving energy and reference motion concerns into constraints. It adopts a configuration similar to Humanoid-Gym, focusing on task-related objectives:
- Tracking linear and angular velocities.
- Foot air time and foot clearance for swing phase.
- Foot contact velocity and number.
- Base orientation and height tracking.
- Penalties for self-collision and excessive contact forces.
- Action smoothness.
This decoupling allows the reward function to focus purely on maximizing locomotion performance, with energy efficiency guaranteed by the constraints.

**E. Training and Validation:**
- **Simulation Environment:** Training is conducted in IsaacGym with parallelization across 8192 environments, significantly accelerating the learning process.
- **Domain Randomization:** Extensive domain randomization (e.g., payload, COM displacement, friction, motor strength, sensor noise, action delay) is applied to enhance sim-to-real transferability.
- **External Perturbations:** Force disturbances and velocity impulses are applied during training to improve policy robustness against unseen real-world conditions.
- **Robot Platform:** The kid-sized humanoid robot BRUCE (70cm tall, 4.8kg, 16 DoFs) is used for both simulation and real-world experiments.
- **Evaluation:** Performance is evaluated against MPC, standard PPO with reward shaping, and other constrained RL methods using metrics like normalized total reward, episode length, mean energy cost, and mean mirror cost. Sim-to-sim transfer to MuJoCo and Gazebo, disturbance rejection tests, and real-world deployments are conducted.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that ECO not only achieves energy efficiency but also maintains robust, stable, and natural walking, facilitating practical deployment on physical humanoid robots.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings across extensive simulations and real-world experiments, demonstrating ECO's significant advancements in energy-efficient humanoid locomotion.

**A. Comparison of Constrained RL Algorithms (Simulation):**
- **ECO (PPO-Lagrangian)** consistently converged to the target energy threshold (60J for 0.1m/s velocity) while maintaining high normalized episode length (close to 1.0), indicating stability and robustness. It achieved the best balance of energy efficiency and stability among all tested methods.
- **P3O** also satisfied both energy and reference motion constraints but required more iterations to converge, suggesting slower learning dynamics compared to ECO.
- **IPO** showed sensitivity to its penalty coefficient (κ_IPO): a low coefficient (1.0) failed to enforce the energy constraint, while a high one (10.0) enforced the constraint but led to overly conservative behavior and reduced episode length (instability).
- **CRPO** exhibited unstable convergence, likely due to its alternating optimization strategy, which struggled with smooth transitions between reward and constraint objectives.
- **Standard PPO (with reward shaping)**, despite extensive tuning of energy penalty coefficients (μ_e), failed to achieve a satisfactory balance. Lower coefficients did not reduce energy, while higher ones led to instability and frequent falls, highlighting the difficulty of intuitive tuning with reward functions.

**B. Sim-to-Sim Transfer:**
- ECO demonstrated strong robustness and generalizability by achieving stable walking across three different simulation environments: IsaacGym, MuJoCo, and Gazebo.
- It maintained consistent gait patterns (e.g., ankle height) during cross-simulator transfers.
- Quantitatively, ECO significantly reduced energy consumption compared to baselines in simulated environments. For a 0.1m/s target speed, ECO consumed approximately 3 times less energy than MPC and 1.4 times less than PPO in Gazebo and MuJoCo, respectively, while accurately tracking velocity commands.
- MPC struggled to track higher target speeds (e.g., 0.2m/s) and incurred the highest energy costs, indicating limitations in its real-time contact dynamics resolution.

**C. Disturbance Rejection Test (Simulation):**
- ECO showed greater robustness to external pushes (from 8 different directions and varying durations) than MPC, especially in the sagittal direction.
- Its robustness was comparable to that of PPO, despite not having encountered pushes longer than 0.001s during training. This indicates that ECO does not sacrifice robustness for energy efficiency and generalizes well to unseen disturbance patterns. Qualitative tests with random drags and thrown balls further confirmed its recovery capabilities.

**D. Energy Constraint Threshold Analysis (Simulation):**
- A linear search strategy revealed that a 60J energy threshold (for a 0.1m/s walking speed over 24s episode) provided the best trade-off between energy efficiency and locomotion stability. Lower thresholds (20J, 40J) led to reduced episode lengths (instability), while higher ones (80J, 100J) resulted in higher energy consumption without significant stability gains. This validates the intuitive tuning approach for physically meaningful constraints.

**E. Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis (Simulation):**
- ECO (PPO-Lagrangian) showed remarkable insensitivity to the learning rate (β1) of the Lagrange multiplier for the energy constraint. Different learning rates (0.001, 0.01, 0.1) all converged to approximately the same 60J energy cost, albeit at different speeds for the Lagrange multiplier itself. This indicates that ECO requires less human effort for hyperparameter tuning compared to methods with fixed penalty coefficients.

**F. More Constraints Setting (Simulation):**
- Attempts to incorporate additional constraints (self-collision, foot contact velocity, foot clearance), commonly used in quadruped robots, led to significant reductions in episode length (instability) for ECO, IPO, and P3O.
- While ECO demonstrated better constraint satisfaction than P3O and IPO, all algorithms struggled with self-collision cost convergence. This highlights the unique challenges of applying a large number of constraints to humanoids due to their smaller feasible control domain and the heterogeneity of cost signals. The study concluded that the two-constraint configuration (energy and symmetry) was most suitable for the task.

**G. Real-World Results (BRUCE Robot):**
- **Unprecedented Energy Reduction:** ECO achieved approximately **6 times lower energy consumption than MPC** and **2.3 times lower than PPO** on the physical BRUCE robot while maintaining comparable walking speeds (approx. 0.12 m/s). This is a landmark achievement, being the first to demonstrate such energy efficiency with constrained RL on a real humanoid.
- **Robustness on Hardware:** BRUCE, controlled by the ECO policy, successfully rejected disturbances and traversed outdoor terrains, confirming the sim-to-real transfer success and robustness.
- **Reduced Body Oscillation:** ECO resulted in significantly less body oscillation (lower frequency and smaller magnitude) compared to MPC and PPO, indicating smoother, lighter steps and reduced body shaking.

**H. Emergent Behaviors:**
- ECO implicitly generated bio-inspired efficient walking behaviors:
 - **More Extended Knees:** The left knee joint angle showed less flexion compared to MPC and PPO.
 - **Lighter Steps:** Reduced knee joint torque, implicitly indicating lighter ground contact.
 - **Reduced Body Shaking:** As noted above, fewer body oscillations.
- These behaviors emerged naturally from the energy minimization constraint, without explicit design, suggesting a strong correlation between these biomechanical principles and energy efficiency.

In conclusion, ECO significantly pushes the boundaries of energy-efficient humanoid locomotion by effectively leveraging constrained RL. Its superior performance, robustness, and ease of tuning compared to existing methods represent a substantial advancement towards practical, long-duration humanoid robot applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "ECO: Energy-Constrained Optimization with Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Walking" paper presents a highly significant advancement in humanoid robotics, with substantial potential impact across several domains.

**A. Addressing a Critical Bottleneck in Humanoid Robotics:**
The most immediate and profound impact of this work is its direct assault on the **energy efficiency bottleneck** in humanoid robots. By demonstrating a 6-fold reduction in energy consumption compared to state-of-the-art MPC and a 2.3-fold reduction compared to standard PPO on a real humanoid, ECO establishes a new benchmark. This is crucial because limited battery life and high power demands severely restrict the operational duration and practical utility of humanoids. A significant increase in energy efficiency directly translates to:
- **Extended Operational Range and Endurance:** Humanoids can perform tasks for much longer periods without needing to recharge.
- **Increased Payload Capacity:** Less energy diverted to locomotion allows for more power to be allocated to manipulation, computation, or carrying heavier payloads.
- **Reduced Actuator Overheating:** Lower power dissipation reduces thermal stress on motors, improving robot longevity and reliability.
- **Broader Real-World Applications:** This breakthrough opens doors for humanoids in long-duration autonomous tasks like surveillance, inspection, logistics, and disaster response, where continuous operation is paramount.

**B. Advancing Constrained Reinforcement Learning for Complex Robotics:**
The paper makes significant methodological contributions to the field of Reinforcement Learning, particularly for complex robotic systems like humanoids:
- **Principled Decoupling of Objectives:** ECO demonstrates the power of constrained RL in disentangling conflicting objectives (task completion vs. energy efficiency vs. stability). By treating energy consumption and reference motion as explicit, physically interpretable constraints rather than fuzzy reward terms, the framework offers a more robust and intuitive approach to policy optimization.
- **Simplified Hyperparameter Tuning:** One of the major pain points in conventional RL is the extensive and non-intuitive tuning of reward weights. ECO significantly alleviates this by replacing arbitrary reward coefficients with physically meaningful constraint thresholds (e.g., specific Joule limits), making the optimization process more efficient and user-friendly. The demonstrated insensitivity to Lagrange multiplier learning rates further supports this.
- **Empirical Validation of CRL Algorithms for Humanoids:** The comprehensive comparison of various constrained RL algorithms (PPO-Lagrangian, CRPO, IPO, P3O) provides valuable empirical insights into their effectiveness and challenges in the context of humanoid locomotion. The selection and successful implementation of PPO-Lagrangian highlight its advantages for dynamic and complex robotic systems where stable convergence is paramount.
- **Insights into Constraint Design for Humanoids:** The findings regarding the difficulty of incorporating a large number of constraints (e.g., self-collision, foot contact velocity) in humanoids due to their smaller feasible domain offer critical guidance for future CRL research. This suggests that a judicious selection of core, high-impact constraints is more effective than indiscriminately adding many.

**C. Promoting Emergent, Bio-Inspired Behaviors:**
A fascinating aspect of ECO's success is its ability to generate emergent, energy-efficient walking behaviors without explicit design or bio-mimetic priors. The observation of "extended knees," "lighter steps," and "reduced body shaking" directly resulting from energy minimization reinforces the link between these biomechanical characteristics and energy efficiency. This finding suggests that constrained RL can implicitly discover natural, efficient movement strategies, potentially reducing the burden of manually crafting complex gaits or motion trajectories.

**D. Strong Sim-to-Real Transferability:**
The successful sim-to-real transfer of ECO policies to the BRUCE robot, coupled with its robustness to disturbances and ability to navigate various terrains, validates the practicality of the approach. The use of extensive domain randomization and perturbations during training is a key enabler for this robust transfer, making the learned policies more applicable in unpredictable real-world environments.

**E. Future Research Directions:**
The paper also naturally paves the way for future research:
- **Scaling to More Complex Constraints:** While two constraints were optimal here, investigating adaptive normalization techniques or hierarchical control structures (e.g., integrating model-based priors) could enable more complex constraint sets for sophisticated behaviors (e.g., loco-manipulation, interaction).
- **Diverse Terrains and Tasks:** Extending ECO to handle transitions between vastly different terrains (e.g., stairs, slopes) or more complex tasks (e.g., opening doors while walking) would require mode-aware constraints or hierarchical planning, as discussed by the authors.
- **Integration with World Models and Vision:** Combining ECO with advanced perception systems and world models could enable the robot to dynamically adjust its locomotion strategy based on environmental observations, further enhancing autonomy and adaptability.

In conclusion, ECO represents a pivotal step towards making humanoid robots practical and deployable in real-world scenarios. By offering a more principled, intuitive, and effective method for energy optimization, it has the potential to unlock new applications and accelerate the development of truly capable and enduring humanoid systems.
