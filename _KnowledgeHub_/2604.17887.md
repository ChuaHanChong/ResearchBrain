# StableIDM: Stabilizing Inverse Dynamics Model against Manipulator Truncation via Spatio-Temporal Refinement

> [!summary] Summary
> StableIDM develops an Inverse Dynamics Model that maintains stable and accurate robot action predictions despite severe manipulator truncation in visual observations. It achieves this by integrating spatio-temporal refinement, leading to a 17.6% improvement in downstream Vision-Language-Action model training success and robust real-world robot control.

## Problem

- Existing Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs) suffer from significant performance degradation when parts of the robot manipulator are outside the camera's field of view (manipulator truncation).
- Under truncation, IDMs face an ill-posed regression problem due to lost structural/geometric cues and struggle with 'causal confusion' by overfitting to irrelevant background information.
- Conventional IDMs exhibit 'temporal memorylessness' and 'isotropic spatial aggregation,' leading to lost fine-grained directional cues and temporal jitter in action predictions.

## Method

- **StableIDM** introduces a ==three-stage pipeline== for synergistic spatio-temporal feature refinement: ==Robot-centric Masking==, ==Directional Feature Aggregation (DFA)==, and ==Temporal Dynamics Refinement (TDR)==.
- ==Robot-centric Masking== uses an auxiliary segmenter (==Segment Anything==) to filter out background noise, directing the model's focus to the visible manipulator geometry.
- ==Directional Feature Aggregation (DFA)== employs ==direction-aware operators== and ==adaptive re-weighting of angular components== to preserve and amplify fine-grained geometric cues from the manipulator's layout.
- ==Temporal Dynamics Refinement (TDR)== includes a ==Temporal Fusion module== that aligns and fuses features from past frames to repair truncated inputs, and a ==Temporal Regressor== (==causal TCN==) that smooths DFA's outputs for kinematic consistency.

## Results

- StableIDM increased accuracy on the AgiBot benchmark's heavily truncated subset to **30.7% (Acc)** and **44.7% (Acc-per-dim)**, significantly outperforming Vidar's **18.6%** and **32.2%** respectively.
- In real-world open-loop replay on a physical platform, StableIDM achieved an average task success rate of **47.4%**, surpassing the strongest baseline, Vidar, at **37.7%**.
- When used as an action annotator, StableIDM-labeled generated videos augmented training for a VLA model, resulting in a **52.9%** task success rate, a **17.6%** improvement over training with real-only data (**35.3%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly compensating for lost structural and geometric information via spatio-temporal refinement is crucial for stabilizing inverse dynamics predictions under severe visual truncation.
> - Robot-centric masking effectively mitigates 'causal confusion' by focusing visual encoders on task-relevant manipulator regions, even when only minimal segments are visible.
> - Combining direction-aware spatial feature aggregation with a multi-stage causal temporal system allows for robust action decoding by recovering missing geometry from history and enforcing kinematic smoothness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have accelerated progress in embodied intelligence by leveraging large-scale vision-language datasets and diverse robotic data. A fundamental challenge in this field is bridging the gap between high-level perception (e.g., visual observations from cameras) and low-level robot control (e.g., joint angle commands). Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs) serve as a mechanism for this bridge, translating visual observations into executable action commands. This capability positions IDMs as critical components in modern robotics pipelines.

IDMs fulfill a dual role: first, as scalable action annotators, they can process vast amounts of video data (from the internet or synthetic generation) to produce action labels, which are essential for training large VLA models. Second, as integrated policies, IDMs can translate visual plans generated by video generation models into concrete robot control commands, enabling robots to execute complex tasks in real-world environments.

Despite their utility, existing IDMs face a significant challenge: sensitivity to "manipulator truncation." This phenomenon occurs when parts of the robot arm move out of the camera's field of view, leaving only a segment visible. As illustrated in preliminary experiments (Figure 1), current IDMs experience a substantial degradation in performance, characterized by increased L1 distance between predicted and ground-truth actions and reduced accuracy, particularly under severe truncation. This performance drop indicates that when a significant portion of the manipulator is unseen, IDMs struggle to infer the complete underlying state of the robot. The primary difficulty stems from the removal of crucial structural and geometric cues, rendering the inverse dynamics regression an ill-posed problem. Consequently, minor visual changes can lead to considerable errors and unstable robot control.

This research addresses this specific limitation by proposing a method to stabilize IDM performance against manipulator truncation. It aims to improve the reliability and utility of IDMs, thereby enhancing their application in both large-scale data annotation for VLA training and robust policy execution in real-world robotics, where visual occlusions and partial observations are common. The work positions itself as a refinement to existing IDM architectures, seeking to overcome a key barrier to their widespread and robust deployment in embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop StableIDM, an Inverse Dynamics Model designed to achieve stable and accurate action predictions even under conditions of severe manipulator truncation. This addresses a critical limitation observed in conventional IDMs, which exhibit significant performance degradation when parts of the robot arm are out of the camera's view.

The motivation for this work arises from several interconnected challenges and requirements within embodied intelligence:

1. **Addressing the Ill-Posed Nature of Truncation:** Standard IDMs, formulated as single-frame regression tasks, inherently suffer from "temporal memorylessness" and "isotropic spatial aggregation." When a manipulator is truncated, these models lack historical context to infer the full geometry of the arm, making the regression problem highly ill-posed. Furthermore, their global self-attention mechanisms mix spatial information uniformly, leading to the loss of fine-grained, direction-sensitive cues that are critical for understanding articulated arm configurations. The research is motivated by the need to explicitly compensate for this missing information and structural ambiguity.

2. **Enhancing Data Generation for VLA Models:** Training advanced VLA models is often bottlenecked by the scarcity of scalable, diverse, and semantically aligned action data. IDMs are envisioned as scalable action annotators that can convert large volumes of video-only data (e.g., internet videos or synthetically generated videos) into (video, action) pairs for training. However, if IDMs are unstable under truncation, the quality of these automatically generated labels would be compromised, limiting the effectiveness of downstream VLA model training. Stabilizing IDMs would enable the generation of higher-fidelity pseudo-labels, thereby augmenting real datasets and accelerating VLA development.

3. **Improving Real-World Policy Deployment:** IDMs also function as lightweight policies when combined with video generation models. In this setup, a video generation model produces a visual execution plan from a text prompt and current observations, and the IDM decodes actions from this plan for real robot replay. Real-world environments frequently involve partial occlusions and manipulator truncation due to dynamic camera viewpoints or object interactions. Unstable action predictions from the IDM would lead to erratic robot behavior, task failures, and unreliable deployment. The motivation is to ensure that IDMs can robustly translate visual plans, even those with artifacts or truncation inherent in generated content, into smooth and reliable robot actions for real-time open-loop control.

4. **Mitigating Causal Confusion:** Under severe truncation, traditional high-capacity visual encoders in IDMs are prone to "causal confusion," where they overfit to irrelevant background correlations (e.g., textures, lighting) instead of focusing on the robot's kinematics. This dilutes the action-relevant signal and leads to unstable predictions. The objective is to design a system that can effectively filter out such noise and prioritize information pertinent to the robot's physical articulation.

In summary, the core motivation is to overcome the fundamental instability of IDMs caused by manipulator truncation, thereby unlocking their full potential as reliable components for data annotation and policy execution within the broader embodied AI ecosystem.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

StableIDM is formulated as a history-conditioned action decoder, $f_\theta$, that maps a brief causal history of visual observations ($o_{t-K+1:t}$) to the current action ($a_t$). The action space is represented as a continuous vector comprising dual-arm joint angles and gripper opening dimensions. The model is designed to recover low-level actions from streaming camera inputs and decode generated visual plans for real-time open-loop deployment, focusing on a fixed-length causal history window to recover geometric cues without predicting future frames.

The framework is a three-stage pipeline, illustrated in Figure 2, designed for synergistic spatio-temporal feature refinement:

1. **Robot-centric Masking:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Manipulator truncation increases the proportion of background observations, leading to visual encoders overfitting to spurious background correlations and a "signal collapse problem."
 - **Approach:** An auxiliary preprocessing step uses Segment Anything (Kirillov et al., 2023) to extract a robot-centric binary mask ($M_t$) for each input frame ($I_t$).
 - **Functionality:** This mask effectively filters out background noise, ensuring that subsequent modules (DFA and TDR) focus their representational capacity on the visible geometry of the manipulator rather than irrelevant environmental distractors. This mitigates causal confusion and maintains focus on physical articulation even with minimal visible manipulator segments.

2. **Directional Feature Aggregation (DFA):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Global self-attention in visual transformers (e.g., ViT) isotropically mixes spatial information, causing fine-grained directional cues to be lost during truncation or occlusion. Monocular RGB observations also lack direct depth information, making 3D structural reasoning under truncation challenging.
 - **Approach:** This core spatial module explicitly collects and amplifies direction-sensitive evidence. It leverages the strong directional layout of the visible manipulator (e.g., edges of links) as a 2D geometric proxy.
 - **Mechanism:**
 - DFA uses direction-aware operators at multiple analysis angles ($\theta_k$) to form feature components on the masked feature map ($\tilde{G}_t$) from a pre-trained DINOv2 visual encoder (Oquab et al., 2023). These operators disentangle spatial information into directional components.
 - These components are concatenated into an anisotropic single-frame representation.
 - The model adaptively re-weights these angular components using global context ($g_t$), which is extracted from the encoder's CLS and register tokens via an MLP. This re-weighting allows dynamic emphasis on directional evidence deemed more reliable under the current viewpoint and truncation state. The final descriptor $z_t$ is computed as: $z_t = \bigoplus_{k=1}^{A} (w_{t,k} \langle \mathcal{D}_{\theta_k}(\tilde{G}_t) \rangle_{M_t})$, where $w_t = \text{softmax}(U g_t / \tau)$.
 - **Purpose:** To maximize the retention of geometric cues along the arm’s orientation, explicitly compensating for spatial ambiguity due to partial observability without relying on auxiliary depth sensors. Local view perturbations are used during training for robustness to calibration noise.

3. **Temporal Dynamics Refinement (TDR):**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Single-frame spatial aggregation alone might be insufficient during severe truncation, and action predictions can suffer from temporal jitter.
 - **Approach:** A multi-stage temporal system operates synergistically at both the input visual features and output decoded features of DFA, maintaining strict causality and fixed latency.
 - **Mechanism:**
 - **Temporal Fusion (Pre-DFA):** Before DFA processes features, a Temporal Fusion module operates on the encoder’s visual feature maps within the causal history window ($K$). It aligns features from the preceding adjacent frame ($G_{\tau-1}$) to the current frame’s coordinate system ($G_\tau$) using a lightweight deformation and sampling field ($W_\phi$). Features are fused via a learned visibility gate ($\Pi_\tau$), which assigns low confidence to truncated or unreliably aligned regions: $\tilde{G}_\tau = G_\tau + \beta_{\text{fusion}} \Pi_\tau \odot (W_\phi(G_{\tau-1}) - G_\tau)$. This provides DFA with a geometrically complete spatial input sequence.
 - **Temporal Regressor (Post-DFA):** After DFA outputs the high-level feature descriptor ($z_t$), a lightweight causal temporal model operates on the feature history sequence ($z_{t-K+1:t}$). It uses a causal TCN (Temporal Convolutional Network) to analyze motion trends and predict a residual correction to a base MLP regressor $h(z_t)$: $\hat{a}_t = h(z_t) + \beta_{\text{TCN}} f_{\text{TCN}}(z_{t-K+1:t})$.
 - **Purpose:** Temporal Fusion repairs the input to DFA by borrowing structural information from adjacent frames. The Temporal Regressor refines DFA's output by smoothing jitter and completing information, enforcing kinematic consistency with a fixed, predictable latency crucial for real-time control.

All components of StableIDM are optimized end-to-end under a unified inverse-dynamics objective. During inference, StableIDM processes a fixed-length causal history window and outputs only the current single-step action, preventing error accumulation over long horizons.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of StableIDM was structured around four key questions, demonstrating its performance in offline prediction, real-world replay, policy deployment, and action labeling quality.

**Q1: Offline Prediction Stability (AgiBot Benchmark)**
- **Context:** The AgiBot benchmark was used, divided into "light" (mask ratio > 15%) and "heavy" (mask ratio < 15%) truncation subsets, and evaluated using overall accuracy (Acc), mean per-dimension accuracy (Acc-per-dim), and L1 prediction error (Distance). Baselines included ResNet-50, AnyPos (single-view variant), and Vidar.
- **Results:** StableIDM demonstrated increased robustness under heavy truncation. On the heavy subset, where baselines significantly degraded, StableIDM achieved an Acc of 30.7%, improving upon Vidar's 18.6%. Acc-per-dim reached 44.7% (compared to Vidar's 32.2%), and L1 distance was reduced to 0.493 (compared to AnyPos's 0.538 and Vidar's 0.573). This indicates StableIDM’s stronger tolerance to missing or truncated manipulator observations.
- **Ablation Study:**
 - Removing the entire spatio-temporal refinement stack (Ours w/o Refinement) caused a notable drop in performance across all metrics, confirming its importance.
 - Removing Directional Feature Aggregation (Ours w/o DFA) led to a sharp accuracy drop under heavy truncation, validating its role in spatial localization and structural reasoning.
 - Removing Temporal Dynamics Refinement (Ours w/o TDR) resulted in a severe increase in L1 distance, indicating significant temporal jitter and confirming TDR’s necessity for kinematic smoothing.
 - Removing robot-centric masking (Ours w/o mask) caused a drop in strict success (Acc) despite a slight reduction in L1 distance, suggesting the model overfitted to static background cues, failing to capture true kinematic articulation.
- **Mask-quality robustness:** StableIDM exhibited robustness to moderate segmentation noise, with only a moderate increase in L1 error even on a dedicated subset with severe mask failures (e.g., 0.517 vs. 0.493 under heavy truncation).

**Q2: Real-World Replay Stability (Physical Platform)**
- **Context:** Action sequences generated by IDMs were executed in open-loop replay on a physical manipulation platform across diverse and challenging tasks (Pick & Place, Microwave Operation, Sink Cleaning) to isolate inherent action decoding fidelity.
- **Results:** StableIDM consistently outperformed all baselines. It achieved an average task success rate of 47.4%, surpassing the strongest baseline, Vidar (37.7%). StableIDM demonstrated particular strength in complex scenarios, such as Microwave Operation (42.3%) and Sink Cleaning (46.2%), where baselines struggled (e.g., Vidar at 29.1% on Sink Cleaning). This confirmed that offline stability translated into robust real-world physical execution.

**Q3: Policy Deployment Stability (Video Generation Models)**
- **Context:** StableIDM was evaluated as an executable policy when combined with a fixed monocular video generation model. It decoded actions from synthetic visual plans (which often contain artifacts and domain shifts) under open-loop test conditions. Grasp success rate was the primary metric.
- **Results:** StableIDM achieved a grasp success rate of 53.8%, substantially outperforming AnyPos (42.3%), Vidar (26.9%), and ResNet (11.5%). The spatio-temporal completion framework of StableIDM provided a strong prior for coherent dynamics, enabling it to smooth over minor generative artifacts and temporal inconsistencies, leading to stable predictions from imperfect inputs. This confirmed its reliability as a policy decoder in generate-decode-execute pipelines.

**Q4: Action Labeling Quality (VLA Training)**
- **Context:** This experiment assessed StableIDM's ability to act as a high-fidelity action annotator. A standard VLA model (Pi-0.5) was trained under two data regimes: "Real Only" (baseline) and "Real + Generated" (augmented with StableIDM-labeled generated videos).
- **Results:** The VLA model trained with StableIDM-labeled generated videos achieved a 52.9% success rate, a 17.6% improvement compared to the baseline trained only on real human-annotated data (35.3%). This indicates that the stability and accuracy of StableIDM are sufficient to generate useful, high-quality pseudo-labels that enhance downstream VLA model training, helping address data scarcity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on StableIDM presents a significant contribution to the field of embodied intelligence by addressing a fundamental limitation of Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs): their sensitivity to manipulator truncation. This work offers a robust solution that has implications across several aspects of robot learning and deployment.

1. **Enhanced Robustness in Challenging Conditions:** StableIDM's primary significance lies in its ability to maintain stable and accurate action predictions even when the robot manipulator is partially or heavily obscured from camera view. This robustness is critical for real-world robotic applications, where dynamic environments, varying camera viewpoints, and object interactions frequently lead to visual truncation. By effectively compensating for lost structural and geometric information through spatio-temporal refinement, StableIDM provides a more reliable foundation for robot control.

2. **Scalable Data Annotation for VLA Models:** A major bottleneck in training large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models is the scarcity and high cost of human-annotated robot trajectory data. StableIDM's validated performance as a high-fidelity action annotator allows it to automatically label generated or internet videos with accurate action commands. This capability significantly expands the availability of training data, enabling VLA models to scale more effectively and potentially generalize to a wider range of tasks and environments without relying solely on expensive human-collected datasets. The demonstrated 17.6% improvement in downstream VLA task success rate using StableIDM-labeled data underscores this impact.

3. **Reliable Policy Deployment from Generative Models:** The integration of video generation models with IDMs to form deployable policies is a promising avenue for robot control. However, visual plans generated by these models can contain artifacts, domain shifts, and inherent truncation. StableIDM's ability to robustly decode actions from such imperfect synthetic visual plans, boosting grasp success rates by 11.5% compared to prior methods, bridges the gap between high-level visual imagination and low-level physical execution. This increases the practical utility of generative foundation models in robotics, enabling robots to execute tasks by "imagining" solutions.

4. **Foundation for Real-Time Closed-Loop Control:** The design choices within StableIDM, such as its causal history window and fixed, predictable latency, render it suitable for real-time closed-loop control systems. By avoiding unbounded or variable latency inherent in more complex recurrent or attention-based temporal mechanisms, StableIDM offers a pragmatic solution for deploying robust action decoders in dynamic physical environments.

5. **Modular and Extensible Architecture:** The modular design, comprising Robot-centric Masking, Directional Feature Aggregation, and Temporal Dynamics Refinement, offers a clear architectural framework that can be built upon. Future research can explore multi-view extensions to further enhance geometric observability or investigate joint optimization of generation and inverse-dynamics modules to improve alignment and reduce execution drift in more complex scenarios. The current limitations, such as targeting single-task execution and decoupled training, suggest clear pathways for continued development.

In essence, StableIDM represents a step towards making inverse dynamics models more practical and dependable for the next generation of embodied AI systems, alleviating data bottlenecks and enabling more robust, intelligent robot behaviors.
