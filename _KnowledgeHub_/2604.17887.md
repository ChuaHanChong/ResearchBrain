# StableIDM: Stabilizing Inverse Dynamics Model against Manipulator Truncation via Spatio-Temporal Refinement

> [!summary] Summary
> StableIDM develops an Inverse Dynamics Model that maintains stable and accurate robot action predictions despite severe manipulator truncation in visual observations. It achieves this by integrating spatio-temporal refinement, leading to a 17.6% improvement in downstream Vision-Language-Action model training success and robust real-world robot control.

## Problem

- Existing Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs) suffer from significant performance degradation when parts of the robot manipulator are outside the camera's field of view (manipulator truncation).
- Under truncation, IDMs face an ill-posed regression problem due to lost structural/geometric cues and struggle with 'causal confusion' by overfitting to irrelevant background information.
- Conventional IDMs exhibit 'temporal memorylessness' and 'isotropic spatial aggregation,' leading to lost fine-grained directional cues and temporal jitter in action predictions.

## Method

- **StableIDM** introduces a ==three-stage pipeline== for synergistic spatio-temporal feature refinement: ==Robot-centric Masking==, ==Directional Feature Aggregation (DFA)==, and ==Temporal Dynamics Refinement (TDR)==.
- ==Robot-centric Masking== uses an auxiliary segmenter (==Segment Anything==) to filter out background noise, directing the model's focus to the visible manipulator geometry.
- ==Directional Feature Aggregation (DFA)== employs ==direction-aware operators== and ==adaptive re-weighting of angular components== to preserve and amplify fine-grained geometric cues from the manipulator's layout.
- ==Temporal Dynamics Refinement (TDR)== includes a ==Temporal Fusion module== that aligns and fuses features from past frames to repair truncated inputs, and a ==Temporal Regressor== (==causal TCN==) that smooths DFA's outputs for kinematic consistency.

## Results

- StableIDM increased accuracy on the AgiBot benchmark's heavily truncated subset to **30.7% (Acc)** and **44.7% (Acc-per-dim)**, significantly outperforming Vidar's **18.6%** and **32.2%** respectively.
- In real-world open-loop replay on a physical platform, StableIDM achieved an average task success rate of **47.4%**, surpassing the strongest baseline, Vidar, at **37.7%**.
- When used as an action annotator, StableIDM-labeled generated videos augmented training for a VLA model, resulting in a **52.9%** task success rate, a **17.6%** improvement over training with real-only data (**35.3%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly compensating for lost structural and geometric information via spatio-temporal refinement is crucial for stabilizing inverse dynamics predictions under severe visual truncation.
> - Robot-centric masking effectively mitigates 'causal confusion' by focusing visual encoders on task-relevant manipulator regions, even when only minimal segments are visible.
> - Combining direction-aware spatial feature aggregation with a multi-stage causal temporal system allows for robust action decoding by recovering missing geometry from history and enforcing kinematic smoothness.
