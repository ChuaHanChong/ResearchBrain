# Flow Matching with Injected Noise for Offline-to-Online Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Flow Matching with Injected Noise for Offline-to-Online RL (FINO) method, developed by KAIST researchers, enhances exploration and sample efficiency in generative policies by incorporating noise injection during offline pre-training and an adaptive entropy-guided sampling strategy for online fine-tuning, demonstrating superior performance across various challenging RL benchmarks.

## Problem

- Generative policies, particularly flow policies, struggle with limited exploration when directly extended from offline pre-training to online fine-tuning in reinforcement learning.
- Policies overly constrained by static offline datasets often remain confined to known paths, preventing discovery of optimal actions and leading to suboptimal online performance.
- Conventional solutions like collecting larger, diverse datasets are often costly and time-consuming, necessitating methods to induce diverse behaviors from existing data.

## Method

- **FINO** modifies the standard flow matching objective during offline pre-training by ==injecting== scheduled noise, encouraging the policy to learn a broader action space beyond observed offline data.
- An entropy-guided sampling mechanism is introduced for online fine-tuning, which adaptively adjusts a temperature parameter to balance exploration (uniform sampling of candidate actions) and exploitation (greedy action selection).
- The one-step policy is guided to consider actions from an expanded, informative region of the action space, facilitating efficient exploration and adaptation.

## Results

- FINO consistently achieved strong performance on OGBench (**35** tasks) and D4RL (**10** tasks), significantly outperforming baselines like ReBRAC, Cal-QL, and its FQL backbone during online fine-tuning.
- The method demonstrated substantial performance gains during online fine-tuning without degrading initial offline pre-training performance, confirming its ability to expand action space while preserving policy mean.
- Ablation studies showed that FINO's noise injection strategy and entropy-guided sampling mechanism were more effective than direct action noise or static entropy-regulated noise scaling for facilitating exploration and balancing the exploration-exploitation trade-off.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting noise into the flow matching objective during pre-training explicitly increases the variance of the learned policy's marginal probability path, enabling wider action coverage for robust exploration.
> - Dynamically adjusting the sampling temperature based on the policy's entropy during online fine-tuning effectively balances exploration and exploitation, leading to more sample-efficient learning.
> - Both the noise injection and entropy-guided sampling components are critical, as removing either leads to degraded performance in exploration or long-term adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the intersection of generative models and reinforcement learning (RL), specifically addressing challenges in the offline-to-online RL framework.

Generative models, such as denoising diffusion probabilistic models and flow matching, have demonstrated capabilities in producing high-quality outputs in various domains. Their expressive capacity allows them to capture complex and multimodal distributions, which has motivated their application to policy design in RL. While conventional Gaussian policies can struggle with multimodal or high-dimensional action spaces, generative policies offer a potential solution by representing more complex action distributions. This has led to strong performance in offline RL, where the target distribution is explicitly defined and learning from pre-collected data is the primary objective.

However, offline RL is inherently limited by the quality and coverage of its static dataset. To mitigate this, offline-to-online RL frameworks have emerged, enabling policies pre-trained on offline data to further refine their behavior through direct interaction with the environment. A common approach in existing work that applies generative policies to offline-to-online RL is to treat online fine-tuning as a direct continuation of offline pre-training. This direct continuation often overlooks specific challenges associated with the online phase, particularly the need for effective exploration beyond the boundaries of the offline dataset. The paper identifies this gap, noting that policies overly confined to the offline data may exhibit limited exploration during online fine-tuning, thereby restricting performance improvement.

The authors build upon existing flow matching-based policy learning methods like Flow Q-Learning (FQL) and aim to adapt them more effectively for the offline-to-online paradigm. The research aligns with recent findings that suggest maintaining diversity in policy behavior can facilitate more effective fine-tuning processes. By introducing mechanisms to explicitly promote policy diversity and manage the exploration-exploitation trade-off during online interaction, this work contributes to advancing the sample efficiency and performance of generative policies in the offline-to-online RL setting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from observations regarding the behavior of generative policies, particularly flow policies, when applied to offline-to-online reinforcement learning. While these policies offer high expressivity and strong performance in purely offline settings, their direct extension to online fine-tuning has shown limitations in exploration.

Specifically, the authors conducted an experiment with Flow Q-Learning (FQL) on a challenging task, `antmaze-giant-navigate`. This experiment demonstrated that the FQL agent, after offline pre-training, tended to stay confined to known paths observed in the offline dataset. This resulted in restricted exploration during online fine-tuning, preventing the agent from discovering alternative, potentially more optimal, routes and leading to suboptimal performance. This behavior highlights that a policy overly constrained by the offline data distribution, while beneficial for stability in a strictly offline context, can impede effective improvement when online interaction is permitted.

The conventional approach to address limited data coverage is to collect larger, more diverse datasets, which is often costly and time-consuming. Therefore, a key motivation for this paper is to enable policies to induce diverse behaviors from a given static dataset without increasing its size.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Develop a novel policy learning approach**: To introduce a method, Flow Matching with Injected Noise for Offline-to-Online RL (FINO), specifically designed for the offline-to-online RL framework.
2. **Enhance sample efficiency**: To improve the rate at which policies learn and adapt during the online fine-tuning phase, especially under limited interaction budgets.
3. **Promote policy diversity through noise injection**: To implement a training strategy during offline pre-training that injects controlled noise into the flow matching objective. This aims to explicitly encourage the policy to learn a broader range of actions and extend its support beyond the exact actions present in the offline dataset, thereby laying a foundation for robust exploration.
4. **Balance exploration and exploitation**: To introduce a mechanism for online fine-tuning that leverages the acquired action diversity for effective exploration while dynamically adjusting its behavior to balance exploration and exploitation. This is intended to adapt to the evolving policy and environment.
5. **Demonstrate consistent performance**: To empirically validate FINO's effectiveness across a diverse set of challenging environments, showing superior performance under limited online interaction budgets compared to existing baselines.

In essence, the research aims to bridge the gap between the expressivity of generative policies and the practical requirements of online exploration in offline-to-online RL, leading to more adaptable and sample-efficient agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Flow Matching with Injected Noise for Offline-to-Online RL (FINO), integrates two main components: a noise injection strategy for flow matching during offline pre-training and an entropy-guided sampling mechanism for online fine-tuning. FINO utilizes Flow Q-Learning (FQL) as its backbone, which applies flow matching to policy design in offline RL.

**Preliminaries:**
- **Offline-to-Online RL:** This framework consists of two stages. In offline pre-training, a policy is learned from a static dataset. In online fine-tuning, the pre-trained policy interacts directly with the environment to refine its behavior.
- **Flow Matching:** This is a generative modeling framework that learns a time-dependent vector field to transform a simple base distribution (e.g., Gaussian noise) into a complex target data distribution via ordinary differential equations (ODEs). The training objective typically minimizes the difference between the learned vector field and the direction of linear paths connecting samples from the base and target distributions.
- **Flow Q-Learning (FQL):** FQL formulates the policy as a state-conditioned flow model. It trains this flow model using a behavior cloning objective adapted from flow matching, mapping a state and noise to an action. FQL also introduces a one-step policy, jointly optimized through distillation from the flow policy and action-value maximization, to enable efficient action selection during environment interaction.

#### 3.1. Noise Injection for Flow Matching

FINO modifies the standard flow matching objective to encourage the policy to learn a wider action space beyond the observed offline data. Standard FQL sets a small constant `σ_min` (effectively zero) in its conditional probability path formulation, causing the distribution to concentrate tightly around individual data points. This narrow coverage limits online exploration.

FINO introduces a noise-injected training scheme by modifying the flow matching objective:
`L_FINO(θ) = E [ ||v_θ(t, s, x_t + ε_t) - (x_1 - (1 - η)x_0)||² ]`
where `ε_t ~ N(0, α_t² I)` is injected noise with a scheduled variance `α_t² = (η² - 2η)t² + (2η)t`.
- `η` is a hyperparameter controlling the variance of the injected noise. When `η=0`, this objective reduces to standard flow matching.
- At `t=0`, `α_0² = 0`, and at `t=1`, `α_1² = η² > 0`, ensuring noise injection specifically at the data distribution end of the flow.

Theoretical results support this design:
- **Proposition 1**: The injected noise induces conditional probability paths `p_FINO_t(x|x_i)` with a mean `tx_i` (same as standard flow matching) but a variance `(1 - (1 - η)t)² I` which is greater than or equal to that of standard flow matching.
- **Theorem 1**: The conditional probability paths induce a unique conditional vector field `v_t(x|x_i) = x_i - (1 - η)x_0`, ensuring the validity of the continuous normalizing flow and satisfying the continuity equation.
- **Theorem 2**: The marginal probability path `p_FINO_t(x)` induced by FINO exhibits greater or equal variance compared to standard flow matching, especially at `t=1`. This implies that the FINO-trained model represents wider action regions, making it suitable for exploration.

A toy experiment demonstrates this: standard flow matching concentrates samples tightly around data points, while FINO with noise injection covers a wider, yet plausibly centered, region of the action space. This expanded flow model, in turn, guides the one-step policy (trained via distillation and action-value maximization) to consider actions from a broader, more informative region of the action space, facilitating efficient exploration.

#### 3.2. Entropy-Guided Sampling

During online fine-tuning, FINO leverages the diversified policy for exploration while maintaining a balance with exploitation.
- **Candidate Action Sampling**: For a given state, the agent samples `N_sample` candidate actions from the policy using multiple base noises.
- **Sampling Distribution**: Instead of deterministically selecting the highest Q-value action, FINO constructs a sampling distribution over candidate actions:
 `p_sampling(i) = exp(ξ · Q_ϕ(s, a_i)) / Σ_j exp(ξ · Q_ϕ(s, a_j))`
 where `ξ` is a temperature parameter. A smaller `ξ` promotes uniform sampling (exploration), while a larger `ξ` favors greedy actions (exploitation).
- **Adaptive Temperature `ξ`**: To dynamically balance exploration and exploitation, `ξ` is adjusted based on the policy's entropy `H`:
 `ξ_new = ξ - α_ξ [H - H_bar]`
 where `H_bar` is the target entropy and `α_ξ` is the learning rate. This adaptation ensures the sampling strategy evolves with the policy's learning progress.
- **Inference**: At inference time, the agent deterministically selects the action with the highest action-value.

#### 3.3. Practical Implementation

- **Backbone**: FQL, with its one-step policy used for environment interaction.
- **Entropy Estimation**: Due to the intractable distribution of the one-step policy, policy entropy is approximated using a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) fitted to multiple actions sampled from the same state (as in prior work).
- **Hyperparameters**: `η` is set to `0.1` for actions in `[-1, 1]`. `N_sample` is set to half of the action dimension to balance diversity and computational cost. A smooth shifted exponential schedule is used for `α_t`. These hyperparameters are fixed throughout training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of FINO was empirically evaluated across a suite of challenging reinforcement learning tasks from OGBench and D4RL, comparing its performance against several established baselines.

**Environments:**
- **OGBench (35 tasks):** A benchmark designed for offline goal-conditioned RL, adapted to a single-task setting. It includes complex navigation tasks (e.g., `humanoidmaze`, `antmaze`, `antsoccer`) and manipulation tasks (e.g., `cube`, `puzzle`).
- **D4RL (10 tasks):** A widely adopted benchmark, including `antmaze` variants and high-dimensional `adroit` dexterous manipulation tasks (`pen`, `door`, `hammer`, `relocate`).

**Baselines:**
1. **ReBRAC:** A Gaussian policy-based method known for strong performance in offline-to-online RL.
2. **Cal-QL:** An offline-to-online RL algorithm extending Conservative Q-Learning (CQL).
3. **RLPD:** An online RL algorithm initialized with offline data, focusing on online training.
4. **IFQL:** An adaptation of Implicit Q-Learning (IDQL) to the flow matching setting, sampling multiple actions.
5. **FQL:** The Flow Q-Learning algorithm, which serves as the backbone for FINO.

**Evaluation Protocol:**
All methods were subjected to a consistent protocol: 1 million offline pre-training steps followed by 500,000 online fine-tuning steps. Performance was measured both after offline pre-training and at the end of online fine-tuning. Results were averaged over 10 random seeds, reporting mean and 95% confidence intervals. Success rate was used for OGBench and D4RL `antmaze` tasks, while normalized scores were used for D4RL `adroit` tasks.

**Overall Performance (Table 1, Figure 3):**
FINO consistently achieved strong performance across the diverse range of evaluated environments. Crucially, it demonstrated significant performance gains during online fine-tuning without degrading the initial offline performance, aligning with theoretical predictions that the method preserves the mean of the probability path while increasing variance.
- **Comparison to ReBRAC:** While ReBRAC performed well on some `antmaze` tasks, it struggled in more complex `humanoidmaze` environments, indicating limitations of conventional policies in such settings.
- **Comparison to IFQL:** FINO outperformed IFQL, suggesting that merely sampling multiple action candidates is insufficient; the *way* diversity is introduced and leveraged is critical.
- **Comparison to FQL (Backbone):** FINO showed notable improvements over its FQL backbone, particularly in navigation tasks where effective exploration is paramount. This highlights the effectiveness of FINO's specialized design for online fine-tuning, demonstrating both efficient exploration and a balanced exploration-exploitation trade-off. Aggregate learning curves further illustrate FINO's consistent superiority and high sample efficiency.

**Ablation Studies and Discussion:**
- **Impact of Noise Injection Point (Figure 4):** A comparison between FINO's noise injection into the flow matching objective and direct Gaussian noise injection into policy actions ("Action Noise") showed FINO's superior performance. Direct action noise sometimes failed to facilitate exploration or even degraded performance (e.g., `door-cloned`). This indicates that FINO's approach, which enables the one-step policy to maximize action-value over an expanded action space, is more informed and effective than simple random perturbations.
- **Comparison with Entropy-Regulated Noise Scaling (Figure 5):** When compared against a baseline that scales Gaussian noise based on policy entropy ("ER-Noise"), FINO's entropy-guided sampling mechanism demonstrated significantly better performance. This suggests that dynamically balancing exploration and exploitation through controlled action sampling is more effective than directly scaling noise, especially in high-dimensional action spaces where finding relevant actions is challenging.
- **Analysis of Noise Injection and Entropy-Guided Sampling (Figure 6, left):** Experiments evaluating individual components ("w/o Noise" and "w/o Guidance") confirmed their critical roles. Removing noise injection resulted in policies confined to the offline dataset, leading to insufficient exploration and reduced performance. Lacking entropy guidance led to performance degradation in later stages, indicating a failure to maintain an appropriate exploration-exploitation balance. Both components are thus essential for sample-efficient learning.
- **Training and Inference Efficiency (Figure 6, middle & right):** FINO exhibited a minor increase in training time compared to FQL but remained competitive with other generative-model-based algorithms like Cal-QL. Inference time was also modest, requiring fewer samples than baselines like IFQL, indicating that the added computational overhead is not substantial.
- **Effect of Action Sample Size (N_sample) (Figure 7):** Performance generally improved with increased `N_sample`, but marginal gains diminished. The chosen `N_sample` (half of the action dimension) represents a practical balance between performance and computational cost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents FINO, a novel methodology that effectively integrates generative models, specifically flow matching, into the offline-to-online reinforcement learning paradigm. The core contributions lie in its two main components: noise injection during offline pre-training and entropy-guided sampling during online fine-tuning.

**Significance:**
1. **Enhanced Exploration in Offline-to-Online RL:** FINO directly addresses the challenge of limited exploration faced by generative policies in offline-to-online settings. By injecting noise into the flow matching objective, it proactively broadens the policy's action space during pre-training, providing a more robust foundation for online exploration. This is significant because it allows agents to venture beyond the strict confines of the offline dataset in a controlled and informed manner, which is crucial for improving performance in complex environments.
2. **Adaptive Exploration-Exploitation Balance:** The entropy-guided sampling mechanism offers a dynamic approach to manage the exploration-exploitation trade-off during online fine-tuning. This adaptability is critical under limited online interaction budgets, enabling the policy to adjust its behavior based on its evolving uncertainty (entropy). This contrasts with static exploration strategies and leads to more sample-efficient learning.
3. **Theoretical and Empirical Validation:** The paper provides theoretical proofs (Theorems 1 and 2) supporting the validity of the noise-injected flow matching formulation and demonstrating its capacity to increase the variance of the marginal probability path, thereby expanding action coverage. These theoretical underpinnings are complemented by extensive empirical evaluations across diverse and challenging benchmarks (OGBench and D4RL), where FINO consistently outperforms various baselines.
4. **Practical Efficiency:** Despite incorporating generative models and additional sampling mechanisms, FINO maintains a modest computational overhead for both training and inference, as demonstrated by efficiency comparisons. This indicates its practical applicability in real-world scenarios where computational resources may be constrained.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement of Generative Policies in RL:** This work demonstrates a principled way to leverage the expressivity of generative models, particularly flow matching, to enhance exploration capabilities in RL. This could inspire further research into designing generative policies that are inherently better suited for dynamic online learning scenarios.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** By facilitating more effective exploration and balancing the exploration-exploitation trade-off, FINO can lead to more sample-efficient learning in offline-to-online RL. This is particularly impactful in applications where environment interactions are costly or time-consuming, such as robotics or autonomous systems.
- **Broader Applicability:** The method's effectiveness across diverse tasks, including complex navigation and high-dimensional manipulation environments, suggests its potential for broad application in various domains requiring adaptable agents.
- **New Directions for Offline-to-Online RL:** The study highlights the importance of designing offline pre-training with subsequent online learning in mind, rather than treating online fine-tuning as a mere continuation. This perspective could open new research avenues for optimizing the two-stage learning process.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the GMM-based approximation of entropy and the unaddressed issue of distribution shift, FINO represents a substantial step toward harnessing the full potential of generative policies in the challenging offline-to-online RL paradigm.
