# Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> DPPO introduces a policy optimization framework for fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion-based robot policies using policy gradient methods, demonstrating robust training, improved performance, and a substantial reduction in the sim-to-real gap on various manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Pre-trained robot policies, particularly those acquired via behavior cloning from expert demonstrations, often yield suboptimal performance due to limitations in the demonstration data.
- Fine-tuning diffusion-based robot policies with reinforcement learning (RL) has been challenging, especially with policy gradient (PG) methods, which were widely considered inefficient or unstable for this policy class.
- Existing RL approaches for improving diffusion policies often relied on off-policy Q-learning or weighted regression, which could suffer from instability in complex, continuous control tasks.

## Method

- Proposes **Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization** (DPPO), a novel framework that conceptualizes the multi-step diffusion denoising process as an embedded ==Markov Decision Process (MDP)==.
- Applies Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) within this two-layer MDP structure, allowing task rewards to effectively influence the entire iterative denoising process to optimize action selection.
- Incorporates practical design choices such as fine-tuning only a subset of denoising steps, leveraging DDIM sampling with exploration noise, and modifying noise scheduling for improved training stability and efficiency.

## Results

- DPPO consistently outperformed or matched alternative diffusion-based and demo-augmented RL methods across various OpenAI Gym, ROBOMIMIC, and Franka Kitchen tasks, demonstrating superior training stability and final performance.
- It was the first RL algorithm to solve the challenging ROBOMIMIC `Transport` task from pixel input to high success rates (>**50%**) and significantly outperformed unimodal Gaussian and Gaussian Mixture Model policy parameterizations fine-tuned with PPO.
- Policies trained with DPPO demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving an **80%** success rate (16/20 trials) on a real robot for a furniture assembly task, where other policy types failed entirely.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models enable structured, on-manifold exploration during fine-tuning, allowing policies to efficiently search within relevant success modes near the pre-training data manifold.
> - The multi-step denoising process of diffusion policies contributes to exceptional training stability for policy gradient updates, making them more robust against noise and enabling the learning of long action chunks.
> - Policies fine-tuned with DPPO exhibit superior robustness to dynamic perturbations and initial state changes, leading to a much smaller sim-to-real gap and successful zero-shot deployment on physical robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen a paradigm shift towards large-scale pre-training followed by fine-tuning, notably in natural language processing and computer vision with the emergence of foundation models. This pipeline is rapidly being adopted in robot learning, where behavior cloning (BC) from expert demonstrations has become a dominant approach for pre-training robot policies. While BC offers a stable way to acquire initial skills, its performance can be suboptimal due to limitations in the expert data, such as suboptimal quality or insufficient coverage of diverse environment conditions.

This paper addresses the critical need to efficiently fine-tune these pre-trained policies using reinforcement learning (RL). RL, with its inherent ability to optimize policies through interaction with the environment, is a natural candidate for refining skills beyond the scope of initial demonstrations. However, a significant challenge arises when these pre-trained policies are parameterized as diffusion models. Diffusion models have gained prominence as a leading policy parameterization in robotics (e.g., Diffusion Policy) due to their high training stability and ability to represent complex, multi-modal action distributions. Despite these advantages, prior work and common belief have "conjectured [policy gradient] to be less efficient" for fine-tuning diffusion-based policies in continuous control tasks (e.g., Psenka et al., Yang et al.). Consequently, much of the existing research on improving diffusion-based policies via RL has focused on off-policy Q-learning or weighted regression methods.

"Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization (DPPO)" directly challenges this prevailing conjecture. It carves out a new path by demonstrating that policy gradient (PG) methods can, surprisingly, be highly effective and even achieve "the strongest overall performance and efficiency for fine-tuning" diffusion-based policies in common benchmarks. This work positions itself at the intersection of generative models (diffusion models) and online optimization (RL policy gradients), aiming to unlock the full potential of diffusion policies by enabling their robust and efficient fine-tuning through a well-established and theoretically sound RL paradigm. By showing the viability and superiority of PG for this specific policy class, DPPO opens new avenues for leveraging the strengths of both diffusion models and reinforcement learning in real-world robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and validate Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization (DPPO), a novel algorithmic framework for fine-tuning diffusion-based policies in continuous control and robot learning tasks using policy gradient (PG) methods from reinforcement learning (RL). The authors are motivated by the recognition that while large-scale pre-training with behavior cloning is emerging as a dominant paradigm for robot policies, it often yields suboptimal performance due to limitations in demonstration data. Reinforcement learning is a natural fit for further optimization, but its application to diffusion models, particularly with policy gradients, has been perceived as inefficient or challenging.

The paper sets out to achieve several key objectives, structured as four main contributions:

1. **Introduce DPPO as a Generic Framework:** The core objective is to propose DPPO, comprising a general algorithmic framework and a set of carefully chosen design decisions. This framework aims to enable robust and high-performing fine-tuning of pre-trained diffusion-based robot learning policies using popular PG methods like PPO, directly challenging the prior belief that such methods are inefficient for diffusion policies.
2. **Demonstrate DPPO's Performance:** A crucial objective is to empirically show that DPPO yields strong training stability and significant improvements in final performance across various challenging robotic tasks. This involves extensive comparisons against a range of alternative RL methods for diffusion policies (including Q-learning and weighted regression) and other demo-augmented RL methods, as well as PG fine-tuning of alternative policy parameterizations (e.g., Gaussian, Gaussian Mixture Models).
3. **Understand the Mechanism of DPPO's Success:** Beyond demonstrating performance, the authors aim to provide insight into *why* DPPO is so effective. This involves investigative experiments to uncover unique synergies between RL fine-tuning and the diffusion parameterization, such as structured and on-manifold exploration, training stability, and robust policy generalization. They also seek to show how their specific design decisions overcome speculated limitations of PG methods.
4. **Tackle Challenging Robotic Tasks and Settings:** A practical objective is to validate DPPO's efficacy in realistic and demanding scenarios. This includes tasks with pixel observations, long-horizon multi-stage manipulation, and, crucially, zero-shot deployment of simulation-trained policies on real robot hardware, thereby demonstrating strong sim-to-real transfer capabilities.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between the expressive power and stability of diffusion models as policy parameterizations and the interactive optimization capabilities of policy gradient RL, thereby enabling the development of more robust, high-performing, and generalizable robot policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DPPO's methodology hinges on a novel conceptualization of the diffusion denoising process as an embedded Markov Decision Process (MDP), which facilitates the application of policy gradient methods.

**Conceptual Framework: Two-Layer "Diffusion Policy MDP"**
The core innovation is to treat the multi-step denoising process of a diffusion model not as a static generative process, but as an inner MDP nested within the conventional environment MDP.
- **Environment MDP (M_ENV):** The standard robot control problem where the agent observes state `s_t`, takes an action `a_t`, receives a reward `R(s_t, a_t)`, and transitions to `s_{t+1}`.
- **Diffusion MDP (M_DP):** For each environment step `t`, the policy samples an action chunk `a_0` (the actual action executed in the environment) by iteratively denoising a noisy input `a_K`. The authors conceptualize this denoising as a sequence of steps `k` (from `K` down to `0`). Each denoising step `k` involves a state `(s_t, a_{k+1})` and an "action" `a_k` (the next denoised version). The key insight is that the likelihood `p_theta(a_k | a_{k+1}, s_t)` at each denoising step is a tractable Gaussian, which is crucial for policy gradient methods. Rewards are only accumulated at the end of the denoising chain (when `a_0` is finalized and executed in the environment), but these rewards can then be propagated back through the denoising steps within `M_DP`. This two-layer structure allows the task reward signal to influence the entire denoising process.

**Policy Optimization with PPO**
DPPO instantiates the policy gradient update within this two-layer MDP using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a widely adopted and stable on-policy RL algorithm.
- **Advantage Estimation:** To estimate the advantage function for PPO updates, DPPO uses Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE). A critical design choice is that the value function `V_phi(s_t)` for advantage estimation *only depends on the environment state `s_t`*, not on the intermediate denoised actions `a_k`. This is found to be crucial for stability, especially in high-stochasticity diffusion policies and continuous action spaces. A "denoising discount" `gamma_DENOISE` is applied to downweight the contribution of noisier (earlier) denoising steps in the policy gradient.

**Best Practices and Design Decisions**
The paper proposes several practical modifications to optimize DPPO's performance and efficiency:
- **Fine-tuning Subset of Denoising Steps:** Instead of fine-tuning all `K` denoising steps, DPPO can fine-tune only the last `K'` steps (e.g., 10-20 out of 100). This significantly reduces computation and memory while maintaining performance, by keeping early denoising steps frozen using the pre-trained weights.
- **Fine-tuning DDIM Sampling:** DPPO can leverage Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIMs) during fine-tuning, which require far fewer sampling steps (`K_DDIM << K`). While DDIM is often deterministic, DPPO uses a positive `eta` (e.g., `eta=1`) during training to introduce necessary exploration noise, reverting to deterministic `eta=0` for evaluation.
- **Diffusion Noise Scheduling:** Modifications to the standard cosine noise schedule are introduced. Specifically, `sigma_k` (the noise level at step `k`) is clipped to a higher minimum value (`sigma_exp_min`) for exploration during action sampling. Separately, `sigma_k` is also clipped to a minimum value (`sigma_prob_min`) when evaluating Gaussian likelihoods for policy gradient updates, which improves training stability by preventing excessively large gradients from tiny variances.
- **Network Architecture:** The paper evaluates both Multi-layer Perceptron (MLP) and UNet architectures as policy backbones. MLP generally fine-tunes more stably with DPPO, while UNet allows for pre-training with larger action chunk sizes (`T_p`) for better prediction and fine-tuning with smaller executed chunk sizes (`T_a`) which is more amenable to policy gradient.

**Pre-training:** The policies are initially pre-trained using a standard behavior cloning (BC) objective, fitting the conditional noise prediction to expert demonstrations.

This comprehensive approach, combining a novel MDP formulation with practical algorithmic refinements, enables DPPO to effectively fine-tune diffusion-based policies, challenging previous assumptions about the incompatibility of policy gradients with this policy class.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DPPO demonstrates strong and consistent performance across a wide range of continuous control and robotic manipulation benchmarks, consistently outperforming or matching existing state-of-the-art methods.

- **Comparison to Diffusion-Based RL Algorithms:**
 - **OpenAI Gym Tasks (Hopper, Walker2D, HalfCheetah):** DPPO shows strong overall performance and training stability. While some baselines like IDQL and DIPO are competitive, others often exhibit worse performance and less stability, frequently dropping performance early in fine-tuning due to Q-value estimation inaccuracies.
 - **ROBOMIMIC Tasks (Lift, Can, Square, Transport):** DPPO emerges as the strongest performer, particularly excelling in the highly challenging `Transport` task. Q-learning-based methods (DQL, IDQL, QSM) and weighted regression methods (DRWR, DAWR) struggle with training instability in these sparse-reward, high-dimensional, continuous action-space tasks, especially `Transport`. DPPO’s on-policy nature and structured exploration prove more robust.

- **Comparison to Other Demo-Augmented RL Algorithms:**
 - **HalfCheetah-v2:** Off-policy methods like RLPD, Cal-QL, and IBRL sometimes achieve better sample efficiency than DPPO, leveraging larger replay buffers.
 - **Franka Kitchen Tasks:** DPPO generally achieves the best performance, especially with complete demonstrations (`Kitchen-Complete-v0`). RLPD and IBRL struggle with noisier and partial demonstration datasets (`Kitchen-Partial-v0`, `Kitchen-Mixed-v0`). Cal-QL is competitive but DPPO often shows superior asymptotic performance. The authors note that DPPO's performance can be sensitive to the quality of pre-training in highly multimodal tasks if the demonstrations are incomplete.
 - **ROBOMIMIC (with PH/MH data):** DPPO consistently achieves much stronger final performance and faster wall-clock times compared to RLPD, Cal-QL, and IBRL. The latter methods often fail to learn or saturate at lower success rates. DPPO efficiently utilizes parallelized environments, leading to its speed advantage.

- **Comparison to Other Policy Parameterizations (PPO fine-tuning):**
 - **ROBOMIMIC (State and Pixel Input):** DPPO (with MLP or UNet backbones) significantly outperforms unimodal Gaussian and Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) policies fine-tuned with PPO. DPPO achieves faster convergence and higher final success rates across all tasks, notably achieving >90% success in the demanding `Transport` task with state input, and being the *first* RL algorithm to solve `Transport` from pixel input to high (>50%) success rates. This highlights DPPO's ability to represent and optimize complex policies better.

- **Evaluation on Furniture-Bench and Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
 - **Simulated Furniture Assembly:** DPPO demonstrates strong training stability and performance improvement in long-horizon, multi-stage tasks (`One-leg`, `Lamp`, `Round-table`), even with `Med` randomness. In contrast, Gaussian-MLP policies frequently collapse to zero success rate in these challenging settings.
 - **Zero-shot Sim-to-Real (One-leg):** A policy trained in simulation with DPPO achieves a remarkable 80% success rate (16/20 trials) on real robot hardware without any real-world fine-tuning. This contrasts sharply with the Gaussian policy (88% sim success), which fails entirely (0% real success) due to volatile and jittery behavior. Even a BC-regularized Gaussian baseline performs poorly in real-world deployment (50% success). This underscores DPPO's strong sim-to-real capabilities and inherent robustness.

- **Ablation Studies (Summary):** Key findings confirm that a value estimator dependent only on the environment state is crucial, there's an optimal range for denoising noise clipping for exploration, fine-tuning fewer denoising steps yields efficiency without sacrificing performance, and DPPO is robust to varying expert data amounts.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate DPPO's superiority in fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion policies across a wide array of challenging continuous control and robot manipulation tasks, highlighting its training stability, higher final performance, and remarkable sim-to-real transfer capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization" carries significant implications for the future of robot learning and potentially broader fields where diffusion models are employed. Its primary significance lies in fundamentally challenging and overturning a prevailing conjecture: that policy gradient methods are inefficient for fine-tuning diffusion-based policies in continuous control. DPPO not only demonstrates their effectiveness but proves their superiority over many existing RL fine-tuning approaches.

The authors identify three key mechanisms contributing to DPPO's strong performance, which collectively highlight its unique advantages:

1. **Structured, On-Manifold Exploration:** Unlike traditional Gaussian policies that inject unstructured noise into final actions, diffusion models, through their multi-step denoising process, enable exploration that is "structured" and tends to stay "near the pre-training data manifold." Each denoising step iteratively refines the action while adding noise, expanding coverage within the learned data distribution without venturing aggressively into irrelevant regions. This is particularly beneficial for fine-tuning, as it allows efficient exploration of relevant success modes identified during pre-training, enhancing sample efficiency and learning stability.
2. **Training Stability from Multi-step Denoising:** DPPO exhibits remarkable training stability. The iterative nature of the denoising process, where the action distribution is progressively refined, makes the policy updates more robust. This contrasts with single-step action generation which can be prone to collapse. This stability is particularly evident when faced with injected noise during training or when dealing with long action chunks, critical for complex, long-horizon tasks.
3. **Robust and Generalizable Fine-tuned Policy:** The policies fine-tuned with DPPO demonstrate superior robustness to perturbations in dynamics and initial state distributions. This inherent robustness, likely stemming from the diffusion model's ability to deconvolve noise, translates directly into a significantly smaller sim-to-real gap, enabling successful zero-shot deployment on physical robots—a critical bottleneck in real-world robotics. The qualitative observation of "corrective behaviors" further underscores this robustness.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Robotics:** DPPO is poised to become a vital component in the pre-training-plus-fine-tuning pipeline for real-world robotic policies. Its ability to robustly fine-tune complex skills from imperfect demonstrations, coupled with excellent sim-to-real transfer, accelerates the development and deployment of general-purpose robots capable of handling diverse and challenging tasks. This could lead to more agile and adaptable robotic systems in manufacturing, logistics, and service industries.
- **Beyond Robotics:** The framework's generality extends its potential impact to other interactive sequential decision-making domains where diffusion models are gaining traction:
 - **Generative AI:** Fine-tuning diffusion-based text-to-image generation models in multi-turn interactive settings with human feedback (e.g., for personalized content creation).
 - **Drug Discovery:** Applying policy search on a molecular level in feedback with simulators for drug design and discovery, building upon prior RL applications in this domain.
 - **Language Modeling:** Adapting diffusion-based language models to interactive problem-solving and planning tasks, where sequential decision-making is key.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
While highly impactful, the paper acknowledges that DPPO's sample efficiency can be lower than some off-policy methods in specific scenarios. Future work could explore integrating DPPO with model-based planning or video prediction techniques to improve sample efficiency, making it more practical for physical hardware training or generative simulations. Further investigation into optimal denoising step curricula for training efficiency and broader sim-to-real transfer with vision-based policies are also highlighted as promising avenues.

In conclusion, DPPO represents a significant step forward in optimizing robot policies by effectively marrying the expressive power of diffusion models with the interactive learning capabilities of policy gradients, opening doors for more capable, stable, and robust AI systems in both virtual and real-world environments.
