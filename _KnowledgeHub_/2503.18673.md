# Any6D: Model-free 6D Pose Estimation of Novel Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> Any6D estimates 6D pose and size of novel objects using only a single RGB-D anchor image, no CAD model or multi-view reference needed. It reconstructs a normalized 3D shape via image-to-3D, jointly refines metric scale and pose through a coarse-to-fine object alignment, then applies render-and-compare pose selection, sharply outperforming prior model-free baselines across five real-world datasets.

## Problem

- Instance-level and category-level pose methods need exact textured CAD models or predefined categories, blocking generalization to unseen objects without fine-tuning.
- Model-free methods like Oryon rely on partial-to-partial point matching between anchor and query views, degrading badly under occlusion or minimal overlap.
- Existing image-to-3D reconstruction methods achieve photorealistic shape but neglect metric-scale 2D-3D alignment, making generated shapes unusable for accurate pose estimation.

## Method

- ==Coarse object alignment==: initializes object size and pose by aligning ==oriented bounding boxes== between the anchor image point cloud and the normalized image-to-3D shape via IoU-maximizing rotation search.
- ==Fine object alignment==: joint size-and-pose refinement module extending ==FoundationPose=='s ==render-and-compare== sampling to jointly sample ==SO(3)== rotations and object-size scales (0.6-1.4x range), alternating refinement and axis alignment.
- ==Full-to-partial matching== pipeline: reconstructs a complete 3D object shape from a single anchor image (via ==InstantMesh==) rather than partial point clouds, enabling robust render-and-compare pose selection under occlusion and non-overlapping views. Together these form **Any6D**.

## Results

- On HO3D, Any6D reaches mean ADD-S/ADD/AR of **98.7**/**40.4**/**38.3** versus Oryon's **4.1**/**0**/**0.2** and Gedi's **71.9**/**9.7**/**7.4**, a large margin under hand occlusion.
- On Toyota-Light and REAL275, Any6D beats Oryon by **9.3** and **18.6** points in ADD(-S) respectively, showing robustness to lighting shifts and viewpoint variation.
- Ablation on HO3D shows removing all alignment steps collapses ADD-S/ADD/AR to **0**/**0**/**0**, while full pipeline (coarse size + refinement + axis alignment) reaches **98.7**/**40.4**/**38.3**, confirming each component's necessity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Full object shape reconstruction beats partial point-cloud matching when occlusion or non-overlapping views break shared-feature assumptions between anchor and query.
> - Image-to-3D generation models produce visually convincing but metrically unscaled shapes, so downstream pose tasks need an explicit size-alignment step, not just shape quality.
> - Jointly sampling size alongside rotation during pose refinement, rather than treating scale as fixed, is necessary when no ground-truth CAD model anchors the metric scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

#### 1.1. Broader Research Landscape in 6D Object Pose Estimation

Object 6D pose estimation, which involves determining the rigid 6D transformation (3D orientation and 3D translation) of an object relative to a camera, is a fundamental problem in computer vision and robotics. Its importance spans various applications, including robotic manipulation, augmented reality, and industrial automation. Over the past decade, significant progress has been made, driven by advancements in deep learning and 3D vision.

The field can be broadly categorized into three approaches:
- **Instance-level methods:** These approaches provide high precision but require exact 3D models (e.g., CAD models with textures) of specific objects. Their primary limitation is the lack of generalization; they can only estimate poses for objects seen during training, necessitating laborious data collection and fine-tuning for new objects.
- **Category-level methods:** Aiming to generalize beyond specific instances, these methods leverage category-specific prior knowledge (e.g., all "chairs" share some canonical shape). While more flexible, they are still restricted to predefined object categories and face challenges in acquiring comprehensive training datasets for canonical pose alignment.
- **Category-agnostic methods:** Representing the frontier of research, these methods seek to generalize across arbitrary, novel objects without prior knowledge of instances or categories. This approach offers the most flexibility for real-world scenarios where objects are often unknown or unanticipated.

Within category-agnostic methods, a further distinction is made:
- **Model-based methods:** These still require a textured 3D CAD model of the object at test time, even if it's a "novel" object (i.e., not seen during training, but its model is available for inference).
- **Model-free methods:** These eliminate the need for explicit 3D models during inference, relying instead on reference images or video sequences of the target object. Early model-free methods often required multiple reference views or video streams (e.g., using Structure-from-Motion, SfM) to reconstruct a 3D point cloud or mesh for pose estimation.

Despite these advancements, existing model-free methods still face significant practical limitations. The dependency on multiple reference views, often requiring known camera poses, is impractical in many real-world robotic applications where a robot might encounter an entirely new object with only a single view available, without prior 3D models or extensive multi-view data. Recent work, such as Oryon, attempted to address this by using a single RGB-D reference image, sometimes with language guidance. However, these methods often struggle with challenges like severe occlusions, non-overlapping views between reference and target images, or objects lacking sufficient texture, as they rely heavily on establishing 2D-3D correspondences which diminish in such difficult conditions.

Any6D emerges in this context as a direct response to these unaddressed challenges. It pushes the boundaries of model-free 6D pose estimation by reducing the dependency to a *single RGB-D anchor image* for *novel objects*, even in highly challenging scenarios with occlusions, non-overlapping views, and diverse lighting. It aims to bridge the gap between impressive image-to-3D generation capabilities and the critical need for metric-scale accuracy in real-world pose estimation.

### 2. Research Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Any6D is to develop a **model-free framework for 6D object pose and size estimation of novel objects using only a single RGB-D anchor image**. This singular constraint dramatically expands the practical applicability of 6D pose estimation, particularly in dynamic and unpredictable environments like those encountered by robotic manipulators.

The motivations behind Any6D are rooted in the limitations of existing state-of-the-art methods:
1. **Dependency on prior object data:** Both instance-level and many category-level methods require precise 3D CAD models or extensive training data for specific objects or categories. Even advanced model-free approaches like Gen6D or OnePose++ necessitate multiview reference images or video sequences, often with known camera poses. This dependency severely limits their effectiveness when a robot encounters an unexpected, previously unseen object in a new environment without readily available 3D models or the ability to capture multiple reference views.
2. **Fragility to occlusions and viewpoint variations:** Methods like Oryon, which utilize a single RGB-D reference, rely on establishing correspondences between images or visible point clouds. This approach degrades significantly when there are occlusions (e.g., by human or robotic arms), minimal overlap between reference and target object regions, or when objects lack sufficient texture. Such conditions are commonplace in real-world manipulation tasks, leading to unreliable pose estimates.
3. **Lack of metric scale in 3D reconstruction:** While recent advancements in image-to-3D models (e.g., InstantMesh, Wonder3D) can generate compelling 3D shapes from a single RGB image, they often produce objects in a normalized space (e.g., [-1, 1] for each axis). This normalized representation lacks metric scale, which is absolutely crucial for accurate 6D pose estimation and downstream robotic tasks requiring precise physical dimensions. Existing image-to-3D methods typically neglect the critical aspect of 2D-3D alignment and metric scale.

To overcome these fundamental barriers, Any6D aims to:
- Accurately estimate both the 6D pose and the *metric size* of unknown objects from a minimal reference (single RGB-D image).
- Develop a robust approach that performs reliably even under challenging real-world conditions, including severe occlusions, large cross-environment variations, non-overlapping viewpoints, and diverse lighting.
- Integrate and refine image-to-3D generation techniques to ensure accurate 2D-3D alignment and metric scale estimation, which has been a persistent challenge.

By addressing these issues, Any6D seeks to unlock more flexible and autonomous robot manipulation, augmented reality, and other vision-based applications where prior object knowledge is limited.

### 3. Methodology and Approach: The Any6D Framework

Any6D is a novel model-free framework designed to estimate the relative 6D pose ($T_{A \to Q}$) between an RGB-D anchor image ($I_A$) and an RGB-D query image ($I_Q$). The core idea is to reconstruct a complete 3D object shape from the anchor image, estimate its true metric scale, and then use this scaled 3D model for robust "full-to-partial" matching with the query image, even when viewpoints are drastically different or occlusions are present.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Any6D Pipeline

The Any6D framework operates in two main stages:
1. **Metric-scale Object Shape Estimation:** From the single RGB-D anchor image ($I_A$), the method first reconstructs a *normalized* object shape ($O_N$) using an image-to-3D model. Critically, it then employs a novel "object alignment" process to determine the actual object's metric size ($s \in \mathbb{R}^3$) and its precise pose relative to the anchor image ($T_{O_M \to A}$), thereby transforming $O_N$ into a *metric-scale object shape* ($O_M$).
2. **Query Pose Estimation and Relative Pose Calculation:** With the reconstructed metric-scale object shape ($O_M$), Any6D then estimates the object's pose in the query image ($T_{O_M \to Q}$). Finally, the relative pose $T_{A \to Q}$ is derived by composing these two transformations: $T_{A \to Q} = (T_{O_M \to A})^{-1} \cdot T_{O_M \to Q}$. This full-to-partial matching strategy, using a complete 3D shape, enhances robustness compared to partial-to-partial matching.

#### 3.2. Coarse Object Alignment

A critical initial step is to obtain a metric-scale 3D representation of the object from the single anchor RGB-D image. Given the limitations of existing single-view metric-scale reconstruction methods, Any6D leverages recent RGB-based single-view reconstruction models, specifically **InstantMesh**, to generate an initial 3D mesh. However, InstantMesh, like most image-to-3D models, produces a normalized shape ($O_N$) typically within a fixed range (e.g., [-1, 1] per axis), lacking real-world metric scale and proper positioning.

To address this, Any6D introduces a "coarse object alignment" step:
- **Center Estimation:** Instead of relying on simple mean of points or axis-aligned bounding boxes (which are unreliable due to partial views and noise in RGB-D data), Any6D proposes using an *oriented bounding box* to determine a more robust coarse center for the object in the anchor image ($I_A$).
- **Initial Scaling and Rotation:** The method then samples various rotation angles and calculates the Intersection over Union (IoU) between the rotated bounding boxes of the anchor image's visible point cloud and the normalized 3D shape ($O_N$). The combination of rotation and scale that maximizes this IoU is used to transform $O_N$ into an initial, coarsely aligned, metric-scale object shape, denoted $O'_M$. This provides a foundational estimate of the object's size and orientation within the anchor's coordinate system.

#### 3.3. Fine Object Alignment

Following coarse alignment, the framework performs a "fine object alignment" to jointly refine both the object's pose and its metric size. This refinement is inspired by the "render-and-compare" strategy of FoundationPose, but uniquely adapted to simultaneously optimize for size.

The fine alignment process involves three intertwined modules:
- **Pose and Size Hypothesis Generation:** Unlike FoundationPose, which samples only pose (SO(3)) hypotheses, Any6D additionally samples different *size variations* along each axis within a predefined range (empirically set as [0.6, 1.4] of the coarse size estimate). This creates a set of combined pose-and-size hypotheses.
- **Refinement and Selection:** Each generated hypothesis (pose + size) is refined using a refinement module (adapted from). The refined hypotheses are then rendered and compared against the cropped observation of the query image. A two-level render-and-compare strategy is employed for selection:
 1. A pose ranking network evaluates each hypothesis by comparing its rendered view to the observed cropped image, producing an embedding that quantifies alignment quality.
 2. Self-attention is applied to the concatenated embeddings of all hypotheses to incorporate global context and generate final scores for selecting the optimal pose and size.
- **Axis Alignment:** An additional axis alignment step is integrated into the pose refinement process for more precise accuracy. This leverages the joint estimation of size and pose, leading to a more accurate alignment of the object's axes, preventing distortions in its proportions along the X, Y, and Z directions.

Once the optimal size and pose ($T_{O_M \to A}$) are determined for the anchor image, the reconstructed object shape is scaled accordingly to obtain the final metric-scale object shape ($O_M$). This $O_M$ is then used in conjunction with the query image ($I_Q$) to estimate the object's pose relative to the query camera ($T_{O_M \to Q}$), also using the render-and-compare strategy. The final relative pose from anchor to query ($T_{A \to Q}$) is computed by composing these transformations: $T_{A \to Q} = (T_{O_M \to A})^{-1} \cdot T_{O_M \to Q}$. This comprehensive strategy allows Any6D to effectively handle disjoint views, occlusions, and environmental variations that challenge correspondence-based methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Any6D was rigorously evaluated on five challenging real-world datasets, demonstrating its superior performance compared to state-of-the-art model-free 6D pose estimation methods.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Metrics

- **Datasets:**
 - **HO3D:** RGB-D images of human-hand object interactions, featuring large viewpoint variations and significant hand occlusions.
 - **YCBInEOTA:** Mid-range RGB-D images of dual-arm robot manipulations, with diverse viewpoints and robot arm occlusions.
 - **Toyota-Light:** Focuses on single-object pose estimation under extreme lighting variations.
 - **REAL275:** RGB-D images across different scenes with limited viewpoint variations and minor occlusions.
 - **LM-O (Linemod Occlusion):** RGB-D images in cluttered scenes with 12 textureless objects, featuring viewpoint variations and realistic occlusions.
- **Metrics:** The primary metrics used are based on the **Average Distance of Model Points (ADD)** and **ADD-S** (for symmetric objects), which measure the mean distance between predicted and ground-truth 3D model points. Results are reported as Area Under the Curve (AUC) and recall rate at 0.1 times the object diameter. Additionally, BOP challenge metrics are used: **Average Recall (AR)** of Visual Surface Discrepancy (VSD), Maximum Symmetry-aware Surface Distance (MSSD), and Maximum Symmetry-aware Projection Distance (MSPD), which evaluate recalls over multiple thresholds.
- **Baselines:** Any6D is compared against Oryon (multi-modal object pose estimation with language guidance), LoFTR (detector-free feature matching with transformers), Gedi (depth-based local descriptors for point cloud registration), and ObjectMatch (robust registration using canonical object correspondences).

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparison with State-of-the-Art

Any6D consistently outperformed all baseline methods across the five diverse datasets:

- **HO3D Dataset (Table 1):** Any6D achieved mean scores of 98.7% for ADD-S, 40.4% for ADD, and 38.3% for AR. This represents a substantial improvement over previous methods; for instance, Oryon scored 23.0% ADD-S, 0% ADD, and 1.0% AR, while Gedi scored 71.9% ADD-S, 9.7% ADD, and 7.4% AR. The near 100% ADD-S performance for many instances highlights Any6D's robustness in scenarios with human-hand interactions and occlusions.
- **YCBInEOAT Dataset (Table 2):** Any6D maintained its superiority with mean scores of 89.3% for ADD-S, 45.6% for ADD, and 37.5% for AR, significantly surpassing Gedi (82.7% ADD-S, 7.7% ADD, 14.0% AR). This validates its effectiveness in challenging robotic manipulation scenarios with occlusions and non-overlapping viewpoints.
- **Toyota-Light Dataset (Table 3):** Any6D demonstrated strong performance under varying lighting conditions, achieving 32.2% ADD(-S), 43.3% AR, 55.8% MSSD, and 58.4% MSPD. This consistently outperformed Oryon by significant margins (e.g., 9.3% higher ADD(-S)).
- **REAL275 Dataset (Table 4):** Any6D showed remarkable generalization across object categories, achieving 53.5% ADD(-S), 51.0% AR, 56.5% MSSD, and 65.3% MSPD. It substantially surpassed Oryon in most metrics, demonstrating superior robustness and versatility.
- **LM-O Dataset (Table 5):** In comparisons where the 3D model was predicted from a single image (aligning with GigaPose's setup), Any6D outperformed GigaPose, achieving 28.6% AR, 36.1% MSPD, 32.0% MSSD, and 17.6% VSD when using Wonder3D for image-to-3D. Even with InstantMesh, its performance (25.2% AR, 29.5% MSPD, 27.4% MSSD, 18.7% VSD) remained competitive or superior in some aspects. This is particularly noteworthy as GigaPose requires an initial pose for size determination, while Any6D estimates both from a single RGB-D image.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons (Figures 4 and 5) further underscore Any6D's effectiveness. In HO3D (Figure 4), where anchor images showed only partially visible objects and query images were occluded or from drastically different angles, Any6D consistently provided accurate pose estimates while Oryon, LoFTR, and Gedi struggled or failed due to their reliance on partial anchor information. Similar robustness was observed in YCBInEOAT (Figure 5), where Any6D accurately tracked objects manipulated by robot arms, handling occlusions from grippers and large viewpoint changes.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of Any6D's key components, especially its novel object alignment module (Table 6):
- **Object Shape:** Comparing different 3D reconstruction approaches, the study showed that Any6D's reconstructed shapes were more complete and accurate (lower Chamfer Distance) than a baseline, which suffered from missing regions, leading to better pose estimation.
- **Object Alignment Components:**
 - Using raw object shape estimation without any alignment resulted in complete failure (0.0 for all metrics).
 - Incorporating coarse size estimation significantly improved ADD metrics, demonstrating the foundational importance of initial size approximation.
 - Adding axis alignment further enhanced performance, particularly for ADD-S and AR, by correcting distortions in object proportions and improving pose accuracy.
 - The full Any6D method, combining coarse size estimation, refinement, and axis alignment, achieved the best results across all metrics, validating the synergistic effect of these components.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Future Directions

Any6D represents a significant advancement in the field of 6D object pose estimation, particularly for its ability to handle novel objects with minimal prior information. Its key contributions and impact stem from:

1. **Reduced Data Dependency:** By requiring only a single RGB-D anchor image, Any6D drastically reduces the data burden compared to traditional CAD-model-based or multi-view model-free methods. This is crucial for real-world robotic applications, where acquiring detailed 3D models or multiple posed reference views for every new object is often impractical or impossible.
2. **Robustness in Challenging Scenarios:** The framework's ability to accurately estimate pose and size under severe occlusions, non-overlapping viewpoints, diverse lighting, and large cross-environment variations is a major breakthrough. This robustness makes it suitable for complex manipulation tasks in unstructured environments, such as human-robot collaboration and service robotics.
3. **Joint Pose and Metric Size Estimation:** The novel joint object alignment process, which simultaneously refines metric scale and pose, addresses a critical limitation of previous image-to-3D generation models that output normalized shapes. This ensures the reconstructed object has accurate real-world dimensions, which is essential for precise robot grasping and interaction.
4. **Full-to-Partial Matching:** By reconstructing a complete 3D object shape from a single view and then using a render-and-compare strategy for full-to-partial matching, Any6D overcomes the limitations of correspondence-based methods that struggle with low overlap or occluded regions.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Robotics:** Any6D can enable more autonomous and versatile robotic manipulation systems, allowing robots to interact with arbitrary objects without prior programming or extensive training. This includes applications in manufacturing, logistics, and assistive robotics.
- **Augmented Reality (AR):** Accurate 6D pose estimation of real-world objects is fundamental for realistic AR experiences, allowing virtual content to be seamlessly anchored to physical objects.
- **General Computer Vision:** The framework's approach to integrating image-to-3D models with metric-scale alignment could inspire future research in single-view 3D understanding and reconstruction.

**Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge a current limitation: Any6D's performance is dependent on the initial accuracy of the 3D shape generated by the image-to-3D model, as it does not currently incorporate shape updating. A promising future direction is to integrate shape refinement or updating mechanisms into the framework. This would further enhance robustness, especially when dealing with objects for which the initial image-to-3D reconstruction might be imperfect, thereby broadening its applicability to even more challenging and diverse real-world scenarios.
