# Sim-to-Lab-to-Real: Safe Reinforcement Learning with Shielding and Generalization Guarantees

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Princeton University developed a "Sim-to-Lab-to-Real" framework for safe reinforcement learning, which integrates Hamilton-Jacobi reachability-based shielding with PAC-Bayes generalization guarantees. The framework enables the training of policies that demonstrate superior empirical safety and performance, along with probabilistic certificates for deployment in unseen environments, validated on a quadrupedal robot.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning policies trained in simulation often fail catastrophically in real-world deployment due to the "reality gap" and lack formal safety or generalization guarantees.
- Existing safe RL methods struggle to enforce hard safety constraints or rely on sparse violation signals, while model-based safety methods are often intractable for complex, high-dimensional, and unknown real-world scenarios with vision-based inputs.
- A lack of formal, probabilistic guarantees for generalization performance and safety hinders the trusted deployment of learning-based autonomous systems in safety-critical applications.

## Method

- A two-stage "Sim-to-Lab-to-Real" training pipeline is introduced, learning a safety-aware policy distribution through a dual-policy architecture (performance and backup) conditioned on latent variables.
- Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis is incorporated to train the backup policy with dense safety signals, enabling a "least-restrictive" shielding mechanism to enforce hard safety constraints.
- The PAC-Bayes framework is applied during the "Lab" stage to fine-tune the policy distribution, providing probabilistic generalization bounds for both performance and safety in unseen real-world environments.

## Results

- The framework demonstrated the lowest safety violation rates during Lab training across all environment settings, reducing violations by 4%-77% compared to previous safe RL methods.
- It achieved significantly tighter PAC-Bayes generalization bounds for task success and safety satisfaction, improving success bounds from 0.366 to 0.786 and safety bounds from 0.367 to 0.794 in challenging environments.
- Policies trained with the framework were successfully deployed on a Ghost Spirit quadrupedal robot in real indoor environments, achieving a 0.767 success rate and a 0.867 safety rate, outperforming baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining HJ reachability for dense, proactive safety signals with a value-based shielding mechanism effectively prevents safety violations during both training and deployment in complex, vision-based tasks.
> - Training a diverse distribution of policies by maximizing mutual information between latent variables and trajectories is crucial for achieving tighter PAC-Bayes generalization bounds and improved adaptation to diverse real-world conditions.
> - The "Lab" stage provides a critical intermediate step for safely fine-tuning policies and certifying their performance and safety, mirroring established industrial engineering practices for robust system deployment.
