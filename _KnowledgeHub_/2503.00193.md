# ProDapt: Proprioceptive Adaptation using Long-term Memory Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> ProDapt introduces a method for integrating long-term memory into diffusion-based robotic controllers using "keypoints," enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks solely with proprioceptive sensor data. This approach allows robots to navigate previously unmanageable obstacle courses, achieving up to 80% success in complex "Elbow" tasks where baseline methods fail, while maintaining real-time inference speeds.

## Problem

- Current diffusion-based robotic controllers heavily rely on exteroceptive sensors (e.g., cameras), making them vulnerable in environments where these sensors can fail or be occluded.
- Diffusion models, like most generative models, lack mechanisms for explicit long-term memory, leading to inefficient or failed behaviors in tasks requiring persistent awareness of past interactions or obstacles.
- Robotic systems operating in extreme environments (e.g., space, underwater) require high robustness and fault tolerance, often needing to operate purely on proprioceptive data due to exteroceptive sensor limitations.

## Method

- **ProDapt** integrates "keypoints" into the diffusion model's conditioning, where keypoints represent crucial past contact events between the robot and its environment.
- A "keypoint manager" identifies unique and informative contact events based on spatial distance and normal force direction, stores them, and feeds the last 10 keypoints to the diffusion model alongside recent proprioceptive observations.
- The diffusion model, a CNN U-Net architecture, is trained to output future actions (end-effector goal positions) conditioned on both the short-term proprioceptive history and the long-term memory keypoints.

## Results

- In simulation, ProDapt achieved **80%** success in a challenging "Elbow" task where all baseline diffusion models (even with extended observation horizons up to H_o=50) failed, demonstrating its ability to leverage long-term memory for complex navigation.
- ProDapt consistently completed tasks faster than baseline approaches, especially in scenarios like "Bucket" (**85.7%** success, faster completion) and "Elbow," while avoiding non-optimal emergent behaviors observed in baselines.
- ProDapt maintained inference times comparable to baselines with short observation horizons, making it practical for real-time control (e.g., 10 Hz), unlike baselines with long observation horizons (H_o=50) which exceeded real-time limits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a curated, explicit long-term memory (keypoints) into the conditioning of a diffusion model significantly enhances its ability to handle complex, proprioceptive-only navigation tasks.
> - This approach allows diffusion-based controllers to overcome limitations imposed by short observation horizons without incurring the high computational costs associated with simply extending the observation window.
> - By learning from past contacts, the robot can develop effective circumvention strategies for unknown obstacles, preventing repetitive collisions and enabling navigation through complex environments previously inaccessible to short-memory controllers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of imitation learning, diffusion models, and robust robotic control, particularly focusing on scenarios requiring operation with limited sensor modalities.

Diffusion models have emerged as a prominent machine learning framework, demonstrating capabilities in generating high-quality samples for various distributions. Their application has extended to imitation learning in robotics, where they are used to generate action sequences based on past observations, enabling robots to replicate complex behaviors across tasks like manipulation, soft robotics, and locomotion. However, a prevailing characteristic of these applications is their reliance on exteroceptive sensors, such as cameras or LiDAR, or task-specific state estimation systems.

The broader research landscape for robotic systems includes a critical requirement for robustness in challenging or compromised environments. In domains such as space exploration, underwater operations, and certain military applications, exteroceptive sensors can be susceptible to failure, damage, or occlusion by environmental factors like dust, mud, or debris. This necessitates the development of robotic control systems that can operate reliably using only proprioceptive information (e.g., joint angles, forces, torques). Existing work in this area includes fault-tolerant systems and approaches to "blind" navigation, such as wall-following or using recurrent neural networks without explicit mapping. However, these often represent specialized solutions or do not fully address general motion planning using contemporary generative models.

Furthermore, the integration of long-term memory is a recognized challenge in machine learning, particularly for sequential decision-making tasks. While architectures like Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), and Transformers have been developed to manage long-range dependencies, current generative models, including diffusion models, do not inherently possess mechanisms for retaining and leveraging long-term historical information over extended periods.

This paper addresses the gap by proposing a method to incorporate long-term memory into diffusion-based controllers specifically for proprioceptive-only navigation. It aims to combine the generative capabilities and expressivity of diffusion models with an explicit memory mechanism, enabling robust task completion in environments where exteroceptive data is unavailable or unreliable. The work differentiates itself from previous blind navigation strategies by leveraging a diffusion model for the entire control stack and introduces a generalizable memory concept for learning-based controllers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a method, termed ProDapt, for integrating long-term memory into learning-based robotic controllers, specifically within the framework of diffusion models. This integration aims to enable robots to complete complex manipulation tasks using solely proprioceptive sensor data.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical limitations observed in current robotics applications and diffusion models:

- **Dependence on Exteroceptive Sensors:** Current diffusion-based imitation learning policies often rely on exteroceptive sensors (e.g., cameras) to perceive the environment. This dependence creates vulnerabilities in challenging operational environments where such sensors can fail, become damaged, or be occluded.
- **Lack of Long-Term Memory in Diffusion Models:** Standard diffusion models, while effective at generating action sequences based on recent observations, typically lack a mechanism to retain and utilize information from events that occurred beyond a short observation horizon. For tasks requiring navigation around persistent obstacles or memory of past interactions, this limited memory can lead to inefficient or failing behaviors, such as repetitive collisions or getting trapped in local optima.
- **Requirement for Robustness in Extreme Environments:** Critical applications in domains like space exploration (e.g., NASA's Artemis program for lunar base construction), underwater robotics, and military operations demand high robustness and fault tolerance. In these settings, phenomena like lunar dust clouds or underwater sediment can severely impair visual sensors, making proprioceptive-only operation a necessary capability for reliable and safe task execution.
- **Addressing Inefficient Navigation:** As a motivating example, the paper describes a scenario where a robot must navigate past an unknown obstacle without exteroceptive sensors. A controller with only short-term memory might repeatedly collide, back up, and then forget the collision, leading to oscillatory behavior. Long-term memory is posited as essential to learn from past contacts and devise effective circumvention strategies.
- **Computational Efficiency:** While increasing the observation horizon (H_o) in diffusion models can extend short-term memory, it incurs higher computational costs, which may be prohibitive in resource-constrained applications like space robotics. The objective is to achieve long-term memory more efficiently.
- **Generalizable Approach:** The research also aims to propose a generalizable concept of incorporating long-term memory that could be applied to various learning-based controllers and types of observations, extending beyond just proprioception and diffusion models.

By addressing these points, the authors intend to enhance the reliability and autonomy of robotic systems operating in adverse conditions, moving towards full motion planning capabilities for blind robots without explicit environmental mapping.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, ProDapt, introduces "keypoints" as a mechanism to integrate long-term memory into diffusion-based imitation learning controllers, primarily for proprioceptive-only tasks.

**A. Problem Formulation and Diffusion Controller Baseline:**
The system is modeled as a discrete, time-invariant system where the state `x_k+1 = f(x_k, u_k)` transitions based on current state and control input. Observations `y_k = h(x_k)` are derived solely from proprioceptive sensors, preventing environmental reconstruction from single observations. The diffusion controllers are based on a receding horizon control approach, similar to. At each time step `k`, the controller `π` takes `H_o` previous observations `y_k-H_o+1:k` and outputs `H_p` future actions `u_k:k+H_p-1`. Only `H_a` of these actions are applied before the next control iteration.

**B. Keypoint Integration:**
The core innovation is the modification of the diffusion model's conditioning to include a curated list of keypoints. Equation 5, representing the standard diffusion policy, is extended to Equation 6:
`u_k:k+H_p-1 = π(y_k-H_o+1:k, q_1:N_kp)`
Here, `q_1:N_kp` represents up to `N_kp` keypoints, which are appended to the list of past observations fed to the diffusion model. If fewer than `N_kp` informative keypoints are available, the remaining inputs are zero-padded (or filled with padding tokens for transformer-based architectures).

**C. Keypoint Manager:**
A "keypoint manager" is responsible for identifying, storing, and selecting relevant past observations. For proprioceptive navigation, keypoints represent past contacts between the robot and its environment. The manager performs the following functions:
- **Contact Detection:** Identifies a contact event based on sensor readings. In the experimental setup, a contact is flagged when the end-effector's force-torque sensor registers a torque exceeding a threshold (`τ_min = 1 Nm`).
- **Keypoint Uniqueness and Information Content:** A detected contact is saved as a keypoint only if it is sufficiently distinct from previously recorded keypoints. Distinctness is defined by:
 - A minimum spatial distance (`d_min = 5 cm`) from all existing keypoints.
 - Alternatively, if within `d_min`, a minimum angular difference (`θ_min = 45°`) in the normal force direction from nearby keypoints, to capture contacts in concave regions like corners.
- **Keypoint Data:** Each keypoint `q_i` stores the x-y position of the end-effector during contact and the sine and cosine of the normal direction of the contact force.
- **Keypoint Selection:** For diffusion inference, the manager provides a maximum of `N_kp` keypoints. In the experiments, this was simplified to maintaining and providing the last `N_kp = 10` keypoints.

**D. Diffusion Model Architecture and Parameters:**
The diffusion models utilize a convolutional neural network (CNN) U-Net architecture, consistent with.
- **Prediction Horizon (H_p):** Set to 20.
- **Action Horizon (H_a):** Set to 10.
- **Observation Horizon (H_o):** Set to 3 for ProDapt (for baselines, H_o ranged from 3 to 50).
- **Diffusion Steps (N_diff):** Set to 45.
- **Conditioning Input:** Each observation comprises the x-y position of the end-effector and the x-y torque. ProDapt's conditioning also includes the `N_kp = 10` last keypoints.
- **Output:** The diffusion model outputs `H_p` actions, each specifying an x-y goal position for the end-effector (position control).
- **Warm-starting:** Inference steps are warm-started using the previous action trajectory as per.

**E. Experimental Setup:**
Experiments were conducted on a Universal Robots UR10e manipulator arm in both NVIDIA Isaac Sim simulation and a real-world environment.
- **Task:** Move the end-effector from a start to a goal point in the x-y plane, avoiding unknown obstacles, relying only on proprioceptive data.
- **Training Data:** 165 teleoperation trajectories were collected in simulation, with an operator guiding the UR10e through the task with random 15 cm cube obstacles, observing end-effector pose and force-torque readings.
- **Evaluation Scenarios (Fig. 3):** Four configurations were used to test performance: "Clear" (no obstacles), "Wall" (linear obstacle), "Bucket" (concave obstacle), and "Elbow" (complex, multi-stage obstacle requiring extended memory).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success rate (reaching goal within 5 cm in 1000 iterations), time to success, and diffusion inference time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation compared ProDapt (H_o=3 with 10 keypoints) against baseline diffusion models with varying observation horizons (H_o = 3, 6, 20, 50).

**A. Simulation Results:**
- **Clear and Wall Tasks:** All approaches achieved 100% success rates. ProDapt generally demonstrated faster completion times in the "Clear" task, moving more directly towards the goal. Baselines exhibited a tendency to drift diagonally before engaging in wall-following behavior. In the "Wall" task, completion times were similar across all approaches.
- **Bucket Task:** ProDapt achieved an 85.7% success rate, occasionally getting stuck. Baselines with short observation horizons (H_o=3, 6) largely failed this task, while those with longer horizons (H_o=20, 50) achieved 100% success. Among the successful trials, ProDapt exhibited faster completion times compared to all baselines.
- **Elbow Task:** ProDapt achieved an 80% success rate. In contrast, all baseline approaches (H_o=3, 6, 20, 50) failed this task (0% success). The baselines became trapped in infinite loops, navigating the vertical section of the elbow but forgetting the initial contact points, preventing them from finding a path around the obstacle. ProDapt, by retaining memory of the elbow's lower wall, was able to navigate past it. ProDapt's failures in this task were attributed to slow movement or idling in regions not adequately covered by training data, leading to exceeding the iteration limit.

**B. Real-World Experiments:**
Real-world experiments on a UR10e robot generally mirrored the simulation findings, demonstrating the robustness of the diffusion model to sim-to-real transfer.
- Similar to simulation, ProDapt demonstrated superior performance in the "Elbow" task, successfully navigating the complex obstacle while baselines failed.
- Baselines with low observation horizons (H_o=3, 6) showed notably worse performance in "Clear" and "Wall" tasks compared to simulation, sometimes failing even in these simpler scenarios.
- The H_o=20 baseline also had a lower success rate on the "Bucket" task in real-world trials.
- All approaches generally required longer completion times in real experiments compared to simulation.
- ProDapt consistently completed every experimental setup faster than the baseline approaches.

**C. Diffusion Inference Time:**
- The mean time required for a single diffusion inference step was measured. ProDapt exhibited inference times comparable to the baselines with short observation horizons (H_o=3, 6).
- As the observation horizon increased for the baselines, the computation time for inference significantly increased. Specifically, the H_o=50 baseline's inference time exceeded 0.1 seconds, which is the required threshold for 10 Hz real-time control, making it impractical for continuous operation at that frequency. This indicates that simply increasing H_o is not a scalable solution for long-term memory in real-time applications.

**D. Discussion:**
The baselines, lacking long-term memory, often exhibited non-optimal behaviors, such as a consistent diagonal drift or wall-following away from the goal, even in clear environments. This behavior, not present in the training data, was likely an emergent heuristic to compensate for insufficient information, preventing direct goal-seeking. ProDapt, by integrating keypoints, avoided these biases, moving directly towards the goal and effectively imitating the human operator's behavior. The "Elbow" task particularly highlighted the significance of long-term memory, demonstrating ProDapt's ability to generalize to an obstacle configuration not explicitly included in the training data, while the short-memory baselines failed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the capabilities of learning-based robotic control, particularly for operations in challenging and unstructured environments. The development of ProDapt and its integration of long-term memory via keypoints into diffusion models offers several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Enhanced Robustness in Adverse Environments:** The primary significance lies in enabling robots to operate reliably using only proprioceptive sensors. This capability is critical for applications in space exploration, underwater missions, and military contexts where exteroceptive sensors (e.g., cameras, LiDAR) are prone to failure, damage, or occlusion by environmental factors like dust or mud. By reducing reliance on external sensing, robots can maintain operational capacity in conditions that would otherwise lead to mission failure.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Generative Models:** ProDapt addresses a fundamental limitation of current diffusion models and other generative models in robotics: their inherent lack of explicit long-term memory. By demonstrating that a curated set of past interactions (keypoints) can effectively serve as long-term memory, the work expands the range of tasks and environments to which these powerful generative models can be successfully applied, particularly those requiring persistent awareness of past events or complex obstacle negotiation.
- **Efficient Proprioceptive Navigation:** The approach provides a method for full motion planning with proprioceptive feedback without requiring explicit environmental mapping. Traditional mapping from sparse contact points can be complex and computationally intensive. ProDapt's ability to "learn to react" to past and present contacts through the expressivity of diffusion models offers a more direct and potentially efficient pathway to proprioceptive navigation.
- **Improved Task Completion and Efficiency:** The experimental results demonstrate that ProDapt not only enables task completion in scenarios where memory-limited baselines fail but also often reduces the time required for successful task execution. This efficiency, combined with maintaining acceptable inference times (unlike baselines requiring excessively long observation horizons), contributes to more practical and deployable robotic systems.
- **Generalizable Framework for Long-Term Memory:** The concept of "keypoints" and a "keypoint manager" is proposed as a generalizable approach for incorporating long-term memory into various learning-based controllers, not just diffusion models or proprioceptive tasks. This framework could potentially be adapted to retain other critical pieces of information, such as important waypoints or object properties, across different sensory modalities and control paradigms.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies clear avenues for future work, including the direct incorporation of memory management into the neural network architecture (moving beyond a hard-coded manager) and comparative studies with other implicit long-term memory methods like RetNet or Mamba. This suggests that ProDapt is not just an endpoint but a stepping stone toward more sophisticated and autonomous robotic intelligence.

In conclusion, ProDapt contributes significantly to the field by enhancing the robustness and intelligence of robotic systems, paving the way for more autonomous and reliable operation in some of the most challenging environments for robotic deployment.
