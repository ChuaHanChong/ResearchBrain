# Mind-Studio: Executable World Models with Lookahead Evaluation for Partially Observable Games

> [!summary] Summary
> Mind-Studio uses a frozen LLM to write a complete, runnable pygame-style world model from object-centric Atari trajectories. Entropy-selected transitions and a compact game skill file guide a single synthesis pass. A K-step lookahead protocol scores the model against real emulator rollouts. On Montezuma's Revenge, chosen-action next-state accuracy rises from 0.3% to 48.7%, and branch-level fidelity improves on Assault and Alien.

## Problem

- Existing symbolic world-model methods are transition-centric. They fit observed input-output pairs or mix local rule experts, and never produce one complete program that runs independently of the real environment.
- In partially observable games, one visible state can hide different internal conditions. Movement, collision priority, enemy behavior, respawn and collection interact locally, and prior methods do not encode explicit mechanism priority.
- Prior methods judge world models by one-step prediction or by their own native planners. This gives no controlled, planning-time measure of how faithful branch rollouts are against the real emulator.

## Method

- ==Entropy-based transition selector== scores rows by attribute changes, relational changes and discrete events, so the LLM context holds rare rule-bearing transitions instead of repeated movement. A ==compressor== packs a static interface plus per-step deltas.
- ==Single-pass LLM synthesis== is guided by a ==game skill file== with object types, legal actions, conventions and static layout. The output is an ==object-centric pygame-style program== with explicit priority ordering, chosen by held-out replay accuracy.
- ==K-step lookahead protocol== holds each candidate action for K frames in both the world model and real ALE, and compares endpoints. A shared ==LLM-as-Planner== harness with subgoal rollback isolates the effect of the lookahead source.

## Results

- On Montezuma's Revenge, chosen-action next-state accuracy reaches **48.7%** versus **0.3%** for PoE-World, with **5/8** subgoals verified. Offline random-state accuracy is **0.16** versus **0.05** for PoE-World.
- Averaged over five planner LLMs, Assault branch accuracy is **0.91** for Mind-Studio versus **0.36** for PoE-World and **0.07** for LLM-as-WM. Alien is **0.338** versus **0.284**, and Skiing is **0.266** versus **0.239**.
- Ablation on Montezuma: removing compression drops Qwen3-Coder holdout accuracy from **0.521** to **0.244**, and removing the random-data-free default set to random data drops it to **0.077**. Higher accuracy ties or improves task outcome in **44 of 56** pairs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Executable code world models can beat rule-ensemble and text-only LLM predictors. Fidelity of the whole program, including rule priority, matters more than fitting individual transitions.
> - Context budget is the bottleneck for LLM synthesis. Curating informative, compressed transitions beats feeding many raw consecutive examples: 16 times more raw examples still scored worse.
> - Aggregate accuracy can mislead. Stationary actions inflate scores while lateral movement fails, so per-action breakdowns are needed. Deterministic models also cannot capture stochastic respawns, which limits how well accuracy predicts task success.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Mind-Studio" contributes to the field of artificial intelligence, specifically in the area of world model synthesis and reinforcement learning, with a focus on partially observable environments. World models are internal representations that describe environment dynamics, enabling agents to reason beyond immediate observations and perform hypothetical rollouts for decision-making.

Traditional approaches to world modeling, particularly in the context of large language models (LLMs), have often focused on predicting the next state or fitting observed transitions. These methods include general predictive models (e.g., Ha and Schmidhuber, 2018; Sutton and Barto, 1998), as well as more recent LLM-based systems that induce programmatic world models from state-action-next-state tuples (e.g., Tang et al., 2024a; Piriyakulkij et al., 2025a). However, a recognized limitation of many existing LLM-based models is their tendency to be "transition-centric." This means they reproduce observed input-output behavior or combine local rule experts, but do not necessarily synthesize a complete, executable program that fully explains how a transition is produced. This distinction is particularly relevant in complex, partially observable environments like Atari games, where the visible state may conceal critical internal conditions, and multiple interacting mechanisms (e.g., movement, collision, enemy behavior, respawn, object collection) govern the dynamics.

Symbolic world models, which represent environments through objects, attributes, relations, and rules (d’Avila Garcez et al., 2019), naturally align with the goal of supporting multi-step reasoning. Code-based symbolic world models further extend this by expressing dynamics through program variables, control flow, functions, and numerical updates (Silver and Chitnis, 2020). Mind-Studio builds upon these concepts by aiming to synthesize such executable, object-centric transition programs.

The paper distinguishes itself from prior work by focusing on the synthesis of a *single, standalone, executable program* rather than an ensemble of weighted rules or free-form language observations. This program is designed to explicitly encode object identities, static scene constraints, action semantics, and update order, ensuring coherent behavior when executed from varying states. The proposed K-step lookahead fidelity protocol also provides a structured method for comparing generated world model rollouts against real environment dynamics, addressing the need for robust evaluation in decision-making contexts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to synthesize an executable world model that captures the underlying mechanisms of an environment as a runnable program, rather than merely fitting surface-level state transitions. This objective is driven by several motivations related to the limitations of existing world model synthesis approaches, particularly in partially observable game environments:

1. **Enabling Robust Hypothetical Rollouts:** A primary motivation for developing world models is to allow agents to simulate action consequences and reason about future states before committing to actions. For effective decision-making, a model should encode sufficient dynamics to support multi-step hypothetical rollouts under various actions. Existing transition-centric models often fall short in providing a complete and coherent predictive capability for extended sequences of actions.

2. **Addressing Partial Observability and Complex Interactions:** In environments like partially observable Atari games (e.g., OC-Atari), the visible state often hides crucial internal conditions. Furthermore, game dynamics are frequently governed by the complex interaction of multiple mechanisms such as movement, collision priorities, enemy behaviors, respawn logic, and object collection. Many current LLM-based methods struggle to synthesize a unified mechanism that explains how these interactions produce state transitions, instead offering fragmented or localized rule sets. The research aims to produce a program that can holistically capture these interwoven mechanics.

3. **Preserving Environmental Semantics and Coherence:** A useful world model should maintain key environmental properties such as object identities, static scene constraints (e.g., wall positions, maze layouts), and the precise semantics of actions. It also needs to correctly model the order in which different rules and updates are applied. The motivation is to synthesize a program where these elements are explicitly encoded, leading to behavior that remains coherent and predictable even when executed from diverse initial states.

4. **Producing an Interpretable and Standalone Artifact:** The research aims to generate a standalone Python program that serves as the world model. This artifact is intended to be executable independently of the real environment, capable of updating object states, rendering predicted scenes, and exposing the underlying rules responsible for each transition. This offers a level of interpretability and inspectability often lacking in purely neural or black-box models.

5. **Evaluating K-step Lookahead Fidelity for Planning:** The ultimate utility of a world model lies in its ability to improve agent planning and decision-making. A specific motivation is to establish a rigorous evaluation protocol—the K-step lookahead fidelity—that directly compares the multi-step rollouts of the synthesized world model against the real environment. This protocol measures how accurately the model predicts end states for candidate actions, thereby assessing its practical utility for an LLM-based planner in real-game scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mind-Studio is a two-stage framework: **World Model Synthesis** and **LLM-as-Planner Evaluation**. The approach aims to generate an executable, object-centric world model (WM) from recorded interaction traces and then evaluate its effectiveness as a lookahead source for an LLM-based planning agent.

#### 3.1. World Model Synthesis Stage

This stage focuses on constructing the executable world model.

1. **Symbolic State Extraction:** The process begins with logged transitions `D = {(o_t, a_t, o_{t+1})}` from a partially observed game. Each raw observation `o_t` is converted into an object-centric symbolic state `s_t` using the OC-Atari RAM parser (Delfosse et al., 2024). This `s_t` is a structured list of game objects (player, enemies, items) with typed attribute slots (position, size, status).

2. **Entropy-Based Transition Selection (`e_T`):** To maximize the information density for the LLM synthesizer, a subset of "rule-bearing" transitions (`e_T`) is selected from the full set `T`. A heuristic score `H(r_i)` is calculated for each row `r_i = (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})`. This heuristic upweights rows exhibiting:
 - Significant object-attribute changes (`ω_change`, considering appearances/disappearances more than ordinary movement).
 - Relational transitions (`ω_rel`, comparing before/after sets of object relations).
 - Discrete game events (`ω_evt`, capturing explicit game events).
 For Montezuma, additional coverage is forced for rare interaction types like ropes and conveyor belts.

3. **Experience Compression `E(·)`:** The selected `e_T` is compressed into a prompt-friendly format. This involves recording:
 - A static interface `P`: Persistent object slots shared across all steps (e.g., unchanging background elements or object types).
 - Per-step deltas `∆P_i`: Only the attributes that changed at step `i` (e.g., player position displacement, lives decrements, contact onsets/offsets). This compact format maximizes information per token for the LLM.

4. **LLM-based Program Generation:** An LLM synthesizer (`g_ϕ`, specifically Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B-Instruct-Turbo in default settings) is prompted to generate the executable world model `M`. The prompt combines:
 - The compressed entropy-selected transitions `E(e_T)`.
 - A "game skill file" `S_game`: This compact file encodes critical game-setting information, including object types, legal actions, domain conventions, and static scene layout (e.g., maze wall coordinates). Static layout entries can be derived from pixel-level scene extraction, enabling applicability even without RAM-based state. The skill file can also include high-level game background and goal descriptions.
 The output `M` is a pygame-style Python program with interfaces for state transition and rendering.

5. **World Model Selection (`M_⋆`):** Multiple candidate programs (`M_cand`) may be generated. The final `M_⋆` used for planning is selected by evaluating each candidate's one-step replay Next-State Prediction (NSP) on a held-out validation split of replay tuples. The program with the highest replay NSP (fraction of exact player `(x,y)` matches) is chosen. An optional refinement loop is considered but not used for reported planning results due to inconsistent gains.

#### 3.2. LLM-as-Planner Evaluation Stage

This stage evaluates the selected `M_⋆` by integrating it into a shared planning harness.

1. **Roles:**
 - **World Model (`M_⋆`):** The synthesized executable program predicting game state changes.
 - **Player:** The controllable character in the Atari game.
 - **LLM Planner (`π_LLM`):** A language model that selects actions by consulting `M_⋆`'s K-step previews.

2. **K-step Lookahead Protocol:** For each planning round `r`:
 - The harness captures the current state from a real ALE snapshot.
 - Every candidate action `a ∈ A_r` is executed for `K` frames under both `M_⋆` and the Real ALE environment (`R_K`). `K` is a game-specific horizon (e.g., 16 for Skiing, 8 for others), determined by maximizing held-out NSP for each game. This is an action-granularity hold, not a multi-step composition of distinct actions.
 - A branch is deemed correct (`h_r(a) = 1`) if the player's pixel-level `(x,y)` position at the end of the `K` frames matches exactly between `M_⋆`'s rollout and Real ALE's rollout (`ϕ(M_⋆^K(s_r, a)) = ϕ(R^K(s_r, a))`).

3. **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Next-State Prediction (NSP):**
 - `NSP_branch`: The average `h_r(a)` across all candidate actions presented to the planner.
 - `NSP_chosen`: The average `h_r(a_⋆_r)` only for the action `a_⋆_r` that the planner actually commits to.
 - **Success Rate (SR):**
 - For Montezuma's Revenge, SR is the fraction of ordered subgoals (e.g., "reach ladder foot," "obtain key") successfully verified in the real environment within a step budget.
 - For other games, it is the game-specific achievement count (e.g., kills, gates reached).

4. **LLM-as-Planner Harness (`Algorithm 1`):** A shared, stable evaluation harness is used across all world model sources.
 - The `π_LLM` receives the current symbolic state `s_t`, the verified subgoal prefix `m`, and a compact table of per-action `M_⋆` previews (`B_t`).
 - `π_LLM` then outputs the next action or a short action chunk.
 - **Subgoal Rollback:** The harness tracks `m` in the real environment. If the `(m+1)`-th predicate `Φ_{m+1}` fires on the true game state, `m` is incremented. If a terminal reset occurs or `N_stuck` non-progress steps accumulate, the environment rolls back to the last verified checkpoint (`s_†_m`). This ensures that planner performance differences primarily reflect lookahead fidelity rather than planner implementation variations.

5. **Baselines:** Mind-Studio's `M_⋆` is compared against:
 - **LLM-as-WM:** The planner LLM directly predicts next states from text.
 - **PoE-World (Piriyakulkij et al., 2025b):** An ensemble-style programmatic world model. For fair comparison, PoE-World's previews are also integrated into the Mind-Studio LLM-as-Planner harness, rather than using its native BFS planner, which was found to be too computationally expensive.

6. **Game Environments:** Experiments are conducted on four OC-Atari games: Montezuma’s Revenge (maze, boundary conditions, stochastic skull), Assault (dynamic evasion), Alien (stochastic spawning), and Skiing (velocity-based physics). These games collectively present diverse challenges for WM synthesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated Mind-Studio's performance in synthesizing executable world models across four OC-Atari games: Montezuma’s Revenge, Alien, Assault, and Skiing. The evaluation focused on model fidelity (Next-State Prediction, NSP) and task success rate (SR) using a shared LLM-as-Planner harness.

#### 4.1. Model Fidelity

**Offline Fidelity:**
- **Replay States (Table 3):** Mind-Studio showed competitive NSP on states used for synthesis. For example, on Assault, it achieved 1.00 chosen-action and branch NSP, matching PoE-World. On Montezuma, it surpassed both baselines with 0.51 chosen-action NSP.
- **Random Holdout States (Table 4):** On unseen random states, Mind-Studio exhibited stronger generalization for Assault (0.98 chosen-action and branch NSP) and Skiing (0.69 chosen-action, 0.70 branch NSP) compared to baselines. PoE-World maintained a slight edge on Alien's random replay.

**Online Fidelity (Table 2):** This measures fidelity during closed-loop planning, reflecting how accurately the WM predicts states actually visited by the planner.
- **Montezuma’s Revenge:** Mind-Studio, with the Qwen3-Coder-480B planner, achieved a chosen-action NSP of 0.49 and branch NSP of 0.25. This was a significant improvement over PoE-World (0.00 chosen-action, 0.12 branch NSP) and LLM-as-WM (0.02 chosen-action, 0.31 branch NSP for chosen action, though higher on branch NSP due to stationary actions).
- **Assault:** Mind-Studio consistently demonstrated high chosen-action NSP (e.95) and branch NSP (0.87) with Qwen3-Coder-480B, notably outperforming PoE-World (0.68 chosen-action, 0.44 branch NSP) and LLM-as-WM (0.03 chosen-action, 0.00 branch NSP). This strong performance held across most other planner backbones.
- **Alien:** Performance varied more by planner. With Qwen3-Coder-480B, Mind-Studio's chosen-action NSP was 0.50 (comparable to PoE-World's 0.48), and branch NSP was 0.38 (higher than PoE-World's 0.28).
- **Skiing:** While Mind-Studio showed some branch NSP (0.15) with Qwen3-Coder-480B, it was similar to PoE-World (0.17) and overall low, reflecting the difficulty of the game.

**LLM-as-WM Failure Mode:** LLM-as-WM, which infers K-frame geometry from text descriptions rather than executing code, consistently exhibited near-zero exact NSP for movement-heavy actions, indicating its limitations in precise, multi-step state prediction.

#### 4.2. Planning Results (Task Outcome)

**Table 2** also presents the task outcome (game score or subgoal count).
- **Montezuma’s Revenge:** Mind-Studio, PoE-World, and LLM-as-WM (with Qwen3-Coder-480B) all reached 5/8 verified subgoals, suggesting that at this horizon, lookahead fidelity (NSP) rather than subgoal count was the primary differentiator between sources. With Claude-4-Sonnet and Gemini-2.5-Flash, Mind-Studio matched the best performance, often achieving 8/8 subgoals alongside baselines.
- **Assault:** Mind-Studio achieved the best or tied-best learned task outcome under 3/5 planners, consistently outperforming LLM-as-WM and often PoE-World. For instance, with Qwen3-Coder-480B, Mind-Studio achieved 84 points, compared to PoE-World's 42 and LLM-as-WM's 63.
- **Alien:** Task outcomes were more variable across planners. With Qwen3-Coder-480B, Mind-Studio, LLM-as-WM, and REAL all achieved 3 points, while PoE-World achieved 0.
- **Skiing:** All learned world models (including Mind-Studio) struggled, with most planners achieving zero task progress, except for Qwen3-Coder-480B, where Mind-Studio achieved 1 point.

#### 4.3. Per-Action Fidelity (Table 5)

Breaking down branch NSP by action revealed distinct patterns:
- **Montezuma:** LLM-as-WM had high NSP for stationary actions (NOOP/FIRE/DOWN) but failed on lateral movement (LEFT/RIGHT). Mind-Studio showed the reverse, with stronger lateral movement NSP (e.g., RIGHT 0.49, LEFT 0.48) but weaker stationary action fidelity (e.g., FIRE 0.00).
- **Assault:** Mind-Studio achieved high NSP (1.00) for UP and LEFT, and a strong 0.61 for RIGHT, demonstrating robust prediction for directional movement.

**NSP–Task-Success Relationship:** Across 56 learned-source pairs, higher chosen-action NSP correlated with tied or improved task outcomes in 44 cases (and strictly improved in 16), suggesting NSP is a useful proxy for task performance. Discrepancies were attributed to the deterministic nature of the synthesized WM (not modeling stochasticity) and the compounding effect of K-frame positional errors.

#### 4.4. Ablation: Synthesis Inputs (Table 6)

Ablation studies on Montezuma’s Revenge highlighted the importance of Mind-Studio's design choices:
- **Compression:** Removing compression (using full `(s, a, s')` prefixes instead of compact deltas) significantly lowered exact holdout NSP for all synthesizers, demonstrating the effectiveness of the compression strategy.
- **Game Skill File (`-setting`):** Removing the game skill file hurt the performance of Qwen models, indicating its value in guiding synthesis.
- **Entropy Selection (`Default` vs. `random-comp.`):** Entropy-selected compact rows generally led to better exact holdout NSP compared to random-policy data.

#### 4.5. Backbone Stability and Task Alignment (Table 7)

Mind-Studio's fixed programs were evaluated with five different planner LLMs:
- **Assault:** Mind-Studio showed robust fidelity, with a mean branch NSP of 0.91 across planners, significantly higher than PoE-World (0.36), and consistently achieving strong task outcomes.
- **Skiing:** Despite a small fidelity edge (0.266 vs 0.239 for PoE-World in mean branch NSP), Skiing remained challenging for task success. The K-hold protocol was identified as potentially problematic for momentum-based physics, where prolonged single-action holds might not reflect optimal cumulative control.
- **Montezuma and Alien:** Task outcomes for these games showed more sensitivity to the specific planner backbone chosen, suggesting that while the world model's fidelity is improved, the planning strategy itself remains a critical factor.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Mind-Studio represents a notable advancement in the field of programmatic world model synthesis, particularly for partially observable environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Synthesis of Executable World Models:** The framework successfully translates interaction experience into a runnable, pygame-style Python program. This "executable world model" is distinct from transition-centric predictors or collections of local rules, as it captures a complete, unified mechanism for environment dynamics, including object identities, static constraints, action semantics, and update order. This provides an interpretable and inspectable representation of the environment's rules.

2. **Enhanced Planning Fidelity:** Mind-Studio's synthesized world models demonstrate improved per-action lookahead accuracy (NSP) compared to LLM-as-WM (direct LLM prediction) and PoE-World (an ensemble-based programmatic WM). This higher fidelity in predicting multi-step outcomes directly supports more effective decision-making for LLM-based planning agents.

3. **Improved Task Performance and Efficiency:** In challenging environments like Montezuma’s Revenge, Mind-Studio-guided LLM-as-Planner matched or exceeded the subgoal attainment of baselines, and importantly, achieved this within a significantly smaller wall-time budget than computationally intensive methods like BFS. This efficiency highlights the practical utility of the approach for complex, long-horizon tasks.

4. **Role of Game Skill File and Entropy Selection:** The research demonstrates the effectiveness of a "game skill file" in guiding LLM synthesis, enabling the model to bridge the gap between raw visual observations (or RAM-derived states) and the structured dynamics knowledge required. Furthermore, the entropy-based selection of rule-bearing traces proves crucial for maximizing the information density within the LLM's context window, leading to more accurate model generation.

5. **Robust Evaluation Protocol:** The K-step lookahead fidelity protocol provides a rigorous and controlled method for evaluating world models. By comparing WM rollouts against Real-ALE rollouts from identical states, the protocol offers a direct measure of model accuracy for planning, separating the quality of the world model from specific planner implementations through a shared LLM-as-Planner harness.

6. **Broader Applicability:** The core design principles of Mind-Studio—skill-file-guided single-pass synthesis evaluated by K-step lookahead fidelity—are proposed as a reusable blueprint. This suggests potential for application in diverse domains beyond Atari games, wherever object-level observations and discrete actions are available, enabling the creation of lightweight, interpretable world models for various AI agents.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Foundational Research:** Mind-Studio contributes to the ongoing effort to integrate symbolic reasoning with neural models, offering a path toward more interpretable and robust AI systems.
- **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning:** By providing highly accurate and executable world models, the framework could enhance model-based reinforcement learning agents, allowing for more efficient planning, exploration, and sample-efficient learning.
- **AI Explainability:** The generation of human-readable Python code as a world model inherently improves the explainability of an agent's understanding of its environment dynamics.
- **Interactive AI Systems:** The ability to synthesize executable models from interaction could be valuable for developing AI agents that can quickly learn and adapt to new, unfamiliar environments through observation and limited interaction.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The paper acknowledges limitations such as errors in geometry-sensitive contexts and stochastic scenarios, suggesting improvements through targeted trace collection and richer transition templates. The findings also indicate that while world models are improved, task success still depends on the planner's ability to explore and formulate routes. Future work could involve extracting explicit transition rules for LLMs, expanding to richer vocabularies or continuous control, and exploring the synergy between world model fidelity and planner-specific exploration strategies.
