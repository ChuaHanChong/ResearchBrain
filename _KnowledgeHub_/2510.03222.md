# Low-probability Tokens Sustain Exploration in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tencent's LLM Department and collaborating universities introduced Low-probability Regularization (Lp-Reg) to overcome exploration collapse in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models by specifically preserving valuable low-probability "reasoning sparks". This approach enabled continuous on-policy training for 3,000 steps and achieved a 2.66% average accuracy improvement on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs) suffers from a scalability bottleneck.
- This bottleneck is characterized by "policy entropy collapse," where models lose exploration capacity and plateau or degrade in complex reasoning tasks.
- Prior entropy-centric solutions often fail by indiscriminately boosting both valuable and irrelevant exploratory tokens, leading to training instability.

## Method

- Developed ==Low-probability Regularization (Lp-Reg)==, an algorithm integrated into policy gradient methods like ==Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==.
- Constructed a =="less-noisy proxy distribution"== by filtering out irrelevant low-probability tokens from the current policy using adaptive or fixed probability thresholds.
- Applied a selective regularization penalty using ==forward KL divergence== to protect identified valuable low-probability tokens ("reasoning sparks") specifically when they receive negative advantage signals.

## Results

- Enabled sustained on-policy training for **3,000 steps**, accumulating over **81,204 GPU-hours**, showcasing a substantial improvement in training stability and scalability.
- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, with an average accuracy of **60.17%** on the Qwen3-14B-Base model, outperforming the previous best by **2.66%**.
- Ablation studies validated core design choices, confirming the necessity of noise filtering, selective regularization of tokens with negative advantage, and the use of forward KL divergence for effective training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The core issue in RLVR scalability is the systematic elimination of specific valuable low-probability exploratory tokens, termed "reasoning sparks," rather than a general loss of policy entropy.
> - "Reasoning sparks" are qualitatively different from high-entropy tokens and exhibit statistically higher average probabilities than irrelevant noise within the low-probability spectrum.
> - Sustained, stable exploration in RL for LLMs requires a targeted approach to preserve these meaningful low-probability tokens, avoiding indiscriminate entropy boosting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly their enhancement through Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLVR has emerged as a powerful technique for improving LLMs' complex reasoning abilities, notably in domains like mathematics, by assigning rewards based on verifiable solutions. Prominent examples include OpenAI's O1 and DeepSeek-R1, which leverage RLVR to generate elaborate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

However, a well-recognized bottleneck in RLVR training is its limited scalability due to "policy entropy collapse," where the model loses its capacity for exploration, leading to performance plateaus and eventual degradation. Previous approaches have primarily tackled this issue by attempting to maintain high *overall* policy entropy through various entropy control mechanisms (e.g., adaptive entropy regularization, selective token updates, modified clipping strategies). These methods treat high entropy as a general proxy for exploration.

This paper differentiates itself by critically analyzing the limitations of these entropy-centric approaches. The authors argue that an indiscriminate focus on maximizing entropy can be counterproductive, amplifying irrelevant "noise" tokens alongside valuable exploratory ones, thereby destabilizing training. This work posits a more nuanced understanding of exploration, moving beyond a simplistic view of "randomness" to identify the *quality* of exploratory tokens.

Specifically, the paper introduces the concept of "reasoning sparks" – valuable, low-probability exploratory tokens (e.g., "wait", "however", "perhaps") that initiate diverse reasoning pathways. It asserts that the systematic *elimination* of these reasoning sparks, rather than a general decline in entropy, is the root cause of the RLVR scalability bottleneck. This reframing represents a significant shift from the prevailing entropy-based perspective, offering a more semantically grounded and causally informed investigation into token probabilities and their role in exploration. By doing so, this research aims to provide a targeted solution that selectively preserves meaningful exploration while filtering out detrimental noise, thereby pushing the boundaries of stable and scalable RL for LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the scalability bottleneck in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs) that hinders their ability to perform complex reasoning tasks. This bottleneck manifests as a performance plateau and subsequent collapse, consistently accompanied by a rapid decay in policy entropy, signaling a loss of exploration capacity.

The motivation for this work stems from a critical insight: existing entropy-control methods, while attempting to address the exploration problem, are often ineffective or even detrimental. The authors argue that an unselective focus on maintaining high *overall* policy entropy risks amplifying "irrelevant tokens" (noise) alongside potentially useful ones, thereby destabilizing the training process. Their analysis reveals a deeper, more specific issue: the gradual and systematic elimination of **valuable low-probability exploratory tokens**, which they term **reasoning sparks**. These reasoning sparks are crucial for initiating diverse and effective reasoning pathways, but they are systematically suppressed during standard RLVR training due to over-penalization.

Therefore, the key objectives are:
1. **Identify and characterize "reasoning sparks":** To formally define and empirically demonstrate the existence and importance of low-probability, semantically meaningful tokens that drive exploration in LLM reasoning.
2. **Diagnose the cause of their elimination:** To show that standard RLVR training methods systematically extinguish these reasoning sparks, leading to exploration degeneracy.
3. **Develop a targeted solution:** To introduce a novel regularization method that selectively preserves these reasoning sparks without amplifying irrelevant noise, thereby enabling stable and sustained exploration.
4. **Achieve state-of-the-art performance and scalability:** To demonstrate that this new approach can lead to improved accuracy on challenging reasoning benchmarks and enable continuous RL training over significantly longer periods compared to existing methods.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond a crude measure of "randomness" (entropy) towards a refined understanding and preservation of *meaningful* exploratory diversity within the LLM's output distribution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this paper's methodology is the introduction of **Low-probability Regularization (Lp-Reg)**, designed to selectively preserve valuable low-probability exploratory tokens (reasoning sparks) while avoiding the amplification of irrelevant noise. Lp-Reg is integrated into existing policy gradient algorithms, specifically Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to stabilize and enhance exploration.

The approach consists of two main components:

#### 3.1. Proxy Distribution Construction ($\pi_{\text{proxy}}$)

The first step is to construct a "less-noisy proxy distribution" ($\pi_{\text{proxy}}$) from the current policy ($\pi_{\theta}$). This proxy serves as a refined reference for regularization.
1. **Filtering Noise Tokens:** The method identifies and filters out tokens presumed to be "noise" – those whose probability under the current policy ($\pi_{\theta}(o|\cdot)$) falls below a specific threshold ($\tau$).
- **Fixed Threshold:** A constant hyperparameter (e.g., $\tau = 0.02$).
- **Min-p Threshold:** An adaptive threshold defined as a fraction ($\kappa$) of the peak probability: $\tau = \kappa \cdot \max_{o' \in V} \pi_{\theta}(o'|\cdot)$, where $\kappa \in (0, 1)$. The min-p strategy is preferred for its adaptiveness.
 This filtering is crucial because, as the authors observe, within the low-probability range, meaningful exploratory tokens generally have higher average probabilities than irrelevant noise tokens.
2. **Probability Renormalization:** After filtering, the probabilities of the discarded tokens are set to zero, and the probability mass is redistributed among the remaining, non-filtered tokens. This process creates a $\pi_{\text{proxy}}$ where valuable low-probability tokens are preserved and their relative probabilities are amplified, effectively forming a high-confidence reference from the "meaningful" subset of the original distribution.

#### 3.2. Low-probability Regularization Objective

The Lp-Reg penalty is added to the standard GRPO objective function (Equation 3). The modified objective function (Equation 6) is:

$J_{Lp\text{-}Reg}(\theta) = E_{B \sim D, (q,a) \sim B, \{o_i\}_{i=1}^G \sim \pi_{\theta}^{\text{old}}(\cdot|q)} \left[ \frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^G |o_i|} \sum_{i=1}^G \sum_{t=1}^{|o_i|} \left( \text{clip}(r_{i,t}(\theta), 0, U) \cdot A_{i,t} - \beta \cdot I \left[ \pi_{\theta}(o_{i,t}|q, o_{i,<t}) < \delta_{B}^{\rho} \land \pi_{\text{proxy}}(o_{i,t}|q, o_{i,<t}) > 0 \land A_{i,t} < 0 \right] \cdot D_{KL}(\pi_{\text{proxy}}(\cdot|q, o_{i,<t})\|\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|q, o_{i,<t})) \right) \right]$

Key aspects of this regularization term include:
1. **Modified Clipping for Policy Gradient:** The standard policy gradient term's clipping is modified by removing the lower bound (0) to avoid penalizing low-probability exploratory actions, while an upper bound (U=10) is added for numerical stability.
2. **Selective Activation of Lp-Reg Penalty:** The regularization term is activated by an indicator function $I[\cdot]$ only when *all three* conditions are met for a token $o_{i,t}$:
- **Low Probability:** Its sampling probability $\pi_{\theta}(o_{i,t}|\cdot)$ is below a dynamic low-percentile threshold ($\delta_{B}^{\rho}$), which is the $\rho$-th percentile of sampling probabilities within the current batch. This ensures focus on the "low-probability tail."
- **Not Noise:** Its probability in the proxy distribution $\pi_{\text{proxy}}(o_{i,t}|\cdot)$ is greater than zero. This condition directly leverages the noise filtering step, ensuring that only tokens deemed meaningful by the proxy are protected.
- **Negative Advantage:** The token receives a negative advantage signal ($A_{i,t} < 0$). This ensures the regularization specifically counteracts the negative feedback that would otherwise lead to the elimination of reasoning sparks. It prevents over-penalization of tokens that are already receiving positive learning signals.
3. **Forward KL Divergence:** The regularization function used is the forward KL divergence, $D_{KL}(\pi_{\text{proxy}}\|\pi_{\theta})$. This choice is deliberate, as it strongly penalizes $\pi_{\theta}$ if it assigns near-zero probability to tokens that $\pi_{\text{proxy}}$ considers plausible. This acts as a "soft regularization target," protecting reasoning sparks from elimination without forcing the policy to strictly match the heuristic proxy, thus allowing for continued exploration.

By combining careful proxy construction with a selectively activated, targeted forward KL regularization, Lp-Reg aims to create a more stable and effective exploratory environment for RLVR.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the significant effectiveness of Lp-Reg in improving the scalability, stability, and performance of RL for LLMs in complex reasoning tasks.

1. **Sustained Exploration and Scalability:**
- Lp-Reg enabled continuous on-policy reinforcement learning for **3,000 training steps**, accumulating over **81,204 GPU-hours** on the Qwen2.5-32B-Base model. This represents a substantial improvement in training stability and scalability, a regime where baseline entropy-control methods typically collapse.
- The method facilitates a more dynamic and healthy policy entropy trajectory, initially decreasing as core reasoning patterns are learned, then gradually increasing to foster exploration as performance improves, and finally stabilizing. This contrasts with baseline methods that induce either rapid collapse or an artificial, indiscriminate increase in entropy.

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance:**
- Lp-Reg achieved state-of-the-art performance on five challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, MATH-500, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench).
- On the Qwen3-14B-Base model, on-policy Lp-Reg achieved an average accuracy of **60.17%**, outperforming the next best method, 80/20, by a notable **2.66%**.
- Lp-Reg's benefits were more pronounced on newer base models (Qwen3-14B), suggesting that as models become more capable, their intrinsic capacity for nuanced, low-probability reasoning increases, which Lp-Reg effectively leverages.

3. **Superiority of On-Policy Training (with Lp-Reg):**
- The experiments consistently showed that on-policy training, particularly with Lp-Reg, exhibited better stability and testing performance than off-policy methods across different model scales. This is attributed to the inherent stability of on-policy updates that avoid distribution shifts.

4. **Ablation Studies Validating Core Design Choices:**
- **Low-probability vs. High-entropy Regularization:** Regularizing tokens with the highest 1% entropy not only failed to improve performance but also couldn't sustain high entropy, which quickly collapsed. This strongly supports the hypothesis that valuable exploration comes from specific *low-probability* tokens ("reasoning sparks"), not general high entropy.
- **Importance of Noise Filtering:** Removing the noise filtering mechanism (i.e., regularizing all low-probability tokens without distinguishing noise) led to catastrophic performance collapse and entropy explosion. This validates the crucial role of the $\pi_{\text{proxy}}$ in filtering out irrelevant noise tokens to stabilize training. The adaptive `min-p` threshold was also shown to be superior to a fixed threshold.
- **Effect of Negative Samples:** Applying Lp-Reg regularization exclusively to tokens receiving *negative advantage signals* led to faster learning and better performance compared to regularizing both positive and negative samples. This confirms that the primary function of Lp-Reg is to protect valuable low-probability tokens from being excessively penalized during negative feedback.
- **Forward vs. Reverse KL:** The use of forward KL divergence ($D_{KL}(\pi_{\text{proxy}}\|\pi_{\theta})$) significantly outperformed reverse KL. This indicates that a "soft" regularization that penalizes policy for discarding plausible tokens from the proxy (forward KL) is more effective than aggressively forcing the policy to strictly imitate a heuristic proxy (reverse KL).

5. **Analysis of Exploratory Token Dynamics:**
- **Probability-Entropy Distinction:** Word cloud analysis and scatter plots showed that "reasoning sparks" (e.g., "but", "wait", "perhaps") are distinct from high-entropy tokens, which are often common function words or formatting characters. This provides empirical support for the "reasoning sparks" concept.
- **Preservation of Reasoning Sparks:** Lp-Reg successfully broadened the probability-entropy distribution of reasoning sparks, allowing them to be sampled across a wider range of states and preventing their collapse into deterministic usage. It also maintained a consistently higher frequency of these tokens during training compared to GRPO.
- **Probabilistic Distinction between Sparks and Noise:** Analysis revealed a consistent statistical gap: meaningful exploratory tokens consistently exhibited a higher average next-token probability than irrelevant noise tokens within the low-probability range. This persistent gap justifies Lp-Reg's filtering mechanism.

In summary, the main findings robustly support the authors' central hypothesis that selective preservation of valuable low-probability tokens (reasoning sparks) is crucial for sustained exploration and performance in RLVR, and that Lp-Reg provides an effective mechanism to achieve this.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers a significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for large language models, particularly in the context of complex reasoning tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Reframing the Exploration Challenge:** The paper's most significant conceptual contribution is shifting the paradigm from merely maximizing *overall policy entropy* to selectively preserving *meaningful low-probability exploratory tokens*, termed "reasoning sparks." This re-conceptualization provides a more granular and semantically informed understanding of effective exploration, moving beyond a blunt measure of randomness. This insight can guide future research towards more nuanced exploration strategies in diverse RL scenarios.

2. **Enhanced Scalability and Stability of RLVR:** The demonstration of Lp-Reg's ability to sustain on-policy training for 3,000 steps and over 81,000 GPU-hours, where other methods collapse, is a major practical breakthrough. This sustained stability addresses a critical bottleneck in scaling RL for LLMs, paving the way for training even more powerful and robust reasoning models. Longer and more stable training can lead to higher performance ceilings for LLMs on complex tasks.

3. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Reasoning:** Achieving a 2.66% absolute improvement in average accuracy on challenging mathematical benchmarks with Lp-Reg highlights its direct impact on LLM capabilities. This indicates that protecting reasoning sparks directly translates to better problem-solving abilities, enabling LLMs to explore more effective and accurate reasoning pathways.

4. **Principled Noise Management:** The finding that indiscriminately boosting entropy can amplify irrelevant noise tokens and destabilize training is a crucial cautionary tale for the community. Lp-Reg's mechanism for distinguishing and filtering out this noise, based on the statistical distinction between reasoning sparks and noise, provides a principled approach to managing the "messiness" inherent in LLM probability distributions. This robust noise filtering makes RL training more reliable.

5. **Insights into LLM Intrinsic Confidence:** The analysis showing a persistent probabilistic distinction between valuable reasoning sparks and irrelevant noise within the low-probability range offers valuable insights into the intrinsic confidence mechanisms of LLMs. This can inform future research on how LLMs internally evaluate token relevance and how these signals can be leveraged for better control and interpretability.

6. **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated in the context of mathematical reasoning, the principles behind Lp-Reg – identifying and protecting valuable low-probability actions in a high-dimensional action space – could potentially be generalized to other complex RL tasks involving LLMs, such as code generation, scientific discovery, or even creative writing, where subtle, low-probability tokens might unlock novel and high-quality outputs.

In conclusion, "Low-probability Tokens Sustain Exploration in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward" provides both a novel theoretical understanding of exploration in LLMs and a highly effective, empirically validated method. Its impact is likely to be substantial, enabling the development of more stable, scalable, and ultimately more intelligent reasoning agents.
