# Normalizing Trajectory Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Normalizing Trajectory Models (NTM) from Apple researchers facilitate high-fidelity image generation in as few as four inference steps, maintaining an exact likelihood over the generative process. This method addresses the Gaussian bottleneck of accelerated diffusion models, achieving strong performance metrics like a 0.82 GenEval score for 256x256 resolution when trained from scratch.

## Problem

- Generative diffusion models require numerous steps for high-fidelity image synthesis, leading to slow inference.
- Accelerating these models by reducing steps causes the reverse conditional distributions to become complex (multimodal, heavy-tailed), poorly approximated by single Gaussians, known as the "Gaussian bottleneck".
- Current methods for faster generation often compromise the exact likelihood framework, losing probabilistic interpretability and training stability.

## Method

- Each reverse generative step p(x_s | x_t) is modeled as an expressive conditional normalizing flow, overcoming the Gaussian bottleneck.
- A deep-shallow architecture employs shallow, invertible "transporter" blocks (f_T) for latent space mapping and a deep "predictor" (f_P) for conditional Gaussian modeling in the latent space.
- The framework supports training from scratch or finetuning from existing flow-matching models using an auxiliary loss that stabilizes training while learning non-Gaussian structures.

## Results

- NTM trained from scratch yielded a 0.82 GenEval score and 79.64 DPG-Bench score for 256x256 text-to-image generation in just 4 steps, surpassing several baseline diffusion models.
- A finetuned NTM model on 512x512 images achieved a 0.76 GenEval and 83.38 DPG-Bench in 4 steps, demonstrating scalability and efficient preservation of pretrained model quality.
- An optional learned denoiser accelerated inference by approximately **9x** (1.88 img/s vs. 0.20 img/s) while maintaining high fidelity (LPIPS 0.121) compared to the full NTM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, few-step generation can be achieved with exact likelihood by explicitly modeling complex, non-Gaussian reverse conditional probabilities using invertible flows.
> - A "deep-shallow" architecture, combining shallow invertible transporters with a deep parallel predictor, efficiently distributes modeling capacity across generative steps.
> - The full NTM's trajectory score denoising can be distilled into a lightweight learned denoiser, enabling very fast, single-forward-pass inference while preserving high fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative modeling, particularly for high-fidelity image synthesis, has been significantly advanced by diffusion-based models (e.g., DDPM, score-based models, flow matching models). These methods typically decompose the generation process into numerous small Gaussian denoising steps. This approach assumes that the reverse conditional probability distribution, p(x_s | x_t), for moving from a noisier state x_t to a slightly cleaner state x_s, can be accurately approximated by a single Gaussian distribution. This assumption holds effectively when a large number of steps are used, allowing each transition to cover a sufficiently small interval where the true reverse conditional remains approximately Gaussian.

However, a major challenge in this landscape is the computational efficiency of these models. To reduce inference time, researchers aim to decrease the number of sampling steps. When the number of steps is reduced, each transition must span a larger interval in the generative trajectory. In such cases, the true reverse conditional p(x_s | x_t) often becomes multimodal and heavy-tailed, resembling a mixture of Gaussians, rather than a single Gaussian. This discrepancy constitutes a "Gaussian bottleneck" that fundamentally limits the quality of few-step generation in standard diffusion and flow-matching models.

Existing approaches to address this efficiency problem typically sacrifice the exact likelihood framework. Distillation methods learn to map noise to data in fewer steps but do not provide a tractable density over the generative trajectory. Consistency models similarly aim for few-step generation without offering explicit likelihoods. Adversarial methods, such as DDGAN, replace the Gaussian reverse with an implicit distribution learned through adversarial training. While this allows for larger step sizes, it often introduces challenges like mode-seeking behavior and training instability, limiting their scalability and probabilistic interpretability.

Normalizing Trajectory Models (NTM) aim to bridge this gap by introducing a method that achieves high-quality few-step generation while uniquely retaining an exact likelihood model of the reverse process. It positions itself as an alternative to methods that sacrifice likelihood for efficiency, offering a principled probabilistic framework for efficient generation. Architecturally, it builds upon advancements in normalizing flows, specifically adapting techniques from STARFlow, by applying them to the conditional reverse steps rather than the marginal data distribution. This positions NTM as an interpolation between deep autoregressive normalizing flows (like STARFlow, which performs generation in a single, complex pass) and flow-matching models (which rely on many simple Gaussian steps), by distributing the modeling complexity across a few, expressive conditional normalizing flow steps.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generative model that can produce high-quality samples in a significantly reduced number of inference steps while simultaneously maintaining an exact likelihood over the generative trajectory. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in the current state of diffusion-based generative models:

1. **Addressing the Gaussian Bottleneck:** Standard diffusion models approximate each reverse step (from a noisier state x_t to a cleaner state x_s) as a simple Gaussian transition. This approximation breaks down when the number of sampling steps is reduced to improve efficiency, as each step then covers a larger interval. In such scenarios, the true conditional distribution p(x_s | x_t) becomes complex, often multimodal or heavy-tailed, which a single Gaussian cannot adequately capture. The key motivation is to overcome this fundamental limitation, enabling faithful few-step generation.

2. **Retaining Exact Likelihood:** Existing methods that accelerate diffusion models (e.g., distillation, consistency models, adversarial training) typically forgo the exact likelihood framework. This sacrifice means these models cannot provide a tractable density over the generated samples or trajectories, which limits their probabilistic interpretability, stability in training, and applicability in tasks requiring density estimation, anomaly detection, or explicit uncertainty quantification. A core motivation for NTM is to achieve efficiency without compromising this probabilistic foundation, ensuring stable and principled training.

3. **Bridging Generative Paradigms:** The research aims to conceptually connect self-supervised representation learning with probabilistic generative modeling. By introducing an invertible transformation that maps data into a latent space where prediction becomes simpler, NTM seeks to leverage insights from representation learning within a tractable likelihood framework, effectively turning a representation-learning objective into exact log-likelihood optimization.

4. **Architectural Efficiency and Flexibility:** The authors are motivated to design an architecture that is both expressive enough to model complex non-Gaussian conditionals and efficient for inference. This involves combining shallow invertible blocks within each step with a deep parallel predictor across the trajectory. Furthermore, the goal is to offer flexibility: the model should be trainable from scratch using stochastic forward trajectories or capable of being initialized and finetuned from existing pretrained flow-matching models, thereby leveraging prior advancements.

5. **Accelerating Inference with Fidelity:** Even with a reduced number of sequential steps, the invertible blocks and backpropagation-based denoising can introduce latency. A specific objective is to develop a mechanism, such as a learned denoiser, that can amortize these computational costs into a single forward pass, providing very fast generation while maintaining the high fidelity enabled by the underlying exact likelihood model and trajectory-level refinement.

In summary, the motivation stems from the need for high-performance generative models that are both efficient in sampling and robustly grounded in a complete probabilistic framework, thereby overcoming the trade-offs often encountered in the current research landscape.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NTM introduces a framework that models each reverse step of the generative process, p(x_s | x_t), as a conditional normalizing flow. This approach aims to address the "Gaussian bottleneck" by allowing for more expressive, non-Gaussian transitions while maintaining an exact likelihood.

#### 3.1. Model Formulation: Conditional Normalizing Flow

The core idea is to transform the data into a latent "u-space" where the conditional distribution for the reverse step becomes tractable for a Gaussian predictor. This is achieved through two main components:

- **Shared Transporter (f_T):** An invertible mapping that transforms both the noisier input x_t and the cleaner target x_s into their respective latent representations, u_t and u_s. This transporter is implemented as a stack of shallow TarFlow-style blocks, which are causal autoregressive flow blocks with spatial NVP coupling. The invertibility and same-dimensionality of f_T are crucial for enabling exact log-likelihood calculations through the change-of-variables formula.
- **Stochastic Predictor (f_P):** This component takes the noisier latent representation u_t and a simple latent variable z ∼ N(0, I) (optionally conditioned on a label y) to predict the cleaner latent representation, u_s_hat. It is parameterized as an affine map, defining p_P(u_s | u_t, y) as a Gaussian distribution N(μ_P, diag(σ_P^2)).

The composition of these two functions, x_s → f_T(x_s) → f_P⁻¹(f_T(x_s)), effectively forms a normalizing flow from x_s to z ∼ N(0, I). The training objective minimizes the exact negative log-likelihood of p(x_s | x_t):
L_NTM = -log p(x_s | x_t) = -log p_P(u_s | u_t) - log |det J_{f_T}|.
This loss is expanded across the entire trajectory as a sum of terms related to the Gaussian prior of z, the predictor's scale (σ_P), and the transporter's Jacobian determinant (log σ_T for each affine coupling).

#### 3.2. Architecture and Training from Scratch

- **Deep-Shallow Architecture:** NTM adopts a deep-shallow design. The predictor (f_P) is a deep Transformer with non-causal full attention, processing all spatial positions in parallel to predict μ_P and σ_P for each denoising step. The transporter (f_T) comprises a few shallow TarFlow-style causal autoregressive flow blocks with alternating scan directions, capturing local spatial details within a single denoising step.
- **Forward Trajectory:** Given clean data x_0, a stochastic forward trajectory (x_t0..., x_tT) is constructed using a Markovian process (Eq. 2.3), satisfying specified marginal noise distributions.
- **Training Modes:**
 - **End-to-end:** The NTM loss (Eq. 3.4) is computed over all T conditional factors in the trajectory.
 - **Pair-wise:** A single consecutive pair (t, s) is randomly sampled per batch element.
- **Flexible Step Counts:** During training, T (number of steps) can be varied (e.g., {4, 8, 16}) per batch, allowing a single model to generate with different step counts. The transporter is conditioned on T to adapt to timestep spacing.
- **Sampling:** Sampling proceeds from noise to data. The predictor runs sequentially over T steps, drawing z ∼ N(0, I) to generate u_s_hat. After all steps, the transporter is inverted via sequential AR decoding to produce the final sample in x-space. Classifier-free guidance is applied by interpolating conditional and unconditional predictor outputs.
- **Trajectory Score Denoising:** Leveraging the exact trajectory likelihood, NTM computes the gradient of the NTM loss with respect to the generated trajectory (∇_x_L_NTM). This gradient represents the joint score of the full trajectory. A covariance-weighted gradient correction (Eq. 3.5) is applied to refine the trajectory, exploiting cross-timestep correlations for more effective denoising than independent per-sample approaches.

#### 3.3. Finetuning from Pretrained Models

NTM can be initialized from a pretrained flow-matching or diffusion model, allowing it to leverage existing high-performance backbones.

- **Initialization:**
 - The pretrained model's velocity field is reinterpreted in u-space.
 - The predictor (f_P) is initialized to match the denoising posterior of the pretrained model (Eq. 3.6), predicting μ_P = μ_post. A multiplicative scale correction (σ_P = σ_post * exp(δ_σ)) is learned, with δ_σ initialized to zero, ensuring σ_P = σ_post at the start.
 - The transporter (f_T) is initialized as an identity mapping.
- **Mean-Alignment Auxiliary Loss (L_aux):** To prevent divergence, an auxiliary loss (L_aux = ||μ_P - μ_FM||^2, Eq. 3.8) is added, aligning NTM's learned mean μ_P with the denoising mean μ_FM from a frozen copy of the pretrained backbone. This stabilizes training, anchoring the predictor near the pretrained solution while the transporter and scale correction learn the non-Gaussian structure. The weight λ for L_aux can be annealed during training.

#### 3.4. Fast Generation via Learned Denoiser

To reduce inference latency from sequential AR decoding and backpropagation-based denoising, a lightweight denoiser network (g_ϕ) can be trained.

- **Distillation:** After the main NTM model converges, g_ϕ is post-trained using MSE loss (Eq. 3.9) to predict the denoised image (x_den_0) directly from the predictor's output u_t0 (cleanest level in u-space) and text embeddings y. The targets (x_den_0) are generated by the frozen NTM model using trajectory score denoising.
- **Inference Pipeline:** The predictor runs over T steps to produce u_t0, then g_ϕ performs a single forward pass to yield the final denoised image. This bypasses iterative decoding and backpropagation, significantly accelerating generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates the effectiveness of Normalizing Trajectory Models (NTM) in achieving high-quality, few-step image generation with exact likelihood.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **Text-to-Image Generation (From Scratch):**
 - NTM, trained from scratch at 256x256 resolution, achieved an overall GenEval score of 0.82 and a DPG-Bench score of 79.64 with only 4 denoising steps.
 - This performance significantly exceeded that of the prior normalizing flow model, STARFlow (0.56 GenEval, requiring 256 autoregressive steps), and was comparable to or outperformed several strong diffusion model baselines that typically require substantially more sampling steps (e.g., SDXL 0.55, PixArt-α 0.48).
- **Text-to-Image Generation (Finetuned):**
 - A finetuned NTM model, initialized from a pretrained flow-matching backbone (FLUX.2-klein) and operating at 512x512 resolution, achieved a GenEval score of 0.76 and a DPG-Bench score of 83.38.
 - While slightly lower in GenEval than the best-performing models (e.g., Qwen-Image 0.87, Nucleus-Image 0.87), it demonstrated the scalability of NTM to higher resolutions and its ability to preserve the quality of pretrained models efficiently.
- **Class-Conditional ImageNet Generation (From Scratch):**
 - For class-conditional ImageNet 256x256, NTM achieved an FID score of 3.83 with 4 steps and 2.80 with 16 steps.
 - This is competitive with STARFlow (FAE) which achieved 2.67 FID but required 256 autoregressive steps. This result indicates that exact likelihood training alone, without additional distribution-level losses (like adversarial or perceptual losses), can produce competitive few-step generation.

#### 4.2. Efficiency and Ablations

- **Learned Denoiser Speedup:** The optional learned denoiser (g_ϕ) significantly accelerated inference. It provided an approximate 9x speedup (1.88 img/s vs. 0.20 img/s) compared to the full NTM with transporter inversion and trajectory score denoising, while maintaining high fidelity to the score-based refinement output (LPIPS of 0.121). This confirms the effectiveness of distilling the self-refinement process into a single forward pass.
- **Transporter Importance:** Ablation studies showed that removing the invertible transporter (effectively reducing NTM to a Gaussian model in x-space) resulted in severely blurry outputs when using few steps. The transporter's role in creating a latent space where the conditional distribution is simpler for the affine predictor was critical for recovering high-quality generation (comparable to 50-step flow matching in 4 steps).
- **Auxiliary Loss for Finetuning:** The mean-alignment auxiliary loss (L_aux) was crucial for stabilizing training when finetuning from pretrained models. Without L_aux, the NLL objective alone led to divergence and catastrophic forgetting, as the predictor drifted from the pretrained solution. The auxiliary loss anchored the predictor mean, allowing the transporter and scale correction to learn non-Gaussian structure stably.
- **Multi-Trajectory Training:** Training with varying trajectory lengths (T ∈ {4, 8, 16}) demonstrated that T=4 provided a favorable quality-speed trade-off for the finetuning setting. Longer trajectories offered finer-grained denoising but at increased computational cost.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

- Samples generated by NTM (Figure 1, 10) demonstrated strong compositional generalization, fine-grained attribute control, and adherence to various artistic styles in both from-scratch (256x256) and finetuned (512x512) settings.
- The finetuned model preserved the visual quality and prompt adherence of its pretrained backbone (FLUX, typically requiring 50 steps) while operating in only 4 steps, suggesting the model effectively captured the non-Gaussian reverse conditional information lost by naive step reduction.

#### 4.4. Discussion on Architectural Trade-offs

- NTM is characterized as an interpolation between STARFlow (deep spatial autoregressive flow in one step) and flow matching (many small Gaussian steps). NTM distributes capacity across multiple denoising steps, using shallow transporters per step and a deep trajectory-level predictor. This design allows the shallow transporters to handle local non-Gaussian residuals, while the deep predictor efficiently performs cross-timestep reasoning in u-space.
- The paper acknowledged a "failure case" for T=1 (single-step generation) with NTM, where severely degraded outputs were observed. This was attributed to the shallow transporter's insufficient capacity to model the entire non-Gaussian structure of the data distribution in one go, unlike a much deeper STARFlow block designed for single-step marginal density modeling. This highlights that NTM's strength lies in distributing the modeling of non-Gaussianity across a few (e.g., 4-8) steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Normalizing Trajectory Models (NTM) present a notable advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly in addressing a long-standing challenge with diffusion-based models. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Breaking the Gaussian Bottleneck with Exact Likelihood:** NTM directly confronts the "Gaussian bottleneck" inherent in few-step diffusion models by explicitly modeling the complex, non-Gaussian nature of reverse conditional probabilities. Crucially, it achieves this while retaining an exact likelihood framework, a feature often sacrificed by other acceleration methods (e.g., distillation, consistency models, GAN-based approaches). This provides a principled, stable, and interpretable probabilistic foundation for efficient generation.

2. **Efficient High-Fidelity Generation:** The ability of NTM to generate high-quality images in as few as four sampling steps significantly improves the inference efficiency of generative models. This reduction in computational cost makes high-fidelity image synthesis more accessible for real-time applications, interactive creative tools, and large-scale content generation where speed is paramount.

3. **Flexible Training and Adaptability:** NTM offers dual training pathways: it can be trained from scratch or efficiently finetuned from existing pretrained diffusion or flow-matching models. This flexibility allows researchers and practitioners to leverage powerful existing backbones, accelerating development and enabling the benefits of NTM to be integrated into established pipelines. The finetuning recipe, particularly the auxiliary loss, provides a robust method for adaptation without catastrophic forgetting.

4. **Novel Architectural Principles:** The deep-shallow architecture, combining shallow invertible blocks (transporters) with a deep, parallel predictor across the trajectory, introduces an effective way to distribute modeling capacity. This design allows for efficient capture of both local non-Gaussian structure within steps and global cross-timestep dependencies, offering a new architectural paradigm for generative modeling.

5. **Enhanced Denoising and Refinement:** The exact trajectory likelihood enables a sophisticated, covariance-weighted score-based denoising mechanism that accounts for correlations across timesteps. The further distillation of this process into a lightweight learned denoiser demonstrates a practical pathway to achieve high quality and extremely fast inference in a single forward pass, pushing the boundaries of real-time generation.

6. **Broader Implications for Generative AI Research:** By providing a model with exact likelihood, NTM opens avenues for research in areas such as:
 - **Content Verification and Anomaly Detection:** Tractable densities can facilitate identifying out-of-distribution samples or generated content that deviates from learned patterns.
 - **Controllable Generation:** A clearer probabilistic model can lead to more predictable and controllable generation processes.
 - **Downstream Applications:** The efficient and high-quality generation can benefit data augmentation, scientific visualization, drug discovery, and creative content creation.

The paper acknowledges potential societal implications, specifically the dual-use nature of powerful generative models. By emphasizing the exact likelihood framework, NTM potentially contributes to more auditable and interpretable models, supporting the development of safeguards like watermarking and provenance tracking. This work suggests a future where high-performance, efficient generative AI is built upon transparent and well-understood probabilistic foundations.
