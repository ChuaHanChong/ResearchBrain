# LGQ: Learning Discretization Geometry for Scalable and Stable Image Tokenization

> [!summary] Summary
> LGQ introduces a method for image tokenization that learns discretization geometry through temperature-controlled soft assignments, resolving issues like VQ collapse and FSQ rigidity. It achieves superior image reconstruction (lowest rFID of 110.64 on ImageNet) while utilizing only 50% of the codebook at K=16,384, demonstrating efficient capacity allocation.

## Problem

- Traditional Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoders (VQ-VAEs) suffer from 'representation collapse,' where only a small subset of learned codebook entries are actively used, hindering scalability and diverse representation learning.
- Structured quantization schemes like Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) offer stability and full code utilization but are rigid, with fixed discretization geometries that may not align with complex, anisotropic data manifolds, leading to inefficient capacity.
- A persistent trade-off exists between the flexible geometries of VQ-based methods and the robust utilization of structured quantizers, limiting the development of scalable and stable image tokenizers for generative models.

## Method

- **LGQ** replaces hard nearest-neighbor lookups with ==temperature-controlled soft assignments== based on Euclidean distances, grounding this in a ==variational free-energy objective== for fully differentiable optimization.
- It employs a ==straight-through soft-to-hard estimator== for gradient flow and ==linearly anneals the temperature== parameter during training to ensure stable learning while progressing towards hard, discrete assignments.
- The framework integrates joint regularization: a ==peakedness regularizer== encourages confident token assignments, and a ==global usage regularizer== promotes balanced utilization across the entire codebook, directly addressing codebook collapse.

## Results

- LGQ achieved the lowest rFID (**110.64**) and best perceptual metrics (SSIM **0.6335**, LPIPS **0.4864**) on ImageNet at K=16,384, surpassing standard VQ, LFQ, FSQ, and SimVQ.
- It demonstrated efficient capacity allocation by utilizing only **50%** (8,199 active codes) of the codebook at K=16,384, compared to baselines like FSQ (**100%**) and SimVQ (**98.9%**) that nearly saturate their codebooks for similar or worse performance.
- LGQ exhibited robust scalability, maintaining superior reconstruction and efficient utilization (**22.5%** active codes) even with a much larger codebook (K=65,536), and ablation studies confirmed the critical role of temperature annealing and joint regularization for optimal performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning the discretization geometry end-to-end, rather than relying on fixed structures or static codebooks, is crucial for achieving efficient and stable discrete tokenization that adapts to the data's intrinsic structure.
> - Temperature-controlled soft assignments, supported by a variational objective, enable stable gradient flow to all codebook entries, overcoming the biased updates and representation collapse seen in traditional VQ.
> - Combining specific regularization techniques (peakedness and global usage) effectively balances the trade-off between confident token assignments and full, balanced codebook utilization, leading to improved rate-distortion characteristics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of discrete representation learning, generative artificial intelligence, and efficient data compression, a rapidly evolving field. In particular, it addresses a core challenge in the development of large-scale multimodal generative models, such as text-to-image diffusion models, speech synthesizers, and video generation systems. These models rely heavily on converting high-dimensional continuous data (like images or audio) into compact, discrete tokens that can be efficiently processed by transformer-based architectures or used as conditioning variables for other generative priors.

The foundational technique for this is the Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE), which introduced a mechanism to map continuous latent representations to discrete codes from a learned codebook. While VQ-VAEs have been highly successful, they suffer from significant scalability issues. A primary problem is "representation collapse," where only a small subset of the learned codebook entries are actively used, leading to inefficient capacity allocation and hindering the model's ability to learn diverse representations. This challenge intensifies with larger vocabulary sizes, a necessity for increasingly complex generative tasks. Existing methods attempt to mitigate this through heuristics like commitment losses, codebook reseeding, or reparameterizations (e.g., SimVQ), but these often add complexity or do not fundamentally resolve the underlying instability.

At the other end of the spectrum are structured quantization schemes, such as Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ). FSQ simplifies the problem by quantizing each latent dimension independently on a fixed grid, guaranteeing full code utilization and avoiding collapse. However, this stability comes at the cost of rigidity; the fixed discretization geometry may not align well with the complex, anisotropic, and data-dependent manifolds of real-world latent representations, leading to inefficient capacity usage.

LGQ positions itself as a crucial bridge between these two extremes. It aims to combine the geometric flexibility and adaptive power of VQ-based methods with the stability and robust utilization characteristics of structured quantizers like FSQ. By proposing a fully differentiable, geometry-learning approach, LGQ seeks to overcome the persistent trade-off between flexible latent space partitioning and stable, complete codebook utilization, thereby enabling more robust and scalable tokenization for the next generation of generative AI. This work directly addresses the "central bottleneck for scalable visual generation" highlighted in the abstract, aiming to provide a more principled and effective mechanism for converting continuous encoder outputs into discrete, semantically rich tokens.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Learnable Geometric Quantization (LGQ) stems from the inherent limitations and trade-offs observed in existing discrete image tokenization methods, especially in the context of large-scale generative models. The authors identify a "persistent trade-off" between the flexible geometries learned by vector-quantized (VQ) tokenizers and the robust utilization achieved by structured scalar or implicit tokenizers (like FSQ).

Specifically, the key motivations are:
- **Addressing Representation Collapse in VQ:** Traditional VQ schemes suffer from biased straight-through optimization, codebook under-utilization, and representation collapse. As vocabulary sizes increase, these problems become more severe, limiting scalability and representational power. The authors emphasize the need to address collapse "at its root."
- **Overcoming Rigidity in Structured Quantizers:** While methods like FSQ offer stability and full utilization, they rely on fixed discretization geometries. This rigidity can lead to inefficient capacity allocation when latent statistics are heterogeneous, anisotropic, or dataset-dependent, as real-world data manifolds are rarely axis-aligned or uniformly distributed.
- **Enabling Scalable and Stable Visual Generation:** The ultimate goal is to facilitate the development of cutting-edge generative systems by providing a tokenizer that is compact enough for efficient latent-space priors, preserves semantically important structures, and effectively utilizes its discrete capacity, even with very large vocabularies. This is crucial for models like text-to-image diffusion models and large language models operating on visual tokens.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives for LGQ are to:
1. **Learn Discretization Geometry End-to-End:** Develop a mechanism that allows the tokenizer to adapt its quantization boundaries directly from the data, moving beyond fixed grids or static codebooks.
2. **Ensure Stable and Differentiable Optimization:** Replace hard nearest-neighbor lookups with a smoother, fully differentiable assignment process that allows gradients to flow to all codebook entries, thereby preventing disjoint updates and promoting balanced utilization.
3. **Promote Confident and Balanced Codebook Usage:** Design regularization strategies that encourage tokens to be assigned to codes with high confidence (peaked assignments) while simultaneously ensuring that the entire codebook is utilized in a balanced manner, preventing both token and embedding collapse.
4. **Achieve Favorable Rate-Distortion Trade-offs:** Produce high-quality reconstructions using a compact set of discrete codes, implying efficient allocation of representational capacity rather than brute-force codebook saturation.
5. **Serve as a Robust Drop-in Replacement:** Create a tokenizer that can seamlessly integrate into existing autoencoder architectures (like VQGAN) and offer superior performance without requiring complex heuristics or extensive tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LGQ proposes a novel Learnable Geometric Quantization framework designed to overcome the limitations of existing VQ and structured quantization methods. Its core approach centers on replacing hard nearest-neighbor assignments with temperature-controlled soft assignments, coupled with specific regularization techniques and a principled variational objective.

The methodology can be broken down as follows:

1. **VQ-VAE Backbone:** LGQ operates within a standard VQGAN-style encoder-decoder architecture. An encoder maps an input image to a continuous latent representation. This latent representation is then discretized by the LGQ module, and a decoder reconstructs the image from the discrete codes.
2. **Temperature-Controlled Soft Assignments:** Instead of directly finding the nearest neighbor (as in classical VQ), LGQ computes soft assignment probabilities $p_{t,k}$ for each latent vector $z_{e,t}$ to every codebook entry $c_k$. These probabilities are derived from a Gibbs distribution based on Euclidean distances:
 $p_{t,k} \propto \exp(-\|z_{e,t} - c_k\|^2 / \tau)$.
 The temperature parameter $\tau$ controls the "softness" of the assignments. High $\tau$ leads to diffuse assignments, while low $\tau$ makes assignments sharper, approaching a hard nearest-neighbor selection.
3. **Variational Free-Energy Objective:** The soft assignment formulation is grounded in a principled variational framework. These probabilities are shown to be the posterior responsibilities in an isotropic Gaussian mixture model and minimize a variational free-energy functional, which balances expected distortion with assignment entropy. This provides a theoretical underpinning for LGQ's behavior.
4. **Straight-Through Soft-to-Hard Estimator:** During training, LGQ uses a straight-through estimator (STE) to bridge the gap between continuous optimization and discrete representation. While the discrete index is selected as the `argmax` of the soft assignments, the gradient flow for the quantized token is maintained by adding a residual correction from the soft average. This allows gradients to update all codebook entries, not just the selected one, while ensuring a discrete output for inference.
5. **Temperature Annealing:** To facilitate the transition from soft, differentiable training to hard, discrete inference, the temperature $\tau$ is linearly annealed over training epochs, starting from a high value (e.g., 1.0) and decreasing to a low value (e.g., 0.1). This gradual reduction ensures stable learning while progressively sharpening the assignments, with theoretical guarantees that this converges to hard quantization in the low-temperature limit.
6. **Loss Function and Regularization:** The total training objective combines a reconstruction loss (L1 distance) with a latent discretization loss $L_{disc}$. The $L_{disc}$ includes:
 - **Squared Error between Latents:** $ \|z_q - z_e\|^2_2 $ to keep quantized and continuous latents consistent.
 - **Peakedness Regularizer ($L_{peak}$):** $E_n[\max(0, 1 - \sum_k p^2_{n,k})]$ penalizes high-entropy assignments, encouraging confident (near one-hot) selections for each token. This ensures that the model commits to specific codes.
 - **Global Usage Regularizer ($L_{bins}$):** $\sum_k \bar{p}^2_k$ minimizes the squared L2 norm of the empirical marginal code usage distribution ($\bar{p}_k$). This penalizes concentrated allocation and encourages balanced utilization across the entire codebook, directly addressing codebook collapse without imposing rigid structures.
7. **Theoretical Properties:** The paper provides theoretical guarantees for LGQ, including:
 - **Soft-to-hard convergence:** As $\tau \to 0$, soft assignments converge to one-hot distributions, effectively recovering hard nearest-neighbor quantization at inference.
 - **Lipschitz continuity:** The gradients of the assignment probabilities are Lipschitz continuous with respect to encoder outputs and codebook parameters, ensuring optimization stability.

By integrating these elements, LGQ learns its discretization geometry end-to-end, adapts to the intrinsic structure of encoder outputs, and maintains stable optimization and balanced codebook utilization through principled regularization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of LGQ primarily focuses on ImageNet at 128x128 resolution, using a VQGAN-style encoder-decoder backbone for fair comparison against several strong baselines: standard VQ, LFQ (MAGVIT-v2), FSQ, and SimVQ. The key findings are categorized across reconstruction quality, codebook utilization, rate-distortion trade-offs, and training dynamics.

1. **Superior Reconstruction Quality and Favorable Utilization:**
 - At a codebook size of $K=16,384$, LGQ achieves the best reconstruction fidelity, indicated by the lowest rFID (110.64), and superior perceptual metrics (SSIM of 0.6335, LPIPS of 0.4864) compared to all baselines (FSQ, VQ, LFQ, SimVQ).
 - Crucially, LGQ accomplishes this while activating **substantially fewer codebook entries** (8,199 active codes, or 50% utilization) compared to baselines like FSQ (100% utilization) and SimVQ (98.9% utilization), which nearly saturate their codebooks. This demonstrates that LGQ's performance stems from efficient, data-aligned capacity allocation rather than simply using more codes.

2. **Robust Large-Vocabulary Scaling:**
 - When scaled to a much larger codebook ($K=65,536$) and evaluated at an early training stage (epoch 9), LGQ consistently maintains its advantage. It yields the lowest rFID (111.08) and highest SSIM (0.6401) while again operating at significantly lower utilization (22.5% active codes) compared to baselines. This indicates LGQ's ability to effectively manage discrete capacity and achieve favorable reconstruction-utilization trade-offs even in high-capacity regimes.

3. **Efficient Rate-Distortion Trade-offs:**
 - LGQ demonstrates a more favorable rate-distortion profile. It achieves comparable or better distortion (lower rFID and LPIPS) at a significantly lower effective representation rate (measured by active codes or entropy) than methods like FSQ and SimVQ, which achieve near-complete utilization but without consistently lower distortion. This implies that LGQ intelligently concentrates its active codes in the most semantically relevant regions of the latent space, maximizing information per code.
 - UMAP visualizations of the latent space confirm this: LGQ activates a compact, well-aligned subset of codebook entries that closely follow the high-density regions of the encoder's latent distribution, providing comprehensive coverage with fewer resources.

4. **Stable Training Dynamics and Adaptive Geometry:**
 - LGQ exhibits smooth and stable convergence across various reconstruction and utilization metrics throughout training, as evidenced by per-epoch plots.
 - Analysis of codebook center drift magnitude reveals that LGQ's centers undergo substantial, structured, and data-dependent movement during early training, indicating active adaptation of the discretization geometry. This movement stabilizes as training progresses, confirming that the codebook functions as an adaptive quantization mechanism rather than a static lookup table.
 - Entropy-based analysis shows LGQ converges to a stable intermediate entropy (perplexity), autonomously selecting an efficient operating rate, unlike VQ (which collapses) or FSQ/SimVQ (which saturate by design).

5. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Temperature Annealing:** Fast annealing of the temperature $\tau$ is critical for optimal performance, achieving the best rFID and LPIPS while maintaining high utilization. Fixed or slow annealing leads to inferior performance or catastrophic collapse.
 - **Regularization:** The joint application of both the peakedness and global usage regularizers is crucial. While individual regularizers might reduce utilization, they also degrade reconstruction quality. Their combined effect ensures a balanced and optimal rate-distortion trade-off, preventing collapse and promoting confident assignments simultaneously.
 - **Matched Effective Capacity:** Even when baseline models are artificially restricted to match LGQ's number of active codes, LGQ still achieves competitive or superior reconstruction quality, particularly outperforming FSQ and SimVQ. This underscores that LGQ's advantage lies in its *efficiency and robustness* in selecting and positioning codes, rather than simply having a certain number of active codes.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that LGQ successfully addresses the long-standing trade-off in discrete tokenization, offering a principled, stable, and highly efficient solution for scalable visual generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Learnable Geometric Quantization (LGQ) represents a significant advancement in the field of discrete representation learning, with broad implications for scalable generative AI. Its primary significance lies in providing a robust and principled solution to fundamental bottlenecks that have limited the practical application and scalability of previous tokenization methods.

**1. Bridging the Gap between Flexibility and Stability:**
LGQ successfully reconciles the geometric flexibility of Vector Quantization (VQ) with the training stability and utilization guarantees of structured quantizers like Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ). By learning its discretization geometry end-to-end through temperature-controlled soft assignments, LGQ overcomes the representation collapse and biased gradients inherent in traditional VQ, while avoiding the rigidity and potential inefficiency of fixed-grid approaches like FSQ. This makes it a more generally applicable and robust tokenizer.

**2. Enhanced Scalability and Efficiency:**
The ability of LGQ to achieve superior reconstruction quality with substantially fewer active codebook entries, even at very large vocabulary sizes, is a critical breakthrough. This means generative models can operate with a more compact, yet equally expressive, discrete latent space. This efficiency translates to:
- **Reduced Memory and Computational Footprint:** Fewer active codes mean less memory is required for the codebook and potentially faster lookup operations.
- **Improved Training of Downstream Models:** A more compact and semantically aligned latent space can simplify the task for autoregressive or diffusion priors (e.g., large language models operating on visual tokens), leading to more efficient training and better generation quality.
- **Better Rate-Distortion Trade-offs:** LGQ's capacity to allocate discrete resources efficiently, focusing on high-density regions of the data manifold, provides a superior balance between compression rate and reconstruction fidelity.

**3. Principled and Stable Optimization:**
The variational free-energy formulation, coupled with theoretical guarantees on convergence to hard quantization and Lipschitz continuity of gradients, offers a principled foundation for LGQ. The use of temperature annealing and tailored regularizers (peakedness and global usage) ensures stable and balanced training dynamics without relying on ad-hoc heuristics like codebook reseeding or complex commitment losses that often plague VQ-based methods. This simplifies the development and deployment of high-performance tokenizers.

**4. Drop-in Replaceability and General Applicability:**
LGQ is designed as a drop-in replacement for existing discrete tokenizers within autoencoder architectures (like VQGAN). This ease of integration allows practitioners to readily upgrade their generative pipelines, immediately benefiting from LGQ's improved stability and performance without extensive architectural modifications. Its general-purpose nature suggests applicability across various modalities.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancing Multimodal Generative AI:** LGQ can directly enhance the capabilities of large-scale generative models in domains like text-to-image synthesis, video generation, and speech synthesis, where efficient and semantically rich discrete tokens are paramount. This could lead to higher-fidelity outputs, faster inference, and more diverse generations.
- **New Avenues for Representation Learning:** By demonstrating that geometry learning is key to efficient quantization, LGQ opens up new research directions in how discrete representations are formed and utilized, potentially inspiring further innovations in unsupervised and self-supervised learning.
- **Democratization of Large-Scale Models:** By making discrete tokenization more stable and efficient, LGQ could contribute to making complex generative models more accessible, reducing the computational resources and specialized expertise needed to train them effectively.

While the current evaluation is limited to ImageNet at a specific resolution, LGQ's theoretical soundness and empirical performance on a challenging dataset like ImageNet suggest its potential to become a cornerstone technology for future advancements in AI-driven content creation and data compression. Future work extending its validation to other datasets, modalities, and higher resolutions, and evaluating its impact on end-to-end generative modeling, will further solidify its importance.
