# LGQ: Learning Discretization Geometry for Scalable and Stable Image Tokenization

> [!summary] Summary
> LGQ introduces a method for image tokenization that learns discretization geometry through temperature-controlled soft assignments, resolving issues like VQ collapse and FSQ rigidity. It achieves superior image reconstruction (lowest rFID of 110.64 on ImageNet) while utilizing only 50% of the codebook at K=16,384, demonstrating efficient capacity allocation.

## Problem

- Traditional Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoders (VQ-VAEs) suffer from 'representation collapse,' where only a small subset of learned codebook entries are actively used, hindering scalability and diverse representation learning.
- Structured quantization schemes like Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) offer stability and full code utilization but are rigid, with fixed discretization geometries that may not align with complex, anisotropic data manifolds, leading to inefficient capacity.
- A persistent trade-off exists between the flexible geometries of VQ-based methods and the robust utilization of structured quantizers, limiting the development of scalable and stable image tokenizers for generative models.

## Method

- **LGQ** replaces hard nearest-neighbor lookups with ==temperature-controlled soft assignments== based on Euclidean distances, grounding this in a ==variational free-energy objective== for fully differentiable optimization.
- It employs a ==straight-through soft-to-hard estimator== for gradient flow and ==linearly anneals the temperature== parameter during training to ensure stable learning while progressing towards hard, discrete assignments.
- The framework integrates joint regularization: a ==peakedness regularizer== encourages confident token assignments, and a ==global usage regularizer== promotes balanced utilization across the entire codebook, directly addressing codebook collapse.

## Results

- LGQ achieved the lowest rFID (**110.64**) and best perceptual metrics (SSIM **0.6335**, LPIPS **0.4864**) on ImageNet at K=16,384, surpassing standard VQ, LFQ, FSQ, and SimVQ.
- It demonstrated efficient capacity allocation by utilizing only **50%** (8,199 active codes) of the codebook at K=16,384, compared to baselines like FSQ (**100%**) and SimVQ (**98.9%**) that nearly saturate their codebooks for similar or worse performance.
- LGQ exhibited robust scalability, maintaining superior reconstruction and efficient utilization (**22.5%** active codes) even with a much larger codebook (K=65,536), and ablation studies confirmed the critical role of temperature annealing and joint regularization for optimal performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning the discretization geometry end-to-end, rather than relying on fixed structures or static codebooks, is crucial for achieving efficient and stable discrete tokenization that adapts to the data's intrinsic structure.
> - Temperature-controlled soft assignments, supported by a variational objective, enable stable gradient flow to all codebook entries, overcoming the biased updates and representation collapse seen in traditional VQ.
> - Combining specific regularization techniques (peakedness and global usage) effectively balances the trade-off between confident token assignments and full, balanced codebook utilization, leading to improved rate-distortion characteristics.
