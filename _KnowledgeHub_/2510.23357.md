# Large language model-based task planning for service robots: A review

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper is a systematic review of LLM-based task planning for domestic service robots, introducing a modality-centric taxonomy (text, vision, audio, multimodal) that organizes existing methods, reviews LLM foundations (pre-training, fine-tuning, retrieval-augmented generation, prompting), and identifies five critical challenges plus matching research directions for real-world deployment.

## Problem

- Prior LLM-robotics surveys broadly cover perception, control, or multi-robot coordination but lack systematic, domain-specific analysis of task planning for domestic service robots.
- Existing reviews organize methods by application or technique rather than input modality, obscuring cross-modal synergies and modality-specific implementation barriers unique to home settings.
- Traditional symbolic task-planning methods assume finite, deterministic, invariant goals and struggle with the dynamic clutter, occlusion, and open-world uncertainty of real domestic environments.

## Method

- ==Modality-centric taxonomy== classifying LLM-driven task planning into four categories: text-based, vision-based (VLM), audio-based, and multimodal (MLLM), each analyzed for architecture and fusion strategy.
- Structured theoretical-foundations synthesis covering ==LLM pre-training== paradigms, full and ==parameter-efficient fine-tuning==, ==prompt-engineering== techniques (==chain-of-thought==, few-shot), and ==retrieval-augmented generation== variants underlying robotic planners.
- Comparative literature tables and architecture diagrams (e.g., LLM-grop, COWP, GRID, ReplanVLM) mapping each surveyed method's core innovation against its limitations across instruction complexity and modality.

## Results

- Synthesizes concrete efficiency gains reported across surveyed LLMs: DeepSeek-v2's Multihead Latent Attention gives **5.76x** faster inference, GLaM's MoE cuts training energy to **one-third** of GPT-3's.
- Identifies **five** recurring failure modes across the reviewed corpus of systems: real-time and safety insufficiency, multimodal fusion difficulty, missing benchmarks, weak active cognition, immature tactile-language integration.
- Cross-references case studies, such as GR00T-Dreams compressing skill acquisition from **months** to **36 hours**, showing dual-system VLA architectures now bypass symbolic planning for direct joint control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Organizing a fast-moving field by input modality, not technique or era, surfaces cross-modal fusion gaps that technique-centric taxonomies tend to miss.
> - Pure-text planners can reason well but structurally cannot perceive; environmental grounding must come from vision, audio, or pre-injected knowledge, not model scale alone.
> - Symbolic-goal invariance assumptions that worked for classical planners break down in dynamic homes; hallucination-robust, uncertainty-aware planning is a prerequisite, not an add-on.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The increasing integration of service robots into daily life necessitates robust and accurate task planning capabilities for intelligent and efficient service delivery in complex environments. Traditionally, robotic task planning has relied on detailed task models and environmental information, often assuming finite, deterministic, and invariant planning goals. These traditional methods exhibit limitations in dynamic, unpredictable real-world scenarios, characterized by low fault tolerance, limited scalability, weak adaptability, and increased perception difficulty due to factors such as clutter, occlusion, and dynamic object relationships.

In parallel, Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated advanced comprehension and generation capabilities, becoming a significant force in artificial intelligence. The integration of LLMs with robotics is recognized as a key enabler for transforming robots from single-task performers into more general-purpose intelligent agents, enhancing their ability to understand and adapt to environments through common-sense knowledge and reasoning.

The existing research landscape includes a surge of reviews exploring LLM-robotics integration. These surveys have covered various aspects such as LLM-enhanced robotic systems (e.g., communication, perception, control), decision-making, path planning, multi-robot coordination, autonomous task discovery, navigation, perception, reasoning, and tool use. While these reviews have provided broad insights, the authors of this paper identify a critical gap: a systematic analysis of task planning *specifically for domestic service robots*. This domain presents distinct challenges due to the unstructured nature of home environments and the diverse patterns of human-robot interaction, which require specialized approaches not fully addressed in prior works. This review aims to fill this gap by focusing on LLM-driven task planning for service robots operating in home environments, utilizing a novel modality-centric taxonomy.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to provide a detailed and comprehensive overview of the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into service robotics, with a particular focus on their role in enhancing robotic task planning capabilities in complex, unstructured domestic environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several factors:
1. **Advancements in LLMs and Robotics:** The rapid progress in both LLMs and robotics is making service robots an increasingly integral part of daily life, offering a wide range of services. To maximize their utility, these robots require sophisticated task planning abilities.
2. **Limitations of Traditional Planning Methods:** Conventional robotic task planning approaches often rely on assumptions of environmental stability and pre-defined models, which are inadequate for dynamic, unpredictable, and cluttered domestic settings. These methods struggle with fault tolerance, scalability, and adaptability, highlighting a need for more intelligent solutions.
3. **LLMs as a Cognitive Core:** LLMs offer unprecedented capabilities in natural language understanding, common-sense reasoning, and processing multimodal data. This makes them suitable as a "cognitive core" or "brain" for service robots, potentially improving autonomy and decision-making by enabling robots to parse ambiguous commands, decompose complex tasks, and generate executable action sequences.
4. **Addressing a Research Gap:** While several reviews have broadly explored LLM applications in robotics, there is a recognized lack of systematic analysis specifically dedicated to task planning for *domestic* service robots. This domain presents unique challenges that require a focused examination.

To achieve its overall objective, the paper outlines three key innovations and contributions:
1. **Systematic Review of LLM Fundamentals:** To establish a foundational understanding, the paper systematically reviews the development of LLMs and provides a concise overview of their core techniques, including pre-training, fine-tuning, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and prompt engineering.
2. **Modality-Centric Taxonomy:** The authors propose and establish the first modality-centric taxonomy for analyzing LLM-based task planning in domestic service robotics. This framework categorizes planning methods based on input modalities (text, vision, audio, multimodal), aiming to reveal unique cross-modal challenges and opportunities for technological development.
3. **Identification of Challenges and Future Directions:** The review critically examines the key obstacles hindering the real-world deployment of LLM-based service robots and proposes actionable research pathways to advance the field, thereby guiding future efforts toward more robust, adaptable, and efficient robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The authors adopt a structured review methodology, organizing their analysis around the input modalities that drive LLM-based task planning in service robots. This approach is designed to systematically categorize and evaluate existing research, highlighting both advancements and limitations within each modality.

The methodology begins by laying a theoretical foundation of LLMs, covering their historical development and core techniques. Subsequently, the paper introduces a novel, modality-centric taxonomy that classifies LLM-driven task planning methods into four main categories: Text-based LLM Planning, Vision-Language Model Planning, Audio-based Planning, and Multimodal Large Language Models Planning. Each category is then explored in detail, discussing specific methods, frameworks, and their applications.

**Theoretical Foundation of LLMs (Section 2):**
- **Background:** Traces the evolution of LLMs from the Transformer architecture (2017) through key models like GPT-1, BERT, T5, GPT-3, GLaM, GLM-130B, and DeepSeek-v2, highlighting advancements in model scale, interaction capabilities, and efficiency.
- **Pre-training:** Discusses the self-supervised learning process on massive unlabeled text data, focusing on Transformer architecture variants (causal decoder, encoder-decoder, prefix decoder), pre-training task designs (autoregressive language modeling, span masking), and large-scale optimization techniques (AdamW, AdaFactor, parallelism, mixed-precision).
- **Fine-tuning:** Explains how pre-trained LLMs are adapted for specific tasks, detailing Full Fine-tuning (FFT) and Parameter-Efficient Fine-tuning (PEFT) methods (e.g., LoRA, adapter layers). It also covers Instruction Fine-tuning and Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) for aligning model outputs with human preferences.
- **Prompt Engineering:** Describes techniques for guiding LLMs to generate accurate outputs without parameter updates, including Instruction Prompting, Zero-shot Prompting, Few-shot Prompting, Chain-of-Thought Prompting, and Recursive Prompting.
- **Retrieval-augmented Generation (RAG):** Explains RAG as a mechanism to overcome LLM limitations in knowledge timeliness and factual accuracy by integrating external knowledge bases. It outlines RAG's two main components (Retrieval and Generation) and discusses typical approaches like Multi-Head RAG, Adaptive-RAG, Blended RAG, Self-RAG, and IM-RAG.
- **Application of LLMs in Robotic Task Planning:** Introduces LLMs as the "brain" of service robots, enabling them to parse ambiguous commands, understand user intent, decompose high-level goals into subtasks, and generate executable action sequences, often enhanced by Visual Language Models (VLMs) for environmental perception.

**Modality-Centric Taxonomy and Detailed Analysis (Sections 3-7):**
The core of the methodology involves segmenting the review based on the primary input modality used for task planning:
1. **Text-based LLM Task Planning (Section 4):**
 - Focuses on how LLMs process natural language instructions to generate action sequences.
 - Examines methods for handling different levels of instruction complexity (short-term vs. long-horizon tasks) and strategies to compensate for the lack of direct environmental perception (static knowledge injection, dynamic state updates).
2. **Vision-based LLM Task Planning (Section 5):**
 - Analyzes the integration of visual perception to enhance robots' understanding of physical environments.
 - Covers methodological and framework innovations (spatial representation, task decomposition, dynamic adaptation) and the concept of active task cognition (visual reasoning, VQA technologies).
3. **Audio-based LLM Task Planning (Section 6):**
 - Investigates how robots understand and respond to voice commands and ambient sounds, especially in scenarios where visual input is limited.
 - Discusses basic model architectures (e.g., CLAP, AudioCLIP), audio comprehension and reasoning, and voice interaction methods.
4. **Multimodal LLM Task Planning (Section 7):**
 - Explores systems that integrate multiple modalities (vision, audio, text, sensor data) to support more complex task planning.
 - Reviews various Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), their architectures, fusion techniques, and practical applications in robot systems and environmental interaction.

**Challenges and Future Prospects (Section 8):**
The final part of the methodology synthesizes the identified limitations and open problems across all modalities, proposing concrete future research directions to guide advancements in the field. This includes addressing issues like real-time performance, safety, multimodal fusion, active task cognition, model evaluation, and the underdeveloped area of tactile language models.

By employing this structured, modality-centric approach, the authors aim to provide a comprehensive yet focused review that systematically analyzes the evolving landscape of LLM-based task planning for service robots.

### 4. Main findings and results

The review systematically categorizes and analyzes the integration of LLMs into service robot task planning based on input modalities, revealing key advancements, challenges, and future directions.

**A. Text-based LLM Task Planning (Section 4)**
- **Instruction Complexity:** LLMs exhibit strong planning abilities for simple short-term tasks, leveraging common-sense knowledge for object rearrangement (e.g., LLM-grop) and generalized planning (e.g., ProgPrompt). Integration with Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) (e.g., Sliver et al., Liu et al.) and Monte Carlo Tree Search (e.g., Tomoya et al.) enhances planning performance by translating natural language into symbolic representations. For complex long-horizon tasks, hierarchical and iterative frameworks (e.g., HM-Diffuser, Plan-and-Act) and hierarchical task decomposition (e.g., Chain-of-Thought prompting, DELTA, LLM-Collab) are employed.
- **Addressing Hallucinations and Uncertainty:** Strategies like KnowNo (conformal prediction for uncertainty quantification), LLM uncertainty estimation (user interaction for disambiguation), and constrained prompt schemes are proposed to mitigate the accumulation of hallucinations and ensure executable action sequences.
- **Environmental Perception Compensation:** To address the inherent lack of physical environmental perception from pure text input, methods include static knowledge injection (e.g., pre-entering object states in LLM-grop, KGGPT using knowledge graphs for robotic skills) and dynamic state updates (e.g., COWP integrating traditional planning with LLMs for common-sense knowledge to handle open-world situations).

**B. Vision-based LLM Task Planning (Section 5)**
- **Methodological and Framework Innovations:** VLMs bridge the perception-reasoning gap, enhancing spatial perception and dynamic adaptation. Innovations include VLMaps (combining visual-language features with 3D reconstruction for spatial target positioning), 3D-grounded VLMs (automating 3D spatial information extraction from 2D inputs), and graph-based methods like GRID (graph attention networks for object relationships) and VeriGraph (scene graphs for iterative plan validation and correction). Dynamic adaptation is facilitated by frameworks like ReplanVLM (internal/external error correction) and ViLaln (optimizing PDDL descriptions via error feedback). For embodied tasks, TaPA aligns LLMs with visual-language perception for physically feasible plans, FGTP combines object ontology with LLMs for fine-grained planning, and systems like GPTArm and GPT-4V are used for real-time visual perception, situational reasoning, and robot teaching from human demonstrations.
- **Active Task Cognition:** This area focuses on robots autonomously deducing task-related information from visual input. Research includes graph-based reasoning systems (e.g., multimodal prior guided segmentation, emotional graphs, 3D scene graphs), VQA frameworks (e.g., RobotVQA for semantic map construction, knowledge-based VQA, SQA with Chain-of-Thought for spatial reasoning, SpatialRGPT), and multimodal Chain-of-Thought reasoning. A key limitation identified is that most approaches remain reactive, requiring explicit queries rather than proactive information identification.

**C. Audio-based LLM Task Planning (Section 6)**
- **Basic Model Architecture:** Foundational models like CLAP (Contrastive Language-Audio Pretraining) use dual-encoder structures to align audio and text, enabling zero-shot audio classification. AudioCLIP and Wav2CLIP further extend this by integrating audio with text-image contrastive models (CLIP) into joint embedding spaces.
- **Audio Comprehension and Reasoning:** Advanced architectures such as Audio Flamingo demonstrate few-shot learning and conversational abilities. LTU (Listen, Think, and Understand) is an audio foundation model showing emergent audio reasoning. GAMA integrates audio comprehension with sophisticated reasoning for semantic analysis of ambient sounds.
- **Voice Interaction:** Methods like "Beyond Text" incorporate vocal cues to improve LLM decision-making in social navigation. SALM (Speech Augmented Language Model) uses adapters for zero-shot adaptation to voice input, and AudioPaLM unifies text and speech models (PaLM-2 and AudioLM) for bidirectional speech-text generation.
- **Performance Assessment and Adaptability:** Benchmarks like MMAU assess expert-level audio understanding. SICL (Speech-based In-context Learning) improves adaptation to dialects and speakers. Pengi formulates all audio tasks as text generation tasks, offering a unified, fine-tuning-free solution.
- **Challenges:** Robustness in complex noise environments and effective error correction remain areas for improvement (e.g., ClozeGER framework for speech recognition errors).

**D. Multimodal-based LLM Task Planning (Section 7)**
- **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** MLLMs integrate multiple modalities to overcome the sensory limitations of single-modal systems. Key architectures include Flamingo (query-based cross-attention), BLIP-2 (Q-Former for image features), MM-REACT (ChatGPT with visual models for multimodal reasoning and action), LLaMA-Adapter (efficient fine-tuning for multimodal applications), MiniGPT-4, LLaVA/LLaVA-1.5 (visual instruction tuning), Kosmos-2 (grounding text to visual scenes with bounding boxes), QWen2-VL (dynamic resolution processing), mPLUG-Owl (modular learning), Otter (contextual instruction execution), and CogCoM ("Chain of Manipulations" for step-by-step problem solving). Fusion techniques commonly involve attention mechanisms (cross-attention, self-attention), feature alignment (contrastive learning, shared query vectors), gating mechanisms, and graph neural networks.
- **Technological Evolution and Practical Applications:**
 - **Evaluation and Optimization:** OmniBench provides a benchmark for multimodal inputs. mPnP-LLM combines single-modal encoders with dynamically configurable LLM modules for resource efficiency. LLM-Bind unifies multimodal inputs into task-specific token sequences. RespLLM integrates audio and text for health prediction.
 - **Robot System Integration:** Systems are being developed that integrate hardware, scene modeling, navigation, grasping, and VLM-based task planning (e.g., Ni et al.). Multimodal language model agents combine routers, chatbots, and task planners for enhanced decision-making (e.g., Chung et al.). VIMA uses multimodal prompt formulas for diverse robot manipulation tasks.
 - **Task Planning and Environmental Interaction:** Inner Monologue integrates multimodal environmental feedback for adaptive planning. MLLMs translate user dialogues and visual information into executable plans (e.g., Liu et al.). GPT-4V, PaLM-E, AVBLIP, and NMM-HRI (voice commands, posture, visual info) are used for embodied mission planning and natural human-robot interaction. Ontology knowledge is integrated for deeper task reasoning (e.g., Tian et al.).
 - **Closed-loop Integration and Embodied Intelligence:** Continuous perception-action cycles, such as those in Inner Monologue with RGB-D sensors, enable dynamic plan updates. Natural language corrections (e.g., AudioPaLM) and RLHF provide adaptation to real-world uncertainties. Breakthroughs in embodied intelligence like Google's Gemini Robotics and NVIDIA's GR00T represent a paradigm shift by directly linking real-time sensor inputs to executable motor commands, bypassing intermediate symbolic planning and integrating physical feasibility checks.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This review provides a detailed and systematic analysis of the emerging field of Large Language Model (LLM)-based task planning for service robots, making several significant contributions and highlighting substantial potential impacts.

**Key Contributions:**
1. **Systematization of a Rapidly Evolving Field:** The paper offers a structured overview of LLM development, core techniques, and their application to robotics. This systematic approach is valuable for researchers and practitioners in navigating the complexities of this interdisciplinary domain.
2. **Novel Modality-Centric Taxonomy:** By proposing the first modality-centric taxonomy for LLM-based task planning in domestic service robotics, the review provides a unique framework for understanding and classifying current research. This classification by input modality (text, vision, audio, multimodal) helps in identifying specific challenges and synergies across different information channels, which is crucial for targeted technological development.
3. **Identification of Critical Gaps and Challenges:** The review meticulously outlines current limitations, including insufficiencies in real-time performance and safety (due to computational demands and potential for hallucinations), challenges in effective multimodal fusion, the lack of unified model evaluation benchmarks, insufficient active task cognition, and the underdeveloped state of tactile language models.
4. **Guidance for Future Research:** Based on the identified challenges, the paper proposes actionable research pathways. These include developing more powerful and real-time LLMs, establishing comprehensive evaluation benchmarks, achieving breakthroughs in multimodal fusion strategies, fostering multimodal-based active task cognition, enhancing human-robot interaction for improved planning, and advancing tactile intelligence in robotic systems. This forward-looking perspective serves as a roadmap for future investigations.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerating Service Robot Deployment:** By addressing the limitations of traditional planning and leveraging the cognitive capabilities of LLMs, this work contributes to the development of service robots that are more robust, adaptable, and efficient. This advancement can facilitate their broader deployment in complex, unstructured domestic environments, enhancing quality of life through autonomous assistance in various household tasks.
- **Advancing General-Purpose Robot Intelligence:** The integration of LLMs as the "cognitive core" moves robots beyond pre-programmed, single-task execution towards general-purpose intelligence. This enables robots to understand nuanced user commands, adapt to dynamic changes, and reason with common-sense knowledge, paving the way for more sophisticated human-robot collaboration.
- **Fostering Interdisciplinary Research:** The review highlights the necessity of combining expertise from artificial intelligence, natural language processing, computer vision, audio processing, and robotics. It encourages interdisciplinary collaboration to tackle the multifaceted challenges of multimodal data fusion and embodied intelligence.
- **Informing Model Development and Evaluation:** The call for standardized evaluation benchmarks will enable more accurate and comparable assessments of LLM performance in robotic contexts. This, in turn, can drive the development of more effective model architectures and optimization strategies tailored to the unique requirements of robotic task planning.
- **Enhancing Human-Robot Interaction:** Emphasizing human-robot interaction as a future direction underscores the importance of intuitive communication interfaces. Improved speech recognition, natural language processing, and affective computing can lead to more natural and efficient interactions, allowing robots to better infer user intentions and adapt task plans accordingly.

In summary, this review serves as a valuable reference that synthesizes current knowledge, articulates the specific challenges facing LLM-based task planning for domestic service robots, and provides strategic directions for future research, ultimately promoting the field toward the realization of truly intelligent and adaptable service robots.
