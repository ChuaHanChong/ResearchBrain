# Bi-Touch: Bimanual Tactile Manipulation with Sim-to-Real Deep Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Bi-Touch is a bimanual tactile manipulation system that integrates affordable dual-arm robotics with optical tactile sensors and deep reinforcement learning to perform complex object manipulation tasks. The system successfully performs bi-pushing, bi-reorienting, and sequential bi-gathering tasks, exhibiting robust generalization to unseen objects and recovery from perturbations in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Achieving human-like dexterity in robotic manipulation, particularly with bimanual systems, remains a significant challenge due to complexity and cost.
- Existing robotic manipulation systems often rely solely on vision, which is insufficient for tasks requiring precise force control, contact information, or handling occluded objects.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for complex bimanual tactile policies is difficult, hindering the transfer of learned skills from simulated environments to physical robots.

## Method

- Developed Bi-Touch, an affordable bimanual robotic platform comprising two Dobot MG400 arms each equipped with a TacTip optical tactile sensor.
- Extended the Tactile Gym 2.0 simulation environment to support bimanual tactile manipulation and trained control policies using deep reinforcement learning (PPO).
- Implemented a sim-to-real transfer framework using domain randomization, GAN-based image translation for tactile feedback, and a novel Goal-Update Mechanism (GUM) for sequential tasks.

## Results

- The Bi-Touch system successfully performed three complex bimanual tasks—Bi-Pushing, Bi-Reorienting, and Bi-Gathering—with diverse real-world objects.
- Policies trained in simulation generalized robustly to unseen objects of varying shapes, sizes, and material properties without additional real-world training.
- The system demonstrated the ability to recover from external perturbations during task execution, highlighting the effectiveness of tactile feedback for robust and adaptive manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating tactile sensing with bimanual manipulation is crucial for achieving human-like dexterity and addressing limitations of vision-only systems in complex tasks.
> - A Goal-Update Mechanism (GUM) within reinforcement learning significantly improves training efficiency and robustness for sequential manipulation tasks.
> - Effective sim-to-real transfer, facilitated by domain randomization and GAN-based tactile image translation, enables policies learned in simulation to generalize robustly to real-world bimanual and tactile challenges, including unseen objects and perturbations.
