# Bi-Touch: Bimanual Tactile Manipulation with Sim-to-Real Deep Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Bi-Touch is a bimanual tactile manipulation system that integrates affordable dual-arm robotics with optical tactile sensors and deep reinforcement learning to perform complex object manipulation tasks. The system successfully performs bi-pushing, bi-reorienting, and sequential bi-gathering tasks, exhibiting robust generalization to unseen objects and recovery from perturbations in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Achieving human-like dexterity in robotic manipulation, particularly with bimanual systems, remains a significant challenge due to complexity and cost.
- Existing robotic manipulation systems often rely solely on vision, which is insufficient for tasks requiring precise force control, contact information, or handling occluded objects.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for complex bimanual tactile policies is difficult, hindering the transfer of learned skills from simulated environments to physical robots.

## Method

- Developed Bi-Touch, an affordable bimanual robotic platform comprising two Dobot MG400 arms each equipped with a TacTip optical tactile sensor.
- Extended the Tactile Gym 2.0 simulation environment to support bimanual tactile manipulation and trained control policies using deep reinforcement learning (PPO).
- Implemented a sim-to-real transfer framework using domain randomization, GAN-based image translation for tactile feedback, and a novel Goal-Update Mechanism (GUM) for sequential tasks.

## Results

- The Bi-Touch system successfully performed three complex bimanual tasks—Bi-Pushing, Bi-Reorienting, and Bi-Gathering—with diverse real-world objects.
- Policies trained in simulation generalized robustly to unseen objects of varying shapes, sizes, and material properties without additional real-world training.
- The system demonstrated the ability to recover from external perturbations during task execution, highlighting the effectiveness of tactile feedback for robust and adaptive manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating tactile sensing with bimanual manipulation is crucial for achieving human-like dexterity and addressing limitations of vision-only systems in complex tasks.
> - A Goal-Update Mechanism (GUM) within reinforcement learning significantly improves training efficiency and robustness for sequential manipulation tasks.
> - Effective sim-to-real transfer, facilitated by domain randomization and GAN-based tactile image translation, enables policies learned in simulation to generalize robustly to real-world bimanual and tactile challenges, including unseen objects and perturbations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Robots that can manipulate objects with human-like dexterity remain a significant challenge in robotics research. While single-arm systems have been widely studied, bimanual manipulation—using two arms simultaneously—offers greater versatility and capabilities for handling complex tasks. This paper introduces "Bi-Touch," a novel bimanual tactile manipulation system that combines affordable dual-arm robotics with sophisticated tactile sensing and deep reinforcement learning.

Most robotic manipulation systems rely heavily on vision, which becomes problematic when objects are occluded or when precise force control is required. Tactile sensing provides complementary information about contact forces, surface properties, and object positions that vision alone cannot capture. By integrating tactile sensing with bimanual manipulation, Bi-Touch addresses a critical gap in robotics research and moves closer to human-level dexterity.

The research tackles several challenges: the high cost and complexity of dual-arm systems, the lack of realistic tactile sensing in bimanual simulators, and the difficulties of transferring learned policies from simulation to real-world hardware. The Bi-Touch system offers an affordable, accessible platform for advancing research in this important domain.

### 2. The Bi-Touch System

The Bi-Touch hardware consists of two key components:

1. **Robotic Arms**: Two Dobot MG400 collaborative robotic arms, each with four degrees of freedom and a payload capacity of 750g. These arms offer a good balance between capability and affordability.

2. **Tactile Sensors**: Each arm is equipped with a TacTip optical tactile sensor. TacTips use a camera to track internal pins that deform when the sensor contacts objects, providing high-resolution tactile feedback.

The system combines these components to enable sophisticated manipulation tasks that require coordination between the two arms and responsive tactile feedback. Unlike many expensive industrial dual-arm systems, Bi-Touch provides an accessible platform for research and development at a lower cost point.

The key design goals for Bi-Touch include:
- Creating an affordable and accessible bimanual tactile system
- Developing a sim-to-real framework for learning complex manipulation skills
- Demonstrating generalization to unseen objects and robustness to real-world conditions

### 3. Simulation Environment and Tasks

To train the system efficiently, the researchers extended the Tactile Gym 2.0 simulation environment to support bimanual manipulation with tactile feedback. Three benchmark tasks were designed to evaluate the system's capabilities:

1. **Bi-Pushing**: Both arms cooperate to push an object to a target position. This tests basic coordination and force control.

2. **Bi-Reorienting**: The arms grasp an object and rotate it to a desired orientation. This tests more complex coordination and precision.

3. **Bi-Gathering**: The arms must collect multiple objects placed in a sequence, requiring planning and sequential manipulation.

Each task presents different challenges and requires specific tactile skills. The simulation accurately models the physical properties of the real system, including the tactile sensor responses, to facilitate effective sim-to-real transfer.

For evaluation, the researchers assembled a diverse set of test objects including everyday items like boxes, tubes, balls, and food containers. These varied in shape, size, and material properties to test the system's generalization capabilities.

### 4. Deep Reinforcement Learning Approach

The researchers employed deep reinforcement learning (DRL) to train control policies for the bimanual manipulation tasks. Specifically, they used Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a state-of-the-art DRL algorithm known for its stability and sample efficiency.

The policy network takes as input:
- Tactile images from both sensors
- Proprioceptive information (arm positions)
- Task-specific information (goal positions/orientations)

It outputs continuous control signals for both arms simultaneously, enabling coordinated movement.

For each task, the researchers designed specific reward functions:

1. **Bi-Pushing**: Rewards are based on reducing the distance between the object and the target position.

2. **Bi-Reorienting**: Rewards combine position accuracy and orientation alignment with the target.

3. **Bi-Gathering**: A novel Goal-Update Mechanism (GUM) was introduced to handle the sequential nature of this task. The GUM dynamically updates subgoals as the task progresses, allowing for more effective learning.

### 5. Sim-to-Real Transfer

A key challenge in robotics is transferring policies trained in simulation to real-world hardware—the "sim-to-real gap." The researchers addressed this challenge through several techniques:

1. **Domain Randomization**: During training, simulation parameters like friction, object mass, and initial positions were randomly varied to make the policy robust to real-world variations.

2. **GAN-Based Image Translation**: A Pix2Pix Generative Adversarial Network was trained to translate real tactile sensor images into the format expected by the simulation-trained policy. This bridged the visual gap between simulation and reality.

3. **Sensor Calibration**: The tactile sensors were carefully calibrated to ensure their responses matched the simulation model.

The real-world setup mirrored the simulation environment, with the dual arms positioned to create a workspace similar to the simulated one. Both arms were connected to a control computer running the policy network, with tactile images processed through the GAN before being fed to the network.

### 6. Experimental Results

The Bi-Touch system was evaluated on all three tasks using both the training objects and previously unseen test objects. The system demonstrated impressive performance:

1. **Bi-Pushing**: The system successfully pushed various objects to target positions, showing robust performance even with unseen objects of different shapes.

2. **Bi-Reorienting**: The system successfully reoriented objects to the target orientation, generalizing well to objects with different geometries and weight distributions.

3. **Bi-Gathering**: The system demonstrated sequential gathering of multiple objects, adjusting to different object properties and positions.

To test robustness, the researchers introduced deliberate perturbations during task execution, such as pushing objects off their expected trajectory. The Bi-Touch system demonstrated an ability to recover from these perturbations and complete the tasks successfully, highlighting the value of the tactile feedback.

These results validate the sim-to-real approach and demonstrate the system's ability to generalize to new objects and conditions. The tactile feedback proved essential for handling object variations and recovering from perturbations.

### 7. Significance and Contributions

The Bi-Touch system represents several important contributions to robotics research:

1. **Accessible Bimanual Tactile Platform**: By creating an affordable dual-arm system with high-quality tactile sensing, the researchers have made bimanual tactile manipulation research more accessible.

2. **Sim-to-Real Framework**: The successful transfer of policies from simulation to real hardware demonstrates an effective approach for training complex manipulation skills efficiently.

3. **Novel Goal-Update Mechanism**: The GUM introduced for sequential tasks represents a valuable technique for training policies on multi-step manipulation tasks.

4. **Generalization and Robustness**: The system's ability to handle unseen objects and recover from perturbations highlights the importance of tactile feedback for adaptive manipulation.

These contributions address significant challenges in robotics research and open new possibilities for developing more dexterous and versatile robotic systems. The combination of bimanual coordination and tactile feedback enables manipulation capabilities that would be difficult or impossible with single-arm or vision-only systems.

### 8. Conclusion

The Bi-Touch system demonstrates how combining bimanual manipulation with tactile sensing and deep reinforcement learning can produce sophisticated manipulation capabilities. By creating an accessible platform and effective sim-to-real training methodology, this research makes significant progress toward human-level robot dexterity.

The system's ability to generalize to unseen objects and recover from perturbations highlights the value of tactile feedback for robust manipulation. The successful implementation of complex tasks like reorienting and sequential gathering demonstrates the potential of bimanual manipulation for practical applications.

This research opens several promising directions for future work, including extending to more complex manipulation tasks, incorporating additional sensing modalities, and applying the techniques to other robotic platforms. The Bi-Touch system provides a valuable foundation for advancing research in tactile-based robot dexterity, bringing us closer to robots that can manipulate objects with human-like skill and adaptability.
