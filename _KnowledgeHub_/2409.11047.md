# TacDiffusion: Force-domain Diffusion Policy for Precise Tactile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TacDiffusion is a force-domain diffusion policy for high-precision robotic insertion. A DDPM noise estimator maps tactile observations (external/internal wrench, end-effector speed) to a 6D wrench, a dynamic-system filter interpolates its low-rate output up to a 1000 Hz impedance controller with feed-forward force. Trained on one cuboid task, it reaches 95.7% zero-shot success on four novel sub-millimeter peg-in-hole tasks.

## Problem

- Prior force-domain assembly controllers target one specific tight-clearance task, with little study of transferability or adaptability to novel parts.
- Transformer and diffusion manipulation policies generalize well but act in motion space, leaving force control unintegrated for precise tactile insertion.
- Learning-based precise manipulation still underperforms, with transformer multi-task policies near 50% success on millimeter-level insertion, unusable in production.

## Method

- **TacDiffusion** is a ==DDPM== whose ==residual-MLP noise estimator== conditions on current and previous 18D tactile observations plus diffusion step, denoising Gaussian noise into a 6D feed-forward wrench action.
- Imitation of a ==behavior-tree== expert insertion skill, so the policy inherits primitive-switching self-adaptability (wiggle, align, push) without an explicit state estimator subtree.
- A second-order ==dynamic-system filter== interpolating the 51-504 Hz diffusion output into a smooth 1000 Hz feed-forward wrench for ==impedance control== with feed-forward force.

## Results

- The 512-neuron model DF3 reached **98%** on the trained cuboid and **95.7%** average zero-shot success across key, two cylinders and octagonal prism, beating the **83.3%** expert baseline.
- Model size trades accuracy for rate: loss fell **0.2751** to **0.0288** as inference dropped **503.8 Hz** to **51.2 Hz**, so DF3 at **141.8 Hz** gave **129.5%** efficiency gain over baseline.
- Disabling the dynamic-system filter lowered success in **16 of 20** model-object cases, with an overall **9.15%** success-rate improvement attributable to the filter.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models can generate force-domain actions, not just motion targets, letting generative multi-modal policies cover contact-rich tasks that position control cannot reach.
> - Bigger generative policies are not better in closed-loop control, since inference-rate loss against a real-time controller can outweigh reduced training loss.
> - Bridging a slow policy to a fast controller needs explicit smoothing, a simple second-order dynamic-system interpolator recovers most of the frequency-misalignment penalty.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and persistent challenge in robotics: achieving highly precise, transferable, and adaptive assembly skills, particularly for tight-clearance insertion tasks. This problem is fundamental to both modern manufacturing and service robotics, where the demand for flexible automation in dynamic, unstructured environments is growing.

**Current Landscape and Challenges:**
Traditionally, high-precision assembly has relied on motion-based control methods, often augmented by indirect force control like impedance control. However, these methods often struggle with robot accuracy limitations and lack the adaptability needed for real-world scenarios where object poses are not perfectly known, or part holders are absent. Human workers excel at such tasks by leveraging fine-grained tactile feedback and adapting their force and motion based on contact information.

Existing approaches to enhance transferability in robotic assembly include:
- **Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL):** Trains policies across multiple objects to generalize.
- **Meta-learning:** Enables domain adaptation through fine-tuning on diverse tasks.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Leverages cost-effective data collection in simulation.
- **Transformer-based Policies (e.g., RVT-2, RT-1, RT-2):** Have shown promise for multi-task learning and generalization, often using visual inputs. However, their success rates for *high-precision* (millimeter-level) insertion tasks are often insufficient for industrial deployment (e.g., RVT-2's 50% success rate).
- **Force-domain Control Methods:** Explicitly design actions in the force domain (force, admittance, hybrid position/force, impedance with feed-forward force). While effective for precision, they typically focus on specific tasks and lack investigation into transferability and adaptability to novel scenarios.

**The Gap TacDiffusion Addresses:**
A significant gap exists in effectively integrating force control with powerful generative models like diffusion models for high-precision tactile manipulation. While diffusion models have demonstrated excellent generalization capabilities in other areas of robotics (e.g., planning, manipulation with visual inputs), their application to *force-domain actions* and *tactile-based control* remains underexplored. The paper explicitly states that it is the "first framework to employ diffusion models in generating force-domain actions for tactile-based robotic manipulation in tight-clearance insertion tasks."

By proposing TacDiffusion, the authors aim to bridge this gap by leveraging the strong generalization capabilities of diffusion models to generate appropriate 6D wrench commands, combined with tactile sensing, to achieve both task-level transferability and control-level self-adaptability. This positions TacDiffusion at the forefront of combining advanced generative AI with nuanced physical interaction for complex robotic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the TacDiffusion framework are multifaceted, addressing critical limitations in current robotic assembly systems:

1. **Develop a Novel Framework for High-Precision Tactile Manipulation:** The overarching goal is to create a robust and effective system for high-precision robotic insertion tasks that can match human dexterity in handling tight-clearance components. This is motivated by the growing demand for flexible manufacturing and service robotics in dynamic environments where precise positioning is often not guaranteed.
2. **Utilize Diffusion Models for Force-Domain Action Generation:** The central hypothesis is that diffusion models, known for their strong generalization capabilities, can be adapted to generate 6D wrench commands (force-domain actions) for tactile manipulation. This aims to overcome the limitations of traditional force control methods which often lack transferability, and to extend the benefits of generative models beyond typical motion or visual-based control.
3. **Achieve High Zero-Shot Transferability:** A key objective is to enable robots to learn from demonstrations on a single task and then generalize this skill *zero-shot* to a variety of novel, high-precision insertion tasks (sub-millimeter level). This is crucial for reducing the prohibitive costs and time associated with retraining or fine-tuning robots for every new object or task variant.
4. **Inherit and Enhance Self-Adaptability:** The framework is motivated by the need for robots to respond dynamically to environmental changes and contact states. By learning from an expert policy based on a behavior tree that incorporates tactile-based primitive-switching logic, TacDiffusion aims to inherit and even outperform this self-adaptability in terms of execution time and robustness.
5. **Address Real-time Control Challenges:** A practical motivation is to tackle the frequency misalignment between the diffusion model's inference speed (typically lower) and the robot's real-time control loop (typically higher, e.g., 1000 Hz). The goal is to design a dynamic system-based filter to smooth and synchronize the model's output with the controller, thereby improving task success rates.
6. **Provide Practical Guidelines for Model Selection:** Recognizing the trade-off between model complexity (accuracy) and inference speed, the authors aim to investigate how model size impacts this balance. This practical motivation seeks to offer insights for optimal model selection, crucial for deploying learned policies on real-world robotic systems with computational constraints.

In essence, TacDiffusion is motivated by the vision of enabling robots to autonomously perform a wide range of contact-rich manipulation tasks with human-like precision, adaptability, and generalizability, by intelligently combining advanced generative AI with force-tactile sensing and robust control strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TacDiffusion proposes a novel framework that integrates a diffusion-based policy with real-time impedance control and a custom filter to achieve precise tactile manipulation. The methodology is structured into two primary functional modules: the diffusion policy-based action generation module and the impedance control with feed-forward-based execution module.

**A. Framework Overview:**
The core idea is to replace a complex sub-tree in a behavior tree (BT)-based insertion skill (derived from previous work) with a diffusion policy. This simplifies the BT into a sequence of skill primitives, where the diffusion model handles the underlying control and primitive switching logic during insertion.

1. **Observation Processing:** The system continuously captures observations `o` from the robot-environment interaction. These observations are multi-modal, including 18-dimensional data: external wrench, internal wrench, and end-effector (EE) speed.
2. **Diffusion Policy for Action Generation:** The diffusion model takes the current (`o_curr`) and previous (`o_prev`) observations as input to predict a 6D wrench, `F_df`, which serves as the force-domain action (`a`).
3. **Dynamic System-based Filter:** A critical component is the dynamic system-based filter. This filter addresses the frequency mismatch between the diffusion model's output rate (e.g., 50-500 Hz) and the robot's high-frequency real-time control loop (e.g., 1000 Hz). It interpolates and smooths `F_df` into a high-frequency `F_ff` (filtered feed-forward force).
4. **Impedance Control with Feed-forward Force:** The filtered `F_ff` is then fed into an impedance controller. This controller simultaneously regulates the robot's motion and force behavior based on the desired goal (insertion hole's pose `x_d`) and the commanded feed-forward force.

**B. Diffusion Model (Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model - DDPM):**
The chosen generative model is a DDPM, which learns to generate data by reversing a noise injection process.

1. **Diffusion Process (Forward):** This process systematically corrupts a "clean" initial action (`a_0`) by progressively adding Gaussian noise over a series of `T` steps. This transforms the action into a noisy state (`a_T`) that approaches a Gaussian distribution. The process is governed by variance schedule parameters (`α_τ`, `β_τ`).
2. **Denoising Process (Reverse):** This is the generative part. A neural network, termed the "noise estimator" (`hat_ϵ(·)`), is trained to predict the noise added at each diffusion step. The network architecture (Fig. 2) consists of linear layers, normalization, and activation functions, with a residual connection. Its input includes the current and previous observations (`o`), the diffused action (`a_τ`), and the diffusion step (`τ`) as positional information. The training objective is to minimize the squared difference between the predicted noise and the actual noise (`L_DDPM`).
3. **Action Reconstruction:** During inference, starting from a random Gaussian sample (`a_T`), the trained noise estimator iteratively removes noise from `a_τ` over `T` steps, reconstructing the clean action `a_0`, which is the predicted 6D wrench `F_df`.

**C. Impedance Control with Feed-forward Force:**
The paper utilizes a standard impedance control law with feed-forward force. The motor torque (`τ_m`) for the n-DOF robot is calculated based on the Jacobian, the feed-forward wrench (`F_ff`), stiffness (`K(t)`) and damping (`D`) matrices, position and velocity errors (`e`, `dot_e`), and robot dynamics terms (mass matrix `M(q)`, Coriolis matrix `C(q, dot_q)`, gravity `g(q)`). The internal wrench applied by the robot is also calculated from the motor torque and robot dynamics.

**D. Dynamic System-based Filter:**
To bridge the frequency gap, a second-order dynamic system is employed:
`¨F_ff = α(β(F_df - F_ff) - ˙F_ff)`
where `F_df` is the raw, lower-frequency output from the diffusion model, and `F_ff` is the smoothed, interpolated, high-frequency (1000 Hz) feed-forward force command sent to the impedance controller. `α` and `β` are constant scaling parameters tuned for effectiveness. This filter effectively smooths out the discrete outputs of the diffusion model, providing a continuous and responsive force command to the robot.

**Data Collection and Training:**
- A dataset of 1500 expert demonstrations was collected on a Franka Emika Panda robot performing a cuboid insertion task, using the group's previous behavior tree-based method.
- Data was recorded at 1000 Hz, pairing 18-dimensional observations with 6-dimensional actions (feed-forward force).
- Four diffusion models (DF1-DF4) with varying neuron numbers (N=128, 256, 512, 1024) in the noise estimator were trained to evaluate the trade-off between inference capability and speed.
- Models were exported to ONNX format for optimized inference speed.

This comprehensive methodology allows TacDiffusion to generate precise force-domain actions, adapt to real-world control frequencies, and inherit complex, adaptable behaviors from expert demonstrations, setting the stage for robust zero-shot transfer in high-precision tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of TacDiffusion yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the framework's effectiveness, transferability, and practical advantages.

**A. Performance on High-Precision Assembly Tasks:**
- **Zero-Shot Transferability:** The most notable result is the excellent zero-shot transfer capability. TacDiffusion, particularly model DF3, achieved an average success rate of **95.7%** across four *novel* high-precision, sub-millimeter-level peg-in-hole tasks (Key, Cyl-S, Cyl-L, Prism). This significantly outperforms the baseline policy, which showed an average success rate of 83.3%.
- **Efficiency:** The "efficiency" metric (task completion rate to mean time per task) was used for comprehensive evaluation. Radar plots demonstrated that all diffusion models achieved over a 10% improvement in efficiency on novel tasks compared to the baseline. Model DF3 specifically showed a **129.5% improvement** in overall performance compared to the baseline, excelling on both demonstrated and novel tasks.
- **Inherited Self-Adaptability:** The diffusion model successfully learned and inherited the tactile-based self-adaptability from the expert demonstrations. This was observed through its ability to perform "wiggle" motions for alignment and to resolve stuck states, switching behaviors based on real-time tactile sensing, similar to the behavior tree logic it was trained on. TacDiffusion also demonstrated improved execution time and robustness compared to the expert policy on novel tasks.

**B. Trade-off between Model Accuracy and Inference Speed:**
- **Model Scaling:** Four diffusion models (DF1-DF4) were trained with increasing neuron numbers (N=128 to 1024). As model size increased, the training and validation loss decreased (implying higher accuracy) but the inference frequency dropped significantly (from 503.8 Hz for DF1 to 51.2 Hz for DF4).
- **Optimal Model Selection:** Model **DF3 (N=512)** was identified as the optimal choice, achieving the best balance. While DF4 had the lowest loss (highest theoretical accuracy), its extremely low inference frequency (51.2 Hz) severely hampered real-time performance, leading to an overall performance drop. DF3, with an acceptable inference frequency of 141.8 Hz, maintained high accuracy and significantly outperformed the baseline and other diffusion models across tasks. This provides a crucial practical guideline for deploying diffusion models in real-time robotic systems.

**C. Effectiveness of the Dynamic System-based Filter:**
- **Mitigating Frequency Misalignment:** The dynamic system-based filter, designed to smooth and interpolate the diffusion model's low-frequency outputs for the high-frequency control loop, was highly effective.
- **Improved Success Rate:** Experiments showed that enabling the filter resulted in higher success rates in 16 out of 20 tested scenarios (combinations of models and objects). Overall, the filter led to a significant **9.15% increase in task success rates**.
- **Smoothed Force Commands:** Visualizations (Fig. 10) clearly illustrated the filter's impact. The raw diffusion outputs (`F_df`) showed higher variability and fluctuations, while the filtered feed-forward force commands (`F_ff`) were smoother and continuous, confirming that the filter successfully mitigated the frequency misalignment and provided more stable inputs to the impedance controller.

In summary, TacDiffusion demonstrated superior zero-shot transferability and efficiency for high-precision tactile manipulation. The research also provided critical insights into the practical considerations of deploying diffusion models, particularly the trade-off between model size, accuracy, and real-time inference speed, and the necessity of a dedicated filtering mechanism to align model outputs with high-frequency robot control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TacDiffusion framework represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly for high-precision assembly tasks requiring tactile feedback. Its contributions have substantial significance and potential impact across several domains:

**A. Scientific Significance:**
1. **Pioneering Force-Domain Diffusion Policies:** TacDiffusion is groundbreaking as the first framework to successfully employ diffusion models for generating 6D wrench (force-domain actions) in tactile-based robotic manipulation. This extends the applicability of powerful generative models beyond traditional vision-based or motion-domain control, opening new avenues for research into multi-modal robot learning.
2. **Demonstrating High Zero-Shot Transferability:** Achieving a 95.7% zero-shot transfer success rate on novel sub-millimeter insertion tasks from single-task demonstrations is a remarkable feat. This showcases the exceptional generalization capabilities of diffusion models in a physically interactive context and significantly pushes the boundaries of what's possible with imitation learning in robotics.
3. **Integrating Self-Adaptability with Generative Models:** By learning from a behavior tree-based expert policy, TacDiffusion successfully inherits and even enhances complex tactile-based primitive-switching logic. This demonstrates a powerful approach to instill adaptive intelligence in robots through learning, crucial for handling uncertainties in real-world environments.
4. **Addressing Real-time Deployment Challenges:** The introduction and validation of a dynamic system-based filter to bridge the frequency misalignment between a learning policy and a real-time controller is a crucial practical contribution. This finding provides a generalizable solution for deploying complex, computationally intensive models in latency-sensitive robotic systems, a common hurdle in robot learning.
5. **Practical Guidelines for Model Optimization:** The systematic investigation into the trade-off between model accuracy and inference speed offers valuable insights for researchers and practitioners. Identifying an "optimal" model size (DF3) that balances these factors is essential for effective real-world deployment of AI-driven robot control.

**B. Potential Impact:**
1. **Advancing Robotic Assembly and Manufacturing:** The high precision and zero-shot transferability of TacDiffusion could revolutionize flexible manufacturing. Robots equipped with such capabilities could efficiently handle a wider variety of parts and assembly tasks without extensive re-programming or retraining, leading to increased automation, reduced setup times, and greater adaptability in production lines.
2. **Enabling New Service Robotics Applications:** Beyond manufacturing, this framework could enable more dexterous service robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in unstructured human environments, from delicate household chores to advanced medical procedures, where tactile feedback and precise force control are paramount.
3. **Facilitating Research in Human-Robot Collaboration:** The ability of robots to perform complex tactile tasks with high precision and adaptability could lead to more seamless and intuitive human-robot collaboration, as robots become more capable of understanding and responding to physical interaction forces.
4. **Informing Future Robot Learning Architectures:** The success of TacDiffusion suggests that integrating generative models with dedicated mechanisms for handling real-time constraints and multi-modal sensory inputs (like tactile and force) is a promising direction for future robot learning research. This could inspire new architectural designs that combine the strengths of different AI paradigms.
5. **Reducing Data Requirements:** The framework's ability to achieve high performance with learning from a single task demonstration, rather than extensive datasets for every new object, significantly reduces the data collection burden, making robot learning more accessible and scalable.

In conclusion, TacDiffusion not only pushes the boundaries of high-precision robotic manipulation by integrating cutting-edge diffusion models with tactile sensing and robust control but also provides practical solutions for their real-world deployment. Its impact is poised to be significant for the future of flexible automation and intelligent robotics.
