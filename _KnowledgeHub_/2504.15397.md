# MirrorVerse: Pushing Diffusion Models to Realistically Reflect the World

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at IISc Bangalore developed MirrorVerse, a framework featuring the SynMirrorV2 dataset and MirrorFusion 2.0 model, to enable diffusion models to generate photorealistic and geometrically accurate mirror reflections. This approach significantly improved reflection quality, outperforming prior methods and commercial tools across synthetic and real-world scenes.

## Problem

- Current generative AI models struggle to produce photorealistic and geometrically accurate mirror reflections, often generating distorted or inconsistent results.
- A lack of diverse, high-quality training data, especially for complex real-world mirror scenarios, prevents models from implicitly learning the physics of reflections.
- Existing synthetic datasets for reflections lack sufficient diversity in object positioning, rotation, and multi-object interactions, limiting models' generalization capabilities.

## Method

- Introduced **SynMirrorV2**, a large-scale synthetic dataset (207,610 images) incorporating diverse object positioning, rotations, grounding, and multi-object scene generation with semantic pairing and collision avoidance.
- Developed **MirrorFusion** 2.0, a dual-branch ==U-Net== architecture (based on **BrushNet**) that reformulates reflection generation as an inpainting task, enhanced by depth map conditioning.
- Employed a three-stage training curriculum: fine-tuning on single-object **SynMirrorV2**, then multi-object **SynMirrorV2**, and finally on real-world data (MSD dataset) to bridge the synthetic-to-real domain gap.

## Results

- MirrorFusion 2.0 produced superior qualitative results on synthetic (MirrorBenchV2) and real-world (GSO, MSD) datasets, accurately capturing object orientation, structure, and multi-object interactions without distortions.
- Quantitatively, the model achieved improved metrics on MirrorBenchV2, including higher PSNR (18.79 vs 18.31 for single objects), SSIM (0.77 vs 0.76), and lower LPIPS (0.108 vs 0.122) compared to baselines.
- A user study showed that 84% of participants preferred MirrorFusion 2.0's generated reflections over baseline methods, indicating a high level of perceived realism and plausibility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diverse synthetic data, specifically randomized object poses and multi-object configurations with collision avoidance, is crucial for teaching diffusion models to generalize complex physical phenomena like reflections.
> - A progressive, staged training curriculum—moving from simple synthetic scenes to complex multi-object interactions and then real-world data—effectively aids in learning intricate spatial and physical relationships.
> - Integrating depth map conditioning into a dual-branch diffusion architecture proves effective for significantly improving the geometric accuracy and photorealism of generated reflections compared to single-branch designs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI, particularly with the advent of diffusion models, has seen unprecedented advancements in image generation, editing, and synthesis. Models like Stable Diffusion and FLUX can create highly realistic and diverse images from text prompts, perform inpainting, and manipulate images with various conditional inputs such as edge maps, depth maps, or segmentation masks. This paper situates itself within this rapidly evolving landscape by addressing a crucial, yet often overlooked, limitation of these powerful models: their struggle with accurately replicating complex physical phenomena, specifically **photorealistic mirror reflections**.

While diffusion models excel at synthesizing novel content, they frequently fail to adhere to the fundamental laws of physics governing light, geometry, and material properties. Previous research has highlighted these shortcomings in areas like shadows, specular reflections, and perspective cues. Mirror reflections pose a unique challenge because they require a nuanced understanding of not only the object's appearance but also its precise spatial relationship to the mirror, its orientation, and the surrounding environment, all within a geometrically consistent reflection.

Existing text-to-image (T2I) models (e.g., Stable Diffusion 3.5, FLUX) often produce geometrically inaccurate or inconsistent reflections when prompted. Similarly, general image inpainting methods (e.g., HD-Painter, Stable-Diffusion Inpainting) are inadequate for this specialized task. Prior work like MirrorFusion attempted to tackle this by introducing synthetic datasets for training; however, these approaches proved limited in generalizing across varied object poses, multiple objects, and real-world complexities.

"MirrorVerse" directly addresses this gap by presenting an enhanced approach to generating reflections, building upon and significantly improving existing methodologies. It advances the state-of-the-art by developing a more robust synthetic dataset and an optimized training curriculum, pushing diffusion models closer to a physically plausible understanding of the world. This work contributes to the broader push for **3D-aware and physically-consistent generative models**, an area gaining significant traction as AI models move beyond mere aesthetic generation to a more foundational understanding of visual reality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **enable diffusion-based generative models to produce photorealistic and geometrically accurate mirror reflections**. The authors explicitly pose the question: "Can current methods be fine-tuned to generate plausible mirror reflections?"

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations and limitations of existing generative models:

1. **Failure of State-of-the-Art Models:** Current powerful text-to-image models (e.g., SD3.5, Flux) and even specialized inpainting methods (e.g., HD-Painter) largely fail to generate plausible mirror reflections, often producing distorted, incorrect, or entirely absent reflections. This is a significant gap in their ability to render realistic scenes.
2. **Lack of Physically Accurate Priors:** The underlying reason for this failure is attributed to "poorly learned priors" by the models, a consequence of the quality and quantity of their training data. Real-world datasets containing diverse, high-quality images of mirrors and their reflections are scarce, making it difficult for models to implicitly learn complex physical laws.
3. **Limitations of Previous Synthetic Data:** While synthetic datasets (e.g., SynMirror) have been proposed as a solution to data scarcity, their lack of diversity in terms of object positioning, rotation, and multi-object scenarios limits their effectiveness, particularly in complex and real-world settings. These datasets struggle to capture the variability needed for robust generalization.
4. **Challenge of Generalization:** Achieving generalization to complex real-world scenes, which often involve multiple interacting objects, occlusions, and varied backgrounds, remains a significant challenge for models trained predominantly on synthetic data.

By addressing these issues, the authors aim to overcome the current inability of diffusion models to understand and replicate the intricate physics of reflections, thereby enhancing their overall realism and utility in various image editing and generation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MirrorVerse project introduces a multi-pronged approach involving a novel synthetic dataset, an adapted model architecture, and a specialized training curriculum.

#### 3.1. Dataset Generation Pipeline: SynMirrorV2

The core of the methodology lies in **SynMirrorV2**, a large-scale, diverse synthetic dataset (207,610 images) designed to address the shortcomings of previous datasets. The generation pipeline leverages 3D assets and realistic rendering techniques:

- **Object Sources:** Objects are sourced from large-scale 3D datasets like Objaverse (800K assets) and Amazon Berkeley Objects (ABO) (7,953 household objects). A curated list of 66,062 high-quality objects is used after filtering.
- **Scene Resources:** Realistic scenes are built using diverse floor textures (CC-textures), HDRI samples for backgrounds (PolyHaven), and various mirror types (full-wall, rectangular). Lighting is controlled with an area-light.
- **Key Augmentations for Single Objects:** To enhance diversity and generalization, SynMirrorV2 incorporates crucial augmentations:
 - **Random Object Positioning:** Objects are placed at random x-y positions within a pre-computed region that ensures both the object and its reflection are visible to the camera and mirror.
 - **Randomized Rotations:** Objects undergo random rotations around their y-axis to introduce pose variability.
 - **Object Grounding:** A simple grounding technique is applied to ensure objects rest realistically on the floor, preventing them from appearing to float.
- **Multi-Object Scenes:** To capture complex spatial relationships and occlusions prevalent in real-world scenes, a novel pipeline is introduced for generating multi-object scenarios:
 - **Semantic Pairing:** Objects are sampled from the ABO dataset, and their semantic categories are manually paired (e.g., chair with table) to ensure coherent scene compositions.
 - **Collision Avoidance:** After positioning a primary object, a paired object is sampled and arranged to prevent overlap, ensuring distinct spatial regions. This yields 3,140 scenes with diverse object configurations.
- **Rendering:** Scenes are rendered at 512x512 pixels using BlenderProc with "cycles rendering" (1024 samples) to accurately capture reflections. Outputs include RGB, depth, normal, and semantic labels.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture: MirrorFusion 2.0

The proposed **MirrorFusion 2.0** model formulates reflection generation as an inpainting task. It builds upon the MirrorFusion framework and employs a dual-branch network architecture similar to BrushNet:

- **Dual-Branch Design:** The model consists of a generation U-Net and a conditioning U-Net.
- **Depth Map Conditioning:** Unlike previous approaches, the conditioning U-Net of MirrorFusion 2.0 is enhanced to accept depth map information. It concatenates the noisy latent representation ($z_t$), a masked image ($z_m$), the inpainting mask ($x_m$), and the depth map ($x_d$) as input.
- **Zero-Convolutions:** Each layer of the generation U-Net is conditioned with the corresponding layer of the conditioning U-Net using zero-convolutions, which helps in guiding the generation process while preserving information.

#### 3.3. Three-Stage Training Curriculum

To improve generalization, especially to real-world scenes, a three-stage training curriculum is employed:

1. **Stage 1 (Single Objects):** The model's weights (both conditioning and generation branches) are initialized from a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.5 checkpoint. It is then fine-tuned on the single-object split of SynMirrorV2 for 40,000 iterations. The increased diversity in object position and rotation in SynMirrorV2 allows for longer, more effective training compared to prior datasets.
2. **Stage 2 (Multiple Objects):** The model is further fine-tuned for 10,000 iterations on the multiple-object train split of SynMirrorV2. This stage explicitly teaches the model to handle complex concepts like occlusions and spatial relationships between multiple objects in a scene.
3. **Stage 3 (Real-World Data):** To bridge the synthetic-to-real domain gap, the model undergoes a final fine-tuning phase for another 10,000 iterations on real-world data from the MSD dataset. For real images, depth maps are estimated using a monocular depth estimator (DepthPro). Text prompts are randomly dropped 20% of the time during training to encourage greater reliance on visual and depth information.

#### 3.4. Inference

During inference, a CFG (Classifier-Free Guidance) value of 7.5 and the UniPC scheduler for 50 timesteps are used. Users provide the mirror mask, and the input depth map is estimated using DepthPro from the masked image.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper provides extensive qualitative and quantitative evaluations, demonstrating the superior performance of MirrorFusion 2.0 trained on SynMirrorV2.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **MirrorBenchV2 (Synthetic Scenes):** MirrorFusion 2.0 significantly outperforms the baseline MirrorFusion and other diffusion-based inpainting models (Figure 1). For single objects, it accurately captures object orientation and structure (Figure 4a), avoiding distortions seen in baselines. For multi-object scenes, it correctly renders multiple objects with accurate positions, orientations, and structures, avoiding floating objects or merged reflections (Figure 4b, Figure 6).
- **GSO (Real-World Scanned Objects):** The model generalizes well to unseen real-world scanned objects, producing accurate and realistic reflections, preserving details like drawer handles or mug geometry (Figure 5).
- **MSD (Real-World Scenes):** After the third stage of fine-tuning on the MSD dataset, MirrorFusion 2.0 demonstrates impressive ability to handle complex real-world scenes, such as cluttered cables on a table or reflections across multiple mirrors, capturing intricate details with high fidelity (Figure 7).
- **Comparison with Commercial Product:** MirrorFusion 2.0 also outperforms Adobe Firefly, a commercial generative AI product, in generating accurate reflections for both single and multiple objects, particularly in maintaining correct positioning and orientation (Figure 18).

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

- **Reflection Generation Quality:** On the MirrorBenchV2 dataset, MirrorFusion 2.0 shows improved metrics for reflection quality:
 - **Single Objects:** Achieves higher PSNR (18.79 vs 18.31), SSIM (0.77 vs 0.76), and lower LPIPS (0.108 vs 0.122) compared to the baseline (Table 2).
 - **Multiple Objects:** Fine-tuning with multiple objects (Ours 50k) further improves performance, yielding higher PSNR (18.00 vs 17.77) and SSIM (0.744 vs 0.743), and lower LPIPS (0.119 vs 0.126) (Table 3).
- **Text Alignment:** CLIP Similarity scores remain competitive, indicating that improvements in reflection quality do not come at the expense of textual alignment (Table 2, 3).

#### 4.3. User Study

A user study involving 29 participants evaluating 40 diverse samples showed a strong preference for MirrorFusion 2.0's generations, with **84% of users preferring its outputs** over the baseline method, underscoring the perceived realism and plausibility of the generated reflections.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Multiple Objects Dataset:** Training without the multi-object split of SynMirrorV2 leads to poor reflection generation and artifacts like object blending, highlighting the necessity of including diverse multi-object scenarios in the training data to learn spatial relationships and occlusions (Figure 8). An ablation study also confirmed that the proposed staged training is superior to joint training on single and multiple object splits (Table 5).
- **Ablation on Architecture:** A comparison with a modified Stable Diffusion Inpainting (SDI+Depth) model, which also takes depth input, revealed that the dual-branch architecture of MirrorFusion 2.0 is superior. SDI+Depth suffered from color leakage and significant artifacts, suggesting that separating conditioning information into a dedicated branch (as in BrushNet-like architectures) is more effective (Figure 9).

#### 4.5. Limitations

While highly effective, the method exhibits minor artifacts in highly complex scenes involving more than two objects (Figure 10). The authors suggest that synthesizing even more training data and further fine-tuning could address these remaining issues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "MirrorVerse" research makes significant strides in advancing the capabilities of diffusion models, particularly in generating physically accurate visual effects. Its contributions have several important implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Realism in Generative AI:** By addressing a fundamental limitation in rendering reflections, this work pushes generative models closer to a comprehensive understanding of the physical world. This is crucial for creating truly photorealistic and immersive synthetic content that is visually indistinguishable from real-world imagery.
- **Improved Image Editing and Content Creation:** The ability to realistically add or modify mirror reflections opens up new possibilities for image editing tools, virtual photography, and digital content creation. Designers and artists can leverage this to create more believable scenes, augment existing images with realistic reflections, or generate complex virtual environments with physically consistent lighting and geometry.
- **Advancements in 3D-Aware Generation:** The emphasis on precise object positioning, rotation, grounding, and multi-object interactions contributes to the broader goal of 3D-aware generative models. By embedding geometric and physical constraints into the generation process, models can develop a more robust internal representation of 3D space and object interactions.
- **Valuable Dataset Contribution:** The SynMirrorV2 dataset itself is a significant contribution to the research community. Its scale, diversity, and explicit inclusion of crucial augmentations (random poses, multi-object scenes, grounding) make it a powerful resource for future research in reflection generation, inpainting, and other physically-based image synthesis tasks.
- **Curriculum Learning for Complex Tasks:** The success of the three-stage training curriculum highlights the effectiveness of progressive learning strategies for tackling complex, multi-faceted generative tasks and bridging the synthetic-to-real domain gap. This approach can inform training methodologies for other challenging physically-based rendering problems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations (minor artifacts in highly complex multi-object scenes) provide clear directions for future research, such as exploring more advanced data augmentation techniques or integrating stronger physical priors to further enhance real-world performance.

In conclusion, "MirrorVerse" represents a significant step towards developing diffusion models that not only generate visually appealing images but also adhere to the underlying physical laws of our world, paving the way for more intelligent and capable AI systems in computer vision.
