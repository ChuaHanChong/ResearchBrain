# Fast-in-Slow: A Dual-System Foundation Model Unifying Fast Manipulation within Slow Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Fast-in-Slow (FiS) proposes a unified Vision-Language-Action foundation model that integrates a fast manipulation module directly within a pre-trained VLM-based reasoning system. This architecture enables high execution frequencies and strong generalization, demonstrating improved success rates and control speeds across diverse simulated and real-world robotic tasks compared to previous dual-system approaches.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while improving generalization through large VLMs, suffer from low execution frequencies due to their size and autoregressive nature, hindering responsive closed-loop control.
- Existing dual-system approaches, which separate a lightweight action model (System 1) from a VLM-based reasoning model (System 2), fail to allow System 1 to fully leverage the rich, pre-trained knowledge embedded within System 2.
- There is a need for robotic policies that can seamlessly coordinate slow, high-level reasoning with fast, real-time action execution, similar to integrated dual-process cognition in humans.

## Method

- A unified dual-system ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== model called **Fast-in-Slow** (FiS) that embeds the fast System 1 execution module directly within the pre-trained VLM-based System 2 by repurposing its final transformer blocks.
- Design of heterogeneous modality inputs (2D images, 3D point clouds, robot state) for System 1 and System 2, alongside asynchronous operating frequencies (e.g., 1:4 ratio) tailored to their distinct roles.
- A dual-aware co-training strategy that optimizes System 1 via a diffusion modeling objective for continuous action generation and preserves System 2's reasoning capabilities using an autoregressive next-token prediction objective.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **69%** across 10 RLBench tasks in simulation, outperforming prior dual-system methods like CogACT (**61%**) and π0 (**55%**), while operating at **21.9** Hz (and up to **117.7** Hz with action chunking).
- Demonstrated superior real-world performance on dual-arm tasks, achieving **68%** mean success on Agilex and **74%** on AlphaBot, compared to π0's **59%** and **61%** respectively.
- Exhibited stronger generalization capabilities in real-world scenarios with unseen objects, complex backgrounds, and varying lighting, showing a smaller performance drop compared to baselines (e.g., **19%** vs. **38%** for unseen objects on AlphaBot).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding a fast execution module (System 1) directly within the final layers of a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (System 2) enables System 1 to inherit and leverage the VLM's extensive knowledge without sacrificing execution speed.
> - Asynchronous operation and heterogeneous modality inputs tailored to each system's role are crucial for balancing high-level reasoning with precise, real-time action generation.
> - A dual-aware co-training strategy, combining diffusion modeling for actions and an autoregressive objective for reasoning, is essential to jointly optimize both systems and preserve the VLM's complex capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Fast-in-Slow" operates within the rapidly evolving domain of robotic manipulation, specifically at the intersection of foundation models, vision-language models (VLMs), and real-time control. Historically, robotic manipulation has struggled with two critical challenges: **generalization** (performing a broad spectrum of tasks across varied environments) and **execution efficiency** (responding quickly and precisely to dynamic conditions).

Recent advancements have seen the emergence of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**, which leverage the immense pre-trained knowledge and common-sense reasoning abilities of internet-scale VLMs. These models, often fine-tuned on robotic datasets, have significantly improved generalization capabilities. However, their autoregressive nature and billion-scale parameters typically lead to **low execution frequencies**, hindering responsive closed-loop control essential for real-world robotic deployment.

To address this efficiency dilemma, the field has increasingly adopted **dual-system approaches**, drawing inspiration from Daniel Kahneman's "Thinking, Fast and Slow" theory. These approaches conceptualize a System 2 (slow, deliberative, VLM-based for high-level reasoning) and a System 1 (fast, intuitive, for real-time action generation). While existing dual-system designs (e.g., π0, CogACT, Helix) have improved execution efficiency by separating System 1 as a lightweight policy head, they suffer from a crucial limitation: System 1, being a distinct and often untrained model, cannot fully leverage the rich, internet-scale pre-trained knowledge embedded within the VLM-based System 2. It relies solely on the features extracted by System 2, thus failing to achieve deeper integration.

"Fast-in-Slow (FiS)" directly addresses this limitation. Instead of maintaining two separate models, FiS proposes a **unified dual-system VLA model** that embeds the System 1 execution module *within* the VLM-based System 2. This is achieved by repurposing the final transformer blocks of the intact VLM to serve as System 1, allowing for partial parameter sharing. This innovative integration aims to facilitate seamless coordination and knowledge transfer between the reasoning and execution components, making System 1 "aware" of System 2's deep understanding without sacrificing speed. The work further refines this concept by designing heterogeneous modality inputs and asynchronous operating frequencies for the two systems, tailored to their distinct roles.

By merging these previously separate entities, FiS-VLA seeks to create a more biologically plausible and computationally efficient "brain" for robots, pushing the boundaries of generalist robot control by synergizing the strengths of large language models for reasoning with real-time, knowledge-aware action execution. This positions FiS-VLA as a significant step towards more robust, adaptable, and generalized robotic manipulation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the "Fast-in-Slow (FiS)" research are to develop a robotic manipulation policy that simultaneously achieves **high generalization capabilities** and **high execution efficiency**.

The core motivations stem from the limitations observed in the current state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:

1. **Low Execution Frequency of Foundation Policies:** While recent VLA models benefit significantly from the common-sense reasoning and knowledge encoded in internet-scale pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), their large parameter counts and autoregressive action generation paradigms lead to inherently low operating frequencies. This restricts their ability to perform responsive, closed-loop control, a critical requirement for effective real-world robotic applications.
2. **Limited Knowledge Transfer in Existing Dual-System Approaches:** Inspired by Kahneman's dual-system theory (System 1: fast, intuitive; System 2: slow, logical), contemporary dual-system VLA models have attempted to mitigate the frequency issue. They typically use a VLM as System 2 for high-level reasoning and a separate, lightweight action model as System 1 for real-time control. While this improves execution speed, the fundamental limitation remains: System 1, being a distinct and often newly initialized component, cannot fully leverage or inherit the rich, pre-trained knowledge embedded within the VLM of System 2. It largely acts as a mere interpreter of System 2's feature outputs, leading to suboptimal performance compared to what might be achieved with deeper integration.
3. **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** The authors are motivated by prior neuroscientific research on dual-process cognition in the human brain, which suggests an integrated, rather than purely separate, functioning of fast and slow cognitive processes. This prompts the central question: "If a VLM model serves as the 'brain' of the robot, can it integrate System 1 and System 2 processes to enable coordinated reasoning and execution?"

To address these motivations, the key objectives of FiS are:

- **To propose a unified dual-system VLA model:** This involves embedding the fast System 1 execution module directly within the VLM-based System 2. This unification aims to enable System 1 to partially share parameters with System 2, thereby leveraging its pre-trained knowledge and facilitating seamless coordination between reasoning and execution.
- **To design heterogeneous modality inputs and asynchronous operating frequencies:** Recognizing the distinct roles of System 1 (fast, reactive control) and System 2 (slow, high-level reasoning), the model is designed to process different types of sensory inputs (e.g., 2D images, 3D point clouds, robot state) and operate at different frequencies, optimizing for both precision and speed.
- **To develop a dual-aware co-training strategy:** This strategy aims to jointly optimize both System 1 and System 2. It ensures System 1 gains action generation capabilities (using diffusion modeling) while simultaneously preserving System 2's high-level contextual reasoning and multimodal representation (using an autoregressive objective).

Through these objectives, FiS aims to overcome the limitations of prior VLA models by offering a foundation model that can achieve superior generalized manipulation performance at significantly higher execution frequencies, paving the way for more capable and robust robotic systems in real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Fast-in-Slow (FiS) proposes a novel **unified dual-system Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model** that deeply integrates a fast execution module (System 1) within a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) for slow reasoning (System 2).

#### 3.1. Core Architectural Concept: Fast-in-Slow Embedding

Unlike previous dual-system VLA approaches that append a separate, lightweight policy head as System 1 to a VLM-based System 2, FiS-VLA repurposes the **final transformer blocks of an intact VLM** to serve as System 1. This means System 1 is not an independently injected module but rather an inherent component of System 2, inheriting its pre-trained knowledge and maintaining coherent understanding of System 2's intermediate reasoning outputs. The complete VLM still forms the core of System 2, ensuring its full reasoning capability. The base architecture is initialized from pre-trained Prismatic VLMs, which consist of a vision encoder and a Large Language Model (LLM).

#### 3.2. Modality Encoders and LLM Backbone

- **Vision Encoder:** FiS employs a dual-encoder setup using both SigLIP and DINOv2 to extract comprehensive visual representations (high-level semantics and local spatial details) from 2D images. These features are concatenated to form a unified visual embedding.
- **Point Cloud Encoder:** To incorporate crucial 3D geometric information for precise manipulation, a lightweight 3D tokenizer is introduced. This tokenizer transforms raw point cloud data (derived from depth maps) into high-dimensional tokens, which are then processed by the *shared vision encoder*. This clever design leverages the VLM's existing vision-language alignment capabilities, avoids significant parameter increase, and maintains computational efficiency, projecting 3D data directly into the LLM's embedding space.
- **LLM Backbone:** The 7B LLaMA2 model, a decoder-only transformer, serves as the LLM backbone. The key is that while System 2 uses the *entire* LLM for deep contextual reasoning, System 1 leverages only the *final few transformer blocks* to condition on the intermediate latent features generated by System 2.
- **MLP Components:** Auxiliary MLPs are used for a pre-trained vision-language projector (mapping 2D/3D features to LLM's textual embedding space), a state encoder for robot proprioceptive state, and diffusion-specific MLPs for injecting timestep and noised actions.

#### 3.3. Dual-System Coordination Design

FiS explicitly designs the two systems to operate heterogeneously based on their roles:
- **Asynchronous Operating Frequencies:**
 - **System 2 (Slow):** Operates at a lower frequency (e.g., 1:n ratio to System 1). It processes 2D visual observations and language instructions to perform high-level semantic understanding and contextual reasoning. Its output is an intermediate latent feature from the LLM, which serves as a guiding condition for a future horizon of System 1's actions (e.g., H actions). During training, asynchronous sampling ensures System 1 learns temporal consistency despite System 2's lower update rate.
 - **System 1 (Fast):** Operates at a higher frequency, generating executable actions in real time for each step. It is conditioned on the periodically updated latent feature from System 2 and the *most recent* high-frequency observations.
- **Heterogeneous Modality Inputs:**
 - **System 2:** Receives language instructions and 2D visual observations, leveraging its internet-scale pre-training for high-level semantic reasoning.
 - **System 1:** Receives low-latency 2D images, the robot's current proprioceptive state, and 3D point cloud data. These modalities are chosen for their direct relevance to precise, real-time action generation and spatial understanding. System 1 integrates these with System 2's latent feature for diffusion-based action generation.

#### 3.4. Training Objective and Recipe: Dual-Aware Co-Training Strategy

To jointly optimize both systems and prevent catastrophic forgetting of System 2's reasoning capabilities, FiS introduces a dual-aware co-training strategy:
- **L_fast (for System 1 - Action Generation):** A diffusion modeling objective is used to generate continuous, reliable actions. It aims to denoise an action sequence $\tilde{a}_{\tau}$ (noised with Gaussian noise at timestep $\tau$) back to the original action $\tilde{a}$. The loss function is:
 $L_{fast} = E_{\tau,c,\tilde{a},\eta} [\|\eta - \pi_{\theta_f}(\sqrt{\beta_{\tau}}\tilde{a} + \sqrt{1-\beta_{\tau}}\eta, c, \tau)\|^2]$
 where $c$ is the conditioning context from System 2 (low-frequency latent feature) and System 1 (high-frequency inputs).
- **L_slow (for System 2 - Reasoning Preservation):** An autoregressive next-token prediction objective with a cross-entropy loss is applied to the entire VLA model (System 2). This ensures System 2 retains its high-level reasoning capabilities, either on discrete action tokens or language-based plans:
 $L_{slow} = -\sum_{i=1}^{D_t} \log P(\hat{a}_i | \text{context}, \theta)$
- **Overall Objective:** The total training objective is a sum of these two losses: $L_{FiS-VLA} = L_{fast} + L_{slow}$.

#### 3.5. Pretraining and Fine-tuning

The model is initialized with parameters from a pre-trained VLM (Prismatic VLMs). It undergoes large-scale pretraining on a curated dataset of over 860K robotic trajectories (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, DROID, ROBOMIND) using single-view 2D RGB images as observation, with System 2 supervised by discrete actions. Subsequently, it is fine-tuned on high-quality, self-collected real-world and simulation data. During fine-tuning, System 2's objective is enhanced with additional language supervision (manually annotated sub-task plans and automated augmentation), and multi-view/multi-modal inputs (e.g., three camera views, point clouds) are incorporated based on task requirements.

This methodology combines a novel architectural embedding, tailored input handling, and a sophisticated co-training strategy to achieve a unified, efficient, and highly capable VLA foundation model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in both simulation and real-world environments demonstrate that Fast-in-Slow (FiS-VLA) achieves state-of-the-art performance in robotic manipulation while maintaining high execution frequencies and strong generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiment (RLBench Benchmark)

- **Superior Manipulation Performance:** FiS-VLA achieved an average success rate of **69%** across 10 diverse RLBench tasks. This significantly outperforms previous state-of-the-art dual-system methods like CogACT (61%) by 8% and π0 (55%) by 14%. FiS-VLA showed superior performance on 8 out of 10 tasks, indicating robust action generation.
- **High Control Frequency:** FiS-VLA operates at **21.9 Hz** (with action chunk set to one) on an NVIDIA 4090 GPU. This is more than twice as fast as CogACT (9.8 Hz) and 1.6 times faster than π0 (13.8 Hz), even though FiS-VLA and CogACT use a larger 7B-parameter LLM compared to π0's 2.6B LLM.
- **Impact of Action Chunking:** An ablation study showed that increasing the action chunk size (predicting multiple future actions at once) significantly boosts the theoretical control frequency without a substantial drop in success rate. With an action chunk of eight, FiS-VLA achieved a remarkable **117.7 Hz** control frequency, highlighting its potential for high-speed, high-fidelity robotic control.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

A detailed analysis of FiS-VLA's components confirmed the efficacy of its design choices:
- **Parameters of System 1's Shared Blocks:** Increasing the number of shared transformer blocks for System 1 within System 2 improved manipulation performance, saturating when two blocks were used. This indicates that embedding System 1 within the VLM-based System 2 successfully enables it to inherit rich pre-trained knowledge, achieving stable manipulation with minimal additional parameters.
- **Different Modality Inputs to System 1:** Each input modality provided to System 1 (robot state, 2D images, 3D point clouds) was crucial for performance. Removing 3D point clouds decreased success rate from 69% to 61%. Removing both 2D images and 3D point clouds led to a drop to 44%, and further removing robot state resulted in only 22% success. This validates that the heterogeneous modality input design is vital for accurate and responsive control.
- **Operating Frequency Ratio:** An optimal asynchronous frequency ratio of **1:4** between System 2 and System 1 yielded the best performance (69%). This ratio strikes a balance between slow reasoning and fast action generation, improving both action generation rate and the richness of observations for the execution module.
- **Dual-Aware Co-training Strategy:** The inclusion of the $L_{slow}$ (autoregressive) objective for System 2 was critical. Without it, manipulation performance dropped from 69% to 62%, underscoring its importance in preserving System 2’s inherent reasoning capabilities and providing effective latent guidance for System 1.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiment (Dual-Arm Robots)

- **Cross-Embodiment Robustness:** FiS-VLA consistently outperformed the baseline π0 across eight real-world dual-arm tasks on two different robot platforms: Agilex Robot (end-effector pose control) and AlphaBot (joint position control).
- **Superior Real-World Success Rates:** On Agilex, FiS-VLA achieved a mean success rate of **68%** compared to π0's 59%. On AlphaBot, it achieved **74%** versus π0's 61%. Significant improvements were seen in complex tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning (e.g., "Place Bottles at Rack": 70% vs. 55%) and manipulation of deformable objects ("Fold towel and put": high improvement over baseline).
- **Qualitative Evidence:** Visualizations demonstrated FiS-VLA's ability to execute diverse tasks, involving sequential manipulation, dual-arm coordination, and fine-grained actions, highlighting effective coordination between high-level reasoning and low-latency control in real-world settings.

#### 4.4. Generalization Experiment

FiS-VLA exhibited strong generalization capabilities in real-world scenarios involving unseen manipulated objects, complex backgrounds, and varying lighting conditions:
- **Reduced Performance Drop:** Compared to π0, FiS-VLA consistently showed a smaller percentage drop in success rate when confronted with these novel scenarios. For instance, with unseen objects on AlphaBot, FiS-VLA's accuracy dropped by only 19% compared to π0's 38%.
- **Robustness to Distractions and Perturbations:** This superior generalization indicates that System 1 effectively inherits VLM knowledge for action generation and better interprets System 2's high-level latent features. System 2's contextual reasoning allows it to focus on relevant objects in cluttered scenes, while System 1 ensures execution remains aligned with real-time visual cues, making the system robust to perceptual challenges.

In summary, FiS-VLA successfully delivers on its promise of unifying fast manipulation with slow reasoning, achieving high performance and efficiency across diverse robotic tasks and challenging real-world conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Fast-in-Slow (FiS) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in the realm of generalist robot learning with foundation models. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several key aspects:

1. **Novel Architectural Paradigm:** FiS introduces a pioneering unified dual-system VLA model. By embedding the System 1 (fast execution) module *within* the VLM-based System 2 (slow reasoning) and allowing for partial parameter sharing, it fundamentally rethinks the architecture of dual-system robotic policies. This design overcomes a critical limitation of previous approaches, where System 1, being a separate and often lightweight model, could not fully leverage the vast, pre-trained knowledge inherent in the VLM. This seamless integration enables a more coherent and knowledge-aware action generation.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between Generalization and Efficiency:** The core contribution of FiS is its ability to simultaneously achieve both high generalization (through VLM-powered reasoning) and high execution frequency (through the integrated, fast System 1). Prior VLA models excelled at one but struggled with the other. FiS demonstrates that it is possible to have both: performing complex, diverse tasks with high success rates while maintaining the low-latency control crucial for real-world deployment (e.g., 117.7 Hz control frequency). This is a vital step towards truly practical and adaptable robotic systems.

3. **Biologically Inspired Design:** The direct inspiration from Kahneman's dual-system theory and neuroscientific insights into human cognition (integrated fast and slow processes) lends a compelling theoretical foundation to FiS. This approach suggests a more natural and efficient way for artificial intelligence to mimic intelligent behavior, potentially opening new avenues for designing more robust and adaptable robotic brains.

4. **Robustness and Generalizability:** The empirical results confirm FiS-VLA's robustness across different robot embodiments (single-arm, dual-arm, varying control modes), diverse tasks, and challenging real-world scenarios (unseen objects, complex backgrounds, lighting variations). Its ability to generalize with a smaller performance drop than baselines under distribution shifts is particularly impactful, indicating a more resilient system for deployment in unstructured environments. The incorporation of heterogeneous modality inputs (2D images, 3D point clouds, robot state) tailored to each system's role further contributes to this robustness.

5. **Advanced Training Strategy:** The proposed dual-aware co-training strategy, which combines diffusion modeling for continuous action generation with an autoregressive objective for reasoning preservation, is crucial for optimizing the unified system. This method effectively balances the needs of low-level control with high-level understanding, preventing catastrophic forgetting and ensuring consistent performance.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robotic Deployment:** By enabling high-frequency, generalized control, FiS-VLA could significantly accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots in various domains, from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and domestic assistance. Tasks requiring quick, precise interactions with dynamic environments would become more feasible.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** More responsive and reliable robots can lead to safer and more intuitive human-robot collaboration, making robots better assistants in complex tasks or caregiving roles (e.g., elder care, home automation).
- **Foundation for Future Research:** FiS provides a strong foundation for future research into adaptive and dynamic robot learning. The identified limitation regarding static configuration of shared parameters and collaboration frequency presents a clear and exciting direction for developing even more versatile and context-aware robotic intelligence. Dynamically adapting between fast and slow reasoning based on task complexity or environmental uncertainty could unlock unprecedented capabilities.

In conclusion, "Fast-in-Slow" presents a highly impactful contribution by demonstrating a unified architectural and training approach that resolves a fundamental tension in generalist robot learning. Its technical advancements pave the way for more intelligent, responsive, and deployable robotic systems in the real world.
