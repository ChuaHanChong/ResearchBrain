# IPD: Boosting Sequential Policy with Imaginary Planning Distillation in Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> IPD introduces a framework that enhances Transformer-based sequential policies in offline reinforcement learning by integrating implicit dynamic programming via a quasi-optimal value function and explicit model predictive control. The method leverages an uncertainty-aware world model to augment suboptimal datasets with reliable, imagined optimal trajectories, demonstrating superior performance and improved stability across D4RL benchmarks.

## Problem

- Transformer-based policies in offline RL primarily perform conditional imitation, struggling to synthesize optimal policies or effectively "stitch" together optimal segments from suboptimal offline data.
- Current Transformer-based methods often lack explicit planning mechanisms and the principles of dynamic programming throughout their training and inference cycles.
- Manually specified `return-to-go` (RTG) values, commonly used to condition Transformer policies, are susceptible to tuning complexities and can lead to unstable decision-making during inference.

## Method

- Learns a robust quasi-optimal value function from the original offline dataset, utilizing Huber-expectile regression to mitigate value overestimation.
- Trains an uncertainty-aware probabilistic ensemble ==world model== that quantifies aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty (via geometric Jensen-Shannon divergence to filter unreliable rollouts), then augments the dataset by generating high-quality, reliable trajectory segments for suboptimal data via Model Predictive Control (MPC) planning within model-confident regions.
- Distills imaginary planning knowledge into the final Transformer policy through a composite loss that integrates MPC-enhanced data, action gradient guidance from the Q-function, and a dynamic quasi-optimal value function to replace conventional `return-to-go`.

## Results

- IPD achieved superior performance across a range of D4RL benchmarks, including Gym, Kitchen, and Adroit tasks, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art offline RL methods (e.g., **103.2** ± **3.7** on `hopper-medium-replay` and **78.4** ± **4.6** on `kitchen-complete`).
- Ablation studies confirmed that MPC-based data generation significantly outperformed greedy Q-learning for data augmentation, supporting the efficacy of the trained world model and MPC's strategic decision-making.
- Using a learned Quasi-Optimal Value (QOV) function as dynamic guidance resulted in significantly lower variance and improved stability during inference compared to manually tuned `return-to-go` values, which often led to performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating implicit dynamic programming (via learned value functions) and explicit model predictive control (MPC) with uncertainty awareness can significantly enhance the planning capabilities and performance of Transformer-based policies in offline RL.
> - Reliable data augmentation using an uncertainty-aware world model and MPC-driven planning is crucial for generating high-quality, imagined trajectories that can effectively improve suboptimal offline datasets.
> - Replacing manually tuned `return-to-go` values with a learned, dynamic quasi-optimal value function provides more stable and robust guidance for Transformer policies during inference, reducing tuning complexity and improving performance consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities across various complex domains; however, its application in real-world scenarios is often constrained by the necessity for active environmental exploration, which can be costly or unsafe. Offline RL addresses this by enabling policy training from static, pre-collected datasets without direct environmental interaction. A central challenge in offline RL is mitigating value overestimation, which arises from distributional shifts between the learned policy and the data collection policy, particularly when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) state-action pairs. Previous offline RL methodologies have focused on policy constraint or value regularization techniques to counteract these issues.

More recently, the Decision Transformer (DT) paradigm and its variants have reformulated offline RL as a supervised conditional sequence generation problem, leveraging the sequence modeling capabilities of Transformers. While these models are proficient at sequence processing, they fundamentally operate on conditional imitation and lack the dynamic programming mechanisms inherent to traditional RL. This limitation hinders their ability to effectively "stitch" suboptimal trajectories present in the dataset into a truly optimal policy. Existing efforts to address this, such as elastic context selection, RL objective regularization, and return-to-go relabeling, have yielded incremental performance improvements without fully integrating planning principles throughout the policy training and inference cycles.

Concurrently, model-based RL (MBRL) has shown promise in improving sample efficiency and generalization by using learned dynamics models for planning or data augmentation. In offline settings, MBRL variants like ROMI, MOCODA, MOBILE, SUMO, and TD-MPC have explored model-based approaches for generating rollouts, addressing OOD generalization, quantifying uncertainty, and performing trajectory optimization.

This work, Imaginary Planning Distillation (IPD), positions itself at the intersection of Transformer-based sequential policies and model-based offline RL. It aims to bridge the identified gap by integrating implicit dynamic programming, through value function learning, and explicit model predictive control (MPC), through imaginary planning within a learned world model, directly into the training and inference processes of Transformer-based sequential policies. This integration seeks to overcome the reliance on mere conditional sequence imitation, thereby enabling the synthesis of optimal trajectories from suboptimal offline data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficacy of Decision Transformer-based sequential policies in offline reinforcement learning. While these policies are powerful in modeling sequences, their reliance on static, potentially suboptimal datasets and their foundational lack of dynamic programming mechanisms prevent them from effectively integrating suboptimal experiences or explicitly planning for optimal outcomes.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving the development of IPD include:

- **Addressing the "Stitching Problem" in Transformer-based Policies:** Decision Transformers primarily perform conditional sequence imitation, meaning they learn to mimic observed trajectories rather than synthesize an optimal policy by "stitching" together optimal segments from various suboptimal trajectories. The research is motivated by the need to imbue these models with the ability to reason about and generate optimal sequences.
- **Integrating Planning into Offline RL:** Current Transformer-based methods do not fully incorporate the principles of planning throughout their training and inference cycles. A core motivation is to seamlessly integrate offline planning, specifically implicit dynamic programming (via value functions) and explicit Model Predictive Control (MPC), into the entire framework of data generation, supervised training, and online inference.
- **Leveraging Suboptimal Data More Effectively:** Offline datasets often contain a spectrum of trajectory qualities, including many suboptimal experiences. The work aims to develop a mechanism to identify these suboptimal segments and augment them with higher-quality, imagined optimal rollouts, thereby enhancing the utility of the available data.
- **Enhancing Robustness and Stability during Inference:** Manually specified `return-to-go` (RTG) values, commonly used to condition Transformer policies, are susceptible to tuning complexities and can lead to unstable decision-making. The motivation is to replace this heuristic with a learned, dynamic, and quasi-optimal value function to improve inference stability and performance.
- **Developing an Uncertainty-Aware Planning Mechanism:** To ensure the reliability of imagined rollouts, particularly when operating within a learned world model, it is crucial to quantify and manage model uncertainty. The research aims to equip the world model with robust uncertainty measures to filter out unreliable imaginary data.

In essence, IPD is motivated by the objective to move beyond mere imitation in Transformer-based offline RL, enabling these powerful sequence models to proactively plan for and generate optimal actions by integrating sophisticated model-based planning and value-based learning techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

IPD proposes a novel framework to integrate implicit dynamic programming and explicit model predictive control (MPC) into the training and inference of Transformer-based sequential policies. The methodology is structured into four distinct phases: learning a quasi-optimal foundation, equipping the world model with an uncertainty measure, augmenting the dataset via MPC, and distilling imaginary planning knowledge into the final Transformer policy.

#### 3.1. Offline Quasi-Optimal Value Function Learning

IPD begins by learning a robust quasi-optimal value function from the original offline dataset. It adopts principles from Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) to mitigate overestimation issues prevalent in offline settings. The method replaces standard expectile regression with Huber-expectile regression (Eq. 1, 2) for value function optimization, enhancing robustness to dataset outliers. A quasi-optimal value function $V_{\psi}(s)$ and Q-function $Q_{\theta}(s, a)$ are learned by optimizing objectives (Eq. 3, 4). Subsequently, a quasi-optimal policy $\pi^{\text{QOP}}_{\omega}$ (Eq. 5) is derived through advantage-weighted regression, configured to align with actions that maximize the Q-function.

#### 3.2. World Model with Uncertainty Measure

To facilitate effective data augmentation, IPD trains a world model that predicts both next states $F_{\phi}(\hat{s}_{t+1} | s_t, a_t)$ and rewards $R_{\phi}(\hat{r}_{t+1} | s_t, a_t)$. This world model is designed to handle the limitations of offline data, such as limited state-action coverage and stochasticity. It utilizes a probabilistic ensemble (PE) world model (Eq. 7) to model aleatoric uncertainty, which arises from environmental randomness. Epistemic uncertainty, stemming from limited knowledge, is quantified by measuring disagreement among ensemble members using a geometric Jensen-Shannon (GJS) divergence (Eq. 8-11), chosen for its computational tractability during imaginary rollouts. A threshold parameter $\kappa$ (Eq. 12) is then used to define a reliable subset of state-action pairs, filtering out trajectories with unacceptable uncertainty levels. Training stability for the world model is enhanced through Gaussian reparameterization and an exponential decay schedule for covariance regularization (Eq. 13-15), integrated into the state-transition consistency loss $L_c$ and the total world model loss $L_{\text{world}}$ (Eq. 16).

#### 3.3. Data Augmentation with Imaginary Planning

This phase leverages the learned quasi-optimal value function and the uncertainty-aware world model to generate reliable, high-quality data.
- **Suboptimal State Identification:** The process identifies suboptimal state-action pairs within the original dataset that could benefit from improvement. For each state, an "imaginary return" $R_{\text{Imagine}}(s_t)$ is computed by rolling out the quasi-optimal policy $\pi^{\text{QOP}}_{\omega}$ for a horizon $H_I$ using the world model (Eq. 17). This is compared to the "real return" $R_{\text{Real}}(s_t)$ from the original dataset trajectory (Eq. 18). States demonstrating a significant positive difference ($R_{\text{Imagine}} - R_{\text{Real}}$) are identified as suboptimal. Trajectories are processed using asymmetric windowing (Eq. 19) to preserve historical context ($H_{\text{con}}$) and allocate future steps ($H_I$) for augmentation. The top $K$ states with the highest potential improvement are selected for augmentation (Eq. 20-22).
- **Reliable Optimal Planning via MPC:** For each selected suboptimal state, an augmented trajectory segment of length $H_I$ is generated (Eq. 23). Actions within this segment are determined by Model Predictive Control (MPC) planning. MPC samples $N_{\text{mpc}}$ candidate trajectories of horizon $H_m$ using the learned world model. The action for the current step ($a_{(i)}^{t+k}$) is chosen from the trajectory that yields the highest cumulative discounted return (Eq. 25). Crucially, all generated state-action pairs must satisfy the uncertainty constraint by remaining within the reliable set $E$ (Eq. 12), ensuring that planning occurs only in model-confident regions. Algorithm 1 summarizes this data augmentation procedure.

#### 3.4. Imaginary Planning Distillation

The final phase distills the knowledge from the imaginary planning into a Transformer-based sequential policy. This involves a composite loss function $L_{\text{IPD}}$ (Eq. 26) that integrates three key components:
- **MPC-enhanced dataset:** The Transformer policy is trained on the augmented dataset $D_{\text{aug}}$, which contains high-quality trajectories generated through MPC, thereby providing enhanced action supervision.
- **Action gradient guidance:** The learned quasi-optimal Q-function $Q_{\theta}(s, a)$ provides an additional regularization signal in the form of action gradients ($\nabla_{\eta}Q(s, \pi_{\eta}(s))$). This term encourages the policy to produce actions that yield higher Q-values, implicitly distilling the principles of dynamic programming.
- **Dynamic Return-To-Go (RTG):** The conventional, often manually tuned, `return-to-go` input for the Transformer is replaced by the learned quasi-optimal value function $V_{\psi}(s_t)$. This dynamic conditioning allows the Transformer to infer the potential of future rewards directly from state inputs, enhancing decision-making stability and eliminating the need for manual RTG specification during inference.

This comprehensive distillation process aims to mitigate the influence of suboptimal supervision and guide the Transformer policy towards generating superior actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of IPD was conducted on the D4RL benchmark, encompassing Gym, Kitchen, and Adroit tasks. Performance was assessed across ten distinct tasks, with all scores normalized against D4RL benchmarks for standardized comparison.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Comparison

IPD consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to a range of state-of-the-art offline RL methods, including traditional Q-learning-based approaches (Conservative Q-Learning (CQL), Implicit Q-Learning (IQL)) and various Transformer-based sequential policy methods (Decision Transformer (DT), Decision Diffuser (DD), Elastic Decision Transformer (EDT), Q-learning Decision Transformer (QDT), Q-value regularized Transformer (QT), and Reinformer).

- **Gym Tasks (e.g., walker-medium, hopper-medium, halfcheetah-medium, and replay variants):** IPD exhibited higher scores, for instance, achieving 103.2 ± 3.7 on `hopper-medium-replay` compared to DD's 100.0 and QT's 102.1. On `walker-medium-replay`, IPD scored 96.2 ± 2.1, surpassing QT's 94.2. This performance is attributed to IPD's effective utilization of MPC, informed by the value function, to generate high-quality data.
- **Kitchen Tasks (kitchen-complete, kitchen-partial):** IPD showed improved generalization to novel states and long-horizon optimization. For `kitchen-complete`, IPD achieved 78.4 ± 4.6 against QT's 75.0, and for `kitchen-partial`, it scored 74.3 ± 2.8, exceeding Reinformer's 73.1.
- **Adroit Tasks (hammer-human-v1, pen-cloned-v1):** IPD also performed well in sparse human demonstration settings. On `pen-cloned-v1`, IPD obtained 92.8 ± 3.7 compared to QT's 90.1, indicating its ability to leverage implicit dynamic programming to guide actions towards higher values even with limited high-quality trajectories.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

A comprehensive ablation study investigated the contributions of IPD's key components:

- **Data Generation (MPC vs. Greedy Q-Learning):** The study compared MPC-based data augmentation with a greedy Q-learning procedure that directly generates actions for rollouts using the quasi-optimal policy. Results indicated that MPC-based data generation significantly outperformed greedy Q-learning (Figure 2, `Walker2d-medium-replay` task). This outcome supports the effectiveness of the trained world model and highlights MPC's capacity for strategic decision-making through sampling multiple trajectories and selecting the optimal one. Furthermore, an approximate linear improvement in IPD's performance was observed as the volume of generated data increased, suggesting a data scaling effect in offline data generation.
- **Quasi-Value Function Guidance (QOV vs. Manually Tuned Return-To-Go):** The research examined the impact of using a learned Quasi-Optimal Value (QOV) function as guidance versus manually engineered Return-To-Go (RTG) values. The findings (Table 1, Figure 3, `halfcheetah-medium-replay` task) revealed that replacing arbitrary RTG with QOV resulted in significantly lower variance and improved stability during inference. Manually varying RTG values led to noticeable performance degradation and instability, as the agent struggled with inconsistent guidance. The learned QOV streamlines the inference process by eliminating manual tuning and providing more consistent, robust guidance.

Overall, the empirical results and ablation studies collectively support that IPD's architectural components, including uncertainty-aware world models, MPC-driven data augmentation, value-guided action imitation, and dynamic return-to-go prediction, contribute to its superior performance and stability in offline RL tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

IPD introduces a framework that advances the capabilities of Transformer-based sequential policies in offline reinforcement learning by systematically integrating planning principles. This work addresses a fundamental limitation of current sequence modeling approaches in offline RL, which primarily rely on conditional imitation and often struggle to synthesize optimal policies from suboptimal data.

The significance of IPD lies in several key areas:

- **Bridging Supervised Learning and Dynamic Programming:** IPD successfully bridges the gap between supervised sequence modeling and the core principles of reinforcement learning, specifically dynamic programming and model predictive control. By distilling "imaginary" planning—which includes both MPC-generated rollouts and value function refinements within a learned world model—into the Transformer policy, the framework enables a more principled approach to optimal policy generation from static datasets.
- **Enhanced Policy Performance and Robustness:** The consistent and often superior performance of IPD across diverse D4RL benchmarks, including Gym, Kitchen, and Adroit tasks, demonstrates its effectiveness. The framework's ability to augment suboptimal datasets with reliable, imagined optimal trajectories, coupled with a value-guided objective, enables the Transformer policy to learn higher-value actions. Furthermore, replacing heuristic `return-to-go` values with a learned quasi-optimal value function significantly improves decision-making stability and robustness during inference, which is a critical practical advantage.
- **Improved Utilization of Offline Data:** By identifying and enhancing suboptimal trajectory segments through uncertainty-aware MPC planning, IPD maximizes the utility of existing offline datasets. This data augmentation strategy enables the learning agent to transcend the limitations of the original data quality, fostering the emergence of policies that outperform the behavior policy that collected the data.
- **Advancements in Model-Based Offline RL:** IPD contributes to the growing field of model-based offline RL by demonstrating a robust method for integrating learned world models, uncertainty quantification, and MPC into a sequence modeling paradigm. The explicit modeling of both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, alongside mechanisms to ensure planning occurs within reliable regions of the state space, provides a foundation for safer and more effective model-based planning in offline settings.
- **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The ability to train high-performing and stable policies from offline data without requiring costly or risky online interaction has significant implications for real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and healthcare. IPD's approach offers a pathway to develop intelligent agents in these domains where data collection is feasible but interactive exploration is not.

In summary, IPD represents a step towards more intelligent and autonomous offline RL agents by fundamentally enhancing Transformer-based policies with integrated planning capabilities. The insights gained from the ablation studies, particularly regarding the benefits of MPC-driven data augmentation and dynamic value guidance, as well as the observed scaling law, open avenues for future research into optimal synthetic data generation and hybrid model-free/model-based approaches within sequence modeling.
