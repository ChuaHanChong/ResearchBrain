# One Policy to Dress Them All: Learning to Dress People with Diverse Poses and Garments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University developed a robot-assisted dressing system capable of generalizing to diverse garments and human arm poses using only partial point cloud observations. The system achieved an average whole arm dressed ratio of 0.86 ± 0.17 and an upper arm dressed ratio of 0.71 ± 0.34 in a human study with 17 participants, demonstrating enhanced performance over a non-distilled baseline.

## Problem

- Robot-assisted dressing faces challenges manipulating deformable clothing in 3D space due to complex dynamics, self-occlusion, and lack of compact state representations.
- Existing robot-assisted dressing systems struggle with generalization, often relying on simplifying assumptions such as fixed garment types or specific human arm poses, limiting their real-world applicability.
- Transferring policies trained in simulation to real-world robots for deformable manipulation is difficult due to the 'sim2real gap' and the necessity of operating with partial, noisy sensor observations.

## Method

- A reinforcement learning framework was developed using a "==Dense Transformation==" policy and Q-function architecture to process partial point cloud observations for intricate deformable cloth manipulation.
- Policy distillation, employing Earth Mover's distance, was used to combine multiple specialized "teacher" policies (each trained for a specific human arm pose sub-range) into a single generalized "student" policy.
- Guided domain randomization was introduced to train the student policy with randomized observations while imitating non-randomized teacher policies, thereby enhancing robustness for real-world deployment.

## Results

- In a human study involving 17 participants and 510 trials, the system achieved an average whole arm dressed ratio of **0.86** ± **0.17** and an upper arm dressed ratio of **0.71** ± **0.34**, with a **57%** success rate (covering **80%** of upper arm length).
- The distilled policy significantly outperformed a 'No Distillation' baseline, achieving a **0.74** ± **0.35** upper arm dressed ratio and a **66%** success rate compared to **0.26** ± **0.27** and **1%** respectively, demonstrating superior generalization.
- The system successfully generalized across five diverse garment types and five distinct static human arm poses, with performance varying consistently with the inherent difficulty of the garment or pose.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A segmentation-type PointNet++ architecture (Dense Transformation policy) is effective for learning dense actions from partial point clouds for complex deformable object manipulation tasks like dressing.
> - Policy distillation using Earth Mover's distance provides a robust method for learning a single generalizable policy across a wide range of diverse conditions (e.g., human poses) by leveraging specialized teacher policies.
> - Guided domain randomization successfully bridges the sim2real gap by training a student policy with randomized inputs while using non-randomized teacher policies for imitation, yielding policies robust to real-world sensor noise and discrepancies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot-assisted dressing addresses a critical need in assistive technology, particularly for older adults and individuals with disabilities who require assistance with activities of daily living. The National Center for Health Statistics reported in 2016 that a significant majority of residents in nursing facilities and at-home care patients require dressing assistance. Automating this task could enhance independence, privacy, and potentially alleviate caregiver shortages.

Despite its potential, robot-assisted dressing presents considerable challenges. Manipulating deformable objects like clothing in a three-dimensional space is inherently complex due to the lack of compact state representations, non-linear dynamics, and self-occlusion issues. Previous research in deformable object manipulation has often focused on two-dimensional tabletop tasks, such as cloth folding or smoothing, which can sometimes be addressed with simpler pick-and-place actions. Robot-assisted dressing, conversely, necessitates dexterous manipulation in 3D and intricate closed-loop actions during physical human-robot interaction.

Prior work in robot-assisted dressing has typically made simplifying assumptions that limit generalization. These assumptions often include a fixed, known garment or a fixed, cooperative human arm pose. Some approaches focus on user modeling or haptic perception, while others employ reinforcement learning for specific garments or collaborative scenarios. However, generalizing a single policy across diverse garment types (e.g., varying sleeve lengths, materials, geometries) and a wide range of human arm poses, especially non-collaborative static poses, remains a significant challenge. This paper aims to advance the field by developing a system capable of addressing this generalization challenge using partial point cloud observations.

The work also contributes to the broader area of policy learning for robotic manipulation from point clouds. While several recent studies have explored reinforcement learning and imitation learning from point cloud observations for rigid object manipulation or tool use, their application to complex deformable cloth manipulation, particularly in human-robot interaction contexts, is less explored. This research seeks to demonstrate the effectiveness of reinforcement learning with specific policy and Q-function architectures for deformable object manipulation using point cloud inputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robot-assisted dressing system that can generalize to dress diverse garments on people holding a wide variety of static arm poses, using only partial point cloud observations from a single depth camera.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key challenges and limitations in existing robot-assisted dressing systems:
- **Societal Need:** A significant portion of the aging population and individuals with disabilities require assistance with dressing, which is a fundamental activity of daily living. A robot capable of providing this assistance could enhance their quality of life, independence, and privacy, while also addressing the growing demand for caregiving services.
- **Deformable Object Manipulation Complexity:** Dressing involves intricate manipulation of highly deformable cloth in 3D space. Unlike rigid objects, cloth lacks a compact state representation, exhibits complex non-linear dynamics, and frequently undergoes self-occlusion, making robust perception and control difficult.
- **Generalization to Diverse Garments:** Different garments (e.g., hospital gowns, cardigans, sleeveless vests) vary significantly in geometry, material properties, and sleeve characteristics (length, width, elasticity). A general-purpose dressing robot needs to handle this diversity without requiring task-specific programming for each garment.
- **Generalization to Diverse Human Poses:** Individuals requiring assistance may have limited range of motion or specific postures. A robot must be able to dress people regardless of their arm pose, avoiding the common assumption of a single fixed pose or a collaborative human who adjusts their arm.
- **Reliance on Partial Observations:** Real-world deployments often rely on economically viable sensors like depth cameras, which provide partial, noisy point cloud observations. The system needs to operate effectively with such visual input, avoiding reliance on full state information or haptic feedback often assumed in simulation or simpler tasks.
- **Sim2Real Transfer Robustness:** Policies trained in simulation often struggle when deployed in the real world due to discrepancies in physics, visual rendering, and environmental conditions. Developing methods for robust sim2real transfer is essential for practical application.

To address these challenges, the research sets out to:
- Learn a single policy that can effectively handle diverse garments and human arm poses.
- Utilize point cloud observations as input to the policy to facilitate generalization across different garment shapes and human anatomies.
- Design a policy architecture and Q-function suitable for reinforcement learning with point cloud data for deformable object manipulation.
- Leverage policy distillation to combine expert policies trained on sub-ranges of arm poses into a single, comprehensive policy.
- Employ "guided domain randomization" to enhance the policy's robustness and enable reliable transfer from simulation to real-world environments.
- Conduct extensive real-world evaluations, including a human study, to validate the system's performance and generalization capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed robot-assisted dressing system integrates several machine learning and robotics techniques, primarily relying on reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation, policy distillation, and guided domain randomization for sim2real transfer.

**A. System Overview**
The system pipeline involves:
1. **RL Policy Training in Simulation:** Dressing policies are learned using RL in a physics-based simulation environment (Softgym based on NVIDIA Flex).
2. **Policy Architecture Design:** A specific neural network architecture for the policy and Q-function is designed to handle partial point cloud observations effectively.
3. **Generalization to Diverse Poses via Policy Distillation:** To manage the complexity of diverse arm poses, the full range of poses is divided into sub-ranges. Individual "teacher" policies are trained for each sub-range, and then these teachers are distilled into a single "student" policy.
4. **Robust Sim2Real Transfer with Guided Domain Randomization:** The distilled student policy is further trained with randomized observations (domain randomization), but guided by imitating the non-randomized teacher policies, to improve its robustness for real-world deployment.
5. **Real-world Deployment and Evaluation:** The final domain-randomized and distilled policy is deployed on a real robot manipulator for evaluation with a manikin and in a human study.

**B. Learning to Dress with Reinforcement Learning**
The dressing task is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP).
- **Observation Space (O):** The policy receives partial point cloud observations. This includes points belonging to the human right arm ($P_h$), garment ($P_g$), and a single point representing the robot end-effector ($P_r$). These point clouds are segmented and concatenated. Point features include 3D position and a one-hot vector indicating their class (garment, arm, or gripper).
- **Action Space (A):** The robot end-effector's delta transformation, represented as a 6D vector (3D translation and 3D axis-angle rotation, with roll rotation set to zero).
- **Reward Function (R):** A composite reward function is designed for simulation training:
 - **Main Reward ($r_m$):** Measures dressing progress. Initially, it's the negative distance from the garment shoulder opening to the person's finger. Once the garment covers the forearm, it measures the dressed distance along the arm, from finger to elbow, and then from elbow to shoulder, with a higher weight for dressing the upper arm to encourage turning at the elbow.
 - **Force Penalty ($r_f$):** Penalizes excessive force applied by the garment to the person, preventing unrealistic simulation behavior and ensuring safety.
 - **Contact Penalty ($r_c$):** Penalizes the robot end-effector moving too close to the person.
 - **Deviation Penalty ($r_d$):** Discourages the garment center from deviating too far from the arm.
- **Policy and Q-function Architecture:**
 - **Policy (Dense Transformation):** Instead of a classification-type PointNet++ that compresses the whole point cloud to a single action, a segmentation-type PointNet++ is used. This architecture outputs a dense per-point action vector for each input point. Only the action vector corresponding to the gripper point is executed.
 - **Q-function:** A classification-type PointNet++ is used. The action sampled from the policy is concatenated as an additional feature to every point in the input point cloud before being processed by the network to output a scalar Q-value.
- **Underlying RL Algorithm:** Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) is employed for training.

**C. Generalization to Diverse Human Poses via Policy Distillation**
Directly training a single policy for a wide range of arm poses is challenging due to potential issues like imbalanced learning speeds or conflicting gradients. Policy distillation is used to address this:
1. **Pose Range Decomposition:** The diverse arm pose range ($\Phi$) is systematically divided into smaller sub-ranges ($\Phi_{sub,i}$) by discretizing each of the three defining joint angles (shoulder lifting, elbow inwards/outwards, elbow lifting) into multiple intervals. This results in $N$ sub-ranges.
2. **Teacher Policy Training:** For each of the $N$ sub-ranges, a separate "teacher" policy ($\pi_{t,i}$) is trained using the RL methodology described above.
3. **Student Policy Distillation:** A single "student" policy ($\pi_s$) is then trained to generalize across all sub-ranges. Its loss function combines the standard SAC training loss ($L_{SAC}$) with a policy distillation loss ($L_{distill}$). The distillation loss minimizes the Earth Mover's distance between the student's action distribution and the action distributions of all $N$ teacher policies. This approach encourages the student to imitate the behaviors of the specialized teachers.

**D. Guided Domain Randomization for Sim2Real Transfer**
To bridge the "sim2real gap" and make the policy robust to real-world observation variations, "guided domain randomization" is proposed:
1. **Teacher Policies (Non-randomized):** The teacher policies are trained without any domain randomization in their observations.
2. **Student Policy (Randomized & Guided):** The student policy ($\pi_s$) is trained with randomized observations ($\tilde{o}$) but is guided by imitating the teachers trained on non-randomized observations ($o$). The distillation loss is modified such that the student receives $\tilde{o}_n$ while the teacher receives $o_n$.
3. **Observation Randomizations:** These include:
 - Random cropping of the garment point cloud (to remove bottom parts, account for real-world noise, and occlusions).
 - Random dropping of garment points (to simulate occlusion by the gripper).
 - Random erosion and dilation of the garment's depth image (to account for geometric differences between simulation and real garments).
 - Random noise added to the gripper position (to mimic real-world camera-to-robot calibration errors).

**E. Task Assumptions**
Two main assumptions are made for the dressing task:
1. **Pre-grasped Garment:** The robot is assumed to have already grasped a part of the garment near the shoulder opening.
2. **Static Human Pose:** The person being dressed is assumed to hold a fixed arm pose throughout the dressing process. This simplifies visual perception (static arm point cloud can be captured once) and avoids the complexities of adapting to human motion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from simulation experiments, manikin experiments, and a real-world human study.

**A. Simulation Experiments**
1. **Comparison of RL Algorithms (Table I):**
 - The proposed **Dense Transformation policy (ours)**, using a segmentation-type PointNet++ for the policy and an action-concatenated classification-type PointNet++ for the Q-function, achieved the highest upper arm dressed ratio across three different pose sub-ranges (e.g., 0.68 ± 0.05 on sub-range 1).
 - The **Direct Vector** baseline, using a classification-type PointNet++ policy, showed comparable performance (e.g., 0.63 ± 0.07 on sub-range 1), but slightly less consistent than Dense Transformation.
 - Using a "Dense Transformation policy + latent Q function" resulted in noticeably lower performance (e.g., 0.55 ± 0.05 on sub-range 1).
 - **TD-MPC** and **Deep Haptic MPC** performed poorly or failed, suggesting their unsuitability or insufficient adaptation for this specific deformable cloth manipulation task with point cloud observations and diverse conditions.
2. **Generalization to Diverse Poses (Table II):**
 - **Policy Distillation (ours)**, using Earth Mover's distance for the distillation loss, significantly outperformed all other baselines for learning a single policy that generalizes across all 27 pose sub-ranges, achieving an average upper arm dressed ratio of 0.68 ± 0.012.
 - Replacing Earth Mover's distance with KL divergence (**Policy Distillation (KL)**) yielded lower performance (0.45 ± 0.010).
 - **PCGrad** (a multi-task learning algorithm) and **No Distillation** (training a single policy on the entire pose range) performed poorly (0.37 ± 0.063 and 0.34 ± 0.10, respectively), highlighting the difficulty of direct multi-task learning for this problem.
 - The **Heuristic Motion Planning** baseline also achieved low performance (0.32), indicating limitations in handling complex arm poses.

**B. Real-World Experiments on a Manikin**
A controlled comparison on a manikin (fixed pose) evaluated the Distilled Policy (ours) against the No Distillation baseline.
- **Dressing Success Rate (Table III):** The Distilled Policy achieved a 100% success rate (50/50 trials) across five different garments. The No Distillation baseline achieved a 0% success rate (0/50 trials).
- **Dressed Ratio (Fig. 7):** The Distilled Policy consistently achieved high whole arm and upper arm dressed ratios (e.g., average whole arm dressed ratio of approximately 0.95), while the No Distillation baseline failed to dress the upper arm significantly (average upper arm dressed ratio near 0), often only dressing the forearm. This indicated the No Distillation policy's inability to turn at the elbow.

**C. Human Study Results and Analysis**
A human study involved 17 participants, with 510 total dressing trials (5 trials per garment per participant using the Distilled Policy, and 1 trial per garment per participant using the No Distillation baseline for direct comparison on a subset of poses).

1. **Comparison with No Distillation Baseline (Table IV & Fig. 8):**
 - On 85 trials where both policies were tested on the same poses and garments, the **Distilled Policy (ours)** significantly outperformed the No Distillation baseline.
 - **Whole Arm Dressed Ratio:** Distilled Policy: 0.86 ± 0.19; No Distillation: 0.63 ± 0.15.
 - **Upper Arm Dressed Ratio:** Distilled Policy: 0.74 ± 0.35; No Distillation: 0.26 ± 0.27.
 - **Success Rate:** Distilled Policy: 0.66 ± 0.47; No Distillation: 0.01 ± 0.11.
 - **Likert Item Responses:** The Distilled Policy received significantly higher agreement (median 6.0, "Agree") compared to the No Distillation baseline (median 3.0, "Somewhat disagree"). A Wilcoxon signed-rank test confirmed a statistically significant difference (p < 0.05).

2. **Overall Distilled Policy Performance (Table V & Fig. 9):**
 - Across all 425 dressing trials with the Distilled Policy, the system achieved an average **Whole Arm Dressed Ratio of 0.86 ± 0.17** and an average **Upper Arm Dressed Ratio of 0.71 ± 0.34**. The **Success Rate** (covering 80% of upper arm length) was 0.57 ± 0.49.
 - Participants' median Likert response for the Distilled Policy was 5.0 ("Somewhat Agree").

3. **Performance Across Different Garments (Fig. 11):**
 - Performance varied consistently across garments, aligning with human intuition regarding their ease of dressing.
 - Sleeveless vest showed the highest performance, followed by elastic cardigans, then the hospital gown, and finally non-elastic, long-sleeve cardigans. This indicated generalization to garments not explicitly trained on (e.g., sleeveless vest) and sensitivity to material elasticity and sleeve length/width.

4. **Performance Across Different Poses (Fig. 12):**
 - Performance varied across the five tested arm poses. The first pose (less sharp bends) achieved the highest performance, while the fifth pose (sharp bends at shoulder and elbow) had the lowest, suggesting increased complexity for poses with acute joint angles.
 - Success rates in the human study were generally lower than on the manikin, possibly due to human arm diversity and subtle, unconscious movements violating the static pose assumption.

5. **Failure Cases:**
 - **Getting stuck:** The policy sometimes ceased making progress, oscillating or stopping, potentially due to sim2real gaps or out-of-distribution arm poses/sizes.
 - **Garment getting caught:** This occurred when the garment experienced high friction on the arm, often due to participants' unintentional movements, or the robot pulling too high, indicating a fidelity gap between simulated and real garment elasticity/friction.

**D. Out-of-Distribution Evaluation and Generalization**
- **Relaxing Static Arm Assumption:**
 - **Simulation:** The system was robust to approximately 8.6 degrees of change in shoulder and elbow joint angles while maintaining 75% of original performance.
 - **Real-world (Single Participant):** The system succeeded in 3 out of 4 trials with 5-degree changes in joint angles but failed with a 10-degree change. It also succeeded in 4 trials with constant arm motions (up to ±10 cm displacement), suggesting some robustness to small, dynamic movements.
- **Dual-Arm Dressing:** Preliminary simulation experiments demonstrated that the Dense Transformation policy could generalize to dual-arm dressing by extracting actions for both gripper points, successfully dressing hospital gowns and cardigans on a fixed pose.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotics by addressing several critical challenges in robot-assisted dressing, pushing the boundaries of what learned policies can achieve in complex human-robot interaction scenarios involving deformable objects.

**Scientific Contributions:**
- **Demonstrated Generalization:** The work provides evidence that a single learned policy can generalize to a diverse range of garments and human arm poses from partial point cloud observations. This moves beyond prior limitations that often assumed fixed garments or poses.
- **Effective RL with Point Clouds for Deformables:** It showcases the effectiveness of a specifically designed policy architecture (Dense Transformation using segmentation-type PointNet++) and Q-function for reinforcement learning in deformable object manipulation, which is a domain with unique perception and control challenges.
- **Advancement in Policy Distillation:** The successful application of policy distillation, particularly using Earth Mover's distance for combining policies trained on different pose sub-ranges, presents a robust strategy for addressing multi-task learning difficulties in high-dimensional, complex robotic tasks. The comparison with KL divergence highlights the potential benefits of Earth Mover's distance in this context.
- **Robust Sim2Real Transfer:** The introduction of "guided domain randomization" offers a method for effectively transferring learned policies from simulation to the real world, addressing the prevalent sim2real gap in robotics. The approach of guiding randomized student policies with non-randomized teacher policies enhances robustness.
- **Comprehensive Real-World Validation:** The extensive human study with 17 participants and 510 trials provides a rigorous validation of the system's capabilities in a realistic setting, including quantitative metrics and qualitative user feedback (Likert scale), which is crucial for assistive robotics research.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Assistive Robotics:** The developed system has the potential to significantly improve robot-assisted dressing solutions, contributing to the independence and dignity of older adults and individuals with disabilities. By offering a more generalized and robust dressing capability, it could make such robots more practical for real-world caregiving.
- **Mitigation of Caregiver Shortages:** As global populations age, the demand for care services, including assistance with daily living, is increasing. Robots with advanced dressing capabilities could provide much-needed support, potentially alleviating strain on human caregivers and allowing them to focus on other aspects of care.
- **Foundation for Future Deformable Manipulation:** The methodologies, particularly the policy architecture, policy distillation, and guided domain randomization, could serve as a foundation for future research in other complex deformable object manipulation tasks, beyond just dressing. This includes applications in manufacturing, logistics, and household robotics.
- **Advancements in Human-Robot Interaction:** While currently assuming static human poses, the system's demonstrated robustness to small, unintentional human movements (as shown in out-of-distribution evaluations) suggests a path toward more interactive and adaptive human-robot dressing. Future work could build upon this to integrate active human collaboration or adaptation to continuous human motion.
- **Inspiration for Sensor Modalities:** The reliance on only partial point cloud observations from a single depth camera, without requiring haptic or other complex sensing, demonstrates a cost-effective and generalizable approach to perception in assistive robotics, which could inspire further development of vision-based manipulation.

The research acknowledges limitations, such as the static arm pose assumption and the challenges of specific failure cases like garments getting caught due to simulator fidelity differences. These areas are identified as future work, alongside fine-tuning policies in the real world and integrating force-torque sensing for improved robustness and safety. Overall, this work represents a substantial step towards generalized and effective robot-assisted dressing.
