# One Policy to Dress Them All: Learning to Dress People with Diverse Poses and Garments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University developed a robot-assisted dressing system capable of generalizing to diverse garments and human arm poses using only partial point cloud observations. The system achieved an average whole arm dressed ratio of 0.86 ± 0.17 and an upper arm dressed ratio of 0.71 ± 0.34 in a human study with 17 participants, demonstrating enhanced performance over a non-distilled baseline.

## Problem

- Robot-assisted dressing faces challenges manipulating deformable clothing in 3D space due to complex dynamics, self-occlusion, and lack of compact state representations.
- Existing robot-assisted dressing systems struggle with generalization, often relying on simplifying assumptions such as fixed garment types or specific human arm poses, limiting their real-world applicability.
- Transferring policies trained in simulation to real-world robots for deformable manipulation is difficult due to the 'sim2real gap' and the necessity of operating with partial, noisy sensor observations.

## Method

- A reinforcement learning framework was developed using a "==Dense Transformation==" policy and Q-function architecture to process partial point cloud observations for intricate deformable cloth manipulation.
- Policy distillation, employing Earth Mover's distance, was used to combine multiple specialized "teacher" policies (each trained for a specific human arm pose sub-range) into a single generalized "student" policy.
- Guided domain randomization was introduced to train the student policy with randomized observations while imitating non-randomized teacher policies, thereby enhancing robustness for real-world deployment.

## Results

- In a human study involving 17 participants and 510 trials, the system achieved an average whole arm dressed ratio of **0.86** ± **0.17** and an upper arm dressed ratio of **0.71** ± **0.34**, with a **57%** success rate (covering **80%** of upper arm length).
- The distilled policy significantly outperformed a 'No Distillation' baseline, achieving a **0.74** ± **0.35** upper arm dressed ratio and a **66%** success rate compared to **0.26** ± **0.27** and **1%** respectively, demonstrating superior generalization.
- The system successfully generalized across five diverse garment types and five distinct static human arm poses, with performance varying consistently with the inherent difficulty of the garment or pose.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A segmentation-type PointNet++ architecture (Dense Transformation policy) is effective for learning dense actions from partial point clouds for complex deformable object manipulation tasks like dressing.
> - Policy distillation using Earth Mover's distance provides a robust method for learning a single generalizable policy across a wide range of diverse conditions (e.g., human poses) by leveraging specialized teacher policies.
> - Guided domain randomization successfully bridges the sim2real gap by training a student policy with randomized inputs while using non-randomized teacher policies for imitation, yielding policies robust to real-world sensor noise and discrepancies.
