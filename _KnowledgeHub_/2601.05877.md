# iReasoner: Trajectory-Aware Intrinsic Reasoning Supervision for Self-Evolving Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The IREASONER framework enhances the implicit reasoning capabilities of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) by introducing a trajectory-aware intrinsic reward for Chain-of-Thought (CoT) steps within a fully unsupervised self-evolution setting. This approach yielded consistent performance gains across eight diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, particularly in visual mathematics.

## Problem

- Existing self-evolving LMMs primarily rely on "outcome-only" rewards, which provide insufficient supervision for the internal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning process.
- Current methods for optimizing CoT in LMMs typically require labeled data, external judges, or offline supervision, limiting their applicability in unsupervised settings.
- Without direct feedback on intermediate steps, LMMs may develop unstable reasoning paths or rely on shortcuts, even if the final answer is correct.

## Method

- IREASONER integrates a novel ==trajectory-aware intrinsic reward== into a Proposer-Solver self-evolution framework that operates solely on unlabeled images.
- It quantifies ==CoT step agreement== by embedding intermediate claims from multiple Solver rollouts and comparing them to dynamically generated per-step prototypes using weighted cosine similarity.
- The Solver's training objective combines traditional answer-level consistency rewards with this new ==step-level agreement reward==, gradually introduced via a warmup schedule to foster stable learning.

## Results

- IREASONER achieved consistent performance gains across eight diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, with an average improvement of **+1.32 points** on general visual understanding and **+1.64 points** on visual mathematics tasks.
- The framework surpassed existing outcome-only self-evolution methods (e.g., EvoLMM) on all general visual understanding benchmarks, showing an average gain of **+0.50 points** on InfoGraphic-VQA and **+0.48 points** on AI2D.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the reward warmup schedule, which led to the largest performance regression when removed, and demonstrated the complementary nature of answer stability and step-wise rewards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-rollout agreement on intermediate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) steps can serve as a robust intrinsic reward signal, enabling step-level supervision without external labels or human intervention.
> - Combining outcome-level consistency with a continuously applied step-level agreement reward, introduced progressively, is more effective than discrete rewards for improving multimodal reasoning.
> - Early reasoning steps are particularly influential for overall consistency, and their consistency is emphasized through higher weighting in the intrinsic reward calculation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large multimodal models (LMMs) capable of self-improvement from unlabeled data represents a notable direction in artificial intelligence research. Traditional methods for enhancing foundation models often rely on supervised instruction tuning, which requires extensive high-quality labeled data. In contrast, self-improvement paradigms, particularly in LMMs, leverage self-play and intrinsic feedback mechanisms to learn from raw, unlabeled data streams. This approach is appealing due to the abundance of unlabeled visual data and the high cost associated with obtaining high-quality multimodal annotations and external reward systems.

Prior work in this domain, such as EvoLMM and VisPlay, has demonstrated the feasibility of self-evolving pipelines for LMMs. These frameworks typically involve a "Proposer–Solver" loop where a model generates visually grounded questions, samples multiple solution attempts, and then updates itself based on intrinsic rewards derived from its own outputs. However, a limitation of these existing frameworks is their primary focus on "outcome-level" rewards. These rewards are typically computed based on final answers, whole responses, or preference scores, leaving the intermediate reasoning process largely unconstrained or weakly supervised.

The importance of intermediate reasoning, often manifested as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), has been recognized in both large language models (LLMs) and LMMs. Eliciting step-by-step CoT during training can help shape the latent computation of instruction-tuned models, even when the explicit goal is not to build a reasoning model. Under outcome-only reward structures, distinct reasoning paths that converge to the same final answer receive similar learning signals, potentially reinforcing suboptimal or hallucinated intermediate steps if they happen to lead to a correct outcome. This lack of direct pressure on the sequence of intermediate claims can hinder the development of reliable vision-language reasoning.

While progress has been made in optimizing CoT for reasoning, many existing approaches still depend on labeled supervision, external judges, or auxiliary reward systems to assess the quality of reasoning steps. Other methods sidestep step supervision by using coarse consistency or reconstruction objectives to curate synthetic training data. Consequently, a gap exists in the ability to evaluate and optimize CoT steps directly within a purely unsupervised self-evolution loop for LMMs.

IREASONER addresses this specific gap by introducing a framework that enables the intrinsic evaluation and optimization of intermediate reasoning steps within a self-evolving LMM without relying on ground-truth labels or external supervision. It connects the two lines of research: self-evolving LMMs and CoT optimization, by providing a step-aware learning signal in an unsupervised setting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the implicit reasoning capabilities of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) through a self-evolving framework that explicitly elicits and rewards the internal agreement of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several identified limitations and challenges in the current landscape of LMM self-improvement:

1. **Weak Constraint on Intermediate Reasoning**: Existing self-evolving LMM frameworks predominantly define intrinsic rewards at the level of final outcomes (e.g., answers, complete responses). This approach provides limited direct supervision or constraint on the intermediate reasoning steps that lead to these outcomes. Consequently, a model might arrive at a correct final answer through an unreliable or ungrounded sequence of intermediate claims.
2. **Inability to Distinguish Reasoning Paths**: When only the final outcome is rewarded, two different reasoning trajectories that happen to yield the same correct answer receive nearly identical learning signals. This prevents the model from differentiating between a robust, visually grounded reasoning process and one that might involve shortcuts, logical inconsistencies, or hallucinated intermediate claims which coincidentally cancel out to produce the correct final result. Stable and reliable vision-language reasoning, however, critically depends on the integrity and consistency of intermediate claims.
3. **Lack of Unsupervised Step-Aware Signals**: While the importance of step-aware signals for improving reasoning is acknowledged in prior work, obtaining such signals typically requires labeled data, external human judges, or complex auxiliary reward systems. These resources are costly and often unavailable in the quantities needed for large-scale model training. This constraint limits the applicability of detailed reasoning supervision in purely unsupervised settings, which are crucial for scaling LMMs using abundant unlabeled data.
4. **Leveraging Unlabeled Data**: The self-evolving paradigm, which learns from streams of unlabeled images, is an appealing path for scaling LMMs. By introducing a mechanism to evaluate intermediate reasoning intrinsically, the work aims to maximize the learning potential from this abundant data without incurring the high costs of human annotation or external verification.
5. **Shaping Latent Computation**: Even when LMMs are not explicitly designed as "reasoning models," they often rely on implicit reasoning processes. By eliciting and supervising explicit step-by-step CoT during training, the goal is to indirectly shape and stabilize these latent computational pathways, leading to more robust and generalized reasoning abilities.

In essence, IREASONER is motivated by the need to develop a more granular and principled approach to LMM self-improvement, moving beyond mere outcome consistency to actively cultivate consistent and coherent intermediate reasoning, all within a purely unsupervised, intrinsic feedback loop.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

IREASONER is designed as a self-evolving framework that operates on unlabeled images to improve an LMM's reasoning capabilities by explicitly leveraging Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and rewarding its internal agreement. It extends the Proposer–Solver self-evolution paradigm, introducing a trajectory-aware reward mechanism.

#### 3.1. Proposer–Solver Self-Evolution Backbone

The framework adopts a Proposer–Solver architecture:
- **Proposer ($\pi_p$)**: Given an unlabeled image $x$, the Proposer policy generates a visually grounded question $q$.
- **Solver ($\pi_s$)**: For a given image $x$ and Proposer-generated question $q$, the Solver policy samples $N$ reasoning rollouts. Each rollout $y_i$ is structured to include an explicit multi-step reasoning trace ($t_i$) followed by a final answer ($a_i$), represented as $\langle\text{<think> } t_i \text{</think><answer> } a_i \text{</answer>}\rangle$.

#### 3.2. Proposer Supervision

The Proposer is trained to generate questions of intermediate difficulty. This is achieved by using an uncertainty-shaped reward based on the empirical answer distribution $p(a | x, q) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}[a_i = a]$. The Proposer's reward $r_p(x, q)$ is derived from the entropy of this distribution, $H(x, q) = -\sum_a p(a|x,q) \log p(a|x,q)$. This mechanism encourages the Proposer to avoid generating questions that are either too easy (high answer consistency, low entropy) or too difficult (random answers, high entropy), thereby maintaining a meaningful learning regime.

#### 3.3. Solver Supervision: Intrinsic CoT Agreement Reward

The core innovation of IREASONER lies in its Solver supervision, which combines answer-level self-consistency with a novel trajectory-aware intrinsic CoT agreement reward.

- **Answer-Level Self-Consistency Reward ($r_{ans}$)**: This component, similar to prior self-evolution work, rewards the Solver for generating answers that are consistent with the majority of its own sampled rollouts. It is defined as $r_{ans_i} = p(a_i | x, q)^\alpha (1 - \eta \bar{\ell}_i)$, where $\alpha > 0$ controls sharpness, $\bar{\ell}_i \in$ is a normalized penalty for excessive pre-answer length, and $\eta \ge 0$ sets the penalty strength.

- **Intrinsic CoT Agreement Reward ($r_{step}$)**: This is the distinctive contribution of IREASONER, providing supervision over intermediate reasoning steps without external labels.
 1. **Dominant Answer Group Identification**: First, the dominant (majority) answer $\hat{a} = \arg \max_a p(a | x, q)$ is identified. The set of rollouts $G = \{i | a_i = \hat{a}\}$ that produced this dominant answer is then defined. A density-based reliability factor $\rho = (|G|/N)^\gamma$ (with $\gamma \ge 0$) is applied to downweight step supervision when few samples agree on the dominant answer.
 2. **Step Embedding**: Each intermediate claim $s_{i,j}$ within a Solver's reasoning trace $t_i = (s_{i,1}, \dots, s_{i,J_i})$ is embedded into a vector $e_{i,j} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ using the model's internal text representations. This is implemented as the $\ell_2$-normalized mean of input-token embeddings for the step text.
 3. **Per-Step Prototypes**: For each step index $j$, a prototype $\mu_j$ is formed by averaging the embeddings of step $j$ from all rollouts within the dominant group $G$. Specifically, $\mu_j = \frac{1}{|G_j|} \sum_{i \in G_j} e_{i,j}$, where $G_j = \{i \in G | j \le J_i\}$. This ensures prototypes are based on rollouts that already agree on the final outcome.
 4. **Step-wise Agreement Scoring**: The agreement of each step $s_{i,j}$ in a rollout $i \in G$ with its corresponding prototype $\mu_j$ is measured using cosine similarity: $r_{i,j} = \text{sim}(e_{i,j}, \mu_j) \in [-1, 1]$.
 5. **Weighted Aggregation**: These per-step agreement scores are aggregated with decaying weights $w_1 > w_2 > \dots$, assigned higher to earlier, grounding-heavy steps. This yields $\tilde{r}_{step_i} = \sum_{j \le J_i} w_j r_{i,j}$.
 6. **Final Intrinsic CoT Agreement Reward**: The final step reward for rollout $i$ is $r_{step_i} = \rho \cdot \tilde{r}_{step_i}$, incorporating the reliability factor.

#### 3.4. Reward Integration for Solver Optimization

The total Solver reward $r_{sol_i}$ is a mixed signal combining the answer-level self-consistency and the intrinsic CoT agreement: $r_{sol_i} = (1 - \lambda(t)) r_{ans_i} + \lambda(t) r_{step_i}$. A warmup schedule for $\lambda(t)$ causes its value to increase over training, initially prioritizing answer-level consistency and gradually shifting weight towards step agreement as the model's reasoning stabilizes.

#### 3.5. Training Mechanism

Both the Solver and Proposer are trained using KL-regularized policy gradients (REINFORCE algorithm) against a frozen reference policy. This regularization helps stabilize the self-evolution process by limiting model drift while allowing reward-driven updates. The KL coefficients are adaptively adjusted online to maintain a target KL budget.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model**: Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.
- **Training Data**: 2.5k unlabeled images sampled from diverse multimodal datasets (ChartQA, AI2D, InfoGraphic-VQA, PlotQA, ChartX, Geometry3K). No ground-truth QA pairs, captions, or external reward models are used during training.
- **Architecture**: Lightweight LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) adapters are used for both Proposer and Solver, keeping the backbone frozen.
- **Hyperparameters**: $N=5$ rollouts, Proposer updated every 5 iterations, maximum step weight of 0.7 for $r_{step}$, 2.5k training steps, AdamW optimizer (learning rate $10^{-6}$, weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping 1.0), bfloat16 precision on 8x AMD MI250X GPUs.
- **Evaluation**: Performance is assessed on eight multimodal reasoning benchmarks (ChartQA, MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse, InfoGraphic-VQA, AI2D, ScienceQA, MMMU) using official splits and standard accuracy metrics, without task-specific tuning.

This methodology aims to intrinsically align and refine the step-by-step reasoning process of LMMs, thereby enhancing their overall reasoning capabilities in a self-supervised manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with IREASONER yielded several findings regarding the efficacy of trajectory-aware intrinsic reasoning supervision for Large Multimodal Models (LMMs).

#### 4.1. Consistent Performance Improvements

IREASONER consistently improved the performance of the Qwen2.5-VL-7B seed model across eight diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- **General Visual Understanding**: On benchmarks such as InfoGraphic-VQA, AI2D, ScienceQA, and MMMU, IREASONER demonstrated average gains of +1.32 points. For instance, InfoGraphic-VQA improved by +1.12 points and ScienceQA by +1.62 points.
- **Visual Mathematics**: Across visual mathematics benchmarks, an average gain of +1.64 points was observed. The most substantial gain was on MathVerse (+2.13 points), with MathVision improving by +1.38 points.

#### 4.2. Superiority of Step-wise Reasoning Reward over Outcome-only Agreement

Compared to EvoLMM, which primarily relies on answer-level agreement, IREASONER showed improved performance on all four general visual understanding benchmarks (e.g., InfoGraphic-VQA by +0.50, AI2D by +0.48). This pattern was particularly evident in tasks where multiple intermediate reasoning paths could lead to the same final answer, suggesting that cross-trajectory step agreement provides a distinct and valuable supervision signal not captured by outcome-level consistency alone. While EvoLMM remained stronger on some highly verifiable short-answer tasks like ChartQA and MathVista, IREASONER's gains on MathVision and MathVerse indicated its effectiveness when intermediate structure is informative and transferable.

#### 4.3. Necessity of Continuous Rewards for Step-level Agreement

The integration of step-level agreement with continuous intrinsic rewards consistently yielded improvements across all eight benchmarks. In contrast, a discrete-reward variant augmented with step-level agreement showed only limited gains. This indicates that smoother credit assignment, where partial alignment in intermediate steps can be rewarded, is more beneficial for leveraging step-level signals.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study Insights

An ablation study provided insights into the contributions of different reward components and design choices:
- **Answer Stability vs. Step-wise Reward**: Using only the answer-stability reward (similar to EvoLMM) yielded large gains on highly verifiable benchmarks like ChartQA (+2.64) and MathVista (+1.94). However, adding step-wise reward in IREASONER further improved InfoGraphic-VQA (+0.44), AI2D (+0.53), ScienceQA (+0.51), MMMU (+0.45), MathVision (+0.67), and MathVerse (+1.20) relative to answer stability alone. This suggests a trade-off, where step-level structure is crucial for tasks benefiting from detailed reasoning.
- **Combined Reward Efficacy**: Using step-wise reward alone produced only marginal gains over the baseline, highlighting its dependence on a foundational answer-level signal. Combining both signals led to substantial improvements (e.g., +1.48 on ScienceQA, +2.04 on MathVerse relative to step-wise reward alone).
- **Mechanism and Shaping Components**: The warmup schedule for the step-level reward component was the most critical design choice, with its removal leading to the largest performance regressions (average -0.68). Removing position decay or density weighting also resulted in consistent, albeit smaller, performance drops. Reward shaping components, such as the length penalty, also had systematic effects on performance.

#### 4.5. Training Dynamics and Reasoning-Trace Analysis

- **Proposer and Solver Stability**: The Proposer's reward and answer entropy remained stable throughout training, indicating that the self-evolution loop maintained an appropriate difficulty regime. Concurrently, the Solver's majority-group density and mean step similarity increased, suggesting a progressive stabilization and alignment of reasoning traces.
- **Optimal Step Budget**: An analysis of the maximum number of reasoning steps used for alignment showed that 8 steps provided a strong balance, yielding consistent gains. Smaller budgets truncated useful information, while excessively large ones introduced noise. The average number of extracted steps stabilized over training, indicating structured intermediate reasoning.
- **Within-mode Divergence**: Even when rollouts produced the same final answer, intermediate reasoning could diverge. Disagreement, measured by leave-one-out step similarity, was observed to concentrate in the middle steps of the reasoning trace, indicating that outcome-only rewards fail to distinguish stable from unstable reasoning within the dominant answer mode. Step-level objectives directly addressed this issue.

#### 4.6. Scaling Across Model Sizes

The observed gains were consistent across different Qwen2.5-VL model sizes (3B, 7B, and 32B), indicating the generality and scalability of IREASONER's approach.

#### 4.7. Reward Reliability and CoT Faithfulness

- **Dominant Group Correctness**: Diagnostic tracking (not used for training) revealed that the dominant-answer group's correctness rate against ground truth improved from approximately 76% in early training to 93% in late training, mitigating concerns about reinforcing incorrect reasoning.
- **CoT Contribution at Inference**: Evaluation of the IREASONER-trained model with CoT suppressed at inference showed that the model still outperformed the baseline. This indicates that step-level training improved the model's underlying representations. Furthermore, a consistent performance gap between the no-CoT and full-CoT settings demonstrated that aligned reasoning chains actively contributed to inferential work during testing, rather than merely serving as post-hoc rationalizations.

In summary, the results indicate that IREASONER's trajectory-aware intrinsic reasoning supervision effectively enhances LMMs' reasoning capabilities, provides a more granular learning signal than outcome-only rewards, and exhibits robustness across different model scales and task types.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of IREASONER represents a development in the field of Large Multimodal Model (LMM) self-improvement, particularly regarding the unsupervised optimization of reasoning processes. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Gap in Unsupervised Reasoning Supervision**: IREASONER addresses a long-standing challenge by providing a mechanism to supervise and optimize intermediate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in LMMs without requiring external labels, human annotations, or auxiliary reward models. Previous self-improvement frameworks for LMMs largely focused on outcome-level rewards, which weakly constrained the internal reasoning trajectory. IREASONER demonstrates that intrinsic, trajectory-aware signals can be derived directly from a model's own samples, offering a path to more nuanced self-correction.

2. **Enhancing Reasoning Robustness and Transferability**: By explicitly rewarding the internal agreement of CoT steps, IREASONER encourages the LMM to develop more consistent and grounded reasoning pathways. This is shown to improve the model's performance on diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, particularly those where the intermediate reasoning structure is critical and can vary despite converging to the same final answer. The ability to distinguish between strong and weak reasoning trajectories, even if they yield the same correct answer, can lead to more robust and generalized reasoning capabilities that transfer better across tasks.

3. **Efficiency and Scalability of LMM Development**: The reliance on abundant unlabeled visual data for self-evolution, combined with the intrinsic nature of the reasoning supervision, makes IREASONER a resource-efficient approach. This reduces the dependency on costly and time-consuming manual annotation or external evaluation processes, which are significant bottlenecks in scaling LMMs. This efficiency supports the potential for continuous, large-scale post-training and adaptation of LMMs.

4. **Deeper Internal Representation Learning**: The finding that IREASONER-trained models still outperform baselines even when CoT is suppressed at inference suggests that the step-level training leads to an improvement in the model's underlying internal representations and implicit reasoning abilities, beyond just generating explicit CoT outputs. This indicates a more profound impact on the model's cognitive architecture.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**: IREASONER provides a starting point for several promising research directions. Future work could explore more sophisticated methods for intrinsic reasoning supervision, incorporate ensemble disagreement for increased robustness against confidently incorrect dominant answers, investigate longer training runs with larger and more diverse unlabeled data streams, and evaluate its applicability across an even broader range of model families. The framework's reliance on access to model internals (log-probabilities and KL regularization) also highlights the importance of open-weight models for advanced self-improvement techniques.

In essence, IREASONER pushes the boundaries of LMM self-improvement by enabling models to not only self-learn correct answers but also to self-refine their internal reasoning processes, all within a practical and unsupervised setting. This contributes to the development of more autonomous and capable general-purpose multimodal AI systems.
