# Process vs. Outcome Reward: Which is Better for Agentic RAG Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ReasonRAG framework from City University of Hong Kong and Huawei's Noah's Ark Lab introduces a process-supervised reinforcement learning approach for agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation, automatically generating fine-grained, step-wise rewards to train Large Language Models. This method achieves an average F1 score of 42.3% across five benchmarks and demonstrates 18x greater data efficiency by requiring only 5,000 queries compared to 90,000 for outcome-based RL.

## Problem

- Outcome-supervised Reinforcement Learning (RL) for agentic RAG suffers from low exploration efficiency and sparse reward signals in complex, multi-step reasoning tasks.
- Gradient conflict in outcome-supervised RL can inadvertently penalize correct intermediate steps when a late-stage error occurs, hindering effective learning.
- The high cost and impracticality of manually annotating high-quality, granular, step-wise reward signals, which are crucial for process-supervised RL.

## Method

- **ReasonRAG**, a ==process-supervised RL== framework, optimizes LLM agents for dynamic RAG by leveraging fine-grained, step-wise rewards instead of sparse final outcome rewards.
- Automatic generation of the RAG-ProGuide dataset using Shortest Path Reward Estimation (SPRE) to assign granular rewards and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for efficient exploration of diverse reasoning trajectories.
- Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is employed to fine-tune the LLM policy on the RAG-ProGuide dataset, directly aligning the model with process-level preference pairs.

## Results

- ReasonRAG consistently outperforms 12 baseline models across five benchmark datasets, achieving an average F1 score of **42.3%** and Exact Match of **34.4%** on complex questions.
- The framework demonstrates exceptional data efficiency, achieving superior performance with only 5,000 queries, representing an **18x** reduction in data requirement compared to the 90,000 queries needed by Search-R1.
- The model shows enhanced multi-hop reasoning capabilities (e.g., **48.9%** F1 on HotpotQA) and robust out-of-domain generalization on datasets like Bamboogle and MuSiQue, indicating highly transferable adaptive reasoning policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Process-supervised RL provides significantly more stable training convergence, reduced computational costs, and enhanced learning efficiency for agentic RAG compared to outcome-supervised methods.
> - Automated data generation techniques like SPRE and MCTS can effectively overcome the practical challenges of creating high-quality, granular process-level reward signals at scale.
> - Fine-grained, step-wise feedback guides LLMs to learn more fundamental and transferable reasoning strategies, leading to improved out-of-domain generalization and robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and Reinforcement Learning (RL), addressing critical limitations in developing autonomous and efficient AI agents.

**Evolution of LLMs and RAG:**
The landscape begins with the rapid advancements of LLMs (e.g., GPT series, PaLM), demonstrating remarkable proficiency in text generation and understanding. However, LLMs inherently suffer from reliance on static training data, leading to issues like factual inaccuracies, outdated information, and "hallucinations." Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) emerged as a powerful solution, equipping LLMs with external, up-to-date knowledge sources to enhance factual accuracy, relevance, and timeliness of responses. Traditional RAG systems, however, are often limited by static, linear workflows, struggling with complex multi-step reasoning, deep contextual integration, and iterative refinement required for sophisticated information-seeking tasks.

**The Rise of Agentic RAG:**
To overcome these limitations, the concept of "agentic RAG" systems has gained prominence. These systems, often inspired by paradigms like DeepResearch, empower LLMs to exhibit agent-like behavior, enabling dynamic retrieval strategies, iterative context refinement, and adaptive workflows. An agentic RAG system allows an LLM to orchestrate a series of actions: dynamically deciding when to retrieve information, formulating search queries, filtering relevant content, and iteratively refining its output. This approach is crucial for handling complex queries that go beyond single-shot retrieval.

**Reinforcement Learning for LLM Agents:**
Reinforcement Learning (RL) has proven highly effective in sequential decision-making tasks and has recently been applied to enhance LLM capabilities, particularly for complex reasoning. Early approaches for agentic RAG relied on prompt engineering or supervised fine-tuning (SFT). While SFT improves reasoning, it often struggles with generalization across domains. The advent of outcome-supervised RL methods (e.g., OpenAI-O1, DeepSeek-R1, Search-R1) marked a significant step forward, optimizing LLMs end-to-end based on the correctness of the final answer. Search-R1, in particular, demonstrated how LLMs could be trained to reason and leverage search engines using outcome-based rewards.

**Addressing the Limitations of Outcome-Supervised RL:**
Despite its promise, outcome-supervised RL faces inherent limitations, especially in multi-step reasoning:
1. **Low Exploration Efficiency:** The model only receives a reward at the very end of a reasoning chain, making it difficult to learn from intermediate errors or correct steps.
2. **Gradient Conflict:** Penalizing an entire sequence for a late-stage error can inadvertently penalize correct early steps, leading to conflicting gradients.
3. **Sparse Rewards:** Infrequent feedback (only at the final outcome) necessitates significantly more training data and steps for convergence.

This paper identifies these challenges as a critical bottleneck for further advancing agentic RAG. It posits that **process-supervised RL**, which provides fine-grained, stepwise rewards throughout the reasoning process, can address these issues by enabling more efficient exploration, reducing gradient conflict, and accelerating learning through denser feedback. While process-supervised RL has been explored for general LLM reasoning, its application to **agentic RAG** specifically, with its unique challenges of dynamic retrieval and iterative refinement, remains largely unexplored. The paper thus fills this crucial gap by developing an automatic, data-efficient framework for process-supervised RL in agentic RAG.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the capabilities of agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems by transitioning from outcome-supervised reinforcement learning (RL) to process-supervised RL**. The authors aim to overcome the inherent limitations of outcome-based reward signals in complex, multi-step reasoning tasks characteristic of agentic RAG.

The specific motivations and objectives can be articulated as follows:

1. **Address Limitations of Outcome-Supervised RL in Agentic RAG:** The current state-of-the-art in RL-driven agentic RAG, exemplified by approaches like Search-R1, relies on outcome-based rewards. This paradigm suffers from:
 - **Low Exploration Efficiency:** Agents struggle to efficiently explore the vast decision space of multi-step reasoning because feedback is only provided at the very end of a possibly long and complex chain of actions.
 - **Gradient Conflict:** An incorrect final answer leads to penalization of the entire sequence, potentially punishing correct intermediate steps and hindering effective learning.
 - **Sparse Reward Signals:** Infrequent feedback makes learning slow and inefficient, requiring large amounts of training data and computational resources to converge.

2. **Improve Training Stability, Reduce Computational Costs, and Enhance Efficiency:** By moving to process-level rewards, the authors aim to provide denser, more informative feedback signals at each intermediate step of the agent's reasoning process. This is hypothesized to lead to:
 - More stable training convergence.
 - Reduced need for extensive training data, thereby lowering computational costs.
 - Faster and more efficient learning for the agentic RAG policy.

3. **Develop an Automatic Framework for Process-Level Reward Annotation:** A major challenge in implementing process-supervised RL is the creation of high-quality, granular, step-wise reward signals. Manual annotation is prohibitively expensive. Therefore, a key objective is to **automatically construct a dataset** that provides process-level rewards for the critical actions in agentic RAG: query generation, evidence extraction, and answer generation. This involves designing:
 - **Effective Process Reward Design:** A reward function that guides the model towards optimal (correct and efficient) reasoning paths, penalizing unnecessarily long or circuitous sequences.
 - **Efficient Exploration and Annotation:** A method to efficiently explore the vast decision space of agentic RAG to identify high-quality intermediate steps without incurring prohibitive manual annotation costs.

4. **Empower LLMs with Autonomous, Dynamic RAG Capabilities:** The ultimate goal is to enable LLMs to autonomously and intelligently orchestrate the entire RAG workflow. This includes making dynamic decisions on when to invoke a search, how to formulate effective search queries, how to extract relevant evidence from retrieved documents, and how to synthesize this evidence into a final answer, all guided by fine-grained process-level policy optimization.

In essence, the research is motivated by the clear advantages of fine-grained supervision for complex sequential tasks, aiming to bring these benefits to the demanding domain of agentic RAG, while simultaneously addressing the practical challenge of generating such supervision automatically and efficiently.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ReasonRAG framework proposes a novel process-supervised reinforcement learning (RL) method to enhance agentic RAG reasoning, primarily by focusing on generating high-quality process-level reward data and using it for policy optimization. The methodology is divided into two main components: Process-Supervised Data Generation and Process-Supervised Preference Optimization, followed by the Agentic RAG Inference workflow.

#### 3.1. Process-Supervised Data Generation

This crucial component aims to automatically create a high-quality dataset, named RAG-ProGuide, containing fine-grained process-level reward signals. This addresses the challenges of designing process rewards and the high cost of manual annotation.

1. **Shortest Path Reward Estimation (SPRE):**
 - **Purpose:** To assign granular reward signals to intermediate steps of the agentic RAG process, balancing final correctness with reasoning efficiency.
 - **Mechanism:** For a partial reasoning sequence `y_1:t` (up to step `t`), SPRE simulates `k` continuations (rollouts) until a final answer is obtained for each. Each `rollout_i` is scored based on the correctness of its final answer (e.g., F1 match to ground truth, `v(rollout_i) ∈`).
 - **Efficiency Penalty:** A decay factor `α ∈ (0, 1]` is introduced, multiplied by `step(rollout_i)` (total steps in the trajectory), to penalize unnecessarily long reasoning paths. This encourages the model to find correct answers with fewer steps. The reward `Q_t` for a partial sequence is an average of these penalized scores across `k` rollouts.

2. **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for Process-level Exploration:**
 - **Purpose:** To efficiently explore the vast decision space of agentic RAG and collect diverse yet meaningful reasoning trajectories, thereby overcoming the challenge of sparse and expensive human annotations.
 - **States and Actions:**
 - **State `s`:** Defined by `(x, y_<i, stage)`, where `x` is the original question, `y_<i` is the sequence of prior reasoning outputs, and `stage` indicates the current point in the agentic flow (`Reasoning`, `Grounding`, `Terminal`).
 - **Actions:** Determined by the `stage`:
 - **Reasoning stage:** Either generate a new query (`<query>...</query>`) for retrieval or directly generate a final answer (`<answer>...</answer>`). If a query is generated, a retrieval operation is performed, and documents are added to the state.
 - **Grounding stage:** Extract relevant evidence spans (`<evidence>...</evidence>`) from retrieved documents. The process then returns to the Reasoning stage.
 - **Terminal stage:** The process ends when a final answer is generated.
 - **MCTS Operations:** MCTS iteratively builds a search tree by performing:
 - **Selection:** Selects promising paths using an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) heuristic, balancing exploration of unvisited nodes and exploitation of high-reward nodes.
 - **Expansion:** From a selected node, the LLM generates a new action (based on its current policy `π_θ(a|s)`), creating a new child node. Instead of full rollouts, an LLM judge evaluates the correctness of this intermediate step against the golden answer to provide an initial reward, making expansion efficient.
 - **Backpropagation:** The SPRE-estimated rewards from simulated outcomes (or LLM judge evaluations) are propagated back up the tree, updating the visit counts `N(s)` and quality estimates `Q(s)` for all nodes along the path.

3. **RAG-ProGuide Dataset Construction:**
 - Using the MCTS framework guided by SPRE, 4,603 questions from PopQA, HotpotQA, and 2WikiMultihopQA are used to generate reasoning trajectories.
 - Branches not leading to a final answer are pruned. For complete trajectories, F1 scores against ground truth are used by SPRE to estimate intermediate node rewards.
 - Post-processing filters out duplicate response sequences and preference pairs with negligible reward differences (<0.01) to ensure data quality.
 - The resulting RAG-ProGuide dataset contains 13,289 high-quality process-level preference pairs, capturing diverse comparative scenarios across query generation, evidence extraction, and answer generation.

#### 3.2. Process-Supervised Preference Optimization

With the RAG-ProGuide dataset, the LLM policy model is fine-tuned using **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**.
- **DPO Objective:** The model `p_θ` is optimized to increase the probability of preferred responses (`y_w_t`) over dispreferred ones (`y_l_t`), given the context (`x`, `y_<t`), directly without training a separate reward model. The `β` hyperparameter controls the KL constraint, balancing alignment with the reference model and preference alignment.

#### 3.3. Agentic RAG Inference Workflow

During inference, ReasonRAG employs an adaptive and iterative workflow, allowing the LLM to autonomously interact with an external search engine.
- The process cycles through three recursive decision states: `Reasoning`, `Grounding`, and `Terminal`.
- In the `Reasoning` state, the LLM decides if it has enough information to answer. If yes, it generates `<answer>...</answer>` and terminates. If not, it generates a query `<query>...</query>`.
- Generating a query transitions the system to the `Grounding` state, where documents are retrieved, and the model extracts relevant evidence (`<evidence>...</evidence>`).
- Extracted evidence is appended to the context, and the process loops back to the `Reasoning` state for further deliberation. Explicit placeholders enhance interpretability and programmatic control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on five benchmark datasets (PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, Bamboogle, MuSiQue) demonstrate the superior performance and efficiency of ReasonRAG. Key findings include:

1. **Superior Performance Across Benchmarks:**
 - ReasonRAG consistently outperforms 12 baseline models, including zero-shot, active, adaptive, RAG-CoT, summary, and existing reasoning-based methods (e.g., Search-o1, AutoRAG, Search-R1).
 - On average, ReasonRAG achieves higher Exact Match (EM) and F1 scores (34.4% EM, 42.3% F1) compared to the best previous RL-based method, Search-R1 (32.8% EM, 40.7% F1).

2. **Exceptional Data Efficiency:**
 - A standout finding is that ReasonRAG, trained on only **5,000 queries** (comprising 13,000 process-level steps), surpasses Search-R1, which requires **90,000 queries** (approximately 270,000 intermediate steps). This stark contrast highlights the significant data efficiency gained by process-supervised RL, demonstrating that high-quality, fine-grained rewards are more impactful than sheer volume of outcome-based feedback.

3. **Enhanced Multi-hop Reasoning Capabilities:**
 - ReasonRAG shows substantial performance improvements on multi-hop reasoning tasks, which are inherently more complex. For instance, on the HotpotQA dataset, it achieves an F1 score of 48.9%, outperforming AutoRAG (43.7% F1) and Search-R1 (47.0% F1). This indicates its strength in handling questions requiring the integration of information from multiple sources through iterative steps.

4. **Strong Out-of-Domain Generalization:**
 - The model exhibits robust generalization to out-of-domain datasets like Bamboogle and MuSiQue, consistently achieving higher F1 scores than baselines. This suggests that ReasonRAG's learned adaptive reasoning policies are more transferable and less prone to overfitting specific training distributions.

5. **Superior Training Efficiency and Faster Convergence:**
 - Comparative analysis of training cost (GPU hours vs. EM scores) reveals that ReasonRAG converges faster and achieves higher performance levels with significantly fewer computational resources than Search-R1. This efficiency is particularly pronounced on complex multi-hop tasks.

6. **Effectiveness of Process-Level Reinforcement Learning (PRL):**
 - Ablation studies comparing different optimization strategies reinforce the benefits of PRL. ReasonRAG (RL-PRL) consistently outperforms variants trained with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and outcome-level RL (RL-ORL).
 - RL-ORL requires substantially more training data (e.g., RL-ORL-10k performs better than RL-ORL-5k) to approach PRL's performance, further underscoring PRL's data efficiency.
 - SFT-trained models show overfitting, performing poorly on multi-hop tasks and generalizing poorly, demonstrating the limitations of simply memorizing preferred paths without active learning for dynamic decision-making.

7. **Impact of Search on Performance:**
 - **Retrieval Steps:** Performance generally improves with more retrieval iterations before saturating, indicating ReasonRAG's adaptive nature in determining the necessary inference depth. Single-hop tasks converge faster (2-3 steps) than multi-hop tasks (3-5 steps).
 - **Number of Retrieved Documents (Top-k):** Performance is sensitive to `k`. While `k=1` is limiting, increasing `k` to 3 yields significant improvements. Further increasing `k` to 5 benefits more complex multi-hop tasks (like 2WikiMultihopQA) by providing richer context, but the gains might plateau for simpler tasks.

Overall, the findings conclusively demonstrate that process-supervised RL, when properly implemented with efficient data generation mechanisms like SPRE and MCTS, offers a more effective and resource-efficient approach to developing highly capable agentic RAG systems compared to traditional outcome-supervised methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Process vs. Outcome Reward: Which is Better for Agentic RAG Reinforcement Learning" holds significant implications for the development of advanced Large Language Model (LLM) agents and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems.

1. **Paradigm Shift in Agentic RAG Training:** The most significant impact is the strong empirical validation of process-supervised RL as a superior paradigm for training agentic RAG systems compared to the prevalent outcome-supervised RL. By demonstrating that fine-grained, step-wise feedback leads to more robust and efficient learning, this work advocates for a fundamental shift in how complex, multi-step LLM behaviors are optimized. This could influence future research and development efforts in designing training methodologies for various LLM agents.

2. **Enhanced LLM Autonomy and Reasoning Capabilities:** ReasonRAG empowers LLMs to act as truly autonomous agents, capable of dynamic decision-making in complex information-seeking tasks. The ability to autonomously generate queries, extract precise evidence, and synthesize answers through iterative reasoning steps significantly extends the capabilities of LLMs beyond mere text generation or single-shot retrieval. This leads to more reliable, accurate, and contextually rich responses, particularly for challenging multi-hop questions.

3. **Dramatic Improvement in Data and Computational Efficiency:** The finding that ReasonRAG achieves superior performance with significantly less training data (5k queries vs. 90k for Search-R1) and fewer GPU hours is transformative. This addresses a critical challenge in LLM research: the escalating costs and resource demands of training large models. By making advanced agentic RAG more data-efficient, the research lowers the barrier to entry, allowing a broader range of researchers and organizations to develop and fine-tune sophisticated AI agents. This also contributes to more sustainable AI development.

4. **Automatic Generation of High-Quality Supervision Data:** The introduction of SPRE and MCTS for *automatically* constructing the RAG-ProGuide dataset is a major methodological contribution. This innovation overcomes the prohibitive cost and scalability issues associated with manual annotation for process-level rewards. The ability to automatically generate dense, high-quality feedback signals opens new avenues for training complex sequential decision-making models without relying on extensive human effort.

5. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** ReasonRAG's strong performance on out-of-domain datasets suggests enhanced generalization capabilities. This implies that the model learns more fundamental and transferable reasoning strategies rather than merely memorizing patterns from training data. Such robustness is crucial for real-world applications where AI agents encounter diverse and unforeseen scenarios.

6. **Broader Applications and Future Research Directions:**
 - **Intelligent Assistants:** The framework can be leveraged to build more capable and interactive intelligent assistants that can perform complex research tasks, answer intricate questions, and provide detailed explanations by dynamically interacting with knowledge bases.
 - **Scientific Discovery:** Agentic RAG systems like ReasonRAG could significantly accelerate scientific research by autonomously navigating vast scientific literature, formulating hypotheses, and synthesizing findings.
 - **Personalization:** The dynamic and adaptive nature of ReasonRAG could be integrated into personalized information retrieval or recommendation systems, tailoring responses and search strategies to individual user needs and preferences (as hinted by some authors' prior work).
 - **Foundation for Complex AI Agents:** This work lays a strong foundation for developing more general-purpose AI agents capable of long-horizon planning and execution in various domains where step-by-step reasoning and interaction with external tools are necessary.
 - **Research into Reward Design:** The success of SPRE encourages further research into sophisticated, automated reward function design for various LLM-driven tasks, exploring different metrics for efficiency and correctness.

In conclusion, ReasonRAG represents a significant leap forward in the field of agentic RAG, demonstrating that prioritizing process-level supervision through innovative automated data generation and optimization techniques yields substantial improvements in performance, efficiency, and generalization. This research not only advances the capabilities of current LLM agents but also provides a crucial blueprint for future directions in training more intelligent and autonomous AI systems.
