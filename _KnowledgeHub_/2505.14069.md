# Process vs. Outcome Reward: Which is Better for Agentic RAG Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ReasonRAG framework from City University of Hong Kong and Huawei's Noah's Ark Lab introduces a process-supervised reinforcement learning approach for agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation, automatically generating fine-grained, step-wise rewards to train Large Language Models. This method achieves an average F1 score of 42.3% across five benchmarks and demonstrates 18x greater data efficiency by requiring only 5,000 queries compared to 90,000 for outcome-based RL.

## Problem

- Outcome-supervised Reinforcement Learning (RL) for agentic RAG suffers from low exploration efficiency and sparse reward signals in complex, multi-step reasoning tasks.
- Gradient conflict in outcome-supervised RL can inadvertently penalize correct intermediate steps when a late-stage error occurs, hindering effective learning.
- The high cost and impracticality of manually annotating high-quality, granular, step-wise reward signals, which are crucial for process-supervised RL.

## Method

- **ReasonRAG**, a ==process-supervised RL== framework, optimizes LLM agents for dynamic RAG by leveraging fine-grained, step-wise rewards instead of sparse final outcome rewards.
- Automatic generation of the RAG-ProGuide dataset using Shortest Path Reward Estimation (SPRE) to assign granular rewards and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for efficient exploration of diverse reasoning trajectories.
- Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is employed to fine-tune the LLM policy on the RAG-ProGuide dataset, directly aligning the model with process-level preference pairs.

## Results

- ReasonRAG consistently outperforms 12 baseline models across five benchmark datasets, achieving an average F1 score of **42.3%** and Exact Match of **34.4%** on complex questions.
- The framework demonstrates exceptional data efficiency, achieving superior performance with only 5,000 queries, representing an **18x** reduction in data requirement compared to the 90,000 queries needed by Search-R1.
- The model shows enhanced multi-hop reasoning capabilities (e.g., **48.9%** F1 on HotpotQA) and robust out-of-domain generalization on datasets like Bamboogle and MuSiQue, indicating highly transferable adaptive reasoning policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Process-supervised RL provides significantly more stable training convergence, reduced computational costs, and enhanced learning efficiency for agentic RAG compared to outcome-supervised methods.
> - Automated data generation techniques like SPRE and MCTS can effectively overcome the practical challenges of creating high-quality, granular process-level reward signals at scale.
> - Fine-grained, step-wise feedback guides LLMs to learn more fundamental and transferable reasoning strategies, leading to improved out-of-domain generalization and robustness.
