# A Vision-Language-Action-Critic Model for Robotic Real-World Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Lab developed the Vision-Language-Action-Critic (VLAC) model and an associated real-world reinforcement learning framework to enable robots to autonomously learn complex manipulation tasks. The system provides generalizable dense rewards and demonstrates robust performance across varied environments, achieving up to 98% success rates with improved sample efficiency.

## Problem

- Real-world robotic reinforcement learning is hindered by sparse and handcrafted reward functions, requiring extensive task-specific engineering.
- Imitation learning for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models demands vast, costly expert demonstrations that often lack diversity and struggle with cross-scene generalization.
- Real-world data collection and exploration for robots are inefficient, expensive, and time-consuming, slowing down policy learning.

## Method

- Introduces **VLAC**, built on ==InternVL==, which unifies action generation (actor) and task progress prediction/done signals (critic) into a single ==autoregressive architecture==.
- Develops a **VLAC** critic component that generates generalizable ==dense rewards== by predicting pairwise progress and task completion from image observations and language goals, trained with specific data construction strategies.
- Integrates **VLAC** into an asynchronous real-world reinforcement learning framework using ==PPO==, enhanced with graded ==human-in-the-loop (HiL)== interventions and multi-robot scaling capabilities.

## Results

- The VLAC critic achieved robust generalization, reaching **0.95** VOC-F1 on out-of-distribution RT1 data with one-shot in-context learning, and effectively distinguished successful from failed trajectories (**0.89** vs **0.44** VOC-F1 on RoboFAC).
- The VLAC actor demonstrated strong initial capabilities, achieving an average **75%** success rate and **82.5%** task progress across five real-world manipulation tasks, and maintained **57-63%** success under extreme lighting and scene shifts.
- The integrated real-world RL framework improved robot success rates from approximately **30%** to **~90%** within **200** episodes; HiL interventions further boosted average success rates to **93-98%** and improved exploration efficiency by **50%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified Vision-Language-Action-Critic architecture can simultaneously provide dense, fine-grained intrinsic rewards and generate actions, simplifying the real-world RL system design and improving learning.
> - Pretraining a critic model on pair-wise progress understanding from diverse multimodal and robotic datasets enables robust, generalizable intrinsic reward generation for novel tasks and environments without manual engineering.
> - Strategic human-in-the-loop interventions, particularly targeted demonstrations and replay, significantly accelerate real-world robot learning and improve sample efficiency by stabilizing exploration and policy improvement.
