# Q-learning with Posterior Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> PSQL introduces a model-free Q-learning algorithm that directly applies Gaussian posterior sampling to Q-values for exploration in tabular episodic MDPs. This approach establishes a near-optimal ~O(H^2 * sqrt(SAT)) cumulative regret bound and provides a novel Bayesian interpretation for the Q-learning update rule, including the (H+1)/(H+n) learning rate.

## Problem

- Effectively managing the exploration-exploitation trade-off in online tabular episodic Markov Decision Processes (MDPs).
- Lack of robust theoretical guarantees for model-free Q-learning algorithms that directly integrate Bayesian posterior sampling for exploration.
- Difficulty in analytically demonstrating near-optimal regret bounds for posterior sampling in complex RL settings due to issues like error accumulation in bootstrapped estimates.

## Method

- Designed Q-Learning with ==Posterior Sampling== (**PSQL**), a model-free algorithm that maintains Gaussian posterior distributions over Q-values for direct exploration.
- Introduced a novel Bayesian derivation of the Q-learning update rule, explaining the specific (H+1)/(H+n) learning rate through a regularized Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective.
- Employed a strategic target construction method for Q-value updates that incorporates controlled optimism using multiple posterior samples for the value function of the next state.

## Results

- Achieved a cumulative regret bound of ~O(H^2 * sqrt(SAT)) for PSQL in tabular episodic MDPs, closely matching the theoretical lower bound.
- The regret bound improves upon prior state-of-the-art methods like RLSVI in terms of state space dependence and matches Staged-RandQL with a simpler algorithmic structure.
- Preliminary empirical evaluation demonstrates that a vanilla variant of PSQL (PSQL*) often shows superior performance compared to UCBQL, RLSVI, and Staged-RandQL in chain and grid-world MDPs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Q-learning update rule, particularly its specific learning rate, can be naturally derived from a regularized Bayesian inference perspective, providing a deeper understanding of its mechanics.
> - Combining posterior sampling with bootstrapped Temporal Difference (TD) learning requires carefully engineering high-probability optimism in the value function target rather than directly in action selection.
> - Novel analytical techniques are needed to manage error accumulation in bootstrapped estimates and ensure optimism is maintained across multiple steps in an episode.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of online Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically addressing the challenge of exploration-exploitation in tabular episodic Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). In this setting, an agent interacts sequentially with an unknown environment, aiming to learn an optimal strategy while minimizing regret against an optimal policy.

Q-learning, a classic dynamic programming (DP)-based algorithm, is a foundational model-free approach that utilizes Temporal Difference (TD) learning to estimate Q-values. While Q-learning and other model-free methods are widely adopted in practice, particularly underpinning modern deep RL algorithms (e.g., DQN, A3C), their theoretical analysis regarding exploration has been a significant area of research.

Two primary strategies for managing the exploration-exploitation dilemma are optimistic approaches, often based on Upper Confidence Bounds (UCB), and Bayesian posterior sampling (also known as Thompson Sampling). Posterior sampling techniques have demonstrated superior empirical performance across various settings, including multi-armed bandits and deep reinforcement learning. However, deriving robust theoretical guarantees for these methods, especially in complex RL environments like MDPs, has historically been more challenging than for UCB-based approaches.

Previous work has established near-optimal regret bounds for UCB-based Q-learning algorithms, such as UCBQL by Jin et al. (2018) and subsequent improvements like Q-EarlySettled-Advantage by Li et al. (2021a). For posterior sampling-based methods in RL, provable guarantees have largely been concentrated on model-based algorithms that estimate transition probabilities and rewards directly, like PSRL. While Randomized Least Squares Value Iteration (RLSVI) by Osband et al. (2016b) and Russo (2019) can be interpreted as maintaining approximate posteriors over value functions and exhibits strong empirical performance, it often reduces to a model-based algorithm in the tabular setting and has faced challenges in achieving optimal regret bounds with respect to state space size. More recently, Staged-RandQL by Tiapkin et al. (2023) offered the first tractable Q-learning based algorithm with regret bounds, but it models Dirichlet posteriors on transition probabilities and implements sampling via learning rate randomization, representing an indirect application of posterior sampling to Q-values.

This paper addresses a gap in the literature by introducing a *simple* Q-learning algorithm that *directly* maintains Gaussian posteriors on Q-values and providing its theoretical regret guarantees. The work aims to provide a clear theoretical foundation for a straightforward and intuitive application of posterior sampling in model-free Q-learning, which is broadly used and empirically effective.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Q-Learning with Posterior Sampling (PSQL), a novel model-free algorithm designed to integrate Bayesian posterior sampling directly into the Q-learning framework. This integration aims to provide an efficient and natural mechanism for managing the exploration-exploitation trade-off in online reinforcement learning.

A significant motivation stems from the observed empirical success of Bayesian posterior sampling techniques in various exploration-exploitation settings. These techniques, including Thompson Sampling, have frequently demonstrated superior performance compared to UCB-based methods in multi-armed bandits and deep reinforcement learning. Preliminary empirical comparisons presented in the paper indicate that PSQL also exhibits promising performance in tabular RL settings when compared to existing UCB-based and randomized approaches. This empirical efficacy highlights a need for theoretical understanding and guarantees for such direct applications of posterior sampling in Q-learning.

A key theoretical objective is to establish provable regret bounds for PSQL in the tabular episodic MDP setting. Historically, the theoretical analysis of posterior sampling algorithms, particularly in complex domains like reinforcement learning, has posed considerable challenges. The authors aim to overcome these difficulties to demonstrate that PSQL achieves a regret bound competitive with known theoretical lower bounds. This objective addresses a theoretical gap, as prior work on posterior sampling with provable guarantees for Q-learning often resorted to more indirect methods, such as inferring posteriors on transition probabilities or using approximate value iteration.

Furthermore, the research is motivated by a desire to offer a more insightful interpretation of the Q-learning update rule. The authors provide a novel derivation of Q-learning from a Bayesian inference perspective, specifically linking it to the maximization of a regularized Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective. This Bayesian interpretation also explains the origin of specific learning rate schedules (e.g., `(H+1)/(H+n)`) that were previously introduced in other Q-learning algorithms primarily for the purpose of theoretical analysis rather than intuitive derivation. This alternative interpretation is intended to provide a deeper understanding of Q-learning dynamics and inform the design of PSQL.

Finally, the authors seek to contribute new technical insights into the core challenges of combining posterior sampling with dynamic programming and TD-learning-based algorithms. These challenges primarily involve managing error accumulation inherent in bootstrapped estimates and ensuring appropriate levels of optimism. By resolving these difficulties, the work intends to lay a foundational analytical framework that can facilitate the study of posterior sampling in more complex and practical RL environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology revolves around the design and analysis of Q-Learning with Posterior Sampling (PSQL), an algorithm that integrates Gaussian posteriors on Q-values for exploration in tabular episodic MDPs.

**Bayesian Interpretation of Q-Learning Update:**
The paper initiates its algorithmic design by providing a novel Bayesian interpretation of the Q-learning update rule. Traditionally, Q-learning updates are viewed as gradient descent steps. The authors demonstrate that if a Gaussian prior `N(bQ_h(s,a), sigma^2/(n-1))` is assumed for the Q-value `Q_h(s,a)` and a Gaussian likelihood `N(Q_h(s,a), sigma^2)` for the target `z`, Bayes' rule leads to a Gaussian posterior whose mean updates according to the Q-learning rule with a learning rate `alpha_n = 1/n`.
Crucially, for theoretical guarantees in RL, a modified learning rate `alpha_n = (H+1)/(H+n)` has been adopted in prior work to account for bias in bootstrapped targets. The paper re-derives this specific learning rate from a "regularized" Bayesian inference problem. By adding an entropy term `lambda_n H(q)` to the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective, with `lambda_n = H/n` and assuming a Gaussian likelihood `N(Q_h(s,a), sigma^2/(H+1))` for `z`, the resulting posterior mean update naturally yields the `(H+1)/(H+n)` learning rate. This derivation forms the conceptual basis for PSQL's update mechanism.

**PSQL Algorithm Design (Algorithm 1 & 2):**
PSQL maintains a Gaussian posterior distribution `N(bQ_h(s,a), sigma(N_h(s,a))^2)` for each state-action pair `(s,a)` at each step `h`, where `bQ_h(s,a)` is the posterior mean and `sigma(N_h(s,a))` is the standard deviation. The variance schedule is defined as `sigma(n)^2 = 64 * (H^3 / (n+1)) * log(KH/delta)`, where `n` is the visit count for `(s,a,h)`.

1. **Initialization:** Q-value estimates are initialized pessimistically at `H` (maximum possible reward) for `h < H+1` and `0` for `h = H+1`. Visit counts `N_h(s,a)` are initialized to zero.
2. **Action Selection (Exploration):** In each episode `k` and step `h`, for the current state `s_h`, the algorithm samples a Q-value `~Q_h(s_h,a)` from the current posterior for *every* action `a`. The agent then chooses the action `a_h` that maximizes these sampled Q-values (`a_h := arg max_a ~Q_h(s_h,a)`). This directly implements the posterior sampling principle.
3. **Target Construction (`ConstructTarget` function):** This step is critical for theoretical tractability and represents a departure from a "vanilla" posterior sampling approach, which would typically use `r_h + max_a' ~Q_{h+1}(s_{h+1},a')`. Instead, PSQL computes a slightly optimistic target `z = r_h + V_{h+1}(s_{h+1})`.
 - To compute `V_{h+1}(s_{h+1})`, the algorithm first identifies a specific action `ba`. This `ba` is chosen as the action that maximizes the posterior mean *plus its standard deviation* for the next state: `ba := arg max_a bQ_{h+1}(s_{h+1},a) + sigma(N_{h+1}(s_{h+1},a))`. This can be interpreted as an optimistic choice of action.
 - `V_{h+1}(s_{h+1})` is then set as the maximum of `J` samples drawn from the Gaussian posterior of `Q_{h+1}(s_{h+1},ba)` (i.e., `V(s') <- max_j ~V^j`, where `~V^j ~ N(bQ(s',ba), sigma(N(s',ba))^2)`). The number of samples `J` is chosen to ensure high-probability optimism.
4. **Q-Value Update:** After observing reward `r_h` and next state `s_{h+1}`, the visit count `n` for `(s_h,a_h,h)` is incremented. The posterior mean `bQ_h(s_h,a_h)` is then updated using the Q-learning rule with the modified learning rate `alpha_n = (H+1)/(H+n)` and the constructed target `z`.

**Regret Analysis Strategy:**
The theoretical analysis aims to bound the total regret over `K` episodes. The main challenges addressed are:
- **Error Accumulation:** Bootstrapping in Q-learning causes errors from later steps (`h+1`) to propagate to earlier steps (`h`), making inductive proofs difficult.
- **Constant Probability Optimism:** Naive posterior sampling typically provides optimism (estimates are upper bounds on true values) with constant probability, which decays exponentially over `H` steps if not managed.
- **Naivety of Multiple Sampling:** Simply taking multiple samples (J > 1) to boost optimism probability can also lead to an `H`-exponential error accumulation.

The paper's proposed solutions for analysis include:
- **Optimism in Target, not Action Selection:** High-probability optimism is specifically engineered for the `V_{h+1}(s')` estimate used in target computation, effectively breaking the recursive multiplicative decay of optimism probability. Action selection, however, remains a single sample from the posterior.
- **Strategic Use of `ba`:** The optimistic action `ba` (mean + std dev maximizing action) is crucial. The analysis demonstrates that the standard deviation of `ba` is often close to that of the actually chosen action `a_h` with constant probability. This property is exploited to relate the optimistic target to the sampled Q-value for the chosen action.
- **Decomposition of Regret:** The regret is decomposed into "optimism error" (`V^*_h - ~Q_{k,h}`) and "estimation error" (`~Q_{k,h} - V^{pi_k}_h`). Bounding these terms involves:
 - Inductively proving `V_{k,h}` (from target computation) is an optimistic estimate of `V^*_h`.
 - Showing that `V_{k,h}(s_{k,h}) - ~Q_{k,h}(s_{k,h},a_{k,h})` is bounded by `~O(sigma(N_{k,h}(s_{k,h},a_{k,h})))`, leveraging the `ba` action and probability boosting arguments.
 - Recursively bounding the posterior mean estimation error and then the cumulative estimation error, tying these bounds to the visit counts and `sigma(N)`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings, encompassing algorithmic design, theoretical guarantees, and empirical observations.

**Algorithmic Contribution:**
The paper introduces Q-Learning with Posterior Sampling (PSQL), a model-free Q-learning algorithm that directly incorporates Gaussian posteriors on Q-values for exploration. This algorithm represents a straightforward implementation of posterior sampling in a Q-learning framework, which is conceptually simpler than prior posterior sampling algorithms for RL that might infer posteriors on transition models or use randomized learning rates.

**Novel Interpretation of Q-Learning:**
A significant theoretical contribution is the re-derivation of the Q-learning update rule from a Bayesian inference perspective. By framing Q-learning as maximizing a regularized Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective that includes an entropy term, the authors provide a principled explanation for the modified learning rate `alpha_n = (H+1)/(H+n)`. This learning rate, previously adopted in other Q-learning algorithms primarily for theoretical tractability, is shown to emerge naturally from a Bayesian framework incorporating additional uncertainty or bias in bootstrapped targets. This offers an intuitive understanding of a key parameter in Q-learning that was previously justified by the mechanics of regret analysis.

**Near-Optimal Regret Bound:**
The primary theoretical result is a regret bound for PSQL in the tabular episodic MDP setting. PSQL is shown to achieve a cumulative regret bound of `Reg(K) <= O(H^2 * sqrt(SAT)) * chi` with high probability (at least 1 - `delta`), where `S` is the number of states, `A` is the number of actions, `T = KH` is the total number of timesteps (`K` episodes, `H` horizon), and `chi = log(JSAT/delta)` represents logarithmic factors.
This bound is demonstrated to closely match the known theoretical lower bound of `Omega(H * sqrt(SAT))`.
- **Comparison to Prior Work:**
 - The `~O(H^2 * sqrt(SAT))` bound for PSQL improves upon the state-of-the-art regret bound for Randomized Least Squares Value Iteration (RLSVI) by Russo (2019), which stands at `~O(H^3 S^1.5 sqrt(AT))`, in terms of its dependence on the state space size `S`.
 - It matches the regret bound achieved by Staged-RandQL by Tiapkin et al. (2023), `~O(H^2 * sqrt(SAT))`, while PSQL is described as being a simpler algorithm with fewer randomized sampling steps.

**Technical Insights in Analysis:**
The proof of the regret bound reveals novel techniques to address the challenges inherent in combining posterior sampling with bootstrapped TD-learning. Specifically, the analysis details how to manage:
- **Error Accumulation:** The recursive nature of Q-learning means errors at later steps propagate.
- **Optimism Decay:** Ensuring that estimates remain optimistic with high probability across multiple decision steps in an episode, without suffering exponential decay of optimism probability.
The proposed solution involves a specific target computation strategy:
- The target calculation (`ConstructTarget` function) uses a subtly optimistic value function estimate `V_{h+1}(s')` derived from `J` samples, rather than a single sample.
- `V_{h+1}(s')` is based on `ba`, an action that maximizes the posterior mean *plus its standard deviation*. This selective optimism and the analysis of the relationship between `ba` and the actually chosen action (`a_h`) are central to overcoming the analytical difficulties, allowing for high-probability optimism without exponential error growth.

**Empirical Performance (Preliminary):**
Preliminary experiments on one-dimensional "chain" MDPs and two-dimensional "grid-world" MDPs demonstrate that a "vanilla" version of PSQL (referred to as PSQL* in experiments, which uses a simpler `r_h + max_a' ~Q_{h+1}(s',a')` target) often shows superior empirical performance compared to UCBQL, RLSVI, and Staged-RandQL. While the theoretically guaranteed PSQL algorithm (with its more complex, optimistic target computation) exhibits a slight performance deterioration compared to PSQL*, it still generally outperforms the benchmark UCBQL algorithm empirically. This observation motivates further research into the theoretical analysis of the vanilla, empirically superior version.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work holds several significant implications and potential impacts across the field of reinforcement learning.

**Advancement in Theoretical Understanding of Model-Free RL:**
The paper provides the first provable regret guarantees for a simple, model-free Q-learning algorithm that directly incorporates Bayesian posterior sampling on Q-values. This is a crucial step towards bridging the gap between the empirical success and theoretical understanding of posterior sampling techniques in a widely used class of RL algorithms. While posterior sampling has shown strong performance in practice, robust theoretical analysis in complex settings like model-free RL has been a persistent challenge. This work provides a foundation for such analysis.

**New Insights into Q-learning Mechanics:**
The novel Bayesian inference-based derivation of the Q-learning update rule, particularly its explanation for the empirically effective `(H+1)/(H+n)` learning rate, offers a fresh perspective on how Q-learning operates. This interpretation moves beyond a purely frequentist or gradient descent view, potentially inspiring new algorithm designs or modifications that are informed by Bayesian principles of uncertainty quantification and regularization.

**Development of Novel Analytical Techniques:**
The methodology introduces specific techniques to address the unique challenges of combining posterior sampling with bootstrapped TD-learning, such as error accumulation and maintaining high-probability optimism across episodes. The strategy of applying optimism selectively (in target computation, not necessarily directly in action selection) and carefully analyzing the "action mismatch" related to the `ba` action (mean + std dev maximizing action) provides new tools for analyzing other posterior sampling-based, bootstrapped RL algorithms. These techniques could be extended to analyze more complex settings, including those with function approximation or deep learning.

**Foundation for Future Research:**
The authors explicitly position this work as a starting point for future research.
- **Analyzing Simpler Variants:** The empirical observation that the "vanilla" PSQL* (which uses a simpler target computation) outperforms the theoretically guaranteed PSQL highlights a future research direction: developing theoretical tools to analyze the simpler, empirically superior versions of posterior sampling-based Q-learning. This would further align theoretical guarantees with practical performance.
- **Tightening Regret Bounds:** The paper notes potential avenues for tightening the `H` dependence in the regret bound, for instance, from `H^2` to `sqrt(H)`. This could involve incorporating techniques from other advanced RL analyses.
- **Generalization to Complex Settings:** The insights derived from tabular MDPs are intended to inform the analysis of posterior sampling in more complex and practical RL environments, such as those with large state/action spaces and function approximation, ultimately contributing to the theoretical robustness of deep reinforcement learning algorithms.

In summary, this research advances the theoretical understanding of exploration in model-free reinforcement learning by providing guarantees for a direct application of posterior sampling in Q-learning, while also offering new analytical techniques and interpretations that can guide future algorithmic development and theoretical investigation.
