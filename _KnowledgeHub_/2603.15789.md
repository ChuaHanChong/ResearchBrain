# Emergent Dexterity via Diverse Resets and Large-Scale Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> OmniReset, a framework developed by researchers including those from the University of Washington and NVIDIA, automates the construction of reinforcement learning problems for dexterous manipulation through diverse simulator resets and large-scale on-policy training. This approach enables the emergence of complex, multi-phase behaviors with minimal human engineering, demonstrating superior robustness in simulation and achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for tasks like peg insertion with an 85.37% success rate.

## Problem

- Current robotic reinforcement learning (RL) methods often lack generality, requiring extensive, task-specific engineering for reward functions, curricula, and demonstrations.
- Standard exploration techniques in parallel simulations frequently suffer from performance saturation, leading to agents getting stuck in local optima despite increased computational resources.
- Developing policies for long-horizon, contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks remains challenging due to their inherent complexity and the demands on exploration.

## Method

- **OmniReset** systematically generates ==diverse initial state distributions== for robot and object configurations, covering various interaction modes (e.g., reaching, near-object, stable grasps, near-goal) to simplify long-horizon exploration.
- The framework employs a single, ==task-agnostic reward function== and fixed algorithmic hyperparameters across all tasks, significantly reducing the need for human engineering and task-specific tuning.
- It leverages massively parallel ==reinforcement learning== (**PPO**) environments and ==Generalized State-Dependent Exploration Noise== (**gSDE**) to enable robust, multi-stage skill acquisition and mitigate ==exploration saturation==.

## Results

- OmniReset achieved consistently high success rates across six challenging dexterous manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming demonstration-based baselines, particularly for 'Hard' variants with diverse initial conditions.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both a large number of parallel environments and a broad diversity of grasp sampling ranges are critical factors for efficient and scalable learning performance.
- A distilled visuomotor policy achieved zero-shot real-world success rates of **85.37%** on Peg Insertion, **56.36%** on Leg Twisting, and **15.38%** on Drawer Insertion, substantially outperforming a diffusion policy baseline trained on 100 demonstrations (**~2%** success aggregated across the three tasks).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematically exposing RL agents to a wide variety of robot-object interactions through diverse simulator resets is highly effective in simplifying long-horizon exploration for complex manipulation tasks.
> - Scaling computation and environment parallelism, when combined with diverse initial state distributions, can effectively overcome exploration saturation and facilitate the emergence of dexterous behaviors without task-specific guidance.
> - Learned policies demonstrate enhanced robustness to perturbations and maintain high success rates across a significantly broader range of initial conditions compared to demonstration-based methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of robot learning, specifically focusing on the application of reinforcement learning (RL) in massively parallel physics simulations for sim-to-real robotic manipulation.

Existing literature demonstrates significant success in applying RL to locomotion and navigation tasks in sim-to-real settings. However, the paper identifies a key challenge: extending these successes to robotic manipulation, particularly for long-horizon, contact-rich tasks. Current methods in manipulation often require extensive, per-task human engineering, including the design of rewards, curricula, and reliance on human demonstrations. This per-task engineering limits the scalability of the paradigm. The authors highlight a contrast with domains such as language modeling, where simply scaling data and compute has led to substantial performance gains with relatively simple algorithms.

The central bottleneck identified in robotic RL for manipulation is the saturation of standard exploration techniques. As parallelism and compute increase, these methods tend to repeatedly sample narrow regions of the state-action space, leading to policies trapped in local minima. While various advanced exploration methods exist, their algorithmic complexity often hinders practical scalability. Consequently, practitioners frequently resort to injecting human intuition through task-specific reward shaping, hand-designed curricula, or demonstrations to reduce the exploration burden. Another complementary strategy involves simplifying the learning problem by using RL only for contact-rich phases, relying on motion planning for other parts, which increases system complexity and may limit adaptive behavior.

This paper challenges the prevailing assumption that dexterous manipulation is inherently too complex to emerge from large-scale RL alone and necessitates extensive scaffolding. Instead, it proposes an alternative by systematically exposing RL agents to a comprehensive set of interactions they might encounter during manipulation. The work positions itself as a method that avoids the need for explicit curricula, demonstrations, or extensive reward shaping, contrasting with existing work that exploits resets for curriculum generation or leverages demonstrations for feasible pathways. It also differentiates from exploration strategies that rely on intrinsic rewards or diversity-driven objectives by demonstrating that large-scale parallelization and a specific resetting scheme can yield emergent dexterity without these advanced exploration incentives.

By aiming to enable comparable scalability to other AI domains, this research seeks to push the boundaries of what can be achieved with large-scale RL in complex robotic manipulation, thereby offering a more generalized and less human-intensive approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and robust framework for on-policy reinforcement learning that can solve a broad class of dexterous manipulation tasks with minimal human engineering effort. The authors aim to overcome the limitations of current approaches, which are characterized by their brittleness, task-specificity, and heavy reliance on extensive per-task engineering.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Addressing Brittleness and Task-Specificity:** Existing RL methods for manipulation often require significant manual effort to design rewards, curricula, and collect demonstrations. This makes them difficult to generalize and scale to a wide variety of tasks. The authors seek a framework that can function with fixed algorithm hyperparameters and minimal domain-specific intervention.
2. **Overcoming Exploration Saturation:** A central bottleneck in robotic RL is that standard exploration techniques often saturate, leading policies to repeatedly sample narrow state-action distributions and become trapped in local minima. This prevents performance from meaningfully scaling with increased compute. The research is motivated by the need to develop a method that allows additional compute to directly translate into broader behavioral coverage and continued performance gains.
3. **Reducing Reliance on Human Engineering:** The paper argues against the common practice of scaffolding RL with human intuition (e.g., through reward shaping, curricula, or demonstrations). The motivation is to demonstrate that, with appropriate system design, such scaffolding can be unnecessary, allowing complex, contact-rich behaviors to emerge organically from large-scale compute and optimization.
4. **Simplifying Long-Horizon Exploration:** The core insight motivating their approach is that long-horizon exploration can be dramatically simplified by systematically exposing the RL algorithm to the diverse set of robot-object interactions fundamental to dexterous manipulation. This systematic exposure, achieved through simulator resets, is hypothesized to enable sparse rewards to propagate smoothly through the state space, facilitating the discovery of multi-stage strategies.
5. **Enabling Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A practical motivation is to develop policies that are sufficiently robust and generalizable in simulation to be transferable to the real world zero-shot, demonstrating robust retrying behavior and achieving non-trivial success rates on challenging physical tasks.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to achieve scalability in robotic manipulation comparable to that seen in other AI fields, by creating a framework that reduces the human engineering burden and effectively leverages large-scale computation to learn complex, emergent dexterous behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named OmniReset, addresses the challenges of dexterous manipulation by generating diverse initial state distributions (resets) for on-policy reinforcement learning, specifically using PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization). The methodology focuses on maximizing coverage of potential robot-object interactions and pathways to the goal, thereby reducing the exploration burden for the RL algorithm.

#### 3.1. Problem Setting and User Inputs

The problem is formalized as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). OmniReset assumes the goal is to manipulate rigid bodies, moving a user-specified target object (s_tar) to a target configuration (G), possibly relative to other objects, within a defined workspace (W). The user input is minimal, requiring only:
1. Identification of the target object (s_tar).
2. A means for sampling goal configurations (G).
3. Selection of an operating region for the robot (W).

#### 3.2. Automatic Generation of Diverse Resets

The core of OmniReset lies in systematically expanding coverage of initial states along two axes:
1. **Pathways to the goal:** Randomizing target object poses.
2. **Robot-object interaction modes:** Randomizing robot arm configurations relative to the object.

The process involves:
- **Grasp Sampling:** Initially, 1000 feasible grasp points on the target object are computed using an existing grasp sampler (Mittal et al., 2023).
- **Goal Offsets:** Feasible offsets for the target object relative to the goal are generated by spawning the object at the goal and applying small random forces to dislodge it, creating a continuum of partially completed states.

Using these pre-computed quantities, OmniReset generates four categories of reset distributions:
1. **Reaching Resets (S_R):** The target object is spawned at diverse poses on the tabletop, and the robot's gripper is spawned at random poses throughout the workspace. This exposes the agent to states where it must approach the target.
2. **Near-Object Resets (S_NO):** The target object is spawned across the tabletop, and the end-effector is reset to one of the pre-computed grasp points with a small random offset. The gripper is randomly set to be open or closed. This covers initial contact situations and non-prehensile interactions.
3. **Stable Grasp Resets (S_G):** The target object is spawned randomly in the air within the workspace, and the gripper is spawned at a feasible grasp point on it. This covers states where prehensile manipulation is already established.
4. **Near-Goal Resets (S_NG):** The target object is spawned at one of the pre-computed offsets from the goal, and the gripper is in contact with it, similar to Near-Object resets. This provides dense coverage for contact-rich behaviors near the goal (e.g., insertion, twisting).

**Practical Implementation:** Resets are sampled during an offline phase, validated (collision checking, stabilization via simulator steps), and cached on-GPU for efficient sampling during online RL training. During training, samples are drawn uniformly from the union of these four validated datasets.

#### 3.3. Algorithmic Decisions for RL Training

OmniReset uses PPO as the base RL algorithm, incorporating several design choices:
- **Task-Agnostic Reward Structure:** A single, fixed reward function is used across all tasks, consisting of a sparse binary success reward (r_success), a dense reward for minimizing target object distance to goal (r_dist), a dense reward for gripper proximity to the target (r_reach), a penalty for large/rapid actions and joint velocities (r_smooth), and a penalty for unsafe states (r_term). No task-specific reward shaping or tuning is performed.
- **Scaling Parallel Environments:** A large number of parallel environments (large PPO batch size) is utilized to stabilize and accelerate learning. This prevents catastrophic forgetting and aids value propagation from sparse rewards by ensuring diverse experiences.
- **Asymmetric Actor-Critic:** The actor observes a compact state representation (robot state history, object poses, previous actions), while the critic has access to additional privileged environment parameters (e.g., object masses, friction coefficients). This enhances training stability, especially with larger observation spaces.
- **Generalized State-Dependent Exploration Noise (gSDE):** This approach allows the policy to learn different, temporally-correlated exploration strategies in various regions of the state-space, which is beneficial for heterogeneous, multi-stage tasks.

#### 3.4. Distillation and Real-World Transfer

The pipeline for sim-to-real transfer involves:
1. Training a state-based RL expert policy using OmniReset.
2. Collecting a large-scale dataset (80,000 trajectories) of expert rollouts with synchronized images and actions, using the photorealistic rendering capabilities of IsaacLab.
3. Training a visuomotor student policy via student-teacher distillation using this dataset. The student uses an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 encoder and a Gaussian MLP head, operating at 10Hz.
4. Deploying the RGB-based policy zero-shot to a real UR7e robot with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper.

To mitigate the sim-to-real gap, extensive randomizations are applied:
- **Visual Randomization:** Varying lighting, backgrounds, object and robot appearance, workspace textures, and camera extrinsics (pose and FOV jitter), along with standard image augmentations.
- **Dynamics Randomization:** Calibrating robot kinematics (using factory-provided URDF), performing system identification for actuator parameters (friction, armature, delay) following PACE, and randomizing controller gains, object mass, and friction during RL training.
- **Action Space Curriculum:** A curriculum that progressively reduces the action space during RL training to encourage smaller, smoother motions, specifically for sim-to-real transfer stability (not for behavior generation in simulation).
- **Controller Design:** Utilizing a torque-level operational space controller in both simulation and reality, tuned empirically.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding OmniReset's performance in simulation and its ability to transfer policies to the real world.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

OmniReset was evaluated on diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks, including Leg Twisting, Drawer Insertion, Peg Insertion, Cube Stacking, Wall Slide, Cupcake Placement, and a complex Four-Leg Table Assembly task.

- **Asymptotic Performance and Sample Complexity (Q1):** OmniReset consistently achieved high success rates across all tasks, notably outperforming baselines (BC-PPO, DeepMimic, Demo Curriculum) on "Hard" variants with wide initial condition distributions. Baselines struggled to make meaningful progress on these complex scenarios, showing moderate success only when starting near the goal, but failing on the full long-horizon task from reaching positions (Figure 5, Figure 12).
- **Scalability with Compute:** OmniReset demonstrated that increasing the number of parallel environments directly translates to improved learning performance and stability, particularly for multi-stage tasks (Figure 8). This indicates effective scaling with compute.
- **Emergent Curricula:** The framework enabled PPO to naturally learn tasks by first mastering near-goal states and then progressively solving from broader search spaces, without any explicit curriculum design by humans.
- **Policy Robustness:** OmniReset policies exhibited high robustness to perturbations of initial conditions, maintaining performance under significant disturbances where baseline policies rapidly degraded (Figure 6). Scatter plots further illustrated that OmniReset policies succeeded from a much broader range of initial conditions compared to baselines (Figure 7).

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Q2)

Ablations on the Leg Twisting Hard task investigated key design decisions:

- **Number of Parallel Environments:** A strong correlation was found between the number of parallel environments and training performance. A larger number of environments (e.g., 65536) was essential for scaling to the complexity of the full multi-stage task, while smaller numbers limited progress to simpler, near-goal stages (Figure 8).
- **Grasp Sampling Range:** Increasing the diversity of grasp sampling (broad vs. narrow range) substantially improved sample efficiency and led to higher success rates (Figure 9). This highlights the importance of dense coverage of robot-object interaction modes.

#### 4.3. Distillation and Real-World Transfer (Q3)

OmniReset policies, after being trained as state-based experts, were distilled into visuomotor policies for zero-shot real-world deployment on a UR7e robot.

- **Real-World Success Rates:** The distilled RGB policies achieved significant zero-shot real-world success rates: 85.37% on Peg Insertion, 56.36% on Leg Twisting, and 15.38% on Drawer Insertion (Table 1).
- **Baseline Comparison:** These real-world results substantially outperformed a behavior cloning Diffusion Policy baseline, which achieved approximately 2% success across tasks when trained on 100 real-world demonstrations.
- **Qualitative Behavior:** The visuomotor policies exhibited robust retrying behavior, recovering from initial failures to complete tasks.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** While real-world success was notable, a performance gap was observed between the state-based RL expert in simulation (e.g., 94.97% for Peg) and the distilled image policy in simulation (55.45% for Peg) and reality. This suggests room for improvement in distillation and transfer techniques.
- **Impact of Randomizations:** Extensive domain and dynamics randomizations, along with precise kinematic and dynamic modeling (including system identification), were crucial for achieving robust transfer.

Overall, the findings indicate that OmniReset effectively addresses the exploration challenge in complex manipulation, enabling scalable RL and robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with minimal human-specific engineering.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, OmniReset, introduces a framework that demonstrates a significant advancement in robotic reinforcement learning, particularly for dexterous manipulation tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Reduced Engineering Overhead:** A primary contribution is the demonstration that complex dexterous behaviors can emerge from large-scale RL with minimal task-specific engineering. By using fixed hyperparameters, no curricula, minimal reward engineering, and no human demonstrations, OmniReset substantially lowers the barrier to applying RL to new manipulation tasks, reducing the time and expert effort traditionally required. This shifts the paradigm from human-intensive design to data and compute-driven learning.
- **Enhanced Scalability and Robustness:** The research successfully addresses the exploration bottleneck by systematically exposing the RL algorithm to a diverse set of robot-object interactions through programmatically generated resets. This approach allows performance to scale directly with increased computational resources, unlike prior methods that saturate. The resulting policies exhibit high robustness to initial condition variability and external perturbations, indicating improved generalization capabilities—a critical factor for real-world deployment.
- **Effective Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** OmniReset facilitates the training of robust visuomotor policies that can be transferred to the real world zero-shot, outperforming demonstration-based baselines. This capability is crucial for accelerating the deployment of learned behaviors on physical robots without requiring extensive real-world data collection, which is often costly and time-consuming. The framework provides a scalable foundation for sim-to-real policies handling broad initial condition distributions.
- **Challenging Prevailing Assumptions:** By achieving emergent dexterity without explicit human scaffolding, OmniReset challenges the long-held assumption in robotics that complex manipulation inherently requires significant human intervention in curriculum design or behavior specification. This could encourage a re-evaluation of current research directions and a greater focus on generalized, scalable learning architectures.
- **New Avenues for Future Research:** The paper identifies several limitations that open promising directions for future work. These include:
 - Improving grasp sampling for complex, non-convex objects and extending it to bimanual or dexterous hand manipulation.
 - Exploring more aggressive dynamics randomization to achieve greater robustness across diverse operating conditions.
 - Bridging the remaining simulation-to-real gap for visuomotor policies, potentially through advanced distillation techniques (e.g., DAgger, direct RL from images) and a deeper understanding of scaling laws for visual data.
 - Further refining contact modeling and control mechanisms to handle fine contact interactions more reliably in the real world.
 - Investigating the integration of demonstrations, when available, to potentially further accelerate learning within the OmniReset framework.

In conclusion, OmniReset presents a robust and scalable method for robotic dexterity, with significant implications for how complex manipulation skills can be acquired and deployed. Its ability to learn emergent, generalizable behaviors with minimal human input contributes to the long-term vision of autonomous robots capable of solving a wide range of real-world tasks.
