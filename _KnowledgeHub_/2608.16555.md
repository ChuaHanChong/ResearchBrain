# Co-design of Neural and Muscle Network based on Embodied Perceptron Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> Embodied Perceptron reframes a robot's physical body as a neural network: mechanical Jacobians (muscle-joint routing) act as weight matrices, nonlinear muscle stiffness acts as ReLU2 activations. Co-optimizing muscle configuration (R), muscle properties (K), and a tiny control policy via CMA-ES lets a musculoskeletal walking robot learn stable gaits with just a single-neuron controller.

## Problem

- Embodied robotics shows well-designed bodies can offload control and computation, but body design still relies heavily on expert intuition rather than theory.
- No unified, theory-driven framework treats controller and body as equally optimizable components within one formal representation.
- Existing hardware-as-policy approaches (auto-differentiable body graphs) are restricted to specific computational-graph formulations, limiting generality.

## Method

- **Embodied Perceptron** representation: expresses muscle length as a ==linear Jacobian mapping== from joint angles (weights = pulley/moment-arm geometry) and torque as a linear mapping back through muscle force.
- ==ReLU2 activation== modeling: uses squared-ReLU to represent passive muscle nonlinearity (slack region, nonlinear stiffness) as the network's built-in activation function.
- Joint ==CMA-ES co-optimization==: jointly searches muscle configuration matrix R, stiffness matrix K, and a minimal neural control policy under a reward penalizing energy use and negative/large weights.

## Results

- A single-neuron hidden-layer controller achieves stable learning and reward comparable to networks up to **32 neurons**, averaged over ten runs, on a wheeled-legged walking task.
- Conventional two-layer feedforward controllers needed more than **16 neurons** to match performance the embodied architecture reached with **1 to 4 neurons**.
- Success criterion of exceeding **15m** walking distance within **5000** simulation steps was met, with optimized R and K matrices visualized as physically plausible mono- and bi-articular muscle layouts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical body structure can be formally treated as computation, not just passive support, absorbing representational burden that would otherwise require a larger neural controller.
> - Co-designing morphology and control jointly, rather than fixing the body first, can yield drastic reductions in required model size and parameters.
> - Gradient-free evolutionary optimization (CMA-ES) suits body-controller co-design well since morphology-conditioned reward landscapes are often noisy or discontinuous for gradient methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The present work addresses a recognized disparity in the field of robotics: while artificial intelligence (AI) technologies have enabled sophisticated control policies, many robots continue to utilize simple physical bodies. This often constrains their adaptability to diverse environments. The broader research landscape, particularly embodied robotics and bio-inspired robotics, suggests that a well-designed physical body can assume some computational and control functions, leveraging physical body-environment interactions to simplify overall control and enhance adaptability. However, the design of such bodies has largely relied on expert intuition, lacking a systematic theoretical foundation.

Several challenges have been identified in the pursuit of advanced embodied systems. First, the precise functional role of the body in terms of control and information processing remains to be fully elucidated. Previous efforts in this area include explorations of physical reservoir computing, where the physical body acts as a nonlinear filter whose output can be processed by linear regression, and studies utilizing control theory to understand how body structure contributes to control functions, such as self-stabilization in passive dynamic walkers. Second, a unified, theory-driven framework for the joint optimization of both the controller and the physical body across movement is currently limited. Research in this direction has involved reinforcement learning for tendon-driven underactuated robots and the optimization of multi-articular muscle arrangements based on human motion data. More recent approaches have attempted to model hardware as a policy within reinforcement learning, using auto-differentiable computational graphs to simultaneously optimize mechanical and computational parameters. However, these representations are often restricted to specific computational graph formulations.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by introducing a theoretical framework, the Embodied Perceptron representation, which aims to bridge the gap between neural networks and physical body systems. By proposing a unified representation, the work seeks to clarify the computational capabilities of the body itself and enable the joint, systematic optimization of both the robot's physical structure and its control policy. The approach endeavors to formalize the concept that the body can actively contribute to control and information processing, thereby potentially reducing the computational burden on the neural controller and advancing the understanding and systematic design of embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations observed in current robotics, where advanced AI control policies are often coupled with relatively simple, traditional robot bodies. This discrepancy can restrict a robot's adaptability and performance in complex environments. The authors identify a need for a systematic, theory-driven framework for designing robot bodies that can be jointly optimized with their controllers, moving beyond reliance on expert intuition.

The key objectives of this study are:

1. **To introduce the Embodied Perceptron representation:** This framework aims to provide a unified theoretical description for both general neural networks and physical body systems. The motivation is to formally link mechanical properties and physical interactions with computational functions typically attributed to neural networks.
2. **To model the physical body as a computational resource:** Specifically, the work seeks to represent physical constraints as "weights" and nonlinear physical properties as "activation functions" within a neural network paradigm. This objective is motivated by the hypothesis that the physical body actively participates in computation and control, rather than merely serving as a passive structure.
3. **To theoretically explain how the body can substitute for neural control:** By formalizing the body's computational role, the authors aim to provide an explicit and theoretical understanding of how physical embodiment can alleviate the computational demands on the neural controller. This is motivated by observed phenomena in biological systems and embodied robotics where body dynamics contribute significantly to behavior.
4. **To demonstrate co-optimization of control policy and muscle configuration:** As an application of the Embodied Perceptron framework, a practical objective is to jointly optimize the neural controller and the physical parameters (muscle configuration and properties) of a musculoskeletal robot. The motivation here is to validate the framework's utility in designing more integrated and efficient robot systems.
5. **To show the benefits of embodied intelligence:** Through this co-optimization, the researchers aim to demonstrate that a well-designed musculoskeletal system can provide inherent stability, improve learning efficiency, and significantly reduce the required size and complexity of the neural controller, potentially enabling effective control with minimal neural resources. This objective is motivated by the pursuit of more robust, adaptable, and resource-efficient robotic systems.
6. **To bridge the informational and physical worlds:** Ultimately, the research is motivated by the broader goal of providing a pathway toward understanding and systematically designing embodied AI systems, by formally connecting abstract control policies with concrete physical structures and interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development and application of the "Embodied Perceptron representation," a theoretical framework designed to unify neural networks and physical body systems.

#### 3.1. The Embodied Perceptron Framework

The core idea is to draw an analogy between a musculoskeletal system and a neural network.
- **Forward Propagation Analogy:** A standard feedforward neural network's forward propagation (`z^(l) = W^(l)h^(l-1) + b^(l)`, `h^(l) = ϕ(z^(l))`) serves as the model.
- **Musculoskeletal System as a Neural Network:**
 - **Muscle Lengths:** The extension of muscles (Δℓ) due to joint angles (θ) is approximated linearly: `∆ℓ = Rθ + b`. Here, the Jacobian matrix `R` (mapping joint angles to muscle lengths) is analogous to a weight matrix, and the bias `b` accounts for initial muscle length deviations.
 - **Joint Torques:** The torque (τ) generated at the joints by muscle tension (f) is given by `τ = R^T f`. Muscle tension `f` is composed of passive force (`f_passive`) and active force (`f_active`).
 - **Nonlinear Muscle Properties:** The passive force (`f_passive`) is modeled using a nonlinear activation function, specifically a squared variant of the Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU), denoted as `ReLU^2(x) = max(0, x)^2`. This function is chosen to approximate biological muscle behavior, including nonlinear stiffness and the force-length relationship of passive tissues, particularly in cases where muscles generate no force below a certain extension. The stiffness is introduced via a diagonal matrix `K`.
 - **Unified System Equations:** The entire musculoskeletal system is formulated as a cascaded neural network mapping joint angles to torques:
 `z^(1) = Rθ + b`
 `z^(2) = −ϕ(Kz^(1)) + f_active` (where `ϕ` is `ReLU^2`)
 `z^(3) = R^T z^(2)`
 `τ = z^(3)`
 The active muscle force `f_active` is the output of a separate neural network representing the control policy. This formulation explicitly treats the Jacobian matrix `R` (muscle configuration) as weights and the muscle's nonlinear properties (`ϕ` and `K`) as activation functions and associated parameters.

#### 3.2. Experimental Setup and Procedure

The proposed framework was applied to a simplified musculoskeletal robot model implemented in the MuJoCo physics engine.
- **Robot Design:** A front-wheeled, rear-legged hybrid robot was designed. Each leg was equipped with ten muscles, controlling three joints. The muscle configuration was represented by a `10x3` Jacobian matrix `R`, and muscle properties (elasticity) by a `10x10` diagonal stiffness matrix `K`.
- **Joint Optimization:** The study jointly optimized three aspects:
 1. **Neural Control Policy:** The parameters of a feedforward neural network (with varying hidden layer sizes) that output `f_active`.
 2. **Muscle Configuration:** The elements of the Jacobian matrix `R`.
 3. **Muscle Properties:** The diagonal elements of the stiffness matrix `K`.
 These parameters (`R` and `K`) are collectively referred to as the Embodied Perceptron in the context of optimization.
- **Optimization Algorithm:** The Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) was employed. This algorithm was selected for its robustness to noisy or discontinuous reward landscapes, often encountered in morphology-control co-optimization, and its independence from differentiable objectives.
- **Reward Function:** A composite reward function was designed to encourage efficient walking:
 `r = q_T - (P_E + P_neg + P_L1)`
 - `q_T`: Total walking distance at the final time step `T`.
 - `P_E`: Energy consumption penalty, calculated as the sum of absolute joint power (`|τ_t,i * v_t,i|`).
 - `P_neg`: Penalty for negative elements in `R` and `K`, encouraging non-negative configurations which typically align with physical interpretations (e.g., pulley radii).
 - `P_L1`: L1 regularization on positive elements of `R`, promoting sparsity and controlling the magnitude of pulley radii.
- **Scaling Mechanism:** An equivalence transformation was introduced to scale the optimized `R`, `K`, `b`, and `f_active` parameters if pulley radii became excessively large, ensuring physical feasibility while maintaining functional equivalence.

#### 3.3. Experimental Design

The study conducted three main experiments:
1. **Experiment A: Impact of Neural Controller Size on Gait Pattern:** The number of neurons in the hidden layer of the neural controller was varied (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 neurons) to observe its effect on learning progress and achieved walking distance over 1000 training iterations, averaged over ten runs.
2. **Experiment B: Robot Structure Designed Through Co-optimization:** This experiment detailed the specific optimized `R` and `K` matrices obtained for a case with a single-neuron controller, visualizing the resulting mono-articular and bi-articular muscle configurations and their elasticity values.
3. **Experiment C: Comparison of Learning Performance:** The performance of the proposed Embodied Perceptron architecture (with body co-optimization) was compared against conventional two-layer feedforward neural networks (controller-only, without body co-optimization) across varying neuron counts to evaluate the efficiency benefits of embodied design.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research produced several key findings regarding the efficacy and implications of the Embodied Perceptron representation and the co-optimization approach.

#### 4.1. Impact of the Number of Neurons in the Neural Controller on Gait Pattern (Experiment A)

- **Learning Capability Across Scales:** The robot demonstrated the ability to learn stable walking gait patterns across all tested neural controller sizes, from a single neuron up to 32 neurons in the hidden layer. Reward values consistently increased and stabilized over 1000 training iterations, indicating effective learning.
- **Efficiency with Minimal Neurons:** A notable finding was that controllers with as few as one or two neurons in the hidden layer achieved stable learning and comparable long travel distances to much larger networks. This suggests that the physical body, when co-optimized, significantly contributes to control capabilities, thereby reducing the computational burden on the neural controller.
- **Performance Trends:** While all network sizes could learn, increasing the number of neurons beyond 16 appeared to slightly reduce learning efficiency, indicating that larger networks did not necessarily yield superior performance in this co-optimization context.

#### 4.2. Robot Structure Designed Through Co-optimization (Experiment B)

- **Optimized Physical Parameters:** The study successfully yielded optimized muscle configuration (Jacobian matrix R) and muscle properties (diagonal stiffness matrix K) for a single-neuron controller.
- **Visualization of Embodied Design:** The results included a visualization of the optimized mono-articular and bi-articular muscle configurations, where color intensity represented the elasticity values. This demonstrated that the proposed method is capable of generating specific, adapted physical body designs. For example, the K matrix showed a wide range of optimized stiffness values for different muscles, suggesting a tailored physical response.

#### 4.3. Comparison of Learning Performance Across Neural Network Architectures With and Without Body Design (Experiment C)

- **Advantage of Embodied Design:** A direct comparison highlighted the superior learning efficiency and effectiveness of the Embodied Perceptron architecture.
- **Reduced Neural Resource Demand:** Conventional two-layer feedforward neural networks (controller-only, without body co-optimization) generally required significantly more neurons, often exceeding 16 in total, to achieve walking performance comparable to the co-optimized system. Smaller conventional networks exhibited poor learning efficiency and limited convergence.
- **Minimalist Control with Embodiment:** In contrast, the Embodied Perceptron architecture achieved stable locomotion behaviors with a minimal neural controller of only 1 to 4 neurons. This substantial reduction in neural resource requirements was attributed to the integration of physical body design, which effectively offloaded representational complexity from the neural controller.

Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the co-optimization of neural and physical systems, guided by the Embodied Perceptron framework, can lead to highly efficient and compact robotic control systems. The body's physical properties and structure are shown to act as a significant computational resource, enabling complex behaviors like locomotion with minimal explicit neural control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper carries several significant implications and potential impacts for the fields of robotics, AI, and neuroscience.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Contributions

- **Unified Framework:** The introduction of the Embodied Perceptron representation provides a novel theoretical framework that unifies neural networks and physical body systems. This framework formalizes the long-standing concept of embodied cognition by explicitly modeling physical constraints as neural network weights and physical nonlinearities as activation functions. This creates a direct, computational link between the informational world of control policies and the physical world of robot morphology.
- **Body as a Computational Resource:** The work explicitly and theoretically demonstrates that the physical body is not merely a passive effector but an active computational resource capable of substituting for parts of neural control. This deepens the theoretical understanding of biorobotic symbiosis and the role of morphology in intelligent systems.

#### 5.2. Practical Implications for Robotics

- **Systematic Co-design:** The framework provides a foundation for the systematic co-design of robot controllers and physical bodies. This moves beyond intuition-driven design processes, offering a principled method for joint optimization that can lead to more effective and integrated robot systems.
- **Reduced Controller Complexity:** A key practical impact is the demonstrated ability to achieve complex behaviors, such as stable locomotion, with significantly smaller and simpler neural controllers (e.g., a single-neuron controller). This translates to reduced computational load, lower memory requirements, and potentially faster learning, which is critical for resource-constrained robotic platforms.
- **Improved Learning Efficiency and Inherent Stability:** The co-optimization approach allows for the embedding of physical intelligence within the body design, which can provide inherent stability to the robot and improve the efficiency of the learning process. This can lead to more robust and adaptable robots that require less data and fewer training iterations to acquire new skills.
- **Pathway to Memory- and Data-Efficient Learning:** By alleviating the representational burden on the neural controller, the approach suggests a promising pathway toward developing memory-efficient and data-efficient learning strategies in robotics.

#### 5.3. Broader Scientific Understanding

- **Bio-inspiration and Biological Parallels:** The findings resonate with observations in biological systems, where simple neural circuits, such as spinal reflexes, interact with complex body dynamics to generate sophisticated behaviors like gait patterns. The research provides a computational model that parallels these biological principles, contributing to a deeper understanding of how intelligence emerges from the interplay of brain, body, and environment.
- **Bridging Disciplinary Gaps:** By formally linking mechanics with control and information processing, the Embodied Perceptron bridges traditionally distinct scientific and engineering disciplines, fostering interdisciplinary research in embodied AI.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Directions

While significant, the study acknowledges certain limitations and outlines future work. The current application focuses on a specific locomotion task, and future work aims to extend the framework to more complex goal-directed motions by redefining reward functions and control objectives. Scaling the framework to more intricate robots and integrating diverse physical constraints and environmental factors is also identified as a direction for advancing embodied intelligence. Furthermore, the reliance on penalty-based optimization for body parameters can occasionally lead to infeasible configurations, and the selection of optimal hyperparameters for these penalties remains an open challenge. Overcoming these limitations would further enhance the generality and robustness of the Embodied Perceptron.
