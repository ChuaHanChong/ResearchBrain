# CME-CAD: Heterogeneous Collaborative Multi-Expert Reinforcement Learning for CAD Code Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Fudan University and ByteDance researchers developed CME-CAD, a heterogeneous collaborative multi-expert reinforcement learning framework that automatically generates high-precision, editable CAD models from 2D engineering drawings. The method achieved an 80.71% IoU and 98.25% executability rate on the new CADExpert dataset, outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods by 8.87 percentage points in IoU.

## Problem

- Traditional CAD modeling is complex and requires specialized engineering skills, making automated generation of precise, editable models challenging.
- Existing methods for CAD code generation, particularly those using Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), are often on-policy, limiting exploration of diverse reasoning paths and robustness.
- A significant lack of high-quality, open-source, industrial-grade datasets with detailed trajectory descriptions and complex operations hinders the development of robust CAD automation.

## Method

- **CME-CAD** introduces a Heterogeneous Collaborative ==Multi-Expert== Reinforcement Learning paradigm with two stages: Multi-Expert Fine-Tuning (MEFT) and Multi-Expert Reinforcement Learning (MERL).
- MEFT leverages diverse pre-trained expert models to generate varied Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paths, using a reverse-task approach to produce reliable CoT by reasoning from code to 2D drawings.
- MERL employs a comprehensive reward function, Expert-Internal Advantage Estimation (EIAE), Multi-Expert Collaborative Learning (MECL), and a Hard Negative Sample Buffering (HSB) mechanism to enhance performance and facilitate knowledge transfer.

## Results

- CME-CAD achieved an IoU of **80.71%**, an 8.87 percentage point improvement over the previous state-of-the-art (CAD-RL* at **71.84%**).
- The method demonstrated high code executability at **98.25%**, surpassing CAD-RL* (**97.32%**), indicating reliable and practical CAD code generation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Hard Negative Sample Buffering (HSB) mechanism had the most substantial effect on overall performance, especially IoU, due to the complexity of the CADExpert dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Collaborative learning among heterogeneous experts, guided by unique system prompts, effectively generates diverse and robust reasoning paths for complex CAD code generation.
> - A comprehensive reward function, evaluating format, executability, geometric accuracy (IoU), and work plane consistency, is critical for precise and industrially relevant CAD outputs.
> - The creation of the CADExpert dataset, featuring 17,299 instances of orthographic projections, expert-generated CoT, and executable CADQuery code, addresses the data scarcity and complexity issues in the field.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of Computer-Aided Design (CAD), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and Reinforcement Learning (RL), addressing a critical need in modern industrial design and smart manufacturing.

**Challenges in CAD Automation:** Traditional CAD modeling is complex and heavily reliant on specialized engineering skills. The "digital-first" paradigm in product development necessitates the automated generation of high-precision, editable CAD models from design concepts. Existing methods for 3D model reconstruction from sketches often produce models that are non-editable and lack the precision required for industrial applications. Furthermore, reliance on text or image-based inputs frequently demands extensive, costly manual annotation, hindering scalability and practical applicability in industrial settings. These limitations highlight a significant gap in current automation capabilities.

**Advancements in CAD Code Generation:** The field has seen several efforts to automate CAD code generation using VLMs, such as CAD-MLLM, GenCAD, Img2CAD, CAD2Program, CADCodeVerify, CAD-Llama, and CAD-Coder. These works generally aim to translate visual or textual representations into design instructions or structured CAD code. CAD-RL, a previous work co-authored by some of the current paper's authors, was a pioneer in generating numerically accurate CADQuery code. While these methods have advanced the field, they still face challenges related to robustness, precision, and alignment with industrial workflows.

**Limitations of Existing Methodologies (RLVR):** Many advanced reasoning tasks, including CAD code generation, leverage Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). While effective, RLVR methods are typically "on-policy," meaning they optimize the model primarily along pre-existing, reward-rich reasoning paths. This limits the exploration of novel information and can lead to models being inherently restricted by biases in their initial knowledge base. In complex scenarios, models often fail to generate correct outputs, making the optimization process difficult and stifling the discovery of more diverse, logically consistent reasoning paths. Recent works have shown the benefits of structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, but the on-policy nature of RLVR can still impede true knowledge exploration.

**The Data Bottleneck:** A pervasive issue across the domain is the scarcity of high-quality, open-source datasets that meet industrial-grade requirements. Existing datasets often lack detailed trajectory descriptions, precise dimensional annotations, and examples of complex industrial operations like chamfering, drilling, and Boolean differences. This data deficit significantly constrains the development and rigorous evaluation of robust CAD code generation models.

CME-CAD directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel training paradigm that emphasizes heterogeneous collaborative learning, moving beyond the constraints of on-policy RL. It also tackles the data problem head-on by creating a comprehensive, industrially relevant dataset. By focusing on orthographic projections as input, it aligns more closely with real-world engineering workflows, differentiating itself from approaches relying solely on sketches or less structured textual inputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the CME-CAD research can be summarized as follows:

1. **Automate High-Precision, Editable CAD Model Generation:** The overarching goal is to enable the automatic generation of CAD models that are not only geometrically accurate but also fully editable and compliant with design constraints, thereby reducing reliance on specialized human expertise in industrial design.

2. **Align with Real-World Industrial Workflows:** The authors are motivated to bridge the gap between academic research and industrial practice. This means moving away from less practical inputs like manually annotated text or abstract sketches and instead focusing on orthographic projections with precise dimensional annotations. These 2D engineering drawings are ubiquitous in industrial settings and inherently contain the necessary information for accurate 3D model construction.

3. **Overcome Methodological Limitations of Existing RL Approaches:** A core motivation is to address the shortcomings of on-policy Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The goal is to move beyond mere refinement within existing knowledge bases and encourage active exploration of diverse and novel reasoning paths. This led to the development of a paradigm that enables models to "learn from the strengths of others," fostering more robust and innovative reasoning capabilities.

4. **Enhance Model Robustness and Performance:** The authors aim to improve the model's ability to generate accurate, constraint-compatible outputs, especially in complex reasoning scenarios where initial strategies often fail. This involves ensuring more reliable learning signals throughout the training process.

5. **Address the Data Scarcity Bottleneck:** A significant motivation is to mitigate the lack of high-quality, open-source, industrial-grade datasets. The creation of CADExpert is driven by the need to provide a comprehensive and challenging benchmark for training and evaluating CAD code generation methods, featuring complex operations and precise annotations.

In essence, CME-CAD is driven by the ambition to create a more intelligent, scalable, and practically applicable solution for automating a critical component of industrial design, leveraging a novel multi-expert learning paradigm and a robust new dataset.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CME-CAD introduces a Heterogeneous Collaborative Multi-Expert Reinforcement Learning paradigm, which operates through a sophisticated two-stage training process: Multi-Expert Fine-Tuning (MEFT) and Multi-Expert Reinforcement Learning (MERL). Additionally, the authors present the CADExpert dataset, crucial for the method's training and evaluation.

#### 3.1. Input and Output

The system takes orthographic projections with precise dimension annotations as input. The desired output is executable, high-precision CADQuery code, which can then be used to render 3D CAD models.

#### 3.2. Multi-Expert Fine-Tuning (MEFT)

The first stage focuses on leveraging the unique capabilities of multiple heterogeneous expert models to generate diverse reasoning paths.

- **Heterogeneous Multi-Expert Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Generation:** The process begins by selecting *N* diverse pre-trained expert models, each guided by a unique system prompt (*P*n) to encourage distinct reasoning styles. For each input instruction and 2D drawing (*I*i), each expert generates a detailed Chain-of-Thought (*C*n_i) explaining its reasoning path and the corresponding CADQuery code (*A*n_i). These form expert-specific samples.
- **Reverse-Task Approach for Reliable CoT:** Recognizing that pre-trained models often struggle with direct CADQuery generation from 2D drawings due to data limitations, the authors employ a reverse-task strategy. They provide the model with orthographic projections *and* their corresponding CADQuery code, guiding it to reason *how* to derive the code. This approach aims to produce more reliable and instruction-relevant CoT.
- **Training Process:** The model is fine-tuned to predict the concatenated CoT (*C*n_i) and final answer (*A*n_i), conditioned on the input (*I*i) and the expert's system prompt (*P*n). The objective is to maximize the likelihood of generating coherent reasoning and correct answers, achieved by minimizing a negative log-likelihood loss function across all experts and samples.

#### 3.3. Multi-Expert Reinforcement Learning (MERL)

The second stage focuses on enhancing performance through cross-expert knowledge transfer and robust learning mechanisms.

- **Reward Function Design:** A comprehensive reward system guides the learning process, evaluating model outputs against four key objectives:
 - **Format Reward (R_format):** Ensures the output adheres to a structured format (reasoning before code) using regular expression matching (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect).
 - **Executability Reward (R_exec):** Validates if the generated CADQuery code is syntactically correct and executable in a Python environment (1 for executable, 0 otherwise).
 - **Geometric Accuracy Reward (R_IoU):** Measures the volumetric overlap between the generated and ground-truth 3D models using the Intersection-over-Union (IoU) metric (Jaccard Index).
 - **Work Plane Reward (R_plane):** Addresses coordinate system consistency by quantifying origin deviation (*Dis_ori*) and normal vector deviation (*Dis_vec*). This ensures accurate spatial alignment, which is critical in 3D modeling.
 The total reward (*R*) uses a gating mechanism: R_format and R_exec must be positive for the overall reward to be positive, making them essential conditions. The total reward combines the weighted R_IoU and R_plane components.

- **Expert-Internal Advantage Estimation (EIAE):** The fine-tuned model acts as the current policy. For each input, *G* responses are sampled for each of the *N* experts, each guided by its unique prompt. An absolute reward (*R*n_g) is calculated for each response. Then, a relative advantage (*A*n_g) is computed for each response within an expert's group by subtracting the average reward of that expert's *G* responses from the individual response's absolute reward. This relative advantage is used in a GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization) loss function, which includes a non-negative truncation term `max(A_n_g, 0)` to prevent over-penalization of exploration, especially in complex code generation tasks.

- **Multi-Expert Collaborative Learning (MECL):** To facilitate knowledge exchange, the experts' average absolute rewards are compared to identify the "best expert" (*E+*) and "worst expert" (*E-*). A KL divergence penalty is introduced, forcing less effective experts (*E-*) to learn from the more effective ones (*E+*). This penalty guides the *E-* to generate high-quality responses *while maintaining its unique reasoning style* (enforced by fixed system prompts). This mechanism promotes knowledge transfer without sacrificing diversity. The use of vLLM inference engine for parallel batch sampling and paged attention ensures computational efficiency, with only a marginal increase in training time compared to a single-expert baseline.

- **Hard Negative Sample Buffering Mechanism (HSB):** This mechanism addresses cases where all experts fail on a particular query. The reinforcement learning data is divided into *M* parts. After training on one part, it serves as a test set. Difficult samples (where an expert generates more than *K* incorrect answers out of *G* responses) are stored in a buffer (*B*) with a probability *K/G*. Subsequently, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is performed on the data in buffer *B* using a standard negative log-likelihood loss, ensuring continuous learning from challenging cases and improving model robustness.

#### 3.4. CADExpert Dataset

To overcome the data bottleneck, the authors created CADExpert, an open-source, industrial-grade benchmark dataset comprising 17,299 instances. It addresses issues like lack of trajectory descriptions, missing precise dimensional information, and simplistic operations in existing datasets.

- **Content:** Each instance includes: orthographic projections with precise dimensional annotations, detailed Chain-of-Thought processes generated by various expert models, executable CADQuery code, and rendered 3D CAD models.
- **Data Generation Process:**
 1. **Random Generation of CADQuery Code with Constraints:** CADQuery code for various basic shapes (rectangles, polygons, circles, polylines) on random base planes is generated. These shapes are then subjected to complex operations like extrusion, drilling, chamfering, and difference operations.
 2. **CADQuery Code Filtering:** A two-stage filtering ensures validity and integrity. First, an executable validation step uses a Python interpreter to check for syntax and runtime errors. Second, a team of 10 experts manually reviews a sample of the generated code, visually inspecting 3D models for geometric accuracy and functional alignment, with multi-round validation for complex cases.
 3. **Generating Annotated Images:** The filtered CADQuery code is executed to produce STEP files. FreeCAD API is used to convert these into orthogonal projections and 2D DXF files. The ezdxf library annotates these DXF files with dimension lines, which are then rendered into raster images.
 4. **Expert CoT Process Generation:** Multiple expert models are fed the generated code and corresponding orthogonal projections to produce diverse reasoning paths, enriching the dataset with varied CoT examples.

This comprehensive methodology, combining multi-expert learning with a meticulously constructed dataset and robust reward mechanisms, is designed to generate highly accurate, executable, and industrially relevant CAD code.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CME-CAD demonstrates its significant advancements in CAD code generation, particularly when benchmarked against state-of-the-art methods on the newly introduced CADExpert dataset.

#### 4.1. Benchmarking CAD Code Generation

- **Overall Superiority:** CME-CAD achieves comprehensive superiority across all evaluation metrics, significantly outperforming existing pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and even a fine-tuned baseline (CAD-RL*).
- **IoU (Intersection-over-Union):** CME-CAD attained an IoU of **80.71%**, representing an **8.87 percentage point improvement** over the previous SOTA method (CAD-RL* at 71.84%). This indicates superior geometric accuracy and volumetric overlap with ground-truth models.
- **Chamfer Distance (CD):** The method yielded remarkably low values for both Mean CD (**1.00**) and Med CD (**0.11**), significantly better than CAD-RL* (Mean CD: 1.38, Med CD: 0.36). Lower Chamfer Distance values signify a closer geometric match between generated and ground-truth 3D models.
- **Executability (Exec.):** CME-CAD achieved an impressive code executability rate of **98.25%**, surpassing CAD-RL* (97.32%). This high rate of executable code is crucial for practical industrial applications.
- **VLM Baselines:** Un-tuned SOTA VLMs like Qwen3-VL (37.04% IoU, 54.79% Exec.) and Doubao-1.6 (35.62% IoU, 52.89% Exec.) performed significantly lower, underscoring the complexity of the CADExpert dataset and the necessity of domain-specific fine-tuning and advanced RL techniques.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study for Multi-Expert Learning and Individual Expert Learning

The ablation studies highlighted the effectiveness of the multi-expert approach:
- **Multi-Expert vs. Single-Expert SFT:** Training with multi-expert data (MoE-SFT) significantly improved performance across all metrics for each individual expert model compared to single-expert Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). For instance, MoE-SFT (Expert3) achieved 64.45% IoU and 96.99% executability, substantially higher than Expert3-SFT (40.91% IoU, 85.35% Exec.). This confirms the successful facilitation of collaborative learning and knowledge transfer among models during fine-tuning.
- **Complete Methodology Impact:** Applying the full CME-CAD methodology (including MERL components) led to further substantial improvements, demonstrating that the collaborative learning and reinforcement learning stages effectively push performance beyond the inherent limitations of single experts, even with multi-expert SFT. The best performing expert within "Ours" achieved 80.71% IoU and 98.25% Exec.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Components of CME-CAD

The individual contributions of CME-CAD's components were also evaluated:
- **Expert-Internal Advantage Estimation (EIAE):** Introducing EIAE alone led to a notable improvement in code executability (from 96.99% to 98.28% Exec. when combined with SFT), primarily by guiding the model to select the most suitable expert for each task.
- **Hard Negative Sample Buffering (HSB):** This mechanism had the most substantial effect on overall performance, particularly in IoU (from 73.89% to 78.14% when added to EIAE). This is attributed to the high complexity of the CADExpert dataset; continuously re-training on difficult samples significantly enhances the model’s ability to handle edge cases.
- **Multi-Expert Collaborative Learning (MECL):** When combined with EIAE (from 73.89% to 76.71% IoU) and especially when all three components (EIAE, HSB, MECL) are used together, MECL fully activates the potential of the training framework, enabling effective collaborative learning and leading to the peak performance of 80.71% IoU.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that CME-CAD provides a robust and highly effective solution for CAD code generation, setting a new state-of-the-art by leveraging heterogeneous expert collaboration and meticulous dataset design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CME-CAD research offers significant contributions to both the academic field of artificial intelligence and the industrial domain of computer-aided design, with several potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Academia-Industry Gap in CAD Automation:** By focusing on orthographic projections as input and generating editable CADQuery code, CME-CAD directly addresses a critical, real-world industrial need. This pragmatic approach facilitates the translation of advanced AI research into practical tools that can be integrated into existing engineering workflows, making product development more efficient and accessible.

2. **Advancing Intelligent Design and Smart Manufacturing:** The ability to automatically generate precise, constraint-compatible, and fully editable CAD models from 2D drawings is a crucial step towards realizing fully automated design processes in smart manufacturing. This reduces the heavy reliance on human engineers' specialized skills and years of training, democratizing access to complex CAD modeling and accelerating the "digital-first" product development paradigm.

3. **Enhanced Reliability and Practical Adoption:** Achieving a code executability rate of 98.25% is a major breakthrough for industrial applications. This high level of reliability means engineers can trust the model's outputs, requiring only minimal modifications for the most complex cases. This fosters confidence in AI-generated designs, promoting wider adoption and allowing engineers to focus on higher-level creative and problem-solving tasks rather than repetitive modeling.

4. **Novel Reinforcement Learning Paradigm:** The CME-CAD paradigm itself, with its focus on Heterogeneous Collaborative Multi-Expert Reinforcement Learning, presents a significant methodological advancement. By allowing diverse experts to learn from each other's strengths while preserving their unique reasoning styles, it overcomes key limitations of traditional on-policy RL. This paradigm could inspire new approaches in other complex reasoning tasks, code generation, and even multi-agent learning scenarios beyond CAD.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research through CADExpert:** The introduction of CADExpert as a high-quality, open-source, industrial-grade dataset is a critical enabler for the entire research community. By providing challenging examples with precise dimensional annotations, complex operations (chamfering, drilling, Boolean differences), and expert-generated Chain-of-Thought processes, CADExpert sets a new standard for benchmarking. It will accelerate the development and rigorous evaluation of future CAD code generation methods, pushing the boundaries of what AI can achieve in design automation.

6. **Potential for Broader AI Applications:** The principles of collaborative learning among heterogeneous experts and robust reward-guided optimization, particularly the hard negative sample buffering mechanism, are generalizable. These ideas could be applied to other domains requiring precise, verifiable code generation or complex problem-solving where diverse reasoning paths are beneficial and data sparsity or difficulty is a challenge.

In conclusion, CME-CAD not only delivers a state-of-the-art solution for CAD code generation but also lays a robust foundation for future innovations in intelligent design, offering a blueprint for collaborative AI systems and providing a vital new resource for the research community.
