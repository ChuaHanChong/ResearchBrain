# Implicit Null-space Manifold Generation for Redundant Robotic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework implicitly represents the geometric structure of null-space manifolds for redundant robotic systems, moving from local solution computation to a reusable global spatial model. This approach successfully generated 1D, 2D, and 3D null-space manifolds for 3-DoF and 7-DoF robots with RMSE as low as 1.96 × 10⁻⁵, enabling proximity queries and projections in microseconds after a millisecond-level construction.

## Problem

- Existing methods for redundant robotic systems primarily exploit redundancy locally, discarding the null-space geometric structure after each solution and limiting its reusability across different tasks.
- Traditional local Jacobian-based techniques are query-driven, focusing on computing individual solutions or trajectories without retaining an explicit, reusable representation of the underlying solution space geometry.
- Previous trace-based methods for self-motion manifolds yield instance-specific point sets, failing to produce a functional configuration-space representation that facilitates geometric queries or downstream planning.

## Method

- The approach constructs an ==implicit scalar field== over the configuration space, where its zero-level set is designed to precisely correspond to the solution manifold.
- It employs **Jacobian-guided sampling** strategies (Newton-based or Zigzag) to efficiently generate data points on and near the solution manifold, leveraging local null-space geometry.
- ==Gaussian Process Implicit Surfaces (GPIS)== are used to build a continuous, probabilistic implicit function from the generated samples, defining a distance field that encodes proximity to the manifold.

## Results

- Jacobian-guided sampling methods were **2-3 times** faster than a random IK baseline, with the Newton-based method adopted for its higher accuracy and efficiency at finer sampling resolutions.
- The framework successfully reconstructed 1D, 2D, and 3D null-space manifolds for 3-DoF and 7-DoF robots, demonstrating an RMSE as low as **1.96 × 10⁻⁵** against ground-truth distances for spatially distributed tasks.
- GP-based implicit representation construction was fast (approx. **0.14 ms**) and enabled subsequent online queries for distance and projection onto the manifold at high speeds (approx. **0.04 ms** per evaluation).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shifting to a representation-centric paradigm, where the entire geometric structure of solution manifolds is explicitly modeled, provides reusable knowledge for redundant robots beyond instance-specific solutions.
> - Leveraging the local geometric information from the Jacobian enables efficient Jacobian-guided sampling that captures both local and global manifold structure without exhaustive exploration of the configuration space.
> - Gaussian Process Implicit Surfaces offer a compact, continuous, and probabilistic representation of the solution manifold, naturally inducing a distance field and enabling analytically computable gradients for efficient queries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic systems frequently possess redundant degrees of freedom, meaning multiple configurations can achieve the same task outcome. These solution sets form manifolds within the configuration space. The established paradigm for addressing problems such as inverse kinematics (IK), redundancy resolution, and motion generation in these systems primarily relies on local Jacobian-based techniques. These methods are query-driven, focusing on computing individual solutions or trajectories for specific task instances. While effective for solution computation, a fundamental limitation of these approaches, both in kinematic and dynamic settings, is their inability to retain an explicit representation of the underlying geometry of the solution space. Consequently, the geometric structure of the null-space is typically discarded once a solution is obtained, thereby limiting its reusability across different tasks or subsequent queries.

This work draws inspiration from classical research on self-motion manifolds, which posits that redundancy-induced solution sets constitute smooth manifolds in the configuration space. Earlier efforts, such as those by Burdick, characterized the dimension and topology of these manifolds. More recently, methods involving ODE-based integration in the Jacobian null space have been developed to compute these manifolds as global solution traces. However, these trace-based approaches primarily yield instance-specific point sets and do not produce a reusable configuration-space representation that facilitates geometric queries or downstream planning.

The paper distinguishes itself from several related research areas:
- **Inverse Kinematics and Redundancy Resolution:** While related to IK, this work does not aim to solve IK problems directly. Instead, it focuses on representing the solution space itself, a departure from the query-driven nature of classical and modern IK solvers that compute specific configurations or trajectories.
- **Self-Motion Manifolds and Geometric Characterization:** The work builds on the theoretical foundation of self-motion manifolds but moves beyond purely analytical characterization or instance-specific tracing. It aims to construct a functional representation of these manifolds.
- **Learning-Based Inverse Models:** Unlike approaches that learn inverse mappings to predict configurations directly, which often rely on dense training data and do not explicitly exploit Jacobian-based geometric structure, this work leverages the local geometric information provided by the Jacobian.
- **Implicit Representations and Distance Fields:** This work employs implicit surface representations and Gaussian process implicit surfaces (GPIS) as a framework for encoding geometry, similar to configuration-space distance fields (CDFs) used for motion planning. However, it applies these tools specifically to model solution manifolds induced by task-defining maps, rather than general object shapes or task-relevant geometry.

In essence, this research seeks to bridge the gap between the theoretical understanding of self-motion manifolds and the practical need for reusable, geometrically-aware representations of these solution spaces in robotics. It contrasts with existing methods by shifting focus from merely computing solutions to explicitly modeling the structure from which solutions arise.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work arises from a recognized limitation in current approaches to redundant robotic systems: while these systems can achieve a given task through multiple configurations, existing methods primarily exploit this redundancy locally. They compute specific solutions or trajectories for individual task instances but do not retain a representation of the geometric structure of the entire solution set. This means that the geometric understanding of the null-space, which represents the allowable self-motions of the robot without affecting the task, is discarded after each computation, limiting its reuse and precluding efficient geometric queries.

The paper's key objectives are:

1. **Develop a Representation-Centric Approach:** The core objective is to shift from a query-driven paradigm of computing individual solutions to a representation-centric approach that focuses on modeling the geometric structure of the solution space itself.
2. **Estimate Geometric Structure of Solution Space:** Specifically, the goal is to estimate the geometry of solution manifolds, which are formed by the set of all configurations satisfying a given task. This is motivated by the understanding that these redundancy-induced solution sets form smooth manifolds in the configuration space.
3. **Construct an Implicit Scalar Field:** To achieve this geometric representation, the work aims to construct an implicit scalar field over the configuration space. The zero-level set of this scalar field is designed to correspond precisely to the solution manifold.
4. **Leverage Jacobian-Guided Sampling:** The method is designed to generate samples efficiently in the neighborhood of the solution manifold using a Jacobian-guided exploration strategy. This strategy is intended to capture both the local and global structure of the manifold without exhaustive exploration of the configuration space or the need for explicit distance supervision.
5. **Induce a Continuous Distance Field:** The resulting implicit representation is intended to be defined over the entire configuration space, naturally inducing a continuous distance field. This field would encode the proximity of any given configuration to the solution manifold, enabling proximity queries and facilitating motion generation.
6. **Support General Task-Defining Maps:** While inverse kinematics provides a convenient testbed, a broader objective is to develop a framework applicable to general differentiable task-defining maps, not restricted solely to forward kinematics. This enables reusable representation of solution spaces beyond standard IK problems.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire to overcome the transient nature of null-space information in conventional methods, by providing a persistent, reusable, and geometrically explicit representation of the entire solution space. This representation is intended to facilitate more sophisticated robotic behaviors and planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for generating implicit null-space manifolds for redundant robotic systems consists of two main components: Jacobian-guided sampling to generate data points near the solution manifold, and Gaussian Process (GP)-based implicit modeling to construct a continuous representation from these samples.

**A. Task Definition and Residual**
The method considers tasks defined by a differentiable mapping `p = f(q)`, where `q ∈ R^n` are configuration variables and `p ∈ R^m` are task variables. For a target task value `p*`, the residual is defined as `r(q) := f(q) - p*`. The solution manifold `M(p*)` is then the set of configurations where `r(q) = 0`. The Jacobian `J(q) := ∂f/∂q = ∂r/∂q` is assumed to be available, as it provides the local null-space geometry.

**B. Jacobian-Guided Sampling**
This component generates samples on and near the solution manifold `M(p*)` in the configuration space. It operates by iteratively advancing along the manifold and correcting deviations. Joint angles are wrapped to `[-π, π]` to handle periodicity.

1. **Initial Point Search:** An initial configuration `q_0` satisfying `r(q_0) ≈ 0` is found using a Gauss–Newton update: `Δq = argmin_Δq ||r(q) + J(q)Δq||^2`, iterated until `||r(q)|| ≤ ε`.

2. **Manifold Traversal:** From a current configuration `q_k`, the algorithm generates a new sample `q_{k+1}` by:
 - Advancing along a null-space direction: `˜q_{k+1} = q_k + βv_k`, where `v_k ∈ Null(J(q_k))`. `v_k` is a non-trivial solution to `J(q_k)v_k = 0`.
 - Restoring constraint satisfaction: `q_{k+1} = Π(˜q_{k+1})`, where `Π(·)` is an operator that maps a configuration back to the manifold's neighborhood.
 The parameter `β` controls the sampling step size. This work primarily focuses on one-dimensional manifolds where a single tangent direction is used, and exploration terminates when a previously sampled point is re-encountered within a `β`-neighborhood.

3. **Newton-Based Traversal:** In this variant, `v_k` and the projection `Π(·)` are recomputed at each step. This method tightly follows the manifold, offering stability but potentially at a higher computational cost per step due to frequent updates. The projection `Π(·)` typically involves running the Gauss-Newton update (Eq. 6) until convergence.

4. **Zigzag Traversal:** This strategy aims to reduce projection effort by relaxing strict convergence to the manifold. It uses a minimum-norm linearized correction direction `d(q) = -J(q)^# r(q)` (where `(·)^#` is the pseudo-inverse). From `˜q_{k+1}`, a small number of correction steps `q_l = q_{l-1} + γ d(q_{l-1})` are performed. The process terminates when the direction to the manifold flips (i.e., `d(q_{l-1}) · d(q_l) < 0`), indicating that the manifold has been crossed. A `γ > 1` is used to intentionally overshoot and promote crossing. This method maintains samples on opposite sides of the manifold without enforcing exact projection, potentially accelerating exploration.

**C. Probabilistic Implicit Function Representation**
A Gaussian Process (GP) is used to construct a probabilistic implicit representation of the solution manifold from the generated samples.

1. **Gaussian Process Implicit Surfaces (GPIS):** The method employs a shifted GPIS to represent a field `ϕ(q)` over the configuration space. This field simultaneously encodes the manifold structure and a notion of distance. The GP is defined as `ϕ(q) ~ GP(1, k(q, q'))`, with a shifted mean of `1` and a squared exponential (SE) covariance kernel `k(q, q') = exp(-||q - q'||^2 / (2ℓ^2))`.
2. **GP Construction:** The sampling points (`ˆq`) are treated as observations, and their function values (`y`) are set to `1`. With observation noise `σ^2`, the covariance matrix `K = k(ˆq, ˆq) + σ^2 E` is formed. The coefficient vector `α` for the posterior mean is computed as `α = K^-1 y`.
3. **Implicit Function Evaluation:** For any query point `q`, the GPIS value `ϕ(q)` is evaluated as `ϕ(q) = k(ˆq, q)^T α`. Configurations where `ϕ(q)` is close to `1` (e.g., `> 0.995`) are considered to lie on the null-space manifold.
4. **Distance Field and Gradient:** The gradient of `ϕ(q)` can be computed analytically. Furthermore, a pseudo-Euclidean distance field `d(q)` from the manifold is estimated using an inverse mapping of the kernel function: `d(q) = sqrt(max(0, -2ℓ^2 log ϕ(q)))`. This allows for proximity queries and gradient-based motion generation towards the manifold.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents experimental results on a planar three-link robot and a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka manipulator to evaluate the proposed framework.

**A. Comparison of Jacobian-guided Sampling Methods**
Experiments using a 3-DoF planar manipulator with a 2D position task (inducing a 1D solution manifold) compared the Newton-based, Zigzag, and a Random Inverse Kinematics (IK) baseline method.

- **Coverage and Uniformity:** Both Jacobian-guided methods (Newton-based and Zigzag) effectively tracked the 1D solution manifold, providing visually uniform sampling. The Random IK method, in contrast, exhibited noticeable gaps and less uniform coverage for comparable sample numbers.
- **Computational Efficiency:**
 - For similar numbers of samples, Jacobian-guided methods were two to three times faster than Random IK.
 - The Zigzag method was faster than the Newton-based method under coarse sampling conditions (larger `β` values, e.g., 1.5, 1.0). This is attributed to its relaxed projection condition, which reduces the number of iterations required for convergence when further from the manifold.
 - For fine sampling (smaller `β` values, e.g., 0.5, 0.1), the Newton-based method became faster. This is because for small steps, the iterate remains close to the manifold, requiring minimal projection iterations, making the additional computations of the Zigzag method (e.g., inner product evaluations) dominant.
- **Accuracy:** The Zigzag method consistently exhibited larger errors in terms of distance to the manifold, which is expected due to its relaxed projection criteria.
- **Selection:** The Newton-based Jacobian-guided sampling method was adopted for subsequent experiments due to its higher accuracy and efficiency at finer sampling resolutions.

**B. Generation of Implicit Null-space Manifolds**
The study assessed the construction of the GP-based implicit representation.

- **Computation Time for GP Construction:** The time required for sampling depended on the robot's degrees of freedom (DoF) and the number of samples (e.g., 2.91 ms for 3-DoF, 8.41 ms for 7-DoF). However, the GP construction time itself was largely independent of robot DoF, primarily depending on the number of sampled points (approximately 0.14 ms for both 3-DoF and 7-DoF cases with similar sample counts).
- **Manifold Reconstruction (3-DoF planar arm):**
 - The proposed method successfully captured the 1D null-space manifold from the sampled points. Configurations with GP inference values close to 1.0 were accurately identified as belonging to the manifold (Fig. 4a,b).
 - A continuous distance field from the manifold was successfully visualized (Fig. 4c). Quantitative evaluation against ground-truth distances for the 1D null-space manifold yielded a Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 7.67 × 10⁻³.
 - The framework also demonstrated the ability to reconstruct a 2D null-space manifold for a 3-link manipulator with a 1-DoF task (Fig. 5).
- **Manifold Reconstruction (7-DoF Franka arm):** The method was applied to a 7-DoF robot arm. Visualizations of the inferred manifold and distance field in a projected 3D configuration subspace demonstrated its capability for higher-dimensional systems (Fig. 7a,b,c). The constructed manifold enabled the computation of robot poses satisfying the end-effector task (Fig. 6).

**C. Global Proximity Queries using Implicit Null-space Manifold**
The generated implicit representation facilitates efficient proximity queries.

- The estimated distance field and its gradient allowed for determining the shortest path from an arbitrary configuration to the manifold. Moving along the gradient direction by the computed distance could approximately reach the manifold in a single step. This projection step was efficient, requiring approximately 0.04 ms per evaluation (including gradient and distance computation).
- Fig. 7(d) illustrates a 7-DoF robot executing a motion guided by this induced distance field to reach the solution manifold.

**D. Reconstruction of Spatially Distributed Tasks**
The framework was extended to tasks with continuous spatial variation.

- **1D Line Task:** For a 7-DoF Franka constrained to follow a line trajectory with a fixed end-effector orientation, the null-space manifolds for discretized points along the line were visualized. This formed a 2D manifold in configuration space, combining the 1D task structure with the single redundant degree of freedom (Fig. 9a). Quantitative evaluation yielded an RMSE of 2.56 × 10⁻⁵.
- **2D Rectangular Task:** For a 7-DoF Franka maintaining orientation while moving over a rectangular region, the resulting configurations formed a 3D manifold in configuration space (Fig. 9b). This manifold arose from the 2D task structure and the robot's single redundant degree of freedom. The RMSE was 1.96 × 10⁻⁵.
- The visualization using color to encode task-space position suggested potential for representing fields directly on the manifold.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The presented work introduces a representation-centric framework that holds several implications for robotics research and applications.

**Shift in Paradigm:**
The core significance lies in a conceptual shift from conventional point-wise solution computation (e.g., solving Inverse Kinematics for a single configuration) to generating reusable, global spatial representations of entire solution sets. Instead of repeatedly calculating individual solutions, the proposed method precomputes and encodes the manifold structure into an implicit representation.

**Advantages over Existing Methods:**

- **Efficiency for Repeated Queries:** Unlike conventional IK, which performs local exploration for each query, this framework amortizes the cost of initial exploration. Once constructed (in milliseconds), the implicit representation enables subsequent queries—such as distance evaluation, gradient computation, and projection onto the manifold—to be executed in tens of microseconds, providing fast online access to solution-space information.
- **Robustness and Scalability beyond ODE-based Methods:** Compared to ODE-based methods that trace manifolds, the proposed approach integrates an explicit projection step, leading to greater stability across a wider range of step sizes. It also provides a natural extension to higher-dimensional null spaces, whereas ODE-based methods typically focus on one-dimensional manifolds.
- **Distinction from Learning-Based Implicit Representations:** While sharing the goal of constructing scalar fields over configuration space, this method differs significantly from learning-based approaches (e.g., Configuration-Space Distance Fields, neural network models). The proposed method directly models the structure of the solution manifold using local geometric information via sampling, leading to construction times on the order of milliseconds. This contrasts with learning-based methods that often require computationally expensive offline training (hours to days) with large datasets, making the proposed approach more agile for scenarios with frequently changing task definitions.
- **Leveraging GPIS:** The use of Gaussian Process Implicit Surfaces (GPIS) is crucial. GPIS enables the construction of meaningful manifold representations from relatively sparse samples, which is advantageous when sampling on solution manifolds is inherently costly. Furthermore, GPIS explicitly models uncertainty, providing confidence-aware information about the represented solution space. The resulting continuous distance field and its analytically computable gradients support efficient query-time operations.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Motion Planning:** The ability to query distances to the solution manifold and compute projection gradients can significantly enhance motion planning algorithms by providing efficient guidance for reaching task-satisfying configurations or for constrained motion.
- **Task-Aware Sampling:** The explicit representation of the solution manifold enables task-aware sampling strategies, allowing robots to explore diverse valid configurations more systematically.
- **Reusable Knowledge:** The creation of a persistent, reusable model of the solution space for a given task allows this knowledge to be leveraged across multiple functionalities (e.g., planning, control, visualization) without re-solving the underlying inverse problem.
- **Generalization to Complex Tasks:** The framework has demonstrated potential for spatially distributed tasks, opening avenues for representing complex task families (e.g., trajectories, surface-wiping motions) as higher-dimensional manifolds in configuration space.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

- **Scalability:** The sampling-based exploration inherently faces the curse of dimensionality, limiting its direct scalability to very high-dimensional systems. This is an acknowledged area for future work, potentially through sparse or approximate probabilistic models.
- **Disconnected Components and Topology:** The current method explores a single connected component from a given seed. Handling multiple disconnected components and topological transitions near singular configurations (where the tangent space becomes ill-defined) requires further development, such as reinitializing from different seeds.
- **Additional Constraints:** The current formulation does not explicitly incorporate self-collisions or other environmental constraints, which are critical for real-world robotic operations.
- **Computational Cost of GP Inference:** While efficient for small sample sets, the inference cost of GP models grows with the number of samples. Established techniques like sparse GP methods could address this without altering the core representation-centric approach.
- **Beyond Kinematic Tasks:** Extending the framework to incorporate broader task definitions, including interaction conditions, represents a future research direction.

In conclusion, this work offers a novel methodology for encoding the geometric structure of robotic solution spaces. By providing a reusable, implicit representation, it has the potential to streamline various robotic tasks, offer richer insights into robot capabilities, and bridge theoretical concepts of self-motion manifolds with practical, real-time robotic applications.
