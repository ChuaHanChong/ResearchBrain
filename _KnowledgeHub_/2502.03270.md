# The Temporal Trap: Entanglement in Pre-Trained Visual Representations for Visuomotor Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research identifies and quantifies "temporal entanglement" as a critical challenge in utilizing pre-trained visual representations (PVRs) for visuomotor policy learning, where PVRs struggle to distinguish visually similar but temporally distinct states. The work proposes a simple Temporal Encoding (TE) baseline that explicitly augments PVR features with a task-progression signal, achieving over a 7% increase in average policy success rate across various PVRs and tasks.

## Problem

- Pre-trained visual representations (PVRs), despite excelling in static image tasks, often underperform in visuomotor control, where temporal dependencies are crucial for sequential decision-making.
- PVRs, optimized for static images, struggle to capture the temporal progression necessary for robotic tasks, leading to 'temporal entanglement' where features from distinct time steps become indistinguishable.
- The robustness of PVRs to minor visual changes, beneficial for static tasks, paradoxically hinders performance in robot learning where subtle temporal changes convey significant state information.

## Method

- Quantified temporal entanglement using novel metrics: average sequential cosine similarity for short-range entanglement, average global cosine similarity for long-range entanglement, and a task-progression probing method.
- Proposed a ==Temporal Encoding (TE)== baseline that injects an explicit task-progression signal (timestep index encoded as a high-dimensional vector) into the PVR latent space, augmenting the policy input.
- Evaluated the TE baseline against conventional temporal integration strategies, including feature augmentation (e.g., FLARE), policy-based methods (Causal Transformers), and PVRs explicitly pre-trained with temporal objectives or video data.

## Results

- A strong negative correlation was found between short/long-range temporal entanglement metrics and policy success rate, establishing entanglement as a key performance bottleneck.
- The Temporal Encoding (TE) baseline significantly boosted average policy success rate by over **7%** (from **56.11%** to **63.35%**) across 14 diverse PVRs, and by **+20%** when combined with a Causal Transformer policy.
- TE consistently outperformed feature augmentation methods (FLARE) and improved performance even for PVRs explicitly trained with temporal objectives (e.g., R3M, VIP), while dedicated Video-PVRs showed limited efficacy for visuomotor tasks in this context.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal entanglement, where PVRs fail to differentiate visually similar yet temporally distinct states, is a fundamental limitation directly impacting visuomotor policy success.
> - The ability of a PVR's latent space to perceive task progression (measured by task-progression loss) strongly correlates with both temporal entanglement and downstream policy performance.
> - Explicit task-progression signals, like the proposed Temporal Encoding, are more effective in mitigating temporal entanglement than implicit temporal modeling or simple feature augmentation, even benefiting PVRs with existing temporal training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of pre-trained visual representations (PVRs) has emerged as a significant development in visuomotor policy learning for robotics. PVRs, which are typically trained on large datasets for general computer vision tasks, offer a promising alternative to training visual encoders from scratch in robot learning applications. Prior research has demonstrated their utility in various robotic tasks, including affordance-based manipulation, semantically precise tasks, and language-guided control.

Despite these advancements, the effective deployment of PVRs in policy learning remains an area of active investigation, with several identified challenges. Previous works have noted that no single PVR consistently achieves optimal performance across different tasks or policy training paradigms, and that policy robustness can degrade under visual perturbations, despite PVRs' purported generalization capabilities.

This work addresses a previously under-recognized issue: "temporal entanglement." The paper argues that PVRs, primarily optimized for static image understanding, inherently struggle to represent the temporal dependencies crucial for sequential decision-making in visuomotor control. It posits that the robustness of these models to minor visual changes, while beneficial for tasks like image classification, paradoxically hinders their performance in robot learning where small temporal changes carry significant state information. By identifying and quantifying this "temporal trap," the research positions itself as adding a fundamental problem to the existing list of challenges in PVR-based robot learning and proposes a specific method to mitigate it. It also evaluates the efficacy of conventional temporal information integration strategies (feature augmentation, architectural changes, temporal pre-training objectives) in light of this newly defined problem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to identify, quantify, and propose a mitigation strategy for the issue of temporal entanglement inherent in the use of pre-trained visual representations (PVRs) for visuomotor policy learning. The authors are motivated by the observation that PVRs, despite their success in static image understanding, often underperform in sequential decision-making tasks where temporal dependencies are critical.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations can be detailed as follows:

- **Identify Temporal Entanglement as a Critical Issue:** The paper aims to establish that PVRs, optimized for static images, struggle to capture the temporal progression essential for visuomotor control. This struggle manifests as "temporal entanglement," where features from distinct time steps, particularly those representing visually similar but temporally different stages of a task, become indistinguishable in the PVR's latent space. The authors highlight a "paradoxical phenomenon": models robust to visual changes in static computer vision benchmarks may underperform in robot learning because this robustness leads to a lack of sensitivity to subtle, but temporally significant, visual cues.
- **Quantify the Impact of Temporal Entanglement:** A central goal is to develop metrics and methods to quantitatively assess both short-range and long-range temporal entanglement within PVR latent spaces. The research seeks to demonstrate a correlation between the degree of temporal entanglement and the success rate of a visuomotor policy. This involves:
 - Measuring average sequential cosine similarity to assess short-range entanglement between adjacent time steps.
 - Measuring average global cosine similarity to assess long-range entanglement across an entire trajectory.
 - Probing PVR latent spaces for their ability to predict task progression, arguing that a lack of such perception signifies general entanglement.
- **Demonstrate the Insufficiency of Existing Temporal Methods:** The authors aim to show that traditional approaches to incorporating temporal information—such as concatenating sequential features (feature augmentation) or using PVRs pre-trained with temporal objectives—are insufficient on their own to address the problem of temporal entanglement comprehensively. This motivates the need for an explicit disentanglement strategy.
- **Propose a Disentanglement Baseline:** Based on the insights gained from quantifying entanglement, the paper seeks to introduce a simple, yet effective, baseline method to mitigate temporal entanglement. This baseline is designed to augment PVR latent spaces with explicit task-progression cues, aiming to improve policy performance.
- **Validate the Proposed Baseline and its Generalizability:** The final objective is to empirically evaluate the proposed disentanglement baseline across a range of PVRs and robotic tasks, comparing its performance against conventional methods and demonstrating its effectiveness and generalizability in improving visuomotor policy success rates.

In summary, the motivation stems from a perceived gap in understanding why PVRs, despite their powerful visual encoding capabilities, often fall short in dynamic robotic tasks. The paper seeks to fill this gap by proposing temporal entanglement as a fundamental cause and offering a direct solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research investigates temporal entanglement in pre-trained visual representations (PVRs) for visuomotor policy learning and proposes a disentanglement baseline. The methodology encompasses imitation learning, a comprehensive evaluation setup, novel quantification metrics, and comparative experiments with existing temporal augmentation techniques.

**A. Imitation Learning via Behaviour Cloning**
The core learning paradigm is behavior cloning. An expert policy $\pi^{\star}$ maps proprioceptive observations ($p \in \mathcal{P}$) and visual observations ($o \in \mathcal{O}$) to actions ($a \in \mathcal{A}$). A dataset of $N$ expert trajectories, $\mathcal{T}_e = \{(p_i^t, o_i^t, a_i^t)_{t=0}^T\}_{i=1}^N$, is generated. A learned policy $\pi_\theta$ is trained to imitate $\pi^{\star}$ by minimizing the L2 action discrepancy:
$E_{(p_i^t,o_i^t,a_i^t)\sim\mathcal{T}_e} \|a_i^t - \pi_\theta(f_{\text{PVR}}(o_i^t), p_i^t)\|_2^2$,
where $f_{\text{PVR}}$ extracts features from $o_i^t$. The policy $\pi_\theta$ is modeled as a shallow 4-layer MLP with ReLU activations and a tanh output layer, predicting the mean of a truncated Gaussian distribution.

**B. Implementation Details**
- **Environment:** Experiments are primarily conducted in the MetaWorld simulation environment, using ten tasks selected for their difficulty. For each task, 25 expert demonstrations are generated, with up to 175 steps per rollout. To validate generality, three robot arm tasks (Push, Pick and Place, Reach) from the OpenAI Robotics Suite are also used, with 50 expert demonstrations per task.
- **PVRs:** A diverse set of 14 PVRs are evaluated, encompassing two main architecture families: ResNet-50 and Vision Transformer (ViT-B/16, with DINOv2 using ViT-B/14). PVRs with various training objectives (MIM, Self-Distillation, Supervised, V-L Contrastive, Contrastive, Clustering, VICReg, Value Function, Time Contrastive+Language) and dataset types (ImageNet, LVD, Kinetics, Ego4D, LAION) are included. The selection specifically includes PVRs with strong temporal components (e.g., R3M, VIP) or those trained with video data (e.g., VC-1). Additionally, three Video-PVRs (TimeSformer, ViViT, VideoMAE) are briefly studied.
- **Policy Training:** Each policy (PVR-task pair) is trained 5 times for 80K steps, using mini-batches of 128 samples, the Adam optimizer, and a learning rate of $10^{-4}$. PVR weights are kept frozen. Interquartile mean (IQM) success rate is reported.

**C. Temporal Entanglement Quantification**
The paper defines and quantifies temporal entanglement at different scales:
- **Short-range Temporal Entanglement:** Measured by the *average sequential cosine similarity*. For each task $t$, the average feature token $\bar{f}^{(t)}$ is computed across all demonstrations and frames. Task-centered features $\tilde{f}^{(i,t)}_n = f^{(i,t)}_n - \bar{f}^{(t)}$ are then used to compute the average cosine similarity between features of consecutive frames across all demonstrations and tasks. A higher similarity indicates greater entanglement.
- **Long-range Temporal Entanglement:** Measured by the *average global cosine similarity*. This metric computes the average cosine similarity between features of all distinct frame pairs within a rollout, across all expert demonstrations and tasks. A higher similarity indicates greater entanglement over the entire trajectory.
- **General Temporal Entanglement via Task-Progression Probing:** A shallow MLP is trained to predict the *task-progression percentage* (normalized timestep index) from the PVR features. The *task-progression loss* (mean-squared error between predicted and ground-truth percentage) is used as a proxy for general entanglement. Lower loss indicates better task-progression perception and, by inference, less entanglement. This loss is correlated with both the short/long-range entanglement metrics and the policy success rate.

**D. Temporal Disentanglement Strategies**
The paper evaluates different approaches to temporal disentanglement:
- **Proposed Baseline: Temporal Encoding (TE):** The timestep index $n$ of each frame is encoded as a 64-dimensional high-dimensional vector using sine and cosine functions at exponentially increasing frequencies:
 $\gamma(n) = [\sin(\frac{2^0 \pi n}{s_0}), \cos(\frac{2^0 \pi n}{s_0}), \dots, \sin(\frac{2^{N_T-1} \pi n}{s_{N_T-1}}), \cos(\frac{2^{N_T-1} \pi n}{s_{N_T-1}})]$.
 This temporal embedding is then concatenated to the policy input, effectively introducing a task-progression signal. The scale parameter is set to 100. The method is presented as a strong baseline, acknowledging limitations for real-world cold-starts.
- **Feature Augmentation-based Disentanglement:** This approach involves concatenating features from the three most recent past observations and their latent differences, as in FLARE. Performance is compared against the proposed TE baseline.
- **Policy-based Disentanglement:** A 1-layer Causal Transformer (CT) is deployed, encoding temporal relationships through context and action chunking of length 12. This is tested in a multi-task setting with R3M features, both with and without the TE augmentation.
- **PVR-based Disentanglement (Video-PVRs):** Three Video-PVRs (TimeSformer, ViViT, VideoMAE), designed for video perception, are evaluated. These models process a buffer of $N_f$ recent frames. Their inference time, success rate, and interaction with TE are assessed.

The methodology is designed to provide quantitative evidence for temporal entanglement, demonstrate its impact on policy performance, and rigorously compare the proposed simple baseline against more complex or conventional approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings regarding temporal entanglement in PVRs and the efficacy of a proposed disentanglement baseline.

**A. Characterization and Quantification of Temporal Entanglement**
- **Visual Evidence of Entanglement:** PCA projections of PVR features from expert demonstrations in a Bin Picking task (Fig. 1) visually suggest temporal entanglement. Features from stages with subtle visual changes (e.g., gripper stopping to pick up the box, descent/ascent phases) tend to cluster tightly, indicating a lack of distinctiveness.
- **Correlation with Policy Success:**
 - **Short-range Entanglement:** A strong negative correlation is observed between the average sequential cosine similarity (short-range entanglement) and policy success rate, particularly within ResNet subgroups (R: -0.98 for non-temporal ResNets) and MIM ViTs (R: -0.82) (Fig. 4, left). PVRs producing more diverse sequential tokens lead to higher success rates.
 - **Long-range Entanglement:** Similarly, a negative correlation exists between the average global cosine similarity (long-range entanglement) and policy success rate. This supports the hypothesis that entanglement affects the entire trajectory, not just adjacent timesteps (Fig. 4, right).
- **Task-Progression Perception as a General Metric:** The ability of a PVR's latent space to predict task progression (measured by task-progression loss) is strongly correlated with both short and long-range entanglement scores (Fig. 6, left). Furthermore, a strong negative correlation is found between task-progression loss and average policy success rate across all PVRs (R: -0.63) (Fig. 6, right). This establishes task-progression perception as a reliable predictor of policy performance and a measure of general temporal entanglement.

**B. Effectiveness of the Temporal Disentangling Baseline (TE)**
- **Significant Performance Boost:** The proposed Temporal Encoding (TE) baseline, which augments PVR features with a task-progression signal, leads to a substantial increase in average policy success rate. Across all PVRs, TE increases the average success rate by over 7% (from 56.11% to 63.35%) compared to using no temporal augmentation (Fig. 3).
- **Superiority over Feature Augmentation:** TE consistently outperforms FLARE, a method based on concatenating recent observations and latent differences. While FLARE increases average success rate by approximately 2% (to 58.34%) and reduces short-range entanglement, TE's gains are significantly higher. TE also dramatically reduces long-range similarity (to 0.1520) compared to FLARE (0.9377). This suggests that extracting a temporal signal solely from nearby observations is insufficient, and a global task-progression signal is more effective.
- **Benefit for Temporally-Trained PVRs:** Even PVRs explicitly trained with temporal objectives, such as R3M and VIP, benefit considerably from TE, with VIP showing an average performance boost of approximately 15% (Fig. 3). This indicates that existing temporal modeling strategies in PVRs may not fully exploit the necessary temporal structure.
- **Statistical Significance and Generalizability:** Statistical analysis (Wilcoxon test, p < $10^{-30}$; paired t-test, p < $10^{-26}$) confirms that TE's gains over no temporal augmentation are statistically significant. TE also significantly outperforms FLARE (Wilcoxon test, p ≈ $4.38 \times 10^{-5}$; paired t-test, p ≈ $1.47 \times 10^{-4}$). Validation on tasks from the OpenAI Robotics Suite (Push, Pick and Place, Reach) further demonstrates TE's generalizability, more than doubling performance on some tasks (Fig. 7).

**C. Comparison with Other Disentanglement Methods**
- **Policy-based Disentanglement (Causal Transformer):** When a 1-layer Causal Transformer (CT) is used for policy learning, augmenting its input with the TE signal (CT+TE) yields a substantial average task success rate boost of +20% (from 68.0% to 86.3%) across four MetaWorld multi-tasks (Table II). This indicates that TE complements and enhances even sophisticated policy architectures designed to handle temporal relationships.
- **PVR-based Disentanglement (Video-PVRs):** Video-PVRs (TimeSformer, VideoMAE, ViViT), designed for video applications, show limited effectiveness for visuomotor policy learning in this context. They struggle to outperform even the average performance of static-image PVRs. Furthermore, they exhibit significantly higher inference times, and their performance appears negatively correlated with the length of the frame buffer ($N_f$) (Table III). This suggests that simply using models trained on video data does not inherently solve the temporal entanglement problem in the way that the explicit task-progression signal of TE does.

In summary, the research establishes temporal entanglement as a critical factor limiting PVR performance in visuomotor tasks, provides tools for its quantification, and introduces a simple yet highly effective temporal encoding baseline that significantly improves policy success by addressing this issue.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of visuomotor robot learning by addressing a fundamental limitation in the current deployment of pre-trained visual representations (PVRs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Identifies a Critical, Previously Under-recognized Problem:** The paper formally identifies and characterizes "temporal entanglement" as a core issue that hinders the performance of PVRs in sequential decision-making tasks. By demonstrating that PVRs' robustness to minor visual changes (beneficial for static image tasks) becomes a detriment in dynamic robotic control due to their inability to encode crucial temporal dependencies, the work offers a new perspective on PVR limitations in this domain. This problem is shown to be distinct from, and not fully addressed by, existing concerns like PVR selection or robustness to visual perturbations.
- **Provides Quantitative Tools for Assessment:** The introduction of metrics like average sequential and global cosine similarity, along with a task-progression probing method, provides researchers with concrete tools to quantify the degree of temporal entanglement in PVR latent spaces. Critically, the strong correlation shown between task-progression loss and policy success establishes a reliable predictor of downstream performance, enabling better selection or adaptation of PVRs for visuomotor tasks.
- **Challenges Existing Assumptions on Temporal Modeling:** The findings suggest that current methods for integrating temporal information—whether through feature augmentation (e.g., FLARE), PVRs explicitly trained with temporal objectives (e.g., R3M, VIP), or even dedicated Video-PVRs—are often insufficient on their own to overcome temporal entanglement. This necessitates a reconsideration of how temporal perception is incorporated into features for robot learning, moving beyond naive fusion or reliance on implicit temporal training.
- **Proposes a Simple and Effective Baseline:** The proposed Temporal Encoding (TE) baseline, despite its simplicity, demonstrates substantial and statistically significant performance gains across a wide array of PVRs and tasks. This provides a straightforward yet powerful mechanism for mitigating temporal entanglement, setting a new benchmark for future disentanglement techniques. The fact that it boosts performance even for PVRs pre-trained with temporal components, and when combined with advanced policy architectures like Causal Transformers, highlights its complementary nature.
- **Paves the Way for Temporally Aware PVRs:** By clearly demonstrating the importance of explicit task-progression signals and the limitations of existing PVRs in capturing them, the research provides a clear direction for future work. It encourages the development of "temporally aware PVRs" that are inherently designed to mitigate temporal entanglement, either through novel pre-training objectives, architectural modifications, or integration strategies that account for sequential task progression rather than just static image content.
- **Improves Robot Learning Performance:** The direct impact of this work is the potential for improved success rates and robustness in visuomotor policy learning. By enabling policies to better differentiate visually similar but temporally distinct states, robots can execute complex multi-step tasks more reliably. This could accelerate progress in various robotic applications, from manipulation in unstructured environments to long-horizon planning.

In essence, this paper redefines a critical challenge in visuomotor policy learning, offers robust methods for its diagnosis, and provides an effective foundational solution that can inform the design of more capable and temporally intelligent robotic systems.
