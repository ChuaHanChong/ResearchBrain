# WoVR: World Models as Reliable Simulators for Post-Training VLA Policies with RL

> [!summary] Summary
> WoVR is a reliability-driven framework designed to enable stable reinforcement learning for Vision-Language-Action policies by explicitly controlling hallucination in learned world models. This method leads to a 29.3 percentage point increase in average LIBERO success rates and a 30.0 percentage point improvement in real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, typically trained with imitation learning, are limited by the coverage and quality of human demonstration data, struggling to optimize beyond observed behaviors.
- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) directly to physical robots is impractical due to high interaction costs, slow data collection, manual environment resets, and the 'sim-to-real' gap of hand-designed simulators.
- Learned world models, while promising for imagination-based RL, suffer from 'hallucination' (inaccurate predictions, error accumulation, and distribution shifts) which corrupts the RL optimization signal.

## Method

- **WoVR** stabilizes the action-conditioned world model using a dual-channel action injection and first-frame anchoring to maintain spatial consistency and causality during long rollouts.
- Hallucination-aware policy optimization integrates Keyframe-Initialized Rollouts (KIR) to shorten effective prediction horizons and employs masked Group Relative Policy Optimization (==GRPO==) to prevent learning from spurious successes.
- Policy-Aligned Co-Evolution (PACE) periodically refines the world model with data from the evolving policy, mitigating distribution shift without requiring continuous, expensive retraining.

## Results

- The WoVR world model consistently outperforms baseline action-conditioned generative models in visual fidelity and temporal consistency across various metrics (LPIPS, FID, FVD, FloLPIPS) and achieves high inference efficiency (23 FPS).
- WoVR significantly improves VLA task success rates on LIBERO benchmarks, achieving an average of **69.2%**, which is a 29.3 percentage point improvement over the base OpenVLA-OFT policy.
- Policies optimized with WoVR demonstrate robust sim-to-real transfer, achieving an average **91.7%** real-robot success rate (+30.0 percentage points) on manipulation tasks without additional online interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Acknowledging and explicitly controlling 'hallucination' in learned world models is crucial for reliable imagination-based reinforcement learning, rather than assuming perfect model fidelity.
> - Combining multi-level controls—at the simulator design, imagined interaction protocol, and policy-model alignment levels—is necessary to effectively mitigate the impact of prediction errors.
> - Strategic initialization of rollouts (KIR) and periodic, policy-driven world model refinement (PACE) are vital for maintaining simulator reliability and enabling effective policy optimization under imperfect dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown promise in robotic manipulation by generating actions conditioned on language instructions and visual observations. Most existing VLA systems rely on imitation learning (IL), which is inherently limited by the quality and coverage of demonstration data. To move beyond these limitations, reinforcement learning (RL) offers the potential for VLA policies to acquire more sophisticated capabilities.

However, applying RL directly to real-world VLA policies presents significant challenges. On-policy RL methods, known for their training stability and efficiency, demand massive parallel environment interaction, repeated resets, and costly data collection from physical robots, rendering them impractical for widespread real-world deployment. While simulation-based alternatives exist, achieving accurate alignment between simulated and real-world dynamics remains a difficult task.

This context has motivated research into using learned world models as simulators for policy optimization, decoupling policy learning from expensive real-world interaction. The emergence of large-scale generative video models has made this direction more viable. Prior work has explored treating pre-trained video generators as simulators for RL in imagination. However, a fundamental limitation of learned world models is their imperfection: they are not faithful simulators and can "hallucinate." Hallucination is defined in this work as a systematic mismatch between imagined and real outcomes in closed-loop interaction. These errors compound in autoregressive rollouts due to the model conditioning on its own generated frames and policy-induced distribution shifts. If RL policies are optimized using such unreliable imagined trajectories, they may learn to exploit systematic model errors rather than genuine task progress, leading to policies that fail in the real world.

WoVR addresses this challenge by reframing the problem from primarily a modeling challenge to a reliability problem. It proposes a framework that explicitly regulates how RL interacts with imperfect imagined dynamics, aiming to control hallucination rather than assuming its absence. This positions WoVR as a contribution to the growing body of work that seeks to make world-model-based RL more practical and robust for real-world robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reliable reinforcement learning framework that enables effective post-training of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies using learned world models as simulators. This endeavor is motivated by the recognized limitations of imitation learning for VLA policies and the practical difficulties of deploying traditional RL on physical robots due to the requirement for extensive real-world interaction.

The core motivation stems from the observation that while learned world models offer a promising alternative to real-world simulators, they suffer from inherent imperfections, specifically "hallucination." Hallucination is characterized as a mismatch between imagined and real outcomes, particularly in closed-loop, autoregressive rollouts. This phenomenon is exacerbated by two main factors:

1. **Autoregressive Error Accumulation**: When a world model generates future frames by conditioning on its own previously generated frames, even small initial prediction errors can compound over time, leading to significant visual degradation, scene drift, and physically incorrect dynamics in long-horizon rollouts.
2. **Policy-Induced Distribution Shift**: As a VLA policy undergoes optimization through RL in the imagined environment, its action distribution continuously evolves. This evolving distribution can drift away from the data distribution used to train the initial world model, leading to out-of-distribution actions for which the world model's predictions become unreliable.

These issues result in spurious success signals in imagination, which can mislead policy optimization. The central question the authors pose is: "If world models inevitably hallucinate, how can RL remain reliable under imperfect imagined dynamics?"

To address this reliability challenge, the authors argue that controlling hallucination is paramount. This motivation leads to the design of WoVR, which aims to regulate world-model-based RL at three interconnected levels:

1. **Simulator-level control**: To improve the intrinsic stability and controllability of the world model itself.
2. **Interaction-level reshaping**: To design interaction protocols that mitigate the impact of accumulated errors during imagined rollouts.
3. **Policy-model alignment**: To ensure that the simulator remains relevant and reliable as the policy evolves.

By tackling these aspects, the research seeks to move beyond simply treating world models as drop-in simulators and instead develop a framework that actively manages their imperfections to facilitate effective and reliable VLA policy optimization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WoVR proposes a reliability-driven world-model-based reinforcement learning framework for post-training VLA policies. It tackles the hallucination problem through a three-pronged approach: strengthening the world model, redesigning imagined interaction, and maintaining policy-model alignment.

#### 3.1. Stabilized Action-Conditioned World Model

The foundation of WoVR is a generative video world model designed to be both action-controllable and rollout-stable. This model is built upon the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video diffusion backbone and adapted for action conditioning.

- **Dual-Channel Action Injection**: Actions are incorporated into the diffusion model through two pathways. They modulate denoising features via timestep-conditioned normalization, and they replace text embeddings in cross-attention to provide global action context. This dual-channel design allows for frame-level control while preserving the original DiT (Diffusion Transformer) architecture.
- **First-Frame Anchored Context**: To stabilize long-horizon autoregressive generation and prevent scene drift or appearance degradation, the model conditions on a combination of the initial frame of the episode (`o_0`) and recent memory frames from the previous chunk (`o_{t-c:t}`). The `o_0` acts as a fixed reference, maintaining global layout, while memory frames preserve local dynamics.
- **Noisy Context Augmentation**: During training, non-reference context frames are mildly noised. This strategy aims to improve the model's robustness to its own self-generated context at inference time, reducing brittle copying from previous predicted frames and closing the train-inference gap.
- **Reward Model**: WoVR supports two reward modeling choices:
 - A lightweight ResNet-based model for binary rewards, which predicts task success probability. This is computationally efficient.
 - A Qwen3-VL-based model for dense rewards, which predicts discrete progress levels. The authors primarily use the sparse reward model due to its efficiency and comparable performance.

#### 3.2. Hallucination-Aware Policy Optimization in Imagination (Keyframe-Initialized Rollouts - KIR)

To mitigate the problem of early error accumulation in long-horizon imagined rollouts, WoVR introduces Keyframe-Initialized Rollouts (KIR).

- **Mechanism**: Instead of always starting imagined rollouts from the episode's initial state (`o_0`), KIR initializes a portion of rollouts from "keyframes" (`o_k`) that are strategically chosen to be near task-critical intermediate states, particularly those where the current policy often fails.
- **Benefit**: By starting closer to decisive moments in the task, KIR effectively shortens the prediction depth required from the world model, thereby reducing the opportunities for compounding errors to lead to hallucinated successes.
- **Integration with GRPO**: The GRPO (Group-Relative Policy Optimization) objective is used for policy updates. Its trajectory-length normalization mechanism, which increases the per-timestep contribution of shorter trajectories, complements KIR by emphasizing gradients from these task-critical segments.

#### 3.3. PACE: Policy–Aligned Co-Evolution Strategy

To address the distribution shift that occurs as the VLA policy evolves and its action distribution diverges from the initial world model's training data, WoVR implements PACE.

- **Mechanism**: PACE allows the world model and the VLA policy to co-evolve through low-frequency, policy-driven refinement of the world model.
 - An initial world model (`WM_Base`) is trained using trajectories collected from the base VLA policy.
 - After an initial phase of policy optimization within `WM_Base`, a limited set of additional rollouts are collected using the *evolved* policy.
 - The world model is then refined (`WM_Evo`) using these new, policy-aligned trajectories.
- **Advantages**: This low-frequency refinement distinguishes PACE from continuous model-based RL updates, avoiding constant online supervision and environment resets. It mitigates accumulated model errors, maintains simulator reliability, and preserves training stability by ensuring the world model remains aligned with the evolving policy's behavior.

These three components collectively form a hallucination-aware RL framework designed to enable reliable policy optimization entirely in imagination, reducing reliance on costly real-world interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate the stability, controllability, and efficiency of the WoVR world model, its effectiveness in improving VLA task performance, and the reliability of policy transfer to real-world manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. World Model Stability, Controllability, and Efficiency

(Table 1 and Ablation Table 5)

- **Quantitative Performance**: WoVR's world model consistently outperformed baselines (EVAC, Cosmos-Predict2, OpenSora) across standard perceptual and distributional metrics (LPIPS, FID, FVD, FloLPIPS). The improvements became more pronounced with increasing rollout horizons (e.g., 512 frames), indicating greater robustness in long-horizon autoregressive generation.
- **Efficiency**: Despite using a larger backbone, WoVR achieved a higher inference throughput of 23 FPS compared to OpenSora's 7 FPS, attributed to requiring fewer diffusion steps and leveraging a 3D VAE for latent encoding.
- **Ablation Studies (World Model Mechanisms)**:
 - Removing the fixed reference frame (`WoVR w/o ref`) led to a significant degradation in performance, particularly at longer rollout horizons, confirming its role in suppressing error accumulation and maintaining global layout.
 - Using only a single-frame context (`WoVR w. mem=1`) also resulted in performance drops.
 - Disabling noisy context augmentation (`WoVR w/o noisy context`) caused noticeable performance reductions, highlighting its contribution to robustness against self-generated context.
- **Qualitative Observations**: Ablated variants of the world model exhibited spatial drift and object disappearance in long-horizon rollouts, whereas the full WoVR model maintained visual stability and consistency with ground truth.

#### 4.2. VLA Task Performance Improvement

(Table 2 and Ablation Table 6)

- **LIBERO Benchmarks**:
 - **One-Trajectory SFT**: WoVR achieved an average success rate of 69.5%, demonstrating substantial improvements (an average increase of 29.0 percentage points) over the base OpenVLA-OFT policy. It significantly outperformed WMPO (50.9% average SR) and GRPO (online) (44.6% average SR), especially on complex tasks like LIBERO-Object and LIBERO-Long. WMPO showed no gain on LIBERO-Long, suggesting its world model was less reliable for extended horizons.
 - **Full-Trajectory SFT**: Even starting from a strong base policy (88.1% SR), WoVR further improved the average success rate to 96.0%, surpassing GRPO (89.7%) and WMPO (92.4%).
- **Ablation Studies (PACE and KIR)**:
 - **PACE**: Increasing the world model's training data from 1,500 to 2,500 base-policy trajectories without PACE only provided a modest 1.7-point improvement in average success rate. This indicated that simply adding more data from the *same* distribution has diminishing returns. The full WoVR with PACE (1,500 base + 1,000 policy-aligned) yielded significantly higher performance, supporting the importance of refining the world model with data from the *evolving* policy distribution. Training curves illustrated that PACE helped recalibrate the imagined success rate to better align with real-environment evaluations.
 - **KIR**: Removing Keyframe-Initialized Rollouts (`WoVR w/o KIR`) reduced the average success rate from 82.5% to 79.7%, indicating that initializing rollouts near task-critical states effectively reduces prediction depth and improves imagined interaction.
- **Reward Modeling**: An ablation on reward modeling indicated that while a dense reward model could improve early sample efficiency, both sparse and dense reward models converged to similar final performance. Given its significantly lower computational cost, the lightweight sparse reward model was selected for main experiments.

#### 4.3. Real-World Transfer

(Table 3, Table 4, and Figure 10)

- **Quantitative Real-World Gains (Table 3)**: WoVR policies demonstrated superior transferability to real-world robotic platforms.
 - On the Franka Emika Panda, WoVR improved the base policy's average success rate by 28.9 percentage points (compared to WMPO's 13.4 points).
 - On the AgileX Piper (a noisier platform), WoVR provided an average gain of 13.4 percentage points (compared to WMPO's 3.4 points), indicating its robustness across hardware with different control characteristics.
- **Generalization to Different VLA Backbones (Table 4)**: WoVR's benefits were not tied to a specific VLA architecture. When applied to a π
0.5
VLA backbone on the AgileX Piper, WoVR consistently improved policy performance, raising the average success rate by 22.3 percentage points across tasks.
- **Qualitative Failure Analysis (Figure 10)**: Visualizations showed that WoVR not only increased success rates but also effectively mitigated common action-level failure modes observed in base VLA policies, such as unstable grasping, premature gripper release, inaccurate placement, and incomplete contact-rich interactions. This suggested that hallucination-aware optimization translated into more reliable physical robot behavior.

These findings collectively demonstrate that WoVR effectively addresses the reliability challenges of world-model-based RL, leading to robust policy optimization that transfers well to diverse real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research identifies hallucination in closed-loop imagined interaction as a fundamental reliability bottleneck for world-model-based reinforcement learning (RL) in the context of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy optimization. By explicitly addressing this challenge, WoVR significantly contributes to making world models practical and reliable simulators for RL.

The significance of WoVR's contributions lies in several areas:

- **Enabling Practical RL for Robotics**: The work demonstrates that it is feasible to perform stable and effective on-policy RL fine-tuning for VLA models largely in imagination, thus mitigating the previously prohibitive requirement for massive real-world interaction and data collection from physical robots. This can dramatically reduce the cost and complexity of developing advanced robotic manipulation skills.
- **Robustness to Imperfect Simulators**: WoVR provides a framework that operates under the realistic assumption of imperfect world models. By implementing mechanisms to control hallucination (stabilized world model, keyframe-initialized rollouts, policy-aligned co-evolution), it enhances the reliability of learning signals, leading to policies that are less prone to exploiting spurious successes in the imagined environment.
- **Superior Performance and Generalization**: The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance in VLA task success rates in simulation (LIBERO) and demonstrates consistent, substantial gains in real-world deployment across multiple robotic platforms (Franka Emika Panda and AgileX Piper) and different VLA backbones. This indicates its broad applicability and effectiveness in diverse robotic scenarios, including those with noisier hardware.
- **Modular and Principled Design**: WoVR's three-level approach to hallucination control (simulator design, interaction protocol, and policy-model alignment) offers a structured and principled way to address the complexities of learned dynamics models. Each component is demonstrated to contribute to the overall reliability and performance.

The potential impact of WoVR is substantial for the field of embodied AI and robotics:

- **Accelerated Robotic Skill Acquisition**: By allowing most RL training to occur in imagination, WoVR can accelerate the iterative development and refinement of robotic manipulation policies, leading to faster prototyping and deployment cycles for new skills.
- **Enhanced Autonomy**: The ability to optimize policies reliably without continuous human supervision or real-world resets during RL training brings closer the vision of autonomous robots that can continuously learn and improve their capabilities.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for future research into a closed-loop learning paradigm where real-world deployment data continually refines the world model, which in turn further improves VLA policies in imagination, creating a virtuous cycle of improvement. It also highlights the importance of formal characterizations of hallucination propagation and regret bounds for learned policies, as well as scaling WoVR to even longer-horizon and more complex mobile manipulation tasks.

In conclusion, WoVR represents a significant step forward in making world-model-based RL a practical and reliable tool for advancing VLA policies, offering a pathway to more capable and autonomous robotic systems.
