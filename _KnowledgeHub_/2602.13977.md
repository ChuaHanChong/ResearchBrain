# WoVR: World Models as Reliable Simulators for Post-Training VLA Policies with RL

> [!summary] Summary
> WoVR is a reliability-driven framework designed to enable stable reinforcement learning for Vision-Language-Action policies by explicitly controlling hallucination in learned world models. This method leads to a 29.3 percentage point increase in average LIBERO success rates and a 30.0 percentage point improvement in real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, typically trained with imitation learning, are limited by the coverage and quality of human demonstration data, struggling to optimize beyond observed behaviors.
- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) directly to physical robots is impractical due to high interaction costs, slow data collection, manual environment resets, and the 'sim-to-real' gap of hand-designed simulators.
- Learned world models, while promising for imagination-based RL, suffer from 'hallucination' (inaccurate predictions, error accumulation, and distribution shifts) which corrupts the RL optimization signal.

## Method

- **WoVR** stabilizes the action-conditioned world model using a dual-channel action injection and first-frame anchoring to maintain spatial consistency and causality during long rollouts.
- Hallucination-aware policy optimization integrates Keyframe-Initialized Rollouts (KIR) to shorten effective prediction horizons and employs masked Group Relative Policy Optimization (==GRPO==) to prevent learning from spurious successes.
- Policy-Aligned Co-Evolution (PACE) periodically refines the world model with data from the evolving policy, mitigating distribution shift without requiring continuous, expensive retraining.

## Results

- The WoVR world model consistently outperforms baseline action-conditioned generative models in visual fidelity and temporal consistency across various metrics (LPIPS, FID, FVD, FloLPIPS) and achieves high inference efficiency (23 FPS).
- WoVR significantly improves VLA task success rates on LIBERO benchmarks, achieving an average of **69.2%**, which is a 29.3 percentage point improvement over the base OpenVLA-OFT policy.
- Policies optimized with WoVR demonstrate robust sim-to-real transfer, achieving an average **91.7%** real-robot success rate (+30.0 percentage points) on manipulation tasks without additional online interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Acknowledging and explicitly controlling 'hallucination' in learned world models is crucial for reliable imagination-based reinforcement learning, rather than assuming perfect model fidelity.
> - Combining multi-level controls—at the simulator design, imagined interaction protocol, and policy-model alignment levels—is necessary to effectively mitigate the impact of prediction errors.
> - Strategic initialization of rollouts (KIR) and periodic, policy-driven world model refinement (PACE) are vital for maintaining simulator reliability and enabling effective policy optimization under imperfect dynamics.
