# Learning Hierarchical Skill Policies with Offline Quality-Diversity Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> QDOS learns hierarchical skill policies from mixed-quality offline data by weighting a VAE skill-extraction objective and a diversity term by per-segment advantage from an IQL value function, then reuses the data to seed online replay via pseudo-labeling. It beats SUPE across antmaze, antsoccer, kitchen, and humanoidmaze, e.g. 80 vs 27 normalized return on antsoccer-arena.

## Problem

- Standard trajectory VAEs used for offline skill extraction reconstruct all segments equally, forcing capacity onto noise and failed attempts rather than useful behaviors.
- Purely diversity-driven skill discovery distinguishes distinct behaviors but cannot tell useful diversity from useless diversity in noisy datasets.
- Prior offline-to-online methods like SUPE assume offline data is coherent and segmentable, so pseudo-labels from a cluttered skill space give the high-level policy unreliable training signal.

## Method

- ==Advantage-Weighted Quality-Diversity Pretraining==: trains an ==IQL== value function to estimate per-segment advantage, then weights the ==VAE== reconstruction loss by exp(advantage/temperature) so high-value segments dominate skill learning.
- ==Weighted mutual-information objective==: reconstructs randomly sampled latent skills from policy rollouts, weighted by advantage, to keep diverse skills distinguishable without encouraging diverse failure modes.
- ==Dual dataset reuse with pseudo-labeling==: offline segments are relabeled with inferred skill codes and ==RND-based== optimistic rewards to seed the online high-level replay buffer alongside on-policy ==SAC==/==IQL== updates.

## Results

- QDOS reaches **80.0** normalized return on antsoccer-arena (alpha=0.3) versus SUPE's 27.0, and **96.0** versus 83.0 on antmaze-large.
- On kitchen-mixed QDOS attains a perfect **100.00** score versus SUPE's 85, showing it stitches fragmented subtasks SUPE fails to compose.
- QDOS reaches first goal faster than SUPE, e.g. **19168** vs 23334 steps on humanoidmaze and **5001** vs 5834 steps on scene.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Weighting skill-extraction losses by estimated advantage is a general fix for mixed-quality offline datasets, not specific to this VAE or domain.
> - Diversity weighting should scale with task structure: unconstrained dynamic tasks benefit from higher diversity weight, tightly constrained manipulation from lower.
> - On already high-quality, structured datasets explicit diversity enforcement adds little, method degrades gracefully toward the unweighted baseline.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research operates at the intersection of several subfields within deep reinforcement learning (RL), specifically addressing challenges in offline RL, unsupervised skill discovery, and offline-to-online RL.

Deep RL has achieved notable success in various control tasks but often requires extensive interaction with the environment, leading to sample inefficiency. This limitation makes it challenging for real-world applications where data collection is costly or unsafe. Offline RL emerged to address this by enabling policy learning from static, pre-collected datasets without further online interaction. However, offline RL methods can be conservative and struggle with distribution shifts, potentially hindering robust exploration when online interaction is permitted.

Concurrently, unsupervised skill discovery methods aim to extract reusable low-level behaviors, or "skills," from large, unlabeled datasets. Approaches like OPAL and SPiRL utilize techniques such as trajectory Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) to encode these skills into a latent space, which a high-level policy can then leverage for complex, long-horizon tasks. This temporal abstraction can simplify problem solving. A limitation of these methods is their reliance on the quality of the offline dataset; they typically treat all data segments equally, meaning that noisy, suboptimal, or irrelevant data can degrade the quality of the learned skill space.

Recent developments have sought to bridge offline skill learning with online fine-tuning in an "offline-to-online" setting. Methods like SUPE demonstrate that reusing offline data for both skill extraction and populating an online replay buffer can accelerate online learning. However, these methods often inherit the limitations of standard VAEs regarding mixed-quality data, as they do not effectively distinguish between useful and unhelpful behaviors during skill extraction. This can result in a "cluttered" skill space, where a high-level policy struggles to select effective actions.

Quality-Diversity (QD) optimization, a paradigm originating from evolutionary computation, seeks to generate diverse sets of high-performing solutions rather than a single optimal one. This concept has been adapted to RL, as seen in DiveOff, to discover multiple task solutions from offline data.

This work, QDOS, integrates these concepts to address the specific challenge of learning robust, diverse, and high-quality skills from mixed-quality offline datasets. It aims to overcome the "mixed-quality dilemma" prevalent in existing offline skill discovery and offline-to-online approaches, where noise and suboptimal actions in the dataset can compromise the utility of learned skills. By incorporating advantage weighting into a Quality-Diversity objective, QDOS intends to filter out low-value data while preserving a diverse set of high-quality behaviors, thereby providing a more effective foundation for subsequent online hierarchical learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework for robust offline-to-online hierarchical reinforcement learning that can effectively extract diverse and high-quality skills from mixed-quality, static offline datasets. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current approaches:

1. **Challenge of Mixed-Quality Offline Data:** Real-world datasets often contain a mixture of optimal trajectories, exploratory noise, failed attempts, and irrelevant behaviors. Standard unsupervised skill discovery methods, such as trajectory VAEs used in prior work (e.g., OPAL, SPiRL, SUPE), typically minimize reconstruction error uniformly across the entire dataset. This approach forces the model to allocate representational capacity to all data segments, including suboptimal or noisy ones. Consequently, the learned "skill space" can become "cluttered," meaning useful skills are entangled with useless or indistinguishable behaviors, making it difficult for a high-level policy to leverage them effectively.

2. **Trade-off between Diversity and Quality:** Existing methods often face a trade-off. Purely diversity-driven methods (e.g., maximizing mutual information) encourage distinct behaviors but do not inherently distinguish between diverse useful behaviors and diverse useless ones. Conversely, purely reward-driven or behavior-cloning approaches tend to collapse to a single mode, failing to capture the diversity of solutions necessary for robust exploration and adaptation in complex environments. A unified framework that can simultaneously ensure both high quality and broad diversity among learned skills is lacking.

3. **Inefficient Online Exploration in Offline-to-Online Settings:** While offline-to-online methods (e.g., SUPE) reuse offline data for skill extraction and online replay, the issue of skill quality persists. If the extracted skills are unreliable or inconsistent due to mixed-quality offline data, the "pseudo-labeling" of offline trajectories with these skills can provide inconsistent signals to the high-level policy, hindering efficient online exploration and robust strategy learning. The high-level policy might waste interaction steps exploring with ineffective skills.

To address these motivations, QDOS proposes two interconnected goals:
- **Skill Discovery:** To learn a low-level, latent-conditioned policy (π_θ(a|s,z)) and a prior (p(z)) that can capture diverse, useful functional behaviors while robustly filtering out noise and suboptimal data from mixed-quality offline datasets.
- **Hierarchical Adaptation:** To learn a high-level policy (π_ψ(z|s)) that effectively selects these high-quality, diverse skills to maximize expected return during online interaction, thereby accelerating exploration and improving final performance in complex, sparse-reward domains.

The overarching aim is to improve the utility of offline datasets for hierarchical reinforcement learning by ensuring that the low-level skill policies provide a clean, structured, and effective action space for the high-level policy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The QDOS framework consists of two primary phases: (1) Advantage-Weighted Quality-Diversity Skill Pretraining and (2) Online Exploration with Trajectory Skills, employing a dual dataset reuse strategy.

**Phase 1: Advantage-Weighted Quality-Diversity Skill Pretraining**

This phase focuses on extracting diverse and high-value skills from a mixed-quality offline dataset by leveraging an advantage-weighted variational autoencoder (VAE) framework combined with mutual information maximization.

1. **Advantage Estimation via Implicit Q-Learning (IQL):**
 - Offline trajectories are segmented into fixed-length segments (τ_[H]).
 - To estimate the quality of these segments, a value function V_ψ(s) is trained on the offline dataset using Implicit Q-Learning (IQL). IQL learns a value function that approximates the upper expectile of the return distribution, aiming to capture the value of the best policy supported by the data without requiring out-of-distribution queries.
 - The IQL value function is trained by minimizing an expectile regression loss (L_V(ψ)).
 - Based on this value function, a weight w(τ_[H]) is computed for each segment, proportional to the exponential of its estimated advantage (A(τ_[H])) divided by a temperature parameter (λ). This weighting mechanism prioritizes high-advantage segments, effectively focusing the VAE on learning from the "good" parts of the dataset while down-weighting "bad" or noisy parts.

2. **Weighted VAE Objective:**
 - A VAE is used to learn the skill space, comprising an encoder f_θ(z|τ_[H]) (mapping trajectory segments to a latent variable z), a decoder (low-level policy) π_θ(a|s,z) (reconstructing actions), and a state-dependent prior p_θ(z|s_0).
 - The learning objective is an Advantage-Weighted VAE loss (L_AW_VAE(θ)). This loss incorporates the computed segment weights, ensuring that the model primarily minimizes the reconstruction error for high-value behaviors.
 - The objective also includes a KL-divergence term that regularizes the learned posterior towards the prior, aiming for a smooth latent space.

3. **Mutual Information Maximization:**
 - To prevent mode collapse (where the policy ignores the latent code) and encourage diversity among the learned skills, the mutual information I(z; τ) between the latent skill z and the generated trajectory τ is maximized.
 - A variational lower bound for mutual information is utilized, which in practice is approximated by minimizing the mean-squared L2 norm between a randomly sampled latent skill (z_rand) from the prior and its reconstruction (ˆz_rand) from a short auxiliary rollout of the policy conditioned on z_rand.
 - Crucially, this diversity objective (L_info(θ)) is also weighted by the trajectory advantage, ensuring that diversity is sought specifically among high-quality behaviors rather than arbitrary or low-value ones.

4. **Total Pretraining Objective:**
 - The final pretraining objective (L_θ) is a weighted sum of the Advantage-Weighted VAE loss and the mutual information maximization loss, controlled by a hyperparameter α_loss.

**Phase 2: Online Exploration with Trajectory Skills**

After pretraining, the low-level skill policy π_θ is frozen. A high-level policy π_ψ(z|s) is then trained to select skills (z) every H environment steps. This phase employs a dual dataset reuse strategy:

1. **Pseudo-labeling Offline Data:**
 - The entire offline dataset D is "pseudo-labeled" to create a replay buffer of high-level transitions.
 - For each offline segment τ_[H], the pretrained encoder q_θ(z|τ_[H]) infers a skill label (ˆz).
 - An optimistic reward estimate (ˆr) is computed for each segment, often using an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) estimator combined with an exploration bonus from Random Network Distillation (RND) applied to a learned reward model.
 - These pseudo-labeled transitions (s_0, ˆz, ˆr, s_H) are added to a dedicated offline replay buffer (D_offline).

2. **Online High-Level Policy Learning:**
 - The high-level policy π_ψ is trained using an off-policy RL algorithm (e.g., Soft Actor-Critic, SAC) that leverages both the D_offline replay buffer and an online replay buffer (D_replay) populated by new interactions.
 - During online interaction, the high-level policy samples a skill z, which the low-level policy executes for H steps. The resulting environment transitions are stored in D_replay.
 - A sampling ratio (e.g., 50/50) is maintained between online and offline data during high-level policy updates to ensure stable learning.

**Implementation Details:**
- PyTorch is used for implementation.
- Adam optimizer is used for pretraining and online phases.
- Segment length H is typically 20.
- IQL expectile parameter τ is 0.9.
- Temperature parameter λ and mutual information weight α_loss are tuned per environment.
- High-level policy uses entropy-regularized actor-critic (SAC).
- Experiments are averaged over 5 random seeds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that QDOS consistently exhibits improved performance and efficiency compared to various baselines across a range of challenging sparse-reward domains, including locomotion (antmaze, humanoidmaze, antsoccer) and manipulation tasks (kitchen, cube, scene).

**Overall Performance (Normalized Returns):**
- **AntSoccer-Arena:** QDOS achieved a normalized return of 0.80 (with α=0.3), substantially surpassing SUPE's 0.27. This indicates its ability to extract and compose fine-grained manipulation skills for object interaction.
- **Kitchen-Mixed:** QDOS attained a perfect score of 4.00 (with α=0.2), outperforming SUPE's 3.40. This suggests effective stitching of fragmented subtasks to solve complex sequential manipulation problems.
- **HumanoidMaze-Medium:** QDOS reached a perfect score of 1.00 (with α=0.3), slightly exceeding SUPE's 0.97. This highlights robust locomotion learning even in high-dimensional and unstable environments.
- **AntMaze-Large:** QDOS achieved 0.96 (with α=0.3), outperforming SUPE's 0.83.
- For tasks like `kitchen-partial` and `scene-task1`, QDOS performed comparably to SUPE, with both methods achieving high returns.

**Goal Finding Efficiency:**
- QDOS demonstrated faster goal discovery compared to SUPE in sparse-reward tasks.
- In the `scene` task, QDOS reached the goal in 5001 steps, which was faster than SUPE's 5834 steps.
- In `humanoidmaze`, QDOS reduced the exploration time by over 4000 steps compared to SUPE. This accelerated exploration is attributed to a cleaner skill space, which allows the high-level policy to avoid exploring with noisy or ineffective skills.

**Hyperparameter Sensitivity:**
- The mutual information weight (α_loss) influences the trade-off between reconstruction quality and skill diversity.
- For complex, dynamic tasks like `antsoccer`, a higher α_loss (e.3) led to better performance, suggesting that a broader range of interaction skills is beneficial.
- For structured manipulation tasks like `kitchen`, a moderate α_loss (e.2) yielded superior results, indicating that excessive diversity can introduce task-irrelevant variations.
- In a failure case analysis on high-quality datasets (e.g., `cube-single`, `cube-double`), reducing α_loss caused QDOS's performance to converge towards the SUPE baseline. This suggests that explicit diversity enforcement is less critical when the dataset is already of high quality and contains structured, clear demonstrations.

**Model Visualization:**
- **Latent Space Structure (t-SNE):** Visualizations indicated that QDOS tends to utilize a broader region of the latent space compared to SUPE, suggesting the capture of a more diverse set of behaviors. SUPE's latent space usage appeared more limited or concentrated.
- **Behavior Visualization:** In `kitchen`, learned skills corresponded to distinct, semantically meaningful object interactions (e.g., "open microwave"). The pseudo-labeling accurately assigned these skills to offline segments. In `antmaze`, skills represented coherent directional movements, and continuous manipulation of the latent variable z allowed for smooth transitions between behaviors. This smoothness is considered beneficial for high-level RL stability.

In summary, QDOS consistently improved both the final returns and the efficiency of online exploration, particularly in challenging environments with mixed-quality offline data, by extracting high-quality and diverse skill representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents QDOS, a framework that addresses a key challenge in leveraging pre-collected datasets for reinforcement learning: effectively extracting useful behaviors when the data contains a mixture of optimal actions, noise, and failed attempts. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

1. **Robust Skill Discovery from Mixed-Quality Data:** The core contribution of QDOS is its ability to distill robust, task-aligned skills while explicitly filtering out noise and failures in the offline pretraining phase. By integrating advantage weighting into a Quality-Diversity objective, it overcomes a limitation of standard unsupervised skill discovery methods, which often learn cluttered or unreliable skill spaces from noisy datasets. This capacity for quality-aware representation learning makes the extracted skills more reliable and directly usable by a high-level policy.

2. **Accelerated Online Exploration and Improved Performance:** By providing a cleaner, more structured, and diverse skill space, QDOS enables the high-level policy to explore more efficiently in online environments. The dual dataset reuse strategy, where offline data is pseudo-labeled with these high-quality skills, further bootstraps online learning. This leads to faster goal finding, improved sample efficiency, and higher asymptotic returns in challenging sparse-reward and long-horizon tasks, as demonstrated across diverse locomotion and manipulation domains. This is crucial for real-world applications where data collection is costly.

3. **Advancement in Offline-to-Online Reinforcement Learning:** QDOS advances the state-of-the-art in offline-to-online RL by providing a more robust foundation for skill extraction, a component that was previously susceptible to dataset quality issues. It offers a practical solution for improving the utility of existing large, unstructured datasets, making the transition from offline pretraining to online fine-tuning more effective.

4. **Integration of Quality-Diversity and Offline RL:** The work successfully integrates principles from Quality-Diversity optimization with offline RL techniques. This demonstrates that seeking diverse, high-performing solutions, rather than a single optimal one, can be beneficial for learning reusable primitive behaviors that enhance exploration in subsequent online phases. The explicit weighting of diversity by estimated advantage ensures that the diversity is task-relevant.

5. **Applicability to Real-World Robotics:** The ability to learn hierarchical policies from mixed-quality data is particularly relevant for robotics. Real-world robotic data is inherently noisy and often contains suboptimal demonstrations. QDOS offers a pathway to leverage such data more effectively for developing robust, adaptable robot behaviors, reducing the need for extensive and potentially hazardous online trial-and-error.

The findings highlight the importance of sophisticated representation learning that accounts for data quality in the context of offline RL. Future research avenues mentioned, such as adaptive tuning of the quality-diversity trade-off and fine-tuning low-level skills online, suggest ongoing potential for further impact by enhancing the adaptability and generality of these hierarchical policies.
