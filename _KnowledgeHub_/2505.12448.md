# SSR: Enhancing Depth Perception in Vision-Language Models via Rationale-Guided Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SSR framework enhances Vision-Language Models' depth perception by transforming raw depth data into structured textual rationales, then encoding them into compact latent embeddings. This method improves spatial reasoning accuracy by up to 13.6% and significantly reduces inference time by integrating depth inferentially.

## Problem

- Visual-Language Models (VLMs) relying on 2D RGB inputs inherently lack precise spatial information, limiting their understanding of 3D relationships crucial for real-world applications.
- Existing methods for integrating depth information often treat it superficially, failing to leverage its inferential value for higher-order spatial reasoning in VLMs.
- This deficiency restricts VLM performance in applications requiring robust spatial understanding, such as robotics and autonomous navigation.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Spatial Sense and Reasoning (SSR)== framework, which transforms monocular depth maps into structured textual rationales via a ==Mamba-based Image-Depth Interpreter (MIDI)==.
- Employs ==knowledge distillation== to encode these rationales into compact, plug-and-play latent embeddings for efficient integration into existing VLM architectures.
- Curates ==SSR-COT==, a million-scale dataset with depth-aware visual-language reasoning annotations, and ==SSRBENCH==, a comprehensive multi-task benchmark for spatial understanding.

## Results

- Achieved up to **13.6%** average and **6.77%** overall accuracy improvement on spatial reasoning benchmarks like SpatialBench and SSRBENCH, outperforming proprietary models such as GPT-4o-mini.
- Demonstrated a substantial reduction in inference time per sample (e.g., from **23.16s** to **0.32s** on SpatialBench) by dramatically decreasing the required Chain-of-Thought tokens.
- Showed enhanced generalization capabilities across various out-of-domain and general Visual Question Answering benchmarks, indicating broader benefits beyond specialized spatial tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transforming raw depth data into textual rationales and distilling them into latent embeddings effectively bridges low-level depth perception with high-level linguistic reasoning.
> - A modular, two-stage training paradigm allows for efficient integration of spatial enhancements into existing VLM architectures without extensive retraining.
> - Explicitly guiding VLMs with depth-aware spatial reasoning, rather than merely appending depth, yields superior and more generalized multi-modal understanding.
