# SSR: Enhancing Depth Perception in Vision-Language Models via Rationale-Guided Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SSR framework enhances Vision-Language Models' depth perception by transforming raw depth data into structured textual rationales, then encoding them into compact latent embeddings. This method improves spatial reasoning accuracy by up to 13.6% and significantly reduces inference time by integrating depth inferentially.

## Problem

- Visual-Language Models (VLMs) relying on 2D RGB inputs inherently lack precise spatial information, limiting their understanding of 3D relationships crucial for real-world applications.
- Existing methods for integrating depth information often treat it superficially, failing to leverage its inferential value for higher-order spatial reasoning in VLMs.
- This deficiency restricts VLM performance in applications requiring robust spatial understanding, such as robotics and autonomous navigation.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Spatial Sense and Reasoning (SSR)== framework, which transforms monocular depth maps into structured textual rationales via a ==Mamba-based Image-Depth Interpreter (MIDI)==.
- Employs ==knowledge distillation== to encode these rationales into compact, plug-and-play latent embeddings for efficient integration into existing VLM architectures.
- Curates ==SSR-COT==, a million-scale dataset with depth-aware visual-language reasoning annotations, and ==SSRBENCH==, a comprehensive multi-task benchmark for spatial understanding.

## Results

- Achieved up to **13.6%** average and **6.77%** overall accuracy improvement on spatial reasoning benchmarks like SpatialBench and SSRBENCH, outperforming proprietary models such as GPT-4o-mini.
- Demonstrated a substantial reduction in inference time per sample (e.g., from **23.16s** to **0.32s** on SpatialBench) by dramatically decreasing the required Chain-of-Thought tokens.
- Showed enhanced generalization capabilities across various out-of-domain and general Visual Question Answering benchmarks, indicating broader benefits beyond specialized spatial tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transforming raw depth data into textual rationales and distilling them into latent embeddings effectively bridges low-level depth perception with high-level linguistic reasoning.
> - A modular, two-stage training paradigm allows for efficient integration of spatial enhancements into existing VLM architectures without extensive retraining.
> - Explicitly guiding VLMs with depth-aware spatial reasoning, rather than merely appending depth, yields superior and more generalized multi-modal understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical limitation within the rapidly evolving field of Visual-Language Models (VLMs). VLMs have made remarkable strides in multi-modal tasks, effectively bridging the gap between image and natural language understanding. However, their pervasive reliance on standard RGB image inputs inherently restricts their ability to grasp precise spatial information, such as relative positions, distances, and three-dimensional object relationships. This deficiency significantly impedes their performance in complex real-world applications, most notably in robotics, where accurate spatial reasoning is paramount.

The existing landscape of solutions to enhance spatial understanding in VLMs includes:
- **Point Cloud Integration:** Some methods utilize point cloud data, which offers rich 3D information. However, these approaches typically necessitate specialized sensors like LiDAR, rendering them impractical for scenarios limited to monocular RGB cameras.
- **Monocular Depth Estimation:** The emergence of generative methods for monocular depth estimation has provided a compelling alternative, allowing depth information to be inferred from standard 2D images without additional hardware. While promising, current VLM methods integrating this depth information often do so superficially. They treat depth as a supplementary input rather than an integral component for higher-order inferential reasoning, diverging from how humans naturally employ depth.

The proposed **SSR (Spatial Sense and Reasoning)** framework directly tackles this "superficial utilization" problem. It positions itself at the forefront of research aiming to imbue VLMs with more human-like spatial intelligence. It builds upon the concept of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which has enabled Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform complex, step-by-step inference. However, unlike traditional CoT approaches that are primarily text-based or multimodal CoT focusing on tasks like code generation or general QA, SSR innovates by:
1. **Transforming raw depth data into structured textual rationales:** This makes the implicit spatial information interpretable and usable for language models.
2. **Employing knowledge distillation:** This compresses these rationales into compact latent embeddings, facilitating efficient, plug-and-play integration into existing VLMs without requiring extensive retraining. This stands in contrast to systems that need to be trained end-to-end from scratch, making SSR a more practical and accessible solution.
3. **Focusing specifically on spatial reasoning:** While other works like SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, and RoboRefer have aimed to improve VLM spatial capabilities through specialized datasets and fine-tuning, SSR integrates *language-based reasoning* within the spatial framework, addressing an omission in prior approaches that often neglected the explicit reasoning process.

By introducing both a novel methodology for depth integration and new datasets/benchmarks (SSR-COT and SSRBENCH), this work not only pushes the technical boundaries of VLM capabilities but also provides critical resources for future research in spatial intelligence, addressing a key bottleneck for the deployment of VLMs in physically embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the SSR framework stems from the inherent limitations of current Visual-Language Models (VLMs) in accurately grasping precise spatial information. Despite their impressive advancements in bridging vision and language, VLMs primarily rely on 2D RGB inputs, which are fundamentally inadequate for understanding complex spatial relationships like relative positions, distances, and depths in a scene. This deficiency severely restricts their ability to perform robust spatial reasoning, a capability vital for real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous navigation, and intelligent interaction.

The authors identify that while monocular depth estimation offers a promising avenue to augment VLMs with spatial cues, existing methods typically integrate this depth information in a superficial manner. Unlike human cognition, which intuitively uses depth as an integral component of broader reasoning processes, current VLMs often treat depth merely as supplementary input, failing to leverage its inferential value for higher-order spatial understanding. For instance, a human can infer "object A is far from object B" by analyzing their spatial relationship, then use this inference in subsequent reasoning steps. Current VLMs struggle to emulate this implicit, inferential use of depth.

To address these critical shortcomings, the key objectives of the SSR project are multifaceted:

1. **Redefine Depth Integration:** To move beyond superficial depth utilization by proposing a novel paradigm that transforms raw depth data into structured, interpretable textual rationales. These rationales are intended to serve as semantically rich intermediate representations that bridge low-level depth perception with higher-level linguistic reasoning.
2. **Enhance Spatial Reasoning Capabilities:** To enable VLMs to generate more accurate and contextually appropriate outputs by making depth an inferential component of the reasoning process, thereby enhancing spatial understanding.
3. **Improve Efficiency and Integrability:** To compress these generated textual rationales into compact latent embeddings using knowledge distillation. This aims to facilitate resource-efficient and "plug-and-play" integration into *existing* VLM architectures without requiring extensive retraining of the entire model.
4. **Develop Comprehensive Training Data:** To curate a large-scale, depth-aware visual-language reasoning dataset, named **SSR-COT**, which is enriched with intermediate spatial reasoning annotations. This dataset is crucial for training models to understand and utilize spatial rationales effectively.
5. **Establish Robust Evaluation Benchmarks:** To introduce a comprehensive multi-task benchmark, **SSRBENCH**, designed to rigorously evaluate VLM performance across various general and spatially-oriented reasoning tasks, thereby filling a gap in existing evaluation protocols.
6. **Demonstrate State-of-the-Art Performance:** To show through extensive experimentation that the SSR framework substantially improves depth utilization and enhances spatial reasoning, ultimately advancing VLMs toward more sophisticated, human-like multi-modal understanding.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to overcome the inherent "2D bias" of current VLMs and equip them with a nuanced, inferential understanding of 3D space, which is critical for their reliability and applicability in complex physical environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SSR framework, short for Spatial Sense and Reasoning, introduces a novel approach to integrate depth information into Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by transforming raw depth data into structured, interpretable textual rationales and then efficiently encoding these rationales. The methodology can be broken down into architectural design, training paradigm, and data collection.

#### 3.1. Architecture: Spatial Sense and Reasoning (SSR)

The SSR framework is built around a key component called the **Mamba-based Image-Depth Interpreter (MIDI)**, designed as a plug-and-play module to enhance spatial understanding.

- **Input Processing:** Given an input image ($X_V$) and a textual query ($X_T$), the framework first employs a pre-trained monocular depth estimation model (Depth Pro) to generate a corresponding depth map ($X_D$).
- **Feature Extraction:** Visual features ($Z_V$) are extracted from $X_V$ using a pre-trained visual encoder (CLIP ViT-L/14), and depth features ($Z_D$) are extracted from $X_D$ using a depth encoder (SigLIP).
- **Semantic Space Projection:** Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) modules (projectors $\phi_V$ and $\phi_D$) map these raw visual and depth features into a unified semantic embedding space, making them compatible with the subsequent language model.
- **Rationale Generation (MIDI Core):** A Mamba-based language model ($f_{LM}$) acts as an intermediate reasoning module. It jointly processes the projected visual features ($Z_V$), depth features ($Z_D$), and the textual query ($X_T$) to generate *latent tokens* ($H_R$). These latent tokens represent the "intermediate spatial rationales." To facilitate knowledge distillation, special tokens are strategically inserted into these rationales.
- **Final Latent Projection:** An additional latent projection module ($\phi_R$) maps these latent rationale tokens ($H_R$) into another semantic embedding space, matching the dimensionality required by the downstream VLM. The output, $Z_R$, is a sequence of spatial-aware latent tokens.
- **VLM Integration:** Crucially, these $Z_R$ tokens are then seamlessly "plugged into" the input sequence of an existing VLM ($f_{VLM}$), alongside the original image ($X_V$) and textual question ($X_T$). The VLM then auto-regressively generates the final answer ($Y_A$), now informed by the depth-based spatial reasoning embedded in $Z_R$.

#### 3.2. Training Paradigm

SSR adopts a two-stage training procedure, leveraging its modular design.

- **Stage 1: Reasoning and Alignment:**
 - **Objective:** To train the MIDI module to generate and encode coherent thought processes (spatial rationales) into latent feature sequences that are consistent with a natural language semantic space.
 - **Data:** Training samples at this stage include ground truth *textual rationales* ($Y_R$) for supervision.
 - **Process:** The latent tokens ($Z_R$) produced by MIDI are fed into a Large Language Model (LLM) (Qwen2.5), which is tasked with reconstructing the original textual rationale. This ensures that the MIDI module not only learns to reason but also aligns its latent representations with the frozen-state LLM's semantic space.
 - **Loss Function:** Standard causal modeling loss ($L_1$).
 - **Outcome:** After Stage 1, MIDI can generate latent tokens ($Z_R$) that can be directly integrated into existing VLMs.

- **Stage 2 (Optional): Co-Training:**
 - **Objective:** To further enhance the overall performance of SSR by jointly training the MIDI module and an existing VLM (Qwen2.5-VL).
 - **Data:** During this stage, the intermediate rationales are discarded, and the VLM is trained to directly generate the final answer. This allows for incorporating additional VQA pairs (e.g., LLaVA-Instruct-150K) to improve generalization.
 - **Loss Function:** Standard cross-entropy loss ($L_2$) for auto-regressive answer generation.
 - **Modularity:** This stage is optional, highlighting the plug-and-play nature of MIDI, which can function effectively even without this fine-tuning.

#### 3.3. SSR-COT Dataset Collection

Recognizing the scarcity of visual-language CoT datasets with detailed spatial reasoning annotations, the authors curated **SSR-COT**, a million-scale dataset of image-depth-question-rationale-answer pairs.

- **Data Sources:** Integrates and enhances data from LLaVA-CoT, Visual-CoT, VoCoT, and SpatialQA.
- **Pipeline (Figure 3):**
 1. **Depth Extraction:** Depth estimations are extracted from raw images using Depth Pro.
 2. **Refinement:** For datasets with long conversations (e.g., VoCoT, SpatialQA), concise, one-turn question-answer pairs are extracted.
 3. **Spatial Attribute Mining:** Using SpatialRGPT and existing bounding box annotations (from Visual-CoT, VoCoT), precise spatial attributes (size, distance, relative positioning) are mined based on intermediate reasoning steps.
 4. **Rationale Generation:** GPT-4o is employed to integrate all extracted information, generating detailed, spatially-enriched reasoning processes (rationales). For SpatialQA, where no intermediate steps or bounding boxes are provided, GPT-4o generates synthetic rationales.
 5. **Quality Control:** Cache pools and iterative sampling quality checks are used. Rationales that improve VQA accuracy when evaluated by Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct are retained; those that degrade performance are discarded and resubmitted for re-annotation.

#### 3.4. SSRBENCH Construction

To provide a dedicated benchmark for evaluating spatial understanding, **SSRBENCH** was created from SSR-COT, ensuring no data overlap.

- **Categories:** Divided into "general understanding" (Existence, Attribute, Action tasks) and "spatial understanding" (Counting, Position, Object tasks).
- **Construction Process:** GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro independently classify sampled image-text pairs from SSR-COT into predefined task categories. Only instances with consistent classifications from both models are included in SSRBENCH.
- **Evaluation:** Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-1M serves as an LLM-Assistant for evaluation, providing both accuracy and a quantitative score (0-5), mitigating biases by comparing with GPT-4o-mini as an additional judge.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Backbones:** Mamba (130M) as the efficient language model for MIDI, Qwen2.5 (3B) as the LLM for Stage 1 alignment, and Qwen2.5-VL (3B/7B) as the VLM for Stage 2.
- **Training Resources:** Utilizes LoRA and FSDP, requiring approximately 19 hours for Stage 1 and 48 hours for Stage 2 on a single Nvidia 8-H800 GPU node.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted across multiple benchmarks robustly demonstrate the effectiveness of the SSR framework in significantly enhancing depth perception and spatial reasoning capabilities in Vision-Language Models.

1. **Superior Performance on Spatial Tasks:**
 - On both SpatialBench and the newly introduced SSRBENCH, SSR (particularly the 7B parameter variant) consistently outperforms state-of-the-art proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o-mini, Claude-3.5-Haiku) and various open-source baselines (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT, SpatialBot, Emu3, Qwen2.5-VL).
 - SSR achieves notable improvements in average question answering accuracy, with a maximum enhancement of 13.6% and an average improvement of 6.77% compared to the top-performing baselines.
 - Specifically, in "spatial tasks," SSR demonstrates its most significant gains, with improvements reaching 20.9% and 22.5% over the backbone model (Qwen2.5-VL) on certain spatial subtasks. This unequivocally validates SSR's ability to effectively leverage depth information for spatial reasoning.

2. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:**
 - Beyond specialized spatial benchmarks, SSR also shows improved performance on out-of-domain datasets like CV-Bench, VSR, and What’s Up.
 - Furthermore, SSR exhibits performance enhancements on general VQA benchmarks such as VQAv2, TextVQA, POPE, MMBench, and GQA. This indicates that the training paradigm of SSR not only boosts spatial understanding but also improves the model's overall generality and generalization capability, suggesting that integrating robust spatial reasoning benefits broader multi-modal comprehension.

3. **Effectiveness of Plug-and-Play MIDI:**
 - Ablation studies confirm the modularity and immediate impact of the MIDI module. When used in a plug-and-play manner (after only Stage 1 training, without Stage 2 co-training with the VLM), MIDI achieves average performance gains of 4.4% and 1.6% on different benchmark datasets, with improvements up to 8.8% on specific tasks.
 - The optional Stage 2 co-training further significantly amplifies these gains, leading to additional average performance improvements of 5.7% and 18.7%. This demonstrates that MIDI provides a strong foundation for spatial reasoning that can be further refined through VLM co-training.

4. **Inference Efficiency:**
 - A critical finding is SSR's dramatic improvement in end-to-end inference speed compared to fine-tuning the backbone VLM on SSR-COT without the latent-reasoning approach. While SSR introduces a modest absolute latency per generated token, its latent-reasoning paradigm drastically reduces the number of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) tokens required per sample (e.g., from 437.28 to 2.62 tokens on SpatialBench).
 - Consequently, the overall inference time per sample is substantially improved (e.g., from 23.16s to 0.32s), making SSR a highly efficient solution for integrating sophisticated reasoning.

5. **Robust Evaluation and Rationale Encoding:**
 - LLM-Assistant evaluations with different LLM judges (Qwen vs. GPT-4o-mini) show high inter-model agreement on SSRBENCH, indicating the robustness and reliability of the evaluation metrics.
 - Analysis of rationale embeddings via cosine similarity visualization (Figure 6) confirms that MIDI effectively captures depth information and spatial reasoning. High diagonal values demonstrate successful mapping of rationales to latent representations, while low off-diagonal values indicate that these latent tokens remain sample-specific rather than collapsing into generic representations, validating the successful distillation of spatial knowledge.

In summary, SSR not only achieves state-of-the-art performance in spatial reasoning but also does so efficiently and with strong generalization, validating its novel architecture and training paradigm.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SSR framework represents a significant step forward in the development of Visual-Language Models (VLMs), addressing a core limitation that has hindered their application in complex real-world scenarios. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Spatial Gap in VLMs:** By effectively integrating depth perception and transforming it into interpretable textual rationales, SSR moves VLMs beyond a superficial 2D understanding of scenes. This is crucial for enabling more human-like multi-modal comprehension, where an intuitive grasp of 3D spatial relationships is fundamental for true intelligence.
2. **Enabling Robust Reasoning for Embodied AI:** The enhanced spatial reasoning capabilities are particularly impactful for embodied AI systems, such as robotics and autonomous vehicles. These applications demand precise understanding of object positions, distances, and interactions within a 3D environment for safe and effective operation (e.g., navigation, manipulation, object avoidance). SSR's ability to interpret and reason with depth data provides a critical foundation for such systems.
3. **Efficiency and Practicality through Knowledge Distillation:** The use of knowledge distillation to compress textual rationales into compact latent embeddings is a key contribution to practicality. This makes SSR resource-efficient and enables its "plug-and-play" integration into existing VLM architectures without the need for extensive retraining. This lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and developers seeking to enhance spatial capabilities, promoting wider adoption and further innovation. The demonstrated significant speed-up in inference time is a major practical advantage.
4. **Novel Datasets and Benchmarks for Spatial Intelligence:** The creation of SSR-COT, a million-scale visual-language reasoning dataset enriched with intermediate spatial reasoning annotations, and SSRBENCH, a comprehensive multi-task benchmark, fills a critical gap in the research community. These resources will serve as invaluable tools for training, developing, and rigorously evaluating future models in spatial intelligence, accelerating progress in this area.
5. **Innovative Approach to Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** SSR extends the concept of CoT reasoning to the domain of spatial perception by generating explicit spatial rationales. This novel approach demonstrates how complex multi-modal information can be systematically processed and reasoned about, opening new avenues for integrating various modalities into advanced reasoning pipelines.
6. **Broader Research Avenues:** As highlighted by the authors, SSR demonstrates that spatial reasoning capabilities can be incrementally enhanced without adversely affecting existing VLM functionalities. This provides an innovative avenue for research communities to integrate additional capabilities into VLMs in a modular fashion, fostering a more composable and extensible approach to VLM development.

While the current study is limited to the Qwen/Qwen-VL series of models, the core methodology of rationale-guided spatial reasoning and efficient latent embedding is highly generalizable. Future work exploring its applicability across a broader range of VLM architectures will further solidify its impact. Overall, SSR not only delivers state-of-the-art performance but also offers a pragmatic and forward-looking framework for building more spatially aware and intelligently reasoning multi-modal AI systems.
