# Toward Physically Grounded JEPA World Models for Goal-Conditioned Robotic Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> An end-to-end action-conditioned JEPA world model that augments latent prediction with two auxiliary heads: inverse dynamics, which recovers the executed action from consecutive latents, and pair-based state alignment, which regresses measured physical configuration and velocity from those same latents. Planning runs in latent space via CEM against a goal embedding, reaching 100% on TwoRoom, 98% on PushT, and 87% on OGBench-Cube.

## Problem

- Latent prediction alone admits a collapsed solution mapping all observations to one representation, so JEPA world models need extra signal to stay informative for control.
- Frozen pretrained-encoder approaches avoid collapse only by leaning on large-scale visual pretraining and costly patch-level rollouts during planning.
- Distributional priors and action-recovery objectives regularize embeddings but never specify which physical aspects of the state the representation must retain.

## Method

- ==State-alignment loss==: a two-layer MLP head regresses the measured physical state from a consecutive latent pair, giving the temporal context needed to ground velocity as well as configuration.
- ==Inverse-dynamics loss==: a second MLP head predicts the executed action chunk from consecutive latents, blocking collapse since identical pairs force a constant-action predictor.
- Lightweight backbone: randomly initialized ==ViT-Tiny/14== CLS encoder to 192-d, six-layer causal ==Transformer== predictor with zero-init ==AdaLN== action conditioning, jointly trained with equal auxiliary weights.

## Results

- Success rates of **100%** TwoRoom, **98%** PushT, **87%** OGBench-Cube beat DINO-WM, PLDM, and LeWorldModel; Reacher **85%** is comparable to LeWorldModel's **86%**.
- Removing state alignment drops success on all four tasks, most sharply Reacher **85** to **63** and PushT **98** to **83**, confirming its contribution over inverse dynamics alone.
- Transition-subspace SVD over 100 trajectories gives r95 of **32.8** for the full model versus **30.2** IDM-only and just **17.0** for the higher-straightening baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding latents in measured physical state is a usable training-only signal: supervision is dropped at deployment, so planning still runs purely in latent space.
> - High average temporal straightening can be a misleading proxy for representation quality, hiding transition energy concentrated in a low-dimensional, near-rank-one subspace.
> - Effective transition dimension is the more diagnostic metric; auxiliary objectives that lower straightening while raising it can still improve downstream planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of world models represents a significant area of research in robotic autonomy, aiming to enable robots to plan and act by learning the dynamics of their environment. Traditional visual world models often rely on objectives such as pixel-level observation prediction or reconstruction. While effective, these methods can be computationally intensive and require modeling intricate appearance variations (e.g., texture, illumination, sensor noise) that may not be directly relevant or could even be detrimental to the downstream control tasks.

Action-conditioned Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) have emerged as an alternative paradigm. JEPA-style world models operate by encoding raw visual observations into compact latent representations and learning to predict how these latent states evolve under specific actions. A key advantage is that they avoid the computationally demanding task of reconstructing future pixels. Instead, planning can occur directly in the latent space by comparing imagined future latent states with a target latent state derived from a goal image.

A fundamental challenge within JEPA-style world models is learning a useful and informative latent representation. The latent prediction objective alone is susceptible to a trivial "collapse" solution, where the model maps all observations to the same, undifferentiated latent representation, rendering it useless for distinguishing states or planning. Prior research has addressed this representational collapse through various strategies:
- **DINO-WM:** This approach leverages large-scale visual pretraining by freezing a DINOv2 visual encoder and learning dynamics over its patch-level features. While it avoids collapse, it depends on extensive pretraining and incurs high computational costs for patch-level rollouts.
- **LeWorldModel:** This method stabilizes end-to-end learning by employing SIGReg to encourage latent embeddings to match an isotropic Gaussian distribution. This technique is computationally efficient and effective, but its distributional prior does not explicitly guide the representation to retain specific physical state information relevant for robotic control.
- **PLDM:** This model utilizes a composite objective that combines latent prediction with VICReg-style variance-covariance regularization, temporal smoothness, and inverse dynamics. In this multi-component scheme, inverse dynamics acts as one of several regularization terms.
- **Inverse Dynamics Focused Approaches:** More recent works, such as SMWM and Delta-JEPA, retain latent prediction while primarily focusing their auxiliary objectives on inverse dynamics. These objectives aim to recover the executed action from consecutive latent representations (or their difference), thereby making latent transitions informative of the actions that caused them.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by investigating whether explicitly grounding latent representations in measured physical states can provide a complementary training signal to inverse dynamics for an end-to-end JEPA world model. The authors propose to augment the latent prediction and inverse dynamics objectives with a state alignment objective, aiming to create more physically meaningful and useful latent spaces for goal-conditioned robotic planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an improved end-to-end JEPA world model capable of supporting robust goal-conditioned robotic planning. The core motivation stems from identified limitations in existing JEPA approaches, particularly concerning the quality and physical grounding of learned latent representations.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

- **Preventing Latent Collapse and Enhancing Representation Informativeness:** The primary latent prediction objective in JEPA models is known to admit a trivial solution where all observations map to identical latent representations, making the model useless for planning. While inverse dynamics objectives (e.g., in SMWM, Delta-JEPA, PLDM) encourage latent transitions to be informative of executed actions by requiring the model to predict the action from consecutive latent states, these objectives do not explicitly ensure that the latent states themselves are well-grounded in the physical configuration or motion of the robot and environment. The authors are motivated to find a mechanism that further discourages collapse and enriches latent representations beyond action recovery alone.

- **Explicitly Grounding Latent Representations in Physical Reality:** Existing regularization methods, such as LeWorldModel's SIGReg, impose generic statistical priors on the latent space (e.g., isotropic Gaussian distribution) but do not inherently specify *which* aspects of the physical state are important to retain for control. The authors hypothesize that directly aligning latent representations with available physical measurements (e.g., joint angles, end-effector positions) during training could provide a stronger, more targeted signal to embed physically relevant information into the latent space. This explicit grounding is expected to make the latent space more meaningful for planning tasks that depend on precise physical interactions.

- **Complementing Inverse Dynamics with State Alignment:** The paper aims to explore the synergistic effects of combining inverse dynamics with a novel state alignment objective. While inverse dynamics ensures that latent transitions reflect actions, state alignment endeavors to anchor these representations to their corresponding physical states and motions. This combination is motivated by the belief that a physically grounded latent space, in addition to being action-aware, will lead to more effective and robust planning.

- **Developing an End-to-End Trainable Model:** The goal is to integrate these objectives into a single, end-to-end trainable JEPA framework that can learn effective world models directly from visual observations and associated physical states, without relying on complex multi-stage training or frozen, large-scale pre-trained encoders.

- **Evaluating Performance on Diverse Robotic Tasks:** A key objective is to empirically validate the effectiveness of the proposed state alignment mechanism. This involves evaluating the model's planning success across a set of benchmark robotic manipulation and navigation tasks with varying complexities, and comparing its performance against established JEPA baselines.

- **Analyzing Learned Latent Space Properties:** Beyond planning performance, the research is motivated to conduct a deeper analysis of the learned latent spaces. Specifically, the authors aim to use temporal straightening and transition-subspace analysis to characterize how latent representations evolve over time and what information they encode. This analysis seeks to understand the structural properties of the latent space that contribute to or detract from planning success, and potentially challenge established metrics like temporal straightening.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces an end-to-end JEPA world model that augments standard latent prediction with two auxiliary objectives: inverse dynamics (IDM) and state alignment (SA).

**A. Offline Data**
The model is trained using a fixed offline dataset `D` composed of trajectories `τ = (o₀, s₀, a₀, o₁, s₁, ..., a_{T−1}, o_T, s_T)`. Each trajectory includes visual observations (`o_t`), corresponding physical measurements (`s_t`), and action chunks (`a_t`) representing a fixed number of low-level actions executed between `o_t` and `o_{t+1}`. Physical measurements (`s_t`) are used only during training as supervision and are not required during deployment for planning.

**B. Model Architecture**
The core architecture consists of:
- **Visual Encoder (E):** Maps visual observations `o_t` to compact latent representations `z_t = E(o_t)`.
- **Latent Predictor (F):** An action-conditioned model that predicts the next latent representation `ˆz_{t+1} = F(z_t, a_t)` given the current latent state and an action.

These components are trained end-to-end with a combined loss function.

**C. Training Objectives**
The training process integrates three distinct loss functions:

1. **Latent Prediction Loss (`L_pred`):** This is the foundational JEPA objective. It minimizes the squared Euclidean distance between the predicted next latent state `ˆz_{t+1}` and the directly encoded ground-truth next latent state `z_{t+1}`.
 `L_pred = E_{(o_t,a_t,o_{t+1})~D} [ ||ˆz_{t+1} - z_{t+1}||₂² ]`
 This objective guides the model to learn the forward dynamics in latent space, but is prone to latent collapse if not regularized.

2. **Inverse Dynamics Loss (`L_idm`):** This objective is designed to prevent latent collapse and make latent transitions informative of the actions. An inverse-dynamics head `H` (a two-layer MLP) predicts the action `a_t` from a pair of consecutive latent representations (`z_t`, `z_{t+1}`).
 `L_idm = E_{(o_t,a_t,o_{t+1})~D} [ ||H(z_t, z_{t+1}) - a_t||₂² ]`
 By requiring accurate action prediction from latent state transitions, this loss encourages the visual encoder `E` to produce distinct latent representations for different actions, thereby discouraging collapse.

3. **State Alignment Loss (`L_sa`):** This is the novel contribution of the paper. A state-alignment head `G` (a two-layer MLP) predicts the physical measurements `s_t` from a consecutive latent pair (`z_{t-1}`, `z_t`).
 `L_sa = E_{(o_{t-1},o_t,s_t)~D} [ ||G(z_{t-1}, z_t) - s_t||₂² ]`
 The use of a consecutive latent pair (`z_{t-1}`, `z_t`) provides temporal context, enabling the model to align not just instantaneous physical configurations but also motion-related quantities like velocity measurements with the latent representations. This objective explicitly grounds the latent representations in their associated physical configuration and motion.

The **total training objective** is a weighted sum of these three components:
`L_total = L_pred + αL_sa + βL_idm`
where `α` and `β` are hyperparameters controlling the influence of state alignment and inverse dynamics, respectively. All components (E, F, G, H) are optimized jointly.

**D. Implementation Details**
- **Encoder:** A randomly initialized ViT-Tiny/14 architecture is used, with its [CLS] token projected to a 192-dimensional latent representation.
- **Predictor:** Implemented as a six-layer causal Transformer. It processes a context of up to three recent latent–action pairs to predict future latents. Action chunks are embedded into 192-dimensional vectors and integrated into each predictor layer via zero-initialized adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN).
- **Heads (G and H):** Both `G` and `H` are two-layer MLPs, applied to concatenated representation pairs. `G` has a hidden dimension of 256, and `H` has a hidden dimension of 512.
- **Data Preprocessing:** Euclidean state quantities and actions are standardized using dataset statistics. Angular state quantities are converted to continuous coordinates to mitigate discontinuities from wrap-around effects.
- **Optimization:** The AdamW optimizer is used for joint optimization of all model parameters.

**E. Latent Planning (Deployment)**
During deployment, only the trained encoder `E` and predictor `F` are utilized; the inverse dynamics and state alignment heads (`H` and `G`) are discarded.
1. Given a current observation `o_t` and a goal image `o_g`, their latent representations `z_t` and `z_g` are obtained using `E`.
2. For a candidate action sequence `a_{t:t+K−1}`, the predictor `F` is rolled out recursively to generate a predicted terminal latent representation `ˆz_{t+K}(a)`.
3. Planning involves optimizing this action sequence to minimize the terminal latent distance to the goal: `a* = argmin_{a_{t:t+K−1}} ||ˆz_{t+K}(a) - z_g||₂²`.
4. This optimization is performed using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), which iteratively samples action sequences, evaluates them via latent rollouts, and refines the sampling distribution based on elite candidates.
5. The optimized action sequence is then executed in the environment, and the planning process is repeated from the updated observation until the goal is achieved or the interaction budget is exhausted.

**F. Experimental Setup**
The model was evaluated on four benchmark tasks adopted from LeWorldModel: TwoRoom (visual navigation), Reacher (articulated reaching), PushT (planar object pushing), and OGBench-Cube (robot-arm manipulation). For state alignment, the full physical state provided by each task was used as supervision. The evaluation protocol involved 50 fixed start–goal problems per task, with goals 25 environment steps ahead and an interaction budget of 50 steps. Auxiliary loss weights were set to `α = β = 1.0`, chosen based on performance on the PushT validation set.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings regarding planning performance and the characteristics of the learned latent spaces.

**A. Planning Performance in Latent Space**
The proposed model (SA+IDM) demonstrated competitive or superior planning success rates across the evaluated benchmark tasks when compared to established baselines (DINO-WM, PLDM, LeWorldModel).
- **Highest Success Rates:** SA+IDM achieved the highest success rates on TwoRoom (100%), PushT (98%), and OGBench-Cube (87%).
- **Comparable Performance:** On the Reacher task, SA+IDM performed comparably to LeWorldModel (85% vs. 86%).
- **Contribution of State Alignment:** An ablation study comparing SA+IDM to an IDM-only variant (without state alignment) indicated that adding state alignment consistently improved planning success across all four tasks. For example, on PushT, SA+IDM achieved 98% success, compared to 83% for IDM-only. On OGBench-Cube, SA+IDM reached 87% versus 85% for IDM-only.
- **Qualitative Assessment:** A successful trial on OGBench-Cube qualitatively demonstrated the model's ability to plan and execute actions to reach an image-defined goal within the given budget.

**B. Temporal Straightening**
Temporal straightening is a metric used to quantify the smoothness and local consistency of latent trajectory evolution. It measures the alignment of consecutive latent displacements.
- **Lower Straightening for SA+IDM:** The SA+IDM model exhibited a lower average temporal straightening score (0.55) on OGBench-Cube compared to the IDM-only model (0.62) and LeWorldModel (0.69).
- **Interpretation of Lower Straightening:** The authors suggest that this decrease in straightening for SA+IDM might arise because explicit physical-state supervision causes physical turns and curvature to be more strongly expressed in the latent displacements, leading to reduced cosine alignment between successive displacements. This implies that the latent space, while less "straight," might be more representative of the underlying physical dynamics.
- **Discrepancy with Planning Success:** Notably, LeWorldModel achieved the highest average straightening score but exhibited lower planning success on OGBench-Cube (74%) compared to SA+IDM (87%). This suggests that a high average straightening score alone does not guarantee superior planning performance.
- **Trajectory-Level Observation:** Visualizations of latent trajectories in PCA projections indicated that LeWorldModel often maintained high alignment but displayed isolated near-reversals, leading to scores close to -1. In contrast, IDM and SA+IDM showed more distributed and less extreme directional changes, even if their average straightening was lower.

**C. Transition-Subspace Analysis**
To further investigate the properties of the learned latent spaces, particularly the discrepancy observed with temporal straightening, the authors performed a transition-subspace analysis. This method characterizes the dominant directions of temporal variation in latent space by applying an uncentered SVD to latent displacements (`∆z_t`) and determining `r_95`, the number of components required to retain 95% of the transition energy.
- **LeWorldModel's Low Dimensionality:** LeWorldModel, despite its high straightening, exhibited a substantially lower effective transition dimension, with a mean `r_95` of 17.0 components.
- **Higher Dimensionality for IDM and SA+IDM:** The IDM-only model had a mean `r_95` of 30.2, and SA+IDM showed the highest effective transition dimension with a mean `r_95` of 32.8.
- **Implication:** This finding indicates that LeWorldModel's high straightening score is associated with its temporal variation being concentrated in a significantly lower-dimensional subspace. This suggests that while displacements might be largely aligned, the space they traverse might be restrictive. In contrast, SA+IDM, with its lower straightening but higher effective transition dimension, implies a richer and more expansive latent space for representing diverse actions and physical changes, which could contribute to its superior planning performance. The argument is made that in a rank-one limit, successive displacements can only be parallel or antiparallel, leading to high average straightening with polarized local scores, consistent with LeWorldModel's observed profile.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the field of robotic control and self-supervised learning:

- **Enhanced JEPA World Model Design:** The introduction of pair-based state alignment as a complementary objective to inverse dynamics provides a novel and effective method for grounding latent representations in physical reality. This approach leverages readily available physical state measurements during training to enrich the visual latent space, without requiring these measurements at deployment. This can lead to more informative and robust world models for robotic planning.

- **Improved Goal-Conditioned Robotic Planning:** The consistent improvement in planning success rates across diverse benchmark tasks (TwoRoom, PushT, OGBench-Cube) demonstrates the practical utility of the proposed state alignment mechanism. By explicitly tying latent representations to physical states, the model appears to learn a latent space that is more conducive to effective long-horizon planning from visual goals.

- **Refined Understanding of Latent Space Metrics:** The research challenges the straightforward interpretation of temporal straightening as a universally desirable characteristic for latent spaces in planning. By demonstrating that high straightening can coincide with a substantially lower effective transition dimension and weaker planning performance (as observed with LeWorldModel), the paper highlights the importance of analyzing multiple dimensions of latent space quality. This encourages a more nuanced perspective, suggesting that a richer, higher-dimensional transition subspace might be more beneficial for expressive action representation, even if it results in less "straight" latent trajectories.

- **Guidance for Future Representation Learning:** The findings suggest that future work in self-supervised learning for robotic control should consider both the smoothness/straightness of latent trajectories and the effective dimensionality or expressiveness of the latent transition subspace. The paper explicitly points towards investigating combinations of distributional safeguards (like SIGReg used by LeWorldModel) with physical grounding, aiming to achieve both well-behaved and rich latent spaces.

- **Broader Applicability:** The concept of grounding visual latent representations with corresponding physical measurements during training could be applicable beyond JEPA models, potentially benefiting other types of world models or representation learning techniques in robotics where physical state information is available during data collection.

In summary, this research provides a step "Toward Physically Grounded JEPA World Models," offering a new objective that improves planning performance and contributes to a deeper understanding of the properties that define useful latent representations for autonomous agents.
