Existing methods predominantly adopt either retrieval-oriented or reasoning-oriented paradigms to tackle this challenge, but both are constrained by two fundamental limitations:(1) They apply the same inference pipeline to all samples without accounting for uneven recognition difficulty, thereby leading to suboptimal accuracy and efficiency; (2) The lack of mechanisms to consolidate and reuse error-specific experience causes repeated failures on similar challenging cases. Specifically, SARE adopts a cascaded design that combines fast candidate retrieval with fine-grained reasoning, invoking the latter only when necessary. In the reasoning process, SARE incorporates a self-reflective experience mechanism that leverages past failures to provide transferable discriminative guidance during inference, without any parameter updates. Extensive experiments across 14 datasets substantiate that SARE achieves state-of-the-art performance while substantially reducing computational overhead.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) struggle with Fine-Grained Visual Recognition (FGVR) due to subtle, localized, and easily confounded discriminative cues, exacerbated by large intra-class variations.
- Existing training-free FGVR methods use uniform inference pipelines, applying the same computational intensity regardless of sample difficulty, leading to inefficient resource allocation and suboptimal performance.
- Current LVLM-based FGVR approaches are stateless, lacking mechanisms to consolidate and reuse error-specific experience, which can lead to repeated failures on similar challenging instances.
- SARE employs a cascaded, dual-system design, featuring a fast retrieval module (System 1) for clear cases and an LVLM-based, experience-guided reasoning module (System 2) for ambiguous ones.
- A statistics-based dynamic trigger selectively activates System 2, based on calibrated retrieval confidence, historical class-conditional evidence, and ambiguity among candidate categories.
- A self-reflective experience library is constructed offline from classification errors, distilling transferable discriminative guidance into structured decision rules to inform System 2's reasoning without parameter updates.
- SARE achieved an average accuracy of 87.68% across 12 diverse datasets, surpassing leading training-free methods by over 8% and training-based baselines like VT-FSL by 1.64%.
- The dynamic trigger successfully adapted processing depth, showing high accuracy for samples processed solely by System 1 and efficiently reserving the computationally intensive System 2 for genuinely ambiguous cases.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the self-reflective experience library provided additional accuracy gains, and this learned experience was transferable, enabling strong generalization across different domains and backbones.
- An adaptive inference mechanism, inspired by human dual-system cognition, can efficiently allocate computational resources by matching processing depth to the perceived difficulty of each sample.
- Learning from past classification failures by distilling error-specific insights into reusable experience can enhance reasoning capabilities and improve accuracy without requiring explicit model parameter updates.
- Combining fast, retrieval-based perception with deliberate, experience-guided reasoning within a dynamically triggered framework effectively mitigates both 'overthinking' on easy samples and 'underthinking' on challenging ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual recognition has seen substantial advancements due to Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), which exhibit strong general capabilities across various tasks. However, Fine-Grained Visual Recognition (FGVR), which involves distinguishing visually similar subordinate-level categories (e.g., different dog breeds or bird species), remains a significant challenge for LVLMs. This difficulty arises from the subtle, localized, and easily confounded discriminative cues that characterize fine-grained differences, often exacerbated by large intra-class variations.

Current approaches to training-free FGVR predominantly fall into two paradigms:
1. **Retrieval-oriented methods:** These methods utilize LVLMs to generate fine-grained textual descriptions and perform multimodal matching. They are noted for their efficiency and scalability but often rely on global representations, struggling to capture the localized discriminative features essential for distinguishing highly similar sub-categories. Examples include methods that leverage hierarchical attribute descriptions or external knowledge bases to enrich textual prototypes.
2. **Reasoning-oriented methods:** These approaches reframe FGVR as a multi-choice visual question-answering (VQA) problem, presenting candidate labels as context for the LVLM. While capable of deeper analysis, these methods face issues such as diffused attention, unstable reasoning, hallucination, and performance degradation as the candidate space expands.

Despite their differing strategies, both paradigms share two fundamental limitations:
1. **Uniform inference pipeline:** They apply the same computational intensity to all samples, regardless of varying recognition difficulty. This leads to inefficient resource allocation, wasting computation on easy cases and providing insufficient analysis for challenging ones. For simpler samples, excessive reasoning can introduce "overthinking" and negatively impact performance.
2. **Stateless inference:** Unlike human experts who learn from past mistakes, these methods lack mechanisms to consolidate and reuse error-specific experience, potentially leading to repeated failures on similar challenging instances.

SARE positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified limitations. It proposes a framework that adaptively combines retrieval and reasoning while incorporating a mechanism for learning from past errors, aiming to improve both accuracy and efficiency in training-free FGVR. The work draws inspiration from human cognitive processes, specifically the dual-system mechanism (rapid, intuitive judgments vs. deliberate, nuanced reasoning) and the ability to distill experience from errors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the SARE framework are to:

1. **Develop a sample-wise adaptive inference mechanism:** This aims to overcome the limitation of uniform inference pipelines by dynamically adjusting the processing depth for each sample based on its perceived difficulty. The goal is to efficiently allocate computational resources, applying fast processing to easy cases and more deliberate reasoning to ambiguous ones.
2. **Incorporate a self-reflective experience mechanism:** This objective addresses the stateless nature of existing methods by enabling the system to learn from past classification failures. The motivation is to distill transferable discriminative guidance from these errors, preventing repeated mistakes on similar challenging cases without requiring explicit parameter updates.
3. **Achieve state-of-the-art performance and computational efficiency:** By integrating adaptive reasoning and experience-guided decision-making, SARE aims to surpass existing training-free FGVR methods in accuracy while substantially reducing computational overhead, particularly for straightforward samples.

These objectives are motivated by direct observations of the limitations in current training-free FGVR approaches. The authors note that FGVR tasks involve instances of widely varying difficulty; some can be resolved with coarse visual cues, while others demand careful inspection of subtle attributes. Existing methods fail to account for this variability, leading to suboptimal performance and efficiency. Furthermore, the absence of an error-learning mechanism in current LVLM-based FGVR models stands in contrast to human learning, where expertise is refined through accumulating and applying insights from mistakes.

To address these, SARE draws inspiration from two human cognitive principles:
- **Dual-system cognition:** Humans employ a System 1 for rapid, intuitive judgments and a System 2 for deliberate, nuanced reasoning when faced with complex or ambiguous situations. SARE conceptualizes fast image-text retrieval as System 1, suitable for clear cases, and LVLM-based nuanced reasoning as System 2, reserved for ambiguous examples requiring focused attention on discriminative details.
- **Experience-driven learning:** Humans continuously distill transferable decision criteria from errors. SARE integrates a self-reflective module to mimic this, converting past classification failures into reusable discriminative guidance for future inference.

The motivation is to create a more intelligent and resource-efficient FGVR system that can adapt its inference strategy to the specific demands of each sample and continuously improve its performance through accumulated experience, mirroring human expert behavior.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SARE (Sample-wise Adaptive REasoning) is designed as a training-free framework for FGVR, addressing uneven sample difficulty and the stateless nature of prior methods. It employs a cascaded design involving a fast retrieval module (System 1), a statistics-driven trigger, and an experience-guided reasoning module (System 2).

#### 3.1. Knowledge Base Construction

To facilitate adaptive inference without parameter updates, SARE constructs three lightweight and reusable knowledge bases offline from a small *k*-shot sub-category support set (D_kshot):

- **Multimodal Prototype Library:** This library contains compact, stable representations for efficient retrieval. For each category *c* in D_kshot, a visual prototype is computed by averaging CLIP image embeddings, and a textual prototype is encoded from an LVLM-generated fine-grained description of the category. These prototypes are used by System 1 for fast matching.
- **Statistical Retrieval Library:** This library captures class-conditional retrieval behavior to calibrate confidence scores, which can vary significantly across sub-categories. Retrieval is performed on D_kshot using the multimodal prototypes, recording the retrieval count (*n_c*) and the number of successful matches (*N*) for each category. These statistics serve as class-conditional priors for uncertainty estimation in the dynamic trigger.
- **Self-Reflective Experience Library:** This library consolidates past errors into reusable discriminative guidance. When an incorrect prediction occurs on D_kshot, a retrospective analysis identifies overlooked discriminative cues from the reasoning trajectory. These cues are abstracted into compact, structured decision rules and stored as experience entries. A lightweight maintenance policy merges semantically complementary rules, filters redundant ones, and replaces low-value entries to keep the library compact and informative. During inference, relevant experience entries are retrieved to guide reasoning in challenging scenarios without parameter updates.

#### 3.2. Sample-wise Adaptive Inference

SARE decouples fast perception from nuanced reasoning to handle varying sample difficulty:

- **System 1: Fast Retrieval-based Perception:** This is a lightweight module that performs nearest-neighbor matching between the query image and the multimodal prototypes (P_v and P_t). It computes similarity scores with minimal computational overhead. System 1 serves two purposes:
 1. Enabling an early exit for samples with clear decision boundaries.
 2. Producing a compact Top-K_c candidate set and similarity-based confidence scores, which are used by the dynamic trigger.

- **System 2: Experience-guided Nuanced Reasoning:** This module is activated only for ambiguous cases with low retrieval confidence. It formulates recognition as a VQA inference. System 2 takes the input image (*I*), the Top-K_c candidate set (*C*) from System 1, and a small set of retrieved experience entries (*E*) as contextual input to the LVLM. The LVLM then provides a step-by-step reasoning chain and a final prediction. By constraining the candidate space and injecting structured experience, System 2 is guided to focus on discriminative fine-grained cues.

#### 3.3. Statistics-based Dynamic Trigger

The dynamic trigger is the decision-making core, controlling the transition from System 1 to System 2. Its goal is to selectively allocate expensive computational resources to genuinely ambiguous cases. It addresses three conditions where System 1 may fail: calibration mismatch, category-dependent confidence bias, and high ambiguity among candidates.

1. **Cross-modal calibration:** Visual (P_img) and textual (P_text) similarities are normalized using temperature-scaled Softmax and linearly fused to obtain P_fuse = λP_img + (1 − λ)P_text (where λ=0.3).
2. **Robust Rank Fusion:** To account for poor calibration, Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) is incorporated. For each category *c*, an RRF score R(c) is computed based on its rank in visual and textual modalities.
3. **Final Retrieval Confidence:** The fused probability and RRF score are combined: ˆp_c = P_fuse(c) + β ⋅ R(c) (where β=0.1).
4. **Trigger Score:** A trigger score G(c) for the Top-1 candidate is calculated:
 G(c) = ˆp_c − η_r log(N / (2*n_c)) − αH(p_c)
 This score incorporates:
 - ˆp_c: Fused retrieval confidence.
 - Second term: A penalty derived from Hoeffding’s inequality that penalizes categories with limited historical retrieval evidence (*n_c*), preventing overconfident decisions.
 - Third term H(p_c): Measures ambiguity (entropy) among the Top-K_c candidates, indicating System 1's inability to clearly separate hypotheses.

If G(c) exceeds a predefined threshold θ, System 1's prediction is accepted; otherwise, System 2 is activated for fine-grained reasoning. This mechanism allows SARE to adapt computation based on perceived difficulty.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experimental validation across 14 datasets, comprising 7 fine-grained, 5 general recognition, and 2 out-of-distribution datasets.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

SARE achieved an average accuracy of 87.68% across all 12 main datasets, demonstrating performance gains compared to baseline methods. It reportedly outperformed leading training-free methods by over 8% and training-based baselines such as FineDefics by 15.88% and VT-FSL by 1.64%. The improvements were particularly evident on fine-grained datasets like Aircraft, Birdsnap, and Dogs, which require discernment of subtle inter-class differences. SARE exhibited consistent high performance across diverse domains, suggesting robust cross-domain generalization.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Dynamic Trigger

Analysis of the dynamic trigger demonstrated its ability to adapt processing depth to sample difficulty. The activation rate of System 2 varied dynamically, increasing for more challenging samples and remaining low for easier ones. For samples deemed sufficiently easy by the trigger (and thus processed only by System 1), the recognition accuracy was consistently high across datasets (Table 5), indicating that the trigger effectively identifies cases where simpler retrieval-based perception is adequate.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments confirmed the contribution of each SARE component:
- Relying solely on retrieval-based System 1 yielded an accuracy of 58.24% on the Stanford Dogs dataset, indicating limitations in capturing subtle fine-grained differences.
- Incorporating reasoning-based System 2 boosted performance to 76.65%, highlighting its capability for deeper analysis and attribute localization.
- The addition of the self-reflective experience library provided a further accuracy gain, contributing to the final reported accuracy of 84.29% on Stanford Dogs, suggesting that distilled experience enhances the model's ability to focus on discriminative attributes.

#### 4.4. Hyperparameter Analysis

SARE demonstrated stability across a range of key hyperparameters:
- **Candidate set size (K_c):** Performance improved as K_c increased, peaking around K_c = 10, then slightly declining due to potential introduction of redundant alternatives.
- **Experience library capacity (E):** An optimal performance was observed at E = 8, with smaller values leading to insufficient guidance and larger values introducing semantic noise.
- **k-shot size (D_kshot):** SARE exhibited robustness to variations in *k*-shot size, with accuracy fluctuating minimally (83%–86%) for *k*-shot values between 1 and 10. This indicates the framework's ability to distill knowledge from minimal examples.
- **Sampling strategies for D_kshot:** SARE showed stable performance (within ~2% variation) across six different sampling strategies (Random, Diversity, Confusion, Centroid, Entropy, Boundary), suggesting low sensitivity to the specific composition of the support set. Strategies prioritizing informative or difficult samples (Boundary, Entropy) yielded slightly higher accuracy.

#### 4.5. Performance on Different Backbones

SARE exhibited model-agnostic robustness, consistently enhancing performance across various visual (VLM) and reasoning (LVLM) backbones. The relative performance gains were more pronounced for weaker backbones (e.g., CLIP RN50 and RN101) and smaller LVLMs, indicating that SARE effectively complements limited perceptual or reasoning capacities rather than relying on architecture-specific characteristics.

#### 4.6. Transferability Analysis

Cross-domain evaluations on ImageNet-V2 and ImageNet-Sketch demonstrated the transferability of the accumulated experience. SARE with an experience library constructed on ImageNet-1K (SARE_src) achieved performance comparable to a target-domain specific experience library (SARE_tgt), with marginal accuracy degradation. This suggests that the learned experience captures transferable discriminative cues, enabling generalization across distribution and style shifts.

#### 4.7. Behavioral Analysis

A controlled study on the Stanford Dogs dataset assessed how SARE mitigates "overthinking" on easy samples and "underthinking" on hard ones. System 1's performance declined with increasing difficulty, while System 2, when applied uniformly, showed a counterintuitive accuracy increase for moderately hard samples, attributed to overreasoning on easier examples. SARE consistently maintained the highest accuracy across all difficulty levels, demonstrating its ability to adapt and avoid these pitfalls.

#### 4.8. Qualitative Analysis

A qualitative case study (Appendix E, Figure 10) illustrated the process of experience construction and its application. It showed how the model distills generalized discriminative rules from past misclassifications (e.g., "Prioritize morphological features over coat color") and how these rules guide System 2 to refocus on critical attributes (e.g., ear morphology) for correct classification of ambiguous samples. Attention heatmaps (Figure 11) further visualized how SARE influences model decisions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SARE framework contributes to the field of Fine-Grained Visual Recognition (FGVR) by introducing a sample-wise adaptive reasoning paradigm that addresses critical limitations of existing training-free LVLM-based methods. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Accuracy and Efficiency:** SARE's core contribution is its ability to dynamically adapt its inference strategy based on sample difficulty. By intelligently routing easy samples through a fast retrieval path (System 1) and reserving expensive LVLM-based reasoning (System 2) for truly ambiguous cases, it achieves improved recognition accuracy while substantially reducing computational overhead. This efficiency is crucial for deploying LVLMs in resource-constrained environments or applications requiring rapid decision-making. The reported performance gains, especially over both training-free and some training-based baselines, suggest a notable advancement in FGVR capabilities without the need for extensive training.

2. **Robustness and Generalizability:** The framework demonstrates consistent performance across a diverse set of 14 datasets, encompassing fine-grained, general recognition, and out-of-distribution tasks. This indicates strong cross-domain generalization and robustness to varying data characteristics. Its model-agnostic nature, showing consistent improvements across different VLM and LVLM backbones, suggests that SARE provides complementary benefits that are not tied to specific architectural choices.

3. **Introduction of Experience-Guided Learning:** The self-reflective experience mechanism is a key innovation, allowing the system to learn from past errors and distill transferable discriminative guidance. This mechanism enables continuous improvement of the model's reasoning capabilities without parameter updates, moving beyond the traditional stateless inference paradigm. The demonstrated transferability of this learned experience across different domains highlights its potential to build more intelligent and adaptable FGVR systems.

4. **Mitigation of Overthinking and Underthinking:** SARE's dynamic trigger effectively addresses the issues of "overthinking" on simple samples and "underthinking" on complex ones. By calibrating confidence scores and incorporating measures of historical difficulty and candidate ambiguity, the trigger ensures that computational resources are allocated judiciously, leading to more reliable and appropriate analysis for each specific instance.

The potential impact of SARE extends to various real-world applications where fine-grained distinctions are critical, such as species identification in biology, product recognition in e-commerce, disease diagnosis in medicine, or quality control in manufacturing. By providing a more accurate, efficient, and robust training-free FGVR solution, SARE could facilitate the broader adoption of LVLMs in these specialized domains, enabling more precise visual analysis without the substantial data annotation and training costs typically associated with fine-tuning deep learning models.

However, the authors also acknowledge limitations, such as the potential for increased computational overhead during the experience derivation process and the conservative nature of the trigger on very challenging datasets. Future work aiming to refine the efficiency of experience generation and improve the accuracy of the dynamic trigger could further enhance SARE's impact. Additionally, exploring the integration of visual features for reasoning, beyond textual context, presents a direction for further development.
