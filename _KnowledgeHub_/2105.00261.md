# DeepMultiCap: Performance Capture of Multiple Characters Using Sparse Multiview Cameras

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepMultiCap captures multiple interacting people from sparse multi-view RGB video without pre-scanned templates. It fuses pixel-aligned implicit functions with an SMPL parametric prior, merges views via a self-attention module instead of mean pooling, and smooths video results by averaging warped signed distance fields. On the new MultiHuman dataset it beats PIFu, PIFuHD and PaMIR, reaching 1.134 Chamfer on closely interacting pairs.

## Problem

- Dense camera rigs, controlled lighting, RGBD sensors or pre-scanned templates make prior high-fidelity performance capture setups expensive and impractical for everyday multi-person use.
- Pixel-aligned implicit methods target single humans and degrade badly under the severe occlusions of close multi-person interaction, where each view observes only fragments of a body.
- Mean-pooling of multi-view embeddings over-smooths high-frequency cues like normal maps, and no high-quality multi-person interactive scan dataset existed for quantitative evaluation.
- Loose clothing such as wind coats and non-human objects remain outside the SMPL-anchored reconstruction scope.

## Method

- A ==self-attention== multi-view fusion module embeds stacked view features into query/source/target with multi-head weights, upweighting salient covisible details and suppressing occluded views instead of averaging.
- A coarse-to-fine ==pixel-aligned== network conditions the coarse level on **SMPL** volumetric semantic features and the fine level on rendered SMPL global normal maps plus 512x512 imagery.
- Temporal fusion warps reconstructed vertices across frames by SMPL blend skinning with distance-decayed vertex weights, then mean-pools the ==signed distance fields== over a 3-frame sliding window.

## Results

- On MultiHuman with 6 input views, the method reaches Chamfer **1.134** on closely interacting pairs and **1.130** on three-person scenes, versus **1.400**/**1.414** for PaMIR and **1.614**/**1.814** for PIFuHD.
- Ablations show the attention module and SMPL global normal maps both matter: removing them raises closely-interacting Chamfer from **1.134** to **1.264** and **1.357** respectively.
- Adding attention to baselines helps too, cutting PIFuHD single-person Chamfer from **0.914** to **0.845**, while PIFu gains only marginally, and real-world ZJU-MoCap comparisons favor the method over DeepVisualHull and Neural Body.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learned attention over views beats mean pooling precisely when views disagree, since averaging destroys the high-frequency normal-map detail that only some viewpoints observe.
> - A parametric body prior acts as a geometry hallucinator for unobserved regions, trading a little single-person accuracy for large robustness gains under occlusion.
> - Prior-driven pipelines inherit their prior's failures: poor SMPL fits produce artifacts, and loose garments or non-human objects fall outside what the proxy can represent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of vision-based human performance capture aims to reconstruct realistic 3D human geometry and motion from visual input. Early advancements in this area often relied on dense camera rigs and specialized lighting systems, which, while capable of achieving high-fidelity results, are prohibitively expensive and complex for widespread adoption. More lightweight multi-view systems have emerged, but often depend on pre-scanned template models, custom RGBD or commercial RGBD sensors, or are limited to single-person reconstruction scenarios.

A significant recent development has been the application of deep implicit functions for 3D representations, such as PIFu (Pixel-Aligned Implicit Function) and PIFuHD. These methods represent 3D surfaces as level sets of an occupancy field function, offering an efficient way to capture geometric details compared to traditional voxel-based or mesh-based representations. PIFu and PIFuHD have demonstrated promising results for single-person 3D reconstruction from RGB images, with PIFuHD further enhancing detail using high-resolution inputs and normal maps.

However, existing implicit function-based methods like PIFu and PIFuHD encounter limitations when extended to multi-person scenarios and sparse multi-view setups. Their average-pooling-based multi-view feature fusion strategy tends to produce over-smoothed outputs and struggles to maintain high-frequency details, especially when dealing with inconsistencies arising from varying depths and partial observations across views. Critically, these methods exhibit significant performance deterioration in multi-person scenes characterized by severe occlusions, where a lack of sufficient observations impedes accurate reconstruction.

This work, DeepMultiCap, aims to address these identified limitations. It positions itself within the implicit function paradigm but specifically targets the challenges of multi-person performance capture using sparse multi-view cameras. By integrating an attention-aware module and parametric body models (SMPL) within a pixel-aligned implicit function framework, DeepMultiCap seeks to robustly reconstruct detailed 3D human shapes even under challenging occlusion conditions. Furthermore, it introduces a temporal fusion method to improve consistency in video-based dynamic reconstructions and contributes a dedicated multi-person dataset to facilitate rigorous evaluation, thereby advancing the capabilities of accessible 3D human performance capture systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel and robust method, named DeepMultiCap, for high-fidelity multi-person performance capture using a sparse multi-view camera setup. This overarching goal is driven by several specific objectives and motivations aimed at overcoming limitations in existing performance capture technologies.

One key objective is to capture time-varying surface details without relying on pre-scanned template models. Many prior light-weight multi-view systems achieve real-time performance but often necessitate template models, which restricts their applicability to novel subjects or dynamic clothing. DeepMultiCap seeks to eliminate this dependency, allowing for more generalizable capture.

A significant motivation stems from the challenges posed by severe occlusions in close-interacting multi-person scenes. Current implicit function methods, such as PIFu and PIFuHD, struggle in such scenarios due to their multi-view feature fusion strategies that can lead to information loss and over-smoothed results when observations are partial or inconsistent. DeepMultiCap aims to overcome this by robustly reconstructing invisible surface areas, leveraging both image-based features and 3D prior information.

To achieve high-fidelity reconstruction, the method also aims to obtain fine-grained geometry details from sparse multi-view images. The limitations of simple feature aggregation techniques used in previous methods motivate the development of a more effective mechanism for adaptively merging information from different viewpoints.

For video inputs, a further objective is to alleviate noise and temporal inconsistencies that can arise during the reconstruction of moving characters. Dynamic performances require not only accurate per-frame geometry but also smooth and consistent transitions over time.

Finally, the researchers identify a critical gap in the community: the lack of high-quality, ground truth 3D scans of multi-person interactive scenarios. This absence hinders the accurate evaluation and development of multi-person performance capture systems. A key objective and motivation for this work is to contribute a novel dataset, MultiHuman, specifically designed for multi-person interactive scenes to address this data scarcity. This dataset is intended to enable comprehensive quantitative and qualitative evaluation of DeepMultiCap and facilitate future research in the field.

In summary, the motivation is to make high-quality, detailed 3D multi-person performance capture more accessible by moving away from expensive, dense setups and template dependencies, while simultaneously addressing the critical technical challenges of occlusion, detail preservation, and temporal consistency in sparse multi-view, multi-person environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeepMultiCap's methodology integrates several components within a coarse-to-fine framework based on pixel-aligned implicit functions, specifically designed to address multi-person performance capture from sparse multi-view cameras. The system pipeline initiates with preprocessing steps followed by a core reconstruction network and a temporal refinement module.

#### 3.1. Preprocessing and Input Generation

The system takes segmented multi-view single-person images and corresponding SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear Model) parameters as input.
1. **SMPL-X Fitting:** SMPL-X models are estimated by fitting to 3D keypoints derived from multi-view video using a light-weight total capture method. This provides an initial pose and shape prior for each individual.
2. **Multi-person Segmentation:** A self-correction method is employed for multi-person image segmentation, utilizing SMPL projection maps to track distinct characters across multi-view scenes. This isolates each person for individual processing.

#### 3.2. Single Person Reconstruction

The core of the reconstruction process for each individual operates on a two-level, coarse-to-fine framework, building upon the principles of PIFuHD.

- **Implicit Function Foundation:** The method represents the 3D human surface as a level set of an occupancy field function, conditioned on image features and 3D point information. The coarse level provides an initial reconstruction, which is then refined by the fine level network utilizing higher-resolution image features.

- **Attention-aware Multi-view Feature Fusion:**
 - To overcome the limitations of simple mean-pooling for multi-view feature aggregation (which can lead to over-smoothing and loss of detail, especially with inconsistencies between views or occlusions), DeepMultiCap introduces a self-attention mechanism.
 - Multi-view features from *n* observations are stacked and processed by a self-attention encoder. This module learns to adaptively weight and aggregate features by computing similarities between query and source features (derived from the stacked multi-view features) to generate an output meta-view feature.
 - A multi-head attention strategy is used, allowing the model to project features into different embedding subspaces to capture diverse geometric patterns. This ensures that salient co-visible details receive higher weights, while features from occluded or less informative regions have reduced influence.

- **Embedding with Parametric Body Model (SMPL):**
 - To enhance robustness against occlusions and provide a reasonable prior for areas with missing visual information, the attention module is combined with the parametric SMPL model.
 - SMPL's semantic features (pose and shape information) are extracted using a 3D convolution network for geometric inference.
 - An informative view representation is generated by rendering SMPL global normal maps. These maps, rendered from the canonical SMPL model space, serve as visual references to guide the attention network in identifying visible body parts across different views.
 - The coarse-level network integrates these SMPL semantic features, while the fine-level network incorporates the rendered SMPL global normal maps alongside predicted frontal normal maps and high-resolution image features.
 - Reconstruction is performed in a normalized SMPL model space (a unit cube), and the results are then transformed back to the world space and aligned.

#### 3.3. Temporal Fusion

For dynamic character capture from video sequences, a temporal fusion method is proposed to enhance consistency between successive frames.
- **Mesh Warping:** Given SMPL models for consecutive frames, reconstructed mesh vertices from one frame are warped to the time of another frame using standard blend skinning.
- **SDF Averaging:** The signed distance field (SDF) of these warped meshes is calculated. A mean pooling operation is then applied to the SDFs across a sliding time window (set to 3 frames in this work) to generate a temporally smoothed and consistent reconstruction.

#### 3.4. Extension to Multi-person Reconstruction

Multi-person scenes are handled by reconstructing each individual separately using the described single-person method. The 3D spatial relationship between individuals is maintained by the multi-view camera setup, allowing direct combination of individual reconstructions.

#### 3.5. Training Data Augmentation

To train the network for robustness against occlusions in interactive scenes, a large-scale dataset of 1700 single human models (from Twindom and THuman2.0) is utilized. Images are rendered, and artificial occlusions are simulated by randomly projecting other persons onto the masks, generating diverse occlusion scenarios.

#### 3.6. MultiHuman Dataset Contribution

To facilitate quantitative evaluation, the authors created the MultiHuman dataset, comprising 150 static multi-person scenes (1 to 3 persons per scene, 278 characters total) captured using a dense camera rig with 128 DSLRs and commercial photogrammetry software. This dataset includes various levels of occlusion and interaction, serving as a benchmark for multi-person performance capture systems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the performance of DeepMultiCap, supported by ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Performance on MultiHuman Dataset

Quantitative evaluation on the newly introduced MultiHuman dataset, categorized by increasing levels of occlusion and number of persons, indicates that DeepMultiCap consistently outperforms prior state-of-the-art methods, including PIFu, PIFuHD, and PaMIR (PIFu + SMPL). The evaluation metrics used are point-to-surface distance and Chamfer distance.
- DeepMultiCap shows lower error values across all categories: single human, occluded single human, two natural-interactive persons, two closely-interactive persons, and three persons.
- The performance gap between DeepMultiCap and prior methods becomes more pronounced as the complexity of occlusions and interactions increases, suggesting improved robustness in challenging scenarios. For instance, in "three person" scenes, DeepMultiCap's Chamfer distance is 1.130 compared to 1.780 for PIFu and 1.814 for PIFuHD.

Qualitative results illustrate the method's ability to reconstruct highly detailed 3D human models even under close interactive conditions with significant occlusions, generating outputs that are closer to the ground truth geometry compared to other methods.

#### 4.2. Performance on Real World Data

Evaluation on the ZJU-MoCap dataset, a multi-view real-world dataset, shows that DeepMultiCap achieves performance exceeding DeepVisualHull, Neural Body, and PIFuHD. This indicates the method's generalization capacity to real-world multi-view video data beyond the synthetic training data and the MultiHuman dataset.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to determine the contribution of key components:

- **Self-attention Module:**
 - Integrating the self-attention module into baseline models (PIFu + Att, PIFuHD + Att) yielded improved performance compared to their mean-pooling counterparts. The improvement was more significant for PIFuHD + Att, suggesting its effectiveness in processing detailed geometric information from normal maps.
 - Removing the attention module from DeepMultiCap ("Ours w/o Att") resulted in a notable degradation of performance across all occlusion levels (e.g., Chamfer distance increased from 0.895 to 1.015 for single human), confirming its role in capturing and preserving geometry details from multiple views.

- **Use of SMPL (Global Normal Maps):**
 - Comparing DeepMultiCap with and without the designed SMPL global normal maps ("Ours w/o SN") showed that their exclusion led to lower reconstruction accuracy (e.g., Chamfer distance increased from 0.895 to 1.063 for single human). This indicates that the SMPL global normal maps effectively guide the attention network in merging multi-view information, providing visual references that improve robustness against occlusions and detail preservation. The results also suggest that the combination of attention and SMPL, rather than just SMPL, is crucial for the observed advantages, as indicated by the larger gap between DeepMultiCap and PaMIR.

- **Temporal Fusion:**
 - Qualitative assessment on real-world image sequences demonstrated that the temporal fusion method enhances reconstruction consistency between continuous frames, reducing temporal inconsistencies that might arise from changing visible parts.

- **Robustness against Occlusions:**
 - Both quantitative and qualitative results indicate that DeepMultiCap maintains high accuracy as occlusions increase. In scenarios with heavy occlusion where body parts are invisible in some views, the method is able to generate smooth and plausible results for the missing regions.

#### 4.4. Limitations

The study acknowledges limitations:
- The method is currently restricted to human subjects due to its reliance on the SMPL parametric model as a 3D reference. It cannot reconstruct other objects.
- Reconstruction quality can be unstable for loose clothing items, such as a wind coat, although it performs well for tight-fitting garments.
- The system is dependent on well-fitted SMPL inputs; inaccurate SMPL parameters can lead to artifacts and reconstruction failures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DeepMultiCap represents an advancement in the field of 3D human performance capture, particularly for challenging multi-person and occluded scenarios using sparse multi-view camera setups.

**Advancement in Performance Capture Capabilities:** The method addresses critical limitations of existing implicit function-based approaches by demonstrating robust and high-fidelity reconstruction of multiple interacting characters, even under severe occlusions. This capability is significant because it moves beyond the constraints of dense camera rigs, expensive equipment, or reliance on pre-scanned templates, making advanced performance capture more accessible. The ability to accurately reconstruct dynamic, detailed surfaces without templates from sparse views is a notable step forward.

**Methodological Contributions:** DeepMultiCap introduces several key methodological innovations:
- **Attention-aware Feature Fusion:** The design of an efficient self-attention module for multi-view feature aggregation provides a robust mechanism for extracting and merging fine-grained geometric details, particularly in the presence of view inconsistencies or partial observations.
- **Integrated Parametric Model Prior:** The strategic combination of pixel-aligned implicit functions with parametric human body models (SMPL), especially through the use of SMPL global normal maps, offers a robust solution for compensating missing information due to occlusions. This integration leverages the semantic knowledge of human anatomy to guide geometric inference.
- **Temporal Consistency for Videos:** The proposed temporal fusion method addresses the challenge of frame-to-frame inconsistency in video sequences, delivering smoother and more plausible dynamic 3D reconstructions.

**Dataset Contribution:** The creation and public release of the MultiHuman dataset is a substantial contribution to the research community. By providing high-quality, ground truth 3D scans of diverse multi-person interactive scenes, it fills a critical gap in available benchmarks for training and evaluating multi-person performance capture systems. This resource is expected to accelerate future research and development in related areas.

**Potential Impact and Applications:** The capabilities of DeepMultiCap could facilitate a range of applications that require realistic 3D human avatars and performances:
- **Tele-presence and Virtual/Augmented Reality:** Enabling more immersive and lifelike digital representations of users.
- **Gaming and Animation:** Streamlining the creation of detailed character models and animations, reducing manual effort and cost.
- **Sportscasting and Analysis:** Providing detailed 3D performance analysis in sports from fewer camera viewpoints.
- **Digital Twins:** Contributing to the creation of high-fidelity digital representations of individuals for various purposes.

**Future Research Directions:** The work also highlights areas for future investigation, such as simplifying the overall pipeline (e.g., autonomous camera calibration, implicit human template learning) and developing lightweight networks for real-time inference. These future developments would further enhance the applicability and practicality of such systems.

In summary, DeepMultiCap advances the state of the art in performance capture by providing a robust, detailed, and more accessible solution for multi-person scenarios, underscored by its innovative architectural components and a valuable dataset contribution, paving the way for broader adoption in research and industry.
