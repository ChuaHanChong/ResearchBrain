# WoCoCo: Learning Whole-Body Humanoid Control with Sequential Contacts

> [!summary] Summary
> WoCoCo presents a unified reinforcement learning framework for learning whole-body humanoid control across diverse, complex multi-contact tasks. It achieves robust real-world performance on activities like parkour jumping, loco-manipulation, and climbing, using end-to-end policies that do not rely on motion priors.

## Problem

- Traditional model-based motion planning for complex multi-contact humanoid tasks is computationally intensive, time-consuming, and susceptible to real-world model inaccuracies and environmental uncertainties.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for multi-contact humanoid control often require tedious task-specific reward engineering, intricate finite state machines, struggle with long-horizon exploration, and suffer from sparse contact rewards.
- Robustly transferring learned policies from simulation to real-world humanoid hardware (sim-to-real gap) remains a significant challenge, often requiring extensive domain randomization that can burden exploration.

## Method

- Decomposition of complex, long-horizon tasks into a series of sequential 'contact stages,' each defined by precise contact and task goals, which the robot must fulfill sequentially.
- A novel, largely task-agnostic reward function combining dense contact rewards, stage count rewards for long-horizon progress, and a count-based curiosity reward using a random neural network hash for efficient exploration.
- A robust three-stage training curriculum for sim-to-real transfer, incrementally introducing domain randomization and gradually increasing regularization terms to ensure stable and reliable real-world deployment.

## Results

- WoCoCo enabled end-to-end reinforcement learning policies to successfully perform four challenging whole-body humanoid tasks in the real world: versatile parkour jumping, anywhere-to-anywhere box loco-manipulation, dynamic clap-and-tap dancing, and bidirectional cliffside climbing, all without relying on motion priors.
- The learned policies demonstrated strong robustness against external perturbations and adaptability to varying environmental conditions across all demonstrated tasks.
- The framework generalized to a 22-DoF dinosaur robot for a ball loco-manipulation task, showcasing its universality beyond humanoid specific kinematics and embodiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense contact rewards are crucial for guiding exploration and learning in tasks with sparse contact events, preventing policies from failing to initiate complex actions.
> - Stage count rewards effectively address the long-horizon exploration problem by incentivizing agents to progress through sequential stages, overcoming 'shortsighted' exploitation of early-stage rewards.
> - A count-based curiosity mechanism, using a random neural network hash on task-agnostic observations, enables stable exploration without leading to problematic or risky behaviors seen with some other curiosity methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of operating in human-centric environments necessitates proficient execution of tasks involving sequential physical contacts, such as walking, climbing, or manipulating objects. This research directly addresses a fundamental challenge in achieving such complex behaviors, positioning itself at the frontier of robotics by integrating and advancing both traditional control methods and modern reinforcement learning (RL) techniques.

Historically, complex multi-contact humanoid activities have been primarily approached using **model-based motion planning and trajectory optimization**. These methods excel at synthesizing precise, intricate motions by leveraging detailed mathematical models of robot dynamics. They can generate highly controlled movements for specific contact plans. However, these traditional approaches come with significant limitations: they are often computationally intensive and time-consuming, making real-time adaptation challenging. Crucially, they heavily rely on simplified or reduced-order dynamics models, which can lead to inaccuracies and compromised real-world performance due to inevitable model mismatch and environmental uncertainties. For example, impressive humanoid demonstrations by Boston Dynamics, while highly dynamic, are often generated through offline optimization and pre-computed behavior libraries, limiting their on-the-fly versatility.

In contrast, **model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL)** has emerged as a powerful paradigm in robotics, demonstrating remarkable robustness against model uncertainties and enabling real-time, agile locomotion for various legged robots. However, applying RL to whole-body humanoid control with specific, sequential contacts has presented its own set of formidable challenges. Existing RL works largely focus on standard locomotion without specific contact sequences, or they are heavily tuned for highly specific scenarios, lacking generalizability. For tasks requiring structured contact sequences (e.g., loco-manipulation or soccer playing), current RL approaches often demand tedious task-specific reward engineering, intricate finite state machine designs with separate policies for each transition, and struggle with long-horizon exploration due to sparse rewards. Furthermore, the robust transfer of policies from simulation to real-world hardware (sim-to-real) remains a persistent hurdle, typically addressed by domain randomization and regularization, which can paradoxically complicate the learning process by burdening exploration.

The authors critically assess this landscape, identifying a crucial missing piece: a "systematic and general method for controlling real-world humanoids under desired contact sequences using RL." They pose the central question of how to achieve the simplicity and adaptability of model-based solvers—which can address multiple tasks with minor cost adjustments—within an RL framework, while retaining RL's inherent robustness. WoCoCo directly positions itself to fill this void. By proposing a unified RL framework that is significantly more general, adaptable, and robust than prior RL efforts for multi-contact humanoid control, WoCoCo represents a pivotal step towards enabling humanoids to perform sophisticated, long-horizon tasks involving dynamic and sequential physical interactions in complex, real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to introduce **WoCoCo (Whole-Body Control with Sequential Contacts)**, a novel and unified reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to enable robust and versatile whole-body humanoid control in tasks that inherently involve sequential contacts with the environment. The authors aim to empower humanoids to perform complex, dynamic activities in real-world scenarios requiring precise and varied physical interactions.

The motivation for developing WoCoCo is rooted in a critical evaluation of the limitations inherent in existing control paradigms for humanoids:

- **Addressing the Inflexibility of Model-Based Methods:** Traditional model-based motion planning, while capable of producing complex motions, suffers from computational slowness and a reliance on simplified dynamics models. This leads to motions that often lack real-world robustness due to model inaccuracies and environmental uncertainties, limiting their applicability in diverse, unstructured settings. The authors are motivated to find an alternative that can deliver high-quality, robust motions without these restrictive assumptions.
- **Overcoming Specialization in Current RL Approaches:** Despite RL's success in robust locomotion, its application to complex humanoid tasks involving specific, sequential contacts has faced significant hurdles:
 - **Tedious Task-Specific Engineering:** Existing RL solutions typically demand extensive, manual tuning of rewards and the design of complex state machines, often requiring separate policies for different task segments. This severely limits their scalability and generalizability to new or longer-horizon tasks. A key motivation is to significantly reduce this arduous manual effort.
 - **Long-Horizon Exploration Challenges:** Tasks with sequential contacts are inherently long-horizon and feature sparse rewards (i.e., success is only defined at the end of a complex sequence). RL agents often struggle with this, exhibiting shortsightedness and failing to explore adequately, preferring to exploit "safe" intermediate states rather than risking failure by pursuing distant goals.
 - **Sparse Contact Rewards:** The precise nature of contact tasks means successful contact events are rare. This makes it difficult for RL agents to receive sufficient positive feedback to guide their learning effectively.
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer Difficulties:** Bridging the simulation-reality gap remains a critical bottleneck. While techniques like domain randomization help, they can introduce additional exploration burdens during training.
- **Achieving Generality and Adaptability akin to Model-Based Solvers:** A central driving question for the authors is: "How can we achieve such simplicity and adaptability with an RL framework?" They note that a single model-based solver can often be adapted to multiple tasks with minor adjustments. Their motivation is to imbue RL with a similar level of generality, making it a more practical and versatile tool for complex humanoid control.

In summary, the key objectives of WoCoCo are to:
1. **Develop a General Framework:** Propose an RL framework that inherently decomposes complex multi-contact tasks into sequential contact stages, thereby enabling a unified and streamlined policy learning pipeline across various tasks.
2. **Enhance Exploration Efficiency:** Design novel, effective reward mechanisms—specifically dense contact rewards, stage count rewards, and task-agnostic curiosity rewards—to effectively address the challenges of sparse rewards and long-horizon exploration.
3. **Ensure Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Implement a compatible and effective sim-to-real pipeline, incorporating a structured curriculum, to facilitate reliable deployment of learned policies on real robotic hardware.
4. **Demonstrate Versatility and Universality:** Showcase the framework's broad applicability by successfully applying it to a diverse set of four challenging whole-body humanoid tasks and one non-humanoid task, proving that a single end-to-end RL policy can solve these complex problems without relying on motion priors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WoCoCo addresses the intricate challenges of learning whole-body humanoid control with sequential contacts through a synergistic combination of a principled task decomposition strategy, a novel task-agnostic reward design, and a robust, structured sim-to-real transfer pipeline.

#### 3.1. Task Decomposition into Sequential Contact Stages

The fundamental conceptual underpinning of WoCoCo is the natural decomposition of complex, long-horizon tasks into a series of discrete "contact stages." Each stage, indexed $i \in \{0, 1, \dots, I\}$, is precisely characterized by two goal components:
- **Contact Goal ($g_{con,i}$):** This defines the desired contact states for specific end effectors (e.g., left foot on a particular stone, both hands on a box's sides).
- **Task Goal ($g_{task,i}$):** This specifies additional task-specific requirements that go beyond just contact, such as maintaining a certain upper body posture or moving an object to a target destination.
The robot is programmed to sequentially fulfill these stages. Progression to the subsequent stage occurs only upon the simultaneous fulfillment of both the contact and task goals for the current stage, typically confirmed via sensor feedback or human observation, after a predefined delay. This decomposition is crucial as it transforms the daunting long-horizon exploration problem into more manageable, successive sub-problems, each focusing on achieving the goals of a single contact stage.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Formulation and Implementation

The problem is cast as an extended Markov Decision Process (MDP) $M = \langle S, A, T, R, \gamma, G_{con}, G_{task} \rangle$, where the agent aims to discover an optimal policy $\pi^*(s_t | g_{con,i:I}, g_{task,i:I})$ to maximize its expected cumulative return.
- **Algorithm:** The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm is chosen for policy optimization due to its proven stability and effectiveness in continuous control tasks.
- **Symmetry Augmentation:** To enhance data efficiency, the PPO algorithm integrates symmetry augmentation, leveraging the humanoid robot's inherent left-right symmetry. This effectively doubles the training data by creating symmetric versions of observations and actions.
- **Simulation Environment:** Training is conducted within Isaac Gym, a high-performance GPU-accelerated physics simulation platform that supports massively parallel reinforcement learning. This allows for rapid iteration and large-scale data generation.
- **Policy Architecture:** All policies are implemented as end-to-end Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), featuring a hidden unit structure for both actor and critic networks. The framework, however, is not restricted to this specific neural network architecture.
- **Observations:** The policy's input observations comprise proprioceptive data (e.g., joint positions and velocities, base linear and angular velocities, projected gravity, and stacked previous actions over 3 control steps) and goal-related observations (current and, for some tasks, future contact/task goals provided by a motion capture system as a prototype).

#### 3.3. WoCoCo Reward Design (Task-Agnostic)

A cornerstone of WoCoCo's generality is its meticulously crafted reward function, designed to be largely task-agnostic, thus enabling broad applicability across diverse scenarios. The total reward is defined as a sum of three components:
$r = r_{WoCoCo} + r_{reg} + r_{task}$
Here, $r_{WoCoCo}$ and $r_{reg}$ are task-agnostic, while $r_{task}$ incorporates minimal task-specific terms.

- **Dense Contact Rewards ($r_{con}$):** This term is crucial for mitigating the sparse reward problem inherent in contact tasks. Unlike simple binary rewards (0-1), $r_{con}$ provides rich, continuous feedback by:
 - Positively rewarding each *correct* contact made by an end effector.
 - Actively penalizing each *incorrect* contact.
 - Additionally, it includes a positive term for the complete fulfillment of both the contact and task goals for the current stage.
 This dense feedback effectively guides the agent towards the desired contact configurations.

- **Stage Count Rewards ($r_{stage}$):** This innovative term directly addresses the long-horizon exploration challenge. It assigns a reward based on the cumulative number of contact stages successfully fulfilled. This incentivizes the robot to actively progress through the sequence, preventing it from getting stuck in an early stage by exploiting local maxima in rewards.

- **Curiosity Rewards ($r_{curi}$):** To further encourage exploration without the risk of the agent learning to exploit "noisy" or uninteresting states (a known issue with some curiosity methods like RND), WoCoCo introduces a novel count-based curiosity mechanism using a random neural network (NN) based hash.
 - A randomly initialized and frozen NN (`f_curi`) maps task-agnostic observations (robot's base states and end-effector states) to a fixed-dimensional output.
 - This output is then converted into a binary hash, effectively mapping the high-dimensional observation to a discrete "bucket ID."
 - The curiosity reward is inversely proportional to the square root of the number of times that specific bucket has been visited ($1 / \sqrt{\text{#. visits}}$). This design encourages the exploration of novel state-space regions, with the reward diminishing as a state becomes more familiar, thus preventing perpetual exploration of already known states.

#### 3.4. Sim-to-Real Transfer Pipeline

Recognizing the notorious reality gap, WoCoCo integrates a robust sim-to-real pipeline to ensure learned policies can be effectively deployed on real hardware:
- **Domain Randomization:** Standard practice where various physical parameters (e.g., friction coefficients, base center of mass offset, link masses, motor gains, control delay) and environmental conditions (e.g., external pushes, varied terrain types) are randomized during training. This makes the policy robust to real-world variations and model discrepancies.
- **Regularization Rewards ($r_{reg}$):** A set of task-agnostic regularization terms are incorporated into the reward function. These terms encourage stable, energy-efficient, and physically plausible behaviors (e.g., penalizing high joint velocities, excessive torques, rapid action changes, and undesirable foot contact forces or orientations).
- **Training Curriculum:** A crucial three-stage curriculum is implemented to manage the exploration burden introduced by domain randomization and regularization rewards:
 1. **Phase 1 (Initial Training):** Policies are initially trained *without* domain randomization until they converge. This allows the agent to efficiently learn the fundamental task without the added complexity of environmental variability. Curiosity rewards are active during this phase to promote initial exploration.
 2. **Phase 2 (Domain Randomization Introduction):** Training then resumes *with* domain randomization enabled. This phase builds robustness into the learned policy by exposing it to a diverse range of simulated conditions.
 3. **Phase 3 (Regularization Weight Increase):** Finally, the weights of the regularization terms are gradually increased (by 20% every 2000 iterations until they double). This process encourages more conservative and stable behaviors, which are essential for safe and reliable real-world deployment.
- **Temporal Memory:** Stacking historical joint states and actions (3 control steps) into the observation space provides the policy with crucial temporal context, further enhancing its robustness and adaptability.

#### 3.5. Task-Specific Rewards ($r_{task}$)

A compelling testament to WoCoCo's generality is the minimal requirement for task-specific reward engineering. For each of the four humanoid tasks and the dinosaur robot task, only one or two intuitive, high-level task-related reward terms are needed. These terms primarily guide the robot towards the overarching objective of the specific task (e.g., tracking an elbow's position, minimizing distance to a box, encouraging arm spread for dancing, or facing a wall for climbing). This minimalist approach to task-specific rewards significantly streamlines the development process for new behaviors.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating intelligent task decomposition, a multi-faceted and dense reward structure, and a carefully staged sim-to-real curriculum, enables WoCoCo to tackle complex multi-contact control problems with remarkable generality, robustness, and real-world applicability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence for the efficacy and generality of the WoCoCo framework through extensive experimentation, encompassing both simulation and real-world deployments on a humanoid robot and a dinosaur robot.

#### 4.1. Real-World Demonstrations on a Humanoid Robot

WoCoCo successfully enabled end-to-end RL-based controllers to master four highly challenging whole-body humanoid tasks. A key achievement here is the ability to accomplish these tasks *without any reliance on motion priors*, which marks a significant advancement for RL in this domain.

1. **Versatile Parkour Jumping (Fig. 2):**
 - **Capabilities:** The humanoid demonstrated the ability to execute continuous jumps involving dynamic foot contact sequences (switching between single and double-foot contacts) with precise landings in 3D space. Crucially, it simultaneously maintained specified upper body postures (e.g., "hug" or "relax") during these highly dynamic and coupled movements.
 - **Robustness:** The policy exhibited strong robustness against external perturbations, such as unseen gravels in simulation. Real-world tests showcased highly dynamic and adaptive behaviors across varying stone heights and distances.
 - **Significance:** To the authors' knowledge, this is the first instance of such versatile parkour jumping being solved by a single end-to-end RL policy.

2. **Anywhere-to-Anywhere Box Loco-Manipulation (Fig. 3):**
 - **Capabilities:** A single end-to-end RL policy controlled the humanoid to first approach a box from arbitrary starting positions and then transport it to a specified destination. This task required seamless whole-body coordination, achieved *without any predefined posture priors*.
 - **Efficiency and Fluidity:** The robot performed efficient turns, transitioned smoothly between walking and picking actions, and could simultaneously approach the destination while picking up the box.
 - **Robustness:** The policy demonstrated robust recovery capabilities, such as regaining balance after stepping on a belt tied to itself.
 - **Significance:** This represents a notable leap in RL-based loco-manipulation, surpassing previous approaches that often depended on finite state machines and separate, task-specific policies.

3. **Dynamic Clap-and-Tap Dancing (Fig. 4):**
 - **Capabilities:** The humanoid learned impressive dynamic dancing skills, incorporating accurate tapping on the ground and optional clapping, by composing diverse contact sequences.
 - **Real-world Success:** The learned policy was successfully deployed on the real robot, showcasing fluid and dynamic entertainment capabilities.
 - **Significance:** This illustrates RL's potential for generating expressive and dynamic humanoid behaviors that involve precise physical interactions, in contrast to methods that rely on human motion references.

4. **Bidirectional Cliffside Climbing (Fig. 5):**
 - **Capabilities:** The humanoid acquired multi-contact locomotion skills, precisely coordinating all its limbs to move along a cliffside.
 - **Robustness and Compliance:** In simulation, the policy proved robust against external pushes and compliant when interacting with unseen gravels. Real-world tests showed the humanoid's ability to adapt to varying contact forces experienced on its hands when interacting with a human-held board.
 - **Significance:** This demonstrates RL's viability for achieving fast and resilient multi-contact locomotion, offering a powerful alternative to traditional model-based controllers for complex climbing tasks.

#### 4.2. Generalization Beyond Humanoids

- **Dinosaur Loco-Manipulation (Fig. 6):** To further underscore WoCoCo's universality, the framework was successfully applied to a 22-DoF dinosaur robot. In this ball loco-manipulation task, the robot learned to push a ball towards various destinations using different end effectors (head, tail, and four feet), showcasing the framework's broad applicability across different robotic embodiments and kinematics.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Focusing on Parkour Jumping)

Detailed ablation studies on the parkour jumping task provided crucial empirical validation for the necessity and individual contributions of each component within the WoCoCo reward design:
- **Ablating Dense Contact Rewards:** When standard 0-1 contact rewards were used instead of dense rewards, the policy failed to explore adequately; the humanoid would not attempt to jump, instead remaining stationary and only tracking upper body postures. This unequivocally confirms the critical role of dense contact rewards in guiding exploration.
- **Ablating Stage Count Rewards:** Without the stage count rewards, the humanoid exhibited "shortsighted" behavior. It intentionally avoided fully fulfilling contact goals to prevent progressing to the next stage, preferring to maximize rewards by staying in its current, "safer" state. This validates the effectiveness of incentivizing long-term progress.
- **Ablating Curiosity Rewards:**
 - The absence of *any* curiosity rewards led to severe under-exploration, preventing the policy from learning the jumping task.
 - Using RND-based curiosity rewards (a common alternative) resulted in the humanoid learning to lean backward in a risky, potentially dangerous manner. This aligns with known observations that RND can sometimes lead to the over-exploration of specific, potentially unsafe, behavioral patterns.
 - In contrast, WoCoCo's proposed count-based curiosity reward, utilizing a random neural network hash, facilitated effective exploration without overfitting to such risky or problematic behaviors.

#### 4.4. Empirical Benefits and Training Stability

- **High Agility and Motion Efficiency:** WoCoCo enables the learning of highly agile and motion-efficient behaviors that are not constrained by the limitations of simplified models or hard-coded motion priors.
- **Versatility:** The policies demonstrate high versatility, generalizing effectively to diverse contact goals through varied task configurations.
- **Robustness:** The learned policies consistently showed robustness against various external perturbations.
- **Training Stability:** The training process proved remarkably stable across five different random seeds for the parkour jumping task, indicating the reliability and consistency of the method despite its curiosity-driven exploration component.

In essence, the main findings decisively confirm WoCoCo's capability to deliver a unified, general, and robust RL framework for complex multi-contact whole-body control. It achieves real-world performance previously considered unattainable with end-to-end RL policies, setting a new benchmark for learned humanoid capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WoCoCo represents a transformative contribution to the field of humanoid robotics and reinforcement learning, carrying profound significance and promising substantial future impact.

#### 5.1. Pioneering Generality and Unification in RL for Humanoids

The most pivotal aspect of WoCoCo is its paradigm-shifting ability to provide a *unified and general RL framework* for learning whole-body humanoid control across a remarkably diverse range of challenging multi-contact tasks. Historically, RL solutions for humanoids have been notorious for their specificity, demanding painstaking task-specific reward engineering, intricate state machine designs, and often requiring distinct policies for different segments of a complex behavior. WoCoCo fundamentally alters this landscape by drastically reducing the development overhead, requiring only one or two intuitive task-specific reward terms alongside its broadly applicable, task-agnostic reward structure. This achievement moves RL significantly closer to the adaptability and ease of use characteristic of advanced model-based optimal control frameworks.

#### 5.2. Enabling Complex, Dynamic, and Robust Real-World Behaviors

WoCoCo conclusively demonstrates that end-to-end RL can generate highly dynamic, agile, and precise whole-body control for tasks such as versatile parkour jumping, nuanced loco-manipulation, expressive dancing, and multi-contact climbing. These are behaviors that were previously either intractable for RL or heavily reliant on pre-defined motion libraries and oversimplified models. The successful real-world validation of these complex skills, particularly *without relying on any motion priors*, is a monumental step towards deploying humanoids autonomously in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments. The proven robustness against various external perturbations (e.g., pushes, unseen gravels) further underscores the practical readiness and reliability of the learned policies.

#### 5.3. Addressing Core Reinforcement Learning Challenges for Robotics

The paper makes significant methodological contributions by effectively tackling several long-standing challenges in applying RL to complex robotic systems:
- **Sparse Rewards:** The introduction of "dense contact rewards" provides crucial, continuous feedback signals. This allows policies to learn effectively from every attempt, rather than struggling with sparse, rare successes (e.g., a simple binary success/failure signal).
- **Long-Horizon Exploration:** The novel "stage count rewards" directly incentivize the agent to progress through the sequential contact stages, intelligently overcoming the "shortsightedness" or tendency to avoid risk that often plagues RL agents in long-horizon tasks.
- **Effective and Stable Curiosity:** The proposed count-based curiosity mechanism, derived from a random neural network hash and applied to task-agnostic observations, offers a robust and stable exploration strategy. This innovation successfully mitigates issues of "over-exploration" or "misleading exploration" sometimes observed with other curiosity-driven methods like Random Network Distillation (RND).
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The meticulously designed three-stage curriculum, integrating domain randomization and regularization with a structured progression, provides a highly effective and practical blueprint for reliably bridging the simulation-to-reality gap, which is indispensable for real-world deployment.

#### 5.4. Broad Applicability and Future Trajectory of Robotics

The successful extension of WoCoCo to a 22-DoF dinosaur robot for a ball loco-manipulation task powerfully illustrates its universality. This demonstrates that the core principles of the framework are not limited to humanoids but can be adapted to a wide array of complex multi-contact robotic platforms. This generalizability promises to significantly accelerate the development of advanced capabilities across the entire robotics spectrum.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Reduced Development Time and Effort:** WoCoCo's generalized framework promises to drastically reduce the time and specialized expertise required to develop new, complex behaviors for humanoids, thereby democratizing access to advanced robotic capabilities.
- **Enhanced Autonomy and Resilience:** Humanoids leveraging WoCoCo-trained policies will be able to operate with unprecedented autonomy and resilience in complex, real-world scenarios, adapting dynamically to varying terrains, objects, and task demands.
- **Expansion into New Application Domains:** The demonstrated ability to reliably perform multi-contact loco-manipulation, agile navigation, and expressive interactions opens up new frontiers for humanoid applications in logistics, manufacturing, disaster response, personal assistance, and entertainment.
- **Advancements in RL Theory and Practice:** The novel reward engineering strategies and the structured sim-to-real curriculum offer valuable insights and practical tools for the broader RL community, particularly for tackling high-dimensional, sparse-reward tasks in robotics.

#### 5.5. Acknowledged Limitations and Future Work

The authors candidly address several limitations, which simultaneously serve as promising directions for future research:
- **Lack of Failure Prediction:** Unlike some model-based methods, WoCoCo does not explicitly predict controller failure. Future work could integrate explicit failure predictors or advanced safety assessment methods.
- **Heuristic Contact Sequences:** The current reliance on heuristically designed contact sequences could be overcome by integrating high-level planners (e.g., sampling-based or LLM-based) to automatically generate these sequences.
- **Explicit Contact Feedback:** The need for explicit contact feedback (from sensors or human observers) to trigger stage transitions could be replaced by policies that implicitly manage these transitions.
- **Reliance on Motion Capture:** The current use of motion capture as a prototype for goal representations will transition to onboard sensing in future iterations, enhancing autonomy.

These acknowledged limitations do not diminish the immense value of WoCoCo but rather highlight the ongoing challenges at the forefront of robotic control and the clear path for future advancements built upon this robust and generalizable framework.
