# WoCoCo: Learning Whole-Body Humanoid Control with Sequential Contacts

> [!summary] Summary
> WoCoCo presents a unified reinforcement learning framework for learning whole-body humanoid control across diverse, complex multi-contact tasks. It achieves robust real-world performance on activities like parkour jumping, loco-manipulation, and climbing, using end-to-end policies that do not rely on motion priors.

## Problem

- Traditional model-based motion planning for complex multi-contact humanoid tasks is computationally intensive, time-consuming, and susceptible to real-world model inaccuracies and environmental uncertainties.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for multi-contact humanoid control often require tedious task-specific reward engineering, intricate finite state machines, struggle with long-horizon exploration, and suffer from sparse contact rewards.
- Robustly transferring learned policies from simulation to real-world humanoid hardware (sim-to-real gap) remains a significant challenge, often requiring extensive domain randomization that can burden exploration.

## Method

- Decomposition of complex, long-horizon tasks into a series of sequential 'contact stages,' each defined by precise contact and task goals, which the robot must fulfill sequentially.
- A novel, largely task-agnostic reward function combining dense contact rewards, stage count rewards for long-horizon progress, and a count-based curiosity reward using a random neural network hash for efficient exploration.
- A robust three-stage training curriculum for sim-to-real transfer, incrementally introducing domain randomization and gradually increasing regularization terms to ensure stable and reliable real-world deployment.

## Results

- WoCoCo enabled end-to-end reinforcement learning policies to successfully perform four challenging whole-body humanoid tasks in the real world: versatile parkour jumping, anywhere-to-anywhere box loco-manipulation, dynamic clap-and-tap dancing, and bidirectional cliffside climbing, all without relying on motion priors.
- The learned policies demonstrated strong robustness against external perturbations and adaptability to varying environmental conditions across all demonstrated tasks.
- The framework generalized to a 22-DoF dinosaur robot for a ball loco-manipulation task, showcasing its universality beyond humanoid specific kinematics and embodiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense contact rewards are crucial for guiding exploration and learning in tasks with sparse contact events, preventing policies from failing to initiate complex actions.
> - Stage count rewards effectively address the long-horizon exploration problem by incentivizing agents to progress through sequential stages, overcoming 'shortsighted' exploitation of early-stage rewards.
> - A count-based curiosity mechanism, using a random neural network hash on task-agnostic observations, enables stable exploration without leading to problematic or risky behaviors seen with some other curiosity methods.
