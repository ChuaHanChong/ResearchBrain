# Image Diffusion Preview with Consistency Solver

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind researchers developed ConsistencySolver, a learnable, high-order ODE solver trained with Reinforcement Learning, enabling a "Diffusion Preview" paradigm for rapid, consistent low-step previews in diffusion models. This framework reduces user interaction latency by up to 55% while delivering high fidelity and strong consistency between previews and full-step generations.

## Problem

- Diffusion models suffer from slow inference due to computationally intensive reverse differential equation solving, hindering interactive applications.
- Existing acceleration methods either yield low-quality, inconsistent previews (training-free solvers) or require costly retraining that can degrade the original model (distillation methods).
- Creative users require rapid iteration and consistent visual feedback, which current slow and unreliable generation processes fail to provide.

## Method

- Introduced the "**Diffusion Preview**" framework, a two-stage workflow where users quickly evaluate low-step previews before committing to full-step, high-fidelity generation.
- Developed ConsistencySolver, a lightweight, trainable, high-order ODE solver designed to adapt its integration strategy for few-step diffusion sampling.
- Optimized ConsistencySolver's policy network using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with non-differentiable perceptual similarity rewards, maximizing alignment between low-step previews and full-step reference images without altering the base diffusion model.

## Results

- ConsistencySolver achieved superior or competitive performance, e.g., an FID of 20.39 at 5 steps on Stable Diffusion, outperforming training-free solvers and matching or exceeding distillation methods while showing higher consistency across metrics.
- On instructional image editing (FLUX.1-Kontext), it reached an Edit Reward of **0.86** and Depth consistency of 25.18 at 5 steps, improving editing accuracy and adherence to instructions.
- The Diffusion Preview paradigm, evaluated through user studies, reduced average inference time by up to **55%** on the LAION dataset, demonstrating practical efficiency gains and maintaining high user satisfaction (**94.2%** on GenEval prompts).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learnable, adaptive ODE solver, trained with RL and perceptual rewards, can effectively bridge the gap between low-step efficiency and high-step fidelity in diffusion models.
> - Consistency between low-step previews and final high-quality outputs is a critical factor for user satisfaction in interactive generative AI workflows, often more crucial than distribution-level metrics alone.
> - The optimal sampling dynamics learned by ConsistencySolver can generalize across different diffusion model architectures and scales without retraining, suggesting transferable knowledge in acceleration strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models have advanced generative artificial intelligence, particularly in high-fidelity visual data synthesis and multimodal content creation. Their capacity to produce diverse, high-quality outputs has driven progress across various generative tasks. However, the computational intensity of their inference process, which involves numerically solving reverse differential equations, limits their practical application in scenarios requiring rapid interaction or operating under resource constraints, such as on mobile devices.

The existing research landscape for accelerating diffusion model inference can broadly be categorized into two main areas:
1. **Training-free ODE solvers:** These methods, exemplified by DPM-Solver, DEIS, and UniPC, aim to optimize the numerical integration of the probability-flow ODE (PF-ODE) associated with diffusion models. While these approaches have reduced the number of function evaluations (NFEs) required, they often rely on theoretical assumptions that may not perfectly align with the model's actual behavior, potentially leading to lower-quality outputs, particularly in low-step preview scenarios. Furthermore, they may not inherently guarantee consistency between low-step previews and full-step refined outputs.
2. **Post-training distillation methods:** This category includes techniques like ODE distillation (e.g., LCM, Consistency Models) and score distillation (e.g., DMD2, Rectified Diffusion). These methods aim to "bake" acceleration directly into the model weights, enabling high-quality generation in fewer steps. However, they typically require substantial and expensive retraining of the diffusion model or auxiliary networks. Distillation often introduces accumulated errors, which can degrade the original ODE path and compromise generation quality. Moreover, these methods can disrupt the deterministic correspondence between noise space and data space inherent in the PF-ODE and may alter the model's learned trajectory, potentially leading to a loss of properties like flexible inference step selection.

This work introduces the "Diffusion Preview" paradigm and "ConsistencySolver" to address the limitations of existing acceleration methods, particularly concerning the generation of high-quality and consistent previews in low-step settings. It positions itself as a method that synergizes the strengths of efficient ODE solving and data-driven learning without modifying the underlying diffusion model, thereby aiming to improve interactive user experiences where rapid feedback and consistent refinement are crucial.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the interactive user experience with image diffusion models by mitigating their slow inference process. This is pursued through the introduction of a novel "Diffusion Preview" paradigm, which is motivated by the need for more efficient and practical generative workflows, particularly in interactive settings.

The key motivations and specific objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Inference Latency:** The inherently slow, computationally intensive nature of diffusion model inference is identified as a significant barrier to their broader adoption and interactive use. Current models require a large number of steps to numerically solve reverse differential equations, resulting in considerable processing time for each image generation.
- **Enabling Rapid Iteration and Feedback:** In creative and design-oriented applications, users often need to iterate quickly, experimenting with different prompts or random seeds to achieve a desired output. The prolonged waiting times for each full-quality generation impede this iterative process, leading to a suboptimal user experience and increased computational costs for unsatisfactory trials.
- **Introducing the Diffusion Preview Paradigm:** To address the above, the research proposes a two-stage workflow:
 1. **Rapid Preview Stage:** In this initial stage, a fast, low-step sampling process quickly generates a preliminary output. This preview serves as a rapid visual approximation of the potential final high-quality result, allowing users to quickly evaluate and decide if the current parameters (e.g., prompt, seed) are promising.
 2. **Refinement Stage:** If a preview is deemed satisfactory, the same parameters are then used in a full-step, resource-intensive sampling process to produce the final, high-fidelity image. This strategy aims to avoid unnecessary full-step computations for trials that would ultimately be discarded.
- **Defining Critical Characteristics for Effective Previews:** For the Diffusion Preview paradigm to be effective, the generated previews must satisfy three core characteristics:
 - **Fidelity:** Previews should visually and structurally resemble the final, high-quality output. This ensures that users can make informed decisions based on the preliminary image, as it accurately represents what the refined image will look like.
 - **Efficiency:** The preview generation process must be computationally minimal to support rapid iteration. This is fundamental to reducing overall user interaction time and computational overhead.
 - **Consistency:** A predictable and stable mapping between the initial parameters of the preview and the final output is essential. This guarantees that refining a satisfactory preview will reliably produce a high-quality result that aligns with user expectations, without unexpected deviations.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Acceleration Methods:** The motivation for developing a new solver, ConsistencySolver, stems from the shortcomings of current acceleration techniques:
 - **Training-free ODE solvers** often produce low-quality previews that lack fidelity in low-step scenarios, failing to provide reliable representations for user evaluation.
 - **Post-training distillation methods** are computationally expensive to retrain, often disrupt the deterministic properties of the PF-ODE, and can suffer from accumulated distillation errors or alter the model's fundamental learned trajectory, leading to quality degradation or loss of flexibility.
- **Developing ConsistencySolver:** The specific objective is to design a lightweight, trainable, high-order ODE solver that:
 - Can be optimized via Reinforcement Learning (RL) to adapt to the sampling dynamics of pre-trained diffusion models.
 - Enhances preview quality and consistency in low-step regimes.
 - Preserves the deterministic PF-ODE mapping critical for consistent refinement.
 - Operates without modifying the base diffusion model, maintaining its integrity and flexibility.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between the high quality of diffusion models and the demand for interactive speed, by providing a method for generating faithful and consistent low-step previews that reliably predict the outcome of high-step generations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the "Diffusion Preview" framework and a novel learnable ODE solver called "ConsistencySolver," designed to optimize the preview generation process.

#### 3.1. Diffusion Preview Framework

The Diffusion Preview framework is an iterative, two-stage workflow for image generation:
1. **Rapid Preview Stage:** Given a text prompt and an initial noise map, a fast, low-step diffusion sampling process generates a preliminary image (preview). The user evaluates this preview.
2. **Refinement Stage:** If the user is satisfied with the preview, the same prompt and noise map are used for a full-step diffusion sampling process to generate the final high-quality image. If not satisfied, the user can modify the prompt or random seed, and the preview stage repeats.

This framework aims to improve sampling efficiency and reduce unnecessary computational costs by deferring high-cost refinement until a promising preview is identified.

#### 3.2. ConsistencySolver Design

ConsistencySolver is a trainable, high-order ODE solver specifically tailored to achieve high-fidelity and consistent previews in few-step diffusion sampling. It dynamically adapts its integration strategy to maximize alignment between low-step previews and high-step reference generations.

- **Mathematical Foundation:** The approach is grounded in solving the Probability Flow ODE (PF-ODE) of diffusion models. It adapts the principles of General Linear Multistep Methods (LMMs), which use multiple prior states to improve accuracy.
- **Formulation:** Given a pre-trained diffusion model $\epsilon_\phi(x_t, t, c)$ (where $x_t$ is noisy input, $t$ is time, $c$ is conditioning), ConsistencySolver computes an update for the state $y_t = x_t/\alpha_t$ at each transition from $t_i$ to $t_{i+1}$ using a weighted combination of past noise predictions:
 $y_{t_{i+1}} = y_{t_i} + (n_{t_{i+1}} - n_{t_i}) \cdot \left[ \sum_{j=1}^{m} w_j(t_i, t_{i+1}) \cdot \epsilon_{i+1-j} \right]$
 Here, $m$ is the solver order (number of historical steps used), and $w_j(t_i, t_{i+1})$ are adaptive coefficients generated by a lightweight neural policy network $f_\theta(t_i, t_{i+1})$, implemented as a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) that takes the current and target timesteps as input.
- **Theoretical Grounding:** The architectural form of ConsistencySolver is derived from classical LMMs with three principled modifications:
 1. **Explicit-only design:** $w_0 = 0$, favoring computational efficiency over stability for non-stiff PF-ODE trajectories.
 2. **Anchor to current state:** $\mu_0 = 1, \mu_j = 0$ for $j \ge 1$, simplifying the recurrence by only using the most recent state as the integration base, while maintaining high-order accuracy via derivative blending.
 3. **Timestep-conditioned coefficients:** The coefficients $w_j$ are made adaptive, allowing the solver to adjust its integration paradigm based on the current denoising timesteps.
 The paper demonstrates that several existing diffusion solvers (e.g., DDIM, PNDM, DPM-Solver-2) can be reinterpreted within this generalized framework by assigning specific constant coefficients. ConsistencySolver, however, treats these coefficients as learnable.

#### 3.3. Training Methodology: Reinforcement Learning (RL)

To find the optimal adaptive multistep ODE solver, the training of the policy network $f_\theta$ is framed as a sequential decision-making problem and optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

- **Offline Dataset Preparation:** Before training, a fixed dataset of prompt–noise–reference triples is generated. For each entry, a prompt $c^{(k)}$ is sampled, a noise $z^{(k)}$ is drawn, and a "ground truth" reference image $x_{gt}^{(k)}$ is generated using full-step sampling with a pre-trained diffusion model (e.g., 40-step multistep DPM-Solver).
- **Training Episode Rollout:** In each PPO episode, a batch of triples from the offline dataset is sampled. For each $(c, z, x_{gt})$, a K-step preview trajectory is unrolled using ConsistencySolver. At each step, the policy network $f_\theta$ processes the current and target timesteps to output the adaptive coefficients $w_j$.
- **Reward and Policy Update:** After the K-step rollout, the generated preview $x_p$ is compared against the pre-computed reference $x_{gt}$ using a scalar similarity reward $R = \text{Sim}(x_{gt}, x_p)$. The reward function can incorporate various perceptual similarity metrics (e.g., depth maps, segmentation masks, DINO features, CLIP similarity). The policy parameters $\theta$ are then optimized using the PPO clipped surrogate objective, with an advantage function computed using batch self-normalization. This directly incentivizes the solver to produce previews that are reliable proxies for the final generation.

#### 3.4. Advantages of RL over Distillation

The paper justifies the choice of RL for training ConsistencySolver based on three advantages compared to distillation methods:
1. **Compatibility with non-differentiable rewards:** RL can utilize non-differentiable perceptual similarity metrics, eliminating the need to backpropagate through the entire diffusion trajectory, which is a source of instability and computational overhead in distillation.
2. **Superior generalization and quality:** RL-trained ConsistencySolver reportedly generalizes better to novel prompt-noise pairs, yielding higher fidelity and consistency.
3. **Reduced training overhead:** RL relies on sparse rewards from the final output, avoiding intermediate gradient storage. Only the compact MLP for the policy participates in loss computation, leading to lower memory usage and more efficient training.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

ConsistencySolver is evaluated on Stable Diffusion for text-to-image generation and FLUX.1-Kontext for instructional image editing. Evaluation metrics include Frechet Inception Distance (FID), Edit Reward, Edit Score, and various consistency metrics (CLIP, DINO, Inception, SegFormer, PSNR for raw images, PSNR for Depth Anything V2). Ablation studies are conducted on solver order, reward models, and model structure to validate design choices.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding ConsistencySolver's performance across various tasks and evaluation criteria.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison on Stable Diffusion (Text-to-Image Generation)

- **Superiority over Training-Free ODE Solvers:** ConsistencySolver consistently outperforms training-free ODE solvers (DDIM, iPNDM, UniPC, DEIS, Multistep DPM-Solver) at equivalent step counts. For example, at 5 steps, ConsistencySolver achieves an FID score of 20.39, which is lower than Multistep DPM-Solver's 25.87, and also demonstrates higher consistency across all evaluated metrics (CLIP, Seg., Dep., Inc., Img., DINO). This indicates improved fidelity and alignment with refined outputs.
- **Competitive Performance against Distillation Methods:** Compared to distillation-based methods (DMD2, LCM, PCM, Rectified Diffusion, AMED, Ours-Distill), ConsistencySolver delivers competitive or superior performance, particularly at 4-8 steps. For instance, at 8 steps, ConsistencySolver achieves an FID of 18.82, outperforming LCM (21.50) and PCM (not reported at 8 steps, but 21.67 at 4 steps), and even AMED (19.22). It also exhibits better consistency metrics. This suggests that ConsistencySolver balances speed and quality effectively without the retraining overhead typical of distillation. As step counts increase to 10-12, ConsistencySolver further refines outputs, achieving FID values as low as 18.53 at 12 steps.
- **Outperformance of Ours-Distill:** The RL-based ConsistencySolver consistently outperforms its distillation-trained counterpart (Ours-Distill), affirming the advantages of the RL-based optimization strategy.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparison on FLUX.1-Kontext (Instructional Image Editing)

- ConsistencySolver demonstrates significant improvements in image editing tasks. At 4 steps, it achieves an Edit Reward of 0.73 and an Edit Score of 5.67, surpassing Euler (0.61 and 5.45 respectively) and other training-free solvers.
- At 5 steps, ConsistencySolver achieves the best results across all metrics, including an Edit Reward of 0.86 and Depth consistency of 25.18, indicating higher editing accuracy, better adherence to instructions, and improved consistency.

#### 4.3. Cross-Model Generalization

- ConsistencySolver, trained solely on Stable Diffusion 1.5, demonstrates transferability to unseen models with different weights, architectures, and scales (SD1.4, DreamShaper, SDXL) without requiring any retraining.
- It consistently outperforms all training-free ODE solvers on these unseen models. For example, on SDXL with 10 steps, ConsistencySolver achieves an FID of 23.32, which is notably better than Multistep DPM-Solver (26.32) and DEIS (26.40). This suggests that optimal sampling dynamics may be shared across different diffusion models.

#### 4.4. Studies on Diffusion Preview (User Studies and LLM Evaluation)

- **Efficiency Gains:** User studies, conducted with both human evaluators and Claude Sonnet 4 as a proxy, show that the Diffusion Preview paradigm significantly reduces overall user interaction time. It reduced average inference time by up to 55% on the LAION dataset with a minor increase in average attempts (from 6.00 to 6.35, as evaluated by Claude Sonnet 4). This confirms the practical efficiency of the preview-and-refine workflow.
- **Importance of Consistency:** A comparison with distillation-based methods (e.g., DMD2) using Claude Sonnet 4 for user satisfaction revealed that despite competitive FID scores, DMD2 satisfied significantly fewer prompts compared to the base model and ConsistencySolver. For instance, on GenEval prompts, DMD2 with and without GAN satisfied only 57.0% and 47.1% of prompts, respectively, while ConsistencySolver maintained 94.2% satisfaction. This highlights that consistency, beyond just distribution-level metrics like FID, is crucial for user satisfaction in iterative generative workflows.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Comparison

- Visual comparisons on both text-to-image and instructional image editing tasks indicate that ConsistencySolver generates previews with sharper details and superior alignment to the refined outputs compared to both training-free ODE solvers and distillation-based methods.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Solver Orders:** An order-4 solver configuration for ConsistencySolver was found to achieve the best balance between accuracy and RL search complexity. Lower orders showed reduced structural fidelity, while higher orders offered only marginal gains.
- **Reward Models:** Among various reward choices (Depth, Inception, CLIP, Img., DINO, Seg.), the Depth reward provided the most balanced trade-off between structural consistency and overall robustness across different step counts, leading to its adoption as the default.
- **Model Structures:** The MLP with a hidden dimension of 256 was found to offer the best balance between performance and efficiency for the policy network $f_\theta$.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework and a method that carries several significant implications for the practical application and user experience of diffusion models.

#### 5.1. Enabling Interactive Generative AI Workflows

The introduction of the "Diffusion Preview" paradigm directly addresses a key limitation of diffusion models: their slow inference speed. By enabling rapid, low-step preview generation followed by selective full-step refinement, the work significantly reduces the latency for user interaction. This can transform how users engage with generative AI, moving from a batch-like generation process to a more fluid, interactive, and iterative design workflow. This paradigm is particularly impactful for creative professionals, designers, and general users who benefit from quick visual feedback.

#### 5.2. Technical Advancement in Diffusion Model Acceleration

ConsistencySolver represents a novel approach to accelerating diffusion model inference. Unlike existing training-free ODE solvers, it is a learnable, high-order solver that adapts its integration strategy. Crucially, it avoids the extensive retraining and potential degradation of the base model associated with distillation-based methods. Its optimization through Reinforcement Learning (RL) allows it to learn an improved sampling strategy directly from data, leveraging non-differentiable perceptual rewards, which is a departure from traditional differentiable objectives.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Fidelity, Efficiency, and Consistency

The reported results indicate that ConsistencySolver achieves a balance of fidelity, efficiency, and consistency superior to many existing methods, especially in low-step scenarios. The ability to generate high-quality previews that closely resemble final outputs, with an efficient computational footprint, and critically, a consistent mapping between preview and final generation, ensures user trust and reduces wasted computation on unsatisfactory drafts. This consistency property is highlighted as a critical factor for user satisfaction in iterative workflows, often overlooked by distribution-level metrics like FID alone.

#### 5.4. Broad Generalizability

The demonstrated cross-model generalization, where ConsistencySolver trained on one model (SD1.5) effectively improves performance on unseen models (SD1.4, DreamShaper, SDXL) without re-training, is a notable finding. This suggests that the learned optimal sampling dynamics might be transferable across different diffusion model architectures and scales. This property could reduce the development and deployment overhead for future diffusion models.

#### 5.5. Reduced Computational Costs and Resource Efficiency

By drastically reducing the number of full-step generations required, the Diffusion Preview framework, powered by ConsistencySolver, can lead to substantial reductions in overall computational energy consumption and inference costs. This is particularly relevant in cloud-based generative services or for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices (e.g., mobile phones), where every computational step counts.

In summary, this work offers a practical and technically robust solution to the challenge of interactive image generation with diffusion models. Its potential impact lies in making powerful generative AI tools more accessible, efficient, and user-friendly across a wide range of applications, from artistic creation to rapid prototyping.
