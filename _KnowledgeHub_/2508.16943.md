# LHM-Humanoid: Learning a Unified Policy for Long-Horizon Humanoid Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation in Diverse Messy Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> LHM-Humanoid presents a benchmark and learning framework that enables a unified policy for long-horizon humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation across diverse, cluttered environments. This approach achieves over 60% success on unseen scenes for two-object sequences and maintains substantial performance across up to five objects, outperforming prior methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid loco-manipulation typically focuses on short-horizon or single-object interactions, lacking sustained whole-body coordination for long-duration tasks.
- Policies often struggle with cross-scene generalization, performing poorly when faced with diverse scene layouts, object categories, and start/goal poses beyond their training distribution.
- Developing a single, unified policy for complex multi-stage tasks is challenging due to the difficulties of direct end-to-end reinforcement learning and the fragility of composing fixed skill libraries.

## Method

- A three-stage training pipeline is introduced, comprising ==dual-teacher policy training==, distillation into a unified policy, and an extension to a ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== model.
- Two goal-conditioned teacher policies are trained: one for the first object's loco-manipulation with an explicit ==release-and-retreat== phase, and another for subsequent objects from diverse, non-canonical states.
- The knowledge from these teachers is distilled into a single, end-to-end student policy using ==DAgger==, which then directly outputs actions for continuous long-horizon episodes without explicit stage boundaries.

## Results

- The **LHM-Humanoid** framework, with its dual-teacher (**72.38%** Success All) and unified student (**71.14%** Success All) policies, significantly outperformed baselines on training tasks, which largely failed (e.g., End-to-End RL at **0.00%**).
- On **66 unseen tasks**, the unified student policy maintained strong generalization with **61.60%** Success All, whereas all baselines experienced substantial performance degradation.
- The learned policy was robust to longer sequences, completing **18.07%** of five-object tasks without further fine-tuning, while other methods collapsed to near-zero success beyond three objects.
- An extension successfully distilled the policy into a VLA model, achieving **63.71%** Success All on two-object tasks using egocentric RGB and natural-language instructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dual-teacher framework with one policy focusing on a stable initial cycle and another on recovery from non-canonical states is effective for handling long-horizon, sequential tasks.
> - An explicit "release-and-retreat" objective after object placement is crucial for ensuring stable inter-task transitions, preventing interference, and allowing the humanoid to prepare for the next task.
> - Distilling the combined knowledge of specialized teachers into a single end-to-end policy using DAgger enables seamless execution across diverse environments and avoids the fragility of hand-engineered skill composition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics and embodied AI has seen advances in various sub-domains. Prior work has contributed to humanoid motion control, with methods spanning kinematic approaches (using VAEs, transformers, and diffusion models) and physics-based control, often leveraging deep reinforcement learning (RL) techniques like DeepMimic-style imitation or adversarial motion priors (AMP). These controllers aim to generate realistic and robust movements for humanoids in simulated environments.

Another related area is human-scene interaction (HSI), which focuses on enabling robots to interact with their environment in contact-rich ways, such as sitting, lying, or manipulating objects. Recent physics-based HSI approaches have moved from inverse kinematics (IK) and optimal control to deep RL to acquire more complex skills, including tool use or object dribbling. Efforts to scale interaction learning, such as SkillMimic and InterMimic, have expanded the repertoire of physics-based interaction. However, many existing controllers in this domain are specialized for narrow task distributions or short-horizon interactions.

Room-scale loco-manipulation is also a well-established area in embodied AI and robotics, with benchmarks like Visual Room Rearrangement and OVMM, and approaches utilizing large language models (LLMs) for planning. These settings typically use simplified robot embodiments, which often reduces the emphasis on humanoid-specific challenges such as maintaining dynamic balance, coordinating the whole body, and handling contact-rich interactions. While works like HumanVLA have advanced heavier object manipulation with physics-based humanoids, episodes generally remain relatively short.

This research aims to integrate and extend these areas by addressing the combined challenges of long-horizon whole-body humanoid loco-manipulation in diverse, cluttered environments. It distinguishes itself by requiring a single, general policy capable of continuous, multi-object interaction across varied scenes without environment resets, thus moving beyond specialized controllers, fixed skill libraries, or single-object/short-horizon tasks common in previous studies. The work also explores the integration of visual and linguistic inputs into this complex control framework, building on advancements in language-conditioned motion synthesis and vision-language-action (VLA) policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce LHM-Humanoid, a benchmark and a learning framework designed for long-horizon whole-body humanoid loco-manipulation in diverse and cluttered scenes. This work aims to enable a humanoid agent to perform a sequence of complex, continuous tasks within a single episode without environment resets.

The specific task defined within this framework involves the humanoid repeatedly executing four sub-tasks:
1. **Walking to a target object.**
2. **Picking up the target object** using diverse whole-body postures while maintaining balance.
3. **Carrying the object** while navigating around obstacles.
4. **Placing the object** at a designated goal location.

This entire sequence must be performed continuously for multiple objects within a single episode.

The motivation for proposing this challenging setting stems from three key requirements that are often simplified in prior research:
- **Long-horizon human-scene interaction:** Existing methods frequently simplify the task horizon to single-step or single-object interactions. This work addresses the need for sustained coordination of locomotion, whole-body manipulation, and object transport over extended periods.
- **Cross-scene generalization:** Many prior works train and evaluate within a single or fixed set of scenes. LHM-Humanoid necessitates a policy that can generalize across substantial variations in scene layouts, obstacle arrangements, object categories (mass, shape, color), and object start/goal poses, even within the same room type. This requires the policy to adapt to novel configurations without prior knowledge or scene-specific fine-tuning.
- **Unified one-policy end-to-end whole-body control:** The goal is to achieve a single, general policy that directly outputs actions, rather than relying on pre-trained skill libraries or invoking multiple, specialized controllers for different sub-tasks or environments. This approach aims to avoid the fragility associated with composing fixed skills or requiring explicit stage boundaries.

The construction of the LHM-Humanoid dataset is motivated by the absence of a benchmark that provides task and scene specifications without offering scene-specific ground-truth motion sequences. This design choice encourages the development of learning approaches that acquire task behaviors from objectives rather than through direct motion mimicry. Furthermore, the high variability within and across room categories within the dataset underscores the insufficiency of room-specific controllers or fixed skill libraries, motivating research into genuine generalization capabilities.

The algorithmic solution, centered on a single deployable policy, is motivated by the observation that direct end-to-end reinforcement learning often fails to converge on such long-horizon, contact-rich tasks due to credit assignment and exploration challenges. Moreover, given the diverse and variable environments, approaches relying on fixed skill composition are likely to be fragile. The research also includes an extension to a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, motivated by the objective of moving towards real-world interactive deployment, where control is driven by egocentric RGB observations and natural language instructions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LHM-Humanoid framework formalizes long-horizon whole-body loco-manipulation in messy scenes and employs a three-stage training pipeline. All policies are trained jointly across 350 diverse scenes and tasks to foster cross-scene generalization. The approach avoids scene-specific ground-truth motion sequences and skill libraries, instead focusing on learning from task objectives.

The pipeline comprises:
1. **Dual-Teacher Policy Training:** This stage involves training two goal-conditioned teacher policies using physics-based reinforcement learning.
 - **Teacher Policy 1: First-Object Loco-Manipulation with Release-and-Retreat:** This teacher completes the initial fetch-carry-place cycle. It is designed to conclude its task in a stable configuration, facilitating a seamless transition to the second teacher.
 - **Pretraining on single-object tasks:** Teacher 1 is initialized by training on 700 decomposed single-object tasks derived from the full two-object episodes. This exposes the policy to varied start states, clutter, and layouts. The reward function for pretraining, `r_total = r_task + r_style`, incorporates an Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) component (`r_style`) to encourage human-like motion, distinguishing it from non-human-like state transitions using a learned discriminator.
 - **Release-and-retreat fine-tuning:** After pretraining, Teacher 1 is fine-tuned to explicitly release the placed object and retreat to a safe distance. This behavior serves to prevent interference with the placed object and to produce a stable initial state for Teacher 2. The fine-tuning reward mechanism specifically rewards increasing distance between the robot's torso and hands and the placed object, but only after the object has been successfully placed within a threshold of its goal.
 - **Teacher Policy 2: Recovery Locomotion and Next-Object Loco-Manipulation from Non-Canonical States:** Teacher 2 takes over immediately after Teacher 1 completes its cycle, without any environment reset. Its role is to complete the subsequent fetch-carry-place cycle for the next object.
 - **Challenge of non-canonical states:** The handoff state from Teacher 1 can involve diverse robot postures, arbitrary orientations (potentially away from the next target), and partially obstructed scenes.
 - **Compound problem solving:** Teacher 2 is trained to solve recovery locomotion (regaining a stable posture), reorientation towards the next target, obstacle-aware navigation, and whole-body loco-manipulation (grasp, carry, place).
 - **Training:** Episodes first execute Teacher 1, then Teacher 2 is rolled out from the resulting non-canonical state and optimized with the same single-object objective as in pretraining. This exposes Teacher 2 to a broader, harder state distribution, promoting robustness to off-nominal initial conditions.

2. **Distillation into a Unified End-to-End Policy:** The two teacher policies are distilled into a single student policy that can execute the entire long-horizon episode seamlessly, without resets or hard-coded stage boundaries.
 - **DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) approach:** This iterative imitation learning algorithm is used for distillation. During data aggregation, Teacher 1 supervises the student for the first-object cycle (locomotion-to-grasp, carry, place, release, retreat), and Teacher 2 supervises for the subsequent next-object cycle. A finite-state machine (FSM) selects the appropriate supervising teacher at each timestep.
 - **Goal:** To enable the student to output actions directly for the full episode, handling the entire state distribution induced by sequential, contact-rich manipulation across diverse scenes. This circumvents the challenges of direct end-to-end RL on such complex tasks.

3. **Extension: Distillation into an End-to-End VLA Model:** As an extension, the unified student policy is further distilled into a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model.
 - **Input modalities:** The VLA model replaces privileged oracle state inputs with egocentric RGB observations and natural language instructions specifying the current placement goal.
 - **Training:** The unified student policy provides action supervision. The VLA model is trained via DAgger to imitate these actions using only RGB and language inputs.
 - **Deployment:** Allows users to issue consecutive natural language instructions, with the VLA policy executing fetch-carry-place cycles with release-and-retreat transitions within the same episode.

**Implementation Details:**
- The system uses Isaac Gym for physics simulation, leveraging 16,384 parallel environments.
- Teacher 1 is trained for 10,000 epochs, Teacher 2 for 7,000 epochs, and the unified student (via DAgger) for 50,000 epochs.
- Simulation runs at 60 Hz, with policy inference at 30 Hz.
- Network architectures: Teacher policies use an AMP-style design (MLP actor, critic, discriminator). The VLA model uses a multi-modal actor fusing visual features (EfficientNet backbone), language features (MLP projection), and proprioceptive features, decoded by a residual MLP.
- The AMP style reward uses reference motion data from OMOMO and SAMP datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using a dataset of 350 training tasks and 66 unseen tasks across four room types (bedroom, living room, kitchen, warehouse), featuring 79 distinct objects. Performance was evaluated using Success 1 (first cycle), Success 2 (second cycle), Success All (both cycles), and placement errors (Dist 1, Dist 2).

**Quantitative Results:**

- **Results on 350 Training Tasks (Table 2):**
 - End-to-End RL baseline exhibited near-complete failure (0.00% Success All), indicating the intractability of direct RL for this complex, long-horizon task.
 - Curriculum RL achieved 47.19% Success All, improving on early stages but showing degradation as tasks compounded.
 - Hierarchical RL and TokenHSI showed limited performance (20.84% and 27.55% Success All, respectively), attributed to the brittleness of skill composition under geometric variations.
 - HumanVLA achieved 29.92% Success All, demonstrating degradation over long horizons due to distribution shift in sequential interactions.
 - InterMimic showed the lowest success (7.45% Success All), indicating poor transfer from motion-only supervision.
 - **LHM-Humanoid-T (dual-teacher) achieved the highest performance (72.38% Success All)** with the lowest placement errors (0.25 m for Dist 1, 0.48 m for Dist 2). The distilled **LHM-Humanoid-S (unified student) was competitive at 71.14% Success All**.
 - Ablation studies confirmed the importance of the release-and-retreat (RR) objective (decreasing Success All by 16.19%) and Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) (decreasing Success All by 61.99%).

- **Results on 66 Unseen Tasks (Table 3):**
 - All baselines experienced performance degradation on unseen scenes. Curriculum RL dropped to 39.50% Success All, and Hierarchical RL to 17.26%, struggling to extrapolate due to narrow training distributions.
 - HumanVLA, InterMimic, and TokenHSI also degraded significantly (25.06%, 5.89%, and 22.89% Success All, respectively).
 - **LHM-Humanoid-T (63.20% Success All) and LHM-Humanoid-S (61.60% Success All) maintained superior generalization capabilities** with lower placement errors (LHM-Humanoid-T: 0.35/0.50 m). This was attributed to RR limiting error propagation and the dual-teacher setup exposing the policy to a broader range of intermediate states.

- **VLA Extension Results (Table 4):**
 - When distilled into VLA models, baseline performance degraded substantially. End-to-End RL collapsed (0.00% Success All), and Hierarchical RL remained weak (16.50%), indicating poor transfer when oracle states are replaced by RGB-and-language inputs.
 - HumanVLA and TokenHSI VLA models showed 23.46% and 23.69% Success All, respectively, still significantly trailing the proposed method.
 - **LHM-Humanoid-S (VLA) achieved 63.71% Success All** with placement errors of 0.42/0.56 m, demonstrating robustness to the modality gap and effective transfer of learned sequential behaviors to egocentric sensing.
 - Ablations again underscored the necessity of both RR and AMP for effective VLA distillation, with removal leading to significant performance drops.

- **Extension to More Than Two Objects (Table 5):**
 - Models trained on two-object episodes were directly evaluated on longer sequences (up to five objects) without fine-tuning.
 - All baselines showed steep degradation, collapsing to near-zero Success All beyond three objects due to error accumulation. Curriculum RL's Success All dropped to 1.67% for five objects, and Hierarchical RL to 0.00%.
 - **LHM-Humanoid-S maintained a substantial advantage**, achieving 60.95% success on the third object, 38.54% on the fourth, and **18.07% overall completion for five-object sequences**. This indicated that the robustness acquired generalizes effectively to longer horizons due to stable inter-stage transitions and broad state coverage.

**Qualitative Visualization (Figure 3):**
Rollouts across diverse environments (warehouse, living room, bedroom, kitchen) showed the policy's ability to generalize to novel furniture arrangements and obstacle placements without scene-specific fine-tuning. Observed strengths included:
- Robust balance during heavy-object transport (e.g., deep-squat lifts for large boxes in the warehouse).
- Contact-aware navigation in cluttered spaces (e.g., navigating narrow passages, re-planning body orientation to avoid collisions, adjusting arm posture).
- Grasp recovery (repositioning hand for reattempt when initial contact is imperfect).
- Smooth and stable release-and-retreat transitions between objects, enabling seamless continuation of subsequent cycles.

**Failure Analysis:**
Two persistent failure modes were identified:
1. **Navigation in narrow, non-convex passages:** The policy occasionally misjudged clearance or deviated from feasible corridors, leading to collisions or deadlocks in geometrically extreme configurations.
2. **Grasp failures for very flat objects:** Objects like pillows or shallow pots were prone to slips or unstable grips under contact and perception noise. Both issues are attributed to configurations not extensively covered by the 350-scene training distribution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces LHM-Humanoid, addressing a significant challenge in humanoid robotics: long-horizon whole-body loco-manipulation in diverse, cluttered, and dynamic environments. By providing a benchmark and a learning framework, the work advances the capability of humanoid agents beyond single-task or short-horizon interactions that have traditionally been the focus of the field.

The core contribution is demonstrating a viable approach to learn complex, continuous multi-stage behaviors from task objectives, rather than relying on brittle skill libraries or direct motion mimicry. The dual-teacher distillation framework, combined with the explicit release-and-retreat objective, provides a mechanism to overcome common challenges in long-horizon reinforcement learning, such as intractable credit assignment, exploration difficulties, and error propagation. This methodology enables the learning of a unified policy that exhibits stable inter-stage transitions and broad state coverage.

The results show substantial improvements over existing baselines in terms of task success and generalization, particularly on unseen scenes and extended multi-object sequences beyond the training regime. This suggests that the learned policy acquires genuinely robust and reusable skills for complex real-world-like scenarios. The successful distillation into a VLA model further broadens the applicability, indicating progress towards intuitive human-robot interaction through natural language instructions and egocentric visual perception.

The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted:
- **Advancing General-Purpose Humanoids:** The ability of humanoids to perform long-horizon, complex tasks in varied environments moves them closer to becoming more autonomous and adaptable agents for household assistance, logistics, or hazardous environments.
- **Framework for Complex Skill Acquisition:** The dual-teacher distillation paradigm offers a novel and effective strategy for training unified policies for multi-stage, contact-rich tasks, which could be adopted in other domains of robotics.
- **Benchmarking and Dataset Contribution:** The LHM-Humanoid benchmark provides a challenging platform for future research, pushing the boundaries of cross-scene generalization, long-horizon control, and whole-body coordination in humanoid robotics.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** The VLA extension lays groundwork for more natural and intuitive interfaces, where users can instruct robots using language for sequential tasks, without requiring explicit programming or detailed state information.
- **Identification of Future Research Directions:** The identified failure modes (complex narrow passages, flat object grasping) highlight areas where further research, potentially involving expanded dataset coverage or improved perception and contact modeling, is needed. The acknowledged limitations regarding simulation-to-reality transfer, fixed object/room categories, and the complexity of the pipeline also provide clear avenues for subsequent investigation.
