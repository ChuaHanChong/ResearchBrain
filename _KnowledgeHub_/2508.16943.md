# LHM-Humanoid: Learning a Unified Policy for Long-Horizon Humanoid Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation in Diverse Messy Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> LHM-Humanoid presents a benchmark and learning framework that enables a unified policy for long-horizon humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation across diverse, cluttered environments. This approach achieves over 60% success on unseen scenes for two-object sequences and maintains substantial performance across up to five objects, outperforming prior methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid loco-manipulation typically focuses on short-horizon or single-object interactions, lacking sustained whole-body coordination for long-duration tasks.
- Policies often struggle with cross-scene generalization, performing poorly when faced with diverse scene layouts, object categories, and start/goal poses beyond their training distribution.
- Developing a single, unified policy for complex multi-stage tasks is challenging due to the difficulties of direct end-to-end reinforcement learning and the fragility of composing fixed skill libraries.

## Method

- A three-stage training pipeline is introduced, comprising ==dual-teacher policy training==, distillation into a unified policy, and an extension to a ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== model.
- Two goal-conditioned teacher policies are trained: one for the first object's loco-manipulation with an explicit ==release-and-retreat== phase, and another for subsequent objects from diverse, non-canonical states.
- The knowledge from these teachers is distilled into a single, end-to-end student policy using ==DAgger==, which then directly outputs actions for continuous long-horizon episodes without explicit stage boundaries.

## Results

- The **LHM-Humanoid** framework, with its dual-teacher (**72.38%** Success All) and unified student (**71.14%** Success All) policies, significantly outperformed baselines on training tasks, which largely failed (e.g., End-to-End RL at **0.00%**).
- On **66 unseen tasks**, the unified student policy maintained strong generalization with **61.60%** Success All, whereas all baselines experienced substantial performance degradation.
- The learned policy was robust to longer sequences, completing **18.07%** of five-object tasks without further fine-tuning, while other methods collapsed to near-zero success beyond three objects.
- An extension successfully distilled the policy into a VLA model, achieving **63.71%** Success All on two-object tasks using egocentric RGB and natural-language instructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dual-teacher framework with one policy focusing on a stable initial cycle and another on recovery from non-canonical states is effective for handling long-horizon, sequential tasks.
> - An explicit "release-and-retreat" objective after object placement is crucial for ensuring stable inter-task transitions, preventing interference, and allowing the humanoid to prepare for the next task.
> - Distilling the combined knowledge of specialized teachers into a single end-to-end policy using DAgger enables seamless execution across diverse environments and avoids the fragility of hand-engineered skill composition.
