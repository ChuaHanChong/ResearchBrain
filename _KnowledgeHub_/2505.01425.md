# GENMO: A GENeralist Model for Human MOtion

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers introduce GENMO, a unified diffusion-based framework that handles both human motion estimation and generation tasks through a dual-mode training paradigm, achieving state-of-the-art performance across global motion estimation, local motion estimation, and music-to-dance generation while enabling direct use of in-the-wild video data with 2D annotations.

## Problem

- Traditional approaches separate motion generation and estimation into distinct tasks requiring specialized models
- Existing methods struggle with long sequence generation and rely heavily on limited 3D motion capture datasets
- Complex post-processing is often needed to handle multi-conditioned motion generation

## Method

- Unified diffusion-based architecture with dual-mode training for both estimation and generation
- Novel estimation-guided training objective leveraging 2D video annotations
- Multi-text injection block enabling temporally-precise text conditioning
- Additive fusion mechanism for handling multiple input modalities

## Results

- State-of-the-art performance in global motion estimation, local motion estimation, and music-to-dance generation
- Maintains robust performance under severe occlusions and truncations
- Successfully generates variable-length motions without post-processing
- Demonstrates effective handling of multiple conditioning signals including text, music, and video

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative priors improve estimation under challenging conditions like occlusions
> - Diverse video data enhances generative expressiveness
> - Estimation and generation tasks can be effectively unified through careful architectural design
> - 2D training data substantially improves motion generation performance
