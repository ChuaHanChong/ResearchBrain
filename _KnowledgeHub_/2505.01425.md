# GENMO: A GENeralist Model for Human MOtion

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers introduce GENMO, a unified diffusion-based framework that handles both human motion estimation and generation tasks through a dual-mode training paradigm, achieving state-of-the-art performance across global motion estimation, local motion estimation, and music-to-dance generation while enabling direct use of in-the-wild video data with 2D annotations.

## Problem

- Traditional approaches separate motion generation and estimation into distinct tasks requiring specialized models
- Existing methods struggle with long sequence generation and rely heavily on limited 3D motion capture datasets
- Complex post-processing is often needed to handle multi-conditioned motion generation

## Method

- Unified diffusion-based architecture with dual-mode training for both estimation and generation
- Novel estimation-guided training objective leveraging 2D video annotations
- Multi-text injection block enabling temporally-precise text conditioning
- Additive fusion mechanism for handling multiple input modalities

## Results

- State-of-the-art performance in global motion estimation, local motion estimation, and music-to-dance generation
- Maintains robust performance under severe occlusions and truncations
- Successfully generates variable-length motions without post-processing
- Demonstrates effective handling of multiple conditioning signals including text, music, and video

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative priors improve estimation under challenging conditions like occlusions
> - Diverse video data enhances generative expressiveness
> - Estimation and generation tasks can be effectively unified through careful architectural design
> - 2D training data substantially improves motion generation performance

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Bridging the Gap:** This research addresses a key limitation in the field of human motion modeling – the traditional separation of motion generation and estimation into distinct tasks with specialized models. This separation hinders knowledge transfer and necessitates maintaining separate models for each task.
- **Synergistic Relationship:** The paper builds upon recent studies that have highlighted the synergistic relationship between motion estimation and generation. Generative models provide robust priors for motion estimation, particularly in challenging scenarios, while large-scale video data enhances the realism of generative models.
- **Generalist Models:** The concept of a unified, generalist model for human motion is gaining traction. GENMO is positioned as a significant advancement in this direction, offering a single framework capable of handling both estimation and generation tasks across multiple modalities.
- **Related Work Context:**
 - **Human Motion Generation:** The paper cites a range of recent works in human motion generation, including those leveraging text, actions, speech, music, and scenes as conditioning signals. The key distinction of GENMO is its ability to handle both generation and estimation, whereas most existing methods focus solely on generative tasks. GENMO addresses the limitations of long-sequence motion generation by introducing a diffusion-based architecture that enables seamless generation of arbitrary-length motions without complex post-processing. It also addresses the data scarcity issue by directly leveraging in-the-wild videos with 2D annotations, reducing reliance on noisy 3D data.
 - **Human Motion Estimation:** The paper references extensive research in human pose estimation from images, videos, and sparse marker data, with recent works focusing on estimating global human motion in world-space coordinates. GENMO extends this direction by jointly modeling generation and estimation within a unified diffusion framework.
- **Diffusion Models:** GENMO leverages diffusion models, a powerful class of generative models that have recently achieved state-of-the-art results in various domains, including image synthesis and human motion generation. By reformulating motion estimation as constrained motion generation, GENMO leverages the strengths of diffusion models for both tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Unifying Motion Estimation and Generation:** The primary objective is to develop a unified model that can perform both human motion estimation and generation within a single framework, eliminating the need for separate specialized models.
- **Improving Accuracy and Diversity:** The researchers aim to achieve accurate global motion estimation while simultaneously enabling diverse motion generation from various input modalities.
- **Leveraging Multimodal Conditioning:** The motivation includes creating a model that can seamlessly integrate and process diverse conditioning signals, such as monocular videos, 2D keypoints, text descriptions, music, and 3D keyframes, at different time intervals.
- **Enhancing Generative Diversity:** The paper aims to enhance the generative diversity of the model by leveraging in-the-wild videos with 2D annotations, overcoming the limitations of existing datasets that are limited in size and diversity.
- **Eliminating Post-Processing:** A key goal is to create a model that generates multi-conditioned motions in a single feedforward diffusion pass, without requiring complex post-processing steps.
- **Addressing Contrasting Objectives:** The research aims to address the challenges posed by the contrasting objectives of generation (producing diverse outputs from abstract inputs) and estimation (precise motion reconstruction from concrete observations).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **GENMO Architecture:** The proposed model, GENMO, is built upon a diffusion model framework and incorporates a novel dual-mode training paradigm. It consists of an additive fusion block, a RoPE-based Transformer block, and a multi-text injection block.
- **Dual-Mode Training:**
 - **Estimation Mode:** The model is trained to perform maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) of motion based on conditional signals. This involves feeding the diffusion denoiser with zero-initialized noise and the largest diffusion timestep, forcing the model to produce accurate motion estimates.
 - **Generation Mode:** The model is trained using traditional diffusion training by sampling noisy motions and timesteps according to a predefined schedule, enabling the model to learn rich generative distributions from the conditioning signals.
- **Estimation-Guided Training Objective:** An estimation-guided training objective is introduced to effectively leverage in-the-wild videos with 2D annotations, expanding the model's generative capabilities. This involves generating a pseudo-clean motion from the estimation mode and then applying a 2D reprojection loss on the predicted clean motion using the 2D keypoint annotations.
- **Variable-Length Motion Handling:** The architecture is designed to process variable-length motion sequences and seamlessly integrate arbitrary combinations of multi-modal conditioning signals at different time intervals.
- **Multi-Text Attention Mechanism:** A novel multi-text injection block is proposed to facilitate text-conditioned motion generation while accommodating multiple text inputs with user-specified time windows.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** GENMO achieves state-of-the-art performance across various tasks, including global motion estimation, local motion estimation, and music-to-dance generation.
- **Unified Framework Validation:** The experimental results validate the efficacy of GENMO as a unified generalist framework for human motion modeling.
- **Bidirectional Benefits:** The results demonstrate bidirectional benefits: generative priors improve estimation under challenging conditions like occlusions, while diverse video data enhances generative expressiveness.
- **Effectiveness of Dual-Mode Training:** Ablation studies demonstrate that omitting the estimation objective significantly degrades global motion estimation performance, confirming its crucial role in enhancing consistency between predicted motions and input videos.
- **Impact of Generative Prior:** Comparison against a pure regression baseline reveals that incorporating generative priors enhances motion quality in human motion estimation tasks.
- **2D Training Enhancement:** Results indicate that incorporating 2D training substantially enhances motion generation performance across both HumanML3D and Motion-X datasets.
- **Robustness Under Occlusion:** Evaluation on the challenging 3DPW-XOCC dataset reveals that the generative prior enables GENMO to maintain robust performance even under severe occlusions and truncations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Advances in Human Motion Modeling:** GENMO represents a significant advancement in human motion modeling by providing a unified framework for both estimation and generation tasks.
- **Improved Accuracy and Realism:** The model's ability to leverage multimodal conditioning signals and generative priors leads to more accurate and realistic human motion representations.
- **Wider Range of Applications:** The generalist nature of GENMO enables it to be applied to a wider range of applications, including gaming, animation, 3D content creation, virtual reality, and augmented reality.
- **Reduced Reliance on 3D Motion Capture Data:** The model's ability to leverage in-the-wild videos with 2D annotations reduces the reliance on expensive and time-consuming 3D motion capture data.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens up new avenues for future research, including integrating camera estimation inside GENMO and enabling facial expressions and hand articulation in the model.
- **Real-time Applications:** The feedforward nature of GENMO, coupled with its ability to handle variable-length motions, makes it potentially suitable for real-time applications.
- **Applications in AI-driven Content Creation:** This type of research can greatly impact content creation pipelines, allowing for more intuitive and automated generation of realistic human motion for various digital media.
