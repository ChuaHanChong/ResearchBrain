# SpatialThinker: Reinforcing 3D Reasoning in Multimodal LLMs via Spatial Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Oxford and UC Santa Cruz developed SPATIALTHINKER, a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that utilizes a multi-objective dense spatial reward function within an online reinforcement learning framework. Trained on a compact 7K synthetic dataset, this model achieved state-of-the-art open-source performance on 3D spatial reasoning benchmarks and improved generalization on real-world visual question answering tasks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) consistently struggle with robust 3D spatial understanding, failing to reliably comprehend complex 3D relationships, depth, and precise object localization.
- Existing methods for enhancing spatial reasoning in MLLMs typically require millions of training samples or explicit 3D inputs, leading to high computational costs and limited scalability.
- Reinforcement learning approaches often use sparse rewards (e.g., final answer correctness), which provide insufficient guidance for learning intricate, multi-step spatial reasoning processes.

## Method

- A synthetic ==STVQA-7K== dataset is created from human-annotated scene graphs enriched with 34 detailed spatial relations for data-efficient supervision.
- A ==multi-objective lexicographically gated dense spatial reward== combines format, accuracy, object count, and ==Complete IoU (CIoU)==-based spatial alignment to provide fine-grained guidance (prevents reward hacking).
- Online ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== trains the MLLM with dense spatial rewards, iteratively refining autoregressive reasoning+answer generation; all model parameters updated.

## Results

- **SPATIALTHINKER-7B** attained **78.2%** average accuracy on CV-Bench and outperformed GPT-4o by **+12.1%** on 3DSRBench.
- Consistently surpassed SFT and sparse-reward RL baselines across **12** spatial benchmarks: **+6.0%** over SFT, **1.8×** the gain of sparse-reward RL.
- Trained on only **7K** synthetic samples, achieved SOTA open-source performance with **81.7%** on VStarBench and strong generalization to real-world VQA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense, structured spatial rewards with lexicographic gating are essential for MLLMs to learn robust 3D spatial reasoning, effectively preventing reward hacking and guiding complex, multi-step processes.
> - Effective spatial grounding can be achieved with a comparatively small, high-quality synthetic dataset (7K samples) when combined with a sophisticated reward mechanism, reducing reliance on massive data collections.
> - Reinforcement learning with rich, dense feedback significantly enhances generalization and transferability of spatial priors, improving performance on both synthetic spatial tasks and diverse real-world visual reasoning benchmarks.
