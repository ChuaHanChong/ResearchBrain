# Towards Forceful Robotic Foundation Models: a Literature Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A literature survey of 25 transformer- and diffusion-based robot manipulation policies that condition on force, proprioception and tactile sensing, comparing them along sensing hardware, demonstration collection, action space, and representation learning. Plotting 64 tasks by force magnitude against makespan, it argues current imitation learning rarely operates in dynamics regimes where force truly matters, and maps what touch-based robot foundation models still require.

## Problem

- Current robot foundation models ingest only vision, language and position or joint data; even recent large models log thousands of episodes per task while recording no force.
- Tactile policy work is fragmented: the reviewed papers span 14 sensor products and 53 distinct tasks, with no shared benchmark and no policy covering more than a handful of tasks.
- Force data stays scarce because collection is heterogeneous and human-bottlenecked, so 76% of reviewed works train on fewer than 200 demonstrations, blocking foundation-model-scale pretraining.

## Method

- A **force-versus-makespan taxonomy** placing 64 tasks in ==logarithmic force bins== (0.1-1N delicate, 1-10N typical, above 10N high) against task durations split at five seconds.
- Single-category distribution analyses over six touch sensor classes, five data collection methods, three ==action spaces==, dataset-size buckets and policy architectures, plus a public per-task reference table.
- A comparative synthesis separating ==visuotactile representation learning== (==GelSight== deformation images, vision-touch-language encoders) from ==low-dimensional force encoding== (raw, MLP, ==Fourier spectrogram==), and ==explicit force control== from ==implicit position control==.

## Results

- GelSight-type finger optical sensing dominates at **36%** of papers, teleoperation is the plurality data collection method at **36%**, and diffusion backbones account for **52%** of architectures.
- Cartesian position control covers **64%** of policies, yet explicit force control pays: ablating learned variable compliance dropped absolute success by **81%**, and force feedback cut task completion time **17%**.
- Only **two** works exceed **2,000** episodes: one with **26,866** teleoperated multimodal tactile trajectories, another with **50,000** simulated contact-rich tool-use skills plus **400** real demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Force is low-dimensional, interpretable and causally linked to motion, so raw or lightly encoded signals often suffice; heavy representation learning is mainly a visuotactile problem.
> - Predicting controller parameters such as stiffness, virtual targets or target wrenches, rather than poses, offloads reactivity to a high-frequency controller that position-space imitation cannot match.
> - Touch is redundant rather than indispensable, since compliant mechanisms and impedance control recover much of its benefit, making force-less minimalist robots a legitimate design path.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits Into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is situated at the intersection of several active research areas:

- **Robotics and Automation:** It addresses a core challenge in robotics: enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks that require precise force control and tactile sensing.
- **Machine Learning (Specifically Imitation Learning):** It focuses on using imitation learning (IL), particularly with Transformer and Diffusion-based models, to train robots to mimic human-level dexterity.
- **Foundation Models:** The paper directly engages with the emerging trend of developing "robot foundation models" – large, general-purpose models that can perform a wide range of robotic tasks. This is very nascent in the field, and this survey helps pave the way for future research.
- **Tactile Sensing:** It emphasizes the importance of tactile sensing (force and touch) in robotic manipulation, contrasting it with the predominantly vision-based approaches in current robot foundation models.

The paper addresses a gap in the current research landscape. While significant progress has been made in robot learning and foundation models, the integration of force and touch information has lagged behind. Most existing foundation models rely primarily on visual input and position control, which can be insufficient for tasks requiring delicate manipulation or precise interaction with the environment. This paper bridges the gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The main objectives and motivations of the paper are:

- **To survey and analyze contemporary methods for integrating force (proprioception and tactile sensing) into robot manipulation policy learning.** The paper aims to provide a comprehensive overview of different approaches for sensing force, collecting data, learning representations, and controlling robots using force information.
- **To articulate when and why forces are needed in robot learning.** The authors aim to identify the specific tasks and scenarios where force control is crucial and to understand the advantages of using force information over relying solely on position control.
- **To highlight opportunities to improve the learning of contact-rich, generalist robot policies.** The paper seeks to identify areas where further research is needed to develop more capable touch-based robot foundation models.
- **To inspire the next generation of robot foundation models.** By juxtaposing different approaches and highlighting the potential of force-based learning, the authors hope to stimulate further research and development in this area.
- **Addressing limitations of vision-centric models:** The authors point out that vision alone may not suffice for true dexterity. Errors in position can lead to large force errors, and force provides a richer representation of motion.
- **Clarifying the role of force:** The authors aim to address the question of how and when forces should be employed during learning, especially considering the potential for implicit techniques like impedance control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper employs a literature survey methodology. The authors systematically review and analyze a collection of relevant research papers in the field of robot learning and manipulation. Their approach involves:

- **Identifying relevant papers:** They focus on papers that address the integration of force and touch sensing in robot policy learning, particularly those using Transformer and Diffusion-based models.
- **Categorizing and comparing approaches:** They categorize the papers based on different aspects, such as force sensing methods, data collection techniques, representation learning methods, and control strategies.
- **Analyzing the strengths and weaknesses of different approaches:** They critically evaluate the various techniques, highlighting their advantages, limitations, and potential areas for improvement.
- **Synthesizing the findings:** They draw conclusions based on their analysis, identifying key trends, challenges, and opportunities in the field.

The survey also includes a background section that provides context on human tactile sensing, force control in robotics, and policy learning. This helps to ground the subsequent analysis in the broader research landscape.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The key findings and results of the survey are:

- **Force is important for specific tasks:** Force and touch are particularly important for tasks such as pouring, peg-in-hole insertion, and handling delicate objects.
- **Current models lack dynamics:** The performance of imitation learning models is not yet at a level where force truly matters in many tasks.
- **Implicit vs. Explicit Force Control:** Force and touch can be measured and controlled implicitly (e.g., through impedance control) or explicitly through force sensors.
- **Need for improved learning:** Current robot foundation models focus predominantly on visual input, while force/touch sensing is a critical modality to improve robot learning.
- **Data collection remains a challenge:** Existing datasets often do not include sufficient force data. Data collection is difficult to scale in contact-rich environments, so improvements in simulation and automated approaches for creating datasets would be beneficial.
- **Control complexity varies:** Control schemes range from simple position control to compliance and admittance control.
- **Visuotactile sensing offers promise:** GelSight sensors provide detailed surface deformation data that can be combined with visual information to improve learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this survey paper are:

- **Provides a comprehensive overview of the field:** The paper offers a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners interested in robot learning and manipulation. It synthesizes a large body of work, providing a clear and organized overview of the different approaches and techniques.
- **Identifies key challenges and opportunities:** The paper highlights the key challenges that need to be addressed to develop more capable touch-based robot foundation models. It also identifies promising areas for future research, such as improving data collection methods, developing more effective representation learning techniques, and exploring novel control strategies.
- **Stimulates further research and development:** By highlighting the potential of force-based learning and identifying areas for improvement, the paper can inspire further research and development in this area.
- **Advances the development of more dexterous robots:** The integration of force and touch information can enable robots to perform more complex and delicate manipulation tasks, leading to the development of more capable and versatile robots. This has implications for a wide range of applications, including manufacturing, healthcare, and assistive robotics.
- **Informs the design of future robot foundation models:** The insights and recommendations in the paper can inform the design of the next generation of robot foundation models, leading to models that are more capable of interacting with the physical world in a safe and reliable manner.

The authors' call for more research into improving the understanding of representing forces and creating more data-efficient models is particularly important, as it directly addresses the need for robots that are cognizant of the physical world and scalable for widespread adoption. This type of high-level analysis is important in guiding future research directions.
