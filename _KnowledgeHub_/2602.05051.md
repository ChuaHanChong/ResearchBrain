# ReFORM: Reflected Flows for On-support Offline RL via Noise Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ReFORM, developed by researchers from MIT and Boston University, introduces a two-stage flow-based offline reinforcement learning method that prevents out-of-distribution actions by constructively enforcing support constraints via reflected flows. The approach demonstrates superior overall performance across 40 OGBench tasks and eliminates the need for per-task hyperparameter tuning, effectively learning multimodal policies.

## Problem

- Out-of-distribution (OOD) errors occur when learned policies generate actions outside the training data's support, leading to unreliable Q-function estimates.
- Existing regularization-based offline RL methods suffer from high hyperparameter sensitivity, requiring extensive and costly tuning for different tasks.
- Traditional policy classes struggle to model complex, multimodal action distributions often present in real-world offline datasets.

## Method

- A two-stage flow-based policy is learned: first, a behavior cloning (**BC**) flow maps a bounded source distribution to the behavior policy's actions.
- A 'noise generator' then uses a reflected flow to manipulate the BC flow's source distribution, ensuring all generated noise remains within the original bounded source.
- A projected Euler method for reflected flow in a hypersphere domain and policy distillation are used to maintain support constraints and improve computational efficiency.

## Results

- ReFORM consistently achieved superior performance across **40** OGBench tasks on both CLEAN and NOISY datasets compared to state-of-the-art baselines, operating with a single set of hyperparameters.
- Visualizations on a toy example confirmed ReFORM's policy exploited multimodal optimal regions strictly within the behavior policy's support, unlike baselines that generated OOD actions.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the critical role of the bounded source distribution and the reflected flow mechanism for effective, on-support noise generation and overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Theoretical analysis indicates that statistical distance regularization (e.g., KL or Wasserstein divergence) either over-restricts policy improvement or does not guarantee on-support actions.
> - Directly enforcing a support constraint by construction through reflected flows provably prevents OOD errors without restrictive regularization.
> - The use of a bounded source distribution for the initial behavior cloning flow is essential for enabling the support constraint mechanism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) focuses on training optimal policies using pre-collected datasets, circumventing the need for further environment interactions. This approach is particularly valuable in domains where real-world interactions are costly or unsafe. A central challenge in offline RL is the **out-of-distribution (OOD) error**, which occurs when a learned policy generates actions not well-represented in the training data. This can lead to unreliable Q-function estimates and suboptimal policy behavior.

Prior research has attempted to mitigate OOD errors by regularizing the statistical distance (e.g., Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence, Wasserstein distance, Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD)) between the learned policy and the behavior policy from the dataset. However, these methods often face two limitations:
1. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: The regularization weight (e.g., α for KL divergence) is a crucial hyperparameter that often requires extensive and costly hand-tuning for each specific task and dataset.
2. **Restrictiveness vs. Completeness**: KL divergence regularization, while preventing OOD actions, can be overly restrictive, hindering policy improvement if optimal actions are rare in the behavior policy's distribution. Conversely, metrics like Wasserstein distance may not fully prevent OOD actions despite strong empirical performance.

Another challenge in offline RL is the need to model **multimodal action distributions**. Traditional policy classes, often relying on unimodal distributions like Gaussians, struggle to capture the complex action variability present in many real-world offline datasets. Recent advancements in generative models, specifically diffusion models and flow policies, offer significantly higher expressivity for modeling such complex distributions. However, integrating these expressive models with OOD error mitigation techniques without sacrificing their expressivity or reintroducing hyperparameter tuning difficulties remains an active area of research.

This work, ReFORM, addresses these combined challenges by proposing an offline RL method based on flow policies that enforces a *support constraint* by construction. This approach aims to provide robust OOD prevention without the need for statistical distance regularization and its associated hyperparameter tuning, while simultaneously maintaining the expressive power of flow-based policies to represent multimodal action distributions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an offline reinforcement learning algorithm that effectively learns high-performing policies from fixed datasets, specifically addressing two critical issues: out-of-distribution (OOD) errors and the need to model multimodal action distributions.

The specific motivations driving the development of ReFORM are detailed as follows:

1. **Directly Address Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Errors with Support Constraints**: The authors identify that existing methods, which typically penalize statistical distances (e.g., KL divergence or Wasserstein distance) between the learned policy and the behavior policy, have inherent limitations.
 - **KL Divergence is Overly Restrictive**: Proposition 1 theoretically demonstrates that while a bounded KL divergence implies the learned policy's support is contained within the behavior policy's support, the inverse is not true. This means that constraining KL divergence can be too conservative, preventing the policy from exploring optimal actions that exist within the behavior policy's support but have low probability density. Such restrictiveness impedes policy improvement beyond the behavior policy.
 - **Wasserstein Distance Does Not Guarantee Support Constraints**: Proposition 2 establishes that constraining the Wasserstein distance, despite its empirical effectiveness in some offline RL settings, does not guarantee that the learned policy's actions remain within the behavior policy's support. This means OOD actions can still occur.
 - Motivated by these theoretical insights, the authors aim for a method that directly enforces the less restrictive *support constraint* (i.e., the learned policy's actions must be within the support of the behavior policy) by construction, thereby provably preventing OOD errors without undue conservatism.

2. **Enable Learning of Multimodal Policies**: Many real-world offline datasets and tasks exhibit complex, multimodal action distributions. Traditional policy representations, such as unimodal Gaussians, are often insufficient to capture this complexity. Recent advancements, like diffusion models and flow policies, offer the necessary expressivity. The motivation is to leverage these powerful generative models to learn complex, multimodal optimal policies.

3. **Eliminate Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: A significant practical challenge with regularization-based offline RL methods is the sensitivity of their performance to the choice of the regularization weight (e.g., `α` in Eq. 3). This hyperparameter often needs to be hand-tuned for each specific environment and dataset, which is computationally expensive and hinders scalability. A key motivation is to develop a method that performs robustly with a constant set of hyperparameters, thereby eliminating the need for such extensive tuning.

4. **Maximize Policy Improvement**: By avoiding overly restrictive statistical distance regularization, the authors aim to allow the learned policy to maximize the expected return more freely, potentially surpassing the performance of the behavior policy, especially in cases where the optimal actions are sparsely represented in the dataset.

In summary, ReFORM is motivated by the need for an offline RL algorithm that offers robust OOD action prevention via principled support constraints, maintains the expressivity required for multimodal policies, and reduces the significant hyperparameter tuning burden prevalent in current methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReFORM (Reflected Flows for On-support Offline RL via Noise Manipulation) is a two-stage flow-based offline RL method designed to address OOD errors and multimodal action distributions by enforcing support constraints constructively.

The core idea is to learn a behavior cloning (BC) flow policy that maps a *bounded source distribution* to the behavior policy's action distribution. Then, a "noise generator" uses a *reflected flow* to manipulate the source distribution of this BC flow policy. By ensuring the manipulated source distribution remains within the bounds of the original source, the final policy's actions are guaranteed to stay within the support of the behavior policy, thus avoiding OOD actions.

The methodology consists of three main components:

1. **Flow-Based Behavior Policy Learning**:
 - **Bounded Source Distribution**: ReFORM first learns a BC flow policy, denoted `ψ_θ1(t, z; s)`. Unlike prior works that often use unbounded Gaussian distributions as sources, ReFORM employs a uniform distribution over a *d-dimensional hypersphere* `q_BC = U(B^d_l)` as its source distribution. The radius `l` of this hypersphere is chosen to minimally enclose the known action space `A`, ensuring `B^d_l` is a reasonable approximation of the support for relevant actions. This choice is critical because it ensures that for any sample `z` from this bounded source, the output of the flow `ψ_θ1(1, z; s)` will be within the approximated support of the behavior policy `p_BC(a|s)`.
 - **Flow Matching**: The BC flow policy's corresponding velocity field `v_θ1(t, z; s)` is trained using a flow matching objective (Eq. 8). This objective minimizes the squared difference between the predicted velocity and the true velocity along linear conditional probability paths from `z` to `a`, where `(s, a)` are sampled from the dataset `D`.

2. **Reflected Flow-Based Noise Manipulation**:
 - **Constrained Noise Generation**: To optimize the policy's performance while maintaining the support constraint, ReFORM learns a *flow noise generator* `ψ_θ2(t, w; s)`. This generator transforms an initial noise variable `w` (also sampled from `U(B^d_l)`) into a manipulated noise `~z = ψ_θ2(1, w; s)`. This `~z` then serves as the input to the BC flow policy `ψ_θ1`. The composed policy is `μ_θ(w; s) = ψ_θ1(1, ψ_θ2(1, w; s); s)`.
 - **Reflected Flow for Support Guarantee**: A key innovation is the use of a *reflected flow* (Xie et al., 2024) for `ψ_θ2`. Standard flow models can generate outputs outside the desired bounded support. The reflected flow ensures that the generated noise `~z` always remains within the bounded hypersphere `B^d_l`, i.e., `supp(~q_BC) ⊆ supp(q_BC)`. This is achieved by solving a reflected Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) (Eq. 9) that includes a reflection term `dL_t`.
 - **Projected Euler Method**: For the practical implementation of the reflected flow in a hypersphere domain, ReFORM proposes a *projected Euler method* (Eq. 12). After each standard Euler integration step, if the predicted point `^z_k+1` falls outside the hypersphere `B^d_l`, it is projected back onto the sphere's surface. This mechanism effectively "reflects" any trajectory attempting to leave the bounded domain, guaranteeing that all generated noise samples remain within `B^d_l`.
 - **Theoretical Guarantees**:
 - **Theorem 1**: The authors formally prove that the target distribution of the noise generator (`~q_BC`) indeed stays within the support of the original BC policy's source distribution (`q_BC`).
 - **Theorem 2**: Consequently, the manipulated action distribution (`~p_BC`) of the BC flow policy is guaranteed to remain within the support of the original BC policy (`p_BC`). This provides a formal guarantee against OOD actions without explicit regularization.
 - **Optimization of Noise Generator**: The noise generator `ψ_θ2` is optimized to maximize the expected Q-value of the final policy `μ_θ` (Eq. 10), where the parameters `θ1` of the BC policy are kept fixed.

3. **Policy Distillation**:
 - **Computational Efficiency**: Calculating the gradient for the actor loss `L_NG` (Eq. 10) typically involves backpropagation through time (BPTT) across the multiple Euler steps of `ψ_θ1`. This can be computationally expensive.
 - **One-Step Policy**: To mitigate this, ReFORM incorporates a policy distillation step. It trains a separate, one-step policy `^μ_θ1(z; s)` to directly map the latent variable `z` to an action `a`. This `^μ_θ1` is learned to mimic the full `ψ_θ1` (Eq. 13), and then used in the actor loss to reduce the BPTT complexity during training of the noise generator.

By constructing the policy in this manner, ReFORM bypasses the need for sensitive statistical distance regularization hyperparameters and provides a principled way to learn expressive, multimodal policies that provably avoid OOD actions within the defined support.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on 40 challenging tasks from the OGBench benchmark, covering locomotion and manipulation, using both CLEAN (expert-generated) and NOISY (suboptimal and noisy) datasets. ReFORM was compared against state-of-the-art flow-based offline RL algorithms: Flow Q-Learning (FQL) with three regularization strengths (FQL(S), FQL(M), FQL(L)), Implicit Flow Q-Learning (IFQL), and Diffusion Steering via RL (DSRL). Notably, ReFORM used a constant set of hyperparameters across all tasks, while baselines relied on hand-tuned hyperparameters from their original papers.

**Q1: How does ReFORM perform compared to other offline RL algorithms with flow policies?**
- **Dominant Performance**: ReFORM consistently demonstrated superior overall performance across both CLEAN and NOISY datasets, as indicated by performance profile curves. It achieved the highest fraction of tasks with normalized scores close to 1, suggesting effective policy improvement without limitation.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness**: Baselines like DSRL and FQL(M), while performing well on CLEAN datasets (due to environment-specific hyperparameter tuning), experienced significant performance drops on NOISY datasets. In contrast, ReFORM maintained strong performance, highlighting its robustness to varying dataset quality and its ability to operate effectively without costly hyperparameter tuning. FQL(S), with weaker regularization, emerged as the second-best method on NOISY datasets, corroborating the paper's argument against overly restrictive regularization.

**Q2: Does ReFORM avoid the OOD issue without limiting the performance improvement?**
- **Toy Example Visualization**: A 2D toy example with a bimodal optimal Q-value function was used for visualization. ReFORM's learned policy distribution successfully identified and concentrated probability mass in both optimal regions (lower-left and upper-right corners), while remaining strictly within the estimated support boundaries of the behavior policy.
- **Contrast with Baselines**:
 - DSRL collapsed to a single mode and did not fully explore the support boundaries, likely due to its unimodal and squashed noise generation.
 - IFQL remained close to the behavior cloning policy, failing to reach maximal performance.
 - FQL variants (using Wasserstein distance regularization) produced actions that clearly ventured outside the behavior policy's estimated support, illustrating the OOD issue that Proposition 2 identified.
- These results empirically validate ReFORM's capability to maximize performance by exploiting the entire support of the behavior policy, without generating OOD actions, consistent with its theoretical guarantees.

**Q3: Is it necessary for the BC policy’s source distribution to have bounded support?**
- **ReFORM(U) (Unbounded Gaussian Source)**: A variant, ReFORM(U), using an unbounded standard Gaussian distribution for both the BC policy's source and the noise generator, failed to learn and suffered from severe OOD problems. This experimentally confirms that the choice of a bounded source distribution (`U(B^d_l)`) for the BC flow policy is crucial for enabling the support constraint and preventing OOD actions.
- **Gaussian(ξ) (Bounded Noise, Unbounded BC Source)**: Further ablations showed that even if the noise generator's output was bounded (as a confidence interval `ξ` of an unbounded Gaussian BC source), performance was highly sensitive to `ξ`. ReFORM, without this additional tuning parameter, achieved better performance. This reinforces the advantage of ReFORM's direct, bounded source approach.

**Q4: Is the reflected flow necessary for generating the targeted noise?**
- **Comparison with Alternatives**: ReFORM was compared against variants using alternative noise generators: ReFORM(MLP) (an MLP mapping state to noise), ReFORM(tanh) (flow with tanh squashing at the end), and ReFORM(Gaussian) (squashed Gaussian).
- **Superiority of Reflected Flow**: All alternative methods performed worse than ReFORM. MLP and Gaussian noise generators failed to capture multimodal distributions effectively, while tanh squashing suffered from gradient vanishing. Visualizations of generated noise further showed ReFORM's reflected flow producing multimodal, concentrated noise, unlike DSRL's unimodal, spread-out noise. This indicates that the reflected flow is effective for generating high-quality, bounded, and multimodal noise.

**Q5: How is our design of the reflection term?**
- **Alternative Reflection Designs**: Two other reflection term designs were tested: ReFORM(Cube) (using a hypercube domain with a different reflection method) and ReFORM(Sphere) (hypersphere domain with a "bouncing back" reflection).
- **Empirical Preference**: Both variants performed worse than ReFORM. The authors hypothesize that compensating for outbound velocities (ReFORM's chosen method) leads to more stable training than reflecting the velocities, although further theoretical explanation is suggested for future work.

**Q6: Is the distillation of the BC flow policy necessary?**
- **ReFORM(NoDistill) (Without Distillation)**: A variant removing the distillation step, requiring backpropagation through the full BC flow policy, showed a slight performance decrease compared to ReFORM. This finding suggests that the computational burden of a long backpropagation chain is detrimental and validates the inclusion of the policy distillation step.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ReFORM represents a notable advancement in offline Reinforcement Learning, offering a robust approach to two critical challenges: out-of-distribution (OOD) errors and the need for expressive policy representations.

**Significance of the Work:**

1. **Robust OOD Avoidance by Construction**: ReFORM's primary significance lies in its novel strategy for avoiding OOD errors. Instead of relying on statistical distance regularization, which has proven to be either overly restrictive (KL divergence) or insufficient (Wasserstein distance), ReFORM enforces the support constraint *by construction*. This is achieved through a two-stage flow policy, where a behavior cloning (BC) flow is combined with a *reflected flow* noise generator. Theoretical proofs (Theorems 1 and 2) formally guarantee that the learned policy's actions remain within the support of the behavior policy, providing a strong, principled foundation for OOD mitigation.
2. **Elimination of Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: A major practical impact of ReFORM is its ability to operate effectively with a *constant set of hyperparameters* across a wide range of tasks and datasets (CLEAN and NOISY). This eliminates the arduous and computationally expensive process of hand-tuning regularization weights, which is a significant bottleneck in applying many existing offline RL algorithms. This characteristic enhances the usability, scalability, and practical applicability of offline RL.
3. **Enhanced Policy Expressivity**: By leveraging flow policies and a reflected flow noise generator, ReFORM can model complex, multimodal action distributions. This expressivity is crucial for discovering optimal policies in environments where the optimal behavior is diverse and cannot be captured by simple unimodal distributions. The comparative experiments demonstrate that ReFORM's noise generation mechanism is superior to simpler alternatives in capturing such complexities.
4. **Improved Performance Across Diverse Settings**: Extensive experiments on 40 OGBench tasks, as well as additional D4RL and visual manipulation benchmarks, demonstrate that ReFORM consistently achieves competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines. Its robust performance on both high-quality (CLEAN) and low-quality (NOISY) datasets further highlights its generalizability.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Broader Adoption of Offline RL**: By simplifying the deployment of offline RL algorithms through reduced hyperparameter tuning and increased robustness to dataset quality, ReFORM could facilitate broader adoption of offline RL in real-world applications where data collection is expensive or online interaction is risky (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving, healthcare).
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The concept of enforcing support constraints by construction using reflected flows offers a new paradigm for offline RL. This could inspire further research into alternative generative models (e.g., diffusion policies) that integrate similar mechanisms for robust OOD control.
- **Advancements in Generative Models for Control**: The specific design of the reflected flow for bounded noise generation, particularly the projected Euler method for hyperspheres, could contribute to the broader field of generative modeling for constrained environments.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The authors identify several promising directions for future research:
- **Computational Efficiency of BPTT**: While policy distillation reduces some BPTT burden, training the noise generator still involves BPTT through the flow. Exploring shortcut models or pre-trained BC models could further improve computational efficiency.
- **Dependency on BC Model's Support Estimation**: ReFORM relies on the initial BC model's approximation of the behavior policy's support. If the BC model itself makes OOD errors or poorly estimates the support, ReFORM inherits this. Future work could integrate stricter BC methods or diagnostics.
- **Advanced Reflection Term Design**: The current reflection term design, while effective, is a nascent area. Research into more adaptive or learned reflection terms could enhance policy improvement.
- **Integration with Advanced Value Functions**: ReFORM employs a standard actor-critic structure. Combining it with more advanced value function learning methods could further improve performance.
- **Learning Speed on Expert Datasets**: ReFORM may learn slower than some regularized algorithms when the dataset consists solely of expert policies, due to the lack of explicit regularization to stay close to the expert. This represents a trade-off between explicit policy adherence and broad support exploration.

Overall, ReFORM presents a significant step towards more reliable and deployable offline RL systems, particularly for complex tasks requiring multimodal policies and robust OOD handling without extensive hyperparameter tuning.
