# Perception Tokens Enhance Visual Reasoning in Multimodal Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This work by researchers from the University of Washington and Google Research introduces "Perception Tokens" to enable Multimodal Language Models (MLMs) to internally generate and reason about intrinsic visual representations like depth maps and bounding boxes. This approach improves performance on visual reasoning tasks, achieving a 6.4 percentage point boost in relative depth estimation on BLINK and substantial gains in object counting over fine-tuned baselines, even surpassing tool-augmented closed-source models in some cases.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Language Models (MLMs) struggle with fundamental visual perception tasks that require detailed understanding of intrinsic image properties, such as 3D structure (e.g., relative depth) or 2D object instances (e.g., precise counting).
- Existing solutions like direct finetuning offer marginal improvements, and tool-using MLMs are computationally inefficient and prone to error propagation from external modules.
- While some advanced models can generate visual outputs (e.g., segmentation masks), they typically lack the ability to internally reason over their own generated outputs for subsequent tasks.

## Method

- Introduces =='Perception Tokens'==, which are intrinsic image representations (e.g., depth maps, bounding boxes) transformed into a tokenized format that MLMs can process and generate.
- Proposes ==AURORA==, a training framework that integrates these perception tokens into the MLM's ==chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning== process, allowing the model to use them as intermediate steps.
- Employs a ==specialist-to-generalist distillation strategy== to train the MLM to predict these tokens, alongside a curriculum learning approach that progresses from atomic token generation to multi-step visual reasoning tasks.

## Results

- LLaVA-AURORA improves relative depth estimation by **6.4** percentage points on the BLINK dataset compared to fine-tuned LLaVA and surpasses GPT-4 Turbo + Tool, with performance gains increasing on more complex datasets like HardBLINK.
- The model achieves substantial gains in object counting, with improvements of **+10.8%** on BLINK, **+11.3%** on CVBench, and **+8.3%** on SEED-Bench over fine-tuned LLaVA models, also outperforming GPT-4 Turbo + Tool.
- The system can decode its learned depth tokens back into coherent depth maps that qualitatively resemble ground truth and quantitatively outperform other methods in relative depth estimation when using its own generated maps, demonstrating the interpretability of its intermediate visual reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal Language Models (MLMs) can be augmented to internally generate and reason over intrinsic visual representations, such as depth maps and bounding boxes, akin to how language models perform chain-of-thought reasoning.
> - Integrating these 'Perception Tokens' enables MLMs to perform deeper visual reasoning, addressing tasks requiring precise spatial or instance-level understanding beyond high-level feature extraction.
> - A training framework combining specialist-to-generalist distillation and curriculum learning is effective for teaching MLMs to predict and utilize these novel visual tokens within their reasoning process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen a rapid convergence of vision and language through the development of Multimodal Language Models (MLMs). These models, often built upon large language model (LLM) architectures, excel at high-level tasks such as Visual Question Answering (VQA), image captioning, and conversational AI, by leveraging their ability to process and generate natural language in response to visual inputs. Architectures like Flamingo, Qwen-VL, BLIP-2, and LLaVA (the base model for this study) have pushed the boundaries of what MLMs can achieve in these domains.

However, the authors identify a critical gap: despite their impressive capabilities, current MLMs still struggle with fundamental visual perception tasks that demand detailed understanding of intrinsic image properties. These "mid-level and low-level" tasks include reasoning about 3D structures (e.g., relative depth estimation) or 2D object instances (e.g., precise object counting or localization). Traditional computer vision, in contrast, historically focused on extracting such intrinsic representations (e.g., depth maps, bounding boxes) to enable geometric and instance reasoning.

The paper highlights the limitations of existing approaches to bridge this gap:
1. **Direct Finetuning:** Simply finetuning MLMs on relevant data often leads to marginal improvements and poor generalization, as the models may not explicitly learn to represent or reason over the underlying perceptual information.
2. **Tool-using MLMs:** While MLMs can be augmented to call external, specialized vision models (e.g., a dedicated depth estimator or object detector), this approach is computationally intensive, memory-inefficient, and prone to error propagation due to cascading failures from separate modules.
3. **Any-to-any Networks (e.g., Unified-IO, LISA):** More recent advancements have enabled MLMs to *generate* visual outputs (like segmentation masks or depth maps). However, a crucial distinction, as pointed out by the authors, is that these models typically cannot *reason over their own generated outputs* to solve a subsequent, related task. They can produce a depth map, but not then use that depth map internally to determine which object is closer.

This research paper directly addresses this critical deficiency by proposing "Perception Tokens." Unlike previous methods, Perception Tokens are designed to be *intrinsic image representations* that MLMs can *internally generate and reason with*, much like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompts allow LLMs to perform multi-step linguistic reasoning. This represents a significant conceptual leap, aiming to endow MLMs with a more human-like, multi-modal reasoning capability that integrates perception directly into the inference process, rather than treating it as an external oracle. By enabling MLMs to leverage depth and bounding box predictions as intermediate reasoning steps, this work aims to expand their scope beyond purely language-based reasoning into a more robust form of visual intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the visual reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Language Models (MLMs) by enabling them to internally generate and reason over intermediate visual representations. The authors identify a core limitation of current MLMs: their inability to produce and utilize fundamental visual perception outputs, such as depth maps for 3D understanding or bounding boxes for 2D object localization, during their reasoning process. While specialized computer vision models excel at these tasks, MLMs typically lack this intrinsic perceptual understanding, limiting their performance on tasks that require detailed visual inference.

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Addressing Deficiencies in Fundamental Visual Perception:** MLMs often struggle with tasks like determining relative depth or accurately counting objects, which require a deeper understanding of visual scene intrinsics beyond superficial image features or linguistic descriptions.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
 - Finetuning MLMs on perception-heavy data has shown limited generalization.
 - Outsourcing computation to external, specialized vision tools is inefficient and introduces architectural complexity and potential failure points.
 - Existing "any-to-any" models can generate visual outputs but cannot intrinsically reason over them.
- **Enabling Intrinsic Visual Reasoning:** The core motivation is to allow MLMs to perform "chain-of-thought" like reasoning but within the visual modality. This means the model would internally generate representations (e.g., a depth map) and then use these representations as explicit intermediate steps to arrive at a final answer for a visual reasoning question. This parallels how linguistic chain-of-thought improves complex language reasoning.
- **Improving Generalization and Interpretability:** By integrating perception tokens intrinsically, the authors aim to improve the model's ability to generalize to novel visual reasoning scenarios and potentially increase the interpretability of its decisions by making its intermediate visual "thoughts" explicit.

In essence, the research seeks to move MLMs beyond being mere "image captioners" or "VQA responders" to becoming genuine "visual reasoners" by providing them with the necessary internal tools for perceptual understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed solution, **Perception Tokens**, is a novel approach to augment Multimodal Language Models (MLMs) with the ability to reason over intrinsic image representations. This is achieved through a new training method called **AURORA**, which integrates these tokens into the MLM's chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning process.

**Core Concept: Perception Tokens**
Perception Tokens are defined as intrinsic image representations (e.g., depth maps, bounding box coordinates) transformed into a tokenized format that can be processed and generated by the MLM. They act as auxiliary reasoning tokens, similar to how CoT prompts guide language models to generate intermediate linguistic steps. For example, to answer a relative depth question, the model might first generate a sequence of depth tokens, then use these tokens to deduce the answer.

**AURORA Training Framework:**

1. **Tokenization of Visual Representations (Section 3.3):**
 - **Pixel-level representation (e.g., Depth Maps):** For fine-grained spatial information, AURORA leverages a **Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQVAE)**. A VQVAE is trained to convert continuous depth maps into discrete token sequences. The paper uses a VQVAE with a codebook size of 128, compressing a 320x320 depth map into a 10x10 grid of 100 code indices. These indices are treated as new tokens (e.g., `DEPTH 0` to `DEPTH 127`), encapsulated by `DEPTH START` and `DEPTH END` tokens, expanding the MLM's vocabulary.
 - **Structured representation (e.g., Bounding Boxes):** For abstracted visual features like bounding boxes, direct encoding into structured tokens is used. Images are resized to 336x336 pixels, and 336 unique pixel tokens (`PIXEL 0` to `PIXEL 335`) are added to the vocabulary. Bounding boxes are then represented as tuples of four such tokens: `(PIXEL x1, PIXEL y1, PIXEL x2, PIXEL y2)`.

2. **Perception Token Prediction (Section 3.2):**
 - **Specialist-to-Generalist Distillation:** The MLM learns to generate these new auxiliary tokens by distilling knowledge from pre-trained, specialized vision models (e.g., Depth Anything for depth estimation, LVIS dataset annotations for bounding boxes). This is achieved through a cross-entropy loss, aligning the MLM's token predictions with the target distributions provided by the specialist models.
 - **Reconstruction Loss (Optional):** To further enhance token prediction and interpretability, a lightweight decoder `g` is introduced. This decoder maps the generated auxiliary tokens back to their original feature space (e.g., a depth map from depth tokens), with a reconstruction loss (L2 norm) reinforcing accuracy. However, ablation studies show this is optional and yields marginal gains, allowing for computational efficiency.

3. **Reasoning with Perception Tokens (Section 3.2):**
 - **Progressive Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** The model is trained to use perception tokens as intermediate steps in its reasoning process. This begins with simpler, single-step reasoning (generating tokens) and progresses to more complex, multi-step inference where the model generates a sequence of reasoning steps including perception tokens and then uses them to derive a final answer.
 - **Constrained Decoding:** During inference, sampling can be restricted to auxiliary tokens for intermediate steps, ensuring they serve their intended role.
 - **Information Bottleneck:** The reasoning chain can be truncated before the auxiliary token, forcing the model to rely solely on the generated perception tokens for subsequent steps.

4. **Curriculum Learning with Progressive CoTs (Section 3.4):**
 - To address challenges like catastrophic forgetting and effectively integrate new tokens, AURORA employs a curriculum learning strategy. Training starts with "atomic tasks" (purely generating the new perception tokens) and gradually introduces "multi-tasking data" that combines CoT reasoning and direct labeling.
 - A temperature-scaled Softmax function (Eq. 3, 4) controls the probability of sampling tasks of increasing difficulty over time. Harder tasks involve full CoT reasoning, while easier tasks focus on token generation.
 - For each downstream task (e.g., relative depth), three types of data subsets are created:
 1. **Token Generation Data:** Prompts the model to generate the perception tokens (e.g., "What is the depth map for the image?" -> sequence of depth tokens).
 2. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Data:** Prompts guide the model to generate perception tokens as intermediate steps before answering (e.g., "The depth map is <tokens>. Therefore, point D is closest to the camera.").
 3. **Direct Labeling Data:** Prompts for direct answers without explicit intermediate steps (e.g., "Which point is closest to the camera?" -> "Point D").
 - A sequential sampler is used for multi-tasking, presenting both CoT and direct labeling questions for the same image.

**Implementation Details (Section 8):**
- **Base Model:** LLaVA 1.5 13B.
- **Fine-tuning:** LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is applied to the language model. The vision backbone is frozen, while the `embed_tokens` and `lm_head` layers (modified to accommodate new tokens) are fully trained.
- **Compute:** Trained on single-node machines with 8 A40 GPUs, with runs completing in less than 10 hours.
- **Inference:** Uses a temperature of 0 for deterministic generation and constrained decoding.

This comprehensive approach allows AURORA to effectively teach MLMs to internalize visual perception, transforming them into more capable visual reasoners.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that "Perception Tokens" significantly enhance the visual reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Language Models (MLMs), particularly for tasks requiring detailed perceptual understanding like relative depth estimation and object counting.

#### 4.1. Relative Depth Estimation (3D Reasoning Task)

- **Benchmarks:** Evaluated on BLINK and newly curated, more challenging HardBLINK datasets (with 3, 4, and 5 marked points and mitigated height-based bias).
- **Performance (Table 1):** LLaVA-AURORA consistently and significantly outperforms all baselines:
 - **Compared to Fine-tuned LLaVA:** Achieves a 6.4 percentage point performance boost on BLINK relative depth estimation.
 - **Compared to Base LLaVA models (LLaVA OneVision, LLaVA 1.5 13B):** Shows a substantial improvement, indicating that the perception tokens provide a meaningful enhancement beyond standard finetuning.
 - **Compared to Closed-Source Models (GPT-4o, GPT-4 Turbo):** LLaVA-AURORA surpasses these models, including GPT-4 Turbo + Tool, which was explicitly provided with ground truth depth maps. This is a critical finding, suggesting that the intrinsic reasoning with perception tokens can be more effective than external tool usage.
 - **Increasing Complexity:** The performance advantage of LLaVA-AURORA becomes even more pronounced as the task complexity increases (e.g., with more marked points in HardBLINK), demonstrating its robust understanding of depth cues.

#### 4.2. Object Counting (2D Reasoning Task)

- **Benchmarks:** Evaluated on CV-Bench, SEED-Bench, and BLINK's counting subtask.
- **Performance (Table 2):** LLaVA-AURORA shows notable improvements:
 - **Compared to Fine-tuned LLaVA and Base LLaVA models:** Achieves substantial gains: +10.8% on BLINK, +11.3% on CVBench, and +8.3% on SEED-Bench.
 - **Compared to GPT-4 Turbo + Tool:** LLaVA-AURORA also outperforms this tool-augmented baseline, highlighting the advantage of intrinsic reasoning over external tool invocation.
 - While LLaVA-AURORA does not yet surpass top-tier closed-source models like GPT-4o or GPT-4 Turbo (which might have access to broader training data or more sophisticated internal mechanisms), the results clearly demonstrate a meaningful improvement in 2D reasoning over comparable open-source and tool-augmented baselines.

#### 4.3. Perception Token Decoding and Fidelity

- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 4) & Quantitative (Table 3):** LLaVA-AURORA is capable of decoding its learned depth tokens back into coherent depth maps that qualitatively resemble the ground truth. Quantitatively, it outperforms Unified-IO in relative depth estimation when using its *own* generated depth maps, indicating that the learned tokens effectively capture spatial relationships. This demonstrates the interpretability of the intermediate visual "thoughts" of the model.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Supplementary Material)

- **Chain-of-Thought Steps (Table 4):** Explicitly guiding the model through both "point location identification" and "depth map generation/reasoning" steps in the CoT prompts leads to the most significant performance improvements in relative depth estimation. This validates the multi-step visual reasoning paradigm.
- **Text Tokens vs. Perception Tokens (Table 5):** Using specialized `PIXEL X` perception tokens for bounding boxes consistently yields higher accuracy in object counting compared to using standard numerical text tokens. This confirms the benefit of a dedicated, structured tokenization scheme for visual features.
- **Perception Token Reconstruction Loss (Table 6, Figure 5):** The inclusion of a reconstruction loss during training, while enforcing consistency, was found to provide only marginal improvements in quantitative performance (MSE) and qualitative fidelity. This suggests that for this specific setup, relying primarily on the distillation objective might be a more computationally efficient choice.

Overall, the findings strongly support the central hypothesis: augmenting MLMs with intrinsically generated and reasoned-over perception tokens leads to substantial improvements in challenging visual reasoning tasks, outperforming finetuning baselines and, in some cases, even highly capable closed-source models or tool-augmented systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Perception Tokens and the AURORA training framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Language Models (MLMs), with far-reaching implications for the development of more capable and robust AI systems.

**Key Significance:**

1. **Bridging the Perception-Reasoning Gap:** The most critical contribution is the ability to bridge the gap between high-level linguistic understanding and fundamental, low-level visual perception. Current MLMs often operate on abstract visual features, struggling with tasks requiring precise spatial or instance-level reasoning. Perception Tokens provide a mechanism for MLMs to internalize and directly reason over concrete visual representations like depth maps and bounding boxes, akin to how humans perceive and process the world.
2. **Enabling Intrinsic Visual Chain-of-Thought:** By allowing MLMs to generate and utilize perception tokens as intermediate reasoning steps, the work introduces a novel concept of "visual chain-of-thought." This moves beyond simply generating visual outputs to actively using these outputs within the model's internal inference process, fostering a deeper form of multimodal reasoning. This is a powerful paradigm shift, as it endows MLMs with a more transparent and interpretable reasoning pathway.
3. **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** The significant performance gains demonstrated across challenging 2D (counting) and 3D (relative depth) reasoning benchmarks, particularly on more complex variants like HardBLINK, underscore the effectiveness of the approach. Crucially, the method outperforms finetuning baselines and, in several instances, even highly sophisticated closed-source models and tool-augmented systems that rely on external vision modules. This suggests improved generalization capabilities, as the model learns to intrinsically understand visual cues rather than just memorize patterns.
4. **Lightweight and Scalable Integration:** Unlike tool-using approaches that demand loading and managing multiple large, specialized external models, AURORA integrates perception capabilities intrinsically. This makes the system more computationally efficient, memory-friendly, and scalable, as the new tokens are seamlessly incorporated into the existing MLM architecture.
5. **Improved Interpretability:** The ability to decode the generated perception tokens back into their original visual forms (e.g., depth maps) offers a degree of interpretability. Researchers and users can visually inspect the intermediate "thoughts" of the model, understanding *how* it arrived at a particular visual reasoning conclusion.
6. **Future-Proof Adaptability:** The framework is designed to be highly flexible and extensible. It can readily incorporate new types of perception tokens (e.g., for human poses, instance segmentation masks) as they emerge, allowing MLMs to continually expand their visual understanding capabilities without requiring fundamental architectural overhauls.

**Potential Impact:**

- **More Robust Multimodal AI:** This research paves the way for the development of MLMs that are not only fluent in language but also deeply grounded in visual reality. Such models could lead to more robust AI systems for real-world applications requiring nuanced visual understanding.
- **Applications in Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Improved 3D reasoning capabilities, particularly relative depth estimation, are crucial for robots navigating complex environments, performing manipulation tasks, or autonomous vehicles perceiving their surroundings.
- **Advanced Visual Content Understanding:** Applications like enhanced visual search, more accurate medical image analysis, and sophisticated content generation tools could benefit from MLMs that can intrinsically understand and reason about fine-grained visual details.
- **Next-Generation AI Assistants:** Future AI assistants could offer more insightful and accurate responses to complex visual queries, performing tasks that require both linguistic comprehension and detailed visual analysis (e.g., "Tell me how many red objects are behind the blue car in this image").

In conclusion, "Perception Tokens" represent a pivotal step towards building truly intelligent multimodal AI systems that can "see" and "reason" about the visual world with an unprecedented level of intrinsic understanding, moving beyond superficial pattern matching to a deeper, more human-like form of visual intelligence.
