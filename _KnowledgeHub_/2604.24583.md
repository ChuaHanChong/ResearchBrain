# Improving Vision-language Models with Perception-centric Process Reward Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A perception-centric process reward model, Perceval, was developed to provide fine-grained, token-level feedback on image-text misalignments, improving Vision-Language Models' visual reasoning during reinforcement learning. This led to an average performance increase of up to 4% in visual search tasks and reduced hallucinations across diverse multimodal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit brittle multi-step reasoning and perceptual errors like hallucinations, hindering performance on complex visual tasks.
- Traditional Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) uses sparse, sequence-level rewards, leading to a "hard credit-assignment problem" that prevents effective correction of specific errors within a reasoning chain.
- Developing high-quality process reward models (PRMs) for multimodal tasks is challenging due to the high cost of step-level annotations and the conditional verifiability of steps.

## Method

- Developed **Perceval**, a ==perception-centric process reward model (PRM)==, which identifies and explains image-text misalignments (hallucinations) at a ==fine-grained, token-level==.
- Integrated Perceval into ==RLVR training== by reallocating coarse sequence-level advantages into ==token-level advantages==, explicitly penalizing hallucinated spans within generated responses.
- Introduced test-time error correction strategies, such as ==Truncate-then-Regenerate==, allowing VLMs to iteratively refine responses by truncating and regenerating problematic segments flagged by Perceval.

## Results

- Achieved average improvements of **~4%** in Visual Search, **~3%** in Math and Chart reasoning, and **~1%** in Perception-intensive Reasoning for the 3B model.
- A **7B model** trained with Perceval achieved performance competitive with tool-augmented SOTA (DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner) on Visual Search tasks without external tools.
- Test-time error correction strategies based on Perceval, particularly **Truncate-then-Regenerate**, consistently outperformed majority-voting approaches on V* and BLINK benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Token-level, perception-centric process reward models effectively overcome the sparse reward problem in RLVR, significantly enhancing visual grounding and reducing hallucinations in VLMs.
> - Strengthening intrinsic perceptual capabilities through fine-grained reinforcement learning generalizes, leading to performance gains across various multimodal reasoning tasks, including mathematical and chart analysis.
> - Enhancing a VLM's inherent perceptual accuracy can achieve performance competitive with or superior to models that rely on external tool manipulation for object grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated capabilities across a range of multimodal tasks, including multimodal mathematics, chart analysis, and general visual question answering. However, their performance on complex visual reasoning tasks remains a challenge. VLMs can exhibit brittle multi-step chains of thought, leading to perceptual or logical errors such as hallucinated objects or incorrect spatial relations, which may deviate from the image context.

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a post-training strategy to enhance the reasoning abilities of VLMs. Algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) assign outcome-level rewards to reasoning traces, aiming to optimize the model's policy towards more consistent reasoning. Despite these advancements, a limitation of conventional RLVR methods is their reliance on sparse, sequence-level rewards. This coarse feedback makes it difficult to pinpoint and correct specific errors within a reasoning chain, contributing to a "hard credit-assignment problem." The entire response receives a single reward, meaning correct and incorrect intermediate steps are not differentiated, which can hinder the model's ability to learn robust visual grounding.

Prior work has explored process reward models (PRMs) in language models to supervise intermediate steps, but developing high-quality PRMs for multimodal tasks is complex due to the expense of step-level annotations and the conditional verifiability of some steps. The current work addresses this by proposing a perception-centric PRM, named Perceval, specifically designed to detect and explain image-text misalignments (hallucinations) at a fine-grained, token-level. This approach seeks to alleviate the sparse-reward problem in RLVR for VLMs by providing more specific feedback on perceptual claims within the reasoning process, thereby aiming to improve the learning of visual grounding and reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the complex visual reasoning ability of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by addressing the limitations of existing reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) approaches.

The core motivation stems from the observation that while RLVR has enhanced VLM reasoning, its prevalent outcome-level supervision is too coarse to effectively diagnose and rectify errors occurring within the model's reasoning chain. Specifically:
- **Sparse Reward Problem:** Traditional RLVR assigns a single scalar reward to an entire generated response. This "sparse reward" signal makes it difficult to attribute credit or blame to individual tokens or intermediate reasoning steps. Consequently, if a VLM generates a multi-step rationale that culminates in an incorrect final answer, the sequence-level reward offers little guidance on which specific perceptual or logical components contributed to the failure.
- **Difficulty in Error Grounding:** VLMs frequently generate "hallucinations," such as mentioning objects or spatial relationships that are not present or are incorrect in the image. With only an overall reward, the model cannot effectively determine whether a failure arose from faulty visual grounding, incorrect attribute recognition, or subsequent logical missteps. This hinders the model's capacity for precise self-correction.
- **Need for Fine-grained Feedback:** To overcome the hard credit-assignment problem, there is a recognized need for process-level supervision that can evaluate intermediate steps rather than just the final outcome. Such fine-grained feedback could identify erroneous perceptual claims directly, allowing for targeted corrective signals during training.

To this end, the research aims to:
1. **Develop a perception-centric Process Reward Model (PRM):** Create an external critic, Perceval, capable of extracting image-related claims from a VLM's response and comparing them against visual evidence in the input image. This PRM should identify claims containing perceptual errors and provide structured feedback.
2. **Integrate PRM into RL training:** Implement a mechanism to incorporate Perceval's fine-grained feedback into the RL optimization process. This involves transforming the coarse sequence-level advantages into token-level advantages, specifically penalizing hallucinated spans identified by Perceval.
3. **Enable Test-Time Error Correction:** Design strategies that leverage Perceval during inference to detect and correct perceptual errors, allowing the VLM to refine its responses iteratively.

By addressing these objectives, the researchers seek to provide a more precise and interpretable supervision signal, thereby enhancing VLMs' intrinsic perceptual grounding and reasoning robustness across various multimodal tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development and integration of Perceval, a perception-centric process reward model (PRM), into both the training and inference stages of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

#### 3.1. Perception-Centric Process Reward Model (Perceval)

Perceval is designed as an external critic to provide fine-grained, process-level supervision by assessing factual consistency between a VLM's response and the input image.

- **Error-finding Schema Design:** Given an image, text query, and VLM response `⟨v, q, o⟩`, Perceval generates a structured verification `V`. It employs a "think-then-answer" paradigm:
 - **Thinking Process:** Perceval first analyzes each claim in the VLM's response, evaluating its consistency with the visual evidence in the image `v` (conditioned on `q`). This thought process is encapsulated within `<think>...</think>` tags.
 - **Decision Output:** Based on the analysis, Perceval provides a final decision within `<answer>...</answer>` tags. If no perceptual errors are found, it outputs "The response is correct." Otherwise, it returns a Python list containing the exact erroneous strings from the VLM's response.

- **Process Reward Model Training:** Perceval is trained using a four-stage pipeline:
 1. **Query Selection:** Images and queries are primarily sourced from perception-intensive visual search datasets (e.g., V* and DeepEyes) requiring precise object localization, supplemented with a small proportion from other domains like mathematical reasoning to ensure broad applicability.
 2. **Rollout Generation:** An open-source VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is used to generate responses for these images and queries. The imperfections and perceptual misalignments in these responses serve as realistic negative examples (hallucinations) for training Perceval.
 3. **Automated Annotation and Verification:** A strong language model (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro) is employed to perform step-by-step, hallucination-focused checks on the generated responses. These annotations adhere to the predefined structured format of Perceval.
 4. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The Perceval backbone (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) is fine-tuned using a standard SFT objective on this aggregated, annotated corpus. The goal is to teach Perceval to emulate detailed, perception-centric verification and produce the structured output.

#### 3.2. RLVR with Process-level Supervision

Perceval is integrated into the reinforcement learning (RL) training process by modifying the GRPO objective to support process-level supervision.

- **Token-level Advantage Reallocation:** Instead of using a coarse sequence-level advantage (`A_i`), the method introduces a fine-grained, token-level advantage (`A'_i,t`).
 1. For each generated response `o_i`, Perceval identifies token spans corresponding to perception-induced hallucinations by parsing its structured verification output.
 2. A binary mask `M_i` is constructed for the response `o_i`, where `m_i,t = 1` if token `t` is part of an identified hallucinated span, and `m_i,t = 0` otherwise.
 3. This mask modulates the original sequence-level advantage `A_i` to form the token-level advantage `A'_i,t`: `A'_i,t = A_i - α * m_i,t * |A_i|`.
 - Here, `α` (alpha) is a hyperparameter controlling the penalty strength.
 - If `m_i,t = 0` (correct token), `A'_i,t = A_i`.
 - If `m_i,t = 1` (hallucination token), the advantage is reduced: `A'_i,t = A_i * (1 - α)` if `A_i > 0`, or `A'_i,t = A_i * (1 + α)` if `A_i < 0` (making the negative penalty stronger).
- **Modified GRPO Objective:** The `A'_i,t` is then substituted into the GRPO objective (Eq. 2), thereby injecting direct, token-level corrective pressure. This method aims to preserve sequence-level preferences while explicitly penalizing ungrounded content.
- **Conditional Strategy:** During RL training, Perceval-based fine-grained advantage rescaling is applied only to perception-related data. For other domains (e.g., mathematical reasoning), the standard GRPO without Perceval intervention is used. This allows investigation into the generalization of perceptual improvements.

#### 3.3. Test-Time Scaling with PRM Guidance

Perceval also facilitates test-time scaling through targeted error-correction loops:

- **Truncate–then–Regenerate:** When Perceval identifies an erroneous claim, the model's hypothesis is truncated before the first token of that problematic span. The VLM then regenerates the response from this verified prefix. This cycle repeats for a maximum of `k` iterations or until no new errors are flagged.
- **Truncate–Thinking–then–Regenerate:** This strategy augments the above by inserting a brief self-reflection prompt (e.g., "Wait, I need to reconsider this reasoning...") after truncation. This encourages the model to reflect on the failure mode before regenerating, aiming to repair the specific misalignment. This also iterates up to `k` times.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using Qwen2.5-VL as the backbone for both reward and policy models, at 3B and 7B scales. The training data for Perceval primarily came from visual search datasets, with RL training also focused on enhancing perception capabilities, while including general reasoning data.

#### 4.1. RL Training with PRM

The method achieved improvements across multiple multimodal benchmarks, including visual search (V*), perception-intensive reasoning (BLINK, MMStar, MME-RealWorld, RealWorldQA), and math/chart reasoning (MathVision, MathVista, ChartQA).

- **Performance Gains:** The proposed method consistently outperformed the GRPO baseline. For the 3B model, average improvements were approximately 4% in Visual Search, 3% in Math and Chart reasoning, and 1% in Perception-intensive Reasoning. A notable gain was observed in the V*pos (Positional Perception) sub-task, improving from 86.95% to 90.43% for the 3B model, indicating enhanced spatial localization. Improvements on BLINK and MMStar suggested increased perceptual fidelity and reduced hallucinations.
- **Generalization Effect:** A significant observation was the generalization ability of the model. Despite the PRM training and RL intervention primarily targeting Visual Search tasks, the model demonstrated consistent performance gains across other domains, including general perception and math reasoning tasks (e.g., MathVision and ChartQA). This "capability transfer" is attributed to strengthening foundational perceptual accuracy, which benefits tasks reliant on fine-grained visual abilities like localizing data points.
- **Comparison with Tool-Augmented Models:** The 7B model trained with the proposed method surpassed Pixel-Reasoner and achieved performance competitive with DeepEyes on Visual Search tasks. This is noteworthy as DeepEyes and Pixel-Reasoner rely on external tool manipulation for object grounding. This finding suggests that enhancing intrinsic perceptual abilities in base multimodal models can rival the performance of tool-augmented methods.

#### 4.2. Test-time Scaling with PRM

The effectiveness of Perceval-based test-time scaling strategies (Truncate–then–Regenerate and Truncate–Thinking–then–Regenerate) was compared against a major voting strategy.

- **Superiority over Major Voting:** PRM-based strategies consistently outperformed major voting on V* and BLINK across different sampling times (k=4, 8, 16).
- **Truncate Strategy Performance:** The Truncate–then–Regenerate strategy showed more significant improvement compared to Truncate–Thinking–then–Regenerate. This was hypothesized to be due to insufficient reflective data in the model's training, leading to poorer instruction-following when reflective prompts were inserted. The Truncate strategy, by allowing regeneration based on the model's own context, aligns more closely with its original distribution, producing more stable outputs.
- **Limitations of Major Voting:** Major voting was observed to converge quickly on difficult tasks, failing to show further improvement without external intervention. This suggested that without specific corrective guidance, the model's inherent capabilities were insufficient to rectify errors.

#### 4.3. Further Analysis

- **Reward Hacking Test:** The hallucination rate, as identified by Perceval, initially declined during training and then stabilized. This stabilization was interpreted as evidence that Perceval effectively guided the policy towards genuine improvement without significant "reward hacking," where the policy overfits the reward model's scoring function without actual quality improvement. The fine-grained, token-level penalization mechanism made it harder for the policy to exploit.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** Experiments on the penalty strength hyperparameter `α` (alpha) in Equation 3 showed a non-monotonic trend. A low `α` (0.03) provided insufficient correction, while an excessively high `α` (0.3) was counterproductive due to "collateral penalization" of syntactically necessary tokens within an erroneous span. An `α` of 0.1 was found to strike an optimal balance, achieving substantial hallucination reduction without destabilizing effects.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** A case study illustrated that the GRPO baseline model hallucinated a spatial relationship without visual grounding. In contrast, the model trained with the proposed method exhibited a deliberate, step-by-step reasoning process, correctly localizing objects and deducing their relative positions. This demonstrated that the RL training process with Perceval enhanced the model's perceptual capabilities and enforced grounding in visual content.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Perceval, a perception-centric process reward model (PRM), represents an advancement in the training of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by addressing the fundamental limitation of sparse, outcome-level rewards in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Fine-grained Error Grounding:** Perceval provides a mechanism for token-level error identification, specifically targeting image-text misalignments (hallucinations) within a VLM's reasoning process. This offers more precise feedback than traditional sequence-level rewards, which struggle with the credit assignment problem in multi-step reasoning.
- **Enhanced Perceptual Robustness:** By penalizing hallucinatory tokens directly, the method fosters better visual grounding and reduces the incidence of perceptually inaccurate statements. The experimental results indicate significant improvements in perception-heavy tasks and a reduction in hallucinations.
- **Improved Generalization:** A notable finding is the generalization of perceptual improvements to broader and more complex reasoning tasks, including mathematical and chart-based reasoning. This suggests that strengthening foundational perceptual abilities can yield widespread benefits across diverse multimodal domains.
- **Competitive Performance with Tool-Augmented Models:** The proposed method, by enhancing intrinsic VLM capabilities, achieved performance competitive with state-of-the-art models that rely on external tool manipulation for object grounding. This highlights the potential of internal model improvement as a viable alternative or complement to tool use.
- **Mitigation of Reward Hacking:** The design of Perceval, with its indirect and fine-grained penalty mechanism, effectively mitigated reward hacking, ensuring that the model learned genuine improvements rather than merely overfitting to a scalar reward function.
- **Practical Inference-Time Refinement:** The development of Truncate–then–Regenerate and Truncate–Thinking–then–Regenerate strategies provides practical means for real-time error correction during inference, offering consistent performance gains over conventional sampling methods like major voting.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **More Reliable VLMs:** The research contributes to the development of VLMs that are more factually grounded and less prone to hallucinations, thereby increasing their reliability in real-world applications such as automated driving, medical image analysis, and scientific research.
- **New Directions for RLVR:** The work proposes a novel approach to integrate process-level supervision into RLVR, which could inspire future research into more sophisticated fine-grained reward mechanisms for complex AI tasks involving multi-step reasoning.
- **Foundation for Advanced Reasoning:** By improving fundamental perceptual capabilities, this method lays a stronger foundation for VLMs to tackle even more complex and abstract visual reasoning challenges, where accurate grounding is a prerequisite.
- **Reduced Annotation Burden (Potentially):** While Perceval itself requires training data, the automated annotation process using strong LLMs for verification could potentially reduce the extensive manual annotation traditionally required for fine-grained process supervision.

Overall, this work represents a significant step towards creating more robust, trustworthy, and performant Vision-Language Models by providing a targeted solution to the sparse reward and credit assignment problems in multimodal reinforcement learning.
