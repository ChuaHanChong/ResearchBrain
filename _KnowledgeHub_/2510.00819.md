# Stabilizing Policy Gradients for Sample-Efficient Reinforcement Learning in LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Oxford developed Curvature-Aware Policy Optimization (CAPO) to stabilize policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning for large language model reasoning, preventing catastrophic policy collapse. CAPO demonstrated up to a 30-fold improvement in sample efficiency on mathematical reasoning tasks by using a novel, tractable second-order geometric model for data selection.

## Problem

- Policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs) suffer from optimization instability, leading to issues like catastrophic updates and policy collapse.
- Current practical implementations of RL for LLMs rely on conservative hyperparameters (e.g., low learning rates, large batch sizes) to ensure stability, which significantly increases computational costs and reduces sample efficiency.
- The vast scale of billion-parameter LLMs and deep architectures exacerbate these stability problems, and the specific context of LLM reasoning remains relatively underexplored for principled stabilization methods.

## Method

- The work formalizes the ==stochastic optimization== problem of policy gradients by explicitly incorporating second-order geometric information, specifically the Hessian of the objective function and the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) of the policy distribution.
- A tractable computational framework is developed to approximate and continuously monitor these curvature terms by restricting the model to the last layer of the LLM and exploiting gradient sparsity, enabling efficient directional curvature computation.
- Curvature-Aware Policy Optimization (CAPO) is designed as an algorithm that leverages the computed curvature estimates to perform data selection, masking out samples from subsets that violate predefined trust-region constraints on objective and policy shifts.

## Results

- CAPO consistently maintained stable performance, preventing policy collapse under aggressive learning regimes, while all baseline methods (GRPO (A), Dr.GRPO, and REINFORCE) catastrophically failed.
- The method achieved up to a 30-fold improvement in sample efficiency on the MATH dataset and a 9-fold improvement on the TEST benchmark set compared to the standard, conservative GRPO.
- The computational model accurately correlated high directional curvatures (mFm_FmF and mHm_HmH) with optimization instability, and CAPO's interventions incurred minimal overhead, peaking at approximately **8%** token rejection during early optimization and quickly dropping below **2%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Second-order geometric information, even when approximated by modeling only the last layer of an LLM, effectively captures critical aspects of the optimization landscape for policy gradients, providing indicators for instability.
> - Monitoring policy shifts (via FIM) and objective shifts (via Hessian) allows for anticipating and preventing abrupt, undesirable updates that lead to catastrophic policy collapse.
> - Integrating curvature awareness into a data selection mechanism provides a sample-efficient and stable learning regime with minimal computational overhead, offering a principled alternative to heuristic stability improvements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The emergence of reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) has marked a significant shift in artificial intelligence research, enabling advancements in areas such as mathematical problem-solving, code generation, and autonomous agentic workflows. This progress is largely attributed to the successful application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques for LLM post-training, particularly policy gradient methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

Despite their success, policy gradient methods in RL inherently face fundamental challenges related to optimization stability. These challenges stem from the non-stationary nature of the RL objective, the high variance of gradient estimates, and pathologies observed in training deep neural networks, leading to issues such as catastrophic updates, policy collapse, plasticity loss, sample inefficiency, and hyperparameter sensitivity. While extensive research has focused on understanding and stabilizing RL optimization dynamics in general, the specific context of LLM reasoning has remained relatively underexplored. In the LLM setting, these problems can be exacerbated due to the vast scale of billion-parameter models, deep architectures, and potentially arbitrary sequence lengths during sampling.

Current practical implementations of RL for LLMs typically employ conservative hyperparameters, such as very low learning rates and large batch sizes, to mitigate these stability issues. While this approach ensures training proceeds without catastrophic failure, it substantially increases the number of LLM generations required, leading to higher computational costs and reduced sample efficiency.

This research addresses this gap by investigating the optimization dynamics of RL in the LLM context. Prior work in general deep RL has explored second-order methods, such as natural gradients with K-FAC, for stabilization. For LLMs, some nascent literature proposes heuristic improvements targeting specific training problems. In contrast, this paper introduces a principled framework that models the optimization landscape using second-order geometry. The work differentiates itself by developing a novel, tractable approximation of curvature information specifically designed for the scale of LLMs and integrates this information into the optimization process through a data selection mechanism, rather than directly computing natural gradients in the full parameter space. This approach aims to provide a more fundamental explanation for observed instabilities and address them uniformly, facilitating more sample-efficient and stable training regimes for LLM reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work arises from the observed optimization instabilities in policy gradient methods when applied to Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning tasks. Despite the critical role of these methods in advancing LLM capabilities, their underlying optimization stability, especially in aggressive learning regimes, remains insufficiently understood and addressed. This deficiency leads to practical implementations relying on conservative hyperparameters, which directly translate to increased computational costs and reduced sample efficiency due to the necessity of more training samples.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this research are detailed as follows:

- **Formalize the Stochastic Optimization Problem with Second-Order Geometry:** The primary objective is to move beyond first-order gradient methods by explicitly formalizing the stochastic optimization problem of policy gradients to include second-order geometric information. This involves considering both the Hessian of the objective function, which describes the local curvature of the performance landscape, and the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) of the policy distribution, which characterizes how policy updates impact the policy's probabilistic output. The motivation here is to provide a more complete picture of the optimization landscape, allowing for a more informed and stable update process.
- **Develop a Tractable Computational Framework for Curvature Tracking:** Given that full second-order matrices (Hessian and FIM) are computationally intractable for billion-parameter LLMs, a key objective is to propose a computationally and numerically tractable framework. This framework aims to approximate and continuously monitor these curvature terms during policy updates. The motivation is to enable scalable analysis and utilization of curvature information without incurring unfeasible memory or computational costs, particularly for billion-parameter models. This involves devising analytical expressions for these quantities within a simplified model.
- **Leverage Curvature Information to Design Optimization Interventions:** A core objective is to practically utilize the tracked curvature information to plan and intervene in the policy gradient steps. The motivation is to anticipate potential abrupt shifts in the objective or policy distribution—which are often precursors to unstable optimization behaviors like catastrophic updates or policy collapse—before these undesirable steps are actually taken. The proposed intervention mechanism focuses on data selection.
- **Design a Curvature-Aware Policy Optimization (CAPO) Algorithm:** Based on the developed framework, the research aims to design a specific algorithm, Curvature-Aware Policy Optimization (CAPO). This algorithm identifies samples (e.g., tokens or trajectories) that contribute excessively to unstable updates, as indicated by the curvature estimates, and masks them out from the policy gradient estimation process. The motivation is to guide the optimization towards more stable regions of the parameter space, akin to trust-region methods.
- **Establish Theoretical Guarantees for Monotonic Improvement:** A theoretical objective is to establish monotonic improvement guarantees for CAPO under realistic assumptions. This is crucial for providing a principled foundation for the algorithm's effectiveness in achieving stable and progressive learning.
- **Empirically Validate Stability and Sample Efficiency:** The final objective is to empirically demonstrate that CAPO ensures stable updates even under aggressive learning regimes, where standard baselines catastrophically fail. Furthermore, the motivation is to show that CAPO significantly improves sample efficiency, reducing the number of required training samples and thus computational costs, with minimal intervention overhead.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to fundamentally improve the reliability and efficiency of RL for LLM reasoning, thereby enabling more scalable post-training advancements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this work is structured around formalizing the RL optimization problem with second-order geometry, developing a scalable computational model for these geometric terms, and integrating this information into a policy optimization algorithm through data-driven interventions.

#### 3.1. Modeling the Optimization Landscape with Second-Order Geometry

The core of the approach begins by formalizing the reinforcement learning optimization problem by explicitly incorporating second-order geometric information, specifically the Hessian of the objective function and the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) of the policy distribution.

- **Higher-Order Objective:** The paper considers the objective function $J(\theta)$ and its Taylor expansion around an update step $\Delta\theta$. This expansion, $J(\theta + \Delta\theta) = J(\theta) + \nabla_{\theta}J(\theta)^{\top}\Delta\theta + \frac{1}{2}\Delta\theta^{\top}H(\theta)\Delta\theta + O(\|\Delta\theta\|^3)$, highlights that standard gradient ascent implicitly ignores the Hessian term $H(\theta)$. In non-convex RL problems, a sufficiently negative Hessian contribution can lead to a decrease in the objective, causing instability. The paper establishes a lower bound for objective improvement under a Lipschitz continuous Hessian.
- **Fisher Information Matrix (FIM):** Beyond objective curvature, changes in the policy distribution are critical for stability. The FIM, $F(\theta) := E_{s \sim d_{\pi}, a \sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s)}[\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a|s)^{\top}]$, is introduced to track the geometry of the policy distribution. It is shown that the directional curvature under Fisher geometry approximates the average KL divergence between policies before and after a small step: $\bar{D}_{KL}(\pi_{\theta} \parallel \pi_{\theta+\Delta\theta}) = \frac{1}{2}\Delta\theta^{\top}F(\theta)\Delta\theta + O(\|\Delta\theta\|^3)$. Enforcing a trust region on this KL divergence is known to lead to monotonic policy improvement.

#### 3.2. Computational Model

A key challenge is the intractability of computing full Hessian and FIM for billion-parameter LLMs. The paper addresses this through a scalable computational model.

- **Last-Layer Model:** The approach restricts curvature modeling to the last layer of the LLM. For a softmax policy $\pi_{\theta}(a|s)$, the pre-softmax logits are expressed as $f_{\theta}(s_t) = Wh_{\bar{\theta}}(s_t)$, where $W \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d_i}$ is the last-layer weight matrix, $K$ is the vocabulary size, and $h_{\bar{\theta}}(s_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_i}$ is the feature vector from the rest of the LLM. The parameters of interest for curvature are $\psi = \text{vec}(W)$.
 - **Last-Layer Model Gradient (˜g(ψ)):** The policy gradient with respect to $\psi$ is derived as $E_{\tau \sim \pi_{\theta}}[\sum_{t=0}^T \gamma^t A(s_t, a_t) (e_a - \pi_{\theta}(s_t)) \otimes h_{\bar{\theta}}(s_t)]$, where $e_a$ is a one-hot action vector.
 - **Last-Layer Model Hessian (˜H(ψ)):** The Hessian of the objective with respect to $\psi$ is derived as $E_{\tau \sim \pi_{\theta}}[\sum_{t=0}^T \gamma^t A(s,a) ((e_a - \pi_{\theta}(s_t))(e_a - \pi_{\theta}(s_t))^{\top} - F(s_t)) \otimes h_{\bar{\theta}}(s_t)h_{\bar{\theta}}(s_t)^{\top}]$.
 - **Last-Layer Model FIM (˜F(ψ)):** The FIM with respect to $\psi$ is derived as $E_{\tau \sim \pi_{\theta}}[((e_{a_t} - \pi_{\theta}(s_t))(e_{a_t} - \pi_{\theta}(s_t))^{\top}) \otimes h_{\bar{\theta}}(s_t)h_{\bar{\theta}}(s_t)^{\top}]$.
- **Computing Directional Curvatures:** The goal is to estimate the quadratic forms $\Delta\psi^{\top}\tilde{C}(\psi)\Delta\psi$ (where $\tilde{C}$ is $\tilde{H}$ or $\tilde{F}$) rather than materializing the full matrices. The paper provides derivations in Appendix D showing that these directional curvatures can be computed efficiently, involving only vector and matrix-vector operations with complexity $O(Kd_i)$ per sample, significantly lower than $O((Kd_i)^2)$.
- **Exploiting Gradient Sparsity:** Further efficiency is gained by leveraging the sparsity of gradients in LLM decoding. Since methods like top-k or nucleus sampling limit active tokens to a small subset (`k`), only `k * d_i` parameters of `W` have non-zero gradients. This reduces memory and computation for directional curvatures to $O(\tilde{k} \cdot d_i)$, where $\tilde{k}$ is the number of generated tokens, typically much smaller than $K$.
- **Modeling the Step $\Delta\theta$:** The planned update step $\Delta\psi$ is modeled either as a simple SGD step or by matching the LLM's actual optimizer (Adam) to improve accuracy.

#### 3.3. Curvature-Aware Policy Optimization (CAPO) Algorithm

CAPO integrates the computational model into the RL training loop as a data selection mechanism.

- **Mechanism:** Given a batch $B$ of trajectories, it is partitioned into disjoint subsets $b_i$. For each subset, a proposed update $\Delta\psi_i$ is computed, and the model-based objective shift $m_H(\Delta\psi_i)$ and policy shift $m_F(\Delta\psi_i)$ are estimated.
- **Trust-Region Constraints:** A subset $b_i$ is accepted if it satisfies predefined trust-region constraints: $\delta_H \le m_H(\Delta\psi_i) \le \delta_{high\_H}$ and $m_F(\Delta\psi_i) \le \delta_F$. Samples from rejected subsets are masked out. The LLM's policy update is then computed using only the accepted samples.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** Theorem 5.1 (and Appendix E and K for finite horizon) provides monotonic policy improvement guarantees for CAPO under assumptions of bounded Hessian operator norm and candidate step norms, and when $\omega \ge C\sqrt{\delta_F}$ (where $\omega$ is the guaranteed minimum improvement and $C$ is a constant related to the advantage function and effective horizon).

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **LLM and Task:** A Qwen2.5-Math-7B LLM is fine-tuned on mathematical reasoning questions from the MATH dataset.
- **Metrics:** Primary evaluation is accuracy on MATH and an average across eight diverse math/STEM benchmarks (referred to as "TEST"). Sample efficiency is measured by performance as a function of training completions.
- **Baselines:** Standard GRPO (conservative hyperparameters), GRPO (A) (aggressive hyperparameters: 5x higher learning rate, 12x smaller batch size), Dr.GRPO, and REINFORCE (all under aggressive regime).
- **CAPO Configuration:** Operates under the aggressive regime, uses token-level selection, and models the optimizer as Adam. Experiments are run across five random seeds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to assess CAPO's ability to prevent instability, improve sample efficiency, and understand the insights provided by the computational model.

- **Prevention of Instability and Sample Efficiency (Figure 2):**
 - The aggressive update regime (higher learning rate, smaller batch size) initially boosts sample efficiency across all methods, including baselines.
 - However, under this aggressive regime, all baseline methods—GRPO (A), Dr.GRPO, and REINFORCE—experienced catastrophic policy collapse, where their performance dropped significantly, often below that of the base model, indicating a complete loss of learning ability.
 - In contrast, CAPO consistently maintained stable performance throughout training, preventing policy collapse and remaining effective long after other methods had failed.
 - **Quantitative Result:** CAPO achieved up to a 30-fold improvement in sample efficiency on the MATH dataset and a 9-fold improvement on the TEST benchmark set compared to the standard, conservative GRPO.

- **Insights from the Computational Model (Figure 3):**
 - **Policy Shifts (m_F):** Unstable methods (GRPO (A), Dr.GRPO, REINFORCE) exhibited consistently high global directional curvatures ($m_F$) during training. Stable methods (CAPO, standard GRPO) maintained significantly smaller and smoother shifts in $m_F$. This indicates a direct correlation between the model's $m_F$ estimates and actual optimization instability.
 - **Objective Shifts (m_H):** Similar trends were observed for $m_H$, where unstable methods showed abrupt, sharp peaks, while stable methods produced smoother, more controlled changes. While high $m_H$ does not directly imply instability like $m_F$, sharp peaks were empirically correlated with training failures.
 - **CAPO's Impact:** CAPO, by applying local bounds on $m_H$ and $m_F$ per token, effectively ensured well-behaved global shifts, aligning with the theoretical rationale presented in Theorem 5.1. These results demonstrate that the proposed computational model, despite its approximations, provides meaningful information about the optimization landscape.

- **Generalizability of Curvature-Aware Selection (Figure 4):**
 - To evaluate broad applicability, the curvature-aware selection mechanism was extended to Dr.GRPO (Dr.CAPO) and REINFORCE (ReinCAPO).
 - In both cases, integrating CAPO's selection strategy significantly improved the stability of the base methods, effectively preventing policy collapse. This finding suggests that the computational model and the intervention mechanism are versatile across different policy optimization objectives.

- **Aggressiveness of CAPO's Intervention (Figure 5):**
 - The token rejection rate for CAPO peaked at approximately 8% during the early stages of optimization, corresponding to periods of more aggressive updates.
 - Subsequently, the rejection rate quickly decreased and remained below 2% for the majority of the training process.
 - This indicates that CAPO guides optimization towards stable curvature regions with minimal intervention, allowing the LLM to leverage most of the available samples.

- **Additional Findings (Appendices H, I, K, L, M):**
 - **Computational Cost:** CAPO introduces minimal overhead, contributing less than 3% to the total step time, primarily from Adam gradient computations (Table 1). Memory usage is also minimal, around 2GB of volatile GPU memory for token-level gradient tensors.
 - **Optimizer Model Ablation (Figure 6):** Matching the optimizer used by the LLM (Adam) for the last-layer model proved more robust for preventing policy collapse in Dr.CAPO and ReinCAPO compared to an SGD-based model.
 - **PPO Clipping and KL Regularization (Figures 7 & 8):** Standard PPO clipping did not prevent collapse in the LLM-RL setup, and more aggressive clipping improved stability at the cost of performance degradation. KL regularization similarly showed a trade-off, with stronger regularization preventing collapse but reducing overall performance. A fundamental limitation of KL regularization observed was its potential to generate unbounded gradients due to the multiplicative log factor, which gradient clipping does not fully resolve.
 - **KL Policy Shift Analysis (Figure 9 & Table 5):** The model's estimated Fisher directional curvature ($m_F$) exhibited a moderately strong, consistent monotonic correlation (Spearman $\rho \approx 0.5-0.6$) with the true estimated policy KL divergence. CAPO effectively maintained consistently small true KL shifts during training, demonstrating its practical implementation of trust-region behavior.
 - **Token Selection and Sample Efficiency (Figure 10):** When accuracy was plotted against the number of *accepted tokens* (instead of completions), the curves closely resembled those based on completions, confirming that masking had a minimal impact on the total generated tokens and did not incur additional inference-time costs.
 - **Comparison with TRPO (Appendix M):** While sharing a common motivation with TRPO (trust-region, natural gradients), CAPO offers a significantly more scalable implementation. TRPO's reliance on Conjugate Gradient for natural gradient approximation and line search procedures is computationally and memory prohibitive for billion-parameter LLMs, whereas CAPO's last-layer model and sparsity optimizations provide a tractable alternative.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions that address critical limitations in the field of Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models (LLMs), holding significant potential for future advancements.

- **Addressing a Core Stability Gap:** The work systematically addresses the under-studied problem of optimization stability in policy gradient methods within LLM reasoning. By formalizing the problem with second-order geometry, it provides a principled framework for understanding and mitigating instabilities that commonly lead to catastrophic failures like policy collapse.
- **Enabling Sample-Efficient Learning Regimes:** The most direct impact of CAPO is its ability to stabilize policy gradients under aggressive learning conditions (e.g., higher learning rates and smaller batch sizes). This capability is crucial because it allows for significantly faster and more efficient training. The demonstrated improvement of up to 30x in sample efficiency over standard GRPO translates directly into substantial reductions in computational costs and training time for LLMs, making advanced RL fine-tuning more accessible.
- **Minimal Intervention and Computational Overhead:** The algorithm achieves these stability and efficiency gains with minimal intervention, rejecting fewer than 8% of tokens, and incurring negligible computational overhead. This "light touch" approach ensures that the benefits of stabilizing optimization do not come at the expense of excessive resource consumption or data wastage, making it practical for large-scale LLM training.
- **Principled Framework vs. Heuristics:** By grounding its approach in second-order stochastic optimization, CAPO offers a more fundamental solution to stability issues compared to many heuristic-based methods commonly employed in LLM-RL. This principled understanding of optimization dynamics can lead to more robust and generalizable algorithms.
- **Broad Applicability:** The demonstrated effectiveness of the curvature-aware selection mechanism when extended to other RL methods (Dr.GRPO and REINFORCE) suggests that the core ideas are broadly applicable across different policy optimization objectives. This versatility could lead to improved stability and efficiency for a wider range of LLM fine-tuning tasks and algorithms.
- **Facilitating Scalable Post-Training:** The ability to achieve stable and sample-efficient learning regimes is paramount for the continued scalability of post-training methods for LLMs. As LLMs grow in size and complexity, efficient fine-tuning becomes increasingly challenging. CAPO's contributions lay a foundation for more robust and scalable development of LLMs with enhanced reasoning capabilities.
- **Opening New Research Avenues:** The work naturally suggests several directions for future research. These include exploring alternative parametrizations of the computational model to capture deeper layer curvature, investigating different intervention mechanisms beyond hard masking (e.g., soft masking or adaptive regularization), and extending the empirical validation to a broader range of LLM architectures and problem settings.

In conclusion, this research presents a method for stabilizing policy gradients in LLM reasoning that significantly improves sample efficiency while maintaining computational tractability. Its principled approach and demonstrated empirical benefits position it as a valuable contribution to the ongoing effort to develop more robust, efficient, and scalable Reinforcement Learning techniques for advanced AI systems.
