# Stabilizing Policy Gradients for Sample-Efficient Reinforcement Learning in LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Oxford developed Curvature-Aware Policy Optimization (CAPO) to stabilize policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning for large language model reasoning, preventing catastrophic policy collapse. CAPO demonstrated up to a 30-fold improvement in sample efficiency on mathematical reasoning tasks by using a novel, tractable second-order geometric model for data selection.

## Problem

- Policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs) suffer from optimization instability, leading to issues like catastrophic updates and policy collapse.
- Current practical implementations of RL for LLMs rely on conservative hyperparameters (e.g., low learning rates, large batch sizes) to ensure stability, which significantly increases computational costs and reduces sample efficiency.
- The vast scale of billion-parameter LLMs and deep architectures exacerbate these stability problems, and the specific context of LLM reasoning remains relatively underexplored for principled stabilization methods.

## Method

- The work formalizes the ==stochastic optimization== problem of policy gradients by explicitly incorporating second-order geometric information, specifically the Hessian of the objective function and the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) of the policy distribution.
- A tractable computational framework is developed to approximate and continuously monitor these curvature terms by restricting the model to the last layer of the LLM and exploiting gradient sparsity, enabling efficient directional curvature computation.
- Curvature-Aware Policy Optimization (CAPO) is designed as an algorithm that leverages the computed curvature estimates to perform data selection, masking out samples from subsets that violate predefined trust-region constraints on objective and policy shifts.

## Results

- CAPO consistently maintained stable performance, preventing policy collapse under aggressive learning regimes, while all baseline methods (GRPO (A), Dr.GRPO, and REINFORCE) catastrophically failed.
- The method achieved up to a 30-fold improvement in sample efficiency on the MATH dataset and a 9-fold improvement on the TEST benchmark set compared to the standard, conservative GRPO.
- The computational model accurately correlated high directional curvatures (mFm_FmF and mHm_HmH) with optimization instability, and CAPO's interventions incurred minimal overhead, peaking at approximately **8%** token rejection during early optimization and quickly dropping below **2%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Second-order geometric information, even when approximated by modeling only the last layer of an LLM, effectively captures critical aspects of the optimization landscape for policy gradients, providing indicators for instability.
> - Monitoring policy shifts (via FIM) and objective shifts (via Hessian) allows for anticipating and preventing abrupt, undesirable updates that lead to catastrophic policy collapse.
> - Integrating curvature awareness into a data selection mechanism provides a sample-efficient and stable learning regime with minimal computational overhead, offering a principled alternative to heuristic stability improvements.
