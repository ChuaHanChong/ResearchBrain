# ERGO: Efficient High-Resolution Visual Understanding for Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nota Inc. developed ERGO, a framework enhancing high-resolution visual understanding in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by employing a reasoning-driven perception reward within a two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline. This approach enabled ERGO to achieve superior accuracy on high-resolution benchmarks with fewer vision tokens and up to 3x faster inference times compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) incur substantial computational overhead when processing high-resolution images, hindering their real-world deployment.
- Existing 'coarse-to-fine' pipelines in LVLMs often fail to accurately identify task-relevant regions from downsampled images if critical visual cues are indistinct, leading to inaccurate high-resolution cropping.
- Direct downsampling of images to reduce computational cost inevitably results in the loss of fine-grained visual details essential for accurate reasoning in complex tasks.

## Method

- **ERGO** implements a two-stage ==coarse-to-fine== pipeline: first, a policy model identifies task-relevant bounding box coordinates from a downsampled image; then, it processes the high-resolution crop to generate a final answer.
- It employs a novel reward design within a Grouped Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO) reinforcement learning framework to foster 'reasoning-driven perception.'
- The reward system includes a region-verification reward ensuring self-contained informative crops, a box adjustment reward penalizing oversized crops, and a Task-driven Contextual Exploration (TCE) reward combining these to guide efficient and accurate region identification.

## Results

- ERGO achieved higher accuracy on high-resolution benchmarks like V* (**81.7%**) and HR-Bench while utilizing significantly fewer vision tokens, demonstrating Pareto-optimality compared to competitive baselines.
- The model delivered practical inference speedups, achieving up to **3x** faster inference (e.g., 1.61 seconds per image-query on V* under a 640x28x28 constraint) compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**.
- ERGO exhibited robust 'Target Coverage Score' under target-object masking, indicating strong contextual reasoning capabilities, and maintained or improved performance across various conventional multimodal benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal contextual information is crucial for accurately identifying task-relevant regions in downsampled images, particularly when direct visual cues are ambiguous or absent.
> - A carefully designed reinforcement learning reward function, specifically the Task-driven Contextual Exploration (TCE) reward, effectively guides LVLMs to perform efficient and accurate 'reasoning-driven perception' by focusing on informative regions.
> - Prioritizing the quality and efficiency of the selected high-resolution region through dedicated rewards significantly improves overall performance in a coarse-to-fine pipeline, complementing end-to-end task accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) has significantly advanced multimodal understanding, enabling applications that require processing both visual and linguistic information. A critical challenge within this domain is the efficient processing of high-resolution images, which is essential for achieving robust performance in real-world scenarios. Current LVLMs often face substantial computational overhead when dealing with high-resolution inputs due to the large number of vision tokens generated.

Recent advancements have explored the concept of "thinking with images," where LVLMs engage in reasoning not only through textual input but also within the visual modality itself. This capability, often facilitated by reinforcement learning (RL)-based post-training, allows models to attend to local, high-fidelity objects and capture fine-grained details by reasoning over cropped image features and bounding-box coordinates. While this approach has demonstrated improvements in high-resolution benchmarks, the underlying issue of computational cost associated with extensive vision token processing remains.

A common strategy to address this efficiency bottleneck is the two-stage "coarse-to-fine" pipeline. This approach initially analyzes a downsampled, coarse-grained image to identify task-relevant regions. Subsequently, only these identified regions are cropped at full resolution and processed in a second, finer-grained reasoning stage. This method aims to reduce overall computational expense while preserving necessary fine-grained visual details. However, a significant limitation of existing methods within this coarse-to-fine framework lies in their ability to accurately infer task-relevant regions from the initial downsampled image. Many contemporary models operate under a "perception-driven reasoning" paradigm, where effective reasoning relies on clearly discernible visual information. When input images are downsampled, critical visual cues can become indistinct, leading to failures in the initial stage of identifying the correct regions for high-resolution cropping. This results in an inability to fully leverage the benefits of the coarse-to-fine pipeline, as the model may focus on irrelevant areas or fail to locate the actual target.

ERGO positions itself within this landscape by addressing the shortcomings of perception-driven reasoning in coarse-to-fine pipelines. It proposes "reasoning-driven perception," where multimodal context is leveraged to determine focal regions, even when explicit visual cues are ambiguous or absent at low resolutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop an efficient high-resolution visual understanding capability for Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) that can overcome the limitations of existing perception-driven reasoning paradigms. Specifically, the model aims to efficiently process high-resolution images while maintaining or improving accuracy, particularly in scenarios where fine-grained visual details are crucial but computationally expensive to process globally.

The motivation for this research stems from several critical observations and challenges:

- **Computational Overhead:** Current LVLMs incur significant computational costs when processing high-resolution images due to the large number of vision tokens required. This computational burden hinders the deployment of LVLMs in real-world applications where efficiency is paramount.
- **Limitations of Downsampling:** A direct approach to reduce vision token count, such as simple input resolution reduction, inevitably leads to the loss of fine-grained visual details. These details are often critical for accurate reasoning, rendering simple downsampling an insufficient solution for many complex tasks.
- **Failure of Perception-Driven Reasoning in Coarse-to-Fine Pipelines:** Existing "thinking with images" models, while powerful at full resolution, often rely on clearly discernible visual anchors for object localization. When integrated into a coarse-to-fine pipeline and presented with downsampled inputs, these "perception-driven reasoning" methods struggle to identify task-relevant regions if the target objects become indistinct. This leads to erroneous focus or complete failure to locate the required information, negating the efficiency benefits of the two-stage approach. Figure 1(b) illustrates this problem, where a straw indiscernible at low resolution causes a baseline model to fail.
- **Need for Contextual Inference:** The authors observe that even when a target object is visually ambiguous in a downsampled image, contextual information (e.g., "straws near coffee cups on tables") can enable a model to infer its likely location. This highlights a need for "reasoning-driven perception" that leverages multimodal context to determine where to focus, rather than relying solely on clear visual evidence.
- **Demonstrated Value of Task-Relevant Regions:** Experiments (Table 1) showed that appending ground-truth high-resolution sub-images to low-resolution inputs significantly enhances model performance, even for models not explicitly trained for this. This indicates that access to task-relevant regions at high resolution is sufficient for strong performance; the challenge lies in autonomously identifying these regions.
- **Inability of Existing Models to Self-Identify Relevant Regions:** Despite the proven value of relevant sub-images, existing RL-trained reasoning models struggle to autonomously and robustly identify such regions when presented with low-resolution inputs (Table 2). This reinforces the need for a novel approach to guide the model's attention.

In summary, the research is motivated by the inefficiency of current LVLMs for high-resolution tasks and the specific failure mode of existing coarse-to-fine methods in identifying relevant regions from low-resolution inputs. The objective is to develop a method that performs "reasoning-driven perception" to efficiently locate and process fine-grained details, thereby achieving high accuracy with reduced computational cost.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ERGO employs a two-stage "coarse-to-fine" reasoning pipeline within a reinforcement learning (RL) framework, specifically Grouped Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO), to achieve efficient high-resolution visual understanding. The core of its approach lies in a novel reward design that encourages reasoning-driven perception.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline

The model's forward process consists of two primary stages:
1. **Coarse Stage (Region Identification):** Given an original image ($I_{orig}$) and a text query ($q$), a policy model ($\pi_\theta$) first analyzes a downsampled version of $I_{orig}$ along with $q$. It then generates an output ($o_{region}$), which includes candidate bounding-box coordinates indicating the region relevant to the query, along with a "thinking trace" (a textual explanation of its reasoning).
2. **Fine Stage (Answer Generation):** The image corresponding to the predicted bounding box is cropped from the original high-resolution image ($I_{orig}$) to create $I_{region}$. The policy model then generates a final answer ($o_{acc}$) based on a multi-turn conditioned setting, which includes the cropped region ($I_{region}$), the text query ($q$), the original image ($I_{orig}$), and the predicted bounding box ($o_{region}$).

#### 3.2. Reward Design

The effectiveness of ERGO is primarily attributed to its meticulously designed reward components, which guide the RL training.

##### 3.2.1. Proposed Reward Components

- **Region-verification reward ($r_{region}$):** This reward addresses a limitation in prior "thinking with images" studies where the reward model might receive the original image ($I_{orig}$) in addition to the cropped region. This could inadvertently allow the reward model to rely on hints from $I_{orig}$ rather than assessing the quality of the cropped region ($I_{region}$) alone. To ensure that the policy model learns to make self-contained crops, $r_{region}$ is computed by evaluating task performance *solely* using the cropped region ($I_{region}$) and the query ($q$). If a frozen, powerful reward model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) correctly answers the question with these inputs, the policy receives a reward. This encourages the policy to identify informative, task-relevant regions that are sufficient for accurate reasoning without external cues.
- **Box adjustment reward ($r_{box}$):** To prevent the policy model from adopting a trivial strategy of consistently selecting the entire image (which would maximize $r_{region}$ but be computationally inefficient), $r_{box}$ penalizes excessively large crops. It is computed using a step function: a reward is given only if the area of the selected region ($Area(I_{region})$) is less than or equal to a fraction ($\gamma$) of the original image's area ($Area(I_{orig})$). Based on an analysis of ground-truth region sizes in various datasets, $\gamma$ is set to 0.6.
- **Task-driven Contextual Exploration (TCE) Reward ($r_{TCE}$):** This is the main proposed reward, combining the $r_{region}$ and $r_{box}$ components as a weighted sum: $r_{TCE} = \alpha \cdot r_{region} + \beta \cdot r_{box}$. The weighting coefficients are set to $\alpha = 1$ and $\beta = 0.5$, balancing the encouragement of informative crops with the penalization of large regions.

##### 3.2.2. Conventional Reward Components

- **Accuracy reward ($r_{acc}$):** This directly promotes correct question-answering. A reward is assigned if the policy model's final answer ($o_{acc}$) matches the ground-truth answer ($o_{GT}$). This component bridges the gap between region quality and final task accuracy.
- **Format reward ($r_{format}$):** This reward ensures that the model's output adheres to a predefined structural format, using specific tags like `<think>`, `<answer>`, and `<zoom>`. This aids in reliable parsing and evaluation during training and inference.

##### 3.2.3. Final Reward Formulation

The total reward ($R$) used for training is a linear combination of all components: $R = r_{TCE} + r_{acc} + r_{format}$.

#### 3.3. Learning Algorithm and Training Details

ERGO utilizes Grouped Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO), an RL framework noted for its sample-efficient optimization in grouped feedback settings.
- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct is used as the policy model due to its robust vision-language reasoning and object-level referring detection capabilities.
- **Reward Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, a more powerful model, serves as the frozen reward model to provide precise reward signals.
- **Training Data:** A subset of ArxivQA and the V* training set are used, following the setup of prior work (DeepEyes) to ensure comparability.
- **Computational Resources:** Training was conducted on 4 H100 GPUs with a global batch size of 128 and 8 rollouts per example. The learning rate was $1 \times 10^{-6}$.

#### 3.4. Baselines and Benchmarks

For evaluation, ERGO is compared against two categories of RL-based post-training methods:
- **Efficiency-oriented models:** MGPO and VisionThink, which also aim for efficient high-resolution understanding.
- **Non-efficiency-oriented models:** PixelReasoner, DeepEyes, TreeVGR, and MiniO3, which possess strong grounding capabilities but are not primarily designed for efficiency. The base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct is also used as a reference.

Performance is assessed across a range of benchmarks:
- **High-resolution VQA:** V*, HR-Bench, MME-RWL, TreeBench, VisualProbe.
- **Conventional multimodal benchmarks:** CV-Bench, MMVP (vision-centric), Hallusion-Bench, POPE, MMBench (general VQA), AI2D, ChartQA (document understanding).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings demonstrating ERGO's effectiveness in efficient high-resolution visual understanding.

#### 4.1. High-Resolution Reasoning with Efficient Visual Processing

- **Superior Accuracy and Efficiency:** ERGO consistently outperforms the original Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct model and all competitive baselines across multiple high-resolution benchmarks (V*, HR-Bench 4K, HR-Bench 8K, MME-RW Lite, TreeBench, VisualProbe). This is observed under various pixel constraints (e.g., 640x28x28 and 1280x28x28 vision tokens), which simulate efficiency-considered scenarios. For instance, at a 1280x28x28 pixel constraint, ERGO achieves an average accuracy of 58.4, surpassing the next best model (MGPO) at 52.6. Even under the stricter 640x28x28 constraint, ERGO maintains a 55.2 average, which is higher than any other model's performance at 1280x28x28.
- **Pareto-Optimality:** Figure 4 illustrates ERGO's position in the Pareto-optimal region on the V* benchmark, demonstrating that it achieves higher accuracy with fewer vision tokens compared to competing methods. At a 1280x28x28 constraint, ERGO utilizes 1,632 vision tokens to achieve 83.8 accuracy on V*, while other models often use a comparable or higher number of tokens for lower accuracy. Notably, at 640x28x28, ERGO uses 1,025 vision tokens to achieve 81.7 accuracy on V*, outperforming all baselines operating with 1280x28x28 constraints (Table 3). This indicates a highly efficient utilization of vision tokens.

#### 4.2. Practical Latency Improvements

- **Significant Speedup:** ERGO demonstrates practical inference speedups. For example, on the V* benchmark, ERGO achieves 81.7 accuracy with an average latency of 1.61 seconds per image-query pair (under 640x28x28 constraint and 1 tool call). This is a substantial improvement compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (4.89s) and even other methods like DeepEyes (2.18s for 1 tool call, 3.42s for 4 tool calls) or MiniO3 (2.03s for 1 tool call, 5.35s for 4 tool calls), while simultaneously achieving higher accuracy (Table 4).
- **Matched Latency Performance:** When models are evaluated under matched latency budgets by adjusting pixel constraints or tool call counts, ERGO consistently achieves the highest accuracy, further underscoring its efficiency-performance advantage (Table 9).

#### 4.3. In-Depth Analysis

- **Contextual Reasoning Robustness:** ERGO's performance is attributed to its ability to identify task-relevant regions even when target objects are visually indiscernible in downsampled inputs. Evaluation under target-object masking (Figure 5) shows ERGO achieves the most robust "Target Coverage Score" in masked conditions, indicating a stronger capacity to leverage contextual information (e.g., "straws near coffee cups") when direct visual cues are absent.
- **Bias-Free Region Prediction:** The box adjustment constant ($\gamma$) does not lead to fixed-size region predictions. Figure 6 demonstrates that ERGO dynamically adapts predicted region sizes based on the characteristics of the data (e.g., smaller regions for MME-RWL, larger for MMVP), indicating that the regularization encourages efficient yet flexible cropping.
- **Conventional Benchmark Performance:** ERGO not only excels in high-resolution specific tasks but also maintains or improves the capabilities of the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model across a broad set of conventional multimodal benchmarks, including general VQA, vision-centric VQA, and document understanding tasks (Table 5). This suggests improved reasoning over semantically relevant regions benefits various tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of TCE Reward:** The ablation studies reveal the critical role of the Task-driven Contextual Exploration (TCE) reward. A model trained primarily with the TCE reward (D) without explicit answer generation still outperforms a model trained solely on accuracy reward (A), even for the final answer accuracy (Table 6a). This highlights that improving the quality of the selected region is fundamental to overall performance in a coarse-to-fine pipeline.
- **Importance of Box Adjustment Reward:** Removing the box adjustment reward leads to the trivial policy of cropping excessively large regions, significantly hindering efficiency (Figure 7) and impairing overall model training performance (B vs. C in Table 6a).
- **Complementary Nature of Accuracy Reward:** While the TCE reward is highly effective, combining it with the accuracy reward (E) yields further benefits, addressing scenarios where the model successfully crops a relevant region but still struggles with the final answer.
- **Robustness to Reward Model:** The performance of ERGO is stable across different sizes (3B, 7B, 72B) and families (Qwen2.5-VL, GLM4.5V, InternVL3) of reward models (Table 6c, 6d). This indicates that the proxy task of assessing cropped image quality is robust and can be reliably performed even by lower-capacity reward models.
- **Data-Driven $\gamma$ Selection:** The data-driven selection of the box adjustment constant $\gamma=0.6$ is shown to be effective (Table 6e). Alternatives like linear annealing for the box adjustment reward (Figure 8) degrade performance because they over-incentivize shrinking regions, leading the policy to prioritize size reduction over semantic meaningfulness (Figure 9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "ERGO: Efficient High-Resolution Visual Understanding for Vision-Language Models" addresses a significant bottleneck in the application of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to real-world scenarios: the computational cost and limitations of processing high-resolution images. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Efficiency-Accuracy Trade-off:** ERGO demonstrates a notable improvement in the efficiency-accuracy trade-off for high-resolution visual understanding. By achieving higher accuracy than existing methods while using significantly fewer vision tokens and delivering practical inference speedups (e.g., 3x faster inference), it pushes the boundaries of what is achievable in resource-constrained environments. This makes sophisticated LVLM capabilities more accessible and deployable.
- **Paradigm Shift to Reasoning-Driven Perception:** The introduction and demonstration of "reasoning-driven perception" mark a conceptual advancement. Unlike "perception-driven reasoning" that relies on clear visual cues, ERGO's ability to leverage multimodal context to infer relevant regions, even when visual information is ambiguous or indiscernible, represents a more robust and intelligent approach to visual grounding. This is particularly impactful for challenging real-world images where objects may be small, occluded, or low-resolution.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalizability:** The model's demonstrated robustness in identifying task-relevant regions under masking conditions, and its ability to maintain or improve performance on conventional multimodal benchmarks while excelling in high-resolution tasks, indicate a more generalized and reliable visual understanding system. This robustness is critical for applications across diverse domains.
- **Improved User Experience and Scalability:** The practical latency improvements translate directly into a better user experience for interactive LVLM applications. Faster response times and reduced computational demands also contribute to the scalability of LVLM deployments, enabling them to handle larger volumes of data and more complex queries without prohibitive infrastructure costs.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The novel reward design, particularly the Task-driven Contextual Exploration (TCE) reward with its region-verification and box adjustment components, provides a robust framework for guiding coarse-to-fine perception. This approach, alongside the empirical insights from the ablation studies (e.g., the importance of data-driven constant selection over linear annealing), offers a valuable foundation for future research in efficient multimodal learning and RL-based visual grounding.
- **Direct Applicability to Real-World Problems:** The focus on efficient high-resolution understanding is directly relevant to many real-world applications such as autonomous driving, medical imaging analysis, detailed product inspection, and complex document understanding, where fine-grained details are paramount and real-time processing is often required.

In conclusion, ERGO addresses a fundamental challenge in LVLMs by enabling efficient and accurate high-resolution visual understanding through a novel reasoning-driven perception paradigm. Its contributions in efficiency, accuracy, and robustness have the potential to significantly impact the development and deployment of next-generation multimodal AI systems.
