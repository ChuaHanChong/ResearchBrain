# Dexterous Contact-Rich Manipulation via the Contact Trust Region

> [!summary] Summary
> A model-based framework for dexterous contact-rich manipulation introduces the Contact Trust Region (CTR) to provide a physically consistent local model of contact dynamics. This approach, integrated with model predictive control, achieves robust object reorientation on hardware and builds global roadmaps in minutes.

## Problem

- Current robots underutilize their full physical embodiment, often restricted to collision-free or end-effector-only interactions, limiting dexterous contact-rich manipulation.
- Existing local linear models of contact dynamics, often combined with symmetric ellipsoidal trust regions, are fundamentally inconsistent with the unilateral nature of contact.
- Handling the non-smoothness and combinatorial complexity of contact dynamics efficiently for real-time control remains a challenge for model-based methods, while learning-based methods are data-intensive.

## Method

- The Contact Trust Region (CTR) is proposed, which augments Taylor approximations from differentiable physics engines with primal and dual feasibility constraints (e.g., non-penetration, friction cone adherence) to accurately model unilateral contact.
- An iterative ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) algorithm is developed, leveraging the relaxed CTR (R-CTR) for efficient local planning by solving a constrained optimization problem derived from a smoothed quasidynamic contact model.
- Local MPC is extended to global planning by constructing roadmaps of stable contact configurations offline, connecting these nodes with local MPC trajectories, and using graph search for online inference to perform long-horizon tasks.

## Results

- The Relaxed Contact Trust Region (R-CTR) consistently demonstrated superior performance in local MPC, achieving lower average tracking errors and fewer catastrophic failures compared to Ellipsoidal Trust Regions (ETR) and full CTR.
- The MPC, enhanced with frequent initial guess heuristics (MPC_Proj), provided robust stabilization for contact-rich tasks in high-fidelity simulation (Drake) and on hardware (IiwaBimanual, AllegroHand), showing strong sim-to-real transfer.
- Roadmaps for global planning were constructed offline in under 10 minutes for complex reorientation tasks (e.g., AllegroHand cube manipulation) and achieved high robustness with 150 consecutive successful hardware traversals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard symmetric trust regions are fundamentally inconsistent with the unilateral nature of contact, leading to unreliable local approximations, while the CTR provides a physically plausible local model.
> - Explicitly incorporating critical feasibility constraints from contact dynamics into the trust region (R-CTR) significantly improves the robustness and accuracy of local manipulation planning, even with smoothed dynamics.
> - Combining an efficient model-based local controller (CTR-MPC) with a roadmap-based global planning strategy allows for scalable, long-horizon dexterous manipulation of complex objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics: enabling dexterous contact-rich manipulation, where robots must autonomously decide where and how to establish contact to achieve complex tasks. Current robots often underutilize their full embodiment, typically restricted to end-effector contact or collision-free motion, a stark contrast to human dexterity.

The broader research landscape for contact-rich manipulation encompasses several approaches:
- **Classical Mechanics-based Methods:** These works leverage concepts like contact Jacobians and wrench-space arguments for local contact descriptions, but are often limited by fixed contact-mode sequences.
- **Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization (CITO):** Formulations that incorporate friction cone and unilateral contact via complementarity constraints. While powerful, iteratively solving these problems often involves linearizing constraints, which can lead to similar limitations as classical methods, especially when dealing with non-smooth dynamics.
- **Differentiable Simulators:** A more recent trend where Taylor approximations of dynamics are built from differentiable physics engines. These often employ symmetric ellipsoidal trust regions (ETR) to bound the local model’s validity.
- **Complementarity-based and Mixed-Integer Formulations:** These attempt to handle global contact mode changes but face significant scalability issues due to the exponential growth of possible contact modes.
- **Smoothing-based Methods:** These relax contact dynamics to approximate non-smooth behaviors, aiding gradient-based optimization. Recent work connects this to barrier smoothing in convex optimization, but naive application with standard controllers (e.g., LQR) can still lead to failures.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While successful in some dexterous manipulation tasks, RL methods are notoriously data-intensive, computationally expensive, and often require extensive parallelization or domain-specific heuristics.
- **Sampling-based Methods:** Can navigate complex, non-convex spaces but are often slow and computationally demanding.

The core gap identified by this paper lies in the fundamental inconsistency between the symmetric nature of standard trust regions (like ETRs) and the unilateral nature of contact dynamics. A local linear model might suggest pulling an object as plausible as pushing it, even when only pushing is physically possible due to contact forces. This leads to overly conservative or unreliable local approximations.

This work directly contributes to this landscape by introducing the **Contact Trust Region (CTR)**. It bridges the gap between differentiable dynamics (which provide local Taylor approximations) and the true, unilateral nature of contact (typically handled by complementarity constraints). By re-incorporating critical feasibility constraints (like friction cones and non-penetration) into the trust region itself, the CTR provides a more physically accurate and computationally efficient local model. This positions the work as a model-based, data-efficient alternative to purely learning-based methods, aiming to achieve dexterous manipulation without the heavy computational burden of RL or the explicit mode-searching of mixed-integer programming. It also offers a principled way to leverage the benefits of smoothed dynamics while mitigating their potential pitfalls.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable **efficient and dexterous contact-rich manipulation** by fully leveraging a robot’s physical embodiment. The authors seek to empower robots to autonomously establish and manage contact, moving beyond the current limitations of collision-free or end-effector-only interactions.

This ambitious goal is driven by several key motivations and broken down into specific objectives:

1. **Develop a "Good Local Description of Contact Dynamics":** The primary motivation is to find a local model of contact mechanics that simultaneously:
 - **Faithfully captures local behavior:** This is crucial for accurate planning and control, especially for the unilateral nature of contact where forces can only act in specific directions (e.g., pushing, not pulling).
 - **Remains efficient for computation:** This is vital for real-time applications and scalability to complex, high-dimensional systems.
 - The paper argues that existing Taylor approximations, often combined with symmetric ellipsoidal trust regions (ETR), are fundamentally inconsistent with unilateral contact. This inconsistency motivates the development of a better local model.

2. **Introduce the Contact Trust Region (CTR) as a Remedy:** To address the limitations of ETRs, the authors aim to propose the CTR, which explicitly incorporates the unilateral nature of contact by augmenting Taylor expansions with crucial, omitted feasibility constraints (e.g., friction cone and non-penetration). This aims to correctly characterize the locally valid region for contact interactions.

3. **Develop an Efficient Model Predictive Control (MPC) Algorithm:** The goal is to build a contact-implicit MPC algorithm that leverages the proposed CTR to synthesize local, contact-rich plans. This MPC aims to be a natural extension of iterative LQR, capable of handling the complexities of non-smooth contact dynamics and guiding the system towards local goals.

4. **Extend to Global Planning:** Recognizing that local MPC is inherently limited by local reachability, a further objective is to extend this capability to global planning. This involves stitching together local MPC plans (via a roadmap approach) to enable efficient, long-horizon manipulation for goals that require non-trivial exploration or transient sub-optimal actions.

5. **Achieve Computational Efficiency and Robustness:** A core motivation is to dramatically reduce computation time compared to state-of-the-art RL or sampling-based MPC methods, ideally enabling offline roadmap building in minutes and fast online inference. The method also aims for robustness, demonstrated through comprehensive evaluations in high-fidelity simulation and on hardware.

In essence, the authors are motivated to provide a more principled, physically consistent, and computationally tractable model-based framework for dexterous contact-rich manipulation, seeking to unlock robot capabilities that currently remain largely in the domain of human skill.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper integrates concepts from convex optimization, differentiable physics, and classical control theory to address the challenges of contact-rich manipulation.

#### 3.1. Foundation: Convex Quasidynamic Differentiable Contact (CQDC) Model

The core of the approach rests on the **Convex Quasidynamic Differentiable Contact (CQDC) model** proposed in prior work (Pang et al., 2023). This model simplifies contact dynamics by assuming a highly damped, quasidynamic system where velocities are omitted from the state. Crucially, it frames the simulation of contact as a **Second-Order Cone Program (SOCP)** at each timestep. The solution to this SOCP yields the next system configuration, and its gradients (derivatives of the solution with respect to state and input) can be efficiently computed via **sensitivity analysis in convex optimization**. This provides a local linear model of the dynamics.

#### 3.2. Smoothing of Contact Dynamics

The true contact dynamics, being piecewise smooth, exhibit discontinuous gradients, which can destabilize gradient-based optimization methods. To mitigate this, the methodology incorporates **log-barrier (interior-point) relaxation** of the SOCP formulation. This process perturbs the complementarity conditions by a small positive constant ($\kappa$), effectively smoothing the dynamics and creating a continuous force field where objects not in contact still exert forces inversely proportional to their distance. This makes the gradient landscape more tractable for optimizers.

#### 3.3. The Contact Trust Region (CTR)

This is the central methodological innovation.
- **Critique of Ellipsoidal Trust Regions (ETR):** The authors argue that standard symmetric ETRs are fundamentally inconsistent with the unilateral nature of contact. An ETR might suggest that "pulling" is as valid as "pushing" in a contact scenario, which is physically impossible.
- **CTR Definition:** The CTR (Definition 1) addresses this by intersecting the standard ellipsoidal trust region with **primal and dual feasibility constraints** derived from the original (un-smoothed) SOCP contact dynamics. These constraints, applied to the linearized primal (next state) and dual (contact force) variables, ensure that the predicted behavior within the trust region is physically plausible (e.g., non-penetration, friction cone adherence). The key insight is to re-incorporate these constraints, which are implicitly ignored when only using Taylor expansions from differentiable simulators, thereby better characterizing the local valid region.
- **Relaxed Contact Trust Region (R-CTR):** The paper also proposes a relaxed variant (Definition 3) that omits the primal feasibility constraint (non-penetration). This relaxation is found empirically to be less conservative, particularly for object motion, as the primal constraint can sometimes needlessly restrict robot motion due to slight gradient mismatches caused by smoothing.
- **Connection to Classical Mechanics:** The paper formally establishes a connection between the R-CTR and classical concepts in manipulation, such as the **wrench set** and **motion cones**, providing an alternative derivation of the motion set from contact forces.

#### 3.4. Local Planning and Control with MPC

The proposed MPC (Algorithm 2) is an iterative, gradient-based approach akin to iLQR:
- **Problem Formulation:** Solves a constrained optimization problem (Algorithm 1, `SubTrajOpt`) at each MPC step to find optimal perturbations (δq, δu) around a nominal trajectory.
- **Dynamics and Constraints:** It uses linear dynamics derived from the Taylor expansion of the smoothed CQDC model and incorporates the R-CTR (or CTR/ETR for comparison) as additional convex constraints. Joint limits and torque limits can also be added.
- **Solver:** Each `SubTrajOpt` problem is a standard SOCP, efficiently solved by off-the-shelf conic solvers (Mosek).
- **Initial Guess Heuristic:** To improve convergence from initial states not in contact, a heuristic is used to guide the robot into contact by leveraging the force-from-a-distance effect of the smoothed dynamics.

#### 3.5. Stabilization under Second-Order Dynamics

To bridge the gap between the quasidynamic planning model and real-world physics, two practical heuristics are introduced for deployment on high-fidelity simulators (Drake) and hardware (Algorithm 3):
- **Action Chunking:** Instead of frequent replanning, MPC computes a longer trajectory, which is then rolled out in its entirety before replanning. This reduces compounding errors from model mismatch.
- **Frequent Initial Guess Heuristics (MPC_Proj):** The initial guess heuristic (Section 4.2) is applied at every MPC iteration to actively pull the robot back into contact, mitigating the "hydroplaning" artifact present in CQDC dynamics and improving contact stability.

#### 3.6. Global Planning with Roadmaps

To overcome the local reachability limitations of MPC, a roadmap-based global planning strategy is employed:
- **Contact Configuration Generation:** This involves solving an optimization problem (40) to find "advantageous" robot configurations ($q_a$) for a given object state ($q_o$) and goal ($q_{o_g}$). The cost function combines the finite-horizon value function of the MPC policy and a **robustness regularizer** (maximum inscribed sphere in the wrench set), which promotes stable grasps. This is solved using sampling-based optimization, with a reduced-order model and contact sampling heuristics for complex systems like AllegroHand.
- **Roadmap Construction (Offline, Algorithm 4):** Nodes in the roadmap represent stable object configurations paired with optimal robot configurations. Edges are formed by connecting these nodes using: 1) local MPC to transition the object, and 2) collision-free motion planning to reposition the robot hand while the object remains stable. Exploiting object symmetries significantly expands the roadmap's coverage.
- **Online Inference:** For a new goal, the planner connects the initial and goal configurations to the nearest roadmap nodes and then uses graph search to find the shortest path.

#### 3.7. Evaluation

The methodology is thoroughly evaluated through:
- **Statistical Analysis:** Thousands of trajectories are run in simulation.
- **Ablation Studies:** Comparisons between R-CTR, CTR, and ETR, and the impact of MPC heuristics.
- **High-Fidelity Simulation:** Using Drake, a state-of-the-art simulator with full second-order dynamics and advanced contact solvers.
- **Hardware Experiments:** On a planar IiwaBimanual system and a 3D AllegroHand system, demonstrating sim-to-real transfer. Performance metrics include translation and rotation errors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings across local planning, stabilization, and global planning, validated through extensive simulation and hardware experiments.

#### 4.1. Local MPC Performance on CQDC Dynamics

- **Superiority of R-CTR:** The **Relaxed Contact Trust Region (R-CTR)** consistently demonstrates the best performance in local Model Predictive Control (MPC) when operating under the Convex Quasidynamic Differentiable Contact (CQDC) dynamics. It achieves the lowest average tracking errors and variance on both the IiwaBimanual and AllegroHand systems. Crucially, R-CTR leads to significantly fewer trajectories with large errors or catastrophic failures compared to other trust region variants.
- **Limitations of CTR and ETR:**
 - **Full CTR (with primal feasibility)** is often overly restrictive, especially for the IiwaBimanual system, leading to higher tracking errors than R-CTR. This suggests that enforcing non-penetration within the *linearized* model can prematurely limit the search space.
 - **Ellipsoidal Trust Region (ETR)**, due to its symmetric nature and inability to capture unilateral contact, performs the worst. It results in larger errors, a wider distribution of errors, and more outliers, as it fails to prune physically infeasible actions (e.g., trying to pull an object when only pushing is possible).
- **Impact of Trust Region Choice:** The benefits of R-CTR are most pronounced in scenarios with unilateral contact or where a stable grasp is not fully formed. In contrast, when the robot has a robust force-closure grasp (e.g., AllegroHand squeezing a cube), the differences between R-CTR and ETR/CTR diminish.
- **Insensitivity to Planning Horizon:** Surprisingly, increasing the planning horizon (T) for local MPC beyond T=1 has little to no effect on the final tracking error. This suggests that the immediate quadratic cost, coupled with the R-CTR, acts as an effective approximation of the true cost-to-go for the goals considered.
- **Low Infeasibility Rate:** The iterative MPC scheme rarely encounters infeasible trust regions, indicating the robustness and tractability of the formulation.
- **Computational Efficiency:** CTR-based `CtrTrajOpt` (the core MPC step) is highly efficient, taking milliseconds to tens of milliseconds per step (e.g., 99.2ms for AllegroHand with R-CTR), which is orders of magnitude faster than learning-based methods that require days or weeks of training.

#### 4.2. Stabilization under Second-Order Dynamics (Simulation and Hardware)

- **Closed-loop is Crucial:** Closed-loop MPC, even when using the simplified CQDC model for planning, significantly outperforms open-loop execution on high-fidelity second-order dynamics (Drake simulation) and hardware. This highlights the indispensable role of feedback in mitigating model mismatches and accumulating errors.
- **Effectiveness of Initial Guess Heuristics (MPC_Proj):** Applying the initial guess heuristics more frequently (at every MPC iteration) is crucial for robust performance. It significantly reduces the incidence of large errors and catastrophic failures (e.g., objects falling off the hand) by consistently bringing the robot back into contact. This also leads to shorter robot path lengths, indicating more efficient movement.
- **R-CTR Advantage Persists:** Even under second-order dynamics, R-CTR remains the top performer, achieving the lowest average errors and tighter error distributions compared to ETR and CTR. The relative performance trends observed on CQDC dynamics generally hold.
- **Sim-to-Real Correspondence:** Hardware experiments yield average errors highly comparable to simulation results, demonstrating strong sim-to-real transfer. This is attributed to careful alignment of simulation and hardware parameters (collision geometries, gains, friction).
- **Task Difficulty:** The AllegroHand task (in-hand cube reorientation) is inherently more challenging than the IiwaBimanual task (planar bucket manipulation), leading to higher tracking errors, especially for poses requiring the cube to be lifted from the palm.

#### 4.3. Global Planning with Roadmaps

- **Efficient Contact Configuration Generation:** The method successfully finds intuitive and robust initial actuator configurations for the AllegroHand system, leading to low errors for subsequent MPC rollouts. This process takes minutes.
- **Fast Roadmap Construction:** For symmetric objects like a cube, a roadmap enabling 24 distinct rotations can be built offline in under 10 minutes on a standard laptop (CPU-only), leveraging object symmetries.
- **High Robustness:** The constructed roadmap demonstrates high robustness in hardware, with 150 consecutive successful edge traversals (transitions between different object orientations) before hardware failure (overheating).
- **Fast Online Inference:** Once built, the roadmap enables fast online inference, allowing the robot to synthesize plans for new goals efficiently by chaining pre-computed local trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of dexterous contact-rich manipulation, offering a principled, computationally efficient, and robust model-based framework with far-reaching potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Fundamental Contribution to Contact Dynamics Modeling

The introduction of the **Contact Trust Region (CTR)** and its relaxed variant (R-CTR) is a fundamental contribution. It solves a core inconsistency in contact dynamics modeling by merging local Taylor approximations (from differentiable simulators) with the unilateral nature of contact (captured by feasibility constraints). This provides a more physically accurate and trustworthy local model than traditional ellipsoidal trust regions, bridging concepts from classical manipulation mechanics (wrench sets, motion cones) with modern optimization-based dynamics. This principled approach is highly generalizable and can be applied to a wide array of contact-rich robotics problems.

#### 5.2. Enabling Robust and Efficient Model Predictive Control

The paper demonstrates how CTR, especially R-CTR, can be integrated into an MPC framework to achieve **robust and efficient dexterous manipulation**. The resulting MPC algorithm is shown to stabilize complex systems through contact in both high-fidelity simulation and on hardware. This offers a powerful model-based alternative to highly data-intensive and computationally expensive learning-based methods, capable of operating with significantly reduced training time and computational resources. The empirical successes, even with a simplified quasidynamic model, highlight the practicality of the approach.

#### 5.3. Advancing Global Planning Capabilities

By combining local MPC with a roadmap-based global planning strategy, the research addresses the challenge of long-horizon manipulation tasks that require non-greedy actions. The ability to efficiently generate "advantageous" contact configurations and then construct a roadmap offline in minutes provides a scalable solution for complex reorientation tasks. This dramatically extends the reach of model-based planners to problems traditionally reserved for methods with extensive exploration or prior demonstrations.

#### 5.4. Strong Sim-to-Real Transfer and Broad Applicability

The compelling results on diverse hardware platforms (IiwaBimanual for whole-body manipulation and AllegroHand for in-hand manipulation), coupled with high sim-to-real correspondence, underscore the practical utility and robustness of the methodology. The approach's reliance on general robot and object URDFs means it can be readily extended to a wide range of contact-rich systems, including different grippers, other robot hands, or even legged robots (with suitable extensions for balance).

#### 5.5. Synergies with Learning-based Methods

Crucially, the authors explicitly highlight how this model-based planner can **synergize with and significantly enhance learning-based methods**:
- **Data Generation for Imitation Learning (IL):** The planner can serve as an automated expert, generating vast, high-quality, and embodiment-specific datasets for contact-rich tasks that are difficult or impossible for human teleoperation. This can overcome major bottlenecks in imitation learning, enabling the training of robust policies without human demonstrations.
- **Efficient Exploration for Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The stable contact configurations and successful trajectories discovered by the planner (e.g., in the roadmap) can act as highly effective exploration mechanisms for RL, dramatically improving sample efficiency and guiding agents towards relevant states.

In conclusion, this work pushes the boundaries of autonomous dexterous manipulation by offering a principled solution to the fundamental problem of modeling and trusting local contact dynamics. The Contact Trust Region, integrated into an efficient MPC and global planning framework, not only provides robust control capabilities in its own right but also offers powerful tools to accelerate and improve learning-based approaches, potentially leading to robots that can interact with the physical world with unprecedented dexterity and versatility.
