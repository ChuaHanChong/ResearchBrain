# Dexterous Contact-Rich Manipulation via the Contact Trust Region

> [!summary] Summary
> A model-based framework for dexterous contact-rich manipulation introduces the Contact Trust Region (CTR) to provide a physically consistent local model of contact dynamics. This approach, integrated with model predictive control, achieves robust object reorientation on hardware and builds global roadmaps in minutes.

## Problem

- Current robots underutilize their full physical embodiment, often restricted to collision-free or end-effector-only interactions, limiting dexterous contact-rich manipulation.
- Existing local linear models of contact dynamics, often combined with symmetric ellipsoidal trust regions, are fundamentally inconsistent with the unilateral nature of contact.
- Handling the non-smoothness and combinatorial complexity of contact dynamics efficiently for real-time control remains a challenge for model-based methods, while learning-based methods are data-intensive.

## Method

- The Contact Trust Region (CTR) is proposed, which augments Taylor approximations from differentiable physics engines with primal and dual feasibility constraints (e.g., non-penetration, friction cone adherence) to accurately model unilateral contact.
- An iterative ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) algorithm is developed, leveraging the relaxed CTR (R-CTR) for efficient local planning by solving a constrained optimization problem derived from a smoothed quasidynamic contact model.
- Local MPC is extended to global planning by constructing roadmaps of stable contact configurations offline, connecting these nodes with local MPC trajectories, and using graph search for online inference to perform long-horizon tasks.

## Results

- The Relaxed Contact Trust Region (R-CTR) consistently demonstrated superior performance in local MPC, achieving lower average tracking errors and fewer catastrophic failures compared to Ellipsoidal Trust Regions (ETR) and full CTR.
- The MPC, enhanced with frequent initial guess heuristics (MPC_Proj), provided robust stabilization for contact-rich tasks in high-fidelity simulation (Drake) and on hardware (IiwaBimanual, AllegroHand), showing strong sim-to-real transfer.
- Roadmaps for global planning were constructed offline in under 10 minutes for complex reorientation tasks (e.g., AllegroHand cube manipulation) and achieved high robustness with 150 consecutive successful hardware traversals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard symmetric trust regions are fundamentally inconsistent with the unilateral nature of contact, leading to unreliable local approximations, while the CTR provides a physically plausible local model.
> - Explicitly incorporating critical feasibility constraints from contact dynamics into the trust region (R-CTR) significantly improves the robustness and accuracy of local manipulation planning, even with smoothed dynamics.
> - Combining an efficient model-based local controller (CTR-MPC) with a roadmap-based global planning strategy allows for scalable, long-horizon dexterous manipulation of complex objects.
