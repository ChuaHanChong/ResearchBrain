# CRoPS: A Training-Free Hallucination Mitigation Framework for Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CRoPS is a training-free framework that effectively mitigates hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models by dynamically integrating a novel text-deficit hallucinated model with a visual-deficit model. The approach consistently reduces hallucination rates by approximately 15-25% on benchmarks like CHAIR for LLaVA-1.5 while maintaining fluency and improving general multimodal reasoning with minimal inference overhead.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate factually incorrect or inconsistent content (hallucinations), severely hindering their trustworthiness.
- Prior training-free hallucination mitigation methods often target a single source of hallucination and lose effectiveness as text generation progresses due to decreasing reliance on visual input.
- Current approaches fail to adequately address hallucinations arising from textual context or co-occurrence biases in training data, especially in later stages of generation.

## Method

- Develops a novel =="text-deficit" hallucinated model== that removes all visual tokens and selectively retains the least important textual tokens, providing a consistent contrastive signal throughout text generation.
- Proposes ==Generalized Contrastive Decoding==, an extended framework capable of integrating multiple distinct hallucinated models to address various sources of errors simultaneously.
- Integrates a visual-deficit model (inspired by SID) and the new text-deficit model with ==dynamically adjusted weights==, adapting their influence based on the generation stage.

## Results

- Demonstrated consistent reduction in hallucination rates across LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, and Qwen2-VL, with approximately **15-25%** reduction on CHAIR for LLaVA-1.5 while maintaining recall.
- Achieved superior performance on multi-dimensional hallucination benchmarks like AMBER, showing a **25-30%** reduction in overall hallucination and **10-15%** reduction in cognitive deviation.
- Introduced minimal inference overhead, performing comparably or faster than other training-free methods, confirming its practicality as a plug-and-play solution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Vision-Language Models' reliance on visual input diminishes significantly during later stages of text generation, making purely visual-token-perturbing methods less effective for hallucination mitigation over time.
> - Hallucinations in LVLMs arise from multifaceted sources, requiring a comprehensive approach that moves beyond single-source mitigation strategies.
> - A novel "text-deficit" hallucinated model, which removes visual tokens and crucial textual context, consistently produces a distinct contrastive signal crucial for effective and sustained hallucination suppression.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and pervasive challenge in the field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs): the phenomenon of "hallucination." LVLMs, such as LLaVA and Qwen2-VL, have made remarkable progress in multimodal comprehension and generation, enabling diverse applications from image captioning to visual question answering. However, their tendency to produce factually incorrect or inconsistent content—hallucinations—severely undermines their reliability and trustworthiness for real-world deployment.

The current research landscape for mitigating hallucinations in LVLMs can be broadly categorized into two approaches:
1. **Training-based methods:** These involve fine-tuning models on hallucination-aware datasets or employing auxiliary models for supervision or revision. While often effective, they demand significant computational resources and careful dataset design.
2. **Training-free methods:** These approaches modify inference-time decoding or attention patterns without requiring additional training. They are highly desirable due to their plug-and-play nature and lower computational overhead.

This work specifically positions itself within the *training-free* paradigm, building upon the framework of **Contrastive Decoding (CD)**. CD operates by identifying a "hallucinated model" and then suppressing the probabilities of hallucinated tokens by subtracting its logits from those of the original model. Existing training-free CD methods, such as Multi-Modal Mutual-Information Decoding (M3ID) and Self Introspective Decoding (SID), have shown promise but suffer from two primary limitations:
- **Narrow Assumptions:** They often rely on a single, specific source of hallucination (e.g., visual token dilution or over-reliance on language priors), failing to capture the full spectrum of hallucination sources (e.g., those arising from training data biases or misleading visual cues).
- **Diminishing Effectiveness Over Time:** Their ability to mitigate hallucinations tends to decline significantly during the later stages of text generation, precisely when hallucinations are most likely to occur. This is because visual tokens, which many methods focus on perturbing, become less influential as the model generates more textual content, relying more on previously generated text.

CRoPS directly addresses these limitations. It acknowledges that existing CD methods, by primarily focusing on removing or perturbing visual tokens, do not fully account for how visual information propagates into text generation over time, nor do they capture all types of hallucinations (e.g., those influenced by textual context or co-occurrence biases in training data). By proposing a novel hallucinated model that focuses on textual inputs and by generalizing the contrastive decoding framework to integrate multiple hallucinated models, CRoPS aims to provide a more comprehensive and consistently effective solution than its predecessors. This work represents a significant step towards developing more robust and trustworthy LVLMs by moving beyond single-source, early-stage mitigation strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a highly effective, training-free framework, named CRoPS, to mitigate the persistent problem of hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations stemming from an in-depth analysis of existing training-free contrastive decoding (CD) methods.

The key motivations and corresponding objectives are:

1. **Addressing the Diminishing Dependency on Visual Tokens (Drawback I):**
 - **Motivation:** The authors empirically demonstrate, through a novel "Visual Dependency (VD(t))" metric, that LVLMs' reliance on visual input significantly decreases as text generation progresses. This means that hallucinated models that solely manipulate or remove visual tokens (like M3ID or SID) lose their effectiveness in suppressing hallucinations during later stages of generation, where hallucinations are most prevalent. The outputs of such "visually hallucinated" models become increasingly similar to the original model's outputs, diminishing the contrastive signal.
 - **Objective:** To devise a novel hallucinated model that remains effective even when visual dependency diminishes. This requires moving beyond visual token manipulation to consider the role of textual context (prompt and past generated tokens) in later-stage hallucinations.

2. **Overcoming the Insufficiency of a Single Hallucinated Model (Drawback II):**
 - **Motivation:** Existing CD methods typically rely on a single hallucinated model, which by design, targets only a specific source or mode of hallucination. For instance, M3ID targets hallucinations from excessive reliance on language priors by removing all visual input, while SID targets hallucinations from irrelevant visual features by perturbing visual tokens. The authors show that such single-model approaches are insufficient to address the diverse and multifaceted nature of LVLM hallucinations, which can stem from various factors like spurious visual cues, statistical biases from training data (e.g., object co-occurrence), or over-reliance on textual context.
 - **Objective:** To generalize the contrastive decoding framework to accommodate *multiple* distinct hallucinated models. This "Generalized Contrastive Decoding" would allow for simultaneously addressing various sources of hallucination by combining different types of hallucinated signals.

By addressing these two critical drawbacks, CRoPS aims to achieve consistent and significant improvements in hallucination mitigation across various LVLMs, tasks, and generation stages, ultimately enhancing the factual grounding and reliability of these powerful models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CRoPS builds upon the principles of Contrastive Decoding (CD) but introduces two key innovations to overcome the limitations of prior training-free methods: a novel hallucinated model that targets textual context and a generalized form of contrastive decoding.

#### 3.1. Background Concepts

- **LVLM Inference and Decoding:** The process begins with an input image (encoded into visual tokens `V`) and a textual prompt (`X`). An LLM, parameterized by `θ`, sequentially generates output tokens `y_t` based on `V`, `X`, and previously generated tokens `y_<t`. The model produces logits, converted to probabilities `p_t` via softmax, from which the next token is decoded.
- **Contrastive Decoding (CD):** This framework (Eq. 2) mitigates hallucinations by subtracting the log probabilities of a "hallucinated model" (`p_hal_t`) from the log probabilities of the original model (`p_orig_t`), scaled by a coefficient `α`. The core idea is to suppress tokens that are strongly predicted by a model prone to hallucination.
- **Attention-based Token Pruning:** Similar to SID, CRoPS utilizes attention weights from an early transformer layer (`l`) to determine the importance of input tokens. For a given query token `y_t`, the importance score `ψ` for a key token `K` is the mean attention weight across all heads (Eq. 3). Tokens with low importance scores are considered "least important."

#### 3.2. Remedy I: A Novel Model to Contrast With (Text-Deficit Hallucinated Model)

Motivated by the observation that LVLM generation becomes increasingly dependent on textual context (prompt and past outputs) and less on visual tokens in later stages, CRoPS proposes a new type of hallucinated model:

- **Mechanism:** Instead of solely removing or perturbing visual tokens, this model (`p_vis-txt-hal_t`) *completely removes all visual tokens* and *selectively removes the most important textual tokens*. The "least important" text tokens (based on attention scores calculated via Eq. 5, considering `X` and `y_<t`) are retained. This simulates a scenario where the model is deprived of both visual grounding and crucial textual context, pushing it towards more generic, potentially hallucinated, language patterns.
- **Token Retention Policy:** The number of retained *least important* text tokens, `η(μ, t)`, is dynamically controlled by a non-decreasing exponential function: `η(μ, t) = β_0 + β_1(1 - e^(-μt))`. This ensures a smooth and saturating increase in retained tokens over time, maintaining sparsity of incoherent tokens as the generation length `t` grows.
- **Effectiveness:** This model (Eq. 7) generates `p_vis-txt-hal_t`, which, unlike purely visual-token-removed models, shows a consistent "Visuotextual Dependency (VTD(t))" that does not diminish over time (Figure 2, left). This implies that `p_vis-txt-hal_t` remains sufficiently distinct from `p_orig_t`, providing a strong and consistent contrastive signal throughout the generation process.

#### 3.3. Remedy II: A Generalized Form of Contrastive Decoding

Recognizing that a single hallucinated model cannot capture all sources of hallucination, CRoPS introduces an extension to the standard CD framework:

- **Mechanism:** Generalized Contrastive Decoding (Eq. 9) allows for integrating `R` different hallucinated models. The final log probabilities (`log p_st_t`) are computed by weighting the original model's log probabilities by `(1 + Σα_r)` and subtracting the weighted sum of log probabilities from `R` hallucinated models (`Σα_r log p_hal(r)_t`). Each `α_r` is a positive coefficient determining the strength of suppression from each hallucinated model.
- **Justification:** This generalization enables CRoPS to simultaneously address various hallucination sources (e.g., visual-only issues and text-context-driven issues) by leveraging distinct hallucinated signals. The authors empirically show (Figure 4, left) that the chosen hallucinated models are indeed sufficiently distinct to justify their combined use.

#### 3.4. CRoPS Framework (Integration)

CRoPS integrates two specific hallucinated models under the generalized contrastive decoding formulation (Eq. 11):

1. **`p_vis-hal_t` (Visual-Deficit Model):** This model is inspired by SID. It removes the *most important visual tokens* (retaining the least important) while keeping all text tokens. It primarily targets hallucinations related to spurious visual cues and co-occurrence biases, which are more prominent in the early stages of generation.
2. **`p_vis-txt-hal_t` (Vision-Text-Deficit Model):** This is the novel model proposed by CRoPS, as described in Remedy I. It removes *all visual tokens* and retains only the *least important textual tokens*. This model is designed to capture hallucinations that arise from over-reliance on textual context, especially in later stages of generation when visual dependency wanes.

- **Dynamic Weighting:** The contrastive coefficients `α` are dynamically applied: `α^(1)` (for `p_vis-hal_t`) is kept constant, as vision-driven hallucinations are more significant early on. `α^(2)_t` (for `p_vis-txt-hal_t`) is designed as `(1 - e^(-γt))/e^(-γt)` to gradually increase over time, reflecting the growing influence of text-induced hallucinations.
- **Overall Algorithm:** Algorithm 1 in the appendix outlines the sequential process, including token pruning, generation of probabilities from original and hallucinated models, generalized contrastive decoding, and token selection.

In summary, CRoPS's methodology strategically combines a novel text-deficit hallucinated model with a generalized contrastive decoding framework, allowing it to adaptively mitigate diverse hallucination sources across the entire generation timeline, offering a more robust and comprehensive solution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CRoPS demonstrates its superior performance compared to state-of-the-art training-free hallucination mitigation methods across a wide range of benchmarks and LVLM architectures.

#### 4.1. Benchmarks and Consistent Gains

CRoPS was evaluated across three widely adopted LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, and Qwen2-VL) and compared against several strong baselines (VCD, ICD, OPERA, ClearSight, SID, M3ID). The evaluation covered six benchmarks, categorizing hallucinations in various ways and assessing general multimodal reasoning:

- **CHAIR Benchmark (Object-Level Hallucination - Table 1):** CRoPS consistently achieves the lowest C_S (hallucinated objects) and C_I (hallucinated captions) scores across all LVLMs. For LLaVA-1.5, it reduces hallucination rates by approximately 15–25% relative to M3ID, maintaining comparable recall. Similar gains are observed for LLaVA-NeXT and Qwen2-VL (8–10% reduction in C_S and C_I). This highlights CRoPS's effectiveness in producing visually faithful descriptions without over-suppressing descriptive quality.
- **AMBER Benchmark (Multi-Dimensional Hallucination - Table 2):** CRoPS outperforms all baselines across all metrics (CHAIR, HAL – overall hallucination, Cog – cognitive deviation). On LLaVA-1.5, it shows a 25–30% reduction in CHAIR and HAL and a 10–15% reduction in Cog, indicating more accurate and semantically coherent generations that mitigate both perceptual and cognitive hallucinations.
- **GPT-4o Assisted Benchmark (Fine-Grained Hallucination & Fluency - Figure 5):** CRoPS demonstrates the lowest Sentence-Level Hallucination Ratio (SHR). Crucially, it achieves this while maintaining superior 1-gram and 2-gram fluency and a higher Words Per Image (WPI), signifying that CRoPS produces fluent and detailed captions without sacrificing factual grounding, unlike some methods that reduce hallucinations by shortening outputs.
- **POPE Benchmark (Binary VQA Hallucination Detection - Table 3 & 11):** In this discriminative task, CRoPS achieves the highest Accuracy and F1 scores across all LVLMs and all subsets (Random, Popular, Adversarial), showing an average performance gain of about 2% over the strongest baseline. Its robustness is particularly evident in the challenging Adversarial subset, confirming strong visual grounding even in restricted answer formats.
- **MME and MathVista Evaluations (General Capabilities - Table 4):** Beyond hallucination-specific tasks, CRoPS improves MME scores (general multimodal perception) and achieves top or near-top performance in MathVista (visual mathematical reasoning). This suggests that CRoPS's contrastive decoding strategy enhances broader multimodal reasoning abilities, not just descriptive fidelity.

#### 4.2. Efficiency and Ablation Studies

- **Latency Comparison (Table 5):** CRoPS introduces minimal additional inference overhead. It is approximately 3 times slower than vanilla sampling (652s vs. 215s on LLaVA-1.5 7B), but its runtime is comparable to or lower than other training-free methods like VCD (550s) and SID (510s), and significantly faster than OPERA (1947s). This makes it a practical plug-and-play solution.
- **Effect of Image and Text Removal (Table 6, Left):** The ablation confirms that the proposed approach of removing *all visual tokens* and *selectively preserving least important text tokens* (CRoPS's novel hallucinated model) is more effective at inducing hallucination (and thus providing a stronger contrastive signal) than other configurations, including random text removal.
- **Choice of Hallucination Model (Table 7):** Replacing CRoPS's novel text-deficit hallucinated model with M3ID's model in the generalized framework yields inferior results, underscoring the specific design effectiveness of CRoPS's second model.
- **Token Retention Policy (Table 6, Right):** The exponential policy for dynamically controlling retained text tokens (`η(μ, t)`) in the text-deficit model outperforms constant, all-but-one, and linear policies, achieving the lowest hallucination scores.
- **Hyperparameter Ablation (Table 8):** Analysis of CRoPS's hyperparameters (`α`, `γ`, `β_0`, `β_1`, `μ`) shows that moderate contrastive strength and gradual masking yield the best results, demonstrating the framework's robustness with a single, shared configuration used across all experiments without model-specific tuning.

In conclusion, CRoPS consistently delivers significant improvements in mitigating hallucinations across various LVLM architectures and tasks, balancing factual accuracy with descriptive richness. Its efficiency and robust design make it a highly effective and practical solution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CRoPS framework represents a significant advancement in enhancing the reliability and trustworthiness of Large Vision-Language Models, with broad implications for their deployment in real-world applications.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical AI Challenge

Hallucination is a fundamental obstacle preventing LVLMs from being confidently used in sensitive domains such as medical diagnosis, legal analysis, scientific research, and critical infrastructure management. CRoPS directly tackles this core issue, moving LVLMs closer to becoming dependable AI assistants. By improving factual grounding and reducing spurious content, CRoPS contributes to making AI outputs more credible and less misleading.

#### 5.2. Practical and Accessible Solution

One of the most impactful aspects of CRoPS is its training-free nature. This characteristic allows it to be easily integrated as a "plug-and-play" module with existing pre-trained LVLMs, avoiding the immense computational cost and time investment associated with retraining or fine-tuning large foundation models. This accessibility democratizes hallucination mitigation, enabling researchers and practitioners to immediately apply CRoPS to their chosen LVLMs without extensive resources. The open-sourcing of the code further amplifies its potential for widespread adoption and further research.

#### 5.3. Comprehensive and Robust Mitigation

CRoPS distinguishes itself by offering a more comprehensive solution than prior methods. By understanding and addressing the limitations of single-source hallucinated models and the diminishing influence of visual tokens over generation time, CRoPS provides a robust defense against diverse types of hallucinations. The generalized contrastive decoding, combined with the novel text-deficit hallucinated model, ensures that both early-stage, visually-driven hallucinations and later-stage, text-context-driven hallucinations are effectively mitigated. Its consistent performance across multiple LVLM families and various benchmarks (captioning, VQA, general perception, mathematical reasoning) highlights its generalizability and effectiveness.

#### 5.4. Advancing Methodological Understanding

Beyond its practical utility, CRoPS introduces conceptual innovations to the field of contrastive decoding. The idea of a "text-deficit" hallucinated model, which selectively removes crucial textual context, expands the toolkit for designing effective contrastive signals. Furthermore, the formalized "Generalized Contrastive Decoding" provides a powerful framework for intelligently combining multiple hallucination signals, opening new avenues for research into more sophisticated multi-signal mitigation strategies.

#### 5.5. Future Directions and Economic Impact

CRoPS’s demonstrated ability to improve LVLM reliability could accelerate their adoption in high-stakes domains, potentially leading to new applications and efficiency gains. While the paper acknowledges limitations such as increased inference latency (though comparable to or better than other CD methods), it also points to future work on optimizing this overhead, for instance, through parallelizing forward passes. This ongoing refinement suggests a path toward real-time, highly reliable multimodal AI systems. The successful mitigation of hallucinations fosters greater user trust, which is essential for the broad commercial and societal impact of advanced AI technologies.

In conclusion, CRoPS marks a crucial step forward in making LVLMs more trustworthy and deployable. Its innovative approach, strong empirical results, and practical applicability position it to significantly impact the development and responsible use of multimodal AI systems.
