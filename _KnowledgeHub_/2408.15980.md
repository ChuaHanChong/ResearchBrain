# In-Context Imitation Learning via Next-Token Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and Autodesk Research developed the In-Context Robot Transformer (ICRT), a framework that allows a real robot to perform novel manipulation tasks solely from human teleoperated demonstrations provided as prompts during inference. ICRT achieved an average success rate of 79.2% on previously unseen pick-and-place and poking tasks, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art robot foundation models without requiring any fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Existing robot foundation models often require additional training or fine-tuning to adapt to unseen tasks or novel environment configurations, limiting their real-world applicability.
- Current few-shot imitation learning approaches in robotics typically involve complex loss functions, prior knowledge, or specific perception modules, hindering simple, general adaptation.
- A need exists for robots to rapidly learn new skills from one or a few demonstrations without parameter updates, mirroring the in-context learning success of large language and vision models.

## Method

- The **In-Context** ==Robot Transformer (ICRT)== frames in-context imitation learning as a next-token prediction problem for visuomotor trajectories, employing a causal transformer model with a frozen, pre-trained vision encoder.
- ICRT is trained on a unique multi-task dataset (ICRT-MT) where multiple actions are possible from the same initial observation, compelling the model to rely on provided prompt demonstrations to disambiguate the intended task.
- The training loss is exclusively computed for actions predicted *after* the prompt trajectories, encouraging the model to generate conditioned responses based on the examples rather than performing unconditional generation within the prompt itself.

## Results

- ICRT achieved an overall success rate of **79.2%** across unseen pick-and-place and poking tasks, substantially outperforming baselines like Octo (**9.2%**) and OpenVLA (**7.5%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the custom ICRT-MT dataset and the 'no prompt loss' strategy were critical for in-context learning, as training solely on the DROID dataset yielded **0%** success.
- ICRT demonstrated robustness to the number of prompt trajectories (1, 2, or 3) and showed compositional generalization to some conceptually similar, albeit unseen, action primitives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-world in-context imitation learning is feasible for robot manipulation, allowing parameter-free adaptation to novel tasks through human teleoperated demonstrations at inference time.
> - Careful design of multi-task training datasets, where prompts are essential to distinguish tasks from identical initial states, is crucial for developing robust in-context learning capabilities in robotics.
> - Computing the loss only on predicted actions following the prompt is a key training strategy that fosters genuine in-context conditioning, enabling the model to effectively learn from demonstrations.
