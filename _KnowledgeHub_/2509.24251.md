# Latent Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR) enables Multimodal Large Language Models to perform autoregressive reasoning directly within a shared visual-semantic embedding space, alternating with text generation. This approach substantially improves performance on tasks requiring fine-grained visual understanding and perception, outperforming methods that rely solely on textual reasoning or external visual tools.

## Problem

- Most MLLMs primarily conduct reasoning in the language space, leading to a 'semantic gap' where crucial visual nuances relevant to a query may be missed.
- Visual information, once encoded, often remains static during the reasoning process, and even tool-augmented methods treat visual input as something external to be pre-processed or re-encoded.
- Reliance on external visual tools for 'thinking with images' introduces rigidity, overhead, and bypasses the potential for MLLMs to inherently manipulate or explore visual semantics.

## Method

- LVR introduces a =='latent reasoning mode'== triggered by special tokens, allowing the MLLM to propagate its last hidden states as input embeddings to reconstruct visual semantics relevant to the query.
- A two-stage training pipeline is employed: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) uses ==visual reconstruction loss== and next-token prediction loss with annotated regions of interest to teach latent visual reasoning.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) with an adapted ==GRPO_latent algorithm== refines the LVR process, using text-based accuracy rewards and a format reward to encourage and optimize latent visual reasoning without explicit intermediate visual supervision.

## Results

- LVR achieved state-of-the-art performance, demonstrating significant gains on perception-intensive benchmarks, including a **2.7%** improvement on V*D.A. and a **5.3%** improvement on V*R.P. subsets.
- The method consistently outperformed both 'Thinking with Images' (e.g., PixelReasoner) and 'Thinking about Images' (e.g., PAPO) paradigms across various visual tasks like Counting, IQ-Test, JigSaw, and Spatial Relation.
- Reinforcement Learning with GRPO_latent further boosted performance over the SFT stage, and the 'Fixed Token' decoding strategy for LVR mode was found to be the most stable and effective.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enabling MLLMs to actively reason over visual information by reconstructing latent visual thoughts in a shared embedding space is more effective than indirectly referencing it via textual Chain-of-Thought or external tool calls.
> - The core LLM can natively align visual and textual semantics in the joint latent space, as adding extra architectural heads for latent reasoning did not yield superior performance.
> - Reinforcement learning, guided by explicit format rewards for activating latent reasoning, successfully encourages and refines the model's ability to perform internal visual computations.
