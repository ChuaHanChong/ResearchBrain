# Proxy Policy Steering

> [!summary] Summary
> Proxy Policy Steering (PPS) adapts a frozen flow-matching VLA at inference time. Two 32M proxy policies are trained: a reference distilled from the base along its denoising trajectories, and a task proxy fine-tuned from it on 50 demonstrations. Their velocity difference, scaled by gamma, is added to the base velocity at every denoising step, lifting a state-of-the-art base policy by 53% absolute success.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning generalist VLAs on few demonstrations overwrites broad pretrained priors like failure recovery, costs heavily at scale, and needs weight access unavailable for API-only bases.
- Existing sampling-time steering only reweights behaviors the base already samples, so it cannot reach task-specific modes the base assigns negligible probability to.
- Residual and candidate-selection methods act after the base commits to a clean action mode, and RL noise-optimization needs non-zero base success or large offline data to bootstrap.

## Method

- Adaptation framed as a ==product-of-experts== density correction: base policy times the task-to-reference proxy density ratio raised to gamma, approximating the shift full fine-tuning would induce.
- Under the linear ==flow-matching== path this correction becomes an additive ==velocity residual==, gamma times task-proxy minus reference-proxy velocity, applied at every denoising step of the frozen base sampler.
- ==Two-stage proxy training==: on-policy ==velocity distillation== of the reference from base rollout trajectories, then task-proxy initialization from that reference plus flow-matching fine-tuning, so shared approximation error cancels.

## Results

- Across 8 real-world and 4 simulation tasks PPS gains **53%** absolute success over the frozen base, with zero-to-one gains on 4 real tasks; applied to an older base it lifts **5%** to **55%**.
- PPS beats LoRA at equal trainable-parameter budget, trains **5x** faster (**2.9h** vs **16.3h** on one A6000), adds **6 ms** latency, and loses only **7.8** points under mid-rollout disturbance versus LoRA's **15.8**.
- Ablations drop success from **64%** to **55%** without the reference proxy, **50%** without trajectory-level velocity distillation, and **48%** without initializing the task proxy from the reference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proxy tuning from language models transfers to continuous control: a matched expert/anti-expert pair steers a frozen generative policy without weight access or gradients.
> - Guidance pairs must share architecture, initialization and approximation error; differencing against the base itself leaks proxy-specific bias into the residual.
> - Steering preserves complementary strengths, cutting wrong-mode failures versus the base and out-of-distribution failures versus a specialist, but it depends on the base being strong.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This research addresses a critical challenge in the deployment of generalist robot policies, particularly Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which are trained on large-scale datasets to exhibit broad manipulation capabilities. The core problem is the specialization of these generalist policies to new, specific downstream tasks using limited task-specific demonstrations, without compromising their pre-trained broad capabilities. The deployment distribution for a new task rarely perfectly aligns with the pretraining data, necessitating adaptation.

Existing adaptation methods generally fall into two categories:
- **Modifying base model parameters:** This includes full fine-tuning, low-rank adaptation (LoRA), parameter merging, and reinforcement learning-based fine-tuning. While effective with abundant data, these methods tend to degrade the base model's broad capabilities (e.g., recovery from failures) when demonstrations are limited, as the fine-tuning process can overwrite crucial manipulation priors. Furthermore, fine-tuning large VLA models is computationally and memory-intensive and often requires direct access to model weights, precluding adaptation of closed-source or API-only base policies.
- **Steering the sampling process:** These methods keep the base model parameters frozen and instead adjust the action sampling. Examples include DSRL (optimizing initial noise with RL), residual policy learning, candidate selection, RFS (combining noise optimization with a post-hoc residual), and DynaGuide (guiding trajectory with a learned dynamics model). A limitation of these approaches is that they typically reweight behaviors the base already produces or guide within its existing distribution. They struggle to elicit task-specific behaviors that the base policy assigns negligible probability to, meaning they cannot steer towards actions outside the base's intrinsic modes.

Proxy Policy Steering (PPS) is positioned as a method that bridges the gap between these two paradigms. It aims to combine the ability to introduce new task-specific behaviors with the preservation of the base model's general priors. The paper draws inspiration from proxy tuning principles developed for language models, where expert and anti-expert models steer a frozen base during decoding. It extends this concept to continuous-control, flow-matching VLA models by demonstrating that under certain conditions, a density-ratio correction translates to an additive velocity-space residual in the flow-matching framework, enabling more direct and effective steering of the denoising trajectory. This places PPS within the broader context of score-space steering for generative models, such as diffusion and energy-based samplers, drawing parallels with classifier-free guidance and compositional methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an inference-time adaptation method for frozen flow-matching generalist robot policies that enables specialization to novel tasks with limited demonstrations, while crucially preserving the broad capabilities embedded in the pre-trained base model.

The motivation for this objective stems from a "fundamental tension" identified in the deployment of advanced VLA models:
- **Need for task-specific behavior:** Generalist policies, despite their broad competence, often require tailoring to specific downstream tasks whose conditions (e.g., object types, scene configurations, precise interaction nuances) may differ from their pre-training data. This adaptation typically relies on only a small number of task-specific demonstrations due to the cost of data collection.
- **Preservation of broad capabilities:** The generalist policies embody extensive manipulation priors, including robust grasping, contact-rich interaction, and recovery from failure states. Modifying the base model's parameters, even with limited adaptations like LoRA, can degrade these valuable capabilities, especially in low-data regimes. This degradation can lead to a loss of robustness and an inability to recover from unexpected situations not covered by the narrow demonstration set.

Specific motivations and objectives include:
- **Eliciting novel behaviors:** Addressing the limitation of prior steering methods that primarily reweight existing base behaviors. The goal is to steer the base towards behaviors it might otherwise assign negligible probability to, effectively reaching new, task-specific action modes.
- **Maintaining robustness:** Ensuring that behaviors like recovery from failure, which are often not exercised in task-specific demonstrations, remain available and effective throughout deployment.
- **Computational and memory efficiency:** Developing an adaptation method suitable for VLA models that are typically very large, making full fine-tuning impractical in many scenarios.
- **Accessibility:** Designing a method that operates without requiring direct access to the base model's parameters or gradients, making it compatible with closed-source or API-only generalist policies.
- **Principled adaptation:** Seeking a method where the task-induced change is clearly isolated and applied, rather than an arbitrary difference between independently trained models, to ensure effective and calibrated steering.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Proxy Policy Steering (PPS) is an inference-time adaptation method designed for frozen flow-matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its core insight is to estimate the directional shift in the base policy's action distribution induced by task supervision and apply this shift to the base sampler without directly modifying the base model's weights.

#### 3.1. Adapting Generalist Policies by Proxy (Conceptual Formulation)

PPS frames adaptation as a relative density correction to the base policy, inspired by product-of-experts (PoE) models. The adapted policy $\tilde{\pi}(a | o, l)$ is defined as proportional to the base policy $\pi_{\text{base}}(a | o, l)$ multiplied by a relative task correction term, raised to a steering strength $\gamma$:

$\tilde{\pi}(a | o, l) \propto \pi_{\text{base}}(a | o, l) \left( \frac{\pi_{\text{task}}(a | o, l)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(a | o, l)} \right)^\gamma$

- $\pi_{\text{base}}$: The frozen generalist robot policy.
- $\pi_{\text{ref}}$: A "reference proxy" representing the behavior of the base policy on target-task observations *before* task supervision.
- $\pi_{\text{task}}$: A "task proxy" representing how the base policy's behavior *would change* under task supervision, learned from demonstrations.
- $\gamma \geq 0$: A scalar controlling the strength of the correction.

This formulation approximates the effect of fine-tuning by interpolating between the frozen base policy and a task-adapted policy. When $\gamma = 1$, the base and reference terms cancel if $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ perfectly matches $\pi_{\text{base}}$, making the adapted policy follow the task policy. When $\gamma = 0$, the adapted policy reverts to the base policy. PPS approximates this relative correction using lightweight proxy policies because direct fine-tuning of a full VLA is often infeasible or undesirable.

#### 3.2. Flow-Matching Proxy Guidance (Velocity-Space Implementation)

For flow-matching VLAs, which use a velocity field to transform Gaussian noise into clean actions, PPS implements the density-ratio correction in the velocity space. The final guided velocity field, $v_{\text{PPS}}$, is constructed by adding a velocity-space residual to the base policy's velocity field:

$v_{\text{PPS}}(x, k, o, l) = v_{\text{base}}(x, k, o, l) + \gamma [v_{\text{task}}(x, k, o) - v_{\text{ref}}(x, k, o)]$

- $v_{\text{base}}$: The velocity field predicted by the frozen base policy.
- $v_{\text{task}}$: The velocity field predicted by the task proxy.
- $v_{\text{ref}}$: The velocity field predicted by the reference proxy.
- $x$: An intermediate action state along the flow path.
- $k$: The noise level (from 1 for pure noise to 0 for clean action).
- $o$: Observation.
- $l$: Language instruction (proxies omit `l` assuming single-task).

The term $v_{\text{task}}(x, k, o) - v_{\text{ref}}(x, k, o)$ captures the change in generation dynamics induced by task supervision beyond what the base already explains. This velocity residual is not an ad hoc choice; the paper derives that, under assumptions of a shared linear flow-matching schedule and per-level correction, the density-ratio composition is equivalent to this additive velocity-space form.

At inference (Algorithm 2), PPS starts with noisy action $x_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and iteratively integrates the guided velocity field $v_{\text{PPS}}$ from $k=1$ to $k=0$ to obtain the final action. If $v_{\text{task}}$ and $v_{\text{ref}}$ agree, the residual cancels, and the frozen base policy retains control, preserving its priors. The method allows proxies to condition on modalities not used by the base (e.g., point clouds for an RGB-only base) as long as the action representation and flow schedule are shared.

#### 3.3. Learning Proxy Policies (Two-Stage Training - Algorithm 1)

The effectiveness of the velocity residual depends on its ability to isolate the task-induced change. PPS achieves this through a specific two-stage proxy training process:

1. **On-policy reference distillation:**
 - The reference proxy $v_{\text{ref}}$ is trained to mimic the frozen base model on observations ($o, l$) sampled from the target task distribution ($D_T$).
 - This involves rolling out the frozen base sampler from noise and collecting intermediate action states $x_k$ along its denoising trajectory.
 - $v_{\text{ref}}$ is then trained via velocity distillation using a mean squared error objective: $L_{\text{ref}} = E_{(o,l)\sim D_T, \epsilon\sim N(0,I)} \sum_{i=1}^K \|v_{\text{ref}}(x_{k_i}, k_i, o) - v_{\text{base}}(x_{k_i}, k_i, o, l)\|^2$.
 - This ensures the reference proxy accurately matches the base's behavior in the relevant regions of the action space where guidance will be applied.

2. **Task specialization:**
 - The task proxy $v_{\text{task}}$ is initialized from the *trained reference proxy* $v_{\text{ref}}$.
 - It is then fine-tuned on the task demonstrations $(o, a) \sim D_T$ using the standard flow-matching objective: $L_{\text{task}} = E_{(o,a)\sim D_T, \epsilon\sim N(0,I), k\sim U} \|v_{\text{task}}(x_k, k, o) - (\epsilon - a)\|^2$, where $x_k = k\epsilon + (1-k)a$.
 - This initialization ensures that both proxies share the same architecture, initial weights, and thus similar approximation errors. Their difference ($v_{\text{task}} - v_{\text{ref}}$) then cleanly isolates the task-induced change, rather than incorporating extraneous differences arising from independent training or architectural discrepancies. This is crucial as small proxies trained on limited data have inherent approximation errors that could otherwise overshadow the task-specific signal.

### 4. Implementation Details

- Each proxy policy uses a lightweight architecture (32M parameters, ~1% of base model size) consisting of a DINOv3 ViT-S/16 visual encoder and a small Gemma transformer action expert.
- Both reference and task proxies are trained for 20,000 steps with a batch size of 64.
- The default steering strength $\gamma$ is fixed at 0.4 without per-task tuning.
- Policies run at 15 Hz with 10 Euler denoising steps and a 15-step action horizon, executing the first 8 steps before replanning.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Proxy Policy Steering (PPS) was conducted across 8 real-world and 4 simulation manipulation tasks, encompassing both in-distribution and out-of-distribution scenarios for the base VLA.

- **Performance on Downstream Tasks:**
 - PPS significantly improved the success rate of the state-of-the-art π$_{0.5}$ base policy, achieving an average absolute gain of 53% across all 12 tasks.
 - It demonstrated "zero-to-one" gains on four real-world tasks (Coffee brewing, Jeans folding, Flower arrangement, Drawer manipulation) where the base policy alone never succeeded.
 - PPS consistently outperformed various baselines: LoRA fine-tuning, Specialist (from-scratch policy), Residual policies, and DSRL (offline Noise-Aliased variant). Specifically, DSRL showed improvements only where the base had non-zero success, while PPS enabled success even from a zero-success baseline. Specialist, despite using the same lightweight architecture as the proxies, performed poorly, indicating the importance of leveraging the base VLA's broad priors.

- **Preservation of General Manipulation Priors and Recovery Behavior:**
 - A key finding is PPS's ability to preserve the base VLA's broad capabilities, including recovery from failure, even though these recovery behaviors were not present in the limited demonstrations. For instance, on the Jeans folding task, PPS demonstrated recovery from human-induced unfolding and dragging perturbations, while LoRA failed due to overfitting to the demonstrated trajectories.
 - Quantitatively, under mid-rollout disturbances, PPS experienced a success rate drop of only 7.8 percentage points, compared to a 15.8-point drop for LoRA and a 26.1-point drop when the reference proxy was omitted (PPS w/o ref), indicating superior robustness.

- **Efficiency and Resource Utilization:**
 - **Training Efficiency:** The complete PPS training pipeline (distillation and fine-tuning of both proxies) was approximately 5 times faster than LoRA fine-tuning (2.9 hours vs. 16.3 hours on a single A6000 GPU). This is attributed to not backpropagating through the large base model.
 - **Inference Latency:** PPS added only 6 ms of inference latency per action chunk and required 0.13 GB of additional GPU memory, as the two lightweight proxies run in parallel with the frozen base policy on a single GPU.
 - **Parameter Efficiency:** PPS achieved its performance with 32 million trainable parameters (approximately 1% of the base model size), demonstrating superior parameter efficiency compared to LoRA, which required 8 times more parameters to reach comparable success rates.

- **Generalizability Across Base Policies:**
 - The PPS method was successfully applied to another VLA family, π$_0$, elevating its average real-world success rate from 5% to 55%. This included achieving zero-to-one gains on all six long-horizon, out-of-distribution tasks for π$_0$. This suggests that the velocity-space residual mechanism is a general property of flow-matching steering rather than being specific to π$_{0.5}$.

- **Multimodal Steering Capabilities:**
 - PPS demonstrated the ability to inject new sensing modalities (e.g., point clouds, audio spectrograms) into an RGB-only base policy. On tasks like "Marble" (using point clouds) and "Phone" (using sound), PPS outperformed all baselines, including those explicitly designed to handle multiple modalities (Specialist, DSRL, LoRA-MM).

- **Analysis of Steering Strength (γ):**
 - A sweep of the steering strength parameter $\gamma$ on simulated tasks showed an optimal range between 0.4 and 0.6, balancing the base's general priors with the task-specific modes amplified by the proxy ratio. The default value of $\gamma = 0.4$ was effective without per-task tuning.

- **Ablation Studies on Proxy Training Recipe:**
 - The ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the specific two-stage proxy training process.
 - **"w/o ref" (replacing $v_{\text{ref}}$ with $v_{\text{base}}$):** Reduced success rate from 64% to 55%. This confirmed that directly using the base model instead of a distilled reference proxy breaks the shared approximation error property, mixing task signal with proxy-specific bias.
 - **"w/o vel" (supervising $v_{\text{ref}}$ on clean actions only):** Reduced success rate to 50%. This violated the condition that the reference proxy matches the base's velocity along the entire denoising trajectory, leading to a residual that did not accurately cancel leftover base behavior.
 - **"w/o tune" (training $v_{\text{task}}$ from scratch):** Reduced success rate to 48%. This showed that initializing the task proxy from the reference is critical for isolating the task-induced change, as independent training introduces differences due to random initialization and optimization trajectories.
 - Velocity alignment analysis further supported these findings, showing that the task-reference residual tracks genuine fine-tuning shifts more accurately than a task-base residual.

- **Failure Analysis:**
 - Compared to the frozen base π$_{0.5}$, PPS reduced "incorrect mode" failures by 69%, indicating its effectiveness in guiding the policy towards task-specific actions.
 - Compared to the Specialist policy, PPS reduced "out-of-distribution (OOD) states" failures by 44%, highlighting the base prior's role in maintaining robustness in less common state regions.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

Proxy Policy Steering (PPS) presents a significant advancement in the deployment and adaptation of generalist robot policies, particularly large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing the Deployment Bottleneck:** PPS offers a practical and effective solution to the challenge of specializing powerful generalist robot policies to novel tasks with limited data. This directly tackles a major bottleneck in transitioning laboratory-trained VLA models to real-world applications where task-specific demonstrations are costly to acquire.
- **Preservation of Foundational Capabilities:** Unlike traditional fine-tuning methods that risk overwriting valuable pre-trained manipulation priors, PPS explicitly preserves the broad capabilities of the frozen base model. This includes essential behaviors like robust grasping, contact-rich interaction, and, critically, recovery from failure states. Maintaining these capabilities makes robot deployment more robust and reliable in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments.
- **Enhanced Accessibility and Efficiency:** The method's ability to adapt large VLA models without modifying their parameters or requiring access to their internal gradients makes it applicable to closed-source or API-only policies. This significantly broadens the scope of VLA deployment. Furthermore, the substantial reductions in training time and the minimal inference overhead associated with PPS contribute to more efficient development cycles and lower operational costs for robotic systems.
- **Generalizable Framework for Generative Policies:** By demonstrating a principled and calibrated analog of proxy-based inference-time adaptation for continuous-action generative policies (specifically flow-matching VLAs), PPS opens a new general path for specializing large models. This extends a technique previously primarily applied to discrete-token language models, suggesting broader applicability across different generative AI architectures.
- **Facilitating Multimodal Integration:** PPS's design allows for the flexible injection of new sensing modalities (e.g., point clouds, audio) into existing base models without requiring their redesign or re-training. This capability enables robots to leverage richer perceptual information to perform tasks that are challenging with the base model's original modalities, thereby enhancing the robot's perception and task performance.
- **Contribution to Robust AI and Robotics:** The method's improved robustness to out-of-distribution states and its ability to recover from unexpected perturbations contribute to the development of more resilient and intelligent autonomous systems. This advancement moves the field closer to general-purpose robots that can learn new skills quickly and operate reliably in complex, open-world settings.

In conclusion, PPS contributes a method that enhances the adaptability, robustness, and accessibility of large generalist robot policies, thereby accelerating their practical deployment and fostering the development of more capable robotic agents.
