# Provably Safe Deep Reinforcement Learning for Robotic Manipulation in Human Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> A deep reinforcement learning framework developed at the Technical University of Munich for robotic manipulators achieves provable human safety in dynamic environments. This system, integrating a high-frequency safety shield, demonstrated a 0% safety-critical collision rate in simulated human-robot interaction tasks while enabling robust learning of complex evasion behaviors.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning (RL) for robotic manipulation lacks formal safety guarantees, hindering its deployment in environments shared with humans.
- The unpredictable and high-degree-of-freedom nature of human movement complicates safety assurance for RL-based manipulators.
- Existing safe RL methods are often limited to simplified or discrete environments, provide only probabilistic safety, or are not directly applicable to continuous, dynamic human-robot interaction.

## Method

- The system integrates a low-frequency ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== reinforcement learning agent with a high-frequency (**250 Hz**) ==safety shield== for ==provably safe trajectory planning==.
- The safety shield continuously plans and verifies both ==intended and failsafe trajectories==, enforcing ==ISO-verified Speed and Separation Monitoring (SSM)== by ensuring the robot can come to a complete stop before any potential human collision.
- If an RL-generated action leads to an unsafe intended trajectory, the shield overrides it with a pre-computed ==failsafe trajectory== that guides the robot to a guaranteed safe, stopped state.

## Results

- The safe agent, equipped with the proposed safety shield, achieved a **0%** safety-critical collision rate in both randomized-goal and human-evasion simulated experiments.
- The baseline RL agent, without the safety shield, experienced safety-critical collision rates of approximately **20%** in the randomized-goal experiment and up to **25%** in the human-evasion scenario.
- In the human-evasion experiment, the safe agent successfully learned to maneuver around humans to reach its goals, demonstrating significantly higher success rates compared to the baseline agent's limited learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Provable human safety in continuous, high-dimensional state spaces with dynamic human motion is achievable by combining an online RL agent with a formally verified, high-frequency shielding mechanism.
> - Implementing a safety shield that prevents episode-ending collisions during training significantly benefits the RL agent's learning process, enabling more effective exploration and the acquisition of complex human-evasion strategies.
> - An inductive safety concept, where intended trajectories are verified against human reachable sets and backed by a guaranteed failsafe trajectory, ensures continuous safety assurances in dynamic environments.
