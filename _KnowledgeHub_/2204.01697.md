# MaxViT: Multi-Axis Vision Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> MaxViT, a hierarchical vision transformer, introduces a Multi-Axis Self-Attention module that efficiently combines blocked local and dilated global spatial interactions with linear computational complexity. It achieves 85.17% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K and demonstrates superior efficiency across classification, object detection, segmentation, and generative tasks.

## Problem

- Standard Vision Transformers incur quadratic computational complexity for self-attention, making them inefficient for high-resolution images and early network stages.
- Current vision transformer designs struggle to effectively and efficiently integrate both fine-grained local features and long-range global dependencies throughout the network.
- Many Vision Transformers lack strong inherent inductive biases for vision, often requiring extensive pre-training data to generalize well and avoid overfitting on smaller datasets.

## Method

- A ==Multi-Axis Self-Attention (Max-SA)== module is proposed, which decomposes self-attention into sequential ==Blocked Local Attention== and ==Dilated Global Attention== for linear complexity and broad receptive fields.
- Max-SA modules are integrated with efficient ==MBConv (MobileNetV2 Inverted Residual Block)== layers within a simple, hierarchical network architecture to combine convolutional inductive biases with global reasoning.
- The overall MaxViT architecture follows a hierarchical design, progressively reducing spatial resolution and increasing channel dimensions, using ==relative self-attention== for enhanced performance on 2D vision tasks.

## Results

- MaxViT achieved state-of-the-art **85.17%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K (224x224 input) and **86.7%** with 512x512 fine-tuning, outperforming previous models while maintaining superior efficiency.
- Demonstrated enhanced parameter and computational efficiency, achieving higher accuracy with notably fewer parameters and FLOPs compared to other leading vision transformers on benchmarks like ImageNet-21K and COCO.
- Exhibited broad versatility and strong performance across diverse downstream tasks including object detection (COCO), instance segmentation, image aesthetic assessment (AVA), and unconditional image generation (ImageNet-1K GANs).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sequential application of blocked local and dilated global attention effectively captures both fine-grained local details and sparse global context across all network stages with linear computational cost.
> - Hybrid architectures that blend efficient convolutions (MBConv) with multi-axis attention provide a robust approach to combine beneficial inductive biases with global reasoning capabilities.
> - The optimal ordering of architectural components within a block varies for different task types, with MBConv followed by local then global attention being beneficial for discriminative tasks, and an inverse order for generative tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "MaxViT: Multi-Axis Vision Transformer" emerges at a critical juncture in computer vision, where the dominance of Convolutional Neural Networks (ConvNets) is being challenged and complemented by Transformer architectures.

Historically, **ConvNets** (e.g., AlexNet, ResNet, EfficientNet, ConvNeXt) have been the backbone of most computer vision tasks due to their inherent inductive biases: locality (processing local regions through convolutions) and translation equivariance (patterns learned in one part of an image are recognized elsewhere). These properties make ConvNets efficient and effective for capturing hierarchical local features. Recent advancements, as seen in ConvNeXt, have shown that modernizing ConvNet designs can still yield highly competitive performance.

The landscape shifted significantly with the advent of **Transformers** in Natural Language Processing (NLP) (e.g., Vaswani et al.'s "Attention Is All You Need," BERT). Their ability to model long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms led to unprecedented success. This inspired the **Vision Transformer (ViT)**, which applied Transformers directly to image patches. While ViT demonstrated impressive performance when pre-trained on massive datasets (like JFT-300M), it exhibited certain limitations:
- **Quadratic Complexity:** Standard self-attention scales quadratically with the number of tokens, making it computationally expensive for high-resolution images, especially in early stages of a network.
- **Lack of Inductive Bias:** Unlike ConvNets, ViT lacks strong built-in inductive biases for vision, leading to a need for extensive pre-training data to generalize well and avoid overfitting on smaller datasets.

These limitations spurred a wave of research into **efficient and specialized Vision Transformers**:
- **Hybrid Models:** Efforts like CoAtNet and CvT combined convolutions (for local feature extraction and inductive biases) with Transformers (for global reasoning) to leverage the strengths of both. MaxViT falls into this hybrid category, but with a more integrated approach.
- **Efficient Attention Mechanisms:** To tackle the quadratic complexity, researchers developed various sparse attention schemes. The **Swin Transformer** became a prominent example by applying self-attention within local, non-overlapping windows and introducing a "shifted window" mechanism to enable cross-window connections. While effective, it still has limitations regarding true global receptive fields. Other approaches include axial attention (attending along one axis then another) and various forms of sparse or local attention.
- **Hierarchical Designs:** Many modern vision transformers adopted hierarchical architectures akin to ConvNets (e.g., Swin Transformer, Pyramidal ViTs) to process images at multiple scales, generating multi-scale feature maps crucial for dense prediction tasks like object detection and segmentation.

MaxViT positions itself as a significant advancement in this evolving landscape by proposing a novel, efficient multi-axis attention mechanism that effectively captures *both* local and global spatial interactions with only linear computational complexity. Unlike Swin, MaxViT explicitly designs a "dilated global attention" component that can "see" globally even in early, high-resolution stages without complex operations like shifted windows or masking. By integrating this attention with efficient MBConv (convolutional) blocks in a simple, hierarchical design, MaxViT aims to unify the best properties of ConvNets and Transformers, addressing the shortcomings of previous approaches and offering a highly scalable and universal vision backbone. Its explicit design for efficient global interaction at all stages is a key differentiator from prior works that might achieve global context only implicitly or at later, lower-resolution stages.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of MaxViT stem from the inherent challenges and limitations of existing Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures, particularly concerning scalability, efficiency, and the balance between local and global feature extraction.

1. **Addressing the Scalability and Computational Cost of Self-Attention:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard self-attention in ViT has a quadratic computational complexity with respect to image size (O(N^2), where N is the number of pixels/tokens). This makes it computationally prohibitive for high-resolution images, especially in the early stages of deep hierarchical networks where feature maps are large.
 - **Objective:** To design an attention mechanism that is efficient and scalable, capable of handling arbitrary input resolutions with only linear computational complexity (O(N)).

2. **Enabling Effective Global and Local Spatial Interactions Throughout the Network:**
 - **Motivation:** While ConvNets excel at local feature extraction due to their inductive biases, they often struggle with long-range dependencies. Full self-attention offers global interactions but is too costly. Existing sparse attention mechanisms (like window-based attention in Swin Transformer) reintroduce locality, but often at the cost of limited global receptive fields, potentially underfitting on very large datasets. The crucial challenge is how to "efficiently incorporate global and local interactions to balance the model capacity and generalizability under a computation budget."
 - **Objective:** To create a novel attention module that can perform *both* local and sparse global spatial interactions within a single block, ensuring that the model has access to global context even in earlier, high-resolution stages of the network.

3. **Developing a Simple, Universal, and High-Performance Vision Backbone:**
 - **Motivation:** Many existing Vision Transformer designs can be complex, requiring specific architectural modifications or training strategies. The authors aim for simplicity and generalizability. Furthermore, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across a diverse range of vision tasks would validate the universality of the proposed building block.
 - **Objective:** To design a simple, hierarchical vision backbone, dubbed MaxViT, by effectively blending the proposed multi-axis attention model with convolutions (MBConv blocks). This backbone should achieve state-of-the-art performance across various tasks (classification, detection, segmentation, aesthetic assessment, generation) and data regimes, showcasing superior scaling properties in terms of accuracy vs. computation and parameters.

In essence, the authors are motivated to bridge the gap between the local efficiency of ConvNets and the global reasoning power of Transformers, without inheriting the quadratic complexity of full attention or the limited global scope of purely local attention. They aim to achieve this through a modular, efficient, and universally applicable architectural design.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of MaxViT's methodology lies in its novel **Multi-Axis Self-Attention (Max-SA)** module and its integration into a hierarchical network architecture.

#### 3.1. Multi-Axis Self-Attention (Max-SA)

The Max-SA module addresses the quadratic complexity of standard self-attention by decomposing it into two sparse forms, ensuring linear complexity while maintaining both local and global receptive fields:

1. **Blocked Local Attention (Block-SA):**
 - **Concept:** Similar to the window-based attention in Swin Transformer, this mechanism partitions the input feature map (X ∈ R^(H×W×C)) into non-overlapping local blocks, each of a fixed size (P×P, e.g., 7×7). Self-attention is then applied independently within each of these local blocks.
 - **Functionality:** Captures fine-grained local spatial interactions effectively.
 - **Complexity:** Linear with respect to image size, as attention is computed only within small, fixed-size windows.

2. **Dilated Global Attention (Grid-SA):**
 - **Concept:** This is a key innovation. Instead of fixed-size windows, the feature map is divided into a fixed G×G grid (e.g., 7×7 uniform grid). This results in "windows" that are adaptively sized (H/G × W/G). Self-attention is then applied *between* the elements of this G×G grid, effectively mixing tokens that are spatially far apart but fall into the same "grid cell" when viewed from a coarser perspective.
 - **Functionality:** Provides a sparse, dilated global receptive field across the entire image with linear complexity. This allows the model to "see globally" without the heavy computation of full attention or the complexities of shifted windows.
 - **Implementation:** The authors highlight that both Block-SA and Grid-SA can be implemented easily without modifying the standard attention operation, simply by rearranging tensor dimensions using tools like `einops`. This makes the design conceptually cleaner and more flexible.
 - **Comparison to Axial Attention:** The authors explicitly distinguish Max-SA from axial attention, noting that axial attention applies attention column-wise then row-wise (O(N*sqrt(N)) complexity), whereas Max-SA uses local then sparse global attention (O(N) complexity) and explicitly accounts for the 2D structure of images.

#### 3.2. MaxViT Block Design

The MaxViT block is the fundamental building unit of the network, sequentially stacking three key components:

1. **MBConv Block (MobileNetV2 Inverted Residual Block):**
 - **Purpose:** Precedes the attention layers. MBConv blocks (with depthwise convolutions, expansion/shrinkage layers, and Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) modules) are highly efficient and effective for local feature extraction.
 - **Benefits:**
 - **Efficiency:** Contributes to the overall computational efficiency of the model.
 - **Inductive Bias:** Provides convolutional inductive biases (locality, translation equivariance), which are beneficial for vision tasks and improve model generalization and trainability.
 - **Implicit Positional Encoding:** Depthwise convolutions can act as a form of Conditional Positional Encoding (CPE), making explicit positional encoding layers unnecessary.

2. **Blocked Local Attention (Block-SA):** As described above, for local interactions.

3. **Dilated Global Attention (Grid-SA):** As described above, for sparse global interactions.

- **Standard Transformer Components:** Each of these components is typically followed by Layer Normalization (LN), Feedforward Networks (FFNs), and skip-connections, consistent with standard Transformer designs.
- **Relative Self-Attention:** MaxViT utilizes a pre-normalized relative self-attention mechanism (inspired by CoAtNet) for its attention operations. This incorporates learned relative positional biases, enhancing translation equivariance and overall performance on 2D vision tasks compared to vanilla self-attention.

#### 3.3. MaxViT Architecture Variants

The overall MaxViT architecture follows a simple, hierarchical design akin to classical ConvNets (e.g., ResNet):
- **Stem (S0):** Initial downsampling using 3x3 convolution layers.
- **Body (S1-S4):** Four stages, each progressively reducing spatial resolution by half and typically doubling the number of channels (hidden dimension).
- **Repeatable Blocks:** Each stage consists of repeated MaxViT blocks. Downsampling between stages is handled within the Depthwise Conv3x3 layer of the first MBConv block of each subsequent stage.
- **Scaling:** Model variants (MaxViT-T/S/B/L/XL) are created by scaling the number of blocks per stage and the channel dimensions, allowing for a range of model sizes and capacities.
- **Classification Head:** Global average pooling is applied to the output of the final stage (S4), followed by a fully connected layer for classification, rather than using a `[CLS]` token.

#### 3.4. Ablation Studies

The authors conducted extensive ablation studies to validate key design choices:
- **Global Grid-Attention:** Confirmed its crucial role, especially in earlier stages, for boosting performance over purely local attention or convolutions.
- **MBConv Layer:** Demonstrated the significant performance boost provided by integrating MBConv blocks.
- **Block Order:** Found that MBConv -> Block-SA -> Grid-SA was optimal for discriminative tasks (classification), while an inverse order (GA -> BA -> C) was better for generative tasks, suggesting different optimal information flow for different goals.
- **Sequential vs. Parallel Max-SA:** Showed that sequentially stacking the local and global attention components significantly outperformed parallel designs, indicating better fusion of local and global cues.
- **Vertical Layout:** Confirmed that their chosen distribution of blocks across stages scaled more effectively for larger models compared to layouts borrowed from Swin Transformer.

This comprehensive methodological approach, from the innovative attention mechanism to the overall architectural design and rigorous ablation, underscores MaxViT's commitment to efficiency, scalability, and universality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MaxViT demonstrates state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across a broad spectrum of vision tasks, showcasing its effectiveness, efficiency, and scalability.

#### 4.1. Image Classification on ImageNet-1K

- **SOTA Performance:** MaxViT achieves new SOTA accuracy on ImageNet-1K without extra data:
 - MaxViT-L (224×224 input) attains 85.17% top-1 accuracy, surpassing CoAtNet-3 by 0.67%.
 - MaxViT-L (512×512 fine-tuned input) achieves 86.7% top-1 accuracy, setting a new SOTA under normal training conditions.
- **Efficiency:** MaxViT models exhibit superior accuracy vs. FLOPs and accuracy vs. parameters trade-offs compared to previous SOTA vision Transformers (e.g., CoAtNet, Swin, ConvNeXt, EfficientNetV2). For instance, MaxViT-S reaches 84.45% top-1 accuracy with comparable throughput to CSWin-B and CoAtNet-2, but with higher accuracy.

#### 4.2. Large-Scale Data Regimes (ImageNet-21K and JFT-300M Pre-training)

- **ImageNet-21K Pre-training:**
 - MaxViT-B achieves 88.38% accuracy (512×512 fine-tuned), outperforming CoAtNet-4 by 0.28% while using significantly fewer parameters (43%) and FLOPs (38%). This highlights MaxViT's superior parameter and computational efficiency.
 - MaxViT-XL achieves a new SOTA of 88.70% (512×512 fine-tuned).
- **JFT-300M Pre-training:**
 - MaxViT-XL attains 89.53% accuracy with 475M parameters, outperforming previous models of comparable sizes, demonstrating strong scalability to massive datasets.

#### 4.3. Object Detection and Instance Segmentation on COCO2017

- **Superior Performance:** Using MaxViT as a backbone (with Cascade Mask-RCNN and FPN), the models consistently outperform other strong backbones like Swin, ConvNeXt, and UViT across various model sizes in terms of mean Average Precision (AP).
- **Computational Efficiency:** MaxViT-S achieves better AP than other base-level models (e.g., Swin-B, UViT-B) with approximately 40% less computational cost (FLOPs), emphasizing its efficiency in downstream tasks. For example, MaxViT-S (107M Params, 595G FLOPs) achieved 53.1 AP, while Swin-B (145M Params, 982G FLOPs) achieved 51.9 AP.

#### 4.4. Image Aesthetic Assessment on AVA Benchmark

- **High Perceptual Correlation:** MaxViT-T (even the smallest variant) outperforms existing aesthetic assessment methods for similar input resolutions.
- **Resolution Scalability:** Performance (PLCC and SRCC) consistently improves as input resolution increases (from 224x224 to 512x512), benefiting from MaxViT's strong non-local capacity to capture both pixel-level details and semantic-level composition. MaxViT-T at 512x512 achieved 0.745 PLCC and 0.708 SRCC.

#### 4.5. Unconditional Image Generation on ImageNet-1K

- **Generative Capability:** MaxViT blocks, when adapted for a GAN generator, achieved better Inception Score (IS) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) (IS of 22.58, FID of 30.77) compared to the SOTA generative Transformer model HiT (IS of 21.64, FID of 30.83), doing so with significantly fewer parameters (18.6M vs. 32.9M). This demonstrates MaxViT's potential as a "universal vision module" for generative tasks.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study Insights

- **Grid Attention is Crucial:** Ablation studies confirmed that the dilated global grid attention is essential for MaxViT's performance, contributing significantly, especially in earlier stages.
- **MBConv Enhances Performance:** The integration of MBConv layers consistently boosts performance, reinforcing the benefits of hybrid architectures.
- **Optimal Block Order:** The sequential order of MBConv -> Block-SA -> Grid-SA proved optimal for discriminative tasks, while an inverse order (GA -> BA -> C) was found to be better for generative tasks, highlighting domain-specific architectural preferences.
- **Sequential Stacking Superior:** Sequentially combining local and global attention modules dramatically outperformed parallel designs, suggesting a more effective fusion of different spatial cues.
- **Efficient Scaling Layout:** The MaxViT's specific vertical layout (number of blocks per stage) demonstrated better scaling performance for larger models compared to the Swin Transformer's layout.

In summary, the findings robustly establish MaxViT as a leading vision backbone that delivers SOTA performance across diverse tasks and scales efficiently with both data size and model complexity, thanks to its innovative multi-axis attention mechanism and thoughtful hybrid design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MaxViT paper presents a significant contribution to the field of computer vision, with far-reaching implications for future architectural designs and applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Breaking the Global-Local Interaction Barrier with Linear Complexity:** MaxViT successfully addresses one of the most persistent challenges in vision Transformers: efficiently achieving both local detail perception and global context understanding across all network stages. By designing multi-axis attention (blocked local and dilated global attention) with linear computational complexity, the paper offers a practical solution that overcomes the quadratic scaling limitation of full self-attention. This is crucial for processing high-resolution images, video, and other large-scale visual data.

2. **Unification of Convolutions and Attention:** MaxViT provides a highly effective blueprint for hybrid architectures by seamlessly blending efficient MBConv layers with the novel multi-axis attention. This demonstrates that combining the inductive biases of convolutions (for local feature extraction and efficiency) with the global reasoning power of Transformers can yield superior performance and robustness, rather than viewing them as competing paradigms. This unification represents a new standard for hybrid vision backbone design.

3. **State-of-the-Art Performance Across Diverse Tasks:** The consistent achievement of SOTA results on a broad spectrum of vision tasks—including image classification (ImageNet-1K, ImageNet-21K, JFT-300M), object detection and instance segmentation (COCO), image aesthetic assessment (AVA), and unconditional image generation (ImageNet-1K)—underscores the versatility and power of the MaxViT block. This strong empirical validation positions MaxViT as a highly competitive and general-purpose backbone for various applications.

4. **Superior Efficiency and Scalability:** MaxViT demonstrates remarkable parameter and computational efficiency. It achieves higher accuracy with fewer parameters and FLOPs compared to many previous SOTA models (e.g., CoAtNet, Swin Transformer). This efficiency is critical for deploying high-performance models in resource-constrained environments or for scaling models to even larger datasets and tasks. The strong scaling curves for accuracy vs. FLOPs and parameters indicate its potential for continued growth and improvement.

5. **Simplicity and Modularity:** Despite its advanced capabilities, the MaxViT architecture maintains a high degree of simplicity, using a repeatable hierarchical structure composed of a basic MaxViT block. This modularity makes it easier for researchers and practitioners to understand, implement, and adapt the model for their specific needs, potentially accelerating further research and development. The ease of implementation using `einops` is also a practical benefit.

6. **Universal Vision Module Potential:** The success of MaxViT in both discriminative (classification, detection) and generative tasks suggests that its core MaxViT block could serve as a truly "universal vision module." This universality could lead to more unified frameworks for computer vision, reducing the need for highly specialized architectures for different problem types.

7. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens several promising avenues for future research:
 - **Extension to Other Modalities:** The multi-axis attention approach is not inherently limited to 2D images and could be extended to other higher-dimensional or multi-modal signals such as videos, 3D point clouds, and vision-language tasks, where efficient capture of long-range dependencies is equally critical.
 - **Automated Architectural Design:** The strong modular candidates and design space explored by MaxViT could inform and enhance automated architectural search (NAS) efforts.
 - **Understanding Optimal Information Flow:** The finding that different block orders (e.g., MBConv-BA-GA for discriminative vs. GA-BA-MBConv for generative) are optimal provides valuable insight into how different tasks benefit from varying sequences of local, global, and convolutional processing.

Finally, the authors conscientiously acknowledge the **societal impact** of large models, particularly regarding computational resource consumption (carbon emissions) and the potential for bias or misuse (e.g., generating misleading media). This responsible AI stance highlights an important aspect of contemporary deep learning research.

In conclusion, MaxViT represents a significant step forward in the design of efficient, scalable, and versatile vision backbones, likely to inspire and influence the next generation of architectures in computer vision.
