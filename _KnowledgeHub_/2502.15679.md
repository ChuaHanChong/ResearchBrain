# BOSS: Benchmark for Observation Space Shift in Long-Horizon Task

> [!summary] Summary
> A new benchmark called BOSS has been introduced to investigate Observation Space Shift (OSS) in visual-input long-horizon robotic tasks, revealing that current imitation learning methods suffer substantial performance degradation due to OSS and that data augmentation alone is insufficient to address it.

## Problem

- Hierarchical imitation learning approaches for long-horizon robotic tasks often fail during sequential skill execution due to discrepancies between training and deployment visual states.
- Pre-trained visuomotor policies struggle when the visual environment changes in ways 'irrelevant' to the task's symbolic state (e.g., an object appearing in a container not part of the skill's direct focus).
- Existing robot learning benchmarks and methodologies do not adequately define, quantify, or address the specific challenge posed by these skill-irrelevant visual shifts during skill transitions.

## Method

- The **BOSS** benchmark is introduced, built on the LIBERO simulator, to systematically define, quantify, and investigate ==Observation Space Shift (OSS)== in long-horizon visual-input robotic tasks.
- A Rule-based Automatic Modification Generator (RAMG) is developed to automatically introduce diverse, structured visual modifications to tasks without altering their symbolic pre-conditions or effects.
- BOSS comprises three progressively challenging scenarios: Single Predicate Shift, Accumulated Predicate Shift, and Skill Chaining, enabling a comprehensive evaluation of OSS impact.

## Results

- Single predicate shifts caused substantial average performance degradation for imitation learning baselines (e.g., up to **67%** Ratio Performance Delta), affecting a high percentage of tasks (up to **68%**).
- Accumulated predicate shifts exacerbated the negative impact, consistently leading to average performance drops exceeding **50%** across all baselines as the number of visual modifications increased.
- Observation Space Shift significantly degrades overall long-horizon task success in skill chaining scenarios, demonstrating a substantial gap between ideal and actual chain success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Observation Space Shift (OSS) is formally defined as visual changes in skill-irrelevant predicates that negatively impact visuomotor policies during sequential skill execution.
> - Data augmentation, even with extensive and diverse datasets, proved largely insufficient to mitigate OSS for existing imitation learning baselines, suggesting a need for algorithmic solutions.
> - The severity of Observation Space Shift (OSS) is directly correlated with the magnitude of visual modifications, but not with the inherent difficulty of the skill being executed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is striving to create versatile robots capable of autonomously performing complex, long-horizon tasks in unstructured environments. Imitation Learning (IL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for teaching robots new skills by demonstrating desired behaviors. However, a significant hurdle in IL, particularly for long-horizon tasks, is the pervasive issue of **covariate shift**, where discrepancies between training and deployment states accumulate over time, leading to performance degradation.

Hierarchical approaches, such as Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) and more recently, large language model (LLM)-based planning, offer a way to manage task complexity by decomposing long-horizon tasks into sequences of simpler, visuomotor skills. While these frameworks provide a high-level structure, the paper identifies a critical, often overlooked problem at the low-level skill execution: **Observation Space Shift (OSS)**. OSS occurs when the visual environment changes between the execution of sequential skills in a way that is "irrelevant" to the symbolic preconditions/effects of the skills but significantly impacts the visual input for downstream visuomotor policies.

Previous work on skill chaining has largely focused on state-based transitions or made strong assumptions (e.g., initial states are always reachable, continuous fine-tuning is feasible) that do not hold for visual-input tasks where arbitrary visual changes can occur. Existing robot learning benchmarks like Meta-World, RL-Bench, D4RL, RoboMimic, and ManiSkill2 have advanced the field in areas like multi-task learning, offline RL, and learning from demonstrations, but none are specifically designed to investigate the challenges posed by visual skill transitions in long-horizon tasks. More recent benchmarks for long-horizon tasks like CausalWorld, Calvin, FurnitureBench, RoboCasa, and LoHoRavens also do not explicitly address the problem of observation space shifts during skill chaining.

This paper positions BOSS as the first dedicated benchmark to formally define, quantify, and investigate OSS in visual-input long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. By building upon the customizable LIBERO simulator and introducing a systematic way to generate observation space shifts, BOSS fills a critical gap in the benchmarking landscape. It moves beyond just evaluating the success of a task to dissecting *why* failures occur at skill transitions in visual-rich environments, thereby pushing the boundaries for developing more robust and generalizable robotic policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to formally introduce and thoroughly investigate **Observation Space Shift (OSS)**, a problem identified as a major impediment to the success of visual-servoing robots in long-horizon tasks, particularly when using hierarchical imitation learning approaches.

The motivation stems from the limitations observed in current hierarchical robotic systems:
- **Sequential Execution Challenges:** While hierarchical methods plan high-level skill sequences, the sequential execution of pre-trained visuomotor skills often fails. This is because the terminal state of a preceding skill may visually alter the environment, presenting an "initial state" to the subsequent skill that was never seen during its individual training.
- **Irrelevant Visual Changes:** The core of OSS lies in changes to "skill-irrelevant predicates" within the visual observation space. For example, if a skill `PlaceObject(potato, bowl)` puts a potato into a bowl, and the next skill is `MoveContainer(bowl, cabinet)`, the presence of the potato (an "irrelevant" visual detail for `MoveContainer`) can disrupt the pre-trained `MoveContainer` policy, which might have only seen empty bowls during training.
- **Limitations of Prior Solutions:** Existing approaches to skill transition problems (e.g., learning transition policies, state alignment via fine-tuning) often abstract away visual inputs or make computationally prohibitive assumptions for real-world visual tasks. They fail to account for the frequent and varied visual shifts that occur naturally in complex, multi-skill environments.

To address these issues, the paper's key objectives are:
1. **Formulate OSS:** Provide a formal definition of Observation Space Shift within the framework of Task and Motion Planning, clearly delineating how changes in skill-irrelevant predicates in the visual observation space disrupt visuomotor policies.
2. **Validate and Quantify OSS Impact:** Develop a comprehensive benchmark, BOSS, to empirically demonstrate the existence and significant negative impact of OSS on the performance of modern imitation learning algorithms in long-horizon tasks.
3. **Facilitate Deeper Understanding:** Design progressively challenging scenarios within BOSS ("Single Predicate Shift," "Accumulated Predicate Shift," and "Skill Chaining") to assess different facets of OSS's negative effects and generate insights into its characteristics.
4. **Investigate Potential Solutions:** Evaluate a straightforward solution—data augmentation through increased visual diversity—to determine its efficacy in mitigating OSS, thereby guiding future research directions.
5. **Set Baselines:** Provide detailed analyses and baseline results for several popular imitation learning algorithms on the BOSS benchmark.

In essence, the paper aims to shine a spotlight on a critical, yet previously unaddressed, bottleneck in robot learning for complex, visually-driven tasks, providing the necessary tools and evidence to spur focused research in this area.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the paper's methodology revolves around the design and implementation of the BOSS benchmark, coupled with a systematic evaluation of various imitation learning baselines.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition: Observation Space Shift (OSS)

The paper formally defines OSS within the context of Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) and Task and Motion Planning (TAMP).
- **Predicates (Ψ):** Boolean properties or relationships in the state space (e.g., `In(potato, bowl)`).
- **Operators (op):** Skills defined by preconditions (`Pre`), effects (`Eff`), and cost.
- **Irrelevant Predicates:** A predicate `ψ_i` is irrelevant for an operator `op` if `ψ_i ∉ Pre ∪ Eff`.
- **OSS Occurrence:** OSS arises when changes in these *irrelevant* predicates within the visual observation space (O) hinder the performance of a visuomotor policy, even though these changes do not affect the symbolic feasibility or expected outcome of the skill.

#### 3.2. The BOSS Benchmark Environment

- **Foundation:** Built on the **LIBERO simulator**, chosen for its flexibility, diverse manipulation scenes, and PDDL-based task generation. LIBERO's PDDL support allows precise control over task definitions, including predicates and operators, which is crucial for systematically introducing and controlling OSS.
- **Robot & Observations:** Features a Franka Emika Panda robot arm. The observation space includes third-person camera views and proprioceptive states (joint angles, gripper states), crucial for visuomotor policies.

#### 3.3. Task Design and the Rule-based Automatic Modification Generator (RAMG)

- **Baseline Tasks:** A set of 44 single-skill tasks are selected from Libero-100 (excluding multi-skill tasks), serving as the "unaffected by OSS" baseline.
- **Modified Tasks:** To simulate OSS, modified counterparts of these tasks are generated. This is achieved using the novel **Rule-based Automatic Modification Generator (RAMG)**.
 - **RAMG Functionality:** Operates on PDDL files to introduce structured, rule-based visual modifications without altering the core symbolic feasibility of the task.
 - **Modification Types:**
 1. Repositioning existing objects or adding external objects.
 2. Changing the state of fixtures (e.g., toggling `DrawerOpen`).
 3. Modifying containment relations (e.g., `In(potato, bowl)`).
 - **Constraints:** RAMG ensures dynamic constraints (e.g., no interference with existing `Pre`/`Eff`, no collisions) and logical consistency are maintained.
 - **Scalability:** RAMG can generate a large number of modified tasks (e.g., 1,727 from 44 base tasks with single modifications), providing extensive test cases.

#### 3.4. The Three Challenges of BOSS

BOSS comprises three progressively difficult challenges:
- **BOSS-C1: Single Predicate Shift:** Evaluates robustness against a single observation modification caused by a preceding skill (e.g., moving `MoveContainer(bowl, cabinet)` with a potato in the bowl). Compares performance on the 44 original tasks vs. 44 single-modified tasks.
- **BOSS-C2: Accumulated Predicate Shift:** Assesses the cumulative negative effects of multiple modifications introduced by several preceding skills (e.g., `MoveContainer` with an open drawer AND a potato in the bowl). Uses RAMG to generate tasks with two and three accumulated modifications.
- **BOSS-C3: Skill Chaining:** Focuses on realistic long-horizon tasks (10 tasks total), each a three-skill chain. The robot is reset to a neutral position after each skill to isolate the effect of *visual* OSS from dynamic transition feasibility issues, focusing purely on visual perception challenges.

#### 3.5. Evaluated Baselines

Four popular imitation learning algorithms, representing diverse architectures, are evaluated:
- **Behavioral Cloning (BC) methods from Libero:**
 - **BC-RESNET-RNN:** ResNet for visual encoding, LSTM for temporal processing.
 - **BC-RESNET-T:** ResNet for visual encoding, Transformer decoder for temporal processing.
 - **BC-VIT-T:** Vision Transformer (ViT) for visual encoding, Transformer decoder for temporal processing.
 - All use BERT embeddings for language instructions and a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) output head for actions.
- **OpenVLA:** An open-sourced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, chosen as a representative generalist policy based on large foundation models.

#### 3.6. Metrics

- **Ratio Performance Delta (RPD):** For BOSS-C1 and BOSS-C2. Calculated as `(Original Success Rate - Modified Success Rate) / Original Success Rate`. A positive RPD indicates negative impact of OSS.
- **Delta to Upper Bound Ratio (DUBR):** For BOSS-C3. `(Actual Chain Success Rate - Chain Upper Bound) / Chain Upper Bound`. "Chain Upper Bound" is the product of individual skill success rates *without* OSS.

#### 3.7. Data Augmentation Experiment

- **Method:** Uses RAMG to generate a large, visually diverse dataset (1,727 new tasks, 57,000 demonstrations) by replaying original demonstrations in modified environments.
- **Evaluation:** Compares baseline performance on OSS-affected skills when trained on original data (Setup A) vs. augmented data (Setup B).

#### 3.8. Factors Influencing OSS (Appendix A)

- Investigates two hypotheses:
 1. **Skill Difficulty:** Is higher skill difficulty correlated with more severe OSS?
 2. **Magnitude of Visual Modifications:** Does larger visual change lead to more severe OSS?
- **Method:** Uses Spearman's rank correlation coefficient (ρ) to quantify monotonic relationships, by isolating variables across specific task sets and incremental modifications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the BOSS benchmark reveal significant insights into the nature and impact of Observation Space Shift (OSS).

#### 4.1. BOSS-C1: Single Predicate Shift

- **Prevalence of OSS:** A substantial percentage of tasks were negatively affected by OSS, even with just a single modification: 68% for BC-RESNET-RNN, 66% for BC-RESNET-T, 50% for BC-VIT-T, and 66% for OpenVLA. This demonstrates that OSS is a frequent problem.
- **Significant Performance Degradation:** For the tasks affected by OSS, the average Ratio Performance Delta (RPD) was considerable: 67% for BC-RESNET-RNN, 35% for BC-RESNET-T, 34% for BC-VIT-T, and 54% for OpenVLA. This indicates a severe drop in success rates for individually trained skills when confronted with single visual modifications.

#### 4.2. BOSS-C2: Accumulated Predicate Shift

- **Exacerbated Impact:** As the number of modifications accumulated (from one to two to three), the negative impact of OSS on task performance intensified. Both the average positive RPD (magnitude of performance drop) and the average occurrence ratio of OSS (frequency) showed an increasing trend across all baselines.
- **Severe Performance Decline:** With multiple modifications, the average RPD across all baselines consistently exceeded 50%, highlighting that accumulated OSS presents a critical barrier to reliable long-horizon task completion.

#### 4.3. BOSS-C3: Skill Chaining

- **Detrimental Effect on Long-Horizon Tasks:** The "Delta to Upper Bound Ratio" (DUBR) for the 10 three-skill chaining tasks was predominantly positive and high. This indicates a substantial gap between the ideal chain success rate (without OSS) and the actual success rate, directly demonstrating that OSS significantly degrades overall long-horizon task performance. Some baselines (e.g., BC-RESNET-RNN) even failed to achieve any success in the ideal case, further underscoring the challenge.

#### 4.4. Can Data Augmentation Mitigate OSS?

- **Limited Efficacy:** Training baselines on a significantly larger and more visually diverse dataset generated by RAMG (Setup B vs. Setup A) showed very limited improvement in mitigating OSS.
 - BC-RESNET-RNN showed a slight improvement (0.25 to 0.31 success rate on modified tasks).
 - However, BC-RESNET-T, BC-VIT-T, and OpenVLA performed either equivalently or worse (e.g., BC-RESNET-T dropped from 0.67 to 0.54, BC-VIT-T from 0.74 to 0.64).
- **Underlying Challenges:** The authors suggest that current baselines may struggle to generalize across such diverse visual distributions. Moreover, the combinatorial explosion of scene variations makes it impractical for data augmentation alone to cover all relevant cases, even with an automatic generator like RAMG. This key finding strongly implies that data augmentation is insufficient on its own and points to the need for algorithmic solutions.

#### 4.5. Factors Influencing OSS (Appendix A)

- **Visual Modification Magnitude Matters:** The analysis using Spearman's rank correlation showed a significant positive monotonic relationship between the magnitude of visual modifications (quantified by the number of incremental changes) and the severity of OSS (RPD) for all BC baselines (ρ = 1.0, p = 0.0). This confirms that larger visual changes lead to more severe OSS.
- **Skill Difficulty Does Not:** No significant correlation was found between skill difficulty (initial success rate) and the severity of OSS across baselines (p-values consistently high, ρ values varied and non-significant). This implies that even easy skills can be severely affected by OSS if the visual changes are substantial, making OSS a widespread problem independent of a skill's inherent complexity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of BOSS and the comprehensive analysis of Observation Space Shift (OSS) represent a significant contribution to the field of robotic learning, particularly for long-horizon tasks.

**Formalizing a Critical Problem:** For the first time, this work formally defines OSS as a distinct problem in visuomotor policy execution within hierarchical robotics. By clearly articulating how "skill-irrelevant" visual changes can derail pre-trained policies, the paper brings attention to a fundamental bottleneck that was previously implicitly grouped under broader issues like covariate shift or simply ignored due to the focus on symbolic planning.

**A Dedicated Benchmark:** BOSS provides a much-needed tool for the research community. Its structured challenges (Single Predicate Shift, Accumulated Predicate Shift, Skill Chaining) offer a systematic way to:
- **Validate OSS:** Empirically confirm its pervasive and detrimental impact on a range of modern imitation learning algorithms.
- **Quantify Impact:** Provide concrete metrics (RPD, DUBR) to measure performance degradation, enabling rigorous comparison of future solutions.
- **Accelerate Research:** Serve as a standardized platform for developing and testing new algorithms specifically designed to mitigate OSS, guiding development towards truly robust and generalizable robotic policies.

**Challenging Data-Centric Solutions:** The finding that extensive data augmentation alone is insufficient to resolve OSS is a crucial insight. It suggests that simply scaling up demonstration datasets might not be the panacea for all challenges in robotic learning, particularly when dealing with the combinatorial explosion of irrelevant visual variations. This finding pushes the community to look beyond mere data quantity and diversity towards more intelligent **algorithmic solutions**.

**Guiding Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly highlights the implications of its findings:
- **Need for Robust Algorithms:** Future research should focus on developing algorithms that are inherently robust to visual variations, perhaps by learning to discern task-relevant visual cues from irrelevant background noise. This could involve attention mechanisms, causal inference in visual perception, or state representation learning that disentangles manipulable objects from their context.
- **Beyond Data Augmentation:** While data is important, the limitations of current data augmentation strategies in addressing OSS underscore the necessity for architectural innovations in visuomotor policies.
- **Addressing the "Irrelevant" Problem:** The core challenge of OSS lies in its "irrelevant" nature. A policy might fail because it pays attention to a potato in a bowl, not because it needs to interact with the potato, but because its training set didn't include such a visual state for the `MoveContainer` skill. This calls for policies that can generalize over such irrelevant visual perturbations.

In summary, BOSS is not just another benchmark; it's a diagnostic tool that identifies a critical failure mode in current robotic learning systems. By providing a clear definition, empirical evidence, and a rigorous testing ground, this work is poised to significantly influence the direction of research in long-horizon robotic manipulation, pushing the field closer to developing genuinely capable and versatile robots.
