# BOSS: Benchmark for Observation Space Shift in Long-Horizon Task

> [!summary] Summary
> A new benchmark called BOSS has been introduced to investigate Observation Space Shift (OSS) in visual-input long-horizon robotic tasks, revealing that current imitation learning methods suffer substantial performance degradation due to OSS and that data augmentation alone is insufficient to address it.

## Problem

- Hierarchical imitation learning approaches for long-horizon robotic tasks often fail during sequential skill execution due to discrepancies between training and deployment visual states.
- Pre-trained visuomotor policies struggle when the visual environment changes in ways 'irrelevant' to the task's symbolic state (e.g., an object appearing in a container not part of the skill's direct focus).
- Existing robot learning benchmarks and methodologies do not adequately define, quantify, or address the specific challenge posed by these skill-irrelevant visual shifts during skill transitions.

## Method

- The **BOSS** benchmark is introduced, built on the LIBERO simulator, to systematically define, quantify, and investigate ==Observation Space Shift (OSS)== in long-horizon visual-input robotic tasks.
- A Rule-based Automatic Modification Generator (RAMG) is developed to automatically introduce diverse, structured visual modifications to tasks without altering their symbolic pre-conditions or effects.
- BOSS comprises three progressively challenging scenarios: Single Predicate Shift, Accumulated Predicate Shift, and Skill Chaining, enabling a comprehensive evaluation of OSS impact.

## Results

- Single predicate shifts caused substantial average performance degradation for imitation learning baselines (e.g., up to **67%** Ratio Performance Delta), affecting a high percentage of tasks (up to **68%**).
- Accumulated predicate shifts exacerbated the negative impact, consistently leading to average performance drops exceeding **50%** across all baselines as the number of visual modifications increased.
- Observation Space Shift significantly degrades overall long-horizon task success in skill chaining scenarios, demonstrating a substantial gap between ideal and actual chain success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Observation Space Shift (OSS) is formally defined as visual changes in skill-irrelevant predicates that negatively impact visuomotor policies during sequential skill execution.
> - Data augmentation, even with extensive and diverse datasets, proved largely insufficient to mitigate OSS for existing imitation learning baselines, suggesting a need for algorithmic solutions.
> - The severity of Observation Space Shift (OSS) is directly correlated with the magnitude of visual modifications, but not with the inherent difficulty of the skill being executed.
