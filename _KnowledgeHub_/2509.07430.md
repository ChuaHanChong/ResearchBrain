# The Choice of Divergence: A Neglected Key to Mitigating Diversity Collapse in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> This research investigates the role of f-divergence selection in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs), identifying reverse-KL divergence as a cause of diversity collapse. It introduces a Diversity-Preserving Hybrid RL (DPH-RL) framework, leveraging mass-covering f-divergences to mitigate diversity collapse and catastrophic forgetting, which results in improved multi-attempt performance (Pass@k) and out-of-domain generalization.

## Problem

- RLVR-tuned LLMs often suffer from 'diversity collapse,' where multi-attempt performance (Pass@k) degrades despite improvements in single-attempt accuracy (Pass@1).
- Catastrophic forgetting of acquired skills and poor out-of-domain (OOD) generalization are common issues in LLMs fine-tuned with current RLVR practices.
- The prevalent use of reverse-Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, known for its mode-seeking property, is hypothesized to actively narrow the policy distribution.

## Method

- Developed the Diversity-Preserving Hybrid RL (**DPH-RL**) framework, which strategically employs ==mass-covering== f-divergences (forward-KL and Jensen-Shannon) within the RL objective.
- Partitioned the training dataset into a 'near-perfect' subset (DpefD_{pef}Dpef) for knowledge retention and an 'exploration' subset (DexpD_{exp}Dexp) for pure policy optimization, using distinct loss functions for each.
- Implemented a computationally efficient 'Generator' form for f-divergence calculation, relying on pre-sampling from the reference policy to avoid real-time inference with a reference model during online training.

## Results

- DPH-RL (particularly DPH-JS) consistently surpassed baselines like GRPO, DAPO, and reverse-KL, achieving up to **4.3%** higher Pass@8 scores on in-domain SQL tasks and exhibiting superior performance for both Pass@1 and Pass@k metrics across various sample counts.
- The framework demonstrated improved out-of-domain (OOD) generalization, with DPH-F's Pass@16 scores being **9.0%** higher than DAPO on cross-domain SQL tasks and DPH methods maintaining higher average OOD performance in mathematical tasks.
- The 'Generator' implementation proved computationally efficient without sacrificing performance, and the '8 of 8' correctness threshold for dataset splitting emerged as the most robust strategy, outperforming other partitioning methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The choice of f-divergence is a critical, often overlooked factor in RLVR that significantly influences solution diversity, catastrophic forgetting, and out-of-domain generalization in LLMs.
> - Mass-covering f-divergences, such as forward-KL and Jensen-Shannon divergence, can act as an effective 'rehearsal mechanism' to preserve the initial policy's broad solution coverage and prevent knowledge loss.
> - By decoupling exploration and retention objectives through dataset partitioning and tailored loss functions, DPH-RL effectively balances learning new solutions with maintaining existing diverse capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has demonstrated utility in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly in domains such as mathematics and code generation. Despite advancements in single-attempt accuracy (Pass@1), a recurring issue in RLVR-tuned models is the stagnation or degradation of multi-attempt performance (Pass@k), often accompanied by catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired skills. This phenomenon, termed "diversity collapse," suggests that RLVR may lead to models overfitting to specific solution paths rather than fostering a broad understanding.

The broader research community has pursued several strategies to counteract this narrowing of output distribution and promote solution diversity. These include controlling entropy (Cui et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025; Liang et al., 2025), directly optimizing the Pass@k metric (Mahdavi et al.; Walder & Karkhanis, 2025), and modifying training paradigms (e.g., using additional data, fine-tuning hyperparameters, or employing hybrid RL+Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) approaches) (Yan et al.; Dong et al., 2025a; He et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b).

This paper identifies a largely overlooked aspect within this landscape: the role and choice of the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence term, which is commonly used to constrain policy updates in RL algorithms. The prevalent use of reverse-KL divergence, known for its mode-seeking property (Bishop & Nasrabadi, 2006), has been noted to potentially narrow the policy distribution. While some work has explored f-divergences in offline RL or alignment tasks (Wang et al., 2024; Han et al., 2025), their application within online RLVR to specifically address diversity collapse and catastrophic forgetting has been limited. This work distinguishes itself by systematically analyzing the impact of different f-divergences within an online RLVR framework, proposing their use not merely as a constraint but as an active mechanism for diversity preservation and knowledge retention.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research stem from addressing the identified shortcomings of current RLVR practices in fine-tuning LLMs:

1. **Systematic Analysis of Diversity Collapse:** The core motivation was to thoroughly investigate the causes of diversity collapse in RLVR, particularly focusing on the impact of the KL divergence term. The authors hypothesized that the standard reverse-KL divergence, by its mode-seeking nature, actively contributes to the policy narrowing, leading to degraded Pass@k performance, catastrophic forgetting, and poor out-of-domain (OOD) generalization. This objective aimed to provide empirical evidence for this hypothesis, demonstrating how existing methods, including those using reverse-KL or omitting a divergence term, fail to adequately retain a diverse knowledge base.

2. **Reconceptualizing the Role of Divergence:** A key motivation was to shift the perspective on the divergence term from a passive policy constraint to an active solution mechanism. The authors sought to develop a framework where the divergence itself serves as a tool for knowledge retention. This involved exploring mass-covering f-divergences, such as forward-KL and Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence, as a "rehearsal mechanism" to continuously reference and preserve the initial policy's broad solution coverage.

3. **Developing a Novel Diversity-Preserving Framework (DPH-RL):** Building on the reconceptualized role of divergence, a central objective was to propose and implement DPH-RL. This framework was designed to proactively leverage mass-covering f-divergences to maintain solution diversity and prevent catastrophic forgetting. The aim was for DPH-RL to surpass existing baselines by improving both single-attempt accuracy (Pass@1) and multi-attempt performance (Pass@k) within the training domain, while also preserving skills on out-of-domain tasks.

4. **Ensuring Computational Efficiency:** Another motivation was to develop a practical solution that does not introduce substantial computational overhead. The authors aimed to achieve this by implementing the f-divergence computation using generator functions, which would only require sampling from the initial policy during a pre-sampling stage, thus eliminating the need for an online reference model during training.

5. **Empirical Validation and Generalization:** The final objective was to conduct extensive empirical validation across different LLM architectures (Llama and Qwen, 7B to 32B models) and complex reasoning tasks (mathematics and SQL generation). This was to demonstrate the robustness, effectiveness, and generalization capabilities of DPH-RL, confirming that the proper selection of a divergence measure is a powerful tool for developing more general and diverse reasoning models, and successfully mitigating the Pass@k degradation relative to the base model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Diversity-Preserving Hybrid RL (DPH-RL) framework, which aims to address diversity collapse and catastrophic forgetting in LLMs fine-tuned with RLVR. The methodology is structured around a novel approach to incorporating f-divergences into the RL objective.

#### 3.1. Core Optimization Problem

The objective is to find an optimal policy $\pi_\theta$ that maximizes the expected cumulative reward while regularizing the policy's deviation from an initial reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ (e.g., a base or SFT model). This is formulated as:
`max_{π_θ} E_{q~D} [ E_{a~π_θ(·|q)} [r(a|q)] - ηD_f (π_θ(·|q)||π_{ref}(·|q)) ]`
where $r(a|q)$ is the reward, $\eta$ is a hyperparameter, and $D_f$ is an f-divergence.

#### 3.2. Dataset Partitioning (Pre-sampling Stage)

To apply the f-divergence effectively, the training dataset $D$ is partitioned into two subsets during a pre-sampling stage:
- **$D_{pef}$ (Near-Perfect Dataset):** This subset contains queries for which the reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ performs well, defined by a specific correctness threshold (e.g., 6 out of 8 or 7 out of 8 attempts). The purpose of this subset is to facilitate knowledge retention, ensuring the model preserves its existing capabilities on mastered tasks. A refinement step ensures that only queries where a final sample is correct are retained in $D_{pef}$ to mitigate sampling bias.
- **$D_{exp}$ (Exploration Dataset):** This subset comprises challenging queries where the reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ struggles. For these, the training strategy focuses on aggressive exploration to discover better solutions.

#### 3.3. Online Training Stage

The model is trained using two distinct loss functions, tailored for $D_{exp}$ and $D_{pef}$ respectively:

- **Loss for $D_{exp}$ (Exploration):** For samples from $D_{exp}$, the KL divergence penalty is entirely removed. The loss function is a standard PPO-clip objective ($L_{DPH-exp}(\theta)$), allowing the model to perform pure policy optimization based solely on the reward signal. This facilitates aggressive exploration on challenging examples.

- **Loss for $D_{pef}$ (Retention):** For samples from $D_{pef}$, the loss function ($L_{pef}(\theta)$) incorporates an f-divergence term: $E_{q~D_{pef}} [D_f (π_θ || π_{ref})]$. This term encourages the new policy $\pi_\theta$ to maintain coverage of all modes of the reference policy $\pi_{ref}$, thereby preserving original diversity and preventing catastrophic forgetting. Two specific f-divergences are investigated:
 - **DPH-F (Forward-KL):** Utilizes $D_{KL}(π_{ref} || π_θ) = E_{a~π_{ref}} [\log(π_{ref}(a|q)) - \log(π_θ(a|q))]$. This divergence penalizes the new policy if it assigns low probability to actions that the reference policy considers highly probable, effectively acting as an "anchor dataset" or "experience replay."
 - **DPH-JS (Jensen-Shannon Divergence):** This symmetric and stable divergence encourages $\pi_\theta$ to maintain high similarity with $\pi_{ref}$, acting as a "regularized rehearsal mechanism via dual-sided sampling."

- **Overall Loss Function:** The total loss is a weighted combination of the exploration and retention objectives: $L_{DPH-RL}(\theta) = L_{exp}(\theta) + \eta L_{pef}(\theta)$, where $\eta$ is the hyperparameter controlling the balance between the two objectives.

#### 3.4. Generator-Based Implementation

A practical aspect of the methodology is the "Generator" form of f-divergence computation. This approach relies on pre-sampling from the reference policy $\pi_{ref}$, creating a static dataset. This eliminates the need for real-time inference with the reference model during the online training loop, contributing to computational efficiency. An alternative "Divergence Definition" form, which requires online resampling, was also explored for comparison but found to be less efficient.

#### 3.5. Enhanced Monotonic Improvement Guarantee

The paper provides a theoretical justification for DPH-RL by deriving an enhanced monotonic improvement guarantee for TRPO-style algorithms. This involves defining a conditional reference policy $\pi_{pef}$ and establishing an assumption (Assumption 1) that the expected advantage of actions from $\pi_{pef}$ is lower-bounded. Theorem 1 demonstrates that this conditional regularization yields a strictly better lower bound on policy improvement for states in $D_{pef}$ compared to the standard TRPO analysis, while gracefully reducing to the standard guarantee for $D_{exp}$. This theoretical component supports the method's ability to accelerate convergence by leveraging known expert behavior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across SQL and mathematical reasoning tasks with various LLM models (Llama 3.1, Qwen 2.5, 7B to 32B) yielded several findings regarding the DPH-RL framework.

#### 4.1. Systematic Analysis of Diversity Collapse

The empirical analysis confirmed that the standard reverse-KL divergence, and even the absence of a KL term (as in GRPO and DAPO), leads to a decline in Pass@k performance and catastrophic forgetting. Specifically, reverse-KL was observed to yield a single solution style, while its absence offered no protection against model drift from diverse knowledge. Models trained with reverse-KL also exhibited poor out-of-domain (OOD) generalization.

#### 4.2. SQL Task Performance (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct)

- **In-Domain Performance (Bird dataset):** DPH-F and DPH-JS methods demonstrated the ability to surpass the base model's Pass@8 scores, indicating improved diversity retention. In contrast, GRPO and DAPO showed Pass@8 scores lower than the base model. DPH-JS achieved Pass@8 scores 4.3% higher than GRPO and 3.3% higher than DAPO.
- **Cross-Domain Performance (Spider dataset):** While all models experienced Pass@k degradation on this cross-domain task, DPH-F and DPH-JS maintained accuracy levels closer to the base model. DPH-F's Pass@16 scores were 9.0% higher than DAPO, suggesting effectiveness in preserving capabilities for simpler tasks.
- **Ablation Study:** Increasing the hyperparameter $\eta$ in DPH-JS led to a steady increase in Pass@16 scores. The "Generator" form of f-divergence computation was found to be significantly more time-efficient than the "Divergence Definition" form while yielding similar performance.
- **Out-of-Domain (OOD) Performance:** SQL-trained models were evaluated on mathematical tasks. GRPO and DAPO showed significant performance drops, indicating overfitting to the SQL domain. DPH-F and DPH-JS maintained higher average OOD performance, surpassing DAPO by 8.35% and 7.6% respectively.
- **Performance with Massive Sampling:** Across varying numbers of samples (k up to 256), DPH methods consistently outperformed GRPO, DAPO, and reverse-KL, with DPH-JS showing superior performance for both Pass@1 and Pass@k metrics.
- **Comparison Under Different f-divergences:** RKL maintained high Pass@k on in-domain test sets but suffered severe performance collapse on OOD tasks, indicating a trade-off with generalization. Alpha divergence showed a trend where increasing $\alpha$ improved Pass@k, aligning with its theoretical approximation to forward-KL. DPH-F and DPH-JS consistently demonstrated strong generalization and preservation of multi-peak distributions.

#### 4.3. Mathematical Reasoning Task Performance

- **Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct:** RL-based improvements were limited for this model. DPH-JS maintained the original Pass@k value and demonstrated a significant improvement in mean@k on the AIME dataset, balancing exploration and preservation. DPH-JS provided a more balanced improvement across Pass@k and mean@k averages compared to GRPO.
- **Qwen2.5-Math-7B:** This more capable model showed substantial improvements (approximately 20% in Mean@k and also in Pass@k). DPH-JS achieved the best overall performance, maintaining diversity closer to the base model on challenging AIME tasks and achieving higher Pass@k on simpler datasets. This was noted to balance solidifying existing knowledge with exploring new boundaries.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Keep and Exploration

The analysis of knowledge retention ("keep rate") and new solution discovery ("additional exploration rate") indicated that GRPO and DAPO often decreased the keep rate. RKL maintained a higher keep rate in-domain but suffered a similar drop to GRPO on OOD tasks. DPH-JS showed higher exploration on the in-domain Bird dataset, while DPH-F showed higher exploration on the cross-domain Spider dataset. DPH methods also demonstrated greater stability during training compared to other approaches which exhibited poor generalization (DAPO) or late-stage diversity collapse (GRPO and RKL).

#### 4.5. Impact of Dataset Splitting ($D_{exp}$ and $D_{pef}$)

Experiments varying the threshold for classifying queries into $D_{pef}$ (e.g., '8 of 8', '7 of 8', '6 of 8' correct attempts) showed that a threshold of '8 of 8' (meaning all 8 attempts were correct) yielded robust performance despite assigning only 8% of the training data to $D_{pef}$. This strategy comprehensively outperformed DAPO and GRPO. Other thresholds like 'Full Data' yielded high Pass@k but suffered from poor greedy performance and potential for trivial learning by mimicking noise. The '8 of 8' threshold was identified as the most straightforward and effective strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly by drawing attention to the often-overlooked role of divergence selection.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that the choice of f-divergence is not merely a technical detail for policy regularization but a fundamental mechanism for mitigating diversity collapse and catastrophic forgetting. By systematically analyzing the limitations of the conventional reverse-KL divergence and proposing a framework based on mass-covering f-divergences (DPH-RL), the authors offer a principled approach to fostering more robust and generalizable LLMs.

The potential impacts are multifaceted:
- **Improved LLM Reliability and Generalization:** The ability of DPH-RL to enhance both in-domain Pass@k and out-of-domain performance suggests that LLMs fine-tuned with this method could be more reliable and exhibit stronger generalization capabilities across varied reasoning tasks. This directly addresses critical limitations of current RLVR paradigms that often lead to models specializing at the expense of broader competence.
- **Enhanced Understanding of RLVR Dynamics:** The work provides a deeper understanding of how different divergence measures influence policy behavior, specifically highlighting the mode-seeking nature of reverse-KL and the mass-covering properties of forward-KL and JS-divergence. This insight can inform future algorithm design in RL for LLMs.
- **Practical and Efficient Solution:** The "Generator" form of implementation makes DPH-RL computationally efficient, requiring only pre-sampling from the reference policy and eliminating the need for an online reference model during training. This ensures that the benefits of diversity preservation do not come with prohibitive computational costs, making it a practical solution for researchers and practitioners.
- **Complementary to Existing Methods:** DPH-RL is noted to be orthogonal to other existing methods focusing on entropy control or reward shaping. This suggests potential for synergistic combinations, where DPH-RL could be integrated with other techniques to achieve further performance gains in LLM reasoning.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper identifies a crucial, overlooked axis for improving RLVR and recommends specific configurations (DPH-JS with '8 of 8' threshold) for future work. It also highlights limitations, such as dependence on the base model's capabilities on challenging datasets and estimation bias from imperfect data, thereby charting clear directions for future research in developing more robust and adaptable RLVR methods.

In summary, this research underscores the importance of a nuanced understanding and selection of divergence measures in RLVR, offering a framework that contributes to the development of LLMs with enhanced reasoning diversity, knowledge retention, and overall generalization.
