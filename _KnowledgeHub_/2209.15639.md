# F-VLM: Open-Vocabulary Object Detection upon Frozen Vision and Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new open-vocabulary object detection framework, F-VLM, leverages frozen Vision and Language Models with a minimal trainable detector head to achieve state-of-the-art results on LVIS and COCO benchmarks. This approach significantly reduces training computational costs, offering up to 226x compute savings over prior methods.

## Problem

- Object detection traditionally struggles with fixed, limited vocabularies due to the high cost of bounding box annotation for novel categories.
- Prior open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) methods relied on computationally intensive multi-stage training pipelines like knowledge distillation or extensive VLM finetuning.
- Effectively transferring the image-level open-vocabulary capabilities of Vision and Language Models (VLMs) to granular object-level detection remained a complex challenge.

## Method

- F-VLM utilizes a completely ==frozen, pre-trained VLM backbone== as the feature extractor.
- A minimal, randomly initialized, and trainable ==detector head (Mask R-CNN with FPN)== is attached to the frozen VLM for class-agnostic object localization.
- During inference, a ==geometric mean== combines localization scores from the detector head with open-vocabulary classification scores derived from the frozen VLM's region embeddings and text embeddings.

## Results

- The largest F-VLM model achieved **32.8 mask APr** on LVIS rare categories, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (ViLD-EN-B7) by **6.5 mask APr**.
- F-VLM demonstrated a significant reduction in training resources, achieving comparable or better performance with up to **2**26x** less compute** and **5**7x** faster** wall clock time than prior methods.
- The method exhibited strong cross-dataset transfer capabilities, outperforming existing approaches by **5.9 AP** on COCO and **3.2 AP** on Objects365 when trained solely on LVIS base categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frozen Vision and Language Model (VLM) backbones inherently possess locality-sensitive features capable of delineating object shapes and boundaries.
> - Features extracted from a frozen VLM are robust enough for accurate region classification, demonstrating strong transferable semantic understanding without finetuning.
> - The geometric mean proves effective for fusing detection scores from the trainable head with VLM's classification scores, ensuring both localization and semantic quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Object detection is a cornerstone of computer vision, aiming to localize and classify objects within an image. Historically, object detection models have been limited to a fixed, relatively small vocabulary of categories (e.g., hundreds or a few thousands), primarily due to the immense cost and labor involved in manually annotating bounding boxes or instance masks for new object classes. This limitation starkly contrasts the expansive vocabulary humans use to describe the visual world.

The concept of **open-vocabulary object detection (OVD)** emerged to address this constraint, enabling detectors to recognize novel categories not explicitly seen during training. Early approaches to zero-shot detection often relied on aligning visual features with semantic word embeddings or synthesizing features for unseen categories. More recently, the advent of **Vision and Language Models (VLMs)**, such as CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and ALIGN (Jia et al., 2021), has revolutionized the field of visual recognition. These models, trained on Internet-scale image-text pairs using contrastive learning, have demonstrated remarkable open-vocabulary capabilities at the *image-level*, effectively performing zero-shot classification across diverse concepts.

The challenge, however, has been effectively transferring this image-level open-vocabulary knowledge to the more granular task of *object-level* detection. Previous state-of-the-art OVD methods typically involve complex multi-stage pipelines:
- **Knowledge Distillation:** Approaches like ViLD (Gu et al., 2022) distill the knowledge from a large pretrained VLM (acting as a teacher) into a standard object detector (the student). This often involves generating pseudo-labels or soft targets for novel categories.
- **Detection-Tailored Pretraining:** Methods such as RegionCLIP (Zhong et al., 2022) or GLIP (Li et al., 2022b) involve extensive pretraining on large datasets of image-caption pairs or even specialized region-text datasets to learn region-level alignments. OWL-ViT (Minderer et al., 2022) finetunes VLMs on detection/grounding datasets.
- **Weakly Supervised Learning:** Detic (Zhou et al., 2022c) leverages image-level supervision alongside limited box annotations.
- **Prompt Optimization:** DetPro (Du et al., 2022) enhances distillation by optimizing prompts for VLMs.

These existing paradigms often require training the entire detector from scratch, finetuning substantial parts of the VLM, or engaging in computationally expensive multi-stage processes. F-VLM directly challenges this complexity by proposing a surprisingly simple and efficient alternative: building an OVD system *upon frozen VLMs*. This approach seeks to exploit the inherent locality-sensitive and discriminative features already present in off-the-shelf, frozen VLM backbones, thereby eliminating the need for knowledge distillation, detection-tailored pretraining, or weakly supervised learning. It aligns with a growing trend in computer vision to decouple task-specific learning from robust, task-agnostic backbone knowledge, as seen in works like ViTDet (Li et al., 2022c). F-VLM positions itself as a streamlined, high-performance, and computationally efficient paradigm for OVD, pushing the boundaries of what can be achieved with readily available, frozen large-scale models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the F-VLM paper are to:

1. **Simplify Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVD):** Develop a less complex and more efficient method for OVD, moving away from the multi-stage training pipelines that typically involve knowledge distillation, detection-tailored pretraining, or weakly supervised learning.
2. **Leverage Frozen Vision and Language Models (VLMs):** Demonstrate that frozen, off-the-shelf VLM features are sufficient and highly effective for OVD, thereby preserving their rich open-vocabulary classification capabilities without additional finetuning.
3. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** Surpass existing methods on challenging OVD benchmarks like LVIS and COCO, while also showing strong cross-dataset transfer capabilities.
4. **Reduce Computational Costs:** Significantly decrease training complexity, speed, and compute requirements compared to current OVD approaches.

The core motivation for F-VLM stems from two key, *surprising observations* about frozen VLMs:

- **Retention of Locality-Sensitive Features:** Despite being trained for image-level tasks (e.g., aligning entire images with text descriptions), frozen VLM backbones retain features that are sensitive to object locality, capable of delineating object shapes and boundaries. This suggests that the VLM implicitly learns a fine-grained understanding of visual elements.
- **Strong Region Classification Ability:** The features extracted from a frozen VLM are robust enough to perform accurate classification of object regions without any finetuning, implying a strong transferable semantic understanding.

These observations led the authors to hypothesize that if a frozen VLM already possesses both localization-relevant features and strong classification abilities at the region level, it should be possible to build an effective open-vocabulary detector by simply adding a minimal, trainable detector head for localization, while fully relying on the VLM for open-vocabulary recognition. This insight motivates the entire F-VLM framework, which seeks to minimize trainable parameters and computational overhead by fully exploiting the pre-learned knowledge in VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

F-VLM proposes a simple and scalable open-vocabulary object detection approach built entirely upon **frozen Vision and Language Models (VLMs)**. The methodology elegantly decouples the localization and recognition tasks, leveraging the strengths of VLMs without modifying their pre-trained weights.

The F-VLM architecture comprises:

1. **Frozen VLM Backbone:**
 - The core of F-VLM is a frozen pre-trained VLM's image encoder, such as CLIP's ResNet or Vision Transformer. This encoder is conceptualized into two parts: a feature extractor `F(·)` (e.g., ResNet-50) and a last feature pooling layer `P(·)` (e.g., an attention pooling layer).
 - The weights of `F(·)` are kept entirely frozen throughout the training and inference processes. This ensures the preservation of the VLM's rich semantic knowledge and open-vocabulary recognition ability. The same image pre-processing scheme used during VLM pretraining is adopted.

2. **Trainable Detector Head (Localization):**
 - To perform object localization, a standard detector head, specifically a **Mask R-CNN** with a **Feature Pyramid Network (FPN)**, is attached to the frozen VLM backbone.
 - Crucially, this detector head is the *only* trainable component of F-VLM and is randomly initialized. It learns to predict object regions (bounding boxes and instance masks) based on the frozen features from the VLM backbone.
 - **Class-Agnostic Localization:** Unlike standard detectors that often predict class-specific bounding boxes and masks, F-VLM uses class-agnostic box regression and mask prediction heads. This means it predicts one box and one mask per region proposal, irrespective of the object category. This design is critical for localizing novel objects, as the detector head does not need to learn categories specific to novel objects.

3. **Text-Embedding Region Classifier (Recognition during Training):**
 - During training, the standard K-way classifier in the detector head is replaced with a text-embedding-based classifier. For each region proposal, a region embedding `r_b` is extracted from the detector head.
 - This `r_b` is then compared (using cosine similarity) with the cached text embeddings of the base categories `C_B` (trained using the VLM's text encoder) and a generic "background" phrase. A learnable temperature `τ` is applied to the logits.
 - The system is trained using a standard softmax cross-entropy loss, where region proposals unmatched to ground truths are treated as background. This setup allows the detector head to learn to classify base categories while maintaining compatibility with the open-vocabulary setting.

4. **Open-Vocabulary Recognition (Recognition during Inference):**
 - At inference time, F-VLM leverages the frozen VLM's full open-vocabulary power for classification. For each detected region proposal `b` (generated by the trained detector head), the VLM's pooling layer `P(·)` is applied to the corresponding cropped backbone features (`R(F(I), b)`) to obtain a VLM region embedding `v_b`.
 - These `v_b` embeddings are then compared via cosine similarity with the text embeddings of *all* categories – both base `C_B` and novel `C_N` – to obtain VLM scores `w(v_b)`. A fixed temperature `T` is used here.
 - **Score Fusion:** The final detection scores `s(r_b)_i` for each region `b` and category `i` are a **geometric mean** combination of the detection scores `z(r_b)_i` from the detector head (Equation 2) and the VLM scores `w(v_b)_i` (Equation 4). Separate weighting parameters `α` and `β` are used for base and novel categories, respectively, to balance the contributions of the detector and VLM. The background score comes directly from the detector. The geometric mean is preferred over the arithmetic mean as it forces both detection and VLM scores to be high for a strong final score.

By training only the detector head on base categories and completely freezing the VLM backbone, F-VLM significantly reduces training complexity and computational cost, effectively transferring the VLM's pre-trained knowledge to the object detection task without complex distillation or finetuning strategies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

F-VLM demonstrates significant advancements in open-vocabulary object detection, setting new state-of-the-art benchmarks and highlighting the efficiency of leveraging frozen Vision and Language Models.

#### 4.1. LVIS Open-Vocabulary Detection Benchmark

- F-VLM achieves compelling scaling behavior: The largest F-VLM model, using an R50x64 backbone, attained **32.8 mask AP** on LVIS rare categories (APr), a **+14.2 APr** improvement over its R50 baseline.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** At the system level, F-VLM surpasses the previous best approach (ViLD-EN-B7) by a substantial **6.5 mask APr** on novel categories (26.3 APr for ViLD vs. 32.8 APr for F-VLM). It also showed a +5.6 overall mask AP improvement.
- The R50 comparison showed F-VLM achieving 18.6 APr, competitive with and often outperforming other R50-based methods that employ distillation or pretraining.

#### 4.2. COCO Open-Vocabulary Detection Benchmark

- F-VLM demonstrates very competitive results on the COCO open-vocabulary detection benchmark, achieving **28.0 Novel AP**. This performance is achieved without additional caption pretraining, weakly supervised learning, or knowledge distillation, making it stand out against methods like RegionCLIP which often rely on such augmentations.
- F-VLM significantly surpasses the CLIP-R50 pretrained version of RegionCLIP (14.2 Novel AP), underscoring the efficacy of its frozen backbone approach.

#### 4.3. Training Resource Benchmark

- **Significant Compute Savings:** F-VLM offers remarkable training efficiency. Compared to ViLD-EN-B7, F-VLM can achieve better performance (32.8 APr vs 26.3 APr) with only 7.4 epochs of training, resulting in a **226× compute saving** and **57× faster wall clock time**.
- Even when matching ViLD's performance (e.g., F-VLM at 27.7 APr), it still provides a 226× compute saving. This efficiency stems from freezing the backbone, simplifying the learning process and drastically reducing the number of trainable parameters.

#### 4.4. Cross-Dataset Transfer Detection

- F-VLM exhibits strong transfer capabilities, demonstrating its potential as a general-purpose detector. When trained on LVIS base categories and directly applied to COCO and Objects365 validation splits without finetuning, the best F-VLM model outperforms existing works (ViLD, DetPro) by **+5.9 AP on COCO** and **+3.2 AP on Objects365**.
- The performance scales consistently with increasing frozen model capacity, closing a significant gap with fully supervised models on both datasets.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies and Key Insights

- **Frozen vs. Finetuned Backbone:** Finetuning the VLM backbone (even with reduced learning rates) was found to improve performance on base categories but slightly *compromised* performance on novel categories, while increasing memory/compute. This reinforces the core F-VLM design decision to keep the backbone frozen.
- **Score Fusion Mechanism:** The **geometric mean** for combining detection and VLM scores was found to be significantly superior to the arithmetic mean (over +8 APr), indicating its effectiveness in ensuring both localization and semantic classification quality.
- **Score Fusion Parameters:** Optimal VLM score weights for novel classes (β = 0.65) and base classes (α = 0.35) were identified. Neither detector scores alone (β=0) nor VLM scores alone (β=1) achieved optimal performance, highlighting the synergy of the combined approach.
- **VLM Temperature:** A fixed, small temperature (T=0.01) for VLM scores was found to be optimal, distinct from the learnable temperature for detector scores.
- **Feature Pyramid Capacity:** Increasing the capacity of the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) improved base category detection without compromising novel category performance, indicating its role in enriching features from the frozen backbone.
- **Background Weight:** A background weight of 0.9 (compared to 1.0) yielded slight performance improvements.

In summary, F-VLM successfully introduces a simpler, more efficient, and highly performant paradigm for open-vocabulary object detection by effectively exploiting the inherent capabilities of frozen Vision and Language Models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The F-VLM paper presents a significant contribution to the field of computer vision, particularly in open-vocabulary object detection, with several far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Open-Vocabulary Object Detection:** F-VLM challenges the prevailing complexity in OVD by demonstrating that state-of-the-art performance can be achieved with a remarkably simple approach: a minimal, trainable detector head built upon a completely frozen Vision and Language Model backbone. This simplification obviates the need for computationally intensive knowledge distillation, extensive detection-tailored pretraining, or complex weakly supervised learning techniques. This could inspire future research to re-evaluate and simplify other complex multi-stage learning pipelines.

2. **Unlocking the Full Potential of Frozen VLMs:** The work highlights the unexpected and powerful capabilities of frozen VLMs, revealing that they inherently possess both locality-sensitive features crucial for precise object localization and strong open-vocabulary classification abilities at the region level. This suggests that large-scale VLMs, once trained, are even more versatile and transferable than previously assumed, potentially serving as universal visual backbones for a wider array of downstream tasks beyond traditional image-level recognition.

3. **Dramatic Reduction in Computational Cost and Training Time:** The most immediate and practical impact of F-VLM is the significant reduction in training resources. By training only a small detector head, F-VLM offers up to 226× compute savings and 57× faster training times compared to previous state-of-the-art methods. This efficiency makes advanced OVD more accessible to researchers and practitioners with limited computational budgets, democratizing access to powerful object detection capabilities. It also accelerates research cycles, allowing for quicker experimentation and iteration.

4. **Enhanced Scalability and Generalization:** F-VLM demonstrates excellent scaling behavior, with performance consistently improving as the capacity of the frozen VLM backbone increases. Its strong cross-dataset transfer capabilities further underscore its generalization power, moving towards more "dataset-agnostic" object detection systems that can adapt to new domains and vocabularies with minimal effort. This is crucial for real-world applications where data diversity and adaptability are paramount.

5. **Facilitating Real-world Applications:** The ability to perform open-vocabulary detection on-the-fly, particularly without finetuning for new categories, is invaluable for applications requiring adaptability to dynamic environments or user-defined objects. The demonstration on Ego4D, an egocentric video dataset, showcases its practical utility in challenging, real-world scenarios, where it successfully detects novel and common objects despite significant domain shifts and complex viewing conditions. This opens doors for more robust and flexible AI systems in robotics, augmented reality, and personalized assistants.

6. **Future Research Directions:** F-VLM's success paves the way for deeper exploration into the internal representations of frozen VLMs. Researchers can investigate how these models encode spatial and semantic information, leading to new insights into visual understanding. The paper also explicitly suggests exploring other types of VLMs (e.g., ViT-based, multimodal encoders, captioning VLMs) within the frozen backbone paradigm, expanding the scope of this efficient approach.

**Ethical Considerations:** The authors responsibly acknowledge the ethical implications of using large VLMs trained on internet-scale data. Such models may inadvertently perpetuate and amplify societal biases or stereotypes present in their training data. While F-VLM demonstrates new capabilities for evaluation and benchmarking, the authors rightly caution that rigorous probing for bias, fairness, and safety is essential before deployment in any real-world application. This commitment to ethical AI research is important for ensuring responsible development and deployment of powerful vision systems.

In conclusion, F-VLM represents a significant leap forward in open-vocabulary object detection, offering a simple, efficient, and highly effective methodology that redefines how frozen Vision and Language Models can be leveraged for complex computer vision tasks. Its impact is poised to accelerate research, democratize access to advanced AI tools, and enhance the robustness of real-world vision applications.
