# F-VLM: Open-Vocabulary Object Detection upon Frozen Vision and Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new open-vocabulary object detection framework, F-VLM, leverages frozen Vision and Language Models with a minimal trainable detector head to achieve state-of-the-art results on LVIS and COCO benchmarks. This approach significantly reduces training computational costs, offering up to 226x compute savings over prior methods.

## Problem

- Object detection traditionally struggles with fixed, limited vocabularies due to the high cost of bounding box annotation for novel categories.
- Prior open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) methods relied on computationally intensive multi-stage training pipelines like knowledge distillation or extensive VLM finetuning.
- Effectively transferring the image-level open-vocabulary capabilities of Vision and Language Models (VLMs) to granular object-level detection remained a complex challenge.

## Method

- F-VLM utilizes a completely ==frozen, pre-trained VLM backbone== as the feature extractor.
- A minimal, randomly initialized, and trainable ==detector head (Mask R-CNN with FPN)== is attached to the frozen VLM for class-agnostic object localization.
- During inference, a ==geometric mean== combines localization scores from the detector head with open-vocabulary classification scores derived from the frozen VLM's region embeddings and text embeddings.

## Results

- The largest F-VLM model achieved **32.8 mask APr** on LVIS rare categories, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (ViLD-EN-B7) by **6.5 mask APr**.
- F-VLM demonstrated a significant reduction in training resources, achieving comparable or better performance with up to **226x less compute** and **57x faster** wall clock time than prior methods.
- The method exhibited strong cross-dataset transfer capabilities, outperforming existing approaches by **5.9 AP** on COCO and **3.2 AP** on Objects365 when trained solely on LVIS base categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frozen Vision and Language Model (VLM) backbones inherently possess locality-sensitive features capable of delineating object shapes and boundaries.
> - Features extracted from a frozen VLM are robust enough for accurate region classification, demonstrating strong transferable semantic understanding without finetuning.
> - The geometric mean proves effective for fusing detection scores from the trainable head with VLM's classification scores, ensuring both localization and semantic quality.
