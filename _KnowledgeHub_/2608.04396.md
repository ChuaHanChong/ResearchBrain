# CofactVLA: Deconfounding Vision-Language-Action Models via Counterfactual Intervention

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University developed CofactVLA, a framework that mitigates the "vision-override phenomenon" in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by deconfounding language instructions from spurious visual cues. It achieved an average success rate of 98.5% on standard LIBERO benchmarks and a 52.3% absolute improvement in real-world out-of-distribution scenarios compared to baselines.

## Problem

- The "vision-override phenomenon" in VLA models, where dense visual streams overshadow sparse language instructions, causing actions to be driven by visually prominent but irrelevant features.
- Causal confusion in VLA policies, leading to overfitting on visual confounders rather than strict adherence to explicit language commands.
- Limitations of existing methods (e.g., data augmentation, scalar extrapolation like Classifier-Free Guidance) in fundamentally addressing internal representational biases or effectively handling non-linear action spaces.

## Method

- **CofactVLA** uses a ==Dual-path Deconfounding Graph (DDG)== with a factual branch (observation + language) and a counterfactual branch (observation only) to dynamically isolate visual confounders.
- ==Action-Level Orthogonal Projection Guidance (OPG)== geometrically projects the factual velocity field away from the counterfactual visual bias to extract deconfounded language semantic intent.
- ==Feature-Level Counterfactual Covariance Reduction (CCR)== identifies and projects out nuisance subspaces corresponding to visual confounders within the latent feature space using covariance analysis.

## Results

- Achieved a state-of-the-art average success rate of **98.5%** on standard LIBERO benchmarks and the highest total success rate of **69.1%** on the challenging LIBERO-Plus out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmark.
- Demonstrated significant real-world efficacy with a **90.8%** average success rate in standard setups and a **75.8%** success rate in OOD scenarios using a 6-DoF robotic arm, marking a **52.3%** absolute improvement over baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic effectiveness of both Action-Level Orthogonal Projection Guidance (OPG) and Feature-Level Counterfactual Covariance Reduction (CCR), with OPG outperforming scalar-based interventions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA models suffer from causal confusion where dense visual inputs create a spurious backdoor path (Image 4 Confounder 4 Action) overriding language instructions, necessitating intervention to protect the true causal effect (Text 4 Action).
> - Dynamically generating a language-masked counterfactual branch allows for the isolation of inherent visual biases (confounders) within a single forward pass.
> - Deconfounding can be effectively achieved at both the action level via geometric orthogonal projection (OPG) and at the feature level by reducing counterfactual covariance (CCR) to eliminate visual confounders while preserving semantic intent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an advancement in robotic control, leveraging pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to enable robots to interpret natural language instructions and execute corresponding physical actions. These models typically operate as end-to-end visuomotor policies, mapping raw visual observations and linguistic input to low-level control actions. Common paradigms involve adapting large-scale VLMs through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or Reinforcement Learning (RL), often by formulating continuous control as a sequence modeling task using autoregressive token decoding or, more recently, through diffusion and flow-matching frameworks for continuous action distributions.

Despite their capabilities, a critical challenge in VLA models is the "vision-override phenomenon." This issue arises from a severe modality imbalance in typical robotics datasets, where dense visual streams inherently contain more information than sparse linguistic instructions. Consequently, VLA models can develop causal confusion, wherein policies overfit to visually prominent but semantically irrelevant features (visual confounders) rather than strictly adhering to the language instruction. This leads to actions being dictated by visual shortcuts (e.g., grasping the most salient object) rather than the intended semantic meaning.

Previous attempts to mitigate the vision-override phenomenon include data-centric approaches, such as linguistic rephrasing or data augmentation. However, these methods are often difficult to scale to diverse, open-world scenarios and may not fundamentally decouple entangled internal representations within the model. Action-level inference strategies, such as Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), aim to suppress visual priors through dual-branch scalar subtraction. The paper notes that such linear extrapolation in high-dimensional, non-linear action spaces can inflate noise, potentially pushing continuous robotic trajectories out-of-distribution and resulting in unsafe or inaccurate executions, while also lacking a mechanism to address structural modality collapse at the representation level.

Causal inference has emerged as a field of increasing interest for enhancing robustness and mitigating distribution shifts in robotics. This work positions itself within this landscape by proposing a causal intervention framework, moving beyond data augmentation or scalar-based inference, to systematically address the internal representational biases that lead to the vision-override phenomenon.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to systematically mitigate the "vision-override phenomenon" in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This phenomenon is identified as a critical vulnerability that hinders the reliable deployment of VLA models in complex, open-world environments.

The motivation for addressing this problem stems from the observation that VLA models, when trained on robotics datasets, are prone to causal confusion due to a severe modality imbalance. Visual streams, being dense and continuous, provide a much richer input signal compared to sparse linguistic instructions. This imbalance frequently causes the models to learn spurious correlations, where visual confounders—such as dominant objects or familiar scene layouts—are inadvertently treated as primary causal drivers for actions. As a result, the model's policy may bypass the explicit language instruction, overfitting to these visual shortcuts. For instance, a robot might consistently grasp a visually salient object without considering if the instruction specifies a different, less prominent object or a particular action not immediately suggested by visual salience.

The authors formalize this issue through a causal lens: ideally, a robotic action (A) should be causally driven by a language instruction (T) and grounded by an image observation (I). However, the training data leads to an unintended spurious backdoor path where the image (I) influences a latent visual confounder (C), which then directly dictates the action (A), overriding the true semantic intent of the language (T).

To counteract this, the research poses a counterfactual question: "What action would the VLA model inherently take in this exact visual scene if the language instruction were entirely absent?" This question forms the basis for their proposed intervention. By constructing a language-masked counterfactual branch during a single forward pass, the model aims to dynamically isolate the pure visual confounder (C). The overarching motivation is to then perform end-to-end causal interventions to sever the spurious causal path (C → A) and to protect and enhance the true causal effect of the language instruction (T → A). This approach seeks to ensure that the robot's actions are robustly and consistently driven by the semantic intent conveyed through language, rather than being swayed by misleading visual cues.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, CofactVLA, addresses the vision-override phenomenon through a novel causal intervention strategy within a Dual-path Deconfounding Graph (DDG). The core idea is to disentangle the influence of language instructions from spurious visual confounders by dynamically creating a counterfactual branch.

#### 3.1. Causal View of VLA and Dual-Path Design

The VLA decision-making process is formalized as a structural causal model. An ideal policy $\pi(A | O, T)$ maps observations (O) and language instructions (T) to actions (A). However, modality imbalance leads to a spurious backdoor path $I \rightarrow C \rightarrow A$, where $C$ is a latent visual confounder.
To mitigate $C$'s effect, CofactVLA employs a dual-branch framework:
- **Factual Branch:** Conditioned on both observation (O) and language instruction (T).
- **Counterfactual Branch:** Conditioned only on observation (O), effectively masking the language instruction. This branch captures the model's inherent visual biases for a given scene, allowing the isolation of the visual confounder $C$.

#### 3.2. Action-Level Deconfounding: Orthogonal Projection Guidance (OPG)

This intervention operates at the output action space, specifically within a continuous flow matching framework. The challenge here is the multi-modal nature of robotic control, where a single language instruction can correspond to multiple valid trajectories. Visual confounders can spuriously bias the selection of these action modes.
- **Problem with prior methods:** Scalar extrapolation methods (e.g., Classifier-Free Guidance) assume orthogonal separability in high-dimensional, non-linear action spaces. When this assumption is violated, they can amplify noise and lead to out-of-distribution or unsafe trajectories.
- **OPG Mechanism:** Let $v_{cond}$ and $v_{uncond}$ be the predicted velocity fields from the factual and counterfactual branches, respectively. OPG geometrically projects the factual velocity field ($v_{cond}$) onto the counterfactual visual instinct ($v_{uncond}$).
 - The projection $v_{proj} = \frac{\langle v_{cond}, v_{uncond} \rangle}{\|v_{uncond}\|^2 + \epsilon} v_{uncond}$ explicitly captures the collinear visual bias.
 - By subtracting this collinear component, the strictly orthogonal velocity component $v_{\perp} = v_{cond} - v_{proj}$ is extracted. This $v_{\perp}$ represents the pure, deconfounded language semantic intent.
 - The final causally intervened velocity field is constructed as $v_{causal} = v_{cond} + \gamma \cdot v_{\perp}$, where $\gamma > 1.0$ is a guidance scale.
This orthogonalization ensures that the intervention re-weights causal mode probabilities along the semantic direction, removing visual bias without distorting the valid action manifold.

#### 3.3. Feature-Level Deconfounding: Counterfactual Covariance Reduction (CCR)

This intervention targets the internal latent representation space, addressing the root cause where visual confounders might dominate attention mechanisms and create spurious shortcuts.
- **Mechanism:** Let $F_f$ and $F_{cf}$ be the flattened attention features (Keys and Values) from the transformer layers of the factual and counterfactual branches.
 - Centered empirical covariance matrices are computed: $\Sigma_f = Cov(F_f)$ and $\Sigma_{cf} = Cov(F_{cf})$.
 - The covariance difference is calculated: $\Delta \Sigma = \Sigma_{cf} - \Sigma_f$.
- **Theoretical Justification:** The method is supported by a "gain-bias decomposition" and "contrastive eigengap" assumptions. These propose that the latent feature space can be decomposed into an observation-driven causal intent subspace ($S_O$) and a spurious visual confounder subspace ($S_C$). The eigenvalues of a whitened covariance difference matrix (M) restricted to $S_C$ are strictly larger than those restricted to $S_O$, making the visual confounder geometrically identifiable.
- **Intervention Process:**
 - Eigendecomposition of $\Delta \Sigma = U \Lambda U^\top$ is performed.
 - The positive eigenspace, corresponding to eigenvalues $\lambda > \epsilon > 0$, is isolated to construct a nuisance basis $U_{bias} \in R^{d \times k}$. This $U_{bias}$ is theorized to span the $S_C$ subspace.
 - The latent features ($F$) are then projected orthogonally away from $U_{bias}$ to eliminate the visual confounder. The causal feature $F_{causal}$ is obtained by $F_{causal} = F - \beta (F U_{bias}) U_{bias}^\top$, where $\beta \in (0, 1]$ controls the intervention strength.
This ensures that semantic language representations, located in the orthogonal negative eigenspace, remain unperturbed. The intervention is applied to the Key and Value states in the final layers of the VLM backbone.

CofactVLA integrates OPG and CCR synergistically: CCR first cleanses the latent representations to provide a semantically pure intent, which then enables OPG to perform a more precise geometric projection during action generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results from extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world robotic environments, demonstrating the efficacy of CofactVLA.

#### 4.1. Main Results on Simulation Benchmarks

- **Standard LIBERO:** CofactVLA achieves a state-of-the-art average success rate of 98.5%, surpassing previous baselines such as $\pi_{0.5}$ (96.9%) and X-VLA (98.1%). It notably achieved 100.0% success on the Object suite and 99.0% on the Spatial suite, indicating enhanced fundamental manipulation capabilities and execution precision.
- **OOD Generalization (LIBERO-Plus):** When evaluated on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, which features seven distinct visual and environmental perturbations, CofactVLA demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization. It achieved the highest total success rate of 69.1%, significantly outperforming the foundational $\pi_0$ baseline (53.6%) and other state-of-the-art models like OpenVLA-OFT_m (67.9%). The model showed particular strength in novel robotic embodiments (Robot, 49.7%) and unseen spatial configurations (Layout, 70.2%), scenarios where models often overfit to familiar visual cues. Consistent performance was also observed across variations in lighting, background, and noise.

#### 4.2. Main Results on Real-World Robot

- **Standard Environments:** In real-world experiments using a 6-DoF AgileX PiPer robotic arm, CofactVLA achieved an average success rate of 90.8% in standard setups, outperforming the $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline (71.0%). The performance gap was particularly pronounced in Task IV (picking a red cube into a yellow plate with distractors), where CofactVLA achieved 88% success compared to $\pi_{0.5}$'s 31%, highlighting its ability to resist visual distractors.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Scenarios:** Under real-world environmental distribution shifts, the $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline's average success rate plummeted to 23.5%. In contrast, CofactVLA maintained an average success rate of 75.8% under OOD conditions, marking a 52.3% absolute improvement. This indicates that explicitly isolating visual biases directly contributes to bridging the generalization gap in physical environments.
- **Additional Real-World Experiments:** On a set of 9 additional manipulation skills, CofactVLA achieved an average success rate of 96.7%, outperforming $\pi_{0.5}$ (93.0%) and other baselines. Gains were observed across various tasks, including object placement, stacking, drawer manipulation, and longer-horizon sequential tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Component Effectiveness:** Both Feature-Level Counterfactual Covariance Reduction (CCR) and Action-Level Orthogonal Projection Guidance (OPG) individually improved the baseline performance (from 97.0% to 97.5% for CCR and 98.0% for OPG). Combining both components yielded the highest average performance of 98.5%, confirming their synergistic operation.
- **Superiority of OPG:** OPG outperformed traditional scalar-based interventions such as naive Addition (94.0%), Subtraction (96.0%), and Classifier-Free Guidance (CAG) (97.5%), supporting the theoretical argument that its geometric approach robustly handles non-orthogonal noise.

#### 4.4. Sensitivity Analyses of Intervention Intensity

- **Action-Level Causal Scale ($\gamma$):** Performance generally improved with increasing $\gamma$, peaking around $\gamma=2$. Excessive guidance ($\gamma=3$) led to minor performance drops, consistent with theory that over-aggressive projection can distort the valid action manifold.
- **Feature-Level Intervention Strength ($\beta$):** A clear performance peak was observed at $\beta=0.15$, achieving the highest average success rate. Overly aggressive covariance penalties ($\beta=0.2$) resulted in performance degradation, suggesting that while moderate penalization suppresses confounders, excessive intervention can inadvertently remove essential, language-aligned visual features.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results and Analyses

- **Mitigating Causal Confusion:** Qualitative comparisons in both simulation and real-world settings illustrated that CofactVLA successfully maintains semantic grounding in visually dense scenes, avoiding premature interactions with irrelevant objects or accurately locating targets amidst distractors, which baselines often fail to do.
- **Generalization to Unseen OOD Environments:** Visualizations demonstrated CofactVLA's robustness to unseen table textures, novel objects, and instruction perturbations, effectively neutralizing spurious confounders and bridging the sim-to-real and environment-to-environment generalization gaps.
- **Failure Modes:** Identified failure cases primarily in simulation occurred under multiple extreme perturbations (e.g., severe lighting changes combined with drastic camera angle shifts). In real-world deployments, failures were predominantly linked to severe physical occlusions where the robot's end-effector blocked the camera's field of view.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper introduces CofactVLA, a framework addressing a fundamental vulnerability in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: the "vision-override phenomenon." This phenomenon, where dense visual inputs inadvertently supersede sparse language instructions, limits the reliability and generalization of robots in complex environments.

The significance of this research lies in its principled approach to deconfounding. By formalizing action generation as a Dual-path Deconfounding Graph (DDG) and employing causal intervention, the authors offer a systematic mechanism to disentangle true language intent from spurious visual cues. The introduction of Action-Level Orthogonal Projection Guidance (OPG) and Feature-Level Counterfactual Covariance Reduction (CCR) represents a methodological advancement. OPG improves upon scalar-based guidance methods by geometrically projecting out visual biases, thereby maintaining valid action trajectories. CCR addresses the problem at a deeper representational level by mathematically identifying and suppressing visual confounders in latent features, ensuring that the model's internal representations are driven by causal semantic intent.

The empirical results underscore the impact of this work. Achieving state-of-the-art performance across diverse simulation benchmarks, particularly demonstrating robust zero-shot generalization on challenging Out-of-Distribution (OOD) tasks, indicates a significant improvement in model reliability. Crucially, the real-world robot experiments provide compelling evidence of CofactVLA's practical efficacy. The observed 52.3% absolute success rate gain under OOD scenarios in physical deployments suggests that the deconfounding mechanisms effectively bridge the generalization gap from training data to novel, unstructured environments.

The potential impact of CofactVLA extends to several areas:
- **Enhanced Robotic Reliability:** By ensuring that robots strictly adhere to human instructions rather than being misled by visual distractors, the framework can contribute to more reliable and predictable robotic operation.
- **Improved Generalization:** The ability to generalize to unseen environments and perturbations without extensive re-training is critical for deploying robots in the diverse and dynamic settings of the real world, reducing development costs and increasing adaptability.
- **Safer Human-Robot Interaction:** Policies that are explicitly grounded in language instructions are inherently safer, as they are less likely to perform unintended actions based on spurious visual correlations. This is particularly relevant for robots operating in close proximity to humans.
- **Advancement in Causality for Robotics:** This work contributes to the growing field of causal inference in robotics, demonstrating how causal principles can be leveraged to address deep-seated issues in multimodal learning.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as dependence on the base VLM's inherent grounding and vulnerability to severe physical occlusions, it provides a foundation for future research. Future work incorporating dynamic multi-view fusion or real-time 3D scene understanding could further enhance robustness, particularly in occlusion-heavy scenarios. Overall, CofactVLA represents a step towards more capable, trustworthy, and deployable VLA systems.
