# InternVLA-M1: A Spatially Guided Vision-Language-Action Framework for Generalist Robot Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> InternVLA-M1 is a spatially guided vision-language-action framework from Intern Robotics and Shanghai AI Laboratory that integrates explicit spatial grounding into generalist robot policy training. The framework achieved superior performance on public benchmarks, including a +14.6% success rate improvement on SimplerEnv Google Robot, alongside enhanced generalization to unseen objects and robustness in dynamic real-world environments.

## Problem

- Translating abstract linguistic instructions, especially those with rich spatial cues, into concrete, continuous robot actions presents a fundamental challenge.
- Existing hierarchical robotic systems are rigid and struggle with scalability and end-to-end learning for diverse, complex tasks.
- Prior data-driven Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often under-generalize high-level instructions involving spatial information due to limited integration of spatial priors and scarce, diverse teleoperated datasets.

## Method

- A dual-system VLA framework combines a VLM Planner (based on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-instruct) for high-level spatial reasoning and an Action Expert (a diffusion policy) for low-level motor control.
- A two-stage spatially guided training pipeline begins with pre-training the VLM on 2.3M+ spatial reasoning samples, followed by joint optimization of VLM and Action Expert on demonstration data with spatial prompting, co-training with spatial grounding data, and gradient decay.
- Development of InternData-M1, a large-scale synthetic dataset of 244K closed-loop episodes, generated by decoupling robot planning from visual rendering, augments real-world teleoperated demonstrations for robust training.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art success rates on public benchmarks, including a +14.6% improvement on SimplerEnv Google Robot and a +9.8% gain on WidowX compared to its non-spatially guided variant.
- Demonstrated enhanced generalization to unseen objects, novel environments, and diverse instructions, showing an average +6.2% improvement over prior works in large-scale simulated pick-and-place tasks.
- Exhibited robustness in real-world cluttered pick-and-place, with a +20.6% success rate improvement on unseen objects through synthetic co-training, and maintained performance in long-horizon tasks under physical interference and dynamic replanning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit spatial grounding, through a 'spatially guided training' principle, is a critical element for building scalable and resilient generalist robot policies, effectively bridging abstract instructions with concrete actions.
> - A dual-system architecture that separates a VLM planner for high-level reasoning from an Action Expert for low-level execution, connected by a lightweight querying transformer, enables robust and efficient learning.
> - Large-scale synthetic data generation, by decoupling robot planning from visual rendering, can effectively mitigate real-world data limitations and provide dense supervision crucial for enhancing visual and language generalization in robotics.
