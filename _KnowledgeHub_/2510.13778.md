# InternVLA-M1: A Spatially Guided Vision-Language-Action Framework for Generalist Robot Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> InternVLA-M1 is a spatially guided vision-language-action framework from Intern Robotics and Shanghai AI Laboratory that integrates explicit spatial grounding into generalist robot policy training. The framework achieved superior performance on public benchmarks, including a +14.6% success rate improvement on SimplerEnv Google Robot, alongside enhanced generalization to unseen objects and robustness in dynamic real-world environments.

## Problem

- Translating abstract linguistic instructions, especially those with rich spatial cues, into concrete, continuous robot actions presents a fundamental challenge.
- Existing hierarchical robotic systems are rigid and struggle with scalability and end-to-end learning for diverse, complex tasks.
- Prior data-driven Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often under-generalize high-level instructions involving spatial information due to limited integration of spatial priors and scarce, diverse teleoperated datasets.

## Method

- A dual-system VLA framework combines a VLM Planner (based on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-instruct) for high-level spatial reasoning and an Action Expert (a diffusion policy) for low-level motor control.
- A two-stage spatially guided training pipeline begins with pre-training the VLM on 2.3M+ spatial reasoning samples, followed by joint optimization of VLM and Action Expert on demonstration data with spatial prompting, co-training with spatial grounding data, and gradient decay.
- Development of InternData-M1, a large-scale synthetic dataset of 244K closed-loop episodes, generated by decoupling robot planning from visual rendering, augments real-world teleoperated demonstrations for robust training.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art success rates on public benchmarks, including a +14.6% improvement on SimplerEnv Google Robot and a +9.8% gain on WidowX compared to its non-spatially guided variant.
- Demonstrated enhanced generalization to unseen objects, novel environments, and diverse instructions, showing an average +6.2% improvement over prior works in large-scale simulated pick-and-place tasks.
- Exhibited robustness in real-world cluttered pick-and-place, with a +20.6% success rate improvement on unseen objects through synthetic co-training, and maintained performance in long-horizon tasks under physical interference and dynamic replanning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit spatial grounding, through a 'spatially guided training' principle, is a critical element for building scalable and resilient generalist robot policies, effectively bridging abstract instructions with concrete actions.
> - A dual-system architecture that separates a VLM planner for high-level reasoning from an Action Expert for low-level execution, connected by a lightweight querying transformer, enables robust and efficient learning.
> - Large-scale synthetic data generation, by decoupling robot planning from visual rendering, can effectively mitigate real-world data limitations and provide dense supervision crucial for enhancing visual and language generalization in robotics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robot policies capable of understanding and executing diverse human instructions in the physical world represents a grand challenge in artificial intelligence and robotics. This work emerges at the intersection of large multimodal foundation models (VLMs) and embodied AI, aiming to bridge a fundamental gap: the disparity between abstract textual instructions (often containing rich spatial cues) and continuous, embodied actions in the 3D physical world.

Prior research has approached this challenge through several avenues:
- **Hierarchical Robotic Systems:** These systems explicitly encode spatial priors by leveraging foundation models. They often decompose tasks into symbolic structures and employ manually designed planning heuristics (e.g., using object detectors for bounding boxes or 3D points for grasp planning). While providing interpretability, this rigid separation between high-level task structures and low-level motor control often hinders scalability to complex and diverse tasks, making end-to-end policy learning difficult.
- **Data-Driven Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** More recent approaches directly learn robot control by leveraging pre-trained VLMs and large-scale teleoperation datasets (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA). These models demonstrate impressive capabilities but tend to overfit fine-grained motor behaviors. A key limitation is their under-generalization to high-level linguistic instructions that involve absolute or relational positions, failing to effectively incorporate core spatial priors (like object recognition, affordance grounding, visual trajectory reasoning, relative localization) into execution. The scarcity and modest diversity of teleoperated datasets compared to web-scale instruction-following corpora exacerbate this issue.

InternVLA-M1 directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel spatially guided VLA framework. It acknowledges the need for transferable, embodiment-agnostic spatial priors as a foundational element, distinguishing itself by explicitly integrating spatial grounding as the critical link between instructions and actions. Unlike rigid hierarchical systems, InternVLA-M1 aims for end-to-end optimization. Unlike previous data-driven VLAs, it *prioritizes* the systematic incorporation of spatial reasoning to enhance generalization, rather than treating spatial cues indirectly. This approach represents a significant step towards creating robot policies that are not only capable of understanding complex instructions but also inherently robust to visual variations and capable of adapting to diverse physical environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of InternVLA-M1 is to advance instruction-following robots toward scalable, general-purpose intelligence through a unified framework for spatial grounding and robot control. The core motivation stems from the recognition that while large multimodal foundation models excel at vision-language understanding, their direct application to physical robotics is hampered by the fundamental challenge of translating abstract linguistic instructions, particularly those rich in spatial information, into concrete, continuous robot actions.

The key objectives can be summarized as follows:
- **Establish Spatially Guided Vision-Language-Action Training:** The central idea is to make spatial grounding an explicit and critical component of the VLA training pipeline. This involves training the robot to first understand "where to act" by aligning instructions with embodiment-agnostic visual positions, and then to decide "how to act" by generating embodiment-aware actions.
- **Bridge Abstract Reasoning with Grounded Execution:** Develop a dual-system framework that seamlessly integrates high-level reasoning (interpreting linguistic instructions and spatial relations) with low-level execution (translating grounded representations into motor commands).
- **Enhance Generalization and Robustness:** Improve the robot's ability to generalize to unseen objects, novel environmental configurations, and diverse linguistic instructions. This includes robustness to real-world complexities such as clustered scenes, physical interference, and dynamic task replanning in long-horizon scenarios.
- **Scale Robot Learning:** Address the limitations of scarce and diverse real-world robot data by leveraging large-scale synthetic data generation for spatial grounding pre-training and action post-training, thus enabling more scalable robot learning.
- **Unify Spatial Understanding and Control:** Clarify the role of spatial priors as general-purpose foundations that transfer across robotic platforms, while allowing embodiment-specific learning to focus on concrete control strategies.

Ultimately, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing VLA models that either lack the flexibility for end-to-end learning or fail to deeply integrate spatial reasoning, thereby enabling more resilient and generalist robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InternVLA-M1 proposes a dual-system, end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework characterized by a spatially guided two-stage training pipeline.

**Model Architecture (Dual-System):**
The framework comprises two main components that operate in conjunction:
- **VLM Planner (System 2):** This acts as a "slow but reliable reasoner" responsible for interpreting linguistic instructions and reasoning about spatial relations. It is built upon `Qwen2.5-VL-3B-instruct` (a large multimodal encoder), indicating a powerful foundation in vision-language understanding.
- **Action Expert (System 1):** This serves as a "fast executor" that translates grounded representations from the VLM Planner into embodiment-specific motor commands. It employs a diffusion policy (`DiT Actor`, 86M parameters) and leverages a `DINOv2` visual encoder (21M parameters) along with a lightweight state encoder (0.4M parameters).
The total model size is approximately 4.1 billion parameters. A lightweight querying transformer (8.7 MB) bridges the VLM Planner and the Action Expert. This transformer uses cross-attention to map variable-length VLM latent planning embeddings into a fixed set of query tokens, stabilizing expert learning.

**Training Recipe (Spatially Guided Two-Stage Pipeline):**
1. **Stage 1: Spatial Grounding Pre-training:**
 - **Objective:** Optimize the VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-instruct) for strong spatial reasoning and planning, explicitly geared towards robotics, rather than generic VLM pre-training.
 - **Data:** This stage utilizes an extensive dataset of over 2.3 million spatial reasoning samples, combined with internet-scale multimodal corpora. These data are categorized into:
 - **Box QA (879K):** For bounding box detection and object localization (e.g., RefCOCO, RoboRef It, synthetic InternData-M1).
 - **Point QA (832K):** For precise point localization (e.g., Pixmo-Points, RoboPoint, RefSpatial, synthetic InternData-M1).
 - **Trajectory QA (684K):** For end-effector trajectory prediction (e.g., A0 ManiSkill subset, MolmoAct, synthetic InternData-M1 waypoint data).
 - **General QA (637K):** For broader multimodal understanding like image captioning and VQA (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision, InternVL3).
 - **Method:** All robot-specific datasets are reformatted into a unified QA-style structure to align with web-scale data, allowing training under a supervised fine-tuning framework. Only the VLM is optimized in this stage.

2. **Stage 2: Spatially Guided Action Post-training:**
 - **Objective:** Specialize the pre-trained spatial priors for embodiment-specific control by jointly optimizing both the VLM and the Action Expert on demonstration data.
 - **Strategies:**
 - **Spatial Prompting:** Auxiliary spatial cues (e.g., "Figure out how to execute it, then locate the key object needed.") are prepended to task instructions. While the VLM doesn't explicitly respond, these prompts enhance spatial awareness and generalization in manipulation.
 - **Co-training with Spatial Grounding Data:** Training alternates between robot trajectory data (optimizing both VLM backbone and Action Expert with an L2 loss for action prediction) and spatial grounding data (updating only the VLM backbone via next-token prediction). This dual-supervision reinforces spatial reasoning while integrating perception and control.
 - **Gradient Decay:** To prevent the Action Expert's gradients from distorting the VLM's multimodal knowledge, a gradient decay factor (e.g., 0.5) is applied within the querying transformer, attenuating gradients propagated back to the VLM.

**Data Collection and Synthesis:**
A critical component is the large-scale data strategy:
- **InternData-M1:** A synthetic dataset of 244K closed-loop generalizable pick-and-place episodes.
- **Scalable Synthetic Data Engine:** Built on GenManip and Isaac Sim, this pipeline automatically generates diverse manipulation trajectories. It decouples robot planning (grasp generation, motion planning, physics rollout) from visual rendering. This allows for rapid generation of physically feasible and task-complete data with randomized object layouts, lighting, materials, and viewpoints from an asset library of 14K objects, 211 tables, 1.6K textures, and 87 dome lights. The renderer produces rich intermediate outputs like bounding boxes and 2D end-effector trajectories, providing dense supervision for both VLM and VLA training.
- **Real-world Demonstrations:** 22 hours of high-quality teleoperated demonstrations for short-horizon and long-horizon tasks, segmented into subtasks with atomic action annotations, are used for fine-tuning and evaluation in real-world settings.

This comprehensive methodology allows InternVLA-M1 to establish a cohesive link between high-level semantic perception and low-level motion control, crucial for robust instruction following.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InternVLA-M1 demonstrates significant advancements across a wide range of simulated and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks, consistently outperforming existing state-of-the-art models.

- **Superior Performance on Public Benchmarks:**
 - **SimplerEnv (Google Robot and WidowX):** InternVLA-M1 achieves new state-of-the-art results. It surpasses its variant *without* spatial guidance by +14.6% on SimplerEnv Google Robot (overall average success rate), +17% on WidowX. On Google Robot, it shows a +5.9% gain in Visual Matching and +5.3% in Visual Aggregation compared to prior SOTA. For WidowX, it registers a +9.8% gain. The model also exhibits stronger spatial reasoning capabilities in box, point, and trace prediction tasks.
 - **LIBERO Franka:** InternVLA-M1 demonstrates strong improvements, especially on spatial and long-horizon tasks, achieving 98.0% and 92.6% success rates respectively. Its object placement success rate of 99.0% highlights robust object grounding.
- **Effectiveness of Spatially Guided Training:**
 - Ablation studies confirm the crucial role of spatially guided pre-training and action post-training. Omitting spatial data and prompting leads to rapid degradation of spatial grounding capabilities and slower convergence in manipulation tasks.
 - Spatially guided training accelerates convergence, substantially improves manipulation success rates, and enhances spatial grounding accuracy.
 - Analysis of Projection-space Similarity (PSS) shows that spatially guided training increases gradient alignment between spatial grounding and manipulation objectives from 0.25 to 0.42, correlating with better spatial perception and faster task convergence.
 - Overall, InternVLA-M1 maintains superior multimodal understanding and spatial grounding capabilities while achieving high robotic manipulation performance (Table 3).
- **Generalization in Large-scale Simulated Pick-and-Place:**
 - Evaluated on 200 tasks and over 3,000 objects in a custom Isaac-Sim benchmark, InternVLA-M1 (especially with mid-training on InternData-M1) achieves an average improvement of +6.2% over prior works like GR00T N1.5.
 - It exhibits strong generalization to unseen objects, new backgrounds (randomized textures/layouts), and unseen instructions (paraphrased, attribute-level, commonsense rewrites), demonstrating the power of simulation-enhanced visual and language generalization.
- **Robustness in Real-world Clustered Pick-and-Place:**
 - In real-world settings, InternVLA-M1 shows strong instruction-following capabilities. With synthetic co-training (InternData-M1), it achieves a +20.6% success rate on unseen objects and novel configurations in cluttered environments, validating the efficacy of synthetic data in bridging the reality gap and enhancing visual generalization.
 - It also maintains robust performance under diverse linguistic expressions (unseen instructions, by attribute, by spatial reasoning, commonsense).
- **Performance in Long-horizon and Reasoning-intensive Scenarios:**
 - In complex tasks like Desktop Sorting, Sorting Items into Drawers, and Making Sandwiches, InternVLA-M1 consistently surpasses existing baselines (e.g., GR00T N1.5, $\pi_0$) by over 10% in in-distribution settings.
 - Crucially, it demonstrates remarkable adaptability under physical interference and task replanning (e.g., maintaining high success rates when objects are moved or new instructions are given mid-execution), highlighting its unified subtask planning mechanism and resilience to dynamic environments.
 - The VLM planner within InternVLA-M1 (Ours-3B) significantly outperforms other large VLMs (Gemini-2.5 Pro, GPT-5, GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL-72B/3B) in task scheduling for these reasoning scenarios (Table 5), underscoring the benefits of its specialized post-training.

These findings collectively underscore that "spatially guided training" is a potent strategy for building highly capable, generalist robotic systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InternVLA-M1 represents a significant advancement in the field of generalist robotics, offering a principled approach to integrating spatial intelligence into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

- **Unifying Principle for Generalist Robots:** The work establishes "spatially guided training" as a unifying principle, demonstrating that explicit spatial grounding is the critical missing link required to achieve scalable and resilient generalist robots. This shifts the paradigm from purely data-driven VLA models or rigid hierarchical systems towards a more integrated and intelligent approach.
- **Bridging the Perception-Action Gap:** InternVLA-M1 effectively bridges the long-standing gap between abstract linguistic instructions (especially those involving complex spatial relationships) and concrete, continuous robot actions. By systematically training the model to understand "where to act" before "how to act," it endows robots with a more human-like understanding of their environment and tasks.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The framework dramatically improves generalization to novel objects, unseen environments, and diverse instructions. This is a crucial step towards truly general-purpose robots that can operate reliably in unstructured, open-world settings, rather than being confined to narrow, pre-programmed tasks.
- **Robustness in Dynamic Real-World Scenarios:** InternVLA-M1's demonstrated robustness to physical interference and its ability to perform dynamic task replanning in long-horizon, reasoning-intensive tasks are particularly impactful. This resilience is vital for deploying robots in complex, unpredictable human environments where adaptability to unforeseen changes is paramount.
- **Leveraging Synthetic Data for Scalability:** The paper showcases a highly effective strategy for scaling robot learning through large-scale synthetic data generation (InternData-M1). By decoupling planning from rendering, the research group can create vast, diverse, and high-quality training data, significantly mitigating the limitations of costly and time-consuming real-world data collection. This approach opens pathways for accelerating robot policy development and deployment.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The commitment to releasing the code, model, and data (InternVLA-M1, InternData-M1) is a substantial contribution to the research community. This openness will enable other researchers to build upon this foundational work, replicate findings, and further accelerate progress in embodied AI and generalist robotics.
- **Pathway to More Intelligent Agents:** By integrating a VLM Planner (System 2) for high-level reasoning with an Action Expert (System 1) for low-level control, InternVLA-M1 lays the groundwork for more intelligent and autonomous robotic agents. This dual-system architecture with explicit spatial prompting allows for complex task decomposition and robust execution, moving closer to robots that can genuinely "think" and "act" in complex ways.

In conclusion, InternVLA-M1 offers a comprehensive and effective framework that not only pushes the boundaries of robot instruction following but also provides a unifying principle for the development of scalable, resilient, and truly generalist robots capable of navigating and interacting intelligently with the physical world.
