# VidBot: Learning Generalizable 3D Actions from In-the-Wild 2D Human Videos for Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VidBot reconstructs metric-scale 3D contact points and interaction trajectories from unconstrained 2D human videos, enabling zero-shot transfer of diverse manipulation skills to novel robot embodiments and environments. The framework demonstrated an 88.2% average success rate in simulation and an 80% success rate across various tasks on real-world robots like Stretch 3 and Spot.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic imitation learning relies on costly, time-consuming, and labor-intensive teleoperated demonstrations, leading to data scarcity for diverse tasks and robot embodiments.
- Existing methods for human-to-robot skill transfer often require constrained lab environments (e.g., static cameras, depth sensors) or extract only 2D pixel-level actions, limiting real-world diversity and direct robot deployment.
- Generalizing learned robotic manipulation skills to novel environments and different robot morphologies in a zero-shot manner remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- A pipeline is developed to extract metric-scale 3D contact points and interaction trajectories from raw, in-the-wild 2D human videos by leveraging Structure-from-Motion, depth foundation models, and hand-object detection.
- A coarse-to-fine affordance learning model is introduced, where a coarse stage predicts high-level 3D contact and goal points, and a fine stage uses a conditional diffusion model to generate detailed 3D interaction trajectories.
- Differentiable cost functions (multi-goal, collision avoidance, contact normal guidance) are integrated during test-time trajectory generation, enhancing robustness and allowing context-aware planning for zero-shot transfer.

## Results

- Achieved an **88.2%** average success rate across 13 diverse manipulation tasks in simulation, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by up to **30%** (e.g., **20%** over Octo, **30%** over VRB).
- Demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to real-world robots (Hello Robot Stretch 3 and Boston Dynamics Spot), achieving an **80.0%** success rate over 55 trials on household tasks in novel environments.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of the coarse affordance prediction stage, multi-goal guidance, and collision avoidance/contact normal guidance for the framework's superior performance and generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abundant in-the-wild 2D human videos can be transformed into a scalable data source for learning generalizable 3D robotic skills, overcoming limitations of costly robot-specific data collection.
> - The integration of geometric computer vision techniques (SfM, depth estimation) with modern AI models (diffusion, foundation models) enables robust extraction and learning of 3D action representations from unconstrained video data.
> - A coarse-to-fine learning architecture combined with test-time cost guidance improves generalization and allows for zero-shot transfer of complex 3D manipulation skills across diverse robot embodiments and environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces a significant challenge: acquiring diverse and scalable data for training policies. Traditional methods, primarily Imitation Learning (IL), rely on costly, time-consuming, and labor-intensive teleoperated demonstrations. While large-scale datasets like Open X-Embodiment and DROID have emerged, collecting data for the combinatorial explosion of robot embodiments, tasks, and environments remains a bottleneck.

This paper addresses this scalability issue by proposing to leverage **"in-the-wild" human videos** available on the internet. This approach is not entirely new; previous works have explored human-to-robot skill transfer. However, these often suffer from limitations such as requiring static cameras, depth sensors, or MoCap systems, leading to constrained "in-lab" settings lacking real-world diversity. Other efforts have focused on learning visual representations from human videos for visuomotor policies, but still typically require task-specific teleoperated data for fine-tuning. More recently, some work has extracted agent-agnostic interaction trajectories, but these are often simplified as 2D pixel-level actions, limiting direct robot deployment.

VidBot distinguishes itself by focusing on **3D affordance learning** (contact points and interaction trajectories with spatial awareness) directly from **RGB-only, in-the-wild monocular human videos**, and enabling **zero-shot transfer** to diverse robot embodiments. This overcomes several limitations of prior art:
- **Beyond 2D pixel-level actions:** Unlike methods that predict 2D pixel-level motions (e.g., VRB), VidBot extracts 3D representations, which are crucial for spatial reasoning and direct robot deployment across different morphologies.
- **Overcoming data scarcity for 3D affordance:** Instead of relying on expensive 3D sensing setups or synthetic data, VidBot proposes a novel pipeline to reconstruct metric-scale 3D affordance from readily available 2D human videos. This is a significant step towards scalable data acquisition for 3D robot learning.
- **Generalization and Zero-Shot Transfer:** The framework is designed to learn embodiment-agnostic representations and uses a coarse-to-fine learning strategy combined with test-time cost guidance, which explicitly accounts for scene context and robot morphology, enabling zero-shot deployment without further fine-tuning.
- **Leveraging Foundational Models:** The work integrates modern computer vision foundational models (e.g., depth foundation models, object detection, segmentation) and Structure-from-Motion techniques to robustly extract 3D information from unconstrained videos, showcasing how these advancements can bridge the gap for robot learning.
- **Diffusion Models in Robotics:** VidBot employs diffusion models for fine-grained trajectory generation, leveraging their power to approximate complex data distributions while enhancing them with novel cost-guided mechanisms for robust, context-aware planning.

In essence, VidBot attempts to bridge the embodiment gap and data scalability challenge by transforming abundant, unconstrained human video data into actionable, generalizable 3D robotic skills, pushing the boundaries of zero-shot robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind VidBot is to **enable future robots to perform a wide variety of household tasks without extensive, costly physical robot learning**. The authors argue that leveraging the vast amount of existing "in-the-wild" human videos on the internet offers a highly scalable solution.

The key objectives are articulated around two central questions:
1. **How can 3D actionable knowledge be extracted from raw RGB-only human videos?** This objective addresses the fundamental data acquisition challenge. The goal is to develop a robust pipeline that can reconstruct temporally consistent, metric-scale 3D affordance representations (contact points and interaction trajectories) from unconstrained monocular videos, making them agnostic to human or robot embodiments.
2. **How can this extracted knowledge be reliably transferred to novel environments and new robot embodiments in a zero-shot manner?** This objective focuses on the learning and generalization aspects. The aim is to create an affordance model capable of performing tasks in entirely new scenarios and with different robot morphologies, without requiring any further task-specific or robot-specific demonstrations.

In summary, the authors aim to develop a framework that makes robot learning from human demonstrations more scalable, more generalizable, and less reliant on costly in-lab data collection, ultimately paving the way for truly versatile household robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VidBot is a comprehensive framework that integrates 3D affordance acquisition from human videos with a novel coarse-to-fine affordance learning model, enhanced by test-time cost guidance.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The framework aims to learn an embodiment-agnostic affordance model $\pi(\{ \tilde{I}, \tilde{D}\}, l)$ that takes an RGB-D frame (image $\tilde{I}$, depth $\tilde{D}$) and a language instruction $l$ as input. The output affordance representation $a$ is defined as 3D contact points $c \in \mathbb{R}^{N_c \times 3}$ and 3D interaction trajectories $\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times 3}$, both expressed in the observation camera's frame.

#### 3.2. 3D Affordance Acquisition from Human Videos

This pipeline converts raw, in-the-wild human videos into structured 3D affordance data:
- **Data Preparation:** For a given video, an SfM system (e.g., COLMAP) estimates camera intrinsics, scale-unaware per-frame poses, and sparse landmarks. A metric-depth foundation model (e.g., ZoeDepth, Metric3D) predicts dense depth for each frame. Hand-object detection and segmentation models (e.g., SAM) identify hand and in-contact object masks. A video inpainting model is used to create "hand-less" frames for robust scene representation.
- **Consistent Pose Optimization:** This crucial step corrects SfM outputs to a metric scale and refines poses for temporal consistency. A global scale $s_g$ is optimized by minimizing depth discrepancies between projected landmarks and predicted depths in static (non-hand/object) regions. Further per-frame pose and scale refinements are performed to ensure multi-view depth consistency, addressing errors from dynamic human movements.
- **Affordance Extraction:** Using the refined poses and scales, 3D hand center points are extracted from each frame and transformed to a common frame (e.g., the first frame). These transformed hand points form the interaction trajectory $\hat{\tau}$. Downsampled hand points from the first frame provide contact points $\hat{c}$, and from the last frame, goal points $\hat{g}$. This process provides the pseudo-ground truth 3D labels for training. The EpicKitchens-100 dataset and its SfM results are leveraged.

#### 3.3. Coarse-to-Fine Affordance Learning

The core affordance model $\pi$ is factorized into a coarse model $\pi_c$ and a fine model $\pi_f$.

##### 3.3.1. Coarse Affordance Prediction

- **Objective:** To extract macro actionable information (contact points and goal points) from high-dimensional image space.
- **Architecture:** The model consists of two networks: $\pi_{goal}^c$ for goal points and $\pi_{cont}^c$ for contact points.
 - An off-the-shelf open-set object detector crops RGB-D images of the objects of interest.
 - **Goal Prediction:** $\pi_{goal}^c$ predicts a goal heatmap (per-pixel probabilities) and corresponding depth. It uses a visual encoder (e.g., from a Vision Transformer), RoI Pooling for object-centric features, an MLP for bounding box features, and a frozen CLIP model for language instruction embeddings. A Perceiver module fuses global context with conditional features.
 - **Contact Prediction:** $\pi_{cont}^c$ similarly predicts a contact heatmap, but omits depth prediction as contact points are assumed to be on object surfaces with valid depth. Only language features are fused.
- **Output:** Pixel coordinates sampled from heatmaps, combined with queried/predicted depths and camera intrinsics, are lifted to 3D to obtain coarse 3D goal points $g$ and contact points $c$.

##### 3.3.2. Fine Affordance Prediction

- **Objective:** To plan fine-grained interaction trajectories conditioned on the coarse predictions.
- **Approach:** This stage is modeled as a conditional diffusion denoising process.
 - **Conditional Input:** The model receives the highest-probability goal point $\bar{g}$ and contact point $\bar{c}$, object feature embedding from a Vision Transformer, and language instruction embedding.
 - **Spatial Awareness:** To integrate scene geometry, a voxelized TSDF map $U$ (from RGB-D) is processed by a 3D U-Net to yield latent features. These spatial features are interpolated along the current denoised trajectory $\tau^k$ and concatenated as input to the denoiser.
 - **Denoiser:** A 1D U-Net (similar to) directly infers the unnoised trajectory $\bar{\tau}^0$ in each step of the diffusion process, which is then used to compute the mean for the reverse (denoising) process.

#### 3.4. Cost-Guided Trajectory Generation

To enhance generalization and robustness, especially given potential noise in extracted data and the embodiment gap, VidBot integrates differentiable cost functions during test-time trajectory generation:
- **Multi-Goal Cost ($J_{goal}$):** Minimizes the distance from the trajectory endpoint to the closest goal point within the predicted set $g$, allowing for selection of the most suitable goal.
- **Collision Avoidance Cost ($J_{collide}$):** Penalizes trajectories that lead to collisions between the agent's (robot's) hand/gripper model and the environment/object, querying values from the pre-computed TSDF map.
- **Contact Normal Guidance ($J_{normal}$):** Encourages the trajectory to align with the object's surface normal at the contact point, providing an intuitive geometric constraint.
- **Total Cost:** $J = \lambda_g J_{goal} + \lambda_c J_{collide} + \lambda_n J_{normal}$.
- **Guidance Mechanism:** At each denoising step, the gradients of $J$ with respect to the noisy trajectory $\tau^k$ are used to perturb the unnoised prediction $\bar{\tau}^0$, guiding the diffusion process towards plausible and context-aware trajectories. The final cost value $J$ also serves as a heuristic for selecting the optimal plan among multiple generated trajectories.

#### 3.5. Affordance Models Training

- **Coarse Model Training:** Loss functions $L_g$ and $L_c$ combine Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) for heatmap probabilities, L2 loss for goal depth regression, and an auxiliary vector field regression loss for direction.
- **Fine Model Training:** The fine model $\pi_f$ is trained with an L2 loss $L_f$ between its unnoised prediction $\bar{\tau}^0$ and the ground-truth extracted trajectory $\hat{\tau}$, with noise injected according to the diffusion process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VidBot's effectiveness was extensively evaluated in both simulation and real-world settings, demonstrating significant advancements.

#### 4.1. Simulation Environments

- **Tasks:** Evaluated on 13 diverse household manipulation tasks from FrankaKitchen, PartManip, and ManiSkill, encompassing actions like opening, pushing, pulling, and picking up.
- **Metrics:** Success rate (%) across 15 trials per task (5 trajectories from 3 viewpoints).
- **Baselines:** Compared against:
 - **Simulator-trained:** GAPartNet, Where2Act.
 - **Teleoperated data pre-trained:** Octo (fine-tuned on VidBot's extracted data).
 - **Human video trained:** VRB (pixel-level actions, lifted to 3D using), GFlow (pixel-level flows, with access to privileged depth/camera data).
- **Results (Table 1):** VidBot achieved an **88.2% average success rate**, significantly outperforming the runner-up, Octo, by nearly 20% (69.2%). Other baselines like VRB (59.0%), GFlow (61.0%), GAPartNet (51.1%), and Where2Act (58.5%) performed considerably worse. This highlights VidBot's ability to learn and transfer complex 3D actions from in-the-wild 2D human videos more effectively than existing methods. The paper notes that VRB and GFlow struggled with accurate contact region inference, so for fair comparison, VidBot's contact configurations were used to standardize. The improvement over VRB (which uses the same video source) by ~30% is particularly noteworthy, attributed to VidBot's explicit 3D priors and learning strategy.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Table 2)

Experiments on a subset of 6 tasks validated the necessity of VidBot's design choices:
- **Impact of Coarse Affordance Prediction (V1: w/o coarse goal pred.):** A significant performance drop from 85.6% to 57.8%, confirming that the coarse stage is crucial for simplifying complex action generation by providing high-level goal configurations.
- **Impact of Cost Guidance:**
 - **Multi-goal guidance (V2: w/o multi-goal guide.):** Largest drop of 12.3%, from 85.6% to 73.3%. This confirms that adapting to multiple potential goals and correcting coarse-stage errors is vital.
 - **Contact-normal guidance (V3: w/o contact-normal guide.):** Performance dropped to 76.7%, indicating its importance as an intuitive geometric hint.
 - **Collision avoidance guidance (V4: w/o collision-avoid. guide.):** Drop to 77.8%, especially effective for pick-up tasks (26.7% increase), demonstrating its role in safe interaction.
- **Impact of Cost-informed Heuristics (V5: w/o cost-informed heuristics):** An 11.1% drop to 74.5% when randomly selecting trajectories instead of using the final guidance cost, highlighting the utility of the cost value as a robust selection criterion.
These ablations confirm that both the coarse-to-fine architecture and the test-time cost guidance are integral to VidBot's superior performance and generalization.

#### 4.3. Robot Learning Applications (Figure 4)

VidBot's learned affordance model serves as a strong prior for downstream robot learning tasks:
- **Visual Goal-Reaching:** In tasks where the agent also accesses a desired object configuration image, VidBot significantly outperformed baselines, showing faster convergence and better overall success rates. This was achieved by probabilistically replacing predicted goal points with those from a buffer of successful trajectories.
- **Exploration:** For tasks maximizing environment changes, VidBot demonstrated consistent and significant improvements over baselines in "coincidental success" (achieving desired configurations without explicit access to them), again by guiding the sampling process with past successful trajectories.

#### 4.4. Real Robot Experiments (Figure 5, Figure 6)

- **Platforms:** Hello Robot Stretch 3 and Boston Dynamics Spot, both equipped with RGB-D cameras.
- **Tasks:** Household tasks within robot capabilities (pushing drawer, opening cabinet, taking tissue) across three human-suited environments.
- **Results:** Achieved an **80.0% success rate over 55 trials**. This critical result demonstrates VidBot's embodiment-agnostic nature and successful zero-shot transferability to real-world robot systems, despite variations in robot morphology and real-world complexities. Qualitative results (Figure 5) visually compare VidBot's accurate 3D affordance predictions against VRB's ambiguous 2D outputs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VidBot represents a significant stride towards **scalable and generalized robot manipulation** by effectively bridging the data gap using readily available human videos. Its core contributions and impact are:

- **Unlocking Scalable Data Sources:** By demonstrating that high-quality 3D affordance data can be extracted from unconstrained 2D human videos, VidBot offers a powerful alternative to costly robot-specific data collection. This could drastically accelerate the development of robotic skills.
- **Bridging the Embodiment Gap:** The framework learns embodiment-agnostic 3D action representations, crucial for enabling robots of different morphologies (e.g., mobile manipulators like Stretch or Spot) to interpret and execute the same learned skills. The successful zero-shot deployment on real robots is a strong validation of this.
- **Enhanced Generalization through Coarse-to-Fine Learning and Cost Guidance:** The novel coarse-to-fine architecture, combined with test-time differentiable cost functions (multi-goal, collision avoidance, contact normal), significantly boosts the model's robustness and adaptability to novel scenes and robot embodiments. This allows for context-aware interaction planning, which is vital for real-world uncertainty.
- **Practical Utility in Downstream Tasks:** Its demonstrated superior performance in robot learning applications like visual goal-reaching and exploration positions VidBot as a strong prior for improving efficiency and performance in various robot learning paradigms.
- **Future Directions for Robotic AI:** VidBot paves the way for a new paradigm where robots can learn complex skills by "watching" humans perform everyday tasks, fostering a more intuitive and accessible approach to robot programming.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors acknowledge that the quality of the extracted data is dependent on the accuracy of the depth foundation models and the SfM pipeline. This suggests an avenue for future improvement by integrating more advanced, learning-based SfM frameworks or more robust foundation models. Another limitation is the precision of tasks; while effective for broad manipulation, highly precise tasks (e.g., unscrewing caps) remain challenging. Future work includes exploring multi-modal affordance data using wearable devices to enable learning of such intricate tasks, potentially offering more direct and precise human demonstrations.

Overall, VidBot offers a compelling vision for robot learning, showcasing how leveraging vast internet resources and advanced AI can lead to more versatile, generalizable, and deployable robotic systems for household assistance and beyond.
