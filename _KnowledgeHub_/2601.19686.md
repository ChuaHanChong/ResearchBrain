# Video-KTR: Reinforcing Video Reasoning via Key Token Attribution

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework named Video-KTR improves multimodal large language models' video reasoning by selectively reinforcing key tokens identified via visual, temporal, and entropy attributions. This method achieved 42.7% accuracy on the Video-Holmes benchmark, exceeding GPT-4o's performance.

## Problem

- Existing MLLMs often use coarse sequence-level rewards or simplistic token selection in reinforcement learning (RL) for video, failing to capture fine-grained dependencies.
- Current methods neglect intricate relationships between visual inputs, temporal dynamics, and linguistic outputs, leading to poor credit assignment and underutilization of visual/temporal cues.
- This leads to models over-relying on linguistic priors, increasing hallucinations, and making the reasoning process less interpretable.

## Method

- Video-KTR introduces a ==modality-aware policy shaping framework== that leverages token-level attribution signals for selective reinforcement learning in video reasoning tasks.
- It identifies =="key tokens"== using three distinct attribution signals: ==visual-aware== (via counterfactual masking), ==temporal-aware== (via frame shuffling), and ==entropy-aware== (via predictive uncertainty).
- Only these identified key tokens contribute to the reinforcement learning update objective, applying a binary mask to focus training on semantically important and modality-sensitive elements.

## Results

- Video-KTR achieved **42.7%** accuracy on the Video-Holmes benchmark, outperforming GPT-4o (**42.0%**) and all open-source baselines, and scored **53.1%** on VideoMMMU and **66.6%** on MMVU.
- The method consistently surpasses vanilla GRPO and other token-weighted RL baselines in controlled experiments, showing gains in complex tasks like "Timeline Analysis" and "Core Theme Inference."
- Analysis confirms that selected tokens are semantically rich, while unselected ones are typically low-information function words; the framework also introduces marginal computational overhead and is effective on smaller MLLMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining visual, temporal, and entropy-aware attribution signals for token selection provides complementary benefits, yielding the most robust and accurate performance for video reasoning.
> - Selective reinforcement learning with a "hard top-r%" token selection strategy, based on these attributions, effectively filters noise and concentrates learning on high-value information.
> - Each attribution strategy targets linguistically distinct token types (e.g., nouns for visual, verbs for temporal), confirming their effectiveness in capturing different aspects of multimodal relevance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on Video-KTR is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: Large Language Models (LLMs), Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for AI reasoning. It specifically targets the nuanced challenges of video understanding and reasoning, an area that has seen comparatively less exploration than static image understanding within the MLLM and RL paradigms.

**The Evolving Landscape of LLMs and MLLMs:** LLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in complex reasoning, often enhanced through RL techniques (e.g., GRPO). This success naturally extended to MLLMs, which integrate visual information, leading to significant advancements in tasks like visual question answering and image captioning. However, most of these advances primarily target static images.

**Gaps in Current Video Reasoning Approaches:** The authors identify several critical limitations in existing video reasoning methods:
1. **Coarse-grained RL:** Many approaches rely on "coarse sequence-level rewards" (e.g., a single reward for the entire output sequence) or simplistic "single-factor token selection" (e.g., solely based on predictive uncertainty/entropy). This broad approach struggles to capture the fine-grained dependencies inherent in video.
2. **Neglect of Modality-Specific Links:** Current methods often overlook the intricate relationships among visual inputs, temporal dynamics, and linguistic outputs. This deficiency leads to a lack of precise credit assignment to specific parts of the input or output.
3. **Underutilization of Visual and Temporal Cues:** Without explicit modeling of modality-specific dependencies, models tend to underutilize visual evidence and over-rely on linguistic priors, increasing the risk of "hallucinations" (generating plausible but factually incorrect information).
4. **Optimization Noise:** Coarse global assumptions, such as penalizing predictions on shuffled frames at a sequence level, can introduce optimization noise, as some tasks might be solvable by static cues, making global temporal penalties counterproductive.
5. **Limited Interpretability:** The lack of fine-grained correspondence between multimodal inputs and output tokens limits both reasoning accuracy and the interpretability of model decisions.

**Video-KTR's Position and Contribution:** Video-KTR directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel *modality-aware policy shaping framework*. It shifts the paradigm from coarse, sequence-level RL to a *selective, token-level reinforcement learning* approach. By unifying three distinct attribution signals—visual-aware, temporal-aware, and entropy-aware tokens—the framework provides a more granular and sophisticated mechanism for identifying and reinforcing critical reasoning steps. This represents a significant advancement by:
- **Enhancing Fine-grained Alignment:** Explicitly linking output tokens to their dependency on visual and temporal cues.
- **Improving Modality-Awareness:** Moving beyond generic reasoning difficulty to understand *why* a token is important (i.e., due to visual information, temporal order, or inherent uncertainty).
- **Boosting Accuracy and Interpretability:** Focusing learning on truly informative content while filtering out noise, leading to more accurate and more easily interpretable reasoning pathways.

The paper claims to be the first to integrate modality-aware token selection into RL for video reasoning, marking a notable step forward in making MLLMs more adept at comprehending the dynamic and complex nature of video content. This work builds upon and significantly extends previous efforts in video MLLMs (e.g., Video-R1, T-GRPO) by providing a more precise and comprehensive framework for token-level optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central goal of this research is to significantly enhance the video reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by addressing the limitations of existing reinforcement learning (RL) strategies in this domain.

**The primary objectives are:**

1. **Develop a Fine-Grained RL Framework for Video Reasoning:** To move beyond the limitations of current RL methods that typically rely on coarse sequence-level rewards or single-factor token selection, which fail to capture the rich interplay between visual, temporal, and linguistic information in videos.
2. **Establish Modality-Aware Connections:** To create explicit, fine-grained links between specific output tokens and their dependence on visual inputs and temporal dynamics within a video. This involves understanding *which* parts of the model's linguistic output are directly tied to *what* is seen and *when* it occurs in the video.
3. **Improve Reasoning Accuracy:** By focusing RL on the most semantically informative and modality-sensitive tokens, the objective is to reduce optimization noise and enhance the model's ability to make accurate predictions in complex video reasoning tasks.
4. **Increase Interpretability:** To provide a clearer understanding of the model's reasoning process by explicitly identifying and reinforcing the "key tokens" that drive its multimodal decisions. This helps in understanding *why* a model arrives at a particular conclusion.
5. **Create a Robust and Generalizable Solution:** To design a framework that is effective across a range of video reasoning and general video understanding benchmarks, is computationally efficient, and can be integrated as a "drop-in extension" to existing RL pipelines.

**The motivation for these objectives stems from several critical challenges in the current state of video MLLMs:**

- **Underexplored Video Domain:** While RL has proven powerful for LLMs and image-based MLLMs, its application to video reasoning is comparatively less mature, leaving a significant gap in the robust handling of temporal and dynamic visual information.
- **Inadequate Temporal Modeling:** Many existing approaches overlook explicit temporal dependencies and causal structures, which are fundamental for understanding events, actions, and narratives in videos. Coarse temporal constraints (e.g., penalizing shuffled frames globally) can be noisy and inefficient.
- **Lack of Visual Grounding:** Without precise semantic alignment between visual inputs and output tokens, MLLMs often struggle to fully leverage visual evidence, leading to over-reliance on linguistic priors and an increased propensity for generating "hallucinations" rather than factually grounded responses.
- **Inefficient Learning:** Treating all tokens equally during reward assignment in long video reasoning sequences dilutes learning signals. This makes training less efficient and prevents the model from prioritizing truly critical information.
- **Limited Diagnosability:** The black-box nature of many MLLMs, especially in complex reasoning tasks, makes it difficult to ascertain *what* evidence (visual, temporal) informed *which* part of the output, hindering error analysis and model improvement.

By addressing these motivations, Video-KTR aims to provide a more nuanced, efficient, and interpretable approach to training MLLMs for superior video reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Video-KTR proposes a modality-aware policy shaping framework that leverages token-level attribution signals to selectively reinforce critical tokens during reinforcement learning (RL) for video reasoning tasks. The core idea is to identify and focus learning on semantically important and modality-sensitive tokens, thereby improving accuracy and interpretability while filtering out low-value content.

#### 3.1. Multi-Perspective Token Importance Analysis

The framework's cornerstone is its ability to identify "key tokens" through three distinct attribution signals:

1. **Visual-Aware Tokens:** These tokens are crucial for grounding linguistic outputs in perceptual evidence, enabling tasks like object recognition, reference resolution, and appearance description.
 - **Identification Method:** Counterfactual masking. For a given video `v` and prompt `t`, the model generates a response `ˆy` with logits `z_i` for each token `y_i`. The approach compares the log-probability of `y_i` under the full visual context (`v`) with its log-probability when the visual input is masked (`~v = 0`).
 - **Score Calculation:** The visual attribution score `Δvis_i` is the difference between `log softmax(z_visible_i)_y_i` and `log softmax(z_masked_i)_y_i`. A larger `Δvis_i` indicates stronger dependence on visual input. Examples include nouns like "person," "door," or adjectives like "blue."

2. **Temporal-Aware Tokens:** These tokens are vital for understanding event order, causality, and temporal dynamics within a video, allowing the model to track changes and reason about progression.
 - **Identification Method:** Frame shuffling. Similar to visual attribution, the log-probability of `y_i` is compared between the original ordered video frames (`v`) and a temporally shuffled version (`ˆv`).
 - **Score Calculation:** The temporal attribution score `Δtemp_i` is the difference between `log softmax(z_ordered_i)_y_i` and `log softmax(z_shuffled_i)_y_i`. A larger `Δtemp_i` signifies greater sensitivity to the video's temporal structure. Examples include temporal adverbs or verbs like "first," "then," and "appear."

3. **Entropy-Aware Tokens:** While visual and temporal attributions capture modality-specific dependencies, entropy-aware tokens address reasoning-critical elements unrelated to direct perception or sequence. These often indicate points of predictive uncertainty or logical pivots.
 - **Identification Method:** Predictive entropy. The entropy `H(i)` of the token's predicted probability distribution is calculated: `H(i) = - Σ_w p(z_i = w) log p(z_i = w)`.
 - **Score Calculation:** High-entropy tokens (e.g., "however," "wait") signal points of semantic ambiguity, conflicting cues, or implicit causality, making them crucial for logical reasoning.

#### 3.2. Token Selection and Policy Update

1. **Token Selection:** After computing attribution scores for each token, the top `r%` of tokens are selected from *each* of the three strategies (visual-aware, temporal-aware, entropy-aware). The final set of "key reasoning tokens" `S` is the union of these three subsets (`S = S_vis ∪ S_temp ∪ S_ent`). This ensures that tokens critical from any of the three perspectives are included.

2. **Reinforcement Learning Framework:** Video-KTR is built upon the GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) framework, a variant of PPO designed for optimizing LLMs. The standard GRPO objective is:
 `J_GRPO(θ) = E_{q,{o_i}}[1/G Σ_{i=1}^G 1/|o_i| Σ_{t=1}^{|o_i|} min(r_i,t * ˆA_i,t, clip(r_i,t, 1-ϵ, 1+ϵ) * ˆA_i,t)]`
 where `r_i,t` is the token-level likelihood ratio and `ˆA_i,t` is the advantage estimate.

3. **Selective Policy Update:** To integrate the token importance, a binary mask `m_i,t ∈ {0, 1}` is applied to the GRPO objective:
 `J_Video-KTR(θ) = E_{q,{o_i}}[1/G Σ_{i=1}^G 1/|o_i| Σ_{t=1}^{|o_i|} m_i,t * min(r_i,t * ˆA_i,t, clip(r_i,t, 1-ϵ, 1+ϵ) * ˆA_i,t)]`
 Only tokens where `m_i,t = 1` (i.e., tokens identified as important in set `S`) contribute to the loss and receive gradient updates. This ensures that training focuses on semantically rich segments (e.g., temporally anchored verbs, visually descriptive nouns, discourse pivots) while filtering out low-information function words.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** Qwen-2.5-VL-7B backbone, pre-trained with a Video-R1 SFT checkpoint.
- **Training Data:** A curated, difficulty-sampled subset of Video-R1 (approx. 15K high-quality samples) combined with 1.5K samples from Video-Holmes.
- **Input Constraints:** Maximum 16 frames (extendable to 64 for inference) with a resolution of 128x28x28 pixels.
- **Training Parameters:** Learning rate 2e-6, global batch size 32, rollout batch size 256, max sequence length 16384, KL divergence coefficient `β=0.4`, 8 sampled responses per prompt at temperature 1.0.
- **Benchmarks:** Five challenging video benchmarks: Video-Holmes, VideoMMMU, MMVU (reasoning-oriented), TempCompass, and VideoMME (general understanding).

This methodology creates a precise and efficient learning mechanism that targets the most informative components of the model's output, leading to enhanced multimodal reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Video-KTR consistently demonstrates state-of-the-art (SOTA) or highly competitive performance across a range of challenging video reasoning and general video understanding benchmarks, validating the efficacy of its modality-aware, token-level reinforcement learning approach.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance on Benchmarks

- **Video-Holmes (Reasoning):** Video-KTR achieves 42.7% accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (42.0%) and all other open-source baselines, and closely matching proprietary models like Gemini-1.5-Pro (41.3%). This is a particularly strong result given Video-Holmes' focus on complex deductive reasoning and integration of dispersed narrative cues from suspenseful short films.
- **Other Reasoning Benchmarks:** On knowledge-intensive benchmarks, Video-KTR attains 53.1% on VideoMMMU and 66.6% on MMVU(mc), further highlighting its robust complex reasoning capabilities.
- **General Video Understanding:** The model also delivers competitive performance on TempCompass (73.5%) and VideoMME (62.5%), demonstrating that its specialized reasoning improvements do not compromise broad video comprehension.
- **Scalability with Frames:** Performance consistently improves with an increased number of input frames (from 16 to 64), underscoring the method's robustness and scalability for longer temporal sequences.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Other RL Methods (RQ1)

- In controlled experiments using identical datasets and training recipes, Video-KTR consistently outperforms vanilla GRPO, T-GRPO, and TW-GRPO. For instance, on Video-Holmes, Video-KTR achieves 41.6% compared to Vanilla GRPO's 38.8%, T-GRPO's 39.7%, and TW-GRPO's 39.5%. This demonstrates the inherent effectiveness of Video-KTR's design, independent of data quality or quantity.
- On Video-Holmes subtasks, Video-KTR shows significant gains in "Timeline Analysis" (TA) and "Core Theme Inference" (CTI), which require advanced temporal reasoning and holistic content understanding, validating its enhanced temporal and structural reasoning.

#### 4.3. Impact of Attribution Signals (RQ2)

- Each attribution signal (Entropy-aware (E), Visual-aware (V), Temporal-aware (T)) individually improves performance over vanilla GRPO. Temporal-aware tokens (T) yield the largest individual gain on Video-Holmes (42.1% vs 38.8% for GRPO).
- However, relying on a single signal can sometimes be detrimental to other benchmarks (e.g., Temporal-aware tokens alone slightly hurt Video-MMMU).
- The full combination of all three signals (E+V+T) consistently achieves the best overall results (e.g., 41.6% on Video-Holmes, 52.6% on Video-MMMU, 65.9% on MMVU), confirming their complementary roles and the benefit of their joint leverage for accurate and robust policy optimization.

#### 4.4. Linguistic Divergence in Token Selection (RQ3)

- Analysis of Part-of-Speech (POS) ratios reveals distinct linguistic distributions for each attribution strategy:
 - **Visual-aware tokens:** Predominantly NOUNs (24.8%), reflecting their role in object grounding.
 - **Temporal-aware tokens:** Emphasize VERBs (21.2%) and PRONs (11.0%), indicative of action transitions and temporal references.
 - **Entropy-aware tokens:** Show more ADVs (8.8%), capturing discourse modulation and uncertainty.
- Word clouds visually confirm that selected tokens are semantically rich (e.g., "object," "person," "appear," "hold," "wait," "consider"), while unselected tokens are dominated by low-information function words (auxiliary verbs, pronouns, determiners, prepositions, punctuation). This validates the attribution mechanism's ability to filter noise and prioritize salient cues.

#### 4.5. Token Weighting Strategies (RQ4) and Distance Metrics (RQ5)

- **Weighting:** A "hard top-20%" selection strategy for tokens consistently outperforms all "soft" weighting variants (Softmax, Sigmoid, Linear, Exponential), indicating that strict selection leads to clearer credit assignment and prevents dilution of learning signals. The union of selected tokens results in approximately 40% of tokens being updated, showing distinct contributions from each attribution signal.
- **Distance Metrics:** The simple log-probability difference (used for `Δvis` and `Δtemp`) is robust, efficient, and outperforms more complex distributional shift metrics (L1/L2 norms, KL/JS divergences, cosine similarity, Hellinger distance), making it a reliable choice.

#### 4.6. Optimization Dynamics and Generalizability

- **Gradient Analysis (Appendix B.1):** Video-KTR exhibits lower loss variance, stronger gradient signals, and higher gradient alignment during training compared to vanilla GRPO. Gradients from selected critical tokens are three times higher and much more directionally consistent than those from masked tokens, indicating more meaningful and stable updates.
- **Computational Cost (Appendix B.3):** Video-KTR introduces only marginal overhead (e.g., ~0.5 hours more training time, minimal memory/latency increase) compared to baselines, making it computationally efficient.
- **Generalizability to Smaller Models (Appendix B.5):** The method consistently outperforms vanilla GRPO when applied to the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model across various frame settings, demonstrating its robustness and effectiveness even for smaller-scale MLLMs.
- **Qualitative Examples (Appendix B.6):** Illustrative examples from Video-Holmes clearly show Video-KTR's enhanced ability to identify key visual/temporal cues, infer complex relationships, motivations, and correct temporal sequences, contrasting with failures or misinterpretations by T-GRPO.

In summary, Video-KTR sets a new benchmark for video reasoning in MLLMs by introducing a principled, efficient, and interpretable token-level RL framework that effectively leverages modality-aware attribution signals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Video-KTR represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal large language models, particularly in their application to video understanding and reasoning. Its contributions have both immediate practical implications and lay a strong foundation for future research directions.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Video Reasoning Capabilities

The most direct impact of Video-KTR is its ability to significantly boost the accuracy of MLLMs on complex video reasoning tasks. By surpassing sophisticated proprietary models like GPT-4o on benchmarks such as Video-Holmes, Video-KTR demonstrates a new frontier in how open-source models can analyze, interpret, and deduce information from dynamic visual content. This improvement is crucial for applications that demand deep comprehension of events, causality, and narratives within videos.

#### 5.2. Improved Interpretability and Trustworthiness

By explicitly identifying and selectively reinforcing "key tokens" that are either visually-aware, temporal-aware, or entropy-aware, Video-KTR offers a level of interpretability often lacking in complex deep learning models. Researchers and developers can gain insights into *what* specific visual cues, temporal sequences, or points of uncertainty the model is attending to when generating its linguistic output. This transparency is vital for debugging, identifying biases, and building greater trust in AI systems deployed in critical domains.

#### 5.3. A Robust and Generalizable Framework

Video-KTR is presented as a "simple, drop-in extension to RL for complex video reasoning," indicating its ease of integration into existing MLLM training pipelines. Its proven effectiveness across various benchmarks, including both reasoning-focused and general video understanding tasks, and its successful application to a smaller MLLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) highlight its robustness and generalizability. The marginal computational overhead further ensures its practical viability for broader adoption.

#### 5.4. Foundation for Future Multimodal AI Research

The methodology of modality-aware token attribution provides a powerful blueprint for future research. This framework could be extended to incorporate other modalities such as audio, motion sensor data, or even physiological signals, creating richer multimodal understanding systems. Furthermore, the core idea of fine-grained, selective reinforcement learning could be adapted for other complex multimodal tasks beyond reasoning, such as video captioning, dense event captioning, or temporal grounding, where precise alignment of output with specific input features is beneficial.

#### 5.5. Real-World Application Potential

The enhanced video reasoning capabilities unlocked by Video-KTR have profound implications for a multitude of real-world applications:
- **Autonomous Systems:** Improved understanding of dynamic environments from video feeds in autonomous vehicles and robotics.
- **Content Creation and Management:** Better video summarization, content moderation, and intelligent search for large video databases.
- **Surveillance and Security:** More accurate event detection and anomaly recognition in security footage.
- **Education and Training:** Development of more intelligent tutoring systems that can understand and respond to user actions in video-based learning environments.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** Enabling more natural and intuitive interactions with AI systems that can interpret complex human actions and intentions from video.

The collaboration between a leading technology company (ByteDance) and top academic institutions (NTU, NUS) underscores the practical relevance and academic rigor of this work. Video-KTR not only pushes the boundaries of MLLM capabilities but also offers a pragmatic, interpretable, and efficient solution to a critical challenge in artificial intelligence, promising to accelerate the development of more intelligent and reliable video-understanding systems.
