# PAC-NeRF: Physics Augmented Continuum Neural Radiance Fields for Geometry-Agnostic System Identification

> [!summary] Summary
> PAC-NeRF introduces a framework that integrates dynamic neural radiance fields with differentiable continuum physics, enabling the simultaneous recovery of geometric structure and intrinsic physical properties of dynamic objects from multi-view video sequences without prior geometry assumptions. The model accurately estimated physical parameters across various materials in synthetic tests and outperformed existing baselines in geometry-agnostic system identification.

## Problem

- Existing system identification methods for inferring physical properties rely on a priori known object geometries, limiting their applicability to complex real-world scenes.
- Dynamic Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) learn object dynamics purely from visual data, often lacking physical interpretability, requiring extensive data, and not inherently adhering to physical laws.
- A unified computational framework was absent for jointly inferring both geometry and physical parameters of highly dynamic objects from visual input alone across a broad range of materials.

## Method

- PAC-**NeRF** integrates neural radiance fields with a differentiable continuum physics engine, specifically the ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==), to enforce conservation laws and enable geometry-agnostic system identification.
- The approach employs a hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian representation, using voxel grids for rendering and particles for robust advection and handling of large deformations, with converters for seamless data transfer.
- The pipeline involves geometry seeding using a static voxel **NeRF** to establish initial object shape, followed by gradient-based optimization of physical parameters by minimizing rendering errors against ground truth videos.

## Results

- PAC-NeRF successfully reconstructed highly dynamic objects and accurately determined their physical properties across nine synthetic scenarios involving diverse materials, with optimized parameters showing close agreement with ground truth values.
- The framework demonstrated superior performance, achieving the best results in 14 out of 17 evaluated categories of physical properties, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines in geometry-agnostic system identification.
- PAC-NeRF was effectively applied to real-world multi-view videos of deformable objects, yielding qualitatively consistent scene reconstructions and parameter estimations, despite minor surface geometry errors with limited camera views.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enforcing continuum mechanics conservation laws within a neural radiance field architecture leads to physically plausible and interpretable dynamic states, contrasting with purely data-driven dynamic NeRFs.
> - A hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian representation robustly couples neural scene representations with differentiable continuum physics, allowing for effective system identification for materials undergoing large deformations and topology changes.
> - Optimizing physical parameters through a fully differentiable rendering-simulation pipeline, leveraging pixel-level image-space signals, can accurately infer intrinsic physical properties without explicit 3D geometry supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the long-standing challenge in computer vision and artificial intelligence of inferring both the geometric and physical properties of objects from visual observations. This capability is inherent in human perception, allowing us to understand object dynamics and material properties from everyday observations.

The broader research landscape can be categorized into two main areas:

- **Geometric Reconstruction from Images:** Significant advancements have been made in recovering the geometric (extrinsic) structure of scenes, notably through methods like structure-from-motion and more recently, differentiable rendering pipelines and neural scene representations such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and its dynamic variants (e.g., D-NeRF, Tretschk et al., Park et al.). While these approaches excel at generating photo-realistic renderings and reconstructing dynamic geometries, they typically learn object dynamics from scratch. This process often requires substantial data and yields models that are difficult to interpret.

- **Physical Property Extraction (System Identification):** Approaches to extract intrinsic physical properties (e.g., mass, friction, viscosity) from images are emerging. However, a significant limitation of existing system identification methods (e.g., Jatavallabhula et al., Ma et al., Jaques et al.) is their reliance on the *a priori* knowledge of object geometries. Some methods additionally assume known rendering configurations. This prerequisite severely constrains their applicability to real-world scenes where object geometries are frequently complex or unknown. Furthermore, differentiable physics simulators, which offer a prescriptive, data-efficient, and generalizable dynamics model, commonly necessitate watertight mesh representations of objects, a condition often not met by raw visual data.

This work specifically aims to bridge the gap between these two areas. It seeks to perform system identification without assuming known object geometries, addressing a key limitation of prior physics-based inference methods. Simultaneously, it introduces physical plausibility and interpretability into dynamic neural scene representations, contrasting with purely data-driven dynamic NeRFs that learn dynamics without explicit physical constraints. By integrating differentiable continuum physics with neural radiance fields, the proposed PAC-NeRF framework attempts to overcome these challenges, enabling joint estimation of geometry and physical parameters from multi-view video sequences for a wide range of materials.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to recover both the geometric structure and the physical properties of highly dynamic objects from multi-view video sequences, without requiring any prior assumptions about the object's geometry or topology.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several limitations in existing research:

- **Geometry-Dependency in System Identification:** Current state-of-the-art system identification techniques, which aim to estimate physical parameters, universally assume that the precise geometric structure of the object is known. This assumption significantly restricts their applicability to controlled environments or scenarios where object shapes are simple and well-defined. In a vast majority of real-world scenes, objects possess complex or unknown geometries, rendering these methods impractical.
- **Lack of Physical Plausibility and Interpretability in Dynamic Neural Scene Representations:** While dynamic Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) can reconstruct the geometries of dynamic objects, they typically learn object dynamics purely from visual data. This "learn-from-scratch" approach often requires extensive training data and results in dynamics models that lack physical interpretability. These models do not inherently adhere to the fundamental laws of physics, potentially leading to unphysical behaviors or requiring significant data to generalize.
- **Need for a Unified Framework:** There is an absence of a unified computational framework that can simultaneously infer both the geometry and the physical parameters of objects from visual input alone. Such a framework would simplify the pipeline for understanding complex dynamic scenes and broaden the scope of applications for physical AI.
- **Broad Material Applicability:** The goal is to develop a method that is general enough to operate across a diverse range of materials, including deformable solids, granular media, plastics, and various types of fluids (Newtonian and non-Newtonian). Existing methods often specialize in certain material types or require specific representations.

To address these motivations, the authors propose PAC-NeRF, a system designed to circumvent the need for known geometries by coupling a neural radiance field with a differentiable continuum dynamics model. This integration aims to produce physically plausible states by enforcing conservation laws, thereby enabling robust geometry-agnostic system identification.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Physics Augmented Continuum Neural Radiance Fields (PAC-NeRF), integrates neural scene representations with differentiable physics engines for continuum materials. It employs a hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian formulation to achieve geometry-agnostic system identification from multi-view videos.

#### 3.1. Continuum Neural Radiance Fields

PAC-NeRF extends the concept of a static NeRF (which models view-independent volume density and view-dependent color) to a dynamic, time-dependent version, denoted σ(x, t) and c(x, ω, t). An efficient voxel discretization (based on Sun et al., 2022) is utilized to represent this dynamic Eulerian (world-frame) NeRF. Image rendering is performed by sampling points along rays through these time-dependent fields and applying a volume rendering integral.
A core aspect of PAC-NeRF is the enforcement of physical plausibility. The appearance and volume density fields are constrained to adhere to continuum conservation laws: Dσ/Dt = 0 and Dc/Dt = 0, where D/Dt is the material derivative incorporating the velocity field *v*. This velocity field, in turn, must satisfy momentum conservation for continuum materials (ρ Dv/Dt = ∇·T + ρg), which is evolved using a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) (Hu et al., 2020).

#### 3.2. Hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian Representation and Particle-Grid Interconverters

The framework employs a hybrid representation to leverage the advantages of both Eulerian (grid) and Lagrangian (particle) views.
- **Eulerian View (Grid):** This representation is used for the NeRF density and color fields, facilitating efficient rendering and collision handling.
- **Lagrangian View (Particles):** This representation is crucial for advecting the neural radiance fields according to the Material Point Method, which robustly handles large deformations and topology changes.
To enable seamless interaction between these two representations, particle-to-grid (P2G) and grid-to-particle (G2P) converters are designed. These converters use trilinear shape functions to transfer field quantities (density, color) between grid nodes and particles.
The advection process begins by initializing an Eulerian voxel field at the first frame (t0). This field is then converted to a Lagrangian particle field using G2P. The particles are subsequently advected over a timestep (δt) using the differentiable MPM, driven by an initial guess of physical parameters (Θ). The advected particle field is then mapped back to the Eulerian view via P2G, producing an updated Eulerian field for collision detection and neural rendering at t1. This voxel-based Eulerian representation reportedly offers a significant speedup (two orders of magnitude) in rendering time compared to traditional MLP-based NeRFs.
Particle properties include density and color values, bound to them from the initial voxel grid. A scalar value αp is associated with each particle to indicate its contribution to the radiance fields, and particles with low αp values are considered "softer" and can be removed if their contribution falls below a defined threshold, enabling dynamic geometry adjustment.

#### 3.3. Geometry-Agnostic System Identification Pipeline

The overall pipeline comprises three phases:
- **Data Preprocessing:** Assumes static, calibrated multi-view cameras. To focus computational resources, a video matting framework (Lin et al., 2021) is used to extract foreground objects and their segmentation masks, removing static backgrounds.
- **Geometry Seeding:** A coarse-to-fine strategy is applied to recover the initial object geometry. A static voxel NeRF is trained on the first frame, optimizing a rendering loss against the foreground segmentation mask. A surface regularizer is introduced to minimize total surface area, promoting a compact and boundary-fitting reconstructed shape. The G2P-P2G conversion ensures consistent correspondences across time.
- **System Identification:** After geometry seeding, the parameters of the initial (t0) field are fixed. The initial velocities of observed particles are estimated using the first few frames (2-3), typically with an L-BFGS optimizer. Subsequently, physical parameters are optimized over the entire video sequence using a gradient-based method (Adam optimizer). The optimization leverages the differentiability of the entire rendering-simulation pipeline, minimizing the reconstruction error between rendered frames and ground truth videos. To improve convergence, the optimizer is warm-started after initial collision events.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The NeRF voxel discretization uses a 160^3 voxel grid to store density and color features. A shallow 2-layer MLP processes query positions, view directions, and color features for view-dependent color fields. The differentiable MPM component is implemented using DiffTaichi. The entire pipeline is optimized for GPU computation, with training times reported around 1.5 hours on an Nvidia 3090 GPU, and simulation plus rendering of a single frame taking approximately 1 second. The framework supports various constitutive models and plasticity models for materials like neo-Hookean elasticity, J-based fluid with viscosity, St.Venant-Kirchhoff with von-Mises plasticity for plasticine, viscoplastic models for non-Newtonian fluids, and Drucker-Prager yield criteria for granular materials (sand).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated PAC-NeRF across a range of experiments, demonstrating its efficacy in geometry estimation and system identification.

#### 4.1. Experiment Setup

- **Dataset:** A synthetic photo-realistic dataset was created, featuring nine distinct scenarios with various continuum materials: Newtonian fluids (Droplet, Letter), non-Newtonian fluids (Cream, Toothpaste), granular media (Trophy), deformable solids (Torus, Bird), and plasticine (Cat, Playdoh). Objects freely fall under gravity and undergo collisions. The dataset includes complex environmental lighting and ground textures. Each scene was captured from 11 uniformly sampled viewpoints. Ground truth data was generated using the MLS-MPM framework.
- **Real-World Data:** PAC-NeRF was also tested on real-world multi-view videos captured using four synchronized Intel RealSense D455 cameras (640x480 resolution, 60 fps). Only RGB data was used, with foreground objects manually segmented.
- **Physical Parameters:** The differentiable MPM supports five material types, each with specific parameters:
 - **Elasticity:** Young’s modulus (E), Poisson’s ratio (ν).
 - **Plasticine:** Young’s modulus (E), Poisson’s ratio (ν), yield stress (τ_Y).
 - **Newtonian Fluid:** Fluid viscosity (μ), bulk modulus (κ).
 - **Non-Newtonian Fluid:** Shear modulus (μ), bulk modulus (κ), yield stress (τ_Y), plasticity viscosity (η).
 - **Sand:** Friction angle (θ_fric).
- **Optimization Pipeline:** Training involved an Adam optimizer for static voxel NeRF (geometry seeding) and physical parameters, and an L-BFGS optimizer for initial velocity estimation.

#### 4.2. System Identification Evaluation

- **Qualitative Results (Synthetic Data):** Figure 4 illustrates that PAC-NeRF is capable of reconstructing highly dynamic objects and accurately determining their underlying physical properties across all tested material types. The visualizations show high fidelity in both reconstructed geometry and simulated dynamics compared to ground truth.
- **Quantitative Results (Synthetic Data):** Table 1 presents a comparison of initial parameter guesses, optimized values from PAC-NeRF, and ground truth values for the nine synthetic problem instances. In each case, the optimized physical parameters exhibited close agreement with the ground truth, indicating the framework's accuracy.
- **Real-World Data Results:** Figure 5 demonstrates PAC-NeRF's application to real-world multi-view videos of a deformable ball. The reconstructed scene qualitatively matches the target videos, suggesting effective minimization of the rendering loss. The authors noted minor errors in surface geometry estimation, attributed to the limited number of input views (four cameras), and suggested that increasing camera views could mitigate this.

#### 4.3. Comparisons with Baselines

The authors compared PAC-NeRF against several state-of-the-art and baseline approaches on a dataset of 10 problem instances per material type, varying object orientations, initial velocities, and physical parameters. These baselines were adapted for a best-effort comparison, as no existing approach directly addresses geometry-agnostic system identification from videos.
- **Approaches Evaluated:**
 - **Multi-view LSTM:** A vision-based method (ResNet + 2-layer LSTM) trained to infer physical parameters implicitly from video features, given privileged training sequences.
 - **D-NeRF + DiffSim:** An adaptation of dynamic NeRF (D-NeRF) integrated with a differentiable simulator, where the learned forward deformation field provides 3D supervision.
 - **NeRF + ∇Sim:** Utilizes a static voxel NeRF to recover geometry, then employs ∇Sim (a state-of-the-art method assuming known geometry and rendering configuration) for physical property estimation. This required mesh extraction and tetrahedral mesh generation.
 - **Random:** A baseline predicting uniformly random values within parameter ranges.
 - **Oracle:** A privileged baseline using ground truth 3D point clouds and Chamfer distance for supervision.

- **Analysis of Results (Table 2):**
 - **PAC-NeRF Performance:** PAC-NeRF achieved the best results in 14 out of 17 categories of physical properties estimated, excluding the Oracle baseline. This demonstrates its superior performance in geometry-agnostic system identification.
 - **Multi-view LSTM:** This method performed less accurately than PAC-NeRF, even with privileged training data, suggesting that implicit learning from videos without explicit physical constraints is less effective for precise parameter estimation.
 - **D-NeRF + DiffSim:** This approach showed poor performance and significant instability. The learned deformation fields in D-NeRF variants do not inherently guarantee physical correctness, leading to noisy and unphysical deformations (e.g., objects breaking apart or partially disappearing, as shown in Figure 7), which severely impeded system identification.
 - **NeRF + ∇Sim:** This baseline also performed poorly. It was limited to FEM simulations (only a subset of materials) and was highly sensitive to time step size, requiring numerous substeps and leading to accumulated numerical errors. Additionally, errors from geometry reconstruction (from NeRF) and approximate rendering configurations further contributed to its failure on the tested scenarios. PAC-NeRF’s Eulerian-Lagrangian representation proved more robust to large deformations and allowed for larger time steps.
 - **Oracle:** Surprisingly, the pixel-wise color loss used by PAC-NeRF occasionally outperformed the 3D Chamfer distance supervision used by the Oracle, highlighting the effectiveness of the differentiable rendering-simulation pipeline in optimizing physical parameters through 2D visual cues.

- **Complex Boundary Conditions:** The method was also successfully applied to scenarios with complex boundary conditions, such as elastic ropes falling onto rigid cylinders, demonstrating accurate parameter estimation (Figure 6, Table 3).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable contribution to the fields of computer vision, artificial intelligence, and physical simulation, offering a novel approach to system identification.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Geometry-Agnostic System Identification:** PAC-NeRF addresses a critical limitation of previous system identification methods by removing the prerequisite of known object geometries. This significantly expands the applicability of physically-informed AI to real-world scenarios where object shapes are often complex, unknown, or highly deformable.
- **Unified Framework for Geometry and Physics:** The research successfully integrates dynamic neural radiance fields with differentiable continuum physics simulations. This unification enables simultaneous estimation of both geometric structure and intrinsic physical properties from image-space signals (multi-view videos) within a single, coherent framework.
- **Physical Plausibility and Interpretability:** By enforcing continuum mechanics conservation laws, PAC-NeRF inherently generates physically plausible dynamic states. This contrasts with purely data-driven dynamic NeRFs, which learn dynamics from scratch and often lack interpretability or physical correctness, requiring extensive data for generalization.
- **Broad Material Applicability and Robustness:** The proposed hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian representation and the use of the Material Point Method (MPM) allow PAC-NeRF to handle a diverse range of continuum materials, including elastic solids, plasticine, granular media, and various fluids, even under large deformations and topology changes. This generalizability is a key advantage over methods limited to specific material types or simulation frameworks.
- **Enhanced Performance:** The experimental results demonstrate that PAC-NeRF significantly outperforms existing baselines in most system identification tasks, even when compared to methods using privileged information or operating under more restrictive assumptions. The effectiveness of pixel-based rendering loss for optimizing physical parameters is also highlighted.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Physical AI and Robotics:** The ability to automatically infer physical properties and geometries from visual data is fundamental for developing more intelligent agents, particularly in robotics for manipulation, interaction, and scene understanding. This could lead to robots that can better predict object behavior, adapt to unknown materials, and perform tasks requiring nuanced physical interaction.
- **Realistic Virtual Environments:** Improved understanding and reconstruction of dynamic physical scenes could enhance the realism and interactivity of virtual and augmented reality environments, computer graphics, and digital twins, where accurate physics simulation is crucial.
- **Scientific Discovery and Engineering Design:** The framework could serve as a tool for inverse design or material science, allowing researchers to infer material properties from observed behaviors, potentially aiding in the development of new materials or understanding complex physical phenomena.
- **Data Efficiency for Dynamics Learning:** By incorporating physics-based priors, PAC-NeRF offers a more data-efficient approach to modeling dynamic scenes compared to purely neural methods, which rely heavily on large datasets.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Camera Requirements:** The method assumes synchronized and accurately calibrated multi-view cameras, which might be challenging to deploy in unconstrained environments.
- **Foreground Segmentation:** It relies on effective video matting or pre-existing foreground masks, which may not always be perfect for complex scenes.
- **Material Specificity:** The current framework assumes the underlying physical phenomenon follows continuum mechanics and does not automatically distinguish between different material types, requiring prior knowledge of the material class for parameter optimization.
Future work directions include:
- Extending the MPM framework to non-volumetric continuum materials like thin-shell objects (e.g., cloth).
- Incorporating implicit MPM for simulating stiff materials more accurately.
- Integrating other differentiable simulators, such as those for articulated body dynamics.
- Enabling interactions with rigid bodies to facilitate manipulation tasks involving NeRF-represented soft bodies.
Addressing these limitations could further broaden the applicability and capabilities of the PAC-NeRF framework.
