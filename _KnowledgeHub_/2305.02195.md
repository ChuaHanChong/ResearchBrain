# CALM: Conditional Adversarial Latent Models for Directable Virtual Characters

> [!summary] Summary
> CALM, developed by researchers at NVIDIA, is a framework for generating realistic and directable character animations. It achieves this by jointly training a motion encoder and generator using adversarial techniques, enabling smooth transitions between diverse actions and precise user control over physically simulated characters.

## Problem

- Current virtual character animation struggles to balance realism with direct user control.
- Existing methods often result in either realistic but uncontrollable motions or controllable but unnatural movements.
- Previous imitation learning methods lacked motion diversity, and latent-conditioned control lacked precise user input.

## Method

- Introduces **CALM**, a multi-phase framework for learning and generating motions, including ==low-level training==, ==precision training==, and inference.
- Jointly trains a ==motion encoder== to map motion data to a ==latent space== and a ==low-level policy== to generate motor actuations from latents via ==adversarial training==.
- Employs a ==conditional discriminator== to ensure generated motions match reference data, alongside ==regularization== (==alignment== and ==uniformity losses== on a unit hypersphere) for a structured latent space.

## Results

- Generates diverse and realistic character motions with smooth interpolation between action types, while enabling precise directional control over character movements and actions.
- Successfully solves downstream tasks such as target reaching and object striking without additional training.
- Achieved **84.2%** motion identification accuracy in a user study, demonstrating improved perceived motion accuracy compared to prior methods like ASE (**66.7%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial training of an encoder and generator can create a semantically structured latent space for character motions, enabling directability.
> - Regularizing the latent space through projection onto a unit hypersphere and applying alignment and uniformity losses improves training stability and semantic clustering.
> - Decomposing the control problem into low-level motion generation and high-level directional control allows for robust and diverse task execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Creating virtual characters that move naturally while remaining responsive to user directives is a significant challenge in computer animation, gaming, and virtual reality. Current approaches often struggle to balance realism with controllability, resulting in either realistic but uncontrollable motions or controllable but unnatural movements.

#### 1.1. Research Context

CALM builds upon several foundational areas in character animation and control:

1. **Imitation Learning**: Earlier approaches like DeepMimic focused on imitating single motion clips but struggled with diverse behaviors.

2. **Latent-Conditioned Control**: Methods such as Adversarial Skill Embeddings (ASE) aimed to maximize motion diversity but lacked direct control over the generated behavior.

3. **Physics-Based Animation**: CALM uses physics-based simulation to ensure realistic dynamics, rather than directly generating pose trajectories.

4. **Representation Learning**: The approach incorporates techniques from representation learning, adapting them specifically for motion control challenges.

The key differentiation of CALM is its ability to combine diverse motion generation with precise user control, moving closer to truly interactive and realistic virtual characters without requiring labeled data or language-based commands.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research paper "CALM: Conditional Adversarial Latent Models for Directable Virtual Characters" introduces a novel framework for generating realistic and directable character animations. Developed by researchers at NVIDIA and collaborating academic institutions, CALM addresses the limitations of existing methods by jointly training a motion encoder and generator using adversarial techniques while maintaining user control over the generated movements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Methodology

CALM operates through a multi-phase framework for learning and generating controllable character motions:

The framework consists of three primary components:

1. **Motion Encoder (E)**: Maps raw motion capture data into a low-dimensional latent representation.

2. **Low-Level Policy (π)**: Acts as a decoder, taking latent vectors as input and generating motor actuations that drive a physically simulated character.

3. **Conditional Discriminator**: Provides feedback to both the encoder and policy, ensuring the generated motions match reference data.

The mathematical formulation of the adversarial training objective can be expressed as:

$$\min_{\pi, E} \max_D \mathbb{E}_{M \sim p_{\text{data}}}[\log D(M, E(M))] + \mathbb{E}_{M \sim p_{\text{data}}, z \sim E(M)}[\log(1 - D(\pi(z), z))]$$

This objective encourages the policy to generate motions that are indistinguishable from the motion capture data when conditioned on the corresponding latent representation.

#### 3.2. Latent Space Representation

A key innovation in CALM is the structured latent space that captures semantic relationships between different motions:

The encoder projects motion data onto the l2 unit hypersphere to improve training stability and reduce the likelihood of unnatural behaviors. The authors employ additional regularization techniques:

1. **Alignment Loss**: Encourages similar motions to have similar latent representations.

 $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} = -\mathbb{E}_{(M_i, M_j) \sim p_{\text{pos}}}[\langle E(M_i), E(M_j) \rangle]$$

2. **Uniformity Loss**: Encourages the latent representations to be uniformly distributed on the hypersphere.

 $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{uniform}} = \log\mathbb{E}_{(M_i, M_j) \sim p_{\text{data}}}[e^{-t\|E(M_i) - E(M_j)\|^2}]$$

These techniques result in a well-structured latent space where semantically similar motions are clustered together, enabling intuitive control and smooth transitions between different actions.

The structure of this latent space can be visualized through comparison matrices, showing how CALM creates more distinct motion clusters compared to previous approaches.

#### 3.3. Training Phases

CALM's training process consists of three phases:

#### 3.4. Low-Level Training (Pre-Training)

This phase jointly trains the motion encoder and low-level policy using the conditional discriminator. The encoder maps motion capture data into latent vectors, which are then used by the policy to generate motor actions for the simulated character. The discriminator provides feedback to ensure the generated motions match the reference data.

#### 3.5. Precision Training

This phase trains a high-level policy to control the direction of motions:

```python
# Simplified pseudocode for precision training
def train_precision_policy():
    # Initialize high-level policy
    high_level_policy = HighLevelPolicy()

    # Freeze low-level policy and encoder
    low_level_policy.freeze()
    encoder.freeze()

    for episode in range(num_episodes):
        # Sample target motion
        target_motion = sample_from_dataset()
        target_latent = encoder(target_motion)

        # Run episode
        state = env.reset()
        done = False
        while not done:
            # Get latent from high-level policy
            z_t = high_level_policy(state, target_latent)

            # Execute action using low-level policy
            action = low_level_policy(state, z_t)
            next_state, reward, done = env.step(action)

            # Update high-level policy
            high_level_policy.update(state, z_t, reward, next_state, target_latent)
            state = next_state
```

#### 3.6. Inference

This phase combines the trained models to compose complex movements without additional training. A finite-state machine (FSM) determines which motion to perform based on the environment state, providing commands to the high-level policy or directly to the low-level policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Real-World Applications

CALM has numerous potential applications:

1. **Gaming and Virtual Reality**: Creating more realistic and interactive NPCs or player avatars.

2. **Animation Production**: Providing tools for animators to quickly generate realistic motion sequences.

3. **Simulation and Training**: Developing realistic simulations for training in sports, military, or healthcare contexts.

4. **Human-Computer Interaction**: Enabling more natural interactions with virtual assistants or interfaces.

For example, the system can generate complex locomotion sequences.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its advances, CALM has several limitations:

1. **Motion Diversity**: While more controllable than previous methods, the diversity of generated motions is still constrained by the training data.

2. **Complex Interactions**: The current system focuses primarily on individual character motions and has limited ability to model interactions with complex environments or other characters.

3. **Computational Requirements**: The physics-based simulation approach requires significant computational resources compared to kinematic methods.

Future work could address these limitations by:

1. Incorporating more diverse training data, including interactions with objects and other characters.

2. Developing more efficient simulation techniques to reduce computational requirements.

3. Integrating natural language interfaces for more intuitive control.

4. Extending the approach to multi-character interactions and group behaviors.

#### 5.3. Conclusion

CALM represents a significant advancement in the field of directable virtual characters by combining the benefits of diverse motion generation with direct user control. By jointly training a motion encoder and generator using a conditional adversarial approach, CALM creates a structured latent space that enables intuitive control over generated motions.

The system demonstrates the ability to generate realistic and diverse behaviors, interpolate smoothly between different motions, and solve downstream tasks without further training. These capabilities make CALM a valuable tool for applications in gaming, animation, simulation, and human-computer interaction.

As virtual environments become increasingly prevalent and important in our daily lives, technologies like CALM that enable more realistic and interactive virtual characters will play a crucial role in creating immersive and engaging user experiences.
