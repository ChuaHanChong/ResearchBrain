# Semantic Mapping in Indoor Embodied AI -- A Survey on Advances, Challenges, and Future Directions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Simon Fraser University conducted a systematic survey of semantic mapping in indoor embodied AI, introducing a novel two-axis taxonomy for map structures and semantic encodings. The work comprehensively analyzes current approaches, highlights their design trade-offs, and identifies critical challenges alongside future research directions for the field.

## Problem

- Intelligent embodied agents require robust internal representations, specifically semantic maps, to perform complex, long-horizon tasks in unfamiliar real-world environments.
- Existing surveys often contextualize semantic mapping within specific downstream applications or general embodied AI advancements, lacking a comprehensive review focused solely on the map-building methods themselves.
- The emerging need for general-purpose, open-vocabulary, and task-agnostic representations demands a principled understanding of the underlying map design choices and their implications.

## Method

- A systematic categorization framework was introduced, classifying semantic map-building methods based on their underlying structural representation (spatial grid, topological, dense geometric, hybrid) and semantic encoding (explicit vs. implicit, closed-vocabulary vs. open-vocabulary).
- A detailed review and critical analysis of existing approaches within each category was provided, tracing their evolution and evaluating their respective strengths and limitations.
- The survey analyzed system design strategies, distinguishing between end-to-end learning and modular pipelines, and discussed the current state of evaluation practices, advocating for intrinsic map-level metrics.

## Results

- The survey established a novel, representation-focused taxonomy for semantic map-building, which unifies diverse research strands and clarifies the inherent design choices and their implications.
- Seven key challenges were identified, including efficiency, scalability, real-time processing, noise and uncertainty, multi-modal fusion, lifelong learning, and the critical lack of standardized intrinsic map evaluation metrics.
- Promising avenues for future research were outlined, aiming to guide innovation towards developing more capable, adaptable, and general-purpose embodied AI systems that can effectively operate in dynamic, unstructured environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The field is rapidly shifting towards developing open-vocabulary, queryable, and task-agnostic map representations, largely driven by advances in large pre-trained vision-language models (LVLMs).
> - Each map structure (spatial grids, topological maps, dense geometric maps, hybrid maps) and semantic encoding method involves inherent trade-offs concerning memory demands, scalability, spatial fidelity, and flexibility for different tasks.
> - There is a strong, reciprocal relationship between semantic mapping in embodied AI and classical Semantic SLAM, with significant methodological overlaps and opportunities for knowledge transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI and Robotics is rapidly advancing, driven by the need for intelligent agents (physical robots or virtual systems) to operate autonomously in complex, unstructured environments. A crucial component for such agents is the ability to construct and utilize internal representations of their surroundings, particularly **semantic maps** that go beyond mere geometry to encode high-level meaning (e.g., object categories, room types, affordances).

This survey distinguishes itself by carving out a unique niche within the extensive literature on semantic mapping. The authors explicitly state that existing surveys tend to focus on general advancements in embodied AI or contextualize semantic mapping within specific downstream applications like navigation or manipulation, thereby centering on "task advancement." For instance, they note that Song et al. (2025) reviewed semantic mapping in indoor robotics but concentrated on the types of semantics acquired and extraction techniques.

In contrast, Raychaudhuri and Chang's survey offers a "comprehensive review of semantic map-building methods, organized around the underlying map representations themselves, independent of specific downstream tasks." This distinct perspective is presented as "timely and necessary, given the recent advances in foundation models and the growing demand for general-purpose, open-vocabulary, task-agnostic representations." By categorizing approaches based on **map structure** (spatial grids, topological graphs, dense geometric, hybrid) and **semantic encoding** (explicit vs. implicit, closed-vocabulary vs. open-vocabulary), the paper provides a foundational, architecture-centric understanding of the field.

The scope is narrowed to **indoor navigation** for embodied agents while also establishing crucial connections to **Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM)**, especially Semantic SLAM, which is rooted in classical robotics. This bridge highlights methodological overlaps and aims to transfer insights between these historically somewhat divergent research communities. By offering a systematic, representation-focused taxonomy, this survey aims to unify diverse research strands and provide a clearer understanding of the trade-offs inherent in different semantic mapping designs, thereby setting a new benchmark for reviewing the core mechanisms of map creation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the critical need for intelligent embodied agents, such as robots, to operate effectively in unfamiliar and complex real-world environments. To perform sophisticated tasks that require more than basic perception and actuation, these agents must develop and maintain robust internal representations of their surroundings. Among these, building and utilizing a **semantic map** is identified as "most crucial in long-horizon tasks," as it enables agents to store structured information about the environment (e.g., object categories, affordances) and reference it for advanced reasoning and decision-making.

The authors highlight a gap in existing literature, noting that while many surveys cover semantic mapping, they often do so within the context of specific applications or general embodied AI advancements. This paper, however, posits that a comprehensive review focusing *specifically* on the **map-building approaches themselves** – decoupled from individual tasks – is essential given the recent emergence of foundation models and the growing demand for versatile, task-agnostic representations.

Therefore, the **key objectives** of this survey are:
1. **Comprehensive Review:** To provide an in-depth review of semantic map-building approaches in embodied AI, with a specific focus on indoor navigation scenarios.
2. **Systematic Categorization:** To introduce a novel categorization framework for semantic mapping approaches based on two fundamental axes:
- **Structural Representation:** How the map is organized (e.g., spatial grids, topological graphs, dense geometric maps, or hybrid combinations).
- **Semantic Encoding:** The type of information stored within the map (e.g., explicit environmental data like object categories, or implicit features learned from sensory input, further distinguished by closed-vocabulary versus open-vocabulary capabilities).
3. **Strength and Limitation Analysis:** To thoroughly explore the advantages and disadvantages of various map-building techniques within this taxonomy.
4. **Identification of Challenges:** To pinpoint current challenges and open problems hindering the advancement of semantic mapping in embodied AI.
5. **Future Directions:** To propose promising avenues for future research, aiming to guide and inspire further innovation in the field.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to offer a principled understanding of semantic map-building, clarify the trade-offs in design choices, and ultimately accelerate the development of more capable and intelligent embodied AI systems that can effectively navigate and interact with the world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic, categorization-driven approach to survey the extensive literature on semantic mapping in embodied AI. Their methodology is structured around a novel **two-axis taxonomy** that allows for a granular and representation-focused analysis, distinguishing it from prior task-centric reviews.

The core of their approach involves:

1. **Defining Semantic Maps:** The survey begins by clearly defining what constitutes a semantic map, emphasizing its role in bridging perception with high-level reasoning by encoding both spatial geometry and contextual semantics. It also outlines the fundamental components of map building: localization, feature extraction, projection (for spatial maps), and accumulation.

2. **Two-Axis Taxonomy for Categorization:** The primary methodological innovation is the classification of map-building methods along two fundamental dimensions:
- **Map Structure:** This axis categorizes how the physical environment's information is organized. The identified structures are:
 - **Spatial Grid Maps:** Top-down, fixed-resolution grids (2D or 3D voxels) that discretize the environment.
 - **Topological Maps:** Graph-like structures where nodes represent significant locations or landmarks, and edges denote relationships or connectivity.
 - **Dense Geometric Maps:** Fine-grained 3D representations, often using point clouds or neural fields, capturing every point in the scene.
 - **Hybrid Maps:** Combinations of two or more of the above structures, leveraging their complementary strengths.
- **Semantic Encoding:** This axis describes the type of information stored within the chosen map structure:
 - **Explicit Encoding:** Directly interpretable information, such as binary occupancy, explored areas, predefined object categories, room types, or audio intensity.
 - **Implicit Encoding:** Learned latent features derived from sensory input, which may or may not be directly interpretable. This is further divided into:
 - **Closed-Vocabulary Implicit Encoding:** Features extracted from models trained on a limited, predefined set of categories (e often vision models like ResNet).
 - **Open-Vocabulary Implicit Encoding:** Features derived from large pre-trained vision-language models (LVLMs) that enable recognition of arbitrary, unseen object categories via natural language queries.

3. **Literature Scope:** The survey primarily focuses on semantic mapping approaches within the context of **indoor mobile robots** and **embodied AI systems**. However, it also explicitly incorporates and discusses relevant advancements from **classical robotics, particularly Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM)**, to highlight methodological overlaps and foundational techniques.

4. **Detailed Analysis of Each Category:** For each combination of map structure and encoding, the authors provide:
- **Historical Context:** Tracing the evolution of approaches from early techniques to modern advancements.
- **Specific Examples:** Discussing numerous research papers (summarized in detailed tables, e.g., Table 1, 3, 4, 5, 6), explaining their core mechanisms, and how they fit into the taxonomy.
- **Strengths and Limitations:** Critically evaluating the advantages (e.g., interpretability, efficiency, spatial fidelity) and disadvantages (e.g., memory demands, scalability issues, reliance on predefined categories) of each approach.

5. **System Design Strategies:** The paper also analyzes system architectural choices, distinguishing between **end-to-end learning** and **modular pipelines**, and discusses their implications for map construction, reusability, and interpretability.

6. **Evaluation Framework:** A dedicated section is devoted to the state of evaluation, differentiating between **extrinsic (task-level) evaluation** and **intrinsic (map-level) evaluation**. The authors highlight the current imbalance, with a strong emphasis on extrinsic metrics, and argue for the greater importance of intrinsic metrics (accuracy, completeness, consistency, robustness) for future progress.

7. **Challenges and Future Directions:** Based on the comprehensive review, the survey identifies seven key challenges (efficiency, scalability, real-time processing, noise/uncertainty, multi-modal fusion, lifelong learning, and lack of standardized evaluation) and subsequently outlines promising research directions derived from these challenges.

By adhering to this structured and analytical methodology, the authors provide a holistic and insightful overview of semantic map-building, effectively organizing a complex and rapidly evolving research domain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive survey by Raychaudhuri and Chang yields several significant findings and observations regarding the state and trajectory of semantic mapping in embodied AI:

1. **Shift Towards Semantically Rich Maps:** A fundamental shift has occurred from traditional geometrically focused maps (e.g., for localization and obstacle avoidance) to semantically rich maps. This evolution is largely driven by advances in deep learning, computer vision, and multimodal perception, which enable agents to interpret and integrate high-level semantic information into their environmental representations.

2. **Emergence of Open-Vocabulary and Task-Agnostic Maps:** The field is actively moving towards developing **open-vocabulary, queryable, and task-agnostic map representations**. This trend is heavily influenced by the recent success of large pre-trained vision-language models (LVLMs) and large language models (LLMs). These models allow maps to encode information about "any" object, not just a predefined set, and enable natural language querying, making the same map reusable across diverse tasks (e.g., "find a red and blue striped zebra toy").

3. **Diverse Map Structures with Inherent Trade-offs:** The survey identifies four primary map structures:
- **Spatial Grids:** Offer dense, structured representations useful for spatial reasoning, but are memory-intensive and struggle with scalability in large environments.
- **Topological Maps:** Provide lightweight, scalable, graph-based representations of significant landmarks and their relationships, but lack dense geometric detail crucial for fine-grained interactions.
- **Dense Geometric Maps:** (e.g., point clouds, neural fields) Offer high-fidelity 3D geometric and semantic detail but suffer from high memory demands, computational inefficiency, and redundancy (capturing empty space).
- **Hybrid Maps:** Combine elements of different structures (e.g., topometric maps merging grids and topological graphs) to leverage their respective strengths and mitigate individual weaknesses, though this adds complexity in management and updates. The survey notes that hybrid maps are more explored in robotics than in embodied AI.

4. **Evolution of Semantic Encoding:**
- **Explicit Encoding:** Represents interpretable information like occupancy, explored areas, or specific object/room categories. While clear and useful for specific tasks, it is inherently limited to a closed vocabulary.
- **Implicit Encoding:** Stores learned latent features. Early forms used features from vision models (e.g., ResNet) for closed-vocabulary tasks. The advent of LVLMs like CLIP has paved the way for **open-vocabulary implicit encoding**, storing embeddings that can be queried with arbitrary text, drastically increasing map flexibility and interpretability.

5. **System Design Architectures:** The paper analyzes the impact of system design, contrasting **end-to-end learning** (simpler, direct task optimization, but less interpretable and generalizable) with **modular pipelines** (more transparent, reusable components, better for transferability and complex reasoning, but risk error propagation). Modular approaches leveraging pre-trained vision models for encoding and separate modules for mapping, exploration, and planning are gaining prominence.

6. **Close Relationship with SLAM:** The survey underscores the strong connection between semantic mapping in embodied AI and **Semantic SLAM** in robotics. Semantic SLAM's focus on globally consistent, pose-aware maps augmented with semantics has significantly influenced embodied AI map-building techniques, particularly in addressing localization and mapping accuracy.

7. **Key Challenges Remaining (as summarized in the paper's dedicated section):**
- **Efficiency:** High memory demands and computational inefficiency, especially for dense and rich maps.
- **Scalability:** Difficulty in accommodating growth in spatial extent, semantic complexity, and temporal evolution in large, dynamic environments.
- **Real-time Processing:** The challenge of building and updating semantically rich maps at speeds required for responsive agent behavior on resource-constrained hardware.
- **Noise and Uncertainty:** The pervasive issue of sensor imperfections, dynamic environmental changes, and ambiguous semantics, often unaddressed in simulated embodied AI settings.
- **Multi-modal Fusion:** The complex task of robustly integrating and aligning diverse sensory inputs (vision, depth, audio, language) to support downstream reasoning.
- **Lifelong Learning:** Enabling maps to continuously adapt to non-stationary environments, distinguishing transient from persistent changes, and preventing catastrophic forgetting over long operational periods.
- **Standardized Evaluation Framework:** A critical lack of intrinsic (map-level) evaluation metrics to assess map quality (accuracy, completeness, consistency, robustness) directly, with current evaluation being predominantly extrinsic (task-level).

These findings collectively highlight a dynamic research area, characterized by rapid innovation in representation learning and a growing recognition of the multifaceted challenges involved in building truly intelligent, adaptable embodied agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey by Raychaudhuri and Chang makes a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and robotics, and its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Structured Guidance for Research:** By systematically categorizing and analyzing existing semantic map-building approaches based on their underlying structures and encodings, the survey provides a much-needed organizational framework. This structured overview will serve as an invaluable guide for both new and experienced researchers, helping them navigate the complex landscape, understand current capabilities, and identify underexplored areas. It helps in formulating new research questions and design choices more effectively.

2. **Accelerating Development of General-Purpose AI:** The paper’s emphasis on the shift towards open-vocabulary, queryable, and task-agnostic map representations is highly significant. This direction is crucial for moving beyond single-task, brittle AI systems towards truly general-purpose embodied agents capable of understanding and interacting with the world in a human-like, flexible manner. By clearly outlining the progress and remaining hurdles in this area, the survey acts as a catalyst for future research aimed at achieving more versatile and robust robotic intelligence.

3. **Bridging Disciplinary Gaps:** The survey explicitly connects concepts and advancements from classical robotics (e.g., SLAM, Semantic SLAM) with the evolving paradigms of embodied AI. This interdisciplinary perspective encourages cross-pollination of ideas and techniques, potentially leading to more robust and theoretically grounded solutions in embodied AI, especially in areas like handling sensor noise and achieving global consistency.

4. **Highlighting Critical Open Challenges:** The detailed exposition of key challenges – encompassing efficiency, scalability, real-time processing, noise/uncertainty, multi-modal fusion, and lifelong learning – provides a clear roadmap for future innovation. By clearly articulating these pain points, the survey directs research efforts towards addressing the most pressing limitations in semantic mapping, fostering solutions that are practical and deployable in real-world scenarios.

5. **Advocating for Intrinsic Map Evaluation:** A particularly impactful aspect is the call for standardized intrinsic (map-level) evaluation metrics. The authors rightly point out that over-reliance on extrinsic (task-level) evaluation can obscure genuine progress in map quality. By promoting metrics for accuracy, completeness, consistency, and robustness of the maps themselves, the survey encourages more rigorous and comparable research, ensuring that advancements truly reflect improvements in semantic understanding rather than merely task performance. This could lead to a more objective and scientific assessment of map representations.

6. **Informing Practical Applications:** Improved semantic mapping techniques have direct implications for a wide range of real-world applications. Enhanced general-purpose and dynamic maps will enable more capable autonomous vehicles, more versatile search-and-rescue robots, efficient automated cleaning systems, and highly interactive home assistants. The insights from this survey can inform the design of future robotic systems, making them more adaptable and effective in complex, dynamic environments.

In conclusion, this survey serves not just as a retrospective of semantic mapping but also as a forward-looking agenda. By providing a clear, representation-centric taxonomy and robustly identifying critical challenges and future directions, it empowers the research community to build more intelligent, adaptable, and ultimately, more useful embodied AI systems.
