# Perceiving, Reasoning, Adapting: A Dual-Layer Framework for VLM-Guided Precision Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology developed a dual-layer framework for VLM-guided precision robotic manipulation, achieving up to 91.01% task success rate in truss assembly and 81.21% in connector docking. This approach integrates semantic understanding with geometric tracking and enables adaptive execution and error recovery.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle to translate high-level task plans into precise sequences of fine motor actions required for intricate robotic manipulation.
- Existing VLM-based robotic systems often lack efficient mechanisms to represent and reason about complex 3D spatial relationships, hindering precision assembly tasks.
- Robotic systems, including VLM-based ones, face limitations in maintaining spatio-temporal coherence and recovering from errors during fine manipulation.

## Method

- A dual-layer framework combines a 2D topology graph for semantic relationships and object states with a 3D spatial network for detailed geometric information, using minimal-volume Gaussian envelopes.
- A progressive VLM planning algorithm adapts execution between "Coarse motion" for global navigation and "Fine manipulation" for precise control and error recovery based on proximity to targets.
- The system employs a spatio-temporal memory structure and dynamic, mode-specific prompt engineering for continuous VLM interaction, enabling real-time adaptation and plan refinement.

## Results

- The framework achieved task success rates of up to **91.01%** for truss assembly and **81.21%** for aviation connector docking, outperforming pure VLM approaches (**0%** TSR) and finetuned MMLMs (by 34-**37%** TSR).
- Ablation studies confirmed the essential role of the dual-layer module (resulting in **0%** TSR without it) and the adaptive interaction algorithm (a **23%** TSR decrease without it) for successful task execution.
- The system demonstrated high Semantic-Location Pair Correctness (**61.67%****-100%**) and Motion Success Rates (**94%** across tasks), indicating effective spatial grounding and precise action execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effectively grounding high-level VLM semantic understanding in precise 3D geometric representations is crucial for successful fine robotic manipulation.
> - An adaptive, iterative planning and execution strategy, continuously informed by real-time feedback and a task memory, empowers VLMs to guide robots through complex, error-prone tasks.
> - Explicitly structured dual-layer representations that integrate abstract semantic relationships with detailed geometric information significantly enhance a VLM's ability to perform precision tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic assembly tasks are fundamental in modern manufacturing, requiring robots to execute precise manipulations within environments characterized by complex spatial relationships. Traditional robotic systems for assembly typically follow a sequential pipeline of perception, task planning, and execution control. While these systems, relying on geometric planning or pure vision-based approaches, have achieved success in structured environments, they often require extensive manual programming for each new task. A key limitation is their struggle with complex spatial reasoning, lack of integrated spatial-semantic understanding, and limited capacity for error recovery during fine manipulation.

The emergence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has introduced new possibilities for developing more flexible and adaptive robotic task planning systems. VLMs are capable of interpreting both visual scenes and natural language, which theoretically offers a means to overcome the limitations of traditional, rigid systems. However, existing VLM-based robotic systems encounter specific challenges. While they may excel at high-level semantic understanding and abstract task planning, they frequently struggle with the precise spatial reasoning required for fine manipulation tasks. Most current VLM approaches lack efficient mechanisms to represent and reason about complex 3D spatial relationships, which limits their effectiveness in scenarios demanding precision assembly. Furthermore, these models often exhibit difficulty in maintaining spatio-temporal relationships and continuously tracking spatial changes while integrating semantic understanding with precise geometric information.

This research aims to address these identified gaps by developing a framework that bridges the divide between high-level VLM semantic comprehension and the precise spatial control necessary for fine robotic manipulation. The work seeks to provide a mechanism for VLMs to effectively guide robots through precise sequences of fine motor actions and enable error correction capabilities, areas where previous VLM applications in robotics have demonstrated limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to perform fast, precise, and error-correctable fine manipulation tasks, guided by Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This objective is motivated by several challenges observed in current VLM-based robotic manipulation systems:

1. **Challenges in Executing Complex Fine Manipulation:** While VLMs exhibit capabilities in high-level task planning, they often struggle to translate these plans into the precise sequences of fine motor actions required for intricate manipulation tasks. Existing methods have difficulty guiding robots with the necessary speed and precision for such operations.
2. **Lack of Efficient 3D Spatial Reasoning:** Many VLM-based robotic systems lack effective mechanisms to represent and reason about complex 3D spatial relationships. This limitation hinders their performance in precision assembly tasks where accurate spatial understanding is critical.
3. **Limitations in Spatio-Temporal Coherence and Error Recovery:** Traditional and some VLM-based systems struggle to maintain spatial-semantic coherence throughout task execution. They also have limited ability to recover from errors encountered during fine manipulation, necessitating robust mechanisms for dynamic adjustment and correction.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines specific innovations:

- **Progressive VLM Planning Algorithm:** The core motivation is to introduce an algorithm that decomposes complex tasks into manageable sub-actions, allowing for progressive refinement and error correction.
- **Dual-Layer Spatial-Semantic Fusion Framework:** A key objective is to enable VLMs to comprehend complex spatial configurations without requiring explicit 3D-specific training. This framework aims to integrate semantic understanding with detailed geometric information.
- **Spatio-Temporal Memory Framework:** The research seeks to develop a system for continuously tracking object positions, their semantic properties, and overall task progress in dynamic and complex environments. This memory structure provides contextual information for VLM interaction.
- **Progressive VLM Interaction Strategy:** A significant motivation is to allow VLMs to dynamically adapt their guidance based on real-time feedback, environmental conditions, and accumulated task history. This strategy is intended to facilitate the generation of precise action plans and enable stepwise error correction.

These objectives collectively aim to enhance robot intelligence in precision tasks by bridging the gap between high-level VLM planning capabilities and the demands of precise physical execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed a progressive VLM planning algorithm, employs a dual-layer framework for VLM-guided precision robotic manipulation. It integrates visual-language models with progressive planning to enable real-time adaptation through continuous VLM interaction, tightly coupling perception and action. The system adaptively switches between two execution modes based on the robot’s proximity to manipulation targets: "Coarse motion" for efficient global navigation (distance > τ) and "Fine manipulation" for precise control and error recovery (distance ≤ τ). This adaptive behavior is realized through three interconnected stages, with continuous feedback for refinement.

**A. Preliminaries and Notations**
The method establishes mathematical foundations and notations for RGB-D camera observations ($Z_t$), scene representation parameters including a topology graph ($G_t$), a spatial network ($S_t$), and a pixel set ($P_t$). A Task Memory Structure ($M$) is defined, comprising a Task Topology Path (TTP), Subtask Status (SS), and Motion Sequence History (MSH).

**B. Progressive VLM Planning Algorithm Structure**
The overall algorithm operates in a closed-loop system, cycling through three stages and adapting motion strategies.

1. **Stage 1 - Spatial-Semantic Mapping:**
 - **Universal Spatial Representation:** RGB-D images are processed, and depth pixels are transformed into the robot's universal coordinate frame using camera intrinsic matrices and rigid body transformations from the camera to the end-effector and then to the robot base.
 - **Multi-Resolution Space Partitioning:** The operational space is partitioned into three concentric zones based on distance from the robot end-effector, defined by thresholds $r_1$ and $r_2$. Each zone uses cubic elements of different edge lengths ($l_1 < l_2 < l_3$) for discretization, ensuring higher precision near the robot and computational efficiency farther away. Each point cloud point is mapped to its corresponding cubic element's center.
 - **Task-Relevant Pixel Selection:** A VLM performs panoptic segmentation on the RGB image to identify pixels belonging to task-relevant objects based on the task description.
 - **Ray-based Registration and Denoising:** For each task-relevant pixel, a ray is cast from the camera center through the pixel. Initial mappings between pixels and space elements are established through these intersections, contributing to the topology graph and spatial network.

2. **Stage 2 - Scene Understanding:** This stage constructs and maintains the dual-layer representation by integrating VLM-based semantic understanding with geometric tracking.
 - **2D Topology Graph (G):** Represents semantic relationships and object states.
 - **Vertices (V):** Each object is a vertex, defined by an identifier, category, a spatial index (linking to the spatial network), and an interaction state.
 - **Edges (E):** Represent relationships between objects (e.g., "supporting", "adjacent") and define physical constraints.
 - **Feature Set (F):** Continuously updated VLM perception information for each object in JSON format.
 - **Topology Graph Update:** VLM determines semantic relationships between object pairs. These relationships are then spatially verified using normalized distances between the objects' Gaussian envelopes. Edges are added based on successful semantic and geometric validation, ensuring coherence between abstract understanding and physical reality.
 - **3D Spatial Network (S):** Maintains detailed geometric information using minimal-volume Gaussian envelopes for each object.
 - Each envelope ($s_i$) is parameterized by its center ($\mu_i$) and covariance matrix ($\Sigma_i$), describing its position, spatial extent, and orientation. The spatial index connects topology graph objects to their respective Gaussian envelopes.
 - **Spatial Network Management:** An adaptive update strategy is employed based on distance-aware processing zones (Far, Mid, Near zones, corresponding to the multi-resolution partitioning).
 - **Far Zone:** Simplified representation with standardized unit Gaussian envelopes, updating only the mean to the object's centroid.
 - **Mid Zone:** Base optimization framework for Gaussian envelopes but with relaxed convergence criteria.
 - **Near Zone:** High-precision envelope optimization for comprehensive geometric tracking. Detailed component-level representations, discretized into fine-grained voxel grids, are maintained for sub-components of objects in this zone.

3. **Stage 3 - Task-Oriented VLM Interaction:** This stage leverages the dual-layer representation and task memory to generate and refine action plans.
 - **Task Memory Structure (M):** A persistent memory tracks hierarchical subtask dependencies (TTP), real-time subtask status (SS), and a history of executed motion sequences (MSH). This provides context for decision-making.
 - **Dynamic Prompt Engineering:** Mode-specific prompt templates are dynamically generated, combining scene information from the topology graph and spatial network with task memory.
 - **Coarse Motion Prompts:** Include high-level goals from TTP, topological relationships from E, subtask status from SS, global spatial constraints from S_global, and historical patterns from MSH. These guide efficient path planning and obstacle avoidance.
 - **Fine Manipulation Prompts:** Incorporate immediate goals from TTP, local geometric descriptions from S_local, real-time sensor feedback ($D_t$), subtask status from SS, and prior manipulation patterns from MSH. These guide precise robot movements and error recovery.
 - **Robot Execution:** VLM responses to these prompts generate ROS functions for robot subtask execution. A feedback loop ensures the robot re-observes the environment after each subtask, updating scene information and task status for subsequent iterations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the progressive VLM planning system through experiments on precision-critical manipulation tasks, focusing on the dual-representation architecture and adaptive execution strategy.

**A. Experimental Setup**
Experiments utilized a 7-DOF Franka arm equipped with a parallel gripper and a wrist-mounted Intel RealSense RGB-D camera. Computations were performed on an NVIDIA RTX 4090D GPU. Five VLM-based models were used for comparison: Deepseek-r1, GPT4o, Claude 3.5, MiniCPMv2.6, and RDT-1 (finetuned with task-specific data).

**B. Benchmark Tasks**
Two benchmark tasks were selected:
1. **Truss Assembly:** Evaluated semantic understanding and spatial reasoning in cluttered environments, requiring identification of task-relevant objects and their spatial relationships for alignment.
2. **Aviation Connector Docking:** Focused on high-precision manipulation, grasp pose identification, sub-millimeter alignment during insertion, and real-time error detection and recovery.

**C. Evaluation Metrics**
Performance was measured using four metrics:
- **SLPC (Semantic-Location Pair Correctness):** Accuracy of identifying and locating task-relevant objects.
- **TPSR (Task Planning Success Rate):** Success rate of generating valid task plans.
- **MSR (Motion Success Rate):** Success rate of executing individual motion sub-tasks.
- **TSR (Task Success Rate):** Overall success rate of completing the entire task.

**D. Performance Comparison (Table I)**
Results from over 200 planning outcomes per configuration indicated performance differences across approaches:
- **Text-only LLM (DeepSeek-R1):** Achieved a 56.44% TPSR for truss assembly, which was lower than VLM models, suggesting the benefit of visual inputs for spatial reasoning.
- **Pure VLM (GPT-4o):** Demonstrated a high TPSR (88.12%) but a 0% TSR, accompanied by a low MSR (25.26%) and 0% SLPC. This indicated a fundamental inability to translate valid high-level plans into successful physical actions due to a lack of grounded spatial understanding.
- **Our Framework (with Claude 3.5 and MiniCPMv2.6):** Exhibited higher overall task success rates, achieving up to 91.01% TSR for truss assembly and 81.21% TSR for connector docking. This framework demonstrated effective grounding of visual-language reasoning in physical space, evidenced by high SLPC values (61.67%-100%), and successfully bridged the planning-execution gap with high MSRs (94% across tasks).
- **Finetuned MMLM (RDT-170M):** Despite finetuning with task-specific data, our framework outperformed RDT-170M by 34-37% in TSR, suggesting that the architectural innovations of our approach address limitations not overcome by additional training data alone.

**E. Ablation Analysis (Table II)**
Ablation studies using MiniCPM v2.6 revealed the contribution of key components:
- **Without Task Memory:** A 6.75% decrease in overall TSR (from 74.96% to 68.21%) and a decrease in TPSR (from 91.96% to 83.60%) was observed. This indicated that the task memory structure contributes to planning quality by leveraging historical strategies.
- **Without Dual-Layer Module:** This configuration resulted in a critical failure, with 0% SLPC and 0% TSR, despite maintaining a high TPSR (91.96%). This finding confirmed the dual-layer representation's essential role in grounding semantic concepts in physical space, without which the system cannot execute tasks.
- **Without Adaptive Interaction Algorithm:** This led to a 23% decrease in TSR (from 74.96% to 51.96%) and a notable decrease in MSR (from 94.26% to 72.28%). This highlighted the importance of the iterative observation-execution cycle and continuous visual feedback for achieving precise motion adjustments and successful fine manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a notable challenge in robotic manipulation: effectively integrating high-level vision-language understanding with the demands of precise physical execution. The reported experimental results indicate that the proposed dual-layer framework and adaptive execution strategy achieve higher success rates on precision-critical tasks compared to methods relying solely on VLMs or finetuned multimodal language models.

The contributions of this work carry several implications:

- **Bridging the Semantic-Geometric Gap:** The research provides a method for VLMs to gain a more effective understanding of 3D spatial relationships, which has been a limitation for existing VLM-based robotic systems. The dual-layer representation, combining 2D topology graphs for semantic relationships with 3D spatial networks for geometric information, contributes to resolving this challenge.
- **Enhanced Precision and Error Recovery:** The progressive planning algorithm, with its adaptive execution modes and iterative feedback loops, demonstrates a capacity for precise motion control and stepwise error correction. This capability is critical for complex assembly tasks and could improve the reliability and autonomy of robotic systems in industrial settings.
- **Modular and Flexible Integration:** The framework's design allows for integration with various VLMs without requiring specific architectural modifications. This modularity could enable robotic systems to leverage advancements in VLM technology while maintaining robust physical grounding.
- **Deployment Versatility:** By decoupling sophisticated reasoning through API-based VLM interaction, the approach supports deployment on robotic platforms with limited onboard computational resources, thus expanding the practical applicability of VLM-guided robots.
- **Efficiency in Data Requirements:** The structural approach to addressing core limitations may offer a more efficient pathway to generalizable robotic manipulation, potentially reducing the extensive demonstration data requirements often associated with purely data-driven, fine-tuned methods.
- **Mitigation of VLM/LLM Limitations:** The explicit use of topology graphs, spatial networks, and a task memory structure is suggested to help mitigate inherent VLM and LLM limitations, such as hallucination and working memory constraints, especially in complex, extended manipulation sequences.

Collectively, this work represents a development towards more capable and autonomous robots in precision-intensive applications, potentially contributing to advancements in areas such as manufacturing automation by enhancing efficiency and safety.
