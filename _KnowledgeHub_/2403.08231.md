# Object Permanence Filter for Robust Tracking with Interactive Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> The Object Permanence Filter (OPF) embeds object-permanence rules into a particle filter for 6-DoF multi-object tracking under occlusion. When measurements vanish, an Object Permanence Update supplies virtual observations from a dynamics module (polynomial motion fit) or an occluder module (Bhattacharyya-distance occluder inference), while an uncertainty module inflates covariance and a feedback module gates robot behavior. Tracking error drops roughly five-fold versus a standard particle filter.

## Problem

- Tracking-by-detection pipelines depend on per-frame detection, so occlusion in human-robot interaction yields missing, noisy, or kinematically infeasible measurements that destabilize robot behavior.
- Prior occlusion-aware particle filters disperse particles by random walk from the last known position, a heuristic search that never encodes object permanence or reasons about which object occludes.
- Learning-based permanence trackers extrapolate deterministic pseudo-ground-truth from last velocity, diverge on wrong labels, and are hard to attach to other trackers or measurement modalities.

## Method

- ==Object Permanence Update== replaces the standard particle-filter update when measurements vanish, feeding a virtual observation and scaled covariance into the importance-weight equation until real measurements return.
- **Dynamics module** classifies an object static or moving over a 50-frame state history, then fits a first-order polynomial over translation plus one axis-angle rotation scalar for prediction.
- **Occluder module** ranks candidate occluders by ==Bhattacharyya distance==, borrowing the occluder's observation; near-ties spawn a duplicate hypothesis, while an exponential covariance scaler drives a feedback safety threshold.

## Results

- General object-permanence tracking cut translation error from **0.06289** to **0.01138** and rotation error from **0.3870** to **0.06869** versus the standard particle filter over 5 runs.
- In the sugar-dropping task the filter reduced translation error to **0.02129** from 0.05018 and rotation error to **0.003411** from 0.01456, removing the baseline's jerky catch-up updates.
- Hardware validation ran at **100Hz** across AprilTags, MediaPipe hands, BundleSDF poses, and motion capture, dropping sugar accurately in **8/10** blindfolded-user trials.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cognitive priors can be injected as explicit filter rules rather than learned, keeping the tracker modular and agnostic to detector type, sensor modality, and update rate.
> - Borrowing an occluder's observation as a virtual measurement is a cheap, principled substitute for random-walk particle dispersion during full occlusion of static objects.
> - Deliberately letting uncertainty grow exponentially during occlusion is a feature: the covariance trace becomes a safety signal for cautious control and human alerts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the field of robotics, particularly in Human-Robot Interaction (HRI): the unreliability of perception systems, especially in the presence of occlusions. As robots move from controlled industrial environments to homes and everyday life, robust perception becomes paramount for safety and fluid interaction.

The majority of modern object tracking algorithms operate under a "tracking-by-detection" paradigm. This approach relies heavily on the continuous and accurate output of object detectors, which can easily fail when objects are partially or completely occluded by humans, other robots, or even themselves. Existing solutions often fall short in complex HRI scenarios:
- **Traditional filters (e.g., Kalman filters):** While effective for combining noisy measurements with dynamic models, they are not inherently designed to handle prolonged missing data due to occlusion.
- **Learning-based approaches (e.g., deep neural networks):** While achieving high performance in detection, they still struggle with occlusions and may require extensive training data. Some memory mechanisms or sensor fusion techniques have been explored, but these often remain within learning-based frameworks and can be challenging to integrate with diverse trackers or measurements.
- **Occlusion-aware particle filters:** Some existing particle filter modifications attempt to handle occlusions, often by resorting to stochastic particle motion or random searches. However, these methods typically lack a principled way to reason about object permanence, leading to less accurate predictions and potentially wide dispersion of particles without explicit knowledge of the occluder. For instance, approaches like Tokmakov et al. use extrapolated object positions during occlusion, which is a step towards permanence but doesn't fully exploit the concept or handle uncertainty robustly across different occlusion scenarios.

This work carves out a niche by proposing a cognitively inspired approach. It draws directly from **Piaget's theory of cognitive development**, specifically the concept of **object permanence**—the understanding that objects continue to exist even when out of sight. While humans intuitively apply this concept, robots typically do not. By explicitly embedding object permanence rules, the paper aims to bridge this gap, offering a more robust and resilient tracking solution than purely reactive, detection-dependent, or heuristic occlusion-handling methods. It seeks to provide a generalizable framework that is "agnostic to measurement type," addressing a significant limitation of many specialized learning-based trackers. This places the OPF as a foundational perceptual enhancement for safer and more natural HRI, moving beyond mere safety-critical control to fundamentally improve the robot's understanding of its environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **incorporate the understanding of object permanence into interactive robots** to achieve robust multi-object tracking in dynamic, multi-agent scenarios. This is accomplished by proposing and integrating a set of assumptions and rules that represent object permanence into a particle filter framework, resulting in the **Object Permanence Filter (OPF)**.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations and needs:

- **Unreliable Perception as a Bottleneck for HRI:** The introduction explicitly states that "unreliable perception is the primary bottleneck for deploying HRI systems in the real world." Current perception systems, even advanced vision-based ones, frequently fail in common HRI tasks due to occlusions (e.g., a human manipulating or occluding an object, another agent blocking a view). These failures manifest as missing, noisy, or kinematically/dynamically infeasible measurements, which can compromise robot behavior and human safety.
- **Beyond Perfect Perception:** Instead of waiting for perfect perception systems to emerge, the authors proactively assume that perception will *always* be unreliable in HRI and therefore needs active mitigation. This pragmatic stance drives the development of a more resilient tracking approach.
- **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** Humans effortlessly apply the concept of object permanence in daily life, enabling them to track objects even when they are not directly visible (e.g., a baby understanding a toy still exists under a blanket, an adult tracking a car behind a building). This cognitive capability is crucial for planning and problem-solving, and its absence in robots limits their ability to interact intelligently and robustly in human environments.
- **Limitations of Current Tracking Paradigms:** Existing "tracking-by-detection" methods are brittle under occlusion. While some previous work addresses occlusion, it often relies on heuristics, random searches, or specialized learning models that lack generalizability and a deep understanding of object persistence. The need for a method that can maintain multiple coherent object representations in dynamic scenarios, similar to human abilities, is highlighted.
- **Enhancing Robot Resilience and Safety:** By developing robust models of object permanence, robots can maintain accurate object tracks despite visual disruptions, leading to improved performance in various HRI tasks, enhancing safety, and fostering more fluid and predictable human-robot interactions.

In essence, the core motivation is to equip robots with a fundamental cognitive ability to reason about the persistent existence and probable location of hidden objects, thereby making their perception systems more robust and their interactions with humans safer and more intelligent.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is the **Object Permanence Filter (OPF)**, which extends the standard Particle Filter (PF) by incorporating rules and assumptions inspired by object permanence. The OPF is designed to provide robust 6-Degree-of-Freedom (6-DoF) object tracking, even in scenarios with heavy and prolonged occlusions, and is agnostic to the type of measurement system used.

The standard Particle Filter operates on a "predict + update" cycle. The OPF primarily modifies the **update step** when no direct measurements are available for a tracked object. This modification, termed the **Object Permanence (OP) Update**, consists of three interconnected modules and is complemented by a Feedback Module:

1. **Particle Filter Foundation:**
 - The OPF utilizes the standard particle filter framework, representing object state (6-DoF: 3D translation and 3D rotation) with a set of weighted particles.
 - To handle the high dimensionality and scale mismatch between translation and rotation, the state is split, and two portions of particles sample each part, improving sampling performance.
 - The predict step propagates particles based on system dynamics and Gaussian noise.
 - The update step normally re-weights particles based on the likelihood of a new observation. Resampling prevents particle degeneracy.

2. **Object Permanence (OP) Update (Activated during Occlusion/Missing Measurement):** When a direct measurement for an object O_i is missing, the OP Update takes over:

 - **Dynamics Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To infer whether the occluded object O_i was static or moving before occlusion and to predict its plausible future motion.
 - **Mechanism:** It maintains a history (H=50 frames) of the object's trajectory (translation and rotation).
 - **Static Detection:** If the maximum change in translation and rotation within the historical window is below pre-defined thresholds (T_δ and R_δ), the object is considered static.
 - **Dynamic Prediction:** If moving, a first-order polynomial (poly(t) = at + b) is fitted to the historical trajectory to predict future translation and a single rotation scalar (derived from axis-angle representation for efficiency and avoiding Euler angle complexities). This predicted state then serves as a "virtual measurement" (y_i_k^occ) for the update step. Importantly, the model is not updated with newly predicted positions to avoid error accumulation.

 - **Occluder Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To identify the most likely occluder(s) when the object is deemed static or when dynamic prediction is not sufficient.
 - **Mechanism:** It uses the **Bhattacharyya distance** to measure the similarity between the occluded object O_i and all other K-1 tracked objects O_j. Smaller distances indicate higher similarity/proximity, suggesting a potential occluder.
 - **Single Occluder:** If there's a significant difference between the two smallest Bhattacharyya distances (above ϵ_occ), the closest object O_p is identified as the single occluder. Its measurement (y_p_k) is used as the virtual observation (y_i_k^occ) for O_i.
 - **Multiple Occluders:** If the two smallest distances are very close (below ϵ_occ), it indicates multiple potential occluders. In this case, a "virtual object" O_i' is created, mirroring O_i's state, and both O_i and O_i' receive virtual observations from the two closest occluders (O_p and O_q respectively), effectively tracking two plausible interpretations of the occluded object's state.

 - **Uncertainty Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To explicitly acknowledge and quantify the increased uncertainty when measurements are missing.
 - **Mechanism:** When an object is occluded, the covariance matrix Q (used in the likelihood calculation of the update step) is scaled exponentially: ˜Q ← α_k Q. The scaling factor α_k = κ^v is an exponentially increasing function dependent on the object's velocity (v) and a constant κ > 1 (empirically set to 1.03). This ensures that uncertainty grows faster for faster-moving occluded objects and with prolonged occlusion.

3. **Feedback Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To monitor the uncertainty of the estimates and provide actionable feedback to the robot or human operator.
 - **Mechanism:** It uses the trace of the scaled covariance matrix (U_PF = trace(˜Q)) as a measure of the filter's uncertainty.
 - **Human Intervention Feedback:** If U_PF exceeds a safety threshold (ϵ_safe), the robot can stop, enter a safety mode, or alert the human operator, signaling that its confidence in the object's state is too low.
 - **Closed-loop Tracking Controller:** The uncertainty U_PF can also modulate a robot's tracking behavior. A proposed control law (Eq. 16) uses decreasing sigmoid functions for proportional (k_p) and derivative (k_d) gains, such that the robot's tracking responsiveness smoothly decreases as uncertainty increases, leading to a more cautious behavior.

In summary, the OPF integrates cognitive insights into a probabilistic filtering framework, allowing robots to "reason" about occluded objects by predicting their dynamics, inferring occluder influence, and explicitly managing the resulting uncertainty, thus providing a much more robust and intelligent tracking capability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper demonstrates the effectiveness and robustness of the Object Permanence Filter (OPF) through a series of comparative simulation experiments and practical hardware validations.

**A. Evaluation Metrics:**
The performance was primarily assessed using:
- **Error Distance:** Quantified as the average L2-norm difference between the predicted and ground-truth positions for both translation and rotation (P_k^n ||p̂_k - p_k||^2 / n).
- **Confidence:** Measured inversely by the trace of the covariance matrix (U_PF = trace(˜Q)), which reflects the uncertainty of the filter's estimate. Lower trace indicates higher confidence.

**B. Comparative Simulation Experiments (PyBullet):**
Two key simulation experiments showcased the OPF's advantages over a standard Particle Filter (PF):

1. **General Object Permanence (OP) Tracking:**
 - **Scenario:** Tracking a ball that is successively occluded by moving mugs (dynamic occluders) and a static tray, testing both static and dynamic occluded objects.
 - **Results (Table I, Fig. 3 top row):**
 - **Superior Accuracy:** The OPF significantly reduced both translation and rotation errors compared to the standard PF. For translation, OPF achieved an error of 0.01138 ± 7.253e-4 versus PF's 0.06289 ± 6.498e-4. For rotation, OPF achieved 0.06869 ± 2.947e-3 versus PF's 0.3870 ± 1.270e-3.
 - **Smooth Tracking:** The standard PF exhibited abrupt updates (laggy and jerky motion) when the occluded object reappeared, as it essentially "froze" its prediction during occlusion. The OPF, however, provided a much smoother, more continuous approximation of the object's true trajectory, accurately predicting its motion even under prolonged occlusion.
 - **Uncertainty Management:** The trace of the covariance matrix in the OPF clearly showed exponential increases during occlusion periods, reflecting the designed growth in uncertainty. These peaks were higher and steeper for longer or faster occlusions, and sharply dropped back to normal levels once the object became visible again.

2. **Sugar-dropping Experiment:**
 - **Scenario:** A robot tracking a moving mug (to drop a sugar cube) while the mug is intermittently self-occluded by the robot's end-effector. This tests the closed-loop tracking controller (Eq. 16).
 - **Results (Table I, Fig. 3 bottom row):**
 - **Improved Smoothness and Precision:** Similar to the first experiment, the OPF-equipped robot could approximate the mug's dynamics accurately during self-occlusion, resulting in smooth tracking trajectories. The standard PF, conversely, led to the robot stopping during occlusion and making jerky, sudden movements upon re-detection, leading to suboptimal performance and potential safety issues.
 - **Quantifiable Improvement:** OPF showed significantly lower translation (0.02129 ± 5.872e-4 vs. 0.05018 ± 6.936e-4) and rotation errors (0.003411 ± 4.629e-4 vs. 0.01456 ± 2.274e-3).
 - **Explicit Uncertainty:** The covariance trace again demonstrated exponential growth during occlusion, confirming the filter's ability to communicate its confidence level.

**C. Hardware Experiments:**
The OPF's generalizability and practical utility were validated on a Franka Research 3 arm with different measurement types.

1. **Cups Game:**
 - **Scenario:** Tracking an object hidden under one of two cups, which are moved by human hands. AprilTags tracked cups/object, and MediaPipe tracked human hands.
 - **Results:** The robot equipped with OPF robustly tracked the hidden object despite complex human-induced occlusions. The robot's motion was smooth, avoiding the jerky behavior seen with standard PFs. The feedback module enabled the robot to actively stop when uncertainty exploded, demonstrating a crucial safety feature.

2. **Sugar-dropping (Hardware Variant):**
 - **Scenario:** Robot tracking a bowl (occluded by a static barrier) and releasing sugar when confident in the bowl's state after occlusion. Blindfolded users generated various trajectories.
 - **Results:** The robot successfully dropped sugar into the bowl with a high probability (8/10), demonstrating accurate tracking and intelligent decision-making based on uncertainty (only releasing when uncertainty was low after occlusion).

3. **Different Measurement Types Validation:**
 - **CV-based pose tracker (BundleSDF on YCB dataset):** Demonstrated OPF's ability to handle complete and prolonged occlusion (e.g., a mustard bottle inside a cookie box).
 - **Marker-based motion capture system (Optitrack):** Showed robust 6-DoF tracking of a baseball during a 0.5-second deliberate motion capture system disablement, accurately modeling its parabolic trajectory. This highlights the OPF's adaptability and performance with high-frequency, precise systems.

In essence, the findings consistently show that the OPF provides substantially more accurate, smoother, and robust object tracking under various occlusion scenarios, both in simulation and hardware. Its explicit management of uncertainty provides valuable feedback for safer and more intelligent robot behavior, and its agnostic nature allows it to integrate with diverse perception systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Object Permanence Filter (OPF) represents a significant advancement in robust perception for interactive robots, with broad implications for the field of Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) and beyond.

1. **Enhanced Robot Resilience and Safety in HRI:** The most direct impact of the OPF is its ability to make robots safer and more resilient in human environments. By enabling robots to maintain accurate object tracks even when objects are occluded, the OPF mitigates a primary bottleneck for HRI deployment. This means robots can predict human intent more reliably, execute handover tasks more smoothly, and avoid undesirable jerky motions or catastrophic failures caused by perception loss. The explicit uncertainty feedback allows for cautious behaviors or alerts, directly contributing to provably safer human-robot collaboration.

2. **Cognitively Inspired Robotics:** The work successfully translates a fundamental principle of human cognitive development—object permanence—into a computational framework. This approach moves beyond purely data-driven or heuristic solutions, offering a more intuitive and principled way for robots to reason about their dynamic environments. This cognitive inspiration could pave the way for more human-like intelligence in robotic perception and decision-making.

3. **Generalizability and Modularity:** A key strength of the OPF is its "agnostic nature" to measurement types. It can be built upon various existing 6-DoF trackers (CV-based, marker-based, etc.), demonstrating its modularity and versatility. This means that existing robotic systems can potentially integrate the OPF without a complete overhaul of their perception stack, accelerating its adoption and impact across different applications and hardware configurations.

4. **Foundation for More Complex HRI Tasks:** Robust object tracking is foundational for many advanced HRI tasks, including collaborative assembly, shared autonomy, and intuitive human-robot communication. By providing a stable perceptual base, the OPF enables the development of more sophisticated higher-level robotic behaviors that depend on a continuous and reliable understanding of object states, even when visual information is incomplete.

5. **Addressing Limitations of Current AI:** While deep learning has revolutionized perception, it still struggles with out-of-distribution data and reasoning about occluded entities in a generalized way. The OPF offers a complementary approach that explicitly models object persistence, potentially enhancing the robustness of learning-based perception systems when integrated.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper opens several avenues for future work, including incorporating physics-based object dynamics and kinematic constraints (e.g., for human skeleton tracking and handovers). This suggests that the OPF framework can be extended to model more complex interactions and environmental factors, further enriching robot intelligence and broadening its application scope. Automated parameter tuning based on object 3D shape is also proposed, which would enhance the generalizability of thresholds for motion and occlusion detection.

In conclusion, the Object Permanence Filter represents a significant step towards enabling robots to perceive and interact with the world more intelligently and robustly, much like humans do. Its theoretical grounding in cognitive science, combined with its practical demonstrations and generalizability, positions it as a valuable tool for accelerating the safe and effective integration of robots into human-centric environments.
