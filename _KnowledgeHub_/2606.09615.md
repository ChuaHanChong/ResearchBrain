# DexPIE: Stable Dexterous Policy Improvement from Real-World Experience

> [!summary] Summary
> Hunan University researchers developed DexPIE, a framework for robustly improving dexterous manipulation policies by integrating real-world experience through human-in-the-loop data collection and a novel continuous optimality-conditioned learning approach. The method achieved an average 37% increase in success rate over the initial behavior cloning policy across three real-world tasks, outperforming other post-training baselines.

## Problem

- Imitation learning policies for dexterous manipulation suffer from compounding errors and high data requirements, limiting their robustness in varied real-world scenarios.
- High-dimensional action spaces and complex contact dynamics in dexterous tasks make effective autonomous exploration challenging for reinforcement learning.
- Long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards lead to credit assignment problems, while existing optimality conditioning often uses limited binary labels, discarding valuable quality information.
- Temporal inconsistencies between smoothly recorded demonstrations and noisy real-world robot deployment create a "demonstration-deployment gap" that hinders policy improvement.

## Method

- An intuitive ==human-in-the-loop== intervention system, using a "human-as-follower" strategy and staged DAgger, facilitates exploration and error recovery for dexterous hands from arbitrary robot states.
- Asynchronous inference, coupled with future-state-referenced relative action padding, ensures temporal consistency between training data and real-world deployment, mitigating distribution shifts.
- A continuous optimality function, derived from a distributional value network, provides fine-grained, continuous supervision for a diffusion policy actor, leveraging nuanced differences in action quality.

## Results

- DexPIE improved success rates by an average of **37%** over the initial Behavioral Cloning policy across three real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, outperforming HG-DAgger and RECAP.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that asynchronous inference led to a **14%** improvement in success rate, while staged DAgger provided an **8%** improvement, highlighting their individual contributions to robust learning.
- The learned value functions accurately captured task progress, hesitation, and failure patterns, confirming the effectiveness of the distributional critic and staged DAgger in improving credit assignment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intuitive human intervention, adapted for high-DoF dexterous hands, is crucial for efficient error recovery and exploration in complex real-world settings.
> - Decomposing long-horizon tasks with staged DAgger provides effective intermediate anchors for value learning, significantly improving credit assignment with sparse rewards.
> - Maintaining temporal consistency between demonstration and deployment data is critical for a coherent training signal, enabling the critic to learn more effectively from real-world rollouts.
> - Continuous optimality conditioning offers a more robust and granular learning signal than binary labels, leading to more stable and precise policy improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. However, many current VLA approaches predominantly rely on Imitation Learning (IL). While IL can enable robots to learn complex tasks from human demonstrations, it typically requires substantial amounts of expert data and often leads to compounding errors during deployment in novel or slightly perturbed environments. These errors can cause performance degradation and limit the scalability of IL-trained policies.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers an alternative paradigm, enabling policies to improve autonomously through interaction with the real world, thus potentially overcoming the limitations of static demonstration data. Incorporating real-world experience allows policies to correct deployment-time errors and perform tasks more robustly. However, extending this RL paradigm to dexterous manipulation presents significant challenges. Dexterous hands operate in high-dimensional action spaces with complex, contact-rich dynamics, making effective exploration difficult.

Recent research has explored Human-In-the-Loop (HIL) RL post-training as a method to mitigate exploration bottlenecks, address Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) issues, and improve sample efficiency, particularly for policies warm-started from demonstrations. Most existing HIL methods, however, have been developed for parallel grippers, and their direct adaptation to the intricacies of dexterous hands remains an open problem.

Furthermore, long-horizon manipulation tasks in real-world RL often necessitate informative and fine-grained reward signals. Sparse rewards, such as binary success indicators, are commonly used due to their simplicity but introduce credit-assignment challenges. Approaches like annotating task stages or using progress labels have been explored, but these can be labor-intensive or rely on external supervision. Some works, such as RECAP, have demonstrated stable policy improvement through advantage-conditioned policy extraction but typically use binary optimality labels, which may not capture the full spectrum of action quality.

Finally, a notable challenge in deploying policies trained on demonstration data is the "demonstration-deployment gap." Inconsistent deployment behaviors, often due to temporal noise from synchronous inference, can introduce distribution shifts between training demonstrations and real-world deployment rollouts. This mismatch can lead to low-quality data for IL and a heterogeneous mixture of behaviors in post-training datasets, complicating critic learning in RL frameworks. DexPIE addresses these challenges by proposing a comprehensive post-training framework specifically designed for stable dexterous policy improvement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a stable and effective framework, DexPIE, for improving dexterous manipulation policies by leveraging real-world experience collected through deployment. This objective is motivated by several specific challenges inherent in learning dexterous manipulation:

1. **Overcoming Imitation Learning Limitations:** Policies trained purely from expert demonstrations frequently suffer from compounding errors when deployed in varied real-world scenarios. They also require substantial amounts of data. DexPIE aims to move beyond these limitations by enabling policies to autonomously learn and correct errors from their own deployment experiences, thereby increasing robustness and reducing reliance on large initial demonstration datasets.
2. **Addressing Exploration Bottlenecks in Dexterous Manipulation:** Dexterous manipulation tasks are characterized by high-dimensional action spaces and intricate contact dynamics, which make effective exploration challenging for autonomous agents. Traditional HIL RL methods are often designed for simpler gripper–arm systems and do not readily transfer to dexterous hands. A key motivation is to develop an intuitive and adaptable human intervention system that facilitates exploration and error recovery for dexterous hands from arbitrary robot states.
3. **Enhancing Credit Assignment in Long-Horizon Tasks with Sparse Rewards:** Real-world dexterous manipulation often involves long task horizons with sparse reward signals, such as binary success or failure. This sparsity complicates credit assignment, making it difficult for the agent to discern the contribution of individual actions to the final outcome. The work aims to mitigate this issue by providing more informative supervision for value function learning, specifically through data collection strategies that introduce intermediate anchors for critic learning.
4. **Bridging the Demonstration-Deployment Gap:** Temporal inconsistencies can arise between the smoothly recorded human demonstrations and robot deployment, particularly when using synchronous inference. These inconsistencies introduce noise and distribution shifts in the collected data, hindering the effectiveness of policy improvement. A motivation is to develop a mechanism that ensures temporal consistency between training data and deployment rollouts, allowing the critic to learn from a more coherent and consistent set of behaviors.
5. **Leveraging Fine-Grained Optimality Information:** Existing policy improvement methods based on optimality conditioning often rely on binary labels (e.g., successful vs. failed trajectories). This binary classification can discard valuable information about the relative quality of different behaviors within a dataset. The paper aims to utilize a continuous optimality indicator that preserves the fine-grained differences in action quality, enabling the policy to leverage this information for more stable and nuanced improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexPIE is structured as a post-training pipeline for dexterous manipulation, integrating several components to achieve stable policy improvement.

#### 3.1. Human-in-the-Loop Data Collection for Dexterous Manipulation

To address exploration and error recovery in real-world dexterous tasks, DexPIE incorporates two main data collection mechanisms:

- **Human-Following Intervention System:** This system facilitates intuitive human intervention during policy deployment. It employs a multi-threaded architecture allowing parallel operation of teleoperation and autonomous policy execution, with seamless switching via keyboard commands. The teleoperation interface utilizes a Vive tracker for wrist pose and Manus gloves for full hand posture, retargeted to the Inspire hand's joint space. A "human-as-follower" strategy is introduced: the operator first aligns their wrist and hand gesture with the robot's current end-effector and dexterous-hand posture. Upon intervention, the system records the current tracker and robot end-effector poses as references. Subsequent relative tracker motions are applied to the robot end-effector, while the policy's hand action is replaced by the retargeted glove action. Smoothing is applied during transitions to prevent abrupt movements. This design enables intuitive corrective control from any robot state.
- **Staged DAgger-based Data Collection:** To improve exploration coverage and address credit assignment in long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards, DexPIE employs staged DAgger. This involves initializing rollouts not only from the initial task state but also from selected intermediate task stages. If an autonomous rollout fails, the environment is restored to the same stage, and the human intervenes before the failure point, guiding the robot to completion. This process generates paired failed and corrected trajectories. These later-stage trajectories provide empirical returns that shorten the effective horizon for value supervision and serve as "intermediate anchors" for critic learning. This approach effectively decomposes the long-horizon value estimation into shorter, stage-wise subproblems.

#### 3.2. Bridging Demonstration-Deployment Gap via Asynchronous Inference

To mitigate the temporal noise and distribution shift between demonstration data and real-world deployment rollouts, DexPIE extends training-time action chunking to a future-state-referenced relative action padding scheme for asynchronous inference:

- **Asynchronous Inference in Relative Action Space:** Synchronous inference can introduce latency-induced pauses, leading to action streams that are temporally inconsistent with training demonstrations. DexPIE uses action chunks of a defined horizon (H). During training, random masking of initial actions (up to a maximum inference delay 'n') is applied to encourage policy flexibility to varying historical action contexts. During deployment, when an action chunk reaches its maximum delay, asynchronous inference is initiated with the current observation. The remaining actions from the current chunk, originally relative to an earlier observation, are transformed into the reference frame of the current observation. These transformed actions serve as a "relative-action prefix" for the next predicted chunk. While these remaining prefix actions are executed, the policy asynchronously predicts the subsequent action chunk. Once the current chunk finishes, its prefix is removed from the newly predicted chunk, and the remaining actions are executed. This process generates a continuous action stream that is temporally aligned with teleoperation data.

#### 3.3. Policy Improvement from Deployment Experience

The framework leverages the collected offline dataset for policy evaluation and improvement:

- **Policy Evaluation:** The method uses a progress-based reward, incorporating sparse terminal rewards and per-step penalties. To handle the sparsity and the multimodal value distributions induced by heterogeneous data from staged DAgger, a distributional value network is employed. Empirical returns from trajectories are modeled as Gaussian distributions and discretized into soft targets over a defined number of value bins. The distributional critic is trained to match these Gaussian soft targets by minimizing the cross-entropy loss. Advantage estimates are then computed using N-step returns from the learned value function.
- **Optimality-Conditioned Policy Improvement:** Based on a product-policy view, policy improvement is achieved by reweighting a reference policy with an optimality indicator. Unlike methods that use binary optimality labels, DexPIE introduces a *continuous optimality function*. This function is defined as a sigmoid transformation of the estimated advantage, scaled by dataset-level quantiles (q_low, q_high) and a temperature coefficient (α). This continuous mapping preserves the relative ordering of action quality, providing fine-grained supervision. The actor, a diffusion policy with a U-Net architecture, is conditioned on this continuous optimality value (encoded via sinusoidal embedding). During training, the optimality indicator can be randomly masked, which supports both direct policy sampling and Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) during inference. The actor is trained to minimize a diffusion policy objective, denoising a noised action sequence conditioned on observation and the continuous optimality indicator.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of DexPIE was evaluated on three real-world dexterous manipulation tasks: Pick-and-Place Bottle, Open Drawer and Tissue Placement, and Lid Opening and Candy Placement. The evaluation involved a UR5 robot arm equipped with a 6-DoF RH56DFX Inspire dexterous hand and a multi-camera perception setup. Policies were evaluated after one iteration of post-training data collection and policy update, with success rates computed over 50 evaluation trials.

- **Quantitative Performance:** DexPIE demonstrated a notable improvement in success rate over the demonstration-based reference policy (Behavior Cloning, BC) and competitive post-training baselines. Across the three tasks, DexPIE achieved an average improvement of 37% in success rate compared to the BC reference policy. It consistently outperformed HG-DAgger and RECAP.
 - **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **HG-DAgger:** While HG-DAgger improved over BC, its performance was inferior to DexPIE. This suggests that while human intervention is beneficial, not leveraging value estimation for long-horizon credit assignment can lead to imitation of lower-quality segments.
 - **RECAP:** DexPIE consistently surpassed RECAP. This finding supports the hypothesis that continuous optimality conditioning, which preserves fine-grained action quality, is more effective than discrete binary labels in guiding policy improvement.
- **Robustness:** DexPIE exhibited stronger robustness to positional variations across tasks. This was attributed to the learning process incorporating diverse failure cases induced by shifted grasping targets and their corresponding human-corrected trajectories. The optimality guidance enabled the policy to differentiate undesirable trajectories from corrective ones, guiding it away from failure patterns.
- **Ablation Study on Temporal Consistency:** An ablation study on Task B demonstrated the impact of asynchronous inference. Policies trained with data collected using asynchronous inference showed a 14% improvement in success rate compared to those using synchronous inference. This indicates that reducing the demonstration-deployment gap by ensuring temporal consistency helps the critic learn a value function induced by a more consistent underlying policy, rather than a mix of mismatched behaviors.
- **Ablation Study on Staged DAgger:** An ablation on Task C evaluated the contribution of staged DAgger. Using staged DAgger yielded an 8% improvement in success rate compared to standard DAgger (initializing rollouts only from the initial state). This result confirms that staged DAgger provides intermediate anchors for learning progress-aware values, effectively decomposing long-horizon tasks and improving credit assignment by enabling the critic to better capture task progress and failure modes.
- **Value Function Visualization:** Visualizations of the learned value functions demonstrated that they accurately capture task progress, hesitation, and failure patterns, including temporary progress regression after human intervention and subsequent recovery. A specific credit-assignment failure case, where the critic misattributed collision failures solely from visual observations, highlighted the importance of informative failure data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DexPIE represents a comprehensive framework for stable dexterous policy improvement in real-world settings. Its significance lies in integrating several novel components to address key challenges in dexterous manipulation learning:

- **Enhanced Policy Improvement from Real-World Experience:** By effectively incorporating experience collected through real-world deployment, DexPIE moves beyond the limitations of pure imitation learning. This ability to self-correct and improve directly from interaction increases policy robustness and reduces the need for extensive initial expert demonstrations.
- **Intuitive Human-in-the-Loop Intervention for Dexterous Hands:** The human-following intervention system provides a practical and intuitive method for humans to guide dexterous manipulation. This is a critical development for high-DoF systems where direct teleoperation can be complex, enabling efficient error recovery and exploration without requiring operators to perfectly align with robot states.
- **Improved Value Learning and Credit Assignment:** Staged DAgger and the distributional value network contribute to more reliable value function learning, particularly for long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards. By providing intermediate progress signals and modeling multimodal value distributions, the framework enhances credit assignment, allowing the policy to discern valuable behaviors more effectively.
- **Mitigation of the Demonstration-Deployment Gap:** The asynchronous inference mechanism, coupled with future-state-referenced relative action padding, helps maintain temporal consistency between training data and deployment. This is crucial for reducing distribution shifts, ensuring that the collected real-world experience is accurately leveraged for policy and critic updates.
- **Fine-Grained Optimality Guidance:** The use of a continuous optimality function, as opposed to binary labels, allows the policy to exploit nuanced differences in action quality. This provides a more robust and granular learning signal, leading to more stable and precise policy improvement.

The successful leveraging of both successful and failure data, combined with quality annotations from policy evaluation, suggests a path toward more efficient data utilization in robot learning. This could unlock the potential of incorporating diverse real-world post-training data into large-scale pre-training initiatives for generalist robots.

The work's limitations, such as the focus on single-arm manipulation and reliance on human intervention for exploration, point towards important future research directions. These include extending the framework to bimanual tasks, handling even longer horizons, adapting to higher-DoF dexterous hands, integrating tactile sensing for finer control, and developing more autonomous and diverse exploration strategies. Overall, DexPIE provides a validated recipe for advancing real-world dexterous manipulation and lays groundwork for further developments in robust and scalable robot learning from experience.
