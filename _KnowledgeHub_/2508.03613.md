# Goedel-Prover-V2: Scaling Formal Theorem Proving with Scaffolded Data Synthesis and Self-Correction

> [!summary] Summary
> The open-source Goedel-Prover-V2 series of language models improves formal theorem proving in Lean through scaffolded data synthesis and verifier-guided self-correction. Its 32B-parameter model achieves 90.4% pass@32 on MiniF2F, outperforming a 671B-parameter system by a large margin while being 20 times smaller, and establishes a new open-source record on PutnamBench with 86 problems solved at pass@184.

## Problem

- Automated formal theorem proving (ATP) in languages like Lean demands absolute logical precision, making it exceptionally challenging for AI systems.
- Existing high-performing ATP models often require prohibitively large parameter counts (e.g., 671 billion) or extensive computational resources for inference.
- The high resource demands limit accessibility for the broader research community and hinder the practical application of these advanced theorem provers.

## Method

- Developed a series of open-source language models based on a "==whole-proof generation==" paradigm for Lean, prioritizing computational efficiency.
- Incorporated verifier-guided self-correction, enabling models to iteratively revise failed proof attempts using specific feedback from the Lean compiler.
- Introduced scaffolded data synthesis methods (formal and informal) to generate diverse and progressively challenging training data.

## Results

- The Goedel-Prover-V2-**32B** model achieves **90.4%** pass@32 on MiniF2F with self-correction, surpassing DeepSeek-Prover-V2-**671B** (**82.4%** pass@32) while being 20 times smaller.
- On PutnamBench, the **32B** model solves 86 problems at pass@184 with self-correction, setting a new open-source record and significantly outperforming DeepSeek-Prover-V2-**671B** (47 problems at pass@1024).
- Verifier-guided self-correction consistently improves performance, yielding an approximately 2 percentage point increase in pass@32 on MiniF2F and enabling 14 additional problems solved on PutnamBench at pass@32.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Verifier-guided self-correction, utilizing compiler error messages and prior attempts, provides significant, sample-efficient performance gains in proof accuracy by mimicking human iterative refinement.
> - Scaffolded data synthesis, through formal and informal methods, is a powerful approach for curating high-quality, progressively difficult training data crucial for complex reasoning tasks.
> - Achieving state-of-the-art performance in ATP does not necessitate massive models or extensive computational resources, demonstrating the effectiveness of targeted methodological improvements over brute-force scaling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Automated theorem proving (ATP) stands as a monumental challenge for artificial intelligence, demanding the rigorous construction of machine-verifiable proofs in formal languages such as Lean. Unlike natural language reasoning, ATP requires absolute logical precision, making it an exceptionally difficult task for AI systems. The field has witnessed rapid progress in recent years, particularly with the advent of large language models (LLMs).

Prior efforts have demonstrated significant breakthroughs:
- **DeepMind's AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry** have achieved International Math Olympiad (IMO) medal-level performance, showcasing the potential for AI to tackle highly complex mathematical problems. These systems often employ sophisticated search algorithms and large computational budgets.
- **Open-source initiatives** like DeepSeek-Prover-V2 (Ren et al., 2025) and Kimina-Prover (Wang et al., 2025) have also made impressive strides on benchmarks such as MiniF2F and PutnamBench. These models typically leverage the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, often through chain-of-thought prompting.

However, a recurring limitation in much of this advanced work is the reliance on either **massive models** (e.g., DeepSeek-Prover-V2 with 671 billion parameters) or **computationally intensive inference** involving complex search algorithms and vast computational resources (e.g., the concurrent Seed-Prover (Chen et al., 2025)). This reliance often makes such systems inaccessible to the broader research community due to prohibitive resource requirements and, in some cases, proprietary nature.

Goedel-Prover-V2 directly addresses these limitations. It positions itself as a series of **open-source models** that aim to achieve and surpass state-of-the-art performance in Lean-based automated theorem proving while prioritizing **computational efficiency** and **modest model scale**. It builds upon the "whole-proof generation" paradigm, where a model generates a complete formal proof in a single pass, a strategy advanced by models like DeepSeek-Prover and Kimina-Prover. However, Goedel-Prover-V2 differentiates itself by deeply integrating **verifier-guided self-correction**—a feature that mimics human proof revision processes and leverages the precise feedback available from formal proof assistants like Lean. This moves beyond pure end-to-end generation to a more interactive and robust proof construction process, akin to how humans iteratively refine their proofs.

Furthermore, the paper places its work within the context of emerging trends such as self-repair and verifier-guided refinement, which seek more efficient methods of guiding proof search without the exhaustive costs of traditional tree-based search. By introducing scaffolded data synthesis, Goedel-Prover-V2 also contributes to the critical area of data curation for formal reasoning, recognizing that high-quality, appropriately challenging data is vital for training robust theorem provers. In essence, Goedel-Prover-V2 represents a significant step towards democratizing high-performance automated theorem proving, demonstrating that frontier advancements are possible with more accessible computational resources and an open-source ethos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the Goedel-Prover-V2 project is to **advance the state-of-the-art in automated formal theorem proving** by developing a new series of open-source language models that are both highly performant and computationally efficient. This primary goal is driven by several key motivations and specific aims:

1. **Achieve New State-of-the-Art Performance:** The most direct objective is to outperform existing top-performing theorem provers, especially on challenging benchmarks like MiniF2F and PutnamBench. The authors specifically aim to surpass the performance of large models like DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B, but with significantly smaller models.
2. **Enhance Computational Efficiency:** A core motivation is to demonstrate that cutting-edge performance in ATP does not necessitate prohibitively large models or massive computational resources for inference. The project seeks to develop models that are efficient in terms of parameter count and test-time compute budget.
3. **Integrate Verifier Feedback for Self-Correction:** A critical design objective is to enable the models to generate whole proofs and, crucially, to iteratively revise and correct these proofs based on real-time feedback from the Lean compiler (verifier). This aims to mimic human-like iterative refinement, improving proof accuracy and robustness.
4. **Improve Data Curation through Scaffolded Synthesis:** Recognizing the importance of high-quality training data, the authors aim to develop novel methods for generating synthetic mathematical problems of increasing difficulty. This "scaffolded data synthesis" is intended to provide better learning signals to the model, allowing it to master increasingly complex theorems systematically.
5. **Mitigate Training Instabilities with Model Averaging:** The project seeks to address common challenges in later stages of LLM training, such as the decrease in output diversity, by employing techniques like model averaging. This aims to maintain and improve the model's ability to explore diverse proof paths.
6. **Democratize Access to Advanced ATP:** A fundamental motivation for releasing Goedel-Prover-V2 as an open-source series (models, code, and data) is to lower the barrier to entry for researchers in the field. By providing accessible, high-performance tools, the authors hope to accelerate community-driven progress in AI systems capable of reliably solving and verifying complex mathematical problems.
7. **Bridge Human and Formal Reasoning:** Ultimately, the project is motivated by the grand challenge of enabling AI systems to bridge the gap between intuitive human mathematical reasoning and the rigorous demands of formal proof verification, contributing to the broader goal of more capable and reliable AI.

These objectives collectively articulate a vision for accessible, efficient, and robust automated theorem proving, moving the field forward not just in raw performance but also in methodology and community engagement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Goedel-Prover-V2 introduces a sophisticated methodology built upon the standard expert iteration and reinforcement learning (RL) pipeline, augmented by three core innovations: scaffolded data synthesis, verifier-guided self-correction, and model averaging. The approach is meticulously designed to optimize both proof generation capability and computational efficiency.

#### 3.1. Core Innovations

1. **Verifier-Guided Self-Correction:** This is a key architectural innovation. Moving beyond single-pass whole-proof generation, the framework explicitly integrates feedback from the Lean compiler (the verifier) into the model's input. After an initial proof attempt, any compilation failures are parsed, and the error messages are fed back to the model as corrective guidance. The model then generates "proof repairs," leading to an iterative self-correction process. This mimics how human mathematicians revise proofs and is crucial for handling the strict logical flow required by formal languages.
2. **Scaffolded Data Synthesis:** To address the challenge of curating high-quality data at appropriate difficulty levels, two methods are employed:
 - **Formal-based Synthesis:** When the prover fails a challenging problem, the Lean tactic `extract goal` is used to identify unsolved subgoals. These subgoals, along with their preconditions, form simpler, well-formed mathematical statements. Both the extracted goal and its negation are added to the training data, teaching the model to recognize both true and false propositions.
 - **Informal-based Synthesis:** This leverages LLMs (specifically Qwen3-32B) to generate simpler or harder variants of existing problems in natural language. The LLM first attempts to solve the original problem to provide context for generating related problems. These informal statements are then formalized into Lean using a dedicated "formalizer" and filtered by another LLM-based system that assesses correctness and difficulty, discarding trivial or incorrect statements.
3. **Model Averaging:** To counter the observed decrease in model output diversity (where pass@1 might improve but pass@N declines) in later stages of SFT and RL, the authors apply model averaging. This technique combines the parameters of the base model ($\theta_0$) with those of the fine-tuned model ($\theta$) using a weighted average $(1-\alpha)\theta_0 + \alpha\theta$. This simple yet effective approach is shown to enhance the diversity of the final model's features, leading to better pass@N performance.

#### 3.2. Training Algorithms and Pipeline

The training pipeline for Goedel-Prover-V2 is a multi-stage process:

1. **Formalizer Training:** A prerequisite step involves training a robust "formalizer" using an expert iteration pipeline. This formalizer translates natural language math problems into Lean statements, relying on Lean syntax checks and LLM-based semantic evaluation to ensure translation quality. Claude Sonnet 4 is used to generate an initial 50K formalized statements.
2. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) & Expert Iteration:**
 - **Initial SFT (S1):** The process begins by performing large-scale inference using DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B and -671B to generate an initial SFT dataset (S1) for complete proof generation.
 - **Model Fine-tuning:** Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B models are then fine-tuned on S1, yielding SFT-S1 models.
 - **Self-Correction Data Integration (S2):** Self-correction data is annotated using SFT-S1 and DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (leveraging NeMo-Skills for inference). This data, including error messages, is incorporated into S1 to create an enhanced dataset S2. SFT is then performed on S2, resulting in SFT-S2 models.
 - **First Model Averaging:** Model averaging is applied between SFT-S2 and the base models, yielding SFT-S2-AVG.
3. **Scaffolded Data Integration (S3):** The SFT-S2-AVG models are used to generate the dataset S3 through the scaffolded data synthesis pipeline. SFT continues on S3, producing SFT-S3, followed by another round of model averaging to get SFT-S3-AVG.
4. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - The SFT-S3-AVG model serves as the starting point for RL.
 - A multi-task RL setup is adopted, with 50% of inputs for whole-proof generation and 50% for first-round self-correction.
 - A hybrid GRPO-based approach is used, incorporating enhancements like removal of group normalization (from Dr.GRPO), clip-higher, overlong penalties, and dynamic sampling (from DAPO). KL regularization is excluded to encourage exploration.
 - Dynamic sampling is modified to focus on problems with pass rates in the range (0, 0.75] during optimization, optimizing learning signals.
 - **Final Model Averaging:** After RL, a final model averaging step is performed, resulting in the RL-AVG models, which are the final Goedel-Prover-V2 models.

#### 3.3. Benchmarks

Evaluation is conducted on a diverse set of benchmarks under Lean 4.9.0-rc1:
- **MiniF2F (Zheng et al., 2021):** 488 high-school level problems from competitions like AMC, AIME, and IMO, using a corrected version.
- **PutnamBench (Tsoukalas et al., 2024):** 644 college-level problems from the William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition.
- **MathOlympiadBench:** A newly constructed benchmark of 360 human-verified formalizations of Olympiad-level problems, addressing quality issues found in other datasets.

The use of sequential self-correction (up to 2 additional rounds) and reporting pass@N metrics (e.g., pass@32, pass@184) are standard evaluation practices in the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Goedel-Prover-V2 demonstrates remarkable performance and efficiency, setting new state-of-the-art records among open-source theorem provers and competitive results against closed-source systems.

#### 4.1. High Performance at Modest Scale

- **MiniF2F Benchmark:**
 - The flagship **Goedel-Prover-V2-32B model** achieves **88.1% pass@32**, which further improves to **90.4% with two rounds of verifier-guided self-correction**. This significantly surpasses the previous state-of-the-art DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B, which achieved 82.4% pass@32, despite Goedel-Prover-V2-32B being roughly 20 times smaller in parameter count.
 - Even the smaller **Goedel-Prover-V2-8B model** achieves **84.6% pass@32**, outperforming the 671B-parameter DeepSeek-Prover-V2 and nearly matching the 70B Kimina-Prover. This highlights the exceptional efficiency of the Goedel-Prover-V2 approach, delivering superior performance with a model 80 times smaller than its predecessor.
- **PutnamBench:**
 - The **Goedel-Prover-V2-32B model** solves **43 problems at pass@32**, which is nearly double DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B's 22 problems under the same metric.
 - With self-correction, Goedel-Prover-V2-32B solves **57 problems at pass@32**.
 - Under a higher budget (pass@184) with self-correction, Goedel-Prover-V2-32B solves an impressive **86 problems**, securing the first place among open-source models on the leaderboard. This far exceeds DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B's record of 47 problems at pass@1024, demonstrating substantial gains in both absolute performance and computational efficiency.
- **MathOlympiadBench:** The flagship model also shows strong performance on this new, challenging benchmark.

#### 4.2. Efficacy of Verifier-Guided Self-Correction

- The integration of verifier feedback for self-correction consistently yields significant performance gains. On MiniF2F, self-correction provides an approximately **2 percentage point increase** in pass@32 for both the 8B and 32B models.
- On PutnamBench, error correction leads to **14 additional problems solved** at pass@32.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of specific compiler error messages and the chain-of-thought from previous attempts in the self-correction process. With extended context (128k tokens) and more iterations, the self-correction mode can achieve up to 92.7% pass@32 on MiniF2F, surpassing even the model's performance without self-correction at pass@8192, underscoring its sample efficiency.

#### 4.3. Sample-Efficient Inference and Scaling Behavior

- Goedel-Prover-V2 models achieve very high pass@N scores with minimal inference overhead (e.g., N=32 or 64), indicating that the models have internalized powerful reasoning strategies during training. This contrasts with prior models that often rely on much larger sampling budgets or test-time reinforcement learning to reach peak accuracy.
- The scaling analysis shows that Goedel-Prover-V2 maintains its performance advantage across various inference budgets, with consistent improvements from self-correction. The 8B model's scalability is particularly noteworthy, surpassing the 671B DeepSeek model at all tested budgets.

#### 4.4. Impact of RL and Model Averaging

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL training consistently improves pass@1 accuracy, indicating better single-shot proof generation. Performance on pass@N also benefits from RL, especially in the correction setting, suggesting that RL helps to improve the model's ability to correct errors.
- **Model Averaging:** This technique is shown to effectively enhance model diversity and improve pass@N metrics for both vanilla proof generation and self-correction. The studies reveal an optimal model averaging ratio that maximizes pass@N, with the benefits being more pronounced for the self-correction mechanism.

In summary, Goedel-Prover-V2 represents a significant leap forward by combining architectural innovations with a refined training pipeline to achieve unprecedented performance in formal theorem proving, all while making these capabilities more accessible through smaller model sizes and open-source availability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Goedel-Prover-V2 marks a significant milestone in the field of automated theorem proving (ATP) and has profound implications for the broader landscape of AI and mathematical reasoning.

1. **Democratization of Advanced ATP:** Perhaps the most impactful contribution is the demonstration that cutting-edge formal theorem proving can be achieved without access to exorbitantly large models, vast computational resources, or proprietary technology. By releasing the models, code, and data open-source, Goedel-Prover-V2 lowers the barrier to entry, enabling a wider community of researchers, students, and practitioners to engage with and build upon advanced ATP capabilities. This fosters innovation and accelerates progress, moving the field beyond resource-intensive approaches.

2. **New State-of-the-Art Baseline with Efficiency:** Goedel-Prover-V2 establishes a new state-of-the-art in open-source ATP, significantly outperforming much larger and more computationally intensive models. This achievement underscores the value of methodological innovations over brute-force scaling alone. The efficiency of the 8B and 32B models makes them practical for deployment in a wider array of applications, from educational tools to integrated development environments for formal verification.

3. **Methodological Innovations for Robust Reasoning:** The paper's core innovations provide a blueprint for future research in LLM-based reasoning:
 - **Verifier-guided self-correction** offers a powerful paradigm for iterative refinement that directly leverages the strengths of formal systems (precise feedback). This approach is highly generalizable to other domains requiring verifiable outputs, such as code generation, scientific simulation, and logical puzzle-solving.
 - **Scaffolded data synthesis** presents an effective strategy for generating high-quality, appropriately challenging training data. This is crucial for training complex reasoning agents and can help overcome data scarcity issues in specialized domains.
 - **Model averaging** as a technique to maintain and enhance output diversity during late-stage training addresses a common challenge in reinforcement learning and fine-tuning, improving the robustness and exploration capabilities of models.

4. **Bridging Intuitive and Formal Reasoning:** The self-correction mechanism explicitly mimics how human mathematicians approach proofs—iteratively refining their work based on feedback. This aligns AI systems more closely with human cognitive processes, potentially leading to more interpretable and robust mathematical AI. The work contributes to bridging the long-standing divide between the flexibility of intuitive human reasoning and the rigor of formal proof verification.

5. **Catalyst for Interdisciplinary Research:** The open-source nature and high performance of Goedel-Prover-V2 are likely to catalyze new research at the intersection of machine learning, formal methods, mathematics, and even automated software engineering. Researchers can now more easily experiment with new algorithms, test hypotheses, and develop applications that integrate sophisticated formal reasoning.

In conclusion, Goedel-Prover-V2 is not merely an incremental improvement; it represents a strategic shift towards more accessible, efficient, and methodologically sound approaches to automated theorem proving. Its contributions will undoubtedly accelerate the development of AI systems that can reliably understand, generate, and verify complex mathematical knowledge.
