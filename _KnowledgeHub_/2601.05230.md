# Learning Latent Action World Models In The Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at FAIR at Meta developed a method to learn effective latent action world models directly from diverse, unlabeled "in-the-wild" video data. The approach enables planning performance on robotic manipulation and navigation tasks comparable to models trained with explicit action labels.

## Problem

- Most successful world models rely on explicit action labels, limiting their scalability and generality when applied to the vast amount of unlabeled real-world video data.
- Prior latent action models (LAMs) were confined to constrained environments, leading to action spaces that lacked transferability and generalization to varied real-world scenarios.
- "In-the-wild" videos present challenges such as ill-defined actions, environmental noise, and a lack of a common embodiment, making it difficult to extract meaningful action representations.

## Method

- A joint training framework for a ==world model and an inverse dynamics model (IDM)== on large-scale, unlabeled "in-the-wild" video datasets, leveraging a frozen ==V-JEPA 2-L visual encoder==.
- Investigation of ==information regularization techniques== -- ==sparsity==, ==noise addition (VAE-like)==, and ==discretization== -- to control the content of continuous latent actions and prevent encoding irrelevant information.
- Evaluation of the learned ==latent action space== through intrinsic metrics like ==future leakage== and ==action transferability==, and practical utility on downstream planning tasks via lightweight controllers and the ==Cross-Entropy Method==.

## Results

- The world model achieves planning performance on robotic manipulation and navigation tasks (e.g., DROID, RECON) that is competitive with baselines trained on domain-specific, action-labeled data.
- Latent actions exhibit minimal future leakage and good transferability, with cycle consistency experiments showing only minor error increases.
- Optimal downstream planning performance is attained with a balanced regularization strength, with noise-added continuous latent actions generally yielding the best results.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous latent actions, particularly when regularized with noise or sparsity, effectively capture complex dynamics in uncurated videos, outperforming discrete action representations.
> - Latent actions learned from diverse "in-the-wild" data encode spatially-localized, camera-relative transformations, enabling broad action transferability across different objects and contexts.
> - Information regularization is critical for balancing the expressiveness of latent actions, ensuring they capture relevant dynamics without overfitting to environmental noise or encoding excessive future information.
