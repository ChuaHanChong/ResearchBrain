# Learning Latent Action World Models In The Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at FAIR at Meta developed a method to learn effective latent action world models directly from diverse, unlabeled "in-the-wild" video data. The approach enables planning performance on robotic manipulation and navigation tasks comparable to models trained with explicit action labels.

## Problem

- Most successful world models rely on explicit action labels, limiting their scalability and generality when applied to the vast amount of unlabeled real-world video data.
- Prior latent action models (LAMs) were confined to constrained environments, leading to action spaces that lacked transferability and generalization to varied real-world scenarios.
- "In-the-wild" videos present challenges such as ill-defined actions, environmental noise, and a lack of a common embodiment, making it difficult to extract meaningful action representations.

## Method

- A joint training framework for a ==world model and an inverse dynamics model (IDM)== on large-scale, unlabeled "in-the-wild" video datasets, leveraging a frozen ==V-JEPA 2-L visual encoder==.
- Investigation of ==information regularization techniques== -- ==sparsity==, ==noise addition (VAE-like)==, and ==discretization== -- to control the content of continuous latent actions and prevent encoding irrelevant information.
- Evaluation of the learned ==latent action space== through intrinsic metrics like ==future leakage== and ==action transferability==, and practical utility on downstream planning tasks via lightweight controllers and the ==Cross-Entropy Method==.

## Results

- The world model achieves planning performance on robotic manipulation and navigation tasks (e.g., DROID, RECON) that is competitive with baselines trained on domain-specific, action-labeled data.
- Latent actions exhibit minimal future leakage and good transferability, with cycle consistency experiments showing only minor error increases.
- Optimal downstream planning performance is attained with a balanced regularization strength, with noise-added continuous latent actions generally yielding the best results.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous latent actions, particularly when regularized with noise or sparsity, effectively capture complex dynamics in uncurated videos, outperforming discrete action representations.
> - Latent actions learned from diverse "in-the-wild" data encode spatially-localized, camera-relative transformations, enabling broad action transferability across different objects and contexts.
> - Information regularization is critical for balancing the expressiveness of latent actions, ensuring they capture relevant dynamics without overfitting to environmental noise or encoding excessive future information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental challenge in the pursuit of artificial general intelligence (AGI): building agents that can reason and plan by predicting the consequences of their actions. This capability is at the heart of various theoretical frameworks for intelligence, including predictive coding, active inference, and reinforcement learning, with seminal works by Friston, Clark, Sutton, and Schmidhuber.

Traditionally, many successful world models (e.g., DreamerV3 by Hafner et al., works by Ha & Schmidhuber) have relied on explicit action labels. These models learn to predict future states given a sequence of past observations and a *known* action. However, the reliance on action labels poses a critical bottleneck: the vast majority of real-world video data available online is unlabeled, diverse, and often lacks a common embodiment (e.g., a specific robot arm or camera wearer). This severely limits the scalability and generality of action-conditioned world models to real-world scenarios.

This limitation has spurred the development of **Latent Action Models (LAMs)**, which aim to discover an action space directly from unlabeled videos. Prior LAM research (Edwards et al., Rybkin et al., Menapace et al., Schmidt & Jiang) has shown promise, but typically operates in constrained environments such as simple robotics simulations, video games, or highly curated manipulation datasets. These narrow domains yield action spaces specialized to a single embodiment, lacking transferability and generalization to the richness of the real world.

"Learning Latent Action World Models In The Wild" directly tackles this generalization gap. By focusing on **large-scale, "in-the-wild" video datasets** like HowTo100M and YouTubeTemporal-1B, the authors push LAMs beyond their traditional boundaries. This move introduces significant new challenges, which the paper meticulously addresses:
1. **Ill-defined "actions":** In unstructured videos, "actions" can range from ego-centric camera movements to complex exo-centric events (e.g., people dancing, cars entering a frame, fingers forming chords).
2. **Environmental noise:** Diverse natural videos contain distractors like swaying leaves, which a model might erroneously encode as actions.
3. **Lack of common embodiment:** Unlike robotic setups, there's no single agent or camera perspective to latch onto across varied "in-the-wild" videos.

The paper leverages the power of **self-supervised learning (SSL)**, specifically employing a frozen V-JEPA 2-L encoder (Assran et al., 2025). This integration highlights a broader trend in AI research: using powerful, universally pre-trained visual encoders to extract robust representations, which then serve as a foundation for more specialized tasks like world modeling. This approach significantly reduces the data requirements for training the world model itself and allows the model to focus on learning dynamics rather than basic visual features.

By demonstrating the viability of LAMs on uncurated internet videos, this work positions itself as a crucial step towards building truly generalist agents. Such agents could learn from the vast repository of human experience available online, moving closer to the vision of systems that understand and interact with the physical world in an intuitive, predictive manner, independent of explicit programming or extensive manual labeling. It also implicitly connects to fields like Visual Language Action (VLA) models and advanced video generation, though the paper emphasizes its focus on predictive world modeling for planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the field of world models by enabling them to learn effective latent action spaces directly from diverse, unlabeled "in-the-wild" video data. This aims to overcome a fundamental limitation of most existing world models and unlock the vast potential of real-world video resources for building intelligent systems.

The specific key objectives and underlying motivations are:

1. **Scale Latent Action Models to Real-World Videos:** The primary goal is to expand the scope of Latent Action Models (LAMs) beyond simplified simulations, video games, or curated datasets to complex, unstructured "in-the-wild" videos (e.g., YouTubeTemporal-1B).
 - **Motivation:** Existing world models are bottlenecked by the scarcity of action-labeled data, which is crucial for predicting action consequences. Unlabeled videos, however, are abundant. Tapping into this resource is essential for developing general-purpose AI that can reason and plan in open-ended real-world environments.

2. **Address Challenges of In-the-Wild Data:** To identify and tackle the unique challenges introduced by the diversity and complexity of natural videos.
 - **Motivation:** "In-the-wild" data introduces ambiguity in defining actions, the presence of environmental noise (e.g., background movement not related to agent actions), and a lack of consistent embodiment. Successfully navigating these issues is paramount for the practical applicability of LAMs.

3. **Study Information Regularization for Latent Actions:** Investigate and compare different techniques for regulating the information content of latent actions.
 - **Motivation:** The Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) component in LAMs can "cheat" by encoding too much information (e.g., the entire next frame) into the latent action, or by capturing irrelevant environmental noise. The goal is to find mechanisms (sparsity, noise addition, quantization) that ensure latent actions capture *minimal, relevant* information about true actions while still being expressive enough to model complex dynamics.

4. **Characterize Learned Latent Actions:** Understand the nature of the actions learned from unstructured videos, particularly regarding transferability and embodiment.
 - **Motivation:** If latent actions are to be truly general and useful, they must be transferable across different scenes and embodiments. Understanding what kind of "embodiment" emerges (e.g., spatially-localized vs. global, object-specific vs. camera-relative) is crucial for assessing their utility and guiding future development.

5. **Demonstrate Practical Utility for Downstream Tasks:** Show that the learned latent action space can serve as a universal interface for control and can be effectively used for planning in realistic scenarios.
 - **Motivation:** The ultimate test of a world model is its ability to facilitate planning and decision-making. If latent actions can be mapped to known control signals, even models trained on general videos could become powerful tools for robotics and embodied AI, eliminating the need for extensive domain-specific action labeling.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the grand vision of building intelligent systems that learn predictive models of the world in an unsupervised manner from the vastness of natural video data, thus breaking free from the constraints of meticulously labeled, narrow datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology involves jointly training a world model and an inverse dynamics model (IDM) on large-scale, unlabeled "in-the-wild" video datasets, with a particular focus on regulating the information content of the learned latent actions.

#### 3.1. Core Architecture and Training Setup

- **Frozen Visual Encoder:** The initial and critical step is to obtain robust visual representations of video frames. The authors use a powerful, pre-trained, and *frozen* frame-causal encoder, **V-JEPA 2-L** (Assran et al., 2025), to map video frames `V_t` into latent visual states `s_t`. This leverages state-of-the-art self-supervised learning for visual feature extraction, allowing the subsequent models to focus purely on dynamics.
- **Joint Training of World Model and Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM):**
 - **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM), `g_ϕ`:** Given the current and future visual states (`s_t`, `s_t+1`), the IDM infers a latent action `z_t` that explains the transition between them. This is a common approach in latent action learning, creating a causal leak (looking into the future to infer action) which necessitates regularization.
 - **World Model (Forward Model), `p_ψ`:** This model predicts the next visual state `s_t+1` given the past sequence of visual states `s_0:t` and the inferred latent action `z_t`.
 - **Loss Function:** The primary training objective is to minimize the L1 norm reconstruction error between the predicted next state `p_ψ(s_0:t, z_t)` and the ground truth `s_t+1`, combined with a regularization term `L_z(z_t)` for the latent action.
- **World Model Implementation:** `p_ψ` is implemented as a **ViT-L (Vision Transformer Large)** architecture, equipped with RoPE (Rotary Position Embeddings) for positional information. It is conditioned on latent actions `z_t` using an adapted **AdaLN-zero** mechanism, which applies frame-wise conditioning.
- **Data and Training Environment:** Models are trained on **YouTubeTemporal-1B** (Zellers et al., 2022), a massive dataset of "in-the-wild" videos, using 16-frame clips at 4 frames per second (fps). Training uses teacher forcing for efficiency. A large-scale distributed setup (64 H100 GPUs) and specialized optimizers (Muon and AdamW) are employed, reflecting the demanding computational requirements.
- **Video Decoder:** For qualitative analysis and perceptual metrics, a separate frame-causal video decoder (also a ViT-L based architecture) is trained to reconstruct pixel frames from the learned latent visual states, using a combination of L1 and perceptual losses.

#### 3.2. Information Regularization for Latent Actions (`L_z(z_t)`)

The paper's central contribution is a detailed study of how to constrain the information content of the 128-dimensional continuous latent action vectors `z_t` to prevent cheating (encoding the entire future state) or capturing excessive noise. Three main mechanisms are explored:

- **Sparsity:** Aims to make `z_t` sparse, concentrating information in a few dimensions. This involves a custom regularization term combining L1 penalties with a **Variance-Covariance-Mean (VCM) regularization** (inspired by VICReg). VCM ensures that the latent dimensions are well-utilized, decorrelated, and centered, allowing sparsity constraints to be effectively applied.
- **Noise Addition (VAE-like):** Limits information by adding noise to `z_t`, conceptually similar to a Variational Autoencoder (VAE). This is implemented by a KL-divergence loss `D_KL(q(z_t|s_t, s_t+1) || N(0,1))` that encourages `z_t` to follow a standard Gaussian distribution, thus regularizing its norm and adding stochasticity.
- **Discretization (Vector Quantization):** A common approach in prior LAMs, this method discretizes `z_t` into a finite codebook of learned vectors. The authors use a standard vector quantization scheme (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) with codebook resets for unused codes, serving as a baseline comparison.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocols

The evaluation encompasses both intrinsic properties of the learned latent actions and their utility in downstream tasks:

- **In-the-Wild Prediction Quality:** Measures the one-step prediction error of the world model when latent actions are inferred by the IDM. This serves as an upper bound on performance and helps evaluate the capacity of different regularization methods.
- **Future Leakage Test:** Assesses whether latent actions encode too much of the future state by measuring the prediction error increase when an abrupt scene change is artificially introduced. A significant error spike indicates minimal leakage.
- **Action Transferability / Cycle Consistency:** Evaluates the generality and re-inferability of latent actions. An action inferred from video A is applied to video B, then re-inferred from B's prediction and reapplied to A. The error increase measures transfer reliability.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visual inspection of generated videos to understand the nature of learned actions (e.g., spatial locality, embodiment).
- **Downstream Planning:** This is a crucial practical evaluation.
 - **Controller Training:** A lightweight controller (MLP or cross-attention adapter) is trained to map *known* real-world actions (from robotic or navigation datasets) and past visual representations to the *learned latent actions*.
 - **Planning Task:** The world model, controlled by this mapping, is used to solve goal-based planning tasks. This involves using the **Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)** to find a sequence of real actions that minimizes the distance between predicted and goal states.
 - **Datasets:** Evaluated on **DROID** (Khazatsky et al., 2024) for robotic manipulation and **RECON** (Shah et al., 2021) for navigation tasks, comparing performance against action-conditioned baselines.
- **Scaling Analysis:** Examines how prediction quality and planning performance scale with increased model size, training time, and quantity of training data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings that underscore the feasibility and effectiveness of learning latent action world models from diverse, uncurated video data.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of Continuous Latent Actions

- **Superiority over Discrete Actions:** The study robustly demonstrates that continuous latent actions, regularized either through sparsity or noise addition, are significantly more effective at capturing the complexity of actions in "in-the-wild" videos compared to discrete (vector quantized) latent actions.
- **Capacity and Adaptation:** Sparse and noisy latent actions can achieve a wide range of performance levels by adjusting their regularization strength, indicating a tunable capacity. In contrast, vector quantization struggles to scale its capacity, remaining close to a deterministic world model and failing to capture richer, more nuanced actions (e.g., a person entering a scene, as shown qualitatively).

#### 4.2. Absence of Future Leakage

- A critical concern with IDM-based latent action learning is the risk of "cheating" by encoding the entire future frame into the latent action. The authors' future leakage test, involving artificial scene changes, showed that all tested models exhibited a significant increase (more than double) in prediction error. This indicates that the latent actions do not simply copy the next frame, but rather capture abstract action-relevant information.

#### 4.3. Transferability and Nature of Learned Actions

- **Action Transferability:** Latent actions demonstrate good transferability between videos. Cycle consistency experiments (inferring actions, applying them to a new video, re-inferring, and reapplying to the original video) show only a minor increase in prediction error. This suggests the learned actions are general and re-identifiable.
- **Spatially-Localized, Camera-Relative Embodiment:** A profound finding is that due to the lack of a common embodiment across "in-the-wild" videos, the learned latent actions tend to encode *spatially-localized, camera-relative transformations*. This means an action often describes "movement at this camera-relative location" rather than "movement of a specific object type."
- **Qualitative Evidence:** This camera-relative property enables remarkable cross-object transfer, such as transferring the motion of a human to a flying ball, or making a person appear and move into a scene, which would be impossible if actions were tied to specific object semantics.

#### 4.4. Practical Utility for Planning

- **Effective Controller Training:** A simple, lightweight controller can be successfully trained to map known, interpretable actions (from robotic manipulation or navigation tasks) to the learned latent actions. Crucially, using past visual representations helps contextualize the camera-relative latent actions.
- **Comparable Planning Performance:** Despite being trained *exclusively on "in-the-wild" videos*, the world model achieves planning performance on robotic manipulation (DROID) and navigation (RECON) tasks that is comparable to, and in some cases competitive with, baselines trained on domain-specific, action-labeled data. For instance, on DROID, it performs similarly to V-JEPA 2-AC, a model trained with known actions.
- **Optimal Regularization:** The study reveals that the quality of unrollings and planning performance is not monotonically correlated with latent action capacity. Instead, a *balanced* regularization strength, allowing for a middle ground in terms of information content, yields the best results. Overly constrained actions lack information, while under-constrained ones contain too much, hindering optimal control. Noisy latent actions generally performed best for planning.

#### 4.5. Scaling Trends

- Scaling up model size, total training time, and the quantity of training data generally improves the quality of IDM predictions on natural videos.
- For downstream planning tasks, training for longer durations shows the most significant performance improvements. Model size has a more nuanced impact, being more beneficial for noisy latent actions. The effect of increasing data quantity is less pronounced after a certain point, suggesting that once a sufficient diversity of actions is captured, further raw data might yield diminishing returns without more sophisticated learning mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant milestone in the development of general-purpose artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of world models and embodied AI. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Breaking the Action Label Bottleneck:** The most profound impact is the demonstration that effective latent action world models can be learned *without explicit action labels* and *from uncurated, "in-the-wild" video data*. This frees AI systems from the constraint of meticulously curated datasets, opening the door to leveraging the vast, ever-growing reservoir of internet videos as a source of learning about the world's dynamics. This scalability is crucial for achieving true generality in AI.

2. **Advancing Generalist Agents:** By learning a transferable, abstract action space from diverse real-world scenarios, this work lays a foundation for building generalist agents. These agents could acquire a broad understanding of how actions influence environments across various contexts and embodiments, rather than being specialized to a single robot or task. This aligns with the long-term vision of AGI, where systems learn continuously from diverse data streams.

3. **Universal Action Interface:** The ability to map known actions to a learned latent action space, and then use this space for planning, introduces the concept of a "universal action interface." This could simplify the control of diverse robotic platforms or embodied agents, allowing a single world model to serve multiple purposes by merely learning a lightweight interface for each specific embodiment. This could significantly accelerate robotics development and deployment.

4. **Leveraging Self-Supervised Learning for World Models:** The successful integration of a frozen, self-supervised visual encoder (V-JEPA 2-L) highlights the power of foundational models in AI. It demonstrates that robust visual representations, learned in a self-supervised manner, are highly effective as input for subsequent, more complex tasks like predictive world modeling, reducing the need for end-to-end training and focusing learning on dynamics.

5. **Robustness to Real-World Complexity:** The study's focus on addressing the challenges of environmental noise, diverse action types, and heterogeneous embodiments inherent in "in-the-wild" videos makes the learned models more robust and applicable to messy, unpredictable real-world scenarios. The finding that latent actions learn "spatially-localized, camera-relative transformations" is a particularly insightful adaptation to this complexity, enabling broader transferability.

6. **Guiding Future Research:**
 - **Information Regulation:** The detailed analysis of sparsity, noise, and quantization as regularization techniques provides crucial insights for future LAM designs, highlighting the strengths of continuous, controlled latent spaces.
 - **Adaptive Latent Actions:** The identified limitation of static regularization coefficients (where ideal capacity varies per video) suggests an important direction for future work: developing adaptive latent action mechanisms.
 - **Direct Latent Space Planning:** The challenges of sampling and planning directly within complex, high-dimensional continuous latent action spaces represent a clear frontier for further research, potentially using advanced generative models like diffusion models.
 - **Single-Stage Training:** The work also points towards the potential of integrating world model learning directly into the self-supervised encoder pre-training pipeline, enabling holistic representation and dynamics learning.

In conclusion, "Learning Latent Action World Models In The Wild" is a pivotal contribution that substantially pushes the boundaries of world modeling. By demonstrating the viability of learning rich, transferable action representations from the vast, unstructured data of the internet, it brings us closer to building intelligent agents that can intuitively understand, predict, and interact with the complexities of the real world.
