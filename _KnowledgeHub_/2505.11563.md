# Object-Centric Representations Improve Policy Generalization in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Object-Centric Representations (OCRs) enhance robot manipulation policy learning and generalization by decomposing visual scenes into discrete entities. These models, particularly when pre-trained on robotic datasets and incorporating temporal dynamics, consistently outperform traditional global and dense feature encoding methods across diverse simulated and real-world tasks, especially under appearance-based visual shifts.

## Problem

- Traditional global and dense visual representations for robot manipulation often entangle task-relevant and irrelevant scene information, leading to brittleness.
- Robotic policies trained with these representations struggle to generalize robustly when faced with real-world distribution shifts such as novel lighting, textures, or cluttered environments.
- Current systems lack the ability to naturally abstract scenes into distinct, manipulable objects, which is a key capability in human perception.

## Method

- The research integrates **Object-Centric** Representations (OCRs) as the visual encoder within a unified visuomotor policy learning framework for imitation learning.
- It benchmarks seven state-of-the-art visual encoders, including global, dense, and object-centric models, across two simulated environments (MetaWorld, LIBERO-90) and a novel real-world task suite.
- ==Key OCR== models (DINOSAUR, VIDEOSAUR) are further pre-trained on a large collection of real-world robotic datasets to adapt their slot attention modules to manipulation-specific dynamics and visual distributions.

## Results

- Object-centric models, specifically VIDEOSAUR* (with robotic data pre-training), achieved the highest average success rates across all evaluated environments, outperforming the best dense models by up to **+9%** in LIBERO-90 and **+26%** in real-world tasks.
- OCRs consistently demonstrated superior generalization capabilities, exhibiting the lowest drops in success rate under appearance-based distribution shifts (texture and lighting changes) compared to other representations.
- Robotic data pre-training improved OCR performance by 10-13 percentage points across environments, and temporal dynamics further boosted performance by up to +26 percentage points in real-world settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric representations introduce an inductive bias that allows policies to disentangle and filter task-irrelevant visual information, focusing on stable, object-level structures.
> - Pre-training the slot attention module of OCRs on robotic datasets significantly improves their performance and robustness in robot manipulation tasks.
> - The inclusion of temporal dynamics modeling within object-centric representations further enhances policy learning, which is critical for complex, real-world manipulation scenarios.
