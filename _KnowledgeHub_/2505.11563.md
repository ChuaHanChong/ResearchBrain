# Object-Centric Representations Improve Policy Generalization in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Object-Centric Representations (OCRs) enhance robot manipulation policy learning and generalization by decomposing visual scenes into discrete entities. These models, particularly when pre-trained on robotic datasets and incorporating temporal dynamics, consistently outperform traditional global and dense feature encoding methods across diverse simulated and real-world tasks, especially under appearance-based visual shifts.

## Problem

- Traditional global and dense visual representations for robot manipulation often entangle task-relevant and irrelevant scene information, leading to brittleness.
- Robotic policies trained with these representations struggle to generalize robustly when faced with real-world distribution shifts such as novel lighting, textures, or cluttered environments.
- Current systems lack the ability to naturally abstract scenes into distinct, manipulable objects, which is a key capability in human perception.

## Method

- The research integrates **Object-Centric** Representations (OCRs) as the visual encoder within a unified visuomotor policy learning framework for imitation learning.
- It benchmarks seven state-of-the-art visual encoders, including global, dense, and object-centric models, across two simulated environments (MetaWorld, LIBERO-90) and a novel real-world task suite.
- ==Key OCR== models (DINOSAUR, VIDEOSAUR) are further pre-trained on a large collection of real-world robotic datasets to adapt their slot attention modules to manipulation-specific dynamics and visual distributions.

## Results

- Object-centric models, specifically VIDEOSAUR* (with robotic data pre-training), achieved the highest average success rates across all evaluated environments, outperforming the best dense models by up to **+9%** in LIBERO-90 and **+26%** in real-world tasks.
- OCRs consistently demonstrated superior generalization capabilities, exhibiting the lowest drops in success rate under appearance-based distribution shifts (texture and lighting changes) compared to other representations.
- Robotic data pre-training improved OCR performance by 10-13 percentage points across environments, and temporal dynamics further boosted performance by up to +26 percentage points in real-world settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric representations introduce an inductive bias that allows policies to disentangle and filter task-irrelevant visual information, focusing on stable, object-level structures.
> - Pre-training the slot attention module of OCRs on robotic datasets significantly improves their performance and robustness in robot manipulation tasks.
> - The inclusion of temporal dynamics modeling within object-centric representations further enhances policy learning, which is critical for complex, real-world manipulation scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation is critically dependent on effective visuomotor policy learning, which maps raw visual inputs to control actions. A central challenge in this domain is enabling robots to learn policies that are both generalizable to novel situations and sample-efficient during training. State-of-the-art approaches often employ imitation learning, utilizing a modular architecture comprising a sensor encoding module, an observation trunk, and an action head. Within this framework, the quality of the visual representation is paramount, as it dictates the policy's ability to extract task-relevant features and remain robust to environmental variations.

Prior work in visual representation learning for robotics has seen significant advancements. These include large-scale pre-training on human egocentric video datasets with objectives like time-contrastive learning and language alignment (e.g., R3M, VC-1), distilling knowledge from diverse vision foundation models (e.g., Theia), and leveraging self-supervised schemes such as masked autoencoding (e.g., DINOv2). Despite these efforts, a common architectural lineage, typically based on ResNets or Vision Transformers, often processes visual scenes into global feature vectors or dense patch-based representations.

The authors highlight a critical limitation of these traditional global or dense representations: they often "entangle task-relevant and irrelevant scene information," leading to brittleness when faced with real-world distribution shifts such as changes in lighting, textures, or cluttered environments. This entanglement makes it difficult for policies to generalize robustly, directly contrasting with human perception, which naturally abstracts scenes into meaningful, manipulable entities. Humans perceive the world not as a flat array of pixels but as a collection of distinct objects, enabling compositional understanding and rapid generalization—a capability largely elusive in current artificial systems due to the "binding problem" in neural networks.

This paper positions itself directly within the growing recognition of the need for structured, abstract, and task-relevant visual scene representations in robotics. Object-Centric Representations (OCRs) have emerged in computer vision as a promising avenue, capable of decomposing images into discrete entities or "slots." While OCRs have been explored for image decomposition, scene reconstruction, and some simplified reinforcement learning tasks, their comprehensive evaluation in complex, multi-object robotic manipulation scenarios, especially concerning generalization under diverse visual conditions, remains largely unexplored. This research aims to address this gap by systematically benchmarking OCRs against established global and dense representations in more realistic and challenging robotic manipulation tasks, both in simulation and the real world. By doing so, it seeks to validate OCRs as a viable pathway to bridge the gap between low-level visual input and high-level symbolic reasoning for robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the acknowledged limitations of conventional global and dense visual representations in visuomotor policy learning for robotics. These representations, while powerful, struggle with robustness and generalization when encountering variations in real-world environments due to their tendency to conflate task-relevant and irrelevant visual information. Drawing inspiration from human perception, which naturally parses scenes into distinct, interactive objects, the authors propose Object-Centric Representations (OCRs) as a structured alternative. OCRs inherently introduce an inductive bias towards symbolic, compositional reasoning by segmenting visual input into a finite set of interpretable entities or "slots."

Against this background, the paper sets out to answer two critical research questions:

**Q1: Can OCR models better enable robotic manipulation policy learning over other visual representations?**
This question aims to ascertain the fundamental performance benefits of integrating OCRs into the robot learning pipeline, comparing their efficacy against established global and dense feature encoding methods across a range of manipulation tasks.

**Q2: Can OCRs enhance policy generalization capabilities in the presence of distractors, new textures, and lighting conditions?**
This objective directly addresses the robustness challenge, investigating whether the structured nature of OCRs allows policies to maintain performance under diverse out-of-distribution visual shifts, a known weakness of traditional representations.

To achieve these objectives, the authors make the following key contributions:
- They develop and open-source a unified framework designed for evaluating various types of visual representations in robotic manipulation tasks.
- They conduct extensive benchmarking of seven distinct visual encoders—encompassing global, dense, and object-centric representations—across two simulated environments (MetaWorld, LIBERO-90) and a novel, replicable real-world task suite using a low-cost robotic arm.
- Crucially, they demonstrate that slot-based object representations significantly improve robot manipulation policy learning and enhance generalization, particularly under realistic distractors and domain shifts, *without* requiring task-specific fine-tuning or extensive pretraining beyond the representation learning itself. This finding underscores the intrinsic benefits of the object-centric inductive bias.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a systematic, comparative approach within a unified visuomotor policy learning framework. The core idea is to substitute the visual encoder module in a standard imitation learning pipeline with different types of pre-trained visual models, including global, dense, and object-centric representations, and then evaluate their impact on policy learning and generalization.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The framework consists of three main components:
1. **Sensor Encoding Module (Vision Encoder):** This module takes raw visual inputs (images/videos) and produces a latent representation. This is the primary component under investigation, where different visual models are swapped in.
2. **Observation Trunk:** A transformer-based model that integrates different modalities, such as visual features, language embeddings for task instructions, and proprioceptive states, processing a sequence of past observations.
3. **Action Head:** A neural network (e.g., MLP) that predicts the final robot action based on the processed observation trunk output.

Crucially, all pre-trained visual models are kept **frozen** during policy training to ensure a fair comparison of the *representation quality* itself, rather than the fine-tuning capabilities of the models.

#### 3.2. Visual Encoders

The paper focuses on Object-Centric Representations (OCRs) but benchmarks them against established global and dense methods.
- **Object-Centric Representations (OCRs):** The core of OCRs is to decompose an input image `O` into a set of `K` object representations or "slots" `S = {s_1..., s_K}`. This is achieved by first encoding the image into dense features `F` using a vision backbone, then extracting slots using **Slot Attention**. Slot Attention is an iterative attention mechanism that allows distinct slots to specialize in representing different parts of the input scene.
 - **DINOSAUR:** This OCR model uses a frozen DINO as its visual backbone and reconstructs the extracted features from the slots as a learning signal.
 - **VIDEOSAUR:** An extension of DINOSAUR for video data, VIDEOSAUR incorporates two main enhancements: a transformer predictor model to initialize slots at timestep `t` from predictions at `t-1` (transmitting temporal dynamics), and a temporal consistency loss using DINOv2 features across consecutive timesteps.
- **Global and Dense Representations:** For comparison, the study includes several state-of-the-art models:
 - **ResNet-50:** A classical convolutional neural network pre-trained on ImageNet, providing dense features.
 - **R3M:** A ResNet-50 variant pre-trained on large-scale human egocentric video (Ego4D) using time-contrastive learning, yielding dense features.
 - **DINOv2:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) model pre-trained self-supervisedly on a massive curated dataset (LVD-142M), offering both global and dense features.
 - **VC-1:** A ViT-based model using masked autoencoding, pre-trained on Ego4D and ImageNet, providing global features.
 - **Theia:** A ViT model distilled from multiple vision foundation models (CLIP, SAM, DINOv2), optimized for robot learning, producing dense features.

#### 3.3. Policy Training

- **Imitation Learning:** Policies are trained by mimicking expert demonstrations.
- **Simulated Environments (MetaWorld, LIBERO-90):** The **BAKU architecture** is employed. This transformer-based model processes a sequence of past observations (visual features, language, proprioception) interleaved with learnable action tokens, with an MLP action head predicting continuous actions. The authors modified BAKU to accept global, dense, or slot-based visual inputs.
- **Real-World Environment (LeRobot):** The **Action Chunking Transformers (ACT)** implementation from the LeRobot library is adapted. Modifications were made to ACT to accept global, dense, or slot-based visual features, changing the 2D positional embedding to 1D as needed. A task-specific language embedding (from ModernBERT) is concatenated for multi-task learning.

#### 3.4. Benchmarks and Experimental Setup

- **Environments:**
 - **MetaWorld:** A simulated benchmark with 10 single-object tabletop manipulation tasks using a Sawyer arm. Used for controlled, structured generalization tests.
 - **LIBERO-90:** A simulated benchmark with 90 multi-object, visually complex tasks across diverse domains (kitchens, offices, living rooms). Challenges multi-object reasoning and generalization.
 - **Real-World LeRobot Setup:** A novel suite of five tabletop manipulation tasks (e.g., "Open Drawer," "Fold bag") using a low-cost SO-100 robotic arm and LeRobot library. Tests practical viability under realistic noise and embodiment constraints.
- **Robotic Pre-training for OCRs:** A crucial step involves further pre-training the *slot attention module* of DINOSAUR and VIDEOSAUR models on a large collection of real-world robotic datasets. This includes BridgeData V2, Fractal, and DROID, totaling over 188,000 trajectories. This self-supervised pre-training uses a reconstruction loss applied to DINO feature maps from temporal video slices, aiming to align the object-centric representations with the specific dynamics and visual distributions of robotic manipulation environments. Ablation studies compare models trained on single-source datasets versus a balanced mixture (Robot Mixt.).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research rigorously evaluated seven visual encoders across diverse simulated and real-world robot manipulation tasks, focusing on two key questions: the improvement of policy learning and enhancement of generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Q1: Do OCRs Improve Manipulation Policy Learning?

The results consistently demonstrate that **Object-Centric Representations (OCRs) significantly improve manipulation policy learning** compared to global and dense representations.

- **Overall Performance:** Object-centric models, particularly **VIDEOSAUR\*** (the version pre-trained on robotic data), achieved the highest average success rates across all environments.
 - **MetaWorld:** While most models performed well (above 60%), OCRs performed comparably to the top baselines. VC-1 showed lower performance, attributed to its MAE-based pretraining being sensitive to domain mismatch.
 - **LIBERO-90:** In this benchmark featuring complex, multi-object scenes, OCRs clearly outperformed all other representations. VIDEOSAUR\* improved over the best dense model (Theia) by **+9%**, highlighting its advantage in scenarios requiring multi-object interaction.
 - **Real-World LeRobot:** OCRs again surpassed other models, with VIDEOSAUR\* reaching a **70% success rate** compared to 50% for the best dense baseline. Surprisingly, ResNet-50 also performed competitively, possibly due to its diverse ImageNet pretraining, aligning with findings in regarding dataset diversity.

- **Impact of Robotic Data Pre-training:** Integrating robotic data into the OCR models' pre-training proved immensely beneficial. Comparing DINOSAUR\* vs. DINOSAUR, and VIDEOSAUR\* vs. VIDEOSAUR, the models with robotic data pre-training showed substantial performance gains:
 - VIDEOSAUR\* increased VIDEOSAUR's mean performance by **13, 11, and 10 percentage points** in MetaWorld, LIBERO, and LeRobot environments, respectively.
- **Impact of Temporal Dynamics:** The inclusion of temporal dynamics modeling, as in VIDEOSAUR\* over DINOSAUR\* (both pre-trained on robotic data mixture), further improved performance:
 - VIDEOSAUR\* outperformed DINOSAUR\* by **+9 and +26 percentage points** in LIBERO and LeRobot, respectively. This suggests that understanding object dynamics over time is critical for complex manipulation, especially in real-world scenarios with less structured motion patterns than internet-scale video datasets.

In summary, for Q1, the findings strongly confirm that object-centric models are not only effective but also scalable across various domains, offering significant performance advantages in both structured simulations and noisy real-world settings.

#### 4.2. Q2: Do OCRs Enhance Generalization Under Visual Distribution Shifts?

The study extensively evaluated generalization under out-of-distribution conditions, including novel distractors, unseen textures, and lighting changes. The results indicate that **OCRs significantly enhance policy generalization capabilities, particularly for appearance-based shifts.**

- **MetaWorld Generalization (Table 2):**
 - Dense models like DINOv2 and Theia generally outperformed ResNet.
 - **OCRs, especially VIDEOSAUR\***, consistently outperformed all baselines under **texture and lighting shifts**.
 - Theia performed best in the **distractor scenario**, likely leveraging its CLIP-based text-image alignment to ignore irrelevant patches. However, Theia suffered sharp performance drops in texture and lighting shifts, whereas OCRs demonstrated much greater robustness.
- **Real-World Generalization (Table 3):**
 - **VIDEOSAUR\*** exhibited strong robustness, showing the **lowest drops in success rate under texture and lighting changes**.
 - Similar to MetaWorld, Theia excelled with distractors but failed under other domain shifts.
 - ResNet-50, while surprisingly resilient to texture and lighting variations, lacked consistent performance across all shifts.

The overall generalization results for Q2 highlight that object-centric representations generalize better across diverse distribution shifts, particularly those that perturb low-level visual appearance (textures, lighting). This robustness is attributed to OCRs' inherent ability to disentangle and filter task-irrelevant background information, focusing instead on stable, object-level structure, which remains consistent despite superficial appearance changes. The failure analysis (Figure 8) for distractors showed that some slots could mistakenly capture distractors, suggesting an avenue for future improvement through semantic grounding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides compelling evidence for the efficacy of Object-Centric Representations (OCRs) in advancing visuomotor policy learning for robotic manipulation. Its significance lies in challenging the prevailing reliance on global or dense visual features and offering a structured, more human-like alternative that yields superior generalization and robustness.

The potential impact of these findings is substantial:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By demonstrating that OCR-based policies outperform traditional methods under diverse distribution shifts (lighting, textures, distractors), the work provides a crucial step towards more reliable and adaptable robotic systems. Robots equipped with such perceptual capabilities would be less brittle to real-world variability, a long-standing bottleneck in deploying autonomous agents outside controlled lab environments.
2. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The strong performance of OCRs in real-world tasks, coupled with their robustness to domain shifts, suggests a promising pathway for better sim-to-real transfer. If representations can inherently filter irrelevant visual noise and focus on object-level structure, policies learned in simulation might transfer more effectively to the complexities and unpredictability of the real world.
3. **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** The success of OCRs reinforces the importance of structured perception, mirroring how humans abstract and reason about discrete entities. This could foster further research into cognitive-inspired architectures that move beyond pixel-level processing towards more symbolic and compositional understanding, enabling higher-level reasoning and planning for robots.
4. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays a strong foundation for future exploration. It explicitly points to limitations such as the lack of inherent semantic grounding in current OCRs and the need for better alignment with robot dynamics. Addressing these could lead to even more powerful and interpretable robotic systems capable of understanding not just "what is an object" but "what can this object do" (affordances) and "how can I interact with it."
5. **Open-Source Contribution:** By open-sourcing their unified framework, the authors facilitate reproducible research and encourage wider adoption and benchmarking of OCRs within the robotics community, accelerating progress in this critical area.

In conclusion, this work highlights that rethinking visual representations from flat, pixel-level features to more structured, object-based encodings is not merely an incremental improvement but a fundamental shift that could unlock significant advancements in the capabilities of robotic agents. OCRs present a highly promising direction for achieving scalable, generalizable, and robust robotic control in complex, dynamic real-world environments.
