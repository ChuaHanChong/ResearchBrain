# Rotation-Aware Point-Cloud Embeddings for Vision-Based In-Hand Reorientation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for vision-based in-hand reorientation leverages a learned rotation-aware point-cloud embedding, where the Euclidean distance in the latent space correlates with the SO(3) geodesic error between object orientations, enabling direct policy learning without explicit pose estimation. This approach achieved a 100% success rate across 100 evaluation trials for tested YCB objects, outperforming methods reliant on brittle external modules.

## Problem

- Dexterous in-hand reorientation is challenging for robots, particularly when specifying goals using raw point clouds due to their unordered and visibility-dependent nature.
- Comparing raw point clouds for control entangles actual object rotation with issues like permutation, resampling, and unstable correspondence structures.
- Current approaches rely on external structures (e.g., explicit 6D pose, dense flow, privileged teachers) that are often brittle, computationally intensive, or dependent on ground-truth information.

## Method

- Develops a two-stage method: first, an offline trained, pose-aware point-cloud encoder, then a model-free ==reinforcement learning== policy.
- The PointNet++ encoder is trained with a Siamese-style metric learning objective to calibrate its latent Euclidean distance to the SO(3) geodesic rotation error between object orientations.
- A PPO-based policy utilizes the frozen current and goal point-cloud embeddings, proprioception, and object centroid metadata as observations for in-hand reorientation, with an occlusion curriculum and fine-tuning applied to the encoder for robustness.

## Results

- The policy achieved a **100%** success rate across 100 evaluation trials for four distinct YCB objects (peach, Rubik's cube, pear, tuna can).
- This method demonstrated comparable success rates to privileged teacher and distilled student baselines, while significantly outperforming baselines relying on external, non-privileged modules (e.g., 6D pose estimators, point-cloud registration).
- Latent space diagnostics confirmed that the metric-trained encoder produces a smooth "delta-norm landscape" where embedding distance systematically correlates with SO(3) geodesic error, a property absent in generic point-cloud encoders.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generic point-cloud encoders, despite preserving object shape, do not inherently organize task-relevant rotational state suitable for closed-loop control comparison.
> - Calibrating the Euclidean distance in a learned latent space to the SO(3) geodesic rotation error provides a smooth, task-relevant control signal for reorientation policies.
> - An intrinsically rotation-aware representation can obviate the need for brittle external scaffolding like 6D pose estimation or dense point-cloud registration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous in-hand manipulation, particularly reorientation, presents a significant challenge in robotics. Specifying goals for such tasks often involves defining desired object states. Point clouds offer a direct method to specify these goals by depicting the desired 3D geometry of an object. This approach bypasses the need for object-specific pose frames or explicit 6D pose estimation, which can be complex and computationally intensive at test time.

However, utilizing raw point-cloud goals for policy learning is challenging. Point clouds are unordered, independently sampled, and visibility-dependent, meaning their comparison can entangle actual object rotation with issues such as permutation, resampling, and unstable correspondence structures. This complexity hinders the extraction of a smooth, control-friendly signal for robotic policies.

Previous research efforts to address this issue have typically introduced additional structure external to the raw point-cloud representation:
- **Explicit Pose Information:** Methods like Geometry-Dex use point-cloud observations for object geometry but provide explicit pose information to the policy for reorientation. However, robust 6D object pose estimation from visual observations remains a difficult problem, as indicated by benchmarks like BOP.
- **Dense Flow Features:** Approaches such as HACMan provide structured current-goal features by specifying how points should move from the current to the target geometry. This relies on robust point-cloud registration, which can be inaccurate under partial observations and occlusion, leading to failure modes.
- **Distillation from Privileged Teachers:** Methods involve training a privileged teacher with access to ground-truth information, then distilling its actions into a student policy that uses raw point clouds. This circumvents brittle pose estimation but depends on the availability of high-quality expert supervision and can result in learned representations coupled to specific embodiments or data collection policies.

The current work identifies a gap in this landscape: existing methods add scaffolding *outside* the point-cloud representation itself. The paper posits that generic point-cloud encoders (e.g., Point-MAE), while preserving object shape or identity, may fail to organize task-relevant state in a manner conducive to current-goal comparison for control. This research aims to close this gap by demonstrating that the representation itself can be learned to make point-cloud goal conditioning usable for direct, model-free policy learning, thereby removing the reliance on external, potentially brittle, or resource-intensive scaffolding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust vision-based in-hand reorientation by learning a rotation-aware point-cloud embedding. This embedding aims to facilitate direct policy learning from observed and goal point clouds, without requiring explicit object pose, relative pose, dense flow, or teacher-action supervision.

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges inherent in existing approaches to robotic manipulation with point-cloud goals:

1. **Complexity of Raw Point-Cloud Comparison:** Raw point clouds present a poorly conditioned input for policy learning. The unordered nature, independent sampling, and visibility dependence mean that discrepancies between current and goal point clouds are not directly interpretable as smooth control signals. Changes in observation entangle object rotation with irrelevant factors like permutation, resampling, and discontinuous nearest-neighbor assignments.
2. **Limitations of External Scaffolding:**
 - **Brittle Pose Estimation:** Relying on 6D object pose estimation, while offering a structured input, is a challenging and often unreliable process, especially from partial visual observations common in dexterous manipulation. Inaccuracies in pose estimation can lead to policy failures.
 - **Difficult Point-Cloud Registration:** Dense flow-based methods require robust point-cloud registration, which itself is a complex problem, particularly under partial observations and occlusions. Failure in registration directly impacts the quality of the control signal.
 - **Dependency on Privileged Data/Teachers:** Distillation methods necessitate a privileged teacher, implying the availability of expert demonstrations or ground-truth state information during the teacher's training. This can make the learned representation dependent on the specific embodiment, exploration strategy, or data collection process, limiting its generalizability and requiring substantial data annotation.
3. **Inadequacy of Generic Point-Cloud Features:** The authors hypothesize that a generic point-cloud encoder, trained for tasks like reconstruction or shape recognition, might preserve sufficient information to identify an object but would not necessarily organize the *task-relevant rotational state* in a way that makes current-goal comparison useful for closed-loop control. The core idea is that for two point clouds of the same object under different rotations, the difference in their latent representation should specifically reflect the difference in these rotations.

Therefore, the central motivation is to develop an internal representation that intrinsically organizes the rotational geometry within the latent space. By calibrating the Euclidean distance in this latent space to the SO(3) geodesic error between object orientations, the goal is to transform the challenging current-goal comparison into a smooth, control-friendly signal. This would enable a model-free reinforcement learning policy to effectively learn to reorient objects solely from these embeddings, proprioceptive feedback, and centroid metadata, without the aforementioned external aids.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves a two-stage process: first, offline training of a pose-aware point-cloud encoder using metric learning, and second, employing this frozen encoder to provide observations for a model-free reinforcement learning policy tasked with in-hand reorientation.

#### 3.1. Pose-Aware Encoder

- **Architecture:** A PointNet++ based encoder, denoted as $\phi$, is utilized. This encoder maps each colored object point cloud $P \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 6}$ (comprising XYZ coordinates and RGB color channels) into a compact 128-dimensional embedding, $z = \phi(P)$. The main configuration uses four set-abstraction stages with radius grouping and specific radii and MLP channel sizes (e.g., (0.15, 0.35, 0.50, 1.0) radii and increasing MLP sizes), followed by mean pooling and a two-layer head to produce the 128-dimensional embedding.
- **Training Objective:** The encoder is trained in a Siamese-style configuration. For any pair of point clouds ($P_1, P_2$) representing the same object at different orientations ($q_1, q_2$), the objective is to make the squared Euclidean distance between their embeddings ($\|\phi(P_1) - \phi(P_2)\|_2^2$) proportional to the SO(3) geodesic rotation error ($2 \arccos |\langle q_1, q_2 \rangle|$). This metric learning objective, formalized as $L_{metric} = \mathbb{E}[(\|\phi(P_1) - \phi(P_2)\|_2^2 - 2 \arccos |\langle q_1, q_2 \rangle|)^2]$, ensures that nearby orientations correspond to nearby embeddings, and distant orientations to distant embeddings.
- **Training Data:** The encoder is trained on colored point clouds sampled from textured mesh models of the YCB dataset. A single encoder is trained to be applicable across multiple objects.
- **Robustness Curriculum:** To enhance robustness to partial observations common in in-hand manipulation, an occlusion curriculum is employed during training. This progressively removes points from the input clouds across four stages, with increasing occlusion probabilities (0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.8), exposing the model to increasingly incomplete geometry. Occlusion is applied to a randomly selected side of the point cloud pair.
- **Fine-tuning:** Following the mesh-based training, the encoder is fine-tuned on a smaller dataset generated by rendering objects with RGB-D cameras within IsaacLab. This step produces point clouds with hand-like occlusions, better matching the observations encountered during downstream policy learning.
- **Deployment:** For policy learning, the trained encoder $\phi$ is frozen. It processes the current object point cloud and the goal point cloud independently to generate their respective embeddings.

#### 3.2. In-Hand Reorientation Policy Learning

- **Task Formulation:** The in-hand reorientation task is framed as a goal-augmented Markov Decision Process (MDP). The robot manipulates a single rigid object from the YCB dataset in a simulated Shadow Hand within the IsaacLab environment. The goal $g$ is a target orientation $q_g \in SO(3)$.
- **Reward Function:** The reward function encourages minimizing the object's position drift from a reference point ($w_p \|p_t - p_{ref}\|_2^2$), reducing the SO(3) geodesic rotation error to the goal ($w_R / (d_R(q_t, q_g) + \epsilon)$), regularizing action magnitude ($w_a \|a_t\|_2^2$), and providing a bonus for successful reorientation ($b$ if $d_R(q_t, q_g) < \beta$). Success is defined as achieving a rotational error below a threshold $\beta$.
- **Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used, employing an asymmetric actor-critic architecture to accelerate convergence.
- **Observation Space (Actor):** The actor receives a composite observation vector $o_t$ at each timestep, comprising:
 - **Learned Embeddings:** The 128-dimensional current object embedding ($z_{obj} = \phi(P_t)$) and the 128-dimensional goal embedding ($z_{goal} = \phi(P_{goal})$), totaling 256 dimensions.
 - **Proprioception:** 133 dimensions including joint positions, joint velocities, hand-relative fingertip positions and orientations, fingertip linear and angular velocities, and previous actions.
 - **Geometric Metadata:** The 3D centroids of both the current and goal point clouds in the environment frame (6 dimensions total), providing explicit relative translation information to complement the rotation-focused embeddings.
- **Asymmetric Critic:** The critic network, which estimates the value function, has access to privileged state information $s_t$. This includes ground-truth object state (pose, velocity), full hand state, and physical contact forces (190 dimensions in total). This access aids faster and more stable policy learning without being available to the actor during inference.
- **Point Cloud Generation for Policies:** Observed point clouds ($P_t$) are generated by fusing data from three 128x128 RGB-D cameras observing the hand. Depth images are converted to 3D points, masked for object points using IsaacLab utilities. Goal point clouds ($P_{goal}$) are obtained by sampling the canonical object mesh and rotating it to the target orientation. To approximate real sensor noise, point jittering and random shuffling are applied to observed point clouds.
- **Baselines:** The policy is compared against several baselines:
 - **Privileged Teachers:** Using ground-truth simulator state or estimator-derived object pose.
 - **Distilled Student:** A PointNet++ student trained via distillation from a teacher, taking raw current + goal point clouds (XYZ, RGB) as input.
 - **Flow Baselines:** Using dense displacement fields computed either from simulator state or via Open3D registration.
 - **Point-MAE Reference:** A policy using a generic, self-supervised Point-MAE encoder fine-tuned on YCB objects, to assess the effectiveness of task-agnostic features.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings regarding both the policy's performance in the reorientation task and diagnostics of the learned point-cloud representation.

#### 4.1. Policy Learning Performance

- **Successful Reorientation:** The policy trained with the proposed rotation-aware point-cloud embedding consistently achieved a 100% success rate across all 100 evaluation trials for the four tested YCB objects (peach, Rubik's cube, pear, tuna can). This indicates the learned representation provides a reliable signal for successful completion of the reorientation task.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - The "Ours" method demonstrated comparable success rates to the teacher policies (using privileged simulator state or FoundationPose estimator), the distilled student policy, and the flow baseline derived from simulator state. This suggests that the learned representation can rival methods that either have access to ground-truth information or are trained through complex distillation processes.
 - However, the "Ours" policy generally required more average timesteps to achieve reorientation compared to the privileged teacher and distilled student baselines. Learning curves in Appendix A4 indicate that with extended training, the policy's speed improves, suggesting the initial slower performance is partly due to the training duration cap.
 - Crucially, baselines relying on external, non-privileged modules (e.g., "Teacher (estimator)" and "Flow (registration)") exhibited significantly lower success rates and much higher average timesteps. This outcome validates the motivation of the work, highlighting the difficulty and unreliability of obtaining structured inputs like 6D pose or accurate dense point-cloud registration in deployment settings.
- **Inadequacy of Generic Encoders:** The policy using an off-the-shelf, self-supervised Point-MAE encoder completely failed to learn the reorientation task. This finding supports the hypothesis that generic point-cloud features, while preserving shape, do not inherently provide the structured, task-relevant rotational signal needed for dexterous manipulation control.

#### 4.2. Representation Diagnostics

To understand why the proposed encoder enables successful policy learning while Point-MAE fails, diagnostics were performed on the latent space properties.

- **Latent Embedding Distance Landscape (Figure 5, Appendix A5.4):**
 - The metric-trained encoder (`Ours`) produced a "delta-norm landscape" where the Euclidean distance between embeddings smoothly and systematically increased with the SO(3) geodesic rotation error. This structured landscape provides a clear and continuous control signal for the policy.
 - The landscape generated by the `Distilled Student` encoder showed qualitative similarities to the `Ours` encoder, suggesting that both learn a rotation-aware structure, albeit through different training mechanisms (direct metric learning vs. action supervision).
 - In stark contrast, the `Point-MAE` encoder generated an unstructured landscape where different relative orientations could result in similar latent distances. This lack of structured information explains its failure to support policy learning for reorientation.
- **Rotation Axis Sensitivity (Figure 3, Appendix A5.2):**
 - `Ours` encoder and the `Distilled Student` encoder demonstrated the ability to organize rotations by axis. For rotations of the same angle but around different axes, the cosine similarity between embedding changes was low, indicating distinct directions in latent space. For rotations around the same axis but varying angles, the cosine similarity was high (close to 1), especially for larger angles, indicating consistent directions. This suggests these encoders capture both the magnitude and axis of rotation.
 - The `Point-MAE` encoder also showed low cross-axis similarity, but it failed to consistently group same-axis rotations, particularly at larger angles. This further confirmed that while it detects changes, it does not encode structured rotational information beneficial for control.
- **t-SNE Visualizations (Figure 4, Appendix A5.1):**
 - Qualitative t-SNE visualizations of the learned embeddings revealed a clear structure where rotations were separated meaningfully by both angle and axis. This separation was less pronounced for near-zero rotations, which are perceptually ambiguous. This visual evidence supports the effectiveness of the metric learning objective in organizing the latent space.
- **Symmetry Stress Cases (Appendix A5.3):**
 - The diagnostics also explored objects with rotational symmetries (e.g., medium clamp, scissors, large marker). In these cases, rotations around different axes that resulted in visually similar configurations of the object also induced highly similar embedding changes (high cosine similarity). This indicates that the encoder accurately reflects the *perceptual* similarity of rotations given the object's geometry, rather than purely abstract SO(3) distances when visual information is ambiguous.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of vision-based robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving dexterous in-hand reorientation.

- **Enabling Direct Point-Cloud Goal Conditioning:** The primary contribution is demonstrating that point-cloud goals can be directly utilized for policy learning without relying on auxiliary, often brittle, intermediate representations like explicit 6D object pose, relative pose, or dense point correspondence. This addresses a fundamental challenge in leveraging the richness of 3D geometry for robot control.
- **Reduced Dependency on Privileged Information:** By learning an intrinsic, rotation-aware representation, the method mitigates the need for privileged information, such as ground-truth simulator states or expert teacher actions, which are often costly or impractical to obtain in real-world settings. This can lead to more autonomous and generalizable learning pipelines for robotic policies.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Real-World Challenges:** The approach circumvents known failure modes associated with inaccurate 6D pose estimation or unreliable point-cloud registration, especially under occlusions and partial observations inherent in in-hand manipulation. The inclusion of an occlusion curriculum and fine-tuning with hand-occluded data explicitly enhances the representation's robustness to realistic visual inputs.
- **Implications for Representation Learning in Robotics:** The findings underscore that the design of the *representation itself* is critical for task-specific robot learning. Generic point-cloud features, while useful for recognition, may not provide the necessary structured signal for control. This work demonstrates how a carefully calibrated metric learning objective can endow a representation with task-relevant geometric properties (i.e., rotation awareness) without explicit task supervision in the form of actions or dense correspondences.
- **Potential for Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Although evaluated in a simulated environment, the methodology incorporates elements that promote sim-to-real transfer, such as using point clouds derived from RGB-D observations with sensor noise approximations (jittering, shuffling) and domain randomization for physical properties. The reliance on 3D spatial structure over photometric cues further enhances robustness to real-world visual variations. This work provides an encouraging foundation for future real-robot validation.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies clear avenues for future work, including extending the embedding to jointly preserve both object shape and general control-relevant state information, and adapting the metric objective to capture transformations over the full SE(3) group to enable more general manipulation tasks beyond pure SO(3) reorientation.
