# VerIPO: Cultivating Long Reasoning in Video-LLMs via Verifier-Gudied Iterative Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> VerIPO cultivates deep, consistent, long-term reasoning in Video-LLMs by introducing a Verifier-guided Iterative Policy Optimization (VerIPO) algorithm. This approach uses a GRPO-Verifier-DPO training loop, leveraging a small LLM to dynamically curate high-quality preference data, resulting in improved accuracy and reasoning length on video reasoning benchmarks while reducing inconsistencies and repetitions.

## Problem

- High-quality, long Chain-of-Thought (Long-CoT) video datasets are scarce and costly, posing a data preparation bottleneck for Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT).
- Existing online RL methods for Video-LLMs often produce shallow, inconsistent, or repetitive reasoning chains and show unstable performance, failing to cultivate deep, long-form reasoning.
- Video-LLMs struggle to generate long, coherent, and contextually consistent step-by-step reasoning for complex video understanding tasks.

## Method

- Introduces ==VerIPO==, a Verifier-guided Iterative Policy Optimization algorithm, structured around a ==GRPO-Verifier-DPO training loop==.
- Utilizes a =="Rollout-Aware Verifier"== (a small LLM) to critically assess the quality, consistency, and repetition of online generated reasoning chains.
- The verifier dynamically constructs high-quality contrastive preference datasets, which are then used by ==DPO== for efficient, targeted policy refinement.

## Results

- VerIPO consistently outperforms strong baselines, including instruction-tuned LMMs and other RL-trained thinking models, on various video reasoning benchmarks, such as a **5.6%** improvement over Video-R1 on Video-MMMU.
- The Verifier-guided DPO and iterative training significantly enhance "Acc-True" (correct answers with consistent reasoning) and increase average response length, while effectively reducing contextual inconsistencies and repetitive outputs.
- An initial "Reasoning Activation" phase using text and image data proves more beneficial for generalizing to video tasks than cold-starting with video-specific Long-CoT data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An iterative, verifier-guided reinforcement learning framework can effectively cultivate deep, long-term, and consistent reasoning in Video-LLMs without requiring large, pre-existing Long-CoT datasets.
> - Dynamically assessing and curating preference data from online model rollouts using a small LLM verifier is crucial for improving reasoning quality and addressing issues like inconsistency and repetition.
> - Combining the broad exploration capabilities of GRPO with the efficient, targeted refinement of DPO, guided by the verifier, leads to stable and superior performance gains in long reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates at the critical intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), specifically Video-LLMs, and Reinforcement Learning (RL). The broader research landscape has seen significant advancements in LLMs' ability to perform complex reasoning, often facilitated by generating step-by-step Chain-of-Thoughts (CoTs). This success has extended to multimodal settings, enabling LMMs to tackle challenging visual-text tasks like visual math and complex image reasoning.

A pivotal technique for activating and enhancing these long-form reasoning capabilities in LMMs is Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT). Methods such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) have been instrumental in aligning LLMs and LMMs with desired objectives, often by learning from human feedback or automated rewards. Recent models like DeepSeek-R1, Skywork R1V, and Vision-R1 have demonstrated the potential of RFT in cultivating reasoning for text and image domains.

However, the application of RFT to **Video-LLMs** for long reasoning faces unique and significant challenges that VerIPO seeks to address:
- **Data Preparation Bottleneck:** High-quality, long Chain-of-Thought (Long-CoT) video datasets are scarce and costly to annotate manually. Automatic methods often introduce noise, making "cold starting" RFT with such data problematic (e.g., Video-R1).
- **Limitations and Instability of Online RL:**
 - **Shallow Reasoning:** Direct RL training on Video-LLMs often produces short, shallow reasoning chains lacking deep logical steps, failing to activate truly long-form reasoning.
 - **Contextual Inconsistency:** Existing RL methods like GRPO can lead to "correct answers based on wrong thinking," where the reasoning chain is inconsistent with the final answer.
 - **Unstable Performance:** RL training does not consistently improve accuracy or CoT length, especially with increased temporal frame inputs, and can show unstable performance gains across diverse video reasoning tasks. Sometimes, RL-trained models even perform worse than direct inference by instruction-tuned models.
 - **Repetitive Outputs:** Models can get stuck in repetitive inference loops.

VerIPO aims to push the boundaries by addressing these practical limitations. Instead of relying on large, pre-existing Long-CoT SFT datasets for initialization, it proposes an iterative, verifier-guided RL approach that *gradually cultivates* long reasoning capabilities. It distinguishes itself by:
1. **Iterative Refinement:** Introducing a novel GRPO-Verifier-DPO training loop that iteratively improves reasoning quality.
2. **Rollout-Aware Verifier:** Leveraging a "small LLM" as a judge to assess reasoning logic and consistency of *online rollouts*, rather than relying solely on static, pre-collected preference data. This makes the data generation for preference optimization dynamic and high-quality.
3. **Efficient Preference Optimization:** Using DPO, which is empirically 7x faster than GRPO, for targeted refinement based on the verifier-curated data, combining the benefits of GRPO's expansive search with DPO's efficient optimization.

By tackling these core issues, VerIPO contributes significantly to making RFT a more stable, efficient, and effective method for developing robust, deep reasoning capabilities in Video-LLMs, moving beyond mere short-video understanding to truly complex, long-form video reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of VerIPO is to **cultivate deep, long-term reasoning chains in Video Large Language Models (Video-LLMs)**. This overarching goal is driven by the recognition that complex video reasoning problems across various domains necessitate elaborate, step-by-step thinking processes, much like Chain-of-Thoughts (CoTs) in textual LLMs.

The motivation for proposing VerIPO stems directly from the significant limitations observed in current Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) methods when applied to Video-LLMs for this purpose:

1. **Overcoming Data Preparation Bottlenecks:** Existing RFT approaches often require substantial amounts of high-quality Long-CoT video data for "cold starting." However, such datasets are incredibly expensive and time-consuming to create through manual annotation, and automatically generated data is prone to noise. VerIPO aims to mitigate this by developing a method that *does not require* a large, pre-existing Long-CoT SFT dataset for initial training, instead growing reasoning capabilities incrementally.

2. **Addressing Limitations and Instability of Online RL:**
 - **Shallow Reasoning:** Current RFT methods (e.g., GRPO), while improving overall performance over Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), frequently yield reasoning chains that are "short, shallow, and lack deep logical steps." VerIPO seeks to enable the generation of truly *deep* and *long* reasoning paths.
 - **Contextual Inconsistency:** A critical issue is the tendency of RL-trained models to produce contextually inconsistent reasoning chains, where the "correct answer is based on wrong thinking." VerIPO aims to ensure the coherence and logical consistency between the reasoning process and the final answer.
 - **Unstable Performance and Length:** Direct RL training for Video-LLMs has shown inconsistent improvements in accuracy and response length, particularly with increased temporal frame inputs. VerIPO is motivated to achieve *stable* and *consistent* improvements in both response length and reasoning quality across diverse video reasoning tasks.
 - **Repetitive Outputs:** The tendency of models to generate repetitive content within their reasoning chains is another limitation VerIPO seeks to resolve.

In essence, VerIPO's key objective is to provide a more robust, efficient, and stable training paradigm that enables Video-LLMs to gradually learn and generate high-quality, long, and contextually consistent reasoning chains for complex video understanding tasks, thereby moving beyond the inherent limitations of current RFT approaches and data constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VerIPO proposes a novel **Verifier-guided Iterative Policy Optimization** algorithm structured around a **GRPO-Verifier-DPO training loop**. This iterative process is designed to gradually enhance a Video-LLM's capacity for generating deep, long-term, and consistent reasoning chains.

The methodology can be broken down into three main phases within each iteration:

#### 3.1. Initial Policy Exploration with GRPO

The process begins by applying Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to an instruction-tuned Video-LLM (specifically, Qwen2.5-VL). GRPO is chosen for its ability to promote wide exploration in the generation space.
- **Reward Function:** Two types of rewards are used:
 - **Accuracy Reward ($r_a$):** Scaled between 0 and 1. For math/multiple-choice questions, it's binary (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect). For distance estimation, it uses Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) providing a continuous reward.
 - **Format Reward ($r_f$):** Binary (0.5 for adherence, 0 for non-adherence) for conforming to a predefined `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>` structure.
- **GRPO Objective:** The standard GRPO objective is used, which maximizes an advantage score derived from the relative performance within a group of generated responses.
- **Key Modifications:**
 - **Removal of KL Divergence:** Unlike standard GRPO, the KL divergence term (which penalizes deviations from a reference policy) is removed. This is done to broaden the model's exploration capabilities and enhance learning flexibility, preventing it from sticking too closely to its initial policy.
 - **Online Filter Strategy:** To mitigate "zero-advantage samples" (i.e., perfectly accurate samples that offer no gradient for policy updates), an online filter is integrated to exclude such samples from training batches, ensuring robust gradient flow.
- **Data Integration:** Initially, GRPO is applied using diverse datasets, starting with simple-modality data (text-only long documents, math-text, image reasoning) to activate general reasoning. Subsequent GRPO rounds incorporate image and video QA data to build video-specific reasoning.

#### 3.2. Sample Curation with Rollout-Aware Verifier

This is the core novel component of VerIPO. Positioned between the GRPO and DPO phases, the Rollout-Aware Verifier leverages a *small LLM* (e.g., Qwen3-8B) as a judge to critically assess the quality and contextual consistency of the reasoning chains (rollouts) generated by the GRPO phase. Its goal is to generate high-quality contrastive data for DPO training.
For each rollout, the verifier extracts the thought content (`r_i`) and final answer (`a_i`). It performs a four-aspect quality assessment:
- **Accuracy Check:** Verifies the correctness of the extracted answer. For distance estimation, an MRA > 0.6 is considered correct.
- **Consistency Check:** Employs Qwen3-8B to re-extract an answer from the reasoning process (`a_r_i`) and compares it to the predicted answer (`a_i`). This identifies "correct answers based on wrong thinking" or vice-versa, ensuring coherence between thought and answer.
- **Repetition Check:** Uses rule-based methods to detect and penalize sentence-level repetitions within the thought content.
- **Length Check:** Prioritizes longer reasoning chains for positive examples, rewarding thoroughness.

Based on these checks, the verifier constructs a high-quality contrastive preference dataset for DPO, categorized into:
- **Single-Turn Preference Pairs:** Negative examples are randomly selected from incorrect rollouts. Positive examples are the longest rollouts that pass all quality and consistency checks, promoting thorough correct reasoning.
- **Repetition Penalty Pairs:** Rollouts flagged for repetition are rejected examples. Chosen examples are the longest, verified, non-repetitive correct rollouts. This specifically targets and penalizes verbose outputs.
- **Reflective Preference Pairs:** For challenging prompts where the model initially struggled (low average accuracy), rejected examples concatenate multiple distinct incorrect rollouts. Chosen examples combine initial incorrect sequences with a correct one, linked by reflective phrases, to simulate self-correction.
- **Inference Consistency Pairs:** Addresses misalignment between thought and answer. If reasoning is correct but the answer is wrong, the correct reasoning with the extracted answer (`a_r_i`) forms the chosen sample. If reasoning is incorrect but the answer is correct, a contextually consistent response with the right answer serves as the chosen sample.

Rollouts with perfect accuracy are generally excluded as "simple examples" to focus training on challenging cases, and 80% of them are progressively discarded in subsequent iterations to speed up training.

#### 3.3. Policy Refinement with DPO

The curated contrastive samples from the Verifier are then used to fine-tune the model via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
- **Efficiency:** DPO is empirically shown to be 7x faster than GRPO, allowing for efficient, targeted optimization.
- **Targeted Optimization:** DPO refines the model's policy to prefer the "chosen" (high-quality, consistent) reasoning paths over "rejected" (incorrect, inconsistent, repetitive) ones, in a controllable direction.
- **Frozen Visual Encoder:** During DPO, the visual encoder is kept frozen, focusing optimization on the MLP and language model parameters.

#### 3.4. Iterative Training Loop

The GRPO-Verifier-DPO process forms an iterative loop, akin to a curriculum learning approach. It starts with reasoning activation using simpler modality data, then integrates video data, and continuously enhances and stabilizes the model's long-term reasoning capabilities. This iterative refinement allows the model to progressively learn from its online experience, combining GRPO's broad exploration with DPO's fast, targeted alignment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VerIPO demonstrates significant improvements and stability in cultivating long-form reasoning in Video-LLMs, outperforming various strong baselines.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Superiority

- **Benchmarks:** Experiments were conducted on four video reasoning benchmarks (VSI-Bench, TOMATO, Video-MMMU, MMVU (mc)) and two long video understanding benchmarks (LVBench, Video-MME).
- **Outperformance:** VerIPO consistently outperforms:
 - **Direct-inference SFT models:** Including powerful models like Qwen2.5-VL-7B (the base model), LLaMA-3.2-V, Gemma-3-IT, and Kimi-VL.
 - **RL-trained thinking models:** Such as Video-R1 and Kimi-VL-Thinking. For example, on Video-MMMU (complex domain reasoning), VerIPO shows a notable ↑5.6% improvement over Video-R1.
- **Generalization:** VerIPO (Iteration1, 7B parameters) achieves impressive scores (e.g., 41.8 on VSI-Bench, 56.2 on Video-MMMU), often surpassing larger LMMs (>11B parameters) like GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, and InternVL2.5 in specific video reasoning tasks.

#### 4.2. Effects of Verifier-Guided DPO and Iterations

- **True Accuracy and Length Improvement:** Table 3 clearly shows that Verifier-guided DPO significantly improves both "Acc-True" (correct answer consistent with right reasoning) and the average token length of responses compared to GRPO alone. Continual GRPO (without DPO) did not show consistent improvements in accuracy or length.
- **Stable Improvement:** Subsequent iterations of VerIPO, especially when trained solely on video data after initial reasoning activation, exhibit stable accuracy improvement and increased reasoning length (e.g., TOMATO "Acc-True" from 26.5 to 31.3, and length increasing by ~100 tokens). This highlights the cumulative benefit of the iterative GRPO-Verifier-DPO loop.

#### 4.3. Impact of Verifier Components

- **Criticality of Preference Pairs:** Ablation studies (Figure 3 (A)) demonstrate that removing specific DPO preference pairs (Reflective Preference Pairs and Inference Consistency Pairs) leads to decreased response length and accuracy, particularly hindering improvements in the inconsistency rate. This confirms the Verifier's role in constructing high-quality, targeted training signals.

#### 4.4. Addressing Inconsistency and Repetition

- **Contextual Consistency:** VerIPO successfully addresses the critical issue of inconsistency between the reasoning process and the final answer (Figure 3 (B)). The VerIPO training loop progressively reduces this inconsistency across iterations, leading to a consistent enhancement in "true accuracy." This is a major improvement over the "cold start" (SFT) models or initial GRPO, which often showed high inconsistency rates.
- **Repetition Reduction:** The Rollout-Aware Verifier's ability to construct specialized preference data for repetition penalty, combined with DPO iterations, significantly decreases the frequency of repetitive content in generated reasoning chains (Figure 3 (C)).

#### 4.5. Impact of Cold Start and Reasoning Activation

- **Cold Start Limitations:** Training with a "cold start" (Supervised Fine-Tuning) using low-quality Long-CoT datasets (like Video-R1-COT 165k) resulted in marginal gains on some metrics but significant performance degradation on general reasoning tasks, which was unrecoverable by subsequent VerIPO iterations. This emphasizes the detrimental effect of poor initial data quality.
- **Benefits of Reasoning Activation:** Conversely, the initial "Reasoning Activation" phase, which uses only image and text data, showed good generalization to the video domain and exceptionally strong performance on complex tasks like Video-MMMU. This suggests that pre-activating general logical reasoning abilities with diverse data is more beneficial than a video-specific "cold start" with potentially noisy data.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Analysis (Case Studies)

- **Longer, Accurate, and Reflective CoTs:** Case studies (Figures 4-9) illustrate that VerIPO enables models to generate longer, more accurate, and logically coherent reasoning chains, sometimes incorporating self-reflection to correct errors. This is particularly evident in challenging science, temporal grounding, and spatial reasoning problems. The inclusion of textual or visual math in the reasoning activation stage appears to aid in generating logical reasoning for scientific problems.

In summary, VerIPO's results validate its core premise: an iterative, verifier-guided approach effectively cultivates robust, long-form reasoning in Video-LLMs, addressing key challenges related to data quality, training stability, reasoning depth, and consistency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VerIPO represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), particularly for Video-LLMs and their ability to perform complex, long-form reasoning. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Enabling Deeper Video Understanding:** By successfully cultivating long and contextually consistent Chain-of-Thoughts (CoTs), VerIPO moves Video-LLMs beyond shallow recognition or short-term action understanding. This is crucial for applications requiring intricate analysis of temporal relationships, causal dependencies, and domain-specific knowledge extraction from extended video sequences (e.g., medical diagnosis from surgical videos, detailed event reconstruction from surveillance footage, scientific discovery from experimental recordings).

2. **Addressing the Data Bottleneck in Video Reasoning:** One of the most pressing challenges in training powerful Video-LLMs is the scarcity and high cost of high-quality, annotated video reasoning data. VerIPO's approach, which does not rely on massive pre-collected Long-CoT SFT datasets for "cold starting," offers a practical solution. Its ability to generate high-quality contrastive samples *from its own rollouts* via the Verifier significantly reduces the reliance on laborious manual annotation, making the development of reasoning-capable Video-LLMs more accessible and scalable.

3. **Improving Training Efficiency and Stability:** The combination of GRPO for broad exploration and DPO for fast, targeted optimization (7x faster than GRPO) provides a more efficient training paradigm. Furthermore, the Verifier's role in curating high-quality preference data addresses the instability and inconsistency issues often observed in direct RL training, leading to more reliable performance gains. This means faster development cycles and more predictable outcomes for research and deployment.

4. **Enhancing Model Interpretability and Trustworthiness:** By encouraging the generation of explicit, logically consistent reasoning chains, VerIPO can improve the interpretability of Video-LLMs' decisions. This is vital in high-stakes domains where understanding "why" a model arrived at an answer is as important as the answer itself, fostering greater trust in AI systems. The reduction in contextual inconsistency ("correct answer based on wrong thinking") directly contributes to this.

5. **Setting a New SOTA for Video Reasoning:** The experimental results demonstrate VerIPO's ability to consistently outperform larger instruction-tuned LMMs and existing RL-trained thinking models on various video reasoning benchmarks. This establishes a new state-of-the-art for reasoning capabilities in 7B-parameter Video-LLMs, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed methodology regardless of significant model size increases.

6. **Insights for Future LMM Development:** The "Discussion" section offers valuable insights into broader challenges in LMMs, such as the inherent limitations of foundation models for long video understanding, the "overthinking" problem for simpler prompts, and the instability of RL training. VerIPO implicitly suggests paths forward, including the need for adaptive reasoning capabilities and sophisticated reward functions that can guide models to choose appropriate reasoning depths based on task complexity. This provides a blueprint for building more versatile and robust LMMs.

Despite its strengths, the paper acknowledges limitations, including potential verifier dependence, data size and response length constraints in the generated CoTs, and computational costs. Future work aims to address these by exploring advanced verifier designs, optimizing the training pipeline, and integrating stronger SFT on base LMMs. Nonetheless, VerIPO's introduction of a robust, verifier-guided iterative optimization approach marks a significant step towards realizing truly intelligent and capable Video-LLMs with strong, long-term reasoning abilities.
