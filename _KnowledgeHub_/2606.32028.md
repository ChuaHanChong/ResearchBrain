# DVG-WM: Disentangled Video Generation Enables Efficient Embodied World Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DVG-WM introduces a two-stage disentangled video generation framework for embodied world models, separating dynamics learning from high-fidelity visual synthesis. This architecture enables robotic manipulation models to predict future states with enhanced visual quality and temporal coherence while achieving up to 3.97x faster inference and an average 28.2% improvement in real-world task success rates.

## Problem

- Existing embodied world models for robotic manipulation suffer from a "fundamental entanglement," attempting to simultaneously learn physical dynamics and synthesize high-resolution visuals within a single generative process.
- This entanglement leads to slow inference speeds for high-resolution outputs, hindering iterative planning, or results in coarse predictions lacking critical contact-rich details necessary for precise manipulation.
- Video diffusion models, despite offering high fidelity, exacerbate the efficiency problem due to their iterative denoising process and inherently high inference costs.

## Method

- Proposes **DVG-WM**, a two-stage ==disentangled video generation== framework that separates dynamics modeling into a low-resolution "preview stage" and high-fidelity visual synthesis into a "refinement stage."
- The preview stage leverages a ==LoRA==-adapted ==CogVideoX-5B== to efficiently capture essential dynamics at low resolution, while the refinement stage, using ==CogVideoX-2B==, reconstructs high-fidelity visuals.
- Introduces ==flow matching== to efficiently cascade between stages and a ==latent degradation== mechanism in the refinement stage to encourage reconstruction of contact-rich details, preventing simple shortcut upscaling.

## Results

- DVG-WM achieved superior video quality (PSNR **20.019**, LPIPS **0.120**, FVD **152.36**) and object-level accuracy (**89%**) on the LIBERO simulation dataset, outperforming monolithic world models.
- The framework demonstrated up to **3.97x** faster inference compared to LVP-14B, attributed to its two-stage design with minimal refinement steps (**K=4**).
- It achieved an average **62.2%** success rate on real-world manipulation tasks with a vision-only Diffusion Policy expert, representing a **28.2%** improvement over CogVideoX, particularly in contact-rich and long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling dynamics learning from high-fidelity visual synthesis allows for optimizing each component independently, overcoming the computational bottleneck and detail retention challenges in integrated video generation models.
> - Flow matching provides an efficient mechanism to directly map low-resolution dynamic representations to high-resolution latents, circumventing the need for full re-generation and significantly speeding up the refinement process.
> - Latent degradation, by perturbing the upscaled low-resolution input, encourages the model to reconstruct crucial contact-rich structures rather than merely upscaling pixels, which is essential for precise robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied world models are an area of increasing focus in robotics, enabling robots to predict future environmental states and facilitate action planning based on initial observations and conditional inputs. These models typically operate by simulating fine-grained action outcomes to evaluate policies or by forecasting future states toward specified goals, such as target images or language instructions.

The mainstream approach for embodied world models often employs video prediction, wherein a pre-trained video generation backbone is fine-tuned with various conditioning inputs (e.g., language, actions, goal images) to produce future latent dynamics or pixel-level predictions. While these video prediction models can achieve considerable visual quality and temporal coherence, their application in real-world robotic manipulation, particularly for iterative planning, is constrained by inference efficiency. This limitation arises from a fundamental entanglement within current systems: the process of accurately modeling physical dynamics and the process of synthesizing high-resolution visual frames are often handled by a single, monolithic video generator.

Dynamics modeling typically necessitates low-level temporal reasoning to capture subtle transitions like contacts, slips, and occlusions. This aspect benefits from focusing on interaction patterns rather than high-resolution textures. Conversely, generating high-resolution frames requires extensive visual synthesis that relies on high-level semantics and detailed texture information. Existing approaches that attempt to satisfy both demands simultaneously frequently result in slow inference speeds for high-resolution outputs or coarse predictions that lack the crucial details necessary for contact-rich manipulation tasks. This inefficiency becomes a significant bottleneck in scenarios requiring repeated queries to the world model for planning, such as in model-predictive control or iterative goal-conditioned planning.

Video diffusion models, a prominent framework for high-fidelity video synthesis, have advanced temporal modeling and long-horizon generation. However, they inherently involve high inference costs due to iterative denoising, which further exacerbates the efficiency problem for embodied manipulation.

This work addresses the aforementioned efficiency and fidelity limitations by proposing a disentangled approach to world modeling. It seeks to separate dynamics learning from high-fidelity visual synthesis, allowing each component to operate at an optimal resolution and computational complexity. This distinction aims to overcome the computational burden and detail retention challenges present in integrated video generation models, thereby positioning DVG-WM as a more efficient and practical solution for embodied world models in robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a perceived bottleneck in existing video-based embodied world models for robotic manipulation: the "fundamental entanglement" between dynamics modeling and high-fidelity visual synthesis. The authors identify that current approaches attempt to achieve both requirements—accurate low-level temporal reasoning for dynamics and expansive high-resolution visual synthesis for frame generation—within a single, undifferentiated generative process.

This entanglement leads to several challenges:
1. **Slow Inference Speed:** Generating high-resolution videos for every prediction in an iterative planning loop is computationally intensive. Accurate dynamics modeling, which focuses on physical interaction patterns (contacts, slips), does not necessarily require high-resolution inputs with redundant texture information. Conversely, high-resolution visual synthesis, demanding rich texture and semantic information, is computationally costly. When these are combined, the inference speed is suboptimal.
2. **Coarse Predictions:** To manage computational load, some models may resort to generating lower-resolution videos, which often results in coarse predictions that fail to retain critical contact-rich details essential for precise robotic manipulation. This trade-off between resolution and detail fidelity hinders the model's utility for tasks requiring fine-grained interaction.

The key objectives of this work are to:
- **Decompose World Modeling:** Explicitly disentangle the process of learning environmental dynamics from the process of synthesizing high-resolution visuals. This decomposition is posited as a solution to the aforementioned dilemma, allowing each component to be optimized separately.
- **Enable Efficient Preview:** Develop a "preview stage" that generates a plausible sequence of low-resolution intermediate visual states. This stage is intended to efficiently capture the essential dynamics and physical interactions, providing a rapid overview suitable for iterative planning without significant computational overhead.
- **Facilitate High-Fidelity Refinement:** Implement a "refinement stage" that takes the low-resolution dynamics from the preview stage and reconstructs high-fidelity videos with fine-grained details. This stage aims to restore high-frequency details and contact-sensitive cues with a minimal number of function evaluations (NFEs).
- **Propose a Cascading Mechanism:** Develop a novel method to efficiently connect the preview and refinement stages. This includes leveraging flow matching to directly map low-resolution dynamics to high-resolution video latents and introducing a latent degradation mechanism.
- **Enhance Contact-Rich Detail Regeneration:** Ensure the model’s ability to regenerate accurate structures of objects and the end-effector at high resolutions, which is considered crucial for successful contact-rich manipulation tasks. The latent degradation mechanism is specifically designed to address this.
- **Demonstrate Improved Performance:** Empirically validate that the disentangled approach leads to improved video quality and faster inference speeds compared to monolithic world models.
- **Validate Real-World Applicability:** Show that the proposed model can serve as an effective action planner for robotic manipulation tasks in real-world environments.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of current video-based world models, making them more efficient and practical for real-world robotic applications that demand both rapid planning and precise execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DVG-WM framework is designed to overcome the limitations of entangled dynamics modeling and visual synthesis by introducing a two-stage, disentangled video generation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The work focuses on goal-conditioned embodied world models that predict future observations $x_{1:N}$ given an initial observation $x_0$ and a language instruction $c$. To manage the computational intensity of high-resolution video generation, a 3D causal Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is employed to compress video pixels $x_T \in R^{H \times W \times T}$ into latent features $z \in Q^{h \times w \times t}$, where $h = H/8$, $w = W/8$, and $t = (T-1)/4 + 1$. The model is designed to predict videos of 49 frames at 720p resolution. The core of the methodology is the two-stage pipeline consisting of a preview stage ($g_p$) and a refinement stage ($g_r$), formulated as $g_p(z_{lr}|x_0^d, c)$ and $g_r(z_{hr}|z_{lr}, x_0, c)$, where $x_0^d$ is a downsampled initial frame for the preview stage.

#### 3.2. Low-Resolution Preview Stage

This initial stage focuses on learning the essential dynamics of the interaction and generating low-resolution video latents ($z_{lr}$) that align with the provided language instruction.
- **Backbone:** The CogVideoX-5B model is utilized as the video generation backbone.
- **Efficiency Adaptations:** To enhance computational efficiency, the learnable positional embedding from the original backbone is replaced with flexible 3D rotary positional embedding, allowing for variable output resolutions.
- **Fine-tuning:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) with a rank of 128 is applied to the attention layers, Feed-Forward Networks (FFN), and layer normalization of the CogVideoX-5B model. This adaptation aims to reduce training costs significantly while enabling the model to capture critical dynamic patterns at a lower resolution. The output of this stage serves as a physically meaningful initialization for the subsequent refinement stage.

#### 3.3. High-Resolution Refinement Stage

The refinement stage is dedicated to fine-grained visual synthesis, transforming the low-resolution dynamics ($z_{lr}$) into high-resolution video latents ($z_{hr}$) with a limited number of function evaluations (NFEs).
- **Backbone:** A smaller model, CogVideoX-2B, is employed. This choice is based on the observation that embodied manipulation scenes are typically structured with slower, more locally constrained state transitions compared to general "in-the-wild" videos, allowing for a more compact model.

The refinement stage incorporates two key mechanisms:

#### 3.3.1. Flow Matching for Cascaded Generation

- **Problem:** A conventional approach of treating low-resolution latents as conditioning input for a new diffusion process would require generating the entire video from scratch, leading to high computational loads despite the preview stage providing a reasonable initialization.
- **Solution:** Flow matching is leveraged to directly establish a mapping from the low-resolution dynamics ($z_{lr}$) to the high-resolution video latents ($z_{hr}$) in the latent space.
- **Mechanism:** Flow matching learns a vector field $v_\theta(z_\tau, \tau | z_{lr}, x_0, c)$ that specifies step-by-step update directions in the latent space. An intermediate latent state $z_\tau$ is defined as a linear interpolation between an upsampled version of $z_{lr}$ (denoted as $\tilde{z}_{lr}$) and the ground-truth high-resolution latent $z_{hr}$: $z_\tau = (1-\tau)\tilde{z}_{lr} + \tau z_{hr}$. The target velocity $u^*(z_\tau, \tau)$ is simply the displacement $z_{hr} - \tilde{z}_{lr}$.
- **Training:** A neural vector field $v_\theta$ is trained to predict this velocity using the flow-matching objective $L_{FM} = E[||v_\theta(z_\tau, \tau | z_{lr}, x_0, c) - u^*(z_\tau, \tau)||_2^2]$.
- **Inference:** Refinement starts from the initialized latent $z_0 = \tilde{z}_{lr}$ and applies a deterministic flow process, performing $K$ Euler updates (typically $K=4$ in experiments) to obtain the final high-resolution latent $z_K$, which is then decoded.

#### 3.3.2. Latent Degradation for Contact-Rich Manipulation

- **Problem:** Directly degrading high-resolution videos at the pixel level and encoding them for training the refinement stage can lead to a "shortcut upscaling" effect. This means the model might learn to simply upscale pixels rather than regenerating fine-grained structures crucial for contacts between the end-effector and objects. This tight correlation between low- and high-resolution videos constrains the model's ability to create new details.
- **Solution:** Latent degradation is introduced to perturb the upscaled output of the preview stage ($\tilde{z}_{lr}$).
- **Mechanism:**
 1. A pixel-space degradation operator ($Deg_{pix}$) is first applied to the ground-truth high-resolution video $x_{hr}$ to mimic the loss of high-frequency visual details, yielding $\hat{x}_{lr}$.
 2. $\hat{x}_{lr}$ is then encoded into a latent space, producing $z_{deg}$.
 3. A latent degradation operator ($Deg_{lat}$) applies Gaussian noise to $z_{deg}$: $\tilde{z}_{lr} = \alpha_s z_{deg} + \beta_s \epsilon$, where $\epsilon \sim N(0, I)$ and $\alpha_s^2 + \beta_s^2 = 1$. The degradation strength $s$ controls how much the input deviates from the pixel-degraded latent.
- **Effect:** This process weakens the one-to-one correspondence between degraded and target videos. It encourages the refiner to rely on its learned generative prior to reconstruct plausible, contact-sensitive structures such as gripper boundaries, object contours, and local interaction geometry. During inference, the preview stage output $z_{lr}$ is upsampled and then a mild latent perturbation is injected as $z_0$ before the refinement steps.

#### 3.4. Action Generation for Imitation Learning

To enable the DVG-WM to perform robotic manipulation, an action expert is integrated to convert the predicted high-resolution videos into executable actions.
- **Action Expert:** A vision-only Diffusion Policy (DP) is employed, as robot states are typically unavailable for predicted future frames.
- **Process:** Predicted frames from DVG-WM at different timestamps serve as conditioning inputs ($h_t$) for the Diffusion Policy, which then denoises a noise sequence into an action sequence via a standard diffusion objective $L_{DP}$.
- **Training Strategy:** The full system is trained in two stages for stability:
 1. The world model (DVG-WM) is first trained independently using the flow-matching objective ($L_{FM}$) to become a reliable video predictor.
 2. Subsequently, the action expert (Diffusion Policy) is attached. It is optimized on demonstrations using $L_{DP}$, while the world model is jointly fine-tuned. Gradients from the imitation learning loss propagate through both the action expert and the world model, aligning visual predictions with downstream action planning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DVG-WM was conducted on both the LIBERO simulation dataset and a real-world dataset, focusing on video quality, object-level accuracy, inference efficiency, and real-world task success rates.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison (LIBERO Simulation)

On the LIBERO dataset, DVG-WM was compared against established video generation world models including CogVideoX-5B, Wan2.1-14B, LongScape, and LVP-14B.
- **Video Quality:** DVG-WM achieved superior visual quality metrics:
 - PSNR: 20.019 (highest)
 - SSIM: 0.783 (competitive with LongScape's 0.788)
 - LPIPS: 0.120 (lowest, indicating better perceptual quality)
 - FVD: 152.36 (lowest, indicating better temporal coherence and realism)
 These metrics represented an average improvement of 3.7% in overall visual quality.
- **Object-Level Accuracy:** DVG-WM substantially improved object-level accuracy to 89%, indicating a more reliable grounding of interactions on correct target objects, which is critical for manipulation tasks. This was higher than LVP-14B (80%) and CogVideoX-5B (76%). The results suggest that the two-stage design, where the preview stage provides contact-rich cues, guides the refinement towards physically meaningful and high-quality generation.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Comparison (LIBERO Simulation)

Qualitative comparisons with CogVideoX on LIBERO tasks revealed:
- **CogVideoX:** While producing satisfactory visual quality, CogVideoX exhibited failures in accurately following instructions, such as showing intent to interact with the wrong object (e.g., a bowl instead of a drawer) and missing critical interaction stages (e.g., the final drawer pulling motion).
- **DVG-WM:** Successfully generated high-quality videos, capturing complete trajectories, and accurately depicted contact-rich details of interactions, such as the gripper's contact with a drawer and its subsequent movement. This demonstrates an improved capability in modeling physical dynamics and following instructions.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Comparison

DVG-WM demonstrated a significant speedup in inference time:
- It achieved up to 3.97x acceleration compared to LVP-14B (88.7s vs. 354.2s).
- It was also significantly faster than CogVideoX-5B (236.8s) and Wan2.1-14B (312.0s).
This efficiency is attributed to the disentangled design, where the initial denoising steps are performed at a much lower resolution, and the refinement stage requires only a minimal number of denoising steps (K=4).

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to validate the design choices of DVG-WM:
- **Disentangled Design Validation:** Qualitative analysis of the preview stage with LoRA fine-tuning showed that while zero-shot CogVideoX-5B produced scene layout changes and missing robots, the LoRA-adapted preview stage, despite losing some texture details, successfully captured contact-rich interaction details and modeled the interaction process correctly. The refinement stage, leveraging CogVideoX, restored texture details effectively, and the full DVG-WM combined these strengths.
- **Component Analysis:**
 - **Latent Degradation:** Replacing latent degradation with pixel-level degradation resulted in degraded video quality (SSIM 0.768 vs. 0.783), supporting the hypothesis that latent degradation encourages reconstruction of contact-relevant structures rather than simple upscaling.
 - **Flow Matching:** A "Naive Cascading" approach, treating low-resolution dynamics as a simple conditioning input for refinement, led to a performance drop, validating the effectiveness of flow matching in directly mapping dynamics to high-resolution latents.
 - **Necessity of Both Stages:** Removing either the preview stage ("Only Preview") or the refinement stage ("Only Refine") individually led to significant drops across all metrics, confirming that both stages are crucial and complementary for achieving high-quality, efficient predictions.
 - **Preview Resolution:** Lowering the preview resolution to 360x240 pixels negatively impacted performance, suggesting the importance of leveraging pretraining knowledge acquired on 256x384 videos.
- **Number of Refinement Steps (K):** Increasing K from 1 to 4 consistently improved all metrics. However, increasing K from 4 to 8 yielded only marginal gains while nearly doubling computation, establishing K=4 as an optimal balance between fidelity and efficiency.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiments

DVG-WM was evaluated on real-world manipulation tasks using Galaxea A1 and UR7e robotic arms.
- **Video Quality (Real-World):** DVG-WM outperformed CogVideoX-5B across all video quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD), indicating superior generation of high-fidelity videos that accurately capture real-world interaction dynamics.
- **Mask-IoU:** DVG-WM achieved a higher Mask-IoU for embodiment regions (0.57 vs. 0.54 for CogVideoX-5B), suggesting improved perception of the physical interactions between the robot and objects.
- **Qualitative Results (Real-World):** DVG-WM successfully predicted realistic future interactions, exhibiting fine-grained contact details between the gripper and objects, proper task completion sequences, and preservation of contact areas and textures (e.g., bread). CogVideoX-5B exhibited ghosting artifacts and motion blur.
- **Action Planning Success Rates:** When equipped with a vision-only Diffusion Policy (DP) action expert, DVG-WM achieved an average success rate of 62.2% across nine real-world manipulation tasks. This significantly outperformed the CogVideoX world model, which achieved 34.0% on average, representing a 28.2% improvement. The gains were particularly pronounced in contact-rich and long-horizon tasks such as adapter insertion, assemble gears, and make a toast. These results validate DVG-WM's effectiveness as an embodied world model for practical real-world robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents DVG-WM as an advancement in embodied world models for robotic manipulation by addressing the long-standing challenge of inefficiency caused by the entanglement of dynamics modeling and high-resolution visual synthesis. The core contribution lies in its disentangled two-stage pipeline, which separates these two aspects into a low-resolution preview stage for dynamics learning and a high-resolution refinement stage for visual synthesis.

The significance of this work is multi-faceted:
- **Improved Efficiency for Iterative Planning:** By enabling a fast preview of dynamics at a lower resolution and subsequent high-fidelity refinement with minimal computational overhead, DVG-WM offers a practical solution for scenarios requiring iterative planning in real-world robotics. The demonstrated speedup of up to 3.97x significantly reduces the bottleneck of inference time.
- **Enhanced Fidelity and Accuracy:** The model not only generates higher-quality video predictions but also exhibits improved object-level accuracy and Mask-IoU, which are crucial for precise robotic interactions. The latent degradation mechanism specifically addresses the challenge of regenerating contact-rich details, preventing the model from merely performing shortcut upscaling.
- **Validated Design Choices:** The ablation studies provide empirical evidence supporting the effectiveness of key architectural components, including LoRA fine-tuning for dynamics capture, flow matching for efficient cascaded generation, and latent degradation for detail regeneration. This validation contributes to the understanding of effective design principles for generative world models.
- **Strong Real-World Performance:** The successful application of DVG-WM in real-world robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating a substantial improvement in task success rates when integrated with an action expert, indicates its practical utility. This real-world validation suggests that DVG-WM can serve as a robust and reliable video planner for complex, contact-rich manipulation.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The disentangled video generation paradigm proposed by DVG-WM offers a promising direction for developing more scalable and efficient embodied world models. This approach could inspire future research in optimizing different aspects of world modeling independently, leading to more robust and versatile robotic systems capable of operating in diverse and dynamic environments.

In summary, DVG-WM represents a step toward more capable and efficient embodied world models, with direct implications for advancing autonomous robotic manipulation by providing both rapid foresight and detailed visual prediction.
