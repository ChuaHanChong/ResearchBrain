# RoboReact: Agentic Skill Distillation from Generated Egocentric Videos for Generalizable Whole-Body Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboReact introduces a framework for acquiring generalizable whole-body humanoid manipulation skills by distilling interaction knowledge from generated egocentric videos. This approach leverages an iterative VLM-guided refinement process and object-centric re-grounding to achieve robust robot policies, demonstrating performance comparable to skills derived from real human demonstrations.

## Problem

- Traditional robot skill acquisition methods are often costly, labor-intensive, and lack generalizability across diverse tasks and scene configurations for high-DoF humanoid robots.
- While video generative models can synthesize human manipulations, transferring these non-metric, imagined behaviors into physically executable whole-body skills for complex humanoid platforms remains a challenge.
- Existing approaches often provide only coarse supervision or require significant 3D spatial information and fine-tuning, struggling to integrate with dynamic, high-DoF humanoid systems.

## Method

- **RoboReact** distills interaction knowledge by extracting ==geometry-preserving keyframes== and recovering metric wrist/hand motions from VLM-selected generated egocentric videos, then retargeting them to a high-DoF humanoid.
- It employs an ==in-context VLM-guided iterative refinement loop==, where a VLM diagnoses failures from robot calibration rollouts and proposes structured, bounded edits to optimize the skill's semantic preservation, interaction geometry, and motion prior.
- The framework ensures robustness through deterministic feasibility projections for proposed edits and utilizes an ==online object-centric re-grounding mechanism== during execution to adapt to real-world object poses.

## Results

- RoboReact achieved an average terminal success rate of **81.3%** across four bimanual manipulation tasks on a Unitree G1 humanoid, matching the performance of skills derived from 'One-Shot Real Prior' videos.
- The sequential VLM-guided refinement significantly improved skill quality, with success rates non-decreasing over **15 rounds** and demonstrating that more capable VLM editors led to higher efficiency and performance.
- The frozen object-centric execution stack exhibited robustness, retaining **80-94%** of its nominal performance under various disturbances, including base-pose perturbations and object shifts, without VLM involvement at test time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generated egocentric videos, when properly grounded and refined, can serve as effective interaction priors for synthesizing complex whole-body manipulation skills for humanoid robots, comparable to using real human demonstrations.
> - An agentic, VLM-driven iterative refinement process is crucial for adapting imagined motions to robot kinematics and real-world geometry, enabling robust skill acquisition without extensive human intervention.
> - Object-centric skill representation combined with online re-grounding allows for generalizable execution, enabling the robot to perform tasks despite variations in object pose, instances, and environmental disturbances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of dexterous manipulation in human environments presents a significant challenge. Traditional methods for acquiring robot skills, such as teleoperated demonstrations, human-motion retargeting, or reinforcement learning (RL) with extensive fine-tuning, typically incur high costs in terms of hardware, data collection, and labor-intensive annotation. These approaches are often specific to particular robot embodiments and lack generalizability across diverse tasks or scene configurations.

Recent advancements in video generative models offer a potential alternative by synthesizing rich manipulation experiences from visual observations. These models can generate human-performed task executions from a single egocentric RGB-D observation, providing visual guidance for skill synthesis. This automation could potentially reduce the reliance on real-world data collection and facilitate the scalable generation of diverse manipulation experiences. However, a significant gap exists in transferring these imagined, often non-metric and low-degree-of-freedom (DoF), behaviors into executable whole-body skills for high-DoF humanoid platforms, where coordinated whole-body movement and loco-manipulation are essential. Prior work often provides only coarse-grained supervision (e.g., point trajectories, affordances, 3D hand poses) and frequently requires additional 3D spatial information, hand-object calibration, or real robot data fine-tuning to translate visual signals into physically executable motions. Moreover, integrating these video-based guidance methods with highly dynamic humanoid systems remains largely unexplored.

RoboReact addresses this gap by proposing a framework that specifically aims to distill generalizable whole-body manipulation skills from generated egocentric videos, ensuring that the synthesized behaviors are both executable on high-DoF humanoid platforms and robust to real-world uncertainties. It situates itself at the intersection of video generation, vision-language model (VLM) reasoning, and closed-loop robot control, aiming to leverage the scalability of generative models while grounding the imagined actions in real-world geometry and robot constraints. This work distinguishes itself from prior efforts that might focus on fixed-base manipulators or lack integrated whole-body coordination, moving towards more autonomous and generalizable skill acquisition for mobile, dexterous humanoid robots.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, RoboReact, that can automatically synthesize generalizable whole-body humanoid manipulation skills. This is achieved by distilling interaction knowledge from generated egocentric videos, with a focus on enabling execution on high-DoF humanoid platforms. The framework aims to overcome the limitations of existing skill acquisition methods, which are often costly, labor-intensive, and lack transferability across varied conditions.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Reduce Data Collection and Annotation Costs**: Traditional methods for acquiring robot manipulation skills, such as teleoperation or human demonstrations, require substantial resources for data collection and expert annotation. The project aims to leverage advancements in video generative models to synthesize diverse manipulation experiences from minimal input (a single egocentric RGB-D observation), thereby automating a significant portion of the skill acquisition process and reducing reliance on expensive hardware-based data collection.
2. **Enable Generalizable Skills**: The goal is to develop skills that are not rigidly tied to specific object configurations or environmental setups. By focusing on distilling object-centric interaction geometry rather than direct trajectory imitation, RoboReact seeks to create policies that can generalize across variations in object shape, pose, and scene background, and recover from execution disturbances.
3. **Bridge the Simulation-to-Reality and Imagination-to-Execution Gap**: Generated videos provide an "imagined" plan of human interaction, which may lack metric accuracy or direct applicability to robot kinematics and dynamics. A core motivation is to develop mechanisms for translating these visual priors into physically executable motions on a real robot, including robust real-world grounding and adaptive refinement.
4. **Facilitate Whole-Body Coordination for High-DoF Humanoids**: While many existing approaches focus on low-DoF manipulators or fixed-base systems, humanoid robots operate with high degrees of freedom and require coordinated whole-body movements for stable and dexterous manipulation, including loco-manipulation. The research is motivated by the need to develop a framework that can generate and execute skills that leverage the full kinematic capabilities of humanoid robots.
5. **Develop Agentic Refinement for Robustness**: Recognizing that initial generated motions may not be perfect, the work is motivated by the need for an iterative refinement process. This process should leverage an agent-based approach, specifically a Vision-Language Model (VLM), to diagnose failures, propose bounded skill adjustments, and adapt to real-world geometric mismatches and execution deviations in a closed-loop manner, without requiring continuous human intervention or task-specific re-training.

In essence, RoboReact aims to provide a scalable, generalizable, and robust solution for humanoid robot skill acquisition by integrating generative AI with grounded control and intelligent, agentic refinement.

### 3. Methodology and approach

RoboReact is formulated as a constrained sequential skill-distillation problem designed to recover an executable and generalizable whole-body skill for humanoid robots. This process relies on a task prompt, an initial egocentric RGB-D observation, and a pre-trained video generation model. The framework operates in two main phases: skill distillation and execution.

**Skill Distillation Phase:**

1. **Video Generation and Selection:** Given an initial RGB-D frame and a language instruction (task prompt), a pre-trained video generation model synthesizes multiple human manipulation videos. A Vision-Language Model (VLM) then selects the most appropriate video (G*) as the interaction prior.
2. **Keyframe Extraction and Retargeting:**
 - **Depth-Aware 3D Reconstruction:** Geometry-preserving interaction keyframes are extracted from the selected generated video. Unlike direct trajectory warping, RoboReact focuses on preserving the underlying hand-object interaction geometry.
 - **Metric Wrist and Hand Motion Recovery:** The framework recovers metric wrist and hand motions from the generated video. Keyframes are chosen to represent critical stages of the manipulation process, such as approach, contact/grasp, manipulation, and completion.
 - **Motion Retargeting:** Human motion captured in these keyframes is retargeted to the high-DoF humanoid platform, preserving the relative hand-object geometry. This includes both end-effector (EE) poses and dexterous hand commands. Missing poses are imputed by falling back to the nearest valid observation.
3. **Constrained Skill Formulation:** A bimanual robot skill is represented as an ordered sequence of K keyframes ($\Pi$), where each keyframe includes the stage (approach, align, fixed), reference object, EE poses for left and right arms, dexterous hand motions, and hand validity masks. The goal is to find the optimal skill ($\Pi^*$) that minimizes a weighted objective function L, subject to robot feasibility constraints (F). The objective function L combines three terms:
 - **Semantic Preservation Objective ($\mathcal{L}_{sem}$):** This term ensures that the distilled skill preserves the task objective, manipulation order, and interaction phases depicted in the generated video. A VLM assesses discrepancies between the structured visual-semantic description of the robot's rollout trace (T) and the generated human video (G*). Keyframes are selected by the VLM to encode the complete manipulation process.
 - **Interaction Geometry Objective ($\mathcal{L}_{geo}$):** This objective preserves the object-centric interaction geometry. Since generated videos are non-metric, the human relative transform is not directly observable. The VLM refines the initial semantic hand-object relation from aligned keyframes into an executable relative transform ($\Delta T^{\diamond,*}_k$). At execution time, this template is re-grounded using the current object pose ($\hat{T}^o_k$). Two preceding keyframes are marked as "approach" frames with object-conditioned clearance biases to prevent collisions.
 - **Motion Prior Objective ($\mathcal{L}_{mot}$):** This term provides a soft prior over reaching direction, wrist orientation, finger configuration, bimanual coordination, and temporal progression. It measures discrepancies between the robot's wrist pose and hand commands, and the corresponding priors recovered from the generated video and retargeted to robot space.
4. **In-Context Optimization Solver (Agentic Refinement):** Direct gradient-based optimization of the objective function is impractical. Instead, RoboReact employs an in-context refinement loop. A frozen VLM acts as an "in-context optimization agent" that iteratively analyzes and adapts the skill.
 - **Calibration Rollouts:** The robot executes the current projected skill ($\bar{\Pi}_n$) in a calibration rollout, recording a trace ($R_n$) including egocentric/third-person RGB-D observations, estimated object poses, commanded/realized EE poses, hand commands, and proprioception. This data is added to an accumulated memory ($M_{n+1}$).
 - **VLM-Guided Edit Proposal:** Using the current skill, rollout memory, the generated video reference, and optional sparse human hints, the VLM proposes structured edits ($\Delta\Pi_n$). The VLM diagnoses failures by labeling relevant keyframes (e.g., success, alignment, grasp, infeasible) and provides supporting evidence. Its output operations are restricted to `keep`, `align`, `offset`, `insert`, and `delete`, which can modify object associations, execution modes, apply bounded pose/hand corrections, or add/remove keyframes.
 - **Feasibility Projection:** Proposed edits are validated against a deterministic validator that checks schema, grounding, offset bounds, required keyframes, and preservation of successful subgoals. Validated edits are then projected onto the feasible robot-skill space (F) via `Proj_F`, which re-grounds object-relative keyframes, applies clearances, and converts wrist targets into joint targets. Invalid or failing projections are rolled back. This ensures that only feasible and safe commands are generated.

**Execution Phase:**

1. **Frozen Skill Execution:** Once the refinement budget is exhausted or a successful skill is obtained, the refined skill ($\bar{\Pi}^*$) is frozen.
2. **Online Object-Centric Re-grounding:** At test time, the VLM is out of the control loop. Perception continuously re-estimates object poses. These updated poses are used to re-ground the object-relative keyframes, aligning the retargeted motion with the observed object pose.
3. **Whole-Body Control:** The re-grounded keyframes generate feasible commands (base, body-height, torso, arm, hand targets) for a low-level whole-body controller (based on HOMIE). This controller tracks the commands while maintaining whole-body balance and enabling coordinated manipulation.

This methodology emphasizes leveraging generative visual priors for interaction structure, grounding them in real-world geometry, and iteratively refining them with VLM-based agentic reasoning and deterministic feasibility checks, culminating in a robust, executable policy for whole-body humanoid manipulation.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments conducted on a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot evaluated RoboReact across four long-horizon bimanual manipulation tasks: Hand Over, Pour Water, Open Box, and Open Drawer. The findings address four research questions:

**Q1: Effectiveness of generated-video skill distillation.**
RoboReact demonstrated consistent superior performance compared to baselines ReKep and YOTO across all four tasks. It achieved an average terminal success rate (SR) of 81.3% and a mean average completed step length (Avg. Len.) of 4.20. Notably, RoboReact's performance closely matched that of a "One-Shot Real Prior" (mean SR 80.0%, mean Avg. Len. 4.20), which uses a task-specific human demonstration video as its initial prior. This indicates that RoboReact successfully distills interaction knowledge from *generated* videos to achieve performance comparable to using *real* human demonstrations, without requiring any teleoperation or human demonstrations for task-specific skill acquisition. The qualitative results illustrated the framework's ability to transfer skills across different table heights, physical locations, and object instances/configurations, attributing this generalizability to object-centric re-grounding. For instance, in the "Open Box" task, RoboReact showed successful executions across various object poses, categories, and scene configurations (Figure 5).

**Q2: Effect of sequential refinement and editor capability.**
The sequential refinement process significantly improved skill quality. Table 2 showed a non-decreasing success rate at every step-wise checkpoint as the refinement budget increased from 0 to 15 rounds. For "Hand Over," terminal success rose from 0/13 at 0 rounds to 11/13 at 15 rounds, and for "Pour Water," it increased from 0/13 to 11/13. The gap between early-step and terminal-step success narrowed with more rounds, indicating that calibration rollouts effectively improved downstream contact, manipulation, and release phases, not just initial reachability.
The capability of the VLM-based policy editor also had a direct impact on refinement efficiency. Table 3 compared two GPT-Codex editors (5.1-mini and 5.6-ultra). The stronger 5.6-ultra editor exploited refinement rollouts more efficiently, leading to higher success rates. After 10 rounds, it achieved an SR of 61.5% and Avg. Len. of 4.15 for "Pour Water," compared to 38.5% and 3.15 for the 5.1-mini. This suggests that improvements in VLM capabilities can directly enhance the skill acquisition process by more effectively converting in-context rollout evidence into structured, bounded edits.

**Q3: Contribution of video-prior quality and refinement context.**
Ablation studies on "Pour Water" (Table 4) revealed the relative importance of different information sources for refinement:
- Removing **VLM-based semantic keyframe selection (w/o KS)** or **rollout memory (w/o Mem.)** degraded the average completed step length (Avg. Len. of 3.69 and 3.92, respectively) more significantly than removing the third-person camera. This indicates that semantic keyframes are crucial for maintaining task progress, and rollout memory is essential for linking current failures to past evidence, supporting long-horizon reasoning.
- Removing the **third-person camera (w/o 3rd.)** had a smaller aggregate effect (Avg. Len. 4.77) but a localized one, specifically cutting pouring-phase success from 12/13 (RoboReact) to 6/13. This suggests that the external view primarily resolves occluded spatial alignment and contact, rather than generally improving perception.
- Table 5 demonstrated that a stronger video generator (Seedance 2.0 vs. Seedance 1.5 Pro) yielded higher post-refinement performance, with Seedance 2.0 achieving an SR of 92.3% for "Pour Water" and 84.6% for "Open Drawer" compared to 84.6% and 69.2% for Seedance 1.5 Pro. This indicates that refinement does not fully negate differences in prior quality; instead, generative video quality and grounded editing play complementary roles.

**Q4: Robustness of frozen object-centric execution.**
The evaluation of the frozen execution stack under various disturbances (Table 6) demonstrated robustness. RoboReact retained 80–94% of its nominal Avg. Len. under all tested perturbations, with terminal completion rates remaining above two-thirds even in the most challenging scenarios.
- **Base-pose perturbation** at the operation position had the smallest effect, showing strong recovery.
- An **object shift before manipulation** had a larger effect but was still well-handled, consistent with the benefit of re-estimating object poses and re-grounding object-relative keyframes.
- **Perturbing squatting** (lowering table height by 0-25cm) was the most damaging, reducing terminal manipulation success from 12/13 (nominal) to 9/13, even though squatting success itself dropped by only one trial. This highlights an error-propagation effect, where an upstream posture deviation can degrade the reachable geometry for subsequent contacts.
These results, obtained without VLM involvement at test time, underscore the robustness of the execution stack, particularly the online object-centric re-grounding mechanism.

In summary, RoboReact demonstrates the feasibility of distilling generalizable whole-body manipulation skills from generated egocentric videos, achieving performance comparable to real human demonstrations. Its iterative VLM-guided refinement, combined with robust object-centric execution, enables significant adaptation to real-world complexities and disturbances, representing a step towards scalable and autonomous humanoid skill acquisition.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The development of RoboReact presents several areas of significance and potential impact within the field of robotics and artificial intelligence:

1. **Scalable Skill Acquisition for Humanoid Robots:** RoboReact offers a method for acquiring complex, whole-body manipulation skills for humanoid robots without relying on extensive teleoperation, human demonstrations, or task-specific hardware data collection. By leveraging video generative models, it proposes a scalable approach to synthesize diverse manipulation experiences, which could significantly reduce the cost and labor associated with training humanoid robots. This could accelerate the deployment of humanoid robots in various human-centric environments.
2. **Generalizability Across Diverse Conditions:** The framework's emphasis on distilling object-centric interaction geometry and employing online object-centric re-grounding contributes to the generalizability of learned skills. This allows robots to adapt to variations in object pose, shape, and scene background, as well as recover from execution disturbances. Such adaptability is crucial for real-world applications where environments are dynamic and unpredictable.
3. **Integration of Generative Models and VLMs with Robot Control:** RoboReact demonstrates a novel integration of recent advancements in video generative models and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with closed-loop robot control. It moves beyond using generative models merely for visual guidance by treating generated videos as actionable priors and employing a VLM as an intelligent agent for iterative, in-context skill refinement. This paradigm suggests a direction for combining the "imagination" capabilities of generative AI with the grounded execution requirements of physical robots.
4. **Agentic Refinement for Robustness and Autonomy:** The VLM-driven trial-and-error refinement loop allows the robot to iteratively diagnose failures and adapt its skills based on calibration rollouts. This "agentic" approach to policy editing, where the VLM proposes structured and bounded adjustments, enhances the robustness of the distilled skills and offers a path toward greater robot autonomy in skill acquisition and adaptation, minimizing the need for continuous human intervention during the learning phase.
5. **Benchmarking and Future Development of Foundation Models:** The empirical findings that RoboReact's performance scales with more capable video generation and VLM models (e.g., Seedance 2.0 and GPT-5.6-ultra) suggest a clear path for continuous improvements as foundation models advance. This establishes a practical benchmark for evaluating and integrating cutting-edge AI models into embodied robotics, potentially driving further research in both areas.
6. **Addressing the Imagination-to-Execution Gap:** By explicitly addressing the challenges of transferring non-metric, imagined behaviors from generated videos to physically executable whole-body motions, RoboReact contributes to bridging a critical gap in robot learning. Its mechanisms for metric reconstruction, motion retargeting, and online re-grounding are key steps in making synthetic data actionable for real-world robotic systems.

Overall, RoboReact offers a comprehensive framework that pushes the boundaries of autonomous skill acquisition for high-DoF humanoid robots, holding implications for more capable, adaptable, and cost-effective robotic systems in diverse applications ranging from industrial settings to service robotics.
