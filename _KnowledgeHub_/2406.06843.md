# HO-Cap: A Capture System and Dataset for 3D Reconstruction and Pose Tracking of Hand-Object Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> HO-Cap is a capture system and dataset for 3D hand-object reconstruction and pose tracking, using eight RGB-D cameras plus a HoloLens headset with a semi-automatic annotation pipeline built on pretrained vision models (no mocap markers, no per-object training). Yields 64 videos, 656K frames, 64 objects, with sub-5-pixel annotation error, usable as robot manipulation demonstrations.

## Problem

- Motion-capture systems for hand-object annotation are expensive, need markers on hands/objects, and introduce calibration artifacts between mocap and image cameras.
- Manual-labeling datasets like DexYCB, OakInk, HOI4D are accurate but labor-intensive given the large number of video frames to annotate.
- Existing automatic pipelines like HO-3D need known 3D object models and cannot handle unseen objects; H2O needs a per-object trained tracker, blocking scale to many objects.

## Method

- ==BundleSDF==-based 3D object reconstruction pipeline that fuses RGB-D sequences with ==SAM2== segmentation masks to build textured meshes for arbitrary rigid objects with no prior 3D model.
- Object pose pipeline combining ==FoundationPose== initialization across eight calibrated views with ==multi-view RANSAC== consistency filtering, then an ==SDF-based optimization== with a smoothness loss term.
- ==MANO==-based hand pose optimization using RANSAC-filtered multi-view triangulated 3D ==MediaPipe== keypoints as anchors, plus a joint hand-object ==SDF optimization== that reduces mesh interpenetration.

## Results

- Sub-5-pixel reprojection error on 800 manually-verified frames: object **3.42px**, left hand **4.58px**, right hand **3.82px** after joint refinement, down from **~3.7-5.3px** initial.
- HaMeR beats A2J-Transformer on hand pose (MPJPE **28.9mm** vs **78.7mm**; PCK@0.1 **79.2** vs **26.8**), yet its 28.9mm error is notably worse than HaMeR's reported numbers on other datasets.
- FoundationPose outperforms MegaPose on novel-object pose (**89.3%** vs **67.1%** ADD, **95.7%** vs **83.0%** ADD-S); RT-DETR trained on the dataset scores **75.9** AP vs CNOS's **25.3** AP zero-shot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Chaining large pretrained vision models (SAM2, MediaPipe, FoundationPose, BundleSDF) with multi-view geometric consistency can replace mocap markers and per-object training for scalable annotation.
> - Occlusion-heavy hand-object interaction remains a stress test for state-of-the-art pose estimators, revealing gaps invisible on cleaner benchmarks.
> - Thin or texture-poor held objects (spatulas, hammers) starve pose optimization of visual signal, an annotation blind spot any similar pipeline should expect and budget for.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The study of hand-object interaction is a cornerstone in various fields, including human-computer interaction (HCI), virtual/augmented reality (VR/AR), and robot learning from human demonstrations. Research in this domain often targets specific sub-problems such as hand detection, hand pose estimation, hand shape reconstruction, hand-object detection, object pose estimation, and object shape reconstruction. The availability of high-quality, richly annotated datasets is crucial for advancing these areas, enabling the training and benchmarking of robust machine learning models.

Existing datasets for hand-object interaction can be broadly categorized by their data acquisition and annotation methods:
- **Motion Capture (Mocap) Systems**: Datasets like FPHA, ContactPose, ARCTIC, and OakInk2 use mocap systems with reflective markers attached to hands and objects. While accurate for pose, these systems are expensive, require meticulous calibration, and introduce visual artifacts dueating to the physical markers, making them less suitable for natural, unconstrained interactions.
- **Manual Labeling**: Large-scale datasets such as DexYCB, OakInk, and HOI4D rely on human annotators to label poses and shapes. While providing high accuracy, this method is extraordinarily time-consuming and labor-intensive, limiting scalability and the diversity of interactions that can be practically covered.
- **Automatic or Semi-automatic Labeling**: Efforts like HO-3D, H2O, SHOWMe, and HANDAL have introduced automated or semi-automated pipelines. However, these often come with limitations: HO-3D is restricted to objects with known 3D models; H2O requires training specific object trackers for each new object, impeding scalability; and fully automated methods can introduce significant annotation errors. Many also lack comprehensive hand annotations or dynamic human-object interactions.

HO-Cap distinguishes itself by addressing the key limitations of these prior approaches. It aims to provide a markerless, scalable, and semi-automatic annotation solution that avoids expensive mocap systems or 3D scanners. By leveraging recent advancements in large pre-trained vision models (e.g., MediaPipe, BundleSDF, FoundationPose, SAM2) and a multi-view camera setup, HO-Cap bypasses the need for domain-specific training for each object or extensive manual labeling. This unique combination allows for the capture and annotation of diverse hand-object interactions, including novel objects whose 3D models were not known beforehand, making it highly valuable for training and evaluating models that need to generalize to new environments and objects. The focus on human demonstrations for tasks like affordance-driven object use, pick-and-place, and handovers also directly caters to the growing demand for data in embodied AI and robot manipulation research, where robots learn complex skills by observing human actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are threefold:

1. **To introduce a novel data capture system** for detailed 3D reconstruction and pose tracking of hands and objects during human interaction. This system is designed to be cost-effective and capable of capturing natural, markerless interactions from multiple perspectives.
2. **To develop a robust semi-automatic annotation method** that can accurately obtain 3D shape and pose annotations for hands and arbitrary objects in collected videos. A central aim here is to significantly reduce the manual annotation effort compared to existing methods, making the process scalable and deployable for a wide variety of objects and interactions without requiring expensive 3D scanners or motion capture systems.
3. **To create and release a new, comprehensive dataset, named HO-Cap**, based on this capture system and annotation method. This dataset is intended to serve as a valuable benchmark and training resource for various research problems related to hand-object interaction, embodied AI, and robot learning from human demonstrations.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several critical gaps in the existing research landscape. Current datasets often suffer from limitations such as:
- **High Cost and Complexity**: Reliance on expensive motion capture systems or 3D scanners, which are not universally accessible.
- **Artificiality of Data**: The use of physical markers on hands and objects can interfere with natural interactions and introduce visual artifacts.
- **Labor-Intensive Annotation**: Manual labeling, while accurate, is prohibitively time-consuming for large-scale video datasets, hindering the creation of diverse and extensive benchmarks.
- **Limited Scalability**: Many automated or semi-automated systems are restricted to objects with known 3D models or require per-object training, making them difficult to scale to a wide variety of novel objects and complex interactions.
- **Insufficient Diversity**: Existing datasets may lack sufficient diversity in interaction types (e.g., bimanual tasks, affordance-driven use), hand shapes, or object types, limiting their utility for training generalizable models.

HO-Cap is motivated by the need for a dataset that overcomes these limitations, offering a scalable, markerless, and accurate solution for capturing and annotating 3D hand-object interactions. By providing such a resource, the authors aim to accelerate progress in fields that demand a nuanced understanding of how humans interact with their physical environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HO-Cap project introduces a sophisticated methodology encompassing a custom data capture setup and an innovative semi-automatic annotation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Data Capture Setup

The hardware setup is designed for comprehensive 3D data capture:
- **Multi-View RGB-D Cameras**: Eight Intel RealSense D455 cameras are strategically positioned around a table to provide full workspace coverage. Additionally, one Microsoft Azure Kinect is placed above the table, primarily utilized for higher-resolution 3D object reconstruction.
- **Calibration**: All RGB-D cameras are meticulously calibrated using Vicalib to determine their intrinsic and extrinsic parameters, ensuring that point clouds from different cameras can be accurately fused into a common 3D world coordinate frame.
- **Ego-centric View**: A Microsoft HoloLens AR headset is worn by participants to capture synchronized ego-centric (first-person) RGB-D videos. The HoloLens's 6D pose is also tracked, allowing its point clouds to be integrated with those from the RealSense cameras.
- **Synchronization**: All cameras are integrated into a ROS (Robot Operating System) server for seamless data acquisition. RGB, depth images, and HoloLens pose data are recorded into a single ROS bag file, with synchronization performed based on timestamps.

#### 3.2. Annotation Method

The core of HO-Cap is its semi-automatic annotation pipeline, designed to derive 3D shapes and poses for hands and objects without requiring expensive 3D scanners or motion capture markers. This method leverages recent advancements in pre-trained vision models and incorporates multi-view consistency and SDF-based optimization.

##### 3.2.1. 3D Object Reconstruction

The annotation process begins by reconstructing 3D models for each unique object used in the interactions.
- **Neural Reconstruction**: Instead of relying on pre-existing CAD models or 3D scanners, BundleSDF, a neural reconstruction method, is employed. A sequence of RGB-D images is captured while an object is manually moved and rotated in front of the Azure Kinect.
- **Segmentation**: SAM2 (Segment Anything Model 2), a unified model for segmenting objects across images and videos, is used to generate segmentation masks. This requires minimal human input: manually selecting two points on the object in the initial frame to prompt SAM2 for mask generation and tracking.
- **Pose and Mesh Generation**: BundleSDF uses feature matching (LoFTR) for coarse pose initialization, followed by an online pose graph optimization to estimate the object's 6D poses. Concurrently, a neural object field learns the object's geometry and appearance. Finally, a textured 3D mesh is extracted from this neural field using marching cubes and color projection. This allows for self-contained 3D model generation for all 64 objects in the dataset.

##### 3.2.2. Object Pose Estimation

With the 3D object models in hand, their 6D poses are estimated in the captured interaction videos:
- **Initialization**: FoundationPose, a unified foundation model for 6D object pose estimation and tracking, provides initial object poses (represented as homogeneous transformations in SE(3)). Segmentation masks from SAM2 are used as input.
- **Multi-view Consistency**: Since FoundationPose operates on single-camera input and can be affected by occlusions, poses are tracked across all eight camera views. A RANSAC-based approach is used to align these multi-view poses into the world frame, identify outliers, and ensure robust tracking consistency, especially when individual views are noisy or suffer tracking failures.
- **SDF-based Optimization**: Initial poses are further refined using a Signed Distance Field (SDF)-based optimization. This minimizes a loss function composed of an SDF term (aligning the fused 3D object point cloud from all cameras to the object's 3D model surface) and a smoothness term (penalizing large jumps in pose between frames). This process ensures high-quality and temporally consistent object pose annotations.

##### 3.2.3. Hand Pose Estimation

Human hands are modeled using the MANO parametric model, which allows for detailed mesh modeling through shape (β) and pose (θ) parameters.
- **MANO Shape Calibration**: Prior to pose optimization, each subject's unique MANO shape parameters are pre-calibrated. Participants align their hands with a rendered neutral MANO mesh in front of a camera, collecting 3D points. Iterative optimization then fits the MANO model's shape and pose to these points.
- **Keypoint-based Optimization**: Direct SDF-based optimization for hands proved challenging due to imprecise hand segmentation by SAM2 and the high dimensionality of MANO pose parameters, which could lead to unrealistic poses. To counter this, a keypoint-based approach is adopted:
 - **2D/3D Keypoint Generation**: MediaPipe is used to detect 2D hand landmarks across multiple views. A RANSAC filtering strategy then triangulates these 2D landmarks into robust 3D keypoints, minimizing projection errors across valid camera views and excluding noisy detections.
 - **Temporal Smoothing**: For frames where MediaPipe detections are poor, linear interpolation is used to fill gaps, followed by cubic spline interpolation to smooth the entire hand motion trajectory, ensuring realistic velocity and acceleration.
 - **Pose Optimization**: The MANO pose parameters (θ) are optimized by minimizing a loss function comprising a 3D keypoint loss (aligning MANO model joints to the estimated 3D keypoints) and a regularization term (L2 norm of pose parameters to prevent overfitting).

##### 3.2.4. Joint Hand-Object Pose Optimization

To enhance realism and prevent inter-mesh intersections, a final joint optimization step refines both hand and object poses simultaneously.
- **Combined SDF Loss**: This process utilizes the SDFs of both objects and hands. A joint loss function is minimized, which includes SDF terms for each object and hand (aligning their segmented point clouds to their respective 3D models), a regularization term for hand poses, and a smoothness term for temporal consistency of both hand and object global transformations.
- **Hand Point Cloud Extraction**: Hand point clouds are generated from depth images using high-quality hand masks from SAM2 (prompted by optimized 3D hand keypoints) and filtered by point-to-mesh distance.
- **Refinement**: This iterative refinement significantly reduces mesh intersections and produces physically plausible and accurate hand-object interaction annotations.

##### 3.2.5. HoloLens Camera Pose Estimation

The raw pose data from the HoloLens often exhibits jitter. To ensure accurate ego-centric views:
- **Object-based Refinement**: The optimized object poses (treated as a single merged entity) are used as a reference. By transforming this combined object pose into the HoloLens camera frame and using FoundationPose with the HoloLens's RGB image, the HoloLens camera pose is refined. This process reduces jitter and improves the alignment of projected objects in the HoloLens view.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive new dataset and validates its quality through detailed statistics and baseline experiments across key computer vision tasks.

#### 4.1. HO-Cap Dataset Statistics and Quality

- **Dataset Size and Scope**: The HO-Cap dataset comprises 64 videos, totaling approximately 656,000 RGB-D frames. These frames are captured from a multi-view setup, including eight calibrated RealSense cameras and a first-person-view camera from a HoloLens headset.
- **Diversity**:
 - **Subjects**: Data was collected from 9 participants, ensuring variability in hand shapes, grasping styles, and interaction patterns.
 - **Objects**: 64 unique objects are included, each with a precisely reconstructed textured 3D mesh model, enabling fine-grained 6D pose annotations.
 - **Interactions**: The dataset features both unimanual and bimanual interactions, covering three distinct task types: affordance-driven object use, simple pick-and-place actions, and handovers between hands. This diversity is particularly valuable for imitation learning and robot manipulation research.
- **Annotation Quality**: The accuracy of the semi-automatic annotation method was rigorously evaluated.
 - **Evaluation Method**: 800 images were randomly selected across 8 RealSense camera views. Manual annotations of visible hand joints and easily identifiable object keypoints were collected.
 - **Results**: The Euclidean distance between the 2D projections of the 3D annotations and these human-labeled 2D points served as the metric. The final annotation error was within 5 pixels for both hands and objects (Object: 3.42±1.73 pixels, Left hand: 4.58±2.72 pixels, Right hand: 3.82±2.29 pixels). Crucially, the error consistently decreased through successive refinement steps (initial, SDF-refined, jointly-refined), validating the effectiveness of the proposed optimization strategies.

#### 4.2. Baseline Experimental Results

The HO-Cap dataset was used to benchmark performance on three critical tasks:

##### 4.2.1. Hand Pose Estimation

- **Models Evaluated**: A2J-Transformer and HaMeR, both trained on external datasets.
- **Metrics**: PCK (Percentage of Correct Keypoints) for 2D hand pose and MPJPE (Mean Per Joint Position Error) in millimeters for 3D hand pose.
- **Findings**: HaMeR significantly outperformed A2J-Transformer, achieving an MPJPE of 28.9mm (versus 78.7mm for A2J-Transformer) and a PCK@0.2 of 91.4% (versus 50.5%). The paper attributes HaMeR's superior performance to its Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone and large-scale training data. The relatively high MPJPE even for HaMeR compared to other datasets suggests that HO-Cap presents unique challenges, particularly due to prevalent occlusions between hands and objects during complex interactions.

##### 4.2.2. Object Detection

- **Scenarios**: Evaluated for both novel object detection (objects not seen during training) and seen object detection (models trained on HO-Cap).
- **Novel Object Detection Models**: CNOS (CAD-based) and GroundingDINO (vision-language model).
- **Seen Object Detection Models**: YOLO11 and RT-DETR (trained on HO-Cap's train/val splits).
- **Metrics**: MSCOCO Average Precision (AP).
- **Findings**:
 - **Novel Objects**: CNOS (AP 25.3) and GroundingDINO (AP 17.0) showed limited performance, generating many false positives, highlighting the inherent difficulty of generalizing to unseen objects without prior training.
 - **Seen Objects**: YOLO11 (AP 71.4) and RT-DETR (AP 75.9) demonstrated strong performance, especially on medium and large objects. RT-DETR slightly outperformed YOLO11, confirming the dataset's utility for training robust object detectors.

##### 4.2.3. Novel Object Pose Estimation

- **Models Evaluated**: MegaPose and FoundationPose, designed to estimate poses for unseen objects using only 3D models. Ground truth 2D bounding boxes were provided.
- **Metrics**: AUC (Area Under Curve) for ADD (Average Distance) and ADD-S (Symmetric Average Distance) metrics.
- **Findings**: FoundationPose (89.3% ADD AUC, 95.7% ADD-S AUC) significantly outperformed MegaPose (67.1% ADD AUC, 83.0% ADD-S AUC). This indicates FoundationPose's robust capabilities in generalizing to novel objects, likely due to its transformer-based architecture and extensive synthetic training, making it a powerful tool for unseen object scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Contributions

The HO-Cap project makes several significant contributions to the fields of computer vision, robotics, and artificial intelligence:

- **Novel Capture System**: The introduction of a scalable, markerless data capture system leveraging multiple RGB-D cameras and an ego-centric HoloLens headset. This system provides a practical alternative to expensive 3D scanners or traditional motion capture setups.
- **Semi-Automatic Annotation Pipeline**: A robust and efficient semi-automatic annotation method that integrates cutting-edge pre-trained vision models (SAM2, BundleSDF, FoundationPose, MediaPipe) with multi-view consistency and SDF-based optimization. This pipeline significantly reduces manual labeling effort and eliminates the need for domain-specific training for object trackers, enhancing scalability to novel objects.
- **Comprehensive Dataset (HO-Cap)**: The creation of a new, large-scale dataset featuring diverse human-object interactions. HO-Cap includes 64 unique objects with reconstructed textured 3D meshes, 656K RGB-D frames from multiple views, and covers various interaction types (affordance-driven use, pick-and-place, handovers), including bimanual interactions. This broad diversity is crucial for generalizing models to real-world scenarios.
- **Benchmark with Baselines**: Provision of a valuable benchmark with baseline experimental results for key perception tasks, including hand pose estimation, object detection (novel and seen objects), and novel object pose estimation. These baselines highlight the dataset's unique challenges and provide a starting point for future research.

#### 5.2. Broader Impact

The HO-Cap dataset and its associated capture and annotation framework have the potential for substantial impact across various domains:

- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) and AR/VR**: By providing high-quality 3D data of natural hand-object interactions, HO-Cap can drive advancements in intuitive and realistic AR/VR experiences, enabling better hand tracking, gesture recognition, and interaction modeling.
- **Embodied AI and Robot Learning**: The detailed 3D shape and pose annotations, especially for human demonstrations of tasks, are invaluable for training embodied AI agents and robots to learn complex manipulation skills. Robots can leverage this data to understand object affordances, replicate dexterous grasps, and perform human-like tasks.
- **Computer Vision Research**: The dataset serves as a rich resource for developing and evaluating new algorithms for 3D hand and object reconstruction, pose tracking, segmentation, and understanding complex human-object relationships, particularly in challenging scenarios involving occlusions and dynamic movements.
- **Benchmarking Large Models**: HO-Cap's diversity and detail make it suitable for evaluating the zero-shot capabilities and generalization performance of large pre-trained models in the context of hand-object understanding.
- **Community Resource**: Making the capture setup and annotation framework available to the community encourages further research and dataset expansion, fostering collaborative progress in the field.

#### 5.3. Limitations

The authors acknowledge several limitations of the current annotation framework, which represent opportunities for future research:

- **Object Reconstruction Challenges**: BundleSDF, while powerful, struggles with certain object types, particularly textureless or metallic objects. This limitation meant such objects could not be included in the HO-Cap dataset.
- **Hand Detection Reliance**: The hand pose estimation pipeline relies heavily on MediaPipe's hand joint detections. Frequent failures or inaccuracies in MediaPipe's output, especially during occlusions, can prevent reliable hand pose estimation, leading to the exclusion of certain video sequences.
- **Occlusion of Small/Cylindrical Objects**: Accurately estimating the 6D pose of small or cylindrical objects (e.g., spatulas, hammers) when significantly held within the hand is challenging. Minimal visible color, depth, or geometric information, combined with inconsistencies across views, hinders reliable pose tracking, leading to the exclusion of these interactions from the dataset.

Overcoming these limitations, such as developing more robust neural reconstruction techniques for difficult materials, improving hand detection under severe occlusion, or integrating stronger physical priors for object pose in highly occluded scenarios, would further enhance the utility and applicability of such systems in the future.
