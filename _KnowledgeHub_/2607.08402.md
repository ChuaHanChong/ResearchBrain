# Swapping Faces, Saving Features: A Dual-Purpose Pipeline for Pedestrian Privacy in ITS

> [!summary] Summary
> A dual-purpose pipeline is introduced to protect pedestrian privacy in Intelligent Transportation Systems by concealing identities while preserving crucial facial attributes for autonomous vehicle training. This approach, evaluated on diverse urban datasets including images of veiled women, successfully achieves effective identity concealment and retains essential features like gaze direction and expressions in challenging low-resolution environments.

## Problem

- The inherent conflict between the need for large, diverse datasets to train autonomous vehicle AI and the imperative to protect pedestrian privacy.
- Existing privacy preservation methods often degrade data usability by obscuring critical facial attributes necessary for AI tasks like pedestrian intention prediction.
- Raw footage of pedestrians presents significant privacy risks, including identity theft, personal information extraction, and surveillance tracking.

## Method

- A five-stage pipeline is developed that includes pedestrian detection (==YOLOV11==), face detection (==SCRFD==), quality enhancement (==Codeformer==), face swapping, and seamless blending (OpenCV's ==SeamlessClone==).
- Two prominent face-swapping models, **Roop** and **Ghost-v2**, are integrated and compared within the pipeline to conceal identity while preserving target facial attributes (expressions, head pose, gaze).
- The pipeline is designed to handle challenging 'in-wild' urban images, including unique cases like veiled women in datasets such as Egy-DRiVeS.

## Results

- Roop achieved better preservation of facial expressions (**1.898** blendshape difference) and structural features (**0.00596** landmark difference) compared to Ghost-v2 on high-quality facial images.
- Ghost-v2 showed slightly higher identity concealment (**0.1393** facial similarity) but struggled with realism, swapping fidelity, and attribute preservation, particularly for veiled women.
- The complete pipeline, specifically utilizing the Roop model and Codeformer, successfully automated the de-identification of multiple pedestrians in raw street images while preserving critical 'looking/not looking' gaze features for downstream AI tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining robust pre-processing (detection, quality enhancement) with face-swapping techniques effectively balances pedestrian privacy with data usability in low-resolution, diverse urban environments.
> - The choice of face-swapping model significantly impacts performance; Roop demonstrated superior realism and attribute preservation, especially for challenging cases like veiled women, compared to Ghost-v2.
> - The Codeformer quality enhancer is crucial for improving the efficacy of face swapping on low-quality, 'in-wild' pedestrian images often found in ITS datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS), particularly autonomous vehicles (AVs), necessitates large-scale and diverse datasets for training artificial intelligence (AI) models. These models are crucial for real-time decision-making, including pedestrian intention and trajectory prediction, which rely heavily on visual cues such as facial expressions, head pose, and body movements. However, the collection and unrestricted access to real-world pedestrian footage introduce significant privacy risks, including identity theft, personal information extraction, surveillance tracking, and deepfake generation.

The broader research landscape has seen efforts to address privacy concerns through various methods, but often with trade-offs. Simple obfuscation techniques like blurring or pixelation, while preserving privacy, degrade image usability by rendering critical facial attributes unrecognizable for AI training. Reverse transformation methods have been shown to be insecure. More sophisticated approaches include the PRO-Face framework, which aims for human unidentifiability but machine identifiability, yet still obscures visual cues needed for pedestrian intention prediction. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), utilized by models like DeepPrivacy, CIAGAN, and DeepPrivacy2, generate synthetic faces to anonymize identities. While these achieve high privacy standards, they generally exhibit limitations in preserving crucial facial attributes, making them unsuitable for AV training datasets that rely on subtle facial cues.

Face swapping emerges as a promising technique within this context. It aims to conceal identity by transferring identity features from a source face to a target while retaining the target's original facial features (expressions, pose, gaze). This approach is distinct from generating entirely new anonymized faces as it maintains the natural distribution and structure of the target face within the frame. Related work includes the 3PFS framework, which developed a pipeline incorporating a fine-tuned Ghost face-swapper for simultaneous attribute preservation and privacy protection. Ghost-v2 is a subsequent version of this model, designed for "in-wild" images. Roop, an informally published face-swapping model, is also noted for its high-fidelity and realistic results through a sequence of pretrained models.

Despite these advancements, a gap exists in methods that explicitly address pedestrian privacy within the specific context of AV datasets, particularly those involving low-resolution "in-wild" images, while ensuring the preservation of essential facial attributes. Furthermore, urban datasets from specific regions, such as the Egyptian Egy-DRiVeS dataset, present unique challenges due to diverse pedestrian appearances, including veiled women. This work positions itself to bridge this gap by developing a comprehensive pipeline that leverages face-swapping techniques to strike a balance between privacy and data usability, with a particular focus on the unique characteristics of specific urban environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent conflict between the need for extensive, diverse, and realistic pedestrian datasets to train AI models for autonomous vehicles, and the imperative to protect the privacy of individuals captured in such data. Autonomous vehicles rely on accurate pedestrian intention and trajectory prediction, which necessitates the analysis of subtle facial cues (expressions, gaze) and body movements. However, raw footage contains identifiable biometric data, leading to significant privacy risks such as identity theft and tracking.

Existing privacy preservation methods often fail to adequately address this dual requirement. Techniques like blurring or pixelation effectively obscure identity but render the data unusable for downstream AI tasks that depend on facial features. Conversely, methods that prioritize usability may not offer sufficient privacy protection. Therefore, a significant challenge is to implement privacy preservation procedures that effectively conceal pedestrian identities without degrading the essential image attributes required for training intelligent transportation systems.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this work are three-fold:
1. **Implement a five-stage pipeline:** The research aims to develop and implement a comprehensive pipeline designed to protect pedestrians' privacy while concurrently maintaining the usability of the data for AV applications. This pipeline is specifically tailored to handle unique cases prevalent in urban Egyptian images, such as various pedestrian appearances, including veiled women, as found in datasets like Egy-DRiVeS.
2. **Utilize and compare face-swapping models:** The study intends to integrate and evaluate two prominent face-swapping models, Roop and Ghost-v2, within the pipeline. The purpose is to assess their effectiveness in de-identifying pedestrians within the ITS context.
3. **Evaluate model performance:** A crucial objective is to quantitatively evaluate the chosen face-swapping models. This evaluation focuses on their performance in terms of both privacy preservation (identity concealment) and usability maintenance (preservation of critical facial attributes like expressions, gaze direction, and head pose), thereby determining which model offers a better balance for the specified application.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a five-stage pipeline designed to protect pedestrian privacy while preserving critical facial attributes for data usability in Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS). The pipeline processes single frames, with source faces for swapping being fixed and randomly chosen from publicly shareable images, ensuring irreversibility and non-identifiability of the source identity from the output. The facial attributes preserved during the transfer include expressions, head pose, and eye gaze direction.

**A. Pipeline Overview**
The pipeline comprises the following sequential stages:
1. **Pedestrian Detection:** Identifies and classifies pedestrians within an input frame.
2. **Face Detection:** Locates faces belonging to the detected pedestrians.
3. **Quality Enhancement:** Improves the quality of the detected faces, particularly for low-resolution images.
4. **Face Swapping:** Transfers facial identity from a source face to the detected target face while preserving target attributes.
5. **Blending:** Seamlessly integrates the swapped face back into the original image.

**B. Stage 1: Pedestrian Detection**
The YOLOV11 pre-trained model is utilized for pedestrian detection. This model, part of the YOLO family, is selected for its computational speed, accuracy, and precision in detecting various objects. Detected pedestrian objects are encapsulated in bounding boxes, cropped, and passed to the subsequent stage.

**C. Stage 2: Face Detection**
The SCRFD pre-trained model is employed for face detection. This model is optimized for efficiency and enhanced detection across various face sizes, poses, and occlusions, making it suitable for challenging AV datasets. Faces are detected within the cropped pedestrian images, bounded, and then cropped for the next stage.

**D. Stage 3: Quality Enhancement**
To address issues arising from low-resolution images common in AV datasets, a quality enhancement stage is introduced using Codeformer. This transformer-based model specializes in blind face restoration. It employs a codebook to store facial features, guiding the restoration process to select features closest to the original face, thus preventing the generation of new features that could compromise attribute preservation.

**E. Stage 4: Face Swapping**
This stage is central to the pipeline, aiming to conceal the target's identity, preserve the target's facial expressions, eye gaze direction, and head pose, and produce realistic output. Two face-swapping models are evaluated:

- **Ghost-v2:** This model extends face swapping to head swapping, incorporating details like head shape and hair for improved attribute preservation, and is designed for "in-wild" scenarios. Its architecture includes:
 - **Aligner:** Performs cross-reenactment by applying the target's pose and expressions to a source identity. It uses encoders to generate embeddings for pose, expressions, and source identity, which are fed into a Conditional GAN (CGAN) to produce the source identity with target attributes.
 - **Blender:** Utilizes color referencing, inpainting techniques, and binary masking to create the final output, ensuring color and lighting alignment and reconstructing missing parts.

- **Roop:** While developed for entertainment, Roop is considered for its high-fidelity results. Its internal stages include:
 - **Face detection:** Uses the SCRFD model to detect faces.
 - **Face analysis:** For multiple faces, it uses facial embeddings to select the closest target face to the source (not applicable in this pipeline's single-face input).
 - **Face swapping:** Employs the pre-trained Inswapper 128.onnx model, which blends the swapped face back into the target image.
 - **Face enhancement:** Integrates the GFPGAN model to improve overall image quality.

**F. Stage 5: Blending**
The final stage uses the `SeamlessClone` function from the OpenCV library, which implements the Poisson image editing technique. This function seamlessly blends the de-identified face back into the original image at the target coordinates by adjusting pixel values at boundaries to ensure smooth transitions in gradients, colors, and lighting.

**G. Evaluation**
The evaluation focuses primarily on the face-swapping models' performance, as they are the core functional component addressing the research objectives. Four quantitative metrics are used:

1. **Landmarks Difference:** Measures the average spatial distance between 478 facial landmarks (representing structure, geometry, pose) in the original and swapped faces. A lower value indicates better preservation of these attributes.
2. **Blendshape Difference:** Calculates the sum of absolute differences between 52 facial expression coefficients (e.g., brow positions) derived from landmarks. A lower value signifies better preservation of facial expressions.
3. **Facial Similarity (Identity):** Computes the cosine similarity between the facial embeddings of the original and swapped faces. A lower value indicates greater dissimilarity, implying more effective identity concealment.
4. **Gaze Vector Similarity:** Uses Gaze-LLE to estimate gaze direction vectors, then calculates the cosine similarity between the original and swapped vectors. A higher value indicates better preservation of the target's gaze direction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the dual-purpose pipeline involved several sets of experiments, primarily focusing on the performance of the integrated face-swapping models, Roop and Ghost-v2, and the contribution of the pre-processing stages.

**A. Set 1: Face Swapping on High-Quality Facial Images**
As a proof of concept, both Roop and Ghost-v2 were initially assessed on high-quality close-up facial images.
- **Visual Observations:** Both models produced realistic outputs that concealed the target's identity without noticeable artifacts like ghosting or color mismatches. They both visually preserved pose and eye gaze direction. A key distinction was that Roop maintained the target's head shape, facial structure, and hair (including eyebrows and beards), whereas Ghost-v2, designed for head swapping, transferred these attributes from the source.
- **Quantitative Evaluation (Table II):**
 - **Blendshape Difference:** Roop exhibited a lower blendshape difference (1.898) compared to Ghost-v2 (2.0478), indicating better preservation of facial expressions.
 - **Landmark Difference:** Roop also showed a lower landmark difference (0.00596) than Ghost-v2 (0.00710), suggesting superior preservation of facial structure, geometry, and pose.
 - **Facial Similarity (Identity):** Ghost-v2 achieved a lower cosine similarity (0.1393) than Roop (0.1997), implying that Ghost-v2 was more effective at rendering the output face dissimilar from the original, thus better concealing identity.
 - **Gaze Vectors Cosine Similarity:** Both models demonstrated high gaze cosine similarity values (Roop: 0.9368, Ghost-v2: 0.9385), indicating excellent preservation of eye gaze direction.

**B. Set 2: Vitality of Pedestrian and Face Detection Models on Raw Street Images**
This set demonstrated the necessity of the initial detection stages.
- **Challenges without Detection:** Ghost-v2 frequently failed to detect distant faces in raw street images, preventing the swap. Roop, despite having an internal face detector, also failed in extreme scenarios such as very distant or highly distorted faces.
- **Effectiveness of Detection Modules:** YOLOv11 and SCRFD models successfully detected multiple pedestrians and their faces, even distant ones (Figure 3), providing cropped facial images for subsequent processing.
- **Swapping on Raw Cropped Faces (Table III):** Despite successful detection, direct face swapping on raw, low-quality cropped faces from street images often yielded unsatisfactory results.
 - Ghost-v2 struggled significantly with occluded faces and produced unrealistic, "ghostly" outputs for veiled women, reportedly due to its head-swapping nature, which attempted to swap the veil with source hair.
 - Roop proved more reliable in attempting swaps even in challenging cases but often produced low-quality or unusable results without further enhancement.

**C. Set 3: Face Swapping on Quality-Enhanced Images**
The Codeformer quality enhancer was applied to the cropped images prior to face swapping.
- **Improved Performance:** Both Ghost-v2 and Roop showed significant improvements in performance after quality enhancement, producing clearer and higher-quality swapped faces (Table IV).
- **Challenges for Ghost-v2:** Ghost-v2 continued to exhibit issues with realism, swapping fidelity, and attribute preservation, particularly for images of veiled women. Its outputs were described as visually unappealing, and facial expressions were not consistently maintained.
- **Roop's Superiority:** Roop consistently outperformed Ghost-v2 in terms of output image realism, attribute preservation, and robustness when handling more challenging cases after quality enhancement.

**D. Final Image Output from the Implemented Pipeline**
The complete five-stage pipeline, utilizing the Roop model due to its demonstrated reliability, was applied to a sample Egyptian street image with multiple pedestrians.
- **Automated Anonymization:** The pipeline successfully detected and concealed the identity of all four pedestrians in the image, even those at varying distances from the camera (Figure 4). The process was fully automated, from input image to anonymized output.

**E. Extra Phase: Usability Testing on Subsequent Task**
To validate data usability, the pipeline's impact on a critical feature for pedestrian intention prediction – "looking/not looking" (based on eye gaze direction) – was assessed.
- **Attribute Preservation:** A pre-trained feature extractor was used to identify looking (green) and not-looking (red) pedestrians before and after pipeline application. The results (Figure 5) demonstrated that the "looking/not looking" feature was maintained for all clearly visible pedestrians, confirming that the pipeline preserved facial attributes essential for downstream AI tasks while ensuring pedestrian privacy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a critical and emerging challenge in the field of Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) and autonomous vehicles (AVs): the ethical imperative to protect pedestrian privacy while maintaining the usability of visual data essential for AI model training. The development and evaluation of a dual-purpose pipeline, particularly tailored for diverse urban datasets, hold several significant implications and potential impacts.

**A. Balancing Privacy and Usability:**
The core significance of this work lies in its successful attempt to strike a balance between preserving pedestrian privacy through identity concealment and retaining the crucial facial attributes required for downstream AI tasks like pedestrian intention and trajectory prediction. Prior methods often prioritized one over the other, leading to either privacy breaches or unusable data. This pipeline demonstrates a viable approach to achieving both simultaneously.

**B. Addressing Real-World Data Challenges:**
The pipeline is designed to handle "in-wild" urban images, which are characterized by low resolution, varying poses, occlusions, and diverse appearances. The integration of robust pedestrian and face detection models (YOLOv11, SCRFD) and a specialized quality enhancer (Codeformer) addresses the practical limitations of raw street footage, significantly improving the efficacy of subsequent face-swapping operations. This makes the solution applicable to real-world scenarios, which is crucial for AV development.

**C. Tailored for Diverse Urban Environments:**
The specific consideration for datasets like Egy-DRiVeS, which include unique pedestrian appearances such as veiled women, highlights the pipeline's adaptability and ethical consideration for culturally diverse contexts. By identifying Roop as a more robust solution that handles such cases better than Ghost-v2, the research contributes to making privacy-preserving technologies more inclusive and effective across different populations.

**D. Validation of Attribute Preservation:**
The "looking/not looking" feature usability test provides concrete evidence that critical facial attributes, specifically eye gaze direction, remain intact after privacy preservation. This is paramount for the ethical development of AVs, as it ensures that AI models can still learn and predict pedestrian behavior accurately without relying on identifiable information.

**E. Potential Impact on Autonomous Vehicle Development:**
- **Ethical Data Sourcing:** Enables AV developers to collect and use diverse real-world pedestrian datasets in an ethical manner, complying with privacy regulations (e.g., GDPR, CCPA) without sacrificing data quality.
- **Improved Model Training:** Provides high-quality, privacy-preserved data that retains essential behavioral cues, leading to more robust and accurate AI models for pedestrian prediction and decision-making in AVs.
- **Accelerated Deployment:** By mitigating privacy concerns, this work can help accelerate the development and public acceptance of autonomous driving technologies.
- **Standard for Privacy-Preserving Datasets:** The proposed pipeline and evaluation methodology could serve as a model for creating future privacy-preserving datasets for ITS and other computer vision applications involving human subjects.

**F. Future Directions:**
The identified future recommendations, such as applying the pipeline to more diverse samples, optimizing for video processing, fine-tuning the quality enhancer for various image sizes, and developing a source face selection algorithm (potentially using generative models), indicate ongoing research and the potential for continuous improvement in this domain. This suggests the work is a foundational step towards comprehensive and practical privacy solutions in ITS.
