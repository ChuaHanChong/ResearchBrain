# FasterViT: Fast Vision Transformers with Hierarchical Attention

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NVIDIA developed FasterViT, a new family of vision models that combine convolutional layers and a novel Hierarchical Attention mechanism to significantly improve the accuracy-throughput trade-off. These models achieve a new state-of-the-art Pareto front in image classification and demonstrate superior performance-throughput balance across object detection and semantic segmentation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViTs) suffer from high computational and memory complexity due to the quadratic scaling of self-attention with image resolution, limiting their inference speed (throughput) for high-resolution images.
- Pure ViTs lack inherent inductive biases like locality and translation equivariance, requiring vast amounts of data for effective training compared to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs).
- Existing efficient ViTs or hybrid models often struggle to achieve an optimal balance between high accuracy and high image throughput, especially for dense prediction tasks and high-resolution inputs.

## Method

- Developed FasterViT, a family of neural networks with a ==multi-stage, hybrid CNN-ViT architecture== that leverages efficient convolutional layers in early stages and Transformer blocks in later stages.
- Introduced ==Hierarchical Attention (HAT)==, a novel attention mechanism where =='carrier tokens'== summarize local windows, enabling efficient global information exchange through global carrier attention and local window-carrier attention.
- Optimized the architecture for hardware efficiency by minimizing memory-bound operations in early stages and strategically placing more expressive HAT-based Transformer operations in later, compute-bound stages.

## Results

- FasterViT models establish a new state-of-the-art Pareto front for ImageNet-1K classification (e.g., FasterViT-2 achieves **84.2%** Top-1 accuracy at **3161 img/sec** vs. ConvNeXt-S at **83.1%** Top-1 and 2008 img/sec).
- On MS COCO object detection, FasterViT backbones lead to better accuracy-throughput trade-offs, outperforming ConvNeXt-B and Swin-B in both box AP and mask AP while achieving **15-30%** higher throughput.
- For ADE20K semantic segmentation, FasterViT-4 with UPerNet achieves **49.1 mIoU**, surpassing comparable Swin and ConvNeXt models while maintaining higher inference speeds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid architecture combining efficient CNNs in early, high-resolution stages and Transformer blocks in later, lower-resolution stages effectively balances inductive bias, global context modeling, and hardware efficiency.
> - The Hierarchical Attention (HAT) mechanism, using learnable 'carrier tokens' for cross-window communication, provides an efficient way to capture long-range dependencies without the quadratic complexity of global self-attention.
> - Hardware-aware design, preferring dense convolutions in memory-bound early layers and expressive Transformer operations in compute-bound later layers, is crucial for optimizing throughput on modern GPUs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has witnessed a paradigm shift with the rise of Vision Transformers (ViTs), initially introduced by Dosovitskiy et al. (2020). ViTs, leveraging the self-attention mechanism, excel at capturing global dependencies within images and have demonstrated superior performance in various tasks like image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation, often outperforming traditional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) when trained on large datasets. Their isotropic architecture and ability to scale with data and model size are significant advantages.

However, pure ViT models face two primary challenges:
1. **High Computational Complexity**: The self-attention mechanism has a quadratic computational complexity with respect to the input sequence length (or image resolution), making it computationally expensive and memory-intensive for high-resolution images. This significantly impacts inference speed (throughput).
2. **Lack of Inductive Bias**: Unlike CNNs, which inherently possess inductive biases like locality and translation equivariance, pure ViTs require vast amounts of data to learn these fundamental image properties, sometimes leading to performance deficits on smaller datasets or requiring extensive pre-training.

To address these limitations, several research directions have emerged:
- **Efficient Attention Mechanisms**: Efforts like LongFormer and BigBird in NLP, and their vision counterparts, aim to reduce the quadratic complexity through sparse or windowed attention (e.g., Swin Transformer, CSwin) or by introducing global tokens (e.g., Twins, EdgeViT, Focal Transformer) that summarize context.
- **Hybrid Architectures**: Combining the best of both worlds, these models integrate CNN components (for efficient local feature extraction and inductive bias) with Transformer blocks (for global modeling). Examples include LeViT, CoaT, CrossViT, and EfficientFormer.
- **Scaling and Optimization**: Developing larger, more capable models (e.g., SwinV2) while also focusing on techniques like knowledge distillation, network compression, and dynamic inference to improve efficiency.

FasterViT enters this landscape by proposing a novel hybrid CNN-ViT architecture and a new efficient attention mechanism, Hierarchical Attention (HAT). It aims to push the "Pareto-front in terms of accuracy and image throughput," directly competing with established efficient models like Swin, ConvNeXt, EfficientFormer, and MaxViT. The core contribution is to efficiently combine the local learning strengths of CNNs with the global modeling capabilities of Transformers, particularly for high-resolution inputs, an area where efficiency becomes paramount.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to design a new family of neural networks, **FasterViT**, that achieves **high image throughput** while maintaining **state-of-the-art accuracy** across diverse computer vision applications. This involves striking an optimal balance between performance and computational efficiency, particularly crucial for real-world deployments where inference speed is a bottleneck.

The motivations driving this objective are rooted in the shortcomings of existing vision models:
- **Inefficiency of Global Self-Attention**: The quadratic complexity of standard self-attention (Op(H^4 * d)) makes it prohibitively expensive for high-resolution images, limiting the practical utility of many powerful ViTs. Even window-based approaches (like Swin) still struggle with capturing long-range dependencies efficiently across windows and can impact throughput in early stages with larger feature maps.
- **Balancing Inductive Bias and Global Context**: Pure CNNs offer strong inductive biases (locality) and are efficient in early layers, but lack global modeling capabilities. Pure ViTs excel at global context but demand extensive data and suffer from the aforementioned efficiency issues. There is a need for architectures that gracefully combine these strengths without incurring significant overhead.
- **Hardware-Aware Design**: A deep understanding of modern GPU architectures (e.g., NVIDIA A100) reveals that early network layers (high spatial resolution, few channels) are often memory-bound, while later layers (lower resolution, many channels) are compute-bound. This insight motivates a hybrid design where efficient CNN operations are preferred in memory-bound stages and more expressive (but potentially more compute-intensive) Transformer operations are reserved for compute-bound stages.
- **Scalability for High-Resolution and Large Models**: The increasing demand for processing high-resolution imagery in various applications (e.g., medical imaging, autonomous driving) necessitates models that scale effectively in both accuracy and speed.

To address these, FasterViT proposes the **Hierarchical Attention (HAT)** mechanism, designed to enable efficient cross-window communication and long-range dependency modeling at a reduced computational cost, thus directly tackling the core efficiency challenge in ViTs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FasterViT's methodology is built upon two core tenets: a **hybrid CNN-ViT architecture** and a novel **Hierarchical Attention (HAT)** module.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture Design Principles

The FasterViT architecture (Figure 3) adopts a multi-stage, hierarchical design, influenced by the insights of modern hardware efficiency:
- **Hybrid Structure**: It combines convolutional layers in the initial stages (Stage 1 and 2) and Transformer-based blocks in later stages (Stage 3 and 4).
 - **Early Stages (CNN-based)**: In the higher-resolution stages (Stage 1 and 2, e.g., 112x112 and 56x56 resolution), strided convolutional layers and residual convolutional blocks are used. This is based on the principle that these initial layers are often memory-bound on GPUs (large spatial dimensions, fewer channels). Dense convolutions are preferred over depth-wise or sparse operations, and memory-bound operations like non-linearity, pooling, and batch normalization are minimized to maximize throughput.
 - **Later Stages (Transformer-based)**: In the lower-resolution stages (Stage 3 and 4, e.g., 14x14 and 7x7 resolution), where operations tend to be compute-bound (smaller spatial dimensions, more channels), Transformer blocks incorporating the novel Hierarchical Attention are employed. This allows for more expressive operations without becoming a bottleneck.
- **Stem and Downsampling**: The network starts with a convolutional stem for initial patch embedding and uses 2D Layer Normalization followed by a strided convolutional layer for downsampling between stages, reducing spatial resolution by half and doubling feature maps.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Attention (HAT) Module

HAT is the central innovation for efficient global modeling (Figure 4). It addresses the limitations of purely local windowed attention (like Swin) by introducing "carrier tokens" (CTs):
- **Carrier Token Initialization**: For each local window (e.g., 7x7), a small number of carrier tokens (CTs, e.g., 2x2 or 4 tokens) are initialized via convolution and average pooling from the window's features. These CTs serve as a learnable summary of their respective local windows. This initialization happens once per resolution stage.
- **Multi-level Attention Mechanism**:
 1. **Global Carrier Attention**: All carrier tokens from *all* local windows undergo a full self-attention (MHSA) step. This enables efficient cross-window communication, allowing information to propagate globally across the entire feature map through these summarized tokens.
 2. **Local Window-Carrier Attention**: Each local window's tokens are concatenated *only* with their own dedicated carrier tokens. A second MHSA step is performed on these combined tokens. This facilitates efficient local and dedicated global information exchange within each window's context, without each local token needing to attend to all other local tokens or all global carrier tokens.
- **Information Propagation**: The updated local tokens and carrier tokens are split back. The carrier tokens are upsampled and merged with the local tokens at the end of the stage to compute the final output, ensuring global information is effectively incorporated into the feature map.
- **Positional Encoding**: To maintain spatial awareness, absolute positional biases are added to both CTs and local tokens, and log-space relative positional biases are applied, inspired by SwinV2. This ensures position-dependent features and flexibility with image size.

#### 3.3. Complexity Analysis of HAT

The paper provides a detailed complexity analysis, highlighting HAT's efficiency compared to full self-attention and other multi-level attention mechanisms:
- **Full Attention**: O(H^4 * d)
- **Windowed Attention (Swin)**: O(k^2 * H^2 * d) – efficient but lacks global context.
- **Hierarchical Attention (HAT)**: O(k^2 * H^2 * d + L * H^2 * d + (H^4 / k^4) * L^2 * d). The dominant term is still the local windowed attention (k^2 * H^2 * d). The term for carrier token attention ((H^4 / k^4) * L^2 * d) scales much better than full attention because (H/k)^2 (number of windows) and L (number of carrier tokens per window) are much smaller than H^2 (full resolution tokens). This makes HAT efficient for high resolutions as L << k.
- **Comparison to Twins**: HAT is shown to be more efficient, especially at higher resolutions, due to its specialized carrier token mechanism versus Twins' global feature map subsampling.

This careful design allows FasterViT to effectively capture both short-range and long-range dependencies while maintaining high computational efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FasterViT demonstrates significant advancements in the accuracy-throughput trade-off across various computer vision tasks.

#### 4.1. Image Classification (ImageNet-1K/21K)

- **SOTA Pareto Front**: As illustrated in Figure 1, FasterViT models (FasterViT-0 to FasterViT-6) achieve a new state-of-the-art Pareto front in terms of ImageNet-1K Top-1 accuracy and image throughput (measured on A100 GPU with batch size 128). This means for a given accuracy level, FasterViT achieves higher throughput, or for a given throughput, it achieves higher accuracy.
- **Superior to Conv-based Models**: FasterViT variants consistently outperform ConvNeXt models in throughput while achieving higher accuracy. For example, FasterViT-2 (84.2% Top-1, 3161 img/sec) significantly beats ConvNeXt-S (83.1% Top-1, 2008 img/sec) and ConvNeXt-T (82.0% Top-1, 3196 img/sec).
- **Superior to Transformer-based Models**: FasterViT models are notably faster than Swin Transformer and SwinV2 families at comparable or better accuracy. For instance, FasterViT-3 (84.9% Top-1, 1780 img/sec) surpasses Swin-B (83.5% Top-1, 1232 img/sec) in both metrics.
- **Superior to Hybrid Models**: Compared to other hybrid architectures like EfficientFormer and MaxViT, FasterViT generally offers a better balance of performance and throughput.
- **Scalability (ImageNet-21K Pre-training)**: Pre-training FasterViT-4 on ImageNet-21K and fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K (Table 3) further demonstrates its scalability, achieving SOTA results (e.g., 87.5% at 384x384 resolution) while maintaining a better throughput than Swin-L and ConvNeXt-L.

#### 4.2. Dense Prediction Tasks (Object Detection & Semantic Segmentation)

- **Object Detection (MS COCO)**: Using Cascade Mask R-CNN, FasterViT models show a better accuracy-throughput trade-off (Table 2). FasterViT-4, for instance, outperforms ConvNeXt-B and Swin-B in both box AP and mask AP, while being significantly faster (15-30% higher throughput). When combined with the DINO object detection model and ImageNet-21K pre-training, FasterViT-4 achieves 58.7 box AP, competitive with Swin-L.
- **Semantic Segmentation (ADE20K)**: With UPerNet or FPN backbones, FasterViT also exhibits superior performance-throughput trade-offs (Table 4 and S.6). FasterViT-4 with UPerNet achieves 49.1/50.3 mIoU (single/multi-scale), outperforming comparable Swin and ConvNeXt models while being faster.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of HAT**: Direct comparison (Table 5) shows that FasterViT models with HAT consistently achieve better accuracy than variants using attention mechanisms from EdgeViT or Twins, sometimes by a significant margin. This validates HAT's learnable information aggregation/propagation and direct access to dedicated CTs.
- **Component-wise Study**: Ablations (Table S.1) reveal that HAT block and CT attention are critical components for accuracy. Removing HAT entirely results in a noticeable accuracy drop. CT propagation and attention bias are also important for performance.
- **Carrier Token and Window Size**: Larger carrier token sizes or window sizes can slightly improve accuracy but at a significant cost to throughput, affirming HAT's efficiency as a mechanism for long-range dependency modeling (Table S.4).
- **Plug-and-Play HAT**: When used as a plug-and-play module in Swin-T (Table 7), HAT improves accuracy across classification (+0.4% Top-1), object detection (+0.5 box AP, +0.6 mask AP), and semantic segmentation (+0.9% mIoU) with minimal throughput overhead, proving its general applicability.
- **Impact of Conv-blocks**: Replacing early-stage Conv-blocks with Transformer-based counterparts significantly reduces throughput and accuracy (Table S.7), validating the hybrid design choice for hardware efficiency.

#### 4.4. Robustness and Multi-Platform Performance

- **Robustness**: FasterViT models, without fine-tuning, demonstrate promising robustness on ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, and ImageNetV2 datasets, outperforming comparable ConvNeXt and Swin models (Table S.2).
- **Multi-Platform Throughput**: Extensive benchmarks across various NVIDIA GPUs (V100, TITAN RTX, A6000), CPU (Intel Xeon), and embedded systems (Jetson Nano) (Figures S.5-S.9) show that FasterViT consistently achieves a strong Pareto front across different hardware, confirming its broad applicability and efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of FasterViT represents a significant step forward in the design of efficient and high-performance vision models, particularly for scenarios demanding high image throughput. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Redefining the Accuracy-Throughput Pareto Front**: FasterViT sets a new state-of-the-art Pareto front, demonstrating that it is possible to achieve top-tier accuracy in computer vision tasks while simultaneously maintaining significantly higher inference speeds compared to existing competitive architectures. This is crucial for real-time applications, large-scale data processing, and deployment on resource-constrained devices.

2. **Effective Hybrid Architecture Design**: The strategic combination of CNNs in early, high-resolution stages and Hierarchical Attention-equipped Transformers in later, lower-resolution stages is a well-founded approach. This design is guided by an understanding of GPU memory and compute characteristics, optimizing the architecture for practical hardware deployment. This emphasizes a holistic, hardware-aware approach to neural network design, which is becoming increasingly critical.

3. **Novel and Versatile Hierarchical Attention (HAT)**: The proposed HAT module is a core innovation. By intelligently using learnable carrier tokens to summarize local windows and facilitate global information exchange at reduced costs, HAT effectively addresses the computational bottleneck of global self-attention. Its demonstrated "plug-and-play" capability, improving accuracy in existing models like Swin-T with minimal overhead, highlights its versatility and potential as a standalone module for future efficient ViT designs.

4. **Broad Applicability Across CV Tasks**: The extensive validation on diverse computer vision tasks, including image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation, confirms FasterViT's generalizability and robustness. Its strong performance across these tasks means it can serve as a highly efficient backbone for a wide array of real-world computer vision applications.

5. **Scalability and Robustness**: The pre-training on ImageNet-21K and fine-tuning on higher resolutions, combined with impressive robustness on challenging out-of-distribution datasets (ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, ImageNetV2), demonstrates FasterViT's capacity to scale to larger datasets and complex real-world variability. Its consistent performance across different hardware platforms, from high-end GPUs to CPUs and embedded systems, further underscores its practical deployability.

In essence, FasterViT provides a blueprint for designing vision models that are not only powerful but also practical for deployment, especially in high-throughput environments. This work will likely influence the development of future efficient vision backbones, paving the way for more widespread adoption of advanced deep learning models in real-time and edge computing applications. The release of code via NVlabs further facilitates reproducibility and community adoption.
