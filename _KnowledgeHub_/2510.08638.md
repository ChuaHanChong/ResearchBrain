# Into the Rabbit Hull: From Task-Relevant Concepts in DINO to Minkowski Geometry

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Kempner Institute, Harvard University, and collaborators investigated DINOv2's internal representations, revealing that they form convex combinations of archetypal landmarks rather than solely sparse linear features. This work introduces the Minkowski Representation Hypothesis, which describes representations residing in Minkowski sums of convex polytopes, explaining phenomena like interpolative geometry and observed deviations from the Linear Representation Hypothesis.

## Problem

- Understanding the internal workings and representational geometry of powerful self-supervised vision transformers like DINOv2 remains largely opaque.
- The prevailing Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) might be insufficient to fully describe the complexity of learned concepts in advanced AI models.
- There is a need to identify the functional specialization of visual concepts recruited by DINOv2 for different downstream tasks.

## Method

- ==Overcomplete Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)== were trained on DINOv2-B to extract a dictionary of **32,000** visual concepts.
- Empirical analysis examined concept recruitment across classification, segmentation, and depth estimation tasks, as well as the geometric and statistical properties of the learned concepts.
- The ==Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (MRH)== was proposed, grounded in ==multi-head attention mechanisms== and Gärdenfors' conceptual spaces, to provide a refined geometric understanding.

## Results

- Downstream tasks recruit differentially specialized concepts: classification uses a broader set, while segmentation relies on 'Border concepts' and depth estimation leverages distinct 'Monocular Depth Cue concepts' (projective, shadow, frequency).
- Analysis revealed a partly dense representation with **three** outlier concepts encoding persistent positional information (left, right, bottom) and an anisotropic dictionary with a sharp singular value decay.
- Empirical signals like piecewise-linear interpolation paths staying close to the data manifold and Archetypal Analysis achieving comparable reconstruction to SAEs support the proposed Minkowski sum structure.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DINOv2's representations exhibit a more complex, anisotropic geometry with coherent subspaces and antipodal concept pairs, challenging the purely sparse and orthogonal view of the Linear Representation Hypothesis.
> - Multi-head attention naturally generates Minkowski sum structures, where individual heads form convex hulls of value vectors, and their aggregation creates larger convex polytopes.
> - Concepts are better understood as archetypal points or bounded convex regions in a latent space, implying that interventions and interpretability methods should consider this non-linear, interpolative geometry.
