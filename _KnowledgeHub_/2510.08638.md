# Into the Rabbit Hull: From Task-Relevant Concepts in DINO to Minkowski Geometry

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Kempner Institute, Harvard University, and collaborators investigated DINOv2's internal representations, revealing that they form convex combinations of archetypal landmarks rather than solely sparse linear features. This work introduces the Minkowski Representation Hypothesis, which describes representations residing in Minkowski sums of convex polytopes, explaining phenomena like interpolative geometry and observed deviations from the Linear Representation Hypothesis.

## Problem

- Understanding the internal workings and representational geometry of powerful self-supervised vision transformers like DINOv2 remains largely opaque.
- The prevailing Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) might be insufficient to fully describe the complexity of learned concepts in advanced AI models.
- There is a need to identify the functional specialization of visual concepts recruited by DINOv2 for different downstream tasks.

## Method

- ==Overcomplete Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)== were trained on DINOv2-B to extract a dictionary of **32,000** visual concepts.
- Empirical analysis examined concept recruitment across classification, segmentation, and depth estimation tasks, as well as the geometric and statistical properties of the learned concepts.
- The ==Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (MRH)== was proposed, grounded in ==multi-head attention mechanisms== and Gärdenfors' conceptual spaces, to provide a refined geometric understanding.

## Results

- Downstream tasks recruit differentially specialized concepts: classification uses a broader set, while segmentation relies on 'Border concepts' and depth estimation leverages distinct 'Monocular Depth Cue concepts' (projective, shadow, frequency).
- Analysis revealed a partly dense representation with **three** outlier concepts encoding persistent positional information (left, right, bottom) and an anisotropic dictionary with a sharp singular value decay.
- Empirical signals like piecewise-linear interpolation paths staying close to the data manifold and Archetypal Analysis achieving comparable reconstruction to SAEs support the proposed Minkowski sum structure.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DINOv2's representations exhibit a more complex, anisotropic geometry with coherent subspaces and antipodal concept pairs, challenging the purely sparse and orthogonal view of the Linear Representation Hypothesis.
> - Multi-head attention naturally generates Minkowski sum structures, where individual heads form convex hulls of value vectors, and their aggregation creates larger convex polytopes.
> - Concepts are better understood as archetypal points or bounded convex regions in a latent space, implying that interventions and interpretability methods should consider this non-linear, interpolative geometry.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have significantly advanced visual representation learning, moving away from convolutional biases towards a more general architecture that processes images as sequences of patch tokens. Self-supervised training objectives, such as those used for DINOv2, have enabled ViTs to extract rich, semantically organized representations without human supervision. DINOv2, in particular, has demonstrated robust performance across a diverse range of downstream tasks including object recognition, segmentation, depth estimation, and even robotics, indicating its ability to internalize structured representations of the visual world.

Despite DINOv2's successes and widespread deployment, the specific nature of its internal representations remains largely uncharacterized. Questions persist regarding what visual features are encoded, how these features are organized geometrically, and how a label-free training objective can yield such effective few-shot transfer capabilities. This gap in understanding highlights the need for advanced interpretability methods.

The field of vision explainability has evolved from attribution-based techniques, which localize "where" a model looks, to concept-based explanations, which aim to uncover "what" internal features are computed. Early concept-based approaches focused on individual neurons, but recognized limitations such as polysemia and basis dependence led to methods identifying concepts as distributed features. The Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) emerged from this, positing that models encode exponentially many features as sparse, quasi-orthogonal directions, with concept extraction formulated as an overcomplete dictionary learning problem. Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) are a practical instantiation of this, generating interpretable concept dictionaries.

This research builds upon the LRH by adopting SAEs as an interpretability backbone, generating a large-scale dictionary of DINOv2 concepts. However, the work then challenges and refines the LRH by presenting empirical observations that suggest a more complex, structured organization beyond simple linear sparsity and quasi-orthogonality. These observations lead to the proposal of the Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (MRH), which posits a convex-geometric structure for representations, grounded in cognitive theory and the architecture of multi-head attention. This work thus contributes to a deeper theoretical understanding of learned representations in large vision models and proposes a new framework for their interpretation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to elucidate the nature of the internal representations learned by DINOv2, a prominent self-supervised Vision Transformer. Specifically, the authors aim to understand what DINOv2 "perceives," which features are encoded, how these features are organized geometrically, and the mechanism by which a label-free objective can lead to such effective and transferable visual representations.

The motivation for this investigation stems from several factors:
- **DINOv2's Empirical Success:** DINOv2 has demonstrated remarkable performance and versatility, becoming a foundational model for a wide array of computer vision tasks, including classification, segmentation, monocular depth estimation, object tracking, and even robotic perception. Its representations are robust, transfer well across domains (e.g., satellite imagery, medical scans), and can be aligned with language, despite never being trained on text. This empirical efficacy highlights a sophisticated internal understanding of the visual world.
- **Lack of Internal Transparency:** Despite its capabilities, the internal workings of DINOv2 remain largely opaque. There is a fundamental lack of clarity regarding the specific visual features it encodes, how these features are structured, and how they contribute to its impressive performance.
- **Limitations of Existing Interpretability Paradigms:** While the Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) and Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) offer a valuable framework for concept extraction, the authors implicitly motivate a deeper investigation by hinting that this baseline might not fully capture the complexity of DINOv2's representations. They seek to move beyond merely identifying "sparse, quasi-orthogonal directions" to uncover more intricate geometric and statistical properties.
- **Bridging Cognitive and Computational Theories:** The motivation extends to grounding the understanding of DINOv2's representations in established cognitive theories, such as Gärdenfors’ conceptual spaces, to propose a more theoretically robust framework for interpretability.

To address these motivations, the study outlines a three-part investigation:
1. **Downstream Usage Analysis:** To analyze how different downstream tasks selectively recruit and utilize concepts from a learned dictionary, aiming to uncover functional specialization within DINOv2's representation space.
2. **Concept Geometry and Statistics Analysis:** To characterize the statistical properties and geometric organization of the extracted concepts, investigating whether they conform strictly to the LRH or exhibit more complex structures.
3. **Refined Representation Hypothesis Development:** To synthesize the empirical observations into a refined theoretical framework—the Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (MRH)—that provides a more accurate account of DINOv2's representational organization and its implications for interpretability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach, combining sparse autoencoder-based concept extraction with detailed statistical, geometric, and functional analyses of the learned representations.

#### 3.1. Concept Extraction via Stable Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)

- **Baseline Hypothesis:** The study adopts the Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) as an initial framework, which posits that neural activations can be expressed as sparse combinations of nearly independent directions.
- **Operationalization:** This hypothesis is operationalized by training a stable Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) (Fel et al. 2025) on DINOv2-B activations. The SAE aims to factorize activations (A) into sparse codes (Z) and an overcomplete dictionary (D), where A ≈ ZD.
- **DINOv2 Configuration:** The SAE was applied to DINOv2-B, which has a latent dimension (d) of 768 and 261 tokens (t) per image (256 patch tokens, 1 CLS token, 4 register tokens).
- **SAE Parameters:** The dictionary (D) was set to contain 32,000 "concept atoms" (c = 32,000), significantly overcomplete relative to the embedding dimension. A sparsity constant (k) of 8 was enforced, meaning each token activation was reconstructed using at most 8 active concepts.
- **Training Data and Constraints:** The SAE was trained on 1.4 million ImageNet-1K images. Key constraints included non-negativity for sparse codes (Z ≥ 0) and constraining dictionary atoms to lie within the convex hull of real activations (D_i ∈ conv(A)), which contributes to stability and interpretability.
- **Performance:** The trained SAE achieved an R² reconstruction fidelity greater than 88%, consistent with prior stability results. This dictionary of 32,000 concepts serves as the interpretability backbone for the entire study.

#### 3.2. Analysis of Downstream Usage (Section 3)

- **Concept Importance Measure:** For linear probes, the importance of each concept for a specific task is quantified as E(Z)W', where E(Z) is the expected concept activation and W' = DW^T defines the alignment between dictionary concepts and task outputs. This measure is supported by faithfulness criteria in linear regimes.
- **Tasks Analyzed:** ImageNet-1k classification, semantic segmentation, and monocular depth estimation.
- **Quantitative Methods:**
 - Comparison of the fraction of the dictionary recruited by each task.
 - Analysis of intra-task concept similarity (cosine similarity histograms of top-100 concepts).
 - Spectral analysis (eigenvalue spectrum) of task-specific sub-dictionaries to assess dimensionality.
- **Qualitative Methods:**
 - **"Elsewhere" Concepts (Classification):** Visualization using token-level attribution and causal masking (RISE Petsiuk et al. 2018) to verify their causal dependence on objects despite activating off-object.
 - **"Border" Concepts (Segmentation):** Visualization via token attribution, showing activation along object contours.
 - **"Monocular Cue" Concepts (Depth):** Targeted perturbation analysis with five controlled image manipulations (e.g., median blur, high-pass filter) and UMAP projection of concept activation profiles to identify distinct cue families.

#### 3.3. Analysis of Token-Type-Specific Concepts (Section 4)

- **Footprint Entropy:** For each concept, the entropy of its token-wise activation distribution (averaged over 1.4 million images) was computed to determine spatial specificity (localized vs. diffuse activation).
- **Categorization:** Concepts were categorized into position-specific, CLS-only, and register-only based on their low-entropy footprints.
- **Qualitative Inspection:** Register-only concepts were visualized to understand the global scene properties they encode (e.g., motion blur, illumination).

#### 3.4. Statistics and Geometry of Concepts (Section 5)

- **Concept Occurrence Statistics:** Plotting conditional energy E(Z_i | Z_i > 0) against firing count for each concept to reveal activation regimes and identify outliers.
- **Concept Co-occurrence:** Spectral analysis (eigenvalue spectrum) of the normalized concept co-activation matrix (Z^T Z). This was compared against two baselines: a random matrix with identical sparsity and a shuffled matrix preserving empirical co-activation distribution (detailed in Appendix E).
- **Dictionary Geometry (D):**
 - **Pairwise Inner Products:** Distribution of dot products (DD^T) compared against random Gaussian vectors and an optimized Grassmannian frame baseline (details in Appendix F) to assess coherence.
 - **Singular Value Spectrum:** Analysis of the singular values of D to determine its effective rank and anisotropy.
 - **Hoyer Sparsity Scores:** Calculation of Hoyer scores for dictionary atoms to assess their alignment with individual neurons (axis-alignment).
 - **Correlation Analysis:** Quantifying the correlation between concept co-activation (Z^T Z) and geometric similarity (DD^T) to examine whether co-occurrence shapes geometry.

#### 3.5. Token-Level Geometry and Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (Sections 6 & 7)

- **Patch Token Embedding Visualization:** Use of Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to project single-image patch embeddings into 2D or 3D and visualize them as RGB color maps, demonstrating local connectedness and alignment with object boundaries.
- **Positional Embedding Analysis (Appendix I):**
 - **Decodability:** Training linear decoders to predict token position from layer activations to assess positional information retention.
 - **Rank Compression:** Analyzing the rank of the positional embedding subspace across layers.
 - **Position Removal:** Projecting token embeddings orthogonal to the positional subspace and re-visualizing via PCA to determine if semantic structure persists without explicit positional cues.
- **Theoretical Development of MRH (Appendix J):**
 - **Connection to Gärdenfors' Conceptual Spaces:** Grounding the hypothesis in cognitive theories of convex conceptual regions.
 - **Multi-Head Attention Mechanism:** Formal demonstration that single attention heads produce outputs within the convex hull of their value vectors, and multi-head attention sums these individual head-polytopes, resulting in a Minkowski sum of convex polytopes.
- **Empirical Signatures of MRH (Section 7.2):**
 - **On-Manifold Trajectories:** Comparing straight-line interpolation between tokens with piecewise-linear geodesics on the token k-NN graph to see which paths stay closer to the data manifold.
 - **Archetypal Analysis (AA):** Using AA (a single-tile MRH case) to reconstruct tokens and assess its efficiency compared to SAE.
 - **Block Structure:** Analyzing the clustering of archetypal coefficients from AA to detect emergent block structures.

**Visualization Tool:** Release of "DinoVision," an interactive web-based visualization tool for the 32,000 concepts, including UMAP projection, activation patterns, and co-occurrence links.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several findings regarding DINOv2's internal representations, moving from functional specialization to statistical properties, and culminating in a new geometric hypothesis.

#### 4.1. Functional Specialization of Concepts

- **Task-Specific Recruitment:** Different downstream tasks recruit distinct and often minimally overlapping subsets of concepts from the 32,000-unit dictionary. Classification utilizes a broad, dispersed set, while segmentation and depth estimation draw upon more compact, localized regions of the concept manifold.
- **Low-Dimensional Task Subspaces:** Quantitative analysis (cosine similarity and eigenvalue spectra of sub-dictionaries) confirms that concepts relevant to specific tasks exhibit higher mutual alignment and form coherent, low-dimensional functional subspaces compared to random concept subsets.
- **"Elsewhere" Concepts for Classification:** For various ImageNet classes, top-important concepts include "Elsewhere" detectors. These concepts activate broadly across tokens, specifically *not* on the object itself, yet their firing is causally dependent on the object's presence. This suggests DINOv2 learns a form of conditional negation ("not the object, but the object exists elsewhere") for classification, distributing class evidence beyond object-centric tokens.
- **"Border" Concepts for Segmentation:** Segmentation tasks primarily recruit "border concepts" that localize along object contours (e.g., limbs, architectural edges). These concepts form a visibly tight cluster in the embedding space, indicating a dedicated representational region for boundary encoding.
- **Monocular Depth Cues:** Despite lacking explicit 3D supervision, DINOv2's depth-relevant concepts fall into three families consistent with visual neuroscience: projective geometry cues (e.g., vanishing lines), shadow-based cues (e.g., soft lighting gradients), and frequency-based cues (e.g., texture transitions).
- **Token-Type Specialization:**
 - Most concepts are positionally agnostic, but a significant tail shows strong localization.
 - A unique "CLS-only" concept fires persistently on the CLS token, potentially acting as an ID.
 - Hundreds of "register-only" concepts activate exclusively on register tokens, encoding global scene properties such as illumination, motion blur, caustic reflections, and artistic style, suggesting registers act as conduits for abstract, non-spatial information.

#### 4.2. Statistics and Geometry of Concepts

- **Concept Occurrence Patterns:** Most concepts exhibit a frequency-energy trade-off, with rarely firing concepts allocating higher energy per activation. However, three outlier concepts show dense, dataset-wide activation at relatively low energy, encoding persistent positional features (left, right, bottom of image). This suggests a hybrid representational regime with both sparse and dense (spatially grounded) features.
- **Co-activation Structure:** The eigenvalue spectrum of the concept co-activation matrix (Z^T Z) decays smoothly without sharp gaps, lying between random and shuffled baselines. This indicates a richly connected, high-dimensional distributed regime where concepts co-activate without forming simple, modular clusters.
- **Dictionary Geometry (D):**
 - **Higher Coherence:** The SAE-learned dictionary exhibits heavier tails in its pairwise inner product distribution and a flatter central peak compared to ideal Grassmannian frames or random vectors, indicating higher coherence and clustered structure rather than maximal incoherence.
 - **Antipodal Concepts:** A small but significant number of concept pairs are nearly antipodal (D_i ≈ -D_j), corresponding to semantically opposed features (e.g., "vertical lines" vs. "horizontal lines," "white shirt" vs. "black shirt"). This implies the model uses polarity to encode meaning.
 - **Global Anisotropy:** The singular value spectrum of the dictionary decays sharply, suggesting a lower effective rank and anisotropic allocation of representational capacity, where some directions are densely populated. This anisotropy often aligns with task-specific subspaces.
 - **Distributed Encoding:** Hoyer scores confirm that dictionary atoms are distributed across neurons rather than being axis-aligned, consistent with distributed representations.
 - **Weak Co-activation-Geometry Link:** The correlation between concept co-activation (Z^T Z) and geometric similarity (DD^T) is weak (r=0.28, R^2=0.08), suggesting that usage patterns do not dominantly determine spatial proximity in concept space.

#### 4.3. Token-Level Geometry and Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (MRH)

- **Smooth, Low-Dimensional Token Manifolds:** PCA projections of patch token embeddings reveal that tokens within an image occupy smooth, low-dimensional, locally connected manifolds that often align with object boundaries, even without explicit supervision for segmentation. This smoothness persists across distinct images.
- **Positional Information Compression:** Positional encodings are high-rank in early layers, allowing precise spatial decoding, but compress significantly to a 2D plane in the final layers. Importantly, the observed "smoothness" in PCA maps of actual image tokens persists even after rigorously removing positional components, indicating it reflects genuine semantic organization.
- **Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (MRH) Proposal:** Synthesizing these observations, the authors propose MRH: tokens are formed by combining convex mixtures of a few archetypes (e.g., "rabbit among animals," "brown among colors"). This implies that activations are organized as Minkowski sums of convex polytopes, and concepts emerge as convex regions rather than unbounded linear directions.
- **Theoretical Foundation for MRH:** The multi-head attention mechanism naturally generates this geometry: a single attention head produces outputs in the convex hull of its value vectors, and multi-head attention additively combines these head-wise polytopes, resulting in a Minkowski sum.
- **Empirical Signatures of MRH:**
 - **Non-linear On-Manifold Trajectories:** Piecewise-linear geodesics on the token k-NN graph remain closer to the data manifold than straight-line interpolations, supporting the idea of feasible displacements arising from "face-walks" within polytopes.
 - **Efficient Archetypal Reconstruction:** Archetypal Analysis, a single-tile MRH case, efficiently reconstructs tokens with remarkably few archetypes (approx. 10 per image), suggesting embeddings concentrate on low-dimensional polytopes.
 - **Emergent Block Structure:** Archetypal decompositions spontaneously organize into block-sparse patterns, supporting the "tiles" assumption of MRH.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant contributions to the understanding and interpretability of large vision models, particularly DINOv2, with broad implications for future research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Deeper Understanding of Vision Transformers

The study moves beyond surface-level attribution to provide a more mechanistic understanding of DINOv2's internal representations. By systematically dissecting how concepts are formed, organized, and utilized across tasks, it sheds light on the computational strategies employed by self-supervised ViTs to build robust and transferable visual features. The discovery of "Elsewhere" concepts, monocular depth cues, and specialized register tokens reveals DINOv2's capacity for sophisticated spatial logic, contextual reasoning, and abstraction of global scene properties, often in alignment with principles observed in visual neuroscience.

#### 5.2. Refinement and Challenge to Existing Interpretability Paradigms

The research challenges the strict assumptions of the Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH), which often underpins concept extraction via sparse autoencoders. While acknowledging LRH as a useful baseline, the findings demonstrate that DINOv2's representations are not solely composed of sparse, quasi-orthogonal directions. Instead, they exhibit higher coherence, anisotropy, antipodal semantic axes, and a partly dense structure (e.g., positional features). This suggests that a purely linear, sparse view may be insufficient to capture the full complexity of learned representations, necessitating a more nuanced framework.

#### 5.3. Introduction of the Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (MRH)

The proposal of the Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (MRH) is a central theoretical contribution. By framing concepts as points and regions within convex polytopes and activations as Minkowski sums of these polytopes, MRH provides a novel, geometrically grounded perspective. This hypothesis is supported by both theoretical arguments, demonstrating how multi-head attention naturally generates such structures, and empirical observations of token-level geometry. MRH offers a refined conceptual space for understanding how models blend archetypal features to represent complex visual information.

#### 5.4. Implications for Interpretability Tools and Practices

If MRH holds, it has profound implications for how interpretability research is conducted:
- **Reconceptualizing Concepts:** Concepts would no longer be solely understood as unbounded linear directions, but rather as landmarks (archetypes) or bounded convex regions in the latent space. This shifts the focus from "what direction" to "what point/region."
- **Bounded Steering:** The notion of "steering" model activations would become inherently bounded. Instead of infinitely extrapolating along a direction, interventions would involve moving an activation towards a specific landmark or within a convex cell. This explains why current linear steering methods often plateau or reverse beyond a certain strength, suggesting that optimal probes should measure barycentric weights or geodesic distances within these convex regions.
- **Non-Identifiability of Decomposition:** A critical insight is the non-identifiability of Minkowski decompositions from a single layer's activation alone. This implies that interpretability methods attempting to recover the "true" constituent concept polytopes or their contributions will generally face non-unique solutions. Consequently, future interpretability efforts may need to exploit the architectural structure (e.g., attention weights, intermediate layer activations) more directly to render factorizations tractable, rather than treating each layer as an isolated black box.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The study opens several avenues for future work, including the development of new concept extraction methods explicitly designed for MRH-like geometries, further empirical validation of MRH across different models and tasks, and the creation of "structure-aware" interpretability techniques that leverage architectural mechanisms to overcome decomposition non-identifiability.

#### 5.6. Accessibility and Community Impact

The release of "DinoVision," an interactive visualization tool for the 32,000 extracted concepts, significantly enhances the accessibility of the findings. This tool allows researchers to explore the rich structure of DINOv2's representations in an intuitive manner, fostering further investigation and hypothesis generation within the broader machine learning community.
