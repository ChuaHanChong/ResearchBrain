# BEHAVIOR: Benchmark for Everyday Household Activities in Virtual, Interactive, and Ecological Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford University introduced BEHAVIOR, a comprehensive benchmark featuring 100 ecologically valid household activities for embodied AI agents in virtual, interactive, and ecological environments. It leverages a predicate logic-based language (BDDL) for flexible task definition, implements these activities in the high-fidelity iGibson 2.0 simulator with a new object dataset, and provides human-centric evaluation metrics along with 500 human VR demonstrations. Experiments showed state-of-the-art reinforcement learning agents struggled significantly, failing to achieve any task progress under the benchmark's full realism and complexity.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI benchmarks often lack the comprehensive realism, diversity, and complexity needed for agents to robustly perform everyday household activities.
- Prior efforts frequently focus on specialized sub-problems (e.g., visual navigation) or offer tasks with limited interaction, shorter horizons, or simplified physics and object models.
- A lack of flexible activity definition, realistic simulation capabilities for diverse interactions, and objective human-centric evaluation hindered the development of general-purpose embodied AI.

## Method

- Developed **BDDL** (BEHAVIOR Domain Definition Language), a ==predicate logic-based language== for semantic definition of 100 ecologically valid household activities, enabling diverse instance generation.
- Specified ==simulator-agnostic functional requirements== and implemented **BEHAVIOR** within iGibson 2.0, a high-fidelity simulation environment featuring a new 3D object dataset and ==extended object states== (e.g., temperature, wetness).
- Introduced a comprehensive evaluation framework with a granular ==success score==, efficiency metrics normalized to human performance, and provided a dataset of 500 human VR demonstrations.

## Results

- State-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithms (SAC, PPO) consistently failed to achieve any goal condition predicates (**Success Score Q = 0**) across a subset of **12** BEHAVIOR activities under full realism.
- The long-horizon nature of activities, challenges in interpreting realistic sensor data, and generating correct, physically-compliant actuation sequences were identified as primary bottlenecks for current AI agents.
- Introducing diversity in object poses or instances rapidly degraded agent performance even in simplified, single-literal tasks, highlighting struggles with generalization across varied configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Defining complex, ecologically valid tasks requires a flexible, semantic language like BDDL that maps abstract concepts to simulator states, enabling principled generation of diverse task instances.
> - Realistic simulation for general household activities necessitates advanced features beyond kinematics, including dynamic object properties and high-fidelity sensor/actuation modeling.
> - Human demonstrations and human-centric metrics are crucial for benchmarking complex, long-horizon tasks, providing realistic baselines and detailed insights into task difficulty and human strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI focuses on developing artificial agents that can perceive, reason, and interact with their environment, mimicking the capabilities and limitations of a physical body. Recent advancements have been observed in areas such as visual navigation, interactive question answering, instruction following, and robotic manipulation. To systematically measure and calibrate progress in these diverse areas, the development of robust benchmarks in simulated environments has become a critical component. Existing benchmarks, including Rearrangement, TDW Transport Challenge, VirtualHome, ALFRED, Interactive Gibson Benchmark, MetaWorld, and RLBench, have contributed to this effort.

However, the authors identify a gap in the current landscape: the activities presented in these benchmarks often represent only a subset of the challenges humans encounter in daily life. Specifically, they contend that existing benchmarks frequently lack the comprehensive realism, diversity, and complexity required to develop artificial agents capable of performing everyday household activities with human-level robustness and flexibility. Many prior efforts tend to specialize in particular sub-problems, such as visual navigation or stationary manipulation, or offer tasks with limited interaction, shorter horizons, or simplified physics and object models. For instance, rearrangement tasks, while important, do not cover the full spectrum of state changes involved in typical household chores (e.g., cooking, cleaning, modifying object states beyond position).

BEHAVIOR addresses these limitations by introducing a benchmark designed to be more comprehensive. It aims to bridge the gap between specialized, simpler simulated tasks and the multifaceted demands of real-world domestic activities. By focusing on a broader range of activities and incorporating more realistic simulation parameters, BEHAVIOR seeks to serve as a unifying benchmark that can guide the development of more general-purpose embodied AI solutions capable of addressing the complexities of human environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to accelerate the development of embodied AI agents that can robustly and flexibly perform everyday household activities, ultimately enabling them to assist humans in domestic environments. To achieve this, the authors identified and sought to address three fundamental challenges inherent in creating a comprehensive benchmark for such agents:

1. **Activity Definition:** A significant challenge lies in identifying and formally defining meaningful activities for benchmarking. Everyday household tasks are often subjective, varying by individual, time, and location. Existing benchmarks often rely on specific, static definitions that limit the diversity and generalizability of the tasks. The motivation here was to create a definition language that is flexible enough to capture the semantic essence of activities, allow for diverse instantiations, and be interpretable without requiring deep expertise in formal logic from human annotators. The aim was to move beyond scene- or pose-specific task definitions towards a more abstract, logic-based representation.

2. **Realistic Realization in Simulation:** Developing simulated environments that can realistically support a wide array of diverse and complex activities presents another major hurdle. Many simulations simplify physics, object properties, or interaction capabilities, which can lead to solutions that do not transfer well to the real world. The motivation was to specify the necessary functional requirements for a simulator to support realistic interactions, including dynamic object properties (e.g., temperature, wetness, cleanliness), varied object categories, and high-fidelity sensor feedback and physical actuation. The goal was to ensure that the simulated challenges accurately reflect real-world complexities, thereby encouraging the development of more robust and generalizable AI.

3. **Objective Evaluation:** Establishing clear, objective metrics for evaluating agent performance, especially across a highly diverse set of activities and relative to human capabilities, is crucial for benchmarking. Binary success metrics provide limited insight into progress, while efficiency metrics need to be comparable across different tasks and environments. The motivation was to introduce a comprehensive evaluation framework that not only measures task completion but also quantifies intermediate progress and efficiency, normalized against human performance. This would provide richer feedback for agents and allow for fair comparisons across different solutions and activity instances. The inclusion of human demonstrations was motivated by the need for a realistic baseline and as a resource for learning paradigms like imitation learning.

In summary, the key motivation for BEHAVIOR was to overcome the limitations of existing embodied AI benchmarks by providing a framework for realistic, diverse, and complex household activities, coupled with a flexible definition language, a capable simulation environment, and a robust evaluation methodology, all grounded in human activity data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce BEHAVIOR through three technical innovations, each designed to address the challenges outlined in their objectives:

1. **BDDL (BEHAVIOR Domain Definition Language):** To address the challenge of activity definition, the authors developed BDDL, a predicate logic-based language. This language enables the semantic description of an activity's initial and goal conditions. BDDL maps simulated physical states to abstract semantic symbols, allowing activities to be defined in a simulator-agnostic manner. For example, instead of specifying exact object coordinates, BDDL predicates describe relationships (e.g., `OnTop(book, shelf)`) or object states (e.g., `Cooked(food)`). This abstract definition facilitates the generation of a high number of diverse activity instances from a single semantic definition, varying in specific object models, poses, and scene layouts. The authors sourced 100 activities from the American Time Use Survey (ATUS) to ensure ecological validity, covering categories like cleaning, food preparation, and maintenance. Two example definitions per activity were crowdsourced from non-expert annotators using a visual, Blockly-based interface, which ensured definitional diversity and semantic validity within the simulation environment.

2. **Simulator-Agnostic Requirements and Implementation in iGibson 2.0:** For the realization challenge, the paper first specifies a set of environment-agnostic functional requirements for any simulator to support BEHAVIOR. These include maintaining an object-centric representation, simulating physical forces and motion, generating realistic virtual sensor signals (e.g., RGB-D images, LiDAR), modeling non-kinematic object properties (e.g., temperature, wetness, cleanliness), providing functionalities to generate valid activity instances based on BDDL initial conditions, and evaluating goal conditions. The authors then provide a concrete, fully functional implementation of BEHAVIOR within **iGibson 2.0**, an updated version of the iGibson simulation environment.
 - **iGibson 2.0 Features:** The simulator uses the Bullet physics engine for realistic physics simulation. It generates high-fidelity virtual sensor signals (RGB, depth, normals, semantic/instance segmentation, optical/spatial flow, LiDAR). It supports extended object states (e.g., temperature, dust, wetness) and their visual manifestations.
 - **BEHAVIOR Object Dataset:** Complementing iGibson 2.0, a new dataset of 1217 3D object models across 391 WordNet-grounded categories was created. These objects are selected based on their relevance to ATUS activities, incorporating realistic physical attributes (mass, center of mass) and visual properties (materials, textures).
 - **Embodied Agents:** BEHAVIOR supports two embodiments: a bimanual humanoid (24 degrees of freedom, DoF), used by human demonstrators in VR, and a Fetch robot (12/13 DoF). Both are capable of navigation, grasping, and interaction. Agents can operate using low-level continuous actions or higher-level action primitives (e.g., `navigateTo`, `grasp`, `placeOnTop`), which can be executed with either full physics simulation or a "partially-simulated" mode for faster prototyping.

3. **Comprehensive Evaluation Metrics and Human-Centric Data:** To address the evaluation challenge, BEHAVIOR introduces a multi-faceted metric system:
 - **Success Score (Q):** This is the primary metric, measuring task progress as the maximum fraction of satisfied ground literals in the goal condition at any given step. It provides incremental feedback beyond a binary pass/fail.
 - **Efficiency Metrics:** Six secondary metrics quantify efficiency: simulated time (T_sim), kinematic disarrangement (D_K, displacement caused by agent), logical disarrangement (D_L, changes in logical state), distance navigated (L_body), and displacement of each hand (L_left, L_right) during manipulation.
 - **Human-Centric Metric:** To enable fair comparisons across diverse activities, scenes, and instances, efficiency metrics can be normalized relative to human performance.
 - **BEHAVIOR Dataset of Human Demonstrations:** A large-scale dataset of 500 successful human demonstrations (758.5 minutes total) performing BEHAVIOR activities in virtual reality (using HTC Vive Pro Eye) is provided. These demonstrations serve as a ground truth for human-centric evaluation and can also facilitate imitation learning approaches. The data includes full action records, navigation, manipulation, and gaze tracking.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper evaluates the performance of two state-of-the-art reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), on a subset of 12 relatively simpler BEHAVIOR activities to demonstrate the benchmark's challenges.

- **Overall Performance of State-of-the-Art RL:** When tested under full realism, diversity, and complexity (using SAC with low-level continuous actions and realistic sensing), agents consistently failed to achieve any goal condition predicates (Success Score Q = 0) across all 12 activities. This indicates that current RL solutions struggle significantly with the benchmark's full scope.

- **Effect of Complexity (Activity Length/Horizon):**
 - Using higher-level "action primitives" (temporally extended actions that shorten the task horizon, trained with PPO) resulted in only marginal success. For example, `bringingInWood` achieved a Q of 0.13, while most other tasks remained at 0.
 - In oracle-driven experiments where agents started just 1 second away from a successful goal state, SAC achieved high success rates (SR) for many tasks. However, this performance deteriorated rapidly as the starting point was moved further from the goal state. At 10 seconds away, SR dropped to 0 for all activities, suggesting that the long-horizon nature of BEHAVIOR activities is a major challenge for current RL.

- **Effect of Realism (Sensing and Actuation):**
 - **Sensing (Full Observability):** Granting agents full observability (ground truth object poses) did not improve performance for policies using low-level continuous actions (Q=0). For policies using action primitives, full observability enabled partial success in some activities, indicating that the perceptual problem (deriving actionable information from realistic sensor data) is a contributing factor to the difficulty.
 - **Actuation (No Physics Simulation):** When action primitives were executed without physics simulation (i.e., actions directly achieved their intended outcomes without collision or dynamic challenges), and combined with full observability, agents achieved significant success (Q=1 for two activities, high Q for others). This finding suggests that the generation of correct, physically-compliant actuation sequences is a critical challenge. The paper concludes that solving the entire interaction sequence, rather than just the task-level strategy, is a key difficulty in BEHAVIOR.

- **Effect of Diversity (Activity Instance and Objects):**
 - Experiments on simplified, single-literal activities showed that while agents could achieve success in a non-diverse setting (fixed object poses, specific object instances), introducing diversity in object poses or instances rapidly degraded performance. This was particularly evident in tasks like "sliced" or "stained," indicating that current RL algorithms struggle with generalizing across varied object configurations and identities even in simpler contexts.

- **Analysis of Human Demonstrations:** The 500 human VR demonstrations revealed insights into human performance:
 - Activity durations ranged from less than 2 minutes to over 11 minutes, confirming the long-horizon nature of BEHAVIOR tasks.
 - Activities exhibited varying room occupancies, with the kitchen being the most prevalent.
 - Manipulation predominantly involved the dominant hand, followed by bimanual grasping for larger objects.
 - A strong correlation (R^2 = 0.826) was observed between the activity volume (number of ground goal literals) and the total activity time, validating activity volume as a measure of logical complexity.
 - Collected gaze data showed a clear correlation with activity goals, suggesting its utility for research in visual attention and active perception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BEHAVIOR represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI research by establishing a novel and comprehensive benchmark for everyday household activities.

- **Advancing Embodied AI:** The benchmark pushes the boundaries of current embodied AI challenges by integrating a high degree of realism, diversity, and complexity. It introduces new types of state changes that agents must achieve (e.g., cleaning surfaces, changing object temperatures), moving beyond simpler rearrangement tasks. The experimental results unequivocally demonstrate that state-of-the-art RL solutions currently struggle with BEHAVIOR, highlighting fundamental research directions required to achieve more general and capable embodied intelligence.

- **Guiding Research Directions:** By systematically breaking down the sources of difficulty (complexity, realism, diversity), the benchmark provides clear guidance for future research. It suggests that advancements are needed in areas such as hierarchical control, task and motion planning (TAMP), robust manipulation under realistic physics, generalization across diverse object instances and configurations, and effective perception from high-fidelity sensor data.

- **Novel Task Definition and Evaluation:** The introduction of BDDL offers a flexible, semantic, and simulator-agnostic language for defining tasks. This allows for principled generation of infinite activity instances and acceptance of diverse, semantically equivalent solutions, fostering more generalizable AI. The human-centric evaluation metrics, coupled with the large dataset of human VR demonstrations, provide a robust standard for measuring progress and offer a valuable resource for imitation learning and studies of human cognition in ecological environments.

- **Accessibility and Collaboration:** The commitment to making BEHAVIOR open-source and freely available (including BDDL definitions, iGibson 2.0 implementation, object dataset, and human demonstrations) aims to democratize access to advanced research tools. This initiative has the potential to foster broader participation in embodied AI research, accelerate scientific discovery, and facilitate fair comparisons between different solutions.

- **Societal Relevance:** By focusing on household activities, BEHAVIOR directly contributes to the development of AI agents that could eventually assist humans in their daily lives. Such advancements carry significant potential for impact on quality of life, but also raise ethical considerations regarding labor impacts and physical safety, which the authors acknowledge. Overall, BEHAVIOR is poised to serve as a foundational platform for training and evaluating a new generation of embodied AI agents capable of intelligent interaction within complex real-world environments.
