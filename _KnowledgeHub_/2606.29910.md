# Sphere-VIO: Fast and Robust Visual-Inertial Odometry via Unified Spherical Representation for Heterogeneous Multi-Camera Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Sphere-VIO introduces a filter-based Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) framework employing a unified spherical representation to support heterogeneous multi-camera systems. The method achieves robust, real-time state estimation on CPU-only embedded platforms, demonstrating superior trade-offs between accuracy, efficiency, and cross-camera generality compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.

## Problem

- Existing multi-camera VIO algorithms lack unified compatibility for heterogeneous camera setups (e.g., pinhole, fisheye) and arbitrary arrangements.
- Ineffective cross-camera feature management leads to redundancy, difficult tracking, and unstable depth estimation due to divergent orientations and distortions.
- Many robust VIO systems incur high computational overhead, hindering real-time performance on resource-constrained devices without GPUs.

## Method

- **Sphere-VIO** introduces a ==Unified Spherical Panorama Model (USPM)== to project observations from diverse physical cameras onto a single spherical surface for unified representation.
- A ==Hierarchical Omnidirectional Feature Alignment (HOFA)== framework provides robust ==semi-direct feature tracking==, cross-camera inverse depth estimation, and adaptive range filtering.
- A multi-camera-adapted ==Error State Kalman Filter (ESKF)== backend uses a 3D ==spherical bearing vector residual== for efficient and accurate state estimation, suitable for CPU-only operations.

## Results

- Sphere-VIO achieved an Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE of **0.1518 m** on the TUM-VI dataset, outperforming the filter-based SchurVINS by **48.8%** and graph-optimized VINS-Fusion by **63.6%**.
- The framework secured **second place** overall on the HILTI 2022 dataset with an ATE RMSE of **0.1821 m**, demonstrating robust tracking in challenging conditions.
- It achieved real-time performance on an Intel NUC 13 embedded platform, processing four camera streams at an average of **0.0180 seconds per frame**, well within typical real-time limits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified spherical representation simplifies feature management and triangulation across heterogeneous multi-camera systems, improving robustness and generality.
> - Task-adaptive semi-direct tracking combined with adaptive depth range filtering effectively addresses challenges in cross-camera feature alignment and stable depth estimation.
> - The use of spherical bearing vector residuals within an ESKF backend offers an efficient and accurate method for VIO in wide-FOV multi-camera scenarios, enabling real-time operation on CPU-constrained hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) is a technique for estimating the motion and position of a sensor rig by fusing visual data from cameras with inertial measurements from an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU). Multi-camera VIO systems aim to overcome the inherent limitations of single-camera or pure visual systems, such as performance degradation under rapid motion, illumination changes, and texture-less environments, by expanding the field of view (FOV), providing stronger observational stability, and enabling complementary perception.

Existing methods in this domain typically exhibit specific limitations. Many algorithms are designed for fixed camera setups, which restricts their compatibility with heterogeneous multi-camera systems comprising various camera models (e.g., pinhole, fisheye) and arbitrary arrangements. A common challenge is the absence of a unified cross-camera representation and association mechanism. This deficit often leads to difficulties in robustly tracking features across different cameras, achieving stable depth estimation, and maintaining real-time performance. Some approaches process features independently per camera without global cross-camera association, leading to redundant computations in overlapping areas. Others, designed for specific setups like 360° fisheye rigs, lack the generality required for arbitrary multi-camera modules.

Furthermore, processing divergent camera orientations and severe optical distortion in multi-camera systems exacerbates cross-camera alignment difficulties. While single-lens panoramic cameras offer a full FOV, they often lack sufficient baseline for reliable scale estimation, necessitating reliance on noisy IMU scale cues, which can lead to unstable scale and long-term drift. Recent works adopting panoramic or general spherical representations often remain confined to specific rigid setups, may not ensure geometric consistency across heterogeneous modules, and frequently depend on computationally expensive stitching processes or optimization-heavy backends, thereby hindering real-time performance.

Sphere-VIO addresses these limitations by proposing a filter-based VIO framework that employs a unified spherical representation designed to be compatible with heterogeneous multi-camera systems. This work aims to balance generality, lightweight efficiency, tracking robustness, and estimation accuracy for arbitrary multi-camera and panoramic VIO systems, thereby advancing the practical deployment of VIO in complex, real-world robotic applications on resource-constrained platforms.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation behind Sphere-VIO stems from the identified limitations in current multi-camera Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) systems when dealing with heterogeneous camera setups and demanding operational constraints. The paper articulates several key challenges that existing methods struggle to address comprehensively:

1. **Lack of Unified Compatibility for Heterogeneous Multi-Camera Systems:** Current multi-camera VIO algorithms are often tailored for specific, fixed camera configurations (e.g., stereo pairs, particular 360° fisheye rigs). They typically lack the flexibility to seamlessly integrate heterogeneous camera modules with diverse intrinsic parameters (e.g., pinhole, MEI, fisheye) and arbitrary spatial arrangements. This restricts their broad applicability and requires significant adaptation for each new hardware setup.

2. **Ineffective Cross-Camera Feature Management:** The absence of a unified representation and association mechanism across multiple cameras leads to several issues. These include redundant processing of features in overlapping fields of view, difficulties in robustly tracking features as they transition between cameras, and inefficient triangulation for depth estimation. Divergent camera orientations and severe lens distortions further complicate cross-camera alignment, leading to mismatches and reduced accuracy.

3. **Unstable Depth Estimation and Scale Drift:** While multi-camera systems theoretically provide stronger baselines, issues like a lack of global geometric consistency for heterogeneous modules or reliance on single-lens panoramic cameras (which inherently lack sufficient baseline) can lead to unstable depth estimates. This forces a greater dependence on noisy IMU data for scale, contributing to unstable scale estimation and potential long-term drift.

4. **High Computational Overhead and Poor Real-time Performance:** Many robust multi-camera SLAM/VIO systems employ optimization-based backends, often involving complex graph optimizations or expensive image stitching procedures. These methods, while accurate, typically incur significant computational costs, making them unsuitable for real-time operation on resource-constrained devices, especially those limited to CPU processing.

5. **Achieving a Balance of Performance Attributes:** A critical challenge is to simultaneously achieve high generality (supporting diverse camera setups), lightweight efficiency (for real-time CPU-only operation), robust feature tracking, and accurate state estimation. Existing solutions often optimize for one or two of these aspects at the expense of others.

To address these challenges, Sphere-VIO sets forth the following key objectives:

- To develop a lightweight, filter-based VIO framework that can universally support arbitrary heterogeneous multi-camera modules.
- To propose a unified cross-camera representation that simplifies feature management and improves triangulation efficiency across diverse cameras without sequential stitching.
- To design a robust cross-camera feature tracking and matching pipeline that integrates epipolar constraints with adaptive depth range filtering, leading to stable depth estimation and eliminating redundancy in overlapping regions.
- To implement an efficient backend for state estimation that minimizes computational overhead, enabling accurate real-time performance on resource-constrained CPU-only devices.
- To demonstrate superior trade-offs between accuracy, robustness, efficiency, and cross-camera generality compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.

### 3. Methodology and approach

Sphere-VIO is presented as a lightweight, filter-based Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) framework tailored for heterogeneous multi-camera systems, leveraging a unified spherical representation. The methodology is structured into three main components: a Unified Spherical Panorama Model (USPM), a Hierarchical Omnidirectional Feature Alignment (HOFA) procedure, and a multi-camera-adapted Error State Kalman Filter (ESKF) backend.

**A. Unified Spherical Panorama Model (USPM)**

The USPM is designed to unify images from arbitrary multi-camera rigs onto a single spherical surface. This model offers flexibility, accommodating all standard camera models (e.g., pinhole, MEI) and not strictly requiring full 360° coverage, making it compatible even with narrow-FOV stereo setups.

- **Forward Projection:** A normalized 3D point from a physical camera frame is rigidly transformed to a virtual panoramic frame, satisfying a spherical constraint. A scale factor is derived for this transformation. The 3D point in the panoramic frame is then normalized to a unit vector, converted into azimuth and elevation angles, and finally mapped to panoramic pixel coordinates. This process is formalized as a unified operator $\mathcal{U}_\pi(\cdot)$. The projection is explicitly formulated to define the mapping from a 3D point to a 2D panoramic pixel.
- **Inverse Projection:** A geometrically consistent inverse operator $\mathcal{U}_\pi^{-1}(\cdot)$ is derived. This operator recovers the corresponding 3D direction from a panoramic pixel, which is crucial for cross-camera feature alignment and remapping. The inverse projection involves converting panoramic pixels back to azimuth and elevation, reconstructing a 3D unit vector, and then transforming it back to the physical camera frame.
- **Physical Camera Projection:** The framework supports Kalibr-compatible camera models by adopting a general physical camera projection operator $\mathcal{C}_\pi(\cdot)$ that incorporates intrinsic parameters and distortion models. This completes the bidirectional mapping between physical camera pixels and the unified spherical panoramic space.
- **Parallelization and Feature Management:** The USPM enables fast panorama construction through parallel remapping (Parallel Unit 1), where each camera's projection is processed independently on multi-core CPUs. Global features (lightweight FAST keypoints) are extracted from the synthesized panorama. For depth initialization, each panoramic feature is reprojected back to its corresponding physical cameras, enabling cross-camera triangulation. To enhance triangulation robustness, cylindrical undistortion preprocessing (from D2SLAM) is selectively applied to non-parallel camera pairs. A dual parallel unit framework further accelerates the pipeline, with Parallel Unit 2 handling adaptive undistortion and Parallel Unit 3 performing parallel epipolar matching (based on SVO 2.0) and depth estimation across all camera pairs.

**B. Hierarchical Omnidirectional Feature Alignment (HOFA)**

The HOFA framework, built upon SVO 2.0 principles, employs a task-adaptive strategy for robust semi-direct tracking within the USPM.

- **Feature Alignment for Known Depth:** For features with established 3D positions (e.g., pre-triangulated), a panoramic-level semi-direct pixel patch matching is performed. This alignment predicts initial feature positions in the current frame and refines them through photometric optimization, utilizing inverse depth information from physical cameras.
- **Inverse Depth Estimation for New Features:** For newly detected features lacking pre-triangulated depth, HOFA performs cross-camera inverse depth estimation. This involves constrained photometric optimization, where the inverse depth is estimated within a unified feasible inverse depth set.
- **Adaptive Range Filtering:** To define this feasible inverse depth set, an analytical relationship is established between a map point's panoramic yaw angle and its inverse depth. By enforcing that the map point must lie within each camera's horizontal FOV, a valid depth range is derived for each contributing camera. Only cameras whose FOV intersects the epipolar line are considered valid. The intersection of these valid ranges forms the unified depth set, which is then passed to a Vogiatzis depth filter for stable convergence, improving estimation efficiency and accuracy.

**C. Multi-Camera-Adapted Error State Kalman Filter (ESKF) Backend**

The backend is designed for efficient integration of visual and inertial measurements, ensuring accurate real-time state estimation on CPU resources.

- **State Definition and IMU Propagation:** The system adopts the 15-dimensional IMU-centric state vector from SchurVINS, comprising IMU orientation, position, velocity, and accelerometer/gyroscope biases. IMU state propagation and covariance prediction are performed using 4th-order Runge-Kutta integration.
- **Spherical Bearing Vector Residual:** Instead of conventional 2D perspective reprojection residuals, Sphere-VIO employs a 3D spherical bearing vector residual. This is tailored for the USPM and omnidirectional projections, mitigating the strong nonlinearity associated with direct pixel errors in such wide-FOV systems. The residual is defined as the difference between the observed unit bearing vector and the predicted unit bearing vector for a map point. Observations exceeding a predefined residual threshold are discarded as outliers.
- **ESKF Update and Schur Complement:** To perform the ESKF update, Jacobians of the residuals with respect to system states and map points are derived. All residuals and Jacobians are stacked to form a global linearized system. The Schur complement-based ESKF update, adopted from SchurVINS, is applied. This method efficiently marginalizes landmark states, avoiding high-dimensional joint optimization and thereby reducing computational overhead despite the use of 3D residuals. Map points are updated independently following state refinement.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experimental evaluation of Sphere-VIO involved comprehensive comparisons on public benchmarks and a custom omnidirectional dataset, alongside ablation studies focusing on diverse camera configurations and computational efficiency.

**A. Comparison With Baselines on Public Datasets**

Sphere-VIO was evaluated against state-of-the-art VIO systems (MAVIS, ORB-SLAM3, VINS-Fusion, SchurVINS) with loop-closure disabled.

- **EuRoC MAV Dataset (Pinhole, low texture, aggressive motion):** Sphere-VIO achieved an average Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE of 0.1072 m. This result surpassed VINS-Fusion by 48.3% but was numerically higher than ORB-SLAM3 (0.0400 m) and SchurVINS (0.0779 m). The authors attribute this to Sphere-VIO’s spherical bearing residual being optimized for wide-FOV multi-camera setups rather than conventional pinhole scenarios that benefit more from planar reprojection residuals.
- **TUM-VI Dataset (Indoor, large-range motion, variable illumination):** Sphere-VIO obtained an ATE RMSE of 0.1518 m. It outperformed the filter-based SchurVINS by 48.8% (0.2965 m) and the graph-optimized VINS-Fusion by 63.6% (0.4171 m). The unified spherical formulation demonstrated robustness in long-corridor environments, where stereo-based alternatives experienced significant drift.
- **HILTI 2022 Dataset (Sparse textures, intensive movement, uneven lighting):** Sphere-VIO ranked second overall with an ATE RMSE of 0.1821 m, trailing only MAVIS (0.0980 m). It outperformed VINS-Fusion, ORB-SLAM3, and SchurVINS by 35.3%, 39.7%, and 55.7% respectively. The multi-camera spherical modeling contributed to stable tracking under harsh conditions, in contrast to the severe drift observed in narrow-FOV stereo pipelines.

**B. Ablation Experiment**

1. **Comparison on Omnidirectional Multi-Camera Dataset:** A custom multi-fisheye dataset (Seeker OMNI-D rig with four 206° fisheye cameras) was used.
 - **Baseline Failures:** ORB-SLAM3 and MAVIS could not adapt to FOVs exceeding 180°. VINS-Fusion and SchurVINS, despite supporting wide-FOV models, failed due to challenges in cross-camera matching from divergent orientations and partial overlaps.
 - **Sphere-VIO Performance:** The stereo variant achieved an average ATE RMSE of 0.1797 m. The multi-camera version, leveraging overlapping coverage, improved to 0.1230 m. On an Intel NUC 13 embedded platform, performance was 0.1341 m, confirming feasibility for resource-constrained deployment. The slightly higher RMSE compared to public datasets was attributed to a smaller 5 cm stereo baseline and occasional duplicate IMU timestamps.

2. **Spherical Triangulation Across Diverse Camera Setups:** The initialization method’s spherical triangulation was evaluated across four configurations: Seeker Omnidirection, HILTI 2022, Seeker Stereo, and TUM-VI Stereo.
 - The Seeker Stereo configuration achieved valid static triangulation only in a 90° horizontal FOV, approximately one-quarter of the full omnidirectional panorama. Features outside this region relied on depth estimation during VIO tracking, introducing uncertainty.
 - In contrast, the four-camera Seeker Omnidirection configuration provided uniform 360° full-coverage triangulation, establishing a more robust initialization.
 - Consistent results on HILTI 2022 and TUM-VI Stereo further demonstrated the generalizability of Sphere-VIO and the universality of the USPM framework to diverse multi-camera layouts.

**C. Computational Efficiency On Multi-Camera Dataset**

Per-frame latency was evaluated on HILTI 2022 (40 Hz cameras, real-time limit ≤ 0.025 s/frame) and the custom dataset (20 Hz, real-time limit ≤ 0.05 s/frame).

- **HILTI 2022:** Stereo VINS-Fusion averaged 0.0257 s/frame, exceeding the real-time limit. SchurVINS achieved 0.0036 s/frame. Multi-camera Sphere-VIO, processing four streams with CLAHE preprocessing, averaged 0.0122 s per frame, which is approximately half the real-time threshold. This efficiency was attributed to the use of three parallel processing units utilizing multi-core CPUs.
- **Custom Dataset:** Sphere-VIO achieved 0.0138 s/frame on a laptop and 0.0180 s/frame on an Intel NUC 13. Both results were well within the 0.05 s/frame limit, validating its practical deployment feasibility on embedded hardware.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

Sphere-VIO presents a notable contribution to the field of Visual-Inertial Odometry, particularly for multi-camera systems. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several key aspects:

- **Enhanced Generality and Adaptability:** The Unified Spherical Panorama Model (USPM) is a core innovation that allows Sphere-VIO to support a broad spectrum of camera models (pinhole, fisheye, etc.) and arbitrary multi-camera configurations without requiring bespoke adaptations for each setup. This flexibility is crucial for wider adoption in robotics, enabling a single VIO framework to be deployed across diverse platforms equipped with heterogeneous sensing hardware, from specialized omnidirectional rigs to standard multi-stereo arrays. This reduces development overhead and increases the versatility of VIO solutions.

- **Improved Robustness in Challenging Environments:** By unifying camera observations into a spherical representation and employing the Hierarchical Omnidirectional Feature Alignment (HOFA) procedure with adaptive depth range filtering, Sphere-VIO achieves more robust cross-camera feature matching and stable depth estimation. This capability is particularly impactful in complex real-world scenarios characterized by challenging illumination, sparse textures, aggressive motion, and divergent camera orientations, where conventional methods often struggle with tracking loss or scale drift. The spherical bearing residual in the ESKF further contributes to robust estimation by being better suited for omnidirectional projections than traditional pixel-based errors.

- **Real-time Performance on Resource-Constrained Devices:** A significant impact of Sphere-VIO is its ability to perform real-time state estimation on CPU-only embedded platforms, as demonstrated on the Intel NUC 13. This is achieved through its lightweight, filter-based ESKF backend, which efficiently manages computational complexity via Schur complement marginalization, and its parallelized front-end processing. This capability broadens the applicability of robust VIO to a wider range of low-cost, low-power robotic systems, such as drones, mobile robots, and augmented/virtual reality devices, that cannot rely on powerful GPUs.

- **Scientific and Technical Advancement:** The specific methodological contributions, including the bidirectional mapping of the USPM for fast panorama synthesis, the HOFA framework for robust feature alignment and joint depth estimation, and the multi-camera-adapted ESKF utilizing spherical bearing residuals, advance the state-of-the-art in multi-camera visual-inertial perception. These innovations provide a principled approach to tackle the complexities of heterogeneous multi-camera integration.

- **Foundation for Future SLAM Systems:** As a pure VIO system, Sphere-VIO inherently suffers from long-term drift. However, by providing a robust, efficient, and generalized VIO frontend, it lays a solid foundation for the development of complete Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems. The authors explicitly state their future work will incorporate lightweight loop closure, suggesting Sphere-VIO’s potential to evolve into a comprehensive and highly adaptable SLAM solution while retaining its core strengths of CPU-only efficiency and multi-camera compatibility. This progression could lead to more robust and deployable autonomous navigation systems.
