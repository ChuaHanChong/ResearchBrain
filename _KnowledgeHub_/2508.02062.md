# RICL: Adding In-Context Adaptability to Pre-Trained Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of Pennsylvania and University of British Columbia introduce Retraining for In-Context Learning (RICL), a method to inject in-context learning capabilities into pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This enables VLAs to adapt to new tasks and environments using a small number of demonstrations, achieving an aggregate complete task success rate of 31.25% on novel real-world manipulation tasks, compared to 2.5% for the baseline, and further finetuning boosted this to 61.67%.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack easy adaptability to new, unseen tasks or environments without extensive parameter finetuning.
- Improving VLA models often requires cumbersome, data-intensive, and computationally expensive finetuning on new demonstration datasets, which is a barrier for end-users.
- Unlike large language models (LLMs) that exhibit in-context learning (ICL) and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), current VLA models do not naturally acquire these flexible adaptation capabilities.

## Method

- **RICL** is a post-training method that finetunes only the language model component of a pre-trained VLA (e.g., π0-FAST-DROID), keeping the image encoder frozen to inject ==in-context learning (ICL)== abilities.
- The approach constructs VLA input context by ==retrieving== the most relevant demonstrations from a small dataset using image embeddings, augmenting the query with images, states, prompts, and action chunks from these 'neighbors'.
- An ==action interpolation layer== combines the LLM's output with a distance-weighted one-hot encoding of the closest retrieved action, allowing for both direct imitation and generalization.

## Results

- RICL-π0-FAST-DROID, using 20 demonstrations for ICL, achieved an aggregate complete task success rate of **31.25%** on 8 novel real-world manipulation tasks, a substantial increase over the vanilla π0-FAST-DROID baseline's **2.5%**.
- When further finetuned on the same 20 demonstrations, RICL-π0-FAST-DROID-finetuned reached **61.67%** aggregate complete task success, nearly doubling the performance of a finetuned baseline VLA (**31.67%**).
- The RICL-VLA demonstrated robust language grounding for unseen objects and inferred novel grasps and motions from context, and showed no degradation of performance on familiar tasks when provided with irrelevant priming demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - In-context learning (ICL) capabilities can be successfully injected into existing pre-trained VLA models post-hoc, bridging the gap between LLM adaptability and robotic foundation models.
> - RICL-trained VLAs can exhibit emergent abilities to synthesize novel action sequences not explicitly present in the retrieval context, suggesting a capacity to generalize beyond direct imitation.
> - The RICL training process effectively primes VLAs, making them significantly more receptive to subsequent task-specific finetuning and leading to greater performance gains compared to finetuning a non-RICL VLA.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is currently undergoing a significant transformation with the emergence of general-purpose "Vision-Language-Action" (VLA) models. These models aim to serve as foundational models for robotics, much like large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have become for their respective domains. VLAs are designed to handle a wide range of tasks and environments, addressing persistent challenges such as data scarcity, robustness, and generalization.

However, a critical limitation of current VLA models, often pre-trained with imitation learning objectives on specific demonstration datasets, is their lack of easy adaptability to new, unseen tasks or environments. "Improving" these models typically necessitates finetuning their parameters on new demonstration datasets, a process that is often cumbersome, data-intensive, and requires computational resources and expertise not readily available to every end-user.

This situation presents a stark contrast to the rapid adoption and versatility of large language models. A key factor in LLMs' success is their emergent ability for "in-context learning" (ICL) and "Retrieval-Augmented Generation" (RAG). ICL allows LLMs to learn new tasks by simply providing a few examples within the input prompt, without any parameter updates. RAG further enhances this by enabling automated retrieval of relevant information from a large corpus to augment the context. These capabilities provide a highly intuitive and powerful interface for quick adaptation.

The present work directly addresses the fundamental gap: **pre-trained VLA models, unlike LLMs, do not naturally acquire ICL abilities due to their training paradigm.** Existing research has explored ICL for robotics using LLMs and VLMs, but these methods often operate in an open-loop fashion or discard crucial visual information, limiting their adaptability in real-world robotic tasks. Other work on generalist agents has explored ICL capabilities in simulated environments, but not specifically for injecting these abilities into *pre-trained VLAs* for general-purpose robotic manipulation.

RICL bridges this critical gap by demonstrating that it is possible to *post-hoc inject* ICL capabilities into existing pre-trained VLA models. By doing so, it aims to imbue robotic foundation models with the same kind of flexible, user-friendly adaptability that has revolutionized natural language processing. This moves robotics closer to a future where end-users can easily teach robots new skills with minimal effort, without needing to delve into complex machine learning pipelines. The paper directly builds upon and specializes the authors' previous work on REGENT, adapting its principles of retrieval-augmented in-context learning to the specific challenges and architectures of pre-trained VLA models in real-world robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enhance the practical utility and accessibility of pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics by making them easily adaptable to novel tasks and environments.

The core motivation stems from a critical observation: while multi-task VLA models show increasing promise as generalist foundation models for robotics, their current mode of improvement—parameter finetuning on new datasets—is a significant barrier to widespread adoption and user-friendliness. For these models to be truly useful to an end-user, there must be a straightforward mechanism to teach them new skills and boost their performance without necessitating deep technical expertise or substantial computational resources.

The authors pose a precise research question: **"How can we inject in-context learning abilities into a pre-trained VLA?"**

The key objectives and motivations can be summarized as follows:

- **Enable User-Friendly Adaptation:** To provide an easy means for end-users to improve a VLA's performance on new tasks using a small number of demonstrations (e.g., 10-20), without requiring any parameter finetuning. This directly addresses the limitation of current VLAs that necessitate extensive retraining for adaptation.
- **Leverage ICL and RAG:** To transfer the successful paradigm of in-context learning (ICL) and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), which has proven highly effective in large language models, to the domain of embodied AI and robotics. The goal is to allow VLAs to learn from examples provided in their context.
- **Boost Performance on Novel Tasks:** To significantly improve a VLA's ability to handle unseen objects, perform novel motions, and operate in new scenes that were not present in its original training data, purely through in-context adaptation.
- **Pain-Free VLA Improvement:** To develop a "post-hoc" method that can be applied to existing, off-the-shelf pre-trained VLAs, priming them to effectively use their context rather than requiring training from scratch.
- **Explore Finetuning Synergies:** To investigate whether combining RICL with targeted finetuning on task-specific demonstrations can lead to even greater performance improvements, following the "finetune like you pretrain" paradigm.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** To release code and model weights to foster further research and facilitate the integration of ICL capabilities into future VLA developments, making this technology accessible to the wider robotics community.

Ultimately, the motivation is to make general-purpose VLA models truly versatile and accessible, allowing them to rapidly acquire new skills and generalize effectively in dynamic, real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this paper's methodology is **Retraining for In-Context Learning (RICL)**, a post-training recipe designed to inject ICL abilities into pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The approach leverages concepts from retrieval-augmented generation and in-context learning, adapting them for the unique challenges of robotic control.

#### 3.1. Base VLA Model

The chosen base VLA for this work is **π0 -FAST-DROID**, a state-of-the-art autoregressive VLA. This model is built by finetuning the PaliGemma Vision-Language Model (which combines a SigLIP image encoder and the Gemma 3B LLM) on a large number of robot trajectories. Specifically, π0 -FAST-DROID was further finetuned on the DROID dataset, collected using the Franka DROID platform across various labs and scenes, making it robust and generalizable to different robot embodiments and environments. The π0 -FAST model uses the FAST tokenizer to integrate action tokens into the text token space for autoregressive prediction.

#### 3.2. RICL Finetuning Process

RICL's primary goal is to convert a generally deployable VLA like π0 -FAST-DROID into one with in-context adaptation capabilities.

1. **Architecture for RICL-VLAs:** The system architecture of RICL-π0 -FAST involves an image encoder, a language model (LLM), and an action interpolation layer (Figure 2). During RICL training, only the LLM component of the base VLA is finetuned, while the image encoder (a DINO-v2 model) is kept frozen. This choice is likely due to the robustness of DINO-v2 features and to focus the adaptation on the sequence processing capabilities of the LLM.

2. **Context Construction for In-Context Learning:**
 - **Query Information:** At each timestep `t`, the VLA receives a query consisting of three images (top, side, wrist), a language prompt describing the task, and proprioceptive state `s_t`.
 - **Retrieval Mechanism:** For a given query, RICL performs retrieval from a small "priming" demonstration dataset (or later, task-specific demonstrations). It embeds only the top query image using the frozen DINO-v2 encoder and compares it with embeddings of top images from the demonstrations using an `ℓ2` distance metric.
 - **Context Augmentation:** The most relevant retrieved demonstrations (referred to as "neighbors") are then placed into the VLA's context, with the closest neighbor positioned leftmost and subsequent neighbors following. These retrieved examples include images, states, the text prompt, and action chunks.

3. **Action Prediction with Interpolation Layer:**
 - The RICL-VLA predicts an action chunk `ˆa_t`. A key component is an "action interpolation layer" (Figure 2, green box). This layer combines the one-hot encoding of the *closest retrieved action* (`a'`) with the raw output of the finetuned LLM (`πθ(retrieved, query)`).
 - The combination is distance-weighted: `πθ_RICL-VLA(retrieved, query) = e^(-λd) * one-hot(a') + (1 - e^(-λd)) * σ(πθ(retrieved, query))`, where `d` is the `ℓ2` distance between the query's top image embedding and its nearest neighbor's top image embedding, and `λ` is a scaling factor. This mechanism ensures that the model can leverage direct imitation from the closest example while still allowing the LLM to generalize and synthesize.

4. **Training Objective:** Unlike REGENT, which minimizes loss over all retrieved and query actions, RICL focuses on minimizing the cross-entropy loss *only over the query's predicted action chunk* corresponding to its prompt and state (`s_t`) tuple. This primes the VLA to use its context effectively for generating its own actions.

#### 3.3. Priming Data for RICL

To train (prime) the RICL-VLA, a specific dataset of 400 demonstrations was collected using the Franka DROID platform, covering 20 distinct pick-and-place tasks. For each state in these demonstrations, it serves as a query, and four neighbors are retrieved from the other 19 demonstrations to construct training input sequences. This ensures that the training process mirrors the deployment scenario.

#### 3.4. Deployment and Further Finetuning

- **In-Context Adaptability (RICL-VLA):** After RICL finetuning, the model (RICL-π0 -FAST-DROID) can be deployed on *new* tasks. For a new task, an end-user provides a small number (10-20) of demonstrations. The RICL-VLA then uses its retrieval mechanism to fetch relevant parts of these demonstrations into its context, leveraging ICL to perform the new task without any parameter updates.
- **Further Finetuning of RICL-VLA:** The paper also explores a "finetuning like you pretrain" strategy. The RICL-VLA is *further finetuned* on the same 10-20 task-specific demonstrations that would typically only be used for retrieval. This finetuning uses the same retrieval-augmented objective as the initial RICL process.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Evaluation Tasks:** 8 diverse real-world manipulation tasks, including:
 - **Unseen Objects:** tasks like "pick up the pokeball," "move the idli plate," "move the squeegee."
 - **Novel Motions:** tasks requiring unfamiliar grasps (idli plate, bagel) or specific dragging motions (squeegee).
 - **New Scenes:** tasks repeated in a "kitchen sink" environment with new cameras/lighting.
 - **Long-Tail Tasks:** tasks involving variations of objects seen in the DROID dataset but appearing infrequently (e.g., "push the lever on the toaster," "open the door of the bottom shelf").
 - **Longer-Horizon Task:** "pick up the bagel and put it in the toaster."
- **Baselines:** Vanilla π0 -FAST-DROID, 'Retrieve and play' (1-nearest neighbor), and a trained-from-scratch Diffusion Policy.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** 10 test rollouts per method per task, with randomized initial positions and orientations, measuring full task success and intermediate checkpoint completion rates.
- **Ablations:** Experiments on the impact of the number of demonstrations (5, 10, 20) for retrieval/finetuning.
- **Robustness:** Qualitative evaluation with human perturbation of objects during execution.
- **No Loss of Capabilities Test:** To ensure RICL doesn't degrade performance on familiar tasks without specific retrieval data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that RICL significantly enhances the adaptability and performance of pre-trained VLA models.

1. **RICL-VLA Outperforms Baseline VLA (without finetuning):**
 - RICL-π0 -FAST-DROID, when provided with 20 task-specific demonstrations for RAG and ICL, dramatically outperforms the vanilla π0 -FAST-DROID baseline on novel tasks.
 - Across all evaluated tasks, the aggregate complete task success rate for RICL-π0 -FAST-DROID was **31.25%**, compared to a mere **2.5%** for π0 -FAST-DROID.
 - Similarly, the aggregate checkpoint completion rate for RICL-π0 -FAST-DROID reached **83.75%**, significantly higher than π0 -FAST-DROID's **21.25%**.
 - Qualitative results highlight RICL's improved language grounding for unseen objects and its ability to infer novel grasps and motions purely from contextual information (e.g., squeegee tasks, sink-idliplate, bagel, toaster, door).

2. **Elicited Latent Actions:**
 - Surprisingly, RICL-π0 -FAST-DROID was observed to predict and execute action sequences that were not explicitly present in the retrieval dataset. For instance, in the "idliplate" task, it pushed the plate from the side, while all retrieval demonstrations involved dipping the gripper into depressions. This suggests an emergent ability to synthesize or generalize beyond direct imitation, leveraging underlying knowledge to accomplish tasks (also noted in the "toaster" task).

3. **Significant Performance Boost with Further Finetuning:**
 - When RICL-π0 -FAST-DROID was further finetuned on the *same* 20 task-specific demonstrations it used for retrieval, its performance improved substantially.
 - The aggregate complete task success rate for **RICL-π0 -FAST-DROID-finetuned** reached **61.67%**, nearly double that of **π0 -FAST-DROID-finetuned** (which achieved 31.67%). This indicates that the RICL pre-training phase better primes the VLA for subsequent task-specific learning.
 - Remarkably, the ICL-only RICL-VLA (31.25% success) performed comparably to the *finetuned* baseline VLA (31.67%), underscoring the power of ICL alone in achieving a performance level that traditionally required parameter updates.
 - The authors hypothesize that the RICL-VLA, having been primed for context utilization, can allocate its capacity more effectively for interpolating between retrieved examples rather than memorizing data, akin to parameter-efficient RAG LLMs.
 - Qualitative evaluations (Figure 5, 9) demonstrate that RICL-π0 -FAST-DROID-finetuned exhibits strong reactivity and robustness to human-induced perturbations during task execution.

4. **Ablation on Number of Demonstrations:**
 - Experiments on the "idliplate" task revealed that a minimum of **10 demonstrations** was generally required for effective in-context adaptation. Using too few (e.g., 5) caused the RICL-VLA to revert to baseline behavior.
 - Performance generally improved with more retrieval demonstrations, demonstrating the scalability of the approach with increased contextual information.
 - RICL-π0 -FAST-DROID-finetuned consistently outperformed π0 -FAST-DROID-finetuned across all tested numbers of demonstrations.

5. **No Loss of Capabilities:**
 - Testing RICL-π0 -FAST-DROID on known tasks with irrelevant (random priming) demonstrations in the retrieval buffer showed no degradation in performance compared to the original π0 -FAST-DROID (both achieved 80% success). This confirms that injecting ICL abilities does not compromise the VLA's original generalization capabilities.

6. **Baseline Comparisons:**
 - Other baselines like 'Retrieve and play' (1-NN) and trained-from-scratch Diffusion Policy showed very low success rates on simpler tasks, highlighting the sophistication and necessity of the RICL approach for robust adaptation.
 - Retrieval latency is negligible (<1ms), and inference time for RICL is only about 1.33x slower than the base VLA, demonstrating its practical efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RICL represents a significant step forward in the field of robot learning and embodied AI, with profound implications for the usability, adaptability, and widespread deployment of robotic systems.

1. **Democratization of Robot Learning:** RICL enables a highly intuitive and user-friendly interface for teaching robots new tasks. By allowing end-users to adapt pre-trained VLA models with a small number of demonstrations (10-20) and without requiring any parameter finetuning, it significantly lowers the barrier to entry for customizing robotic behaviors. This democratizes robot learning, moving it beyond the realm of expert researchers and into the hands of a broader user base.

2. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The ability of RICL-VLAs to adapt to unseen objects, novel motions, and new scenes purely through in-context learning directly addresses critical limitations in robot generalization. This makes robots more robust and versatile in dynamic, unstructured, and novel real-world environments, where it's impractical to pre-train for every conceivable scenario.

3. **Bridging the Gap to Foundation Models:** This work successfully translates the powerful paradigms of In-Context Learning (ICL) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) from large language models (LLMs) to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotics. By endowing VLAs with LLM-like adaptability, RICL accelerates the realization of truly generalist robotic foundation models that can learn and evolve with minimal intervention.

4. **Computational Efficiency and Practicality:** The method avoids expensive, full-model finetuning for every new task, saving significant computational resources and time. The efficient retrieval mechanism and minimal increase in inference time make RICL a practically viable solution for real-time robotic deployment. The finding that an ICL-only RICL-VLA can achieve comparable performance to a *finetuned* base VLA is particularly impactful, highlighting the power of context-based adaptation.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - **Scaling Up:** The paper explicitly points to future work on scaling RICL with more priming demonstrations and larger model parameters, suggesting avenues for even greater performance.
 - **Diverse Motions and Datasets:** Addressing limitations in handling significantly novel motions (e.g., playing tennis) by including more diverse actions in the priming dataset.
 - **Human Demonstrations:** Reducing the reliance on teleoperated demonstrations by integrating learning from human videos, further increasing scalability and accessibility.
 - **Emergent Abilities:** The observation of "elicited latent actions" suggests that RICL-VLAs might not merely imitate but also synthesize and generalize intelligently, opening up research into understanding and leveraging such emergent capabilities.

6. **Open Science Contribution:** The release of code and model weights for RICL-π0 -FAST is a valuable contribution to the research community, enabling reproducibility, benchmarking, and accelerating further innovation in in-context learning for robotics.

In essence, RICL makes VLA models significantly more flexible and easier to interact with, pushing robotics closer to a future where intelligent agents can quickly acquire new skills from limited examples, much like humans do. This paves the way for a new generation of adaptable and widely deployable robotic systems.
