# PLACE: Proximity Learning of Articulation and Contact in 3D Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> PLACE synthesizes realistic 3D human bodies inside 3D scenes by explicitly modeling body-scene proximity. A two-stage basis-point-set scheme encodes the local scene, then encodes minimum distances from selected scene points to the body surface; a conditional VAE generates this proximity feature, a regressor produces body vertices, and interaction-based optimization fits SMPL-X. Perceptual studies rate it more natural than prior work, nearly matching real captured interactions.

## Problem

- Prior scene-conditioned body generators encode scenes as RGB, depth, or semantic maps, leaving 3D structure and occluded geometry implicit and hard to constrain.
- Without an explicit contact representation, earlier generators produce bodies that float or inter-penetrate furniture, falling short of captured human-scene interaction realism.
- Existing affordance-based interaction representations rely on dense affordance maps, scene semantics, action labels, or class annotations, making them costly and scene-specific.

## Method

- Two-stage ==basis-point-set (BPS)== encoding: fixed random basis points in a unit sphere select local scene vertices as scene BPS, whose minimum distances to the **SMPL-X** body surface form the interaction feature.
- A ==conditional VAE== with a scene-feature autoencoder generates body proximity features, with multi-layer intermediate feature concatenation for stronger scene conditioning, plus an MLP regressor predicting body vertices.
- Training combines reconstruction, robust ==Charbonnier KL==, collision, and feet-only contact losses; interaction-based optimization (**AdvOptim**) fits SMPL-X using a data-driven BPS-feature reconstruction term.

## Results

- Unary user study scores **3.71** on PROX and **3.84** on MP3D versus **3.47** and **3.23** for PSI, approaching the **3.94** ground-truth rating.
- Binary study: users prefer PLACE over PSI **70.1%** on MP3D and **69.0%** on PROX, and rate it against real PROX ground truth at **48.5%** versus **51.5%**.
- Physical plausibility reaches **0.98** non-collision and **0.99** contact on PROX with larger cluster size **2.72**, exceeding PSI's **0.94** and **2.53** after geometry-aware fitting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encoding interaction as distances between scene basis points and body surface gives a compact, geometry-only representation consistent across arbitrary bodies and scene layouts.
> - Restricting the contact loss mask to the feet suppresses floating better than full-body contact terms, and collision loss can shrink pose diversity.
> - Distance-field representations still miss thin structures such as tabletops and are too sparse for hand-object detail, motivating coarse-to-fine encoding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The work addresses a challenge within computer graphics and vision concerning the automated generation of realistic 3D human bodies naturally posed within high-fidelity 3D environments. Historically, achieving high realism in human-scene interactions for applications such as augmented/virtual reality, special movie effects, and synthetic data generation has often necessitated manual animation.

Prior automated approaches for generating human bodies in 3D scenes have utilized various scene representations, including 2D images, depth maps, or semantic maps, often coupled with parametric human models like SMPL-X. While these methods offer straightforward integration into deep learning architectures, the authors identify a limitation: their generated human-scene interactions frequently lack naturalness and physical plausibility. This is attributed to an insufficient explicit representation of the 3D scene structure and, crucially, the proximity and contact relationships between the human body and the environment. Without an explicit 3D understanding of proximity, enforcing constraints such as non-inter-penetration and appropriate contact becomes challenging, particularly for occluded regions of the scene.

This paper positions itself by proposing a novel interaction representation that directly models physical contact and proximity in 3D. It builds upon and extends techniques like Basis Point Sets (BPS) for 3D object representation to explicitly capture human-scene interactions. The work differentiates itself from methods that rely on scene semantics or specific action labels by proposing a purely geometry-based approach. It aims to overcome the limitations of previous methods, such as those that generate 3D body skeletons based on scene appearance and depth (e.g., Li et al.) or use conditional Variational Autoencoders (cVAEs) to generate human meshes from RGB-D images and semantic segmentation (e.g., Zhang et al., also known as PSI). By focusing on explicit 3D proximity, PLACE seeks to achieve a higher degree of realism and physical plausibility in synthesized human-scene interactions, moving towards fully automated synthesis that rivals manually created or captured real-world interactions.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the automated synthesis of realistic 3D human bodies naturally situated within 3D environments. The authors identify a significant limitation in existing methods: their generated human-scene interactions often lack naturalness and physical plausibility. This deficiency is primarily motivated by the observation that current approaches frequently omit an explicit and effective representation of how humans interact with the 3D world, specifically through body-scene contact and proximity.

The core motivation is the belief that explicit modeling of the contact relationship and proximal relationships between the human body and the surrounding 3D scene is essential for synthesizing realistic human-scene interactions. Existing methods, which commonly rely on 2D scene representations like images, depth maps, or semantic maps, often fail to explicitly capture the nuanced 3D structure of the scene and the precise proximity to the human body. This omission, particularly for regions occluded from camera views, makes it difficult to enforce fundamental 3D constraints such as avoiding inter-penetration and ensuring proper contact.

To address these identified problems, the paper sets forth several specific objectives:

1. **Develop an explicit interaction representation:** The primary objective is to propose a novel representation that coherently models 3D scene geometry and person-scene contact relationships. This representation is intended to be purely geometry-based, efficient, compact, and consistent across diverse human bodies and scene structures. It is also designed to operate without requiring explicit scene semantics, action type labels, or affordance class annotations.
2. **Design a generative model (PLACE):** Based on the proposed interaction representation, the authors aim to develop a generative model capable of synthesizing plausible contact and proximal relationships between a human body and a 3D scene.
3. **Infer expressive 3D human bodies:** A subsequent objective is to infer detailed and expressive 3D human body meshes that naturally comply with the synthesized human-scene proximity relationships. This includes refining the body mesh through an optimization process that incorporates the novel interaction representation.
4. **Demonstrate superior performance:** A key objective is to quantitatively and qualitatively demonstrate that the proposed method significantly improves upon the state-of-the-art in terms of naturalness, diversity, and physical plausibility of the synthesized human-scene interactions. The goal is for the generated results to approach the realism of real human-scene interactions.
5. **Reduce modality dependence:** The method is motivated to achieve these advancements without relying on additional input modalities (e.g., depth or mesh semantics) that are often required by previous works.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to make a substantial contribution towards fully automated and realistic 3D human body synthesis in complex 3D environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named PLACE (Proximity Learning of Articulation and Contact in 3D Environments), focuses on explicitly modeling the proximity between the human body and the 3D scene. This is achieved through a novel, purely geometry-based interaction representation and a generative model based on a conditional variational autoencoder (cVAE).

#### 3.1. Proximity Representation for Interaction

The core of the methodology is a two-stage Basis Point Set (BPS) encoding scheme, inspired by Prokudin et al., to represent the scene, the human body, and their interactions. BPS encodes a 3D object into a feature vector comprising minimum distances from a fixed set of random basis points to the object's surface.

- **Stage 1: BPS encoding for 3D Scene:**
 - A fixed set of $N = 10^4$ uniformly random basis points, $V_o \subset R^{N \times 3}$, are defined within a unit sphere. These points remain constant for all scenes during training and testing.
 - Input scene meshes are normalized into this unit sphere.
 - For each point in $V_o$, its nearest neighbor on the normalized scene mesh vertices is identified, and the minimum distance is computed. These distances form the scene feature, $x_s \in R^{N \times 1}$.
 - The $N$ selected scene vertices (the nearest neighbors identified for $V_o$) form $V_s \subset R^{N \times 3}$, which serve as the scene BPS for encoding the body in the subsequent stage.
 - To capture local scene relevant to the human, a "local scene" concept is introduced. A virtual 3D cage is specified around the human body, and only scene vertices within this cage are considered. Points are regularly sampled on the top and four side faces of this cage (representing "ceiling" and "walls") and included in the scene BPS. This ensures that the scene BPS can encode the full human body, even in simple environments like a floor.

- **Stage 2: BPS encoding for 3D Body:**
 - The scene BPS, $V_s$ (including points from the virtual cage surfaces), is then used to encode the 3D human body. The human body is represented by an SMPL-X mesh with vertices $V_b \subset R^{10475 \times 3}$.
 - The body feature, $x_b \in R^{N \times 1}$, is derived by computing the minimum distance from each point in the scene BPS $V_s$ to its nearest point on the human body mesh $V_b$.
 - This $x_b$ is scene-dependent and explicitly encodes the contact and proximal relationships between the human body and the scene, serving as the interaction representation.
 - The consistency of this representation across different bodies and scene structures is maintained because the initial basis points $V_o$ are fixed, ensuring that each dimension in $x_s$ and $x_b$ corresponds to a consistent spatial location.

#### 3.2. PLACE Generative Model

The model architecture integrates a conditional variational autoencoder (cVAE) to generate plausible contact and proximal relationships, followed by a regressor to infer the full body mesh.

- **Network Architecture (Figure 3 in paper):**
 - A scene feature auto-encoder, E, processes the scene feature $x_s$.
 - A cVAE, denoted as G (Generator), takes the scene feature $x_s$ and a latent variable $z$ (sampled from a standard normal distribution during inference) to generate the body feature $x_b$. During training, $x_b$ is encoded and then reconstructed. Intermediate features from the decoder of E are passed to the decoder of G to provide stronger scene conditioning.
 - A body mesh vertex regressor, H, is a multilayer perceptron. It takes the reconstructed body feature ($x_b$) and the scene BPS ($V_s$, which is processed by its own auto-encoder F) as input to directly produce the 3D vertex coordinates of the full human body mesh, $V_b$. It also regresses a global 3D translation $\Delta V^{rec}_b$. All coordinates are relative to the rescaled 3D cage center.

- **Losses and Learning:**
 The training loss (Eq. 1) consists of four terms:
 1. **Reconstruction Term ($L_{rec}$):** An L1 loss that measures the difference between the input and reconstructed scene feature ($x_s - x_s^{rec}$), body feature ($x_b - x_b^{rec}$), and vertex-wise body mesh ($V_b - \tilde{V}_b^{rec}$).
 2. **KL-divergence Term ($L_{KL}$):** Uses the Charbonnier function on the KL-divergence between the inference posterior $q(z|x)$ and a standard normal prior $N(0, I)$ to prevent posterior collapse.
 3. **Collision Term ($L_{coll}$):** Minimizes inter-penetration between the generated body and the scene. It is the average of negative signed distance field (SDF) values for body vertices that penetrate the scene.
 4. **Contact Term ($L_{contact}$):** Encourages physical contact for specific body parts. It sums the Geman-McClure error function of distances from body vertices identified as contact points (using mask C, limited to feet in this work) to the scene mesh. This term is applied after 75% of training epochs.

- **Placing People in Novel Scenes (Inference):**
 1. A 3D cage is randomly positioned and trimmed within a novel scene and transformed into the unit sphere.
 2. The scene BPS ($V_s$) and scene feature ($x_s$) are computed from this local scene.
 3. A latent vector $z$ is sampled from $N(0, I)$, and the cVAE (G) generates a body feature ($x_b$).
 4. The regressor (H) uses $V_s$ and $x_b$ to produce body mesh vertices, which are then transformed back to the original scene coordinates.

- **Interaction-based Optimization (Post-processing):**
 To refine the directly regressed body vertices into a valid SMPL-X mesh and enhance physical plausibility, an optimization step is performed. This optimization (Eq. 6) minimizes a loss function $L(\theta)$ with respect to SMPL-X parameters $\theta$ (global translation, orientation, body shape, pose, hand pose).
 The loss terms include:
 - Vertex-wise reconstruction between the regressed vertices and the optimized SMPL-X mesh.
 - BPS feature reconstruction between the generated $x_b$ and the body feature extracted from the optimized SMPL-X mesh, enforcing consistency in contact relationships.
 - Collision ($L_{coll}$) and contact ($L_{contact}$) terms, similar to training, but applied to the optimized SMPL-X mesh.
 - Prior losses for natural body pose ($L_{vposer}$), hand pose ($L_{hand}$), and body shape ($L_{shape}$).
 This optimization stage is referred to as `AdvOptim`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates PLACE on three datasets: PROX, MP3D, and Replica, comparing it against state-of-the-art methods PSI and Li et al.. Evaluations are based on perceptual naturalness, diversity, and physical plausibility. The model variants considered are `Ours` (trained without $L_{coll}$ and $L_{contact}$), `Ours + L_contact` (with contact loss), and two post-processing optimization schemes: `SimOptim` (simple optimization without $L_{contact}$ and $L_{coll}$ in Eq. 6) and `AdvOptim` (advanced optimization with all terms in Eq. 6).

#### 4.1. Perceptual Naturalness

User studies conducted on Amazon Mechanical Turk (AMT) assessed naturalness:
- **Unary User Study (Table 1):** Turkers rated individual generated results (1-5 score).
 - `Ours + L_contact + AdvOptim` achieved the highest average scores among all methods: 3.71 on PROX and 3.84 on MP3D. These scores exceeded those of baseline methods Li et al. + $L_f$ and PSI + $L_f$.
 - On PROX, `Ours + L_contact + AdvOptim` approached the realism of ground truth (3.94 ± 0.99).
- **Binary User Study (Table 2):** Turkers directly compared two results.
 - `Ours + L_contact + AdvOptim` was preferred over `PSI + L_f` by 70.1% of users on MP3D and 69.0% on PROX.
 - In direct comparison with PROX ground truth, `Ours + L_contact + AdvOptim` was rated as more realistic by 48.5% of users, indicating that the generated interactions were difficult to distinguish from real ones.

#### 4.2. Diversity

Diversity was measured by clustering SMPL-X parameters into 20 clusters and evaluating the entropy of cluster IDs and the average cluster size (distance to cluster center). Higher values indicate better diversity.
- **Results (Table 3):**
 - `Ours + L_contact + SimOptim` achieved comparable entropy and cluster size to baselines.
 - `Ours + L_contact + AdvOptim` demonstrated a notable increase in cluster size across all datasets, suggesting that the generated bodies are more widely distributed within the scene and exhibit greater variability in poses.

#### 4.3. Physical Plausibility

Physical plausibility was assessed via non-collision and contact scores, using scene Signed Distance Fields (SDFs). Higher scores are desirable for both metrics.
- **Results (Table 3):**
 - On PROX, `Ours + L_contact + SimOptim` yielded higher non-collision scores and comparable contact scores compared to baselines, indicating reduced inter-penetration.
 - `Ours + L_contact + AdvOptim` consistently improved both non-collision and contact scores across PROX and Replica datasets.
 - The model demonstrated its effectiveness on the Replica dataset, which has high-quality SDFs, reinforcing findings from PROX. MP3D SDFs were noted as less reliable due to scan noise.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

- **Generated Humans (Figure 5):** The generated human bodies in PROX, MP3D, and Replica scenes illustrate varied, natural interactions and plausible poses.
- **Latent Space Interpolation (Figure 6):** Interpolating in the latent space of the cVAE resulted in smooth transitions of body poses, maintaining natural interactions with the fixed scene, even without post-optimization.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **Training Losses (Tables 4 and 5):**
 - Excluding $L_{contact}$ and $L_{coll}$ from training resulted in lower contact scores, indicating more floating bodies. $L_{contact}$ was effective in improving contact.
 - The BPS-based approach showed inherent effectiveness in avoiding inter-penetration, as high non-collision scores were observed even without $L_{coll}$ in training. However, $L_{coll}$ sometimes decreased cluster size, possibly due to conflicts with reconstruction loss given inter-penetration in ground truth data.
- **Optimization Scheme (Tables 4 and 5):** `AdvOptim` (which incorporates collision and contact terms in post-processing) significantly improved both physical plausibility and diversity metrics over `SimOptim`.
- **Architecture (Appendix Table S1):** Components like multi-layer intermediate feature concatenation and decoders in E and F were crucial for diversity by enforcing stronger scene conditioning. Regressing body vertex coordinates directly was found to learn more diverse pose details compared to regressing SMPL-X parameters.
- **3D Cage Size (Appendix Table S2):** Smaller cage sizes generally led to increased cluster size (diversity), attributed to greater randomness in global positions of generated bodies. A 2m$^3$ cage was chosen as a balance for capturing the full body near relevant scene objects.

#### 4.6. Limitations and Failure Cases (Figure S7)

- The method may struggle with severe inter-penetration, particularly with thin scene structures like table surfaces, if the initial collision loss is already very small or difficult for `AdvOptim` to resolve while preserving the generated body and features.
- Detailed and diverse hand poses are not consistently well-synthesized, possibly due to the sparsity of scene BPS in modeling fine hand-object interactions.
- Occasional floating bodies or slight inter-penetration with the scene can occur, potentially influenced by floating examples in the training data or non-zero generated body features at contact points. The optimization step partially mitigates these issues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper introduces a novel and significant advancement in the field of 3D human-scene interaction synthesis. Its primary contribution is the development of a purely geometry-based, compact, and consistent representation for human-scene proximity and contact. This representation is independent of semantic labels or action-specific annotations, making it broadly applicable across various bodies and scene structures and simplifying data requirements compared to many prior approaches.

The generative model, PLACE, built upon this representation, demonstrates a capability to synthesize highly realistic human body meshes with natural poses and plausible contact relationships within diverse 3D environments. The extensive quantitative and qualitative evaluations, particularly the perceptual user studies, indicate that PLACE achieves state-of-the-art performance, with generated human-scene interactions approaching the realism of real-world captures. The binary user study, where nearly half of the users found the generated results indistinguishable from ground truth, underscores this significant leap in realism.

The practical implications of this work are substantial. The ability to automatically generate realistic human-scene interactions is crucial for numerous applications, including:
- **Augmented and Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** Populating virtual environments with naturally interacting avatars enhances immersion and realism.
- **Special Movie Effects:** Automating character placement and interaction can reduce labor-intensive manual animation processes.
- **Synthetic Dataset Generation:** Creating large, diverse datasets of human-scene interactions can be invaluable for training machine learning algorithms in areas such as 3D human pose estimation, scene understanding, and human-object interaction recognition.
- **Robotics and Simulation:** Generating physically plausible human interactions can inform robot behavior and improve simulation environments.

By operating solely on 3D mesh inputs and foregoing the need for additional modalities like depth or semantic maps, PLACE offers a more streamlined and potentially robust approach to human-scene synthesis. While limitations such as challenges with thin structure penetration and detailed hand interactions are noted, these represent clear avenues for future research. Overall, this method makes a substantive step towards the fully automatic synthesis of realistic 3D human bodies in 3D scenes, promising to impact a wide array of computer vision, graphics, and robotics applications.
