# Nipping the Drift in the Bud: Retrospective Rectification for Robust Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Xidian University introduced BudVLN, an online training framework for Vision-Language Navigation that addresses exposure bias and instruction-state misalignment by dynamically switching between optimality-seeking and error rectification. The framework established new state-of-the-art performance on R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks, achieving an R2R-CE success rate of 57.6% and reducing training costs by 76% compared to DAgger approaches.

## Problem

- Exposure bias in imitation learning, where agents trained on ground-truth states encounter out-of-distribution states during inference, leading to compounding errors.
- Inefficiency of pure Reinforcement Learning for complex, long-horizon VLN tasks due to sparse rewards and vast state-action spaces.
- Instruction-State Misalignment in DAgger-style approaches, where expert corrections from off-track states generate semantically inconsistent supervision that conflicts with original instructions.

## Method

- BudVLN employs an Adaptive Mutual Exclusion Strategy to dynamically route training: proficient samples are optimized for path efficiency via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- Failing samples trigger Retrospective Rectification, synthesizing semantically consistent corrective trajectories by rolling back to the last valid historical state and providing forward-looking oracle demonstrations.
- The framework unifies optimality-seeking exploration with targeted, history-aware supervised correction, ensuring consistent gradients and efficient policy updates.

## Results

- BudVLN achieved state-of-the-art performance on R2R-CE (SR **57.6%**, SPL **51.1%**) and RxR-CE (SR **56.1%**, SPL **46.6%**) using only monocular RGB inputs.
- Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic effect of GRPO and Retrospective Rectification, with the full model outperforming individual components in both success rate and path efficiency.
- The framework demonstrated improved training efficiency, requiring approximately **76%** fewer GPU hours (27 GPU hours) compared to standard DAgger pipelines (114 GPU hours).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Correcting errors from off-track states can introduce semantic inconsistencies (Instruction-State Misalignment), which negatively impact language grounding in VLN agents.
> - A dynamic balance between reinforcing proficient behaviors (via GRPO) and targeted, semantically consistent error correction (via Retrospective Rectification) is crucial for robust and efficient online learning.
> - Re-anchoring to a valid historical state before generating corrective demonstrations ensures that supervision aligns with both the agent's observed context and the original instruction's intent.
