# Flow Actor-Critic for Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> KAIST researchers developed Flow Actor-Critic (FAC), an offline reinforcement learning framework that utilizes flow models for both policy generation and a novel critic penalization based on precise behavior density estimation. This approach effectively mitigates out-of-distribution value overestimation, achieving state-of-the-art results on OGBench and strong performance on D4RL benchmarks, notably improving performance by 238% on "puzzle-3x3-play".

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning frequently encounters value overestimation for out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, leading to inaccurate and inflated Q-value estimates.
- Existing offline RL methods often rely on simplified models for behavior policies (e.g., Gaussian distributions), which are insufficient to capture complex, multi-modal action distributions present in diverse datasets.
- Robustly identifying OOD regions for targeted Q-value penalization has been challenging, leading to indirect or less effective regularization strategies.

## Method

- Propose **Flow Actor-Critic** (FAC), an actor-critic method that leverages ==normalizing flow== models for both expressive policy (actor) design and conservative value function (critic) learning.
- Introduce a novel critic penalization mechanism that directly uses the flow model's accurate behavior density estimation to identify and penalize Q-values in OOD regions.
- Employ a one-step flow policy for the actor, simplifying optimization and maintaining expressiveness compared to multi-step generative models.

## Results

- FAC achieved the highest overall average performance (60.3) on the OGBench benchmark, significantly outperforming baselines like FQL (43.6) and CAC (25.3).
- Demonstrated substantial performance gains on complex tasks, including a **238%** improvement over FQL on "puzzle-3x3-play", and competitive or superior results across D4RL MuJoCo, Antmaze, and Adroit domains.
- Empirical analysis confirmed that FAC's critic penalization effectively prevents Q-value overestimation from OOD actions, and its performance is robust to hyperparameter tuning when actor regularization is sufficient.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Normalizing flow models provide superior, high-fidelity density estimates for complex, multi-modal action distributions, enabling robust distinction between in-distribution and OOD actions.
> - Directly penalizing Q-values based on flow-derived behavior density effectively mitigates OOD overestimation, preserving unbiased Q-values in high-confidence regions and conservatively underestimating them in low-confidence areas.
> - The synergistic combination of an expressive one-step flow actor and a conservatively regularized flow critic guides policy optimization towards valuable actions within the data support, enhancing stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) frequently encounters scenarios where direct interaction with the environment is impractical, costly, or unsafe. Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) addresses this by learning optimal policies from static, pre-collected datasets without further environment interactions. A primary challenge in offline RL is value overestimation for out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, where a learned policy might suggest actions not present in the training data, leading to inaccurate and inflated Q-value estimates.

Existing approaches to mitigate OOD value overestimation generally fall into two categories: constraining the target policy to remain close to the behavior policy observed in the dataset (actor regularization, AR) or explicitly suppressing Q-values in OOD regions (critic penalization, CP). While these methods have demonstrated effectiveness, they often rely on simplified models for behavior policies, such as Gaussian distributions.

However, as offline RL datasets grow in size and diversity, their underlying behavioral distributions can become complex and multi-modal. Simple policy models become insufficient to capture such intricate distributions, leading to limitations in accurately estimating behavior policy support. This necessitates the development of more expressive policy classes. Recent advancements have seen the emergence of diffusion models and flow models, adopted from generative modeling in computer vision, as promising candidates for expressive policies in RL.

Diffusion policies, while capable of generating samples close to the behavior policy, face challenges with unstable policy optimization due to their iterative, multi-step nature. Flow models offer an alternative, providing one-step policy generation and the ability to compute exact data densities, a property often difficult or computationally expensive to obtain with diffusion models. Previous work on flow policies (e.g., Park et al., 2025) primarily focused on actor design, utilizing flow models for regularization but not fully leveraging their capability for accurate behavior density estimation in critic penalization.

This work builds upon the foundation of expressive flow policies by proposing a joint utilization of flow models for both actor regularization and a novel form of critic penalization. It addresses the gap in previous flow-based methods by exploiting the flow model's capacity for precise behavior density estimation to directly identify and manage OOD regions, a problem that has historically been challenging for offline RL algorithms to tackle directly. The integration of an expressive actor with a conservatively regularized critic, both informed by the flow model, positions this work as an advancement in robust policy learning within complex offline datasets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing complexity and multi-modality observed in modern offline reinforcement learning datasets. Traditional offline RL methods, which often employ simple Gaussian policies or basic behavior cloning for policy regularization, struggle to adequately model and operate within such intricate action distributions. This inadequacy can lead to policies that either fail to capture the full scope of beneficial actions within the dataset's support or exploit erroneously high Q-value estimates in out-of-distribution (OOD) regions, ultimately degrading performance.

Specifically, the authors identify several key motivational points:
- **Need for expressive policies:** As offline datasets become more diverse, their behavioral distributions are often multi-modal and complex. Standard Gaussian policies are insufficient to capture these distributions, necessitating more expressive models.
- **Limitations of existing expressive policies:** While diffusion models have been adopted for their expressiveness, their iterative nature poses challenges for stable policy optimization in the RL context.
- **Underutilized potential of flow models:** Recent work (Park et al., 2025) introduced flow models for expressive actor design in offline RL, demonstrating promising results. However, these methods primarily used flow models to constrain the actor to a flow-based behavior policy estimate. They did not fully exploit the flow model's inherent ability to provide an *accurate and tractable density estimate* of the behavior policy.
- **Challenge of OOD region identification:** A long-standing difficulty in offline RL is accurately identifying OOD regions to prevent Q-value overestimation. Many methods resort to indirect penalization techniques (e.g., CQL, SVR) because a direct and robust estimation of behavior density, crucial for distinguishing in-distribution (ID) from OOD regions, has been elusive, especially for complex, multi-modal distributions.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this paper are:
- **Propose Flow Actor-Critic (FAC):** To introduce a new actor-critic method for offline RL that leverages flow models.
- **Jointly exploit flow models for actor and critic:** The method aims to not only use the flow model for actor design (as in previous flow policies) but also for conservative critic acquisition.
- **Develop a novel critic regularizer:** To achieve conservative critic acquisition, the paper proposes a new critic regularizer based on the expressive flow behavior proxy model. This regularizer directly uses the flow model's density estimation capability to identify and penalize Q-values in OOD regions.
- **Leverage flow behavior density for OOD detection:** The flow model's ability to provide strong density contrast is intended to allow for direct identification of ID versus OOD regions, enabling more targeted and effective Q-value penalization.
- **Improve offline RL performance:** The ultimate objective is to achieve improved performance on standard and challenging offline RL benchmarks by addressing the limitations of less expressive policies and indirect OOD handling mechanisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Flow Actor-Critic (FAC) integrates flow models for both policy (actor) and value function (critic) learning in offline RL. The core idea is to leverage the flow model's ability to accurately estimate complex, multi-modal action distributions, not just for action generation, but also for robustly identifying out-of-distribution (OOD) regions to prevent Q-value overestimation.

The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The paper first reviews offline Reinforcement Learning within the Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework, emphasizing the goal of maximizing expected discounted return from a static dataset $\mathcal{D}$. It highlights the central challenge of Q-value overestimation for OOD actions and introduces common strategies like support-constrained policy optimization (e.g., minimizing distance between target and behavior policy) and regularization methods (Eqs. 1 and 2).
It then introduces Normalizing Flows and Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNF) as a framework for modeling complex distributions. Flow Matching (FM) is presented as an efficient way to train CNFs by learning a velocity field (Eq. 5). A key aspect is the ability of flow models to compute data density (Eq. 7) and construct a flow-based policy, termed a "behavior proxy policy" $\hat{\beta}_\psi(\cdot|s)$ (Eq. 6), which maps Gaussian noise to actions conditioned on state.

#### 3.2. Motivation: Strength of Flow Behavior Proxy Density

To empirically demonstrate the advantage of flow models, the authors conducted an experiment on a synthetic 2D Gaussian mixture dataset with four modes. They compared the sampling and density estimation capabilities of a flow model (using Euler method with 10 steps) against a simple Gaussian model, a Conditional VAE, and diffusion models (with 10 and 50 denoising steps).
The results (Figure 1) indicated that:
- **Gaussian models** fail to capture multi-modality, placing high density in regions with actual zero density.
- **VAEs** identify modes but generate OOD samples and provide imprecise ELBOs for density.
- **Diffusion models** (especially with T=50) show good sampling but also yield poor ELBO-based density estimates, making them unsuitable as density estimators.
- **Flow models** demonstrated both accurate sampling within the true data distribution and a strong, high-fidelity estimate of the true log-density, clearly distinguishing ID from OOD regions. This superior density estimation ability forms the core motivation for the proposed critic penalization.

#### 3.3. Critic Penalization with Flow Behavior Proxy Density

Building on the flow model's strong density estimation, the paper proposes a novel critic penalization mechanism to directly address OOD Q-value overestimation.
- **Limitations of prior methods:** Critic penalization methods like CQL and SVR are discussed. CQL's penalization can incorrectly affect ID regions, while SVR, though more refined, relies on simple Gaussian behavior models for importance sampling, which can become unstable and inaccurate with complex, multi-modal distributions (e.g., leading to exploding IS ratios).
- **Proposed Weight Function:** A weight function, $w_{\hat{\beta}_\psi}(s, a) = \max(0, 1 - (\hat{\beta}_\psi(a|s)/\epsilon))$ (Eq. 8), is introduced. This function utilizes the flow behavior proxy density $\hat{\beta}_\psi(a|s)$ and a threshold $\epsilon > 0$. The weight is zero in confidently in-distribution regions ($\hat{\beta}_\psi(a|s) \geq \epsilon$) and increases linearly as density falls below $\epsilon$, effectively pinpointing OOD regions.
- **New Critic Loss:** The proposed critic loss (Eq. 9) penalizes Q-values using this weight:
 $\min_Q \alpha E_{s \sim \mathcal{D}, a \sim \pi} [w_{\hat{\beta}_\psi}(s, a) Q(s, a)] + \text{TD Loss}$.
 This formulation ensures that penalization occurs primarily in OOD regions (where $w > 0$), while preserving the standard Bellman operator in confidently in-distribution regions.
- **Theoretical Properties:** Proposition 1 demonstrates that this penalization modifies the Bellman operator $T_\pi Q(s,a)$ to $T_\pi^{\text{FAC}} Q(s,a)$, which equals $T_\pi Q(s,a)$ for actions with $\hat{\beta}(a|s) \geq \epsilon$ (ID region with positive true density) and applies a penalty otherwise. It also ensures $-\infty$ for actions truly outside the behavior policy's support. Further theoretical analysis (Propositions 2 and Theorem 1) establishes that this operator is a $\gamma$-contraction, guaranteeing a unique fixed point. The fixed point provides unbiased Q-values in high-confidence ID regions and conservatively underestimated Q-values in low-confidence regions.
- **$\epsilon$ Design:** The choice of $\epsilon$ is dataset-driven: (1) a dataset-wide constant threshold (minimum density in the dataset) for broad state coverage/low action diversity, and (2) a batch-adaptive threshold (minimum density in the current mini-batch) for limited state coverage/high action diversity.

#### 3.4. Enhanced Flow Actor Optimization

The actor in FAC is a one-step flow policy $\pi_\theta$ that directly maps Gaussian noise $z$ to an action $a$ via a single flow step $a_\theta(\cdot; s, u=1): A \to A : z \mapsto a$. This simplifies gradient backpropagation compared to multi-step flow policies while retaining expressivity.
The actor is optimized using a standard offline RL objective (Eq. 11), maximizing the Q-value while regularizing the target policy $\pi_\theta$ to stay close to the flow behavior proxy policy $\hat{\beta}_\psi$ in action space:
$\max_{\pi_\theta} E_{s \sim \mathcal{D}, a \sim \pi_\theta} [Q_\phi(s, a)] - \lambda E_{s \sim \mathcal{D}, z \sim N(0,I)} [\|a_\theta(s, z) - a_\psi(s, z)\|_2^2]$.
The critical enhancement here is that the Q-function $Q_\phi(s,a)$ used in this objective is the *penalized* critic derived in Section 4.3. This ensures that the actor, while maximizing value, is guided by a Q-function that has been conservatively regularized in OOD regions. This prevents the actor from being drawn to overestimated Q-values outside the data support, which could occur if an unpenalized Q-function were used with weak distance regularization.

#### 3.5. Practical Implementation (Algorithm 1)

FAC involves a two-stage training process:
1. **Stage 1: Behavior Proxy Training:** A flow behavior proxy policy $\hat{\beta}_\psi$ is trained using the flow matching objective (Eq. 6) on the offline dataset $\mathcal{D}$. After training, its density $\hat{\beta}_\psi(a|s)$ is evaluated for all $(s,a)$ pairs in $\mathcal{D}$ and stored.
2. **Stage 2: Actor and Critic Training:** Twin critics $Q_{\phi_1}, Q_{\phi_2}$ (for stability) and the one-step flow actor $\pi_\theta$ are trained concurrently. The critics are updated with the proposed penalized loss (Eq. 9), using target networks. The actor is updated with the objective in Eq. 11, guided by the penalized Q-function.

The overall approach combines the expressiveness of flow models for multi-modal action distributions with a principled, density-aware critic penalization, creating a robust framework for offline RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Flow Actor-Critic (FAC) on two distinct sets of offline RL benchmarks: the recently introduced OGBench and the standard D4RL datasets. Comparisons are made against a comprehensive suite of baselines, including methods based on Gaussian policies, diffusion policies, and other flow policies.

#### 4.1. Performance on the OGBench Benchmark

FAC demonstrated superior performance across the OGBench benchmark, which includes 50 state-based and 5 pixel-based singletasks spanning diverse goal-conditioned locomotion and manipulation environments.
- **Overall Performance:** FAC achieved the highest overall average performance (60.3) compared to all other baselines (e.g., FQL: 43.6, CAC: 25.3, ReBRAC: 31.0), indicating a substantial improvement.
- **Comparison with Flow Baselines:** FAC outperformed FQL (a flow policy-based baseline that only uses actor regularization) with a clear margin, suggesting the effectiveness of the proposed critic penalization.
- **Comparison with Expressive Policies:** FAC demonstrated an advantage over diffusion policy-based baselines (IDQL, SRPO, CAC) and multi-step flow policy-based baselines (FAWAC, FBRAC, IFQL). This highlights the benefit of FAC's one-step flow actor, which avoids the practical difficulties of optimizing iterative generative models.
- **Complex Action Spaces:** On higher-dimensional humanoid tasks, FAC outperformed Gaussian policy-based methods like ReBRAC, suggesting that the expressive actor in FAC contributes significantly in complex action spaces.
- **Manipulation Tasks:** FAC achieved strong performance on state-based and pixel-based manipulation tasks, including perfect success on `puzzle-3x3-play`, which requires combinatorial reasoning and trajectory stitching. This represents a significant performance improvement (e.g., 238% over FQL on `puzzle-3x3-play`).

#### 4.2. Performance on the D4RL Benchmark

FAC showed competitive or superior performance across all three domains (MuJoCo, Antmaze, Adroit) of the D4RL benchmark, reinforcing the generality of the proposed method.
- **MuJoCo Tasks:** FAC matched or exceeded strong Gaussian policy-based baselines on MuJoCo tasks, which are known for dense reward functions and susceptibility to value overestimation. For instance, FAC achieved an average score of 88.2, outperforming strong baselines like EPQ (90.9) and ReBRAC (88.5). This suggests that the flow-based critic penalization in FAC successfully mitigates value overestimation bias, which negatively impacted FQL on some of these tasks.
- **Antmaze Tasks:** As task difficulty increased, many baselines (including strong Gaussian and diffusion methods) showed performance degradation. In contrast, FAC maintained robust performance and consistently improved over FQL, achieving an average of 90.5, significantly higher than other baselines.
- **Adroit Tasks:** On these challenging tasks with high-dimensional action spaces and sparse datasets, FAC was comparable to the best Gaussian policy-based baselines. It achieved notable improvements on specific tasks like `pen-cloned` and `hammer-human`.

#### 4.3. Empirical Analysis of Components

The paper includes an empirical analysis of FAC's key components:
- **Effect of Critic Penalization:** When evaluating policies by sampling multiple actions and taking the maximum Q-value (an operation prone to OOD overestimation), FAC maintained consistent performance with an increasing number of candidate actions (N). In contrast, FQL (without the proposed critic penalization) showed significant degradation, confirming that FAC's critic penalization effectively handles potential OOD actions.
- **Sensitivity to $\alpha$ and $\lambda$:** Experiments on `puzzle-3x3-play-task4` demonstrated a synergistic effect between the critic penalization coefficient $\alpha$ and the actor regularization coefficient $\lambda$. With adequate $\lambda$, FAC's performance was robust to $\alpha$, indicating reliable support-aware policy optimization.
- **Influence of Flow Behavior Proxy Fidelity:** The fidelity of the flow behavior proxy (controlled by the number of Euler integration steps, T) directly impacted performance. FAC achieved high performance for T $\ge$ 10, indicating that sufficient fidelity in density estimation is crucial.
- **Impact of Critic Penalization Weight Functions:** Comparing linear, convex, and concave weight functions for critic penalization, the linear and concave functions yielded comparable performance, while the convex function led to degradation. The linear weight proved to be a simple yet effective choice.
- **Impact of $\epsilon$ Schemes:** The choice between batch-adaptive and dataset-wide constant thresholds for $\epsilon$ was task-dependent, with each performing better on different tasks, leading to specific recommendations for each domain.

#### 4.4. Online Fine-tuning

In the offline-to-online setting, FAC achieved strong performance on OGBench and D4RL Antmaze tasks. This suggests that the critic penalization design, by avoiding excessive suppression of high Q-value neighborhoods near the dataset support boundary, may enable beneficial exploration during the online fine-tuning phase. However, performance gains were smaller on D4RL Adroit tasks, possibly due to extreme dataset sparsity limiting guidance towards promising regions.

In summary, FAC consistently demonstrated strong performance across diverse offline RL benchmarks, establishing a new state-of-the-art for several challenging tasks. The empirical analysis provides evidence for the effectiveness of its joint actor-critic design and the principled use of flow model density for OOD action handling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Flow Actor-Critic (FAC) contributes to the field of offline reinforcement learning by addressing the critical challenge of learning robust policies from complex, multi-modal datasets. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Improved Handling of Complex Data Distributions:** Traditional offline RL methods often struggle with multi-modal action distributions common in diverse datasets. FAC, by leveraging highly expressive flow models for both actor and critic, provides a more sophisticated approach to modeling these distributions, leading to policies that better capture the nuances of expert behavior within the dataset.
- **Principled Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Action Detection and Penalization:** A core contribution of FAC is its novel critic penalization mechanism, which directly utilizes the tractable density estimation capability of flow models. Unlike indirect methods, FAC can robustly identify OOD regions and apply targeted penalization to Q-values. This reduces value overestimation, a significant source of performance degradation in offline RL, and promotes learning conservative yet effective policies.
- **Enhanced Policy Optimization Stability:** By coupling an expressive one-step flow actor with a conservatively regularized flow critic, FAC achieves a synergistic effect. The penalized Q-function guides the actor towards valuable actions within the data support, preventing it from exploiting erroneously high Q-values in unseen regions. This leads to more reliable and stable policy optimization, particularly important for challenging tasks.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Empirically, FAC demonstrates new state-of-the-art performance on a wide range of tasks across the OGBench and D4RL benchmarks. This includes significant improvements on tasks requiring combinatorial reasoning and trajectory stitching, as well as robust performance across varying task difficulties and state-action distributions. This positions FAC as a competitive baseline for future research in offline RL.
- **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The ability to learn high-performing and robust policies from static, complex datasets has substantial implications for real-world applications where data collection is expensive, hazardous, or time-consuming (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving, healthcare). FAC's design, which emphasizes fidelity to observed behavior while mitigating risks from OOD actions, makes it a promising candidate for such deployments.
- **Advancement in Generative Models for RL:** This work further establishes the utility of normalizing flow models as powerful generative primitives within RL. By demonstrating their joint utility for both action generation and density-based critic regularization, it opens new avenues for research into integrating advanced generative modeling techniques into offline and potentially online RL settings.
- **Insight into Offline-to-Online Transfer:** The results from offline-to-online fine-tuning suggest that FAC's critic penalization, by not excessively suppressing high Q-value regions near the dataset boundary, may facilitate beneficial exploration during subsequent online learning phases. This could lead to more efficient adaptation and performance improvement when limited online interaction becomes available.

In summary, FAC represents an important methodological advance in offline RL, offering a robust and high-performing solution to the challenges posed by complex, multi-modal datasets. Its principled approach to OOD handling through flow-based density estimation is a key innovation with broad implications for future research and application.
