# PixelRefer: A Unified Framework for Spatio-Temporal Object Referring with Arbitrary Granularity

> [!summary] Summary
> PixelRefer presents a unified framework for fine-grained spatiotemporal object understanding in images and videos, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks. The framework incorporates a scale-adaptive object tokenizer and an efficient object-centric infusion module, alongside a new 2.2 million-sample instruction-tuning dataset, enabling significant computational cost reductions for video processing.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) excel at holistic scene understanding but lack precise, fine-grained, object-centric reasoning capabilities, crucial for detailed visual referring tasks.
- Previous region-level MLLMs were often task-specific, leading to high computational overhead and sacrificing the general-purpose capabilities and flexibility of foundational MLLMs.
- A shortage of high-quality, diverse instruction-tuning datasets exists for truly fine-grained, pixel-level object understanding, particularly for complex spatiotemporal reasoning in videos.

## Method

- Introduces the Vision-Object Framework (==PixelRefer==) featuring a ==Scale-Adaptive Object Tokenizer (SAOT)== that dynamically processes objects of arbitrary granularity to generate semantically rich and compact representations.
- Develops the Object-Only Framework (==PixelRefer-Lite==) with an ==Object-Centric Infusion (OCI) module== that pre-integrates hierarchical local and global visual context into object tokens, significantly reducing LLM computational load.
- Curates ==PixelRefer-2.2M==, a comprehensive open-source instruction-tuning dataset for foundational object perception and visual instruction tuning, addressing the data scarcity for fine-grained spatiotemporal tasks.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance across numerous benchmarks, including a **+12.7%** improvement on Ferret-Reasoning and outperforming GPT-4o by **8.1%** on VideoRefer-Bench Q for multi-choice QA in videos.
- PixelRefer-Lite demonstrated dramatic efficiency gains, reducing FLOPs by approximately **100x** and memory usage by **5x** for video inputs, making advanced region-level MLLM capabilities more practical.
- The Scale-Adaptive Object Tokenizer (SAOT) significantly outperformed baseline methods, especially for small regions (**+15.6%** on LVIS), and the Object-Centric Infusion (OCI) module synergistically improved performance by effectively infusing local and global context into object tokens.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An LLM's attention mechanisms predominantly focus on region-level tokens for answer generation, highlighting the importance of rich, compact object representations for object-centric tasks.
> - Global visual tokens primarily contribute in early LLM layers, with their activity diminishing in deeper layers, suggesting that pre-fusing essential global context into object tokens can drastically improve efficiency without compromising semantic fidelity.
> - The curated PixelRefer-2.2M dataset, through its diverse and progressive inclusion of data types, systematically enhances the model's capabilities across various fine-grained visual reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed a remarkable surge in the capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which can process and understand information from both text and visual inputs (images and videos). These models, such as GPT-4V, LLaVA, and Qwen-VL, have demonstrated strong general-purpose comprehension in open-world scenarios, interpreting entire scenes and generating coherent responses. However, a significant limitation of most existing MLLMs lies in their predominant focus on *holistic, scene-level understanding*. They often fall short when precise, *fine-grained, object-centric reasoning* is required—a capability crucial for tasks known as visual referring, which demands interpreting the semantics of specific, localized regions within visual scenes.

Earlier attempts to address this gap, like the Set-of-Mark (SoM) approach, involved overlaying visual markers directly onto images. While innovative, these methods often suffered from visual ambiguity, occluding content or blending into backgrounds, and lacked flexibility, requiring full image reprocessing for marker adjustments. Subsequently, a new wave of *region-level MLLMs* emerged, which employed explicit visual prompts or localized queries to extract object-level visual representations, aligning them with LLMs for improved spatial understanding in images. However, the extension of such fine-grained understanding to dynamic videos remained comparatively underdeveloped, often relying on coarse bounding boxes or external tracking, leading to inconsistent representations in complex temporal scenarios.

More recently, research has aimed for *unified region-level understanding across both images and videos*. Models like the Describe Anything Model (DAM) introduced focal prompts and localized vision backbones to integrate global context into regional representations. While effective for fine details, DAM was limited to single-object descriptions, incurring high computational overhead for multiple regions. The Perception Anything Model (PAM) extended SAM 2 by incorporating a semantic perceiver but was largely confined to captioning tasks and required vast training data due to its reliance on semantics-agnostic features. Critically, both DAM and PAM adopted task-specific architectures that could undermine the inherent general-purpose capabilities and flexibility of foundational MLLMs.

PixelRefer positions itself directly to address these persisting challenges. It aims to develop a *unified region-level MLLM framework* that not only enables *advanced fine-grained spatiotemporal understanding* over user-specified regions of *arbitrary granularity* but also *preserves the general-purpose capabilities* of modern MLLMs. By building upon a general-purpose MLLM backbone and integrating region-level representations in a modular fashion, PixelRefer seeks to overcome the limitations of prior task-specific designs, providing a more versatile, efficient, and robust solution for object-centric understanding in both static and dynamic visual data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop PixelRefer, a unified, flexible, and efficient region-level MLLM framework that can perform advanced fine-grained spatiotemporal object understanding across both images and videos with arbitrary semantic granularity. This framework aims to support a broad range of object-centric referring tasks, moving beyond simple captioning, while critically preserving the general-purpose capabilities inherent in modern MLLMs.

The motivations driving the design of PixelRefer are rooted in two key empirical observations derived from preliminary analysis of how MLLMs interpret object-level tokens, coupled with the recognized gaps in existing datasets and model architectures:

1. **Observation on LLM Attention and Object Tokens:** The authors found that an LLM’s attention mechanisms predominantly focus on region-level tokens corresponding to referred objects, especially for answer generation. This suggests that the quality and semantic richness of object tokens are paramount for accurate object-centric tasks.
 - **Motivation for Scale-Adaptive Object Tokenizer (SAOT):** To leverage this observation, a key motivation was to design a novel tokenizer capable of generating precise, semantically rich, and compact object representations from diverse, free-form regions. This tokenizer needed to effectively handle objects of varying scales (from tiny to large) and preserve crucial spatial context without being overly redundant.

2. **Observation on Global Visual Tokens and Computational Overhead:** The analysis also revealed that while global visual tokens (representing the overall scene) contribute significantly in the early layers of an LLM for broad context, their activity diminishes in deeper layers where object tokens remain highly active. Despite this, global visual tokens account for a substantial portion of the LLM's computational overhead, especially with high-resolution or long video inputs.
 - **Motivation for PixelRefer-Lite and Object-Centric Infusion (OCI) Module:** This insight motivated the development of an efficient variant, PixelRefer-Lite. The goal was to pre-fuse the essential global visual context directly into the object tokens via a lightweight "Object-Centric Infusion" (OCI) module before they enter the LLM. This "Object-Only Framework" design aims to drastically reduce computational cost and memory usage by minimizing the number of tokens fed into the LLM, without compromising the semantic fidelity required for accurate reasoning.

3. **Gap in Existing Datasets:** The researchers identified a critical shortage of high-quality, diverse instruction-tuning datasets that could support truly fine-grained, pixel-level object understanding, particularly for complex spatiotemporal reasoning tasks involving multi-object relationships, multi-turn QA, and future event prediction in videos.
 - **Motivation for PixelRefer-2.2M Dataset:** To address this, a significant motivation was to curate a new, comprehensive open-source dataset, PixelRefer-2.2M. This dataset, structured for Foundational Object Perception and Visual Instruction Tuning, aims to provide robust supervision for aligning language with both global visual context and local object regions, thereby enabling the training of models for higher-order visual reasoning capabilities.

In essence, PixelRefer is motivated by the need for more intelligent, efficient, and versatile MLLMs that can delve into the specifics of visual content, not just its broad strokes, making them suitable for a broader array of real-world applications demanding precision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PixelRefer introduces a unified framework comprising two complementary paradigms: the **Vision-Object Framework** (for PixelRefer) and the **Object-Only Framework** (for PixelRefer-Lite), both built upon a robust, general-purpose MLLM backbone, specifically VideoLLaMA 3.

#### 3.1. Vision-Object Framework (PixelRefer)

This framework directly integrates both global visual tokens and object-level tokens into the LLM, enabling reasoning over holistic scene context alongside precise object-level representations. Its core innovation lies in the **Scale-Adaptive Object Tokenizer (SAOT)**.

- **Scale-Adaptive Object Tokenizer (SAOT):** Designed to generate accurate and informative object tokens across varying scales, addressing the challenges of unreliable feature extraction from extremely small or highly scale-variant regions.
 - **Dynamic Object Processing:** Given a binary mask for a target region, SAOT adaptively computes a scaling ratio. Small objects (e.g., fewer than `n * patch_size` pixels) are upscaled to ensure fine-grained details are captured, while large objects (e.g., more than `100 * patch_size` pixels) are downscaled to reduce redundancy and computational overhead. This normalization ensures consistent processing fidelity.
 - **Contextual Padding:** The bounding box of the target object is enlarged with contextual padding, and this expanded region is fed into the vision encoder. This step enriches the region-level embeddings with surrounding contextual information.
 - **Mask Feature Extractor:** A binary mask is applied to the extracted feature map to isolate object-specific features, represented as `F_M`.
 - **Relative Positional Encoding:** To counteract localization ambiguity introduced by contextual padding, relative positional coordinates are computed and fused with the masked features, forming position-aware object tokens (`F_MP`).
 - **Abundant Feature Aggregation:** To mitigate redundancy, especially in large or homogeneous regions where many tokens might represent similar features, k-means clustering is applied to `F_MP`. This merges redundant tokens into `n` representative object tokens, which are then passed through an MLP to generate the final compact representations.

#### 3.2. Object-Only Framework (PixelRefer-Lite)

Motivated by the observation that global visual tokens primarily contribute in early LLM layers, PixelRefer-Lite aims for efficiency by significantly reducing the token count fed to the LLM. It achieves this through the **Object-Centric Infusion (OCI) module**.

- **Object-Centric Infusion (OCI) Module:** This lightweight module pre-integrates contextual visual information into object tokens before they reach the LLM, eliminating the need to pass dense global visual tokens directly.
 - **Hierarchical Cross-Attention Infusion:** OCI employs a two-step cross-attention strategy:
 1. **Local-to-Object Attention:** Fine-grained visual embeddings extracted from locally expanded image regions (similar to contextual padding in SAOT) are injected into the object tokens. This provides immediate, detailed context.
 2. **Global-to-Object Attention:** Object tokens are then further conditioned on scene-level embeddings derived from the raw, global image. This step integrates broader, long-range scene dependencies and holistic semantics.
 - After this hierarchical infusion, only the enhanced object tokens (`T_O`) and linguistic tokens (`T_X`) are concatenated and fed into the LLM for decoding, leading to substantial reductions in computational cost and memory.

#### 3.3. Extension to Videos

The framework is inherently designed to generalize to videos. A video is treated as a sequence of images across time. Timestamp embeddings are prepended to each object token to incorporate temporal information. For object mask extraction in videos, SAM 2 is utilized to generate high-quality segmentation masks on sampled video frames, with each mask independently processed through the OCI module.

#### 3.4. PixelRefer-2.2M Dataset Curation

To facilitate robust fine-grained instruction tuning, the authors meticulously curated **PixelRefer-2.2M**, an open-source object-centric instruction dataset organized into two stages:

- **Foundational Object Perception Data (1.4M samples):** Focuses on strengthening fine-grained regional alignment.
 - **Region Recognition:** Combines object-level (LVIS, Visual Genome, RefCOCO/+) and part-level (PACO, Pascal-Part, PartImageNet) annotations, along with temporal alignment data (VideoRefer-Short, MEVIS).
 - **Regional Image Detailed Caption:** Aggregates diverse data from DAM (SAM, OpenImages, LVIS, COCOStuff, Mapillary, PACO), Osprey-caption, and MDVP-Data.
 - **Regional Video Detailed Caption:** Leverages VideoRefer-Detailed, DAM-SAV, and HC-STVG for temporal and contextual understanding.

- **Visual Instruction Tuning Data (0.8M samples):** Aims to instill instruction-following capabilities.
 - **Image Region QA:** Incorporates Osprey-QA, MDVP-LVIS, MDVP-PACO (GPT-4/GPT-4o annotated), and VCR (for commonsense reasoning).
 - **Video Object QA:** Includes 75K VideoRefer-QA samples, constructed via a multi-agent pipeline, for precise QA over dynamic objects.
 - **Region Caption:** 250K captions sampled from the Foundational Object Perception data to maintain descriptive generation alignment.
 - **General QA:** Samples from LLaVA-Video and LLaVA-OV to broaden open-ended instruction-following capabilities.

#### 3.5. Training Strategy

The models are initialized with VideoLLaMA 3-Image weights and trained using a progressive two-stage strategy: first for foundational object perception, then for visual instruction tuning, employing a cosine learning rate scheduler with warm-up.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation across a broad range of benchmarks validates the effectiveness and efficiency of the PixelRefer framework.

#### 4.1. Leading Performance Across Benchmarks

PixelRefer consistently achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance in fine-grained spatiotemporal object understanding tasks, often with fewer training samples (2.2M) compared to previous advanced MLLMs that required 8M+ samples.

- **Image-level Benchmarks (Table 1):**
 - **Category Recognition (LVIS, PACO):** PixelRefer-7B achieved SOTA on both, notably on the challenging PACO dataset (91.7% SSim, 85.3% SIoU), outperforming the previous best by 4.3% SSim and 10.4% SIoU. This highlights SAOT’s effectiveness for small, part-level regions. PixelRefer-Lite-7B also surpassed prior SOTA.
 - **Phrase-level Caption (VG, Ref-L4):** PixelRefer-7B attained the best CIDER score (168.2%) on VG, significantly outperforming PAM-3B by 45.9%. PixelRefer-2B improved CIDER on Ref-L4 by 32.2%.
 - **Detailed Caption (DLC-Bench, Ref-L4 CLAIR):** PixelRefer-7B achieved SOTA on DLC-Bench (68.3%), and PixelRefer-Lite-2B surpassed previous SOTA on Ref-L4-CLAIR by 5.6%.
 - **Reasoning Questions (Ferret-Reasoning):** PixelRefer substantially improved the SOTA score from 70.4% to 83.1% (+12.7%), demonstrating enhanced high-level reasoning capabilities.

- **Video-level Benchmarks (Tables 2, 3, 4):**
 - **VideoRefer-Bench D (Detailed Description):** PixelRefer-7B (single-frame mode) achieved leading average performance (3.70), even surpassing DAM-8B (which uses multi-frame inputs with denser masks).
 - **VideoRefer-Bench Q (Multi-choice QA):** PixelRefer-7B secured the best average performance (79.4%), exceeding the closed-source GPT-4o by 8.1% and topping all subcategories (Basic, Sequential, Relationship, Reasoning, Future Prediction questions). This validates its strong spatiotemporal video understanding.
 - **HC-STVG (Human-centric Detailed Caption):** PixelRefer-7B achieved SOTA across all metrics (METEOR, CIDER, BLEU@4, ROUGE-L, SPICE), consistently outperforming DAM-8B. PixelRefer-Lite-7B also delivered comparable performance, highlighting its efficiency without significant accuracy loss.

#### 4.2. Efficiency Analysis (Tables 5, 6)

- **Significant Reductions in FLOPs and Memory:** The Object-Only Framework (PixelRefer-Lite) achieved dramatic efficiency gains. For video inputs, PixelRefer-2B required 11.15T FLOPs and 24.6GB memory, while PixelRefer-2B-Lite reduced this to just 0.11T FLOPs and 5.1GB memory (approximately 100x FLOPs reduction and 5x memory reduction). Similar improvements were observed for 7B models and image inputs.
- **Reduced Inference Time:** PixelRefer-Lite variants showed notable reductions in per-item inference time compared to PixelRefer and other SOTA methods like DAM and PAM, making them highly practical for real-world deployments.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Object Token Scaling (Table 7, Fig. 10):** The Vision-Object Framework showed significant improvements up to 8 object tokens, then plateaued, indicating that global context already provided much of the necessary information. The Object-Only Framework, however, continuously improved with more tokens, eventually surpassing the Vision-Object model on HC-STVG with 32 tokens, demonstrating its reliance on a richer object token budget in the absence of dense global tokens. Different object tokens were found to focus on distinct regions, providing complementary details (Fig. 11).
- **SAOT Design (Table 8, Fig. 12):** SAOT significantly outperformed vanilla Mask Pooling (+6.8% on LVIS, +8.6% on DLC-Bench), particularly for small regions (+15.6% on LVIS, +9.6% on DLC-Bench), affirming its effectiveness in preserving details for tiny objects. Contextual expansion and positional embedding also contributed positively.
- **OCI Module Design (Table 9):** Both Local-to-Object Attention and Global-to-Object Attention individually improved performance over a baseline without context. Their combined use yielded the highest performance (+4.4% on LVIS, +21.7% on HC-STVG), confirming their complementary roles in hierarchically infusing local and global context.
- **Impact of Diverse Training Data (Table 10):** The progressive inclusion of different data types (Region Recognition, Image/Video Detailed Caption, Region QA, General QA from PixelRefer-2.2M) systematically enhanced the model’s capabilities across various tasks, demonstrating the dataset's efficacy in building a robust knowledge foundation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PixelRefer framework represents a significant advancement in the capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in bridging the gap between holistic scene understanding and precise, fine-grained object-centric reasoning across both images and videos. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling True Fine-Grained Spatio-Temporal Understanding:** PixelRefer moves beyond scene-level comprehension to offer robust, arbitrary-granularity understanding of user-specified regions. This capability is crucial for applications that require not just "what is in the image," but "what exactly is `<object>` doing," "what is the relationship between `<object0>` and `<object1>` over time," or "what is the handle of the cup?" This level of detail has been largely unaddressed by prior general-purpose MLLMs.

2. **Unified and Flexible Framework:** By providing a unified approach for both images and videos and supporting a wide array of tasks—from simple object recognition and detailed captioning to complex multi-object reasoning and future event prediction—PixelRefer offers unparalleled flexibility. Its modular design, built upon a general-purpose MLLM backbone, ensures that it can seamlessly integrate region-level understanding without sacrificing the broader capabilities of the base model, making it highly adaptable for diverse use cases.

3. **Breakthrough in Computational Efficiency:** The introduction of PixelRefer-Lite and its Object-Centric Infusion (OCI) module is a major stride towards making advanced region-level MLLM capabilities practical for real-world deployment. The dramatic reduction in FLOPs (up to 100x) and memory usage (up to 5x) for video processing, coupled with faster inference times, addresses a critical bottleneck in deploying high-resolution and long video understanding systems. This efficiency democratizes access to powerful MLLM features, allowing their use in resource-constrained environments or for large-scale processing.

4. **Novel Methodological Contributions:** The paper introduces two innovative components: the **Scale-Adaptive Object Tokenizer (SAOT)** and the **Object-Centric Infusion (OCI) module**. SAOT's dynamic processing, contextual padding, relative positional encoding, and abundant feature aggregation significantly improve the fidelity and compactness of object representations, especially for challenging small objects. OCI's hierarchical local-to-global context infusion provides an elegant and efficient solution for enriching object tokens without increasing LLM load.

5. **High-Quality Open-Source Dataset (PixelRefer-2.2M):** The meticulously curated PixelRefer-2.2M dataset is an invaluable contribution to the research community. By addressing the previous scarcity of diverse, high-quality object-centric instruction-tuning data for spatiotemporal reasoning, it provides a robust foundation for future research. This dataset enables the training of models capable of multi-object interactions, multi-turn QA, and future event prediction, pushing the boundaries of MLLM capabilities.

6. **Broadening Real-World Applications:** The enhanced capabilities of PixelRefer are critical for a multitude of advanced applications:
 - **Human-Computer Interaction:** More intuitive and precise interactions where users can refer to specific objects or events in complex scenes.
 - **Embodied AI/Robotics:** Enabling robots to understand and interact with specific parts of their environment with greater accuracy and nuance.
 - **Medical Diagnostics:** Assisting in analyzing specific lesions, anatomical structures, or changes in medical images and videos with fine-grained detail.
 - **Remote Sensing and Surveillance:** Interpreting specific features, objects, or events in satellite imagery or surveillance feeds.
 - **Content Creation and Accessibility:** Generating highly detailed descriptions for specific elements within visual media, improving accessibility for visually impaired users or aiding in content indexing.

In conclusion, PixelRefer not only demonstrates SOTA performance across challenging benchmarks but also provides a more principled, efficient, and versatile framework for object-centric multimodal understanding. Its open-source contributions (code, demo, dataset) will likely catalyze further research and development in this vital area, driving the next generation of intelligent visual AI systems.
