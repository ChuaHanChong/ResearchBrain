# Unifying Object-Centric World Models and Diffusion Policy: A Hierarchical Framework for Multi-Stage Robotic Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldDP introduces a hierarchical framework that unifies object-centric visual world models with diffusion policies to address multi-stage robotic manipulation. This approach achieves superior performance, with a 30% success rate on the complex Cube-Triple task for all three objects and a 20% completion rate for the Scene-Single-Composite task, surpassing existing methods.

## Problem

- Current visual world models struggle with long-horizon, multi-stage robotic tasks, often succeeding only on short-horizon or single-stage objectives.
- Existing world models frequently rely on patch-level features, which are suboptimal for capturing fine-grained details critical for robust robotic interaction.
- Single-tier planning in current methods faces computational and planning difficulties when applied to complex multi-stage tasks.

## Method

- **WorldDP** proposes a hierarchical framework combining an upper-tier ==object-centric== world model for high-level planning and a lower-tier goal-conditioned diffusion policy for execution.
- An Object-Centric Encoder (OCE), guided by SAM2 segmentation masks, extracts discrete object-centric state representations from raw image observations.
- The upper-tier planning utilizes a Particle Filter for multi-modal optimization and incorporates a contact predictor into the MPC cost function to generate precise, interaction-focused subgoals.

## Results

- WorldDP achieved a **30%** success rate for manipulating all three cubes in the Cube-Triple task and a **20%** full task completion rate for Scene-Single-Composite, outperforming all comparative baselines.
- The framework demonstrated competitive performance on simpler tasks, with a **72%** success rate on Cube-Single and an average of **74%** on the Scene-Single-Direct subtasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Object-Centric Encoder, Particle Filter, and Contact Predictor individually contribute to the framework's enhanced effectiveness on complex tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining object-centric representations with a hierarchical planning structure effectively decomposes and executes complex multi-stage robotic tasks.
> - A robust low-level diffusion policy can efficiently track and execute subgoals generated by a high-level world model, enhancing overall task success.
> - Integrating a contact predictor and employing a Particle Filter for multi-modal optimization significantly improves the relevance and precision of generated subgoals for robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper, WorldDP, addresses challenges in robotic manipulation, particularly for multi-stage tasks, by integrating advancements in visual world models and diffusion policies.

Visual world models have emerged as a significant area of research for learning complex system dynamics from observational data, especially image-based observations. These models often serve as transition functions within Model Predictive Control (MPC) frameworks to plan trajectories by optimizing action sequences. Prior work in this domain, such as DINO-WM (Zhou et al., 2024) and V-JEPA (Assran et al., 2025), has demonstrated potential in learning from reward-free, suboptimal trajectories and generalizing to new task configurations without fine-tuning. However, these methods typically succeed on short-horizon or single-stage objectives, such as reaching or grasping, and exhibit limitations when applied to long-horizon, multi-stage robotic tasks requiring complex sequential planning.

A primary challenge identified in existing world models is their reliance on patch-level features from large-scale encoders (e.g., DINOv2), which can be suboptimal for capturing fine-grained details necessary for robust dynamics learning in robotics. This paper builds upon the concept of object-centric representations, which learn distinct embeddings for individual objects, as explored in works like Haramati et al. (2026) and Slot Attention frameworks (Locatello et al., 2020). WorldDP specifically leverages DINOv2 patch features and guides representation learning with SAM2 (Ravi et al., 2025) segmentation masks to enhance feature grounding, distinguishing its approach from prior methods that either operate on raw pixels or use different guidance mechanisms.

Another limitation addressed by WorldDP is the single-tier planning structure prevalent in many existing world-model-based planners. These methods often optimize entire action sequences directly, which proves difficult for multi-stage tasks. Hierarchical execution, where high-level components predict subgoals for lower-level components to track, is a recognized paradigm in control and reinforcement learning (e.g., HECRL by Haramati et al., 2026). WorldDP extends this concept to latent world models, differing from approaches like HWM (Zhang et al., 2026) by utilizing a robust Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2025) for low-level subgoal tracking instead of another world model, aiming for faster and more robust execution.

Furthermore, existing planning methods often rely on optimization techniques like the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), which might not adequately capture the multi-modality inherent in robotic action spaces. WorldDP introduces the use of a Particle Filter for multi-modal optimization, proposing an alternative to CEM for improved subgoal generation. The incorporation of a contact predictor into the MPC cost function also aims to address the issue of imprecise subgoal generation by encouraging the model to identify and plan for pivotal interaction frames.

In summary, WorldDP positions itself as an advancement over existing world model frameworks by proposing a hierarchical structure that combines object-centric state representations, multi-modal planning with a contact-aware cost function, and robust low-level execution via diffusion policies, specifically tailored for the complexities of multi-stage robotic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce WorldDP, a novel world model framework designed to enable multi-stage robotic manipulation. This framework aims to overcome the limitations of current visual world models, which primarily succeed in single-stage tasks but struggle with tasks requiring complex sequential planning and long-horizon control.

The motivation for developing WorldDP stems from several identified shortcomings and opportunities in the existing research landscape:

1. **Addressing Limitations in Multi-Stage Tasks:** Current visual world models, despite their potential in learning complex system dynamics and integrating with MPC, have demonstrated limited success in multi-stage robotic tasks. These tasks, such as multi-object rearrangement or sequential interaction with environmental mechanisms, demand intricate sequential planning that existing single-stage methods often cannot handle effectively. The goal is to create a framework capable of decomposing and executing such long-horizon tasks.

2. **Improving State Representation with Object-Centric Features:** Many existing world models utilize patch-level features from large-scale visual encoders (e.g., DINOv2, V-JEPA). While these features are effective for some computer vision tasks, they can be suboptimal for learning dynamics in robotic contexts, as they may omit fine-grained details crucial for interacting with specific objects. The motivation here is to develop an object-centric representation that explicitly disentangles environmental entities (robot, objects, background), thereby facilitating better attention and individual planning for each item. This aims to provide a more precise and grounded state representation for dynamic learning.

3. **Enabling Hierarchical Planning for Long-Horizon Tasks:** Existing world-model-based planners often employ a flat, single-tier hierarchy, directly optimizing entire action sequences given a goal image. This approach becomes computationally intensive and inherently difficult for multi-stage tasks. The motivation is to introduce a hierarchical planning structure where a high-level world model can optimize for intermediate "subgoals," simplifying the overall task into manageable segments.

4. **Enhancing Subgoal Execution with Robust Policies:** While hierarchical latent world models have explored optimizing for intermediate subgoals, the lower-level execution often involves direct physical action optimization, which can be sensitive to the quality of subgoal selection. The motivation is to integrate a robust, low-level policy that can efficiently track and execute these subgoals. A diffusion policy is chosen for its robustness in short-horizon control and its ability to compensate for suboptimal subgoals to a certain extent, offering faster execution compared to repeated world model planning.

5. **Generating Precise and Action-Relevant Subgoals:** For manipulation tasks, subgoals should ideally represent critical interaction points, such as the moment a robot makes contact with an object. Current models may not inherently generate such pivotal states. A specific motivation is to encourage the generation of these critical subgoals by incorporating a contact predictor into the MPC cost function, thereby guiding the planning process towards more actionable intermediate states.

6. **Adapting Optimization for Robotic Task Multi-Modality:** Robotic tasks often have multiple distinct, equally valid action sequences that can lead to the same goal, indicating an inherent multi-modality in the action space. Traditional optimization methods like the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), which often rely on single Gaussian distributions, may not effectively explore this multi-modal landscape. The motivation is to employ an optimization strategy, such as a Particle Filter, that can maintain a diverse set of hypotheses and better capture these varied solutions, leading to more robust planning.

In summary, WorldDP is motivated by the need for a more capable and efficient framework for multi-stage robotic tasks, achieved through enhanced state representations, hierarchical planning, robust low-level execution, and a more suitable optimization strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldDP is structured as a hierarchical framework that combines an object-centric world model with a goal-conditioned diffusion policy. The system is designed to address multi-stage robotic manipulation tasks by decomposing them into sequential subgoals.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem involves finding a sequence of actions {a_0, a_1...} to transition from an initial observation I_0 to a target goal image I_g. Actions a_k are 5-dimensional vectors controlling end-effector (x, y, z) coordinates, yaw, and gripper opening. The framework is trained on a "play" dataset of image-action trajectories (I_0, a_0, I_1, a_1...) which are reward-free and may contain suboptimal interactions.

#### 3.2. Object-Centric Encoder (OCE)

The OCE is responsible for extracting discrete, object-centric state representations from raw image observations.
- **Input Processing:** An input image I_k is first processed by a frozen DINOv2 encoder (Oquab et al., 2023) to generate patch-level features z_k ∈ R^(P×d), where P is the number of patches and d is feature dimensionality.
- **Slot Attention:** Following the slot attention framework (Locatello et al., 2020), the OCE initializes N slots ¯s_k ∈ R^(N×d) representing entities like the robot, objects, and background. These slots are refined by a Slot Corrector through recursive cross-attention and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) blocks, using z_k for queries/values and ¯s_k for keys. The output is the object-centric state representation s_k ∈ R^(N×d).
- **Training:** The Slot Corrector is trained using a Slot Decoder. The decoder predicts per-slot reconstructions ˆz_n_k ∈ R^(P×d) and masks ˆm_k ∈ R^(N×P). The reconstructed patch-level embedding ˆz_k is aggregated from ˆm_k and ˆz_n_k.
 - **Reconstruction Loss:** L_l2_oce = ||z_k - ˆz_k||^2_2 enforces the reconstructed patches to match the DINOv2 input.
 - **SAM2 Guidance:** To improve feature grounding and prevent small objects from being under-represented, the training incorporates an auxiliary loss. Ground-truth segmentation masks m_k are generated using SAM2 (Ravi et al., 2025) and downsampled to patch resolution.
 - **Tversky Loss:** A Tversky loss L_tv is used to supervise the predicted masks ˆm_k against m_k, with hyperparameters α=0.99 and β=0.01 to balance false positives and negatives. An inverse-size weighting scheme prioritizes small objects.
 - **Total OCE Loss:** L_oce = L_l2_oce + λ_oce * L_tv (with λ_oce = 1). The OCE is trained independently and kept frozen during subsequent dynamics model training.

#### 3.3. Conditional Diffusion Transformer (CDiT) Dynamics Prediction Model

This model learns the transition dynamics in the object-centric latent space.
- **Architecture:** It adapts the Conditional Diffusion Transformer (CDiT) architecture from Goswami et al. (2025), but processes object-centric states s_k ∈ R^(N×d) instead of patch-level states. The CDiT has a depth of 12 layers and 4 attention heads.
- **Action Encoding:** An action sequence a_k1→k2 is compressed into a latent action embedding a_k1 ∈ R^32 via a transformer-based action encoder, which conditions the CDiT through Adaptive Layer Normalization layers.
- **Training:** For each training sample, 5 frames are randomly sampled from a 100-frame data horizon. The model predicts the next state ˆs_k_i+1 conditioned on previous ground-truth states {s_k_i..., s_k_1} using teacher forcing, minimized by an MSE loss. Random temporal skips are employed to enhance robustness.
- **Inference:** During task execution, the model operates autoregressively, feeding predicted states and subsequent action chunks back into f_θ to roll out future states.

#### 3.4. Task Execution: Hierarchical MPC

The trained dynamics model is integrated into a two-tier hierarchical MPC framework for task execution.
- **Upper Tier (World Model Planning):**
 - **Goal:** From an initial state s_0 and a goal state s_g (derived from I_g), the world model plans for high-level subgoals over a horizon of T world-model steps.
 - **Optimization via Particle Filtering (PF):** Instead of CEM, a Particle Filter is used for multi-modal optimization.
 - Initialization: Spatial trajectories for the end-effector to keypoints are generated, concatenated with random yaw and gripper commands, and encoded to form latent seed action sequences (initial particles).
 - Sampling & Evaluation: Q action sequences ¯a^{q} are sampled around these seed means with standard deviation σ. Autoregressive rollouts through the dynamics model f_θ generate predicted state trajectories s^{q}.
 - Selection: A cost function C(ˆs^{q}_T, s_g) evaluates each sequence. The top M candidates are selected as refined means for the next L iterations. The highest-ranking sequence defines the optimal subgoal sequence {ˆs^{o}_1..., ˆs^{o}_T}.
 - **Object-Centric Cost Function:**
 - Operates on N-2 object embeddings (excluding agent and background).
 - C_dist: Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted object embeddings ˆo^{q}_T and target object goal states o_g.
 - Contact-Prediction Cost: A lightweight MLP contact predictor maps state s_k to an (N-2)-dimensional vector of contact probabilities. C_ce is a cross-entropy loss between the rollout contact trajectory c^{q} and a one-hot reference vector c_ref for the target object. This encourages subgoals to represent critical manipulation phases (e.g., grasping).
 - Total Cost: C(ˆs^{q}_T, s_g) = C_dist + λ_plan ⋅ C_ce, where λ_plan balances objectives. For single-object tasks, the object with the largest embedding delta between start and goal frames is identified for optimization.
 - **Hierarchical Execution:** For multi-stage tasks requiring sequential manipulation (e.g., Cube-Triple), planning and execution occur iteratively: plan for one object, execute its subgoals, capture a new observation, then re-plan for the next object.

- **Lower Tier (Goal-Conditioned Diffusion Policy - DP):**
 - **Purpose:** Sequentially tracks and executes the optimal subgoals {ˆs^{o}_1..., ˆs^{o}_T} generated by the upper-tier world model.
 - **Architecture:** Adapts the transformer-based Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2025). Input consists of flattened object-centric current and goal states, along with robot end-effector position and velocity.
 - **Output:** Generates 40-step action sequences.
 - **Enhancements:** Randomized goal-sampling for temporal generalization during training. During evaluation, actions operate in the end-effector's operational space and are executed via an inverse kinematics controller. An end-effector-mounted depth camera dynamically corrects residual pose errors for precise grasping.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Robotic Tasks:** Evaluated on OGBench (Park et al., 2025) variants: Cube-Single, Cube-Triple, Scene-Single-Direct, and Scene-Single-Composite. These tasks vary in complexity, requiring precise control, task inference, causal dependencies, and multi-object manipulation.
- **Datasets:** 2 million frames per environment configuration, collected from randomized agent interactions, lacking rewards or goal annotations.
- **Training Settings:** Separate OCEs, Dynamics Models, Diffusion Policies, and Contact Predictors are trained for each environment. Images are resized to 224x224. Dynamics model training uses 4 context frames from a 100-frame window. Training for 3-10 epochs with AdamW optimizer and one-cycle learning rate schedulers.
- **Baselines:** Compared against DINO-WM, LeWM, HECRL* (an adapted HECRL with DP), and Goal-Conditioned Diffusion Policy (DP40, DP100). All baselines are trained on the same data with their original hyperparameter configurations where applicable.
- **Evaluation:** 50 held-out test trajectories per task. Success defined by Euclidean distance for cubes or by states of drawer/window/buttons for scene tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on several multi-stage robotic benchmarks demonstrate the performance of WorldDP relative to existing methods and reveal the contributions of its individual components.

#### 4.1. Performance on Multi-Stage Manipulation Tasks

WorldDP consistently demonstrates superior performance on complex multi-stage manipulation tasks:
- **Cube-Triple:** In the task of manipulating three cubes, WorldDP achieved a 100% success rate for placing at least one cube, 72% for at least two cubes, and 30% for all three cubes. This significantly outperforms all baselines, with the next best method (HECRL\*) achieving 78%, 34%, and 12% respectively for the same metrics.
- **Scene-Single-Composite:** For tasks involving a prerequisite button press followed by manipulation, WorldDP achieved a 60% button press success rate and a 20% full task completion rate. While DP100 had a higher button press success (78%), its full task completion (14%) was lower than WorldDP. Other baselines, DINO-WM and LeWM, showed 0% full task success.

#### 4.2. Performance on Simpler Tasks

Even on less complex tasks, WorldDP generally performs well:
- **Cube-Single:** WorldDP achieved a 72% success rate. While DP100 and HECRL\* showed higher success rates (98%), WorldDP demonstrated competitive performance.
- **Scene-Single-Direct:** This task involves a single object modification (button, drawer, or window). WorldDP achieved an average success rate of 74% across these subtasks (81.25% for Button, 76.47% for Drawer, 64.71% for Window), outperforming all baselines on average. For context, HECRL\* averaged 30%, and DP40 averaged 44%.

#### 4.3. Impact of World Model and Diffusion Policy Unification

Ablation studies investigated the contribution of WorldDP's hierarchical structure:
- **"w/o DP" (World Model planning directly optimizes actions):** This configuration yielded lower success rates across nearly all tasks compared to the full WorldDP, indicating the benefit of the low-level Diffusion Policy for executing subgoals.
- **"DP Only" (Standalone Diffusion Policy):** The standalone DP (DP40) performed marginally better than WorldDP only on the simple button-pressing task but showed lower performance on most other tasks, particularly those requiring more complex multi-stage planning.
- **Policy Horizon:** Using a 100-step diffusion policy (DP100) within WorldDP resulted in slightly lower performance (e.g., 58% on Scene-Single-Direct, 8% on Scene-Single-Composite) compared to the default 40-step policy (74% and 20% respectively). This suggests that the shorter horizon is more effective for executing the closely spaced and precise subgoals generated by the upper-tier world model.

#### 4.4. Impact of Object-Centric Encoding (OCE)

The role of object-centric representation was evaluated:
- **"w/o OCE" (Using raw DINOv2 patch-level embeddings as states):** This variant performed worse than the full WorldDP, except for matching performance on the Window task within Scene-Single-Direct. This indicates that object-centric encoding contributes to overall performance.
- **Optimization with Image Embeddings vs. Object Embeddings:** When optimizing using image embeddings in the cost function instead of object embeddings, performance on Scene-Single-Composite remained identical (20% success). However, performance on the more challenging Cube-Triple task significantly degraded, dropping from WorldDP's 30% to 6% for 3-cubes, 72% to 32% for 2-cubes, and 100% to 82% for 1-cube. This highlights the importance of the object-centric cost function for complex, multi-object tasks.

#### 4.5. Contributions of Particle Filter (PF) and Contact Predictor (CP)

The individual components of the planning strategy were ablated:
- **"w/o PF" (Using Cross-Entropy Method instead of Particle Filter):** This modification degraded performance, suggesting the Particle Filter's advantage in handling the multi-modality of robotic action spaces.
- **"w/o CP" (Omitting the Contact Predictor):** The absence of the Contact Predictor caused a substantial drop in success rate, underscoring its role in generating critical and precise subgoals related to physical interactions.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Visualizations

- **Task Execution:** Visualizations of tasks like Scene-Single-Composite demonstrate the framework's ability to decompose complex tasks into sequential planning phases. The system plans subgoals for one object (e.g., buttons), executes them via the Diffusion Policy, and then re-plans for subsequent objects (e.g., drawer and window).
- **OCE Predictions:** Visualizations of predicted slot masks show coarser, patch-level boundaries that encapsulate objects of interest, consistent with the OCE operating on DINOv2 patch features.
- **World Model Rollouts:** Open-loop rollouts, such as on the Cube-Triple dataset, illustrate the world model's capacity for accurate simulation, predicting future states given an initial frame and a sequence of ground-truth actions.

In summary, WorldDP demonstrates improved capabilities for multi-stage robotic tasks through its hierarchical design, object-centric representations, and specialized planning components, outperforming comparative baselines. The ablations confirm the individual contributions of the Diffusion Policy, Object-Centric Encoder, Particle Filter, and Contact Predictor to the overall framework's effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces WorldDP, a hierarchical framework for multi-stage robotic tasks, demonstrating a method for integrating object-centric world models with diffusion policies. The work addresses several limitations present in existing approaches to robotic manipulation and offers advancements with potential impact on the field.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Enhanced Multi-Stage Robotic Manipulation:** WorldDP demonstrates the capability to solve complex, long-horizon multi-stage robotic tasks, which have historically posed challenges for single-tier world models. By outperforming established baselines on tasks like multi-object rearrangement (Cube-Triple) and causally dependent interactions (Scene-Single-Composite), the framework indicates progress in robust robotic control.
- **Effective Hierarchical Planning:** The hierarchical structure, which decomposes complex tasks into simpler subgoals planned by a high-level world model and executed by a low-level diffusion policy, represents a significant contribution. This approach leverages the strengths of both components: the world model's physically grounded long-term planning and the diffusion policy's efficient and robust short-term execution.
- **Utility of Object-Centric Representations:** The adoption of object-centric state representations, built upon DINOv2 features and guided by SAM2 masks, is shown to improve dynamics learning and planning. This approach enables the model to reason and plan individually for distinct environmental entities, which is crucial for multi-object manipulation.
- **Advanced Planning Optimization:** The use of a Particle Filter for optimizing latent action sequences is a novel application within world model-based planning for robotics. Its ability to accommodate the inherent multi-modality of robotic action spaces, where multiple valid solutions exist, contributes to more robust subgoal generation compared to single-modal optimizers.
- **Contact-Aware Subgoal Generation:** The integration of a contact predictor into the MPC cost function is a method for encouraging the generation of pivotal, physically meaningful subgoals (e.g., grasp points). This mechanism improves the precision and actionability of the intermediate states generated by the planner.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Broader Applicability of World Models:** This work expands the practical applicability of visual world models beyond simple, single-stage tasks, making them more suitable for real-world robotic scenarios that commonly involve sequential and complex interactions.
- **Foundational for Generalist Robots:** The ability to learn and execute complex tasks from reward-free, unannotated trajectories, combined with the focus on object-centric interactions, could contribute to developing more generalist robotic agents capable of adapting to diverse environments and tasks.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The insights into combining different learning paradigms (world models for planning, diffusion models for control), effective representation learning, and multi-modal optimization strategies could inspire further research at the intersection of embodied AI, latent space planning, and robot learning. For instance, scaling model capacity and enabling cross-embodiment learning are identified as potential future extensions.

#### 5.3. Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors

Despite its successes, the paper also identifies certain limitations:
- **Reliance on Image-Based Goals:** The framework currently relies on image-based goals, which can be restrictive. Future work might explore more flexible language-based or abstract goal specifications.
- **Computational Intensity of Particle Filter:** The sampling-based Particle Filter optimization is computationally intensive, requiring up to two minutes of test-time overhead at the start of a task. The authors suggest gradient-based or parallelized optimization as potential mitigation strategies.
- **Training Data Sampling:** The current approach uses random frame sampling during dynamics model training. The authors propose that smarter sampling strategies could further enhance performance.

Overall, WorldDP presents a significant step towards more capable and autonomous robotic systems by providing a comprehensive, hierarchical framework for multi-stage manipulation, while also highlighting avenues for future improvement.
