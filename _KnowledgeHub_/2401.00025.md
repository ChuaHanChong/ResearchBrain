# Any-point Trajectory Modeling for Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Any-point Trajectory Modeling (ATM) introduces a two-stage framework that leverages abundant unlabeled video data to pre-train a model capable of predicting future 2D point trajectories. This pre-trained model then guides a robot policy, trained on minimal action-labeled demonstrations, leading to enhanced performance on diverse manipulation tasks and enabling cross-embodiment skill transfer.

## Problem

- High cost and time consumption of collecting sufficient action-labeled demonstration data for robot learning, leading to a significant data scarcity bottleneck.
- Prior video pre-training methods struggle to effectively bridge the gap to policy learning, either by only learning general visual features (lacking temporal dynamics) or by computationally intensive pixel-level prediction (prone to hallucinations).
- Difficulty in transferring robot skills across different embodiments (e.g., human to robot) or robot morphologies due to reliance on task-specific features or limited generalization of learned representations.

## Method

- A two-stage framework: first pre-trains a '==Track Transformer==' model to predict future 2D trajectories of arbitrary points from action-free video datasets, using a self-supervised approach with off-the-shelf point trackers and masked modeling.
- Second, a visuomotor policy is trained with behavioral cloning on a minimal set of action-labeled demonstrations, taking the current observation and the predicted future point trajectories from the frozen Track Transformer as input.
- The policy architecture integrates the predicted trajectories through both early and late fusion, effectively transforming the end-to-end control problem into a sub-goal following task without requiring explicit language instructions during policy execution.

## Results

- Outperformed state-of-the-art video pre-training baselines on the LIBERO benchmark, achieving an average **63%** success rate compared to the highest baseline of **37%** on over 130 language-conditioned manipulation tasks.
- Demonstrated successful human-to-robot and robot-to-robot (cross-morphology) skill transfer by leveraging unlabeled human or different robot videos for trajectory modeling.
- Improved data efficiency, achieving comparable or better performance with only **4%** of action-labeled robot demonstrations relative to baselines requiring **20%** or more.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Any-point trajectory modeling provides a structured, physically grounded, and computationally efficient representation for motion dynamics, avoiding the issues of pixel-level video prediction models.
> - Decoupling the learning of general motion priors from abundant unlabeled video data and task-specific policy learning with minimal labeled data makes robot skill acquisition more scalable and data-efficient.
> - Predicted point trajectories serve as dense, concrete subgoals, simplifying the downstream policy learning problem and enabling more robust control.
