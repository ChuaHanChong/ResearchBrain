# SoMoFormer: Multi-Person Pose Forecasting with Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> SoMoFormer is a transformer encoder for multi-person 3D pose forecasting that treats each joint coordinate trajectory, DCT-encoded, as an input token instead of each timestep. Learned joint-type, person-identity, and grid-position embeddings let attention span joints of all people, predicting whole future sequences for everyone in one non-recurrent pass. It sets state of the art on SoMoF, CMU-Mocap, and MuPoTS-3D.

## Problem

- Most pose forecasters predict one person at a time, discarding global translation and the social context of other people moving in the same scene.
- Trajectory forecasters reduce each person to a 2D ground-plane point, giving useful translation but no detailed body pose or joint-level dynamics.
- Prior transformers use timestep tokens, so multi-person extension needs mixed time-and-person query sequences, repeated passes per person, and recurrent variants accumulate long-horizon error.

## Method

- Problem reformulated as joint-sequence trajectory completion: each token is one Cartesian coordinate of one joint across all input plus constant-padded future frames, ==DCT==-encoded in the frequency domain.
- Learned embeddings added per token for joint type and person identity, plus a concatenated grid-cell position embedding encoding each person's global scene location after translation is subtracted.
- Training uses auxiliary ==L2 loss== on every one of six layers, random rotation about the vertical axis, random first-frame sampling, sequence reversal, and random person permutation with padding masks.

## Results

- On the SoMoF 3DPW test set, overall VIM is **46.3** versus **49.4** for the best prior transformer, improving at every horizon from 100ms (**9.1**) to 900ms (**91.9**).
- On MuPoTS-3D, MPJPE is **0.173**/**0.305**/**0.423** at 1/2/3 seconds against **0.206**/**0.393**/**0.574** for the multi-range baseline; CMU-Mocap improves to **0.42**/**0.80**/**1.06**.
- Ablation on the 3DPW validation set drops overall VIM from **50.3** baseline to **47.4** with learned embeddings, **43.5** adding augmentation, and **41.6** adding grid positioning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Choosing the token axis matters more than model capacity: tokenizing by joint rather than timestep makes multi-agent extension a matter of appending queries.
> - Frequency-domain padded-trajectory completion lets one forward pass emit an entire future sequence, avoiding recurrent error accumulation over long horizons.
> - Attention learned symmetric left-right and distance-decaying inter-person structure without any skeleton prior, but gains concentrate on genuinely interactive data rather than synthesized multi-person mixtures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human pose forecasting is a research area with applications in robotics, computer graphics, and autonomous vehicle tracking. The field broadly addresses predicting future human body configurations based on observed past movements.

Historically, approaches to human motion prediction have branched into several categories:
- **Human Trajectory Forecasting:** These methods model individuals as 2D points, focusing on predicting their global movement on a plane. While useful for spatial navigation, they do not provide detailed body pose information. Early works utilized RNNs and GANs, with more recent advancements employing Transformer networks to model trajectories.
- **Single-Person Pose Forecasting:** This area focuses on predicting the future joint coordinates of a single person, typically without considering global translation. Initial methods involved Hidden Markov Models, linear dynamics models, and hand-crafted features. More recent developments have shifted towards end-to-end neural networks, often employing RNNs, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), or attention-based models to capture motion dynamics and predict entire sequences non-recurrently.
- **Multi-Person Pose Forecasting:** With the increasing demand for understanding complex environments, research has expanded to predict the poses of multiple individuals simultaneously. This domain adds the challenge of modeling social interactions and dependencies between people. Previous works in this area have attempted to integrate social context through pooling mechanisms, graph attentional networks, or transformer-based architectures. However, these methods often exhibit limitations such as reliance on recurrent architectures (leading to error accumulation over long horizons), modeling the entire pose sequence as a time series (which complicates multi-person scenarios due to variable numbers of people or makes it difficult to model joint relationships), or the necessity to predict poses for one person at a time, requiring multiple passes for a complete scene. Specifically, existing transformer-based approaches for pose forecasting typically structure input as a time series, posing difficulties for direct extension to multi-person scenarios due to the challenge of simultaneously handling temporal and person-series sequences.

SoMoFormer positions itself within the multi-person 3D pose forecasting domain. It seeks to address the identified limitations of prior work by reformulating the input representation for transformer models, moving from time-series to joint-series inputs. This reformulation is intended to enhance the model's ability to capture intricate joint relationships and simultaneously predict the poses of multiple individuals in a single pass, distinguishing it from methods that predict person-by-person or rely on sequential temporal processing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel method for multi-person 3D pose forecasting that accurately predicts future human motion while simultaneously modeling complex interactions between body joints and between multiple people in a scene.

The motivation for SoMoFormer stems from several observed limitations in the existing research landscape:
- **Complexity of Human Motion:** Human motion is inherently complex, involving intricate body dynamics and posture changes. When multiple people are present, an individual's motion can also be influenced by the movements of others. Current models often struggle to fully capture these multi-faceted dynamics.
- **Limitations of Time-Series Modeling:** Many existing pose forecasting methods model the entire pose sequence as a time series. This approach often overlooks the underlying kinematic and anatomical relationships between different joints within a single human body, potentially limiting the model's understanding of human body dynamics.
- **Inefficiency in Multi-Person Scenarios:** A significant number of prior works are designed for single-person pose forecasting or, when extended to multi-person scenes, are limited in their efficiency. Some methods predict the future pose sequence of only one person at a time, requiring multiple passes to generate predictions for an entire scene. This can be computationally intensive and unsuitable for real-time applications.
- **Challenges with Transformer Generalization:** While attention-based models, particularly Transformers, have shown promise in long-term motion prediction, their application to multi-person pose forecasting has been constrained. Previous Transformer architectures often use a fixed input size with a time-series input, making direct generalization to scenes with a variable number of people difficult or requiring complex input query structures that introduce challenges like multiple input queries corresponding to the same timestep.
- **Accumulation of Errors in Recurrent Architectures:** Methods relying on recurrent neural networks (RNNs) tend to accumulate errors over longer prediction horizons, which affects the accuracy of long-term motion forecasts. The objective is to develop a non-recurrent approach that mitigates this issue.
- **Need for Unified Prediction:** The motivation includes creating a unified framework capable of performing multi-person pose forecasting in a single pass, effectively learning relationships both within a person's joints and between different people.

In summary, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of prior time-series, single-person, or inefficient multi-person forecasting approaches by introducing a problem reformulation that allows a Transformer architecture to attend over individual joint trajectories for all people concurrently, thereby enhancing the model's capacity to learn comprehensive human motion and social dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SoMoFormer employs a Transformer Encoder architecture designed to unify multi-person pose forecasting. The methodology fundamentally reformulates the pose forecasting problem by considering input queries as joint trajectories rather than time-series poses.

#### 3.1. Problem Reformulation

Instead of treating the input as a sequence of full-body poses over time, SoMoFormer processes the input as a sequence of *joint trajectories*. Each input token to the Transformer represents the entire trajectory (over the observed and future timeframes) of a single Cartesian coordinate (x, y, or z) of a specific joint. For a scene with N people, each having J joints, the model accepts N × J × 3 input queries in total. This structure enables the model to perform attention over individual joints from all people concurrently.

#### 3.2. Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)

To represent human motion, which is continuous and smooth, the model utilizes the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT). Each joint coordinate sequence (combining observed history and a padded future sequence) is transformed into a set of DCT coefficients. These coefficients form the input tokens for the Transformer. The use of DCT allows the model to predict the entire trajectory for all future frames simultaneously, rather than step-by-step. The output of the Transformer, also in the DCT domain, is then converted back to Cartesian coordinates using the inverse DCT. A constant-padded sequence for the future frames is used as an initial guess, simplifying the prediction task to refining these coefficients.

#### 3.3. Proposed Architecture

The core of SoMoFormer is a Transformer Encoder.
- **Input Tokens:** Each token is a DCT-encoded vector representing one Cartesian dimension (x, y, or z) of a joint's trajectory across the entire (input + future) timeframe.
- **Global Translation Removal:** To improve generalization, global translation is removed from the joint coordinates by subtracting the neck or pelvis joint coordinate of the last known frame for each person. This translation is re-added after prediction to recover global positions.
- **Learned Embeddings:**
 - **Joint Type Embedding:** A learned embedding unique to each joint type (e.g., right knee, left shoulder) is added to the joint query. This helps the model disambiguate joint types and learn internal body dynamics.
 - **Person Identity Embedding:** A learned embedding specific to each person's identity is also added to the joint query. This allows the model to differentiate between individuals and model inter-person interactions.
 - **Grid Positioning Embedding:** To reintroduce global location information after its removal from joint coordinates, the scene is divided into a grid of cells. Each cell is assigned a randomly-initialized, learnable positional embedding. For each person, this grid embedding (determined by their neck joint's position at the last known frame) is *concatenated* to the joint trajectory representation. This helps the model learn relationships based on the relative global positions of people.

#### 3.4. Training Enhancements and Data Augmentation

Several strategies are employed during training to enhance model robustness and generalization:
- **Random First-Frame Selection:** Subsequences are sampled from longer training sequences by randomly selecting a start frame. This increases the diversity of training examples.
- **Reversed Pose Sequences:** Pose sequences are reversed and used as additional training data.
- **Random Rotation:** Scenes are randomly rotated about the vertical y-axis within [0, 2π]. This augmentation prevents overfitting to specific orientations and aids the learned grid embedding in seeing varied scenes.
- **Random Person Permutation:** The order in which people's joint queries are input to the model is randomly permuted. This makes the learned identity embeddings more robust and ensures fair exposure to non-padded training examples across all identity embeddings, especially in scenes with varying numbers of people requiring padding.

#### 3.5. Loss Function

The model uses an L2 loss to minimize the error between the ground-truth and predicted 3D joint coordinates. A key aspect is the application of an *auxiliary loss* at the output of *every* layer of the 6-layer Transformer encoder. This encourages iterative refinement of the predicted motion at each layer, with specific weights (λ) assigned to the loss from each layer.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The Transformer encoder consists of 6 layers, 8 attention heads, and a hidden dimension of 1024. Training is conducted for 256 epochs with a batch size of 512, using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.001, which is decayed by 0.1 towards the end of training. The auxiliary loss weights are λ₆ = 1 and λ₁₋₅ = 0.2. A 5x5 grid (25 unique grid embeddings) is used for global positioning. The model is developed using PyTorch.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings based on quantitative evaluation, an ablation study, and qualitative analysis.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

SoMoFormer's performance was evaluated against state-of-the-art methods on multiple datasets:

- **SoMoF Benchmark (3DPW Test Set):** On the 3D Poses in the Wild (3DPW) dataset, evaluated using the SoMoF benchmark, SoMoFormer consistently outperformed previous methods in Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and Visibility-Ignored Metric (VIM) for long-term prediction. For a prediction horizon of 930 ms (after 1070 ms input), SoMoFormer achieved an overall VIM of 46.3, compared to the best prior method, FutureMotion (49.4), and significantly lower than other baselines (e.g., Mo-Att + ST-GAT at 149.6). This performance advantage was maintained across all reported prediction timesteps (100ms, 240ms, 500ms, 640ms, 900ms).

- **CMU-Mocap and MuPoTS-3D Test Sets:** SoMoFormer was also evaluated on the CMU-Mocap and MuPoTS-3D datasets, reporting MPJPE over 1, 2, and 3-second prediction horizons.
 - On **CMU-Mocap**, SoMoFormer achieved MPJPEs of 0.42 (1 sec), 0.80 (2 sec), and 1.06 (3 sec), outperforming Multi-Range (0.456, 0.840, 1.11), LTD (0.480, 0.869, 1.181), and HRI (0.503, 0.932, 1.422).
 - On **MuPoTS-3D**, SoMoFormer showed a more significant improvement with MPJPEs of 0.173 (1 sec), 0.305 (2 sec), and 0.423 (3 sec), substantially lower than Multi-Range (0.206, 0.393, 0.574), LTD (0.191, 0.337, 0.466), and HRI (0.261, 0.469, 0.714). The authors note that the substantial performance difference on MuPoTS-3D, which contains real social interactions rather than synthesized poses, suggests the model's effectiveness in modeling multi-person dynamics.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted on the SoMoF 3DPW validation set to assess the contribution of individual components of SoMoFormer:
- **Baseline:** The baseline model (without learned embeddings, data augmentation, or grid positioning) achieved an overall VIM of 50.3 and MPJPE of 12.0.
- **+ Learned Embeddings:** Adding learned joint type and person identity embeddings improved performance to an overall VIM of 47.4 and MPJPE of 11.5. This indicates that these embeddings assist the model in disambiguating joint types and learning relationships.
- **+ Data Augmentation:** Incorporating random first-frame selection, sequence flipping, and person permutation further improved results to an overall VIM of 43.5 and MPJPE of 10.6. This suggests that data augmentation significantly enhances the model's generalization capabilities.
- **+ Grid Positioning:** The inclusion of grid positioning embeddings yielded the best performance, with an overall VIM of 41.6 and MPJPE of 10.1. This demonstrates the importance of providing global location information for modeling social interactions in multi-person scenarios.

The study concluded that each introduced component contributes positively to the model's overall performance.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

- **Interactions between Multiple People:** Analysis of the attention weights revealed a correlation between the physical distance between two people and the average attention score between their respective joint pairs. Closer proximity generally resulted in higher attention scores, indicating that SoMoFormer effectively models social interactions by attending more strongly to nearby individuals.
- **Visualized Examples:** Visualizations of predicted motion sequences on the 3DPW test set demonstrate that SoMoFormer's predictions closely match the ground truth, even in complex multi-person scenarios, such as two people walking side-by-side.
- **Attention Weights Visualization:** Detailed visualizations of attention weights across different layers and for specific joints provided insights into the model's learned representations:
 - Different layers learned to attend to distinct sets of input joints, which correlated with plausible human body dynamics (e.g., right-side joints attending to the right hip in Layer 2, strong symmetrical attention between leg joints in Layers 4 and 5).
 - The model implicitly learned to treat left and right sides of the body symmetrically without explicit skeletal information.
 - Individual joint attention patterns varied with motion: for example, a left foot joint in a standing pose primarily attended to other leg joints, but in a walking scenario, it exhibited stronger attention to the neck joint (representing overall motion) and less to arm joints. This suggests that the model develops a nuanced understanding of which joints are most relevant for predicting specific motions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SoMoFormer presents a novel framework for multi-person pose forecasting that introduces a reformulation of the input problem for Transformer architectures. This work contributes to the field in several ways:

- **Architectural Innovation:** The core innovation lies in treating human motion input as a sequence of joint trajectories rather than a time-series of poses. This allows the Transformer to perform attention over individual joints from all people simultaneously, providing a unified and non-recurrent approach to multi-person pose forecasting. This departs from prior methods that either predict one person at a time or struggle with variable numbers of people due to time-series input constraints.
- **Performance Advancement:** SoMoFormer establishes new state-of-the-art performance on challenging multi-person pose forecasting benchmarks, including the SoMoF benchmark on 3DPW, CMU-Mocap, and MuPoTS-3D datasets. The significant improvement on MuPoTS-3D, which features real social interactions, suggests the model's enhanced ability to capture complex inter-person dynamics.
- **Enhanced Representational Learning:** The explicit modeling of joint relationships and inter-person interactions through learned joint type, person identity, and grid positioning embeddings, combined with data augmentation strategies, enables the model to learn rich and semantically meaningful representations of human motion. The analysis of attention weights provides evidence of this, showing that the model attends to joints in a manner consistent with human biomechanics and social context.
- **Efficiency and Generalizability:** By predicting an entire future pose sequence for multiple people in a single pass without recurrence, SoMoFormer offers potential for increased efficiency compared to recurrent or person-by-person prediction methods. Its design naturally extends to scenes with varying numbers of people, addressing a common challenge in multi-person modeling.
- **Broad Applicability:** The capabilities of SoMoFormer have potential impact across various domains:
 - **Autonomous Vehicles:** Accurate prediction of human motion and intentions is crucial for safe navigation in environments with pedestrians, enabling more reliable operational decisions.
 - **Robotics:** Anticipating human activities can facilitate reactive robotic responses and improve human-robot collaboration.
 - **Computer Graphics and Gaming:** Generating realistic and natural human motion for skeletal meshes can enhance the visual fidelity and interactivity of virtual environments.
 - **Low-Latency Applications:** The non-recurrent, single-pass prediction of entire trajectories may make SoMoFormer suitable for applications requiring high efficiency and low latency.

The detailed analysis of attention weights also opens avenues for further research into the interpretability and semantic meaning of the learned representations, potentially leading to a deeper understanding of human motion modeling.
