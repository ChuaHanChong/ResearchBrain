# Learning Versatile Humanoid Manipulation with Touch Dreaming

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework is presented for enabling humanoid robots to perform versatile, contact-rich manipulation by integrating stable whole-body control, dexterous hand capabilities, and a "Touch Dreaming" method for predictive tactile learning. This approach achieved a 30 percentage point increase in success rate on real-world tasks compared to existing behavioral cloning methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid loco-manipulation is challenging due to tight coupling between whole-body stability and end-effector dexterity, especially under frequent and uncertain contact changes.
- Existing humanoid systems often lack a practical, integrated platform that combines robust whole-body control, full dexterous-hand capabilities, and effective tactile sensing for contact-rich tasks.
- Current learning paradigms struggle with partial observability and abrupt contact changes, and integrating tactile information often requires separate pretraining or complex multi-stage inference, limiting its use in general humanoid policies.

## Method

![[2604.13015-method.png]]
*The HTD architecture uses modality tokenizers to process multimodal inputs, a transformer trunk for reasoning, and modular experts for action prediction and touch dreaming.*

- An ==RL-based lower-body controller (LBC)== was developed using a ==teacher-student framework== to provide stable whole-body execution and track complex commands during manipulation.
- A ==VR-based teleoperation system== was established for efficient real-world data collection, integrating LBC, upper-body inverse kinematics, dexterous hand retargeting, and distributed tactile sensing.
- The **Humanoid Transformer with Touch Dreaming (HTD)** policy was designed, featuring a multimodal encoder-decoder Transformer that incorporates a ==touch dreaming== auxiliary objective to predict future hand forces and tactile latents, regularizing the policy to learn contact-aware representations.

## Results

- The proposed LBC achieved superior or comparable tracking performance across most metrics for linear velocity, torso height, and orientation, indicating tighter whole-body regulation compared to baseline controllers.
- HTD consistently outperformed ACT baselines on five diverse real-world contact-rich tasks, demonstrating an average **30.0 percentage point** improvement in success rate and **17.9 percentage point** improvement in task score.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the "Touch Dreaming" objective, particularly with latent tactile supervision, significantly improved performance, yielding a **30%** relative gain in average success rate compared to raw tactile prediction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective integration of a robust lower-body controller with VR-based teleoperation is crucial for collecting high-quality, multimodal data necessary for complex humanoid loco-manipulation.
> - Supervising future tactile signals in a learned latent space using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher is more effective and stable than predicting raw tactile data, providing semantically rich and noise-robust contact-aware representations.
> - A single-stage behavioral cloning policy, augmented with predictive touch objectives, can successfully learn versatile, contact-rich behaviors from demonstrations, simplifying deployment by avoiding multi-stage inference or explicit world models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are recognized for their potential in general-purpose physical assistance. The broader research landscape in humanoid robotics has seen progress in whole-body control, teleoperation, and learning systems. However, real-world humanoid loco-manipulation, which involves coordinated movement, stable balance, and dexterous interaction with objects, remains challenging. This is due to the physical coupling between whole-body stability and end-effector dexterity, especially under frequent and uncertain contact changes. Existing systems often face limitations in jointly supporting stable whole-body execution, full dexterous-hand control, and tactile sensing or modeling in a single practical platform for contact-rich manipulation.

Previous work in humanoid control and teleoperation has explored various control interfaces (e.g., root tracking, joint-space tracking, body-keypoint tracking) and approaches to robustness (e.g., decomposition of control functions, adaptive compliance, hybrid optimization-and-learning frameworks, unified controllers). Teleoperation systems have also advanced, leveraging RGB- or pose-based shadowing and immersive VR interfaces. Building on these, recent imitation learning efforts have enabled humanoids to acquire skills from demonstrations, with advancements in data collection scalability, policy parameterizations, and human-data supervision.

However, a noted gap is the limited integration of tactile sensing and modeling into learned humanoid manipulation policies that also feature whole-body control and full end-effector dexterity. While some works have demonstrated the value of tactile information in visuo-tactile manipulation, they often focus on arm-hand systems, rely on separate tactile pretraining, explicit world models, multi-stage inference, or manually designed virtual targets specific to certain sensors. The integration of predictive latent learning, common in architectures like JEPA, into single-stage whole-body humanoid imitation policies handling locomotion and rapidly changing contact, has been less explored. This paper aims to bridge these gaps by developing an integrated system that combines stable whole-body execution, tactile-enabled dexterous manipulation, and a novel predictive touch learning method within a unified policy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work stems from the inherent difficulties of enabling humanoid robots to perform versatile, contact-rich, and dexterous loco-manipulation in real-world environments. Specifically, the challenges arise from:
- **Whole-body stability and end-effector dexterity coordination:** Humanoid manipulation is intricately linked to torso posture, base motion, and foot-ground stability, making reliable hand-object contact crucial for both local accuracy and global stability.
- **Frequent and uncertain contact changes:** Real-world interaction often involves complex and dynamic contact patterns that are difficult to infer from vision alone and can destabilize the robot.
- **Lack of integrated systems:** Few existing robotic platforms practically combine robust whole-body control, full dexterous-hand capabilities, and effective tactile sensing and modeling in a single pipeline for learning contact-rich tasks.
- **Limitations of current learning paradigms:** Purely action-supervised behavioral cloning struggles in scenarios with partial observability and abrupt contact changes. While tactile sensing offers complementary information, existing tactile learning methods often require separate pretraining, complex multi-stage inference, or task-specific target engineering, hindering their integration into general humanoid policies. Predictive latent learning, which has shown promise in other domains for learning meaningful representations, has not been widely applied to single-stage whole-body humanoid imitation policies that must handle diverse actions and rapidly changing touch.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this research are:
1. **Develop a robust and stable whole-body execution backbone:** This involves creating an RL-based lower-body controller (LBC) capable of maintaining whole-body stability and tracking complex commands during manipulation, handling disturbances from upper-body movements.
2. **Establish a practical data collection system for contact-rich humanoid manipulation:** This objective focuses on integrating VR-based teleoperation with the LBC, upper-body inverse kinematics (IK), dexterous hand retargeting, and distributed tactile sensing to efficiently collect high-quality, multimodal demonstrations.
3. **Propose a multimodal, touch-aware policy learning framework:** The goal is to design a single-stage learning architecture, Humanoid Transformer with Touch Dreaming (HTD), that effectively incorporates touch as a core modality alongside vision and proprioception.
4. **Enhance representation learning for contact-rich tasks through "Touch Dreaming":** This objective aims to improve the policy's contact-awareness by introducing auxiliary objectives to predict future hand-joint forces and future tactile latents, supervised by an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) target encoder for stability.
5. **Evaluate the system's performance on a diverse set of real-world contact-rich tasks:** Demonstrate the versatility and effectiveness of the proposed framework across tasks requiring tight-tolerance insertion, hybrid prehensile/non-prehensile manipulation, deformable object handling, tool-mediated contact, and bimanual loco-manipulation.
6. **Investigate the impact of predictive touch dreaming and latent tactile supervision:** Through ablation studies, quantify the benefits of tactile observations and the specific design choices of touch dreaming (latent vs. raw prediction) on manipulation performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is structured around an integrated system comprising four main stages: LBC training, VR-based teleoperation and data collection, policy learning with Humanoid Transformer with Touch Dreaming (HTD), and deployment.

**A. A System for Versatile Humanoid Dexterous Manipulation**
The overall system provides a stable execution backbone for data collection and policy deployment.

**B. Lower-body Controller (LBC)**
An RL-based lower-body controller (LBC) is trained in massively parallel simulation using IsaacLab to ensure stable whole-body execution.
- **Teacher-Student Framework:** A teacher policy, trained with PPO and privileged information (foot contact indicators), learns to track base velocity, torso orientation, and height commands. It is designed to handle disturbances from replayed arm motions sampled from the AMASS dataset. This teacher policy is then distilled into a student policy via DAgger, which observes only real-world available information (proprioception and a short history).
- **Observations and Actions:** The student policy takes base angular velocity, projected gravity vector, lower-body joint positions/velocities, and previous lower-body action as proprioceptive observations. It outputs 15-DoF lower-body target joint positions for the legs and waist.
- **Training Details:** Command signals are uniformly sampled from predefined ranges, and domain randomization is applied to physics parameters to enhance sim-to-real transferability.

**C. Teleoperation and Data Collection**
A VR-based teleoperation system facilitates the collection of real-world humanoid demonstrations.
- **Motion Mapping:** Human head, wrist, and hand motions from VR are transformed into a unified robot reference frame. These signals generate torso pose commands (roll, pitch, yaw, height) for the LBC, 6D wrist pose targets for an IK solver, and hand targets for dexterous retargeting. Base velocity commands are provided by a joystick.
- **Execution Stack:** The LBC executes torso and base commands. An IK solver maps desired wrist/end-effector poses to upper-body joint targets. A DexPilot-based hand retargeting module converts human hand targets into dexterous hand joint targets, optimizing fingertip-distance consistency.
- **Multimodal Data Recording:** During teleoperation, synchronized RGB images from head and wrist cameras, robot and hand proprioception, per-joint force feedback from dexterous hands, and 1062-dimensional tactile readings (distributed across 17 spatial regions on fingers and palms) are recorded.

**D. Learning Dexterous Manipulation with Touch Dreaming**
Humanoid Transformer with Touch Dreaming (HTD) is a multimodal encoder–decoder Transformer designed to learn contact-aware policies from these demonstrations.
- **Modular Architecture:** HTD consists of modality tokenizers, an encoder–decoder transformer trunk, modular action experts, and modular dream experts.
- **Modality Tokenizers:** Each observation stream (multi-view vision via pretrained ResNet, robot/hand proprioception via MLPs, hand-joint forces via MLPs, and tactile inputs) is encoded into a fixed number of tokens using cross-attention aggregation.
- **Per-Finger/Region Tactile Encoder:** Tactile observations are anatomically decomposed by finger and palm. Each region's input is processed by dedicated CNN branches (single-layer for small patches, two-layer for larger) and then fused by an MLP into a compact embedding, which is then tokenized via cross-attention. This architecture is also used for the EMA target encoder.
- **Transformer Trunk:** An encoder fuses multimodal observation tokens into a unified representation. A decoder generates a fixed set of output tokens, with learnable query embeddings providing a structured interface for downstream experts.
- **Modular Action Experts:** These experts decode control outputs from the decoder's output tokens. Each expert uses a cross-attention layer to predict a specific action modality: end-effector pose targets, torso pose targets, velocity commands, and hand actions. Action chunking is employed, where a short horizon of targets is predicted at each step.
- **Modular Dream Experts and Touch Dreaming:** These auxiliary heads predict future hand joint force vectors and future finger/region tactile latents, conditioned on current multimodal observations. This "touch dreaming" objective regularizes the shared transformer trunk to learn contact-aware representations. These experts are only used during training.

**E. Training Paradigm**
HTD is trained in a single-stage behavioral cloning (BC) paradigm, augmented with touch dreaming.
- **EMA Teacher for Tactile Latents:** For tactile dreaming, predictions are made in a learned latent space rather than raw sensor space. An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher tactile tokenizer, whose parameters are a decaying average of the student's and through which gradients are stopped, provides stable, temporally consistent latent targets. This prevents mode collapse in the student's tactile latent space.
- **Objective Function:** The total loss is a weighted sum of:
 1. **Behavioral Cloning Loss:** Smooth L1 loss for action chunk prediction across all action modalities.
 2. **Future Hand Joint Force Prediction Loss:** Smooth L1 loss for predicting future hand joint force vectors.
 3. **Tactile Dreaming Loss (Latent Supervision):** Combines a cosine direction loss to align the predicted latent vector's orientation with the EMA teacher's target, and a smooth L1 magnitude alignment loss to match vector norms. This dual objective encourages learning semantically rich and stable contact structure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results supporting the effectiveness of the proposed system and the HTD policy.

**A. LBC Strategy Comparison**
- **Tracking Performance:** The LBC was benchmarked against FALCON and AMO in simulation. The proposed LBC achieved superior or comparable tracking performance across most metrics (Table II). Specifically, it demonstrated lower errors in linear velocity (E_v: 0.1420 ± 0.0568 m/s), torso height (E_h: 0.0280 ± 0.0438 m), and torso orientation (E_y: 0.0126 ± 0.0051 rad, E_p: 0.0487 ± 0.1796 rad, E_r: 0.0157 ± 0.0065 rad) compared to baselines. This indicates tighter torso pose regulation, which is beneficial for contact-rich manipulation.
- **Stable Controllable Range:** The LBC supports a wide stable region for various body configurations. In simulation, it achieved stable command-tracking ranges for base height (h ∈ [0.33, 0.80] m), torso roll (ϕ_torso ∈ [−0.38, 0.35] rad), torso pitch (θ_torso ∈ [−0.92, 1.41] rad), and torso yaw (ψ_torso ∈ [−1.50, 1.34] rad). The roll range was found to be the most restrictive compared to training ranges.

**B. Real-World Versatile Humanoid Manipulation Performance**
HTD was evaluated on five real-world contact-rich tasks against two decoder-only ACT baselines (Visual + Proprio and Visual + Proprio + Touch).
- **Superior Performance:** HTD consistently outperformed both ACT baselines across all five tasks in both success rate and task score (Fig. 6). On average, HTD achieved a 30.0 percentage point improvement in success rate and 17.9 percentage point improvement in task score over the stronger ACT variant, representing relative gains of 90.9% and 31.1% respectively.
- **Impact of Touch Input vs. Touch Dreaming:** Simply adding force and tactile observations to ACT (ACT (Visual + Proprio + Touch)) did not consistently improve performance over ACT (Visual + Proprio), indicating that raw touch input alone is often insufficient. HTD's gains were particularly noticeable in tasks demanding contact-aware control or complex whole-body coordination, such as Insert-T (tight tolerance), Towel Folding (deformable objects), and Cat Litter Scooping (tool use and whole-body reachability). In Tea Serving, HTD showed significantly higher reliability in bimanual loco-manipulation involving carrying objects.

**C. Impact of Predictive Touch Dreaming and Latent Tactile Supervision**
An ablation study on two tasks (Insert-T, Towel Folding) investigated the specific contributions of touch observations and touch dreaming (Fig. 7).
- **Touch Input Alone:** Adding touch observations without touch dreaming (w/o TD vs. w/o Touch and TD) showed mixed results, improving performance on Towel Folding but not on Insert-T, and yielding only small average differences.
- **Predictive Touch Objective:** Incorporating a predictive touch objective (Dream Raw Tactile and Dream Latent Tactile) consistently improved performance over the variant without touch dreaming (w/o TD) on both tasks and average metrics.
- **Latent vs. Raw Tactile Prediction:** Dream Latent Tactile (the full HTD method) achieved the best overall performance and significantly outperformed Dream Raw Tactile, with a 30% relative gain in average success rate. This indicates that supervising future touch in a learned latent space, using an EMA teacher, is more effective and stable than directly predicting raw, high-dimensional tactile signals.

**D. Meaningful and Robust Contact-Aware Representations**
Qualitative analysis of touch dreaming during real-world rollouts (Fig. 8) revealed insights into the learned representations.
- **Force Prediction:** HTD effectively predicted future hand force trajectories, tracking both the timing and magnitude of contact events.
- **Tactile Latent Similarity:** Tactile latent similarity remained high during sustained contact, indicating consistent representation. While temporary drops occurred during abrupt contact transitions, the overall similarity remained robust.
- **Contact-Aware Latent Patterns:** The predicted tactile latent heatmaps were observed to be close to the ground-truth heatmaps. The learned latent space captured contact-relevant structure, with distinct, high-intensity patterns emerging during richer contact events and showing larger peak values under stronger forces. This suggests that the latent space filters out noise and sparsity of raw readings, focusing on semantically meaningful contact dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by presenting an integrated and effective framework for versatile, contact-rich manipulation. The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Advancement in Humanoid Loco-Manipulation:** The development of a robust RL-based LBC, coupled with a comprehensive VR-based teleoperation and data collection system, provides a practical foundation for acquiring and executing complex whole-body skills on real humanoid robots. This addresses the challenge of integrating stability with dexterity.
2. **Novelty in Multimodal Policy Learning:** The Humanoid Transformer with Touch Dreaming (HTD) architecture represents an advancement in multimodal learning for robotics. By treating touch as a core modality and integrating future touch prediction (dreaming) as an auxiliary objective within a single-stage behavioral cloning paradigm, the paper demonstrates an effective method for learning contact-aware representations.
3. **Effectiveness of Latent Tactile Prediction:** The finding that supervising future tactile signals in a learned latent space, enabled by an EMA teacher, is superior to raw tactile prediction is a key methodological contribution. This approach provides a compact, stable, and semantically rich learning signal, overcoming common issues of sparsity and noise in raw tactile data. This technique could be adopted in other areas of robotic tactile perception.
4. **Demonstrated Versatility and Robustness:** The successful evaluation across five diverse real-world tasks, including those requiring tight precision, deformable object handling, tool use, and bimanual loco-manipulation, indicates the framework's versatility and robustness to complex contact dynamics and whole-body coordination challenges. This expands the repertoire of tasks humanoids can reliably perform.
5. **Simplified Deployment:** By using dream experts only during training and not inference, the system maintains a straightforward deployment pipeline, avoiding the computational overhead or potential instability of multi-stage inference or explicit world models.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a baseline and integrated system for future research in humanoid skill learning, particularly for tasks involving intricate physical interaction. It opens avenues for exploring more advanced predictive objectives, different latent space learning techniques, and broader generalization to novel objects and environments.

In summary, "Learning Versatile Humanoid Manipulation with Touch Dreaming" offers a comprehensive solution for enhancing humanoid robots' ability to interact with the physical world through improved whole-body control, dexterous manipulation, and contact-aware learning, potentially accelerating the development of general-purpose humanoid assistants.
