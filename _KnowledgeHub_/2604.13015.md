# Learning Versatile Humanoid Manipulation with Touch Dreaming

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework is presented for enabling humanoid robots to perform versatile, contact-rich manipulation by integrating stable whole-body control, dexterous hand capabilities, and a "Touch Dreaming" method for predictive tactile learning. This approach achieved a 30 percentage point increase in success rate on real-world tasks compared to existing behavioral cloning methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid loco-manipulation is challenging due to tight coupling between whole-body stability and end-effector dexterity, especially under frequent and uncertain contact changes.
- Existing humanoid systems often lack a practical, integrated platform that combines robust whole-body control, full dexterous-hand capabilities, and effective tactile sensing for contact-rich tasks.
- Current learning paradigms struggle with partial observability and abrupt contact changes, and integrating tactile information often requires separate pretraining or complex multi-stage inference, limiting its use in general humanoid policies.

## Method

![[2604.13015-method.png]]
*The HTD architecture uses modality tokenizers to process multimodal inputs, a transformer trunk for reasoning, and modular experts for action prediction and touch dreaming.*

- An ==RL-based lower-body controller (LBC)== was developed using a ==teacher-student framework== to provide stable whole-body execution and track complex commands during manipulation.
- A ==VR-based teleoperation system== was established for efficient real-world data collection, integrating LBC, upper-body inverse kinematics, dexterous hand retargeting, and distributed tactile sensing.
- The **Humanoid Transformer with Touch Dreaming (HTD)** policy was designed, featuring a multimodal encoder-decoder Transformer that incorporates a ==touch dreaming== auxiliary objective to predict future hand forces and tactile latents, regularizing the policy to learn contact-aware representations.

## Results

- The proposed LBC achieved superior or comparable tracking performance across most metrics for linear velocity, torso height, and orientation, indicating tighter whole-body regulation compared to baseline controllers.
- HTD consistently outperformed ACT baselines on five diverse real-world contact-rich tasks, demonstrating an average **30.0 percentage point** improvement in success rate and **17.9 percentage point** improvement in task score.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the "Touch Dreaming" objective, particularly with latent tactile supervision, significantly improved performance, yielding a **30%** relative gain in average success rate compared to raw tactile prediction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective integration of a robust lower-body controller with VR-based teleoperation is crucial for collecting high-quality, multimodal data necessary for complex humanoid loco-manipulation.
> - Supervising future tactile signals in a learned latent space using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher is more effective and stable than predicting raw tactile data, providing semantically rich and noise-robust contact-aware representations.
> - A single-stage behavioral cloning policy, augmented with predictive touch objectives, can successfully learn versatile, contact-rich behaviors from demonstrations, simplifying deployment by avoiding multi-stage inference or explicit world models.
