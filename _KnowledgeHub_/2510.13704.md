# Simplicial Embeddings Improve Sample Efficiency in Actor-Critic Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Simplicial Embeddings (SEM) are integrated as an architectural component in deep reinforcement learning to improve sample efficiency and final performance across actor-critic agents. This approach imposes a geometric inductive bias on latent representations, yielding more stable learning dynamics across a variety of continuous and discrete control tasks.

## Problem

- Modern deep reinforcement learning agents often trade off high wall-clock training speed for poor sample efficiency, requiring excessive environment interactions.
- Non-stationarity in actor-critic learning leads to representation collapse, causing unstable value learning, diminished feature diversity, and suboptimal policy gradients.
- Existing methods for learning structured or discrete representations frequently involve complex auxiliary losses, additional networks, or biased gradient estimators.

## Method

- Introduces **Simplicial Embeddings** (SEM), a lightweight, differentiable architectural component integrated into the hidden features of actor and/or critic networks.
- SEM partitions latent vectors into 'L' groups and applies a group-wise softmax, geometrically constraining features to be bounded, sparse, and discrete.
- The method functions like an activation function, avoiding complex auxiliary losses or biased gradient estimators often used in explicit discretization.

## Results

- SEM consistently improves sample efficiency and asymptotic performance across FastTD3, FastSAC, and PPO on diverse benchmarks including HumanoidBench, IsaacGym, Atari, and MTBench, often with lower variance and no runtime speed loss.
- Diagnostic analysis shows SEM increases effective rank of features, bounds feature norms, and significantly reduces critic instability, as evidenced by lower TD errors, critic disagreement (Q-gap), and Cramér distance.
- Applying SEM to the actor's penultimate layer yields optimal gains and outperforms alternative regularization methods like C-RELU, Gumbel + straight-through, and Vector Quantization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A geometric inductive bias, as enforced by simplicial embeddings, can directly mitigate representation collapse and stabilize learning dynamics in non-stationary deep RL environments.
> - Shaping the actor's latent features with SEM is particularly impactful for improving policy gradients, accelerating early learning, and enhancing overall agent stability.
> - Simple, differentiable architectural constraints can be more effective and computationally efficient than complex auxiliary objectives or biased estimators for robust representation learning in RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and persistent tension in Deep Reinforcement Learning (Deep RL): the trade-off between **wall-clock training time** (how fast an agent can be trained using available compute) and **sample efficiency** (how many environment interactions are needed to achieve desired performance).

- **Massively Parallel Deep RL:** Recent advances have focused on accelerating wall-clock time through large-scale environment parallelization, leading to "fast" actor-critic variants like FastTD3, FastSAC, IMPALA, and SEED RL. While these methods can rapidly solve complex tasks (e.g., humanoid locomotion), they often suffer from poor sample efficiency, requiring a vast number of environment interactions. This becomes a significant limitation in real-world applications such as robotics, where interactions are costly and time-consuming, and raises concerns about computational resources and sustainability.
- **Sample-Efficient Deep RL:** Concurrently, another line of research strives for high sample efficiency, often employing techniques like model-based RL (TD-MPC2, Dreamer), auxiliary losses for representation learning (CURL, SPR), or sophisticated architectural components and regularization (SimBaV2, BRO, Neuroplastic Expansion, For SALE). While these methods achieve strong returns with fewer interactions, they frequently come with increased algorithmic complexity, computational overhead, or specific design constraints.
- **Representation Learning in Deep RL:** The paper highlights that well-structured representations are crucial for improving generalization and sample efficiency. It notes that previous successful agents like TD-MPC2 and Dreamer V2/V3 use discrete latents but often rely on auxiliary model-based losses to shape these representations. The authors argue that such auxiliary losses add complexity and computational burden.
- **The Proposed Solution – Simplicial Embeddings (SEM):** This work positions itself as a lightweight, architectural solution to enhance sample efficiency without sacrificing wall-clock speed or adding complex auxiliary objectives. By introducing Simplicial Embeddings (SEM), the authors aim to provide a *geometric inductive bias* that encourages sparse and discrete features. This directly addresses the problem of *representation collapse* and *instability* in value learning, which is exacerbated by the non-stationary nature of deep RL targets (i.e., the constantly evolving policy and data distribution). The paper contrasts SEM with other methods for learning discrete representations (e.g., Gumbel + straight-through, Vector Quantization), emphasizing SEM's differentiability without biased estimators.

In essence, this paper seeks to bridge the gap between fast, throughput-oriented RL and data-efficient RL by proposing a simple yet powerful architectural component that regularizes representation geometry. It contributes to the growing understanding of how stable and structured representations can mitigate fundamental challenges in deep RL, such as value overestimation, divergent gradients, and neuron dormancy, which are amplified in non-stationary learning environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **improve the sample efficiency and final performance of actor-critic reinforcement learning agents** by integrating **Simplicial Embeddings (SEM)** into their network architectures.

This objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Addressing the Wall-Clock vs. Sample Efficiency Tension:** As outlined in the broader research landscape, modern deep RL agents often face a trade-off. While massive parallelization can reduce wall-clock training time, it frequently requires an excessive number of environment interactions. This "data hunger" is problematic for real-world applications (e.g., robotics) where interactions are expensive, and contributes to high computational and energy costs, impacting sustainability. The authors aim to find a solution that offers the benefits of both worlds.
- **Leveraging Structured Representations for Stability:** The authors note that well-structured representations are known to enhance generalization and sample efficiency in deep RL. They posit that the non-stationarity inherent in actor-critic learning (where the policy and data distribution continuously change) exacerbates "representation collapse." This phenomenon, characterized by reduced feature diversity, inactive neurons, and ill-conditioned regression problems for the critic, leads to unstable value learning and poor policy gradients.
- **Seeking Lightweight and Principled Solutions:** Current methods for achieving structured or discrete representations often involve complex auxiliary losses, additional networks, or biased gradient estimators (e.g., straight-through estimation). The authors are motivated to find a simpler, "lightweight representation layer" that can induce desirable geometric inductive biases without incurring significant computational overhead or algorithmic complexity. Simplicial embeddings are proposed as such a solution, akin to a novel activation function.
- **Stabilizing Critic Bootstrapping and Policy Gradients:** The paper specifically highlights that errors in the critic's value estimates propagate recursively, and when representations are poorly conditioned, these errors amplify. By constraining embeddings to simplicial structures, the authors hypothesize that the resulting sparse and discrete features will stabilize critic bootstrapping and strengthen policy gradients, leading to more robust and efficient learning.

In summary, the motivation is to develop a simple, architecturally-driven method to counteract representation instability caused by non-stationarity in deep RL, thereby boosting sample efficiency and asymptotic performance without sacrificing the fast wall-clock speeds achieved by modern parallel training frameworks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work lies in the integration of **Simplicial Embeddings (SEM)** as an architectural component within existing deep reinforcement learning algorithms. The methodology can be broken down into several key aspects:

#### 3.1. Simplicial Embeddings (SEM) – The Core Mechanism

- **Geometric Inductive Bias:** SEM imposes a lightweight geometric constraint on latent representations. Given the output of an encoder `fψ(s)` (state observation `s` mapped to a latent vector `z`), this vector is partitioned into `L` groups, each of size `V`.
- **Group-wise Softmax:** Within each of these `L` groups, a softmax function is applied:
 `˜zℓ,v = exp(zℓ,v /τ) / Σv′=1^V exp(zℓ,v′ /τ)`
 where `τ > 0` is a temperature parameter controlling the degree of sparsity.
- **Properties of `˜z`:** This transformation ensures several desirable properties:
 - **Boundedness:** The resulting embedding `˜z` lies in the product space `∆V −1 × · · · × ∆V −1` (L categorical distributions), preventing feature norms from exploding and thus stabilizing value estimates.
 - **Sparsity and Discreteness:** The softmax competition, especially at low `τ`, drives near one-hot encodings, inducing sparse and discrete features.
 - **Group Structure:** Partitioning features into modular subspaces (`L` groups) promotes diversity and can increase effective rank.
- **Lightweight and Differentiable:** Crucially, SEM functions akin to an activation function. It is fully differentiable, avoiding the biased gradient estimators (e.g., straight-through estimation) often associated with explicit discretization methods (Gumbel-softmax, Vector Quantization). It requires no auxiliary losses or additional networks, adding effectively zero computational overhead.

#### 3.2. Integration into Actor-Critic Architectures

- SEM is applied to the hidden features of both the actor and critic networks. The authors build on prior work suggesting that the penultimate layer plays a critical role in representation quality.
- **Critic Integration:** For the critic, SEM replaces a baseline linear head with a structured projection. In the context of distributional critics (C51), it regularizes the value estimates.
- **Actor Integration:** For the actor, SEM is applied at the penultimate layer before the final linear+tanh activation, ensuring that the policy is conditioned on bounded and sparse features.
- The default configuration for most experiments involves applying SEM to the **actor only** (with `V=64`), as this configuration yielded the most stable gains.

#### 3.3. Theoretical and Empirical Justification

- **Non-stationarity and Representation Collapse:** The paper formally analyzes how non-stationarity (e.g., shifting target distributions due to an evolving policy in RL) can lead to increased neuron dormancy and reduced effective rank (representation collapse).
- **CIFAR-10 Toy Experiment:** A controlled experiment on CIFAR-10 demonstrates that shuffling labels (mimicking non-stationarity) leads to oscillating losses, rising neuron dormancy, and collapsing feature rank. The addition of SEM mitigates this instability, preserving feature diversity.
- **SEM's Role in RL:** SEM counteracts these issues by enforcing energy preservation (each block having unit mass) and promoting diversity through intra-block competition and multiple blocks, thus preventing covariance deflation and sustaining gradient energy.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

- **Baseline Algorithms:** The method is evaluated on leading actor-critic algorithms:
 - **FastTD3:** A high-throughput off-policy method (based on TD3) designed for parallel simulation, large-batch updates, and distributional critics.
 - **FastSAC:** A high-throughput off-policy method (based on SAC).
 - **PPO:** A popular on-policy method.
 - Also briefly evaluated with FastTD3-SimBaV2 and Flow Q-Learning (FQL).
- **Environments:** A diverse set of benchmarks covering continuous control, pixel-based tasks, and multi-task learning:
 - **HumanoidBench:** Challenging humanoid robot locomotion and manipulation tasks (28-h1hand tasks, 12-h1, 12-g1 tasks).
 - **IsaacGym:** High-performance GPU-based physics simulation (9 IsaacGym tasks).
 - **Arcade Learning Environment (ALE):** Pixel-based discrete control (Atari-10 suite, 27 Atari games).
 - **MTBench:** Large-scale multi-task robotics benchmark (MT50 manipulation tasks).
 - **Booster T1:** A real-robot benchmark for sim-to-real transfer.
 - **OGBench:** Offline-to-online RL benchmark for Flow Q-Learning.
- **Metrics and Diagnostics:** Beyond standard episode returns, the authors use a comprehensive suite of diagnostics to understand *why* SEM works:
 - **Representation Quality:** Effective rank, dormant neurons, feature norms, Gini sparsity, simplex entropy.
 - **Optimization Stability:** Actor/Critic loss, TD error, critic disagreement (Q-gap), Cramér distance (for distributional critics).
- **Ablation Studies:** Extensive ablations are performed on FastTD3 to:
 - Determine optimal SEM placement (actor, critic, or both) and hyperparameters (`L`, `V`, `τ`).
 - Assess robustness to core FastTD3 design choices (number of environments, replay buffer size, batch size, CDQ, C51, noise scale).
- **Comparison to Alternatives:** SEM is compared against other representation learning methods like C-RELU, Gumbel + straight-through, and Vector Quantization.

This multi-faceted approach, combining theoretical justification, controlled experiments, extensive empirical evaluation across diverse benchmarks and algorithms, and in-depth diagnostic analysis, provides a robust methodology for understanding the benefits of simplicial embeddings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several consistent and significant findings demonstrating the effectiveness of Simplicial Embeddings (SEM) in deep reinforcement learning:

- **Consistent Improvement in Sample Efficiency and Final Performance:**
 - Across FastTD3, FastSAC, and PPO, SEM consistently improves both sample efficiency (agents converge faster) and asymptotic performance (achieve higher final returns) across a variety of continuous-control (HumanoidBench, IsaacGym, MTBench, Booster T1) and discrete-control (Atari) environments.
 - SEM agents also exhibit lower variance across random seeds, indicating improved training stability.
 - Crucially, these gains are achieved *without any loss in runtime speed* and often with slight improvements (Table 2 in the appendix shows comparable or slightly faster wall-clock times).

- **Stabilization of Learning Dynamics and Representation Quality:**
 - **Combatting Representation Collapse:** Diagnostic metrics (Figure 4) show that SEM significantly increases the *effective rank* of actor features and, in later stages, critic features. This indicates that SEM prevents representation collapse, maintaining diverse and expressive features, which is essential for robust value learning.
 - **Feature Norm Control:** SEM bounds actor feature norms, which prevents issues like exploding Q-values and divergent gradients often associated with unconstrained representations.
 - **Sparsity and Discreteness:** SEM layers' activations become markedly sparser (higher Gini index) and more sharply peaked (lower simplex entropy) as training progresses (Figure 6). This aligns with SEM’s design principle of inducing sparse, discrete features, which is linked to better stability and robustness.
 - **Reduced Instability in Critics:** SEM leads to lower and more stable TD errors, reduced critic disagreement (Q-gap), and lower Cramér distance between predicted and target value distributions (Figure 5). This indicates more calibrated value estimates, mitigating a primary failure mode in bootstrapped critics.

- **Optimal Placement of SEM:**
 - Applying SEM to the **actor's penultimate layer** (Figure 3) consistently yields the most significant and stable gains, both in terms of early learning acceleration and asymptotic performance. While applying it to the critic or both also shows benefits, the actor-only application often proves most effective and efficient. This suggests that shaping the features directly guiding policy gradients is particularly impactful.

- **Robustness to FastTD3 Design Choices and Hyperparameters:**
 - SEM maintains its performance advantages even when FastTD3's core design choices (e.g., number of parallel environments, replay buffer size, batch size, Clipped Double Q-learning, Distributional Critics, exploration noise scale) are varied or removed (Figure 8). This demonstrates SEM's robustness and complementary nature to existing stabilization techniques.

- **Superiority over Alternative Regularization Methods:**
 - SEM outperforms other methods for inducing structured or discrete representations, such as C-RELU, Gumbel + straight-through estimation, and Vector Quantization (Figure 7, left). The authors attribute this to SEM being fully differentiable, thus avoiding the biased gradient estimators that these explicit discretization methods often require.

- **Impact of SEM Parameters (L and V):**
 - Increasing the simplex dimensionality (`V`) generally improves performance up to a point.
 - Providing too much free capacity by increasing the number of simplices (`L`) can deteriorate returns, suggesting that a degree of constraint on representation capacity is beneficial (Figure 7, middle and right).

- **Generalizability Across Diverse RL Settings:**
 - Beyond fast actor-critic methods, SEM also improves the performance of **PPO** on both continuous-control (IsaacGym) and pixel-based (Atari-10 suite) tasks (Figure 10).
 - It enhances multi-task learning in robotics (MTBench) and improves sample efficiency and stability during the offline-to-online transition in Flow Q-Learning (Figure 11).

In summary, the main findings strongly support that Simplicial Embeddings are a powerful, lightweight architectural component that addresses fundamental instability issues in deep RL, leading to substantial and consistent improvements in sample efficiency and final performance across a wide range of algorithms and environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction and thorough evaluation of Simplicial Embeddings (SEM) in deep reinforcement learning have several significant implications and potential impacts on the field:

- **Bridging the Efficiency Gap:** The work directly addresses one of the most pressing challenges in modern deep RL: the tension between high wall-clock training speed (often achieved through massive parallelization) and sample efficiency (the number of environment interactions). By demonstrating that SEM can consistently improve sample efficiency without sacrificing wall-clock speed, the paper offers a practical solution to bridge this gap, enabling faster learning with fewer costly interactions.
- **Foundation for More Robust and Sustainable RL:** Improving sample efficiency has direct implications for the sustainability and applicability of RL.
 - **Real-World Robotics:** Fewer interactions are crucial for robotics, where real-world data collection is expensive, slow, and potentially damaging. SEM could accelerate the development and deployment of RL agents in physical systems.
 - **Computational and Energy Footprint:** Data-hungry algorithms consume substantial computational resources and energy. By making RL agents more sample-efficient, SEM contributes to "Green AI" initiatives, reducing the environmental impact of large-scale RL training.
- **Principled Approach to Representation Learning:** The paper offers a principled, architectural solution to representation learning in RL, contrasting with methods that rely on complex auxiliary losses or biased gradient estimators. SEM's mechanism of constraining representations to a product of simplices provides a geometric inductive bias that intrinsically promotes sparsity, discreteness, and boundedness. This approach is elegant and computationally efficient, as it functions like a standard activation function.
- **Mitigating Fundamental Instabilities in Deep RL:** The detailed diagnostic analysis provides strong evidence that SEM directly combats core instability issues in deep RL, particularly representation collapse and issues with bootstrapped critics. By improving effective rank, bounding feature norms, and reducing TD error and critic disagreement, SEM contributes to more stable optimization, more reliable gradients, and better-calibrated value estimates. This understanding can inform future research into designing more stable and efficient RL algorithms.
- **Broad Applicability and Generalizability:** The demonstrated effectiveness of SEM across various actor-critic algorithms (FastTD3, FastSAC, PPO, FQL), different control settings (continuous, discrete, pixel-based), and diverse benchmarks (humanoid robotics, IsaacGym, Atari, multi-task learning) highlights its broad applicability. This suggests that SEM is not a niche solution but a general-purpose architectural component that can enhance a wide range of RL agents.
- **Inspiration for Future Architectural Innovations:** The success of SEM, a lightweight architectural modification, may inspire further research into geometric priors and structured representations within neural networks for RL. This could lead to a new wave of innovations that focus on intrinsic network properties rather than external regularization or complex auxiliary tasks.
- **New Avenues for Understanding Generalization:** By improving sample efficiency and stabilizing representations, SEM indirectly contributes to better generalization. Agents that exploit structure from fewer trajectories tend to be more robust under distributional shifts, which is crucial for real-world deployment.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Not a Universal Remedy:** SEM might still face challenges in tasks with extreme distribution shift or very sparse rewards.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** SEM introduces hyperparameters (`L`, `V`, `τ`) that require tuning, though the paper generally used default baseline hyperparameters for consistency. Adaptive schedules for these parameters are a promising future direction.
- **Untested Domains:** The current evaluation focuses on continuous control and Atari, leaving its impact on large-scale vision or language-conditioned RL untested.
- **Integration with Other Advancements:** Future work could explore integrating SEM into more general-purpose algorithms (e.g., MR.Q), value-based algorithms, or in conjunction with other architectural priors (e.g., MoEs, Residual Nets).

Despite these limitations, the paper makes a compelling case for Simplicial Embeddings as a valuable addition to the deep RL toolbox, offering a simple yet powerful means to improve learning efficiency and stability. Its impact could be felt in both academic research, by guiding the design of more robust RL algorithms, and in practical applications, by enabling more efficient and sustainable development of intelligent agents.
