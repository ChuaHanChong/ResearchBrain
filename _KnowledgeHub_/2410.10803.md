# Generalizable Humanoid Manipulation with 3D Diffusion Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> A system enables a Fourier GR1 humanoid robot to perform various manipulation tasks in diverse, previously unseen real-world environments, achieving zero-shot generalization from data collected in a single training scene. This is demonstrated through over 2000 real-world policy rollouts, leveraging egocentric 3D vision and an improved diffusion policy.

## Problem

- Autonomous manipulation skills for humanoid robots are often confined to specific training environments, lacking generalization to novel scenes.
- Existing learning methods, especially 2D image-based ones, exhibit limited generalization to varied camera viewpoints, object appearances, and scene layouts.
- Collecting diverse, high-quality demonstration data for full-sized humanoid robots in the wild is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.

## Method

- Development of an integrated real-world system using a ==Fourier GR1== humanoid robot equipped with an egocentric Intel RealSense L515 LiDAR camera and a height-adjustable cart.
- A whole-upper-body teleoperation system utilizing Apple Vision Pro for collecting high-fidelity, human-like demonstration data exclusively from a single training environment.
- Introduction of an improved 3D Diffusion Policy (iDP3) featuring egocentric 3D representations, a pyramid convolutional encoder, scaled vision input, and an extended prediction horizon to enhance generalization from noisy data.

## Results

- Demonstrated zero-shot generalization for manipulation skills (e.g., Pick&Place, Pour) on a Fourier GR1 humanoid robot in diverse, unseen real-world environments, trained solely on data from a single scene.
- Validated the system's robustness and effectiveness through over 2000 real-world policy rollouts on the physical robot.
- iDP3 significantly outperformed various diffusion policy baselines in both performance and generalization, with image-based methods failing to generalize to novel scenes, objects, or viewpoints.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the pyramid convolutional encoder, scaled 3D vision input, and a longer prediction horizon were essential for iDP3's improved accuracy and smoother behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric 3D visual representations are crucial for enabling generalization of manipulation skills to unseen scenes, objects, and camera viewpoints for humanoid robots.
> - High-fidelity human teleoperation, capturing whole-upper-body movements including waist DoF, provides effective demonstration data for learning versatile manipulation skills.
> - Specific architectural improvements to the 3D Diffusion Policy, such as a pyramid convolutional encoder and appropriately scaled 3D input, are necessary for robust learning from noisy human demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous robots capable of operating in diverse, unstructured real-world environments has long been a central aspiration in robotics. Within this grand challenge, humanoid robots represent a particularly promising, yet exceptionally difficult, pathway due to their complex kinematics, balance challenges, and the high fidelity required for human-like interaction and manipulation.

Recent years have seen significant advancements in humanoid robot hardware (e.g., Boston Dynamics Atlas, Tesla Optimus, Figure 01, Fourier GR1), as well as in teleoperation systems and learning algorithms for these platforms. Researchers have demonstrated impressive feats in humanoid locomotion (e.g., parkour, challenging terrains) and increasingly capable manipulation.

However, a critical bottleneck has remained: **generalization**. While humanoid robots can perform complex manipulation tasks, these skills are often confined to the specific training environments and objects. The ability for a humanoid robot to execute a learned manipulation skill—such as picking up a cup—in a completely *unseen* kitchen, office, or meeting room, with *varying lighting, backgrounds, and object types*, has largely been elusive. This limitation stems from two primary challenges:
1. **Limited generalization capabilities of current learning methods:** Many learning-based approaches, especially those relying heavily on 2D image data, struggle with changes in camera viewpoints, object appearances, and scene layouts outside their training distribution. They often overfit to specific visual cues.
2. **High cost and difficulty of data acquisition:** Collecting diverse, high-quality, in-the-wild manipulation data for full-sized humanoid robots is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and often dangerous, making it difficult to train policies robust enough for generalization. Existing teleoperation systems, while advanced, still face challenges in scalability and capturing whole-body human-like motions.

This paper directly addresses these generalization challenges by presenting a novel approach that significantly pushes the boundaries of what's possible with humanoid manipulation. It positions itself as a critical step forward from prior works by explicitly focusing on **real-world scene generalization from single-scene training data**, an aspect largely missing in previous humanoid manipulation studies (as highlighted in Table I and the related work section).
- Existing teleoperation systems like OpenTeleVision and HATO might lack movable bases or waist DoF, limiting workspace.
- Systems like HumanPlus and OmniH2O offer whole-body teleoperation but learned skills are often scene-specific.
- Maniwhere achieves scene generalization for tabletop pushing but is hard to scale to complex humanoids.
- Even the authors' previous work, 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), primarily showed object/view generalization with tabletop robot arms, not full humanoids in diverse scenes.
- Other generalization efforts like Robot Utility Model required data from 20 scenes, contrasting sharply with this paper's single-scene data approach.

By integrating a sophisticated teleoperation system with an improved 3D visuomotor policy, this work seeks to unlock the potential for humanoid robots to truly operate autonomously in the varied and unpredictable real world, moving beyond controlled laboratory settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for this research is to advance the long-held goal of creating intelligent humanoid robots capable of autonomous operation in diverse and unstructured environments. The authors identify a significant gap in the current state of the art: autonomous manipulation by humanoid robots has been predominantly restricted to specific, known scenes, lacking the ability to generalize learned skills to novel environments.

The key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Develop a Real-World Humanoid Robot Learning System:** The primary objective is to construct a comprehensive system that facilitates the acquisition of generalizable manipulation skills for a full-sized humanoid robot. This involves integrating advanced hardware and software components.
2. **Acquire Human-Like Imitation Learning Data:** To teach complex manipulation skills effectively, the system aims to collect high-fidelity, human-like robot demonstration data. This necessitates a sophisticated teleoperation system that maps human movements, including whole-upper-body degrees of freedom, to the humanoid robot.
3. **Enable Generalizable Skill Learning with 3D Visuomotor Policies:** The core technical objective is to develop and refine a learning algorithm that can extract robust and generalizable policies from noisy human demonstration data. Specifically, this involves improving upon the 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) to address its limitations for general-purpose robots and complex real-world scenarios. The goal is for the robot to learn skills that are invariant to scene variations, object types, and camera viewpoints.
4. **Demonstrate Zero-Shot Generalization from Single-Scene Training:** Crucially, the authors aim to show that, using data collected from *only a single training scene*, the learned policy can autonomously perform manipulation tasks in diverse, *unseen real-world environments* (e.g., kitchens, offices) without any additional calibration, data, or fine-tuning. This objective directly addresses the high cost and difficulty of acquiring diverse datasets.
5. **Rigorous Evaluation:** To validate the effectiveness and robustness of their system and policies, a significant objective is to conduct extensive real-world evaluation, including thousands of policy rollouts on the physical robot.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between impressive lab demonstrations and practical, deployable humanoid robot capabilities, making general-purpose humanoid manipulation in the wild a tangible reality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose an integrated real-world robotic system, termed "Generalizable Humanoid Manipulation with 3D Diffusion Policies," which combines hardware, data collection, and an improved learning algorithm.

#### 3.1. A. Humanoid Robot Platform

The system is built around the **Fourier GR1**, a full-sized humanoid robot.
- **Degrees of Freedom (DoF):** The system activates 25 DoF of the robot's upper body (head, waist, arms, hands), utilizing two Inspire Hands. The lower body (legs) is disabled for stability and replaced by a cart, simplifying control while allowing focus on manipulation.
- **Sensing:** An Intel RealSense L515 LiDAR camera is mounted on the robot's head, providing high-quality egocentric 3D point cloud data. The choice of LiDAR is critical due to its superior depth sensing compared to standard RGB-D cameras, which had previously shown suboptimal performance with DP3.
- **Mobility and Height Adjustment:** The robot is fixed on a **height-adjustable cart**, allowing the robot to interact with objects at varying heights without complex whole-body balance control, which is often a major challenge for legged humanoids. This design decision pragmatically addresses a common real-world variability.

#### 3.2. B. Human-Like Robot Data

To obtain high-quality, human-like demonstrations, the authors developed a **whole-upper-body teleoperation system**:
- **Human Input:** The **Apple Vision Pro (AVP)** is used to capture accurate, real-time 3D positions and orientations of the human operator's head, hands, and wrists. This consumer-grade device provides an accessible means for high-fidelity motion tracking.
- **Robot Control:** Human movements are mapped to robot joints:
 - Robot arm joints are computed using **Relaxed IK** to track human wrist positions.
 - Robot waist and head joints are directly controlled by the human head's rotation.
 - A key feature is the incorporation of the robot's **waist DoF**, which significantly expands the robot's operational workspace, particularly for tasks at different heights.
- **Egocentric Feedback:** Real-time visual feedback from the robot's LiDAR sensor is streamed back to the human operator, enabling immersive and intuitive egocentric teleoperation.
- **Data Representation:** Collected trajectories consist of observation-action pairs. Observations include point clouds, images, and robot proprioceptive data (joint positions). Actions are represented as target joint positions, found to be more accurate than end-effector poses due to real-world noise.
- **Single-Scene Data Collection:** Crucially, all demonstration data for learning is collected in *only one single scene*.

#### 3.3. C. Improved 3D Diffusion Policy (iDP3)

The core learning algorithm is an enhanced version of the 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), modified for robustness and generalization on humanoid robots:
- **Egocentric 3D Visual Representations:** Unlike the original DP3, which uses world-frame 3D representations requiring precise camera calibration and point cloud segmentation, iDP3 directly uses 3D representations from the *camera frame*. This eliminates the need for camera calibration and object segmentation, making it directly applicable to mobile robots and humanoids where camera mounts are not fixed.
- **Scaling Up Vision Input:** To compensate for the lack of segmentation and handle extraneous background points, iDP3 significantly increases the number of sampled points in the point cloud input. This "straightforward but effective solution" allows the policy to capture more of the entire scene without relying on complex foundation models. Instead of Farthest Point Sampling (FPS), a cascade of voxel and uniform sampling is used for faster inference.
- **Improved Visual Encoder:** The MLP visual encoder in DP3 is replaced with a **pyramid convolutional encoder**. The authors found that convolutional layers produce smoother behaviors, and incorporating pyramid features enhances accuracy, particularly when learning from noisy human data.
- **Longer Prediction Horizon:** To mitigate jittering inherent in human demonstrations and noise from sensors, iDP3 uses an extended prediction horizon. This helps the diffusion policy learn more stable and accurate actions over time.
- **Onboard Computing:** iDP3 is designed to perform real-time inference (15Hz) using only the robot's onboard computing resources, facilitating practical deployment.

#### 3.4. D. Real-World Deployment

After training iDP3 using data from a single scene, the policy is deployed to diverse, unseen real-world scenarios. The egocentric 3D representations and lack of reliance on calibration/segmentation mean that the policy can be transferred to new environments with "no additional efforts."

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling findings that underscore the effectiveness of their integrated system and the iDP3 algorithm in achieving generalizable humanoid manipulation.

#### 4.1. A. Generalization to Diverse Unseen Real-World Scenarios

The most significant finding is the demonstration of **zero-shot generalization**. Using data collected exclusively from *one single training scene*, the Fourier GR1 humanoid robot, empowered by iDP3, successfully performs autonomous manipulation skills in diverse *unseen real-world environments*. These environments include kitchens, meeting rooms, and offices, none of which were cherry-picked, showcasing the robustness of the approach beyond controlled lab settings. The tasks demonstrated include Pick&Place, Pour, and Wipe. The authors claim to be the first to achieve this level of scene generalization for a full-sized humanoid with single-scene 3D imitation learning.

#### 4.2. B. Rigorous Policy Evaluation

The effectiveness of the policies is validated through extensive real-world experimentation, involving **more than 2000 episodes of policy rollouts** on the physical robot. This rigorous evaluation methodology provides strong empirical evidence for the system's capabilities, mitigating concerns about cherry-picked results or insufficient testing.

#### 4.3. C. Effectiveness of iDP3 vs. Baselines (Table II)

- **iDP3 significantly outperforms vanilla Diffusion Policy (DP)** (with ResNet18 encoder), original 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), DP with a frozen R3M encoder, and iDP3 using the original DP3 encoder. For instance, across four training settings for Pick&Place, iDP3 achieved 75 successful grasps out of 139 attempts, while the original DP3 achieved 0/0 and vanilla DP 24/106.
- A strong image-based baseline, **DP with a finetuned R3M encoder (DP (*R3M))**, showed competitive or even superior performance in the *training environment* (99/147 vs iDP3's 75/139). However, this baseline *failed to generalize* to unseen scenarios and objects (Section IV-D), highlighting a critical limitation of image-based methods in real-world deployment.
- The authors acknowledge that current 3D visual observations from the L515 LiDAR are still noisy, suggesting further potential for improvement with more accurate 3D sensing hardware.

#### 4.4. D. Ablation Studies on iDP3 Modifications (Table III)

Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of each modification to iDP3:
- **Improved Visual Encoder:** Replacing the linear (MLP) encoder with a pyramid convolutional encoder significantly improved both policy smoothness and accuracy. The "Conv+Pyramid" combination (used in iDP3) performed best (75/139 total successful grasps) compared to linear (58/127) or other convolutional variations (49/131, 66/134).
- **Scaled Vision Input (Number of Points):** Increasing the number of sampled points in the point cloud input from 1024 (DP3 default) to 4096 (iDP3 default) proved beneficial (56/129 vs 75/139), while further scaling to 8192 showed saturation or slight decrease in performance for the specific tasks tested.
- **Longer Prediction Horizon:** An appropriate prediction horizon was found to be crucial. A very short horizon (4 steps, as in DP3) led to complete failure (0/0 success), while a horizon of 16 steps (iDP3) yielded optimal performance (75/139). A very long horizon (32 steps) also saw reduced performance (55/130).

#### 4.5. E. Training Efficiency (Figure 7)

iDP3 demonstrated significantly reduced training time compared to image-based Diffusion Policy, even when scaling up 3D vision input. This efficiency is a practical advantage, especially as the number of demonstrations increases.

#### 4.6. F. Generalization Capabilities of iDP3 (Table IV)

Compared to the strongest image-based baseline (DP with finetuned R3M), iDP3 exhibited superior generalization across multiple dimensions:
- **View Invariance:** iDP3 consistently grasped objects even under large camera viewpoint changes, attributed to its egocentric 3D representations. Image-based DP struggled significantly with such changes, even for training objects (Figure 8).
- **Object Generalization:** iDP3 naturally handled a wide range of unseen objects (various cups/bottles) with high success rates, thanks to its 3D representations. Image-based DP, even with color jitter augmentation, achieved low success rates with new objects (Figure 9).
- **Scene Generalization:** iDP3 maintained surprising robustness across highly varied *unseen real-world scenes*, whereas image-based methods struggled due to overfitting to visual cues in the training environment (Figure 6).

In summary, the main findings highlight that the carefully designed system, particularly the robust egocentric 3D representations and the refined diffusion policy, allows for unprecedented generalization capabilities in humanoid manipulation, making a strong case for 3D visuomotor policies over 2D image-based methods for real-world robotic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research represents a substantial stride towards the ambitious goal of truly general-purpose humanoid robots operating autonomously in complex human environments. Its significance and potential impact can be multifaceted:

#### 5.1. A. Breakthrough in Humanoid Generalization

The most significant contribution is demonstrating that a full-sized humanoid robot can perform diverse manipulation skills in *unseen real-world environments* from data collected in *only a single scene*. This addresses a critical, long-standing bottleneck in humanoid robotics: the inability to generalize skills beyond constrained training setups. It significantly lowers the bar for data collection, which has historically been a major barrier for humanoid robot development.

#### 5.2. B. Validation of 3D Visuomotor Policies

The work provides strong empirical evidence for the efficacy of 3D visuomotor policies, particularly egocentric 3D representations, over traditional 2D image-based methods for robustness, view invariance, object generalization, and scene generalization in real-world settings. This paradigm shift could influence future research in robot perception and learning, encouraging greater adoption of 3D sensing and representation learning.

#### 5.3. C. Data Efficiency for Complex Robots

Achieving robust generalization with minimal training data (from a single scene) is a game-changer for complex, high-DoF robots like humanoids. It mitigates the immense cost and effort associated with collecting large, diverse datasets, making advanced manipulation skills more accessible and practical for deployment outside research labs.

#### 5.4. D. Practical System for Real-World Deployment

The integrated system, incorporating a robust humanoid platform, an intuitive teleoperation system, and an efficient learning algorithm (iDP3), is designed for real-world deployment. Its ability to run real-time inference on onboard computing without requiring external calibration or segmentation makes it highly practical for open-world applications.

#### 5.5. E. Inspiration for Future Research Directions

The identified limitations, such as the tiring nature of AVP teleoperation, noisy depth sensors, and the current avoidance of whole-body control, offer clear avenues for future research. This includes developing more scalable data collection methods, improving 3D sensing hardware, and integrating these generalizable manipulation skills with advanced whole-body control for fully mobile humanoids. The work also highlights the need for 3D pre-trained visual models comparable to R3M for further enhancing 3D policy learning.

#### 5.6. F. Advancing Human-Robot Interaction and Service Robotics

By enabling humanoids to perform useful tasks like "Pick&Place," "Pour," and "Wipe" in diverse environments, this research moves closer to realizing humanoids as capable assistants in homes, offices, and industrial settings. The generalizable nature of these skills means a robot trained once could theoretically be deployed in various instances of these environments, paving the way for more widespread adoption of service robotics.

In conclusion, "Generalizable Humanoid Manipulation with 3D Diffusion Policies" presents a pivotal advancement in humanoid robotics. By demonstrating unprecedented generalization capabilities from single-scene data, the authors have opened new possibilities for the practical deployment of humanoid robots and set a high bar for future research in robust robot learning.
