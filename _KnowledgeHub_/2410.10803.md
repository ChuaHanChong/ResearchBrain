# Generalizable Humanoid Manipulation with 3D Diffusion Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> A system enables a Fourier GR1 humanoid robot to perform various manipulation tasks in diverse, previously unseen real-world environments, achieving zero-shot generalization from data collected in a single training scene. This is demonstrated through over 2000 real-world policy rollouts, leveraging egocentric 3D vision and an improved diffusion policy.

## Problem

- Autonomous manipulation skills for humanoid robots are often confined to specific training environments, lacking generalization to novel scenes.
- Existing learning methods, especially 2D image-based ones, exhibit limited generalization to varied camera viewpoints, object appearances, and scene layouts.
- Collecting diverse, high-quality demonstration data for full-sized humanoid robots in the wild is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.

## Method

- Development of an integrated real-world system using a ==Fourier GR1== humanoid robot equipped with an egocentric Intel RealSense L515 LiDAR camera and a height-adjustable cart.
- A whole-upper-body teleoperation system utilizing Apple Vision Pro for collecting high-fidelity, human-like demonstration data exclusively from a single training environment.
- Introduction of an improved 3D Diffusion Policy (iDP3) featuring egocentric 3D representations, a pyramid convolutional encoder, scaled vision input, and an extended prediction horizon to enhance generalization from noisy data.

## Results

- Demonstrated zero-shot generalization for manipulation skills (e.g., Pick&Place, Pour) on a Fourier GR1 humanoid robot in diverse, unseen real-world environments, trained solely on data from a single scene.
- Validated the system's robustness and effectiveness through over 2000 real-world policy rollouts on the physical robot.
- iDP3 significantly outperformed various diffusion policy baselines in both performance and generalization, with image-based methods failing to generalize to novel scenes, objects, or viewpoints.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the pyramid convolutional encoder, scaled 3D vision input, and a longer prediction horizon were essential for iDP3's improved accuracy and smoother behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric 3D visual representations are crucial for enabling generalization of manipulation skills to unseen scenes, objects, and camera viewpoints for humanoid robots.
> - High-fidelity human teleoperation, capturing whole-upper-body movements including waist DoF, provides effective demonstration data for learning versatile manipulation skills.
> - Specific architectural improvements to the 3D Diffusion Policy, such as a pyramid convolutional encoder and appropriately scaled 3D input, are necessary for robust learning from noisy human demonstrations.
