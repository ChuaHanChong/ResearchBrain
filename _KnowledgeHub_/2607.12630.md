# Instance-Enriched Semantic Maps for Visual Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research develops "Instance-Enriched Semantic Maps" for zero-shot open-vocabulary Visual Language Navigation (VLN), employing a 2.5D instance-level representation that incorporates detailed natural language captions and a Multi-Type Expert Fusion Retrieval (MTEFR) system for robust query processing. The approach reduces storage requirements by approximately 96% compared to 3D scene graphs while enhancing object retrieval and navigation success rates, particularly for complex and abstract natural language instructions.

## Problem

- Existing semantic maps often lack sufficient instance-level object detail, struggling to differentiate between multiple instances of the same category or interpret nuanced, attribute-based queries.
- Spatial representations in VLN face a trade-off between semantic expressiveness and storage efficiency: 2D maps are efficient but lose vertical information, leading to ambiguity, while 3D maps offer rich detail but incur substantial storage costs.
- Current LLM-based reasoning for VLN often processes diverse query formulations uniformly, leading to inconsistent goal selection when instructions involve varying levels of detail, context, or abstract intent.

## Method

- A ==2.5D instance-level open-vocabulary mapping== framework that builds a global representation storing per-instance ==feature embeddings== and category labels, preserving vertical distinctions in a top-down grid while optimizing storage by discarding individual masks.
- An ==instance captioning pipeline== utilizing ==LLaMA== and ==GPT-4== to generate detailed natural language descriptions of visual attributes (e.g., color, material) and room context for each object instance, enriching the semantic map.
- A **Multi-Type Expert Fusion Retrieval (MTEFR)** system that uses a ==Gate LLM== to dynamically weigh query components, routes them to four specialized ==expert LLMs== (object, room, description, abstract), and fuses their rankings to identify the most suitable target instance.

## Results

- The 2.5D mapping demonstrated improved instance-level semantic labeling, achieving prediction-normalized AUC_top_pred scores of **0.356** on Replica and **0.371** on HM3DSEM, outperforming HOV-SG by a large margin (e.g., **0.093** on Replica).
- The proposed method achieved a storage reduction of approximately **96%** (average **10 MB** per scene) compared to HOV-SG (average **248 MB**) and **92%** compared to VLMaps (average **118 MB**) on HM3DSEM datasets.
- The MTEFR module achieved superior object retrieval success rates (**O-SR**) and navigation success rates (**N-SR**) across various query types, with abstract queries seeing a particular gain (**14.00%** O-SR for MTEFR vs. **2.00%** for HOV-SG).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A 2.5D instance-level representation can effectively balance semantic expressiveness and storage efficiency by preserving crucial vertical distinctions without the high computational and memory costs of full 3D reconstructions.
> - Enriching semantic maps with fine-grained natural language captions, generated by LLMs, provides essential attribute-based information that significantly improves the robustness and specificity of language grounding for complex queries.
> - An adaptive multi-expert LLM-based retrieval system, capable of dynamically weighing and combining evidence from specialized experts, substantially enhances goal instance selection across diverse and nuanced natural language instructions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual Language Navigation (VLN) is a field of artificial intelligence that aims to enable embodied agents to navigate complex environments by interpreting natural language instructions. Current VLN systems often rely on constructing semantic spatial maps and utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs) for reasoning and decision-making.

Historically, spatial representations in VLN have evolved from simple 2D feature maps and graph-structured panoramas to more complex 3D reconstructions. Early 2D methods are computationally efficient but suffer from a fundamental limitation: they collapse the vertical dimension, leading to semantic ambiguity when multiple objects occupy the same horizontal space at different heights. Recent instance-aware 2D extensions have attempted to mitigate this but remain unable to resolve vertical overlap.

In response to these geometric limitations, research has shifted towards high-fidelity 3D representations, including hierarchical scene graphs, volumetric reconstructions, and Gaussian-based 3D splatting. While these 3D paradigms facilitate enhanced spatial-language reasoning by preserving rich geometric detail, they typically incur substantial storage requirements, which limits their scalability, particularly in large-scale or long-term robotic deployments.

In parallel, advancements in open-vocabulary semantic mapping leverage pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to enable zero-shot navigation without task-specific fine-tuning. However, many existing zero-shot maps represent instances primarily through fixed-dimensional embeddings, which may not be sufficient for resolving fine-grained, attribute-based, or abstract queries. Common VLM pipelines, such as those combining Segment Anything (SAM) with CLIP, can suffer from over-segmentation and background noise, impacting object-level consistency.

LLMs have also been integrated into VLN for high-level instruction decomposition and action planning. However, existing LLM-based reasoning approaches often lack the instance-level semantic grounding required to distinguish between multiple objects of the same category or interpret queries referencing specific visual properties. Keyword-based query processing, as seen in some prior works, discards descriptive content, limiting the system's ability to handle abstract or attribute-rich instructions.

This work addresses the complementary limitations of prior 2D and 3D approaches by proposing a 2.5D instance-level representation that aims to balance semantic expressiveness and storage efficiency. It also seeks to enhance query robustness by enriching per-instance information with natural language captions and introducing a multi-expert LLM-based retrieval system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient framework for zero-shot open-vocabulary Visual Language Navigation (VLN) that overcomes limitations in existing semantic mapping and language grounding methods. The key motivations stem from several identified shortcomings:

1. **Lack of instance-level object detail and robustness to diverse user queries:** Existing semantic maps often struggle to differentiate between multiple instances of the same object category or interpret nuanced instructions that refer to specific visual attributes (e.g., color, material) or abstract concepts. This limits reliable navigation, especially in complex indoor environments where precise object identification is crucial. The paper highlights that current systems often rely on category-level labels or fixed-dimensional embeddings, which are insufficient for handling fine-grained attribute-based or abstract queries.
2. **Trade-offs between semantic expressiveness and storage efficiency in spatial representations:** While 2D representations are efficient, they lose vertical dimension information, leading to semantic ambiguity. Conversely, high-fidelity 3D representations offer rich spatial detail but incur substantial storage costs, limiting their scalability in real-world robotic applications. There is a need for a representation that preserves crucial vertical distinctions while remaining storage-efficient.
3. **Inconsistent goal selection across diverse query formulations:** Natural language instructions can vary significantly, ranging from simple object category names to complex queries involving room context, visual descriptions, or abstract functional references. Existing LLM-based reasoning often processes queries uniformly, leading to inconsistent performance because it may not effectively leverage or prioritize different types of information present in a query. A more adaptive and structured approach to query processing is required.

To address these motivations, the authors propose Instance-Enriched Semantic Maps, aiming to achieve:
- **Enhanced object understanding and disambiguation:** By building instance-level 2.5D rich information maps that capture vertical distinctions and store diverse semantic attributes, natural language captions, and room-level context. This aims to reliably preserve small objects and provide fine-grained detail.
- **Consistent and robust goal instance selection:** Through an LLM-based target selection mechanism (Multi-Type Expert Fusion Retrieval, MTEFR) that dynamically routes queries to specialized experts and integrates their outputs. This aims to handle structurally diverse queries more effectively than conventional keyword-extraction or single-LLM approaches.
- **Storage-efficient semantic representation:** By utilizing a 2.5D representation combined with LLM-based captioning, which significantly reduces storage requirements compared to 3D scene-graph approaches while maintaining sufficient spatial and semantic information for navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Instance-Enriched Semantic Maps, operates through a multi-stage pipeline, encompassing mapping, semantic segmentation, instance captioning, and goal-directed navigation.

#### 3.1. Instance-Level 2.5D Open-Vocabulary Mapping

This stage constructs a global 2.5D representation that stores instance-level open-vocabulary information.
1. **Instance-Centric Representation:** From RGB-D observations, SEEM (Segment Everything Everywhere All at Once) extracts candidate instance masks and their open-vocabulary feature embeddings at each time step. These candidates are reconstructed in 3D, transformed into the global coordinate frame, and projected onto a top-down grid map. Each candidate instance `I_cand` comprises a top-down binary mask `m_cand` and a feature embedding `f_cand`.
2. **Instance Association:** Newly detected candidate instances are matched against accumulated existing instances (`I_exist`) from previous time steps. Matching relies on both geometric similarity (`phi_geo`, using max-overlap to mitigate truncation effects) and semantic similarity (`phi_sem`, cosine similarity of embeddings). An overall similarity score is a convex combination of these two, allowing a new candidate to be associated with an existing instance if both similarity thresholds are met.
3. **Instance Fusion:** If a candidate matches an existing instance, their masks are logically OR-ed, feature embeddings are updated via a weighted average, and the observation count (weight) is incremented. If no match is found, the candidate is added as a new instance. After all frames are processed, a second association-fusion pass merges long-range duplicates to enforce global instance consistency, yielding the definitive instance dictionary `I_final`.
4. **Instance-Level 2.5D Map Creation:** The per-instance masks are aggregated to create the global 2.5D map `M`. Each grid cell `M_x,y` can store indices of multiple instances occupying that cell, enabling the representation to preserve vertical distinctions—hence the "2.5D" designation. Once the map is constructed, the individual instance masks are discarded to prevent redundancy.
5. **Open-Vocabulary Instance Categorization:** Feature embeddings of the final instances (`F_final`) are categorized by computing cosine similarity with text embeddings (`E_icat`) generated from a predefined language-category list (`L_icat`) using a pre-trained SEEM text encoder. The category with the highest similarity score is assigned as the label `l_final` for each instance.

#### 3.2. Room-Level Semantic Segmentation

This stage generates semantically labeled room maps by combining structural priors with VLM-based semantic recognition.
1. **Instance-Level Room Separation:** Structural elements like walls, doors, and large furniture are extracted from the instance segmentation masks, integrated with an obstacle map to define navigable areas. The Line Segment Detector (LSD) is applied to this room-boundary mask to detect wall-like linear structures. Accepted line segments are rasterized, refined via morphological operations, and then analyzed for connected components, yielding unique structural room instance maps `S_final`.
2. **Semantic Labeling:** CLIP VLM extracts dense image-text embedding maps. For a set of predefined room-type categories, CLIP text embeddings are generated. Pixel-wise similarity between the image and text embeddings forms an initial room semantic segmentation map. To mitigate noise and enforce spatial consistency, this map is refined by leveraging the structural room instance map `S_final`. For each room segment, the final label is assigned via majority voting of pixel-wise labels within that segment.

#### 3.3. Instance Captioning

Detailed natural language descriptions are generated for each object instance to enrich the semantic map.
- **LLaMA 3.2 Vision** is used to generate initial descriptive captions for each instance, conditioned on representative keyframe images and the instance's predicted category label. This process aims to extract visual properties such as color, material, texture, and geometric shape.
- **GPT-4** then refines these captions by eliminating redundancy, distilling essential visual attributes into compact descriptions, and extracting structured key-value tags (e.g., color: red, material: wood). Each instance is also explicitly linked to its associated room category based on the `R_final` map, providing spatial context.

#### 3.4. Multi-Type Expert Fusion Retrieval for Open-Vocabulary Navigation

This module identifies the target instance that best satisfies a natural language query, leveraging the instance-enriched semantic map.
1. **Obstacle Map Construction:** A binary obstacle map `O` is created. Instances are marked as obstacles if their mean height falls within a specified range `[h_min, h_max]` or if their category belongs to a predefined list of obstacle categories (`L_ocat`).
2. **Multi-Type Expert Fusion-based Goal Instance Retrieval:**
 - **Gate LLM:** An LLM (`LLM_gate`) analyzes the user query `Q` and outputs a weight vector `(w_e)` across four expert types: object category (obj), room context (room), descriptive attributes (desc), and abstract semantic intent (abs). These weights indicate the query's emphasis on different information types.
 - **Type-Specialized Expert LLMs:** Four independent expert LLMs (Object, Room, Description, Abstract) rank a set of candidate instances `C` (up to `G` instances for tractable prompt length). Each expert focuses solely on its designated information type (e.g., the 'obj' expert on category matching, the 'desc' expert on visual attributes from captions). Each expert returns a top-`kappa` ranking, from which a rank score `nu_e,d` is derived for each candidate `d`.
 - **Score-Level Fusion:** Two complementary scores are computed: a weighted sum score `S_sum` (aggregating evidence from all experts according to gate LLM weights) and a peak score `P` (capturing the strongest evidence from any single expert). These are combined via a convex combination using a `lambda` schedule to produce a final score `U_d`. The candidate `d*` with the highest `U_d` is selected as the goal instance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The framework was evaluated on the Matterport3D (MP3D), Replica, and Habitat-Matterport 3D Semantics (HM3DSEM) datasets, using the Habitat simulator.

#### 4.1. 2.5D Open-Vocabulary Mapping

- **Instance-Level 2.5D Open-Vocabulary Mapping:** The proposed method demonstrated improved instance-level semantic labeling. In prediction-normalized Area Under the Curve (AUC_top_pred), the method achieved 0.356 on Replica and 0.371 on HM3DSEM, significantly outperforming HOV-SG (0.093 on Replica, 0.085 on HM3DSEM). This indicates greater robustness to spurious predictions and over-segmentation. The average absolute difference between predicted and ground-truth instance counts was substantially lower for the proposed method (2.6 vs. 86.6 on Replica; 67.4 vs. 417.6 on HM3DSEM for HOV-SG). In matching-normalized AUC (AUC_top_match), the method achieved 0.921 on Replica and 0.941 on HM3DSEM, outperforming HOV-SG (0.817 and 0.914, respectively). VLMaps performed notably worse due to 2D point-wise feature blending.
- **Small Object Preservation:** For small objects (bottom 20% occupancy area), the method achieved AUC_top_pred scores of 0.189 on Replica and 0.186 on HM3DSEM, compared to HOV-SG's 0.060 and 0.050, respectively. The instance-count gap for small objects was also significantly reduced (11.6 vs. 100.9 on Replica; 8.0 vs. 477.0 on HM3DSEM). While HOV-SG showed a slight edge in ground-truth-normalized AUC for HM3DSEM small objects (0.192 vs. 0.174), the overall results confirmed the robustness of the 2.5D representation in preserving small object semantics.
- **Effectiveness of 2.5D Instance Mapping over 2D:** When compared against 2D baselines (VLMaps, VLMaps+SEEM, CLIP Map), the 2.5D approach consistently outperformed them across metrics (Pixel Accuracy, Mean Accuracy, Mean Intersection Over Union, Frequency Weighted Mean Intersection Over Union). For instance, on MP3D, the proposed method (with secondary fusion) achieved an Acc of 0.507 and mIoU of 0.238, surpassing VLMaps+SEEM (0.447 Acc, 0.226 mIoU). This demonstrated that preserving vertical distinctions reduced semantic ambiguity compared to 2D projections.

#### 4.2. Room Semantic Segmentation

The method achieved improved room segmentation quality by leveraging structural priors. On the MP3D dataset, it increased Pixel Accuracy from 0.73 to 0.89, Mean Accuracy from 0.72 to 0.86, and Mean Intersection Over Union (mIoU) from 0.53 to 0.75 compared to a CLIP-based pixel-wise baseline. The structural constraint effectively reduced pixel-level noise and enhanced spatial coherence. A failure case was observed in open-plan areas where a lack of physical partitions prevented LSD from delineating boundaries, leading to incorrect merging of semantic regions.

#### 4.3. Navigation

Quantitative evaluation across five HM3DSEM scenes and query types demonstrated enhanced object retrieval (O-SR) and navigation success (N-SR).
- **Object Retrieval Success Rate (O-SR):** The proposed method (Full+MTEFR) achieved the highest average O-SR across all query types. It showed particularly large gains over HOV-SG for description-based and abstract queries. For the challenging abstract query type (a,r,d), MTEFR achieved 14.00% O-SR, compared to HOV-SG's 2.00%. The comparison with HOV-SG (w/ desc.), which received the same enriched captions, indicated that the MTEFR's fusion-based retrieval mechanism, rather than just richer information, was critical for performance improvement.
- **Navigation Success Rate (N-SR):** MTEFR achieved the highest conditional N-SR among primary baselines across most query types. For example, on the object-only query (o), MTEFR achieved 38.57% N-SR, while HOV-SG achieved 15.00%. VLMaps showed the lowest N-SR (e.g., 12.00% for (o)) due to its inability to identify specific target instances.
- **Ablation Studies:** These confirmed the individual contributions of room context and descriptive attributes. The full MTEFR model consistently achieved superior overall performance, demonstrating the complementary nature of different information types and the effectiveness of structured expert routing.
- **Failure Case Analysis:** Abstract queries sometimes struggled when expressions admitted overly broad semantic connections, leading to incorrect retrieval. Additionally, limited viewpoints could lead to inaccurate visual attribute descriptions by the captioning module.

#### 4.4. Storage Cost

The proposed method demonstrated significant storage efficiency. On HM3DSEM scenes, it required an average of 10 MB, representing approximately a 96% reduction compared to HOV-SG (avg. 248 MB) and 92% reduction compared to VLMaps (avg. 118 MB). This reduction was achieved while preserving instance-level semantic information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Visual Language Navigation by introducing a unified framework that addresses critical limitations in existing approaches. The development of Instance-Enriched Semantic Maps provides a representation that is both semantically rich and storage-efficient, offering a practical balance between the expressiveness of 3D methods and the efficiency of 2D methods.

The **instance-level 2.5D mapping** improves the accuracy and completeness of environmental representations. Its ability to preserve vertical distinctions and reliably map small objects, combined with significantly reduced storage requirements, has implications for long-term and large-scale robotic deployments where computational resources and memory are constrained. This addresses a fundamental trade-off in robotic mapping.

The **Multi-Type Expert Fusion Retrieval (MTEFR) module** enhances the robustness of language grounding by enabling adaptive interpretation of diverse natural language queries. By dynamically routing queries to specialized LLM experts and fusing their outputs, the system can consistently identify target objects even when instructions are abstract, descriptive, or referential to spatial context. This improvement in query processing is relevant for human-robot interaction, allowing users to issue more natural and varied commands without requiring rigid phrasing. The demonstrated improvement in object retrieval and navigation success, particularly for complex query types, indicates a step towards more intuitive and reliable robot control.

The **instance captioning mechanism** enriches the semantic map with detailed natural language descriptions of visual properties and spatial context, moving beyond simple categorical labels. This depth of information is critical for supporting the MTEFR module and for enabling nuanced object understanding by embodied agents.

The findings collectively indicate a potential for more capable and generalizable embodied agents. The modular architecture of the proposed framework, with distinct stages for mapping, segmentation, captioning, and navigation, facilitates potential integration with various robotic platforms. The compact map footprint further supports deployment on resource-constrained embedded systems. While challenges remain in real-time deployment due to computational overhead and in handling certain abstract queries or open-plan environments, the work lays a foundation for future research in lightweight model integration, advanced contextual reasoning, and improved semantic understanding in complex, unstructured spaces.
