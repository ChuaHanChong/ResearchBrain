# Self-Instruct: Aligning Language Models with Self-Generated Instructions

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-Instruct bootstraps instruction-following from a vanilla pretrained language model. Starting from 175 human-written seed tasks, the model generates new instructions and input-output instances, filters low-quality or near-duplicate ones, then finetunes on the result. On GPT3 it produced 52K instructions and gained 33% on Super-NaturalInstructions, nearly matching InstructGPT-001 with almost no human annotation.

## Problem

- Instruction-tuned models depend on human-written instruction data, which is expensive to collect and limits how much instruction data can be scaled.
- Human-written instruction sets skew toward popular NLP tasks, especially classification, so they lack diversity and creativity and narrow the tuned model's generality.
- Vanilla pretrained language models cannot follow instructions, and the strongest instruction-following models rely on private user data and human annotation that outside researchers cannot reproduce.

## Method

- ==Iterative bootstrapping== pipeline: seeded with 175 tasks, each round samples 8 ==in-context tasks== (6 human, 2 model-generated) to prompt the model to write new instructions, which join a growing ==task pool==.
- Two-way ==instance generation==: a few-shot classifier labels each instruction as classification or not. ==Input-first generation== handles non-classification tasks, and ==output-first generation==, which creates class labels before inputs, avoids label-biased inputs.
- ==Filtering== and finetuning: a new instruction is kept only if its ==ROUGE-L similarity== to every existing instruction is below 0.7, plus keyword and format heuristics. The original model is then finetuned on the data using varied prompt templates.

## Results

- On the **119** unseen SuperNI tasks, GPT3 with Self-Instruct scores **39.9** ROUGE-L versus **6.8** for vanilla GPT3, beating T0-style training (**37.9**) and nearly matching InstructGPT-001 (**40.8**).
- Adding Self-Instruct data to SuperNI training raises GPT3 from **49.5** to **51.6** ROUGE-L. The **52,445** generated instructions carry **82,439** instances, and **92%** of sampled instructions describe valid tasks.
- On expert-written novel user-oriented tasks, human evaluation shows Self-Instruct far ahead of models tuned on public T0 or SuperNI data, leaving only a **5%** absolute gap to InstructGPT-001. Only **54%** of sampled instances were fully correct.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A pretrained model can generate its own alignment data from a tiny human seed set, so a moderate-quality synthetic dataset can substitute for costly annotation.
> - Noisy data still teaches format and intent. Only 54% of instances were fully valid, yet tuning worked, so diversity and coverage matter more than per-example correctness.
> - Simple diversity control (a ROUGE-L threshold) plus output-first generation for classification tasks prevents mode collapse and label bias in self-generated data. This is a reusable recipe for synthetic-data loops.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. The Rise of Instruction Tuning

The field of Natural Language Processing has seen a dramatic shift with the emergence of large pre-trained language models (LLMs) like GPT-3, T5, and others. These models, trained on vast amounts of text data, exhibit remarkable zero-shot and few-shot learning capabilities, meaning they can perform new tasks with minimal or no explicit task-specific training data. A key development enabling their generalization to a wider array of tasks is "instruction tuning." This process involves fine-tuning pre-trained LMs on datasets composed of natural language instructions paired with corresponding inputs and desired outputs. Models like InstructGPT, T0 (Sanh et al., 2022), and Tk-Instruct/SUPER-NATURALINSTRUCTIONS (Wang et al., 2022) have demonstrated that explicitly training LMs to follow instructions significantly enhances their ability to generalize to unseen tasks and prompts. This paradigm shifts the interaction with LMs from merely predicting the next token to acting as a versatile agent that can understand and execute human commands.

#### 1.2. Challenges in Data Collection

Despite the success of instruction tuning, its primary bottleneck lies in the acquisition of high-quality, diverse, and creative human-written instruction data. Existing public datasets, such as PROMPTSOURCE and SUPER-NATURALINSTRUCTIONS (SUPERNI), while extensive, are often limited by:
- **Quantity:** Human annotation is expensive and time-consuming, making it difficult to scale.
- **Diversity:** Human-generated instructions tend to cluster around popular, well-defined NLP tasks (e.g., sentiment analysis, summarization, question answering), limiting the coverage of novel or more open-ended real-world user queries.
- **Creativity:** Annotators may struggle to conceive of the full breadth of tasks and interaction styles that LLMs are potentially capable of handling.

This dependency on human-curated data hinders the generalizability and practical applicability of instruction-tuned models, leading to a need for alternative, more scalable methods for generating instructional signals. Proprietary models like OpenAI's InstructGPT (Ouyang et al., 2022) showcase superior instruction-following, but their underlying data collection processes, often involving private user data and extensive human feedback (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback - RLHF), remain largely opaque and inaccessible to the wider research community. This lack of transparency impedes academic understanding and the development of open-source alternatives.

#### 1.3. Related Work and Distinctions

SELF-INSTRUCT fits into a broader category of research exploring how LMs can be used to generate their own training data, a concept sometimes referred to as "self-training" or "knowledge distillation."
- **LM-based Data Generation/Augmentation:** Previous works have used LMs to generate data for specific tasks (Schick and Schütze, 2021) or for general data augmentation (Feng et al., 2021). SELF-INSTRUCT differs by focusing on generating *new task definitions* (instructions and instances) from scratch, rather than augmenting data for pre-defined tasks.
- **Instruction Generation:** Some studies (Zhou et al., 2022b; Honovich et al., 2022b) generate instructions given a few examples of a task. SELF-INSTRUCT is "task-agnostic," generating novel tasks entirely.
- **Self-Training:** Traditional self-training (He et al., 2019) typically involves using a trained model to label unlabeled data for a *specific target task*. SELF-INSTRUCT, in contrast, generates a *variety of tasks* and their corresponding instances from minimal seeds.
- **Knowledge Distillation:** While SELF-INSTRUCT involves a form of "knowledge distillation" where the LM distills knowledge into itself, it is distinct from standard distillation that typically transfers knowledge from a larger teacher model to a smaller student model. Here, the model generates its own training data to improve its instruction-following capabilities.
- **Concurrent Work (Unnatural Instructions):** The paper notes a parallel work, "Unnatural Instructions" (Honovich et al., 2022a), which also generates large-scale instruction data using GPT-3. Key differences include the seed tasks used (SUPERNI vs. newly written), the generation model (InstructGPT-002 vs. vanilla GPT-3), and the specific pipeline details. Both approaches are seen as complementary contributions to expanding instruction data.

By proposing a semi-automated, bootstrapping approach that relies primarily on the capabilities of the pre-trained LM itself, SELF-INSTRUCT directly addresses the data bottleneck in instruction tuning, aiming to democratize access to advanced instruction-following capabilities without relying on prohibitively expensive human annotation or private data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The **main objective** of this research is to develop an effective and scalable framework for improving the instruction-following capabilities of pre-trained language models with **minimal human-labeled data**.

The **motivation** stems directly from the challenges identified in the broader research landscape:
1. **Over-reliance on Human Annotation:** Current instruction-tuned LMs heavily depend on human-written instruction data, which is costly to collect and inherently limited in quantity, diversity, and creativity. This limitation hinders the generality and robustness of the fine-tuned models.
2. **Lack of Task Diversity:** Human-curated datasets tend to focus on established NLP tasks, leading to models that might excel in these areas but struggle with novel, creative, or user-oriented instructions that don't fit traditional academic definitions.
3. **Opacity of Commercial Models:** The success of models like InstructGPT and ChatGPT, which demonstrate impressive instruction-following, comes with a lack of transparency regarding their training data and methodologies. This makes it difficult for the academic community to understand their capabilities, replicate their success, or build open-source alternatives.

SELF-INSTRUCT aims to overcome these issues by bootstrapping an LM's instruction-following abilities from its own generations, thereby providing an **almost annotation-free method** for aligning pre-trained LMs with instructions. This approach seeks to unlock the potential of LLMs to generate diverse and valuable training data, paving the way for more general-purpose and openly accessible instruction-following models.

### 3. Methodology: SELF-INSTRUCT Framework

SELF-INSTRUCT is an iterative bootstrapping pipeline designed to automatically generate instruction-following data (instructions, inputs, and outputs) from a pre-trained language model, which is then used to fine-tune the model itself. The process begins with a small seed set of human-written tasks and continuously expands this set.

#### 3.1. Defining Instruction Data

The target instruction data comprises a set of instructions ($I_t$), each defining a natural language task ($t$). For each task, there are one or more input-output instances ($(X_{t,i}, Y_{t,i})$). The goal is for a model $M$ to produce $Y_{t,i}$ given $I_t$ and $X_{t,i}$. The framework flexibly allows instructions that don't require an explicit input (i.e., $X$ can be empty) to encourage diversity in task formats (e.g., "Write an essay about school safety" vs. "Write an essay about the following topic" with "school safety" as input).

#### 3.2. Automatic Instruction Data Generation

The core of SELF-INSTRUCT consists of four iterative steps:

1. **Instruction Generation:**
 - The process starts with a small *seed set* of 175 manually written tasks (1 instruction, 1 instance per task), primarily crafted by the authors and their labmates to be diverse and interesting.
 - In each iteration, the pre-trained LM (GPT-3 "davinci" engine in this study) is prompted to generate *new instructions*. The prompt includes 8 in-context examples: 6 from the human-written seed tasks and 2 from previously model-generated tasks. This mix promotes both quality and diversity. The model generates new instructions until it stops, reaches a length limit, or generates a specific stop token.

2. **Classification Task Identification:**
 - Since classification tasks require a different instance generation strategy (to avoid input bias towards one label), the newly generated instructions are classified.
 - The LM is prompted in a few-shot manner (with 12 classification and 19 non-classification examples from the seed set) to determine if the instruction represents a classification task (i.e., has a small, limited output label space).

3. **Instance Generation:** For each new instruction, input-output instances are generated:
 - **Input-first Approach (for non-classification tasks):** The LM is prompted with the instruction to first generate a suitable input and then produce the corresponding output. This mimics the standard instruction-following setup.
 - **Output-first Approach (for classification tasks):** To mitigate output bias, the LM first generates possible class labels. Then, for each generated label, it generates an input conditioned on that label. This ensures a balanced representation of different class outputs.
 - In-context examples from other tasks (a fixed set of seed tasks) guide this generation process.

4. **Filtering and Postprocessing:**
 - To maintain diversity and quality, generated data undergoes several filtering steps:
 - **Instruction Filtering:** New instructions are added to the task pool only if their ROUGE-L similarity with any existing instruction is less than 0.7. Instructions containing keywords problematic for LMs (e.g., "image," "picture") are also excluded.
 - **Instance Filtering:** Exact duplicate instances or instances with identical inputs but different outputs are removed.
 - **Heuristic-based Filtering:** Invalid generations (e.g., instructions too long/short, outputs repeating inputs) are identified and discarded.

This iterative process allows for the continuous expansion of the instruction dataset.

#### 3.3. Finetuning the Language Model

Once a large-scale instruction dataset is generated, the original pre-trained LM (GPT-3 "davinci") is fine-tuned on this data.
- The instruction and instance input are concatenated to form a prompt.
- The model is trained in a standard supervised learning manner to generate the instance output.
- To ensure robustness to various input formats, multiple templates are used to encode the instruction and input (e.g., "Task:", "Input:", "Output:", varying line breaks).
- Fine-tuning is performed using OpenAI's API with default hyperparameters, except for setting the prompt loss weight to 0, and training for 2 epochs. The resulting model is denoted as GPT3$_{\text{SELF-INST}}$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluates SELF-INSTRUCT by applying it to GPT-3 and comparing the resulting model (GPT3$_{\text{SELF-INST}}$) against various baselines across two distinct evaluation settings.

#### 4.1. Characteristics of the SELF-INSTRUCT Data

- **Scale:** The process generated over 52,000 instructions and more than 82,000 input-output instances after filtering.
- **Diversity:**
 - Analysis of verb-noun structures in generated instructions revealed a wide range of intents and textual formats, indicating significant creativity beyond typical NLP tasks.
 - ROUGE-L similarity analysis showed a decent number of new instructions with low overlap (many below 0.4 ROUGE-L) with the initial 175 seed tasks, confirming the model's ability to generalize to novel task definitions.
 - The generated instructions, inputs, and outputs also exhibited diverse length distributions.
- **Quality:**
 - A human quality review of 200 randomly sampled instructions and their instances showed:
 - 92% of instructions described valid tasks.
 - 79% of inputs were appropriate for their instructions.
 - 58% of outputs were correct and acceptable responses.
 - 54% of instances had all fields (instruction, input, output) valid.
 - While there is noise (46% imperfect outputs), the authors argue that even partially correct or format-adhering generations provide useful training signals.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Generalization on SUPERNI Benchmark

GPT3$_{\text{SELF-INST}}$ was evaluated on the SUPER-NATURALINSTRUCTIONS (SUPERNI) benchmark, which consists of 119 diverse NLP tasks, in a zero-shot setting (no in-context examples from the target task). ROUGE-L was used as the primary metric.

- **Significant Improvement over Vanilla LM:** The vanilla GPT-3 performed poorly (ROUGE-L of 6.8), often generating irrelevant or repetitive text. GPT3$_{\text{SELF-INST}}$ achieved a ROUGE-L of 39.9, marking a substantial **33.1% absolute improvement** over the original model. This highlights the effectiveness of self-generated data for instruction alignment.
- **Competitive with InstructGPT-001:** GPT3$_{\text{SELF-INST}}$ nearly matched the performance of InstructGPT-001 (ROUGE-L of 40.8), a model trained with private user data and human annotations, demonstrating that self-generation can achieve comparable results to costly proprietary methods.
- **Superior to Publicly Trained GPT-3 Baselines:** GPT3$_{\text{SELF-INST}}$ outperformed GPT-3 models fine-tuned on publicly available instruction datasets like T0 (37.9 ROUGE-L vs. 39.9 ROUGE-L for SELF-INSTRUCT), despite T0 involving tremendous human labeling efforts.
- **Complementary to Existing Data:** When combined with the SUPERNI training set, SELF-INSTRUCT still provided additional gains (GPT3$_{\text{SELF-INST}}$ + SUPERNI Training: 51.6 ROUGE-L), showing its value as complementary data even when large amounts of labeled instruction data are present.

#### 4.3. Generalization to Novel User-Oriented Instructions

To assess practical value and generalization to diverse, unfamiliar instructions, the authors curated a new set of 252 "user-oriented" instructions covering various domains (e.g., email writing, coding, productivity). Human evaluators (authors of the instructions) rated model responses on a four-level scale (A: valid and satisfying, B: acceptable with minor imperfections, C: relevant but significant errors, D: irrelevant/invalid).

- **Strong Performance Against Public Datasets:** GPT3$_{\text{SELF-INST}}$ significantly outperformed GPT-3 models trained on T0 or SUPERNI data (based on A+B ratings). This further validates the diversity and practical utility of the self-generated data.
- **Close to InstructGPT-001:** GPT3$_{\text{SELF-INST}}$ was notably close in performance to InstructGPT-001, demonstrating only a 5% absolute gap in the combined A+B rating.
- **Confirms InstructGPT Superiority:** The evaluation also confirmed the impressive capabilities of newer InstructGPT engines (002 and 003), which still hold a lead, likely due to more expansive data, RLHF, or algorithmic advancements.

#### 4.4. Impact of Data Size and Quality

- **Data Size:** Experiments varying the number of generated instructions used for fine-tuning revealed that performance consistently improved with more data, plateauing around 16,000 instructions. This suggests that scale in self-generated data is beneficial, though diminishing returns apply.
- **Data Quality (Distillation):** Fine-tuning GPT-3 with data where the outputs were regenerated (distilled) by InstructGPT-003 (the best available model) led to a 10% performance increase. This indicates that improving the quality of the self-generated data, perhaps through human filtering or stronger teacher models, offers significant avenues for future work.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Limitations

#### 5.1. Significance and Impact

The SELF-INSTRUCT framework represents a significant advancement in the development of general-purpose language models, offering several key contributions:

- **Annotation-Free Alignment:** It provides an almost human-annotation-free method for aligning pre-trained LMs to follow instructions, drastically reducing the cost and effort associated with instruction tuning. This democratizes access to building highly capable instruction-following models.
- **Data Diversity and Creativity:** By bootstrapping from the LM's own generative capabilities, SELF-INSTRUCT produces a large, diverse, and creative dataset of tasks that extends beyond the scope of traditional NLP benchmarks, covering a wide range of user-oriented applications.
- **Transparency and Open Science:** The release of the large synthetic dataset (52K instructions, 82K instances) and the curated set of novel user-oriented tasks for evaluation are crucial contributions to the open science community. This promotes transparency regarding the success of instruction-tuned models and facilitates future research on building better, openly accessible instruction-following LMs, counteracting the opacity of commercial systems.
- **Paving the Way for Open-Source Alternatives:** The paper directly informs subsequent open-source efforts, such as Stanford Alpaca (Taori et al., 2023) and Baize (Xu et al., 2023), which have successfully adapted similar self-instruction generation techniques to fine-tune smaller, open-source LLMs to achieve impressive instruction-following abilities, mirroring the capabilities of proprietary models like ChatGPT.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors openly acknowledge several limitations that highlight avenues for future research:

- **Tail Phenomena and Bias Reinforcement:** As SELF-INSTRUCT relies on LMs, it inherits their inherent biases and limitations. The method might perform best for common language uses and struggle with "tail phenomena" (low-frequency contexts) or highly creative/uncommon instructions. There's a concern that the iterative process could amplify problematic social biases present in the pretraining data, especially given observations about the difficulty in producing balanced labels. Future work needs to rigorously study these biases and develop mitigation strategies.
- **Dependence on Large Models:** The approach's effectiveness is closely tied to the inductive biases of the underlying LM, implying it might work best for larger models. This could create access barriers for researchers with limited computing resources, though the success of Alpaca on smaller LLaMA models offers some optimism.
- **Data Quality Improvement:** While the self-generated data is effective, its quality is not perfect (46% of outputs were found to contain minor or significant errors). Future work can focus on improving this quality through more sophisticated filtering, human expert feedback (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), or distillation from even stronger "teacher" models. The experiment showing a 10% gain from InstructGPT-003 distillation underscores this potential.
- **Multi-modal Applications:** The current work focuses on text-based instructions. Extending SELF-INSTRUCT to multi-modal settings (e.g., image-based instructions) is a promising future direction.

In conclusion, SELF-INSTRUCT provides a powerful and practical framework for advancing instruction-following language models, demonstrating that self-generated data can significantly enhance an LM's ability to generalize to new tasks, while simultaneously fostering transparency and open research in a rapidly evolving field.
