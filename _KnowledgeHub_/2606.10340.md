# OMG: Omni-Modal Motion Generation for Generalist Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University developed OMG, an omni-modal motion generation framework, to enable generalist humanoid control by interpreting diverse human intents. The system leverages a large-scale, curated multi-modal motion corpus and a diffusion-based Motion Transformer, achieving real-time execution of complex, compositional commands on a physical Unitree G1 robot with high fidelity and stability.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning policies for humanoid control are often specialized to specific skills and require extensive reward engineering, limiting their scalability.
- Motion tracking systems, while scalable for given trajectories, offer limited autonomy when faced with high-level, multi-modal human instructions.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, unified, and multi-modal humanoid motion data, which hinders the development of generalist controllers.

## Method

- Curated **OMG**-Data, a large-scale (1000+ hours) multi-modal motion corpus unified for the Unitree G1 robot embodiment through meticulous preprocessing, retargeting, and simulation-in-the-loop filtering.
- Developed OMG-DiT, a Diffusion Transformer backbone designed to map diverse control modalities (language, audio, human reference motions) into robot-executable whole-body trajectories in real time.
- Integrated OMG-DiT into a generator-tracker hierarchical control framework for robust deployment, separating a shared motion prior from modality-specific condition interfaces and utilizing non-invasive adapters for extensibility.

## Results

- OMG-XL achieved an FID score of 6.03 for text-to-motion generation and **0.00%** fall rate for audio-to-motion, outperforming existing baselines in both quality and physical executability.
- Demonstrated significant sample efficiency in finetuning, achieving comparable performance with just **1%** of target data as models trained from scratch with **100%** of the data.
- Showcased zero-shot compositional control by combining language and audio commands (e.g., 'Wave Hands and Turn Around' to an 'Energetic hip-hop beat'), and achieved real-time inference with a **51.7 ms** sampling time for a 60-frame future chunk on a physical Unitree G1 robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling motion generation for humanoids with large-scale, high-quality multi-modal data can lead to emergent 'foundation-model-like' properties such as predictable scaling, sample-efficient adaptation, and zero-shot compositional generalization.
> - A unified diffusion-based architecture with separated motion prior and modality-specific interfaces allows for flexible and extensible processing of diverse, compositional inputs while ensuring real-time robot executability.
> - Careful data curation, including retargeting to a specific robot and simulation-in-the-loop filtering for dynamic feasibility, is crucial for developing robust, real-time humanoid motion generators.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid whole-body control has made progress through various methods, notably reinforcement learning (RL) which has enabled agile locomotion and dexterous manipulation. However, many existing RL-based policies are often specialized to specific skills and require extensive reward engineering, limiting their scalability to a wider range of tasks. Motion tracking, another paradigm, offers scalability by training policies to replicate reference motions. While effective for executing given trajectories, motion tracking systems typically offer limited autonomy when faced with high-level, multi-modal human instructions.

A proposed abstraction to address these limitations is the generator-tracker hierarchy. In this framework, an upstream motion generator translates high-level conditions into future whole-body trajectories, which a downstream tracker then executes on the robot. This hierarchical structure draws inspiration from biological motor systems, where a "brain" plans high-level movements and a "cerebellum" handles reactive execution. However, realizing this vision for general-purpose humanoid control faces two primary challenges:
1. **Data Scarcity and Heterogeneity:** Unlike large-scale datasets available for text or vision, high-quality, unified, and multi-modal humanoid motion data is scarce, fragmented, and often tailored to specific tasks or robot embodiments.
2. **Generator Modality and Extensibility:** The motion generator must be capable of conditioning on diverse, compositional, and extensible multi-modal inputs (e.g., language, audio, human demonstrations) while remaining adaptable to new control interfaces.

Existing work in motion generation, particularly in computer graphics, has advanced generative architectures for expressive conditioning on various inputs. However, these models often produce human motions in an offline manner and do not inherently address the requirements for real-time, robot-executable control. Conversely, while approaches coupling motion generation with tracking systems exist in robotics, they are frequently limited to a single command modality or a narrow task domain. The present work aims to bridge this gap by focusing on general-purpose, omni-modal motion generation for real-time, robot-executable humanoid control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a general-purpose humanoid control system that can interpret diverse human intents and translate them into physically executable robot motions. This is framed as building a scalable "motion generation brain" on top of a "reactive motion tracking cerebellum."

The motivation stems from the limitations of current humanoid control methods and the challenges in realizing the generator-tracker paradigm:

1. **Addressing Data Limitations:** The scarcity, fragmentation, and lack of a unified representation in existing humanoid motion datasets hinder the development of generalist controllers. A primary motivation is to overcome this by curating a large-scale, high-quality, and unified multi-modal motion corpus specifically tailored for a humanoid robot. This aims to enable the supervised scaling of humanoid motion generation, similar to how large datasets have propelled progress in other AI domains.
2. **Enabling Omni-Modal and Compositional Control:** Current motion generators are often confined to a single input modality or lack the ability to combine different types of control signals. A key motivation is to design a motion generator capable of reasoning with multiple modalities (language, audio, human demonstrations) and their compositions. This requires an architecture that can process these diverse inputs flexibly and allow for future expansion to new modalities without extensive re-engineering.
3. **Achieving Real-Time Robot Executability:** Many advanced motion generation techniques are developed for offline applications in graphics and do not guarantee physical realism or real-time performance on actual robots. A core motivation is to ensure that the generated motions are dynamically feasible and can be executed robustly by a low-level motion tracker on a physical humanoid robot in real time.
4. **Demonstrating Foundation Model Properties:** The researchers are motivated to explore whether scaling motion generation for humanoids can lead to emergent "foundation-model-like" properties. These properties include predictable scaling behavior with increased model capacity, sample-efficient adaptation to new tasks and data distributions, and zero-shot compositional generalization where independently learned capabilities can be combined. This investigation aims to contribute to the broader goal of developing generalist AI agents for robotics.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Introduce OMG, an omni-modal motion generation framework for generalist humanoid whole-body control.
- Curate OMG-Data, a large-scale multi-modal humanoid motion corpus (1000+ hours) unified for the Unitree G1 embodiment.
- Develop OMG-DiT, a diffusion-based motion generation backbone supporting extensible and compositional conditioning from language, audio, and human reference motions.
- Validate OMG through extensive experiments, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance, foundation-model-like properties, and real-world robot executability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The OMG framework addresses the identified challenges through a two-pronged approach: meticulous data curation and a unified diffusion-based motion generation model, integrated into a generator-tracker hierarchy.

#### 3.1. OMG-Data: Scaling Multi-Modal Data for Humanoid Motion Generation

The researchers curated OMG-Data, a large-scale, multi-modal humanoid motion corpus of over 1000 hours, unified into the Unitree G1 robot's motion space. This involved a pipeline of curation, preprocessing, retargeting, annotation, and filtering.

- **Data Curation and Preprocessing:** A diverse set of publicly available motion datasets from both graphics and humanoid domains (e.g., AMASS, HumanML, SnapMoGen, AIST++) were aggregated. These datasets covered text labels, audios, and human reference motions. Initial steps included validation checks to purge corrupted files or samples with severe missing frames. For multi-modal sequence blocks with synchronized audio, raw acoustic streams and audio features were preprocessed to be frame-aligned and temporally synchronized with motion clips.
- **Motion Retargeting and Annotation:** To achieve a unified motion space, all heterogeneous motion representations were retargeted to the Unitree G1 humanoid's embodiment using General Motion Retargeting (GMR). For datasets lacking native language descriptions or requiring finer semantic granularity, retargeted motion sequences were rendered in simulation and then labeled using Seed-1.8, a multi-view visual language model (VLM), with multi-view videos or keyframes as visual inputs. Motions were segmented into training clips based on language annotation boundaries, audio phrase cuts, or uniform/sliding windows.
- **Simulation-In-The-Loop Filtering:** To ensure dynamic feasibility and prevent invalid or physically impossible configurations, a filtering step was applied. Each motion sequence was rolled out in the MuJoCo rigid-body simulator using a joint-space PD tracking controller. Fall detection heuristics (based on root height and tilt angle thresholds over consecutive frames) were used to identify and reject infeasible trajectories, ensuring the dataset contained dynamically plausible motions for humanoid deployment.

#### 3.2. OMG-DiT: Unified Diffusion Backbone for Motion Generation

OMG-DiT is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone designed to map diverse control modalities into robot-executable whole-body trajectories in real time. Its core design principle is to separate a shared motion prior from modality-specific condition interfaces.

- **Problem Formulation:** The control problem is decomposed into a motion generator (π_ϕ) that predicts future whole-body reference observations from multi-modal conditions, and a low-level motion tracker (π_ψ) that converts these references into executable robot actions. This work focuses on π_ϕ, leveraging existing general-purpose trackers like HoloMotion for execution.
- **Motion Representation and Canonicalization:** Each motion frame is represented as a 125-dimensional feature vector in a canonical root-centric coordinate frame. This vector includes canonicalized root position, 6D root rotation representation (to avoid discontinuities), joint angles, and local positions of non-root body links. Canonicalization removes global translation and heading ambiguities by anchoring the representation to the last observed state, thereby simplifying the modeling task for the diffusion backbone. All feature channels are normalized using training-set statistics.
- **Model Architecture:** OMG-DiT is implemented as a Diffusion Transformer trained with an x-prediction objective, directly generating in motion space without a learned motion tokenizer. History motion (L=10 frames) is injected via cross-attention. Denoising timesteps are injected by concatenation with noisy motion features. Rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) are used for temporal self-attention. Three model variants (B, L, XL) with increasing parameters (50M, 300M, 500M) are used for scaling analysis.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained as a conditional diffusion model to predict the clean future motion (H=60 frames) from a corrupted future segment, conditioned on history and a subset of available modalities. A cosine noise schedule and DDIM sampling (50 steps) are used. Random dropout of each conditioning modality during training (e.g., 0.3 for text, 0.1 for audio, 0.5 for human reference) facilitates classifier-free guidance.
- **Omni-Modal Condition Encoding:**
 - **Pretraining:**
 - **Language:** Frozen T5 encoder (max length 50) projects text into global context tokens, injected via cross-attention.
 - **Audio:** Frame-aligned 35D acoustic features are projected by MLPs and injected via per-layer FiLM modulation.
 - **Human Reference Motion:** 66D frame-aligned human joint positions are projected by MLPs and injected via per-layer FiLM modulation.
 - **Task-specific Finetuning:** New modalities (e.g., Pico keypoints for teleoperation) are incorporated using non-invasive, zero-initialized adapters (e.g., FiLM modules) which modulate the pretrained backbone without altering its initial function, enabling sample-efficient adaptation.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance and Composition:** Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is used at inference. For single conditions, guidance compares a conditional branch with a null-condition branch. For compositional conditions, modality-specific guidance branches are used, allowing for a weighted sum of guidance directions from different modalities (e.g., text and audio), enabling zero-shot composition of command combinations.
- **Real-Time Deployment:** To achieve real-time inference, the system utilizes runtime acceleration methods such as ONNX/TensorRT export, FP16 precision, and Diffusion Transformer caching. The motion generator runs on an off-board workstation, while the motion tracker operates on the robot's onboard NVIDIA Orin chip.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate OMG's performance against baselines, its adaptation capabilities, and its foundation-model-like properties.

#### 4.1. Pretraining Experiments

- **Text to Motion:** OMG-L and OMG-XL significantly outperformed existing human motion generation models (GENMO, HYMotion, Kimodo variants) in motion generation quality. OMG-XL achieved a FID score of 6.03, compared to 43.78 for GENMO and 24.68 for HYMotion, indicating higher fidelity to real motion distribution. OMG also exhibited higher R-precision (e.g., R@3 of 86.91% for OMG-XL vs. 30.86% for GENMO), demonstrating better semantic alignment. Critically, OMG maintained high tracking fidelity and low failure rates (e.g., 0.78% fall rate for OMG-XL vs. 1.46% for GENMO), indicating the generated motions were physically executable on the Unitree G1.
- **Audio to Motion:** In audio-conditioned generation, OMG maintained superior performance in audio alignment (e.g., BeatAlign 0.51 for OMG-XL vs. 0.57 for GENMO) and motion quality (e.g., FID-k 40.46 for OMG-XL vs. 506.07 for GENMO). OMG also achieved perfect physical executability with a 0.00% fall rate, demonstrating robust rhythmic and expressive motion generation without compromising physical stability.
- **Human Reference to Motion:** OMG-L and OMG-XL substantially outperformed both optimization-based (GMR, PHC, OmniRetarget) and learning-based (NMR) retargeting methods. OMG-XL achieved lower MPJPE (18.84 mm vs. 81.95 mm for GMR) and end-effector error (52.26 mm vs. 219.23 mm for GMR), demonstrating superior fidelity to human reference motions. The low fall (0.39%) and joint-limit violation (23.05%) rates indicated high physical executability, suggesting that the learned generator functions as an effective implicit retargeting module.

#### 4.2. Finetuning Experiments

- **Few-shot Text-to-Motion Finetuning:** When finetuned on the unseen AMASS-CMU dataset, pretrained OMG models consistently outperformed models trained from scratch. Notably, with only 1% of the target data, finetuned OMG achieved a FID of 0.1378, which was comparable to training from scratch with 100% of the data (FID 0.1001), indicating significant sample efficiency and positive transfer from pretraining.
- **Pico Keypoint-Based Teleoperation:** For adaptation to a novel modality, Pico keypoint-conditioned teleoperation, finetuned OMG models consistently outperformed from-scratch training under identical data budgets. For instance, with 100% of the data, finetuned OMG achieved an MPJPE of 25.73 mm, significantly better than the 39.42 mm for the from-scratch model. This demonstrated that the pretrained model provides a reusable prior for integrating new control interfaces.

#### 4.3. Analysis (Foundation Model Properties)

- **Zero-shot Compositionality:** The system demonstrated the ability to combine language and audio conditions at inference, producing motions that simultaneously followed both instructions. For example, the humanoid could "Wave Hands and Turn Around" while adhering to an "Energetic hip-hop beat," showcasing qualitative distinctions from either condition applied individually.
- **Scaling Behavior:** Experiments with OMG-DiT variants (B, L, XL) showed that performance, measured by MPJPE, consistently improved with increased model size. This suggests that humanoid motion generation is a scalable objective that benefits from greater model capacity.
- **Runtime Acceleration:** A series of optimizations, including `torch.compile`, ONNX Runtime, TensorRT, FP16 precision, and DiT caching, significantly reduced inference time. OMG-DiT-B achieved a 25.1x speedup, bringing sampling time for a 60-frame future chunk down to 51.7 ms, facilitating real-time deployment.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance Analysis:** Analysis confirmed that classifier-free guidance improves instruction following. Moderate guidance scales (e.g., ω=2.0 for human reference) optimally improved alignment, while overly strong guidance could degrade performance. For multi-modal composition, increasing the guidance scale for a specific modality (e.g., text) strengthened adherence to that particular instruction while maintaining adherence to others (e.g., audio rhythm).
- **Perceptive Locomotion Adaptation:** As an additional experiment, OMG's pretrained motion prior was adapted to a perceptive locomotion task involving egocentric visual commands. The pretrained model achieved a higher success rate (58.9%) compared to a randomly initialized scratch model (48.9%), indicating positive transfer to visually grounded control tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OMG framework represents a step towards general-purpose humanoid whole-body control, addressing key limitations of prior approaches.

- **Advancement in Generalist Humanoid Control:** By enabling omni-modal motion generation, OMG moves beyond specialized, single-skill policies or motion trackers that lack high-level autonomy. This provides a unified interface for diverse human intents (language, audio, human reference) to be translated into robot actions, contributing to the development of more versatile humanoid robots.
- **Paving the Way for Foundation Models in Robotics:** The demonstration of foundation-model-like properties—including predictable scaling, sample-efficient adaptation to new data distributions and modalities, and zero-shot compositional generalization—suggests a promising path toward building large-scale, pre-trained models for humanoid robotics. This could lead to a paradigm shift in how robot capabilities are acquired and deployed, similar to the impact of foundation models in natural language processing and computer vision.
- **Comprehensive Data Resource:** The creation of OMG-Data, a large-scale (1000+ hours), meticulously curated, unified, and robot-executable multi-modal motion corpus for the Unitree G1, is a significant contribution. This dataset facilitates supervised learning at scale for humanoid motion generation, overcoming a long-standing obstacle in the field.
- **Robust and Extensible Architecture:** OMG-DiT, with its shared diffusion backbone and non-invasive, zero-initialized adapters for new modalities, provides a flexible and extensible architecture. This design allows for the seamless integration of new control interfaces without requiring extensive retraining of the entire model, thus fostering future research and development in multi-modal robot control.
- **Validated Hierarchical Control:** The work validates the effectiveness of the generator-tracker hierarchy, demonstrating that an upstream motion generator can produce high-quality, physically executable trajectories that a downstream tracker can robustly execute on a real robot in real time. This hierarchical approach offers a structured way to manage the complexity of whole-body humanoid control.

While the work acknowledges limitations such as primary focus on flat-ground motions and the modular nature of the generator-tracker hierarchy, the demonstrated capabilities mark a substantial contribution towards more intelligent and adaptable humanoid robots.
