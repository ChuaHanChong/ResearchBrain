# OMG: Omni-Modal Motion Generation for Generalist Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University developed OMG, an omni-modal motion generation framework, to enable generalist humanoid control by interpreting diverse human intents. The system leverages a large-scale, curated multi-modal motion corpus and a diffusion-based Motion Transformer, achieving real-time execution of complex, compositional commands on a physical Unitree G1 robot with high fidelity and stability.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning policies for humanoid control are often specialized to specific skills and require extensive reward engineering, limiting their scalability.
- Motion tracking systems, while scalable for given trajectories, offer limited autonomy when faced with high-level, multi-modal human instructions.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, unified, and multi-modal humanoid motion data, which hinders the development of generalist controllers.

## Method

- Curated **OMG**-Data, a large-scale (1000+ hours) multi-modal motion corpus unified for the Unitree G1 robot embodiment through meticulous preprocessing, retargeting, and simulation-in-the-loop filtering.
- Developed OMG-DiT, a Diffusion Transformer backbone designed to map diverse control modalities (language, audio, human reference motions) into robot-executable whole-body trajectories in real time.
- Integrated OMG-DiT into a generator-tracker hierarchical control framework for robust deployment, separating a shared motion prior from modality-specific condition interfaces and utilizing non-invasive adapters for extensibility.

## Results

- OMG-XL achieved an FID score of 6.03 for text-to-motion generation and **0.00%** fall rate for audio-to-motion, outperforming existing baselines in both quality and physical executability.
- Demonstrated significant sample efficiency in finetuning, achieving comparable performance with just **1%** of target data as models trained from scratch with **100%** of the data.
- Showcased zero-shot compositional control by combining language and audio commands (e.g., 'Wave Hands and Turn Around' to an 'Energetic hip-hop beat'), and achieved real-time inference with a **51.7 ms** sampling time for a 60-frame future chunk on a physical Unitree G1 robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling motion generation for humanoids with large-scale, high-quality multi-modal data can lead to emergent 'foundation-model-like' properties such as predictable scaling, sample-efficient adaptation, and zero-shot compositional generalization.
> - A unified diffusion-based architecture with separated motion prior and modality-specific interfaces allows for flexible and extensible processing of diverse, compositional inputs while ensuring real-time robot executability.
> - Careful data curation, including retargeting to a specific robot and simulation-in-the-loop filtering for dynamic feasibility, is crucial for developing robust, real-time humanoid motion generators.
