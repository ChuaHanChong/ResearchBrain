# MotionGS-SLAM: Event-Modulated Gaussian Splatting for Motion-Blur Robust SLAM

> [!summary] Summary
> MotionGS-SLAM reframes motion blur in Gaussian Splatting SLAM as a forward generative process instead of an inverse deblurring problem. An event-modulated Gaussian kernel uses event-camera motion cues to anisotropically stretch and adaptively temporally sample each Gaussian during rasterization, jointly optimizing intra-exposure trajectory and 3D geometry, cutting average tracking error from 9.22cm to 4.44cm versus MonoGS.

## Problem

- Image-only deblurring GS-SLAM methods invert an ill-posed signal where high-frequency content is already irreversibly destroyed by severe blur.
- Prior event-guided NeRF/3DGS reconstruction methods require precomputed poses via offline SfM, making them incapable of online real-time SLAM tracking.
- Existing GS-SLAM systems assume sharp well-exposed input, so tracking and mapping degrade catastrophically under aggressive motion or low-light long exposures.

## Method

- ==Physics-based blur formation model== renders each blurry frame as a temporal integral over N discrete poses along an ==SE(3) trajectory== between start and end exposure poses.
- **MotionGS-SLAM**'s ==event-modulated Gaussian kernel== with dual modulation: spatial anisotropic stretching via a motion-aligned covariance prior, plus adaptive temporal sampling density tied to aggregate scene velocity.
- ==4D spatio-temporal hash grid== performs real-time event-Gaussian association, reducing complexity from quadratic brute-force to near-linear per-Gaussian event queries.

## Results

- On EventReplica, average PSNR rises to **28.05dB** and LPIPS drops to **0.171**, beating MonoGS (23.12dB/0.391) and EGS-SLAM (27.62dB/0.182) while sustaining **1189.7** rendering FPS.
- Average absolute trajectory error falls to **4.44cm**, a **51.9%** reduction versus MonoGS's 9.22cm and **14.2%** improvement over EGS-SLAM's 5.17cm across seven sequences.
- Ablation shows event association alone cuts room0 ATE from 12.76cm to **6.10cm**, then spatial modulation adds the largest LPIPS gain (0.340 to 0.250), full model reaching **3.98cm** ATE.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating an ill-posed inverse problem as a well-constrained forward generative process can outperform deconvolution-style correction when a high-temporal-resolution auxiliary sensor is available.
> - Decoupling static 3D geometry from motion-induced deformation via separate covariance terms lets a physical artifact be modeled without corrupting the underlying scene representation.
> - Spatial and temporal blur modeling address distinct failure modes (perceptual sharpness versus photometric accuracy) and combine synergistically rather than redundantly.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a foundational technology for autonomous robots, enabling them to determine their position and concurrently construct a map of their environment. Traditional vision-based SLAM systems have demonstrated efficacy in localization but often yield sparse geometric maps. The emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has recently advanced the field, leading to a new class of SLAM systems (e.g., GS-SLAM, SplaTAM, MonoGS) capable of generating dense, photorealistic scene representations in real-time. These systems hold promise for applications such as augmented reality and digital twinning.

However, a critical limitation of current 3DGS-based SLAM methods, as well as many other vision-based SLAM systems, is their reliance on high-quality, sharp input images. In real-world robotic applications, particularly under conditions of aggressive motion or low-light necessitating long exposure times, input images are frequently corrupted by severe motion blur. This degradation obscures high-frequency details essential for feature matching and photometric consistency, leading to inaccurate camera tracking and subsequent artifacts in the reconstructed 3D map.

Prior attempts to address motion blur in SLAM or 3D reconstruction often involve deblurring modules or image-only processing techniques. These methods typically aim to solve an ill-posed inverse problem: recovering sharp image content from a degraded, blurry observation. Such inverse approaches struggle with severe or complex motion blur because critical high-frequency information may be irreversibly lost during the blur formation process, making reliable inversion challenging. For example, some approaches like I$^2$-SLAM model the camera's imaging process, but as image-only methods, they are fundamentally constrained by the information available in the corrupted signal.

A parallel development in computer vision is the increasing use of event cameras. These bio-inspired sensors respond asynchronously to changes in pixel intensity with microsecond temporal resolution, rather than capturing intensity frames at a fixed rate. Crucially, event cameras are inherently immune to motion blur. This characteristic makes them valuable for high-speed or challenging lighting conditions. Recent research has explored combining event streams with implicit neural representations like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) or explicit representations like 3DGS for scene reconstruction. Works such as E2NeRF, Ev-DeblurNeRF, EvaGaussians, and Event3DGS have demonstrated the capability of event data to facilitate the reconstruction of high-fidelity 3D scenes from blurry images. However, many of these event-based 3DGS/NeRF systems are designed primarily for *offline reconstruction*, requiring pre-computed camera poses (e.g., from Structure-from-Motion, SfM) rather than performing real-time online localization and mapping.

MotionGS-SLAM positions itself within this landscape by addressing the fundamental challenge of motion blur in online 3DGS-based SLAM. It diverges from traditional deblurring by reformulating the problem as a *generative, forward-modeling* task. Instead of attempting to remove blur, it physically simulates the blur formation process. Furthermore, it integrates the high-temporal-resolution motion cues from event cameras directly into an *online SLAM framework*, specifically leveraging them to modulate the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering process. This approach aims to bridge the gap between robust event-based reconstruction and real-time SLAM, providing a solution that is less susceptible to motion blur artifacts than previous vision-only or offline event-based methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) system that robustly operates in the presence of severe motion blur, a condition that causes conventional vision-based SLAM systems to fail. The motivation stems from a recognized limitation in existing SLAM technologies: their assumption of sharp, high-quality visual input. This assumption is often violated in practical robotic applications, particularly during dynamic movements or in low-light environments where longer camera exposure times are necessary, leading to pronounced motion blur. Motion blur degrades image quality, hindering feature extraction and matching, which are critical for accurate camera tracking and consistent 3D map construction.

The authors identify a fundamental issue with traditional approaches to motion blur: they typically treat blur as an artifact to be removed through deconvolution, which is an ill-posed inverse problem. This means that recovering the original sharp image from a severely blurred observation is inherently ambiguous, as vital high-frequency information may be irreversibly lost.

To overcome this limitation, the core motivation of MotionGS-SLAM is to introduce a paradigm shift. Instead of attempting to invert the blur, the system proposes to embrace motion blur as a natural physical consequence of camera motion during image exposure. This leads to a reformulation of the challenge as a *well-constrained forward problem*: generatively modeling the blur formation process within the rendering pipeline.

Specific objectives derived from this motivation include:

- **Generative Blur Modeling:** To design a system that explicitly models the physical process of motion blur formation within its differentiable rendering pipeline, rather than attempting to remove blur artifacts.
- **Leveraging Event Camera Data:** To integrate the microsecond temporal resolution and motion-blur immunity of event cameras to provide precise, real-time motion cues. These cues are intended to guide the generative blur modeling process.
- **Event-Modulated Gaussian Kernel Development:** To introduce a novel mechanism, termed an event-modulated Gaussian kernel, which dynamically adapts the rasterization of each 3D Gaussian based on local motion information derived from event data.
- **Dual-Modulation Strategy:** To implement a dual-modulation approach for this kernel:
 - **Spatial Modulation:** Transforming 2D Gaussian projections from isotropic "dots" into anisotropic, motion-aligned elliptical "brush strokes" whose orientation and eccentricity correspond to detected motion direction and magnitude.
 - **Temporal Modulation:** Adaptively varying the sampling density of the exposure integral based on the aggregate local velocity across the scene to accurately model the cumulative photometric effect of motion.
- **Joint Optimization of Trajectory and Geometry:** To enable the simultaneous optimization of continuous intra-exposure camera trajectories and the 3D scene geometry (represented by Gaussians) using blur-aware photometric and event-based constraints. This joint optimization is intended to enhance tracking precision and map quality despite degraded inputs.
- **Performance Improvement:** To demonstrate significant improvements in trajectory accuracy and map quality over existing state-of-the-art visual and 3DGS-SLAM methods, particularly under severe high-motion and low-light conditions, without relying on a depth sensor.

By achieving these objectives, MotionGS-SLAM aims to establish a more robust and physically consistent framework for SLAM in challenging real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MotionGS-SLAM operates on a dual-input stream consisting of blurry RGB images and corresponding event data. The system's architecture is centered on a physics-based generative model for motion blur, guided by events, within a 3D Gaussian Splatting framework.

**A. Motion Blur Image Formation Model**

The scene is represented as a collection of 3D Gaussians, each defined by a mean, covariance, color, and opacity. Unlike standard rendering, which assumes a static camera pose, motion blur is modeled as the integration of light over a non-zero exposure interval ($\Delta t_m$), during which the camera undergoes continuous motion. The camera's trajectory within this interval, $T(t)$, is parameterized by its start ($T_{st}$) and end ($T_{ed}$) poses using an SE(3) geodesic interpolation. The blurred image ($\bar{C}_m$) is then approximated by discretely summing $N$ latent sharp images rendered at intermediate poses along this interpolated trajectory.

**B. Motion Blur Aware Tracker**

The tracker aims to estimate the precise intra-exposure camera trajectory for each incoming blurry frame by optimizing the start and end poses ($T_{st}$, $T_{ed}$).

1. **Real-Time Event-Gaussian Association:**
 To provide local motion context for each 3D Gaussian, events are efficiently associated with them. A multi-resolution 4D hash grid is employed to index events by their spatial (x, y) and temporal (t) coordinates, as well as polarity (p). For each visible 3D Gaussian projected onto the 2D image plane, the hash grid is queried for events within its 3$\sigma$ axis-aligned bounding box and within the current exposure window. A Mahalanobis distance criterion is then applied to filter these candidate events, ensuring they are spatially relevant to the Gaussian's projection. This hash-based approach reduces the computational complexity of association.
 From the associated event set for each Gaussian ($E_j$), local motion statistics are computed. A polarity-weighted average displacement vector ($v_j$) is determined, representing the dominant local motion flow. Its magnitude ($\nu_j$) is used as the estimated motion speed, and its angle ($\phi_j$) as the motion direction.

2. **Event-Modulated Gaussian Kernel:**
 The derived motion statistics ($\phi_j$, $\nu_j$) drive a dual-modulation mechanism that adapts how each 3D Gaussian is rasterized. This approach separates the true 3D geometry from motion blur effects using a two-component covariance design, preventing blur modeling from corrupting the underlying static 3D map.

 - **Spatial Kernel Modulation:** A motion-aligned prior covariance ($\Sigma_{2D_{j,prior}}$) is constructed for each Gaussian's 2D projection. This prior covariance is anisotropic, with its orientation aligned with the motion direction ($\phi_j$) and its elongation (controlled by a velocity-dependent stretch factor $\kappa_j$) proportional to the motion speed ($\nu_j$). This creates an elliptical "brush stroke" pattern characteristic of motion blur. A regularization loss ($L_{shape}$) enforces that the projected 2D covariance of the optimized 3D Gaussian aligns with this motion-aligned prior. To maintain stability, the 3D covariance of each Gaussian ($\Sigma_j$) is decomposed into a base covariance ($\Sigma_{base,j}$) encoding static geometry and a motion-induced deformation ($\Delta\Sigma_j$).
 - **Temporal Kernel Modulation (Adaptive Sampling):** The sampling density for the exposure integral ($N_m$) is adaptively adjusted based on the aggregate motion across all visible Gaussians (average motion magnitude $\bar{\nu}_m$). In scenes with higher average motion, more discrete camera poses are sampled along the intra-exposure trajectory to accurately model the complex blur formation. Conversely, fewer samples are used for slower motion, maintaining computational efficiency. The number of samples is clamped between minimum and maximum bounds.

3. **Blur Aware Tracking Optimization:**
 The tracking front-end estimates the exposure start and end poses ($T_{st}$, $T_{ed}$) for each incoming frame by minimizing a combined objective function:
 $L = L_{photo} + \lambda_{evt}L_{LI}$
 - **Photometric Loss ($L_{photo}$):** A robust error metric (e.g., Charbonnier loss) quantifies the difference between the observed blurry image ($I_{obs}$) and the blurred image ($\bar{C}_m$) synthesized by the physics-based renderer. The rendering process incorporates the event-modulated spatial and temporal kernels.
 - **Event Alignment Loss ($L_{LI}$):** This loss ensures that the camera motion and 3D scene representation are consistent with the observed event stream. It penalizes discrepancies between the observed event polarities and the sign of logarithmic intensity changes rendered at event timestamps, providing geometric constraints, particularly in low-texture areas.

**C. Mapping with Keyframe Management**

The mapping back-end refines the 3D Gaussian map and camera poses over a sliding window of keyframes, ensuring long-term consistency.

1. **Keyframe Selection:** New keyframes are inserted based on criteria such as view overlap with the latest keyframe falling below a threshold or camera translation exceeding a certain multiple of the average scene depth. When the keyframe limit is reached, the keyframe with the highest neighbor overlap is removed to maintain view diversity and computational bounds.
2. **Bundle Adjustment:** A bundle adjustment process is performed over the sliding window, jointly optimizing the 3D Gaussian map (G) and the keyframe poses ({T}). The comprehensive objective function for this optimization combines the photometric loss, event alignment loss, and the image-plane shape prior loss ($L_{shape}$) across all keyframes in the window:
 $L = \sum_{w \in W} (L_{w_{photo}} + \lambda_{evt}L_{w_{LI}}) + \lambda_{shape}L_{shape}$
 The $L_{shape}$ term, as defined in Eq. (6), encourages the projected 2D Gaussian kernels to align and elongate according to detected motion, enforcing a physically meaningful blur explanation while preserving the underlying 3D geometry of the Gaussians.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of MotionGS-SLAM was evaluated through extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets, comparing it against several state-of-the-art SLAM and GS-SLAM methods.

**A. Datasets and Metrics**

- **Datasets:**
 - **EventReplica (Synthetic):** An adaptation of the Replica dataset, generating challenging sequences with significant, physically realistic motion blur by averaging many intermediate frames over long exposures. Poisson-Gaussian noise was added to simulate low-light conditions. Event streams were synthesized using ESIM.
 - **Real Database:** Three indoor/outdoor scenes captured with a Color-DAVIS346 camera under low-light conditions, featuring varying degrees of motion blur alongside temporally aligned event streams.
- **Metrics:** Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) in centimeters for trajectory accuracy. PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS for mapping quality on the synthetic dataset.

**B. Comparison with State-of-the-Art**

- **Synthetic EventReplica Evaluation:** MotionGS-SLAM was compared against ORB-SLAM2, PhotoSLAM, MonoGS, and EGS-SLAM.
 - **Reconstruction Quality (Table I):** MotionGS-SLAM demonstrated superior reconstruction fidelity. It achieved an average PSNR of 28.05 dB, improving upon MonoGS (23.12 dB) and EGS-SLAM (27.62 dB). Similarly, its average SSIM was 0.842 (vs. 0.728 for MonoGS, 0.833 for EGS-SLAM), and its LPIPS was 0.171 (vs. 0.391 for MonoGS, 0.182 for EGS-SLAM). PhotoSLAM and MonoGS exhibited significant degradation with persistent blur.
 - **Throughput (Table I):** MotionGS-SLAM achieved the highest average rendering frame rate of 1189.7 FPS, exceeding EGS-SLAM (1168.3 FPS), MonoGS (1102.1 FPS), and PhotoSLAM (1058.6 FPS).
 - **Tracking Accuracy (Table III):** MotionGS-SLAM attained the lowest ATE across all seven sequences. Its average ATE was 4.44 cm, representing a 51.9% improvement over MonoGS (9.22 cm) and a 14.2% improvement over EGS-SLAM (5.17 cm). ORB-SLAM2 and PhotoSLAM frequently experienced tracking failures due to blurred inputs.
- **Real-World Data Evaluation:**
 - **Tracking Accuracy (Table II):** On challenging low-light handheld scenes, MotionGS-SLAM achieved an average ATE of 3.55 cm, outperforming EGS-SLAM (4.83 cm), PhotoSLAM (16.58 cm), and ORB-SLAM3 (28.46 cm).
 - **Qualitative Results (Fig. 3, Fig. 4):** Visual comparisons showed that image-only baselines like Photo-SLAM and Mono-GS produced blurry reconstructions with significant ghosting and artifacts. While the event-based EGS-SLAM offered improvements, it still presented soft details. MotionGS-SLAM consistently reconstructed sharp, high-fidelity scenes with clear details and accurate geometry.

**C. Ablation Study**

An ablation study on the EventReplica dataset investigated the contribution of each component of MotionGS-SLAM (Table IV, Fig. 5).

- **Baseline (A0 - MonoGS):** A standard GS-SLAM system using only blurry images, performing poorly with an ATE of 12.76 cm on the `room0` sequence.
- **Blur Model Only (A1):** Adding the physics-based exposure integration renderer without event guidance provided only marginal gains (ATE reduced to 11.80 cm on `room0`). This indicated that the blur model alone is insufficient without precise motion cues.
- **Event Association (A2):** Integrating event data with standard event losses significantly improved performance, reducing ATE to 6.10 cm on `room0` (a 52% reduction from baseline). This highlights the critical role of events in disambiguating motion. However, perceptual metrics (e.g., LPIPS of 0.340) suggested that standard event losses did not fully inform the blur rendering process.
- **Spatial Modulation Only (A3):** Adding the spatial kernel modulation (anisotropic deformation) further enhanced reconstruction quality. LPIPS improved from 0.340 (A2) to 0.250 on `room0`, marking the largest perceptual gain across components. This validated the approach of modeling motion streaks geometrically.
- **Temporal Modulation Only (A4):** Incorporating adaptive temporal sampling contributed to photometric accuracy and ATE improvement (ATE 5.10 cm, PSNR 23.10 dB on `room0`).
- **Full Model (A5 - Ours):** Combining both spatial and temporal modulation yielded the best overall performance, with an ATE of 3.98 cm, PSNR of 24.55 dB, and LPIPS of 0.214 on `room0`. This demonstrated the synergistic effect of the dual-modulation strategy.

The ablation study confirmed that event data is essential and that modeling both the spatial manifestation (anisotropic shape) and temporal accumulation (adaptive sampling) of blur is crucial for robust SLAM under severe motion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MotionGS-SLAM introduces a methodological shift in addressing motion blur within Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems. By reformulating motion blur from an ill-posed inverse problem (deblurring) into a well-constrained forward generative modeling problem, the system provides a more robust framework, particularly for scenarios with severe blur. This paradigm shift represents a fundamental contribution to vision-based SLAM.

The significance of this work extends to its capability to overcome a major limitation of existing SLAM systems: their susceptibility to motion blur. By leveraging the high temporal resolution and blur immunity of event cameras, MotionGS-SLAM enables reliable operation in challenging conditions such as aggressive camera motion, dynamic environments, and low-light settings where traditional vision-only systems typically fail or exhibit degraded performance. This enhanced robustness is critical for real-world robotic applications.

A key impact is the achievement of high-fidelity 3D scene reconstruction. Despite receiving severely degraded (blurry) input images, the system consistently produces sharp, detailed, and geometrically consistent 3D Gaussian maps. This is attributed to the novel event-modulated Gaussian kernel with its dual-modulation strategy, which accurately models the physical process of blur formation. The ability to reconstruct accurate and visually rich environments from challenging data has implications for applications requiring precise environmental understanding.

Furthermore, the integration of event data directly into the *online* SLAM front-end for real-time tracking represents an advance over previous event-based 3DGS/NeRF reconstruction methods that often rely on offline processing or pre-computed poses. MotionGS-SLAM demonstrates that event cameras can serve as active guidance signals for the entire SLAM process, contributing to both localization accuracy and map quality simultaneously. The system also maintains high rendering frame rates, indicating its practical applicability for real-time systems.

Potential impacts of MotionGS-SLAM include:

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** Robots equipped with this technology could navigate and interact more reliably in dynamic and unconstrained environments, improving performance in areas like autonomous driving, drone operation, and mobile manipulation.
- **Advanced Augmented Reality (AR) and Digital Twinning:** The generation of sharp, photorealistic 3D maps from challenging input could lead to more stable and immersive AR experiences and more accurate digital representations of physical spaces, even under non-ideal capture conditions.
- **Reduced Hardware Constraints:** By enabling robust SLAM with standard cameras (albeit combined with event cameras), the reliance on specialized hardware for sharp image acquisition (e.g., high-speed shutters, brighter lighting) might be lessened, potentially reducing costs and increasing system flexibility.
- **Foundational Research for Image Degradation:** The physics-based generative modeling approach for motion blur could serve as a template for handling other types of image degradations (e.g., rolling shutter, varying illumination) within differentiable rendering and SLAM pipelines, opening new avenues for future research in robust perception systems.

Overall, MotionGS-SLAM offers a robust solution to a pervasive problem in visual SLAM, leveraging a synergistic combination of sensor modalities and a novel modeling paradigm to achieve significant performance improvements.
