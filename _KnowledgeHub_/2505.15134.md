# The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Entropy Minimization in LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign explores entropy minimization as a standalone, unsupervised objective to enhance Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning. The study demonstrates that minimizing uncertainty in LLM generations, across fine-tuning and inference stages, significantly improves performance on challenging math, coding, and scientific reasoning tasks, often outperforming supervised methods and achieving competitive results with advanced proprietary models while being more computationally efficient.

## Problem

- Adapting large language models (LLMs) for complex reasoning tasks often requires extensive supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), which are data-intensive and computationally expensive.
- Existing unsupervised LLM improvement methods, like self-consistency, often rely on answer extraction, limiting their applicability to tasks like code generation.
- Many current scaling methods for LLMs at inference time incur high computational overhead.

## Method

- ==EM-FT==, an unsupervised fine-tuning method, directly minimizes the empirical ==token-level entropy== of model-generated outputs via gradient descent, making token predictions more deterministic.
- ==EM-RL== frames entropy minimization as a reinforcement learning objective, maximizing negative token-level or sequence-level entropy as a pseudo-reward signal using policy gradients.
- ==EM-INF== applies entropy minimization at inference time by treating output logits as free parameters and optimizing them to minimize the induced probability distribution's entropy before token sampling, without updating model parameters.

## Results

- EM-FT (unsupervised finetuning) improved performance by **8%** on average across math and coding, achieving comparable or better results than strong RL baselines trained on **60K** labeled examples.
- EM-RL (unsupervised RL) improved base model performance by **11%** on average, outperforming output-verified RL baselines on multiple math and coding tasks.
- EM-INF (inference-time logit optimization) consistently improved base model performance by **~3%** on average, was **3x** more computationally efficient than self-consistency, and enabled a 32B model to match or exceed proprietary models like GPT-4o on the SciCode benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing internal uncertainty (entropy) within an LLM's generations can effectively elicit and reinforce latent reasoning abilities acquired during pre-training, without requiring external labeled data.
> - The effectiveness of entropy minimization depends on the base model's inherent capability, as it amplifies existing reasoning strengths but does not create entirely new ones.
> - Confidence from entropy minimization correlates with correctness on objective reasoning tasks but may not be reliable for subjective tasks requiring human value alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen explosive growth, with pre-trained models demonstrating impressive emergent capabilities across various tasks. However, effectively adapting these models to specific downstream tasks, especially for complex reasoning, often requires extensive supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), both of which are data-intensive and computationally expensive. This paper directly addresses this challenge by exploring an alternative, largely unsupervised paradigm: **entropy minimization (EM)**.

Historically, entropy concepts have been used in machine learning in several ways:
- **Entropy Regularized Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Often, entropy is *maximized* in RL objectives (e.g., Soft Actor-Critic) to encourage exploration and prevent the policy from collapsing to a single mode. This work explicitly differentiates itself, stating it aims to *reduce* uncertainty, which is largely unexplored as a standalone objective for LLMs.
- **Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL):** EM has been a cornerstone of SSL for decades (e.g.,), where it encourages models to make confident predictions on unlabeled data, essentially self-training. This paper applies similar principles to the LLM adaptation context, extending it beyond typical classification tasks to complex generation and reasoning.
- **Test-Time Adaptation (TTA):** EM has also been used for unsupervised adaptation of pre-trained classifiers to new data at test time (e.g.,). EM-INF in this paper builds on this idea for LLMs, aiming to adapt *at inference time* without parameter updates.
- **Unsupervised and Few-Shot RL for LLMs:** Recent work has explored self-improvement methods for LLMs, such as self-critique, self-verification, and self-consistency (e.g.,). These methods often rely on various forms of self-supervision or pseudo-rewards derived from model outputs (e.g., majority voting for answers). This paper directly compares against and aims to improve upon these by proposing a more fundamental, universally applicable "reward" signal: negative entropy. It argues that unlike self-consistency, which requires answer extraction (limiting its use for tasks like code generation), EM is task-agnostic.

Agarwal et al. position their work as a systematic exploration of EM as a *standalone* objective for LLM adaptation, both post-pretraining (fine-tuning) and at inference time. This is a significant departure from common practices that heavily rely on labeled data or complex reward modeling. The core hypothesis is that powerful pre-trained LLMs already possess substantial reasoning capabilities, and EM can "elicit" or "reinforce" these inherent capabilities by simply making the model more confident and deterministic in its outputs, without needing external supervision or complex architectural changes. This work challenges the assumption that alignment with human values or external truth signals is always necessary for improving reasoning, suggesting that internal consistency and confidence can be a powerful proxy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate the "unreasonable effectiveness" of entropy minimization (EM) as a standalone objective for improving Large Language Models' (LLMs) performance, particularly on challenging reasoning tasks like math, physics, and coding.

The core motivations stem from several observations and hypotheses:
1. **Leveraging Pre-trained Capabilities:** Modern LLMs are trained on vast amounts of data and exhibit impressive general capabilities. The authors hypothesize that these models are "reasonably capable" even before specific fine-tuning, and that their confidence often correlates with correctness. EM aims to capitalize on these inherent capabilities by encouraging the model to be more confident in its outputs.
2. **Reducing Reliance on Labeled Data:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and traditional Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) require large, high-quality labeled datasets, which are expensive and time-consuming to create. The paper seeks to answer: "Can entropy minimization alone improve their performance? And how far can they go without any labeled data?" The implicit goal is to develop unsupervised or weakly supervised methods that can achieve competitive performance.
3. **Computational Efficiency:** Many inference-time scaling methods (e.g., self-consistency, iterative refinement) require multiple forward passes or complex iterative processes, increasing computational overhead. The authors aim to develop more efficient adaptation strategies.
4. **Task Agnosticism:** Methods like self-consistency often rely on problem-specific output extraction (e.g., numerical answers in math), limiting their applicability. The authors seek a method that can be universally applied across various reasoning tasks, including code generation where direct answer extraction is challenging.

In essence, the paper investigates whether making LLMs more "decisive" and "certain" about their generations, by concentrating probability mass on confident outputs, can unlock latent reasoning abilities acquired during pre-training, all without external ground truth.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose three distinct approaches to entropy minimization, each tailored to different stages of LLM adaptation:

1. **EM-FT: Unsupervised Finetuning by Direct Token-Level Entropy Minimization**
 - **Concept:** This method mirrors supervised fine-tuning (SFT) but operates entirely unsupervised. Instead of a cross-entropy loss against labeled ground truth, it directly minimizes the empirical total *token-level entropy* of outputs sampled from the model itself.
 - **Mechanism:** Given an input prompt, the model generates an output sequence. For each token in the generated sequence, the method calculates the entropy of the model's predictive distribution over the vocabulary. The sum of these token-level entropies across the sequence is then minimized via gradient descent, effectively making the model's token predictions more deterministic.
 - **Objective:** Minimize $\hat{H}_{tok}(\pi_\theta) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} H(\pi_\theta(\cdot|y_i^{<t}))$.
 - **Distinction:** Unlike SFT, EM-FT doesn't use any external labels; it relies solely on the model's self-generated outputs and aims to reduce uncertainty *within* its own generated probability distributions.

2. **EM-RL: Reinforcement Learning with Negative Entropy Rewards**
 - **Concept:** EM-RL frames entropy minimization within a reinforcement learning paradigm, where the only reward signal to be maximized is the *negative* entropy of the generated sequences. This is analogous to the REINFORCE algorithm but with entropy serving as the sole form of "supervision."
 - **Reward Schemes:**
 - **EM-RL-sequence:** Maximizes the negative *trajectory-level entropy*. The reward for a sequence `y` is its log-probability: $r_{traj}(y) = \sum_{t=1}^{|y|} \log\pi_\theta(y_t|y_{<t}) = \log\pi_\theta(y)$. Maximizing this reward encourages the model to assign higher probabilities to the full generated sequences it produces, favoring overall confident trajectories.
 - **EM-RL-token:** Maximizes the negative *token-level entropy*. The reward for a sequence `y` is the negative sum of per-token entropies: $r_{tok}(y) = -\sum_{t=1}^{|y|} H(\pi_\theta(\cdot|y_{<t}))$. Maximizing this reward encourages the model to be more deterministic at each individual generation step.
 - **Optimization:** EM-RL uses policy gradients, often with a KL-regularizer to prevent drastic deviation from the base model and an RLOO-style baseline for variance reduction.

3. **EM-INF: Inference-Time Logit Optimization**
 - **Concept:** This is the most constrained EM approach, applying entropy minimization at the very end of the pipeline, *during inference*, without any parameter updates to the pre-trained LLM.
 - **Mechanism:** For each token generation step, EM-INF treats the model's output logits (before softmax) as "free parameters." It then uses a few steps of gradient descent to optimize these logits *solely to minimize the entropy of the induced probability distribution*. A minimum entropy threshold is applied to prevent over-optimization (which would result in pure greedy decoding). After this logit adjustment, the next token is sampled from the modified, lower-entropy distribution.
 - **Efficiency:** This approach requires only O(n) forward passes of the LLM (where n is sequence length), making it computationally efficient compared to methods requiring multiple full generations (like self-consistency).
 - **Distinction:** Unlike EM-FT and EM-RL which fine-tune the model parameters, EM-INF only modifies the logits *at inference time* and does not require any training data or parameter updates, making it suitable for online adaptation. It's noted that logit optimization can change the *order* of non-top logits, unlike temperature scaling which only proportionally sharpens the distribution.

**Training Setup and Baselines:**
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-Math-7B (for math), Eurus-2-7B-SFT (for coding), and Qwen2.5-7B/32B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (for inference-time and limitations).
- **Training Data:** 35K math prompts from Numina math and 25K coding prompts from Eurus-2 coding split. Notably, this data is *unlabeled* for EM methods.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** A wide array of challenging math (MATH-500, AMC, AIME, Minerva, Olympiad Bench), coding (LeetCode, LiveCodeBench-v2), and scientific reasoning (SciCode, UGPhysics) tasks. Individualistic Value Reasoning (IndVal) is used to explore limitations.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Supervised:** SFT, RLOO (Reinforce-like with output verification), GRPO (Generative Reinforcement with Policy Optimization), all trained on 60K *labeled* examples.
 - **Unsupervised/Inference-time:** Self-consistency (SC-RL as a training baseline, SC as inference-time), Iterative Refinement, Adaptive Temperature Scaling.
 - **Proprietary Models:** GPT-4o, Claude 3 Opus, Gemini 1.5 Pro are used for comparison on SciCode.

The methodological rigor involves a systematic comparison across different adaptation stages (fine-tuning vs. inference), different entropy estimation methods (token-level vs. trajectory-level), and against a strong set of both supervised and unsupervised baselines, using diverse and challenging benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several compelling findings across its three proposed entropy minimization approaches:

**A. EM-FT (Unsupervised Finetuning):**
- **Strong Performance without Labels:** EM-FT significantly improves performance over the base model (by 8% on average across math and coding) without using *any* labeled data.
- **Competitiveness with Supervised Methods:** EM-FT, even with just one sampled trajectory (N=1), achieves comparable or better performance than strong RL baselines like GRPO and RLOO on tasks such as Minerva (math) and LeetCode (coding), which require output verification over multiple sampled trajectories and rely on 60K labeled examples. This highlights its computational efficiency (1.0 x 10^17 FLOPs vs. 13.1 x 10^17 FLOPs for RL baselines).
- **Dependence on Pretraining Distribution:** EM-FT's effectiveness is observed on tasks similar to the pretraining distribution (e.g., Math, LeetCode) but less so on tasks like LiveCodeBench (which has newer, potentially out-of-distribution problems), suggesting that EM leverages existing pretraining priors.

**B. EM-RL (Reinforcement Learning with Negative Entropy Rewards):**
- **Competitive with Labeled RL:** Unsupervised EM-RL methods (EM-RL-sequence and EM-RL-token) improve base model performance by 11% on average, achieving competitive or superior results compared to output-verified GRPO and RLOO on most math and coding tasks (outperforming on AMC, Minerva, and LeetCode by ~4.5% on average).
- **Task Flexibility:** Unlike self-consistency (SC-RL), which requires answer extraction and is inapplicable for code generation, EM-RL can be universally applied.
- **Impact of Reward Design:** Token-level and sequence-level entropy rewards demonstrate the efficacy of using model confidence as a pseudo-reward. EM-RL also showed improvements on LiveCodeBench, where EM-FT did not, suggesting that the RL framework can better enhance learning from unlabeled experience.

**C. EM-INF (Inference-Time Logit Optimization):**
- **General Performance Improvement:** EM-INF consistently improves the performance of base models across math, coding, and physics tasks by ~3% on average, without any parameter updates or training data.
- **Outperforming Frontier Models on SciCode:** EM-INF enables Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct to match or exceed the performance of proprietary models like GPT-4o, Claude 3 Opus, and Gemini 1.5 Pro on the highly challenging SciCode benchmark (improving Qwen's performance on the main problem by 6% and outperforming GPT-4o by 3%).
- **Superior Efficiency:** EM-INF is 3x more computationally efficient than self-consistency and sequential refinement (Figure 1), achieving better performance while requiring significantly less time. This makes it highly practical for compute-bound scenarios and online adaptation.
- **Benefit in High Uncertainty:** EM-INF's ability to change the relative order of non-top logits (unlike adaptive temperature scaling) proves beneficial in high-uncertainty tasks like SciCode, leading to better reasoning chains.

**D. Limitations and Dependencies:**
- **Dependence on Base Model Capability:** EM is most effective when the pre-trained model is *already capable* in the tasks of interest. Experiments with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct showed less substantial improvements (and sometimes even performance degradation) compared to Qwen-2.5 on math reasoning. This suggests that if the base model lacks fundamental reasoning behaviors, EM cannot magically create them, and its confidence signals might be unreliable.
- **Correlation between Confidence and Correctness:** EM's effectiveness hinges on the assumption that model confidence correlates with correctness. On tasks like "Individualistic Value Reasoning" (IndVal), where Qwen-2.5-7B struggled due to biases from pretraining (e.g., favoring coarse values over nuanced individual preferences), EM offered no improvements. This highlights that for tasks requiring alignment with human values or external ethical considerations, confidence alone is not a reliable proxy for quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Entropy Minimization in LLM Reasoning" carry significant implications for the future of LLM development and deployment:

1. **Challenging the SFT/RLHF Paradigm:** The most profound impact is the demonstration that substantial performance gains in LLM reasoning can be achieved *without any labeled data*. This questions the prevailing reliance on expensive supervised fine-tuning and complex reward modeling (e.g., RLHF) for improving reasoning capabilities. It suggests that pre-trained LLMs might be "over-trained" in terms of knowledge and latent abilities, and simple confidence reinforcement can unlock these.

2. **Unlocking Latent Capabilities:** The paper provides strong evidence that many pre-trained LLMs possess previously underappreciated reasoning capabilities. EM acts as a mechanism to "elicit" or "reinforce" these inherent priors, pushing the model to be more decisive and focused in its reasoning paths.

3. **Efficiency and Practicality:** EM-INF stands out as a highly practical and computationally efficient method for inference-time scaling. Its ability to improve performance, even outperforming some frontier proprietary models on complex tasks like SciCode, while being 3x more efficient than self-consistency, makes it appealing for real-world applications where compute resources or real-time adaptation are critical. This could lead to more efficient deployment of LLMs, reducing the operational costs of achieving high performance.

4. **New Baselines for Research:** The authors explicitly call for EM-FT, EM-RL, and EM-INF to be included as standard baselines in the evaluation of future post-pretraining and inference-time scaling algorithms. This is crucial for better attributing performance improvements to actual algorithmic innovations versus the intrinsic capabilities of the underlying base model. By establishing strong unsupervised baselines, researchers can more accurately gauge the true value added by novel supervised or more complex unsupervised methods.

5. **Understanding LLM Behavior:** The limitations section, particularly the observations about the dependence on base model capability and the "confidence-correctness" correlation, offers valuable insights into the failure modes of EM. This fosters a deeper understanding of when internal consistency is a reliable signal for improvement and when external supervision (e.g., for value alignment) remains indispensable. It suggests that while EM can amplify existing strengths, it cannot create capabilities that are entirely absent.

In conclusion, this paper contributes a novel and surprisingly effective set of unsupervised methods for enhancing LLM reasoning. By demonstrating the power of simply minimizing uncertainty, it opens new avenues for research into more efficient and label-free LLM adaptation, pushing the boundaries of what can be achieved with self-supervision.
