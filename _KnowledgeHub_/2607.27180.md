# HumanCLAW: Can Vision-Language Models Act Through a Body?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Meta, Nanyang Technological University, and University of Washington introduce HumanCLAW, a framework and benchmark for evaluating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in physically simulated environments using continuous full-body human motion. The work reveals that state-of-the-art VLMs currently struggle with embodied self-awareness and egocentric spatial understanding, achieving a maximum 16.8% success rate on complex find-navigate-interact tasks.

## Problem

- Evaluating embodied AI agents is challenging because task failures can arise from either high-level decision-making or low-level motor control, making it difficult to isolate the VLM's generalist reasoning capacity.
- Existing embodied AI benchmarks either simplify action execution through abstract primitives or tightly couple VLM decisions with learned control policies, which limits the assessment of physically grounded action intelligence.
- There is a need for an evaluation framework where VLMs can operate a complex body with continuous full-body motion and real physical consequences, while factoring out low-level motor control complexities.

## Method

- The **HumanCLAW** framework uses a closed-loop system where off-the-shelf VLMs process egocentric observations to output parameterized atomic skill commands at a sub-second rate.
- A skill-conditioned motion generator, comprising a ==Diffusion Transformer== and ==ControlNets==, translates VLM commands into continuous, realistic human motion, effectively abstracting away low-level pose generation.
- A ==half-physics== simulation allows the world to follow rigid-body physics while kinematically driving the human body, isolating VLM action decisions from motor control failures like balance loss.

## Results

- No current VLM reliably solves the HumanCLAW-Bench, with the highest performing model, Gemini-3.1, achieving only a **16.8%** success rate for the full 'find-navigate-interact' task.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that removing the skill-specific verifier caused a drastic collapse in navigation and interaction success rates, and that mid-level objectives were essential for successful interaction sequences.
- Error analysis highlighted 'egocentric self-spatial awareness' as the dominant navigation failure mode (nearly **two-thirds** of failures), while 'body awareness' errors accounted for **81%** of interaction failures, such as 'sitting into thin air.'

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art VLMs predominantly lack embodied self-awareness and egocentric self-localization, struggling to accurately determine their body's position relative to the environment and anticipate action outcomes.
> - Reasoning components within the VLM harness, such as a skill-specific verifier, short textual memory, and mid-level objectives, are crucial for enhancing action intelligence and enabling compositional task execution.
> - While perception of targets is often adequate once in view, the primary bottlenecks for VLMs in embodied tasks lie in precise navigation (e.g., stopping accurately) and interaction (e.g., aligning for sitting).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a challenge within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically the evaluation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) when tasked with acting through a physical body. The broader research landscape for embodied AI involves developing agents that can perceive, reason, and act in physical or simulated environments.

Existing approaches to embodied AI often fall into two categories:
1. **Symbolic or Abstracted Action Agents**: Benchmarks like VirtualHome, ALFRED, and VisualAgentBench simplify action execution by abstracting motion through simulator-defined action primitives, scripts, or pre-animated routines. In these settings, VLMs do not grapple with the physical consequences of full-body motion, which limits the scope of evaluating true "action intelligence" in a physically grounded sense.
2. **End-to-End Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Policies**: Models such as RT-2, Octo, and MolmoAct learn to generate executable motion directly from embodied data. While these systems achieve physical grounding, they often entangle the VLM's decision-making with a learned control policy, making it difficult to isolate the generalist reasoning capacity of the VLM from the embodiment-specific motor control. Furthermore, for complex embodiments like humanoids, low-level motor execution challenges (e.g., maintaining balance) can confound the evaluation of high-level decision-making.

The paper identifies a gap between these two extremes: a need for an evaluation framework where the agent's decision-making is decoupled from low-level execution, yet the body still acts freely with real physical consequences. Previous work on spatial intelligence in VLMs typically focuses on open-loop question answering over images or videos, not on closed-loop decision-making in an interactive physical environment where the model's own body state is part of the problem.

HumanCLAW aims to bridge this gap by introducing a framework that allows off-the-shelf VLMs to operate in a physically simulated world using continuous full-body motion, while factoring out low-level motor control complexities. This aligns with a philosophical commitment that generalizable action intelligence should arise from reasoning rather than extensive, embodiment-specific action data fitting. The framework integrates advancements in controllable human motion generation (e.g., Diffusion Transformers and ControlNets) to provide a reliable, semantically aligned interface for VLM-issued skills.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether contemporary Vision-Language Models (VLMs) possess "action intelligence" when operating through a physical body. Action intelligence is defined as the capacity to decide, moment by moment, what a physical body should execute next within a closed-loop system, where each decision leads to physical outcomes that inform subsequent decisions. This capacity is considered the operational component of spatial intelligence, translating spatial understanding into embodied decisions through action selection, parameterization, and sequencing as physical events unfold.

The motivation for this investigation stems from several challenges inherent in evaluating VLM agency in physical environments:

1. **Decoupling Decision-Making from Motor Control**: A primary challenge in evaluating embodied agents is that task failure can stem from various sources. If an agent fails a task, it is difficult to ascertain whether the VLM made an incorrect high-level decision (e.g., choosing the wrong path) or if the low-level motor controller failed to execute the chosen action effectively (e.g., losing balance during a walk). The paper argues that to measure a VLM's "action intelligence"—its capacity for choosing appropriate actions—these two aspects must be separated. The goal is to isolate the VLM's moment-to-moment choice-making ability from the complexities of motor execution.

2. **Bridging the Text-to-Motion Gap for Complex Embodiments**: For complex bodies like humanoids, generating full-body motion directly from VLM text outputs at a high frequency (e.g., hundreds of pose dimensions changing many times per second) is computationally and representationally prohibitive. Existing benchmarks either abstract away this motion entirely (e.g., using symbolic commands) or rely on end-to-end learning that tightly couples decisions with embodiment-specific control policies. The motivation is to create a physically grounded interface where VLM decisions translate into realistic, continuous full-body motion without requiring the VLM to generate low-level pose data, thereby enabling physically relevant outcomes.

3. **Evaluating Long-Horizon Abstract Tasks**: The research aims to assess VLM performance on abstract, long-horizon tasks (e.g., "find a sofa and sit on it") that require a sequence of spatially aware, physically grounded decisions rather than a single command or a pre-programmed script. This probes the VLM's ability to maintain task coherence, adapt to environmental feedback, and compose atomic skills over time.

4. **Identifying Bottlenecks in Embodied Reasoning**: By carefully decoupling and evaluating, the study seeks to pinpoint the specific limitations of current state-of-the-art VLMs in embodied settings. The motivation is to move beyond general performance metrics and diagnose whether failures originate from perception, high-level planning, spatial reasoning, or body awareness.

In summary, the key objectives are to build an evaluation framework (HumanCLAW) that allows VLMs to act freely in a physical world with decoupled decision-making and continuous full-body motion, and then to use this framework to develop a benchmark (HumanCLAW-Bench) to rigorously test and identify the strengths and weaknesses of current VLMs in embodied action intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HumanCLAW framework and HumanCLAW-Bench are designed to enable and evaluate VLM action intelligence in a physically simulated environment while decoupling high-level decisions from low-level motor control.

#### 3.1. HumanCLAW Framework

The framework operates in a closed-loop fashion at a sub-second rate (0.5s per step). At each timestep, a harnessed, off-the-shelf VLM processes an egocentric RGB observation ($o_t$) and a textual history ($h_t$) to output a parameterized atomic skill command ($\langle s_t, c_t \rangle$). This command is then translated into a 0.5-second chunk of continuous full-body motion by a skill-conditioned motion generator. Finally, a locomotion-decoupled physical simulator executes this motion, updates the world state, and provides the next observation ($o_{t+1}$), closing the loop.

**A. Egocentric Action Decision (VLM Harness)**
The VLM is guided through a contextual reasoning harness to infer low-level motion skills.
- **Contextual Prompting**: The VLM receives a detailed prompt specifying its role (e.g., "blue humanoid robot"), the high-level instruction (e.g., "find a sofa and sit on it"), a list of available atomic motion skills, and rules for their usage. A compact textual history of previous decisions, visual states, and reasoning traces is provided to ensure action coherence.
- **Ego-Perception and Spatial Understanding**: Before selecting a skill, the VLM is required to output a structured textual description of the current egocentric observation, detailing visible objects, their approximate distances, target visibility, and presence of obstacles or affordances. This step externalizes the VLM's spatial interpretation.
- **High-to-Mid-to-Low Reasoning**: The VLM follows a structured reasoning process:
 1. Interprets the long-horizon instruction based on the current visual state (e.g., search, approach, align, interact).
 2. Derives a mid-level objective (e.g., move toward an object) that can persist over several seconds.
 3. Converts this mid-level objective into a low-level motion skill for the current 0.5-second step.
 The VLM dynamically decides whether to inherit or revise the previous mid-level objective, ensuring both hierarchical planning stability and reactive behavior.
- **Low-Level Motion Skill Choice**: The VLM selects a skill (e.g., `walk`, `turn`, `sit`) and predicts its continuous parameters (e.g., displacement, angle, height) in a structured JSON format.
- **Skill-Specific Spatial Response Verifier**: Before execution, a verifier checks the proposed skill for potential unintended consequences (e.g., walking into an obstacle, stopping prematurely). This verifier uses compact, skill-specific prompts to assess safety and appropriateness. If a check fails, the proposal is rejected, and a corrected skill from the same action pool is used.

**B. Skill-Conditioned Motion Generation**
This module translates VLM-issued skill commands into continuous, realistic human motion.
- **Atomic Skills with Deterministic Text Interface**: A predefined set of semantically unambiguous motion skills is used: `walk(x, z, ψ)`, `side_step(x)`, `step_back(z)`, `turn_in_place(θ)`, `climb_upstairs(h, d)`, `walk_downstairs(h, d)`, `sit_in_place(h)`, `stop`. The VLM composes these primitives, while the generator realizes them.
- **Motion Base DiT**: A skill-agnostic Diffusion Transformer (DiT) serves as a general prior for natural human motion. It is trained for receding-horizon continuation, predicting 15 future frames (0.5s) at 30 fps from 5 past frames. The model uses a "history-as-token" conditioning approach, concatenating past and noisy future states directly as tokens, allowing direct attention to recent poses for kinematic continuity. It is trained on AMASS motions, with segments calibrated into a chunk-local frame to model local human dynamics.
- **Plug-and-Play Skills as ControlNet**: Semantic control for each atomic skill is added via a ControlNet-style adapter. The base DiT is frozen, and each skill gets its own trainable control branch. Skill parameters (e.g., distance for walking, height for sitting) are extracted from motion geometry in curated AMASS clips, used to train skill-specific encoders. This modular design allows new skills to be added without retraining the entire system and ensures smooth transitions between skills due to the shared motion backbone.

**C. Locomotion-Decoupled Physical Simulation (Half-Physics)**
To isolate VLM action intelligence from motor control failures, HumanCLAW employs a "half-physics" simulation.
- The world follows rigid-body physics, including collision, friction, gravity, and interaction with articulated and movable objects.
- The human body, however, is kinematically driven by velocities derived from the generated motion sequence rather than simulated joint torques.
- This approach ensures that the agent physically interacts with the environment (e.g., blocked by walls, displaces objects), but failures are directly attributable to the VLM's action decisions (e.g., walking into a wall) rather than issues like balance loss or imperfect motor tracking. The environment is built on AI Habitat with the Bullet physics engine.

#### 3.2. HumanCLAW-Bench

This benchmark assesses long-horizon action intelligence through a "find-navigate-interact" task.
- **Task Design**: The agent receives an instruction "find a <obj>, navigate to it with zero distance, and finally sit on it." The task is progressive: find, then navigate (reach within 20cm), then interact (sit on it, making pelvis contact). Interaction is limited to "sit" on objects like sofas, beds, or toilets.
- **3D Scene Setup**: 1,218 episodes across 41 diverse indoor houses from the HSSD dataset. Six target categories are included (chair, bed, couch, potted\_plant, toilet, tv). Plausibly movable objects are dynamic, while large fixtures are static.
- **Difficulty Analysis**: Each episode is scored based on three geometric dimensions: geodesic distance to target, navigational choice (number of turns and rooms traversed), and obstacle density. Episodes are categorized as easy, medium, or hard.
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **Progressive Success Rate**:
 - `FindSR`: Target visible (≥100 pixels in ego view) AND acknowledged by VLM. (`GeoFindSR` for objective visibility only).
 - `NavSR`: Minimum distance to target AABB ≤ 20cm AND VLM actively stops. (`GeoNavSR` for objective distance only).
 - `InteractSR`: Agent sits, pelvis contacts target, AND VLM actively stops. (`GeoInteractSR` for objective pelvis contact at any point).
 - **Action Quality**: `Motion Jerk` (third-order derivative of pelvis trajectory), indicating motion coherence (lower is smoother).
 - **Body Awareness and Physical Disturbance**: `Collisions` (fraction of steps with non-ground collision), `#Dtb` (number of disturbed objects), `dDtb` (average displacement distance of disturbed objects).
 - **Cost**: Average input and output tokens consumed per episode.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation involved nine state-of-the-art VLMs on HumanCLAW-Bench, yielding several key findings.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Motion Fidelity

The skill-conditioned motion generator, composed of a skill-agnostic Motion Base DiT and per-skill ControlNet adapters, demonstrated high functional fidelity. It accurately realized commanded magnitudes for skills like walking, turning, side-stepping, stepping back, and sitting. For instance, walking tracked commanded step distance at an achievement ratio of 0.97, and turning at 0.99, with small standard deviations (e.g., 0.022 for walk). In contrast, a text-to-motion baseline (MoMask) prompted with identical natural language commands yielded generic motions, largely ignoring requested magnitudes and showing significantly higher variance (e.g., 1.92 achievement ratio for walk with 1.03 std dev). This confirmed that the motor layer is reliable and that failures observed in the benchmark are attributable to VLM decision-making, not low-level execution.

#### 4.2. Benchmark Performance

**Finding 1: No current VLM reliably solves the benchmark.**
- The highest performing model, Gemini-3.1, achieved only a 16.8% success rate for the full "find-navigate-interact" task. Four of the nine tested models achieved 0.2% or less for interaction.
- A significant drop-off occurred between stages: FindSR ranged from 32.6% to 64.9%, NavSR from 0.8% to 42.4%, and InteractSR from 0% to 16.8%. This indicated that even if a VLM could find a target, it struggled to navigate to it, and further struggled to interact after reaching it.
- The "landing rate" (NavSR/FindSR) for Gemini-3.1 was 0.65, meaning it reached only 65% of what it found. For other models, this rate was substantially lower.
- There was no direct correlation between high token consumption and success; Claude-4.8 used the most tokens but performed poorly, while Gemini-3.1 achieved the best results with lower cost.
- Action quality (Motion Jerk) also varied, with GPT-5.5 showing the most coherent motion (4.2 jerk score) and Gemini-2.5 the least (8.7 jerk score), indicating a decoupling between task success and motion smoothness.
- Gemma-4-31B was the strongest open-source model, performing comparably to or better than several proprietary models.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on a 100-episode mini-validation set with Gemma-4-31B provided insights into the harness components.
**Finding 2: Reasoning scaffolds—memory, mid-level objectives, and verification—enhance action intelligence; longer text or visual history provides limited benefit.**
- **Verifier's Critical Role**: Removing the skill-specific verifier caused a drastic collapse in NavSR (from 27.0% to 2.0%) and InteractSR (from 18.9% to 0.0%), while FindSR remained relatively stable. This indicated that the verifier was essential for closing the control loop and committing to successful action sequences.
- **Text History Saturation**: While a short textual history was necessary (NavSR and InteractSR dropped to 11.0% and 0.0% without it), increasing history length beyond 10 steps did not significantly improve performance but increased computational cost.
- **Visual History Degradation**: Adding a few visual history frames was neutral, but too many (e.g., 10 frames) degraded performance significantly, suggesting that flooding the VLM with past visual context could dilute the signal for current decision-making.
- **Mid-Level Reasoning for Interaction**: Removing the mid-level objective component maintained FindSR and NavSR at competitive levels but caused InteractSR to collapse from 18.9% to 0.0%. This indicated that mid-level objectives were crucial for sustaining short compositional routines required for interaction, such as aligning and sitting.

#### 4.4. Error Analysis and Key Findings

Detailed error analysis of failed episodes across all nine VLMs highlighted specific failure modes.
**Finding 3: Perception is important but not the primary bottleneck.**
- The difference between `GeoFindSR` (target objectively visible) and `FindSR` (target visible and acknowledged by the model) was small (5-10 percentage points for strong models). This suggested that once a target was in the egocentric view, it was almost always recognized.
- The dominant "Find" failures (38%) were attributed to "ineffective exploration" (agent searching incoherently), followed by target being in view but not seen (23%). This implies that bringing the target into view, rather than recognition itself, was the main challenge at this stage.

**Finding 4: Egocentric self-localization is the navigation bottleneck.**
- Even after finding a target, 68% of episodes failed to navigate to it (reach within 0.2m and stop).
- Nearly two-thirds of navigation failures were due to "egocentric self-spatial awareness" issues:
 - **Body awareness (34%)**: Agent physically at or against the target but misjudging its own state (e.g., continuing to navigate after arrival, or being jammed against an obstacle but continuing to issue forward commands).
 - **Ego-spatial distance hallucination (30%)**: Agent stopping and declaring arrival while still far from the target.
- Other failures (mid-level reasoning, perception lapses, ineffective actions) were less frequent. The core weakness was determining the body's relative position to the target and recognizing arrival.

**Finding 5: Reaching the target is not equivalent to interacting with it.**
- After successfully navigating to the target seat (within 0.2m), 71% of episodes still failed to sit.
- Overwhelmingly, these failures (81%) were "body awareness" errors:
 - **Sitting into thin air (58%)**: Agent issued a `sit` command but its pelvis never made contact with any surface.
 - **Sitting on the wrong object (14%)**: Pelvis landed on a different object or the floor.
 - **Standing after sitting (9%)**: Agent briefly made contact but then moved off the seat before stopping.
- Only 19% were decision failures (agent reached but never issued a `sit`). This highlighted that successful interaction requires precise body placement and anticipating the spatial outcome of actions, a finer-grained manifestation of the egocentric spatial deficit.

**Finding 6: No current VLM perceives its own body.**
- Collision analysis (Table 5) showed that collisions concentrated in body parts not typically in the egocentric view (legs/feet at 28-45% of steps, arms/hands at 20-35%), while the head (often in view) collided less than 7% of the time.
- Examples showed agents bumping into furniture or jamming legs against objects despite them being visible in the egocentric view, without the agent registering the contact or responding appropriately. This suggested a lack of an inherent body model or proprioceptive awareness. VLMs seem to process external visual information but struggle to integrate it with an understanding of their own body's state and interaction with the environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HumanCLAW framework and HumanCLAW-Bench represent a contribution to the field of embodied AI by providing a controlled, physically grounded, and detailed evaluation platform for Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

**Novel Evaluation Paradigm**: The research introduces a method to decouple high-level action decision-making from low-level motor execution, a critical distinction for assessing the "action intelligence" of general-purpose models. This decoupling, combined with continuous full-body motion and half-physics simulation, offers a more realistic yet analytically tractable environment compared to purely symbolic or fully dynamic, torque-controlled simulations. It allows failures to be attributed directly to the VLM's reasoning capabilities, rather than being confounded by motor control challenges.

**Comprehensive Benchmark**: HumanCLAW-Bench provides a challenging, long-horizon "find-navigate-interact" task across diverse indoor environments. The progressive nature of the task and the detailed metrics (success rates, action quality, body awareness, computational cost) offer a multifaceted assessment. The difficulty stratification based on geometric factors further enables targeted analysis of VLM performance under varying complexities.

**Identification of Key Bottlenecks**: The study's main findings highlight "embodied self-awareness" and "egocentric self-localization" as critical missing capacities in current state-of-the-art VLMs, rather than fundamental perception or low-level motor competence. This diagnosis—that models struggle with knowing where their body is, its relation to the environment, and the consequences of its actions—provides a clear direction for future research. It suggests that merely improving visual recognition or increasing context windows may not be sufficient to achieve robust embodied agency.

**Implications for VLM Development**: The results imply a need for VLMs to develop:
- **Persistent Spatial Memory**: To maintain an understanding of the environment and the body's place within it over time.
- **Calibrated Termination Mechanisms**: To accurately assess when a subtask (e.g., navigation, interaction) is completed.
- **Internal Body Models**: To infer the body's state, predict the spatial consequences of actions, and detect physical interactions (e.g., collisions) without explicit proprioceptive sensors.

The philosophical stance that generalizable action intelligence arises from reasoning, with the motor layer as a fixed prior, positions this work to influence how future embodied agents are conceptualized and built. It suggests that progress in foundation models' reasoning capabilities could directly transfer to embodied tasks without extensive, embodiment-specific retraining.

**Future Research Trajectories**: The paper outlines several avenues for future work, including expanding the interaction vocabulary, exploring finer granularity of atomic skills, bridging the gap to physical humanoids, integrating tactile or proprioceptive feedback, and investigating targeted VLM training for body awareness. HumanCLAW-Bench is presented as a testbed for agents that can not only perceive and plan but also act intelligently through a body.
