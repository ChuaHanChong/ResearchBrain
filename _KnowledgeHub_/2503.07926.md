# Learning Gentle Grasping Using Vision, Sound, and Touch

> [!summary] Summary
> A robotic system was developed to perform gentle grasping of fragile objects by integrating vision, touch, and auditory sensing in an end-to-end learning framework. The approach achieved a 79% success rate for stable and gentle grasps in real-world scenarios, outperforming vision-only methods by 17 percentage points and chance by 44 percentage points.

## Problem

- Robots face a significant challenge in gently grasping fragile or deformable objects without causing damage while maintaining stability.
- Achieving gentle grasping requires balancing sufficient force for stability with minimal force to prevent object damage, a trade-off difficult to manage with traditional methods.
- Defining and automatically labeling "gentleness" for training robotic learning algorithms has historically been a complex and labor-intensive hurdle.

## Method

- An end-to-end action-conditional learning framework integrates raw data from vision (RGB-D camera), touch (DIGIT sensors), and sound (microphone) to predict grasp stability and gentleness.
- A self-supervised data collection process automatically labels "gentleness" based on sound thresholds, where excessive noise indicates potential damage, and "stability" through object presence detection after lifting.
- The robot optimizes regrasp actions by maximizing predicted stability while adhering to a learned gentleness probability constraint, using a multi-fingered Allegro Hand.

## Results

- The multi-modal model (Vision + Touch + Action) achieved 88.24% predictive accuracy for concurrently classifying both grasp stability and gentleness outcomes.
- In real-world grasping tests, the multi-modal approach secured a 79% rate of stable and gentle grasps, surpassing vision-only methods by 17 percentage points and chance grasping by 44 percentage points.
- DenseNet121 demonstrated the highest performance among tested CNN architectures for processing vision and tactile inputs within the multi-modal framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-conditional modeling is essential for predicting grasp outcomes, with action inputs proving particularly crucial for determining grasp gentleness.
> - The model effectively learns the inherent trade-off between grasp stability and gentleness, demonstrating that higher predicted grasping forces correlate with increased stability but decreased gentleness.
> - Auditory cues, including object-specific sounds and environmental noises like actuator strains or collisions, serve as effective, automatically detectable indicators for excessive force and potential non-gentle interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic grasping has seen remarkable progress, evolving from classical model-based approaches, which relied heavily on geometric analysis, to contemporary model-free and end-to-end learning paradigms. However, a persistent and industrially relevant challenge remains: **gentle grasping** of fragile or deformable objects, such as fruits, without causing damage. This paper directly addresses this critical gap.

Historically, much of robotic grasping research, particularly in machine learning, has focused on vision-only approaches due to the ease of data acquisition and the advancements in image-based algorithms. While these methods have achieved fair performance, they often fall short in tasks requiring dynamic interaction with objects, where external cameras cannot fully capture subtle contact dynamics or applied forces. This limitation has driven a growing recognition of the crucial role of **tactile sensing**, mirroring its importance in human dexterity. The advent of vision-based tactile sensors like GelSight and DIGIT has been a game-changer, as they provide high-resolution "tactile images" that can be seamlessly integrated with visual data using powerful convolutional neural networks (CNNs), thus facilitating multi-modal learning.

Building upon this trend, prior works, including some by the lead author, have successfully demonstrated that combining vision and touch significantly improves grasping performance, even for adjusting grasping forces. However, many of these studies have either utilized simpler parallel-jaw grippers or tested on rigid, non-deformable objects, limiting their applicability to truly fragile items. Furthermore, defining and automatically labeling "gentleness" for training purposes has been a considerable hurdle, often requiring complex force modeling or manual tuning. Some approaches focused on hardware development (e.g., soft robotic hands) or analyzed force through marker displacements, which often entails system-specific control theories and higher engineering costs.

This paper positions itself at the forefront of this landscape by introducing an **unprecedented integration of auditory sensing** alongside vision and touch for gentle grasping. While sound is often overlooked in robotics compared to vision and touch, humans heavily rely on auditory cues for motor learning and dynamic environmental interactions. By using sound as a direct, automatically measurable indicator of excessive force or potential damage (e.g., a "crunching" sound), the authors propose an elegant solution to the challenge of defining and labeling gentleness. This approach significantly reduces the engineering effort associated with manual calibration or analytical force modeling.

Moreover, the work moves beyond simpler grippers by employing a **multi-fingered Allegro Hand**, enabling more complex, flexible, and human-like grasping policies. It extends previous action-conditional models (e.g., Calandra et al.) by explicitly incorporating the gentleness prediction and solving a constrained optimization problem. The self-supervised data collection methodology further aligns with contemporary trends in robotics, aiming to minimize manual annotation and facilitate large-scale data acquisition in real-world settings. In summary, this research advances multi-modal robotic manipulation by demonstrating the synergistic power of vision, touch, *and* sound in tackling one of robotics' most delicate and challenging tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable robotic manipulators to perform **gentle grasping** of fragile objects stably and reliably. This task, termed "gentle grasping," requires robots to apply not the maximum possible force for stability, but rather the *minimum necessary force* to secure the object without causing damage.

The key motivations driving this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing a Critical Gap in Robotic Capabilities:** While robots excel at precise and forceful manipulation, handling delicate objects like fruits or sensitive industrial components without damage remains a significant challenge. This limitation restricts robotic deployment in numerous high-demand applications, particularly in agri-food, logistics, and healthcare, where excessive force can lead to significant economic loss or compromise.
- **Balancing Stability and Gentleness:** The core dilemma in gentle grasping is the inherent trade-off between applying sufficient force to ensure a stable grasp (preventing slippage or dropping) and applying minimal force to prevent damage (ensuring gentleness). The research aims to develop a system that can effectively navigate this trade-off.
- **Leveraging Multi-Modal Perception:**
 - **Limitations of Vision-Only:** Relying solely on external cameras for grasping fragile objects is prone to errors due to limited understanding of internal object state or subtle contact dynamics.
 - **Importance of Touch:** Human manipulation heavily relies on tactile feedback for delicate tasks. Integrating touch sensing, especially high-resolution vision-based tactile sensors, is therefore a natural and necessary step to capture crucial contact information.
 - **Novelty of Sound:** The paper identifies auditory signals as a crucial, yet often overlooked, modality. For many fragile objects, sound naturally indicates excessive force (e.g., a crunch, a crack, or even actuator sounds under strain). Using sound offers a direct and automatic mechanism to define and measure "gentleness," which is otherwise difficult to quantify and label for learning algorithms.
- **Developing an End-to-End Learning Framework:** The authors are motivated to move away from traditional, labor-intensive approaches that require analytical force modeling or meticulous sensor calibration. Instead, they propose an end-to-end learning control framework that can directly learn complex policies from raw multi-modal sensor inputs, significantly reducing engineering effort and enhancing adaptability to new objects or environments.
- **Enabling Flexible Manipulation with Multi-Fingered Hands:** Unlike many previous studies that utilized simpler parallel-jaw grippers, this work aims to implement gentle grasping with a multi-fingered hand. This choice is motivated by the desire to achieve more flexible, dexterous, and human-like grasping policies that can adapt to a wider variety of object shapes and manipulation requirements.

In essence, the paper seeks to push the boundaries of robotic manipulation by developing a robust, adaptive, and self-supervised learning system that intelligently integrates vision, touch, and sound to master the intricate art of gentle grasping.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose an **end-to-end action-conditional learning control framework** for gentle grasping, modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). This framework leverages raw sensory inputs from vision, touch, and sound to predict both the stability and gentleness of future grasping actions.

#### 3.1. Hardware Setup

The experimental setup operates entirely in the real world due to the challenges of simulating sound. It comprises:
- **Robotic Hand:** Allegro Hand (ver. 4.0), a four-fingered hand with 16-Degrees of Freedom (DoF). For simplicity in this work, only five joints on the thumb and middle finger are activated, reducing the problem to approximate two-dimensional kinematics.
- **Manipulators:** Two 7-DoF xArm7 manipulators. One holds the Allegro Hand, the other fixes the external camera.
- **Vision Sensor:** RealSense D435i RGB-D camera, providing RGB images (640x480 resolution) at 30 Hz.
- **Tactile Sensors:** DIGIT sensors are mounted on each fingertip, capturing high-resolution RGB tactile images (240x320 resolution) at 60 Hz.
- **Auditory Sensor:** A standard commodity microphone attached to the Allegro Hand, recording audio data at 44.1 kHz.
- **Target Object:** A deformable toy specifically chosen for its clear acoustic response (sound emission) when subjected to excessive grasping force.

#### 3.2. Action-Conditional Multi-Modal Model

The core of the methodology is a deep neural network `f(s_t, a_t)` that predicts two probabilities: `f_s ∈` for grasp stability and `f_g ∈` for grasp gentleness, given the current sensory state `s_t` and a proposed future action `a_t`.
- **Input Processing:**
 - **Visuo-Tactile Images:** Raw RGB images from the external camera and tactile images from two DIGIT sensors are resized to 256x256 and then randomly cropped to 224x224 for data augmentation. Background subtraction is applied to tactile images to highlight deformation. These images are processed by independent Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs).
 - **Action `a_t`:** This input specifies the relative end-effector motion (`∆x, ∆y, ∆z, ∆ψ`) and the absolute joint angles of the multi-fingered hand (`j_1..., j_16`). These are processed by Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs).
- **Network Design:**
 - The authors primarily use **DenseNet121** as the CNN backbone for vision and tactile streams, fine-tuning its final layer. They also explore EfficientNetV2 B0 and ResNet50 for comparison.
 - The outputs from the five branches (RGB, two tactile images, and two action networks) are concatenated and passed through a two-layer fully-connected network.
 - The final output layer uses sigmoid functions to produce the binary grasp success and gentleness probabilities.
- **Training:**
 - The model is trained end-to-end on a dataset of `(s_i, a_i, o_i)` tuples, where `o_i` represents the binary outcome labels for stability and gentleness.
 - Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss is minimized.
 - Pre-trained ImageNet weights are used for the CNNs, and an exponential learning rate schedule is employed.

#### 3.3. Data Collection (Self-Supervised)

A self-supervised automatic data collection process generates a dataset of 1,500 grasping samples (expanded to 9,000 via augmentation and using two moments of observation) over 25 hours. This process involves four phases:
- **Before Grasping:** Object detection (x, y position) via RealSense depth data. End-effector positioned above the object with open hand.
- **Initial Grasping:** End-effector moves to the detected object position with small perturbations, hand closes to apply force, and sensory data (RGB, depth, tactile images) are recorded.
- **Regrasping:** A random regrasp action `a_1` (relative end-effector motion and absolute joint angles) is sampled and executed. Sound is recorded for 3 seconds.
 - **Gentleness Labeling:** If the recorded sound exceeds a pre-set manual threshold, the grasp is labeled as non-gentle (0); otherwise, it's gentle (1). Crucially, even actuator or collision sounds can trigger the non-gentle label.
- **Lifting:** The robot attempts to lift and hold the object.
 - **Stability Labeling:** If the object remains in the fingers for 4 seconds, it's labeled stable (1); otherwise, unstable (0). A logistic regression model, initially trained on supervised data and fine-tuned during data collection, uses fingertip displacements (derived from proprioception) to automate this labeling.
- **Reset:** The robot resets for the next trial, placing the object randomly if the previous grasp was successful.

#### 3.4. Regrasp Optimization

Once trained, the model `f` is used to select the optimal regrasp action `a*`. Given the current state `s_t`, the robot samples `N` action candidates `A = {a^(j)_t}`. For each candidate, `f_s` and `f_g` are predicted. The optimal action `a*` is chosen by solving a constrained optimization problem:
`a* = arg max_a∈A f_s(s_t, a)` subject to `f_g(s_t, a) > T_gentle`
where `T_gentle` is a pre-defined threshold for gentleness probability (e.g., 0.95). If the selected `a*` also yields `f_s(s_t, a*) > T_success` (another threshold, e.g., 0.95), the robot lifts the object. Otherwise, it performs an unconstrained optimization for stability only to determine the next end-effector motion for a subsequent grasp attempt.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence for the efficacy of their multi-modal, action-conditional learning framework for gentle grasping.

#### 4.1. Model Evaluation and Predictive Performance

- **Multi-modal Superiority:** The model integrating **Vision + Touch + Action** inputs demonstrated the highest predictive accuracy (88.24%) for concurrently classifying both grasp stability and gentleness outcomes (4-class classification) in a 5-fold cross-validation over 9,000 samples.
- **Contribution of Modalities:**
 - **Action Inputs:** Models incorporating action inputs significantly outperformed the unconditional "Vision + Touch (no action)" variant, confirming the importance of action-conditional modeling. Action inputs were particularly crucial for predicting grasp gentleness, as hand pose directly influences grasping force.
 - **Tactile Inputs:** Removing tactile networks primarily degraded stability prediction, indicating that touch provides vital information about object contact and stability that vision alone cannot capture.
 - All proposed models substantially outperformed a majority class baseline.
- **CNN Backbone Performance:** Among the tested CNN architectures, **DenseNet121** yielded the best predictive accuracies (88.24% for both stability and gentleness predictions), followed by EfficientNetV2 B0 (83.01%) and ResNet50 (79.70%). The authors attribute DenseNet's performance to its effective feature reuse mechanism.
- **Confusion Matrix Analysis:** The confusion matrix for the best-performing model showed an overall accuracy of 88.83% on validation data. A slightly higher misclassification rate was observed for the "failure, non-gentle" (0,0) cases, attributed to their inherent rarity in the dataset.

#### 4.2. Understanding the Learned Model

- **Grasping Force Representation:** To analyze the model's learned policies, the authors investigated alternative metrics for grasping force in a multi-fingered hand. They concluded that **fingertip position error** (deviation between observed and target fingertip positions) was the most reliable proxy for actual grasping force. This metric effectively distinguished grasp stability labels and, at extreme values, gentleness labels. Fingertip displacement also correlated with gentleness but was less effective for stability. Differential tactile pixel values showed little correlation with force.
- **Trade-off Learning:** Analysis of the relationship between the fingertip position error (force proxy) and the model's predicted probabilities revealed that as the "force" metric increased, the predicted **grasp success probability increased**, while the predicted **gentleness probability decreased**. This demonstrates that the model successfully learned the intuitive trade-off between grasp stability and gentleness, indicating its ability to guide appropriate force selection. Notably, the study found that environmental noise, such as sounds from actuators or collisions under excessive force, also served as important cues for gentleness detection, suggesting potential generalizability beyond objects with inherent "damage sounds."

#### 4.3. Real-World Gentle Grasping Evaluation

- **Superior Performance:** In 100 real-world grasping tests, the multi-modal model (Vision + Touch + Action) significantly outperformed baseline approaches:
 - It achieved a **17% higher rate for stable and gentle grasps** compared to the vision-only model. Specifically, 79% of grasps were both stable and gentle for the multi-modal model, compared to 64% for the vision-only variant (a 15 percentage point difference).
 - It achieved a **44% higher rate for stable and gentle grasps** compared to chance grasping (where 29% were stable and gentle for chance grasping, a 50 percentage point difference from 79%).
- **Individual Metrics:** The multi-modal model also performed better on individual metrics, achieving 92% stable grasps (vs. 89% for vision-only, 73% for chance) and 89% gentle grasps (vs. 79% for vision-only, 56% for chance). These results conclusively validate the effectiveness of integrating vision, touch, and sound for achieving stable and gentle robotic manipulation in real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving fragile objects, and has several important implications and potential impacts:

- **Enabling Dexterous Manipulation of Fragile Objects:** The primary significance lies in its direct contribution to solving the challenging problem of gentle grasping. By successfully demonstrating a robot's ability to grasp delicate objects stably and gently using a multi-fingered hand and multi-modal sensing, the work opens doors for robust robotic deployment in sectors such as:
 - **Agri-Food:** Automated harvesting, sorting, and packaging of fruits and vegetables without bruising or damage.
 - **Healthcare:** Handling delicate medical instruments, assisting in patient care, or packaging sensitive pharmaceuticals.
 - **Logistics and Manufacturing:** Managing fragile components, intricate assemblies, or sensitive electronics.
 - **Assistive Robotics:** Providing more capable and adaptable assistive robots for elderly or disabled individuals, handling everyday objects with care.

- **Pioneering Multi-Modal Integration with Auditory Sensing:** The paper effectively highlights and capitalizes on the often-overlooked utility of auditory signals in robotics. By integrating sound with vision and touch, it establishes a more comprehensive perceptual framework for robots. This demonstrates that sound can serve as a powerful, intuitive, and automatically labelable indicator of physical interaction quality (e.g., gentleness, collision, material properties), inspiring future research into its broader application in robotic perception and control.

- **Reduced Engineering Effort through End-to-End Learning:** A key practical impact is the drastic reduction in engineering effort. The end-to-end learning approach, coupled with self-supervised data collection, bypasses the need for laborious tactile sensor calibration, complex analytical force modeling, and extensive manual labeling. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing and deploying tactile-enabled robotic systems, making them more accessible and scalable for diverse applications.

- **Foundation for More Adaptive and Generalizable Systems:**
 - The framework's ability to learn the trade-off between stability and gentleness from data implies a higher degree of adaptability to variations in object properties or environmental conditions.
 - The observation that even environmental noise (e.g., actuator sounds, collision sounds) can contribute to gentleness detection suggests a potential for generalizing the approach to a wider variety of objects, even those without an inherent "damage sound."
 - The availability of the dataset and videos promotes open science and provides a valuable resource for other researchers to build upon and compare against.

- **Future Research Directions:** The authors explicitly outline limitations and future work, such as developing object-independent stability labeling using tactile images, incorporating more expressive gentleness functions learned from audio spectrograms, and leveraging advanced multi-modal tactile sensors like Digit360. This forward-looking perspective indicates a clear path for further improvements and expands the scope of this foundational work.

In conclusion, this research not only presents a robust solution for a critical problem in robotics but also establishes a strong precedent for the synergistic power of truly multi-modal perception, ushering in an era of more capable, adaptive, and dexterous robotic manipulators.
