# Autonomously Acquiring Robot Manipulation Skills with Language-Driven Quality-Diversity

> [!summary] Summary
> This work parameterizes quality-diversity robot learning entirely from a free-form task description. An LLM samples a success condition, three candidate fitness functions, and a factored mesh of behavior descriptors (timestep selector composed with measured quantity), which a multi-BD MAP-Elites-Success algorithm exploits by maintaining one grid per descriptor. Across four Genesis manipulation tasks it yields far larger, more diverse motion-primitive archives than hand-crafted and naive-inference baselines.

## Problem

- Quality-diversity pipelines demand experts hand-write success conditions, fitness signals and diversity metrics per skill, which blocks autonomous open-ended skill acquisition on new tasks.
- LLM reward-shaping methods learn autonomously but converge to a single high-performing policy, leaving robots brittle to unseen task instances and deployment-time constraints.
- Unsupervised diversity-metric learning removes expert effort but yields representations disconnected from the task, sacrificing explainability and task-relevant behavioral coverage.

## Method

- Casts archive design as ==functional exploration==: the ==behavior-descriptor space== is factored into g∘h, separating what quantity to measure from which timestep to measure it, both sampled without replacement by an LLM.
- Generates three ==candidate fitness functions== (each a mean over per-timestep values) in one inference pass, trains a short run per fitness, and keeps only the archive with the most successes.
- **Multi-BD MAP-Elites-Success** keeps a separate grid per inferred descriptor; individuals are inserted into every grid via ==local competition==, and per-grid selections are concatenated before mutation.

## Results

- On the grasping task the method returns **403.3 ± 218.0** successful individuals versus **144.7** for naive LLM inference and only **5.8** for the expert hand-crafted MES baseline.
- Push and drawer tasks show the largest gaps: **85518** versus **4028** successes on push, and **2720** versus **110** on drawer, with ground-truth BD coverage of **140.6** and **334.4** respectively.
- The plain genetic algorithm collapses in sparse-interaction settings, scoring **0** successes on grasping and **8.6** ground-truth BD coverage on push despite finding **455** individuals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factoring an intractable design space into two low-dimensional sub-spaces makes zero-shot LLM sampling reliable without task-specific prompts, fine-tuning or few-shot examples.
> - Maintaining several heterogeneous diversity streams combines dense driving descriptors that fill archives fast with sparse describing descriptors that preserve genuinely distinct task approaches.
> - Expert-designed diversity metrics can underperform under limited budgets because their sparsity starves early exploration; multi-descriptor archives remove the need for strong hand-coded priors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper integrates two distinct but increasingly relevant areas in robotics: Quality-Diversity (QD) evolutionary algorithms and Large Language Models (LLMs).

Quality-Diversity algorithms, such as MAP-Elites, have demonstrated utility in robot learning by generating diverse libraries of motion primitives. These diverse solutions enable robots to adapt to unforeseen constraints during deployment, for example, by learning various gaits for locomotion or robust grasping motions. However, a notable limitation of traditional QD methods is their reliance on expert designers to define crucial components: the success condition, fitness function, and diversity metrics (behavior descriptors). This manual design process for each new skill diminishes a robot's autonomy and scalability.

Concurrently, Large Language Models have emerged as a significant tool in robotics, primarily due to their capacity to process free-form instructions and generate code. This capability allows LLMs to bridge the semantic gap between human instructions and robot control systems. Recent research has explored using LLMs as reward designers, inferring reward code to enable robots to learn novel skills autonomously. While effective in acquiring skills, these LLM-based reward-shaping techniques typically produce single, high-performing solutions rather than a diverse set of behaviors. This can lead to brittleness when deployed in real-world scenarios, where environmental variability demands adaptability beyond a single optimal trajectory.

This research addresses a gap at the intersection of these two fields. Existing autonomous robot skill acquisition methods often focus on obtaining individual, high-performing solutions, which can limit adaptability. Conversely, while QD methods generate diverse and adaptable motion primitives, their parameterization (success conditions, fitness signals, diversity metrics) still requires substantial expert input, thus constraining autonomy. This paper aims to combine the autonomous skill learning capabilities of LLMs with the diversity-generating strengths of QD algorithms. By enabling LLMs to autonomously design the necessary functional components for QD, the work seeks to develop a robot learning pipeline that can generate diverse, adaptable motion primitive archives solely from free-form natural language task descriptions, thereby enhancing both autonomy and robustness in robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations in current autonomous robot skill acquisition methodologies. Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms are recognized for their ability to generate diverse sets of solutions, known as motion primitive archives, which enhance a robot's adaptability to novel or constrained task instances. However, a significant barrier to the widespread autonomous application of QD algorithms is the requirement for expert-designed success conditions, fitness functions, and behavior descriptors (diversity metrics). The manual, trial-and-error process of crafting these functionals for each new task substantially limits a robot's autonomy.

In parallel, Large Language Models (LLMs) offer a pathway to autonomy by interpreting free-form language instructions and inferring reward functions for robotic tasks. While this approach allows robots to learn skills from high-level descriptions, the output is typically a single high-performing solution. This singular focus on optimality can render the learned skills brittle and less adaptable when faced with variations or unexpected conditions in the real world.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:
- To propose an approach that autonomously generates diverse motion primitive archives by leveraging Quality-Diversity algorithms, requiring only a free-form natural language description of the task as input.
- To overcome the challenge of designing appropriate fitness and diversity metrics for QD algorithms by introducing an autonomous exploration mechanism capable of reliably identifying relevant sets of functionals across the fitness and behavior descriptor (BD) spaces.
- To formalize the problem of parameterizing QD algorithms for robotic skill acquisition as a functional exploration problem, viewing the design of these metrics as a search within lower-dimensional functional spaces.
- To develop an LLM-based sampling technique designed to explore the success condition, fitness, and BD functional spaces without needing task-specific prompts, fine-tuning, few-shot examples, or any expert intervention.
- To introduce a method for effectively utilizing the inferred samples, referred to as a "BD mesh," to generate diverse trajectory data using a multi-BD variant of the MAP-Elites Success (MES) algorithm. This multi-BD approach is specifically adapted to leverage the heterogeneous nature of the LLM-inferred behavior descriptors.
- To empirically validate the proposed exploration technique and the overall method through numerical experiments on a set of robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating its capability to generate diverse motion primitive archives and outperform classical QD algorithms with both expert-written and LLM-inferred metrics, as well as a generic genetic algorithm baseline.
- To contribute design guidelines and identify future research directions for enhancing the autonomous acquisition of robotic skills through QD techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) with Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms to enable autonomous robot skill acquisition from natural language task descriptions. The approach formalizes the generation of diverse robotic motion archives as a functional design problem, where LLMs are employed to autonomously infer the critical components of a QD algorithm.

**Formalization of the Design Problem:** The research frames the motion primitive archive design problem as finding an optimal archive \( A^* \) that maximizes the likelihood of achieving unknown instances of a known task \( t_i \in \mathcal{T} \). This objective emphasizes the need for diverse solutions that provide robustness to varying task requirements. The authors utilize a variant of the MAP-Elites Success (MES) algorithm, which aims to populate an archive with diverse, high-performing individuals based on a success condition, a fitness function, and behavior descriptors.

**LLM Inference for Multi-BD MES (Autonomous Parameterization):**
The core innovation lies in using LLMs to autonomously design the necessary functionals for the MES algorithm:
1. **Success Condition (SC):** Given its critical role and relative ease of design compared to fitness and diversity metrics, a single success condition is inferred by the LLM for a given task. This reduces computational overhead while ensuring a foundational criterion for task completion.
2. **Fitness Signal (f):** The LLM is used to generate multiple candidate fitness functions. The paper assumes fitness can be expressed as the mean of a value computed at each simulation timestep, simplifying the functional space. Three distinct fitness functions are generated in a single LLM inference pass, sampled without replacement to ensure broad coverage of the fitness space. These three functions are then used to initialize three separate multi-BD archives. After a brief training period, the archive exhibiting the highest number of successful individuals is selected, and its corresponding fitness function is retained for the main archive generation process, while the others are discarded.
3. **Behavior Descriptor (BD):** Acknowledging the complexity of inferring optimal BDs zero-shot, the method restricts the search space for BDs. Instead of directly inferring a complete BD, the problem is split into two parts, which are explored sequentially:
 - **Timestep (h):** The LLM first infers a set of four relevant timestamps within the simulation (e.g., first simulation step, first contact, last timestep). These are sampled without replacement.
 - **Measured Quantity (g):** Separately, the LLM infers a set of four simple and meaningful physical quantities (e.g., gripper XYZ position, object XYZ position). These are also sampled without replacement.
 The behavior descriptor (BD) mesh \( \phi \) is then constructed by composing these inferred timestamps \( h_j \) with the measured quantities \( g_k \), resulting in a set of candidate BDs \( \{g_k \circ h_j\} \). This splitting manages LLM context size and facilitates broader exploration.

**Multi-BD MAP-Elites Success Algorithm:**
To leverage the heterogeneous BD mesh, a multi-BD variant of the MES algorithm is employed:
- **Archive Structure:** Instead of a single archive, a separate grid is maintained for each behavior descriptor derived from the BD mesh \( \phi \). This creates a "multi-BD archive."
- **Individual Insertion:** During the evaluation step, each newly generated individual is inserted into *every* BD grid through local competition, similar to standard MES.
- **Selection for Evolution:** At the selection stage, individuals are selected from each grid independently. The resulting lists of selected individuals from all grids are then concatenated to form the population for mutation and further evaluation. This ensures that diversity is driven along multiple, potentially heterogeneous, behavioral dimensions simultaneously.

**Candidate Solution Template and Perception API:**
- **Solution Representation:** A candidate solution is parameterized as a 6-DoF initial robot position and orientation, coupled with a 3-DoF linear motion vector. The robot's gripper closes at the initial pose, then executes a linear motion while maintaining a constant gripper orientation.
- **Mutation Operator:** A Gaussian mutation operator is applied to the initial XYZ position, initial RPY orientation, and motion vector components.
- **Search Space Constraints:** Initial pose, gripper rotation, and motion vector amplitudes are constrained to ensure physically realistic and task-relevant interactions (e.g., initial position within a 20cm cube around the object, gripper pointing downwards, positive vertical motion component).
- **LLM Perception Input:** The LLM is provided with a "perception API" detailing available information from the simulator, including object position/orientation, contact information (robot/table, gripper/object), gripper pose, and joint positions for articulated objects. This information forms the basis for LLM inference of functionals.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments are conducted on four simulated robotic manipulation tasks using the Genesis simulator, which supports GPU parallelization for efficient evaluation. The LLM used is GPT-4o with a temperature of 0, indicating a preference for deterministic and consistent outputs. The performance is measured by the "Success archive size" (number of successful individuals identified by a ground-truth success condition) and "Ground-truth BD coverage" (projection of the archive into an expert-designed ground-truth BD space). Baselines include: Naive inference (standard MES with LLM-inferred metrics without the proposed exploration), Hand-crafted MES (standard MES with expert-written SC, fitness, and ground-truth BD), and a Generic Genetic Algorithm (GA) with LLM-inferred fitness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation compared the proposed language-driven Quality-Diversity (QD) method against several baselines across four robotic manipulation tasks in the Genesis simulator: Grasp the mug, Turn on the hot water, Open the drawer, and Slide the mug. Performance was assessed based on the total number of successful individuals in the archive ("Success archive size") and the diversity of these solutions as measured by their coverage in a ground-truth behavior descriptor (BD) space ("Ground-truth BD coverage").

**Overall Performance:**
The proposed method consistently demonstrated superior performance in generating large archives of successful and diverse motion primitives across all tasks.
- **Grasping Task (Hard Exploration):** For "Grasp the mug," the proposed method yielded an average success archive size of 403.3 ± 218.0 and GT BD coverage of 375.5 ± 180.5. This significantly outperformed the "Naive inference" baseline (144.7 ± 15.9 successes, 140.5 ± 12.3 GT BD coverage) and the "Hand-crafted MES" baseline (5.8 ± 2.9 successes, 5.8 ± 2.9 GT BD coverage). The generic genetic algorithm (GA) baseline failed to find any successful solutions (0 ± 0).
- **Drawer Task:** Similar trends were observed for "Open the drawer." The proposed method generated 2720.4 ± 1245.6 successful solutions with 334.4 ± 154.1 GT BD coverage, significantly higher than "Naive inference" (110.0 ± 8.64 successes, 97.0 ± 13.6 GT BD coverage) and "Hand-crafted MES" (113.2 ± 15.0 successes, 113.2 ± 15.0 GT BD coverage). The GA again showed minimal success (0.6 ± 0.5).
- **Faucet Task (Easy Exploration):** On the "Turn on the hot water" task, which was described as an easier exploration problem, all methods filled the ground-truth BD space. However, the proposed method produced a substantially larger archive (13492.2 ± 1042.5 successes) compared to "Naive inference" (7932.0 ± 3780.1 successes), "Hand-crafted MES" (85.0 ± 0 successes), and the GA (525.6 ± 19.4 successes).
- **Push Task:** For "Slide the mug," the proposed method generated the largest archive (85518.0 ± 891.9 successes) with high GT BD coverage (140.6 ± 5.7). This again outperformed "Naive inference" (4028.2 ± 2052.1 successes, 137.6 ± 4.1 GT BD coverage) and "Hand-crafted MES" (109.8 ± 1.6 successes, 109.8 ± 1.6 GT BD coverage). The GA, while finding many individuals, exhibited very poor GT BD coverage (8.6 ± 1.9).

**Analysis of Performance Differences:**
- **Superiority over Naive Inference:** The "Naive inference" baseline performed significantly worse due to the LLM's difficulty in inferring optimal fitness and BD components zero-shot without the proposed exploration mechanism. The sequential, split-space exploration strategy for BDs and the fitness selection mechanism in the proposed method appear to be critical for obtaining more suitable QD parameters.
- **Outperformance of Hand-crafted MES:** The proposed method also generally outperformed the "Hand-crafted MES" baseline, even though the latter used expert-defined, ground-truth BDs. The authors attribute this to the nature of "describing BDs," which are often used as ground-truth metrics and tend to be sparse, leading to smaller archives that struggle to generate successes. In contrast, the LLM-inferred BDs, often acting as "driving BDs," are denser and facilitate rapid archive filling.
- **Advantage of Multi-BD Approach:** The multi-BD variant of MES was identified as a key factor in the superior performance. By maintaining multiple streams of diversity, the approach effectively combined the rapid archive-filling capability of driving BDs with the ability to maintain diversity along the more sparse, ground-truth-like dimensions. This approach also reduced the need for strong priors, which are often required in traditional QD methods to handle sparse describing BDs.
- **Ineffectiveness of Genetic Algorithm:** The poor performance of the generic genetic algorithm baseline across most tasks confirmed the necessity of diversity metrics for effective exploration in sparse interaction settings, highlighting a fundamental advantage of the QD paradigm.
- **Variance:** The results indicated a relatively high variance across trials for the proposed method, suggesting potential areas for future refinement, such as integrating mechanisms like injected random solutions to improve consistency.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that the proposed language-driven quality-diversity approach, by autonomously leveraging LLMs to design QD functionals, is effective in generating large and diverse archives of robot manipulation skills, significantly outperforming conventional QD and genetic algorithm baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of autonomous robotics by addressing several critical challenges in robot skill acquisition, particularly concerning adaptability and user accessibility. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood in the following aspects:

1. **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** The most direct impact is the substantial increase in robot autonomy. By enabling LLMs to autonomously infer crucial components of Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms—success conditions, fitness functions, and behavior descriptors—the method eliminates the need for expert designers to manually craft these elements for each new task. This paradigm shift allows robots to acquire new manipulation skills from simple, free-form natural language descriptions, moving towards truly open-ended robotic capabilities.

2. **Increased Adaptability and Robustness:** The method generates diverse archives of motion primitives, rather than single optimal solutions. This diversity is crucial for robotic systems operating in dynamic or uncertain real-world environments. An archive of varied solutions allows a robot to adapt to unseen task instances, environmental constraints, or perturbations at deployment time (zero-shot adaptation), thereby enhancing robustness and reducing brittleness often associated with single-policy approaches. This is particularly valuable for complex manipulation tasks where environmental variations are common.

3. **Automated Data Generation for Foundation Models:** The ability to autonomously generate large datasets of diverse robotic trajectories has significant implications for training advanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Current VLA models often rely on extensive and costly human-demonstrated datasets. This work presents a pipeline for autonomously generating such data in simulation, which could drastically reduce deployment costs and accelerate the development of more generalist robot models by providing a scalable source of varied training data, potentially combining simulated and real-world data more effectively.

4. **Bridging the Semantic Gap:** The research effectively leverages the power of LLMs to bridge the semantic gap between high-level human language instructions and the low-level technical requirements of robot learning algorithms. This makes robot programming and skill acquisition more accessible to non-experts, fostering broader adoption and application of robotic technologies.

5. **Advancement in Quality-Diversity Research:** The paper extends the applicability and practicality of QD algorithms. By proposing an LLM-based exploration mechanism for functional spaces (fitness and BDs) and demonstrating the efficacy of a multi-BD variant of MAP-Elites Success, it offers new strategies for addressing the fundamental challenge of metric design in QD. The findings, particularly the performance benefits of "driving BDs" and the multi-BD approach, provide new insights and design guidelines for future QD research in robotics.

**Potential Future Work and Directions:**
The authors identify several promising avenues for future research that build upon this work:
- **Curriculum Learning Integration:** Combining the proposed QD approach with curriculum-based learning pipelines could enable the acquisition of more complex motions. Diverse trajectories generated by QD are naturally suited for facilitating skill chaining and progressive learning, allowing robots to tackle high-dimensional search spaces by composing simpler learned skills.
- **Automated Solution Template Generation:** Currently, the candidate solution template (e.g., 6-DoF pose and 3-DoF motion vector) is fixed. Automatically generating task-tailored templates, including varying numbers of waypoints, interpolation procedures, or gripper synergies, along with introducing task-specific priors, could significantly expand the diversity of approachable tasks (e.g., to more complex manipulation or locomotion scenarios).
- **Sim2Real Transferability:** A crucial next step is to rigorously evaluate the transferability of these language-driven QD-acquired skills to physical robots. This would involve adapting domain randomization techniques to the proposed setting and further refining motion templates to enhance sim2real performance.

In conclusion, this research marks a step towards more autonomous, adaptable, and user-friendly robotic systems by integrating the strengths of LLMs for autonomous functional design with the diversity-generating capabilities of Quality-Diversity algorithms. Its impact spans from fundamental advancements in robot learning theory to practical implications for data generation and the accessibility of robotics.
