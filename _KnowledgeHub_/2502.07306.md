# TRAVEL: Training-Free Retrieval and Alignment for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at George Mason University introduced TRAVEL, a training-free, modular framework for Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) that leverages the zero-shot capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This approach achieved 70.0% Precision@10 for last landmark localization and an average normalized Dynamic Time Warping (nDTW) score of 88.92% on the complex R2R-Habitat dataset.

## Problem

- Existing end-to-end Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) models struggle with the complexities of natural language instructions and require extensive, computationally expensive training.
- Prior modular VLN approaches were typically limited to simpler instructions and smaller datasets, often failing to robustly ground landmarks in complex environments.
- Agents face challenges in accurately parsing language, grounding phrases to visual concepts, and integrating temporal cues for navigation.

## Method

- The **TRAVEL** framework decomposes the VLN task into four distinct, training-free modules: instruction parsing, landmark retrieval, path generation, and path-instruction alignment.
- It employs a zero-shot LLM (LLama-3.1-8B-Instruct) for extracting sequential landmarks from instructions and a VLM (SigLIP) for zero-shot visual grounding of the last landmark in panoramic views to retrieve potential goal nodes.
- Path hypotheses are generated via ==Breadth-First Search==, and then aligned and ranked using either a dynamic programming algorithm (PANO2LAND with GPT-4o grounding) or direct GPT-4o rating based on the instruction and landmark sequence.

## Results

- Last landmark localization demonstrated a Precision@10 of **70.0%** using SigLIP on panoramic views, outperforming the **34.4%** achieved by VLMaps with CLIP.
- The PANO2LAND approach achieved an average normalized Dynamic Time Warping (nDTW) score of **88.92%** (with an accuracy of **57.45%** for nDTW > **87%**) on the R2R-Habitat dataset.
- The system successfully generated a viable path (ground-truth or highly similar) among its hypotheses in **65.72%** of episodes on average.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modern VLMs like SigLIP achieve significantly superior last landmark localization performance (70.0% Precision@10) on panoramic views compared to previous methods using CLIP on semantic occupancy maps (34.4%).
> - A modular, training-free architecture leveraging zero-shot LLMs and VLMs can effectively tackle complex VLN tasks, offering improved interpretability and adaptability without task-specific training.
> - Explicit, individual landmark grounding combined with dynamic programming alignment (PANO2LAND) yields slightly better performance than direct VLM rating, highlighting the benefit of structured decomposition for complex path evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) involves developing agents that can navigate through environments by interpreting natural language instructions. This task presents significant challenges in parsing language, grounding phrases to visual concepts and actions, and incorporating temporal cues. The existing research in VLN generally falls into two main categories: end-to-end approaches and modular approaches.

**End-to-End Approaches:** These methods typically frame VLN as a supervised multi-modal sequence-to-sequence learning task. They train models to take natural language instructions, visual observations, and navigation actions, then output a sequence of low-level actions or waypoints. These approaches have been supported by large-scale datasets such as Room-to-Room (R2R) and Room-Across-Room (RxR), which provide agents with panoramic images and navigation instructions in complex indoor environments like Matterport3D. While end-to-end models have shown gradual improvements through various architectures, training techniques, and representations, they still exhibit a notable performance gap compared to human capabilities. Their performance often degrades in novel environments or with more complex instructions, and they frequently struggle with grounding landmarks, understanding spatial relationships, and interpreting action phrases. Furthermore, training these models, particularly those incorporating Reinforcement Learning (RL) or Imitation Learning (IL), requires extensive high-quality training episodes and significant computational resources due to online agent interaction.

**Modular Approaches:** An alternative class of methods adopts a modular design, employing separate components for processing natural language inputs, grounding noun phrases in images or maps using semantic segmentation or detection, and integrating these with traditional map-based navigation techniques. Prior modular efforts, however, have often been evaluated on smaller-scale datasets with simpler natural language instructions, such as point and object goal navigation. Examples include CLIP-Nav, which used GPT-3 for instruction decomposition and CLIP for grounding, but was limited by CLIP's ability to associate landmarks and its dependency on a navigable graph. VLMaps built global vision-language semantic occupancy maps using CLIP embeddings for navigation, primarily for simpler instructions. Lang2LTL translated natural language into linear temporal logic but required parallel datasets for fine-tuning LLMs, limiting complexity. LM-Nav used GPT-3 for landmark extraction and CLIP for grounding on a topological map but was designed only for navigating to unique landmark sequences.

**This Work's Position:** The presented research, TRAVEL, positions itself within the modular paradigm. It leverages the zero-shot capabilities of contemporary Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to address the limitations observed in prior modular approaches, particularly regarding instruction complexity and dataset scale. By decomposing the VLN problem into distinct sub-modules – instruction parsing, landmark retrieval, path generation, and path-instruction alignment – the authors aim to combine the strengths of these advanced pre-trained models. The work specifically tackles complex instructions from the R2R-Habitat dataset, an environment that has typically challenged both end-to-end and earlier modular systems. A key aspect of this work is its explicit comparison with semantic map-based approaches like VLMaps, demonstrating superior performance in landmark grounding using a topological map with per-node panoramas. In doing so, the authors aim to provide insights into the current capabilities and limitations of state-of-the-art LLMs and VLMs when applied to the multi-faceted task of vision-language instruction following.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and evaluate a modular, training-free approach for the Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) task. This approach is designed to leverage the zero-shot capabilities of state-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for processing complex natural language instructions.

The key specific objectives and underlying motivations are:

1. **Decompose the VLN Problem:** The authors aim to break down the complex VLN task into four distinct, manageable sub-modules:
 - Extracting landmarks and their sequence from natural language instructions.
 - Retrieving potential goal locations based on the last landmark.
 - Generating path hypotheses from the start to these goal locations.
 - Aligning and ranking these path hypotheses against the instruction's landmark sequence.
 - **Motivation:** This modularity is intended to simplify the problem, allow for the independent application and evaluation of specialized models (LLMs, VLMs), and potentially lead to more interpretable and debuggable systems compared to monolithic end-to-end architectures.

2. **Leverage Zero-Shot LLMs for Instruction Parsing:** The objective is to utilize pre-trained LLMs (specifically LLama-3.1-8B-Instruct) directly, without fine-tuning, to parse complex navigation instructions. This involves extracting salient landmarks and their prescribed order of visitation.
 - **Motivation:** Traditional end-to-end VLN systems often struggle with the nuances of natural language parsing and instruction understanding, especially with complex or novel instructions. Leveraging advanced LLMs in a zero-shot capacity aims to capitalize on their extensive general linguistic knowledge for robust instruction comprehension without the need for domain-specific language training.

3. **Employ Zero-Shot VLMs for Visual Grounding and Goal Retrieval:** The work seeks to use pre-trained VLMs (SigLIP and GPT-4o) to visually ground the extracted landmarks within the environment. This involves identifying potential goal locations by matching the last landmark to panoramic images associated with topological map nodes.
 - **Motivation:** Accurate visual grounding is fundamental for navigation. Previous methods using semantic maps with models like CLIP showed limitations. The motivation is to demonstrate that more recent VLMs, when applied to panoramic views, can achieve superior landmark recognition, thereby improving goal localization and overall navigation performance in a zero-shot manner.

4. **Develop a Path Alignment and Ranking Mechanism:** A core objective is to design a method to evaluate and rank different path hypotheses based on their alignment with the natural language instruction. This includes developing a dynamic programming-based algorithm (PANO2LAND) and exploring direct rating by a VLM (GPT-4o).
 - **Motivation:** Generating multiple plausible paths is insufficient without a robust mechanism to determine which path best fulfills the instruction. The alignment mechanism aims to quantitatively assess how well a sequence of visual observations along a path corresponds to the sequence of landmarks specified in the instruction, thus guiding the agent towards the most faithful trajectory.

5. **Evaluate Performance on Complex Datasets:** The research specifically aims to evaluate the proposed approach on the R2R-Habitat dataset, known for its complex navigation instructions in realistic indoor environments.
 - **Motivation:** Many prior modular approaches were tested on simpler instructions. Demonstrating efficacy on a complex benchmark like R2R-Habitat would validate the robustness and scalability of the training-free, modular design for challenging VLN scenarios.

6. **Quantify the Impact of Visual Grounding:** A detailed quantification of how effectively visual grounding influences the overall navigation performance is an explicit objective.
 - **Motivation:** Understanding the contribution of each module, particularly the visual grounding component, is critical for identifying strengths, weaknesses, and areas for future improvement in the VLN pipeline.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to present a novel and effective modular framework for VLN that harnesses the capabilities of modern foundation models, potentially offering a more interpretable, adaptable, and training-efficient alternative to existing methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, named TRAVEL, is a modular, training-free system for Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) that decomposes the task into four interconnected sub-modules. It primarily operates in a zero-shot setting, leveraging state-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

#### 3.1. Topological Map Construction

The agent first constructs a topological map of the environment. This map, denoted as graph G, is built using all unique waypoints and trajectories from the training episodes of the R2R-Habitat dataset. Each node `v` in the graph represents a 360° RGB panorama, capturing the visual information at that location. Edges `e` in the graph signify connectivity between nodes, with a weight of 1, indicating traversability. This process ensures that every node corresponding to ground-truth waypoints in the training data has a representation in the constructed topological map, establishing a consistent basis for navigation.

#### 3.2. Instruction Parsing: Landmark Extraction

Given a natural language navigation instruction, a pre-trained LLM, specifically LLama-3.1-8B-Instruct, is prompted to analyze the instruction. Its role is to extract a sequential list of landmarks and the order in which they are intended to be visited by the agent. This step translates the free-form natural language into a structured, ordered sequence of visual targets.

#### 3.3. Goal Node Retrieval: Last Landmark Localization

Following landmark extraction, the system identifies the last landmark phrase in the sequence. To determine potential end locations for navigation, it then searches for the top-k most probable goal nodes in the topological map that visually correspond to this last landmark. This retrieval is performed using a state-of-the-art VLM, SigLIP. SigLIP computes the cosine similarity between the panoramic images associated with each graph node and the textual description of the landmark. This zero-shot recognition mechanism aims to narrow down the set of possible destination points, focusing path generation on relevant areas.

#### 3.4. Path Hypothesis Generation

Once the top-k goal locations (nodes) for the last landmark are identified, the system generates multiple path hypotheses. From the agent's starting pose, a Breadth-First Search (BFS) algorithm is applied on the topological map to compute the shortest path to each of the k retrieved goal nodes. This results in k distinct path hypotheses, each represented as an ordered sequence of panoramas (graph nodes) that the agent would traverse.

#### 3.5. Path-Instruction Alignment and Ranking

To select the most faithful path from the generated hypotheses, the system quantifies the alignment between each path and the original instruction, specifically the extracted sequence of landmarks. Two distinct approaches are introduced for this stage:

- **Approach I: PANO2LAND (Dynamic Programming Alignment)**
 This approach frames the problem as a sequence-to-sequence alignment. Let `X = [X_0, X_1..., X_p]` be the sequence of panoramas along a path hypothesis, and `Y = [Y_0, Y_1..., Y_l]` be the sequence of landmark phrases extracted from the instruction. A binary grounding matrix `A` is constructed where `A_ij` indicates whether landmark `Y_j` is present and visible in panorama `X_i`. The binary grounding scores for this matrix are obtained using another state-of-the-art VLM, GPT-4o, which is prompted in a zero-shot manner to provide these scores for each panorama and landmark phrase. Paths where the number of nodes is less than the number of landmarks are discarded.
 Subsequently, a normalized alignment score is computed for each path using a Dynamic Programming (DP) formulation (Algorithm 1), conceptually similar to the Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) problem. This algorithm finds the longest sequence of correctly ordered and grounded landmarks within a given path. The normalized score reflects how well the path's visual sequence matches the instruction's landmark sequence.

- **Approach II: GPT-4o Direct Rating**
 In this alternative approach, the system directly prompts GPT-4o to rate each path hypothesis on a scale of 1 to 5. The input to GPT-4o includes the full sequence of panoramas for a path, the original natural language instruction, and the extracted sequence of landmark phrases. This method bypasses the individual landmark grounding and the explicit dynamic programming alignment of Approach I, relying on GPT-4o's internal reasoning capabilities to assess the overall alignment quality.

#### 3.6. Evaluation Metric

For the final evaluation, the normalized dynamic-time warping (nDTW) metric is employed. nDTW is computed between the ground-truth path and the best-aligned path selected by either Approach I or Approach II. This metric is chosen over Success Rate (SR) because it provides a more nuanced measure of path fidelity, considering not only proximity to the final goal but also the intermediate alignment with landmarks that should be visited in sequence. An nDTW score above 87% is empirically defined as a successful path.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings concerning the performance of the modular, training-free VLN approach, TRAVEL, particularly in comparison to existing methods and across its internal sub-modules.

#### 4.1. Last Landmark Localization Performance

A direct comparison was conducted for the effectiveness of last landmark indexing using different VLMs and environmental representations.
- **VLMaps with CLIP:** Achieved a Precision@10 of 34.4% when localizing 127 landmarks by populating an occupancy map with CLIP embeddings.
- **Ours with SigLIP:** Demonstrated superior performance, achieving a Precision@10 of 70.0% using SigLIP on panoramic views within the topological map.
This result indicates that recognizing landmarks directly in panoramic views with a modern VLM like SigLIP is substantially more effective than using open-vocabulary semantic occupancy maps derived from models like CLIP for goal retrieval. This finding supports the choice of a panoramic-based topological map representation and the use of SigLIP for visual grounding.

#### 4.2. Full Pipeline Quantitative Results (Table 2)

The entire pipeline was evaluated across five different environments from the R2R-Habitat dataset, each comprising 21 episodes.

- **Hypothesis Path Generation Accuracy (HYPO PATH GEN Accuracy):**
 The average accuracy for retrieving the ground-truth path or a highly similar one among the generated path hypotheses was 65.72% (±6.33%). This metric reflects the upper bound of the system's potential, indicating that in approximately two-thirds of the episodes, a viable path was generated. The authors identified several factors for cases where the correct path was not retrieved, including failure to ground the last landmark, encountering dramatically different landmarks, or highly frequent landmarks (e.g., "door") where the correct location might not be among the top-k retrieved goals.

- **Approach I (PANO2LAND with GPT-4o Grounding):**
 This approach, which involves explicit individual landmark grounding via GPT-4o and dynamic programming for sequence alignment, yielded an average nDTW score of 88.92% (±1.28%). The average accuracy (defined as nDTW > 87%) for this approach was 57.45% (±6.31%).

- **Approach II (GPT-4o Direct Rating):**
 The approach where GPT-4o directly rates the entire path sequence based on the instruction and landmarks produced an average nDTW score of 88.34% (±1.20%). The average accuracy (nDTW > 87%) was 50.15% (±5.81%).

#### 4.3. Comparison of Approaches

Approach I (PANO2LAND) consistently achieved slightly higher average nDTW scores and higher accuracy compared to Approach II (GPT-4o Direct Rating). The authors hypothesize that the explicit decomposition and sequential grounding of individual landmarks in Approach I provide a benefit, particularly in situations where GPT-4o might struggle with grounding action phrases or complex instructions in its internal, less interpretable ranking mechanism. The explicit nature of the dynamic programming alignment in PANO2LAND contributes to its marginally better performance.

#### 4.4. Error Propagation and Visual Grounding Impact

The findings implicitly highlight the impact of error propagation within the modular pipeline. The quality of initial landmark extraction by the LLM and the accuracy of VLM-based landmark retrieval directly influence the subsequent stages of path generation and alignment. The work quantifies how these visual grounding capabilities are critical determinants of overall navigation performance, underscoring that limitations in early modules can cap the performance of later stages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "TRAVEL: Training-Free Retrieval and Alignment for Vision-and-Language Navigation" offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of embodied AI, particularly Vision-and-Language Navigation.

**Validation of Modular, Training-Free VLN Architectures:** The work demonstrates the viability and effectiveness of a modular approach for complex VLN tasks. By leveraging the zero-shot capabilities of pre-trained LLMs (LLama-3.1-8B-Instruct) for instruction parsing and VLMs (SigLIP, GPT-4o) for visual grounding and alignment, the system bypasses the need for extensive end-to-end training. This is significant because it suggests a path towards developing more agile and adaptable navigation systems that can interpret instructions without large, task-specific datasets and computational overhead for training.

**Superiority in Landmark Grounding:** The direct comparison showing SigLIP's superior performance over CLIP (as used in VLMaps) for landmark localization in panoramic views is a notable finding. This indicates that the choice of VLM and the specific visual representation (per-node panoramas vs. occupancy maps) have a substantial impact on the fidelity of visual grounding, which is a foundational component for successful navigation. This could guide future research towards optimizing visual encoding strategies and VLM selection for embodied tasks.

**Insights into Foundation Model Capabilities and Limitations:** The study provides empirical insights into the strengths of LLMs for extracting structured information (landmark sequences) from natural language and the capabilities of VLMs for visual grounding in a zero-shot context. Concurrently, it exposes the limitations of these models, particularly concerning their handling of spatial and temporal phrases, action phrases, and ambiguous landmarks, which can lead to errors in early stages that propagate through the pipeline. This diagnosis is crucial for understanding where future improvements in LLMs and VLMs for embodied AI should be directed.

**Enhanced Interpretability and Debuggability:** The modular design of TRAVEL inherently offers better interpretability compared to opaque end-to-end models. Failures can be attributed to specific modules (e.g., LLM instruction misinterpretation, VLM grounding errors, or path alignment issues), facilitating diagnosis and targeted improvements. This modularity also allows for independent advancements in each component, meaning a better LLM or VLM could be seamlessly integrated to improve overall performance without redesigning the entire system.

**Addressing Challenges in Complex Environments:** By demonstrating competitive performance on the R2R-Habitat dataset, which features complex instructions and realistic indoor environments, the work pushes the boundaries of modular VLN. It shows that such approaches can handle instruction complexity that previously challenged systems, moving beyond simpler object-goal navigation.

**Foundation for Future Research and Practical Applications:** The research lays a foundation for future exploration into integrating exploration and navigation seamlessly, deploying agents in unseen environments, and fine-tuning VLMs specifically for navigation tasks. In practical terms, this approach could contribute to the development of more robust autonomous robots for tasks like assistive navigation in homes or warehouses, where detailed human instructions need to be accurately translated into actions without extensive pre-training in every specific environment. The zero-shot nature also reduces the barrier to deploying such systems in new settings.
