# Learning to Reason in 13 Parameters

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta FAIR researchers developed TinyLoRA, a parameter-efficient finetuning method that enables large language models to acquire complex mathematical reasoning skills by training as few as 13 parameters. This approach, when combined with reinforcement learning, achieved 91% accuracy on GSM8K, demonstrating a significant advancement in ultra-low-capacity model adaptation.

## Problem

- Finetuning large language models traditionally requires updating billions of parameters, leading to high computational and memory costs.
- Existing parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) methods, while reducing parameters, still operate in the range of millions or tens of thousands, limiting their application in highly constrained environments.
- There is a need to understand the fundamental minimum number of parameters necessary for effective adaptation, especially for complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- Introduced ==TinyLoRA==, a novel PEFT method that extends LoRA-XS by replacing the trainable r×r matrix with a ==low-dimensional trainable vector projected through a fixed random tensor==, significantly reducing parameters per module.
- Implemented ==aggressive weight tying strategies==, allowing the trainable vector to be shared across multiple or all layers of the model, enabling total trainable parameters to be reduced to single digits.
- Utilized ==Reinforcement Learning (specifically GRPO)== as the primary learning algorithm, demonstrating its unique efficiency in leveraging sparse, reward-driven signals for highly constrained parameter updates.

## Results

- A Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model achieved **91%** accuracy on the GSM8K dataset by training only **13 parameters**, marking a **15%** absolute improvement over its **76%** baseline.
- When trained on the more difficult MATH dataset, TinyLoRA with **196** parameters retained **87%** of the absolute performance improvement across a suite of six challenging math benchmarks.
- Qwen models consistently demonstrated greater parameter efficiency than Llama models in low-parameter finetuning, often requiring **10x** fewer parameters to reach equivalent performance levels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning exhibits 100-1000x greater parameter efficiency than supervised finetuning in extremely low-parameter regimes, due to its ability to make more 'information-dense' updates.
> - Larger pre-trained language models are more amenable to ultra-low-parameter adaptation, requiring smaller relative update sizes to achieve performance gains on specific tasks.
> - The findings empirically support the concept of intrinsic dimensionality, suggesting that complex capabilities can be amplified or 'unlocked' in LLMs with remarkably few trainable parameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several critical and rapidly evolving areas in artificial intelligence: **parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT)**, **reinforcement learning for language models (RLHF/RLVR)**, and the **scaling laws of large language models (LLMs)**.

The exponential growth in the size of LLMs has made full finetuning prohibitively expensive and resource-intensive. This challenge has spurred extensive research into PEFT methods, with **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)** (Hu et al., 2021) emerging as a leading technique. LoRA significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters by introducing small, low-rank matrices (adapters) that are added to the frozen weights of a pre-trained model. While conventional LoRA and its variants (e.g., LoRA-XS) have successfully reduced trainable parameters from billions to millions or tens of thousands, this paper questions whether even these smaller counts are truly necessary. It pushes the boundary of parameter efficiency to an unprecedented extreme, aiming for updates in the order of *tens* of parameters.

Concurrently, **reinforcement learning (RL)** has become a powerful paradigm for aligning and enhancing the capabilities of LLMs, especially for complex tasks like mathematical reasoning and code generation. Methods such as Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) (OpenAI et al., 2024; Shao et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2025) have shown impressive results in teaching models to "reason." This paper investigates the interaction between RL and PEFT, specifically demonstrating that RL is uniquely suited for learning with extremely low-capacity updates, in contrast to supervised finetuning (SFT). This finding contributes to a growing body of literature comparing SFT and RL, which often suggests that RL can lead to better generalization, less forgetting, and a more focused amplification of existing model capabilities (Chu et al., 2025; Shenfeld et al., 2025; Rajani et al., 2025).

Furthermore, the work touches upon the concept of **intrinsic dimensionality** (Li et al., 2018; Aghajanyan et al., 2020), which posits that complex models often lie on a low-dimensional manifold, implying that only a small number of parameters might be sufficient to guide their adaptation. By demonstrating effective learning with just 13 parameters, this paper provides empirical evidence supporting this principle, especially when coupled with the efficient signal provided by RL. The explicit comparison across various model sizes (from 0.5B to 7B) also contributes to the understanding of finetuning scaling laws (Zhang et al., 2024a), suggesting that larger base models are more amenable to ultra-low-parameter adaptation.

In summary, this paper represents a significant leap forward in parameter-efficient finetuning by introducing a method that scales adapter sizes down to previously unimaginable levels, simultaneously highlighting the unique advantages of reinforcement learning in this extreme low-capacity regime.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to challenge the conventional understanding of how many parameters are truly necessary for finetuning large language models to acquire or enhance reasoning capabilities, particularly for mathematical tasks. The authors explicitly question whether even rank=1 LoRA, which still involves millions of parameters for large LLMs, is a necessary minimum.

The primary motivations driving this investigation are:

1. **Extreme Parameter Efficiency:** To develop a method that can scale low-rank adapters to sizes as small as a single parameter, significantly beyond the capabilities of existing LoRA variants. This push for ultra-low parameter counts is motivated by several practical advantages:
 - **Reduced Memory Usage:** Finetuning fewer parameters decreases GPU memory consumption.
 - **Lower Communication Costs:** Essential for distributed training setups.
 - **Enhanced Inference-Time Capacity:** Smaller adapters allow more LoRAs to be stored in memory and served concurrently, enabling efficient personalization at scale.
 - **Mitigation of Forgetting:** Low-rank updates have been shown to help retain important information from the base model.
2. **Exploring the Information Content of Learning Signals:** To investigate the fundamental differences in how supervised finetuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) utilize information for model updates. The authors hypothesize that RL, with its clean, reward-driven signal, can learn effectively with much less capacity compared to SFT, which must absorb more extraneous information from full demonstrations.
3. **Understanding Scaling Trends:** To analyze how the efficacy of extremely low-parameter adaptation methods changes with the size of the backbone language model, anticipating that larger pre-trained models might require even smaller relative updates for specific tasks.

Ultimately, the paper aims to demonstrate that, under specific conditions (i.e., using reinforcement learning on large pre-trained models), state-of-the-art reasoning performance can be achieved with an astonishingly small number of trainable parameters, leading to highly compact and efficient model updates.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces **TinyLoRA**, a novel parameter-efficient finetuning method designed to drastically reduce the number of trainable parameters below existing LoRA variants, coupled with a rigorous experimental comparison between reinforcement learning (RL) and supervised finetuning (SFT) in this low-capacity regime.

#### 3.1. TinyLoRA Parameterization

TinyLoRA builds upon the principles of LoRA and LoRA-XS (Bałazy et al., 2025) but introduces a critical modification to scale adapter sizes down to single-digit parameters.

- **LoRA Refresher:** Conventional LoRA adapts a frozen linear layer $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$ by adding a low-rank update $AB$, where $A \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$. The number of trainable parameters is $O(dr)$.
- **LoRA-XS Baseline:** LoRA-XS reduces the per-module parameter count to $O(r^2)$ by applying the update $U\Sigma R V^\top$, where $U, \Sigma, V$ are derived from the truncated SVD of $W$, and only $R \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}$ is trainable. This learns to recombine dominant singular directions.
- **TinyLoRA Innovation:** TinyLoRA further reduces the trainable parameters by replacing the $r \times r$ matrix $R$ with a low-dimensional trainable vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^u$. This vector is then projected through a fixed random tensor $P \in \mathbb{R}^{u \times r \times r}$ to form the $r \times r$ update matrix. The update rule becomes $W' = W + U \Sigma (\sum_{i=1}^u v_i P_i) V^\top$. Each module now trains only $u$ parameters.
- **Weight Tying (Parameter Sharing):** To achieve even smaller update sizes, TinyLoRA introduces aggressive weight tying. The trainable vector $v$ can be shared across multiple modules within a layer or even across all layers of the model. By defining a weight tying factor $n_{tie}$ (number of modules sharing a single $v$), the total trainable parameters can be reduced to $O(nmu/n_{tie})$, where $n$ is the number of layers and $m$ is the number of modules per layer. In the extreme case of full weight tying ($n_{tie} = nm$), the total trainable parameters reduce to just $u$, potentially as low as one parameter.

#### 3.2. Experimental Setup

- **Base Models:** The experiments utilize instruction-tuned language models from the Qwen-2.5 (Qwen et al., 2025) and Llama-3 (AI@Meta, 2024) families, with sizes ranging from 0.5B to 7B parameters.
- **Learning Algorithms:**
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The primary learning algorithm is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024), a policy gradient method known for its effectiveness in reasoning tasks. All RL experiments use exact-match reward.
 - **Supervised Finetuning (SFT):** Used as a baseline, employing next-token prediction loss.
- **Datasets:**
 - **GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021):** A dataset of 7,500 math word problems, used for initial evaluations and the primary comparison between RL and SFT.
 - **MATH Training Set (Hendrycks et al., 2021):** A larger and more difficult dataset, partitioned by difficulty, with training focused on the hardest subset (8,523 problems).
 - **Additional Benchmarks:** For comprehensive evaluation of reasoning, datasets such as MATH500, Minerva, GAOKAO, OlympiadBench, CollegeMath, AIME 24, and AMC23 are used.
- **Training Details:**
 - **Hyperparameter Sweeps:** A critical aspect of the methodology involves extensive sweeping over learning rates (from $10^{-7}$ to $2 \times 10^{-4}$) to find the optimal rate for each update size, averaged over three random seeds. This addresses the known interaction between update size and effective learning rate.
 - **RL specifics:** For GSM8K, no KL penalty is used, training for 3 epochs with 4 samples per problem, batch size 64. For MATH, settings follow SimpleRL (Zeng et al., 2025), including KL coefficient 0.001, temperature 1.0, batch size 256, and 8 generations per response.
- **Implementation:** Experiments are conducted within the open-source VERL framework (Sheng et al., 2024), leveraging vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023) for inference. A novel approach is used to circumvent vLLM's limitations on low LoRA ranks: training uses merged model weights for inference and the true LoRA model only for the final forward pass, with numerical mismatches mitigated by truncated importance sampling.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocol

- **Metric:** Pass@1 accuracy is the primary evaluation metric for all math reasoning benchmarks.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against:
 - Untrained base models (0 parameters).
 - Full finetuning (millions to billions of parameters).
 - Standard LoRA at various ranks.
 - LoRA-XS at various ranks.
- **Ablations:** Detailed ablations are performed on TinyLoRA's hyperparameters, including the frozen SVD rank $r$, the trainable projection dimension $u$, and the weight tying factor $n_{tie}$, to understand their impact on performance and guide optimal configuration. Precision (bf16, fp16, fp32) and sharing strategies (structured, tiled) are also ablated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several striking findings, fundamentally reshaping the understanding of parameter-efficient finetuning and the role of reinforcement learning.

#### 4.1. Extreme Parameter Efficiency with TinyLoRA and RL

The most prominent finding is the ability of TinyLoRA, when combined with reinforcement learning (GRPO), to achieve high performance on reasoning tasks with an extraordinarily small number of trained parameters.

- On the **GSM8K dataset**, the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model, with a base accuracy of 76%, reaches **91% accuracy by training only 13 parameters** in bf16 (a total of 26 bytes). This represents a 15% absolute improvement with an update size several orders of magnitude smaller than conventional LoRA.
- Even with a single trainable parameter, TinyLoRA achieves around 82% accuracy on GSM8K, demonstrating a noticeable improvement over the baseline.
- The results show a smooth performance curve across TinyLoRA, LoRA-XS, and LoRA, indicating that TinyLoRA successfully extends the efficiency frontier. For instance, 95% of the net performance improvement on GSM8K is recovered with just 120 trained parameters.

#### 4.2. RL vs. SFT in Low-Parameter Regimes

A key contribution is the clear demonstration of RL's superior efficiency over SFT when operating with a very limited parameter budget.

- On **GSM8K**, while RL (GRPO) achieves 91% accuracy with 13 parameters and 95% with 120 parameters, SFT performs significantly worse: 83% with 13 parameters and 84% with 120 parameters. This implies that RL is **100-1000x more efficient** in the low-parameter regime, making fundamentally more "information-dense" updates.
- The hypothesis is that SFT requires absorbing many more bits of information (both relevant and irrelevant) from demonstrations, making it less suitable for low-capacity updates, whereas RL receives a sparser, cleaner, reward-driven signal.

#### 4.3. Performance on Diverse Math Benchmarks

The strong performance of TinyLoRA extends beyond GSM8K to more complex reasoning benchmarks.

- When trained on the harder **MATH dataset** using the SimpleRL framework, TinyLoRA with just **196 parameters** retains **87% of the absolute performance improvement** across a suite of six difficult math benchmarks (MATH500, Minerva, GAOKAO, OlympiadBench, AIME24, AMC23).
- Even for the 3B parameter Qwen model, training with just 16 parameters yields notable improvements across these difficult benchmarks.

#### 4.4. Scaling Across Model Backbones

The study investigates how TinyLoRA's effectiveness scales with the size of the base LLM.

- The results indicate that **larger models are more programmable with smaller updates**. As the model size increases (from 0.5B to 7B Qwen2.5-Instruct), the required update size to reach a threshold of, for example, 95% of peak performance, decreases.
- Specifically, **Qwen models generally exhibit greater parameter efficiency** than Llama models in the low-parameter regime, often requiring 10x fewer parameters to reach equivalent performance. The Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model achieves 94.7% accuracy on GSM8K with only 13 parameters, while Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct shows less responsiveness below 500 parameters. This suggests potential differences in their architectures or pre-training.

#### 4.5. Bit-Constrained Scaling and Ablations

The research also explores practical aspects of TinyLoRA, such as precision and parameter sharing.

- In the **bit-constrained regime** (tens or hundreds of parameters), **tiled parameter sharing** (sharing across modules of similar depth, agnostic to type) outperforms structured sharing (sharing only between similar module types).
- Surprisingly, storing parameters in **fp32 precision** is found to be more performant bit-for-bit than bf16 or fp16, despite its larger memory footprint per parameter.
- Ablations on TinyLoRA's hyperparameters show that using a **frozen SVD rank $r=2$** often yields the best performance, with diminishing returns or even degradation at higher ranks. For a fixed parameter budget, it is generally better to have **more expressive per-module updates (higher $u$) with less weight sharing (smaller $n_{tie}$)**, suggesting parameters are best spent on fewer, unique parameters rather than many shared ones.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has profound significance and substantial potential impact across several dimensions of AI development and deployment.

1. **Enabling Ultra-Efficient LLM Adaptation:** The demonstration of high-performance reasoning with as few as 13 trainable parameters opens up unprecedented possibilities for cost-effective and resource-light adaptation of large language models. This could democratize access to advanced LLM capabilities, allowing individuals, small teams, or organizations with limited computational resources to customize powerful models for specific tasks.
2. **Facilitating On-Device and Edge AI:** The ability to achieve significant performance improvements with just tens of bytes of model updates could revolutionize on-device AI. Imagine personalizing LLMs directly on smartphones, smart home devices, or embedded systems, where memory, storage, and computational power are severely constrained. This could lead to highly private and personalized AI experiences without reliance on cloud infrastructure.
3. **Advancing Personalized AI:** For large-scale service providers, the ability to store and serve thousands or millions of tiny adapters concurrently means massively scalable personalization. Each user or specific task could have its own ultra-lightweight adapter, offering tailored LLM behavior with minimal overhead.
4. **Deepening Understanding of LLM Learning:** The stark contrast in efficiency between RL and SFT in the low-parameter regime provides crucial insights into the fundamental mechanisms of how these learning paradigms operate. It suggests that RL extracts a highly concentrated, "information-dense" signal, making it exceptionally effective for targeted capability amplification, while SFT might be inherently more "information-diffuse." This finding could guide future research into designing more efficient learning algorithms for LLMs.
5. **Implications for AGI and Intrinsic Dimensionality:** The results bolster the theory that complex LLMs operate within a much lower intrinsic dimension than their total parameter count suggests. The fact that a handful of parameters can steer a multi-billion parameter model to new reasoning capabilities implies that much of the knowledge is already latent within the pre-trained weights, and adaptation mainly involves "unlocking" or "re-orienting" this existing knowledge. This understanding is critical for pushing the boundaries of what models can learn and how efficiently they can be trained for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
6. **Guidance for Future Research and Development:** The findings provide concrete guidelines for practitioners: prioritize RL for low-capacity finetuning, leverage larger base models for greater efficiency, and consider the specifics of parameterization (e.g., $r=2$ for frozen SVD rank, maximizing $u$ before increasing $n_{tie}$). The surprising performance of fp32 in bit-constrained scenarios also merits further investigation.

While the current findings are primarily demonstrated on mathematical reasoning tasks, the potential for generalization to other domains is immense. This work sets a new benchmark for parameter efficiency, opening up exciting avenues for more accessible, efficient, and personalized AI applications.
