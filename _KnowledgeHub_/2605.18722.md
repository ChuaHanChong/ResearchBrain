# Dexora: Open-source VLA for High-DoF Bimanual Dexterity

> [!summary] Summary
> Dexora is an open-source Vision-Language-Action (VLA) system that enables dual-arm, dual-hand, high-DoF (36 DoF) robot manipulation, addressing a key gap in VLA embodiment coverage. It achieves an average success rate of 89.6% on basic tasks and 66.7% on dexterous tasks in the real world, demonstrating robust generalization across various robot embodiments.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models are limited in embodiment coverage, focusing on either low-DoF dual-gripper control or single-arm dexterous manipulation, failing to unify both for complex tasks.
- Collecting high-fidelity demonstration data for robots with 36 DoF across two arms and two hands is challenging, complex, and prone to noise and variability.
- Ensuring VLA policies can effectively learn from noisy teleoperated data and generalize robustly across diverse tasks and different robot embodiments remains a significant hurdle.

## Method

- Developed **Dexora**, an open-source VLA system with a physical platform integrating two 6-DoF AIRBOT arms and two 12-DoF XHANDs, totaling 36 DoF for manipulation.
- Designed a hybrid teleoperation pipeline combining an exoskeleton for arm control and Apple Vision Pro for markerless finger tracking, enabling scalable and embodiment-matched data collection in both real and simulated environments.
- Implemented a discriminator-guided, quality-aware training recipe that uses a pre-trained policy to score demonstration quality, allowing the VLA model to prioritize and learn from higher-quality teleoperated clips.

## Results

- Dexora achieved an average real-world success rate of **89.6%** on 12 basic manipulation tasks and **66.7%** on 6 complex dexterous tasks, outperforming baseline VLA models like GR00T N1 and π0.
- The system demonstrated strong out-of-distribution generalization capabilities, maintaining high success rates across various unseen visual and environmental conditions.
- A policy trained on the 36-DoF Dexora platform successfully transferred to and controlled lower-DoF embodiments (single-arm gripper, dual-arm grippers, single-arm single-hand) with minimal fine-tuning.
- Ablation studies confirmed that including real-world data is critical for dexterous skill acquisition and that the discriminator-guided training improved task success rates and kinematic smoothness by effectively leveraging mixed-quality data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying dual-arm and high-DoF dexterous manipulation within a VLA framework is essential for generalist robots to perform tasks requiring complex bimanual coordination and in-hand reorientation.
> - A hybrid teleoperation system leveraging specialized inputs for gross and fine motions significantly simplifies the collection of high-fidelity, high-DoF robot demonstration data.
> - Training with a data-quality-aware mechanism, guided by a discriminator, effectively mitigates noise in teleoperated demonstrations, leading to more stable and higher-performing robot policies.
> - Policies trained in rich, high-DoF action spaces can successfully generalize to and control simpler, lower-DoF robot embodiments with minimal adaptation, suggesting a pathway towards universal robot controllers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a central direction in embodied AI, aiming to enable robots to understand natural language instructions, perceive their environment visually, and execute complex physical actions. Existing VLA systems, however, exhibit fundamental limitations in their embodiment coverage. Prior work typically focuses on either dual-gripper control (low-Degrees of Freedom, DoF) or single-arm dexterous hand manipulation (high-DoF but often single-arm). This distinction is critical because many real-world tasks intrinsically require both the coordinated effort of two arms and the fine-grained articulation provided by dexterous hands with multiple fingers. For instance, tasks like piston insertion demand bimanual coordination, while bottle opening or complex book retrieval benefit significantly from high-DoF fingers that can perform in-hand reorientation and torsional manipulation.

Dexora positions itself by addressing this specific gap, presenting the first open-source VLA system designed to natively handle dual-arm, dual-hand, and high-DoF manipulation. This work aims to bridge the capabilities of low-DoF dual-arm systems and single-arm dexterous systems, moving towards more generalist robotic control.

The research also builds upon advancements in several related areas:
- **Teleoperation Systems:** Existing teleoperation methods vary from kinesthetic teaching rigs and VR/MR headset-based pose tracking to vision-only retargeting, exoskeletons, and joystick controllers. Dexora integrates a hybrid approach, combining the precision of exoskeletons for arm kinematics with the convenience of markerless hand tracking (Apple Vision Pro) for fine finger movements. This approach seeks to improve the accuracy and ease of collecting high-DoF, dual-arm, dual-hand demonstrations.
- **Dexterous Manipulation:** The field of dexterous manipulation has seen progress in grasp synthesis (moving from analytical sampling to generative models like diffusion and normalizing flows) and policy learning (via reinforcement learning and imitation learning). While these areas have advanced robustness and sim-to-real transfer, many pipelines remain hand-centric, reward-sensitive, and limited in multi-arm coordination. Dexora differentiates itself by pursuing a VLA model that operates in a high-dimensional, dual-arm, dual-hand action space, with an end-to-end learning paradigm.
- **VLA Models:** Current VLA models, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, and GR00T N1, have made strides in leveraging large-scale data and language understanding for robot control. However, these often simplify the embodiment to single-arm grippers or bimanual parallel-jaw grippers. Dexora's contribution is in extending the VLA paradigm to simultaneously learn synchronized arm-hand trajectories in a 36-DoF action space, proposing a pathway for policies trained in rich dexterous settings to generalize to simpler embodiments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and release Dexora, an open-source Vision-Language-Action (VLA) system capable of natively controlling dual-arm, dual-hand robots with 36 degrees of freedom (DoF). This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and sub-objectives:

- **Addressing the Embodiment Gap in VLA Models:** The core motivation stems from the limitations of current VLA systems, which are typically restricted to either low-DoF dual-gripper control or single-arm dexterous hand manipulation. This prevents them from performing tasks that inherently require both dual-arm coordination and high-DoF dexterous fingers. For example, tasks like piston insertion necessitate two arms, while bottle opening or retrieving objects from a cluttered shelf require the complex articulation of fingers. Dexora aims to fill this void by unifying these capabilities within a single system.

- **Enabling Scalable and High-Fidelity Data Collection for Complex Tasks:** Acquiring demonstration data for robots with 36 DoF across two arms and two hands is inherently challenging. The research aims to design and implement a novel hybrid teleoperation pipeline that simplifies the collection of such complex manipulation data. This pipeline is motivated by the need to decouple gross arm kinematics from fine finger motions, thereby making long data collection sessions practical, accurate, and operator-friendly. Furthermore, the system is designed to drive both a physical robot and an identical digital twin in MuJoCo, enabling scalable and embodiment-matched data generation that reduces the sim-to-real gap.

- **Constructing a Large-Scale, Embodiment-Matched Dataset:** A significant objective is to assemble a comprehensive training corpus that supports the learning of dual-arm, dual-hand dexterity. This includes generating a large synthetic dataset to provide scale and task diversity, complemented by a real-world teleoperated dataset to introduce fine-grained realism, particularly for complex dexterous tasks that are difficult to simulate. The motivation is to establish a foundational dataset that can facilitate the development of VLA models in realistic, high-DoF settings.

- **Mitigating the Impact of Noisy Teleoperation Data:** Teleoperated demonstrations often suffer from noise, instability, and variability due to human factors, sensing errors, and latency. Training VLA models on such heterogeneous data without careful consideration can degrade policy learning. A key objective is to develop a data-quality-aware training recipe that can discern and down-weight low-quality demonstrations, thereby stabilizing the learning process and ensuring the policy primarily benefits from high-quality trajectories. This involves designing a system to establish explicit quality criteria and a discriminator model to score demonstration quality.

- **Achieving High Performance and Robust Generalization:** The research aims for Dexora to outperform existing VLA baselines on both basic and dexterous manipulation benchmarks, demonstrating its ability to handle complex bimanual coordination and in-hand manipulation. A further objective is to investigate the generalization capabilities of a policy trained in a rich, high-DoF setting, specifically testing its robustness to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios and its ability to transfer to simpler, lower-DoF embodiments (e.g., single-arm grippers or dual-arm grippers) without requiring extensive re-architecture. This explores the hypothesis that training in a high-DoF space might provide a more "universal" controller.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Dexora employs a multi-faceted approach encompassing hardware design, data collection, and a novel VLA training framework.

#### 3.1. A. Dual-Arm Dual-Hand System

The physical platform integrates two 6-DoF AIRBOT robotic arms, each equipped with a 12-DoF XHAND dexterous hand. Each XHAND features 12 independently actuated joints, including lateral ab/adduction for the thumb and index fingers, enabling human-like in-hand reorientation and torsional manipulation. This results in a total of 36 DoF for manipulation.

#### 3.2. B. Hybrid Teleoperation Pipeline

To facilitate data collection for this high-DoF system, a hybrid teleoperation interface was designed:
- **Gross Arm Motion:** A custom, lightweight dual-arm exoskeleton backpack captures the operator's shoulder-elbow-wrist angles. These angles are directly mapped to the robot's joint space, aiming to provide drift-free, low-latency arm trajectories without the issues (jitter, singularities) common in vision-only retargeting methods.
- **Fine Finger Control:** Markerless 3D finger skeletons are provided by Apple Vision Pro, which are then retargeted to the XHANDs after a brief calibration. Joint limits and safety constraints are enforced during this process. This glove-free input aims for operator convenience during extended data collection.
- **Digital Twin Integration:** The interface simultaneously drives both the physical robot and a MuJoCo-based digital twin that mirrors the robot's kinematics and controllers. This allows operators to seamlessly collect demonstrations in both real and simulated environments, ensuring embodiment-matched data and reducing the sim-to-real gap. All sensing streams (four RGB views, 36-DoF joint states) are logged at 20 Hz, time-aligned.

#### 3.3. C. Dataset Construction

A large-scale, embodiment-matched dataset was assembled based on the principle of sim-real complementarity:
- **Synthetic Data:**
 - **Object and Task Generation:** Qwen2.5-VL was used to mine Objaverse for manipulable objects and automatically assign physical parameters. A set of 200 tasks across three basic families (Pick-and-Place, Assemble/Disassemble, Articulated Objects) was created.
 - **Trajectory Synthesis:** For each task, 3-5 teleoperated "seed" demonstrations were collected. The DexMimicGen recipe was then applied to synthesize an additional 500 trajectories per task by randomizing initial states and retargeting seed actions. Scene layouts and success criteria were auto-generated by Qwen.
 - **Scale:** The synthetic dataset comprises 100,000 trajectories (361 hours, 6.5 million frames), all logged with the same observation-action protocol as the real system.
- **Real-World Data:**
 - **Scope:** Data was collected on the physical robot. Beyond basic tasks, dexterous tool-use scenarios and bimanual dexterous scenes, challenging to simulate, were included.
 - **Scale:** 200 tasks were curated, with 50 teleoperated demonstrations collected per task, totaling 10,000 episodes (177.5 hours, 3.2 million frames).
 - **Standardization:** All recordings were converted to the LIBERO-2.1 standard and open-sourced. This dataset is intended to fine-tune the VLA model, specializing basic competence into dexterous, bimanual skills.

#### 3.4. D. Framework: Discriminator-Guided Quality-Aware Training

To address the variability and noise inherent in teleoperated data, Dexora implements a discriminator-guided training approach:
- **Data Quality Criteria:** Episode-level quality is assessed using two pillars:
 - **Pre-screening:** Kinematic smoothness and steadiness, quantified by low root mean square (RMS) acceleration (A_ep) and jerk (J_ep) across time and dimensions. Episodes in the lowest 20% for both metrics are selected, retaining approximately 18% of the data.
 - **Post-validation:** From the pre-screened set, positive demonstrations (S_high) are designated by open-loop replay success (task completion without collisions). This yields approximately 15% high-quality demonstrations. Quality is scored at the episode level, with a movement-coverage guard, to prevent trivially low metrics from stationary, uninformative chunks.
- **Discriminator Model:**
 - **Input:** For each episode, K sub-clips are uniformly sampled. Each clip's input token sequence includes the proprioceptive state, action chunk (length L), and a log-π chunk score. This log-π score, a policy-compatibility proxy, is derived from a pre-trained diffusion policy, representing how well the policy explains the given action chunk (higher score indicates better explanation). Multi-view RGB observations and language instructions are concatenated as a condition stream.
 - **Architecture:** A shallow stack of Transformer blocks processes the tokens, followed by global averaging and a small MLP head with a sigmoid activation to output a clip quality score d(C_k) ∈ (0, 1].
 - **Training:** The discriminator is trained with a positive-unlabeled objective, using the replay-validated high-quality subset (S_high) as positives and the remaining real data (D_real \ S_high) as unlabeled. This objective seeks to assign high scores to high-quality clips and low scores to others.
- **Diffusion Transformer (Policy Model):**
 - **Architecture:** A decoder-only Transformer serves as the diffusion model for the policy.
 - **Input:** Current observation (o_t), language instruction (ℓ), current joint angle state (s_t), and noisy actions (a_t:t+L-1) are projected into a latent space. Language and multi-view image inputs are encoded by T5 and SigLip encoders, respectively, and alternately injected as conditional tokens.
 - **Output:** The model predicts the action noise, which is then used to denoise and generate the predicted action sequence (a_t:t+L-1). Standard DDPM is used for sampling during training, and DPMSolver++ for acceleration during inference.

#### 3.5. E. Data-Quality-Aware Training Recipe

The VLA model training proceeds in three stages:
1. **Pre-training:** The diffusion-transformer policy (π_θ) is initially pre-trained on the large synthetic dataset to establish basic manipulation competence.
2. **Discriminator Training:** With the pre-trained policy frozen, the discriminator model is trained using the real-world dataset. The log-π proxy, computed from the pre-trained policy, along with the defined quality criteria (kinematic smoothness, replay success), is used to train the discriminator to score demonstration quality.
3. **Post-training:** The diffusion-transformer policy (π_θ) is then post-trained on the real-world dataset. During this stage, the discriminator's scores d(C_k) are converted into weights (w_i), which are incorporated into the diffusion loss (L_π). This weighting mechanism down-weights low-quality clips, ensuring the policy primarily learns from higher-quality demonstrations, with a short weight warm-up period.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Dexora focused on its performance on basic and dexterous manipulation tasks, its generalization capabilities, and the contributions of its core methodological components.

#### 4.1. A. Evaluation Results in Real World

**Basic Tasks Evaluation:** Dexora was evaluated on a suite of 12 basic tasks, categorized into Pick-and-Place (5 tasks), Assemble/Disassemble (5 tasks), and Articulated Objects (2 tasks). These tasks included both single-hand and bimanual scenarios.
- **Quantitative Performance:** Dexora achieved an average success rate of 89.6%, outperforming baselines such as GR00T N1 (82.1%), π0 (50.4%), and Diffusion Policy (DP) (34.2%).
- **Task-Specific Performance:** Dexora demonstrated high success (≥90%) on 7 out of 12 tasks and consistently showed strong performance on bimanual tasks, such as "Separate nested bowls" (80% success) and "Lift basket" (80% success), which require coordinated dual-arm actions.
- **Baseline Comparison:** GR00T N1 was competitive on simpler, mostly single-hand tasks. π0 showed significant degradation when its gripper-centric action space was mapped to high-DoF hands, suggesting that low-to-high DoF mapping without embodiment-matched data is problematic. These results indicate the importance of embodiment-matched, high-DoF data for effective policy learning, particularly for bimanual coordination.

**Dexterous Manipulation Tasks Evaluation:** Six dexterous tasks, requiring in-hand tool use and complex coordinated bimanual manipulation, were used for benchmarking.
- **Quantitative Performance:** Dexora achieved the best average success rate of 66.7%, significantly higher than GR00T N1 (51.7%), π0 (26.7%), and DP (6.7%).
- **Capabilities Demonstrated:** Dexora successfully performed tasks like "Use pen" (65% success), where the robot executes a handover and then presses the pen tip to write, and "Cut leek" (80% success), involving one hand stabilizing an object while the other uses a tool. These results highlight Dexora's ability to leverage its 12-DoF hands and bimanual training for in-hand manipulation and dual-arm coordination.
- **Limitations:** The "Twist cap" task exhibited the lowest success rate (25%) across all models, including Dexora. This was attributed to the task's requirement for precise normal-force regulation, fingertip friction, and fine in-hand alignment to generate stable torsional wrench while preventing slip, which is challenging without tactile feedback and with relatively low-friction rigid fingertip pads.

#### 4.2. B. Generalization

**Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** Dexora's OOD robustness was tested on the "Pick apple to the plate" task under six varied conditions: unseen background, unseen lighting, unseen object, occlusion, clutter, and height change.
- **Performance:** Dexora maintained high success rates across all six OOD conditions, indicating strong generalization capabilities to visual and environmental shifts.

**Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** The research explored whether a policy trained on a high-DoF dual-arm, dual-hand platform could generalize to lower-DoF embodiments.
- **Tested Configurations:** The Dexora policy was transferred to three configurations:
 - EC-1: Single-arm gripper (Franka Emika Panda, 6-DoF arm + 1-DoF gripper).
 - EC-2: Dual-arm grippers (Cobot Magic ALOHA, 2 × (6-DoF arm + 1-DoF gripper)).
 - EC-3: Single-arm single-hand (Unitree G1 7-DoF arm + Inspire Hand 6-DoF).
- **Adaptation Method:** Adaptation involved padding unused action dimensions and masking absent camera observations, followed by fine-tuning with 100 demonstrations per task.
- **Results:** The policy successfully transferred to these simpler embodiments, completing tasks such as a three-step pepper handover (EC-2). Grasping tasks transferred readily. The performance on dexterity-demanding tasks showed larger gaps, supporting the hypothesis that projecting a high-DoF policy to lower-DoF embodiments (dimension reduction) is a better-posed problem than "lifting" a gripper policy to dexterous hands (synthesis).

#### 4.3. C. Ablation Study

**Effectiveness of Training Data Composition:** Three post-training regimes were compared: "Sim Only," "Sim + 50% Real," and "Sim + All Real."
- **Findings:** Success rates consistently improved with the inclusion of more real-world data. Dexterous tasks showed the most substantial gains, with "Use pen" increasing from 0% (Sim Only) to 65% (Sim + All Real) and "Cut leek" from 10% to 85%. This indicates that while simulation data is effective for bootstrapping basic skills, real-world data, especially complex dexterous tasks, is crucial for developing advanced dexterous capabilities.

**Effectiveness of Discriminator Model:** The impact of the learned data-quality discriminator was evaluated by comparing vanilla post-training with quality-aware post-training.
- **Findings:** The discriminator improved task success rates and reduced kinematic metrics (mean normalized joint acceleration and jerk) during inference. For "Corn → plate," success increased from 85% to 95%, with reduced acceleration and jerk. For "Lift basket," success rose from 55% to 80% with similar kinematic improvements.
- **Qualitative Observations:** The quality-aware model executed smoother and more coherent motions. Visual analysis of joint trajectories showed lower variance and fewer reversals, mitigating issues like high-frequency oscillations that could lead to task failure (e.g., dropping objects due to jitter). This demonstrates that the discriminator effectively helps the policy learn from mixed-quality demonstrations by prioritizing and weighting high-quality segments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Dexora represents a notable contribution to the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation, particularly in the domain of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

**Advancement in Embodied AI and VLA Models:** This work addresses a critical gap in existing VLA systems by unifying dual-arm, dual-hand, and high-DoF dexterity within a single open-source framework. Prior VLA models typically focused on either low-DoF grippers or single-arm dexterous hands, leaving a substantial range of complex manipulation tasks unaddressed. Dexora's capability to natively control a 36-DoF robot for bimanual dexterous tasks pushes the boundaries of what VLA systems can achieve, moving towards more generalist and human-like robotic manipulation.

**Novel Methodology for Data Collection and Training:** The introduction of a hybrid teleoperation pipeline, combining exoskeleton-based arm control with Apple Vision Pro for markerless finger tracking, offers a practical and scalable solution for collecting high-fidelity, high-DoF demonstration data. Furthermore, the discriminator-guided quality-aware training recipe provides a mechanism to effectively learn from noisy teleoperated demonstrations. By prioritizing high-quality clips and down-weighting suboptimal ones, this approach enhances policy stability and performance, a significant challenge in real-world imitation learning.

**Pathway Towards Universal Robot Controllers:** A key finding is Dexora's ability to generalize across different embodiments. The learned policy, trained on a complex dual-arm, dual-hand platform, successfully transfers to simpler configurations like single-arm grippers or dual-arm grippers with minimal adaptation. This supports the hypothesis that training in a rich, high-DoF action space can yield a more broadly applicable controller, suggesting a viable route for developing universal robot policies that can be projected to control a variety of robotic hardware. This could streamline robot development and deployment across different applications.

**Catalyst for Future Research:** The release of Dexora's models, data, and code as open-source resources is poised to catalyze further research in several promising directions:
- **Contact-aware Control:** The identified limitation in tasks like cap twisting due to the absence of tactile feedback highlights the need for integrating tactile sensing into VLA models to achieve more robust contact-rich manipulation.
- **Long-horizon Reasoning and Hierarchical Planning:** The current work primarily focuses on reactive control. Future efforts could explore combining Dexora's dexterous capabilities with memory, subgoal decomposition, and language-guided tool use to enable long-horizon and more complex robotic task execution.
- **Data Quality and Curation:** The success of the discriminator emphasizes the ongoing importance of data quality in robot learning. Further research could explore more sophisticated methods for identifying, curating, and leveraging mixed-quality data.

In summary, Dexora represents a significant step forward in enabling robots to perform complex, dual-arm, dual-hand dexterous manipulation through VLA models, with implications for the development of more capable and versatile robotic assistants.
