# Metis-RISE: RL Incentivizes and SFT Enhances Multimodal Reasoning Model Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Meituan introduces Metis-RISE, a hybrid training framework that first uses Reinforcement Learning to activate latent multimodal reasoning in MLLMs, then applies Supervised Fine-Tuning to refine these abilities. The approach yields state-of-the-art performance on reasoning benchmarks for both 7B and 72B parameter open-source models, demonstrating competitive results against leading proprietary models.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) only methods for MLLMs are sample-inefficient and struggle to activate reasoning abilities not already somewhat present in the base model.
- Conventional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) before RL restricts a model's exploratory capacity, limiting discovery of new reasoning pathways during the RL phase.
- Models, even after RL, often exhibit inconsistent application of correct logic (inefficient trajectory sampling) or completely lack fundamental reasoning capabilities for certain problems.

## Method

- A two-stage hybrid training framework: Reinforcement Learning (RL) for incentivization, followed by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for enhancement, notably omitting an initial cold-start SFT.
- The initial RL phase, using a modified ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== and a ==hybrid format/accuracy reward==, directly encourages exploration and activates latent reasoning without prior SFT constraints.
- The subsequent SFT phase uses a curated dataset combining ==self-distilled successful trajectories== (for consistent application) and expert-augmented solutions (for absent capabilities) to refine and solidify the model's reasoning.

## Results

- Metis-RISE-7B achieved an average score of **46.4** on multimodal reasoning benchmarks, establishing state-of-the-art performance among open-source models in the ≤10B parameter category.
- Metis-RISE-72B reached an average score of **56.6**, becoming the top-performing open-source model in the >10B category and demonstrating competitiveness with proprietary models like ChatGPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-Pro.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the initial RL phase boosted performance by **4.8 points**, and the subsequent SFT enhancement phase further improved it by **2.4 points**, validating the cumulative benefits of the proposed two-stage approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Initiating training with Reinforcement Learning (RL) is more effective for activating latent reasoning and encouraging exploration in MLLMs than starting with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
> - A targeted SFT phase, following RL, can specifically address inconsistent reasoning application and fill fundamental knowledge gaps, further enhancing overall model performance.
> - The synergistic combination of RL-first incentivization and problem-specific SFT enhancement leads to cumulative improvements in multimodal reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of advanced reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) is a paramount focus in contemporary AI research. Recent breakthroughs, such as DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI-O1, and Seed1.5-Thinking, have demonstrated the efficacy of Reinforcement Learning (RL) in enhancing LLMs' reasoning prowess, enabling them to tackle increasingly complex tasks. This success has naturally spurred interest in applying similar RL paradigms to the multimodal domain, with a new wave of MLLMs aiming to achieve sophisticated reasoning by integrating visual and textual information.

However, the authors identify several critical limitations within existing approaches that Metis-RISE seeks to overcome. Current methods often either:
1. **Rely solely on RL training:** While powerful, RL-only approaches can suffer from sample inefficiency, making it difficult to learn effectively, and may struggle to "activate" reasoning capabilities that are entirely absent in the foundation model.
2. **Employ a conventional "cold-start" Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) before RL:** This pipeline, common in many applications, can inadvertently restrict the model's exploratory capacity during the subsequent RL phase, potentially leading to suboptimal convergence and limiting the discovery of novel reasoning pathways. Furthermore, many studies have been conducted on relatively small models (e.g., 3B or 7B parameters), raising questions about the generalizability and robustness of their findings to larger scales.

Metis-RISE addresses these challenges by proposing a novel, non-traditional sequence of training stages. Instead of the typical SFT-then-RL, it uniquely starts with an RL phase, followed by a strategically designed SFT stage. This "RL-incentivizes, SFT-enhances" philosophy positions Metis-RISE as a significant contribution to the evolving landscape of MLLM training, offering a more effective pathway to unlock and refine complex multimodal reasoning abilities, particularly at scale.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Metis-RISE is to develop a hybrid training framework that more effectively boosts the multimodal reasoning capabilities of MLLMs, addressing the shortcomings of conventional RL-only or SFT-first pipelines. The key motivations are rooted in overcoming two distinct problems:

1. **To activate latent reasoning and improve exploration efficiency:** Existing RL-only methods are often sample-inefficient and struggle to activate reasoning capabilities that are not already somewhat present in the base model. Metis-RIis motivated to use an initial RL phase to proactively *incentivize* the model's exploration and activate its latent reasoning potential without the constraints imposed by early SFT.
2. **To enhance and solidify reasoning capacities by addressing specific deficiencies:** Even after RL, models face challenges such as "inefficient trajectory sampling" (where the model possesses the correct logic but applies it inconsistently) and "fundamental capability absence" (where the model entirely lacks the necessary reasoning for certain problems). The motivation for the subsequent SFT phase is to strategically *enhance* the model by directly targeting these two issues using a carefully curated dataset.

In summary, Metis-RISE aims to bypass the limitations of existing training paradigms by orchestrating RL and SFT in a synergistic and sequential manner: RL first to encourage discovery and initial activation, and then SFT to refine and inject specific knowledge based on identified gaps, thereby leading to robust and advanced multimodal reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Metis-RISE introduces a two-stage hybrid training framework: **RL Incentivization** followed by **SFT Enhancement**, distinctively omitting an initial cold-start SFT phase.

#### 3.1. RL Incentivization Phase

The initial phase directly begins with Reinforcement Learning. The rationale for this is empirical observation suggesting that early SFT can restrict a model's exploratory capacity during subsequent RL training.

- **Core RL Algorithm:** Metis-RISE adopts **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** as its foundational RL algorithm. GRPO is chosen for its effectiveness in aligning model behavior with preferences and its computational efficiency compared to standard PPO. GRPO works by estimating the advantages of model-generated responses by comparing a group of candidate outputs for the same query. The advantage for a trajectory is computed by normalizing its reward against the mean and standard deviation of rewards within its group.
- **Incorporated Modifications to GRPO:** To further enhance stability and effectiveness, Metis-RISE integrates several modifications inspired by recent advancements in reasoning-oriented LLMs (DAPO, VAPO):
 - **Elimination of KL Divergence:** The traditional KL divergence term, which limits policy deviation from a reference, is removed. This allows greater exploration, recognizing that evolving reasoning models may significantly diverge from their initial state.
 - **Online Data Filtering:** Only prompts yielding "meaningful gradient signals" (i.e., those with success rates strictly between 0 and 1 across multiple attempts) are used for training. This prevents gradient attenuation from consistently failed or perfectly solved prompts.
 - **Asymmetric Clipping:** The conventional single clipping parameter (ε) is decoupled into ε_low and ε_high. Increasing ε_high allows for greater increases in low-probability tokens, fostering broader exploration of the response space.
 - **Token-Level Policy Loss:** The policy loss is calculated at the token level by aggregating across individual tokens, ensuring appropriate weighting of each token's influence on gradient updates.
 - **Soft Overlong Punishment:** A proportional penalty is applied for responses exceeding a predefined maximum length within a specified punishment interval.
- **Reward Design:** The RL phase utilizes verifiable data and a hybrid reward structure:
 - **Format Reward:** A binary reward (1 or 0) is given if the model adheres to a predefined output format, specifically enclosing thinking processes in `<think>` tags and the final answer in `<answer>` tags. This enforces structured output necessary for verification and clear reasoning.
 - **Accuracy Reward:** After satisfying the format, a rule-based verifier assesses the semantic correctness of the answer. A value of 1 is assigned for correct answers, 0 otherwise. This provides direct supervision for accurate reasoning.
 - A system prompt is used to guide the model to output its thinking process and final answer in the specified LaTeX format with a `\boxed{}` command.

#### 3.2. SFT Enhancement Phase

Following the RL incentivization, a targeted Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) phase is introduced to address two specific limitations identified during the RL stage: inefficient trajectory sampling and fundamental capability absence. The SFT dataset is meticulously curated from a prompt pool P:

- **Self-Distilled Reasoning Trajectories (for inefficient sampling):** For prompts where the RL-trained model demonstrates inconsistent success (correctness score between 0 and 1 after K-shot sampling), one *correct* trajectory generated by the RL model itself is randomly selected. This self-distilled trajectory serves as a supervision signal, reinforcing effective reasoning paths that the model can discover but doesn't consistently apply.
- **Expert-Augmented Knowledge Injection (for capability absence):** For prompts where the RL model consistently fails (correctness score of 0 across all attempts), it's inferred that the model fundamentally lacks the necessary reasoning capability. In these cases, a stronger external reasoning expert (e.g., advanced proprietary models) generates high-quality solutions. These expert-generated trajectories are then injected into the SFT dataset to impart new knowledge.
- **Dataset Construction:** The final SFT dataset (`D_SFT`) is a combination of these self-distilled and expert-augmented subsets. Filtering criteria were applied, e.g., removing true/false questions and multiple-choice questions with low pass rates, to mitigate reward hacking.

This two-pronged SFT approach allows the model to both consolidate its existing, but inconsistent, reasoning skills and acquire entirely new capabilities, thereby leading to improvements in consistency and coverage of correct reasoning paths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Metis-RISE was used to train two MLLM variants: Metis-RISE-7B and Metis-RISE-72B, built upon the Qwen2.5-VL series. Evaluations were conducted using VLMEvalKit on the OpenCompass Multimodal Reasoning Leaderboard, covering six challenging benchmarks: MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse, DynaMath, WeMath, and LogicVista.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - **Metis-RISE-7B** achieved an average score of 46.4, surpassing all other open-source models in the ≤10B parameter category (e.g., VLAA-Thinker-7B at 42.5, InternVL3-8B at 41.4). This establishes it as the state-of-the-art for its size on these multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
 - **Metis-RISE-72B** achieved an impressive average score of 56.6, making it the top-performing model in the >10B open-source category, significantly outperforming models like InternVL3-78B (51.0) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (50.3).
 - Notably, Metis-RISE-72B's performance is competitive with, and in some cases exceeds, prominent proprietary models like ChatGPT-4o-202504 (54.8) and Claude 3.7 Sonnet (50.4), matching Gemini-2.0-Pro (56.6). It ranked fourth overall on the OpenCompass Multimodal Reasoning Leaderboard at the time of evaluation.

- **Ablation Study:** An ablation study on Metis-RISE-7B clearly demonstrated the cumulative benefits of each stage:
 - **Baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B):** 39.2 average score.
 - **Baseline → RL Incentivization:** Average score increased by **4.8 points to 44.0**. This highlights RL's effectiveness in incentivizing exploration and activating latent reasoning.
 - **RL → SFT Enhancement (Mixed SFT):** Further improved the average score by **2.4 points to 46.4**. This demonstrates SFT's crucial role in refining and consolidating reasoning, addressing issues like inconsistent application and knowledge gaps. Different SFT variants (MM SFT, Text SFT, Mixed SFT) all contributed positively, with Mixed SFT (combining multimodal and textual SFT data) yielding the best results.
 - Similar progressive improvements were observed with Metis-RISE-72B (baseline 50.3 → RL 55.2 → SFT 56.6), validating the general effectiveness and scalability of the multi-stage approach.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

- **Training Dynamics (Metis-RISE-72B):** During the initial RL phase, a consistent increase in accuracy reward was observed, coupled with a corresponding upward trend in the average response length. This suggests that the RL model learns to engage in more extensive and elaborate "chain-of-thought" reasoning, which directly correlates with improved problem-solving capabilities.
- **Example Problem Solving:** A detailed example of a multi-step analytic geometry problem (Figure 4) showcased Metis-RISE-72B's strong mathematical reasoning. The model systematically broke down the problem, correctly interpreted geometric concepts, applied formulas, and integrated algebraic manipulations to arrive at the correct solution, demonstrating advanced problem-solving proficiency. Additional examples in the Appendix illustrate capabilities in plane geometry, chart reasoning, logical reasoning, counting, and diagram analysis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Metis-RISE presents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal reasoning for large language models, offering a potent pathway to developing more capable and reliable MLLMs.

The primary significance lies in its **novel training paradigm**: the strategic sequencing of RL (incentivization) before SFT (enhancement). By departing from the conventional SFT-first approach, Metis-RISE successfully mitigates critical limitations:
1. **Enhanced Exploration and Latent Capability Activation:** The initial RL phase effectively activates latent reasoning abilities and overcomes the sample inefficiency often associated with RL-only methods, allowing the model to explore and discover reasoning pathways more freely.
2. **Targeted Refinement and Knowledge Injection:** The subsequent SFT phase is intelligently designed to address specific weaknesses—inconsistent reasoning (via self-distillation) and fundamental knowledge gaps (via expert augmentation). This targeted approach ensures that the model not only consolidates existing, albeit unreliable, skills but also acquires entirely new ones.
3. **Scalability and State-of-the-Art Performance:** The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across both 7B and 72B parameter models, including competitiveness with leading proprietary models, underscores the framework's scalability and effectiveness. This indicates that the proposed methodology is robust and can yield high-performing models across different scales.

The potential impact of Metis-RISE is substantial:
- **Improved Multimodal AI Systems:** The ability to equip MLLMs with stronger, more consistent reasoning capabilities will directly lead to more intelligent and versatile AI systems capable of understanding and interacting with complex visual and textual information in diverse applications, from scientific research and education to real-world problem-solving.
- **A New Blueprint for MLLM Training:** Metis-RISE offers a validated alternative to existing MLLM training pipelines. Researchers and developers can adopt this "RL-incentivizes, SFT-enhances" strategy to improve their own models, potentially accelerating advancements in multimodal reasoning.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** Meituan's commitment to open-sourcing Metis-RISE models and code will foster collaboration and further innovation within the AI community, allowing others to build upon this foundational work.

Looking ahead, the authors propose exciting future work, including iterative application of the Metis-RISE framework (RL → SFT → RL → SFT...) to continuously push reasoning boundaries, and extending the framework to non-verifiable data domains by developing model-based verifiers. These avenues promise to broaden the applicability and refine the capabilities of MLLMs even further, expanding their utility beyond formally verifiable tasks like mathematics to more nuanced, open-ended multimodal reasoning challenges.
