# Temporally Consistent Transformers for Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Temporally Consistent Transformers (TECO) were developed for video generation, producing coherent long-form video content over hundreds of frames by addressing temporal inconsistencies. The model established superior long-horizon consistency and achieved sampling speeds 20-60x faster than comparable transformer-based baselines on challenging new 3D environment datasets.

## Problem

- Current video generation models struggle to maintain temporal consistency over long durations, often inventing new content for previously seen areas that reappear.
- There is a lack of standardized benchmarks and metrics specifically designed to rigorously evaluate long-range temporal dependencies in video generation.
- Existing transformer-based approaches for video generation face prohibitive computational costs due to their quadratic complexity when scaled to very long sequences.

## Method

- The Temporally Consistent Transformer (**TECO**) architecture was designed, leveraging ==VQ-GAN== for discrete latent representations, spatial downsampling before a ==temporal transformer==, and a ==spatial MaskGit prior== for efficient per-frame generation.
- **DropLoss** was introduced, selectively computing reconstruction and prior losses on a subset of time indices to enhance training scalability and reduce computational costs for long sequences.
- Three new long-horizon video datasets (DMLab, Minecraft, Habitat) were curated by rendering long walks through complex 3D scenes to serve as benchmarks for evaluating temporal consistency.

## Results

- TECO consistently surpassed all baseline models on new long-range consistency benchmarks, achieving an FVD of **48** on DMLab compared to **96** for the next best baseline (Perceiver AR).
- The model demonstrated significantly faster sampling speeds, performing **20-60x** faster than comparable transformer-based baselines like Perceiver AR and Latent FDM while maintaining high sample quality.
- Qualitatively, TECO was uniquely capable of generating 3D mazes in DMLab that maintained coherence with ground truth over long traversals, avoiding inconsistent or fictitious scene elements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning more compact representations through bottlenecking helps models prioritize and better learn long-horizon temporal information over fine image fidelity details.
> - Training efficiency for long sequences can be substantially improved by selectively applying computationally intensive loss calculations (DropLoss) without significant performance degradation.
> - The use of VQ-latent dynamics with a MaskGit prior significantly outperforms alternative latent dynamics models in terms of maintaining temporal consistency and overall sample quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in video generation have yielded models capable of producing high-fidelity and diverse video samples, primarily attributed to increased computational resources and efficient neural architectures. This progress, however, has largely been concentrated on generating short video sequences. When these models are applied to longer horizons, they tend to exhibit a fundamental limitation: temporal inconsistency. Specifically, if generated content moves out of view and later reappears, the model frequently "invents" new, different content rather than reproducing the previously observed scene.

Existing research has explored various strategies to address long-term dependencies in video, including hierarchical latent variables (e.g., Clockwork-VAE), strided sampling with frame interpolation, training on sparse sets of frames, and modeling videos through compressed representations. Despite these efforts, many current methods encounter difficulties when scaling to datasets with numerous long-range dependencies. Transformer-based approaches, while powerful, face challenges due to their quadratic computational complexity with respect to sequence length, making them computationally intensive for very long videos. Recurrent models, such as Clockwork-VAE, also face limitations in training time and scalability to complex data.

Furthermore, a notable gap in the research landscape is the absence of established benchmarks designed specifically for rigorously evaluating temporal consistency in video generation, particularly for complex data with long-range dependencies. Existing metrics like FVD (Frechet Video Distance) are often more sensitive to image fidelity rather than long-range temporal coherence, and many datasets do not intrinsically possess strong long-range dependencies that necessitate advanced modeling techniques. This paper positions itself by identifying and addressing this dual challenge: the lack of robust models for long-horizon temporal consistency and the deficiency of suitable evaluation benchmarks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance video generation capabilities by developing a model that can produce temporally consistent videos over extended horizons, thereby overcoming the limitations of current approaches that struggle with long-range dependencies.

The motivation for this work stems from several detailed considerations:

1. **Addressing Temporal Inconsistency in Long Videos:** Current video generation algorithms, while proficient in short-term predictions, frequently fail to maintain consistency over longer durations. This manifests as models generating new, inconsistent content when a camera revisits a previously observed area, rather than reproducing the original scene elements. This "temporal inconsistency" is a significant hurdle for applications requiring coherent and predictable long-term video sequences.
2. **Lack of Standardized Benchmarks for Long-Range Consistency:** A critical observation is the absence of established benchmarks specifically designed to rigorously evaluate long-range temporal dependencies in video generation models. Existing datasets often do not present strong long-range consistency challenges, and conventional metrics like FVD prioritize image fidelity more than the intricate temporal coherence required for dynamic, interactive environments. This deficiency makes it difficult to quantitatively assess and compare models' abilities to handle long-term consistency.
3. **Computational Inefficiency of Existing Methods:** Many state-of-the-art video generation models, particularly those based on transformers over latent spaces, incur prohibitive computational costs when scaled to long video sequences. The quadratic complexity of attention mechanisms in transformers becomes a bottleneck for videos containing hundreds or thousands of frames, limiting their practical applicability and training scalability. Other methods, like recurrent networks, also face training time limitations.
4. **Enabling Advanced Applications:** The ability to generate accurate and temporally consistent long videos is foundational for various advanced applications. These include improved synthetic data generation for training embodied AI agents, more realistic simulations for robotics, and enhanced tools for content creation and virtual environments where maintaining environmental continuity is essential.

Based on these motivations, the paper sets out the following key objectives:

- **Curate and Introduce Challenging Long-Horizon Video Datasets:** To fill the benchmark gap, the authors aim to create three new datasets (DMLab, Minecraft, Habitat) by rendering long walks through complex 3D scenes. These datasets are explicitly designed to contain long-range dependencies, requiring models to remember and consistently regenerate previously observed content.
- **Perform Comprehensive Benchmarking:** To understand the current state of the art, the paper intends to conduct a detailed evaluation of existing video generation models on these newly introduced challenging datasets, specifically analyzing their performance in learning long-horizon dependencies.
- **Develop an Efficient and Scalable Generative Model (TECO):** The core objective is to propose a novel generative model, the Temporally Consistent Transformer (TECO), designed to overcome the computational and consistency limitations of prior work. TECO is intended to leverage compressed representations and an efficient transformer architecture to model long-term dependencies effectively.
- **Demonstrate Superior Performance and Efficiency:** The ultimate goal is to show that TECO can achieve substantial improvements in long-term temporal consistency and sample quality across various difficult video prediction tasks, while simultaneously offering significantly faster sampling speeds compared to existing models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Temporally Consistent Transformer (TECO) model and a new benchmark suite to evaluate video generation with long temporal dependencies.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: VQ-GAN and MaskGit

The proposed method builds upon existing generative model components:
- **VQ-GAN (Vector-Quantized Generative Adversarial Network):** This autoencoder is used to compress video frames into discrete latent representations. It consists of an encoder (E), a decoder (G), a codebook (C) of embeddings, and a discriminator (D). The encoder maps a high-dimensional image (x) to a lower-dimensional latent representation (h), which is then quantized by finding the nearest neighbor in the codebook to produce discrete latents (z). The decoder reconstructs the image from these latents. Training involves a VQ loss (Equation 1), a perceptual loss ($L_{LPIPS}$), and an adversarial loss ($L_{GAN}$) to ensure high-fidelity reconstructions.
- **MaskGit:** This model is employed to learn distributions over discrete tokens, such as those produced by a VQ-GAN. It operates by masking a portion of tokens in a sequence and training a transformer to predict the masked tokens. During inference, MaskGit uses an iterative sampling process, generating tokens in chunks and refining them, which is designed to be more efficient than purely autoregressive approaches.

#### 3.2. TECO Architectural Design

TECO is designed to efficiently model long sequences by adopting a specific architecture that contrasts with prior spatio-temporal transformers which model all codes directly, leading to quadratic complexity issues. TECO components include:
- **Encoder:** This CNN module, denoted as $E(x_t, x_{t-1})$, encodes the current frame $x_t$ while conditioned on the previous frame $x_{t-1}$ by channel-wise concatenation. The output is quantized via the codebook $C$ to produce discrete latents $z_t$. An $l_2$-normalization is applied to the codebook and embeddings to enhance codebook utilization.
- **Temporal Transformer:** To manage the computational load of long sequences, TECO first applies a strided convolution to spatially downsample each discrete latent $z_t$. This spatially compressed representation is then fed into a large transformer that models temporal dependencies across the sequence. After processing, a transposed convolution upsamples the representation back to the original spatial resolution of $z_t$, resulting in $h_t = ConvT(Transformer(Conv(z_{<t})))$. This design enables the transformer to focus on temporal relationships in a more compact latent space.
- **Decoder:** The decoder is an upsampling CNN that reconstructs the output frame $\hat{x}_t = D(z_t, h_t)$. Here, $z_t$ represents the posterior at time $t$, and $h_t$ is the temporal transformer's output, summarizing past information. By concatenating $z_t$ and $h_t$ channel-wise, the decoder learns to reconstruct frames while promoting $z_t$ to encode information relative to $h_t$, thus achieving compressed codes. The reconstruction loss is a cross-entropy loss $L_{recon}$.
- **Spatial MaskGit Prior:** A MaskGit model is utilized as a prior, $p(z_t | h_t)$, to predict future discrete latents. During training, a random mask $m_t$ is applied, and the MaskGit learns to predict the masked tokens, conditioned on $h_t$ (concatenated with masked $z_t$). During inference, a chunk-based iterative sample-and-revise mechanism is employed, generating frames in batches and refining them.

The final training objective for TECO combines these components: $L_{TECO} = L_{VQ} + L_{recon} + L_{prior}$.

#### 3.3. DropLoss

To further enhance training scalability and efficiency, the authors introduce "DropLoss." This technique involves computing the reconstruction loss ($L_{recon}$) and the prior loss ($L_{prior}$) only on a randomly selected subset of time indices (e.g., 10%) during each training iteration. The temporal transformer still processes the full sequence to compute $h_t$ for all timesteps, but the computationally intensive decoder and MaskGit operations are selectively applied. This significantly reduces training costs, particularly for longer sequences, while theoretically still enabling the model to learn accurate predictions for all timesteps over many iterations.

#### 3.4. Datasets for Long-Horizon Consistency

To address the lack of suitable benchmarks, the paper introduces three new, challenging video datasets, along with evaluation metrics tailored for long-range consistency:
- **DMLab-40k:** Consists of 40,000 action-conditioned 64x64 videos, each 300 frames long, depicting an agent navigating procedurally generated 7x7 3D mazes with varying textures. This dataset requires models to revisit and consistently render parts of the maze.
- **Minecraft-200k:** Comprises 200,000 action-conditioned 128x128 videos, 300 frames long, of a player traversing procedurally generated Minecraft marsh biomes. The agent's movement causes parts of the scene to go out of view and reappear.
- **Habitat-200k:** Features 200,000 action-conditioned 128x128 videos, 300 frames long, generated from real 3D indoor scans (HM3D, MatterPort3D, Gibson). An agent navigates between random locations, requiring consistent scene reproduction.
- **Kinetics-600:** A large-scale real-world video dataset (400,000+ videos, up to 300 frames) used for action recognition. It is included to evaluate scaling to complex, natural videos, though it has fewer inherent long-range dependencies.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Metrics and Baselines

- **Metrics:** Standard metrics like FVD, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS are used. However, for a more robust measure of temporal consistency, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS are applied by conditioning models on a large number of past frames (e.g., 144) and actions to reduce stochasticity, expecting a deterministic match with ground truth for observed areas. FVD is used for stochastic generation (e.g., 264 predicted frames conditioned on 36). For 3D environments, 3D visualizations of reconstructed mazes are also used.
- **Baselines:** TECO is compared against state-of-the-art models from various categories: FitVid (variational CNN/LSTM), Clockwork VAE (hierarchical latent variables), Perceiver AR (autoregressive over VQ-GAN latents, designed for long sequences), and Latent FDM (diffusion model trained in latent space). All baselines are trained on VQ codes or VAE latents for fair comparison regarding data compression.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

Models were trained for 1 million iterations on TPU-v3 instances under fixed compute budgets. VQ-GANs were pre-trained separately. All video models output 16x16 grids of discrete latents per frame, irrespective of original video resolution. Ablation studies were conducted on the Something-Something-v2 (SSv2) dataset to evaluate architectural choices.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TECO against various state-of-the-art baselines on both novel and existing datasets yielded several key findings regarding temporal consistency, sample quality, and computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance in Long-Horizon Temporal Consistency

- **DMLab, Minecraft, and Habitat:** TECO consistently outperformed all baseline models across all quantitative metrics (FVD, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) on the three newly introduced datasets designed to test long-range temporal consistency (Table 1). For example, on DMLab, TECO achieved an FVD of 48, significantly lower than Perceiver AR (96), CW-VAE (125), FitVid (176), and Latent FDM (181). Similar trends were observed for PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.
- **Performance vs. Horizon:** Plots illustrating metrics over time (Figure C.1) showed that TECO maintained higher PSNR, SSIM, and lower LPIPS values over hundreds of predicted frames compared to baselines. While most models exhibited a degradation in consistency as the prediction horizon increased, TECO's degradation was substantially slower and less severe, indicating its ability to leverage long-term context more effectively.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations of generated 3D mazes in DMLab (Figure 6) demonstrated that TECO was the only model capable of generating mazes that retained consistency with the ground truth, avoiding fictitious corridors or extending outside the maze boundaries. Baselines often created inconsistent or implausible environments. On Habitat, while all models struggled with the complexity, TECO's FVD was significantly better, and it generated traversals that more closely matched the data distribution compared to baselines that either blurred predictions or exhibited repetitive agent behaviors.

#### 4.2. Enhanced Computational Efficiency

- **Sampling Speed:** TECO demonstrated orders of magnitude faster sampling speeds compared to transformer-based baselines like Perceiver AR and Latent FDM (Figure 1, Figure 5). On Minecraft, TECO's sampling speed was comparably fast to FitVid and CW-VAE (which had lower sample quality) but was 20-60x faster than Perceiver AR and Latent FDM, while still maintaining high sample quality. This efficiency is attributed to its architecture that aggressively downsamples in space before the temporal transformer and uses a spatial MaskGit for parallel per-frame generation.

#### 4.3. Performance on Complex Real-World Data (Kinetics-600)

- Even on Kinetics-600, a dataset with less pronounced long-range dependencies, TECO demonstrated improved FVD (799 without top-k sampling) compared to Latent FDM (960) and Perceiver AR (1022), suggesting that its ability to incorporate longer contexts contributes to more stable and higher-quality generations, even in complex natural videos.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Architectural Insights

- **DropLoss Effectiveness:** Ablations confirmed that DropLoss significantly improved training scalability by reducing training step time (up to 60% faster) with only a minimal increase in FVD (Table F.1a). This efficiency gain is particularly beneficial for training on very long sequences.
- **VQ-latent Dynamics with MaskGit Prior:** The study showed that TECO's approach of using VQ-latent dynamics with a MaskGit prior significantly outperformed alternative formulations for latent dynamics models, such as using one-hot encodings or different prior types (Table F.1b, F.1c).
- **Conditional Encodings:** Conditional encodings, where the current frame is encoded based on the previous frame, were found to learn better representations for video predictions (Table F.1d).
- **Codebook Size:** While an optimal VQ codebook size exists, the performance was not highly sensitive to it, as long as the number of codes was not excessively large (Table F.1e).
- **Comparison to Direct VQ-GAN Latent Prediction:** Models that directly predicted VQ-GAN latents without TECO's latent dynamics exhibited worse temporal consistency and overall sample quality, despite potentially faster training for some configurations (Table F.3). This suggests that TECO's bottlenecking of latent representations helps prioritize learning long-horizon information over imperceptible image statistics.

#### 4.5. General Insights from Training Dynamics

- **Fidelity vs. Long-Range Dependencies Trade-off:** The research identified an inherent trade-off. Allocating computational resources towards higher-resolution representations tends to improve image fidelity, while bottlenecking representations prioritizes learning long-horizon information. TECO's architecture, by learning more compact representations, achieved a better balance in this trade-off, leading to superior overall performance.
- **Training Saturation:** An observed phenomenon was that short-horizon metrics (e.g., FVD for short clips) tended to saturate earlier during training, whereas long-horizon consistency metrics (e.g., LPIPS for full trajectories) continued to improve until the end of training (Figure G.1). This finding reinforces the motivation for efficient architectures that can be trained for more gradient steps given a fixed compute budget.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions to the field of video generation, with potential impacts across various domains.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Long-Horizon Video Consistency

The introduction of TECO represents a notable advancement in the ability of generative models to produce temporally consistent videos over extended horizons (hundreds of frames). Prior models frequently failed to maintain coherence when content reappeared in the frame, a limitation TECO demonstrably mitigates. This capability is critical for moving beyond short, isolated video clips towards generating realistic, continuous, and predictable long-form content.

#### 5.2. Establishment of Robust Benchmarks

The curation of three challenging video datasets (DMLab, Minecraft, Habitat) is a substantial contribution. These datasets are specifically designed with explicit long-range dependencies, compelling future research to address the issue of temporal consistency directly. Coupled with the proposed modified evaluation metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS with extensive conditioning), this work provides a more rigorous and relevant framework for assessing long-horizon video generation capabilities, which was previously lacking in the research community.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Computational Efficiency and Scalability

TECO's architectural innovations, particularly its spatial downsampling before the temporal transformer and the use of a spatial MaskGit, along with the DropLoss technique, significantly improve training efficiency and reduce sampling time. This addresses a major bottleneck in scaling transformer-based models to long video sequences. Making long-horizon video generation more computationally feasible could accelerate research and facilitate the deployment of such models in practical applications.

#### 5.4. Broader Applications

The ability to generate long, temporally consistent videos has implications for various fields:
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Reliable long-term video prediction can provide agents with a better understanding of their environment's dynamics, supporting improved planning, navigation, and interaction in complex, persistent worlds. This could lead to more robust and intelligent robotic systems.
- **Virtual Reality and Gaming:** Consistent procedural content generation for virtual environments can enhance immersion and realism, reducing visual glitches or inconsistencies that break user experience.
- **Content Creation and Film:** Tools capable of generating coherent long video sequences could streamline creative processes, enable new forms of digital content, and assist in tasks like scene extension or animation.
- **Synthetic Data Generation:** High-quality, long-form synthetic video data, especially with consistent object and environment persistence, can be invaluable for training other machine learning models, particularly in areas where real-world data collection is expensive, scarce, or privacy-sensitive.

#### 5.5. Guiding Future Research

The paper also highlights limitations and potential future research directions. These include the need for better evaluation metrics for newly generated scene parts (where ground truth correlation is low), exploration of even more efficient sequence models, and investigating methods to train directly on pixels with advanced generative losses (e.g., diffusion or GANs) to overcome reconstruction artifacts from VQ-GAN compression. These identified avenues can serve as a roadmap for subsequent advancements in the field.
