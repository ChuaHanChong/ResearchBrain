# Semantic-Contact Fields for Category-Level Generalizable Tactile Tool Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A research effort from Institute for Infocomm Research and National University of Singapore introduces Semantic-Contact Fields (SCFields), a 3D representation combining visual semantics with physically-grounded contact estimates to enable category-level generalization for robotic tool manipulation. This approach facilitates zero-shot transfer across various tools in contact-rich tasks, achieving high success rates and efficiency on real-world robots for scraping, drawing, and peeling.

## Problem

- Robotic tool manipulation requires both high-level semantic understanding (where to grasp/apply) and precise physical grounding (force regulation), a dual requirement that existing methods struggle to combine effectively.
- Vision-centric policies often lack physical awareness for contact-rich tasks, while tactile-aware methods are typically instance-specific and fail to generalize across diverse tool geometries within a category.
- Acquiring large-scale, diverse real-world tactile data is costly, and simulating soft tactile sensors accurately poses a significant sim-to-real domain gap, hindering the learning of generalizable tactile representations.

## Method

- Developed **Semantic-Contact Fields (SCFields)**, a unified 3D representation that fuses category-level semantic features with dense, extrinsic contact probability and 3D force vector estimates across the tool surface.
- Proposed a two-stage ==Sim-to-Real learning pipeline== that pre-trains a perception model on large-scale simulation data for geometry-aware contact priors, then fine-tunes it with a small real-world dataset using pseudo-ground-truth labels derived from geometric heuristics and ==analytical force optimization==.
- Implemented a ==3D diffusion policy== conditioned on SCFields, allowing the robot to perform end-effector actions guided by both functional semantics and force-aware contact estimates, enabling robust category-level generalization.

## Results

- SCFields enabled policies achieved superior success rates and normalized cleaning efficiency in scraping (e.g., **79.6%** success rate, **84.7%** normalized efficiency on unseen tools), crayon drawing (**0.78** consistency on unseen crayons), and peeling tasks on novel tool instances, outperforming vision-only and raw tactile baselines.
- The perception model, aligned with real-world scraper data, demonstrated transferability to more complex tools, producing accurate force estimates for the peeler task, which led to significantly higher contact rates (**90.0%**) and average peel lengths (**4.52cm**) on unseen peelers.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of explicit force estimates, as policies lacking this component showed substantially lower task efficiency and higher failure rates (e.g., 'No Force' policy achieved only **27.6%** normalized cleaning efficiency for unseen scrapers compared to **84.7%** for SCFields).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing explicit, geometry-aware extrinsic contact estimates (probability and force vectors) with semantic fields creates a robust 3D representation capable of supporting category-level generalization in contact-rich robotic tasks.
> - A two-stage Sim-to-Real learning pipeline effectively bridges the domain gap for tactile sensing, leveraging the scalability of simulation for general priors and data-efficient real-world alignment to adapt to physical sensor characteristics.
> - Explicit contact force vectors are crucial for regulating interaction dynamics in manipulation, preventing common failure modes such as hovering or excessive pressure, which policies relying solely on contact probability or raw tactile signals often exhibit.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements in generalist policies, particularly with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which demonstrate an ability to interpret high-level semantic commands and generalize geometrically across diverse object variants. However, a limitation of these vision-centric approaches is their lack of physical grounding, which hinders performance in contact-rich tasks requiring precise force regulation. These models are often described as "physically naive."

Conversely, policies leveraging tactile or haptic sensing are adept at managing local contact dynamics, providing physical grounding for manipulation. However, existing tactile-aware methods typically map raw sensor signals directly to actions, making them instance-specific. This approach generally fails to generalize across tools with varying geometries, as a direct mapping does not account for changes in tool shape or functional parts.

A significant challenge in bridging this gap is the difficulty in acquiring diverse, large-scale real-world tactile data due to collection costs and the complexity of simulating soft tactile sensors, which makes direct zero-shot sim-to-real transfer challenging. Accurately simulating the non-linear deformation of soft tactile sensors and fine-grained contact mechanics (e.g., friction coefficients, hysteresis) is computationally intensive and difficult to calibrate, leading to a substantial sim-to-real domain gap. Prior attempts to combine vision and touch, such as integrating tactile readings as occupancy points or relying on wrist force-torque sensors, have provided some benefits but often lack explicit estimates of distributed contact location and force at the tool tip, which are critical for effective tool use.

This work positions itself to address the dual requirements of semantic transferability and physical grounding for tool manipulation. It aims to develop a representation that is both semantically rich and physically informed, thereby enabling robust generalization to unseen tool instances in contact-rich tasks while overcoming the tactile sim-to-real transfer problem. It seeks to provide an explicit representation of extrinsic contact, including force direction and magnitude, directly on the tool's geometry, which is an identified gap in current approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable category-level generalization for contact-rich tool manipulation tasks by developing a unified 3D representation that combines semantic understanding with physically grounded contact feedback. This objective is motivated by several identified challenges:

1. **Dual Requirements for Generalizable Tool Manipulation:** Robustly manipulating diverse tool variants requires two distinct understandings:
 - **Semantic Understanding (Functional Affordance):** The ability to identify where on a tool to grasp and where to apply it for its intended function (e.g., the blade of a peeler, the tip of a crayon). Current vision-centric generalist policies (e.g., VLA models) excel at this but often lack precise physical control.
 - **Physical Understanding (Contact Dynamics):** The ability to precisely regulate interaction forces and maintain stable contact during tool use. Existing contact-aware policies leveraging tactile sensing are proficient in this aspect but are typically instance-specific and do not generalize to tools with different geometries.

2. **Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
 - **Vision-Only Models:** While capable of geometric generalization, these models are "physically naive." They struggle in tasks where visual semantics are insufficient to determine precise force application or where contact is visually ambiguous.
 - **Instance-Specific Tactile Policies:** Policies that directly map tactile signals to actions without an intermediate generalized representation fail to adapt to changes in tool geometry. They provide local contact management but lack broader semantic or geometric transferability.
 - **Ambiguity in Current Vision-Touch Fusion:** Methods integrating tactile feedback often either treat it as generic geometric occupancy or rely on wrist-mounted force sensors, which provide only a net wrench and make inferring distributed contact at the tool tip ambiguous. Critically, these methods often neglect the explicit estimation of contact force magnitude and direction, which are crucial for dynamic force regulation.

3. **The Sim-to-Real Gap for Tactile Data:** Learning robust, generalizable tactile representations is hindered by two factors:
 - **Data Collection:** Collecting diverse real-world tactile data at scale, covering various tool geometries and interaction scenarios, is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
 - **Simulation Fidelity:** Simulating the complex, non-linear deformation of soft tactile sensors and accurate fine-grained contact mechanics remains a significant challenge. Models trained solely in simulation often exhibit a "reality gap," leading to phantom forces or missed contacts when deployed in real environments.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes **Semantic-Contact Fields (SCFields)**. The core idea is that while tool geometries within a category vary, the physical interaction at the "effective part" (e.g., the blade of a peeler) remains functionally invariant. Therefore, an explicit, geometry-aware representation of extrinsic contact, encompassing both contact probability and force, can facilitate skill transfer. The key objectives are:
- To develop a unified 3D representation that fuses category-level semantic features with dense, extrinsic contact probability and force estimates.
- To establish a two-stage Sim-to-Real learning pipeline that leverages the scalability of simulation for learning geometry-aware contact priors and a data-efficient real-world alignment stage for robustly interpreting real tactile sensor responses.
- To enable a robot manipulation policy, conditioned on these SCFields, to achieve zero-shot generalization to novel tool instances and environmental variations in contact-rich tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a two-stage learning process designed to estimate Semantic-Contact Fields (SCFields) and then use them to condition a generalizable manipulation policy.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The system is framed as two distinct learning problems:
1. **Extrinsic Contact Field Estimation:** Learning a perception mapping $f_\phi$ that takes raw observations (tool point cloud $P_{tool}$, environment point cloud $P_{env}$, tactile sensor readings $T$, and proprioceptive state $q$) and outputs a dense extrinsic contact field $F_c$ over the tool surface. $F_c$ assigns a contact probability $c_i$ and a 3D force vector $f_i$ to each point $p_i$ in $P_{tool}$.
2. **Generalizable Policy Learning:** Learning a diffusion policy $\pi_\theta(a_{t:t+H}|O_t)$ that predicts a sequence of actions $a$ given an observation $O_t$. The critical aspect is designing $O_t$ to be invariant to instance-specific geometry while retaining high-fidelity physical feedback.

#### 3.2. Contact Field Estimation

This stage focuses on learning the multimodal perception model $f_\phi$ for dense extrinsic contact estimation.

#### 3.2.1. Model Architecture

The system employs a **Tactile-as-PointCloud architecture** based on PointNet++ to unify visual and tactile inputs.
- **Unified Input Representation:** The input is a composite point cloud $P_{total} = P_{obj} \cup P_{env} \cup P_{tactile}$. $P_{obj}$ and $P_{env}$ represent sampled surfaces of the tool and environment. $P_{tactile}$ represents the 3D coordinates of tactile sensor markers projected into the world frame. Each point $p_i$ in $P_{total}$ is augmented with a feature vector encoding its source type (Object, Env, Tactile) and a history of tactile marker displacements. This allows the network to implicitly learn the relationship between tool geometry, sensor deformation, and contact.
- **Network Architecture:** A PointNet++ encoder-decoder processes $P_{total}$. The encoder fuses sparse tactile signals with dense tool geometry. The decoder upsamples features back to the resolution of $P_{obj}$. Two parallel heads then predict the scalar contact probability $c_i \in$ and regress the 3D extrinsic force vector $f^{ext}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ for each point.

#### 3.2.2. Simulation Data Generation

To obtain dense ground-truth (GT) labels for contact locations and forces, which are inaccessible in the real world, a large-scale synthetic dataset (300 tool instances, 320,000 frames) is generated using a multi-simulator pipeline.
- **Simulation Pipeline:** IsaacGym is used for high-throughput rigid-body dynamics, and TacSL simulates GelSight sensor force fields and depth data.
- **Labeling:** A replay strategy is employed: Open3D computes Signed Distance Functions (SDF) for soft contact probability from penetration depth, and PyBullet replays interactions to extract discrete contact forces, which are then extrapolated to the dense point cloud on the tool surface using a distance-weighted kernel interpolation modulated by local geometry and depth.

#### 3.2.3. Real-World Pseudo-GT Generation

To address the sim-to-real gap, a small real-world alignment dataset is collected, and pseudo-ground-truth labels are generated using geometric heuristics and analytical force optimization.
- **Heuristic Contact Probability:** For a structured task (scraping a flat surface), geometrically plausible contact candidates are identified using a height threshold. A signal-based gating filter, based on mean tactile marker displacement, confirms actual contact. Points within the candidate set are assigned soft contact probability labels inversely proportional to their distance from the surface.
- **Analytical Contact Force Optimization:** To estimate dense force vectors without external force sensors, a Second-Order Cone Program (SOCP) is solved. This optimization minimizes the discrepancy between the observed tactile net wrench $W_{tac}$ (computed from tactile signals) and the wrench induced by a distribution of point forces at candidate contact points. It regularizes force magnitudes inversely to their heuristic contact probability and enforces physical plausibility by bounding force magnitude within a friction cone (compressive forces, limited shear).

#### 3.2.4. Two-Stage Model Training

A two-stage strategy transfers physical priors from simulation to the real world. Both stages minimize a composite loss: $L_{total} = \lambda_{prob}L_{prob} + \lambda_{force}(\lambda_{mag}L_{mag} + \lambda_{dir}L_{dir})$.
- **Contact Probability Loss ($L_{prob}$):** Focal Loss is used to handle class imbalance (contact vs. free space).
- **Force Magnitude Loss ($L_{mag}$):** Adaptive Weighted Mean Squared Error (MSE) is used, prioritizing high-force regions.
- **Force Direction Loss ($L_{dir}$):** Cosine Similarity loss is applied only to points with significant ground-truth force magnitude.
- **Stage 1: Sim Pre-training:** The model is trained on the large-scale simulation dataset with domain randomization, learning the fundamental mapping between tool geometry and force distribution across diverse tools and interaction poses.
- **Stage 2: Real-World Alignment:** The model is fine-tuned on the small, pseudo-labeled real-world dataset. Random augmentations are applied to input point clouds to enhance data diversity and prevent overfitting, adapting to real sensor characteristics while preserving simulation-learned priors.

#### 3.3. Semantic-Contact Fields for Policy Learning

SCFields are constructed as a unified 3D observation fusing semantic information with aligned extrinsic contact estimates.
- **SCFields Construction:** For each point $p_i$ in the object point cloud $P_{obj}$, a feature vector $x_i = [f^{ext}_i \parallel c_i \parallel S_i]$ is created. $f^{ext}_i$ and $c_i$ are the contact force and probability estimates from the fine-tuned contact estimator. $S_i$ represents 3D Semantic Fields adapted from GenDP, capturing functional semantics (e.g., "blade," "handle").
- **Policy Implementation:** A 3D Diffusion Policy framework is used. The dense SCFields point cloud is processed by a PointNet++ backbone to extract a global feature vector. This vector conditions the diffusion policy's denoising network, allowing it to predict a sequence of end-effector actions based on both functional semantics and force-aware contact estimates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Contact Field Model Evaluation

The contact field perception module was evaluated in isolation to assess its accuracy in predicting contact probability and force vectors.

#### 4.1.1. Evaluation 1: Architecture Validation (Sim-to-Sim)

Using a held-out simulation test set, the architecture's capacity to learn complex contact physics was assessed.
- **Performance:** The "Ours (Sim-Only)" model achieved the best contact F1 Score (0.587, and 0.462 in statistical appendix) for binary contact detection. Ablations showed that Focal Loss (compared to BCE Loss) was crucial, and preserving the 3D spatial structure of tactile markers was important.
- **Insights:** NCF (Neural Contact Fields) performed poorly (F1: 0.043), attributed to its fixed-pose assumption. Force MSE values were similar across several models in simulation, suggesting that force regression in this setting was less discriminative. However, removing the contact probability head increased Force MSE (0.0158 vs 0.0147), indicating that contact probability provides a spatial support signal for force regression.

#### 4.1.2. Evaluation 2: Real-World Alignment Accuracy

The efficacy of the two-stage pipeline on real-world data was assessed using held-out Scrapers (seen in alignment) and Crayons (unseen in alignment but seen in simulation). Pseudo-ground truth was generated using the heuristic labeling pipeline.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** The "Sim-Only" model performed poorly on real tactile inputs (F1: 0.002 on Scrapers, 0.008 on Crayons), highlighting the domain gap.
- **Alignment Benefits:** "Ours (Aligned)" significantly outperformed "Sim-Only" and "Real-Only" on unseen crayons (F1: 0.657, Force MSE: 0.0085), demonstrating successful transfer of sensor alignment from scraper interactions to novel tools when combined with simulation-learned priors. The "Real-Only" model fit seen data well (F1: 0.458 on Scrapers) but generalized less reliably to crayons (F1: 0.614), indicating insufficient geometric priors from limited real data alone.
- **Role of Tactile Feedback:** The "No-Tactile" baseline, relying only on geometry, showed worse force prediction (Force MSE: 0.0432 on Scrapers vs. 0.0254 for Ours), confirming that geometric proximity is insufficient for loaded contact estimation.
- **Role of Contact Probability:** The "No Contact Probability" ablation degraded force estimation substantially on scrapers (Force MSE: 0.0377 vs. 0.0254), suggesting its utility as a spatial support estimator for extended or ambiguous contact regions.

#### 4.1.3. Evaluation 3: Qualitative Generalization (Peeler Task)

Qualitative assessment on the Peeler task (complex interactions with irregular carrots, unreliable heuristic GT). The model was pre-trained on simulated peeler data but fine-tuned only on real-world scraper data.
- **Performance:** "Ours" produced localized force estimates at the blade-carrot interface, unlike "Sim-Only" (missed contacts), "Real-Only" (noisy forces), and "No-Tactile" (failed to infer loaded contact). This suggested that scraper-based real alignment can transfer to more complex curved tools, leveraging simulation-provided contact priors.

#### 4.2. Policy Evaluation

Policy performance was evaluated on a Franka Emika Panda robot with GelSight Mini sensors across three contact-rich tasks.

#### 4.2.1. Comparison with Baselines

- **Scraping (Table III):** "Ours (SCFields)" achieved the highest Success Rate (SR) and Normalized Cleaning Efficiency (Eff Norm) on both seen and unseen tools (e.g., Unseen Tools: SR 79.6%, Eff Norm 84.7%). Vision-Only and Raw Tactile baselines performed significantly worse (e.g., Unseen Tools: Raw Tactile SR 50.0%, Eff Norm 27.3%), confirming their limitations in leveraging high-dimensional tactile data or providing physical grounding.
- **Crayon Drawing (Table IV):** "Ours (SCFields)" achieved the highest drawing consistency score on both seen (0.86) and unseen (0.78) crayons, outperforming baselines.
- **Peeling (Table V):** "Ours (SCFields)" showed superior performance on both seen and unseen peelers, with significantly higher contact rates, cut-in rates, and average peel lengths (e.g., Unseen Peelers: Contact 90.0%, Cut-in 73.3%, Avg Peel Length 4.52cm) compared to Vision-Only (Contact 50.0%, Cut-in 33.3%, Avg Peel Length 1.12cm) and Raw Tactile.

#### 4.2.2. Role of Explicit Force & Generalization

- **"No Force" Ablation:** This ablation, where the policy was trained with only contact probability but no explicit force vectors, showed failure modes such as hovering or excessive pressure, resulting in lower success rates and efficiency across tasks (e.g., Unseen Scraper Eff Norm 27.6% vs. 84.7% for Ours). This indicates that explicit force vectors are necessary for regulating interaction dynamics beyond just localizing contact.
- **Cross-Category Generalization:** The Peeler task served as a strong validation. Although the policy used imitation learning on peeling demonstrations, the underlying perception model was aligned using only scraper data. The significant performance of "Ours" on this task demonstrated that SCFields successfully transfers the invariant concept of "functional contact" learned from simulation and real-world scraper alignment to novel, more complex tools like peelers.

#### 4.3. Crayon Picking Experiment (Appendix D)

A preliminary experiment for crayon picking, which requires identifying and grasping the handle of an asymmetric crayon, showed that including the semantic field significantly improved Directional Accuracy and Grasp Success Rate on both seen and unseen crayons, guiding the robot to grasp the correct functional part of the tool.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Semantic-Contact Fields (SCFields) as a novel 3D representation designed to enhance robotic manipulation capabilities, particularly in contact-rich tool use scenarios.

The primary significance lies in its ability to bridge critical gaps in current robot learning paradigms:
1. **Unified Representation:** SCFields successfully fuses category-level visual semantics with dense, physically-grounded extrinsic contact estimates (contact probability and force vectors). This unified representation provides both the "where" (semantic affordance) and the "how" (force regulation) necessary for effective tool manipulation.
2. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer for Tactile Sensing:** The proposed two-stage learning pipeline addresses the long-standing challenge of tactile sim-to-real transfer. By leveraging large-scale simulation for geometry-aware contact priors and a data-efficient real-world alignment stage with pseudo-labels, the system overcomes the limitations of both purely simulated and purely real-world tactile learning. This approach obviates the need for expensive instrumentation or high-fidelity tactile simulators.
3. **Category-Level Generalization:** Experiments across scraping, crayon drawing, and peeling tasks demonstrate that SCFields enables robust zero-shot generalization to novel tool instances and environmental variations within a category. This is a notable improvement over vision-only policies, which are physically naive, and raw-tactile policies, which often fail to generalize due to their instance-specific nature. The performance on the Peeler task, where the contact field model was aligned using different tool data (scrapers), highlights the system's ability to transfer the concept of functional contact across different tool types.
4. **Value of Explicit Force Estimates:** The ablation studies confirm that explicit contact force vectors are crucial for regulating interaction dynamics, preventing common failure modes like insufficient loading or excessive pressure that occur with policies relying solely on contact probability or raw tactile signals.

The potential impact of this work includes:
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** Robots equipped with SCFields could perform a wider range of contact-rich tasks in unstructured environments, from domestic chores to industrial assembly and surgical applications, with improved reliability and adaptability.
- **More Capable Generalist Policies:** The methodology contributes towards building more physically grounded generalist robot policies, moving beyond purely visual reasoning to incorporate nuanced physical interaction.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The approach provides a framework for integrating multimodal sensing (vision, touch, proprioception) into a coherent, task-relevant representation. It lays groundwork for exploring autonomous discovery of novel tool manipulation strategies using reinforcement learning or world models, potentially moving beyond the current imitation learning paradigm.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Reliance on Imitation Learning:** The current system is limited to tool usage patterns present in demonstrations, restricting the discovery of novel functional affordances or creative tool use. Future work may explore reinforcement learning or world models for autonomous strategy discovery.
- **Observability of Functional Contact:** The framework assumes that the functional contact region is at least partially observable in the tool point cloud.
- **Sensing Complexity:** The current setup relies on multiple RGB-D cameras and tactile sensors. Future work aims to reduce sensing complexity and improve temporal tracking for fully occluded contact states.
