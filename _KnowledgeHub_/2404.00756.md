# Recover: A Neuro-Symbolic Framework for Failure Detection and Recovery

> [!summary] Summary
> The Recover framework, developed by researchers at Samsung AI and the University of Plymouth, introduces a neuro-symbolic approach for online failure detection and recovery in robotic systems. This framework integrates symbolic AI's structured reasoning with Large Language Models' flexible planning, achieving 100% accuracy in failure detection through its ontology and successfully recovering from approximately 70% of detected failures in simulated environments.

## Problem

- Traditional model-based or policy-based robotic control struggles with the complexity and unpredictability of dynamic, real-world environments.
- Pure Large Language Model (LLM) approaches for robotics often lack grounding and suffer from hallucinations, leading to unreliable failure detection and recovery.
- Robots require the ability to identify failures, understand their causes, generate appropriate recovery strategies, and adapt to changing conditions in real-time without extensive retraining.

## Method

- The **Recover** framework employs a ==neuro-symbolic== approach, integrating ==symbolic knowledge representation== via a comprehensive ontology (==OntoThor==) with Large Language Models for flexible planning.
- It operates through an ==online, cyclical process==: action execution, environment observation, symbolic explanation (==scene graph==, audio classification), ==ontological reasoning== for subgoal verification and recovery strategy generation, LLM-based planning, and plan execution.
- ==OntoThor== provides structured knowledge of objects, properties, relationships, and a detailed ==taxonomy of failures== with associated logical rules for detection and guidance for recovery.

## Results

- OntoThor's logical rules achieved **100%** accuracy in failure detection, outperforming LLM-based subgoal verification which achieved approximately **50%** accuracy.
- The framework demonstrated an approximately **70%** recovery rate for detected failures and effectively identified **100%** of safety issues, recovering from **93%** of them.
- Recover significantly reduced monetary costs compared to pure LLM-based methods while achieving a **59%** task completion rate for simple tasks and **33%** for complex tasks after recovery.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Neuro-symbolic integration effectively combines structured reasoning from ontologies with flexible planning from Large Language Models, mitigating limitations of individual approaches like LLM hallucinations.
> - A comprehensive failure taxonomy and a dedicated ontology (OntoThor) enable accurate, rule-based failure detection and guide context-aware recovery strategies.
> - The online, cyclical process of observation, explanation, reasoning, and planning allows for real-time adaptation and recovery during robotic task execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robots operating in dynamic environments alongside humans face numerous challenges, particularly when unexpected situations arise. The ability to detect and recover from failures is essential for reliable robotic systems, especially those interacting with humans in everyday settings like homes, hospitals, or workplaces.

In a paper by Cristina Cornelio from Samsung AI and Mohammed Diab from the University of Plymouth, a novel framework called "Recover" is introduced to address this critical challenge. This neuro-symbolic approach combines the reasoning capabilities of symbolic AI with the flexibility of neural networks, creating a robust system for online failure detection and recovery in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. The Challenge of Failure Detection and Recovery

Traditional approaches to robotic control face significant limitations when operating in unpredictable environments:

1. **Model-based approaches** struggle with the complexity and unpredictability of real-world environments.
2. **Policy-based approaches** require extensive training data and often fail when encountering novel situations.
3. **Pure Large Language Model (LLM) approaches** suffer from hallucinations and lack grounding in the physical environment.

The core challenge lies in developing systems that can:
- Identify failures in real-time
- Understand the nature and cause of failures
- Generate appropriate recovery strategies
- Adapt to changing conditions without extensive retraining

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Neuro-Symbolic Integration

Recover addresses these challenges through neuro-symbolic integration, combining:

1. **Symbolic knowledge representation** (ontologies and logical rules) for structured reasoning about the environment and potential failures.
2. **Neural components** (Large Language Models) for flexible planning and adaptation to novel situations.

This hybrid approach leverages the complementary strengths of both paradigms:

```
Symbolic components: Provide structured knowledge, logical reasoning, and explainability
Neural components: Offer flexibility, adaptability, and the ability to handle natural language
```

The integration helps overcome limitations inherent to each approach when used in isolation. For example, the structured knowledge from the ontology helps ground the LLM's responses, reducing hallucinations and improving the quality of recovery plans.

#### 3.2. The Recover Framework

The Recover framework operates through a cyclical process of:

1. **Action Execution**: The robot executes an action according to its current plan.
2. **Environment Observation**: The robot perceives the environment through multi-modal sensors (visual, auditory).
3. **Explanation**: The framework converts sensory data into a symbolic representation:
 - Visual data is processed into a scene graph
 - Audio is classified into relevant categories
 - These representations populate an ontology with instances of the current state
4. **Reasoning**:
 - For subgoal verification to determine success or failure
 - For recovery strategy generation when failures are detected
5. **Planning**: An LLM-based planner generates recovery plans when failures occur
6. **Plan Execution**: The robot executes the new plan, continuing the cycle

This process happens online during task execution, allowing for real-time failure detection and recovery without interrupting the robot's operation.

#### 3.3. OntoThor: An Ontology for Failure Detection

A key contribution of this work is OntoThor, a comprehensive ontology specifically designed for the AI2Thor simulator environment. OntoThor serves multiple crucial functions:

1. **Knowledge Representation**: It represents objects, their properties, spatial relationships, and states within the environment.
2. **Failure Classification**: It defines a taxonomy of possible failures that might occur during task execution.
3. **Logical Rules**: It incorporates rules for detecting specific failure types based on environmental conditions.
4. **Recovery Guidance**: It provides guidance for recovering from each failure type.

The ontology is designed to be extensible, allowing for the addition of new failure types and recovery strategies as needed. It also includes personalized features like human preferences and dietary restrictions, extending beyond traditional failure categories.

#### 3.4. Failure Taxonomy

The authors developed a comprehensive taxonomy of failures that robots might encounter, categorized into:

1. **Environmental Failures**:
 - ObstructedPathFailure: When the robot's path is blocked
 - OccupiedPutFailure: When a target location for an object is already occupied
 - DirtyObjFailure: When an object is dirty and needs cleaning
 - EnclosedObjectFailure: When an object is enclosed within another
 - ObstructedPickUpFailure: When an object cannot be picked up due to obstruction

2. **Safety Failures**:
 - OpenObjectSafetyFailure: Related to open objects creating safety hazards
 - ApplianceOnSafetyFailure: When appliances are left on unsafely
 - ObjectOnFloorSafetyFailure: When objects on the floor create tripping hazards
 - ObjectBrokenOnFloorSafetyFailure: When broken objects create safety risks

3. **Preference Violation Failures**:
 - DietaryConstraintsViolationFailure: When dietary restrictions are violated
 - MissingNavigationFailure: When navigation information is missing

4. **Agent Failures**:
 - DroppingObjFailure: When objects are dropped
 - PerceptionFailure: When the robot fails to perceive the environment correctly

5. **Planning Failures**:
 - ActionExecutionFailure: When actions cannot be executed as planned

This taxonomy is encoded in the ontology with logical rules for detecting each failure type based on environmental conditions and task requirements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Evaluation

The authors evaluated the Recover framework in a simulated kitchen environment (AI2Thor) on a variety of tasks, including:

- Simple tasks: Making toast, heating a cup
- Complex tasks: Preparing meals with multiple steps and ingredients

The evaluation focused on several key metrics:

1. **Failure Detection Accuracy**: The ability to correctly identify when failures occur
2. **Recovery Rate**: The percentage of detected failures that could be successfully recovered from
3. **Task Completion Rate**: The percentage of tasks that could be completed after recovery
4. **Safety Issue Handling**: The ability to detect and recover from safety-related failures
5. **Cost Efficiency**: The computational and monetary cost of the approach compared to alternatives

The Recover framework was compared against a baseline approach that relied solely on LLMs for both failure detection and recovery planning.

#### 4.2. Results and Findings

The experimental evaluation revealed several significant findings:

1. **Superior Failure Detection**: OntoThor's logical rules achieved 100% accuracy in failure detection, significantly outperforming the LLM-based subgoal verifier (LLM-SGV), which only achieved approximately 50% accuracy.

2. **Effective Recovery Planning**: The recovery rate was approximately 70%, demonstrating the framework's ability to generate viable recovery plans for most detected failures.

3. **Task Complexity Impact**: The completion rate was higher for simple tasks (59%) compared to complex tasks (33%), indicating that task complexity affects recovery success.

4. **Excellent Safety Performance**: The framework identified 100% of safety issues and successfully recovered from 93% of them, highlighting its effectiveness for safety-critical applications.

5. **Cost Efficiency**: Recover demonstrated a significant reduction in monetary costs compared to pure LLM-based methods, making it more economically viable for real-world deployment.

The integration of symbolic knowledge with LLM planning proved particularly effective at:
- Reducing hallucinations in LLM outputs
- Providing context-aware recovery strategies
- Handling novel failures not seen during development

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its promising results, the Recover framework has several limitations:

1. **Simulation Environment**: The current evaluation is limited to a simulated environment, which may not fully capture the complexities of real-world scenarios.

2. **Single Failure Assumption**: The framework assumes there is at most one failure per task, which may not hold in complex real-world situations.

3. **LLM Biases**: The LLM-based planner can have inherent biases (e.g., assuming a robot with two arms) that can limit its performance in certain scenarios.

The authors propose several directions for future research:

```
- Exploring simulator scenarios with human interaction
- Deeper integration of LLMs with ontologies
- Further refinements in prompt engineering
- Extending the framework to handle nested failures (failures that occur during recovery)
- Real-world deployment and testing
```

#### 5.2. Conclusion

The Recover framework represents a significant advancement in robotic failure detection and recovery, demonstrating the power of neuro-symbolic integration for creating more reliable and adaptable robotic systems. By combining the structured reasoning capabilities of ontologies with the flexible planning abilities of Large Language Models, Recover achieves superior performance in detecting and recovering from a wide range of failures.

The framework's ability to handle safety issues and preference violations makes it particularly valuable for robots that interact closely with humans. Its online operation enables real-time adaptation to changing conditions, a crucial capability for robots operating in dynamic environments.

As robots become increasingly integrated into our daily lives, frameworks like Recover will be essential for ensuring they can operate reliably and safely alongside humans, even when unexpected situations arise. The combination of symbolic knowledge and neural flexibility provides a promising path forward for the next generation of robust robotic systems.
