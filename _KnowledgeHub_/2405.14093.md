# A Survey on Vision-Language-Action Models for Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, crucial for embodied AI, are comprehensively organized in this survey, detailing their architectures, training methodologies, and essential resources for language-conditioned robotic tasks. The work presents a novel taxonomy classifying VLAs by hierarchical control (low-level policies and high-level planners), identifying key components and future research opportunities.

## Problem

- The rapidly evolving landscape of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lacked a comprehensive and structured overview.
- Researchers faced challenges in understanding and categorizing the diverse architectures and approaches emerging in embodied AI for language-conditioned robotic tasks.
- A need existed to bridge the gap between advanced vision-language understanding and the generation of actionable outputs for physical robots.

## Method

- A structured methodology was employed to systematically review, categorize, and synthesize existing research on ==VLA models==.
- A novel ==hierarchical taxonomy== was developed, segmenting models into ==low-level control policies== and ==high-level task planners== based on their function in robotic systems.
- Comprehensive summaries of datasets, simulators, benchmarks, and data collection methods were aggregated to provide a resource hub for the community.

## Results

- The survey defines VLAs and Large VLAs (LVLAs), identifying their distinct role in enabling language-conditioned robotic tasks.
- Analysis found VLA systems predominantly use a ==hierarchical control paradigm== (low-level policies + high-level planners) built on components like ==Pretrained Visual Representations (PVRs)==, dynamics learning, and world models, trending toward Transformer and diffusion-based action generation.
- Persistent challenges include significant data scarcity for real-world robot interactions, the sim-to-real gap, and the need for more comprehensive and fine-grained evaluation benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a critical and emerging category of multimodal AI for embodied systems, directly linking perception, language, and physical action.
> - The hierarchical control framework, comprising high-level task planners and low-level control policies, is a prevalent and effective approach for managing complexity in VLA systems.
> - The integration of large language models (LLMs) and large vision-language models (VLMs) as core components provides enhanced versatility and generalizability for VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey positions Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models as a cornerstone for the development of artificial general intelligence (AGI), specifically within the domain of embodied AI. Embodied AI involves controlling physical agents to interact with the physical world, with robotics being a prominent application area. The work is situated within the broader context of multimodal AI, building upon the advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs).

Prior research has addressed various aspects of embodied AI through surveys focusing on foundation models in robotics (e.g., Firoozi et al., Wang et al., Hu et al.) or real-world robot applications (e.g., Kawaharazuka et al.). This paper distinguishes itself by providing the first comprehensive survey dedicated specifically to VLA models. It aims to capture the rapidly evolving landscape of this emerging class of multimodal models, which are designed to address language-conditioned robotic tasks by generating actions. The authors highlight that VLAs offer advantages in versatility, dexterity, and generalizability in complex environments compared to earlier deep reinforcement learning (RL) approaches, making them suitable for a broader range of applications, from industrial settings to household tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to present a comprehensive survey of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for embodied AI, synthesizing the current state of the field and identifying future research directions. This objective is motivated by several factors inherent in the development of intelligent embodied agents.

Firstly, embodied AI requires agents to integrate information from multiple modalities – specifically, to understand natural language instructions, visually perceive their environment, and generate appropriate physical actions. This necessitates multimodal capabilities that VLA models are designed to provide.

Secondly, conventional robotic policies, often based on deep reinforcement learning, have historically been limited to a narrow set of tasks within controlled environments. There is an increasing demand for more versatile, multitask policies that can adapt to diverse environments and tasks, similar to the generalization observed in LLMs and VLMs. Specifying tasks through natural language offers a more intuitive human-robot interface, further necessitating the development of language-conditioned robotic policies.

Thirdly, the rapid emergence and proliferation of VLA models, a term notably coined by the RT-2 model, has created a need for a structured and consolidated overview of the field. Without such a survey, researchers may lack a clear understanding of the diverse approaches, technical components, and interconnections within this rapidly developing area.

To address these motivations, the authors outline several specific contributions:
1. **Comprehensive Review:** To provide a detailed review of emerging VLA models, encompassing their components, architectures, training objectives, and application to various robotic tasks.
2. **Taxonomy Development:** To introduce a taxonomy that organizes VLAs based on a hierarchical framework common in robotic systems. This taxonomy includes essential components, low-level control policies (executing actions based on language commands and environment perception), and high-level task planners (decomposing long-horizon tasks into subtasks).
3. **Resource Summary:** To compile and summarize necessary resources for VLA development, such as recent datasets and benchmarks from both real-world and simulated environments, and to discuss methods for addressing challenges like data scarcity and inconsistency.
4. **Future Directions:** To identify and discuss current challenges facing VLA models and outline promising avenues for future research, including aspects like safety, the development of foundation models, real-world deployment, and broader multimodal integration.

The survey also proposes a generalized definition of "VLA" as "any model capable of processing multimodal inputs from vision and language to produce robot actions that accomplish embodied tasks," differentiating this from the original "Large VLA" (LVLA) concept, which specifically refers to models adapted from LLMs or large VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research paper presents a systematic review and analysis of existing literature on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. As a survey, it does not introduce new empirical data or develop novel models but rather synthesizes and categorizes existing knowledge.

The core of the methodology involves developing a comprehensive taxonomy for VLA models, which serves as the organizational framework for the survey. This taxonomy is structured into three main research lines:
1. **Components of VLA:** This section dissects the fundamental building blocks that contribute to VLA functionality. It covers:
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Discusses the foundational role of RL, including deep Q-networks (DQN), Transformer-based approaches (Decision Transformer, Trajectory Transformer), and the synergy with LLMs through methods like RL from human feedback (RLHF) and verbal RL frameworks (Reflexion, Eureka).
- **Pretrained Visual Representations (PVRs):** Analyzes various methods for obtaining robust visual embeddings, including contrastive learning (CLIP, R3M, VIP) and masked autoencoders (MVP, RPT, Voltron), as well as advanced self-supervised methods (DINOv2, I-JEPA) and model distillation (Theia). A comparative table (Table I) details their objectives and applications.
- **Video Representations:** Briefly notes the use of video for temporal and 3D information.
- **Dynamics Learning:** Explores objectives aimed at understanding forward (predicting next state) and inverse (predicting actions from state transitions) dynamics. Methods like Vi-PRoM, MaskDP, MIDAS, SMART, PACT, and VPT are discussed, with technical details in Table II.
- **World Models:** Defines world models as mechanisms for encoding commonsense knowledge and predicting future states, enabling model-based control (Dreamer series, IRIS, TWM). This is further specialized into:
 - **LLM-Induced World Models:** Leveraging LLMs' knowledge for abstract world models (DECKARD, LLM-DM) or as combined policies and world models (RAP, Tree-Planner, LLM-MCTS).
 - **Visual World Models:** Generating visual representations of future states (Genie, 3D-VLA, UniSim, E2WM).
- **Reasoning:** Examines the application of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, primarily from LLMs, to enhance decision-making in high-level task planning (ThinkBot, ReAct, RAT, Tree-Planner) and low-level control policies (ECoT, CoT-VLA).
- **Policy Steering:** Reviews techniques to improve VLA performance during inference without retraining (V-GPS, RoboMonkey).
- **Strengths and Limitations:** Provides a critical assessment of each component category.

2. **Low-Level Control Policies:** This section focuses on VLA models designed to execute immediate, fine-grained actions. The approaches are categorized and analyzed based on their architectural elements, including:
- **Non-Transformer Control Policies:** Earlier methods like CLIPort and BC-Z.
- **Transformer-Based Control Policies:** The dominant paradigm, including Interactive language, Hiveformer, Gato, RoboCat, RT-1, Q-Transformer, and ACT.
- **Control Policies for Multimodal Instructions:** Models handling diverse input modalities (VIMA, MOO).
- **Control Policies with 3D Vision:** Approaches leveraging 3D inputs for richer environmental understanding (PerAct, Act3D, RVT, RoboUniView).
- **Diffusion-Based Control Policies:** Utilizing generative diffusion models for action generation (Diffusion Policy, SUDD, Octo, MDT, RDT-1B).
- **Diffusion-Based Control Policies with 3D Vision:** Combining diffusion with 3D inputs (DP3, 3D Diffuser Actor).
- **Control Policies for Motion Planning:** Integrating motion planning (language costs, VoxPoser, RoboTAP).
- **Control Policies with Point-Based Actions:** Leveraging VLMs to predict or select spatial points for actions (PIVOT, RoboPoint, ReKep).
- **Large VLA (LVLA):** Models adapting large VLMs for robotics, such as RT-2, RT-H, RT-X, OpenVLA, π0, RoboMamba, SpatialVLA, TinyVLA, CogACT, DexVLA, HybridVLA, LAPA, WorldVLA, UniVLA, and GR00T N1.
- A comprehensive table (Table III) summarizes these models' vision/language encoders, action decoders, architectures (also visually represented in Figure 5), training objectives, datasets, environments, and tasks. Strengths and limitations, including action types and scaling laws, are discussed.

3. **High-Level Task Planners:** This section examines VLA models responsible for decomposing complex, long-horizon tasks into manageable subtasks.
- **Monolithic Task Planners:** Single LLM or MLLM models for plan generation.
 - **End-to-End Task Planners:** MLLMs like PaLM-E and EmbodiedGPT.
 - **End-to-End Task Planners with 3D Vision:** Integrating 3D visual inputs (LEO, 3D-LLM, ShapeLLM).
 - **Grounded Task Planners:** Generating executable plans considering low-level policy capabilities (SayCan, Translated ⟨LM⟩, (SL)3).
- **Modular Task Planners:** Assembling off-the-shelf LLMs and VLMs.
 - **Language-Based Task Planners:** Using natural language for information exchange (Inner Monologue, LLM-Planner, LID, Socratic models).
 - **Code-Based Task Planners:** Generating executable programs via APIs (ProgPrompt, ChatGPT for robotics, CaP, DEPS, ConceptGraphs).
- Table IV summarizes these planners. Figure 6 illustrates the interaction paradigms of modular planners. Strengths and limitations are also provided.

Finally, the survey includes an extensive summary of relevant **Datasets and Benchmarks** (Tables V, VI, VII), covering real-world, simulated, automated collection, human data, and embodied question answering (EQA) benchmarks. It concludes by discussing **Challenges and Future Directions**, offering a forward-looking perspective on research needs in safety, foundation models, real-time responsiveness, multimodality, and ethical implications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey identifies and synthesizes several key findings regarding Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in embodied AI:

1. **VLA Models as a Converging Field:** The field of embodied AI is increasingly converging on VLA models, which integrate vision, language, and action modalities. This integration is driven by the successes of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), enabling robots to understand complex instructions, perceive diverse environments, and execute actions more effectively than previous deep reinforcement learning (RL) methods.

2. **Hierarchical Framework Dominance:** Current robotic systems primarily adopt a hierarchical framework. This involves high-level task planners that decompose complex, long-horizon user instructions into a sequence of subtasks, which are then executed by low-level control policies responsible for generating fine-grained actions. The survey extensively categorizes and analyzes research across both these hierarchical levels.

3. **Evolution of VLA Components:**
- **Perception (PVRs):** Advancements in pretrained visual representations (PVRs) are critical. The trend is moving from image-level representations (e.g., CLIP) to pixel-level and 3D features (e.g., DINOv2, I-JEPA, Theia), which provide richer detail crucial for precise robotic manipulation. Video representations are also gaining traction for their temporal and 3D information.
- **Dynamics and World Models:** There is a growing focus on dynamics learning (forward and inverse) and the development of world models. These enable model-based planning and control, allowing agents to predict future states and explore action sequences in imaginary spaces. LLM-induced world models leverage commonsense knowledge for abstract planning, while visual world models generate explicit visual or 3D representations of future states.
- **Reasoning:** LLM-driven Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is being integrated into VLAs to enhance decision-making and task planning, both at high-level linguistic abstraction and for guiding low-level actions.

4. **Architectural Trends in Control Policies:**
- **Shift to Transformers:** Robotic control policies have largely transitioned from non-Transformer architectures to Transformer-based models (e.g., RT-1, Gato, VIMA), leveraging their capacity for sequence modeling to handle diverse observations and actions.
- **Multimodal Inputs:** Policies are increasingly designed to handle multimodal instructions (e.g., language, demonstration videos, visual cues), improving task specificity and generalization.
- **3D Vision Integration:** The incorporation of 3D vision (e.g., point clouds, voxels) is recognized as providing richer spatial information for robust control, particularly in manipulation tasks.
- **Diffusion Models for Action Generation:** Diffusion models, successful in generative AI, are being adapted for visuomotor policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy, Octo, RDT-1B), offering capabilities for multimodal action distributions and high-dimensional action spaces.
- **Large VLA (LVLA) Emergence:** "Large VLAs" (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA) are emerging by adapting large VLMs, jointly trained on web-scale vision-language data and robot data. These models demonstrate emergent generalization capabilities, but also introduce challenges related to computational cost and inference speed.

5. **Task Planning Paradigms:**
- **Monolithic vs. Modular:** Task planners are developed either as monolithic end-to-end MLLMs (e.g., PaLM-E) that can perform high-level reasoning and generate plans, or as modular systems that compose off-the-shelf LLMs and VLMs.
- **Language-Based vs. Code-Based:** Modular planners utilize either natural language descriptions to exchange information between modules (e.g., Inner Monologue) or leverage LLMs' code-generation capabilities to invoke perception and control APIs (e.g., ProgPrompt, ChatGPT for robotics).
- **Grounded Planning:** A key aspect is "grounded" planning, where high-level actions are constrained by the actual capabilities (affordances) of the low-level control policies.

6. **Data and Benchmarking Landscape:**
- **Data Scarcity:** Real-world robot data remains scarce and inconsistent, posing a significant challenge. This drives reliance on simulated environments for data collection, despite the "sim-to-real gap."
- **Automated and Human Data:** Automated data collection methods and leveraging human demonstrations are explored to mitigate data limitations, although both present their own complexities (e.g., human-to-robot motion transfer, data labeling).
- **Evolving Benchmarks:** Benchmarks are diversifying to evaluate not only task success but also relevant underlying abilities like spatial reasoning and physics understanding (e.g., Embodied Question Answering).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey contributes to the field of embodied AI by consolidating and structuring the knowledge surrounding Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, thereby offering several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Foundation for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** The paper reinforces the understanding that embodied AI, enabled by VLA models, is a critical step towards AGI. By detailing the progress in equipping agents with perception, language understanding, and physical action capabilities, the survey underscores the potential for developing truly intelligent systems that can operate autonomously in complex real-world environments.

2. **Accelerating Robotic Capabilities:** VLA models represent a shift from task-specific, limited robotic controllers to more versatile, generalizable systems. The ability of VLAs to understand natural language instructions and adapt to diverse visual inputs can significantly expand the range and complexity of tasks robots can perform. This has the potential to move robotics beyond controlled industrial settings into more dynamic and unstructured environments, such as households, healthcare, and autonomous vehicles.

3. **Guiding Research and Development:** By presenting a comprehensive taxonomy of VLA components, control policies, and task planners, the survey provides a clear framework for researchers. This structured overview can facilitate understanding of the interdependencies between different research areas, help identify gaps in current knowledge, and inform the design of future VLA architectures. The detailed comparison tables of methodologies and resources serve as a valuable reference for practitioners entering or specializing within this domain.

4. **Highlighting Critical Challenges and Opportunities:** The explicit discussion of challenges, such as safety, data scarcity, real-time responsiveness, and achieving robust generalization, points to crucial areas requiring concerted research effort. Conversely, the outlined future directions, including the development of truly unified VLA foundation models, enhanced multimodal integration (beyond just vision and language), sophisticated long-horizon task planning, and addressing ethical implications, present fertile ground for innovative solutions.

5. **Resource Consolidation:** The extensive compilation of datasets, simulators, and benchmarks is a practical contribution that can streamline research efforts. It provides a centralized reference for evaluating new models and encourages standardized comparisons across studies, which is essential for measuring progress and fostering reproducible research in a rapidly evolving field.

6. **Informing the Broader AI Community:** The survey's generalized definition of VLA and its distinction from "Large VLAs" helps to clarify terminology and delineate the scope of this research area for the wider AI community. This clarity can foster interdisciplinary collaborations and facilitate the transfer of insights from other fields, such as core LLM and VLM development, into embodied AI.

In summary, this survey serves as a timely and foundational document for the VLA research community. It not only synthesizes the current state of a rapidly expanding field but also actively aims to shape its future trajectory by highlighting key advancements, identifying critical limitations, and articulating promising avenues for impactful research and development.
