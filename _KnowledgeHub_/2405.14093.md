# A Survey on Vision-Language-Action Models for Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, crucial for embodied AI, are comprehensively organized in this survey, detailing their architectures, training methodologies, and essential resources for language-conditioned robotic tasks. The work presents a novel taxonomy classifying VLAs by hierarchical control (low-level policies and high-level planners), identifying key components and future research opportunities.

## Problem

- The rapidly evolving landscape of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lacked a comprehensive and structured overview.
- Researchers faced challenges in understanding and categorizing the diverse architectures and approaches emerging in embodied AI for language-conditioned robotic tasks.
- A need existed to bridge the gap between advanced vision-language understanding and the generation of actionable outputs for physical robots.

## Method

- A structured methodology was employed to systematically review, categorize, and synthesize existing research on ==VLA models==.
- A novel ==hierarchical taxonomy== was developed, segmenting models into ==low-level control policies== and ==high-level task planners== based on their function in robotic systems.
- Comprehensive summaries of datasets, simulators, benchmarks, and data collection methods were aggregated to provide a resource hub for the community.

## Results

- The survey defines VLAs and Large VLAs (LVLAs), identifying their distinct role in enabling language-conditioned robotic tasks.
- Analysis revealed that VLA systems predominantly utilize a ==hierarchical control paradigm==, with distinct low-level control policies and high-level task planners.
- Key components like ==Pretrained Visual Representations (PVRs)==, dynamics learning, and world models were identified as crucial for VLA functionality, alongside trends towards Transformer-based architectures and diffusion models for action generation.
- Persistent challenges include significant data scarcity for real-world robot interactions, the sim-to-real gap, and the need for more comprehensive and fine-grained evaluation benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a critical and emerging category of multimodal AI for embodied systems, directly linking perception, language, and physical action.
> - The hierarchical control framework, comprising high-level task planners and low-level control policies, is a prevalent and effective approach for managing complexity in VLA systems.
> - The integration of large language models (LLMs) and large vision-language models (VLMs) as core components provides enhanced versatility and generalizability for VLA models.
