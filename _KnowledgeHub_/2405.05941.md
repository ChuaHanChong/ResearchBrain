# Evaluating Real-World Robot Manipulation Policies in Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SIMPLER is an open-source framework designed for reliable evaluation of real-world robot manipulation policies within simulation. It demonstrates strong correlation with real-world performance across diverse tasks and policies by addressing control and visual disparities between simulated and physical environments.

## Problem

- Evaluating robot manipulation policies on real hardware is expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to reproduce across different research labs.
- Traditional simulated evaluations often fail to accurately predict real-world policy performance due to significant control and visual disparities between simulation and reality.
- Existing metrics like Pearson correlation do not fully capture whether a simulation correctly ranks policies according to their real-world performance.

## Method

![[2405.05941-method.png]]
*SIMPLER's Visual Matching approach for closing the real-to-sim appearance gap: (1) green-screening simulated interactive assets onto real-world backgrounds and (2) texture matching that projects real object textures onto simulation assets while tuning robot arm colors from real videos.*

- The **SIMPLER** framework provides open-source simulated environments that faithfully replicate common real robot manipulation setups, including Google Robot and BridgeData V2.
- It addresses the control gap by using ==system identification== to fine-tune simulated robot controller parameters to match real-world trajectories.
- The visual gap is mitigated through techniques like ==green screening== real backgrounds onto simulated scenes, ==texture baking== real object textures onto simulated assets, and aggregating results from varied visual environments.

## Results

- SIMPLER achieves strong Pearson correlation coefficients (**r > 0.85** for Google Robot tasks, **r = 0.890** overall for BridgeData V2 tasks) between simulated and real-world success rates across various policies.
- The framework demonstrates low Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) values, indicating accurate ranking of policies by their real-world performance.
- SIMPLER accurately predicts a policy's robustness and performance drops when subjected to various distribution shifts, including changes in camera pose, table texture, distractor objects, lighting, and background.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - System identification and visual adaptation techniques, such as green screening and texture matching, are crucial for bridging the control and visual gaps between simulation and reality for reliable policy evaluation.
> - Physics simulation provides a more accurate prediction of a robot policy's real-world performance than the commonly used validation action Mean Squared Error (MSE).
> - The Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) metric effectively quantifies the reliability of policy ranking in simulation, offering a robust measure of simulation fidelity beyond simple correlation.
