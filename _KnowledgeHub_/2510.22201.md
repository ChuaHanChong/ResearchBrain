# ACG: Action Coherence Guidance for Flow-based Vision-Language-Action models

> [!summary] Summary
> Action Coherence Guidance (ACG) is a test-time method that improves the temporal smoothness and consistency of robot actions generated by flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. By steering the policy away from an artificially incoherent action generation, ACG increased task success rates on RoboCasa by 6.7% and on real-world Strawberry stacking by 30.8%, producing smoother trajectories without additional training.

## Problem

- Flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often generate incoherent actions (jerks, pauses, jitter) by memorizing noise present in human demonstration data.
- This lack of action coherence leads to unstable robot movements and trajectory drift, causing task failures, especially in fine-grained manipulation.
- Existing solutions like action chunking or general Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) either have limitations or are not effective enough at addressing intra-chunk action smoothness for manipulation.

## Method

- Introduces **ACG**, a training-free, ==test-time guidance== strategy that steers ==flow-matching==-based VLA policies away from an intentionally constructed incoherent vector field.
- Formulates a guided vector field by subtracting a specifically engineered 'incoherent' vector field (scaled by a guidance parameter) from the original policy's ==denoising vector field==.
- The 'incoherent' vector field is constructed by modifying ==self-attention== layers within the transformer-based policy, replacing ==attention maps== with identity matrices to disrupt temporal communication among action tokens.

## Results

- ACG consistently improved task success rates across diverse benchmarks: **6.7%** on RoboCasa (from **32.6%** to **39.3%**), **3.4%** on DexMimicGen, and a substantial **30.8%** on real-world Strawberry stacking (from **43.6%** to **74.4%**).
- Quantitatively, ACG achieved the lowest JerkRMS (**1.156 rad/s³**) among all methods, indicating the smoothest movements, and outperformed other baselines like Classifier-Free Guidance and White Noise Guidance.
- The method successfully generalized to other flow-based VLA models (π₀ and SmolVLA), demonstrating notable performance improvements over their vanilla versions without requiring additional training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly guiding a generative policy away from an internally constructed 'incoherent' version of itself can effectively enhance action smoothness and consistency in robot control.
> - Disrupting the temporal self-attention mechanism within transformer-based flow models by replacing attention maps with identity matrices is an effective way to create a strong negative guidance signal for action incoherence.
> - Intra-chunk action coherence (smoothness within a generated action sequence) is a critical factor for successful fine-grained robotic manipulation, distinct from and complementary to inter-chunk consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is increasingly leveraging generative models, particularly diffusion and flow matching models, as policies for robot control. These Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are designed to learn complex action distributions from human demonstrations, enabling robots to generalize across diverse scenes and language instructions. Examples include models like GR00T-N1, π₀, and SmolVLA, which employ generative action heads to provide stability and expressiveness for continuous control tasks.

A persistent challenge in imitation learning, particularly with these high-capacity generative models, is their sensitivity to noise present in human demonstrations. Imperfections such as jerks, pauses, or jitter in recorded human actions can be memorized by the policy, leading to a degradation in "action coherence." Action coherence refers to the smoothness and consistency of successive actions, quantifiable by metrics such as variability or jerk. The loss of action coherence in deployed robot policies manifests as unstable actions during critical moments (e.g., fumbling near an object) or cumulative minor action noise leading to trajectory drift, both of which can result in task failure, especially in fine-grained manipulation requiring precision.

Prior efforts to address action coherence include techniques like action chunking, which generates multiple actions simultaneously to reduce compounding errors and promote smoother trajectories. Temporal ensembling has also been applied, though its computational cost can be high for non-autoregressive flow matching policies. More recently, guidance strategies from the flow matching literature, such as Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), have been explored to improve sample quality and condition adherence without additional training. CFG, by steering generation away from an unconditional vector field, aims to strengthen conditional signals. However, direct application of CFG using language conditions in VLA models can lead to instability, and other guidance methods have focused on inter-chunk coherence.

This work introduces Action Coherence Guidance (ACG) as a novel test-time guidance strategy. Unlike existing approaches that might smooth actions post-generation, apply noise, or focus on coherence between action chunks, ACG specifically targets *intra-chunk* coherence. It adapts the concept of "perturbation guidance," previously used in visual generation to steer models away from intentionally degraded versions of themselves, to robot control. By explicitly constructing an "incoherent" version of the action generation process and using it as negative guidance, ACG aims to reduce noise and enhance the smoothness of actions predicted within a single chunk, thereby filling a gap in improving fine-grained manipulation performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from a critical limitation observed in contemporary flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their susceptibility to generating incoherent actions when trained on human demonstration data. Despite their powerful generative capabilities, these models often learn and replicate imperfections inherent in human demonstrations, such as momentary pauses, sudden jerks, or fine-grained jitter. This "noise" is memorized due to the models' high generative capacity, leading to a reduction in the temporal coherence of the learned robot policies.

Action coherence, defined as the smoothness and consistency of successive actions, is paramount for robust and reliable robotic manipulation. A lack of coherence leads to two significant types of failures during robot deployment:
1. **Instability:** Incoherent actions can cause erratic or unstable movements, particularly at critical points in a task. For instance, during a pick-and-place operation, a robot might exhibit fumbling behavior near the target object or inadvertently push it away, leading to task failure.
2. **Trajectory Drift:** Even minor, seemingly insignificant action noise can accumulate over time, causing the robot's end-effector trajectory to gradually deviate from the intended path. This drift can result in the robot missing its target or performing actions outside the desired operational envelope.

These failures are particularly catastrophic in fine-grained manipulation tasks where precision, stability, and smooth contact are essential. Therefore, ensuring high action coherence in generated action sequences is a fundamental requirement for enhancing the reliability and overall success rates of robot policies.

The key objective of this paper is to develop and evaluate a novel, training-free, test-time guidance algorithm, termed Action Coherence Guidance (ACG). ACG is designed to directly address the problem of action incoherence in flow-based VLA models. The specific goals are:
- To devise a mechanism that intentionally perturbs the internal workings of a trained flow-based VLA model to produce an "incoherent" action generation vector.
- To leverage this incoherent vector field to guide the original policy during inference, steering it away from unstable and temporally inconsistent behaviors and towards more stable and coherent actions.
- To demonstrate that this guidance strategy improves action coherence quantitatively (e.g., lower jerk, less variation) and qualitatively (smoother trajectories).
- To validate the effectiveness of ACG in boosting manipulation performance, measured by task success rates, across a diverse range of simulated and real-world robotic tasks, without requiring any additional model training or fine-tuning.
- To assess ACG's generalizability across different flow-based VLA model architectures and its robustness to hyperparameter variations.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a practical and effective solution for enhancing the reliability and precision of robot policies derived from imitation learning, thereby moving closer to deploying more robust and capable autonomous manipulation systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Action Coherence Guidance (ACG), is a training-free, test-time guidance strategy designed to improve action coherence in flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. It builds upon principles of conditional flow matching and perturbation guidance.

**A. Flow Matching Policy Background**
The foundation of the VLA models used in this work is a rectified flow matching policy. This policy is trained via imitation learning on human demonstrations, where each demonstration `D` consists of sequences of language instructions (`ℓ_t`), observations (`o_t`), and actions (`a_t`). The goal is to model the conditional action distribution `p(A_t | o_t, ℓ_t)`, where `A_t = [a_t..., a_{t+k-1}]` represents an action chunk of `k` future actions.

During training, a neural network `v_θ` (the policy) is learned to predict a denoising vector field. Given a noisy action chunk `A_τ_t` (constructed from a clean chunk `A_t`, a flow matching timestep `τ`, and sampled noise `ϵ`), the policy `v_θ(A_τ_t, o_t, ℓ_t, τ)` is trained to match the conditional denoising vector `u(A_τ_t | A_t) = A_t - ϵ`. The training objective minimizes the squared difference between the predicted vector field and the target denoising vector.

During inference, the policy generates an action chunk by integrating the learned vector field `v_θ`. Starting from random noise `A₀_t ~ N(0, I)`, the action chunk is iteratively denoised using forward Euler integration: `A_τ+δ_t = A_τ_t + δ v_θ(A_τ_t, o_t, ℓ_t, τ)`, where `δ` is the integration step size.

**B. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) for Flow Matching Policy**
ACG draws inspiration from Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), a technique used in generative models to enhance the influence of conditioning signals. CFG modifies the generated distribution by amplifying the conditional distribution relative to an unconditional one. For flow-based models, this translates to a guided vector field:
`v_CFG(λ)_θ = (1 + λ) v_θ(A_τ_t, o_t, ℓ_t, τ) - λ v_θ(A_τ_t, o_t, ∅, τ)`
Here, `v_θ(A_τ_t, o_t, ℓ_t, τ)` is the conditional vector field, `v_θ(A_τ_t, o_t, ∅, τ)` is the unconditional vector field (e.g., without language instruction `ℓ_t`), and `λ` is the guidance scale. CFG strengthens conditional generation by pushing the denoising direction away from the unconditional vector field.

**C. Action Coherence Guidance (ACG) Method**
ACG proposes a novel guidance strategy where the policy is steered away from an *intentionally constructed incoherent vector field* rather than an unconditional one. The guided distribution for ACG is formulated as:
`π_ACG(λ)(A_t | o_t, ℓ_t) ∝ π_θ(A_t | o_t, ℓ_t) * (π_θ(A_t | o_t, ℓ_t) / π_IC_θ(A_t | o_t, ℓ_t))^λ`
where `π_IC_θ` denotes the distribution induced by an incoherent denoising vector.
This leads to the ACG guided vector field:
`v_ACG(λ)_θ = (1 + λ) v_θ(A_τ_t, o_t, ℓ_t, τ) - λ v_IC_θ(A_τ_t, o_t, ℓ_t, τ)`
Here, `v_θ` is the original (base) denoising vector field, and `v_IC_θ` is the specifically constructed *incoherent* denoising vector field. The term `λ` is the guidance scale. The action chunk is then generated by integrating `v_ACG(λ)_θ` via forward Euler integration.

**D. Constructing the Incoherent Action Generation Vector (v_IC_θ)**
The core innovation of ACG lies in how `v_IC_θ` is constructed. Flow matching policies typically employ a transformer-based architecture, utilizing self-attention layers to ensure temporal coherence among generated tokens (actions across timesteps within a chunk). The self-attention mechanism, `Attn(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKᵀ/√d)V`, where `softmax(QKᵀ/√d)` forms the attention map, allows each action token to communicate with and attend to other action tokens, thereby promoting temporal consistency.

To generate an *incoherent* action vector field `v_IC_θ`, ACG modifies the self-attention mechanism. Specifically, in designated self-attention layers, the attention map is replaced with an *identity matrix* `I`. This modification forces each action token to attend *only to itself* and prevents it from communicating with other tokens within the sequence.
`Attn_IC(Q, K, V) = I * V = V`
By disrupting this temporal communication, `v_IC_θ` produces an action chunk with significantly reduced temporal coherence. This "incoherent" vector then serves as the negative guidance direction, enabling ACG to steer the sampling process *away* from these unstable behaviors and *towards* smoother, more coherent action sequences.

**E. Implementation Details**
The GR00T-N1 model is primarily used. For generating `v_IC_θ`, ACG replaces the attention map with an identity map in specific self-attention layers (e.g., layers 4-6 out of eight total layers, with a default guidance scale of 3.0). This requires a second forward pass of the model during inference, where selected self-attention layers are modified, incurring a computational overhead (approximately 1.5x with partial perturbation).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated ACG's effectiveness in improving action coherence and manipulation performance across simulated and real-world benchmarks, using GR00T-N1 as the primary VLA model.

**A. Overall Performance Improvements**
ACG consistently demonstrated performance gains across all evaluated benchmarks:
- **RoboCasa (24 tasks):** ACG improved success rates by 6.7% over the Vanilla GR00T-N1 baseline (39.3% vs. 32.6%).
- **DexMimicGen (9 tasks):** ACG showed a 3.4% improvement (44.0% vs. 40.6%).
- **Real-world SO-101 (2 tasks):**
 - **Three Strawberries:** ACG achieved a substantial 30.8% increase (74.4% vs. 43.6%).
 - **Tic-Tac-Toe:** ACG improved by 9.6% (56.7% vs. 38.3%).
These results indicate that ACG generalizes across diverse manipulation tasks and environments without requiring additional training, functioning as a plug-and-play enhancement.

**B. Comparison with Baselines**
- **Vanilla GR00T-N1:** Serves as the base performance without any coherence enhancement.
- **Action Smoothing Methods (Ensemble, Action Smoothing, Feature Smoothing):** These methods yielded modest improvements over the vanilla baseline (e.g., Ensemble (n=5) at 42.9% average, Feature Smoothing at 44.9% average). While they indicate the importance of smoothness, direct manipulation of actions or features can blur fine-grained details or cause hesitations, leading to marginal gains.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** CFG achieved 42.5% average success, performing worse than most smoothing-based approaches in manipulation tasks. This suggests that strengthening language conditioning, as CFG does, is not directly analogous or as effective as directly addressing action coherence for manipulation performance.
- **White Noise Guidance (WNG):** WNG, which perturbs intermediate action features with white noise to create a negative guidance direction, was the second-best performing baseline, averaging 47.7% success. This supports the concept of perturbation guidance. However, WNG exhibited a trade-off: small noise didn't sufficiently disrupt temporal structure, while large noise eroded pretrained knowledge, leading to a balance issue between coherence and accuracy. ACG outperformed WNG by 4.3% on RoboCasa and 8.8% on Three Strawberries, indicating superior disruption of temporal coherence without degrading task-relevant information.

**C. Task-Wise Performance Analysis**
ACG demonstrated particular effectiveness in fine-grained manipulation skills:
- **Press Button:** A significant 23.1% improvement.
- **Insertion:** An 11.8% improvement.
These results suggest that action coherence is especially critical for tasks demanding high precision and delicate interactions.

**D. Action Coherence Analysis**
To quantitatively assess action coherence, two metrics were used: Action Total Variation (ATV, rad/s) and JerkRMS (rad/s³). Lower values indicate greater coherence.
- **Incoherent (v_IC_θ):** The intentionally constructed incoherent variant exhibited substantially worse action coherence (ATV: 4.509, JerkRMS: 1.993) compared to Vanilla GR00T-N1 (ATV: 1.314, JerkRMS: 1.353), confirming its design goal.
- **ACG:** ACG achieved the lowest JerkRMS (1.156) among all methods, indicating the smoothest movements. Its ATV (1.130) was comparable to ensemble methods (e.g., Ensemble (n=5): 0.984).
Qualitatively, visualizations of gripper endpoint trajectories (Fig. 6) showed that Vanilla GR00T-N1 often produced jerky, unstable motions that could knock objects away. Ensemble methods provided smoother trajectories but sometimes hesitated or blurred distinct action paths. ACG, in contrast, generated accurate and temporally consistent actions, leading to higher success rates without sacrificing accuracy. This highlights ACG's ability to produce highly coherent action sequences critical for manipulation.

**E. Ablation Study**
- **Hyperparameter Impact:**
 - **Guidance Scale (λ):** Increasing `λ` initially improved performance, aligning with prior guidance literature. However, excessively large scales led to performance degradation due to deviation from the pretrained distribution.
 - **Number of Incoherent Self-Attention Layers:** Using 2 to 6 incoherent layers consistently improved performance, demonstrating robustness to this choice.
 - **Positions of Incoherent Layers:** Perturbing middle and later self-attention layers generally yielded better results, while perturbing early layers occasionally hurt performance.
- **Comparison with Self-GAD:** Self-GAD, a concurrent work, focuses on *inter-chunk* coherence. When evaluated under the receding horizon setting, both ACG and Self-GAD outperformed the baseline, but ACG (intra-chunk coherence) achieved higher success rates. Combining both methods yielded the best results, suggesting their complementary nature and indicating that intra-chunk coherence is more critical for fine-grained manipulation than inter-chunk consistency alone.
- **Generalization to Other Flow-based VLA Models:** ACG was successfully applied to π₀ and SmolVLA, two other standard flow-based VLA models. It demonstrated notable performance improvements over the vanilla versions of these models (e.g., for π₀ on Three Strawberries: 53.3% vs. 41.1%; for SmolVLA: 22.2% vs. 16.7%). This confirms ACG's generalizability across different DiT-based flow architectures by simply modifying self-attention layers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Action Coherence Guidance (ACG), a novel and effective test-time guidance strategy that addresses the critical issue of action incoherence in flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The work demonstrates that explicitly guiding a policy away from an intentionally constructed incoherent action generation vector can significantly enhance the smoothness and stability of robot movements, leading to improved task success rates.

The significance and potential impact of ACG can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Robot Reliability and Precision:** By improving action coherence, ACG directly contributes to more stable and precise robot control. This is particularly impactful for fine-grained manipulation tasks where subtle movements and accurate object interactions are crucial. The consistent performance gains observed across diverse simulated and real-world benchmarks, especially in tasks like button pressing and insertion, suggest that ACG can make robot policies more robust and dependable in practical applications.

2. **Novel Application of Perturbation Guidance:** ACG extends the concept of "perturbation guidance" from visual generation to robot control. This approach, which involves using an intentionally degraded version of the model as negative guidance, offers a new paradigm for inference-time policy improvement in robotics. It contrasts with traditional Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) by focusing on an internally generated "undesirable" characteristic (incoherence) rather than simply an "unconditional" state.

3. **Training-Free and Plug-and-Play Solution:** A key advantage of ACG is its nature as a test-time, training-free method. This means it can be readily applied to existing pre-trained flow-based VLA models without requiring any costly retraining or fine-tuning. This "plug-and-play" capability makes ACG a highly practical and accessible tool for researchers and practitioners aiming to improve the performance of their robot policies.

4. **Addressing Intra-Chunk Coherence:** The work highlights the importance of coherence *within* an action chunk, a largely underexplored area compared to inter-chunk consistency. By showing that intra-chunk coherence guidance (ACG) outperforms inter-chunk guidance (Self-GAD) for manipulation tasks, and that their combination yields further benefits, the research emphasizes a critical aspect of action generation for fine-grained control.

5. **Generalizability Across Model Architectures:** The successful application of ACG to various flow-based VLA models (GR00T-N1, π₀, SmolVLA) demonstrates its broad applicability. This suggests that the underlying mechanism of perturbing self-attention layers to induce incoherence is effective across different transformer-based diffusion and flow models used in robotics.

6. **Inspiration for Future Research:** The paper's conclusion frames ACG not just as a tool but as a "principle for producing coherent and reliable actions in robotics." This perspective could inspire future work in several directions, including exploring other methods for constructing "negative" guidance signals, extending coherence guidance across chunk boundaries more effectively, and optimizing the computational overhead associated with the dual forward passes.

While ACG introduces a computational cost due to requiring a second forward pass (approx. 1.5x with partial perturbation), its significant performance benefits and ease of integration position it as a valuable advancement for deploying more capable and robust robotic manipulation systems.
