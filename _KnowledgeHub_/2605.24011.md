# ActQuant: Sub-4-bit Action-Guided Quantization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ActQuant introduces an action-guided mixed-precision post-training quantization framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling their deployment on resource-constrained edge robotic platforms. The method achieves robust task success rates at sub-4-bit precision, compressing VLA models by up to 5.3x while maintaining performance (e.g., 95.0% success rate at 3.0 bits-per-weight for OpenVLA-OFT compared to 96.9% FP16).

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have substantial computational footprints (e.g., 14 GB for OpenVLA-OFT), making their deployment on resource-constrained edge robotic platforms impractical.
- Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods, optimized for LLMs, cause severe performance degradation in VLA models at sub-4-bit precision due to mismatch in optimization goals and compounding errors in closed-loop control.
- Current VLA-specific quantization efforts are either training-based (not post-hoc), target higher bit-widths (e.g., 4-bit weights), or yield hardware-unfriendly mixed-precision schemes, failing to meet the need for efficient sub-4-bit solutions.

## Method

- An action-guided mixed-precision post-training quantization (PTQ) framework, **ActQuant**, assigns bit-widths at the inter-tensor level using an action-aware sensitivity score based on the ==Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC)==.
- Intra-tensor scale optimization refines quantization by optimizing per-block scales using an Action-Mixed Fisher (AMF) importance signal, which blends action-head and language-modeling losses to capture VLA-specific sensitivities.
- The OmniModel.cpp pipeline is an agentic C/C++ deployment system that automates the conversion of PyTorch VLA models to a native GGML runtime, ensuring efficient execution of ActQuant-quantized models on edge hardware.

## Results

- ActQuant maintained high task success rates in simulation (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT: **95.0%** at **3.0** bits-per-weight (bpw) and **90.1%** at **2.5** bpw, compared to **96.9%** FP16), outperforming competing methods that collapsed below **3.0** bpw.
- Real-world deployment on a UR3 arm using a "pi_0.5" model quantized at **3.0** bpw achieved a **75.0%** average success rate (vs. **77.5%** FP16), alongside a memory reduction from **6.7** GB to **2.7** GB (**2.5x** compression).
- The OmniModel.cpp pipeline enabled ActQuant-quantized models to achieve up to **1.5x** inference speedup on workstations and **1.3x** on edge devices compared to PyTorch, while also supporting CPU/GPU memory splitting for constrained hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Quantizing VLA models effectively at sub-4-bit precision requires explicit consideration of action prediction quality, as methods optimized solely for language modeling signals lead to performance collapse in closed-loop robotic control.
> - A hardware-friendly mixed-precision strategy that allocates bit-widths per-tensor based on action-relevance and optimizes per-block scales using an action-aware Fisher information is crucial for preserving VLA performance at very low bit-widths.
> - Automating the complex conversion of PyTorch VLA models to native C/C++ runtimes with an agentic pipeline significantly streamlines deployment and ensures that algorithmic quantization gains translate into practical on-device efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an approach to generalist robotic manipulation, integrating visual observations and natural language instructions to generate low-level motor commands. Architectures such as OpenVLA and the π family models have demonstrated capabilities in discrete and continuous action generation, respectively. These models, however, are characterized by their substantial computational footprints. For example, OpenVLA-OFT, which is built upon a 7B LLaMA2 large language model (LLM) and a vision encoder, requires approximately 14 GB of weight memory in FP16 precision.

The considerable memory and computational requirements of VLA models pose a significant challenge for their deployment on edge platforms, such as robotic hardware equipped with accelerators like the NVIDIA Jetson Orin family, which typically offer 8–16 GB of unified memory. To bridge this gap, aggressive quantization, particularly in the sub-4-bit weight regime, has emerged as a potential solution for reducing model size and enabling on-device inference.

While post-training quantization (PTQ) methods have been extensively investigated for LLMs (e.g., AWQ, GPTQ), these methods often exhibit severe performance degradation when applied to VLA models in the sub-4-bit regime, frequently collapsing below 3 bits-per-weight (bpw). This limitation arises because LLM-centric quantization typically optimizes for language modeling signals, which may not adequately preserve the features critical for a downstream action head to decode into effective control signals. Quantization errors in VLA models can also compound over closed-loop robotic rollouts, leading to a rapid decline in task success rates.

Existing VLA-specific quantization efforts have explored various approaches:
- **Training-based methods:** BitVLA and QAIL involve full training pipelines, which are not post-hoc or training-free.
- **Weight-activation quantization:** QuantVLA targets 4-bit weights and 8-bit activations, which does not address the sub-4-bit weight regime.
- **Mixed-precision methods:** QVLA uses channel-wise bit allocation, which can be hardware-unfriendly due to irregular memory access patterns and has not shown sufficient accuracy below 4 bits. Other mixed-precision schemes, such as codebook-based or channel-wise approaches, also often yield hardware-unfriendly memory access patterns. Layer-wise allocation, while hardware-friendly, is often too coarse to capture the sensitivity variations required for accuracy below 4 bits.

This research addresses the need for a hardware-friendly, mixed-precision PTQ framework specifically designed for VLA models that can maintain high performance in the aggressive sub-4-bit weight quantization regime, thereby enabling practical deployment on edge robotic platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the computational intensity of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which renders their deployment on resource-constrained edge robotic platforms impractical. Modern VLA models, often built with large language model backbones and sophisticated vision encoders, demand gigabytes of memory for storing their weights, far exceeding the capacities of typical on-board accelerators.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Enabling Edge Deployment for VLA Models:** The core problem ActQuant aims to solve is the large memory footprint of VLA models, which prevents their use in real-world robotic systems where compute resources are limited. Successful deployment on a robot itself requires significant model compression.

2. **Addressing Performance Degradation in Sub-4-bit Quantization:** Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods, predominantly developed for Large Language Models (LLMs), fail to maintain adequate performance in VLA models when pushed to aggressive sub-4-bit quantization levels. Performance often collapses below 3 bits-per-weight (bpw). This degradation occurs because:
 - **Mismatch in Optimization Goals:** LLM-focused methods optimize for language modeling, which is only loosely connected to the action prediction quality crucial for VLA tasks.
 - **Compounding Errors in Closed-Loop Systems:** Small quantization errors in VLA models can accumulate over successive action predictions in a closed-loop control system, leading to substantial deviations and failure in robotic tasks.
 - **Lack of VLA-Specific Low-Bit Solutions:** Existing VLA-specific quantization methods either require extensive re-training (e.g., BitVLA, QAIL) or target higher bit-widths (e.g., QuantVLA at 4-bit weights), or are not designed for hardware efficiency (e.g., QVLA's channel-wise allocation).

3. **Leveraging Existing Hardware Efficiencies:** Mainstream on-device runtimes, such as those built on the GGML tensor library (e.g., llama.cpp), already provide efficient block-quantization kernels for 2-4-bit inference. However, algorithmic methods for VLA models have not yet exploited this system-level support effectively. The motivation is to develop an algorithmic approach that aligns with these hardware capabilities.

4. **Developing a Hardware-Friendly Mixed-Precision Quantization Scheme:** Current mixed-precision approaches for quantization are often unsuitable for efficient hardware execution due to irregular memory access patterns (e.g., codebook-based or channel-wise quantization). Layer-wise allocation, while hardware-friendly, is too coarse to achieve the necessary accuracy at very low bit-widths. This work is motivated by the need for a "per-tensor middle ground" that allows bit-widths to vary across weight matrices based on their action relevance, yet remains uniform within each matrix to ensure hardware-friendly dense integer kernel operations.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the ActQuant framework are:
- To introduce an action-guided mixed-precision PTQ framework that assigns bit-widths at the inter-tensor level (one bit-width per weight matrix) based on action prediction contribution.
- To refine quantization at the intra-tensor level by optimizing per-block quantization scales using an action-aware importance signal.
- To develop an agentic conversion pipeline (OmniModel.cpp) that ports diverse VLA architectures into a native C/C++ runtime, enabling efficient execution of ActQuant-quantized models on edge hardware.
- To demonstrate that this approach can maintain high task success rates for VLA models in the sub-4-bit regime, specifically at or below 3 bpw, with significant memory compression and inference speedups, both in simulation and on a physical robot.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ActQuant is an action-guided mixed-precision post-training quantization (PTQ) framework operating in two distinct stages, complemented by a native C/C++ deployment pipeline called OmniModel.cpp. The method targets the weights of the vision encoder ($E_v$) and the transformer-based language backbone ($G$), while keeping the projector ($P$) and action head ($H$) in full precision due to their criticality and relatively small parameter count. The quantization scheme adopted is block quantization, where each block of elements within a weight matrix has its own scale and zero point, and all elements within a block are encoded with the same bit-width.

**Stage 1: Inter-Tensor Bit Allocation**
This stage addresses the assignment of a single bit-width to each weight matrix ($W_j^{(\ell)}$) within the VLA backbone, based on its contribution to action generation.

- **Action-aware Sensitivity:** A sensitivity score, $S(W_j^{(\ell)})$, is defined using the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC). This kernel-based measure quantifies statistical dependence between random variables without requiring density estimation. The sensitivity for a weight matrix $W_j^{(\ell)}$ is computed as:
 $S(W_j^{(\ell)}) = - \alpha \text{ HSIC}(X, Z_j^{(\ell)}) + \beta \text{ HSIC}(Z_j^{(\ell)}, Y)$
 where $X$ represents the input hidden states, $Z_j^{(\ell)}$ are the output hidden states of $W_j^{(\ell)}$, and $Y$ denotes the ground-truth action labels from a calibration set. The RBF kernel is utilized for HSIC computation.
 - The term $\beta \text{ HSIC}(Z_j^{(\ell)}, Y)$ acts as a task-relevance score. A higher value indicates that the output of $W_j^{(\ell)}$ is statistically aligned with the ground-truth actions, suggesting its importance for control and thus warranting a higher bit-width.
 - The term $-\alpha \text{ HSIC}(X, Z_j^{(\ell)})$ measures input redundancy. A large value suggests that the layer's output contains information already present in its input, implying it can tolerate more aggressive quantization. This term is subtracted to prioritize matrices that introduce novel, task-relevant information.
 - $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are hyperparameters used to balance the two HSIC terms.

- **Layer-balanced Bit Allocation:** To convert sensitivities into bit-width assignments, the method minimizes the sum of squared per-layer errors under a global average bits-per-weight budget ($\bar{b}$). The predicted reconstruction error for a matrix $W_j^{(\ell)}$ at a given bit-width type $t_j^{(\ell)}$ is modeled as $S(W_j^{(\ell)}) \eta(t_j^{(\ell)})$, where $\eta(t) = 2^{-2b(t)}$. The objective is:
 $\min_{\{t_j^{(\ell)}\}_{\ell, j}} \sum_{\ell=1}^L E_\ell^2 \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \frac{\sum_{\ell, j} b(t_j^{(\ell)}) |W_j^{(\ell)}|}{\sum_{\ell, j} |W_j^{(\ell)}|} \le \bar{b}$
 where $E_\ell = \sum_{j \in J} S(W_j^{(\ell)}) \eta(t_j^{(\ell)})$ is the total error for layer $\ell$. Minimizing the sum of squared errors ensures that large errors in a single sensitive layer are disproportionately penalized, promoting a balanced distribution of quantization error across layers.

- **Solver:** A greedy procedure is employed. Starting with all tensors at the lowest bit-width, the algorithm iteratively upgrades the bit-width of the tensor that yields the highest gain-per-bit ratio (reduction in total squared error per additional bit), until the average bits-per-weight budget is met.

**Stage 2: Intra-Tensor Scale Optimization**
With the per-tensor bit-widths fixed from Stage 1, this stage optimizes the per-block scales within each matrix to further minimize quantization error, using an action-aware importance signal.

- **Hessian Approximation:** The objective is a Hessian-weighted reconstruction problem. The intractable Hessian matrix is approximated by its diagonal elements, derived from the empirical Fisher information matrix.

- **Action-Mixed Fisher (AMF):** The Fisher information is tailored to reflect the VLA model's action loss. For architectures like OpenVLA-OFT that have both an action-head loss ($L_{act_d}$) and a language-modeling (LM) head categorical loss ($L_{cls_d}$), the AMF loss is defined as a convex combination:
 $L_{AMF_d}(\theta) = \alpha L_{act_d}(\theta) + (1-\alpha) L_{cls_d}(\theta)$, where $\alpha \in$.
 For models with only an action-head pathway (e.g., $\pi_{0.5}$), $\alpha$ is set to 1, reducing AMF to an Action-Only Fisher. The diagonal elements of the AMF Fisher matrix provide per-element importance scores, $F_{AMF_{ii}}$, computed from the squared gradients of $L_{AMF_d}$. This blended approach incorporates both action-head and categorical signals, capturing cross-pathway covariances that single-loss Fishers would miss.

- **Block-Level Scale Optimization:** For each block $b$ within a matrix, a per-element weight sensitivity $\omega_{b,i}$ is defined, multiplying the AMF sensitivity by a local weight magnitude factor:
 $\omega_{b,i} = \sqrt{F_{AMF_{ii}}} \sqrt{\sigma_b^2 + w_i^2}$
 This weighting ensures that quantization noise is penalized most heavily for elements that are both critical for task performance (high $F_{AMF_{ii}}$) and have a large magnitude within their block. The per-block scales ($s_b$) and quantized codewords ($q_i$) are then optimized by minimizing a weighted least-squares reconstruction error, iteratively alternating between:
 1. Assigning each codeword $q_i$ to the codebook entry closest to $w_i/s_b$.
 2. Updating $s_b$ with a closed-form solution derived from the stationarity condition of the weighted least-squares problem, given the fixed codewords.
 This iterative process, a constrained analogue of Lloyd-Max scalar quantization, monotonically decreases the objective and converges to a local minimum.

**OmniModel.cpp: Agentic Pipeline for VLA Deployment**
To ensure the practical on-device benefits of ActQuant, OmniModel.cpp is introduced. This agentic pipeline automates the conversion of PyTorch VLA models into native C/C++ inference programs compatible with the GGML computation graph.
- **Motivation:** Standard GGML/llama.cpp primarily supports decoder-only LLMs, lacking built-in support for diverse VLA backbones. Manual porting is laborious.
- **Architecture:** OmniModel.cpp utilizes a three-layer agent:
 1. **Shared Knowledge Base:** Stores PyTorch source code, checkpoints, an initial C++ skeleton, and a repository of previously converted backbones to facilitate reuse.
 2. **Procedural Skills Library:** Contains reusable, self-contained subtasks (e.g., GGUF serialization, GGML operator registration, Python binding setup).
 3. **Automated Verification Loop:** The agent operates within a sandbox, performing compilation and semantic codebase searches. Intermediate tensors from the PyTorch reference are cached. After each module is compiled, its output cosine similarity is compared against the cache. If similarity falls below a threshold (e.g., 0.99), a reflector component diagnoses the discrepancy, patches the implementation, and rebuilds, iterating until numerical parity is achieved.
- **Conversion Process:** The pipeline follows a sequence: analyzer, planner, GGUF converter (which serializes ActQuant's per-block scales and per-tensor bit-widths), C++ model design, and validator.
- **Benefit:** This pipeline enables ActQuant-quantized VLA models to be executed directly and efficiently using GGML's existing low-bit kernels, resulting in reduced memory footprint and lower inference latency on edge hardware.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ActQuant was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and on a physical robot, comparing it against established weight-only PTQ baselines (AWQ, GPTQ) and a VLA-specific mixed-precision method (QVLA).

**Simulation Results on LIBERO Benchmark (Table 1):**
- **OpenVLA-OFT:**
 - ActQuant demonstrated robustness in aggressive quantization, retaining a 95.0% success rate at 3.0 bits-per-weight (bpw) and 90.1% at 2.5 bpw, relative to the 96.9% FP16 baseline.
 - In contrast, QVLA's performance significantly declined, reaching only 37.4% at 3.0 bpw and collapsing to 0.0% at 2.5 bpw.
 - AWQ and GPTQ showed even earlier degradation, with their success rates dropping from above 89% at 3.0 bpw to 0.0% at 2.0 bpw across all suites.
 - Memory footprint: At 2.5 bpw, ActQuant compressed the OpenVLA-OFT model from 14.3 GB to 2.7 GB, representing a 5.3x reduction.
- **$\pi_{0.5}$:**
 - ActQuant achieved a 94.8% success rate at 3.0 bpw. It was also the only method capable of operating at 2.0 bpw, albeit with a reduced success rate of 48.0%, whereas all other baselines completely failed at this level.

**Real-World Robot Deployment (Table 3):**
- To validate the transferability of simulation gains, ActQuant-quantized models were deployed on a 6-DoF Universal Robots UR3 arm.
- A $\pi_{0.5}$ model quantized with ActQuant at 3.0 bpw achieved a 75.0% average success rate across four manipulation tasks, closely matching the 77.5% of the FP16 baseline.
- This deployment resulted in a memory reduction from 6.7 GB to 2.7 GB (a 2.5x compression), confirming that the memory and performance benefits observed in simulation generalize to physical hardware.

**Inference Speedup with OmniModel.cpp (Table 2):**
- The OmniModel.cpp pipeline, producing a Q4_K_M build of OpenVLA, consistently achieved the lowest per-token inference latency across various hardware platforms:
 - NVIDIA RTX A6000 workstation: 1.5x speedup (233 ms for PyTorch vs. 153 ms for OmniModel.cpp).
 - Apple MacBook Pro M4 Pro: 1.3x speedup (1319 ms vs. 999 ms).
 - NVIDIA AGX Thor edge box: 1.3x speedup (598 ms vs. 465 ms).
- Furthermore, the C/C++ build from OmniModel.cpp supports a 50/50 CPU/GPU memory split, providing a deployment option for edge devices with limited GPU VRAM, which PyTorch lacks.

**Ablation Analysis (Table 4):**
An ablation study on OpenVLA-OFT at approximately 2.6 bpw quantified the contribution of each ActQuant component:
- A basic RTN (round-to-nearest) block quantization yielded only 4.5% success.
- Introducing a magnitude-weighted scale optimization recovered substantial performance, increasing success to 57.7%.
- Replacing magnitude weighting with the Action-Only Fisher signal further boosted success to 88.4%, demonstrating the importance of action-aware sensitivities.
- Incorporating the LM-head pathway to form the full Action-Mixed Fisher incrementally improved performance to 89.9%.
- Finally, adding the HSIC-based inter-tensor bit allocation, completing ActQuant, raised the success rate to 92.1%, which is only 4.8 percentage points below the FP16 reference.
This analysis indicates that the proposed components contribute independently and additively to the overall accuracy, with scale choice being particularly influential in the sub-4-bit regime.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ActQuant represents a methodological advance in the aggressive quantization of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, addressing critical barriers to their real-world deployment on edge robotic platforms. The work's significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Edge Deployment for VLA Models:** By achieving functional task success rates at sub-4-bit precision, ActQuant facilitates the deployment of complex VLA models directly on resource-constrained robotic hardware. This allows advanced robotic capabilities to move from cloud-dependent or high-end workstation environments to autonomous on-device operation.
- **Pioneering Sub-4-bit Accuracy in VLA:** ActQuant is presented as the only method capable of operating accurately below 3 bits-per-weight (bpw) for VLA models, and in some cases even at 2.0 bpw, a regime where previous methods exhibited severe performance collapse. This expands the practical range of aggressive quantization for robotic applications.
- **Significant Resource Reduction:** The framework demonstrates substantial memory compression (up to 5.3x for OpenVLA-OFT and 2.5x for $\pi_{0.5}$ on a UR3 arm) and inference speedups (up to 1.5x on a workstation and 1.3x on edge devices). These reductions directly translate to lower hardware requirements, reduced energy consumption, and faster decision-making cycles for robots.
- **Hardware-Friendly Design:** ActQuant's mixed-precision strategy, which allocates bit-widths per-tensor while maintaining uniformity within a tensor, is compatible with existing efficient low-bit integer kernels in tensor libraries like GGML. This ensures that algorithmic gains translate directly into practical system-level benefits without requiring custom hardware or complex software overhead.
- **Automated and Robust Deployment Pipeline (OmniModel.cpp):** The introduction of OmniModel.cpp streamlines the complex process of converting PyTorch VLA models to native C/C++ runtimes. This agentic pipeline reduces manual engineering effort, accelerates deployment cycles, and ensures numerical parity with the original full-precision models, thereby lowering the barrier for VLA model integration into diverse hardware ecosystems.
- **Increased Accessibility and Sustainability:** By making powerful VLA models more compact and efficient, ActQuant contributes to:
 - **Lowering Research and Development Costs:** Reducing the need for high-end GPUs makes robotics research and development more accessible to a broader community.
 - **Broader Application Scope:** Enables the use of VLA models in new applications such as assistive robotics, household tasks, and industrial automation where cost and energy efficiency are paramount.
 - **Environmental Benefits:** Lower energy consumption during inference for quantized models contributes to more sustainable artificial intelligence practices.
- **Enhanced Privacy:** On-device deployment inherently mitigates privacy concerns associated with streaming raw sensor data to remote servers for processing.

**Potential Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
- ActQuant currently focuses on weight-only quantization; extending it to include activation quantization could yield further compression, which is left for future work.
- The work has not characterized performance degradation when the calibration data distribution significantly differs from the deployment distribution, which is a practical consideration for real-world robotic tasks.
- OmniModel.cpp's operator coverage is dependent on underlying GGML kernels; expanding its procedural-skill library to support a wider range of novel architectural primitives is an ongoing effort.

**Broader Impacts (as identified by the authors):**
The positive impact includes making advanced robotics more accessible, reducing energy consumption, and enhancing privacy. However, the authors also acknowledge potential negative impacts. Cheaper on-device VLA inference could lead to deployment in settings with weaker operator oversight. Quantized policies might also exhibit subtly different failure modes compared to their full-precision counterparts, particularly under distribution shift, which could lead to physical harm in closed-loop robotic control. The authors address these concerns by emphasizing transparent reporting of performance metrics (including real-robot success rates) and stressing the continued necessity of standard robotic safety measures such as supervised operation, geofencing, and emergency-stop hardware, regardless of the quantization method employed.
