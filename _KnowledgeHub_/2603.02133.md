# SimRecon: SimReady Compositional Scene Reconstruction from Real Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> SimRecon reconstructs simulation-ready, object-centric 3D scenes from real videos via a Perception-Generation-Simulation pipeline: 2DGS reconstruction plus semantic segmentation, then per-object 3D generation conditioned on Active Viewpoint Optimization views, then Scene Graph Synthesizer-guided hierarchical physics assembly. On ScanNet it reaches 4.34 Chamfer Distance, 62.65 F-Score, 24.43 PSNR in 21 minutes, beating compositional baselines while yielding physically stable scenes.

## Problem

- Holistic neural reconstruction returns monolithic scenes without object boundaries or per-object geometry, so results cannot support physical interaction, semantic reasoning, or simulation.
- Existing compositional methods pick object views heuristically, so occluded or cluttered objects yield incomplete, deformed geometry from single-object generative completion models.
- Prior outputs are visual only: in-situ or post-hoc collision-resolution placement leaves floating and interpenetrating objects, and tightly coupled pipelines resist swapping components.

## Method

- **SimRecon** ==object-centric representation== splits each primitive into intrinsic attributes (6-DoF pose, ==PBR mesh==, mass, material) and relational attributes encoding supported-by and attached-to edges in a scene graph.
- **Active Viewpoint Optimization** casts view choice as information gain, maximizing a differentiable accumulated-opacity proxy with a depth regularizer, then expands to K views by multiplicative opacity decay.
- **Scene Graph Synthesizer** clusters objects by ==DBSCAN==, infers per-region relation triplets with a ==VLM==, merges subgraphs online with conflict adjudication, then assembles hierarchically via ==BFS== with gravity settling and fixed constraints.

## Results

- On ScanNet geometry, SimRecon reaches **4.34** Chamfer Distance, **62.65** F-Score, **87.37** Normal Consistency, versus InstaScene at **6.90** / **49.69** / **82.55** and DPRecon at **9.26** / **46.12** / **78.28**.
- Rendering improves to **24.43** PSNR, **0.924** SSIM, **0.153** LPIPS, **73.56** MUSIQ, above InstaScene's **22.35** PSNR and **0.302** LPIPS, at **21** minutes average versus **29** minutes and **10h42m**.
- Ablations show visibility-only view objectives lose structural completeness, removing the depth term collapses viewpoints onto object surfaces, and single-shot global or merge-without-conflict-resolution graphs become incoherent.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treat view selection as an optimizable information-gain objective, not a heuristic: rendering-derived differentiable proxies like accumulated opacity give generative models far better conditioning.
> - Physical plausibility comes from constructive assembly that mirrors real building order, not from correcting collisions after placement; support hierarchies should drive placement sequence.
> - Decomposing a pipeline into perception, generation, and simulation with explicit bridging modules keeps each stage swappable, so downstream quality tracks improvements in off-the-shelf components.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D scene reconstruction from multi-view images has seen advancements, particularly with neural representations enabling progress in 3D geometry reconstruction and novel view rendering. However, many of these approaches represent scenes holistically, treating them as monolithic entities without explicit object boundaries or individual object geometries. Such holistic representations are limited in their applicability for tasks requiring physical interaction, semantic reasoning, or simulation, as they do not provide the discrete, manipulable components needed for these applications.

Concurrently, efforts in creating 3D indoor simulators for embodied AI tasks have typically relied on several methods: manual asset placement, specialized hardware capture with extensive annotation, or procedural generation using rule-based or learned layout models. While these methods have contributed to embodied AI research, they often involve significant manual effort, result in simplified or artificial layouts that diverge from real-world complexity, or depend on well-reconstructed scan data with further manual engagement.

A more recent direction in 3D reconstruction is compositional 3D reconstruction, which aims to create object-centric representations directly from multi-view images. This approach is more inherently suited for simulation and interaction. Existing compositional methods, however, face several limitations. They frequently rely on heuristic view selection strategies for single-object generation, which can result in incomplete or implausible geometries, especially for occluded or complex objects. Furthermore, their outputs are often primarily visual representations, lacking the physical properties and coherent assembly required for a "simulation-ready" scene. This leads to a "real-to-sim" gap characterized by physical implausibility. Additionally, some approaches employ specialized methods for semantic reconstruction and object generation that are tightly coupled to their specific pipelines, limiting their extensibility and ability to integrate advances from other research areas.

SimRecon addresses these limitations by proposing a comprehensive "Perception-Generation-Simulation" pipeline. It aims to bridge the fidelity gap between raw video input and simulation-ready, compositional 3D scenes. The work differentiates itself by focusing on the transition points between these stages, specifically tackling the visual fidelity of generated assets and the physical plausibility of scene assembly, which are critical for complex real-world scenarios. By developing bridging modules, SimRecon seeks to create a robust and extensible framework that can leverage existing and future advancements in its constituent stages.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named SimRecon, for reconstructing simulation-ready, compositional 3D scenes from real-world videos. This involves transforming cluttered video input into physically assembled scenes composed of individual, manipulable 3D assets.

The motivation for this work stems from the limitations of current 3D scene reconstruction and simulation creation methods. While holistic 3D reconstruction can achieve high visual fidelity, it provides non-structural representations unsuitable for interaction or semantic reasoning. Conversely, existing 3D simulator creation methods often require extensive manual input, specialized hardware, or result in artificial scene layouts. Compositional 3D reconstruction offers a more suitable paradigm for simulation and interaction, but current approaches exhibit specific weaknesses that SimRecon aims to overcome.

The paper identifies two key challenges that lead to a "real-to-sim" gap when naively combining perception, generation, and simulation stages:
1. **Visual Infidelity of Generated Assets:** Conventional methods for generating individual 3D objects from multi-view inputs often rely on heuristic view selection. In complex, cluttered scenes, these heuristics can yield occluded, incomplete, or uninformative input views, leading to deformed or visually unfaithful generated geometries and appearances for individual objects. The motivation is to acquire optimal generation conditions that provide complete and high-quality observations of each object.
2. **Physical Implausibility of the Final Scene:** After individual assets are generated, their assembly into a coherent scene within a physical simulator is challenging. Simple in-situ placement based on initial observations or post-processing collision resolution methods (e.g., corrective placement) frequently result in physically unstable configurations, such as floating objects or object penetrations. The motivation is to ensure physically plausible and stable scene construction that mirrors real-world building principles.

To address these challenges, SimRecon focuses on designing two bridging modules:
- **Active Viewpoint Optimization (AVO):** This module aims to bridge the "Perception to Generation" gap by intelligently searching for optimal viewpoints in 3D space. The objective is to acquire maximally informative projected images as conditions for single-object 3D completion models, thereby ensuring visually faithful individual assets.
- **Scene Graph Synthesizer (SGS):** This module aims to bridge the "Generation to Simulation" gap by inferring physical interdependencies between objects. It guides the construction of the scene from scratch within 3D simulators, following a native, constructive principle based on supportive and attached relationships, to ensure the physical plausibility and stability of the assembled scene.

Ultimately, the motivation is to create a fully automated, extensible pipeline capable of generating diverse and realistic simulation environments directly from raw video input, thereby unlocking new possibilities for embodied AI, robotics, and other applications requiring functional 3D content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SimRecon proposes a "Perception-Generation-Simulation" pipeline with an object-centric scene representation. The framework proceeds through initial semantic reconstruction from video input, followed by single-object generation, and culminating in the assembly of these assets within a physical simulator.

#### 3.1. Object-Centric Scene Representation

Instead of holistic scene representations, SimRecon defines a scene as a structured set of discrete, high-level object primitives (o_i). Each object primitive is characterized by two categories of attributes:
- **Intrinsic Attributes (A_int,i):** These define the object in isolation. They include spatial attributes (scale, rotation, translation forming a 6-DoF pose), appearance attributes (complete geometric mesh with PBR textures), and physical attributes (semantic label, material, center of mass, mass).
- **Relational Attributes (A_rel):** These define the object's context within the scene by encoding supportive, spatial, and functional relationships with other objects. These interactions are organized into a Scene Graph G, where edges represent relationships (e.g., "supported by," "attached to").

Attributes are progressively populated: semantic reconstruction provides initial spatial attributes and labels, 3D asset generation completes geometry and appearance and infers physical properties, and the scene graph guides physical assembly. The pipeline utilizes 2DGS for 3D reconstruction, SceneSplat for semantic segmentation, Rodin for single-object generation, and Blender/Issac Sim for final assembly. Qwen2.5-VL is used for attribute and scene graph inference.

#### 3.2. Active Viewpoint Optimization (AVO)

AVO is designed to address the visual infidelity of generated assets by providing optimal input conditions for 3D generative models.
- **Problem Formulation:** The generation quality of 3D models from image inputs is heavily dependent on the informativeness of those images, especially under occlusion. Heuristic view selection often fails to capture complete object information.
- **Information Theory Formulation:** AVO models the optimal view projection as an information-gaining task, aiming to optimize a viewpoint `v` that maximizes the information gain about the object's complete geometry `X_i` from an initial view `v_0`. This is expressed as `IG(v) = H(X|v_0) - H(X|v)`.
- **Differentiable Proxy:** Directly computing entropy is intractable. SimRecon proposes maximizing a differentiable proxy based on the accumulated opacity in 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering. The total information proxy `A(v)` is defined as the sum of accumulated opacities `α(p, v)` for all pixels `p` corresponding to the object from viewpoint `v`. Maximizing `A(v)` serves as a surrogate for maximizing `IG(v)`.
- **Single View Optimization with Constraints:** The objective is to find a single optimal viewpoint `v*` by minimizing `L_AVO(v) = L_IG(v) + L_depth(v)`. `L_IG(v)` is the negative of `A(v)`. `L_depth(v)` is a depth regularization term that encourages the rendered depth at object pixels to remain close to a target depth, which is proportional to the object's size. This prevents the viewpoint from collapsing too close to the object surface. Camera parameters are optimized by applying relative transformations to the differentiable Gaussian parameters.
- **Iterative Viewpoint Expansion:** To generate a set of `K` informative views, an iterative strategy is employed. At each iteration, `v*_k` is found by minimizing the single-view loss based on currently remaining potential information (effective opacities). After `v*_k` is selected, effective opacities are updated via multiplicative decay, reducing their values based on their rendered contribution from `v*_k`. This ensures subsequent iterations focus on less observed regions. The process continues until `K` views are generated or a coverage threshold is met. Rendered appearance from these views, with occlusions inpainted, serves as input to the generative model.

#### 3.3. Scene Graph Synthesizer (SGS)

SGS addresses the physical implausibility of scene assembly by creating a scene graph that guides constructive placement.
- **Problem Formulation:** Direct placement or post-hoc collision resolution often results in physically unstable or implausible scene configurations. Understanding physical interdependencies is crucial for robust assembly.
- **Region-based Scene Graph Inference:**
 - The set of object instances is first partitioned into spatial regions using DBSCAN clustering of object centroids.
 - For each region, an optimal observation viewpoint is obtained using an adapted AVO objective, maximizing information gain across objects within that region.
 - A projection image from this viewpoint, annotated with instance IDs, is fed to a Vision-Language Model (VLM) (Qwen2.5-VL) with a structured prompt to infer "(Child ID, Relation, Parent ID)" triplets, specifically focusing on "supported by" and "attached to" relationships. Floor and wall entities are treated as foundational base nodes. This results in local subgraphs `G_k` per region.
- **Online Scene Graph Merging:**
 - A global scene graph `G` is progressively synthesized by iteratively merging the local subgraphs `G_k`. `G` is initialized with base nodes (Floor, Wall).
 - A Breadth-First Search (BFS) is performed from base nodes in each `G_k` to process edges.
 - When a new edge `e_new = (o_i, r_new, o_j)` from `G_k` is considered:
 - If `o_i` or `o_j` is new to `G`, the node(s) and edge are added.
 - If both `o_i` and `o_j` exist in `G`, a conflict check is performed. A conflict arises if no path exists between them, or an existing path is inconsistent with `r_new` (e.g., incorrect hierarchy).
 - Upon conflict detection, an "adjudication viewpoint" is re-optimized for the relevant path nodes, relations are re-inferred via VLM, and the updated relations are merged into `G`, replacing conflicting ones.
 - If a consistent path exists, `e_new` is considered redundant and discarded.
 - This iterative process yields a globally consistent scene graph `G`.
- **Hierarchical Physical Assembly:** The synthesized scene graph `G` guides the construction in the physical simulator.
 - Base nodes (Floor, Wall) are placed and designated as passive rigid bodies.
 - A BFS traversal of `G` is performed, starting from base nodes.
 - For each edge `e_ij = (o_i, r, o_j)`, where `o_i` is an already placed parent and `o_j` is the child:
 - If `r` is "supported by": `o_j` is placed at its initial position but slightly above `o_i`. `o_j` is temporarily set as an active rigid body, physics simulation is briefly activated to allow it to settle via gravity and collision onto `o_i`, then `o_j` is typically converted back to a passive rigid body for stability.
 - If `r` is "attached to": a fixed constraint is applied between `o_i` and `o_j` to simulate anchoring.
 - This physics-based, hierarchical assembly ensures semantic coherence and physical stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evaluations of SimRecon on the ScanNet dataset, comparing it against both single-view and scene-level compositional 3D reconstruction baselines, as well as a 3D indoor simulator.

#### 4.1. Compositional 3D Reconstruction

- **Quantitative Performance (Table 1):** SimRecon demonstrated superior performance across multiple metrics for both geometric fidelity and novel-view rendering quality.
 - **Geometric Fidelity:** SimRecon achieved a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 4.34, an F-Score of 62.65, and a Normal Consistency (NC) of 87.37. These metrics were notably better than state-of-the-art compositional methods like InstaScene (CD 6.90, F-Score 49.69, NC 82.55) and DPRecon (CD 9.26, F-Score 46.12, NC 78.28), and significantly outperformed single-view methods like Gen3DSR and SceneGen.
 - **Rendering Quality:** SimRecon also showed improved rendering fidelity, with higher PSNR (24.43), SSIM (0.924), MUSIQ (73.56), and lower LPIPS (0.153) compared to all baselines. For instance, InstaScene achieved PSNR 22.35 and LPIPS 0.302.
 - **Inference Time:** SimRecon completed processing in 21 minutes on average, which was competitive with InstaScene (29 minutes) and considerably faster than DPRecon (10 hours 42 minutes). Single-view methods were faster but yielded substantially lower quality.
- **Qualitative Performance (Figure 3):** Visualizations of reconstructed scenes indicated that SimRecon generated object geometries and appearances that were more faithful to the ground truth compared to other methods, which often suffered from deformed artifacts or incomplete structures, particularly in complex scenes.

#### 4.2. Projected Images Comparison (Figure 4)

A qualitative comparison of different viewpoint sampling strategies highlighted the effectiveness of Active Viewpoint Optimization (AVO):
- Heuristic methods, such as selecting the input view with maximum 2D object visibility or sampling canonical views, often produced projected images that were occluded or failed to capture the complete 3D structure of the target object. This led to malformed geometry and appearance in the subsequent 3D generation step.
- In contrast, AVO actively optimized for an ideal viewpoint in 3D space, successfully capturing the full structure and appearance of the target object. This approach proved robust and adaptive to objects of varying scales, providing more informative conditions for 3D generative models.

#### 4.3. Physical Construction Comparison (Figure 5 and 6)

- **Simulated Scene Plausibility:** When comparing physical scene construction in a 3D simulator (Blender) against MetaScenes, SimRecon demonstrated superior physical plausibility. MetaScenes, which relies on a retrieval-based strategy and post-hoc Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) search for collision resolution, often resulted in scenes with floating objects or penetrations, indicating a lack of accurate contact relationships.
- **Scene Graph Guidance:** SimRecon's Scene Graph Synthesizer (SGS) guided a hierarchical, physics-informed assembly process. This constructive approach ensured both semantic coherence and physical stability from the outset, resulting in physically plausible final configurations without typical issues like floating or interpenetrating objects.
- **SGS Visualization (Figure 6):** The progressive nature of the Scene Graph Synthesizer was illustrated, showing how optimal region images led to the inference of local graphs, which were then progressively merged into a coherent global graph through a conflict resolution mechanism.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Figure 7 and 8)

Ablation studies validated the contributions of AVO and SGS:
- **Active Viewpoint Optimization (AVO) Ablation (Figure 7):**
 - An optimization objective based solely on maximizing 2D object visibility was insufficient; it provided broad coverage but lacked depth and structural completeness, leading to less refined generated objects.
 - Omitting the `L_depth` constraint in AVO caused the optimized viewpoint to collapse impractically close to the object surface, leading to distorted perspectives. The full AVO with `L_depth` yielded balanced and informative views.
- **Scene Graph Synthesizer (SGS) Ablation (Figure 8):**
 - A single inference on a global projected image for scene graph synthesis failed to capture all objects and their relations accurately due to inherent complexity and occlusion.
 - A naive merging process that lacked conflict resolution resulted in an incoherent and "messy" graph with inconsistent relationships, unsuitable for guiding physical construction. The full SGS, with its progressive merging and conflict resolution mechanism, produced a globally consistent and physically meaningful scene graph.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SimRecon presents a methodological advancement in compositional 3D scene reconstruction by providing a robust framework to generate simulation-ready scenes from real-world video inputs. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Bridging the "Real-to-Sim" Gap:** The most direct impact of SimRecon is its capability to bridge the discrepancy between real-world visual data and the functional requirements of simulation environments. By focusing on both visual fidelity of individual assets and physical plausibility of scene assembly, the framework moves beyond purely visual reconstruction to create truly interactive and physically coherent digital twins of real environments.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI and Robotics:** The ability to automatically generate diverse, physically plausible, and object-centric simulation environments from arbitrary videos can significantly benefit embodied AI research. This includes training and evaluating agents for tasks such as robot navigation, manipulation, and embodied reasoning in environments that closely mimic real-world complexity and interaction dynamics, reducing the reliance on costly manual scene creation or less realistic procedural generation.
- **Enhanced 3D Content Creation:** For applications like virtual reality, augmented reality, game development, and digital twin initiatives, SimRecon offers a pipeline for automated 3D asset and scene generation. This can streamline content creation workflows, allowing developers to quickly populate virtual worlds with objects derived from real-world observations, potentially reducing development time and costs.
- **Modular and Extensible Framework:** The design of SimRecon, particularly its "Perception-Generation-Simulation" pipeline and the introduction of bridging modules, promotes modularity. This extensibility allows for the integration of future advancements in each stage (e.g., more sophisticated semantic segmentation models, higher-fidelity 3D generative models, or advanced Vision-Language Models) without requiring a complete overhaul of the entire system.
- **Robustness to Complex Real-World Scenarios:** The Active Viewpoint Optimization module addresses a core challenge in complex scenes with occlusions, ensuring that generated assets are complete and faithful. Similarly, the Scene Graph Synthesizer ensures that the assembly of these objects respects physical laws, even in cluttered environments, leading to more reliable simulation outcomes.

Overall, SimRecon contributes a comprehensive and robust approach to creating functional 3D environments from raw sensory data, opening avenues for more realistic simulations and intelligent agents capable of operating in complex, dynamic real-world settings.
