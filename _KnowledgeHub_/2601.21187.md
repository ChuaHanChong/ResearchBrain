# FRISM: Fine-Grained Reasoning Injection via Subspace-Level Model Merging for Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FRISM introduces a fine-grained, subspace-level model merging framework to enhance Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with reasoning capabilities from Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). This approach achieves state-of-the-art reasoning performance, outperforming baselines by 2.0% to 2.4% on various benchmarks, while rigorously preserving the VLM's original visual perception with high parameter efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing model merging techniques for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) operate at a coarse, layer-wise granularity when integrating reasoning capabilities from Large Reasoning Models (LRMs).
- This coarse-grained approach often leads to a trade-off where enhancing reasoning capabilities degrades the VLM's original visual perception.
- Traditional methods like post-training are resource-intensive, requiring extensive labeled multimodal reasoning data and incurring high computational costs.

## Method

- FRISM decomposes LRM task vectors into orthogonal subspaces using ==Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)== for fine-grained control over reasoning injection.
- It employs ==lightweight, learnable adaptive merging coefficient modulators== for each subspace, dynamically adjusting their influence.
- A ==label-free self-distillation strategy== with dual-objective optimization balances visual preservation (via KL divergence) and reasoning maximization (by amplifying effective injected subspaces).

## Results

- FRISM achieves state-of-the-art reasoning performance, surpassing base VLMs by **2.0%** (7B scale) to **2.4%** (32B scale) on benchmarks like MathVista, MathVision, and MMMU.
- The method rigorously preserves or slightly improves visual perception on benchmarks such as TextVQA and POPE, avoiding the degradation seen in other merging techniques.
- FRISM demonstrates high efficiency, achieving comparable reasoning performance to resource-heavy post-training methods (e.g., DRIFT) with significantly fewer trainable parameters (e.g., **0.05M** vs. ~**7000M**) and shorter training times (e.g., **0.43 hours**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model capabilities, such as reasoning, are not uniformly distributed across layers but are encoded within distinct subspaces of the parameter space.
> - Fine-grained, subspace-level merging enables selective injection of reasoning components, effectively mitigating the trade-off between reasoning enhancement and visual perception preservation.
> - A label-free self-distillation framework can effectively tune reasoning injection by balancing consistency with the original VLM and maximizing the spectral magnitude of injected subspaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). VLMs, such as LLaVA, Qwen-VL, and InternVL, demonstrate capabilities across visual understanding, image recognition, and text generation by building upon Large Language Models (LLMs). Concurrently, LRMs, including Deepseek-R1 and OpenAI-o1, have pushed the boundaries of complex reasoning tasks like mathematical problem-solving and programming. A growing area of research focuses on integrating the strengths of LRMs into VLMs to enhance their reasoning abilities for multimodal tasks.

Existing approaches to improve VLM reasoning often involve expensive post-training or fine-tuning, which demands extensive labeled vision-language reasoning datasets and significant computational resources. Model merging has emerged as an efficient alternative, seeking to combine the weights of pre-trained VLMs and LRMs with minimal additional training or data annotation. Early efforts, such as Bring Reason to Vision (BR2V), demonstrated the feasibility of this concept. However, conventional model merging techniques, often operating at a coarse-grained layer level, frequently encounter a fundamental trade-off: enhancing reasoning capabilities tends to compromise the VLM's original visual perception abilities, leading to a performance degradation in either domain. Methods like FRANK and IP-Merging have attempted to mitigate this by employing layer-wise merging or selective merging based on parameter similarity, but these still exhibit limitations in distinguishing parameters beneficial for reasoning from those causing visual interference, resulting in a performance gap compared to resource-intensive post-training methods.

FRISM addresses this limitation by proposing a fine-grained approach to model merging. It shifts the merging granularity from the layer level to the subspace level, recognizing that different capabilities (reasoning vs. vision) may reside in distinct parameter subspaces within a single layer. This fine-grained control aims to overcome the inherent trade-off observed in previous merging techniques, allowing for more precise injection of reasoning capabilities while preserving visual integrity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to efficiently enhance the reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by merging them with Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), while simultaneously preserving the VLMs' visual perception abilities.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and identified limitations in the current research landscape:
1. **Trade-off in Existing Merging Methods:** Current model merging techniques, which typically operate at a coarse-grained layer level, often force a compromise. When an LRM's reasoning capabilities are injected into a VLM, there is an unavoidable trade-off between improving reasoning performance and maintaining the VLM's pre-existing visual perception. This entanglement of capabilities at the layer level prevents optimal performance in both domains. As illustrated by empirical results on vision-language reasoning and perception datasets, existing methods often show a significant drop in one capability when the other is enhanced.
2. **Insufficient Granularity of Layer-Level Merging:** The authors hypothesize that a model layer is not the atomic unit of capability. Instead, reasoning capabilities and visual representations might reside in distinct *subspaces* within a single layer's parameter space. Layer-wise merging, which applies a uniform scaling coefficient across an entire layer, is therefore considered too coarse to disentangle these capabilities effectively. This leads to suboptimal performance, as beneficial reasoning components requiring high injection magnitude may be coupled with visual noise, limiting overall improvement.
3. **Low-Rank Nature of Parameter Updates:** Research indicates that parameter updates in Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly those related to high-level or reasoning functions, often exhibit a low-rank nature and are compactly encoded within specific parameter subspaces. This observation suggests that Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) could be a suitable tool to decompose LRM task vectors into these finer-grained, capability-specific subspaces.
4. **Scarcity of Labeled Multimodal Reasoning Data:** Developing powerful vision-language reasoning models typically requires extensive labeled multimodal reasoning datasets for post-training. Such datasets are expensive and time-consuming to curate. An efficient merging strategy should ideally minimize or eliminate the reliance on such scarce resources.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of FRISM are:
- To propose a **fine-grained merging paradigm** that operates at the subspace level rather than the layer level, allowing for more precise control over reasoning injection.
- To leverage **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)** to decompose LRM task vectors into orthogonal subspaces, treating these subspaces as reasoning priors.
- To introduce a mechanism for **adaptively tuning the scaling coefficients** of each individual subspace, enabling selective and heterogeneous injection of reasoning components.
- To develop a **label-free self-distillation learning strategy** with dual-objective optimization to guide the adaptive merging process. This strategy should balance visual preservation (minimizing KL divergence from the original VLM) and reasoning maximization (maximizing the spectral magnitude of injected subspaces) without requiring multimodal reasoning annotations.
- To demonstrate that this subspace-level modulation can **maximize reasoning capabilities with minimal vision loss**, effectively filtering beneficial reasoning components while suppressing those that cause visual degradation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FRISM (Fine-grained Reasoning Injection via Subspace-level Model Merging) proposes a two-stage framework to address the limitations of coarse-grained model merging.

**Preliminaries:**
The method begins by defining task vectors. For a VLM (θ_vlm) and an LRM (θ_lrm) fine-tuned from a pre-trained base model (θ_base), their respective task vectors are calculated as the difference between the post-trained and base model weights: τ_vlm = θ_vlm - θ_base and τ_lrm = θ_lrm - θ_base. Existing model-level merging methods combine these as θ_vlrm = θ_base + λ_vlm * τ_vlm + λ_lrm * τ_lrm. Layer-level merging applies similar coefficients per layer. Crucially, FRISM only applies merging to the Large Language Model (LLM) part of the VLM, keeping vision-related layers (e.g., visual tower, projection layers) fixed to preserve visual integrity.

**Motivation for Subspace-Level Merging:**
The core idea is that reasoning and visual capabilities are distributed within distinct subspaces of a layer's parameter space. Empirical analysis reveals that different SVD subspaces of an LRM task vector achieve peak performance at distinctly different scaling coefficients when injected into a VLM. This heterogeneity suggests that uniform layer-wise scaling is suboptimal and that fine-grained, subspace-level modulation is necessary for optimal reasoning injection with minimal visual loss.

**Stage 1: Decomposition & Initialization**
1. **SVD Decomposition:** For each linear layer `l` (with dimension d_out × d_in) in the LRM's LLM component, Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is applied to its corresponding reasoning task vector τ_lrm^(l):
 `U^(l), S^(l), V^(l)⊤ = SVD(τ_lrm^(l))`
 Here, U ∈ R^(d_out × r) and V ∈ R^(r × d_in) are orthogonal matrices of singular vectors, and S = diag(σ_1..., σ_r) ∈ R^(r × r) is a diagonal matrix of singular values. These singular values represent the base intensity of each subspace.
2. **Initialization of Merged Layer:** The orthogonal bases (U, V) and the base singular values (S) derived from the LRM task vector are *frozen* throughout the training phase, preserving the semantic direction of the reasoning task.
3. **Adaptive Merging Coefficient Modulator:** A lightweight, learnable parameter `g_l` ∈ R^r (a vector of rank `r`) is introduced for each layer `l`. This vector is initialized to zero and acts as an adaptive modulator for the singular values. The merging coefficient for the VLM is fixed at 1.0 (following IP-Merging) to stabilize visual performance.
4. **Merged Layer Formulation:** The merged layer weights (θ_merged^(l)) are formulated as:
 `θ_merged^(l) = θ_vlm^(l) + Δθ_eff^(l) = θ_vlm^(l) + λ_lrm * U^(l) * Sigmoid(g_l) ⊙ S^(l) * V^(l)⊤`
 Where `Δθ_eff^(l)` is the modulated subspace of the reasoning task vector, `λ_lrm` is a global merging coefficient for reasoning, and `⊙` denotes element-wise multiplication. The Sigmoid activation function ensures that the scaling factor for each subspace lies within (0, 1), making `S_eff = Sigmoid(g_l) ⊙ S^(l)` the gated effective singular values. This design ensures parameter efficiency, as only the `g_l` parameters are trainable.

**Stage 2: Injection & Training**
To optimize the trainable parameters `g_l`, a label-free self-distillation framework with dual-objective optimization is employed:
1. **Visual Preservation (L_distill):** The original base VLM (θ_vlm) is treated as a frozen teacher model, and the merged model (θ_vlrm, parameterized by the learnable `g` gates) is the student. To prevent the injected reasoning subspaces from disrupting the VLM's visual alignment, the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the output probability distributions of the teacher and student models is minimized on a common visual-language perception calibration dataset (e.g., VizWiz).
 `L_distill = E[KLD(P(·|x; θ_vlm) || P(·|x; θ_vlrm))]`
 This objective enforces consistency in visual predictive behavior.
2. **Reasoning Maximization (L_inject):** To encourage the effective absorption of reasoning capabilities, and in the absence of labeled VL reasoning datasets, a maximization term for the spectral magnitude of the injected subspaces is introduced:
 `L_inject = - Σ ||Sigmoid(g^(l)) ⊙ S^(l)||^2`
 This term encourages the `g` parameters to increase, thereby enlarging the merging coefficients of reasoning subspaces defined by the LRM. This term is normalized in practice to manage scale variations.
3. **Total Objective:** The final loss function `L` balances these two objectives using a hyperparameter `α`:
 `L = L_distill + α * L_inject`
 During training, only the `g` parameters are updated, while VLM weights and decomposed singular vectors remain frozen. This optimization process aims to identify subspaces that offer an optimal trade-off, selectively integrating reasoning components that are orthogonal to visual perception capabilities while suppressing those that might cause visual degradation.

**Theoretical Analysis (Summary):**
A theoretical analysis (provided in the appendix) frames FRISM as a constrained optimization problem. By approximating visual degradation using a Taylor expansion involving the Hessian matrix of the visual loss, it shows that FRISM acts as an adaptive spectral filter. This filter selectively amplifies reasoning subspaces (by increasing `λ_i`) that minimally degrade visual performance (i.e., align with flat directions of the visual Hessian, where `h_i` is small) while suppressing subspaces that cause significant visual degradation (`h_i` is large). The `L_inject` term serves to minimize the parameter distance to the LRM, acting as a proxy for reasoning injection in a label-free setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experiments across various Vision-Language Model (VLM) architectures and scales, demonstrating the effectiveness and robustness of FRISM.

#### 4.1. Experiments on Qwen2.5-VL Series Models

Experiments were conducted using Qwen2.5-VL models (3B, 7B, and 32B scales) merged with corresponding Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) like SmallThinker-3B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, and QwQ-32B.
- **Consistent Reasoning Improvement:** FRISM achieved consistent and significant improvements in reasoning capabilities across all model scales compared to baseline merging methods (Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, DARE, IP-Merging) and the base VLM. For the 3B scale, FRISM improved the average reasoning score by 1.8 points; for 7B scale, by 2.0 points (reaching 49.4 average accuracy); and for 32B scale, by 2.4 points. Notably, FRISM's performance on the 7B scale was close to that of ThinkLite-VL-7B, a model that underwent high-cost post-training.
- **Visual Capability Preservation:** Unlike existing methods that often show significant degradation in visual perception tasks (TextVQA, POPE, SeedBench), FRISM largely preserved or even slightly improved the model's visual capabilities. This indicates its ability to inject reasoning without compromising visual understanding.

#### 4.2. Keyword Analysis

An analysis of generated tokens on the MathVision dataset for Qwen2.5-VL-32B merged with QwQ-32B showed that FRISM generated the most reasoning-related self-reflection tokens (e.g., "Re-examine", "Correcting", "Alternative", "Testing", "Revisit") compared to the base model and other baselines. Simultaneously, FRISM also showed an improvement in vision-related tokens (e.g., "The image", "Visible", "Position"), further validating its balanced capability enhancement.

#### 4.3. Experiments on More Types of Models

FRISM's applicability was tested on diverse VLM-LRM pairs:
- ThinkLite-VL-7B merged with DeepSeek-R1-distilled-Qwen-7B: In this challenging scenario, where VLM and LRM parameter differences were significant, existing merging methods largely failed to yield gains or even degraded performance. FRISM, however, achieved performance gains on nearly all tasks (1.2 points average reasoning improvement) while retaining visual performance.
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct merged with OpenThinker3-7B: FRISM achieved the highest reasoning score and maintained perception performance, outperforming baselines where IP-Merging suffered from performance collapse.
- InternVL3-8B merged with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B: FRISM showed remarkable performance improvements (1.0 points average reasoning improvement) on both reasoning and perception tasks.
- Llama-based models (LLAVA-Next-8B merged with DeepThought-8B): FRISM again demonstrated significant improvements in VL reasoning tasks while preserving good VL perception, confirming its cross-architecture applicability.

#### 4.4. Efficiency Analysis

A comparison with efficient post-training methods like DRIFT (Huang et al., 2025) highlighted FRISM's computational efficiency:
- FRISM (full-rank) required only 0.53M trainable parameters and 0.49 hours of training, achieving comparable or superior reasoning performance (44.0 average on MathVista, MVerse, MVision, R1-OV) to DRIFT, which required ~7000M parameters and ~2 hours of training.
- A low-rank variant of FRISM (retaining top-256 singular values) further reduced trainable parameters to 0.05M and training time to 0.43 hours, while still achieving competitive reasoning performance (43.5 average).
This demonstrates that FRISM effectively bridges the performance gap with resource-heavy post-training approaches while being significantly more computationally efficient and not requiring labeled reasoning data.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Ablation on Decomposition Process (w/o SVD):** A variant without SVD (adaptive layer-wise merging) showed only marginal improvement over the base VLM. In contrast, FRISM (with SVD) significantly outperformed this variant, empirically confirming the advantages of subspace-level merging.
- **Ablation on Injection Loss (w/o L_inject):** Removing the `L_inject` term caused the model's performance to degrade back to that of the base VLM, demonstrating the critical role of this term in encouraging the absorption of reasoning capabilities.
- **Ablation on Gate Range:** Using a Sigmoid gate (bounded filter) for `g` yielded better performance than a Softplus gate, indicating the importance of controlled, conservative injection to prevent visual drift and destabilization.
- **Ablation on Decomposition Strategy:** SVD consistently outperformed random masks for decomposition, underscoring SVD's effectiveness in identifying meaningful low-rank subspaces.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Analysis

Visualizations of attention maps showed that while the Base VLM focused on specific visual details, baseline merging methods often exhibited a shift or dispersion in visual attention. FRISM, however, maintained visual attention regions similar to the base model while also focusing on critical reasoning keywords in the text (e.g., "reason", "step"), indicating effective reasoning injection without compromising visual robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FRISM framework introduces a novel approach to enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by addressing a fundamental limitation in existing model merging techniques: the trade-off between injecting reasoning and preserving visual perception.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Fine-Grained Reasoning Injection:** FRISM's primary contribution is shifting the granularity of model merging from a coarse layer level to a fine-grained subspace level. By leveraging Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to decompose Large Reasoning Model (LRM) task vectors, it enables the identification and selective modulation of specific parameter subspaces. This fine-grained control allows the model to absorb reasoning-dominant components more precisely, minimizing interference with visual representations.
- **Balanced Capability Enhancement:** The work demonstrates that FRISM can consistently improve VLM reasoning performance across diverse benchmarks, model architectures, and scales, while simultaneously preserving or even slightly enhancing the original visual perception capabilities. This effectively resolves the reasoning-vision trade-off observed in previous merging methods.
- **Parameter and Data Efficiency:** FRISM is a highly efficient solution. It requires a minimal number of trainable parameters (e.g., 0.53M for 7B models) and significantly less training time compared to conventional post-training or fine-tuning approaches. Furthermore, its label-free self-distillation strategy eliminates the dependency on scarce and costly labeled multimodal reasoning datasets, utilizing only common vision-language perception data for calibration. This makes advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible for deployment and research.
- **Versatility and Robustness:** The method functions as a plug-and-play solution compatible with various VLM architectures (Qwen2.5-VL, ThinkLite-VL, InternVL3, Llama-based) and LRM sources. Its consistent performance across diverse experimental settings, including challenging scenarios where other merging methods fail, highlights its robustness.
- **Theoretical Grounding:** The accompanying theoretical analysis provides insights into FRISM's mechanism as an adaptive spectral filter. It suggests that the optimization process selectively amplifies reasoning subspaces that do not significantly degrade visual performance while suppressing those that do, providing a principled basis for its effectiveness.

**Potential Impact:**
The development of FRISM has several potential impacts:
- **Advancement in Multimodal AI:** It provides an efficient pathway for integrating specialized capabilities from different foundation models. This could accelerate the development of more capable and robust multimodal AI systems, particularly for tasks requiring complex visual reasoning.
- **Reduced Resource Demands:** By offering a highly parameter- and data-efficient method, FRISM lowers the barriers to enhancing VLM reasoning, making it feasible for researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources or access to large labeled datasets.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The concept of subspace-level merging opens new avenues for exploring fine-grained parameter manipulation in large models. Future work could investigate similar techniques for injecting other specialized capabilities (e.g., factuality, creativity) or for more complex model fusion scenarios.
- **Practical Applications:** Improved VLMs with enhanced reasoning capabilities could benefit various applications, including advanced visual question answering, medical image analysis, scientific discovery, and autonomous systems, where robust interpretation and logical inference from visual and textual information are critical.

**Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges certain limitations, including the potential for increased output length and inference time in merged VLMs, a common issue in this field. Additionally, while effective, the reasoning objective currently relies on subspace magnitude as a proxy due to the absence of labeled data, rather than a direct supervision signal for reasoning transfer. Lastly, as a task vector-based merging method, FRISM may not generalize to models with drastically different sizes or architectural structures. These limitations suggest directions for future research.
