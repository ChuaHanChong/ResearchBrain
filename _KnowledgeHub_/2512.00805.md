# Thinking with Drafts: Speculative Temporal Reasoning for Efficient Long Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> SpecTemp introduces a dual-model framework for long video understanding, employing a lightweight draft MLLM for rapid frame selection and a powerful target MLLM for iterative reasoning. This approach enhances inference efficiency by 10-23% across various video lengths while achieving competitive or improved accuracy compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Long videos present significant computational challenges for multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) due to their extended temporal contexts and the context window limitations of current models.
- The 'thinking-with-frames' paradigm, while effective for complex spatio-temporal reasoning, creates an efficiency bottleneck by accumulating extensive multi-modal contexts with high visual redundancy.
- Analysis revealed that over 90% of visual tokens in existing MLLM contexts contribute minimally to reasoning, indicating substantial computational inefficiency.

## Method

- **SpecTemp** proposes a cooperative dual-model design consisting of a lightweight 3B ==Draft MLLM== for dense visual perception and a powerful 7B Target MLLM for high-level temporal reasoning and verification.
- It implements an iterative speculation-verification process where the Draft MLLM rapidly selects a compact set of salient frames from densely sampled regions, which the Target MLLM then verifies and integrates into its accumulated reasoning trace.
- The framework is optimized through a two-stage process: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the SpecTemp-80K dataset to build foundational capabilities, followed by reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) using specialized reward signals for both models.

## Results

- SpecTemp reduced inference latency by approximately 14-**19%** on short-form videos and 10-**23%** on long-form videos, compared to baseline MLLMs like Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** and VideoChat-R1.5, while maintaining or improving accuracy.
- The framework achieved substantial accuracy improvements on long-form benchmarks, including a +**12.0%** gain on Video-Holmes and +**8.0%** on MLVU over Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** (average 14.5 frames vs 16 frames).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the dual-model collaboration, specialized training strategies (SFT+RL), and custom reward components are essential for achieving optimal efficiency and performance, outperforming single-model or uniform sampling baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling temporal perception from high-level reasoning through a cooperative dual-model design can effectively mitigate the efficiency bottleneck in long video understanding without compromising accuracy.
> - Speculative decoding principles, traditionally used for LLM inference acceleration, can be adapted to the multi-modal domain to achieve reasoning-level acceleration in complex video tasks.
> - A custom-built, large-scale dataset (SpecTemp-80K) with synchronized coarse and fine-grained annotations is crucial for effectively training iterative, dual-level reasoning systems in video understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of long video understanding is a significant area of research aimed at enabling artificial intelligence systems to coherently perceive and reason over extended temporal contexts, akin to human intelligence. Recent advancements in multi-modal large language models (MLLMs), particularly those enhanced by reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), have improved reasoning capabilities in vision-language tasks.

A prominent paradigm, "thinking-with-frames," has emerged to address complex spatio-temporal understanding in videos. This approach involves an iterative process: MLLMs first extract temporal clues from long segments to identify salient regions, then examine densely sampled frames within those regions to refine local understanding. This allows for progressive focus on informative segments, facilitating temporally grounded reasoning.

Despite its effectiveness, the "thinking-with-frames" paradigm faces an efficiency bottleneck. Existing methods accumulate extensive multi-modal contexts, comprising high-level reasoning traces and numerous densely sampled visual tokens, which negatively impacts inference speed. Analysis reveals substantial visual redundancy, with language tokens attending to only a small subset of video tokens, and a long-tailed distribution of attention scores where a significant majority of visual tokens contribute minimally to reasoning.

Concurrently, speculative decoding has gained traction in accelerating large language model (LLM) inference. This technique employs a smaller, lightweight "draft" model to rapidly generate speculative tokens, which a stronger "target" model then verifies in parallel. This can lead to notable acceleration while maintaining or improving reasoning accuracy.

This research, SpecTemp, positions itself at the intersection of these two areas. It aims to overcome the efficiency limitations of the "thinking-with-frames" paradigm by integrating the principles of speculative decoding. By decoupling temporal perception from reasoning through a cooperative dual-model design, SpecTemp seeks to enhance the efficiency of long video understanding without compromising reasoning quality, effectively extending reasoning-level speculative acceleration to the complex domain of multimodal video data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient framework for long video understanding that mitigates the computational bottlenecks of existing "thinking-with-frames" MLLMs while preserving or enhancing reasoning accuracy.

The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing the Efficiency Bottleneck in Long Video Understanding:** Long videos pose inherent challenges for MLLMs due to their complex dynamics and the limited context windows of current models. Processing such extended temporal data results in high computational demands and slow inference times. The "thinking-with-frames" paradigm, while improving reasoning by focusing on relevant segments, exacerbates this issue by progressively expanding the multi-modal context with redundant visual tokens. The authors observed that over 90% of visual tokens in typical MLLM contexts receive negligible attention scores, indicating significant inefficiency. This motivated the development of a mechanism to reduce this redundancy.
- **Decoupling Temporal Perception from Reasoning:** The authors recognized that the tasks of identifying salient visual information (perception) and performing high-level logical reasoning can be separated. Current "thinking-with-frames" models often intertwine these processes, leading to the larger, more capable model performing computationally expensive frame exploration. The motivation was to offload the rapid, dense exploration of visual cues to a more lightweight component, freeing the powerful reasoning model to focus on its core strength.
- **Leveraging Speculative Decoding Principles:** Inspired by the success of speculative decoding in accelerating LLM inference, the authors sought to adapt this paradigm to the multi-modal domain. The core idea is to use a less resource-intensive "draft" model to propose relevant visual information (frames) and then allow a more robust "target" model to verify and integrate these proposals into its reasoning process. This approach was motivated by the potential for significant acceleration without a compromise in output quality, by selectively engaging the heavier model only for critical verification steps.
- **Developing a Cooperative Dual-Model Design:** The proposed solution involves a hierarchical architecture comprising a lightweight draft MLLM and a powerful target MLLM. This design is motivated by an analogy to human brain function, where a fast perceptual subsystem rapidly explores the environment, and a slower, more cognitive module validates and integrates insights. This cognitive inspiration underpins the choice of a dual-system to balance efficiency (via the draft model's rapid exploration) and accuracy (via the target model's careful verification).
- **Creating a Specialized Dataset for Dual-Model Training:** Existing video understanding datasets are not tailored for the iterative, dual-level reasoning process proposed by SpecTemp. To effectively train the cooperative framework, there was a motivation to construct a new dataset, SpecTemp-80K, featuring synchronized annotations at both coarse (evidence spans for the target model) and fine-grained (frame-level evidence for the draft model) levels. This dataset is crucial for supervising both models' specialized roles and for facilitating reinforcement learning.
- **Demonstrating Empirical Efficacy:** A key motivation was to empirically validate that the proposed SpecTemp framework could achieve competitive or superior accuracy across diverse video understanding benchmarks, ranging from short-form to long-form videos, while simultaneously delivering significant improvements in inference efficiency compared to existing "thinking-with-frames" methodologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SpecTemp framework introduces a dual-system cooperative approach for efficient long video understanding, grounded in speculative temporal reasoning and reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Overview of SpecTemp

SpecTemp operates with two distinct MLLMs:
- **Target MLLM ($\pi_{target}$):** A 7B-parameter model responsible for high-level temporal reasoning, verifying proposals from the draft model, and generating the final answer.
- **Draft MLLM ($\pi_{draft}$):** A lightweight 3B-parameter model dedicated to dense visual perception and selecting salient frames. The computational asymmetry, where $|\pi_{draft}| \ll |\pi_{target}|$, is central to its efficiency.

The system follows an iterative speculation-verification process (Algorithm 1, Figure 2) for up to $T_{max}$ rounds (set to 3 in experiments).

#### 3.2. Iterative Speculation-Verification Process

1. **Initialization:**
 - Given a long video $V$ and a textual query $W_q$, the target MLLM first receives a set of uniformly sampled frames, $V^0_s$.
 - The target MLLM performs initial temporal reasoning: $(T_0, V^0_d, W_a) = \pi_{target}(W_q, V^0_s)$.
 - $T_0$ is the initial reasoning trace. $V^0_d$ represents densely sampled frames from a temporal region predicted by the target MLLM as requiring further examination. $W_a$ is the answer if immediately determinable, leading to early termination.
 - If an answer is not determined, the target model predicts a temporal evidence region.

2. **Speculation (by Draft MLLM):**
 - In iteration $t \in [1, T_{max}]$, the draft MLLM receives $V^t_d$, which are densely sampled frames (e.g., 1 frame per second) from the temporal region predicted by the target MLLM in the previous step ($t-1$).
 - The draft MLLM also conditions on the reasoning trace $T_{t-1}$ from the *current iteration's* context.
 - It then selects a compact set of sparse, representative frames, $V^t_s = \pi_{draft}(V^t_d; T_{t-1})$, which are deemed most informative.

3. **Verification (by Target MLLM):**
 - The target MLLM receives the sparse frames $V^t_s$ proposed by the draft model.
 - It integrates these with the query $W_q$ and the *accumulated reasoning trace* from all previous rounds, $T_{<t} = \{T_0, \dots, T_{t-1}\}$: $(T_t, V^t_d, W_a) = \pi_{target}(V^t_s; W_q, T_{<t})$.
 - The target MLLM reasons and verifies the draft's proposal. If sufficient information is gathered, it generates $W_a$ (early termination). Otherwise, it predicts a new temporal region ($V^t_d$) for further dense sampling, initiating another round of speculation.

#### 3.3. Policy Optimization

SpecTemp undergoes a two-stage optimization process:
1. **Cold-start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** This stage builds foundational capabilities. The target MLLM is fine-tuned for high-level verification and segment prediction, while the draft MLLM is trained for fine-grained frame selection. This uses the SpecTemp-80K dataset.
2. **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT):** Following the GRPO algorithm, RFT models keyframe speculation and response generation as a unified policy learning process. The objective is to maximize the expected return by jointly optimizing the target and draft models' policies based on reward signals.

#### 3.4. Reward Design

Distinct reward formulations encourage specialized behaviors in each model:
- **Target Model Rewards ($R_{target}$):**
 - $R^{format}_{target}$: A binary reward for adhering to the prescribed output format (e.g., proper use of `<think>`, `<segment>`, `<answer>` tokens).
 - $R_{answer}$: Measures the correctness of the final answer against ground truth, typically using metrics like exact match or F1-score.
 - $R_{IoU}$: Assesses the quality of temporal localization by computing the Intersection-over-Union between the predicted evidence segments and ground-truth temporal regions.
 - Total reward: $R_{target} = R^{format}_{target} + R_{answer} + R_{IoU}$.
- **Draft Model Rewards ($R_{draft}$):**
 - $R^{format}_{draft}$: A binary reward for ensuring the correct output format for frame selection.
 - $R_{visual}$: Encourages the selection of informative and diverse frames. It consists of two CLIP-based similarity terms:
 - `Sim_CLIP(q, f_i)`: Rewards frames ($f_i$) that are highly relevant to the question ($q$).
 - `− max_{f_j \in F_{prev}} Sim_CLIP(f_i, f_j)`: Penalizes redundancy by discouraging the selection of frames similar to previously selected ones ($F_{prev}$).
 - Total reward: $R_{draft} = R^{format}_{draft} + R_{visual}$.

#### 3.5. SpecTemp-80K Dataset Construction

A large-scale dataset with synchronized dual-level annotations was constructed to facilitate training:
1. **Data Collection:** Videos were curated from 9 diverse public sources, categorized by duration: short-form (<1 min), medium-length (1-10 min), and long-form (>10 min). The dataset comprises 80,142 video-question-answer triplets.
2. **Annotation:** GPT-4o was employed to generate hierarchical annotations:
 - Scene-level captions describing overall narratives and key events, providing coarse temporal evidence spans for the target model.
 - Frame-level captions capturing fine-grained visual details, providing evidence for the draft model's selection.
3. **Trajectory Generation:** GPT-4o synthesized multi-round reasoning trajectories that simulate the iterative speculation-verification process, including structured `<think>`, `<segment>`, `<frame>`, and `<answer>` tokens.
4. **Validation:** A subset of the generated trajectories was manually verified to ensure annotation quality, reasoning consistency, and accuracy of temporal evidence/frame selections.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that SpecTemp maintains competitive accuracy while significantly accelerating inference across various video understanding benchmarks.

- **Performance on Short-form Videos:**
 - SpecTemp (average 13.7 frames) consistently outperformed Qwen2.5-VL-7B (16 frames) in accuracy across TempCompass, MVBench, MMVU, and VSI-Bench, with gains ranging from +2.1% to +4.6%. Concurrently, it reduced inference latency by approximately 14% (from 2.1s to 1.8s).
 - When configured to utilize a higher average of 47.6 frames, SpecTemp achieved comparable or superior accuracy to VideoChat-R1.5 (64 frames) while being 19% faster (4.7s vs 5.8s). These results indicate the effectiveness of adaptive frame selection in identifying salient temporal information, leading to both improved understanding and computational efficiency for shorter videos.

- **Performance on Long-form Videos:**
 - For long-form videos (Video-Holmes, LongVideoBench, MLVU, Video-MME), SpecTemp (average 14.5 frames) achieved substantial accuracy improvements over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (16 frames), particularly on Video-Holmes (+12.0%) and MLVU (+8.0%). This was accompanied by a latency reduction of approximately 10% (from 4.1s to 3.7s).
 - When scaled to an average of 58.1 frames, SpecTemp surpassed VideoChat-R1.5 (64 frames) in accuracy and was 23% faster (8.9s vs 11.5s). These findings support the framework's ability to effectively manage long-form video understanding by critically identifying relevant temporal segments for both accuracy and efficiency.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Frame Selection Strategies:** Comparisons among uniform sampling, target-model-guided uniform sampling, CLIP-based selection, and SpecTemp's Target + Draft strategy demonstrated that the trained dual-model approach achieved the best performance. The Target + Draft method, using an average of 14.1 frames, outperformed other strategies, confirming the benefit of a specialized draft model for adaptive frame selection.
 - **Efficiency Analysis:** A detailed latency breakdown revealed that LLM prefill is the dominant factor in inference time. SpecTemp achieved the lowest overall latency (2.3s) compared to Qwen2.5-VL-7B (2.5s) and VideoChat-R1.5 (2.8s). This reduction is attributed to offloading dense exploration to the smaller 3B draft model, thereby minimizing the computational overhead for the 7B target model.
 - **Model Collaboration Analysis:** Evaluation on LongVideoBench showed that using only the 7B model yielded high accuracy (54.1%) but higher latency (3.1s), while a 3B-only model was fast (1.7s) but had significantly lower accuracy (40.3%). The SpecTemp (L+S) approach, combining the 7B target and 3B draft, achieved the highest accuracy (57.5%) and the best efficiency score (25.0) with a balanced latency (2.3s). This validates that decoupling perception from reasoning improves both performance and efficiency.
 - **Training Strategy Analysis:** Ablations on LongVideoBench confirmed the importance of both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL). SFT established foundational capabilities and output structure, while RL, especially with IoU and visual information gain rewards, refined collaborative decision-making, leading to the highest accuracy (57.5%) and format accuracy (99.5%) with the full SFT+RL strategy for both models.
 - **Ablation on Iteration Numbers:** The optimal maximum iteration number was found to be $T_{max}=3$, balancing accuracy improvements (e.g., +2.8% on LongVideoBench compared to 1 iteration) and inference latency. Beyond three iterations, diminishing returns and occasional performance degradation were observed.
 - **Reward Components:** Both the IoU reward for the target model and the visual information gain reward for the draft model independently contributed to performance gains over the SFT baseline. Their combination yielded the best results, indicating complementary supervision for the dual-model system.
 - **Frame Allocation Strategies:** Within fixed frame budgets (16 and 64 frames), SpecTemp's default balanced allocation strategies (e.g., 10 initial + 2 per iteration for 3 rounds) consistently outperformed alternatives that either over-prioritized initial sampling or iterative refinement, demonstrating an optimal equilibrium between global understanding and local examination.
 - **Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH) Evaluation:** SpecTemp exhibited robust visual information retrieval from extremely long videos (up to 2000 frames), maintaining over 80% accuracy across various needle depths. The dual-model approach (Target 7B + Draft 3B) surpassed using the 7B model alone, underscoring its efficacy in precise long-range visual retrieval while improving efficiency.

- **Case Studies:** Qualitative examples demonstrated SpecTemp's iterative reasoning process, where the target MLLM refined temporal segments and the draft MLLM proposed key frames, validating the effective collaboration between the models for long video understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a critical challenge in multi-modal large language models: the efficiency bottleneck when processing long videos. By introducing SpecTemp, the authors offer a method that maintains competitive accuracy while substantially reducing inference latency, which has several significant implications.

The primary significance lies in its novel integration of speculative decoding principles into the "thinking-with-frames" paradigm for video understanding. This represents a shift from token-level acceleration to reasoning-level acceleration in a multi-modal context. The cooperative dual-model design, featuring a lightweight draft MLLM for rapid perception and a powerful target MLLM for deliberate reasoning and verification, provides a balanced approach to resource allocation and task decomposition. This design is not only computationally efficient but also draws an analogy to cognitive processes, suggesting a biologically inspired mechanism for intelligent processing.

The construction of the SpecTemp-80K dataset is a notable contribution. This large-scale dataset, with its synchronized dual-level annotations (coarse evidence spans and fine-grained frame-level evidence), is specifically tailored to train such iterative, multi-level reasoning systems. It provides a valuable resource for future research in adaptive video understanding.

The empirical results demonstrate that SpecTemp achieves significant improvements in inference efficiency (19-23% faster on long-form videos) without sacrificing, and often improving, reasoning accuracy compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. This efficiency gain is particularly impactful for long-form videos, where processing time is a major constraint. The ability to perform precise long-range visual information retrieval, as shown in the Needle-in-a-Haystack evaluation, further underscores its robustness.

The potential impact of SpecTemp is considerable across various applications requiring efficient and accurate long video comprehension. These include:
- **Real-time video analysis:** Enabling faster processing in applications like surveillance, content moderation, and live event monitoring.
- **Enhanced human-computer interaction:** Facilitating more responsive AI assistants that can understand complex, extended video narratives.
- **Educational and training content:** Improving the ability of AI to summarize, query, and extract information from long lectures or instructional videos.
- **Robotics and autonomous systems:** Providing more efficient visual perception for agents operating in dynamic, long-duration environments.

While the framework introduces computational overhead during training due to its dual-model and reinforcement learning components, the significant inference-time benefits suggest a favorable trade-off for deployment. Future work exploring more parameter-efficient training or hierarchical memory mechanisms for ultra-long videos (exceeding 2-3 hours) could further extend SpecTemp's applicability, positioning it as a foundational step towards more scalable and human-like long video understanding systems.
