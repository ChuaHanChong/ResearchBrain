# FRASA: An End-to-End Reinforcement Learning Agent for Fall Recovery and Stand Up of Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> FRASA, developed by the Rhoban Team, introduces an end-to-end Deep Reinforcement Learning agent for humanoid robots that unifies fall recovery and stand-up tasks. This method dramatically reduces training time to minutes and consistently outperforms a world-champion Key Frame Based method, recovering from various fall postures up to 50% faster.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots are highly unstable, prone to falls, and susceptible to damage, requiring robust recovery mechanisms.
- Existing fall prevention (e.g., MPC, DRL push recovery) methods do not address recovery *after* a fall, and traditional stand-up routines (KFB) lack versatility and adaptability.
- Current DRL approaches for stand-up often suffer from prohibitively long training times or reliance on expert-defined key frames.

## Method

- A DRL agent, FRASA, trained with the CrossQ algorithm and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), maps raw sensor inputs directly to motor commands for unified fall recovery and stand-up.
- The approach leverages the robot's bilateral symmetry by controlling only 5 symmetric degrees of freedom, significantly accelerating the learning process.
- Extensive domain randomization (e.g., friction, mass, actuator parameters) and modeling of real-robot sensor delays ensure robust sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- FRASA agents achieved full training in an average of 13 to 37 minutes, accumulating 235,000 to 575,000 timesteps, a significant speedup compared to existing DRL methods.
- In stand-up tasks, FRASA recovered from supine positions in 2.678 seconds (53% of KFB time) and prone positions in 2.135 seconds (68% of KFB time).
- For strong disturbances (7.3J), FRASA recovered frontwards falls in 2.44 seconds (42% of KFB time) and backwards falls in 2.26 seconds (51% of KFB time), consistently outperforming the KFB method of the RoboCup 2023 world champion Rhoban Team.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An end-to-end DRL framework can effectively unify fall recovery (creating new contact points during a fall) and stand-up, offering a more adaptive and comprehensive solution.
> - Strategic DRL algorithm choices (CrossQ+SAC) combined with exploiting robot symmetry enable complex humanoid behaviors to be learned in minutes, a substantial reduction from previous training times.
> - Thorough domain randomization, including real-world communication delays and mechanical variations, is critical for achieving successful and robust policy transfer from simulation to a physical robot.
