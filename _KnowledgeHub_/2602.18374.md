# Zero-shot Interactive Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Surrey and University of Sheffield developed the Zero-Shot Interactive Perception (ZS-IP) framework, enabling robots to extract hidden information and resolve semantic queries in cluttered environments through physical interaction. The system integrates Vision-Language Models with multi-strategy manipulation and memory-guided action planning, achieving high success rates in interactive perception tasks, often outperforming existing methods like MOKA.

## Problem

- Robots in cluttered or partially observable environments face challenges due to occluded objects, as traditional active perception cannot physically alter the scene to reveal information.
- Integrating high-level semantic reasoning from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with low-level physical interaction remains a bottleneck for generalizable robotic manipulation, especially for tasks requiring temporal context or involving unseen objects.
- Existing interactive perception methods struggle with contact-rich actions like pushing in cluttered scenes, often relying on limited 2D spatial annotations that do not effectively capture manipulation affordances.

## Method

- An iterative ==Zero-Shot Interactive Perception (ZS-IP) framework== that integrates a VLM (GPT-4o) with a robot manipulator, allowing it to dynamically interact with the environment to resolve semantic queries.
- Introduction of an ==Enhanced Observation (EO) module== which generates specialized visual augmentations like "pushlines" for robust pushing, grasping keypoints, and a virtual grid for precise robot movements.
- A ==Memory-Guided Action Module== that stores interaction history and scene context, enabling context-aware action selection, prevention of redundant actions, and support for multi-step manipulation plans.

## Results

- The ZS-IP framework achieved high success rates (**0.8-1.0** SR) in less complex interactive perception tasks, showcasing its robust reasoning and manipulation capabilities in unstructured scenes.
- In complex, multi-step scenarios, ZS-IP (especially "In-Context" configuration) demonstrated an SR of **0.7-0.8**, outperforming the MOKA baseline (SR **0.0-0.5**) by effectively leveraging memory and strategic action sequencing.
- The "pushlines" visual augmentation significantly improved performance in contact-rich pushing tasks (Task VIII SR **0.8**), and GPT-4o proved to be the most effective VLM compared to other models within the ZS-IP framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Specialized visual augmentations, such as "pushlines" derived from object geometry, are effective in capturing affordances for contact-rich actions, significantly improving pushing performance in cluttered environments.
> - Integrating a memory-guided action module with a VLM allows robots to retain temporal context from previous interactions, enabling more robust multi-step reasoning, dynamic action selection, and efficient task termination.
> - Zero-shot interactive perception can be achieved by tightly coupling high-level VLM reasoning with enhanced visual affordances and memory, allowing robots to resolve complex semantic queries in partially observable scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

**Challenges in Robotic Perception:**
Robots operating in dynamic, semi-structured environments, such as e-commerce warehouses or customer return processing centers, frequently encounter cluttered or partially observable scenes. A significant challenge in robotic perception is the presence of occluded or hidden objects, which limits the robot's ability to gather complete information. Traditional active perception methods address this by dynamically adjusting the robot's viewpoint to acquire additional visual data. However, these methods are often limited to static observations and do not allow the robot to physically alter the environment to resolve occlusions.

**Evolution of Active and Interactive Perception:**
Interactive perception (IP) extends active perception by enabling robots to physically interact with objects and modify the environment's state to uncover hidden information. This approach is crucial for navigating complex scenarios where viewpoint changes alone are insufficient. Prior research in IP, such as MOKA and PIVOT, has explored visual prompting and 2D spatial annotations to support manipulation tasks. However, these methods often rely on discrete annotations, which can struggle in cluttered environments, and may lack the semantic integration necessary for generalizable, goal-directed manipulation across varied object categories.

**Role of Vision-Language Models in Robotics:**
Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have significantly enhanced high-level reasoning capabilities in robotics. These models facilitate natural language interpretation and complex task planning. Despite these developments, VLMs often exhibit limitations in spatial reasoning due to insufficient training data concerning object relationships and interactions. While efforts like NEWTON, PROST, PIQA, SayCan, Code as Policies, and Tidybot integrate LLMs with robotic control, many of these approaches remain constrained by static scenes, predefined object categories, or lack the ability to adapt to dynamic or partially observable environments that necessitate physical interaction. Furthermore, some models aimed at enhancing spatial representations, like SpatialVLM, primarily rely on static annotations and do not fully leverage dynamic interaction.

**Gaps Addressed by This Work:**
The current research landscape highlights a critical bottleneck in integrating high-level semantic reasoning (from VLMs) with low-level physical interactions for generalizable robotic manipulation. Existing methods often fail to accommodate queries dependent on temporal context, such as tracking an object's location before and after manipulation, or to generalize to unseen objects effectively. This paper introduces Zero-Shot Interactive Perception (ZS-IP) to bridge these gaps. ZS-IP aims to enable robots to iteratively manipulate their environment, uncover occluded information, and resolve semantic queries by tightly integrating VLMs with enhanced visual affordances and memory-driven action planning, specifically addressing the limitations of prior interactive perception techniques in cluttered and dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that allows robots to extract hidden information and execute manipulation plans in complex, partially observable environments by physically interacting with objects and altering the state of the surroundings. This is driven by several key motivations:

- **Addressing Occlusion and Ambiguity:** A central motivation is to overcome the challenge of robotic perception in cluttered or partially observable environments where target objects may be occluded or entirely hidden. Traditional active perception, while improving viewpoints, does not allow for physical rearrangement of the workspace, which is often necessary to reveal hidden information. ZS-IP aims to enable robots to dynamically change the environment through interaction.

- **Integrating Semantic Reasoning with Physical Interaction:** Current robotic systems often struggle to combine advanced high-level reasoning from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with precise, low-level physical manipulation. Many existing VLM-integrated approaches are limited to simple pick-and-place tasks or static environments and cannot handle queries requiring complex, multi-step interactions or temporal context. This work seeks to create a cohesive framework where VLMs can interpret natural language queries, reason about object relationships, and directly guide diverse physical interactions.

- **Enhancing Robotic Manipulation Strategies:** The motivation extends to endowing robots with a broader range of manipulation capabilities beyond standard grasping or pick-and-place. Specifically, the authors aim to develop strategies for contact-rich actions like pushing and pulling, which are critical for clearing clutter or accessing obscured areas. This involves creating novel visual augmentations that better capture the affordances for these actions, moving beyond grid-based annotations optimized for simpler tasks.

- **Improving Generalization and Contextual Awareness:** Conventional robotic perception and manipulation methods often exhibit limitations in generalizing to unseen objects or adapting to dynamic, unstructured scenes. A core motivation for ZS-IP is to achieve zero-shot capabilities, allowing robots to interpret novel queries and interact with unfamiliar objects without extensive prior training. This is supported by integrating a memory-guided action module that allows the robot to accumulate and leverage temporal context from previous interactions, ensuring precise, context-aware action selection and task termination.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Zero-Shot Interactive Perception (ZS-IP) framework is designed to integrate multi-strategy manipulation with a memory-driven Vision Language Model (VLM) for resolving semantic queries in partially observable environments.

**Zero-Shot Interactive Perception (ZS-IP) Framework Overview:**
The system comprises a robotic manipulator with an in-hand camera, acting as an agent that interacts with its environment to uncover hidden objects and answer queries. The process is iterative, involving scene analysis, physical interaction, and data gathering until the VLM can provide a conclusive answer.

**Iterative Perception-Action Loop:**
The robot starts in a home configuration, capturing an initial observation `o_0`. Given a natural language query `q`, the Perception Analyser evaluates `o_0` to produce a response `z_0`. If the query is not resolved, the Enhanced Observation module generates annotated images, which are then passed to the Memory-Guided Action Module. This module generates an action `a_t` that modifies the workspace, leading to a new observation `o_{t+1}`. This updated observation is fed back into the Perception Analyser, continuing the loop until the query is resolved or a maximum iteration limit is reached.

**Perception Analyser (PA):**
The PA, implemented using the GPT-4o model, interprets visual data (`o_t`) and assesses its sufficiency to answer the query `q`, generating `z_t = F_VLM(o_t, q)`. `z_t` includes an evaluation of relevant objects, spatial relationships, context, and a binary decision on whether further interaction is required. The VLM temperature parameter is set to zero for consistency.

**Enhanced Observation (EO) Module:**
If the query is unresolved, the EO module (`o_t_tilde = EO(o_t)`) augments the visual perception. A segmentation mask, generated by Grounded SAM based on a text descriptor from the VLM, is used to segment the target object. This enhances the VLM's ability to convey predictive outputs for robot motion.
- **Pushlines (EO_P):** Two principal push lines are generated along the principal axes (using PCA) of the object's segmentation mask, passing through its center and oriented perpendicular to its shape. Four additional push lines are created near the edges of the mask. These lines are annotated with pre-contact (P) and post-contact (D) points (e.g., P1-P6, D1-D6), defining virtual push trajectories. This augmentation is designed to capture affordances for contact-rich actions.
- **Grasping Keypoints (EO_G):** A set of five key points is generated for grasping: four points marking the object boundary from the segmentation mask and the object's centroid. These are generated using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) and annotated as P1-P5.
- **Virtual Grid (EO_AP):** A 2D virtual grid is constructed in the robot's workspace and projected into the image frame, anchored by an ArUco marker. The transformation of grid vertices from the camera frame to the robot's base frame is continuously updated. This grid provides precise coordinates for robot movements, and depth information for objects is retrieved from the depth camera using the geometric center of the segmentation mask.

**Memory-Guided Action Module:**
This module ensures context-aware action selection and task termination.
- **State Representation:** The system records the robot's current configuration (`x_t`), visual observations (`o_t`, `o_t_tilde`), a summary of the scene (`s_t`) after each interaction, the VLM's chain of thought, visited states, robot trajectory, and task context.
- **Memory Block:** Information gathered from each movement is stored in `M_t = {S_0..., S_{t-1}}`, where `S_t = {s_t, o_t, o_t_tilde, x_{t-1}}`. This historical data is retrieved and provided as context to enable dynamic and intelligent interaction, preventing redundant actions and enhancing efficiency.
- **ZS-IP Policy (π):** The policy `a_t = π(M_t, x_t, z_t, o_t_tilde)` first determines the appropriate interaction type. It then utilizes the current observation and memory to select a precise motion trajectory. Actions are transformed from the camera frame to the robot's base frame. For reach-to-grasp/push movements, OMPL planner is used, while pushing movements employ the Pilz Industrial Motion Planner for continuous trajectories.

**Robot Interaction Types:**
The framework evaluates four categories of robot actions:
1. **Move Camera-in-Hand (`a_1`):** The robot adjusts its wrist-mounted camera's orientation to improve the view of a target object.
2. **Push (`a_2`):** The robot executes pushing actions, guided by the optimal push line selected from `EO_P`.
3. **Grasp (Pick-and-Place) (`a_3`):** Grasping actions are guided by `EO_G` for the grasp location, and `EO_AP` helps determine the placement location.
4. **Lift to Stationary Camera (`a_4`):** The robot grasps an object using `EO_G` and positions it in front of a fixed secondary camera (Cam2) for detailed inspection (e.g., reading text).

All generated key points, push lines, and the 2D grid are mapped into the robot's base frame. Zoomed-in images are utilized for precise selection.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments were conducted using a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda arm equipped with an Intel RealSense RGB-D camera (Cam1) on its end-effector and an additional RGB camera (Cam2) fixed on the table. An ArUco marker anchored a 2D virtual grid in the workspace, with objects sourced from the YCB object set. GPT-4o served as the Perception Analyser.

**Performance Metrics:**
The evaluation used metrics adapted from the R2R-VLN dataset: Success Rate (SR), Total Length (Position) (TL), Total Length (Position, Successful) (TLS), Position Error (PE), and Oracle Success Rate (OSR).

**Baseline Comparisons:**
ZS-IP's performance was compared against:
- **MOKA (Mark-Based Visual Prompting):** A state-of-the-art motion generation framework for open-world robotic manipulation, adapted with EO for a fair comparison.
- **PIVOT:** An ablation study was performed to compare ZS-IP against PIVOT on specific tasks.
- **Other VLMs:** Ablation studies also compared GPT-4o with other VLM models like Gemini 2.0 Flash, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and GPT-4 Turbo.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ZS-IP on a 7-DOF Franka Panda arm across eight interactive perception tasks with varying levels of occlusion and complexity yielded several findings.

**Overall Task Success Rates:**
ZS-IP exhibited high success rates in less complex tasks, demonstrating its reasoning, perception, and memory-guided manipulation capabilities.
- **Task I (push eraser to reveal paper clips):** Achieved an SR of 0.9.
- **Task II (move tin to reveal screw):** Achieved an SR of 0.9, successfully altering lighting conditions.
- **Task IV (pick block to reveal hidden text):** Achieved an SR of 1.0, leveraging temporal memory to track the block's position.
- **Task V (place two blocks in a box while preserving spatial relationship):** Achieved an SR of 0.8, with reported failures attributed to spatial reasoning errors.
- **Task VI (lift eraser to read surface text using a secondary camera):** Achieved an SR of 0.8, effectively utilizing the fixed camera.
The system demonstrated resilience to positional and orientational variance, indicating generalization to unstructured scenes. Task III, which required precise pushing to retrieve a pen drive under clutter, had an SR of 0.6, with failures due to delicate manipulation demands and exceeding the five-iteration limit.

**Performance in Complex Scenarios (Task VII and VIII):**
Tasks VII and VIII represented the highest complexity and were assessed across various framework configurations.
- **Task VII (lift eraser between two objects to read text):** This task required multi-step reasoning, involving pushing obstructing objects before grasping the eraser.
 - ZS-IP Push and ZS-IP Grasp configurations achieved an SR of 0.0, indicating limitations when the task required a sequence of actions or if the object remained occluded after initial interactions. ZS-IP Push failed because the eraser's bottom face remained occluded, necessitating lifting. The VLM sometimes prematurely assumed text language.
 - The baseline ZS-IP (without in-context examples) achieved an SR of 0.2.
 - **ZS-IP In-Context** demonstrated a significantly higher SR of 0.7. This improvement was attributed to its ability to delay the grasping action until the eraser was fully cleared from obstructions, leading to more robust execution. Although it sometimes resulted in a higher total path length (TL) due to the VLM suggesting removal of all surrounding obstacles, it ensured successful grasping under optimal conditions.
 - **MOKA** also failed this task (SR 0.0), primarily due to its reliance on a discrete 2D grid which struggled with suggesting accurate pre- and post-contact points for pushing, and its lack of memory retention.

- **Task VIII (locate strawberry inside a cup partially obstructed by an inclined book):** This task demanded precise scene analysis and controlled interaction.
 - **ZS-IP Observe** (SR 0.1) was efficient in navigating the camera but failed to correctly classify the strawberry in six instances due to shadows cast by the inclined book, hindering reliable object recognition and demonstrating a lack of sufficient contextual understanding for disambiguation.
 - **MOKA** (SR 0.5) showed limitations with its fixed 2D grid, frequently leading to misaligned push points and unintended scene disruptions like the cup toppling over.
 - **ZS-IP Push** (SR 0.8) and **ZS-IP In-Context** (SR 0.8) performed well, requiring precise sideways pushes.
 - The baseline ZS-IP (without in-context examples) achieved an SR of 0.7. These results highlighted the advantage of ZS-IP's closed-loop perception-action framework and the impact of pushlines in complex, unstructured environments.

**Comparison with Baseline Methods:**
- **MOKA:** ZS-IP generally outperformed MOKA, particularly in pushing tasks. MOKA's challenges stemmed from its dependence on a fixed 2D grid and a lack of memory retention for prior states, making it less adaptive to cluttered and complex scenarios.
- **PIVOT:** An ablation study comparing ZS-IP with PIVOT (with and without interactive perception) on Tasks IV, VI, and VIII indicated that ZS-IP achieved higher success rates. This was attributed to the visual reasoning capabilities induced by the Enhanced Observation (EO) module.

**Contribution of Enhanced Observation and Memory:**
The introduction of **pushlines** (EO_P) was critical for substantially improving pushing performance, effectively capturing affordances for contact-rich actions. The **memory-guided action module** proved crucial for tasks requiring multi-step reasoning, such as Task VII, by enabling the retrieval of past data, facilitating sequential decision-making, and avoiding redundant actions.

**VLM Model Comparison:**
A comparison of different VLM models for Task III, Task IV, Task VI, and Task VIII showed that **GPT-4o** yielded the highest success rates compared to GPT-4 Turbo, Gemini 2.0 Flash, and Claude 3.5 Sonnet, indicating its stronger reasoning capabilities within this framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Zero-Shot Interactive Perception (ZS-IP) framework presents an advancement in robotic manipulation by integrating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with physical interaction capabilities, addressing several challenges in the field.

**Advancements in Robotic Interaction:**
ZS-IP's core contribution lies in its ability to couple high-level semantic reasoning from VLMs with multi-strategy robotic manipulation (pushing, grasping, lifting, and viewpoint adjustment). This integration is designed to enable robots to operate effectively in complex, partially observable environments where physical interaction is necessary to extract hidden information. The introduction of "pushlines" as a novel 2D visual augmentation for pushing actions is a specific innovation that demonstrably improves the robot's ability to perform contact-rich manipulations, capturing affordances more effectively than conventional grid-based methods.

**Addressing Real-World Challenges:**
The framework's capability to iteratively manipulate the environment, uncover occluded objects, and resolve natural language queries contributes to overcoming a significant bottleneck in robotic perception. This is particularly relevant for applications in unstructured or semi-structured settings, where objects may be cluttered, unknown, or hidden from direct view. The memory-guided action module allows the robot to build temporal context, leading to more precise, context-aware action selection and task completion, which is vital for multi-step reasoning tasks. The zero-shot nature of the framework also implies a potential for enhanced generalization to novel objects and scenarios without extensive task-specific training.

**Potential Impact:**
The principles demonstrated by ZS-IP could have implications for various robotic applications:
- **E-commerce and Logistics:** Robots in warehouses could more efficiently pick, pack, and sort diverse items, including those partially hidden or ambiguously positioned, and process customer returns requiring identification of specific contents.
- **Manufacturing and Assembly:** Enhanced interactive perception could improve capabilities for tasks involving cluttered workbenches or delicate component handling.
- **Agricultural Robotics:** Future applications in agriculture, such as identifying and picking ripe fruits obscured by foliage while avoiding unripe ones, could benefit from the ability to interactively explore and discern objects.
- **Service Robotics:** In home or office environments, robots could perform tasks requiring object retrieval from cluttered spaces or organization of items based on semantic understanding.

**Future Research Directions and Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations and areas for future work:
- **Depth Information and Dexterity:** The current framework is constrained by low-resolution depth information, limiting precision in cluttered environments. Future work aims to enhance manipulation capabilities to full SE(3) transformations for pushing and grasping, moving beyond current R2/R3 translations and SO(2) rotations.
- **Real-time Integration and Scalability:** Integrating large models in real-time for multi-modal reasoning remains an open challenge.
- **Environmental Generalization:** Extending ZS-IP to more realistic, less controlled real-world scenarios beyond laboratory settings is planned.
- **Common-Sense Reasoning:** Further enhancement of VLM common-sense reasoning capabilities is required. For instance, distinguishing between stacking objects versus arranging them side-by-side, based on human common sense, is an identified discrepancy.
- **Multi-Modal Sensing:** Incorporating additional sensing modalities, such as tactile feedback and proprioceptive sensing, is anticipated to enable richer environmental feedback and more dexterous manipulation.

By addressing these limitations, ZS-IP has the potential to further advance the capabilities of robotic systems in complex and dynamic environments, moving towards more autonomous and intelligent physical interaction.
