# Differences That Matter: Auditing Models for Capability Gap Discovery and Rectification

> [!summary] Summary
> An automated framework named AuditDM helps diagnose and improve multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by training an MLLM to act as an auditor, generating question-image pairs that reveal specific capability gaps. This method identifies over 20 failure categories and facilitates annotation-free rectification, allowing smaller models to outperform larger ones on various benchmarks without manual labeling.

## Problem

- Conventional MLLM evaluation methods provide sparse, aggregate scores that mask heterogeneous performance shifts and fail to offer interpretable insights into model failures.
- Understanding and addressing complex MLLM behaviors for real-world deployment is hindered by the prohibitive cost and lack of scalability of human-intensive evaluation.
- The absence of a systematic, interpretable framework for diagnosing specific capability gaps makes targeted rectification and continuous improvement of MLLMs challenging.

## Method

- Introduces **AuditDM**, an RL-based framework that trains an MLLM as an "==auditor==" to actively generate challenging question-image pairs.
- The auditor aims to maximize disagreement between a target MLLM and a reference ensemble, using generated or edited images and complex questions.
- Identified failure modes are converted into annotation-free synthetic data, enabling targeted fine-tuning and rectification of the target MLLM.

## Results

- AuditDM demonstrated a **91.1%** search success rate in identifying valid errors in PaliGemma2-**3B**, significantly outperforming prompt-engineered baselines.
- The framework automatically revealed over 20 distinct failure categories, including unexpected weaknesses like increased hallucination and struggles with emotion understanding in the larger PaliGemma2-**28B** model.
- Fine-tuning with AuditDM's annotation-free synthetic data consistently improved model performance, allowing PaliGemma2-**3B** to surpass its **28B** counterpart on benchmarks like AI2D, and Gemma3-**4B** to exceed Gemma3-**12B** on Seed-Bench-IMG, MMMU, and RealWorldQA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Larger MLLMs do not universally outperform smaller variants; the PaliGemma2-28B model, for instance, showed a higher propensity for hallucination and struggled more with emotion and counting than its 3B counterpart.
> - Model auditing with generated challenging inputs can effectively pinpoint and interpret specific failure categories and brittle decision-making patterns in MLLMs.
> - Weakness-targeted synthetic data generated by an auditor provides an annotation-free and highly efficient method for fine-tuning, enabling smaller models to significantly close performance gaps with or even surpass larger models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has introduced a new frontier in AI research, with models achieving impressive performance on various benchmarks. However, this paper identifies a critical gap in the existing research landscape: the inadequacy of conventional evaluation methods in truly understanding and addressing MLLM capabilities and their limitations, particularly for real-world deployment.

**Critique of Existing Evaluation Paradigms:**
- **Standard Benchmarks:** While widely used (e.g., VQAv2, MMBench), the authors argue that these benchmarks suffer from fixed knowledge scopes, leading to "blind spots" and potentially biased comparisons. Furthermore, they often condense complex model behaviors into "sparse scores," masking heterogeneous performance shifts and concentrating significant capability gaps in the "long tail" of less common scenarios.
- **Human Online Testing:** Acknowledged as a method for comprehensive evaluation, but deemed "prohibitively expensive and time-consuming" to scale for the vastness of MLLM capabilities.
- **Adversarial Attacks:** Prior work in this area largely focuses on model safety, such as "jailbreaks" or data exfiltration, often relying on optimization-based methods to induce malicious behavior. AuditDM distinguishes itself by targeting *inherent weaknesses and recurring failure patterns* that are not necessarily malicious but represent fundamental capability gaps, using an optimization-free approach for diagnosis in a single inference step.
- **Synthetic Data Generation:** Existing methods for synthetic data are primarily used for general pre-training or fine-tuning, aiming for generic alignment or diversity (e.g., GPT-4V based captioning, diffusion-based image rendering). AuditDM, however, proposes a novel use of synthetic data generation: it actively generates *weakness-targeted* samples, moving beyond generic objectives to directly address identified capability gaps.
- **Self-Evolution and Weak-to-Strong Learning:** These paradigms explore autonomous model improvement through self-generated data or adversarial dynamics. While AuditDM shares a principle of iterative improvement, it explicitly trains a *model-specific auditor* to systematically uncover and summarize interpretable capability gaps and failure modes, providing a more targeted and diagnostic approach compared to general self-improvement loops.

**Contribution to the Landscape:**
AuditDM introduces "model auditing" as a novel evaluation paradigm specifically designed to uncover and interpret hidden divergence patterns that conventional methods miss. It aims to transcend simple error detection by systematically discovering capability gaps, summarizing interpretable weaknesses, and providing actionable feedback for rectification. This work fits into the growing need for more transparent, robust, and continually improving AI systems, especially as MLLMs become more integrated into critical applications. It offers a scalable, annotation-free, and interpretable alternative to current evaluation and improvement methods, directly addressing the challenge of model diagnosis and targeted refinement in a post-data-scaling-plateau era.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an automated, interpretable, and rectifiable framework for identifying and resolving capability gaps in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

**Core Objectives:**
1. **Introduce Model Auditing:** To establish "model auditing" as a new paradigm for systematically discovering model capability gaps and diagnosing weaknesses in MLLMs. This paradigm emphasizes interpretability and actionability over mere performance scores.
2. **Develop AuditDM Framework:** To design and implement AuditDM, a reinforcement learning-based framework that trains an MLLM to act as an "auditor." This auditor automatically identifies and interprets MLLM failure modes by generating challenging question-image pairs that maximize disagreement between a target model and reference models.
3. **Enable Annotation-Free Rectification:** To demonstrate that AuditDM can convert discovered failure modes into annotation-free training data. This data then facilitates targeted retraining and continual improvement of MLLMs, closing identified capability gaps.

**Driving Motivations:**
- **Limitations of Conventional Evaluations:** The authors observe that despite rapid advancements and high benchmark scores, conventional MLLM evaluations fail to provide sufficient interpretability and often obscure significant capability differences crucial for real-world deployment. They highlight the challenge of understanding "what changes, and why" when models are retrained or fine-tuned, rather than just "who wins the leaderboard."
- **Need for Interpretability and Actionability:** There is a strong need to go beyond aggregate scores to gain interpretable insights into *why* models fail and *where* their brittle behaviors persist. This diagnostic understanding is essential for making informed decisions about model selection, deployment, and improvement.
- **Cost and Scalability of Human Evaluation:** Traditional methods like human online testing, while providing deep insights, are not scalable for the complexity and breadth of modern MLLMs due to their prohibitive cost and time requirements.
- **Diminishing Returns of Data Scaling:** The paper posits that as general data scaling approaches diminishing returns, a more targeted approach to model diagnosis and improvement becomes essential. Auditing, by focusing on specific weaknesses, offers a more efficient path for continual learning and refinement.
- **Bridging the Gap between Diagnosis and Rectification:** A significant motivation is to create a closed-loop system where discovered failures are not just identified but directly translated into actionable training signals to improve the model, fostering an iterative process of evaluation and enhancement.

In essence, the research aims to provide a practical and systematic answer to the question of how to genuinely understand and continuously enhance the capabilities of complex MLLMs, making them more reliable and transparent for diverse applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AuditDM proposes an innovative reinforcement learning (RL) framework that trains an MLLM to serve as an "auditor" capable of actively discovering and rectifying capability gaps in other MLLMs. The core idea revolves around maximizing divergence between a target model's responses and those of a reference ensemble.

**The AuditDM Framework (Refer to Figure 1 and 3):**

1. **Auditor Model:** An MLLM (specifically, a fine-tuned Gemma3-4B in experiments) is designated as the auditor. This auditor's role is to analyze an input image and generate challenging content.

2. **Question-Image Pair Generation (Sec 3.1):** The auditor generates two types of outputs to construct a "failure-inducing" pair (\(Q^*, I^*\)):
 - **Text Instruction for \(I^*\) Generation (Probing Visual Weaknesses):**
 - **Image Generation:** The auditor produces a detailed caption (\(C\)) for the input image (\(I\)) and then enriches it with challenging semantic elements. This caption is fed to an image diffusion model (G, e.g., FLUX.1-dev) to synthesize an altered image (\(I_g = G(C)\)) that embodies these challenging attributes.
 - **Image Editing:** Alternatively, for more targeted analysis, the auditor generates explicit editing commands (\(E\)) for an image-editing model (E, e.g., FLUX.1-Kontext-dev). This produces an edited image (\(I_e = E(I, E)\)) with controlled modifications, systematically probing the target model's response to visual perturbations.
 - **Direct \(Q^*\) Generation (Probing Textual Weaknesses):** The auditor also generates complex, nuanced questions (\(Q^*\)) designed to challenge the target MLLM. These questions can be generated for the original image (\(I\)), the synthesized image (\(I_g\)), or the edited image (\(I_e\)). In practice, the framework combines all three pairing levels: (\(Q^*, I^*\)), (\(Q^*, I\)), and (\(Q, I^*\)).

3. **Divergence of Models (Sec 3.2):**
 - Once a pair (\(Q^*, I^*\)) is generated, it is presented to the **target MLLM** (\(M_{tar}\)) and a **reference MLLM** (\(M_{ref}\)) or an **ensemble of reference MLLMs**.
 - **Disagreement Quantification:** The responses from \(M_{tar}\) and \(M_{ref}\) are compared using a binary semantic-agreement judge. This judge returns 1 if the two answers differ in meaning and 0 otherwise, producing a disagreement signal \(s(Q^*, I^*) = D(M_{tar}(Q^*, I^*), M_{ref}(Q^*, I^*))\).
 - **Attribution of Failures:** To ensure identified failures are genuinely due to the target model and not artifacts of the auditor or diffusion model, AuditDM relies on two assumptions: (1) if the ensemble agrees, the question-image pair is likely meaningful and answerable; and (2) it's rare for \(M_{tar}\) to be uniquely correct while all ensemble models are wrong. The auditor is updated only when \(M_{tar}\)'s prediction conflicts with the ensemble's consensus.

4. **RL-based Auditor Training (Sec 3.3):**
 - The auditor \(A\) is fine-tuned using **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**. This RL-style approach iteratively refines the auditor to generate instructions that maximize cross-model disagreement.
 - GRPO uses group-relative normalization of the disagreement signal to form advantages, optimizing the auditor to favor examples that expose discrepancies.
 - This training process allows the auditor to learn complex textual and visual patterns that induce failures, ultimately becoming a fast, single-pass diagnostic tool.

5. **Failure Mode Rectification (Sec 3.4):** After the auditor is trained and identifies failure modes, these insights are converted into training signals for the target MLLM:
 - **Augmenting Labeled Data:** Auditor-generated samples (with pseudo-labels derived from the ensemble's consensus) are mixed with the original training data to create a comprehensive training set.
 - **Bootstrapping Unlabeled Data:** An iterative, annotation-free process is proposed:
 1. Auditors (including checkpoints from different training steps) generate questions, new images, and pseudo-labels from an unlabeled image pool.
 2. These are aggregated, deduplicated, and used to fine-tune the target MLLM.
 3. New auditors are trained on the refined target MLLM, and the process repeats. This iterative mechanism enables continual rectification and improvement.

**Implementation Details:** The experiments utilized PaliGemma2 and Gemma3 models as targets, with Gemma3-4B serving as the auditor. FLUX.1 models were used for image generation and editing. Auditor training involved AdamW optimizer over 1,000 steps, with specific learning rate schedules. The reference ensemble for single-model analysis included a mixture of PaliGemma2, Gemma3, and Qwen2.5-VL. For pseudo-annotation validation, Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT-5 API were used, with human re-verification on disagreements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by Liu et al. demonstrate the effectiveness of AuditDM in both diagnosing MLLM weaknesses and driving model improvements.

#### 4.1. AuditDM for Model Failure Detection (Sec 4.1)

- **Effectiveness of Finding Model Weaknesses (Sec 4.1.1):** AuditDM significantly outperforms baseline methods in discovering model failures. When attempting to identify valid errors in PaliGemma2-3B, AuditDM achieved an impressive **91.1% search success rate**, compared to only 21.4% for a prompt-engineered baseline (Table 2). This highlights AuditDM's efficiency in actively uncovering model limitations. The discovered weaknesses were diverse and interpretable, providing richer insights than simple error flags.

- **Weakness Analysis of PaliGemma2 (Sec 4.1.2):**
 - AuditDM automatically identified **over 20 distinct failure categories** within the PaliGemma2 family (Fig. 4, Fig. 5).
 - **PaliGemma2-3B Specific Weaknesses:** The smaller 3B model exhibited pronounced weaknesses in tasks requiring world knowledge, clock reading, size comparison, action recognition, and sign understanding.
 - **PaliGemma2-28B Specific Weaknesses:** Intriguingly, AuditDM revealed several categories where the larger 28B model underperformed its 3B counterpart. The 28B model showed a significantly higher propensity for **hallucination errors** and struggled more with **emotion understanding, color recognition, and counting**. This finding challenges the assumption that larger models are inherently more robust across all capabilities.
 - **Interpretability with Image Editing:** By leveraging image editing, AuditDM clarified how subtle visual modifications could substantially alter model predictions (Fig. 6). Minor, task-irrelevant alterations could cause the 28B model to fail while the 3B remained correct, suggesting that the models rely on different, sometimes brittle, visual cues for their decisions. This capability offers valuable interpretability into model vulnerabilities.

#### 4.2. AuditDM for Model Improvement (Sec 4.2)

- **Per-task Fine-tuning Results (Sec 4.2.1):**
 - AuditDM consistently improved the **PaliGemma2-3B model across all 8 diverse academic benchmarks** (VQA, text-VQA, image segmentation, image captioning) when used to generate synthetic data for fine-tuning.
 - The gains were substantial, for example, raising PaliGemma2-3B's performance on **AI2D from 76.0 to 85.3**, enabling it to **surpass the PaliGemma2-28B model** (Table 3). Similar improvements allowed the 3B model to outperform or match the 10B and 28B variants on several other benchmarks.
 - Smaller gains were observed for grounding tasks (e.g., RefCOCO), attributed to potential misalignment of bounding box annotations in synthesized or edited images.

- **General Benchmark Results (Sec 4.2.2):**
 - When applied to **Gemma3-4B** and evaluated on 8 widely used, task-diverse multimodal benchmarks (MMBench, SEED-Bench, MMMU, etc.), AuditDM delivered **substantial gains on every benchmark without any human annotations** (Table 4).
 - Gemma3-4B + AuditDM significantly narrowed the performance gap with the larger Gemma3-12B and Gemma3-27B models, even **surpassing Gemma3-12B on Seed-Bench-IMG, MMMU, and RealWorldQA**. These results underscore AuditDM's efficacy as a scalable, data-efficient, and annotation-free path for MLLM improvement.

- **Ablation on Different Auditing Components (Sec 4.2.3):**
 - The ablation study (Table 5) revealed that all three auditing components (probing questions, image generation, image editing) contribute to performance gains, though their effectiveness varies by task.
 - **Probing question generation** generally yielded the largest gains for VQA tasks, suggesting that crafting more informative and targeted questions is a highly effective way to improve MLLMs.
 - **Image editing** slightly outperformed image regeneration for general VQA, possibly due to fewer biases or artifacts introduced by full regeneration.
 - For tasks requiring precise visual grounding or complex diagram understanding (like RefCOCO or AI2D), image generation and regeneration were more challenging due to difficulties in producing accurate pseudo-annotations or generating complex diagrams.

In summary, the main findings robustly demonstrate AuditDM's capability to effectively diagnose diverse and sometimes counter-intuitive weaknesses in state-of-the-art MLLMs, and critically, to leverage these discoveries for significant, annotation-free, and continual model improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of AuditDM represents a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models, offering profound implications for their development, evaluation, and deployment.

#### 5.1. A New Paradigm for MLLM Evaluation

AuditDM moves beyond conventional, aggregate-score-based benchmarks by introducing "model auditing" as a systematic and interpretable evaluation paradigm. This shifts the focus from merely reporting "who wins the leaderboard" to understanding "what changes, and why," providing diagnostic insights into specific capability gaps and failure modes. This granular understanding is crucial for moving MLLMs from lab environments to robust real-world applications.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Interpretability and Trust

By automatically generating interpretable exemplars (challenging questions and counterfactual images) that reveal specific model weaknesses, AuditDM significantly improves our understanding of MLLM decision boundaries and vulnerabilities. Identifying subtle visual cues that alter predictions, as shown in the PaliGemma2-28B example, helps characterize model brittleness and biases. This increased interpretability is vital for building trust in AI systems, allowing developers and users to understand *why* a model might fail in certain scenarios.

#### 5.3. Efficient and Annotation-Free Model Rectification

Perhaps the most impactful contribution is AuditDM's ability to translate discovered failure modes into annotation-free training data. This enables targeted fine-tuning and continual improvement of MLLMs without the prohibitive cost and time associated with manual data labeling or human online testing. The demonstration that a smaller 3B model, when enhanced with AuditDM, can surpass its 28B or 12B counterparts highlights the efficiency and power of this targeted data generation approach. This offers a scalable and practical solution for model refinement.

#### 5.4. Addressing Limitations of Data Scaling

As the benefits of scaling models with ever-larger, generic datasets begin to diminish, AuditDM offers a strategic pathway for continued progress. By focusing on generating *weakness-targeted* data, it provides an effective mechanism for continual learning, enabling models to overcome specific deficiencies even when broad data acquisition yields diminishing returns. This shifts the paradigm from "more data" to "smarter data."

#### 5.5. Practical Tool for AI Developers

AuditDM serves as a valuable diagnostic tool for MLLM developers. It can systematically identify and categorize failure types, provide specific examples of why a model might underperform, and then directly generate the data needed to address these issues. This closed-loop system for diagnosis and rectification streamlines the development process, making MLLMs more robust and reliable for diverse tasks.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The limitations identified, such as challenges in generating images with dense text or complex diagrams, and the computational complexity of the pipeline, open up new avenues for research. Future work can focus on developing stronger ensembles for pseudo-labeling, more specialized generative models for complex visual data, and optimizing the auditing process for reduced computational cost.

In conclusion, AuditDM provides a timely and powerful framework that addresses fundamental challenges in MLLM evaluation and improvement. By enabling automatic, interpretable discovery and rectification of capability gaps, it significantly contributes to the development of more robust, reliable, and trustworthy multimodal AI systems, paving the way for a deeper understanding and more controlled evolution of these complex models.
