# FutureSim: Replaying World Events to Evaluate Adaptive Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed FutureSim, an environment that replays real-world events from timestamped news to evaluate AI agents' long-horizon adaptive forecasting capabilities. The benchmark revealed significant room for improving test-time adaptation in frontier models, with GPT 5.5 achieving 25% top-1 accuracy but many open-weight models demonstrating negative Brier skill scores.

## Problem

- Evaluating AI agents' long-horizon adaptation in dynamic, open-ended environments that accurately reflect real-world temporal evolution.
- Existing benchmarks either rely on human-designed simulations, serving as imperfect proxies, or involve live deployments and prediction markets that lack reproducibility, scalability, or cost-effectiveness.
- Assessing how agents update initial beliefs and integrate new information beyond their knowledge cutoffs in a grounded and controlled setting.

## Method

- Introduces **FutureSim**, a ==chronological simulation environment== that replays real-world events from timestamped news articles to evaluate agent adaptation on forecasting tasks.
- Agents operate in a ==sandboxed, open-ended setting== with access to an offline news corpus and basic shell/file tools, autonomously selecting questions, and updating predictions.
- Employs ==Brier Skill Score== and ==top-1 accuracy== for evaluation, alongside a ==custom harness== designed to optimize the performance of open-weight models.

## Results

- GPT 5.5 achieved the highest performance with **25%** top-1 accuracy and a positive Brier skill score, consuming **12.4M tokens** over **3,700 turns**, while many open-weight models showed negative Brier skill scores.
- Ablation studies confirmed that increasing reasoning effort consistently improved performance but plateaued, and that the ability to write and fetch memories was essential for preventing knowledge drift.
- Multi-agent experiments demonstrated that agents' predictions converged towards an aggregate when exposed to peer information, suggesting a role for social learning or competitive dynamics in forecasting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Significant room exists for enhancing test-time adaptation in frontier models, as evidenced by an "anchoring" effect where agents struggle to adapt away from initially poor beliefs.
> - Memory mechanisms and continuous, agentic search for fresh evidence are crucial for performance, with daily news updates and iterative search outperforming static contexts or single queries.
> - Harness design plays a vital role in eliciting optimal agent performance, with a custom harness notably improving open-weight models' calibration and accuracy compared to native environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in evaluating AI agents, particularly their ability to adapt over long horizons in dynamic, open-ended environments. While existing benchmarks, such as ARC-AGI 3 and VendingBench, serve as proxies for adaptation, the authors argue for evaluations that more closely align with real-world temporal evolution.

The paper situates FutureSim within several relevant research areas:
- **Continual Learning Evaluations:** Prior work emphasizes the use of real-world time as a source of distribution shift for language models (Lazaridou et al., 2021; Yao et al., 2022). FutureSim extends this by focusing on forecasting future events, requiring reasoning under uncertainty and long-horizon information seeking actions.
- **Long-Horizon Environments:** Benchmarks like BALROG, ARC-AGI 3, GAIA-2, Vending-Bench, and YC-Bench explore long-horizon agent interaction within games and simulations. The authors note that the dynamics in these environments are often human-designed or model-simulated, limiting their fidelity to societal evolution. Live deployments (e.g., Anthropic’s Project Vend) offer realism but are characterized as slow, expensive, and lacking reproducibility. FutureSim aims to circumvent these issues by deriving environment dynamics directly from real-world event timestamps, similar to approaches like SWE-Evo for coding agents but generalized to diverse domains through prediction tasks derived from timestamped source documents (Chandak et al., 2026).
- **Judgemental Forecasting Benchmarks:** The domain of language model forecasting of discrete real-world events has received attention. Earlier evaluations (Halawi et al., 2024; Karger et al., 2025) focused on static forecasting questions with retrieved evidence. More recent benchmarks, such as ProphetArena (Yang et al., 2026) and PredictionArena (Zhang et al., 2026b), evaluate agents through live trading on prediction markets. FutureSim provides a replayable environment that supports controlled ablations of search, memory, harness design, and adaptation, an advantage over live-market evaluations which are difficult to reproduce and analyze under controlled conditions. KellyBench (Grady et al., 2026) focuses on statistical model building for sports, contrasting with FutureSim’s aim to evaluate agents on diverse, general world events from evolving evidence.

The paper also includes a comparative table, positioning FutureSim as a benchmark that combines world reasoning, reproducibility, adaptation testing, open-endedness, and a long horizon length, distinguishing it from several contemporary benchmarks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient method for measuring the adaptive capabilities of AI agents in dynamic, open-ended environments that reflect realistic use-cases. The authors propose building grounded simulations that replay real-world events in their chronological order to achieve this.

Specifically, the core objectives and motivations for FutureSim are:
- **Evaluating Adaptive Agents:** To test how AI agents adapt to new information by forecasting world events beyond their knowledge cutoff. This involves interacting with a chronological replay of real news articles and resolving questions as the simulated period progresses.
- **Alignment with Real-World Evolution:** The authors contend that adaptation should be measured in environments that mirror how the real world evolves, rather than solely relying on human-designed games or simulations which may serve as imperfect proxies for complex societal dynamics.
- **Forecasting as a Testbed:** Forecasting is identified as a suitable domain for studying long-horizon test-time adaptation for two main reasons:
 1. **Belief Alignment and Updating:** It assesses whether agents’ initial beliefs (priors) are consistent with real-world developments and if they appropriately update these beliefs in response to new information. This mirrors observations in human expert forecasters, who continuously update their beliefs for improved accuracy.
 2. **Groundedness in Reality:** A predictive setting ensures that agents’ actions do not alter the underlying environment, thereby allowing the evaluation to remain grounded in real-world events rather than requiring the simulation of counterfactual worlds.
- **Scalability and Reproducibility:** The methodology aims to bootstrap simulations from timestamped source documents, offering a scalable and reproducible alternative to live deployments or prediction markets that present challenges in control and cost.
- **Supporting Emerging Research Directions:** The benchmark is designed to facilitate research into various advanced AI capabilities, including long-horizon test-time adaptation, strategies for searching under uncertainty, memory mechanisms, harness design, and multi-agent dynamics.

The authors summarize their main contributions as:
- Developing FutureSim, an environment for realistically evaluating AI agent adaptation on forecasting tasks by replaying world events after agents' knowledge cutoffs. This involves sandboxing agents and providing access to reliably dated offline news snapshots.
- Designing FutureSim as an open-ended environment where agents autonomously select questions to forecast, determine outcomes and probabilities, and decide when to update predictions, using a minimal set of actions (`submit_prediction` and `next_day`).
- Demonstrating substantial opportunities for enhancing test-time adaptation in frontier models, even when operating within their native agent harnesses. The benchmark’s structure supports studies into reasoning for uncertain search, memory, harness optimization, and multi-agent interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FutureSim environment is designed as a chronological simulation with a daily time-step cadence, mirroring real-world dates.

#### 3.1. Environment Design

- **Tasks:** The environment maintains the state of forecasting questions in a CSV file. Each question includes background information, resolution criteria, a resolution date, and the agent's most recent forecast. Agents are permitted to submit a probability distribution over multiple possible outcomes, which they generate themselves. Upon a question's resolution date, the ground-truth outcome is added to the state file.
- **Context:** At each time-step, the context comprises documents that became available by that specific date. The Common Crawl News (CCNews) dataset is utilized for its reliably dated snapshots of news articles, ensuring that information remains unaffected by future updates.
- **Agent Interaction:** The environment provides a minimal interface with two core actions:
 1. `submit_forecast(question_id, outcomes)`: Allows agents to register or update their probability distribution for an active question.
 2. `next_day`: Advances the simulation by one day, triggering environment state updates and evaluations.

#### 3.2. Evaluation

The evaluation of forecasts uses two primary metrics:
- **Brier Skill Score (BSS):** This metric is adapted for free-form questions with multiple outcomes, based on the multi-category Brier score. It measures the accuracy and calibration of agents' probabilistic forecasts. The formula is BSS(q) = 1 - $\sum_{o \in \Omega_q \cup \{y_q\}} (p_q(o) - \mathbf{1}[o = y_q])^2$, where $p_q(o)$ is the predicted probability for outcome $o$, and $\mathbf{1}[o = y_q]$ is an indicator function for the ground truth. A score of 1 indicates a perfectly confident correct answer, 0 for abstaining, and -1 for confidently assigning all probability to incorrect outcomes.
- **Accuracy:** This measures the top-1 accuracy, defined as the proportion of questions where the outcome assigned the highest probability by the agent matches the ground-truth answer.
 Metrics are reported as the mean across all questions, with unresolved questions contributing 0 to the score.

#### 3.3. Benchmarking Frontier Agents

- **Forecasting Questions:** 330 short-answer questions were curated from Al Jazeera articles, resolving between January 1st and March 28th, 2026, thus falling after the knowledge cutoffs of the evaluated models. The question generation pipeline refined methodologies from Chandak et al. (2026), incorporating stronger models, filtering easy questions, and improving the reliability of resolution dates by identifying the earliest inferable date. Answer matching for agent predictions is performed by DeepSeek v3.2, with a limit of five outcomes per question.
- **Search Corpus:** A local, deduplicated snapshot of CCNews, containing 7.36 million articles from January 2023 to March 2026, serves as the search corpus. Approximately 7.12 million articles are available on day zero, with an additional 244,000 new articles added over the 88-day simulation. This offline corpus addresses issues found with date filtering in external search APIs.
- **Tools Available to Agents:** Agents have full access to a shell and file tools (e.g., Bash, Read, Write, Grep). Articles are organized by date (e.g., `articles/YYYY/MM/DD/`). A hybrid semantic and keyword search tool, powered by LanceDB and Qwen3 8B embeddings, allows agents to search the news corpus, returning 512-token chunks. The `search_news(query, from_date, to_date)` tool caps `to_date` at the current simulation date.
- **Sandboxing:** Agents are carefully sandboxed using `bwrap` to prevent leakage of future information. This entails disabling live web search, restricting file system access to date-gated article directories, and enforcing read-only access to environment state files.
- **Agents Tested:** The evaluation included GPT 5.5 (using Codex), DeepSeek V4 Pro (using Claude Code), Opus 4.6 (using Claude Code), Qwen3.6 Plus (using OpenCode), and GLM 5.1 (using Claude Code). All models were run at their maximum reasoning effort within their recommended harnesses. Additional prompting was applied to DeepSeek and Qwen to encourage prediction updates.
- **Our Baseline Harness:** A custom harness was developed to improve the performance of open-weight models, featuring context consumption feedback, structured per-question and global memory tools, a forced memory update phase, and procedural forecasting guidelines.

#### 3.4. Ablation Studies

The paper conducted several ablations to isolate the impact of different capabilities:
- **Test-Time Adaptation:** Experiments were designed to compare adaptation across agents by fixing initial forecasts to the lowest-performing agent's baseline. Another ablation tested GPT 5.5's performance when making direct predictions one day before resolution versus continuous sequential updates.
- **Memory:** The impact of memory was assessed by ablating agents’ ability to write and retrieve memories.
- **Search:** The value of an evolving context and agentic search was evaluated by comparing scenarios with daily news updates versus a frozen corpus, and full agentic search versus a single search query.
- **Inference Scaling:** GPT 5.5 was evaluated at five different reasoning effort levels to study the effect of test-time compute.
- **Multi-Agent Dynamics:** Three identical DeepSeek v3.2 agents were run in a competitive setting, where they could observe a crowd aggregate prediction, to study multi-agent behavior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of frontier agents in FutureSim yielded several key findings regarding their adaptive capabilities and areas for improvement.

#### 4.1. Overall Agent Performance

- FutureSim demonstrated a clear differentiation in capabilities among closed and open-weight frontier models.
- GPT 5.5 achieved the highest performance, leading in both top-1 accuracy (25%) and Brier skill score.
- A notable finding was that many open-weight frontier models exhibited a negative Brier skill score, indicating their predictions were less reliable than abstaining from prediction altogether.
- Across all models, a general trend of improving prediction accuracy over the simulation period was observed as new information became available.
- The long-horizon nature of FutureSim was evidenced by GPT 5.5's performance, which involved consuming 12.4 million unique tokens and performing 3,700 turns, including multiple sequential context window compactions, in a single run.

#### 4.2. Impact of Harness Design

- The custom-designed harness significantly improved the performance of open-weight models in both accuracy and Brier skill score. DeepSeek V4 Pro, Qwen3.6 Plus, and GLM 5.1 showed marked improvements in this custom harness, with Qwen3.6 Plus even showing deterioration in its recommended OpenCode harness's calibration.
- Claude Opus 4.6 also demonstrated better calibration within the custom harness compared to its native Claude Code environment, suggesting that general coding harnesses may not be optimal for specific forecasting tasks.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Agent Behaviors

- **Confidence and Calibration:** While GPT 5.5 achieved the highest accuracy, it also exhibited overconfidence in some incorrect predictions; 27.4% of its wrong final predictions assigned at least 0.5 probability to an incorrect top answer, and 9.1% assigned at least 0.75.
- **Update Frequency:** DeepSeek V4 Pro frequently updated its predictions across most questions, which contributed to its substantial improvement from initial weaker forecasts. However, it sometimes made null predictions that reduced its score.
- **Conservatism:** GLM 5.1 was observed to be more conservative, with the lowest overconfidence rate among wrong answers (3.7% above 0.5 probability), but its performance was limited by making the fewest updates.
- **Abstention:** Qwen3.6 Plus abstained from more questions than other models, submitting predictions for only 36.7% of the questions.
- **Self-Conditioning:** A recurring observation across models was "self-conditioning," where agents treated their prior rationales and lessons in memory as definitive truths, occasionally leading to subsequent overconfident errors.

#### 4.4. Comparison to Human Aggregate Forecasts

- In comparison to human aggregates from Polymarket on corresponding questions, GPT 5.5's prediction updates generally aligned with market movements. On certain questions, such as the Super Bowl and Portugal Runoff winner, GPT 5.5's predictions sometimes led the market.
- However, GPT 5.5 also performed less effectively on other markets, including the Grammy awards and specific UK constituency elections, which involve aggregating human preferences.
- Detailed analysis of GPT 5.5's trajectory for the Nepal Prime Minister market indicated updates that were appropriate given the cited evidence, though slightly lagging the human aggregate, potentially due to differences in search corpus freshness.

#### 4.5. Capabilities Tested through Ablations

- **Test-Time Adaptation:** When agents were initialized with a shared, low-performing prediction set, GPT 5.5, Claude Opus 4.6, and DeepSeek V4 Pro showed similar levels of improvement, but none managed to reach their original performance levels. This highlighted an "anchoring" effect, where agents struggled to adapt away from poor initial beliefs. Furthermore, GPT 5.5 achieved significantly higher accuracy (31.2% vs. 24.8%) when allowed to predict each question individually one day before its resolution with maximum available context, compared to sequentially updating predictions during the simulation, indicating an inefficiency in long-horizon adaptation.
- **Memory:** Ablating the ability to write and fetch memories at test time resulted in poorer performance across all three tested models, underscoring the importance of memory for storing feedback, search information, and past rationales, and for preventing knowledge drift.
- **Search:** Daily updates to the news corpus led to significantly better accuracy (24.8% vs. 17.9% without updates), demonstrating the value of continuous search for fresh evidence. Full agentic search also doubled the accuracy compared to using a single semantic search query per question, highlighting the importance of sequential, iterative information-seeking.
- **Inference Scaling:** Increasing GPT 5.5's reasoning effort consistently improved both accuracy and Brier skill score, although the benefits plateaued at the highest effort levels. Low reasoning effort resulted in a highly negative Brier skill score, suggesting that more test-time tokens contribute to better-calibrated predictions.
- **Multi-Agent Dynamics:** In experiments with multiple DeepSeek v3.2 agents, their predictions converged toward the aggregate prediction over time when exposed to peer information. This contrasted with independent runs where predictions diverged. Anecdotal evidence also suggested slightly higher initial accuracy for agents operating in the multi-agent context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of FutureSim represents a contribution to the evaluation of AI agents, particularly in dynamic and open-ended environments. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Realistic and Reproducible Evaluation:** FutureSim provides a grounded and reproducible benchmark for evaluating AI agent adaptation. By replaying real-world events based on timestamped news articles, it addresses limitations of existing benchmarks that often rely on human-designed simulations or non-reproducible live-market interactions. This grounded approach increases the relevance of evaluations to real-world applications.
- **Measurement of Long-Horizon Adaptation:** The benchmark is specifically designed to measure long-horizon test-time adaptation, a crucial capability for AI agents operating in continually evolving environments. The findings reveal that current frontier models, even in their native harnesses, demonstrate significant room for improvement in this area.
- **Identification of Current Agent Limitations:** The research highlights specific weaknesses in current AI agents, such as negative Brier skill scores indicating poor calibration, overconfidence in incorrect predictions, and an "anchoring" effect where agents struggle to adapt away from initial beliefs. These identified gaps can guide future research efforts.
- **Catalyst for Advanced AI Research:** FutureSim's flexible design and the conducted ablation studies demonstrate its utility for investigating various emerging AI capabilities. It can support research into:
 - **Improved Test-Time Adaptation:** Encouraging exploration of methods like continued fine-tuning or test-time training to help models integrate new information more effectively.
 - **Enhanced Search Strategies:** Driving innovation in reasoning-intensive sequential search agents and more sophisticated underlying search tools for dynamic, Bayesian-like search settings.
 - **Long-Term Memory Mechanisms:** Facilitating the development of alternative approaches to long-term memory management for transformer-based language models, crucial given context window limitations.
 - **Optimized Harness Design:** Providing a platform to develop and evaluate improved orchestration and prompting strategies that can better elicit agent potential.
 - **Complex Multi-Agent Dynamics:** Enabling studies on how agents interact, compete, and evolve in the presence of others, including investigations into self-play and communication channels.
- **Insights into Agent World Models:** While FutureSim focuses on a predictive setting where agent actions do not alter the environment, it offers valuable insights into agents' internal world models and their ability to integrate and adapt to new information. This understanding is foundational for building more sophisticated and robust AI systems.
- **Relevance to Economically Valuable Tasks:** Forecasting world events is an economically significant task. The observation that GPT 5.5's predictions sometimes outperformed human market aggregates on certain events suggests the potential for AI in real-world forecasting and decision-making applications.

In summary, FutureSim aims to advance the evaluation of AI agents by providing a realistic, adaptable, and reproducible environment. It serves to benchmark current capabilities, uncover limitations, and foster research into the core adaptive mechanisms required for AI to function effectively in dynamic, real-world scenarios.
