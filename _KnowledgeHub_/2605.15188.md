# FutureSim: Replaying World Events to Evaluate Adaptive Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed FutureSim, an environment that replays real-world events from timestamped news to evaluate AI agents' long-horizon adaptive forecasting capabilities. The benchmark revealed significant room for improving test-time adaptation in frontier models, with GPT 5.5 achieving 25% top-1 accuracy but many open-weight models demonstrating negative Brier skill scores.

## Problem

- Evaluating AI agents' long-horizon adaptation in dynamic, open-ended environments that accurately reflect real-world temporal evolution.
- Existing benchmarks either rely on human-designed simulations, serving as imperfect proxies, or involve live deployments and prediction markets that lack reproducibility, scalability, or cost-effectiveness.
- Assessing how agents update initial beliefs and integrate new information beyond their knowledge cutoffs in a grounded and controlled setting.

## Method

- Introduces **FutureSim**, a ==chronological simulation environment== that replays real-world events from timestamped news articles to evaluate agent adaptation on forecasting tasks.
- Agents operate in a ==sandboxed, open-ended setting== with access to an offline news corpus and basic shell/file tools, autonomously selecting questions, and updating predictions.
- Employs ==Brier Skill Score== and ==top-1 accuracy== for evaluation, alongside a ==custom harness== designed to optimize the performance of open-weight models.

## Results

- GPT 5.5 achieved the highest performance with **25%** top-1 accuracy and a positive Brier skill score, consuming **12.4M tokens** over **3,700 turns**, while many open-weight models showed negative Brier skill scores.
- Ablation studies confirmed that increasing reasoning effort consistently improved performance but plateaued, and that the ability to write and fetch memories was essential for preventing knowledge drift.
- Multi-agent experiments demonstrated that agents' predictions converged towards an aggregate when exposed to peer information, suggesting a role for social learning or competitive dynamics in forecasting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Significant room exists for enhancing test-time adaptation in frontier models, as evidenced by an "anchoring" effect where agents struggle to adapt away from initially poor beliefs.
> - Memory mechanisms and continuous, agentic search for fresh evidence are crucial for performance, with daily news updates and iterative search outperforming static contexts or single queries.
> - Harness design plays a vital role in eliciting optimal agent performance, with a custom harness notably improving open-weight models' calibration and accuracy compared to native environments.
