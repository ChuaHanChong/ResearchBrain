# UniPhysGen: Unified Physical Grounding for Simulation-Ready 3D Assets

> [!summary] Summary
> UniPhys is an automated pipeline turning raw heterogeneous 3D meshes into simulation-ready assets by SAM-guided physical part decomposition, LVLM property grounding, geometry-constrained articulation candidate selection, and simulation-driven verification. It yields UniPhys-40K and UniPhys-Bench, and trains UniPhysGen, a Qwen3-based model jointly predicting articulation and physical properties, reaching 89.96% joint-type accuracy and 0.099 pivot error.

## Problem

- Most 3D repositories offer realistic geometry but no articulation kinematics or intrinsic properties like material, density, friction, mass, leaving assets simulation-unaware.
- Prior grounding work treats articulation and physical-property estimation as separate tasks, producing jointly inconsistent semantics, unstable interactions, and implausible simulated motion.
- Existing joint frameworks and datasets assume canonicalized, clean pre-segmented parts and heavy manual curation, generalizing poorly to heterogeneous artist, CAD, and generative meshes.

## Method

- **UniPhys** pipeline: multi-view ==SAM== masks lifted to mesh give a part-count prior, ==PartField== candidates aligned by ==Hungarian matching==, ==Apriori itemset mining== refines physically meaningful parts.
- Articulation reduced to ==LVLM== semantic selection over geometry-constrained axis-pivot candidates from contact regions and motion planes, then four-level ==simulation verification== filters or refines annotations.
- **UniPhysGen**: Qwen3-1.7B with ==Sonata point encoder==, ==physical semantic alignment pretraining==, ==SO(3) augmentation== plus spherical axis parameterization, and shared global voxel positional encoding for pivots.

## Results

- On UniPhys-Bench UniPhysGen reaches **89.96%** joint-type accuracy, **0.099** pivot error, **84.95** limit mIoU, and **80.46** structure mIoU, beating URDFormer, Real2Code, and PARTICULATE.
- Physical properties beat NeRF2Physics: material accuracy **77.92%** versus **53.09%**, density ALDE **0.438** versus **0.807**, friction MAE **0.062** versus **0.142**, mass MnRE **0.663** versus **0.524**.
- Ablations: SO(3) augmentation lifts challenging-rotated joint accuracy **50.09%** to **80.53%**, unified global positional encoding cuts pivot error **0.319** to **0.099**, and merging **60%** of parts barely changes scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Perceptual segmentation priors from vision foundation models often coincide with physically meaningful part boundaries, making them cheap supervision for simulation-oriented mesh decomposition.
> - Let geometry enumerate feasible articulation candidates and let language models only choose among them; direct continuous 3D parameter regression from semantics stays unstable.
> - Rotation augmentation removes upright-orientation shortcuts but destabilizes Cartesian axis outputs, so the output parameterization, here spherical angles, must match the enlarged reasoning space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the domain of 3D computer vision, embodied AI, and robotics simulation. Recent advancements in generative 3D modeling and the proliferation of large-scale 3D repositories have led to an abundance of visually realistic 3D assets. However, a significant limitation of most existing assets is their lack of "physically grounded semantics." These semantics, which include articulation information (e.g., joint types, axes, pivots, motion limits) and intrinsic physical properties (e.g., material, density, friction, mass), are essential for realistic interaction, physical simulation, and training embodied AI agents.

Current research efforts in physical grounding for 3D assets are primarily fragmented. Many approaches address articulation grounding and intrinsic physical property estimation as independent problems. This independent treatment can lead to inconsistencies when these semantics are combined for physical interaction and simulation, potentially resulting in physically implausible motions or unstable interactions. More recent unified frameworks have attempted to jointly model articulation and intrinsic physical properties. However, these often rely on simplifying assumptions, such as canonicalized part decompositions or clean object structures, which limit their robustness and generalization to the diverse and often inconsistent 3D assets found in real-world repositories (e.g., artist-designed meshes, procedurally generated structures).

Concurrently, existing physically grounded datasets also exhibit limitations. While large repositories provide geometric and visual content, they generally lack physical semantics. Datasets that include articulation annotations, such as PartNet-Mobility and GAPartNet, often do not include intrinsic physical properties. Datasets that attempt to combine both, such as PhysXNet and PhysDB, frequently require extensive manual annotation or are built upon pre-structured assets, thus limiting their scalability and applicability to heterogeneous 3D data. Furthermore, existing datasets often focus solely on semantic annotation, with limited mechanisms for validating the grounded results through simulation.

This work positions itself to bridge these gaps by presenting a scalable framework for unified physical grounding that is robust to heterogeneous 3D assets and incorporates simulation-driven validation. It aims to overcome the fragmentation of prior approaches, the restrictive assumptions of existing unified methods, and the limitations of current datasets by providing a comprehensive solution for transforming raw 3D meshes into simulation-ready assets with consistent, unified physical semantics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is the increasing demand for 3D assets that are not only visually realistic but also "simulation-ready." The current disparity between the visual fidelity of available 3D assets and their fundamental lack of physically grounded semantics presents a critical bottleneck for applications in embodied AI, robotics, and interactive simulations. These applications require objects to behave realistically, necessitating explicit information about how their parts move (articulation) and their material properties (intrinsic physical properties). The core challenge is the automatic transformation of raw 3D meshes, which often lack such information, into assets with jointly consistent and physically plausible semantics.

The specific objectives of this work are articulated through the three key challenges it aims to address:

1. **To enable physically meaningful structural decomposition:** Existing methods for decomposing 3D objects into parts primarily optimize for geometric or semantic consistency. However, for physical grounding, a decomposition must preserve structures relevant to motion (articulation) and material properties. The objective is to develop a decomposition framework that prioritizes these physically meaningful abstractions to support accurate downstream physical inference.
2. **To achieve geometry-aware articulation grounding:** While large multimodal models can provide strong semantic priors about object functionality, they often struggle to accurately ground these high-level understandings into precise, geometrically valid 3D articulation parameters. The objective is to devise a strategy that combines semantic reasoning with geometric constraints to infer physically consistent articulation configurations robustly, especially under diverse and potentially ambiguous geometries.
3. **To implement simulation-driven consistency verification:** Automatically generated physical annotations, irrespective of their sophistication, may contain physical implausibilities or lead to unstable behaviors in simulation. A key objective is to establish a rigorous, automated verification framework that uses physics simulations to identify and filter out inconsistent annotations, thereby ensuring the generated assets are truly "simulation-ready" and physically consistent.

In addition to these methodological objectives, the work aims to construct large-scale, high-quality datasets and a unified model:
- **Dataset Construction:** To build UniPhys-40K, a large-scale, physically grounded dataset, and UniPhys-Bench, a carefully verified benchmark for evaluating unified physical grounding.
- **Model Development:** To propose UniPhysGen, a unified physical grounding model capable of jointly reasoning over articulation semantics and intrinsic physical properties under heterogeneous part decompositions, mitigating common biases found in existing models.

These objectives collectively aim to bridge the gap between visually appealing 3D content and functionally interactive, physically consistent simulation environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a comprehensive framework consisting of the UniPhys pipeline, two large-scale datasets (UniPhys-40K and UniPhys-Bench), and the UniPhysGen model.

**A. UniPhys Pipeline (Automated Physical Grounding)**
UniPhys is a scalable, automated pipeline designed to transform diverse 3D meshes into simulation-ready assets with unified articulation and intrinsic physical property semantics. It comprises four stages:

1. **Physically Meaningful Structural Decomposition:**
 - This stage focuses on generating part decompositions that are relevant for physical interactions. It leverages perceptual grouping priors by lifting multi-view segmentations from the Segment Anything Model (SAM) into mesh space. These lifted masks guide the estimation of an initial part cardinality prior (K).
 - PartField (a geometry-aware decomposition method) generates candidate decompositions around K. These candidates are then aligned with the perceptual priors using Hungarian matching to select the best-fitting decomposition.
 - Finally, the decomposition is refined by applying Apriori-based frequent itemset mining on cross-view component groupings, identifying stable merge patterns to reduce over-segmentation while preserving motion- and material-sensitive structures.

2. **Intrinsic Physical Property Grounding:**
 - After decomposition, this stage infers physical properties for each part and the entire object. It creates "context-preserving part-centric observations," where the target part is rendered with its original texture, and the rest of the object serves as a semi-transparent contextual reference.
 - These observations are organized into a unified part atlas and fed into a Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM), specifically GPT-5. The LVLM, guided by a structured JSON prompt, jointly predicts part-level properties (e.g., material, density, friction, affordance) and object-level properties (e.g., scale, mass) based on geometric, contextual, and semantic cues.

3. **Geometry-aware Articulation Grounding:**
 - This stage addresses the challenge of accurately predicting 3D articulation parameters. Instead of direct regression, UniPhys generates a set of geometrically constrained articulation hypotheses (e.g., candidate axes and pivots) from local contact geometry and neighboring relations.
 - For axes, it samples in-plane directions from a fitted motion plane and includes global coordinate axes, resulting in ten candidates. For revolute joints, it clusters contact points to generate six pivot candidates.
 - These candidates are rendered as visual-semantic hypotheses. The LVLM (GPT-5) then semantically selects the most plausible articulation configuration, combining geometric feasibility from the rendered candidates with semantic understanding from provided textual descriptions of part function and movement.

4. **Simulation-driven Consistency Verification:**
 - This final stage ensures the physical plausibility and simulation-readiness of the grounded annotations. It uses physics simulation (MuJoCo) to check for inconsistencies.
 - **Intrinsic physical property verification** covers four levels: (1) numerical feasibility and material-specific priors, (2) intra-part physical consistency (e.g., hardness-to-Young's modulus ratio), (3) structural consistency across parts (e.g., friction for graspable parts), and (4) object-level mass consistency (sum of part masses against global object mass).
 - **Articulation consistency verification** evaluates: (1) contact preservation during motion, (2) penetration stability to detect interpenetration, and (3) contact identity consistency to ensure the correct physical interface is maintained. Annotations failing these checks are either filtered or refined.

**B. UniPhys-40K and UniPhys-Bench Datasets**
- **UniPhys-40K:** A large-scale dataset comprising over 40,000 3D assets, automatically processed by the UniPhys pipeline. It aggregates data from diverse repositories like Objaverse, HSSD, 3D-FUTURE, ABO, and PartNet. It provides unified articulation semantics and intrinsic physical properties, covering a spectrum of object scales, categories, materials, and articulation patterns. Texture recovery efforts were applied to datasets like PartNet to enable material-aware property grounding.
- **UniPhys-Bench:** A high-quality benchmark of 1,927 articulated objects with 5,469 motion-relevant components. It was constructed through a human-in-the-loop process, where annotations from UniPhys were manually verified and corrected, ensuring physical consistency across heterogeneous assets. This benchmark serves for rigorous evaluation, featuring simulation-driven validation of annotations.

**C. UniPhysGen Model**
UniPhysGen is a unified physical grounding model that maps 3D input geometry to structured physical semantics (part-level intrinsic properties, articulation kinematics, articulation structure, and object-level intrinsic properties). It is built upon the Qwen3-1.7B LVLM backbone, with Sonata serving as the point cloud encoder. UniPhysGen operates directly on heterogeneous 3D asset decompositions without assuming canonicalized structures.

The model incorporates several innovations for robustness:

1. **Physical Semantic Alignment Pretraining:** This stage pretrains the model to learn a shared representation space by aligning local part geometry and global object context with structured physical semantic descriptions (part identity, functional semantics, articulation behavior, intrinsic properties). This unified representation supports robust physical reasoning across downstream tasks.

2. **Geometry-Robust Articulation Grounding:**
 - **SO(3) Augmentation:** To mitigate geometric shortcut learning and view-dependent bias, canonical z-axis rotation augmentation is replaced with arbitrary 3D rotations from SO(3). This compels the model to rely on motion-relevant geometric semantics.
 - **Spherical Articulation Axis Parameterization:** To stabilize axis estimation under arbitrary rotations, normalized articulation axes are represented using spherical coordinates (polar and azimuth angles) instead of direct Cartesian vectors. This removes directional sign ambiguity and provides a smoother, continuous angular representation space.
 - **Unified Global Positional Encoding:** A shared global voxel coordinate system is introduced for both part-level and object-level point clouds. This preserves global spatial correspondence, which significantly improves pivot localization accuracy under heterogeneous decompositions.

3. **Articulation Structure Grounding:** UniPhysGen predicts motion-dependent part groups by reasoning over part identities, geometric locations, and global object context. This allows it to capture articulation-aware structural dependencies without relying on predefined hierarchies or templates.

4. **Object-Level Physical Grounding:** The model performs holistic object-level physical reasoning, predicting global properties such as object category, dimensions, and mass. This task leverages the shared pretrained physical semantic representation to maintain consistency across different semantic granularities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the capabilities of UniPhysGen across various tasks and evaluation settings.

**A. Unified Physical Grounding Results**
Quantitative evaluations on UniPhys-Bench indicated that UniPhysGen consistently outperformed prior methods across most metrics for both articulation semantics and intrinsic physical properties.
- **Articulation Semantics:** UniPhysGen showed advantages in articulation structure reasoning (e.g., 80.46% mIoU for articulation structure, compared to 79.61% for URDFormer), pivot localization (0.099 distance error, superior to 0.108 for Articulate-Anything), and motion limit estimation (84.95% mIoU, compared to 72.16% for Articulate-Anything). Joint type accuracy was competitive at 89.96%.
- **Intrinsic Physical Properties:** The model achieved superior performance in intrinsic physical property grounding, including density (0.438 ALDE, outperforming NeRF2Physics at 0.807), friction (0.062 MAE), and material classification (77.92% accuracy).
Qualitative results further illustrated that UniPhysGen produced more stable articulation behaviors and coherent physical property estimations compared to baselines. Geometry-centric methods like PARTICULATE were observed to be susceptible to geometric shortcuts, while Articulate-Anything sometimes produced physically inaccurate pivots and motion limits despite reasonable joint-type predictions.

**B. Robustness and Ablation Analysis**
- **Geometric Shortcut Mitigation:** Experiments comparing different rotation augmentations demonstrated the effectiveness of UniPhysGen's approach. Canonical Z-axis rotation led to a substantial performance degradation on challenging, rotated assets (50.09% joint type accuracy). Implementing SO(3) augmentation improved this to 80.53% but destabilized Cartesian axis prediction under arbitrary rotations. The combination of SO(3) augmentation with spherical axis parameterization provided a more balanced and robust axis estimation, maintaining performance across diverse test conditions.
- **Heterogeneous Part Decomposition Robustness:** UniPhysGen maintained strong articulation grounding performance even when parts were merged, leading to coarser decompositions. This indicated robustness to variations in part granularity and structural abstraction.
- **Global Positional Encoding:** The unified global positional encoding strategy significantly improved articulation grounding performance, notably reducing pivot error from 0.319 (with separate encodings) to 0.099. This highlighted the benefit of preserving global spatial correspondence for cross-scale geometric reasoning.
- **Image Modality Ablation:** While image features contributed to improvements in appearance-related properties (material classification, density, friction), their overall impact was limited. For properties dominated by 3D geometry and functionality (scale, mass, affordance), image features sometimes led to a slight performance degradation, suggesting that 3D geometry and structural-functional context remain the primary cues.
- **Model Scaling Analysis:** Scaling the model backbone from 0.6B to 1.7B consistently improved performance across both articulation semantics and intrinsic physical properties, particularly in structure reasoning and material-aware grounding. This indicated that larger models better supported unified geometric-semantic reasoning.

**C. Simulation-Based Embodied Interaction**
Qualitative validation in Isaac Sim demonstrated that assets grounded by UniPhysGen could be directly deployed and supported stable and physically consistent embodied interactions, such as drawer pulling, faucet turning, and laptop closing.

**D. PartNet-Mobility Results (Supplementary)**
On the standard PartNet-Mobility benchmark, UniPhysGen achieved competitive performance comparable to state-of-the-art methods like PARTICULATE, with superior motion limit estimation. Furthermore, UniPhysGen exhibited stronger orientation robustness under non-canonical rotations compared to PARTICULATE, reinforcing the benefits of its SO(3) augmentation and spherical axis parameterization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniPhys framework, encompassing the UniPhys pipeline, UniPhys-40K dataset, UniPhys-Bench benchmark, and UniPhysGen model, offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on related research fields and applications:

1. **Bridging the Reality-Simulation Gap:** The work directly addresses the challenge of making visually rich 3D assets functional for physics simulations and embodied AI. By enabling the automatic transformation of raw, heterogeneous 3D meshes into simulation-ready assets with unified physical semantics, it reduces a significant bottleneck in content creation for virtual environments.

2. **Scalable and Robust Physical Grounding:** The UniPhys pipeline provides an automated and scalable solution for annotating 3D assets, which is crucial given the vast and growing repositories of 3D models. UniPhysGen’s innovations, such as SO(3) augmentation and spherical axis parameterization, improve robustness against heterogeneous part decompositions and geometric shortcuts, which were limitations of prior methods. This enhances the reliability of physical grounding across diverse asset types.

3. **Unified and Consistent Semantics:** By jointly reasoning over articulation semantics and intrinsic physical properties, UniPhysGen ensures that these attributes are consistent with each other. This unified approach leads to more physically plausible motions and stable interactions in simulations, which is essential for realistic robotic tasks and embodied agent training.

4. **Advancing Benchmarking Standards:** The introduction of UniPhys-40K and the meticulously verified UniPhys-Bench benchmark provides a valuable resource for the research community. UniPhys-Bench, with its human-in-the-loop and simulation-driven verification processes, sets a higher standard for evaluating unified physical grounding, enabling more rigorous comparisons and fostering further development in the field.

5. **Enabling Advanced Embodied AI and Robotics:** The ability to generate simulation-ready assets that support stable and realistic physical interactions in environments like Isaac Sim directly benefits research in embodied AI, robot learning, and virtual prototyping. It allows for the training of agents in more dynamic and physically accurate virtual worlds, which can improve transferability to real-world scenarios.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for future research in several directions, including more advanced physically grounded 3D understanding, novel interaction paradigms for embodied agents, and the development of even more expressive and robust physical asset generation methods. The public availability of the code and dataset further facilitates community engagement and accelerates progress in these areas.

In summary, UniPhysGen represents a step towards creating a more seamless integration between visual 3D content and functional simulation environments, with broad implications for the development of intelligent systems capable of interacting with the physical world.
