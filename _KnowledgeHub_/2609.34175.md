# FailPatch: Failure Residual Patching for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FailPatch adapts a frozen VLA policy using both successful demonstrations and outcome-only failed deployment rollouts. A Null-gated Residual Expert Bank inserted in the action hidden space learns action-grounded patches from demonstrations, while a Preserve-Redirect-Trust objective decides when to keep the base policy or redirect failure-associated representations toward local success prototypes. With 0.52% trainable parameters, it gains 11.0 points on RoboTwin and 16.7 points on real robots.

## Problem

- Success-only supervised fine-tuning of VLA policies learns from demonstrations that rarely cover the deviated, failure-prone states reached when long-horizon execution errors compound across action chunks.
- Rollout-based RL methods exploit failures but require reward or value estimation, repeated on-policy environment interaction, heavy compute, and sometimes human expert corrections.
- Runtime failure detection and recovery systems add external detectors, supervisors, or replanners at deployment, and failed trajectories alone lack the corrective-action labels conventional supervised learning needs.

## Method

- **Null-gated Residual Expert Bank**: eight zero-initialized ==SwiGLU bottleneck experts== plus a parameter-free ==Null route==, inserted before the frozen action projection and selected by ==sparse Top-1 routing== at inference.
- Asymmetric supervision: demonstrations train experts through ==softmin-weighted candidate action losses== and a detached router target, while deployment chunks are split into reliable and failure-associated sets using offline GPT-5.5 ==failure-onset localization==.
- **Preserve-Redirect-Trust** objective: Preserve favors the Null route on reliable states, Redirect routes failure representations toward a 20-nearest-neighbor median ==success prototype== with a margin, and Trust bounds patch magnitude.

## Results

- On **four** long-horizon RoboTwin tasks with pi0.5, clean success rises from **49.5%** to **60.5%**, beating Full SFT at **52.75%** and PPO at **55.75%** with **40.1x** fewer trainable parameters and **6x** faster training.
- Clean-to-random generalization improves from **28.0%** to **37.5%**; on a real dual-arm platform, average success climbs from **41.1%** to **57.8%** across block ranking, bowl stacking, and dishcloth folding.
- Ablations show Redirect alone adds **7.25 points** over Demo-Only REB; a single parameter-matched expert or removing Null drops to **55.75%** and **56.5%**; human-annotated onsets give **61.0%** versus **60.5%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Failed rollouts without corrective actions still carry useful supervision about when to intervene; separating how to act from when to trust extracts it without reinforcement learning.
> - Naively conditioning action learning on outcome labels hurts: episode-level outcome prompting fell below the base policy, so failure signals should gate interventions rather than shape actions directly.
> - Action-proximal hidden states linearly encode execution reliability better than visual features or predicted actions, making them a cheap interface for probing and patching frozen policies with localized, abstention-capable residuals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

In long-horizon robot manipulation, small action errors can accumulate. Successful demonstrations show a robot what to do, but often omit the states it enters after a grasp, placement, or other action starts to go wrong. Failed deployment rollouts reveal where trouble begins, but they do not supply the corrective actions that conventional supervised training needs.

FailPatch gives each data source a different job. Successful demonstrations teach candidate corrections. Deployment rollouts teach the system when to leave the original policy alone and when to intervene.

### 2. How failures become a training signal

Successful demonstrations contain action targets, and they train the candidate patches. Deployment rollouts contain terminal outcomes. An offline vision-language model (GPT-5.5) identifies failure onset in failed episodes. It also filters successful episodes for visually apparent intermediate deviations.

These judgments produce chunk labels:

- Failed chunks before onset count as reliable.
- Onset and later chunks count as failure-associated.
- Visually reliable successful chunks also count as reliable.

For each failure-associated representation, the method retrieves the 20 nearest task-specific reliable-success representations. Their coordinate-wise median serves as a local success prototype.

FailPatch inserts a router and eight residual experts just before the frozen VLA's action projection. This location is the action-hidden representation, meaning the internal features immediately before the action projection. The Null expert passes the original representation through, and a selected expert adds a residual patch.

Three training objectives shape the routing:

- **Preserve** favors Null and penalizes unwanted changes in reliable states.
- **Redirect** favors residuals that make margin-constrained progress toward a local reliable-success prototype.
- **Trust** penalizes the size of selected shifts.

At inference, sparse top-one routing either keeps the base representation or applies one patch. The offline labels and prototypes are discarded after training. Failed trajectories need no correction labels, and no additional failure detector or replanner is used during execution.

(B) Demonstration action supervision and the Preserve, Redirect, and Trust constraints shape a router and residual expert bank on the action-hidden representation. (C) Routing chooses Null/Keep or an expert patch before the frozen action head outputs an action.

### 3. What the comparisons establish

Success rate is the share of evaluation trials that meet the task's completion predicate. In clean simulation, the model is π0.5 and the tasks are four long-horizon RoboTwin tasks. Each task rate is measured over the same 100 environment seeds, and the average is the unweighted mean of the four rates.

On this average, FailPatch raises mean success from 49.50% for the 50-Task Base to 60.50%, an 11.00-percentage-point gain. The comparison uses the same adaptation demonstrations and evaluation seeds.

Two controls test whether the gain comes from the failure supervision rather than from merely training a residual bank:

- **Demo-Only REB** has the same architecture but no deployment-trajectory supervision. It averages 49.25%.
- **Outcome-Prompt SFT** uses rollout-derived labels directly in action learning. One variant assigns episode-level labels to every chunk, and the other uses temporally localized chunk-level labels. These variants average 40.75% and 46.00%, respectively.

These controlled comparisons support the authors' case for separating action supervision from reliability supervision. They do not prove that every alternative way of using failures would fail.

### 4. The boundary

FailPatch uses deployment failures without corrective-action labels. Demonstrations supply the action grounding, and rollout outcomes guide when a patch replaces the base behavior. Its main limitation is its dependence on offline VLM judgments of failure onset from RGB. The authors note that onset can be ambiguous when causes are occluded or depend on unobserved force or contact information.

Within those limits, the result reframes what failures can contribute: they can supervise *when to intervene* even when they cannot say *what action to take*.
