# ENCP: Episode-Normalized Conformal Prediction for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> ENCP (Episode-Normalized Conformal Prediction) is a post-training uncertainty wrapper for vision-and-language navigation that divides each step's nonconformity score by the policy's residual confidence and calibrates one maximum score per episode. This yields route-level coverage of the ground-truth action at every step with probability at least 1-alpha. Across four VLN policies and three scores on R2R and REVERIE, it meets all reported step-coverage targets, and its set size drives help-seeking.

## Problem

- Navigation success metrics and max-softmax values do not say whether a specific action is trustworthy, and neural policies are overconfident under deployment shift.
- Existing self-monitoring and help-request heads in VLN rely on empirical heuristics, giving no finite-sample guarantee tied to a user-chosen error rate.
- Standard split conformal prediction pools individual steps, but steps inside an episode are dependent and lengths vary, so pooled calibration collapses thresholds and undercovers.

## Method

- **Confidence-adjusted score**: any base score (==THR==, ==APS==, or ==RAPS==) is divided by one plus a residual-confidence weight, a parameter-free rule using one minus the max action probability.
- **Episode-level calibration**: each episode contributes a single number, the maximum normalized ground-truth score across its steps, so the ==conformal quantile== bounds every step simultaneously.
- **Learning-based weight variant**: a small two-layer network predicts the weight from ==entropy==, max probability, top-two gap, log action-set size, step index and alpha, fit on a disjoint split before calibration.

## Results

- ENCP keeps step coverage above one minus alpha for every backbone, score and alpha on R2R and REVERIE val-unseen, while pooled base scores undercover in every matching entry (for example **0.854** versus **0.965** at alpha equals **0.10**).
- Under exchangeable splits over **300** repetitions, mean trajectory coverage deviates from nominal by at most **0.0017** across six conditions and three alpha levels; seen-to-unseen coverage drops below target.
- Help-seeking on DUET lifts success from **71.2%** to **76.9%** at a **6.4%** ask rate, **91.1%** at **32.3%**, and **98.8%** at **68.7%**; learned weights enlarge sets (**6.83** to **7.63**) without consistent coverage gain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Choosing the calibration unit to match the dependence structure, an episode rather than a step, restores distribution-free guarantees without touching the underlying policy.
> - Calibrating the worst step per episode buys the strict simultaneous guarantee, but one hard step can inflate the threshold and therefore the prediction-set size.
> - Coverage alone is not enough: set size must be reported, and a size-based ask budget does not transfer across policies, datasets, or distribution shift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A navigation agent following the instruction “Pass the sofa; enter the second doorway” makes a chain of decisions. A wrong turn changes what the agent sees next, so a confident mistake can compound along the route. A model’s largest softmax probability is not itself a calibrated probability that its action is wrong. The paper’s schematic contrasts the intended route with a wrong turn and labels a policy probability of 0.82 as a high-confidence wrong action. The schematic illustrates the problem; it is not a measured result. The proposed method, ENCP, judges uncertainty over the whole route rather than one decision at a time. A broad set of possible actions can then become a reason for the agent to ask for help.

### 2. Calibrate the route, not the step

Conformal prediction turns model outputs into sets calibrated to a requested error level. A one-action set may let the agent proceed, while a broader one can prompt a query. The usual version calibrates single steps. Route steps are dependent because actions affect later observations, and routes can differ in length. Single-step coverage also does not imply that every action on the route is covered.

ENCP changes the calibration in two steps. First, it rescales each step’s nonconformity score, which is larger when an action fits the policy’s predictions less well. The parameter-free adjustment divides the score by $2-p_{\max}$, where $p_{\max}$ is the policy’s highest action probability at that step. This preserves the action ranking at a step, but it can admit more actions when the policy’s maximum probability is lower. Second, ENCP keeps the worst adjusted score from each complete calibration episode as that episode’s value. It then sets a threshold from those values and includes actions whose adjusted scores fall below it. In the static evaluations, the underlying navigation policy is not retrained or changed.

The guarantee assumes that complete calibration and test episodes are exchangeable, meaning their joint distribution is unchanged by permuting episode order. Under that premise, the parameter-free construction covers the ground-truth action at every step of a test episode with probability at least $1-\alpha$. This holds even when steps within an episode are dependent and episode lengths vary. The guarantee is marginal over episodes. It does not apply conditionally to a particular realized calibration set, and it does not hold under arbitrary building shift.

The closest predecessor is [[2307.01928|KnowNo, “Robots That Ask for Help”]]. KnowNo already calibrates multi-step planning at the sequence level using a worst-step confidence score. ENCP is best read as an extension of that approach to the variable-length episodes and changing action sets of vision-and-language navigation.

### 3. Coverage has a set-size cost

Step coverage is the mean, across episodes, of the fraction of steps whose ground-truth action is included. Reported set sizes are mean numbers of actions and include the argmax fallback. Coverage is computed from the raw set, and an empty raw set counts as uncovered.

One representative setting uses R2R validation-unseen with DUET and the THR score at $\alpha=0.10$, which gives a 0.90 target. There, the step-pooled base method has step coverage 0.854 and mean set size 2.6. ENCP has step coverage 0.965 and mean set size 6.7. Across R2R and REVERIE, ENCP meets all displayed step-coverage targets, while the corresponding base-CP entries under-cover.

Table I: Base and ENCP step coverage and mean set size at α = 0.10, 0.20, and 0.30, for R2R and REVERIE validation-unseen models with THR, APS, and RAPS scores. ENCP meets each displayed target, base CP falls below it, and ENCP’s sets are generally larger.

These are empirical step-coverage results on a seen-to-unseen evaluation. They are not evidence that the exchangeability-based route guarantee holds across that shift. This comparison also changes both episode-level calibration and confidence normalization, so it does not isolate their separate effects.

A second comparison tests whether learning the rescaling rule helps. At R2R, THR, and $\alpha=0.10$, learned weighting makes sets larger in every row without a consistent coverage gain. This finding is limited to the tested learned features, target, and calibration split. In that comparison, the parameter-free rule is the simpler tested choice.

Table II: Parameter-free versus learning-based weighting for DUET, HAMT, R-prev, and R-osc on R2R validation-unseen, THR, α = .10. The learned variant’s coverage is not uniformly better, and its set is larger in all four rows.

### 4. The contribution and its boundary

ENCP converts uncertainty about a changing sequence of decisions into a set-size signal that can prompt a handoff before a mistaken action propagates. Its route-level guarantee rests on exchangeable complete episodes. The step-coverage gains on unseen buildings are empirical, and they carry a cost in larger action sets.
