# Enhancing the Reasoning Ability of Multimodal Large Language Models via Mixed Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO) and a scalable automated pipeline for constructing multimodal reasoning preference data (MMPR) to improve the complex reasoning abilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The approach enabled InternVL2-8B-MPO to achieve 67.0% accuracy on MathVista, an 8.7-point gain, making its performance comparable to a 10 times larger model, and established new state-of-the-art results on MathVision.

## Problem

- Current open-source Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit substantial limitations in complex multimodal reasoning tasks, especially with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting.
- The supervised fine-tuning (SFT) paradigm for MLLMs suffers from a "distribution shift" during CoT inference, often leading to performance degradation compared to direct answers.
- A critical scarcity exists in high-quality, large-scale multimodal reasoning preference datasets, which are essential for advanced alignment techniques like Preference Optimization.

## Method

- Developed ==Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO)==, an algorithm that combines DPO (preference), BCO (quality), and SFT (generation) losses to effectively train MLLMs for enhanced reasoning.
- Created a scalable automated pipeline for constructing the ==MultiModal PReference dataset (MMPR)==, comprising approximately 3 million samples, including a novel ==Dropout Next-Token Prediction (DropoutNTP)== method for efficient negative sample generation.
- Integrated specialized Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods, such as background knowledge-based or visual content-based CoT, directly into the data generation process to ensure effective multimodal input integration into reasoning.

## Results

- MPO-trained InternVL2-8B-MPO achieved **67.0%** accuracy on MathVista, an **8.7-point** improvement over its baseline, demonstrating performance comparable to the 10 times larger InternVL2-76B.
- InternVL2-76B-MPO set a new state-of-the-art on the MathVision benchmark with **30.6%** accuracy and showed substantial gains across various other multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- The MPO approach consistently and significantly outperformed standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for reasoning tasks, effectively mitigating the detrimental CoT performance degradation observed in SFT-only models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preference Optimization (PO) techniques, when thoughtfully adapted, can fundamentally strengthen multimodal reasoning and Chain-of-Thought capabilities in MLLMs beyond just mitigating hallucinations.
> - An automated and efficient data generation pipeline like DropoutNTP can overcome the significant bottleneck of scarce multimodal reasoning preference data, making advanced MLLM alignment more feasible and cost-effective.
> - A mixed objective function, MPO, which strategically combines preference, quality, and generative losses, is crucial for simultaneously achieving accurate reasoning alignment and maintaining generative fluency in MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of multimodal artificial intelligence, specifically addressing a crucial limitation in the current generation of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): their constrained reasoning capabilities, particularly with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting.

The field of LLMs has witnessed remarkable success, largely attributed to a training paradigm involving extensive pre-training followed by supervised fine-tuning (SFT). This paradigm has been extended to MLLMs, leading to impressive performance in various multimodal tasks. However, as the paper points out, a significant gap remains in complex multimodal reasoning. Current open-source MLLMs often exhibit a "distribution shift" when attempting CoT reasoning. During SFT, models are trained via teacher forcing, predicting tokens based on ground-truth prefixes. In inference, however, models predict tokens based on their *own* previous outputs, which can deviate from the training distribution. This issue is exacerbated in CoT, where longer rationales magnify the distribution shift, often leading to a *decrease* in performance compared to direct answers, as demonstrated by InternVL2-8B on MathVista.

To tackle this, the authors draw inspiration from the success of Preference Optimization (PO) techniques (like DPO and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback, RLHF) in the Natural Language Processing (NLP) domain. In NLP, PO has been instrumental in aligning LLMs with human preferences, improving helpfulness, honesty, and harmlessness, and more recently, enhancing reasoning abilities. While RLHF and PO have been explored for MLLMs, their application has predominantly focused on mitigating hallucinations rather than systematically boosting complex reasoning. This paper explicitly fills this "under-explored" gap, presenting a "systematic study on using PO to strengthen the multimodal reasoning capabilities of MLLMs."

Furthermore, the work addresses the critical challenge of data scarcity. High-quality, large-scale multimodal *reasoning preference* datasets are rare and expensive to annotate. Existing multimodal preference datasets largely target hallucination issues and perceptual tasks, lacking the complexity required for reasoning-intensive domains like science or mathematics. By introducing an automated data construction pipeline, the authors contribute significantly to overcoming this data bottleneck, making advanced multimodal reasoning training more accessible.

In essence, this research pushes MLLM capabilities beyond mere perception and basic QA, towards more human-like, multi-step reasoning, by leveraging and adapting state-of-the-art alignment techniques from the NLP domain and creating the necessary multimodal infrastructure.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to significantly **enhance the multimodal reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**, with a particular emphasis on improving their performance when employing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

This overarching goal is driven by several critical motivations and observations:

1. **Limited Reasoning in Open-Source MLLMs:** Despite remarkable progress in MLLMs, open-source models still exhibit substantial limitations in complex reasoning tasks. As highlighted by the paper, models like InternVL2-8B perform worse when attempting CoT reasoning on benchmarks like MathVista compared to providing direct answers. This indicates that current training paradigms fall short of enabling robust, multi-step reasoning from multimodal inputs.
2. **The "Distribution Shift" Problem from Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The prevailing pre-training and SFT paradigm for MLLMs suffers from a distribution shift between training and inference. During SFT, models are trained on ground-truth sequences, but at inference, they must generate sequences autoregressively based on their own potentially error-prone outputs. This discrepancy becomes more pronounced and detrimental when generating longer rationales required by CoT, leading to performance degradation. The motivation is to mitigate this shift and enable more reliable self-generated reasoning.
3. **Scarcity of Multimodal Reasoning Preference Data:** A major practical hurdle for applying advanced alignment techniques like Preference Optimization (PO) to multimodal reasoning is the lack of suitable training data. Existing multimodal preference datasets are primarily focused on reducing hallucinations or improving natural image understanding, not on complex, step-by-step reasoning. Manual annotation of reasoning preference data (comparing different reasoning processes) is prohibitively time-consuming and costly, necessitating an automated solution.
4. **Under-Explored Potential of Preference Optimization for Multimodal Reasoning:** While PO has shown immense success in NLP for improving reasoning and alignment, its application to enhancing *multimodal reasoning* in MLLMs remains largely unexplored. Previous applications in MLLMs have largely focused on addressing hallucination. The authors are motivated to conduct a systematic study to demonstrate the broader applicability of PO for strengthening MLLM reasoning abilities.
5. **Desire for More Capable and Efficient MLLMs:** The ultimate motivation is to develop MLLMs that can perform complex reasoning tasks effectively and efficiently. The goal is not only to improve larger models but also to enable smaller models to achieve performance comparable to much larger ones through sophisticated fine-tuning, thereby democratizing access to powerful MLLM capabilities.

By addressing these challenges, the authors aim to inspire further advancements in MLLMs, pushing them towards a higher level of intelligence where they can robustly understand and reason across diverse multimodal information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a comprehensive two-pronged approach, tackling both data and model aspects, to enhance the multimodal reasoning abilities of MLLMs:

#### 3.1. Data-Side: Scalable Multimodal Preference Dataset Generation (MMPR)

To overcome the scarcity and high cost of multimodal reasoning preference data, the paper introduces an automated pipeline to construct a large-scale dataset named **MMPR (MultiModal PReference dataset)**, comprising approximately 3 million samples. Each sample consists of an image, an instruction, a chosen (preferable) response, and a rejected (less preferable) response. The pipeline differentiates between two types of instructions:

- **For instructions with clear ground truths:** For tasks where a definitive correct answer exists, the model is prompted to provide a reasoning process followed by a "Final Answer: `***`" format. Responses that match the ground truth are labeled as chosen responses ($\text{y}_c$), while those that do not match (or fail to provide a clear answer) are labeled as rejected responses ($\text{y}_r$). Preference pairs are then formed by pairing a correct $\text{y}_c$ with an incorrect $\text{y}_r$.
- **For instructions without clear ground truths:** For open-ended questions where a single ground truth answer is not easily defined, the authors propose **Dropout Next-Token Prediction (DropoutNTP)**.
 1. An initial instruction model generates a response `y` given the image and instruction. This `y` is designated as the chosen response ($\text{y}_c$).
 2. `y` is then truncated by half, leaving a prefix `y<j`.
 3. The model is prompted to complete `y<j` *without access to the image input*. This completion, combined with `y<j`, forms the rejected response ($\text{y}_r = [\text{y<j}, \text{~y>=j}] $).
 4. The rationale is that generating a continuation without the visual context will naturally introduce more hallucinations and lower-quality content compared to the original, visually-grounded response, thus creating a valid preference signal.
- **Efficiency and Diversity:** DropoutNTP is shown to be significantly more efficient than previous methods like RLAIF-V's divide-and-conquer, consuming fewer tokens per preference pair. The MMPR dataset is built from diverse sources, including general VQA, science, chart, mathematics, OCR, and document tasks, ensuring broad coverage of multimodal reasoning challenges.

#### 3.2. Model-Side: Improved Multimodal Large Language Model with Preference Optimization (MPO)

The core of the model-side approach is **Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO)**, an algorithm designed to leverage the generated MMPR dataset effectively. MPO aims to enable the MLLM to learn not only the relative preference between responses but also the absolute quality of individual responses and the process for generating preferred ones. This is achieved through a combination of three distinct loss functions:

- **Training Objective:** $L = w_p L_p + w_q L_q + w_g L_g$
 - **Preference Loss ($\text{L}_p$): Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** This loss trains the model to maximize the likelihood of chosen responses over rejected responses, directly learning from preference pairs without the need for an explicit reward model. It's based on the Bradley-Terry model and helps align the model with desired reasoning patterns.
 - **Quality Loss ($\text{L}_q$): Binary Classifier Optimization (BCO):** This component helps the model understand the "absolute quality" of individual responses. It trains a binary classifier where chosen responses are mapped to a high reward (e.g., 1) and rejected responses to a low reward (e.g., 0). This ensures the model internalizes the notion of a "good" versus "bad" response.
 - **Generation Loss ($\text{L}_g$): Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Loss:** This is applied to the chosen responses and is crucial for teaching the model the *process* of generating high-quality outputs. While SFT alone can introduce distribution shifts, its inclusion in MPO (with a distinct purpose of maintaining absolute generation quality and preventing model collapse) helps ensure that the model can fluently produce preferred responses, balancing the discriminative nature of preference losses with generative fluency.
- **Chain-of-Thought with Multimodal Input:** During the data sampling process for MMPR, the authors incorporate specialized CoT methods tailored for multimodal contexts. This includes:
 - **Background Knowledge-based CoT:** For science problems, guiding the model to introduce relevant background knowledge before reasoning.
 - **Visual Content-based CoT:** For chart, OCR, and document analysis, prompting the model to explicitly analyze visual elements before reasoning.
 - **Grounded CoT:** For general VQA, requiring the model to link referenced objects in its response to specific regions in the image.
 These methods enrich the training data, ensure effective integration of multimodal information into reasoning, and enhance data diversity.

The MPO algorithm, combined with the large-scale, high-quality MMPR dataset, forms a robust framework for significantly improving the multimodal reasoning and CoT performance of MLLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO) and the MMPR dataset in enhancing MLLM reasoning capabilities.

- **Significant Improvement in Multimodal Reasoning:**
 - MPO-trained models, InternVL2-8B-MPO and InternVL2-76B-MPO, demonstrate superior performance across a wide array of multimodal reasoning benchmarks, including M3CoT, MMMU, MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse (Vision-Only), WeMath, and LogicVista.
 - **MathVista Breakthrough:** InternVL2-8B-MPO achieves an accuracy of 67.0% on MathVista, marking an impressive 8.7-point improvement over its base model, InternVL2-8B. Crucially, this performance is comparable to the 10 times larger InternVL2-76B, highlighting MPO's efficiency and ability to boost smaller models.
 - **State-of-the-Art on MathVision:** InternVL2-76B-MPO sets a new state-of-the-art performance on the MathVision benchmark with 30.6% accuracy.
- **Enhanced General Abilities and Hallucination Mitigation:**
 - Beyond reasoning, the MPO-trained models also show improved performance on general Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmarks like MMVet and reduced hallucinations on MMHalBench. This indicates that the reasoning enhancements generalize to broader capabilities and lead to more trustworthy outputs.
- **Scalability of MPO:** The improvements are not limited to smaller models; InternVL2-76B-MPO shows a *more significant* performance gain over its pre-MPO counterpart compared to the 8B model's improvement. This demonstrates that MPO is effective and scalable across different model sizes within the InternVL2 family.
- **MPO Outperforms Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Reasoning:**
 - An ablation study directly comparing MPO with SFT (using the same chosen responses from MMPR as SFT data) reveals that MPO consistently and substantially outperforms SFT. For instance, on M3CoT, the MPO-trained model achieves 79.2%, an 11.4-point lead over the SFT counterpart.
 - Crucially, SFT-trained models still exhibit the detrimental effect of CoT reasoning (performance degradation with CoT responses compared to direct answers) on benchmarks like MMVet and POPE, while MPO models do not. This confirms that SFT alone is insufficient to address the distribution shift issues hindering multimodal CoT.
- **Effectiveness and Efficiency of DropoutNTP:**
 - The proposed DropoutNTP method for generating negative samples for open-ended questions proves effective. It achieves comparable performance in reducing hallucinations (e.g., on Object HalBench and MMHal-Bench) to the more complex RLAIF-V method, but with significantly higher efficiency (57.5% of RLAIF-V's token cost). DropoutNTP also outperforms other data construction pipelines like SeVa, POVID, and STIC.
- **Importance of Mixed Loss and Frozen Reference Model:**
 - Extensive ablation studies on various preference optimization algorithms reveal the critical role of combining preference loss with SFT loss for improving CoT reasoning. Many DPO variants struggled to enhance CoT performance without the SFT component.
 - The studies also indicate that maintaining a frozen reference model during training (as in DPO and BCO, which constitute MPO) is crucial for enhancing overall reasoning abilities, outperforming methods that update the reference model (like TR-DPO) or are reference-model-free (like ORPO).
- **Positive Impact on Text-only Performance:**
 - Despite being trained exclusively on multimodal data, the MPO-trained model shows an *improvement* in average performance on several text-only benchmarks. Notable gains are observed in complex science reasoning (TheoremQA, +5.2 points over baseline) and instruction following (IFEval, +4.1 points), suggesting that improved multimodal reasoning and alignment generalize to enhanced logical understanding and adherence to instructions in text-only contexts.

In summary, the main findings strongly support MPO as a highly effective and efficient method for enhancing multimodal reasoning, especially for CoT, by strategically combining preference, quality, and generative losses and leveraging a novel, scalable data generation pipeline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the development of capable Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with broad implications for the AI community and various application domains.

- **Elevating MLLM Reasoning Capabilities:** The most immediate and significant impact is the substantial improvement in the multimodal reasoning abilities of MLLMs, particularly their capacity for effective Chain-of-Thought (CoT) processing. By addressing the "distribution shift" issue that plagues SFT-based MLLMs, this work enables models to generate more reliable, detailed, and accurate rationales, moving beyond shallow pattern recognition to deeper, multi-step comprehension. This is crucial for applications requiring complex problem-solving, such as scientific discovery, medical diagnosis, and intricate data analysis.
- **Democratizing High-Performance MLLMs:** The fact that InternVL2-8B-MPO can achieve reasoning performance comparable to the 10x larger InternVL2-76B is a game-changer. This demonstrates that intelligent fine-tuning strategies can bridge the performance gap between models of vastly different sizes, making high-performance MLLMs more accessible. Smaller, yet highly capable, models require less computational power for training and inference, lowering barriers to entry for researchers and developers and enabling wider deployment in resource-constrained environments (e.g., edge devices).
- **Novel and Efficient Data Generation:** The introduction of the automated preference data construction pipeline and the DropoutNTP method is a major contribution. It tackles the critical bottleneck of data scarcity and high annotation costs for multimodal reasoning tasks. This scalable approach allows for the creation of high-quality preference datasets (like MMPR) at a controlled cost, which is vital for the continuous improvement and scaling of MLLMs. The release of this dataset empowers future research by providing a rich resource for multimodal alignment.
- **Refined Preference Optimization for Multimodality:** The Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO) algorithm provides a robust and effective framework for aligning MLLMs. By thoughtfully combining preference, quality, and generation losses, MPO addresses the complexities of multimodal output generation, preventing issues like repetitive responses while fostering both accuracy and fluency. The detailed ablation studies offer valuable insights into the mechanics of PO in multimodal contexts, guiding future research in this area.
- **Broadened Scope of AI Alignment:** This study successfully demonstrates that preference optimization can achieve more than just reducing hallucinations in MLLMs; it can fundamentally strengthen core reasoning capabilities. This expands our understanding of AI alignment techniques and their potential to imbue models with more sophisticated cognitive functions. The observed spillover effect into improved text-only reasoning and instruction following further underscores the holistic benefits of MPO.
- **Open-Source Catalyst:** The commitment to releasing the code, data, and models is highly significant. This open-source contribution will accelerate research and development within the MLLM community, allowing others to build upon these advancements, validate findings, and explore new directions, fostering a collaborative ecosystem for multimodal AI innovation.

In conclusion, this work represents a pivotal advancement in MLLM research, pushing the boundaries of multimodal reasoning and offering practical, scalable solutions for model training and data generation. Its impact is likely to resonate across both academic and industrial landscapes, paving the way for more intelligent, efficient, and trustworthy multimodal AI systems.
