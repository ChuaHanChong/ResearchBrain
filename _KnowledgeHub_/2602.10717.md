# Say, Dream, and Act: Learning Video World Models for Instruction-Driven Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Say, Dream, and Act" framework from Fudan University introduces a system for robot manipulation that uses an accelerated, high-fidelity video world model to anticipate future states and generate long-horizon, spatially consistent action plans. The method achieves a 98.1% total success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, significantly improving performance on instruction-driven, long-horizon tasks by predicting outcomes in real-time.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems often lack the ability to anticipate environmental changes and action consequences, leading to errors in complex, dynamic tasks.
- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) provide high-level guidance but struggle with precise low-level control due to limited spatial reasoning and inability to explicitly model action outcomes.
- Current video world models are limited by short prediction horizons, spatial inconsistencies, and high computational costs, making them unsuitable for real-time, long-horizon robotic control.

## Method

- A high-fidelity ==video world model== based on ==COSMOS-PREDICT2== is developed, adapted to the robotic domain and accelerated via ==adversarial distillation== for few-step inference.
- A =="length-agnostic" imagination== mechanism compresses long execution trajectories into a fixed number of ==keyframes==, allowing the world model to efficiently predict entire task progressions.
- An =="in-context conditioned" action model== integrates imagined trajectories with real-time observations, enabling continuous ==error correction== and robust grounding of actions.

## Results

- The distilled and domain-adapted world model significantly improved video generation quality (e.g., FVD from **571.46** to **211.84** for Cosmos-2B) and maintained strong spatial referring abilities.
- The "Say, Dream, and Act" framework achieved a state-of-the-art **98.1%** total success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming existing VLA models across various generalization settings.
- The framework demonstrated particular strength in long-horizon tasks, achieving a **95.4%** success rate on LIBERO-Long, enabled by its efficient and accurate anticipatory capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity video generation models can be effectively distilled and domain-adapted for real-time robotic applications, significantly reducing inference latency without sacrificing prediction quality.
> - Robotic manipulation tasks often do not require high-frequency, frame-by-frame predictions, allowing for "length-agnostic" imagination to provide efficient, long-horizon foresight.
> - Integrating imagined future states as "in-context examples" with real observations allows action policies to robustly compensate for minor prediction inaccuracies, enhancing closed-loop control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Say, Dream, and Act" addresses critical limitations within the rapidly evolving landscape of robot manipulation, particularly at the intersection of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), world models, and generative AI.

**Challenges in Current Robotic Systems:**
Historically, robotic manipulation systems have struggled with anticipating environmental changes in response to actions, leading to errors and inefficiencies. This paper identifies a core gap: while humans naturally predict object motion and environmental dynamics, most robotic systems lack this predictive foresight, hindering precise, long-horizon control.

**Limitations of Existing Paradigms:**
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** Recent VLMs (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA) have shown promise in high-level decision-making and conditioning actions on instructions. However, the authors point out that VLMs often fall short in producing accurate low-level actions dueability to limited spatial reasoning, slow inference, and, crucially, their inability to explicitly model the *consequences* of actions. They provide high-level guidance but not predictive visual feedback.
- **World Models:** World models aim to predict future observations, providing rich visual context for action models. The paper highlights three major limitations of current world models:
 1. **Limited temporal horizon:** Many models predict only short sequences, requiring repeated, computationally expensive rollouts for long-horizon tasks (e.g., UniPi, Seer, GVF-TAPE).
 2. **Poor spatial consistency:** Predictions often suffer from distortions or inconsistent robot embodiments, compromising accuracy for precise manipulation.
 3. **High computational cost:** While large video generation models (e.g., diffusion models like COSMOS-PREDICT2) offer high predictive quality, their iterative nature makes real-time deployment challenging for "in-time" applications.

**Contribution to the Landscape:**
"Say, Dream, and Act" positions itself as a unified framework that overcomes these limitations by integrating a high-fidelity, *accelerated* video world model with an *in-context conditioned* action model. It aims to bridge the gap between high-quality video generation (often too slow for real-time control) and the need for accurate, long-horizon predictive capabilities in robotics. By leveraging adversarial distillation for fast video generation and a novel length-agnostic imagination mechanism, the work significantly advances the state-of-the-art in predictive robot control. It moves beyond using world models merely for data generation (e.g., DreamGen) or short-term predictions, instead enabling rapid, spatially consistent, and long-horizon imagination directly integrated into the action policy. The "in-context conditioned action model" also refines the use of world model outputs, recognizing that even the best models might have imperfections and providing a mechanism for the robot to ground its actions in real observations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind "Say, Dream, and Act" is to enable robots to **anticipate and reason about future states in complex, dynamic environments** for precise, long-horizon manipulation tasks. The authors aim to address several specific challenges that hinder current robotic manipulation systems:

1. **Lack of Predictive Capability:** The fundamental problem is that most existing robotic systems lack the ability to forecast how the environment will evolve in response to actions. This deficiency leads to errors, inefficiency, and unsafe behavior in real-world scenarios.
2. **Limitations of VLMs for Low-Level Control:** While VLMs provide high-level guidance, their inability to explicitly model action consequences, combined with slow inference and limited spatial reasoning, makes them insufficient for precise, low-level robotic control.
3. **Short-Horizon and Spatially Inconsistent World Models:** Current world models often predict only short sequences of future frames, necessitating repeated rollouts for long-horizon tasks, which is inefficient. Moreover, their predictions frequently suffer from spatial inconsistencies or distortions (e.g., inconsistent robot embodiments), reducing their utility for accurate manipulation.
4. **High Computational Cost of High-Quality Video Generation:** Advanced diffusion-based video generation models can produce high-quality predictions but are computationally expensive due to iterative denoising, rendering them impractical for real-time robotic deployment.

To tackle these motivations, the key objectives of the "Say, Dream, and Act" framework are:

- **Develop a Robust and High-Fidelity Video World Model:** The primary goal is to create a world model that can reliably predict future states, ensuring temporal coherence, spatial accuracy, and consistent robot embodiment over long horizons.
- **Enable Fast, Efficient Video Prediction:** The framework seeks to significantly accelerate the inference speed of high-quality video generation models, making them suitable for real-time robot control without sacrificing prediction fidelity.
- **Facilitate Length-Agnostic Imagination:** To overcome the short-horizon limitation, the objective is to allow the world model to imagine entire execution trajectories, independent of specific low-level action step counts, providing holistic future context.
- **Integrate Predictive Foresight with Action Generation:** The system aims to combine the enhanced world model with a robust action model that can leverage both imagined trajectories and real observations to produce accurate, executable actions, even in the presence of minor world model imperfections.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance in Instruction-Driven Manipulation:** Ultimately, the framework aims to demonstrate significant improvements in metrics critical for robotic manipulation, such as embodiment consistency, spatial referring ability, and task completion, especially for long-horizon tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Say, Dream, and Act" framework is structured into three distinct yet integrated stages: **Say** (World Model), **Dream** (Length-Agnostic World Imagination), and **Act** (In-Context Conditioned Action Model).

#### 3.1. Say: World Model

This stage focuses on developing a robust, high-fidelity, and efficient video-based world model.

- **Selection:** The authors first benchmarked several state-of-the-art video generation models (e.g., Cosmos-2B, Cosmos-14B, Cosmos-Droid, Wan-14B) on metrics crucial for embodied decision-making, including embodiment consistency, task completion rate, and spatial referring ability. **COSMOS-PREDICT2** was selected as the backbone due to its strong performance in preliminary evaluations.
- **Domain Adaptation and Distillation:** To make the selected world model reliable for robotic manipulation and computationally efficient, two key techniques are applied:
 - **Domain Adaptation:** Even strong general-purpose video models can exhibit spatial inconsistencies in novel robotic scenes, camera viewpoints, or embodiments. Domain adaptation fine-tunes the world model to align its predictions with the target robotic domain (e.g., specific robot arm, camera setup). This is achieved by incorporating a reconstruction loss (L_rec) from the original COSMOS-PREDICT2 objective, applied to noisy latents during training to preserve the generative prior while adapting to new data distributions.
 - **Adversarial Distillation for Fast Generation:** To reduce the computational cost of iterative denoising inherent in diffusion models, the adapted model is distilled. This involves training a lightweight discriminator (D) initialized from the pretrained diffusion transformer (DiT) weights, augmented with 3D convolutional heads. The discriminator encourages the generator (G) to produce high-fidelity predictions in fewer denoising steps.
 - **Denoising Formulation:** The standard diffusion process predicts a denoised latent (x̂₀) from a noisy input (x_t) conditioned on auxiliary information.
 - **Noise Schedule for Few-Step Distillation:** A specific noise schedule is employed, sampling noise levels (σ_t) from a discrete set tailored to the target number of denoising steps (T_g, typically 8 in experiments), which significantly reduces inference time compared to the original model's 35 steps.
 - **Latent Adversarial Distillation:** An adversarial loss (L_D_adv for discriminator, L_G_adv for generator) is introduced. The discriminator is trained to distinguish between clean latents (x₀) and denoised predictions (x̂₀), both diffused with auxiliary noise (σ'), making it more robust. The generator is optimized to fool the discriminator.
 - **Combined Objective:** The generator's objective combines the adversarial loss (λ * L_G_adv) with the reconstruction loss (L_rec), balancing high-quality, fast generation with domain-specific fidelity.

#### 3.2. Dream: Length-Agnostic World Imagination

This stage enables the world model to generate long-horizon video predictions efficiently, independent of the exact number of low-level action steps.

- **Key Insight:** Robotic manipulation often doesn't require a video frame for every single action step; generating high-frequency frames is redundant and computationally expensive. The authors propose that manipulation is a "low-frequency prediction problem."
- **Trajectory Compression:** Arbitrary-length execution trajectories (T frames) are compressed into a fixed number of keyframes (n ≪ T, typically n=93) using uniform temporal sampling. This means the model learns to generate a representative sequence of frames that captures the overall progression of the task rather than rigidly adhering to a per-action frame rate.
- **Frame-Rate-Agnostic Video Generation:** The video diffusion model is fine-tuned to learn this frame-rate-agnostic capability, allowing it to holistically imagine the entire execution process and anticipate long-horizon outcomes without computational overhead tied to trajectory length.

#### 3.3. Act: In-Context Conditioned Action Model

This final stage integrates the imagined trajectories with real-world observations to produce robust robot actions.

- **Addressing Spatial Inaccuracies:** The distilled world model, while accurate, might still exhibit occasional spatial inaccuracies. The action model is designed to compensate for these.
- **In-Context Conditioning:** The action model treats the imagined trajectories as "in-context examples" rather than strict commands. This is crucial because it allows the model to leverage the rich predictive context while still grounding its actions in real-time sensory input.
- **Model Architecture:** A transformer-based backbone, inherited from existing action models like ACT, is used. This model receives both the generated future frames (from the "Dream" stage) and real historical observations.
- **Error Correction and Grounding:** By consuming both imagined trajectories and real observations, the action model can continuously correct for spatial errors present in the generated video, ensuring that the actual robot movements are aligned with the real environment. It acts like a "next-token predictor," using the imagined trajectory as a demonstration to guide real-world execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the "Say, Dream, and Act" framework significantly improves robotic manipulation by leveraging fast, high-fidelity video world models.

#### 4.1. Zero-shot Spatial Referring Abilities (World Model Evaluation)

- **Metrics:** Embodiment Consistency (EC), Referring Success Rate (RSR), Interaction Success Rate (ISR), and Task Completion Rate (TCR) were used to evaluate how well video generation models predict robot embodiment and task semantics.
- **Base Model Performance (Table 2, Figure 3):**
 - Original Cosmos2 series models (2B and 14B) showed strong spatial referring and manipulation abilities, outperforming other baselines.
 - Cosmos-14B-Droid, despite fine-tuning on robotic data, performed worse due to a domain gap (multiview training mismatching single-view test setup), highlighting the need for targeted domain adaptation.
 - Wan-14B performed poorly, struggling with complex instructions and robot embodiment.
- **Qualitative Observations (Figure 3):** General-purpose models often failed to capture gripper structures, camera poses, or correct action reasoning. Domain-adapted models produced predictions consistent with the true robot configuration.

#### 4.2. Generation Quality among Video Models (Table 1)

- **Raw Cosmos vs. Wan:** Raw Cosmos models generally outperformed the Wan model across metrics (FVD, SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS).
- **Impact of Domain Adaptation (C-2B-DA):** Domain adaptation led to substantial improvements in generation quality. For Cosmos-2B, FVD dropped from 571.46 to 211.84, while SSIM increased from 0.70 to 0.82, indicating enhanced temporal coherence and fidelity. This confirms the effectiveness of aligning the model with the specific robotic domain.
- **Cosmos-14B-Droid-F.T. Degradation:** The specialized Droid model performed worse than the base Cosmos models (FVD 1098.24, SSIM 0.23), reinforcing the observation that naive fine-tuning can hurt performance if the observation setup is mismatched.
- **Domain Adaptation + Distillation (C-2B-DA+Dis):** Combining domain adaptation with latent adversarial distillation maintained strong performance (SSIM slightly improved to 0.84, PSNR increased to 26.82, with a modest FVD increase to 238.09) while enabling efficient few-step inference. This demonstrates a successful trade-off for significant computational gains.
- **Consistency across LIBERO tasks (Table 1a):** The adapted Cosmos-2B model showed consistent performance across different LIBERO task suites (L-10, L-Object, L-Spatial, L-Goal), suggesting good generalization.

#### 4.3. Close-Loop Policy Evaluation (Table 3, Figure 4)

- **World Model Generation (Figure 4):** The model successfully predicted entire task execution processes, generating complete sequences of robot manipulation frames, including long-horizon tasks like picking up multiple objects.
- **Overall Policy Performance (Table 3):** Dream4manip consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance on the LIBERO benchmark across all generalization settings:
 - **Total Success Rate:** 98.1%, the highest among all compared VLA models, even using a moderate-sized backbone (1.22B parameters) compared to some larger VLA models.
 - **LIBERO-Spatial:** 99.4% success rate, indicating strong robustness to spatial variations.
 - **LIBERO-Object:** 99.2% success rate, showing effective object-level generalization.
 - **LIBERO-Goal:** 98.2% success rate, demonstrating the benefit of imagination for multi-stage reasoning.
 - **LIBERO-Long:** 95.4% success rate, confirming its strength in challenging long-horizon tasks.
- These results highlight that integrating a high-quality, fast, video-based world model significantly improves closed-loop control across diverse manipulation tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation of Denoising Steps (Table 4)

- The study showed a clear trade-off between video quality and generation speed.
- Increasing denoising steps from 1 to 10 sharply improved SSIM and PSNR while sharply decreasing FVD and LPIPS.
- Beyond 10 steps, improvements slowed and metrics stabilized, suggesting that **10 denoising steps offered a good balance** between quality and speed for the Cosmos2 2B model after distillation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Say, Dream, and Act" framework represents a significant advancement in robot manipulation, particularly for instruction-driven, long-horizon tasks in complex environments. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

1. **Enabling Anticipatory Robots:** By providing robots with a robust, fast, and high-fidelity video world model, the framework allows them to "dream" about future outcomes before "acting." This predictive capability is crucial for anticipating environmental changes, object motions, and the consequences of their actions, leading to more intelligent and adaptive robotic behavior.
2. **Overcoming VLM and Traditional World Model Limitations:** The work effectively addresses the shortcomings of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by providing the explicit action consequence modeling they lack. It also mitigates the issues of short temporal horizons and spatial inconsistencies prevalent in earlier world models, while resolving the computational bottleneck of large video generation models through adversarial distillation.
3. **Improved Efficiency and Real-time Application:** The successful distillation of high-fidelity video generation models for few-step inference makes predictive world modeling practical for real-time robotic deployment. This significantly reduces latency and computational cost, which are critical for responsive and interactive robot control.
4. **Enhanced Manipulation Performance:** The state-of-the-art results on the challenging LIBERO benchmark, particularly in long-horizon tasks and across various generalization settings, underscore the practical utility of the framework. Improvements in Embodiment Consistency, Referring Success Rate, Interaction Success Rate, and Task Completion Rate directly translate to more reliable and precise robot manipulation.
5. **Robustness to Imperfect Predictions:** The "in-context conditioned action model" design is innovative in its ability to leverage imagined trajectories as guidance while simultaneously grounding actions in real observations. This allows the system to correct for potential minor errors in the world model's predictions, enhancing overall system robustness.
6. **Foundation for Generalist Robots:** The framework's ability to unify predictive world modeling and action reasoning into a single, fast policy lays important groundwork for developing more general-purpose, adaptive embodied intelligence. This moves towards robots that can anticipate, plan, and adapt effectively in novel and dynamic real-world scenarios.

**Potential Impact:**
The advancements presented in this paper have broad implications across various sectors:
- **Manufacturing and Logistics:** More reliable and efficient robotic manipulation can lead to significant automation improvements in factories, warehouses, and supply chains.
- **Service Robotics:** Enhanced predictive capabilities can enable robots to perform more complex tasks in domestic or public service environments, such as assistive robotics for the elderly or disabled.
- **Exploration and Hazardous Environments:** Robots with better foresight can operate more autonomously and safely in unpredictable or dangerous settings, like space exploration, disaster relief, or hazardous waste handling.

While the authors acknowledge remaining challenges such as scaling to diverse embodiments, managing error accumulation in very long-horizon predictions, and further optimizing for resource-constrained robots, "Say, Dream, and Act" offers a compelling path forward for creating more intelligent, anticipatory, and capable robotic systems.
