# TwinVLA: Data-Efficient Bimanual Manipulation with Twin Single-Arm Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> TWINVLA presents a framework for data-efficient bimanual robotic manipulation by composing two pre-trained single-arm Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This approach achieves performance comparable to or exceeding existing bimanual systems while drastically reducing the need for bimanual demonstration data and computational resources.

## Problem

- High-quality, large-scale datasets specifically for bimanual robotic manipulation are scarce, making it challenging to train effective policies.
- Current state-of-the-art bimanual VLA models rely on thousands of hours of proprietary bimanual demonstrations and immense computational resources for pre-training, limiting accessibility and reproducibility.
- Existing bimanual control methods often involve specialized architectures or monolithic VLA models, both of which struggle with data efficiency and generalization.

## Method

- The framework duplicates two instances of a pre-trained single-arm VLA (==SingleVLA==) and integrates them into a bimanual system, selectively replicating the VLM backbone while sharing the vision encoder and action head.
- A ==Joint Attention== mechanism enables efficient coordination between the two replicated VLM backbones, allowing cross-arm information flow via shared self-attention layers with a causal mask.
- Shared inputs (language, ego-centric image) are processed using a ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== mechanism, and attention re-weighting is applied to shared modality tokens to preserve pre-trained knowledge during fine-tuning.

## Results

- TWINVLA demonstrated superior data and compute efficiency, requiring **~50 episodes** of bimanual fine-tuning data and **~25 H100 GPU-days**, significantly less than baselines like RDT-1B (thousands of hours and GPU-days).
- On real-world bimanual tasks, TWINVLA achieved an average success rate of **76%** on the Anubis robot, outperforming RDT-1B (**45%**) and closely approaching π0 (**80%**), which uses vastly more resources.
- The system consistently outperformed RDT-1B in simulation benchmarks (e.g., **75.8%** vs **61.6%** on Tabletop-Sim Easy) and showed improved language following capabilities, while ablation studies confirmed the critical role of each architectural component.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex bimanual skills can be learned efficiently by leveraging and coordinating knowledge from pre-trained single-arm VLA models, rather than requiring extensive bimanual pre-training from scratch.
> - A modular architecture with lightweight coordination mechanisms (e.g., Joint Attention) offers a data- and compute-efficient alternative to monolithic VLA models for bimanual control.
> - Techniques like attention re-weighting and MoE integration are critical for effectively transferring and preserving pre-trained knowledge in a new, more complex architecture while optimizing resource usage.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has witnessed significant advancements with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which learn intricate robotic skills from vast datasets. These models, trained on large-scale robotic datasets, have demonstrated remarkable performance in single-arm manipulation tasks, exhibiting generalization across diverse objects, environments, and tasks. However, extending these successes to **bimanual manipulation** – tasks requiring coordinated control of two robotic arms – presents a substantial challenge.

The primary hurdle lies in data scarcity. While public datasets for single-arm manipulation are abundant (e.g., Open X-Embodiment Collaboration et al., 2024), high-quality, large-scale datasets specifically for bimanual tasks are rare. Existing state-of-the-art bimanual VLA models, such as RDT-1B (Liu et al., 2024) and π0 (Black et al., 2024), overcome this by relying on massive, often proprietary, datasets (thousands of hours of demonstrations) and immense computational resources for pre-training. This reliance severely limits reproducibility, accessibility, and further research progress for the wider community.

Current approaches to bimanual control broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Specialized Architectures:** These methods often introduce explicit inductive biases for arm interdependencies or hierarchical planning, sometimes limiting their generalizability across tasks or requiring specific backbone constraints (e.g., Anybimanual by Lu et al., 2025).
2. **Monolithic VLA Models:** This common strategy extends unimanual VLAs by training a single large model on a mixture of single-arm and bimanual data, typically using embodiment-specific decoders or shared zero-padded action spaces. While powerful, these models necessitate prohibitive bimanual data collection and pre-training costs, as exemplified by RDT-1B and π0.

TwinVLA carves out a novel path by proposing a **modular, data-efficient alternative** to these existing paradigms. Instead of attempting to learn bimanual coordination from scratch or through monolithic training on scarce bimanual data, it leverages the vast knowledge embedded in **pre-trained single-arm VLAs**. This approach is inspired by neuroscience, which suggests human bimanual control involves the coordination of arm-specific motor primitives rather than a single monolithic controller. By composing two instances of a single-arm VLA and integrating them through a lightweight coordination mechanism, TwinVLA aims to achieve high-performance bimanual manipulation with minimal bimanual data, thereby democratizing access to complex robotic capabilities and accelerating research in this challenging domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **develop a data-efficient framework for bimanual robotic manipulation** that significantly reduces the reliance on large, specialized bimanual datasets. The authors aim to demonstrate that complex bimanual skills can be learned by effectively repurposing and coordinating existing single-arm VLA models.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing Bimanual Data Scarcity:** Publicly available bimanual datasets are extremely limited, making it difficult for most research groups to train high-performing bimanual policies. The paper seeks to circumvent this bottleneck by leveraging abundant public single-arm data.
- **Improving Data and Compute Efficiency:** Traditional monolithic approaches to bimanual VLA training (e.g., RDT-1B, π0) demand thousands of hours of proprietary bimanual demonstrations and extensive computational resources (e.g., thousands of H100 GPU-days). TwinVLA is motivated to achieve comparable or superior performance with substantially less data and compute. Specifically, it aims to eliminate the need for *bimanual pre-training* entirely and only require a *small amount of bimanual fine-tuning data*.
- **Enhancing Reproducibility and Accessibility:** By relying primarily on publicly available single-arm datasets and a modular architecture, TwinVLA seeks to offer a more reproducible and accessible pathway for developing advanced bimanual manipulation capabilities, fostering broader participation in the field.
- **Leveraging Biological Inspiration:** The design is motivated by the human brain's coordination of two arms through dedicated neural circuits, suggesting that a modular, coordination-centric architecture might be more effective than a monolithic one for bimanual control.
- **Preserving Single-Arm VLA Capabilities:** The approach aims to transfer and preserve the strong generalization capabilities learned by single-arm VLAs during pre-training, ensuring that the bimanual policy benefits from this rich prior knowledge.

In essence, the research strives to establish a practical, scalable, and data-efficient paradigm for high-performance bimanual manipulation, shifting the focus from "more data" to "smarter data utilization and architectural design."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TwinVLA is a modular framework designed to compose two copies of a pre-trained single-arm VLA into a coordinated bimanual VLA. Its methodology is built upon three core principles: selective module duplication, cross-arm fusion via joint attention, and efficient shared representation through Mixture-of-Experts.

#### 3.1. SingleVLA: The Foundational Single-Arm Policy

The first step is to establish a strong single-arm VLA, named **SingleVLA**.
- **Backbone:** SingleVLA utilizes a lightweight, compact VLM backbone based on Eagle2-1B (0.8B parameters), chosen for its efficiency and strong performance, making it suitable for subsequent duplication without excessive model size.
- **Pre-training:** SingleVLA is pre-trained on a large-scale public single-arm dataset, an 800-hour subset of the Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset. This pre-training is crucial for acquiring generalizable knowledge across diverse environments and embodiments.
- **Action Space:** To facilitate transferability, actions are converted into absolute end-effector (EEF) poses with 6D rotations (10-dimensional action space), defined relative to the robot's base frame. This embodiment-agnostic representation mitigates error accumulation and drift, a common issue with DOF-specific joint positions.
- **Conditional Flow Matching:** The VLA is trained using a conditional flow matching objective, where an action head predicts a reference flow from a noised action chunk to the target action, conditioned on the VLM output and proprioception.
- **Frequency Matching:** To address varying control frequencies across different datasets in OXE, a frequency matching technique (inspired by π0-FAST) is applied, resampling all datasets to 20 Hz via interpolation, ensuring temporal alignment.

#### 3.2. TwinVLA Architecture: Modular Composition

Once SingleVLA is pre-trained, TwinVLA is constructed by duplicating and integrating two instances of it.
- **Selective Module Duplication:** Instead of duplicating the entire SingleVLA, only the VLM backbone (the decision-making component) is fully replicated for each arm. The vision encoder (for general visual understanding) and the DiT action head (for low-level motor control) are *shared* between the two arms. Each arm also gets its own lightweight proprioception encoder. This selective duplication keeps the overall model size compact (1.3B parameters), comparable to RDT-1B (1.2B), and minimizes computational overhead.
- **Joint Attention for Cross-Arm Fusion:** To enable coordination between the two replicated VLMs, TwinVLA employs a **Joint Attention mechanism**, inspired by Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT). This involves sharing only the self-attention layers across the VLM backbones. The Q, K, V representations from both arms' backbones are concatenated, self-attention is performed, and then the outputs are split back into arm-specific streams. Other components like projections remain arm-specific.
 - **Causal Joint Attention Mask:** A specially designed attention mask preserves causality while allowing interaction. It embeds lower-triangular masks within each arm's region, treats the shared modality as fully accessible, and permits each arm to attend to half of the other's tokens, enabling symmetric cross-arm interaction.
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Integration for Shared Inputs:** Shared inputs (language prompt, ego-centric image) would redundantly feed into both VLMs, increasing VRAM. To address this, these shared tokens are processed as a single sequence using an MoE mechanism. This dynamically routes shared tokens between the two VLM experts (the left and right arm VLMs) using a gating function.
 - **Task Arithmetic for Other Components:** For other transformer components (e.g., Layer Normalization, Projection layers) associated with shared inputs, a simple parameter averaging strategy (task arithmetic with λ=0.5) is used across the two VLMs. This efficiently simulates shared layers without physically merging parameters, reducing VRAM usage by 21% and enabling training on single GPUs.
- **Attention Re-weighting:** A crucial technique to preserve the pre-trained knowledge of SingleVLA. When the second arm's tokens are introduced, the total sequence length for joint attention doubles. Due to softmax normalization, the proportional attention allocated to the shared inputs would dilute. To counter this, attention scores for the shared modality tokens are re-scaled (doubled) and then re-normalized. This restores the proportional attention to shared inputs, aligning with pre-training conditions, and significantly reduces the initial fine-tuning loss (by 40%).

#### 3.3. Training Paradigm

TwinVLA follows a data-efficient paradigm:
1. Pre-train a robust SingleVLA on large-scale public single-arm data.
2. Duplicate and integrate this SingleVLA into the TwinVLA architecture.
3. Fine-tune TwinVLA on only a *small amount of bimanual demonstration data* for the target task, without requiring additional bimanual pre-training.

This modular, coordination-centric design allows TwinVLA to effectively transfer single-arm priors to bimanual control, demonstrating a practical and reproducible pathway to bimanual manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TwinVLA was evaluated across a diverse range of bimanual tasks in real-world and simulation settings, consistently demonstrating strong performance and significant data and compute efficiency compared to existing methods.

#### 4.1. Comparative Baselines

The study compared TwinVLA against:
- **RDT-1B (Liu et al., 2024):** A monolithic VLA of comparable size (1.2B parameters) that requires extensive mixed single-arm and bimanual pre-training (approx. 2,400 hours) and high compute (approx. 1,440 H100 GPU-days). This serves as the direct baseline.
- **π0 (Black et al., 2024):** A state-of-the-art, larger VLA (3.3B parameters) trained on over 10,000 hours of proprietary robot data and incurring even higher computational costs. This acts as an upper-bound reference.
- **Diffusion Policy (DP) (Chi et al., 2024a):** A strong baseline method (271M parameters) trained only on target-task demonstrations, used to highlight the benefits of pre-training.

#### 4.2. Data and Compute Efficiency (Figure 2 analysis)

- **Data Efficiency:** TwinVLA requires significantly less data. It uses ~800 hours of *single-arm* data for pre-training and only ~50 episodes of *target bimanual data* for fine-tuning. This contrasts sharply with RDT-1B (~2,400 hours total data, including bimanual) and π0 (~10,900 hours, mostly proprietary bimanual data).
- **Compute Efficiency:** TwinVLA achieved its results with only ~25 H100 GPU-days, whereas RDT-1B and π0 required over 1,000 H100 GPU-days for their training and pre-training. This highlights an order-of-magnitude reduction in computational resources.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments (Anubis Robot)

- **Setup:** Five long-horizon bimanual tasks ("Fold towel," "Extract hexkey," "Carrot to bag," "Brush to dustpan," "Take towel off") were performed on the Anubis dual-arm robot. Each method was fine-tuned on 50 episodes per task and evaluated with 20 rollouts.
- **Results:** TwinVLA significantly outperformed RDT-1B, achieving an average success rate of 76% compared to RDT-1B's 45%. This performance was remarkable given TwinVLA's pre-training solely on single-arm data. TwinVLA also narrowed the gap to the highly resource-intensive π0 (80% success rate), showcasing its effectiveness. DP's low performance (35% average) underscored the importance of pre-training.

#### 4.4. Simulation Experiments (RoboTwin 2.0 & Tabletop-Sim)

- **Setup:**
 - **RoboTwin 2.0:** A benchmark of 50 bimanual tasks, evaluated under "Easy" (novel instructions) and "Hard" (texture, object position, height variations) settings.
 - **Tabletop-Sim:** A novel benchmark for dexterous bimanual manipulation, with 5 tasks (4 single-tasks, 1 multi-task language following) also under "Easy" and "Hard" conditions.
- **Results:**
 - TwinVLA consistently outperformed RDT-1B in most scenarios. For instance, in Tabletop-Sim, TwinVLA's average success rates were 75.8% (Easy) and 42.9% (Hard), compared to RDT-1B's 61.6% (Easy) and 39.8% (Hard).
 - Notably, TwinVLA *outperformed* π0 in Tabletop-Sim Easy tasks (75.8% vs. 72.5%), suggesting an advantage in scenarios requiring higher dexterity and bimanual coordination, despite π0's extensive bimanual pre-training.
 - On RoboTwin Hard tasks, TwinVLA (8.9%) was slightly lower than RDT-1B (13.7%) but still significantly better than DP (0.6%).

#### 4.5. Data Efficiency in Fine-tuning

- **Results:** In the Tabletop-Sim Easy environment, TwinVLA exhibited a steep learning curve. With only 50 demonstrations, it quickly surpassed RDT-1B's performance, confirming its exceptional data efficiency in adapting to new bimanual tasks.

#### 4.6. Policy Robustness

- **Unseen Scenes/Instructions:** TwinVLA showed good robustness, outperforming RDT-1B by 3.3% in Tabletop-Sim Hard tasks (different textures and objects) and 7.48% in RoboTwin Easy tasks (novel instructions).
- **Real-World Challenging Scenes:** For the "Fold towel" task, TwinVLA was robust to low-light conditions (45% success vs. RDT-1B's 15% and π0's 40%), but less effective with distractors (25% vs. π0's 60%).

#### 4.7. Language Following Evaluations

- **Setup:** Evaluated on the "Put X into pot" multi-task in both simulation and real-world.
- **Results:** TwinVLA demonstrated superior language following, outperforming both RDT-1B and π0 (80.6% in simulation, 51.8% in real-world, compared to RDT-1B's 55.5% and 33.3%, and π0's 79.2% and 62.9%). This suggests TwinVLA effectively preserves the language understanding capabilities from single-arm pre-training during fine-tuning.

#### 4.8. Ablation Studies

A sequential ablation study confirmed the critical contribution of each design component:
- **Attention Re-weighting:** Removing it increased initial fine-tuning loss by 40% and reduced final performance by 1.1% (sim) and 4.0% (real), showing its role in mitigating input distribution shift.
- **MoE Integration:** Removing MoE increased VRAM usage by 21% and reduced performance by a further 1.1% (sim) and 5.0% (real), indicating its efficiency in handling shared inputs without performance compromise.
- **Joint Attention:** This caused the most significant performance drop, 4.0% (sim) and 27.0% (real), particularly impacting tightly coupled bimanual tasks. This confirms joint attention as a critical mechanism for bimanual coordination.
- **Effect of Single-Arm Pre-training (Scratch):** Training from scratch without the OXE dataset resulted in severe performance degradation (4.6% in sim, 46.0% in real), validating the necessity of robust pre-training.
- **Twin Structure vs. Monolithic:** Direct comparison revealed TwinVLA outperformed RDT-1B (a monolithic model of comparable size) by 26.0% in the real world, 5.0% in simulation, and 21.8% in language-following tasks on average. This strongly supports the inductive bias of the modular Twin Structure as highly beneficial for bimanual manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TwinVLA framework represents a significant step forward in robotic manipulation, particularly for complex bimanual tasks. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating a **data-efficient and scalable pathway** to high-performance bimanual control by strategically leveraging abundant public single-arm data.

The key significance and potential impact are:

- **Democratization of Bimanual Robotics:** By largely eliminating the need for expensive and labor-intensive bimanual data collection and massive computational resources for pre-training, TwinVLA makes advanced bimanual manipulation research more accessible to a wider academic and industrial community. This can accelerate innovation and foster greater reproducibility in the field.
- **Paradigm Shift in Robotic Learning:** This work champions a modular, composition-based approach over monolithic training for embodied AI. It shows that rather than training a single, massive model on heterogeneous data from scratch, a more efficient strategy can be to compose specialized, pre-trained modules with lightweight coordination mechanisms. This paradigm could be transformative for other complex multi-modal or multi-embodiment learning problems.
- **Enhanced Data Utility:** TwinVLA demonstrates how existing single-arm datasets, typically overlooked for bimanual tasks, can be effectively repurposed and integrated to solve new, more complex challenges. This maximizes the utility of currently available data and reduces the pressure for continuous, costly data collection.
- **Scalability for Generalist Robotics:** The modular design, combined with data and compute efficiency, offers a more scalable approach towards building generalist robots capable of diverse manipulation tasks. It provides a blueprint for how future robotic systems can integrate various skills and embodiments without starting anew for each complex scenario.
- **Inspiration for Other Domains:** Beyond bimanual manipulation, the principles introduced in TwinVLA—such as leveraging modular relationships to bridge data gaps and improve efficiency—could be applied to other challenging domains in robotics, such as mobile manipulation, human-robot interaction, or multi-robot coordination, where inherent dataset imbalances across modalities are common.
- **Strong Performance with Reproducibility:** TwinVLA not only achieves performance comparable to or sometimes exceeding state-of-the-art models (like π0) that rely on vastly more resources but does so using publicly available data and a transparent architectural design, enhancing trust and further development.

**Limitations:** The paper acknowledges limitations, including the generalization remaining somewhat limited due to the visual disparity between two-arm setups and single-arm pre-training distributions. Further research into mechanisms that prevent this disparity could enhance generalization and model explainability. Additionally, the use of absolute end-effector (EEF) pose control, while beneficial for transfer, might be further optimized by exploring relative absolute actions or shared representations for even greater transfer efficiency.

In conclusion, TwinVLA represents a compelling step towards more intelligent, data-efficient, and accessible robotic systems, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in complex manipulation tasks with practical constraints.
