# ForeAct: Steering Your VLA with Efficient Visual Foresight Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> ForeAct provides Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with efficient, step-by-step visual foresight planning, enabling them to interpret high-level instructions as imagined future observations. This framework achieves an 87.4% average success rate on diverse real-world robotic manipulation tasks, improving generalization and data efficiency.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle to effectively translate high-level language instructions into concrete, low-level action sequences for complex, multi-step tasks.
- Existing VLAs often lack the capacity to simultaneously perform complex semantic reasoning and precise visuo-motor inference, limiting their accuracy and generalization in unstructured environments.
- Previous visual prediction or video generation methods for robot control are often computationally expensive, slow for real-time operation, open-loop, or incompatible with modern VLA architectures.

## Method

- **ForeAct** introduces a hierarchical framework where a VLM decomposes high-level instructions into subtasks, and an efficient ==visual foresight planner== generates step-by-step imagined future observations (goal images).
- The foresight image generation module uses a linear ==Diffusion Transformer== with a 32x compression autoencoder, extensively pre-trained on over 1 million multi-task, cross-embodiment episodes to learn robust embodied dynamics.
- The framework provides dual guidance (visual and textual) to a base VLA model, which then focuses on visuo-motor inference, allowing for seamless integration without architectural modifications to the VLA.

## Results

- The foresight image generation module efficiently predicts high-quality 640x480 future observations within **0.33** seconds on an H100 GPU, suitable for real-time closed-loop robotic control.
- ForeAct achieves an **87.4%** average success rate across 11 diverse real-world tasks, representing a **40.9%** absolute improvement over vanilla VLA baselines and **30.3%** over VLM-augmented baselines.
- The framework demonstrates robust generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios (spatial permutations, compositional tasks) and exhibits strong data efficiency, achieving high performance with reduced training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing VLAs with explicit visual instructions, in addition to text, simplifies their task by allowing them to focus on visuo-motor execution rather than complex semantic reasoning.
> - Pre-training an efficient foresight image generator on a large, diverse dataset of embodied dynamics is crucial for enabling robust and generalizable future observation prediction.
> - A modular, efficient planning layer can significantly enhance VLA performance and generalization on complex, multi-step tasks without requiring modifications to the base VLA architecture.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI and robotics is rapidly advancing, with a strong focus on developing general-purpose robots capable of operating in complex, unstructured real-world environments. A particularly promising direction involves Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to translate high-level language instructions and visual observations directly into executable robot actions.

However, the paper identifies a critical limitation of existing VLA models: their struggle to effectively "ground" high-level, abstract language instructions into concrete, low-level action sequences. While current state-of-the-art VLAs (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, π0.5) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in simpler tasks, they often fall short when confronted with the complexity, diversity, and long-horizon nature of real-world scenarios. This limitation stems from the inherent difficulty for VLAs, often with limited model capacity, to perform both complex semantic reasoning (interpreting instructions) and precise visuo-motor inference (executing actions) simultaneously.

Previous research has explored various avenues to address this:
- **Unified Planning and Control:** Some works attempt to integrate planning directly into the VLA model. However, this often leads to increased latency due to larger model sizes and risks "catastrophic forgetting" of general-purpose capabilities when fine-tuned on specific robot data.
- **Hierarchical Frameworks:** Others delegate planning and reasoning to separate, more capable models (e.g., large language models), which then provide subtask instructions to the VLA. While this mitigates forgetting and allows for more robust planning, it doesn't fundamentally resolve the VLA's challenge in translating *textual* subtasks into precise visual goals. The VLA still receives language, not direct visual guidance, leaving the grounding problem largely unaddressed.
- **Visual Prediction for Robot Control:** There's a growing body of work leveraging visual prediction or video generation to aid robot control, often for model-based reinforcement learning or generating goal images. However, existing video-generation-based methods typically suffer from high computational expense, slow inference times (making them impractical for closed-loop control), predominantly open-loop designs (ignoring real-time environmental feedback), and incompatibility with state-of-the-art pretrained VLAs.

ForeAct positions itself as a novel and effective solution by addressing the core VLA grounding problem through *step-by-step visual foresight planning*. Instead of relying solely on textual instructions, ForeAct provides VLAs with *imagined future observations* (visual instructions) at each step. This approach allows the VLA to offload high-level semantic reasoning to a dedicated planner and focus primarily on visuo-motor inference. By doing so, ForeAct integrates the benefits of hierarchical planning with efficient visual guidance, directly tackling the limitations of previous VLA and visual prediction methods by being efficient, closed-loop, and seamlessly integrable. It represents a significant step towards enabling VLAs to scale effectively to complex, open-world robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind ForeAct is to overcome the limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which struggle to effectively execute complex, multi-step tasks in diverse, unstructured real-world environments. The authors posit that this struggle primarily arises from the difficulty VLAs face in accurately grounding high-level language instructions into concrete, executable action sequences.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the ForeAct framework are:

1. **Enhance VLA Performance through Visual Guidance:** To move beyond solely language-based instructions by providing VLAs with "visual instructions" in the form of imagined future observations. This aims to simplify the VLA's task, allowing it to concentrate on direct visuo-motor inference rather than complex semantic reasoning, thereby improving accuracy and generalization.
2. **Develop an Efficient and General Planning Framework:** To create a planner that can guide a VLA step-by-step using these imagined future observations and corresponding subtask descriptions. Crucially, this planner must be efficient enough for real-time, closed-loop control in real-world robotics.
3. **Ensure Seamless Integration with Existing VLAs:** To design the planning framework such that it can be easily integrated with state-of-the-art VLA models without requiring any architectural modifications, thereby maximizing its applicability and accessibility.
4. **Achieve Robust Embodied Dynamics Learning:** To train the core foresight image generation module on a large and diverse dataset of multi-task, cross-embodiment episodes to enable it to learn robust and generalizable embodied dynamics.
5. **Demonstrate Superior Performance and Generalization:** To rigorously evaluate the ForeAct framework on a diverse set of real-world multi-step tasks, proving its effectiveness, robustness, and ability to generalize to novel and out-of-distribution scenarios compared to existing baselines.

In essence, the paper aims to answer the question: "What if we provide VLAs with visual instructions rather than relying solely on language, enabling them to follow a visually guided sequence toward task completion?"

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ForeAct framework operates as a general and efficient planning layer that augments a base VLA model with step-by-step visual and textual guidance. The core idea is to predict an imagined future observation (a goal image) along with a subtask description, which the VLA then uses to generate actions.

The methodology can be broken down into the following components:

#### 3.1. Visual Foresight Planning (ForeAct) Formulation

The framework re-conceptualizes the VLA's action generation process. Instead of `π(A_t | I_t, q_t, l)` (actions given current observation, proprioception, and overall instruction), ForeAct uses `π_l(A_t | [I_t, G_t], q_t, l_t)π_h(G_t, l_t | I_t, l)`. Here, `π_h` is the visual foresight planner, which provides the predicted future observation `G_t` and a subtask description `l_t`. `π_l` is the VLA model, which now receives `G_t` as an additional visual input and `l_t` as its language condition.
The planner `π_h` itself is composed of two modules: `π_g` (foresight image generation model) and `π_v` (VLM subtask planner). So, `π_h(G_t, l_t | I_t, l) = π_g(G_t | I_t, l_t)π_v(l_t | I_t, l)`.

#### 3.2. Efficient Foresight Image Generation (`π_g`)

This is the core of ForeAct, responsible for producing high-quality (640x480) future observations efficiently.
- **Architecture:** Inspired by SANA, it employs a 32x deep compression autoencoder to map high-resolution images to a compact latent space. A linear DiT (Diffusion Transformer) then performs efficient attention computation within this latent space. The model is adapted from text-to-image generation to image-conditioned generation by concatenating the conditioned image with noise input.
- **Pre-training:** To learn robust embodied dynamics and ensure generalization, the generator is extensively pre-trained on over 1 million multi-task, cross-embodiment episodes. This data is curated from open-source datasets like AgiBot-World Colosseo, RoboMind, Galaxea Open-World, and Bridge, specifically selecting those with high camera resolutions and diverse robot embodiments.
- **Data Preprocessing:** Long-horizon tasks are decomposed into subtasks (totaling 1.16 million subtasks). Condition frames are sampled at 1-second intervals, paired with future frames chosen to be half a subtask length later, ensuring meaningful action changes. This yields approximately 10 million data pairs.
- **Training Details:** Flow matching is used as the training objective. The model is initialized with SANA's pre-trained weights, trained for 800K steps on 64 H100 GPUs with a global batch size of 512.
- **Finetuning:** A lightweight finetuning stage is applied to adapt the pretrained model to the specific robot type and new environments of the real-world deployment dataset.

#### 3.3. VLM for Reasoning and Monitoring (`π_v`)

This component handles the high-level reasoning and task decomposition.
- **Reason-Execute-Monitor Cycle:** The VLM (e.g., Qwen-3-VL-8B-Instruct) takes the overall task description and current observation. It first reasons to generate an immediate, actionable subtask instruction (`l_t`). After the VLA executes, the VLM monitors the updated state, assesses progress, and dynamically re-plans the next subtask or recovers from failures.
- **Prompting Strategy:** A two-stage prompting approach is used: an initial system prompt for task setup (t=0) and a follow-up prompt for continuous monitoring and subtask generation (t>0), ensuring closed-loop interaction.

#### 3.4. Adaption to VLA Models

A key advantage is the seamless integration. The ForeAct framework requires *no architectural modifications* to existing VLA models. During VLA fine-tuning, the current observation is concatenated with the target future observation (the last frame of the subtask episode) to form the visual input. During inference, the *generated* foresight image `G_t` is appended to the current observation `I_t`, and the generated subtask instruction `l_t` serves as the language input.

#### 3.5. Cloud-Edge Closed-Loop Deployment

To ensure responsive real-world control, a hierarchical deployment pipeline is designed:
- **Edge Host (e.g., RTX 5090):** Handles reactive local control, captures observations, and sends them to the cloud. The fine-tuned VLA policy runs here for rapid action dispatch.
- **Cloud Server (e.g., H100 GPU):** Hosts the VLM and foresight image generator (with vLLM for VLM efficiency). It performs high-level planning and foresight generation, then sends a dual-guidance packet (text `l_t`, visual `G_t`) back to the edge host.

This integrated approach leverages the strengths of large-scale pre-training for general dynamics, VLM reasoning for task decomposition, and efficient diffusion models for visual goal generation, all while maintaining compatibility with existing VLA architectures and ensuring real-time performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness, efficiency, and generalization capabilities of the ForeAct framework across diverse real-world and simulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Efficient Foresight Image Generation

- The foresight image generation module achieves a high-quality 640x480 future observation prediction within **0.33 seconds** on an H100 GPU using 8 denoising steps. This speed is critical for real-time closed-loop robotic control.
- **Effectiveness of Pre-training:** Pre-training on 1.16 million subtasks across diverse embodiments drastically improves performance. The pretrained model achieves near-perfect fidelity (1.00) and quality (1.00) on in-distribution tasks, and robust performance on out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks (fidelity 0.88, quality 0.96). In contrast, a model trained from scratch without pre-training completely fails on OOD tasks (0.00 score) and performs poorly on in-distribution tasks (0.18 fidelity).
- **Superiority over Image Editing Models:** State-of-the-art image editing models (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Flash Image, GPT-Image, Qwen-Edit) fail to generate correct robot actions or understand real-world physics and object dynamics, often making no meaningful modifications or hallucinating objects. Their high latency also makes them impractical for robotic control.

#### 4.2. Real-World Benchmark Evaluation

- Evaluated on 11 diverse, multi-step real-world tasks (Kitchen, Workspace, Factory) using the Galaxea R1 Lite robot.
- ForeAct achieves an impressive **87.4% average success rate**, demonstrating significant improvements:
 - **+40.9% absolute improvement** over the vanilla π0 baseline (46.5%).
 - **+30.3% absolute improvement** over the VLM-augmented π0 baseline (VLM + π0, 57.1%).
- ForeAct consistently outperforms baselines across all 11 tasks, maintaining an average success rate of over 70% in all tasks.
- **Scalability with stronger VLAs:** When integrated with the more capable π0.5 backbone, ForeAct boosts its average success rate from 70.3% to **88.2%** on a representative set of tasks, confirming its essential enhancement for general VLA models.

#### 4.3. Simulation Benchmark Evaluation

- On the LIBERO simulation benchmark with the π0.5 model, ForeAct consistently improves performance, raising the average success rate from 96.8% to **97.5%**, even for baselines that already achieve near-perfect scores.

#### 4.4. Generalization to Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) Tasks

- ForeAct demonstrates strong robustness in OOD settings (spatial permutations, compositional tasks, joint shifts), where baseline models suffer substantial performance drops.
- Qualitative examples show ForeAct successfully completing complex OOD tasks (e.g., picking multiple different vegetables sequentially), while baselines get stuck in loops or fail to adapt. This highlights its ability to generalize to unseen task compositions and object layouts.

#### 4.5. Data Efficiency

- ForeAct exhibits strong sample efficiency. On the challenging Clean_Rubb task, it achieves a >90% success rate with only 60% of the training data, and 79% with just 20% of the data. This significantly outperforms the VLM-augmented baseline across all data regimes, suggesting that visual foresight guidance reduces the need for exhaustive demonstration coverage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ForeAct framework represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation, offering a practical and powerful solution to a core challenge facing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Semantic-Action Gap:** ForeAct directly addresses the critical limitation of VLAs in grounding high-level language instructions into concrete, executable actions. By providing explicit, step-by-step visual guidance in the form of imagined future observations, it effectively translates abstract semantic goals into tangible visual targets. This fundamental shift allows VLAs to focus their capacity on visuo-motor execution rather than complex high-level semantic reasoning, leading to substantially improved performance.
2. **Enhanced Accuracy and Generalization:** The demonstrated +30.3% to +40.9% absolute improvements in success rates on diverse real-world tasks, along with strong performance in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, underscore ForeAct's ability to boost both accuracy and generalization. This robustness is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments where perfect training data coverage is impossible.
3. **Practical and Efficient for Real-World Deployment:** The foresight image generation module's speed (0.33s for 640x480 images on an H100 GPU) makes ForeAct practical for real-time, closed-loop robotic control. This emphasis on efficiency, consistent with the expertise of the contributing institutions, is vital for transitioning research prototypes into deployable systems. The cloud-edge deployment strategy further highlights its readiness for real-world scenarios.
4. **High Compatibility and Modularity:** ForeAct's design allows for seamless integration with existing state-of-the-art VLA models without requiring any architectural modifications. This "plug-and-play" capability means that advanced planning and visual guidance can be easily adopted by a wide range of current and future VLA systems, accelerating the development of more capable robots.
5. **Data Efficiency:** The framework's ability to achieve high success rates with significantly less training data is a major advantage. By simplifying the VLA's task, ForeAct reduces the reliance on vast quantities of meticulously collected teleoperation data, making it more feasible to train robots for complex tasks where data collection is expensive and time-consuming.
6. **Paving the Way for General-Purpose Robots:** By enabling VLAs to handle complex, multi-step tasks in diverse settings, ForeAct contributes significantly to the long-term goal of developing truly general-purpose robots. Such robots could assist humans with a wide array of chores in unstructured environments, ranging from household tasks like cooking and cleaning to industrial assembly.

In conclusion, ForeAct provides a powerful, efficient, and generalizable solution that fundamentally enhances the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action models. It represents a critical step towards building more reliable and adaptable embodied AI systems, with wide-ranging potential impact on robotics research and real-world applications.
