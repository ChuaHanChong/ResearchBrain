# Multiplayer Nash Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Multiplayer Nash Preference Optimization (MNPO) generalizes Nash Learning from Human Feedback (NLHF) to an N-player game, addressing the limitations of two-player models in aligning large language models with complex human preferences. The framework consistently surpasses existing preference optimization methods, achieving improvements on benchmarks such as AlpacaEval 2.0 (up to 59.64%) and demonstrating unique problem-solving capabilities on AIME-24 (3.33% where others scored 0%).

## Problem

- Traditional RLHF based on the Bradley–Terry model fails to capture the non-transitive and heterogeneous nature often observed in real-world human preferences.
- Current Nash Learning from Human Feedback (NLHF) methods are restricted to two-player interactions, leading to a "single-opponent bias" that oversimplifies complex, multi-source preference signals.
- This two-player limitation can result in unstable optimization, narrow policy exploration, and an incomplete approximation of the diverse real-world preference landscape.

## Method

- Introduces Multiplayer Nash Preference Optimization (**MNPO**), generalizing NLHF to an ==N-player game== where each policy simultaneously competes against a population of other policies.
- Develops Time-Dependent MNPO (TD-MNPO), an algorithm that adaptively constructs opponent sets using weighted combinations of historical policies, offering provable convergence guarantees in homogeneous settings.
- Extends the framework to a Heterogeneous Multiplayer setting (HT-MNPO) to address scenarios with diverse preference signals from multiple sources, aiming for effective stationary points without formal convergence guarantees.

## Results

- MNPO consistently surpassed existing preference optimization methods on instruction-following (e.g., MT-Bench score of 7.03 for TD-MNPO, 7.52 for HT-MNPO) and preference alignment benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2.0 win rates up to **59.64%**).
- The framework maintained strong performance across 11 academic benchmarks, achieving the highest average score of **71.08%**, indicating effective preservation of foundational model capabilities.
- MNPO demonstrated superior performance in complex mathematical reasoning and coding tasks, achieving the only non-zero score (**3.33%**) on AIME-24 and the best score (**61.59%**) on HumanEval.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalizing Nash games to a multiplayer setting provides a principled and scalable framework to model complex, non-transitive, and heterogeneous human preference dynamics.
> - The Time-Dependent MNPO (TD-MNPO) approach unifies many existing preference optimization algorithms by adaptively constructing opponent sets from historical policies, leading to more stable and robust learning.
> - Optimizing against a population of policies, even in heterogeneous preference environments, helps maintain or improve the foundational capabilities of LLMs while significantly enhancing alignment quality and complex reasoning performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses limitations within the field of aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences, a process typically achieved through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). The conventional RLHF paradigm relies on reward-based methods often grounded in the Bradley–Terry (BT) model. However, recent research indicates that real-world human preferences frequently exhibit non-transitive patterns and heterogeneous structures, which violate the core assumptions of the BT model, namely transitivity and scalar reward functions.

To address these limitations, game-theoretic formulations have emerged, framing preference optimization as the pursuit of Nash equilibria. This paradigm, known as Nash Learning from Human Feedback (NLHF), conceptualizes an aligned policy as an optimal strategy that competing policies cannot exploit. Algorithms such as INPO, ONPO, and EGPO have been developed under the NLHF framework, offering theoretical and empirical guarantees.

Despite these advancements, existing NLHF formulations are primarily restricted to two-player interactions, where a single policy competes against one opponent. This two-player constraint introduces a "single-opponent bias," which limits the models' ability to capture the full complexity of realistic preference structures. Real-world alignment scenarios often involve multiple annotators, diverse evaluation criteria, or a population of historical model checkpoints, generating multi-source and potentially conflicting preference signals. Optimizing against a single distribution at a time can lead to unstable optimization, narrow exploration, and an incomplete approximation of the broader preference landscape.

This paper introduces a framework designed to overcome these limitations by generalizing NLHF to a multiplayer regime. It proposes modeling alignment as a competition against an entire population of policies rather than a single opponent, aiming to provide a more comprehensive and robust approach to LLM alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified shortcomings of existing LLM alignment techniques:
- **Inadequacy of Bradley–Terry Model**: Traditional RLHF, based on the Bradley–Terry model, struggles to represent the non-transitive and heterogeneous nature often observed in real-world human preferences. This limits the fidelity of alignment.
- **Limitations of Two-Player Nash Games**: While Nash learning from human feedback (NLHF) offers a more robust framework than traditional RLHF, current implementations are confined to two-player interactions. This "single-opponent bias" oversimplifies the complex dynamics of real-world preference landscapes, which inherently involve multiple, potentially conflicting, sources of feedback. This simplification can lead to suboptimal or unstable model alignment, characterized by oscillatory behavior and narrow policy exploration.
- **Need for a Multi-Agent Perspective**: Real-world preference alignment often involves scenarios with multiple annotators, diverse evaluation criteria, several reward models, or even a series of historical model versions. A framework capable of explicitly modeling competition against a population of opponents is necessary to capture this inherent complexity. The authors propose that a multiplayer setting can offer a principled mean-field approximation, thereby reducing gradient variance, stabilizing optimization processes, and more accurately reflecting diverse preference structures.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:
- **Develop a Multiplayer Nash Preference Optimization (MNPO) Framework**: Introduce a novel framework that extends NLHF to an *n*-player game. In this formulation, each policy simultaneously competes against a population of other policies while being regularized towards a reference model.
- **Establish Theoretical Guarantees**: Demonstrate that MNPO, particularly in homogeneous preference oracle settings, retains the equilibrium guarantees characteristic of two-player methods. This includes establishing well-defined Nash policies and duality gaps to quantify alignment quality, while also enabling richer competitive dynamics.
- **Introduce Algorithmic Innovations**:
 - Propose **Time-Dependent MNPO (TD-MNPO)**, an algorithm where opponent sets are adaptively constructed using weighted combinations of historical policies, providing provable convergence guarantees for homogeneous scenarios.
 - Introduce a **Heterogeneous Extension (HT-MNPO)** to address scenarios involving diverse preference sources, acknowledging that this extension may operate without formal convergence guarantees but aiming for strong empirical performance.
- **Conduct Comprehensive Empirical Evaluation**: Validate MNPO's effectiveness by comparing its performance against existing NLHF baselines on instruction-following and reasoning benchmarks. The evaluation aims to demonstrate superior alignment quality, especially under conditions of heterogeneous annotators and mixed-policy evaluation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Multiplayer Nash Preference Optimization (MNPO) by extending the existing two-player Nash learning from human feedback (NLHF) paradigm to an *n*-player game.

#### 3.1. RLHF Preliminaries and General Preference Oracle

The paper begins by contrasting its approach with the Bradley–Terry (BT) model, which assumes transitive preferences and a scalar reward function. Instead, it adopts a general preference oracle *P*: *X* × *Y* × *Y* → that directly provides binary preference signals. In the two-player game, an objective *J*(π1, π2) is defined where a max-player π1 aims to maximize its winning rate against a min-player π2, both regularized by a KL divergence to a reference policy πref. The Nash equilibrium (π\*1, π\*2) occurs when neither player can unilaterally improve their objective, with π\*1 = π\*2 = π\*. The "duality gap" quantifies how closely a policy approximates this Nash policy.

#### 3.2. RLHF as Multiplayer Games (Homogeneous Setting)

To generalize to multiple players, the paper first focuses on a homogeneous setting where all *n* players share the same preference oracle.
- **Plackett–Luce Reward Learning Assumption**: The traditional BT model for pairwise comparisons is extended to a Plackett–Luce framework for one-vs-many comparisons. This model calculates the probability of a response *y*i being preferred over a pool of alternatives {*y*j}j̸=i based on learned reward function *R*(*x*, *y*). The objective for learning *R* maximizes the gap between *y*i's reward and the log-sum-exp of other alternatives. This formulation reduces to the BT objective when *k*=2.
- **Homogeneous Multiplayer Preference Oracle**: Each policy πi competes against the other *n*-1 players using a shared oracle P. The objective function *J*(πi, {πj}j̸=i) is defined, where πi maximizes its expected preference against the population while maintaining proximity to πref via a KL penalty. This formulation ensures symmetric treatment, decentralized optimization, and generalizes the two-player case.
- **Nash Equilibrium and Duality Gap**: In this symmetric *n*-player game, a Nash equilibrium π\* exists where no player can improve by unilateral deviation. A unified duality gap is defined to measure policy approximation to π\*.
- **Multiplayer Nash Preference Optimization (MNPO)**:
 - **Theoretical Framework**: The paper draws upon the multiplicative weights update rule (Freund & Schapire, 1999) to propose an iterative update (Eq. 10) for policies π(t+1)i. This update proportionaly adjusts policy probabilities based on the average advantage over opponents and is regularized by the geometric mean of opponent policies. This theoretical framework provides convergence guarantees to an ϵ-approximate Nash equilibrium.
 - **Algorithmic Innovation - Loss Function**: Due to the intractability of the normalization factor in the theoretical update, the authors derive a practical loss function. They define *h*t(π, *y*, *y*’) (Eq. 12) as the log-ratio between two responses for a candidate policy, normalized by the aggregate log-ratios of opponent policies. The update minimizes a squared difference loss *L*t(π) (Eq. 14) between *h*t(π, *y*, *y*’) and the weighted preference advantage. A simplified loss *L*’t(π) (Eq. 15) is then introduced, which is mathematically equivalent to *L*t(π) up to an additive constant, further simplifying implementation by replacing a complex preference term with a hyperparameter.

#### 3.3. Reward-Enhanced MNPO and TD-MNPO

- **Reward-Aware Integration**: MNPO can incorporate quantitative reward signals as auxiliary guidance. The loss function (Eq. 15) is shown to be interpretable as a special case of Reward-aware Preference Optimization (RPO) (Sun et al., 2025) using a squared distance metric, bridging qualitative and quantitative alignment.
- **Time-dependent Multiplayers (TD-MNPO)**: A key innovation is how opponent policies are constructed. Instead of static opponents, TD-MNPO (Eq. 17) uses a weighted mixture of recent historical policies {πt-j} (n ≤ t+1) as opponents. This method is shown to unify many existing preference optimization algorithms (e.g., DPO, IPO, SPIN, INPO) by varying *n*, opponent selection, distance metric, and target reward gap. This adaptive approach aims for smoother policy evolution, greater robustness, and stable convergence.

#### 3.4. Multiplayer Game with Heterogeneous Preference Oracles (HT-MNPO)

- **Heterogeneous Setting**: The framework is extended to scenarios where preference signals come from multiple distinct sources (e.g., different annotators or reward models for helpfulness, safety). Each player πi is associated with its own reward model *r*i(*x*, *y*) and preference oracle *P*i.
- **Game-Theoretic Properties**: This heterogeneous setting results in a general-sum game, which lacks the symmetry required for formal Nash equilibrium convergence guarantees that apply to homogeneous constant-sum games. The paper defines a player-specific duality gap to quantify the incentive for deviation.
- **HT-MNPO Algorithm**: The HT-MNPO loss (Eq. 18) allows each policy to optimize using its own oracle and a mixture of opponent policies {πj}j̸=i, internalizing a reward-gap signal specific to its own reward model. While lacking formal guarantees, this formulation aims to find effective stationary points for alignment with heterogeneous or conflicting evaluators.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Models**: Gemma-2-9B-it as the base LLM.
- **Training**: Online RLHF over T=3 iterations, using fresh prompt sets.
- **Reward Models**: ArmoRM-Llama3-8B-v0.1 for TD-MNPO preference signals. Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B and Athene-RM-8B are additional reward models for HT-MNPO to simulate heterogeneous oracles.
- **Hyperparameters**: AdamW optimizer, cosine learning-rate scheduler, grid search for history weights and regularization parameters.
- **Data**: Gemma2-Ultrafeedback-Armorm dataset (~60K training samples).
- **Evaluation**:
 - **Instruction-Following and Preference Alignment**: MT-Bench, AlpacaEval 2 (length-controlled win rate), and Arena-Hard v0.1 (win rate). Judged by GPT-5-mini.
 - **Academic Benchmarks**: 11 benchmarks covering instruction following (IFEval), general knowledge (GPQA, MMLU, ARC), commonsense reasoning (HellaSwag, TruthfulQA, Winogrande), and math/coding (GSM8K, Minerva-Math, AIME-24, HumanEval).
 - Comparison also against a range of open-source and closed-source LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of MNPO across various benchmarks indicates consistent performance improvements over existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Instruction-Following and Preference Alignment

- **Consistent Outperformance**: MNPO consistently surpassed existing preference optimization methods (DPO, SimPO, SPPO, INPO) on AlpacaEval 2.0 (Length-Controlled Win Rate), Arena-Hard (Win Rate), and MT-Bench (Score/10). All evaluations were performed using GPT-5-mini as the judge.
- **AlpacaEval 2.0**: TD-MNPO achieved a score of 57.27%, an improvement of 1.18 to 2.92 points over the best and worst baselines, respectively. HT-MNPO using Athene-RM-8B achieved the highest score among all tested models at 59.64%.
- **Arena-Hard**: TD-MNPO scored 52.26%, demonstrating a 4.23-point improvement over INPO (48.03%), which was the next best baseline. MNPO also showed competitive performance against larger open-source models (e.g., surpassing Tulu-2-DPO (70B) and Mixtral-IT (141B)) and some closed-source models.
- **MT-Bench**: TD-MNPO attained a score of 7.03, outperforming all baselines. HT-MNPO with ArmoRM-Llama3 achieved 7.52.
- These results suggest that the multiplayer formulation contributes to better alignment with human preferences in open-ended generation tasks, particularly in accommodating diverse preferences and non-transitive relationships.

#### 4.2. Knowledge and Reasoning Capabilities

- **Maintained Performance**: MNPO maintained strong performance across a suite of 11 academic benchmarks covering instruction following, knowledge, and commonsense reasoning.
- **Overall Average**: MNPO achieved the highest average score of 71.08 across these benchmarks, outperforming the SFT baseline (70.28%) and all other preference optimization methods.
- **Specific Strengths**: MNPO recorded the best performance on GPQA (33.33%), indicating capability in graduate-level reasoning. It also performed competitively on instruction following (IFEval: 73.94%) and maintained solid results on knowledge (MMLU: 75.63%) and commonsense reasoning tasks.
- The stability of MNPO's performance across various cognitive tasks, in contrast to some baseline methods that exhibited degradation on specific academic benchmarks (e.g., SimPO on TruthfulQA), suggests that the multiplayer framework helps preserve foundational model capabilities while enhancing preference alignment.

#### 4.3. Mathematical and Coding Performance

- **Superiority in Complex Tasks**: MNPO achieved the highest average score of 48.10% across mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, Minerva-Math, AIME-24) and coding (HumanEval) benchmarks, surpassing all baseline methods.
- **AIME-24**: On the challenging AIME-24 benchmark, MNPO was the only method to achieve a non-zero score (3.33%), whereas all other methods, including the SFT baseline, scored 0.
- **HumanEval**: MNPO also delivered the best coding performance among all methods, scoring 61.59% on HumanEval.
- These findings suggest that the multiplayer optimization framework is particularly beneficial for complex reasoning tasks that may involve multiple potential solution strategies.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Appendix D)

- **Impact of Number of Players (Table 5)**: Ablation studies on TD-MNPO revealed that increasing the number of players (n) consistently improved alignment quality, with diminishing returns observed beyond n=3 players. For instance, moving from n=1 (SimPO-like) to n=3 players yielded a significant improvement on AlpacaEval 2.0.
- **Heterogeneous Settings (Table 8)**: In HT-MNPO, 3-player configurations consistently outperformed 2-player configurations across different reward models, demonstrating the benefits of a multiplayer setup even in heterogeneous environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Multiplayer Nash Preference Optimization (MNPO) represents a notable advancement in the field of aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Principled and Scalable Framework**: MNPO offers a principled and scalable framework that extends Nash learning from human feedback (NLHF) to multi-agent interactions. This generalization is designed to better capture the complex, non-transitive, and heterogeneous nature of real-world human preferences, moving beyond the limitations of single-opponent or pairwise interaction models.
- **Theoretical Grounding for Multiplayer Alignment**: For homogeneous preference oracles, MNPO is shown to inherit the equilibrium guarantees of two-player methods, including well-defined Nash policies and duality gaps. This provides a formal theoretical basis for understanding multi-agent alignment dynamics, which has been an underexplored area in RLHF.
- **Unified Perspective on RLHF**: The time-dependent MNPO (TD-MNPO) formulation demonstrates its ability to subsume numerous existing preference optimization methods (e.g., DPO, IPO, SPIN, INPO) as special cases. This unification provides a broader, more coherent understanding of the RLHF landscape and potentially simplifies algorithm design.
- **Improved Alignment Quality and Robustness**: Empirical evaluations consistently demonstrate that MNPO outperforms established baselines across a range of instruction-following, preference-alignment, and reasoning benchmarks. This indicates an improvement in alignment quality and robustness, particularly beneficial in scenarios involving diverse annotators or complex evaluation criteria.
- **Enhanced Complex Reasoning Capabilities**: The superior performance of MNPO in challenging academic benchmarks, especially in mathematical reasoning (AIME-24) and coding (HumanEval), suggests that the multiplayer optimization framework is particularly effective for tasks requiring sophisticated problem-solving and handling multiple potential strategies. This implies a potential for LLMs trained with MNPO to exhibit enhanced cognitive capabilities.
- **Addressing Real-World Complexity**: By explicitly modeling competition against a population of opponents, MNPO offers a more realistic approach to alignment problems where feedback originates from multiple, potentially conflicting, sources. This is crucial for developing LLMs that can effectively navigate the nuanced and varied demands of real-world applications.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens new avenues for research, particularly in exploring alternative equilibrium concepts for heterogeneous game settings where formal convergence guarantees are currently lacking. It also prompts investigation into more nuanced feedback mechanisms to continue model improvement as the gap between preferred and dispreferred responses narrows.

In summary, MNPO contributes a theoretically grounded and empirically validated approach to LLM alignment that is better equipped to handle the inherent complexity of human preferences, offering a scalable foundation for future generations of alignment techniques.
