# Source-Lifted Flow Matching for Intervenable Multimodal Imitation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) developed Source-Lifted Flow Matching (SL-FM), a method that transforms the passive stochasticity of multimodal generative policies into an explicit control mechanism for robot actions. SL-FM achieves an average score of 0.823 across multimodal benchmarks and enables purposeful redirection of robot routes in 91.1% of interventions on the D3IL Avoiding task, demonstrating enhanced controllability without altering the core velocity field.

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning methods struggle with multimodal action distributions, often averaging diverse valid behaviors into a single, suboptimal action.
- Existing generative policies (like flow matching) can represent diverse behaviors but lack a direct mechanism for users or high-level planners to select a specific behavioral mode.
- When different multimodal paths converge and cross in action space, a shared velocity field can average incompatible velocities, leading to a loss of identity for selected behavioral branches.

## Method

- A ==state-conditioned source prior== models the flow's source as a ==mixture of Gaussians==, allowing for 'K' selectable handles that capture different behavioral modes.
- **Orthogonal Source Lifting** introduces an auxiliary ==lifted space== where only the flow's source endpoint is lifted and regularized towards ==orthogonal anchors==, preserving the identity of different handles even if their action components overlap.
- A ==responsibility floor== in the ==flow-matching loss== ensures all learned handles receive sufficient training signal, preventing "dead modes" and enhancing intervenability.

## Results

- SL-FM achieved an average score of **0.823** across multimodal benchmarks (D3IL Avoiding, D3IL Aligning, PushT), outperforming standard Flow Matching (**0.802**).
- The method enabled direct source intervention, successfully redirecting future robot routes in **91.1%** of counterfactual interventions on D3IL Avoiding, with **57.8%** resulting in successful and distinct routes.
- Source handles exhibited semantic alignment, correlating with specific subtasks in D4RL Kitchen, and proved effective as a high-level action space for hierarchical control, achieving **91.7%** joint route-and-task success on Avoiding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Geometric manipulation of the flow's source in a lifted space can convert passive stochasticity into an active, explicit control handle for multimodal behaviors, without altering the core velocity field.
> - Maintaining distinct source identities through orthogonal lifting prevents the averaging of incompatible velocities at action-space crossings, crucial for preserving chosen behavior branches.
> - A responsibility floor during training is effective in ensuring all learned behavioral modes are robust and intervenable, preventing "dead handles" in multimodal imitation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of imitation learning has increasingly incorporated generative policies to model complex, multimodal action distributions. Traditional behavioral cloning methods often assume a unimodal Gaussian policy, which can be insufficient for tasks where multiple valid strategies exist for a given observation (e.g., navigating an obstacle from different sides). Recent advancements, such as Diffusion Policy and flow-matching policies, have enabled the representation of such diverse behaviors, providing a richer action space for robots. These methods address the need to preserve multiple valid strategies rather than collapsing them into averaged actions.

However, a distinction exists between representing diverse behaviors and directly controlling them. Existing flow-matching policies typically use source noise as a passive sampling mechanism. While resampling the source can lead to varied rollouts, users or higher-level planners generally lack a direct mechanism to choose a specific continuation from a fixed decision state. This limits their utility in scenarios requiring deliberate policy steering, human-in-the-loop control, or planning over divergent but valid future trajectories.

Current approaches to exposing control in generative policies typically involve:
- **Mode-conditioned fields:** Directly conditioning the policy or velocity field on a discrete mode variable. This approach, while effective, alters the fundamental nature of the model by learning separate, mode-specific dynamics, making the discrete input a selector of expert behaviors rather than an intervention on a single shared flow.
- **Inference-time guidance or latent adaptation:** Methods like DynaGuide, DSRL, ReinFlow, or approaches that optimize noise variables or latent actors to steer policies. These techniques expose control through guidance, value optimization, or latent space manipulation.
- **Source and coupling design in generative flows:** Much work in this area, particularly in computer vision, focuses on improving transport geometry, reducing crossings, or enhancing sample efficiency (e.g., OT-CFM, rectified flows). While relevant to flow modeling, these methods do not explicitly address the problem of exposing an actionable intervention variable for sequential decision-making in control tasks.

This work, Source-Lifted Flow Matching (SL-FM), positions itself to address the gap between passive multimodal sampling and direct user intervention. It seeks to provide an explicit, local control handle over multimodal behaviors within a flow-matching framework, without resorting to mode-specific velocity fields or external guidance mechanisms. The central challenge addressed is to make source identity survive multimodal path crossings in a shared velocity field, a problem not fully tackled by existing source or coupling designs in the context of action control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a flow-matching policy that transforms the passive stochasticity inherent in existing multimodal generative policies into an actionable, intervenable control mechanism. Specifically, the authors aim to allow a user or high-level planner to directly select among valid multimodal continuations from a given state.

This objective is motivated by several detailed considerations:

1. **Enabling Deliberate Behavior Selection:** Current flow-matching policies can generate diverse actions (e.g., a robot choosing to pass an obstacle on the left or right), but this diversity typically arises from random sampling of a source distribution. The user or planner cannot explicitly dictate which of these valid behaviors should occur from a shared decision point. The research seeks to expose a "source handle" that allows for such deliberate selection.

2. **Maintaining a Shared, Latent-Free Velocity Field:** A core constraint and motivation is to achieve this intervenability *without* conditioning the velocity field directly on a discrete mode variable (e.g., by adding a `z` input to `v_theta`). Such direct conditioning would necessitate learning separate, mode-specific dynamics, fundamentally changing the model's structure. The goal is to retain the computational efficiency and conceptual simplicity of a single shared velocity field, where the intervention effect is instead carried solely by the geometry of the source.

3. **Addressing Path-Crossing Ambiguity:** A significant technical challenge arises when different multimodal paths converge and cross in action space. If a velocity field is shared and only conditioned on the action coordinate, it may average incompatible velocities from different intended branches at these crossing points. This averaging can lead to a loss of the selected source branch's identity, resulting in composite or unintended trajectories rather than distinct, chosen continuations. The motivation is to design a mechanism that allows the identity of the chosen source branch to be preserved through such crossings.

4. **Improving Downstream Utility for Planning and Control:** Policies that offer direct control over multimodal outcomes are more valuable for advanced applications like hierarchical planning, where a high-level component needs to select a long-horizon strategy, or for human-in-the-loop robot control, where a human operator might intervene to choose a specific action path. The research aims to provide an interface suitable for these contexts.

5. **Mitigating "Dead Modes" in Multimodal Learning:** When learning a mixture of behaviors, some modes may receive insufficient training data or responsibility, leading to "dead" components that do not perform reliably. The research aims to develop a training mechanism that ensures all available handles remain trainable and contribute to the policy's overall competence, thereby enhancing the robustness of the intervention interface.

In summary, the motivation is to create a flow-matching policy that offers explicit control over multimodal robot behaviors through a source-based intervention handle, while adhering to the principle of a single, shared velocity field and robustly handling challenges like path crossings and dead modes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Source-Lifted Flow Matching (SL-FM) is designed to enable source-intervenable conditional flow matching for multimodal imitation learning. It achieves this through three main components: a state-conditioned source prior, Orthogonal Source Lifting, and floor-weighted flow training.

#### 3.1. Modeling the Source Prior

Instead of using an unstructured source distribution, SL-FM models the source `x_a_0` as a state-conditioned mixture of isotropic Gaussians. This mixture allows for `K` selectable source handles, indexed by `z ∈ {1, ..., K}`. For each robot state `s`, a neural network (the prior network `φ`) predicts the parameters `(π_k(s), μ_k(s), σ_k(s))` for each of the `K` Gaussian components. Here, `π_k(s)` are the mixture weights, `μ_k(s)` are the component means in action space `R^d_a`, and `σ_k(s)` are the diagonal scales.

In free deployment, a handle `z` is sampled from the learned mixture weights `π_φ(·|s)`, and then `x_a_0,z` is sampled from the corresponding Gaussian component `N(μ_z(s), σ_z^2(s)I)`. For intervention, the user or planner directly sets `z = k`, bypassing the sampling of `z`. The source prior is trained using a negative log-likelihood objective `L_src` to fit the demonstrated action distribution `p_data(a|s)`. This training induces a soft responsibility `r_k(s, a)` for each handle `k`, indicating its relevance to a given demonstrated action `a` in state `s`. This responsibility is dynamic and state-conditioned, allowing for flexible assignments in sequential control tasks.

#### 3.2. Orthogonal Source Lifting

This component addresses the challenge of preserving source identity when multimodal paths cross in action space. SL-FM introduces an auxiliary lifted space `x̄ = [x_a, x_g]`, where `x_a ∈ R^d_a` is the original action component, and `x_g ∈ R^d_g` (with `d_g ≥ K`) is an auxiliary lift coordinate.

The core idea is to lift *only the source endpoint* of the flow. For a given handle `k`, the lifted source endpoint is defined as `x̄_0,k = [x_a_0,k, λτ_k(s)]`, where `x_a_0,k` is the action-space source point, `λ` is a lift scale, and `τ_k(s)` are state-dependent lift embeddings. These embeddings are regularized towards orthogonal anchors `{e_k}` (e.g., one-hot vectors) in the auxiliary space via `L_anc`, ensuring distinct representations for each handle.

Crucially, the *target endpoint* `x̄_1` for *all* handles is defined as `[a, 0]`. This means all target actions are placed on a "zero-lift plane" (`x_g = 0`), ensuring that the policy preserves the original demonstration distribution `p_data(a|s)`. The lifted interpolation path becomes `x̄_t,k = [(1-t)x_a_0,k + ta, (1-t)λτ_k(s)]`. For any `t < 1`, different handles `k` maintain separation in the `x_g` coordinate, even if their `x_a` components overlap. This prevents the shared velocity field from averaging incompatible velocities at crossings, thereby preserving the identity of the chosen source branch. At execution, only the action component `x_a` of the terminal state `x̄_1` is used. The velocity field `v_θ(x̄_t, t, s)` operates on the continuous lifted state `x̄_t` but does not receive the discrete handle `z` as input.

#### 3.3. Floor-Weighted Flow Training

SL-FM trains a single, shared velocity field `v_θ` for all handles. To supervise this field, the soft responsibilities `r_k(s, a)` from the source prior are used to weight the flow regression loss for each lifted path.

To prevent "dead handles" (components that rarely receive high responsibility and thus lack training signal), a *responsibility floor* `γ_k(s, a) = (1 - α)r_k(s, a) + α/K` is introduced. Here, `α ∈` is a hyperparameter that controls the amount of uniform supervision. A non-zero `α` ensures that every handle `k` receives some minimum training signal, promoting robustness and intervenability even for less frequently demonstrated modes. This floor only affects the weighting of the flow-matching objective and does not alter the deployment prior `π_φ(z|s)`.

The floor-weighted flow-matching objective `L_W-FM` is calculated as `E[sum_k sg(γ_k(s, a)) ||v_θ(x̄_t,k, t, s) - ū_t,k||^2]`, where `sg(·)` denotes a stop-gradient operation to prevent the flow loss from directly influencing the responsibility assignments. The total training objective combines `L_W-FM`, `L_src`, and `L_anc` with scalar weights.

#### 3.4. Deployment and Source Intervention

During inference, free deployment involves sampling `z` from `π_φ(·|s)`, constructing `x̄_0,z`, and then integrating the shared velocity field `d_x̄_t/dt = v_θ(x̄_t, t, s)` from `t=0` to `t=1`. The action component of the final `x̄_1` is then executed. For intervention, the evaluator directly sets the handle `z=k` at a chosen decision state, constructs `x̄_0,k` using this fixed `k`, and proceeds with integration using the *same shared velocity field*. This protocol ensures that interventions operate solely by modifying the initial source point in the lifted space, adhering to the design principle of a latent-free velocity field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SL-FM encompassed both free-deployment imitation performance and the proposed intervention interface across several robot control benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Task Performance Across Multimodal Benchmarks

- **D3IL Avoiding:** SL-FM achieved a success rate of 0.825 and a route entropy of 0.921, outperforming the standard Flow Matching (FM) policy (0.798 success, 0.933 entropy) and other baselines like CPD, MM-FM, and Modal coupling. A notable ablation, "SL-FM w/o lift" (0.733 success, 0.907 entropy), indicated that the Orthogonal Source Lifting mechanism is crucial for preserving handle identity and performance, especially in tasks with path crossings.
- **Overall Benchmarks:** Across D3IL Avoiding, D3IL Aligning, and PushT, SL-FM demonstrated the best average score (0.823) compared to FM (0.802), CPD (0.740), MM-FM (0.168), Modal coupling (0.599), and a direct z-conditioned field (0.764). The performance gains were more pronounced in tasks characterized by frequent local branching.
- **D4RL Kitchen:** On this multi-task manipulation benchmark, the K=8 SL-FM variant improved over FM by +0.42 completed tasks in 500-step rollouts, suggesting the structured source design contributes to performance beyond simple route control.

#### 4.2. Source-Intervention Controllability

- **Same-Prefix Counterfactual Interventions:** On D3IL Avoiding, a crucial test involved replaying identical prefixes and then changing only the local source handle for five policy calls at a high-entropy decision point.
 - Changing the source handle redirected the future route in 91.1% of matched paired interventions.
 - A "both-success route-change rate" of 57.8% was achieved, indicating that both branches successfully completed the task while taking different routes, demonstrating effective and valid behavioral redirection.
 - In contrast, FM noise resampling and a direct z-conditioned field yielded lower rates of successful route changes.
 - Interventions where the handle was kept the same resulted in 0% route changes, confirming that the observed changes were attributable to the handle selection, not replay variability.
- **Mutual Information and Semantic Alignment:**
 - Analysis of mutual information between sampled source handles and realized route labels in free rollouts showed that handle samples were informative about future branch choices, with peaks aligning with decision phases around obstacles.
 - In D4RL Kitchen, a non-uniform heatmap of source-handle–subtask correlations indicated that specific handles were associated with particular subtasks (e.g., handle z5 with the kettle, z7 with microwave). This suggests that the source handles can capture semantically meaningful, task-specific behavioral patterns.

#### 4.3. Downstream Route Control

- To evaluate the handle as a high-level control interface, a frozen SL-FM policy was combined with a downstream selector (using CEM/PPO) that chose the `z` handle every five policy calls. This setup achieved high joint route-and-task success rates (e.g., 91.7% over all 24 route targets on Avoiding). This demonstrates that the source handle can function as an action space for hierarchical control.

#### 4.4. Design Ablations and Sensitivity

- **Responsibility Floor:** Moderate values for the `α` parameter in the responsibility floor improved task success while preserving route entropy. This supports its role in mitigating dead handles and enhancing robustness without inducing a collapse to unimodal behavior.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness:** The method showed viability across reasonable ranges for parameters such as anchor penalties, source scales, source-alignment weights, and the number of modes `K` (typically `K=4-10`). Very large `K` led to reduced reliability.
- **Stability Factors:** Unstable lift scales (`λ`) and excessive Euler integration steps were identified as factors that could reduce the system's robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on Source-Lifted Flow Matching (SL-FM) contributes to the field of imitation learning and robot control by introducing a method for active intervention in multimodal generative policies, which has several potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Actionable Multimodal Control:** SL-FM moves beyond merely representing diverse behaviors to providing a direct, actionable interface for selecting specific behavioral modes. This is a crucial step towards more controllable and interpretable robot policies, allowing users or planners to steer robot behavior with greater precision.

2. **Preserving Model Integrity:** A key innovation is achieving this intervenability while maintaining a single, shared, and latent-free velocity field. This avoids the need for multiple mode-specific policy heads or significant changes to the core flow-matching formulation, potentially leading to more computationally efficient and generalizable models compared to directly conditioning the velocity field on discrete modes.

3. **Leveraging Source Geometry for Robustness:** The introduction of Orthogonal Source Lifting demonstrates that geometric manipulation of the source distribution can effectively preserve the identity of chosen behavioral branches, even through complex action-space crossings. This addresses a fundamental challenge in multimodal generative modeling, where shared dynamics might otherwise average conflicting trajectories.

4. **Foundation for Hierarchical and Human-in-the-Loop Control:** The exposed source handle provides a compact, low-dimensional interface that can be readily integrated with high-level planners, such as model predictive control (MPC) or hierarchical reinforcement learning agents. It can also serve as an intuitive control point for human operators to guide robot actions, enhancing human-robot collaboration by offering explicit choices at decision points.

5. **Applicability to Diverse and Unlabeled Data:** The method's ability to learn soft responsibilities for different modes without requiring explicit, hand-labeled mode annotations in the training data makes it particularly well-suited for large, heterogeneous imitation datasets. Such datasets often contain varied styles, imbalanced modes, or trajectories of mixed quality, where manual mode labeling would be impractical. SL-FM offers a way to extract and organize these latent behavioral patterns.

6. **Broader Implications for Generative Models:** The principle of converting passive latent randomness into an active intervention variable through structured source manipulation could extend beyond flow matching to other generative policy paradigms (e.g., diffusion models). This work suggests a generalizable approach for enhancing the controllability of complex generative models in sequential decision-making tasks.

In summary, SL-FM offers a principled approach to providing fine-grained control over multimodal robot behaviors. By demonstrating the efficacy of source-based intervention without modifying the core dynamics model, this research opens avenues for more versatile and interpretable robot learning systems.
