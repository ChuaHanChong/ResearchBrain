# Object-Centric World Models from Few-Shot Annotations for Sample-Efficient Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> OC-STORM is an object-centric model-based reinforcement learning framework that enhances world models by incorporating object representations extracted via few-shot annotations from pretrained video segmentation networks. This approach achieved superior sample efficiency on the Atari 100k benchmark and demonstrated significantly faster convergence in complex Hollow Knight boss fights.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning (RL) agents often suffer from sample inefficiency, particularly when learning from high-dimensional pixel observations.
- Existing Model-Based RL (MBRL) methods relying on pixel-level reconstruction losses tend to focus on large background elements, neglecting small, task-critical objects.
- Previous object-centric RL (OCRL) approaches were impractical due to the extensive manual labeling required for object segmentation.

## Method

- The **OC-STORM** framework integrates pretrained, few-shot video segmentation networks (e.g., Cutie, SAM2) to extract compact, vector-based object features from raw observations with minimal annotations.
- A categorical Variational Autoencoder (VAE) discretizes both the extracted object features and downsampled visual inputs into distinct latent representations.
- A spatial-temporal dynamics model, based on either a transformer (STORM) or RNN (DreamerV3) backbone, predicts future states by modeling interactions between object and visual latents.

## Results

- OC-STORM achieved a mean human-normalized score of **134.8%** on the Atari 100k benchmark, outperforming the STORM baseline (**107.2%**) and other competitive MBRL algorithms.
- The framework demonstrated significantly faster convergence and higher win rates in challenging Hollow Knight boss fights, for instance, achieving a **48.0%** win rate on Mage Lord compared to STORM's **5.0%**.
- Cutie-based object features yielded better performance than SAM2-based features due to superior preservation of positional and global context within its feature design.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric priors, derived from few-shot segmented objects, effectively redirect world model capacity towards decision-relevant entities, leading to improved dynamics prediction.
> - Vector-based object representations are more robust and computationally efficient for dynamics modeling compared to mask-based representations, as they semantically summarize high-resolution visual input.
> - Modern open-set video object segmentation models can be practically integrated into MBRL, addressing the historical annotation bottleneck for object-centric learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities across various domains, including board games and robotic control. However, a persistent challenge is its sample inefficiency, particularly in environments where agents learn from high-dimensional pixel observations. Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) offers a solution by training a world model to generate synthetic experience, thereby reducing the need for extensive real-world interaction.

Standard MBRL approaches often rely on pixel-level reconstruction losses (e.g., L2-loss) for training their world models. This method, while effective in some cases, frequently directs model capacity towards large, static background elements, leading to neglect of small, sparse, yet task-critical objects. Consequently, the learned dynamics models may fail to capture essential information for effective policy learning, particularly in visually complex and dynamic environments. Examples provided in the paper, such as the STORM baseline failing to reconstruct critical characters in Hollow Knight, illustrate this limitation.

Object-centric (OC) representations are emerging as a method to address this issue by guiding the world model to represent the environment in terms of discrete, interacting entities. Historically, the implementation of object-centric RL (OCRL) has been constrained by the requirement for extensive, task-specific manual labeling. However, recent advancements in open-set segmentation and tracking foundation models, such as SAM, Cutie, and GroundingDINO, have enabled the generation of high-quality object segmentations with minimal annotations. This development makes OCRL a more practical and potentially powerful approach.

This work integrates these modern computer vision advancements into MBRL. By leveraging pretrained segmentation networks with few-shot annotations, the proposed OC-STORM framework aims to improve the world model's ability to predict the dynamics of decision-relevant objects and their interactions. This approach seeks to enhance sample efficiency without necessitating extensive labeling or direct access to internal environment states, thereby positioning itself as a method to bridge the gap between advanced visual perception and efficient decision-making in complex visual domains for reinforcement learning. The paper builds upon existing transformer-based world models like STORM and extends them with object-centric priors, contributing to the growing field of integrating structured inductive biases into deep RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the sample inefficiency of deep reinforcement learning, particularly when operating directly on high-dimensional pixel observations. While successful in many domains, current deep RL agents often require significantly more environmental interactions than humans to master a task. Model-based RL (MBRL) attempts to mitigate this by learning a world model to simulate environment dynamics, generating additional experience for policy training.

However, existing MBRL methods that rely on pixel-level reconstruction losses face a specific limitation: the reconstruction objective is often dominated by large, visually prominent background elements. This can lead to a failure in accurately capturing or predicting the dynamics of small, sparse, yet critically important objects within complex, dynamic scenes. For instance, in games like Hollow Knight, a standard world model might reconstruct the background accurately but overlook essential characters like the boss, which are fundamental for successful policy execution. This oversight directly hinders the policy's ability to learn effective control strategies.

The central hypothesis guiding this work is that an object-centric (OC) representation can more effectively direct the world model's capacity towards semantically meaningful entities. By explicitly modeling individual objects and their interactions, the system can potentially achieve improved dynamics prediction accuracy for decision-relevant elements and, consequently, enhance sample efficiency.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this research are:

- **Develop an Object-Centric MBRL Framework:** Introduce OC-STORM, a novel framework that enhances a learned world model by incorporating object representations.
- **Leverage Pretrained Segmentation Networks with Few-Shot Annotations:** Integrate object representations extracted by pretrained segmentation networks (e.g., Cutie, SAM2). A crucial objective is to achieve this with a minimal number of annotated frames (e.g., 6-12), avoiding extensive labeling requirements or reliance on privileged internal environment information.
- **Improve Tracking of Decision-Relevant Object Dynamics:** Train the world model to specifically track the dynamics of objects critical for decision-making and their inter-object interactions.
- **Enhance Sample Efficiency:** Demonstrate that by focusing model capacity on task-relevant entities through object-centric priors, the framework can significantly improve sample efficiency compared to existing baselines.
- **Demonstrate General Applicability:** Evaluate the framework across diverse and challenging visual domains, including the Atari 100k benchmark and the visually complex boss fights in Hollow Knight, without requiring extensive domain-specific adaptations.
- **Provide Practical Insights:** Conduct comprehensive empirical evaluations and ablation studies to generate practical insights for the development of future object-centric reinforcement learning methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The OC-STORM framework operates in two main stages: (1) self-supervised learning of an object-centric (OC) world model that captures environment dynamics, and (2) training an actor-critic policy using trajectories imagined by this learned world model.

#### 3.1. Object Feature and Visual Input Extraction

The method processes raw observations (`o_t`) through two distinct pathways to generate inputs for the world model:
- **Object Features (`s_obj_t`):** A frozen, pretrained video segmentation network (e.g., Cutie or SAM2) extracts compact, vector-based feature representations for a user-defined number of key objects (`K`). This extraction is conditioned on a minimal set of annotated frames (6-12 annotations per task/boss). The chosen segmentation models, Cutie and SAM2, are selected for their temporal consistency, retrieval-based flexibility, compact vector embeddings, and robustness to out-of-domain generalization. The object features leverage the object-attention mechanisms within these models.
- **Visual Input (`s_vis_t`):** The original observation (`o_t`) is resized to a lower resolution (64x64 pixels), consistent with standard practices in pixel-based MBRL, to serve as a raw visual input stream.

#### 3.2. Latent State Discretization via Categorical VAE

To mitigate compounding prediction errors inherent in autoregressive models operating on high-dimensional inputs, a categorical Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is employed. This VAE encodes both the object features and visual inputs into discrete latent representations.
- **Encoders (`q_ϕ`):** Separate architectures are used for different modalities. Multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) encode the `K x obj_dim` object feature vectors into `K x 16x16` discrete object latents. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) encode the `3 x 64x64` visual observations into `32x32` discrete visual latents. The chosen latent dimensions (16 categories x 16 classes for objects, 32 categories x 32 classes for visuals) follow established configurations from prior work (e.g., DreamerV3).
- **Decoders (`p_ϕ`):** Symmetric architectures reconstruct the inputs from these discrete latents.
- **Straight-Through Gradient Estimator:** This technique is used to enable differentiable sampling from the categorical distributions.

#### 3.3. Spatial-Temporal Object-Centric Dynamics

The core innovation lies in a spatial-temporal architecture that models object and visual dynamics, allowing for interaction between these modalities. The framework is designed to be compatible with both transformer-based (STORM) and recurrent neural network (RNN)-based (DreamerV3) world model backbones.
- **STORM Backbone:** A transformer architecture is utilized, featuring alternating spatial and temporal attention blocks.
 - **Spatial Attention:** Operates across all `K` object tokens and one visual token at each timestep, capturing inter-object relationships and object-scene interactions.
 - **Temporal Attention:** Processes each token type independently across the sequence of `L` timesteps to model dynamics.
 - Actions are incorporated by concatenating them with the latent representations.
- **DreamerV3 Backbone:** The RNN is augmented with spatial attention mechanisms at each timestep to achieve object-scene interaction, analogous to the transformer's spatial attention.
The sequence model (`f_ϕ`) takes sequences of object latents, visual latents, and actions as input, outputting hidden states (`h_t`) that encode the combined object-visual state and dynamics.

#### 3.4. Prediction Heads

The hidden states (`h_t`) generated by the sequence model are used to predict future environmental signals:
- **Dynamics Predictor (`g_dyn_ϕ`):** An MLP predicts the distribution of the next latent state (`z_t+1`).
- **Reward Predictor (`g_rew_ϕ`):** A self-attention mechanism aggregates information from all object and visual tokens (using a special query token akin to BERT's [CLS] token) and passes the resulting representation through an MLP to predict the reward (`r_t`).
- **Termination Predictor (`g_term_ϕ`):** Similar to the reward predictor, this mechanism predicts the termination probability (`τ_t`).

#### 3.5. Training the World Model and Policy

- **World Model Training:** The world model is trained end-to-end using a self-supervised objective that combines:
 - **Reconstruction Loss:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) for reconstructing the original visual inputs.
 - **Reward Loss:** Symlog two-hot loss for reward prediction.
 - **Termination Loss:** Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) for termination signal prediction.
 - **Dynamics Loss (`L_dyn`):** Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between the encoder's output and the dynamics predictor's output (with stop-gradient on the encoder).
 - **Representation Loss (`L_rep`):** KL divergence between the encoder's output and the dynamics predictor's output (with stop-gradient on the dynamics predictor), regularized with a 0.5 coefficient to prevent posterior collapse.
- **Policy Training:** The actor-critic policy (`π_θ` and `V_ψ`) is trained entirely on imagined trajectories generated by the world model, following the DreamerV3 algorithm. The policy and value function use the concatenation of object latents, object hidden states, visual latents, and visual hidden states as input features. The training involves policy gradient updates with value function baselines, using λ-returns and an exponential moving average (EMA) of critic parameters for stabilization.

#### 3.6. Segmentation Model Selection and Robustness

Cutie and SAM2 are selected for their suitability based on four key properties: temporal consistency and efficiency for video processing, retrieval-based flexibility for few-shot annotations, compact vector representations, and out-of-domain robustness. The framework also includes a mechanism to handle segmentation model failures by setting object feature vectors to zero if an object cannot be reliably tracked, contributing to its robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of OC-STORM was conducted across two distinct benchmarks: the Atari 100k benchmark and challenging boss fights in Hollow Knight.

#### 4.1. Atari 100k Benchmark

- **Overall Performance:** Object-centric methods generally outperformed the baseline world models (DreamerV3 and STORM) on the Atari 100k benchmark, measured by human-normalized scores (HNS) for 100,000 environment frames. OC-STORM achieved a mean HNS of 134.8% and a median HNS of 43.8%, demonstrating superior sample efficiency compared to the STORM baseline (107.2% mean HNS, 35.5% median HNS). When compared against other MBRL algorithms (Table 2), OC-STORM achieved competitive performance, surpassing DreamerV3, SPR, IRIS, and TWM in HNS mean and median, and closely matching or exceeding the performance of more recent methods like ∆-IRIS and DIAMOND in several specific games.
- **Segmentation Model Comparison:** Cutie-based variants of OC-STORM yielded higher scores than SAM2-based ones. This was attributed to Cutie's object-feature design, which aggregates visual features within masked regions, thus better preserving positional and global context compared to SAM2's prototype vectors designed for classification.
- **Representation Method Comparison:** Vector-based object representations demonstrated superior performance compared to mask-based representations (e.g., FOCUS). Mask-based methods were found to suffer from resolution issues (low-resolution masks lose detail, high-resolution masks are computationally expensive) and noisiness, making their dynamics harder to predict. Vector representations, being semantically summarized from high-resolution inputs, were more consistent and computationally efficient.
- **Categorized Performance:** The 26 Atari games were categorized into "Obj-detectable" (where all decision-relevant objects could be consistently identified) and "Otherwise" groups. In the "Obj-detectable" category, vector-based OC methods substantially outperformed the baseline (e.g., Cutie-OC-STORM HNS mean of 186.2% vs. STORM HNS mean of 142.4%). In the "Otherwise" category, OC methods still achieved performance comparable to the baseline, indicating robustness even with incomplete object detection.

#### 4.2. Hollow Knight Boss Fights

- **Sample Efficiency and Performance:** In the visually complex boss fights of Hollow Knight, OC-STORM consistently converged significantly faster and achieved stronger performance compared to the STORM baseline, especially in more challenging scenarios like Mage Lord and Pure Vessel. For instance, on Mage Lord, OC-STORM achieved an average return of 28.0 with a 48.0% win rate, while STORM achieved 19.6 with a 5.0% win rate. On Hornet Protector, OC-STORM reached a 100.0% win rate, compared to STORM's 66.7%.
- **Reward Shaping and Action Space:** The study also highlights that the proposed reward configuration (positive reward for hitting enemies, virtual termination upon being hit) and a multi-binary-discrete action space (directly mapped to keyboard presses) were more robust and led to significantly improved performance compared to "legacy rewards" settings used in prior work.

#### 4.3. Analysis and Ablations

- **Completeness of Object Representation:** Qualitative evidence, such as observation reconstructions in Atari Boxing using only two object feature vectors (for white and black players), indicated that these compact features effectively capture object state and positional information.
- **Robustness to Segmentation Model Failures:** Experiments on Atari Boxing and Pong demonstrated that agent performance gradually degraded as the probability of randomly zeroing object feature vectors increased. This suggests that OC-STORM exhibits robustness in handling unstable object detection results from the vision model.
- **Continuous Control (Meta-world):** OC-STORM exhibited higher sample efficiency than the STORM baseline and, in some tasks (e.g., `reach`, `basketball`), outperformed MWM on 4 Meta-world continuous control tasks, suggesting out-of-the-box applicability to continuous control domains.
- **Policy Design:** Ablations showed that MLP-based policies generally performed better than attention-based policies for the actor-critic networks, as attention-based policies tended to overfit pre-learned behaviors and struggled in non-stationary games like Pong.
- **Number of Annotations:** Increasing the number of few-shot annotation masks (e.g., from 1 to 6 for Atari, or 12 for Hollow Knight) enhanced the robustness and performance of the agent, even in visually "static" environments where objects could undergo significant state changes (e.g., player punching in Boxing).
- **Computational Overhead:** The computational overhead introduced by integrating Cutie was measured to be modest, with the system maintaining real-time performance across various evaluated settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents OC-STORM, an object-centric model-based reinforcement learning framework that addresses critical limitations of current deep RL methods, particularly their sample inefficiency in visually complex environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** The work demonstrates that by integrating object-centric priors, world models can direct their representational capacity towards semantically meaningful entities, leading to improved dynamics prediction and, consequently, state-of-the-art sample efficiency on challenging benchmarks like Atari 100k and visually rich modern games like Hollow Knight. This is a crucial step toward making RL agents more practical for real-world applications where data collection is expensive or time-consuming.
- **Practical Integration of Advanced Computer Vision:** OC-STORM successfully integrates recent advancements in open-set, few-shot video object segmentation (e.g., Cutie, SAM2) into an MBRL pipeline. This approach overcomes the historical barrier of extensive manual labeling often required for object-centric methods, making it more scalable and adaptable to novel domains without relying on privileged internal environment states.
- **Improved Robustness in Complex Visual Domains:** The framework exhibits robustness in handling complex, dynamic visuals, where traditional pixel-reconstruction-based world models often fail to capture small, task-critical objects. The ability to perform well even when object detection is incomplete, and its resilience to segmentation model failures, indicates its potential for deployment in less controlled environments.
- **Guidance for Future OCRL Research:** The comprehensive empirical evaluations and ablation studies provide practical insights into effective object representation methods (favoring vector-based over mask-based) and the choice of vision models (Cutie over SAM2 for features in this context), as well as policy architecture and annotation strategies. These findings can inform and accelerate future research in object-centric reinforcement learning.
- **Broader Applicability:** The demonstration of OC-STORM's effectiveness across diverse domains—from Atari games to continuous control tasks in Meta-world and the visually rich Hollow Knight—suggests its general applicability beyond specific game environments. This indicates a potential pathway for developing more generalist and adaptable agents for a wide range of real-world control and decision-making problems, particularly in robotics, autonomous navigation, and human-computer interaction where perception of specific objects is paramount.
- **Bridging Perception and Control:** The work represents a meaningful step in bridging the gap between advanced visual perception capabilities from modern computer vision foundation models and efficient control learning in reinforcement learning, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in sample-efficient RL for complex visual settings.
