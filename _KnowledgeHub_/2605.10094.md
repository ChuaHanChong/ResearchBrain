# Retrieve-then-Steer: Online Success Memory for Test-Time Adaptation of Generative VLAs

> [!summary] Summary
> Retrieve-then-Steer stores progress-calibrated successful observation-action segments from a frozen generative VLA's own deployment into an online memory, retrieves and DTW-filters state-relevant chunks into an elite prior, then injects it via confidence-adaptive guidance into the flow-matching sampler's intermediate state, boosting closed-loop success without parameter updates.

## Problem

- Benchmarks treat test episodes as independent zero-shot trials, ignoring that real deployment repeats in the same or slowly changing environment.
- Fine-tuning and RL/human-in-the-loop methods exploit deployment experience only via heavy parameter updates, feedback, or safe exploration.
- Test-time steering methods use myopic generate-then-select sampling, discarding candidates instead of reusing successful cross-episode experience.

## Method

- ==Online Progress-Calibrated Memory== stores observation-action prefixes up to the progress peak, using a pretrained ==VLAC critic== to score progress without human labels.
- ==Retrieval-based Action Prior== selects top-K similar chunks, removes outliers via pairwise ==DTW== trajectory-consistency filtering, and aggregates them into a soft-weighted elite prior.
- **Confidence-Adaptive Prior Guidance** injects the elite prior at an intermediate ==flow-matching== state, mapping retrieval confidence to guidance strength t0. Together these form **Retrieve-then-Steer**.

## Results

- On LIBERO-10, method raises pi0 from **81.6%** to **84.4%** and pi0.5 from **92.4%** to **94.4%**, beating TACO test-time scaling's **83.8%**.
- On SimplerEnv with CogACT, average success rises from **75.8%** to **79.5%**, with largest **5.5-point** gain on the hardest long-horizon drawer-and-apple task.
- Ablations show Direct Replay of retrieved actions drops success to **87.8%** and unverified memory drops it to **87.6%**, versus **94.4%** for the full filtered prior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Successful trajectories from repeated deployment are environment-verified evidence, not disposable samples, and can steer a frozen policy without retraining.
> - Injecting a retrieved prior into an intermediate generative-sampler state beats directly replaying or post-hoc interpolating retrieved actions.
> - A smaller, high-precision memory of verified successes outperforms a larger noisy one; false-positive contamination hurts more than missed positives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly generative VLAs utilizing diffusion or flow-matching mechanisms. These models demonstrate potential for general-purpose control by generating expressive and temporally coherent action sequences. However, a gap exists between their capabilities in benchmark evaluations and their reliability in real-world deployment.

Current evaluation paradigms typically treat test episodes as independent, zero-shot trials. In contrast, real-world robots often operate repeatedly in static or slowly changing environments, where physical layouts, camera viewpoints, calibration errors, and task patterns exhibit inter-episode correlation. This divergence leads to stability issues, as VLAs can be prone to failure in nearly identical states due to perception noise, viewpoint shifts, or accumulated errors, even after occasional task completion.

Existing research approaches address these challenges in several ways:
- **Pre-training and downstream fine-tuning:** Methods like RT-1, OpenVLA, and π₀ enhance policies before deployment, but they do not enable continuous learning from test-time successes or adaptation to local deployment conditions.
- **Reinforcement learning and human-in-the-loop methods:** These approaches utilize deployment experience but typically require additional feedback, safe exploration strategies, and often involve heavy parameter updates to the policy.
- **Test-time policy steering:** Approaches such as RoboMonkey, MG-Select, and TACO focus on enhancing action decisions during inference through sampling, external evaluators, or internal confidence signals. However, these often follow a "generate-then-select" paradigm, incurring extra inference overhead and discarding valuable cross-episode experience that could be reused.
- **Retrieval-augmented and memory-based robot learning:** These methods leverage historical experience, demonstrations, or task context, often retrieving relevant trajectories from offline datasets for few-shot imitation or local policy adaptation. Some use memory as a replay buffer for continual learning. However, these often rely on offline data or require policy updates, limiting their applicability for lightweight test-time adaptation of frozen VLAs.

This paper positions itself by proposing a novel "retrieve-then-steer" paradigm that directly leverages successful test-time experience as a soft behavioral prior for generative VLAs. Unlike previous methods, it focuses on constructing an *online success memory* during continuous deployment, storing environment-verified evidence without requiring offline datasets, extra feedback, or parameter updates to the base VLA policy. This non-parametric approach aims to improve the reliability of frozen VLAs by continually adapting their behavior based on their own successful interactions in a specific environment, thereby addressing the limitations of existing paradigms regarding persistent online deployment and efficient reuse of experience.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that while Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit strong potential for general-purpose robotic manipulation, their closed-loop reliability often degrades significantly under local deployment conditions. The authors identify a key disconnect between standard VLA evaluation paradigms, which treat test episodes as independent zero-shot trials, and the reality of real-world robotic operations, where robots frequently perform repetitive tasks in consistent or gradually changing environments.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Exploiting Persistent Deployment:** The work aims to redefine VLA deployment from a series of isolated test episodes to a persistent online process. This perspective recognizes that real-world environments often exhibit strong correlations in physical layouts, camera viewpoints, calibration errors, and task patterns across repeated operations. The authors hypothesize that successful executions in such persistent settings provide environment-verified evidence of reliable behavior patterns that current VLA evaluation and adaptation methods largely ignore.

2. **Improving Closed-Loop Reliability of Frozen VLAs:** Despite strong foundational capabilities, VLAs frequently suffer from instability during real-world execution. A robot might occasionally succeed but often fails in perceptually similar states. The authors motivate that successful executions (e.g., a precise grasp or placement) implicitly encode crucial environment-specific information like visual geometry, actuation biases, and execution timing. The objective is to leverage these past successes to improve the robustness and stability of a *frozen* VLA policy, which cannot undergo further parameter updates during deployment.

3. **Reusing Self-Generated Successful Experience:** A primary motivation is to enable a partially competent VLA to improve its reliability by reusing its *own* successful test-time interactions. Instead of discarding successful trajectories after evaluation, the authors propose treating them as a valuable, reusable resource for future action generation. This contrasts with methods relying on offline demonstrations, external human feedback, or continuous policy fine-tuning.

4. **Formulating a Soft Behavioral Prior:** The paper seeks to formalize successful test-time experience as a soft behavioral prior for generative VLAs. This means that instead of merely generating actions from scratch or selecting from multiple candidates, the system should be "steered" towards behaviors proven effective in the target environment, while still allowing the VLA to condition on real-time observations and refine its generative process.

5. **Developing a Lightweight, Non-Parametric Adaptation Mechanism:** A core objective is to achieve this adaptation without requiring heavy parameter updates or extensive retraining, which can be computationally expensive and unsafe in real-world settings. The goal is to develop a lightweight, non-parametric test-time adaptation (TTA) framework that can operate online. This leads to the "retrieve-then-steer" paradigm, where successful segments are retrieved and then used to guide the generative action sampler.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between VLA capabilities and real-world robustness by systematically accumulating and reusing self-generated successful experiences during continuous deployment, thereby enhancing policy reliability and closed-loop stability in a non-parametric and computationally efficient manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, "Retrieve-then-Steer," is an online success-memory guided test-time adaptation framework for generative Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. It operates on a frozen VLA during continuous deployment, learning from its own successful executions without requiring parameter updates. The framework consists of three main components: Online Progress-Calibrated Memory, Retrieval-based Action Prior, and Confidence-Adaptive Prior Guidance.

#### 3.1. Online Progress-Calibrated Memory

This component is responsible for building and maintaining a memory of successful observation-action segments.

- **Trajectory Buffering and Memory Representation:** During each test episode, the robot maintains a temporary buffer `B^(i)` to record candidate memory entries `(k^(i)_t, a^(i)_t)`. The retrieval key `k^(i)_t` is derived by reusing the VLA's visual encoder. Multi-view RGB image features are processed by reshaping spatial patch tokens into a grid, downsampling, flattening, concatenating features from all views, and normalizing. The action `a^(i)_t` is the action chunk generated by the VLA.
- **Interval-based Progress Calibration:** To identify genuinely successful and reusable experiences, a pretrained VLAC critic (Φψ) is used as a progress estimator. For each task, a successful demonstration video from the training set acts as a reference process (R), providing in-context information about the task execution procedure. The critic predicts the signed progress change `c^(i)_τm` over intervals `[τm-1, τm]` in a trajectory, indicating whether the task progresses or regresses. These interval-level scores are accumulated into a trajectory-level progress score `v^(i)_τm`, initialized at 0.
- **Progress-Peak Prefix Selection and Memory Update:** After an episode, the completion score `P^(i)` is defined as the maximum accumulated progress `max_τm(v^(i)_τm)`. The progress-peak timestep `τ*i` is the time at which this maximum progress is achieved. This approach handles potential regressions after near-success (e.g., overshooting). If `P^(i)` exceeds a predefined success threshold `η`, the episode is deemed successful. Only the observation-action segments *before* the progress peak (`B^(i)_+`) are retained and added to the online success memory `M`. This filters out failed, regressive, or redundant post-success motions.

#### 3.2. Retrieval-based Action Prior

When the online success memory is not empty, relevant successful actions are retrieved to assist subsequent action generation.

- **Successful Action Retrieval with Similarity Gating:** Given the current observation's retrieval key `k_t`, its cosine similarity `s_i` to all keys `k_i` in the memory `M` is computed. The top-K candidates with the highest similarity scores are selected, and weakly related results are removed using a similarity threshold `γ_sim`, forming the initial candidate set `I_sim`.
- **DTW-based Trajectory Consistency Filtering:** To mitigate state-level similarity leading to action-level mismatches, Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is applied. Pairwise multivariate DTW distances `d_ij` are computed among the action chunks `a_i` and `a_j` in `I_sim`. For each candidate `a_i`, an inconsistency score `r_i` is calculated as the median distance to other candidates. Candidates with excessively high `r_i` are removed, yielding the final consistent candidate set `I`.
- **Elite Action Prior Aggregation:** The filtered candidates in `I` are aggregated into an "elite action prior" `a_elite`. This aggregation uses similarity-based soft weights `w_i`, where weights are normalized exponentials of similarity scores scaled by a temperature parameter `τ`. For action components in Euclidean spaces (e.g., positions, joint angles), linear weighted aggregation is used. For orientations, the geodesic mean on SO(3) is computed. For gripper commands, weighted averaging is used for continuous values, and weighted voting for discrete commands, with a conservative fallback strategy for strong conflicts.

#### 3.3. Confidence-Adaptive Prior Guidance

The retrieved `a_elite` provides a local behavioral reference, but its reliability may vary. This component injects `a_elite` into the generative sampler as a soft constraint, adapting its strength based on retrieval confidence.

- **Initialization for Flow-Matching Samplers:** For a VLA with a flow-matching action head, the sampler typically starts from Gaussian noise `x_1 = ϵ`. Instead, the sampling process is initialized from an intermediate state: `x_t0 = (1 - t_0)a_elite + t_0ϵ`. Here, `t_0 ∈` controls the guidance strength; smaller `t_0` means stronger prior guidance by placing the initial state closer to `a_elite`.
- **Initialization for Diffusion Action Heads (Appendix B):** For diffusion models, `a_elite` is first perturbed to an intermediate noise level `x_n0` using the diffusion forward process (`x_n0 = sqrt(¯α_n0)a_elite + sqrt(1 - ¯α_n0)ϵ`). The original diffusion policy then performs conditional denoising from `x_n0` downwards.
- **Confidence Estimation:** The guidance strength `t_0` (or `n_0` for diffusion) is adapted based on a retrieval confidence score `c`. This score combines the average retrieval similarity `¯s_top-K` (normalized as `~s`) and the action-level dispersion `σ_DTW` (standard deviation of `r_i` from DTW filtering). The confidence is calculated as `c = α~s - βσ_DTW`, where `α` and `β` are coefficients. A higher `c` indicates a more reliable prior.
- **Adaptive Guidance Strength:** The confidence `c` is mapped to the sampling starting time `t_0` (or noise step `n_0`) via a sigmoid function: `t_0 = t_min + (1 - t_min)σ(-γc)`. Higher confidence shifts `t_0` towards `t_min` (strongest guidance), while lower confidence moves it towards 1 (recovering the original sampler).
- **Generative Refinement:** After initializing from `x_t0` (or `x_n0`), the VLA's original conditional velocity field (for flow-matching) or denoising process (for diffusion) integrates from `t_0` to `0` (or `n_0` to `1`). This generates the final action chunk `ˆa_t:t+H-1`, allowing the VLA to refine actions conditioned on the current observation while being steered by the successful prior. If retrieval fails, the system falls back to the original VLA sampler.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the "Retrieve-then-Steer" framework across both simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating improved task success and closed-loop stability.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

**Benchmarks:** The method was tested on the challenging LIBERO-10 suite for long-horizon, multi-stage manipulation, and SimplerEnv for robustness under realistic deployment conditions.

**Baselines:** Primary baselines included generative VLA policies like π₀ and π₀.₅ (flow-matching), and CogACT (diffusion). Comparisons were also made against the test-time scaling method TACO and other VLA policies such as OpenVLA, RT-1, and Octo.

**Results:**
- **LIBERO-10 (Table 1):** The method improved both base policies. For π₀, the average success rate increased from 81.6% to 84.4%, surpassing TACO (83.8%). For the stronger π₀.₅ policy, it improved from 92.4% to 94.4%. Notable gains were observed in long-horizon and multi-stage tasks (e.g., "Turn on Stove and Place Moka," "Black Bowl in Drawer," "Moka Pots on Stove"), suggesting that online success memory stabilizes closed-loop execution.
- **SimplerEnv (Table 2):** The method improved CogACT's average success rate from 75.8% to 79.5%, with consistent gains across all four tasks (e.g., "Open Top Drawer and Place Apple" saw a 5.5-point increase). This indicates effectiveness under layout variations and visual perturbations.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

**Platforms:** Evaluations were conducted on an OpenArm-based dual-arm system and an ALOHA-PiPER system.

**Tasks:** Four tasks were chosen: "Bowl Stacking," "Cube Handoff," and "Sequential Test-Tube Placement" on OpenArm, and "Bimanual T-shirt Folding" on ALOHA-PiPER. These covered long-horizon, bimanual, fine-grained, and deformable-object manipulation.

**Results:**
- **OpenArm Tasks (Figure 3a, Table 3):**
 - "Bowl Stacking": Success rate improved from 72.0% to 80.0% with π₀.₅ + Ours.
 - "Cube Handoff": Success rate improved from 40.0% to 52.0%.
 - "Sequential Test-Tube Placement": The full 4/4 success rate increased from 18.0% to 24.0%, and the average completed length improved from 1.54 to 1.94, demonstrating enhanced stability in long-horizon sequential tasks.
- **ALOHA-PiPER T-shirt Folding (Figure 3b):**
 - In-domain (yellow T-shirts): Success rate improved from 42.0% to 50.0%.
 - Out-of-domain (white T-shirts): Success rate improved from 36.0% to 46.0%.
 - Overall, performance increased from 39.0% to 48.0%, indicating improved robustness to appearance shifts in deformable-object manipulation.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analyses

- **Continuous Deployment Analysis (Figure 4a, Table 6):** On "Moka Pots on Stove" (LIBERO-10), the cumulative success rate of π₀.₅ + Ours gradually improved and stabilized at a higher level as the online memory accumulated successful experience, demonstrating the benefit of persistent deployment. The gain was maintained even with a bounded memory capacity (e.g., 4k entries achieving 70.2% vs. 71.2% for unlimited memory).
- **Component Ablation (Table 4):**
 - Using "Top-1 Retrieval" alone improved success from 92.4% to 93.6%.
 - "Top-K Soft Aggregation" further improved to 94.0%.
 - The full method (Top-K + DTW prior + intermediate initialization) reached 94.4%, indicating the benefits of multi-candidate aggregation and consistency filtering.
 - "Direct Replay" of retrieved actions performed poorly (87.8%), while "Output Interpolation" (93.0%) was better but inferior to "Intermediate Initialization" (94.4%), confirming that injecting the prior into the generative sampler for refinement is more effective.
- **Effect of t₀ (Figure 4b):** Adaptive `t₀` (dynamic guidance strength) achieved 70% success on "Moka Pots on Stove," outperforming the best fixed `t₀` setting (68%), highlighting the advantage of confidence-adaptive guidance.
- **Effect of Success-Memory Construction (Table 5):**
 - Storing all unverified trajectories significantly reduced success (87.6%), confirming that noisy experiences are detrimental.
 - Using predicted successful *full* trajectories also underperformed the base policy (91.8%), indicating that untrimmed trajectories can contain regressive segments.
 - The proposed "Predicted Successful Prefix Memory" achieved 94.4%, close to "Oracle Successful Full Trajectory" (94.8%), emphasizing the importance of both reliable success verification and progress-peak truncation. The VLAC critic's high precision (0.970) for memory construction was deemed critical.
- **Inference-Time Analysis (Table 7):** The prior-guided sampler without retrieval was slightly faster (0.95×) than the base VLA due to fewer effective generation steps. The full method, including retrieval and filtering, introduced a moderate overhead (1.10×), indicating a favorable trade-off between efficiency and reliability.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity (Table 8):** The method was found to be relatively robust to choices for K (retrieval size), `γ_sim` (similarity threshold), `τ` (aggregation temperature), and `η` (success threshold), with the default settings providing a stable balance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Retrieve-then-Steer" framework contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a novel approach to improve the reliability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly in persistent online deployment settings.

The key significance and potential impact are:

1. **Enhanced Reliability in Real-World Deployment:** By systematically accumulating and reusing successful self-generated experiences, the method directly addresses the closed-loop instability and brittleness of VLAs in real-world scenarios. The demonstrated improvements in task success rates, especially for long-horizon and multi-stage tasks in both simulation and physical robots, indicate a step towards more robust and dependable robotic systems.

2. **Lightweight and Non-Parametric Adaptation:** A significant impact is the ability to adapt a frozen VLA without requiring parameter updates or additional training. This non-parametric, test-time adaptation (TTA) mechanism is computationally efficient and avoids the complexities and safety concerns associated with continuous learning or fine-tuning in deployment, making it suitable for practical applications where model retraining is undesirable or infeasible.

3. **Paradigm Shift from "Generate-then-Select":** The proposed "retrieve-then-steer" paradigm offers an alternative to the "generate-then-select" approaches common in test-time policy steering. By guiding the generative process with a learned prior *before* action generation, it enables more informed action sampling, potentially leading to higher quality initial actions and more stable policy execution, while also avoiding the need to discard valuable cross-episode experience.

4. **Leveraging Environment-Specific Evidence:** The framework effectively capitalizes on the idea that successful past executions constitute "environment-verified evidence." This allows the VLA to implicitly learn and adapt to local deployment conditions, such as specific camera viewpoints, object layouts, or robot calibration biases, which are often difficult to capture through large-scale pre-training alone.

5. **Improved Handling of Complex Tasks:** The consistent gains observed in long-horizon, multi-stage, bimanual, and deformable-object manipulation tasks highlight the method's ability to stabilize complex behavioral patterns and reduce accumulated errors over time. This is crucial for expanding the capabilities of robots to more intricate and prolonged tasks.

6. **Progress-Calibrated Memory for Quality Control:** The introduction of a progress-calibrated memory, using a VLAC critic and progress-peak truncation, ensures that only high-quality, task-relevant successful segments are stored. This mechanism is vital for preventing memory contamination by failed or suboptimal experiences, demonstrating the importance of conservative memory construction for effective retrieval-augmented policies.

7. **Generalizability Across Generative Architectures:** The methodology demonstrates adaptability to different generative VLA architectures (flow-matching and diffusion), suggesting a broader applicability to the evolving landscape of VLA models.

**Potential limitations and future work**, as noted by the authors, include the method's reliance on stable environments, the need for occasional initial successes to build memory, the dependence on the progress estimator's reliability, and potential issues with state aliasing. Addressing these aspects could further enhance the framework's robustness and scalability in more dynamic and complex real-world scenarios.
