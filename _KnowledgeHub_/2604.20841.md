# DeVI: Physics-based Dexterous Human-Object Interaction via Synthetic Video Imitation

> [!summary] Summary
> DeVI, developed at Seoul National University, introduces a framework to enable physics-based dexterous human-object interaction by imitating motions from text-conditioned synthetic 2D videos. It consistently outperforms 3D demonstration-based baselines in tracking human and object motion, achieving an average MPJPE (All) of 25-41mm on the GRAB dataset compared to baselines' 91-142mm.

## Problem

- Physics-based human motion simulation often overlooks complex dexterous Human-Object Interactions (HOI) or is confined to limited actions, thereby restricting its direct applicability to robotic manipulation.
- Acquiring high-quality 3D HOI data through motion capture systems is prohibitively expensive and limits scalability across diverse objects and interaction scenarios.
- Directly leveraging 2D synthetic videos for 3D physics-based character control faces challenges due to inherent depth ambiguity and the difficulty in reconstructing accurate 4D HOI (3D human and object pose, interaction dynamics).

## Method

- **DeVI** generates 2D HOI videos from text prompts using a pre-trained ==image-to-video diffusion model==, which then serves as the motion guidance source.
- It extracts ==hybrid imitation targets== comprising reconstructed 3D human motion and tracked 2D object trajectories from the synthetic videos, avoiding reliance on imprecise 3D object reconstruction.
- A ==Visual HOI Alignment== procedure refines the initial 3D human pose reconstruction to ensure better spatio-temporal alignment with both the reference video and the 3D object, enabling feasible hand-object interactions for ==reinforcement learning==.

## Results

- DeVI quantitatively outperformed existing 3D demonstration-based baselines (PhysHOI, SkillMimic, InterMimic) on the GRAB dataset, achieving an average Mean Per Joint Position Error (All) of **25-41mm**, substantially lower than baselines (**91-142mm**).
- The Visual HOI Alignment component significantly reduced pixel error for hand joints (from **25.6 to 3.74 pixels**) and all joints (from **27.4 to 11.0 pixels**), while also improving human-object contact distance from **101mm to 18.7mm** at contact frames.
- The framework demonstrated a higher success ratio on the GRAB dataset (**50.0%**) compared to baselines (e.g., InterMimic's **0.500%**) under specific error thresholds, and successfully generated diverse, physically plausible HOI for novel objects, multi-object scenes, and text-driven variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Text-conditioned synthetic 2D videos offer a scalable and diverse source for guiding physics-based dexterous human-object interaction, overcoming the limitations of expensive 3D motion capture data.
> - A hybrid imitation target, combining robust 3D human motion estimates with stable 2D object trajectories, provides effective guidance for reinforcement learning without the over-constraining or imprecision of full 4D HOI reconstruction from 2D.
> - The 'Visual HOI Alignment' optimization is crucial for refining initial 3D human pose reconstructions, ensuring accurate hand-object spatial alignment that enables physically plausible dexterous interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The modeling of physics-based human motion is recognized as a fundamental requirement for equipping robots with the capacity to replicate complex Human-Object Interactions (HOI) and perform robust manipulation tasks. Historically, research in physical motion simulation has often concentrated on human motion in isolation, frequently neglecting the complexities inherent in HOI, which restricts its direct applicability to robotic manipulation. While more recent investigations have begun to address HOI within physical environments, these studies have tended to either overlook dexterous hand-object interactions or confine their scope to a limited set of actions, such as sports.

A promising trajectory in recent research involves leveraging high-quality 3D demonstrations as imitation targets for physics-based HOI. However, the acquisition of such precise 3D HOI data, often through motion capture systems (e.g., the GRAB dataset), is prohibitively expensive and typically remains constrained to a small number of objects and interaction scenarios. This limitation poses a significant challenge to the scalability and generalization of these methods.

Concurrently, advancements in large-scale video generative models have enabled the synthesis of high-fidelity 2D HOI videos across a diverse range of scenarios and object categories. These synthetic videos can depict complex dexterous manipulations that are difficult to capture with traditional motion capture systems. Despite this rich source of interaction knowledge, directly utilizing purely 2D, synthetic videos for physics-based character control in 3D environments presents its own set of challenges. The conversion of 2D video cues into precise 3D HOI motion cues is an ill-posed problem, primarily due to the inherent depth ambiguity and the difficulty in achieving accurate spatio-temporal alignment between objects and hands in 3D when lifted from 2D observations. While 3D human mesh recovery algorithms have shown progress in lifting human poses from images, reconstructing full 4D HOI (including precise object pose and interaction dynamics) from 2D video remains an open challenge.

DeVI positions itself to bridge this gap by developing a framework that leverages the scalability and diversity offered by text-conditioned synthetic videos for physics-based dexterous human-object interaction. It addresses the fundamental problem of extracting usable 3D information from 2D video for realistic physical simulation, thereby aiming to overcome the dependency on expensive 3D motion capture data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop DeVI (Dexterous Video Imitation), a novel framework that enables physically plausible dexterous agent control for interacting with previously unseen target objects. This is achieved by leveraging the capabilities of text-conditioned synthetic videos as a source of motion guidance.

The specific objectives and underlying motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Generative 2D Cues:** The motivation stems from the recognition that while video generative models can synthesize visually plausible 2D HOI videos, their inherent two-dimensionality and potential imprecision in 3D cues make them challenging to use directly for physics-based character control in 3D. A key objective is to devise a method to extract robust and usable 3D motion information from these 2D videos, specifically for dexterous manipulations.

2. **Eliminating Reliance on High-Quality 3D Kinematic Demonstrations:** Current state-of-the-art approaches often require expensive and labor-intensive 3D motion capture (mocap) demonstrations for learning complex HOI. A central motivation for DeVI is to circumvent this data bottleneck. By utilizing readily available synthetic videos, the framework aims to enable zero-shot generalization across a wide array of diverse objects and interaction types without the need for pre-captured 3D mocap data.

3. **Introducing a Hybrid Imitation Target:** The challenge of accurately reconstructing full 4D HOI from 2D video, particularly obtaining precise 6D object poses and spatio-temporal alignment between the human and the object, motivates the introduction of a hybrid imitation target. This target combines 3D reconstructed human motion (where 3D recovery is more robust) with robust 2D object trajectories (to avoid imprecise 3D object reconstruction). The objective is to provide sufficient, yet not over-constraining, cues for the reinforcement learning (RL) framework to infer a physically plausible 3D solution.

4. **Developing a Novel Hybrid Tracking Reward:** To facilitate the learning of a humanoid control policy from the proposed hybrid imitation targets, the research aims to design a specific reward function. This "hybrid tracking reward" integrates 3D human tracking components (e.g., joint positions, velocities, rotations) with 2D object tracking components and a contact reward, ensuring both accurate human pose imitation and realistic object interaction.

5. **Demonstrating Generalization and Diversity:** A significant objective is to showcase the framework's ability to generalize across diverse HOI scenarios, including interactions with novel objects sourced from the internet, multi-object scenes, and text-driven variations in actions. This aims to validate the utility of video generative models as flexible, HOI-aware motion planners.

6. **Validating Component Efficacy:** The research also seeks to specifically evaluate the effectiveness of its core components, such as the "Visual HOI Alignment" procedure, in refining the initial 3D human pose reconstruction to ensure better alignment with both the reference video and the 3D object in the scene, thereby enabling feasible hand-object interactions in simulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeVI operates as a novel framework for learning a physics-based humanoid control policy from synthetic 2D videos. The methodology encompasses scene initialization, 2D HOI video generation, extraction of hybrid imitation targets, and subsequent reinforcement learning.

**Scene Initialization:**
The framework focuses on tabletop scenarios. An initial scene `S = {H, O}` is defined, comprising a 3D human represented by the SMPL-X model and a target 3D object. The human is parameterized by SMPL-X shape, body pose, hand pose, global body root orientation, and translation. The object is defined by its global orientation and translation. Physically valid initial states are assumed, with objects placed on a table.

**2D HOI Video Generation:**
To enhance the realism of the synthesized video and improve 3D human mesh recovery, the generic SMPL-X surface mesh is replaced with a more realistic textured human mesh (from the THuman2.0 dataset). This textured mesh is deformed to match the scale, pose, and location of the initial SMPL-X model using an automatic rigging process. A camera with specified intrinsic and extrinsic parameters is used to render the initial scene, including the textured human, object, a fixed table, and background, into a 2D image `I`. This rendered image `I` serves as the initial frame for a pre-trained image-to-video diffusion model (Wan, with LightX2V LoRA for faster inference), which then generates a 2D HOI video `V = {I_t}` guided by a text prompt (e.g., "A person [action] [object] with [hand index]").

**Extracting Hybrid Imitation Targets:**
Directly using the 2D video `V` for 3D physics-based RL is challenging due to inherent limitations in 3D reconstruction from 2D. Therefore, DeVI extracts "hybrid imitation targets" `ˆg_hybrid = {ˆh, ˆo}`:
- `ˆh` represents estimated 3D human motion (body/hand joint locations and pose parameters).
- `ˆo` represents 2D object trajectories (tracked 2D points of visible object vertices).

The motivation for this hybrid approach is that accurate 3D object pose recovery from video remains difficult, and robust 3D spatio-temporal alignment between humans and objects is an ongoing research problem.

1. **Initializing 3D Human Reference:** An off-the-shelf monocular world-grounded human motion estimator (GVHMR) and a hand pose estimator (HaMeR) are applied to the synthesized 2D video `V` to provide initial estimates of 3D body motion and 3D hand poses. These outputs are combined to reconstruct a unified SMPL-X human motion sequence. The body pose is refined by adjusting wrist joint angles to incorporate the more accurate hand estimates. A global rigid transformation is then applied to align the reconstructed human's initial pose (`H_t=1`) with the initial SMPL-X human `H` in the input scene.

2. **Refining 3D Human Reference via Visual HOI Alignment:** The coarsely reconstructed SMPL-X human motion may not be perfectly aligned with the reference video or the 3D object. To address this, an optimization procedure called Visual HOI Alignment is introduced. This process optimizes body and hand pose parameters `{ˆθ_b_t}` and `{ˆθ_h_t}` by minimizing a total objective `L_total`. This objective includes:
 - `L_b` and `L_h`: 2D body and hand projection losses, aligning the projection of SMPL-X joints to the estimated 2D joints from the estimators.
 - `L_tc`: Temporal consistency loss, promoting smooth transitions between frames using geodesic distance for rotations.
 - `L_HOI`: An HOI loss, which is a one-sided Chamfer distance from specific SMPL-X body parts (e.g., left hand, as indicated by the text prompt) to the initialized object vertices. This loss encourages contact between the human and object at appropriate time instances. Only upper body poses are optimized to avoid issues with occluded lower body joints.

3. **Generating 2D Object Reference:** For the object, visible vertices of its 3D mesh are identified via ray casting and projected into 2D image space. These projected vertices are then tracked across the video frames using a video tracker (CoTracker3) to construct the 2D object reference `ˆo`. Vertices heavily occluded during tracking are filtered out.

**Learning Humanoid Control Policy:**
The control problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) to learn a control policy `π_θ` using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The humanoid character is represented by an SMPL-X model. The state `s_t` includes human and object components, and the action `a_t` defines PD target angles for all body and hand joints. The goal vector `g_t` consists of future `k` entities of the 3D human reference `ˆh`.

A "hybrid tracking reward" `R(s_t, a_t, g_t) = R_h * R_o * R_contact` is designed:
- **Human Tracking Reward (`R_h`):** Encourages the humanoid to track the synthetic 3D human motion `ˆh`. It is a product of joint position, velocity, and rotation rewards, local hand joint position and rotation rewards (wrist-centric), and a power penalty for smooth motion. All joint difference rewards are exponential functions of the negative squared error.
- **Object Tracking Reward (`R_o`):** Encourages the 2D projection of the simulated object to follow the reference 2D object trajectory `ˆo`. It is an exponential function of the negative squared error between the reference 2D points `ˆx_t` and the projection of the simulated object's visible vertices `x_t`.
- **Contact Reward (`R_contact`):** Encourages the humanoid to establish contact with the target object. It combines a contact force reward (`R_cf`) and a contact distance reward (`R_cd`). This reward is modulated by a binary contact label `ψ_t`, which is automatically inferred from the 2D object point motion in the generated video. This contact estimation pipeline analyzes pixel velocities of hand joints and object vertices to determine contact frames.

The actor-critic network architecture uses a transformer-based actor and MLP-based critic. The actor outputs PD targets, while the critic estimates the value function. Training is performed in Isaac Gym using 4096 environments, with updates via PPO. Early termination is implemented based on 3D body/fingertip joint distance errors and 2D object pixel distance errors to improve sample efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of DeVI was evaluated through extensive experiments, focusing on both qualitative and quantitative comparisons against established baselines.

**Qualitative Results:**
- **Simulated HOI for Various Objects:** DeVI demonstrated the ability to generate diverse physically plausible HOI motions for novel objects, driven by text prompts. This included both general interactions (e.g., picking up) and object-specific affordances (e.g., drinking from a coke can, wearing a hat), showcasing the video diffusion model's capability as an HOI-aware motion planner.
- **Efficacy of Visual HOI Alignment:** An ablation study confirmed the critical role of the Visual HOI Alignment process. Without this alignment, reconstructed 3D human motions exhibited significant misalignment with the reference video and the 3D object, particularly around hand-object interaction regions, leading to infeasible interactions. With alignment, the reconstructed 3D human motion was effectively aligned to both the video and the 3D object, enabling robust simulation of dexterous interactions.
- **Target Awareness and Text Controllability:** The framework successfully generated interactions in complex multi-object scenes (e.g., a frying pan on an induction cooker) without requiring explicit scene understanding, leveraging the video diffusion model's inherent scene perception. Furthermore, DeVI exhibited text controllability, producing distinct simulated motions from different text prompts for the same initial scene.
- **Comparison with Baselines and Non-Tabletop Scenarios:** Qualitative comparisons indicated that DeVI produced comparable or superior results to 3D demonstration-based baselines (PhysHOI, SkillMimic, InterMimic) in tracking human and object motion, despite relying solely on 2D object trajectories. The methodology was also shown to be applicable to non-tabletop scenarios like pushing or pick-and-place.

**Quantitative Results:**
- **Comparison with Baselines (GRAB Dataset):** DeVI was quantitatively compared against PhysHOI, SkillMimic, and InterMimic using the GRAB dataset. To ensure a fair comparison, 2D projections of 3D object vertices were used as imitation targets for DeVI, mirroring the 2D cues it extracts from synthetic videos. DeVI consistently outperformed all baselines across all reported metrics, including Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) for body, hand, and all joints, root joint error (`T_root`), object translation error (`T_obj`), and object orientation error (`O_obj`). For instance, DeVI achieved an average MPJPE (All) of approximately 25-41mm, significantly lower than the baselines which ranged from 91-142mm. The results suggest that DeVI's hybrid tracking reward, particularly its 2D object tracking component, effectively guides the policy to imitate reference motions, outperforming methods that use 6D object pose tracking rewards. This implies that 2D tracking provides a well-balanced guidance without the over-constraint that can hinder policy optimization with dense 6D rewards.
- **Ablation Study on Visual HOI Alignment:** The ablation study on Visual HOI Alignment provided quantitative evidence of its importance. This component significantly reduced pixel error (MPJPE in pixels) for hand joints (from 25.6 to 3.74) and all joints (from 27.4 to 11.0), indicating improved alignment with the video frames. Crucially, it also dramatically reduced the human-object contact distance (`d_HOI`) at contact frames (from 101mm to 18.7mm) and improved contact precision (`C_prec`), validating its role in generating human motion feasible for 3D interaction.
- **Success Ratio on GRAB:** DeVI demonstrated a higher success ratio on the GRAB dataset compared to baselines. For instance, when considering MPJPE (All) < 0.2m, `T_obj` < 0.2m, and `O_obj` < 1.0, DeVI achieved a 50.0% success rate, whereas InterMimic achieved 0.500%, SkillMimic 0.125%, and PhysHOI 0.0625%. This further substantiates the effectiveness of using 2D object trajectories as references, demonstrating that this relatively sparse signal can lead to more robust policy learning than precise 6D poses under certain conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DeVI introduces a significant advancement in the field of physics-based human-object interaction for robotic manipulation and virtual character control. The core contribution lies in its ability to leverage text-conditioned synthetic videos as a scalable and diverse source of motion planning, thereby addressing a critical bottleneck in the field: the prohibitive cost and limited availability of high-quality 3D motion capture data for complex dexterous interactions.

The potential impacts and significance of this work include:

1. **Reduced Data Dependency:** By circumventing the need for expensive 3D kinematic demonstrations, DeVI lowers the barrier to entry for developing and testing complex manipulation skills. This enables researchers to rapidly prototype and evaluate a broader range of HOI scenarios, accelerating progress in robotics and virtual reality.
2. **Enhanced Generalization and Diversity:** The framework's ability to operate in a zero-shot manner with diverse, unseen objects and to generate varied interactions from simple text prompts, including multi-object scenes, represents a substantial step towards more generalizable and versatile physics-based controllers. This leverages the inherent generalization capabilities of large-scale video generative models.
3. **Robustness to 2D-to-3D Challenges:** The introduction of "hybrid imitation targets" (3D human motion combined with 2D object trajectories) offers a practical solution to the inherent difficulties of precise 4D HOI reconstruction from 2D video. This pragmatic approach acknowledges the strengths and weaknesses of current 3D reconstruction techniques, providing a balanced set of cues for the reinforcement learning process.
4. **Improved Dexterous Manipulation:** The quantitative results demonstrate that DeVI outperforms existing methods in modeling dexterous hand-object interactions, indicating its utility for tasks requiring fine motor control. This can lead to more agile and precise robotic manipulators.
5. **Scalability for Robot Learning:** The methodology provides a scalable pathway for generating vast amounts of training data in simulation. This could significantly impact the development of robust and adaptable robot manipulation policies, as synthetic data generation can be automated and customized to specific needs.
6. **Future Avenues for Research:** The work highlights areas for future development, such as addressing perspective artifacts in video diffusion models (e.g., through multi-view generation) and refining automatic contact estimation (e.g., using affordance grounding). These directions point towards further improvements in the naturalness and physical accuracy of generated interactions.

In summary, DeVI represents a notable step towards creating scalable, diverse, and physically plausible dexterous human-object interactions in simulation, with broad implications for robotic learning, virtual character animation, and the broader understanding of complex physical interactions.
