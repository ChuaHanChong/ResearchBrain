# ViTAR: Vision Transformer with Any Resolution

> [!summary] Summary
> ViTAR introduces a Vision Transformer architecture capable of efficiently processing images of arbitrary resolution by integrating an Adaptive Token Merger (ATM) for computational reduction and Fuzzy Positional Encoding (FPE) for robust positional awareness. The model achieves high accuracy across various vision tasks while drastically reducing computational overhead, and is fully compatible with self-supervised learning frameworks like MAE.

## Problem

- Traditional Vision Transformers (ViTs) suffer significant performance degradation and encounter Out-of-Memory (OOM) errors when processing input images at resolutions different from or higher than their training resolution.
- Previous multi-resolution training methods, like ResFormer, have a limited effective resolution range and incur substantial computational costs when scaling, making them impractical for very high-resolution inputs.
- Existing resolution-robust positional encoding methods often introduce fixed spatial inductive biases (e.g., convolution-based) that are incompatible with powerful self-supervised learning (SSL) frameworks like Masked Autoencoders (MAE).

## Method

- The ==Adaptive Token Merger (ATM)== module iteratively processes and merges input tokens into a fixed, manageable number of "grid tokens" using a shared-weight ==GridAttention mechanism==, drastically reducing computational complexity at high resolutions.
- ==Fuzzy Positional Encoding (FPE)== introduces controlled positional perturbations (random uniform offsets) during training to build robust positional awareness and generalization across resolutions, without using fixed spatial inductive biases.
- ViTAR employs a simplified ==multi-resolution training strategy==, leveraging ATM's efficiency to train on a broader range of resolutions with consistent batch resolution and standard cross-entropy loss.

## Results

- ViTAR achieves up to **80.4%** ImageNet-1K accuracy at **4032×**4032 resolution, outperforming all baselines and operating successfully where others fail due to OOM errors (e.g., **1**7x** FLOPs reduction** at **1120×**1120, **49G** FLOPs vs. **863G** FLOPs).
- It demonstrates strong performance across diverse tasks, achieving **48.3 AP_b** on COCO2017 for object detection and **49.0 mIoU** on ADE20K for semantic segmentation, outperforming ResFormer by **0.6 points** in both cases.
- ViTAR integrates seamlessly with MAE self-supervised pre-training, maintaining high accuracy (**82.0%** at **4032×**4032) and resolution robustness, which standard ViT+MAE models fail to achieve (e.g., **13.4%** at **1792×**1792).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative and adaptive token merging through modules like ATM can significantly reduce the quadratic computational complexity of ViTs for high-resolution inputs, making them practical for real-world scenarios.
> - Introducing 'fuzziness' or controlled perturbation to positional encodings during training (FPE) is an effective strategy to improve resolution generalization and prevent overfitting to specific resolutions at inference time.
> - To maintain compatibility with powerful self-supervised learning techniques like MAE, resolution-robust positional encoding methods must avoid introducing fixed spatial inductive biases.
