# OVAMOS: A Framework for Open-Vocabulary Multi-Object Search in Unknown Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> The OVAMOS framework from the University of Michigan integrates Vision-Language Models, frontier-based exploration, and a POMDP approach to enhance open-vocabulary multi-object search in unknown environments. It achieved a 55.0% success rate and 0.497 MSPL in simulations, nearly doubling the performance of prior methods, and demonstrated robust recovery from occlusions in a real-world office setting.

## Problem

- Unstable object detection and partial occlusions in complex environments lead to significant observation uncertainty, especially in multi-object search tasks.
- Robots struggle with robustly recovering from failed detections and adaptively replanning when target objects are not immediately visible.
- Prior foundation model-guided multi-object search systems often abandon promising regions if initial detections fail, leading to suboptimal exploration paths.

## Method

- Developed **OVAMOS**, a unified framework integrating ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== for semantic understanding, frontier-based exploration, and a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) for robust planning.
- Introduced a VLM-guided POMDP to manage observation uncertainty and simplify belief updates using a Bayesian-inspired value decay function.
- Employed POUCT (Partially Observable Upper Confidence Trees) to efficiently solve the POMDP, enabling adaptive replanning and recovery from detection failures.

## Results

- OVAMOS achieved a **55.0%** success rate and **0.497** Multi-Object Success weighted by normalized inverse Path Length (MSPL) in simulated environments, outperforming prior methods like Finder (**28.3%** SR, **0.198** MSPL).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of POMDP-based planning and the value decay mechanism, which individually contributed to improved success rates and efficiency.
- A real-world deployment on a TurtleBot in a 50 m^2 office environment successfully located three objects, showcasing robust recovery and adaptive replanning for an initially occluded target.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating VLM-based semantic reasoning with a POMDP framework allows for principled decision-making under partial observability, effectively managing observation uncertainty.
> - A value decay mechanism applied to the object value maps is crucial for preventing the robot from repeatedly revisiting regions that falsely retain high-value scores.
> - Combining frontier-based exploration with targeted moves to VLM-guided candidate points, within a POMDP, enables robust recovery from occlusions and missed detections.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Object search is a foundational challenge in robotics, particularly for autonomous agents operating in complex indoor environments. The ability of robots to locate and retrieve multiple specified objects, known as Multi-Object Search (MOS), is critical for tasks in settings such as workplaces and households. This research builds upon and addresses limitations within existing paradigms for object search, which generally fall into three categories: probabilistic planning, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), and foundation model-based methods.

Probabilistic planning methods, often formulated as Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs), have been utilized to manage uncertainty in object locations and perception. While effective for tracking multiple objects and facilitating thorough exploration by maintaining belief states, these methods face computational challenges when scaled to large, complex environments due to the inherent complexity of belief state updates over extended planning horizons.

DRL-based approaches train robots through repeated environmental interactions to develop search strategies. These methods can learn effective policies but often exhibit inefficiencies in exploration and limited generalizability, particularly when deployed in novel or unseen environments, requiring extensive training data.

More recently, foundation model-based approaches, leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) or Vision-Language Models (VLMs), have emerged. These models enhance object search by incorporating semantic reasoning about object-scene and object-object relationships. Initial applications primarily focused on Single-Object Search (SOS), improving target localization efficiency. However, existing multi-object search frameworks that incorporate foundation models, such as Finder, often do not adequately address the complexities arising from observation uncertainty (e.g., occlusions, detector noise) and the need for robust recovery mechanisms in intricate, partially observed spaces. Such methods typically rely on frontier-based exploration, which may not revisit promising areas if initial detections fail, leading to suboptimal search paths or failures in finding occluded objects.

The OVAMOS framework positions itself at the intersection of these paradigms. It integrates VLM-based reasoning, frontier-based exploration, and a POMDP framework to combine the semantic understanding and generalizability of foundation models with the uncertainty management and robust planning capabilities of probabilistic methods, while seeking to mitigate their respective drawbacks. The work aims to overcome the limitations of prior foundation model-guided MOS systems that struggle with observation uncertainty and the recovery from failed detections.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient framework for Multi-Object Search (MOS) in unknown, complex indoor environments, specifically addressing challenges posed by observation instability, occlusions, and the need for adaptive replanning.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges inherent in robotic object search:
1. **Observation Uncertainty:** Object detection in real-world environments is often unstable. Factors such as partial occlusion, poor lighting conditions, or the detector's limitations can lead to missed detections or incorrect identifications, as illustrated by a robot failing to recognize a partially occluded water cup. This uncertainty is exacerbated in MOS tasks, where robots must track multiple objects simultaneously.
2. **Recovery from Failures and Replanning:** When initial observations are unreliable or objects are not immediately visible, the robot must be capable of recovering from detection failures and replanning its actions. Existing foundation model-based MOS frameworks, while improving initial search efficiency through semantic priors, often lack mechanisms to systematically revisit regions or generate alternative viewpoints when objects are missed. Their reliance on continuously exploring new frontiers can lead to abandonment of valuable areas.
3. **Exploration in Novel Environments:** Robots are frequently deployed in environments they have not encountered before. Efficient exploration strategies are necessary to map these unknown spaces and identify potential object locations without relying on extensive pre-training or environmental priors.
4. **Computational Efficiency for Multi-Object Search:** While POMDPs are effective for managing uncertainty, their computational cost significantly increases with environmental complexity and the number of objects, making them impractical for large-scale MOS problems. Deep Reinforcement Learning methods, on the other hand, can be computationally expensive to train and often struggle with generalizing to novel scenarios.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a novel integration of existing techniques:
- **VLM-based Reasoning:** To enhance search efficiency by leveraging the semantic understanding of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). VLMs infer relationships between objects and their environment, providing valuable cues for where objects might be located, even without direct visual confirmation.
- **Frontier-Based Exploration:** To guide navigation and systematically explore unknown regions of the environment, ensuring comprehensive coverage.
- **Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) Framework:** To explicitly model observation uncertainty and enable the robot to plan actions under partial observability. This allows for principled decision-making, including the ability to recover from detection failures by seeking additional viewpoints or revisiting promising areas. The framework aims to simplify POMDP belief updates and action selection through VLM guidance, thereby mitigating the typical computational challenges of large-scale POMDPs.

The key contributions outlined by the authors are:
- The development of OVAMOS, a unified open-vocabulary multi-object search framework that integrates VLMs, frontier-based exploration, and POMDP-based planning for efficient and robust search.
- The introduction of VLM-guided POMDP, which leverages VLMs to simplify belief updates and action selection within the POMDP, improving uncertainty handling while managing computational complexity.
- Extensive evaluation in 120 simulated scenarios across Habitat-Matterport3D (HM3D) scenes and a real-world robot experiment in a 50 m^2 office environment, demonstrating improvements in efficiency and success rate over baseline methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The OVAMOS framework consists of three primary modules: a mapping module, a planning module, and a navigation controller, designed to enable a mobile robot to search for a set of K static target objects in an unknown environment while minimizing travel distance.

#### 3.1. A. Problem Formulation

The robot's state at time *t* is defined by its 2D position and orientation: x_r(t) = (x, y, ϕ) ∈ R^3. The environment contains static objects, O_env, from which a subset of K target objects, O_tgt, must be located at unknown positions. The objective is to locate all objects in O_tgt, minimizing cumulative travel distance.

#### 3.2. B. Mapping Module

This module processes sensor inputs and textual prompts to create a representation of the environment.

1. **Multi-Layer Value Map and Object Map:**
 - A pre-trained BLIP-2 Vision-Language Model is used to compute cosine similarity scores between the robot's current RGB observation and text prompts for each target object.
 - A cone-shaped confidence mask is generated for the camera's Field of View (FOV), with confidence decreasing from the optical axis.
 - Value maps for different target objects are updated using a weighted averaging technique that biases towards higher confidence values, handling overlapping observations over time.
 - These individual value maps are then normalized and aggregated to form a shared value representation, treating objects as independent for joint belief computation.
 - For precise object detection, models such as Grounding DINO, YOLOv7, and Segment Anything Model (SAM) are employed to detect, segment, and store identified objects in an "object map."

2. **Obstacle Map and Frontier Map:**
 - Depth and odometry data are used to construct a top-down 2D obstacle map, identifying non-traversable regions.
 - Frontiers, defined as boundaries between explored and unexplored areas, are extracted from the obstacle map. Midpoints along these boundaries serve as potential frontier waypoints, guiding exploration into unknown spaces.

#### 3.3. C. Planning Module

This module maintains a belief representation of object locations and uses a planning algorithm to select optimal actions. The planning is framed as an Object-Oriented POMDP (OO-POMDP).

1. **Update the Action Space and Belief with Candidate Points and Selected Frontier:**
 - The raw value map v(x,y) is processed:
 - The frontier with the highest value is selected as a representative exploratory point (x_f, y_f).
 - A Bayesian-inspired decay function is applied to the value map: v'(x, y) = 1 / (1 + exp((u(x,y) - τ) / κ)), where u(x,y) is the update count and τ, κ are decay constants. This function causes values to decrease over time if objects are not detected in those regions, preventing fixation on misleading high-value areas.
 - After thresholding v'(x,y) to isolate high-value regions, DBSCAN clustering is applied to yield a set of discrete candidate points C = {(x_ci, y_ci)}.
 - The **action space A** is defined as the union of actions to move to candidate points (A_cand) and an action to move to the selected frontier (A_frontier). This balances targeted search with general exploration.
 - The **belief b_t** over the target object's location is represented as a discrete probability distribution over these candidate points, where p_i is proportional to the decayed value v'(x_ci, y_ci).
 - During POMDP simulations, the standard Bayesian rule is used for belief updates. However, for real-world execution, the belief update is approximated directly using the decayed value map. This simplification bypasses explicit reliance on a complex observation model while maintaining adaptability, as the decayed value map inherently reflects updated object likelihoods based on detector information.

2. **POMDP Components:**
 - **State Space (S):** Combines the robot's 2D position (s_r) and the target object's 2D position (s_t).
 - **Observation Space (O):** A binary indicator {0, 1} representing whether the target object is detected or not.
 - **Action Space (A):** "MoveTo(g)" commands, where g is either a candidate point or the selected frontier.
 - **Transition Model (T(s, a, s')):** Target objects are static. The robot's state transitions deterministically based on the chosen action.
 - **Observation Function (O):** Pr(o=1 | s', a) models detection probability, which is 1 if the robot is within a detection threshold δ of the target, and exponentially decays with increasing distance beyond δ.
 - **Reward Function (R(s, a)):** Consists of three components: a negative reward for distance traveled (λ_move * d(s_r, s'_r)), a positive reward for exploring frontiers (λ_frontier * I(a ∈ A_frontier)), and a positive reward for being within detection range of a target (λ_target * I(d(s'_r, s_t) ≤ δ)).
 - **Discount Factor (γ):** A constant (0, 1) balancing immediate and future rewards.

To solve this POMDP efficiently, the **Partially Observable Upper Confidence Trees (POUCT)** algorithm is employed. POUCT is a Monte Carlo Tree Search-based method that builds a search tree using a generative function to simulate transitions, observations, and rewards, thereby selecting the optimal next action. After each real-world step, the maps are updated, new candidate points and frontiers are extracted, and the action space and belief are refreshed for the next planning cycle.

#### 3.4. D. Navigation Controller

This module receives the target location from the planning module and translates it into discrete movement commands (move forward, turn left, turn right) for the robot to execute.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The framework was evaluated through both simulated experiments and real-world robot deployment.

#### 4.1. A. Simulation Experiments

1. **Setup:** Experiments were conducted in 120 simulated episodes across five scenes from the Habitat-Matterport3D (HM3D) dataset. Each episode involved searching for three target objects, with the robot initialized at a random location. An object was considered found if the robot was within 1 meter of it when calling "stop." Episodes terminated upon finding all objects or reaching a maximum of 500 steps. Key parameters included reward weights (λ_move=20, λ_frontier=500, λ_target=500), a discount factor (γ=0.95), a detection threshold (δ=1 m), and a decay parameter (β=1).
2. **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Success Rate (SR):** Percentage of episodes where all target objects were successfully found.
 - **Multi-Object Success weighted by normalized inverse Path Length (MSPL):** A measure combining success with efficiency, normalized by the optimal path length.
3. **Comparison Baselines:**
 - **Random Walk:** A naive movement strategy (lower bound).
 - **VLFM:** A state-of-the-art Single-Object Search (SOS) method combining VLM-generated value maps with frontier-based exploration, adapted for MOS by sequential SOS tasks.
 - **Finder:** A state-of-the-art MOS method that uses multi-layer value maps and object-to-object scores to guide frontier selection.
4. **Results and Analysis:**
 - As summarized in Table I, OVAMOS demonstrated superior performance compared to all baselines. Random Walk had an SR of 0.0% and MSPL of 0.0. VLFM achieved an SR of 12.5% and an MSPL of 0.075, indicating its limitations in multi-object tasks. Finder improved upon VLFM with an SR of 28.3% and an MSPL of 0.198 by tracking multiple objects. OVAMOS significantly surpassed Finder, achieving an SR of 55.0% and an MSPL of 0.497. This represents nearly double the success rate and more than double the MSPL compared to Finder, highlighting the increased accuracy and efficiency attributed to the integrated POMDP-based planning.
 - Qualitative analysis (Fig. 3) illustrates a key advantage of OVAMOS: its ability to recover from occlusions and missed detections. In scenarios where an object (e.g., a toilet) was initially occluded, Finder, relying solely on frontier exploration, would abandon the region. In contrast, OVAMOS, guided by its value map and POMDP planning, recognized the region's potential and continued exploration, eventually finding the object.
5. **Ablation Study:**
 - An ablation study (Table II) investigated the impact of core OVAMOS components. Removing the POMDP-based planning ("OVAMOS w/o POMDP") resulted in an SR of 27.5% and MSPL of 0.154, performing worse than the full OVAMOS. This indicated that directly selecting the highest-value frontier or candidate point (a greedy strategy) without considering navigation effort or balancing exploration/exploitation led to suboptimal performance.
 - Disabling the value decay mechanism ("OVAMOS w/o value decay") led to a success rate of 45.0% and MSPL of 0.410, a drop from the full framework. This demonstrated the importance of value decay in preventing the robot from repeatedly exploring regions that maintain high-value scores despite not containing the target object, thus avoiding getting trapped and improving search effectiveness.

#### 4.2. B. Physical Experiments

1. **Setup:** OVAMOS was deployed on a TurtleBot platform in a cluttered 50 m^2 office environment. BLIP was used instead of BLIP-2 due to GPU memory constraints. The robot was tasked with finding three objects: a chair, a fridge (easily detectable), and an umbrella (challenging, occluded by a trash can).
2. **Results:** The complete task was executed in 6 minutes and 56 seconds. The fridge and chair were detected relatively quickly (at 2'41" and 4'01", respectively). The umbrella remained hidden for an extended period due to unfavorable viewpoints. After the environment had been fully explored and no new frontiers were available (from 5'43" onward), the robot continuously replanned its trajectory based on the updated value map information. This iterative replanning eventually led the robot to an effective viewpoint from which the occluded umbrella was detected. This real-world validation demonstrated OVAMOS's recovery and replanning capabilities, particularly in scenarios where the environment is fully explored but target objects remain undetected.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OVAMOS framework represents an advancement in the field of multi-object search for autonomous robots operating in unknown and complex environments. By integrating Vision-Language Models (VLMs), frontier-based exploration, and a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) framework, the research addresses critical limitations of prior approaches, particularly concerning observation uncertainty, robust recovery from detection failures, and efficient exploration in novel settings.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Robustness and Efficiency:** The framework's ability to nearly double the success rate and more than double the MSPL compared to existing state-of-the-art methods like Finder in simulated environments suggests a notable improvement in both the effectiveness and efficiency of multi-object search. The real-world experiments further corroborate its robustness in practical, cluttered settings, demonstrating adaptive replanning and successful detection of occluded objects.
- **Principled Uncertainty Management:** The VLM-guided POMDP allows for more principled decision-making under partial observability. By modeling observation uncertainty and incorporating a value decay mechanism, OVAMOS can prioritize candidate points and avoid getting trapped in misleading high-value regions, a common challenge in purely reactive or greedy exploration strategies.
- **Improved Generalizability:** Leveraging open-vocabulary VLMs allows the robot to understand and search for a wide range of objects without requiring specific training data for each target, enhancing the framework's adaptability to novel objects and environments.
- **Recovery from Detection Failures:** The integration of POMDP planning provides the robot with a mechanism to systematically revisit promising areas and seek additional viewpoints when initial detections fail due to occlusions or sensor noise. This capability is critical for reliable object retrieval in dynamic and cluttered real-world scenarios.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge several limitations of the current framework. It is presently constrained to 2D environments, which simplifies the planning space but may not fully represent the complexity of real-world 3D tasks. The robot's action space is relatively simple, primarily focusing on 2D navigation. Additionally, the camera viewpoint remains fixed, limiting the robot's ability to actively adjust its perception to resolve ambiguities or improve object visibility. These constraints limit the immediate applicability of the method to the full complexity of real-world MOS tasks.

**Potential Impact:** Despite its current limitations, OVAMOS lays groundwork for more capable and autonomous robotic systems. Its advancements in robust, open-vocabulary multi-object search can potentially impact various applications, including:
- **Service Robotics:** Enabling robots in workplaces or households to reliably gather multiple items for tasks (e.g., retrieving tools for assembly, finding ingredients for cooking).
- **Logistics and Warehousing:** Improving the efficiency of robots tasked with inventory management or order fulfillment, especially in environments with varying item visibility.
- **Search and Rescue:** Developing more effective autonomous agents for locating multiple specified items in unknown or hazardous environments.

The authors propose future work that includes extending the search space to 3D environments, incorporating more complex actions such as manipulating occlusions, and enabling dynamic camera adjustments to obtain better viewpoints. These enhancements are intended to further improve the adaptability and effectiveness of this approach, contributing to the development of more versatile and intelligent robotic agents.
