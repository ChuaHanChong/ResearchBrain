# OVAMOS: A Framework for Open-Vocabulary Multi-Object Search in Unknown Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> The OVAMOS framework from the University of Michigan integrates Vision-Language Models, frontier-based exploration, and a POMDP approach to enhance open-vocabulary multi-object search in unknown environments. It achieved a 55.0% success rate and 0.497 MSPL in simulations, nearly doubling the performance of prior methods, and demonstrated robust recovery from occlusions in a real-world office setting.

## Problem

- Unstable object detection and partial occlusions in complex environments lead to significant observation uncertainty, especially in multi-object search tasks.
- Robots struggle with robustly recovering from failed detections and adaptively replanning when target objects are not immediately visible.
- Prior foundation model-guided multi-object search systems often abandon promising regions if initial detections fail, leading to suboptimal exploration paths.

## Method

- Developed **OVAMOS**, a unified framework integrating ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== for semantic understanding, frontier-based exploration, and a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) for robust planning.
- Introduced a VLM-guided POMDP to manage observation uncertainty and simplify belief updates using a Bayesian-inspired value decay function.
- Employed POUCT (Partially Observable Upper Confidence Trees) to efficiently solve the POMDP, enabling adaptive replanning and recovery from detection failures.

## Results

- OVAMOS achieved a **55.0%** success rate and **0.497** Multi-Object Success weighted by normalized inverse Path Length (MSPL) in simulated environments, outperforming prior methods like Finder (**28.3%** SR, **0.198** MSPL).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of POMDP-based planning and the value decay mechanism, which individually contributed to improved success rates and efficiency.
- A real-world deployment on a TurtleBot in a 50 m^2 office environment successfully located three objects, showcasing robust recovery and adaptive replanning for an initially occluded target.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating VLM-based semantic reasoning with a POMDP framework allows for principled decision-making under partial observability, effectively managing observation uncertainty.
> - A value decay mechanism applied to the object value maps is crucial for preventing the robot from repeatedly revisiting regions that falsely retain high-value scores.
> - Combining frontier-based exploration with targeted moves to VLM-guided candidate points, within a POMDP, enables robust recovery from occlusions and missed detections.
