# HyperSim: A Holistic Sim-To-Real Framework For Robust Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> HyperSim, developed by CloudRobo Lab, Huawei, presents a holistic framework for robust sim-to-real transfer in robotic manipulation through high-fidelity environment synthesis, adversarial trajectory generation, and co-training with real data. The approach achieved up to a 95% overall success rate for a deep-bin transfer task on a humanoid robot using few-shot co-training and demonstrated enhanced robustness to dynamic perturbations.

## Problem

- Acquiring sufficient high-quality real-world robotic interaction data is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, hindering the development of data-driven policies and robot foundation models.
- Existing simulation environments are oversimplified, lacking the visual realism and geometric complexity of real-world settings.
- Current synthetic data generation methods produce limited data diversity and suffer from a persistent sim-to-real gap, leading to policies that do not generalize well to physical robots.

## Method

- Employs a hybrid scene synthesis strategy combining a constraint-aware generator for interactive foregrounds with Geometry-aware 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== for photorealistic and geometrically accurate backgrounds.
- Implements an adversarial trajectory generation mechanism that injects perturbations into target objects during simulated interactions to broaden the state-action distribution and foster recovery behaviors.
- Utilizes a sim-and-real co-training strategy that jointly optimizes robot policies using abundant synthetic data and limited real-world demonstrations to learn domain-invariant representations.

## Results

- Zero-shot transfer with the full HyperSim framework (3DGS-ADSim) achieved up to a **75%** overall success rate (SR₃) for π₀ policies on a deep-bin transfer task without any real-world data.
- Few-shot co-training, using only 35 human demonstrations, further boosted the π₀ policy's overall success rate (SR₃) to **95%** on the physical robot.
- Policies trained with adversarial trajectories demonstrated superior robustness to dynamic perturbations, achieving a **35%** higher first-attempt success rate (SR₁) compared to non-adversarial baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating high-fidelity visual rendering via 3D Gaussian Splatting with adversarially diversified training data significantly reduces the visual and dynamic sim-to-real gap.
> - Adversarial trajectory generation, which includes dynamic perturbations and recovery actions, is crucial for fostering robust policies capable of handling unexpected real-world uncertainties.
> - Foundation models benefit substantially from high-quality synthetic data, achieving high zero-shot transfer performance and further improving with minimal real-world co-training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is transitioning towards data-driven, end-to-end policy learning, influenced by advancements in multimodal large models. Unlike vision and language models that leverage vast internet-scale data, embodied agents require complex action-observation sequences detailing physical interactions. The acquisition of such high-quality real-world robotic datasets is resource-intensive, involving substantial hardware and human labor. This challenge is exacerbated by the increasing parameter count and data demands of emerging robot foundation models, making reliance solely on physical data impractical.

Synthetic data generation offers a scalable alternative to address this data bottleneck. However, existing simulation methodologies face three primary limitations:
1. **Oversimplified Environments:** Current simulations often feature generic settings (e.g., a floating table against a void background) that fail to capture the complexity and clutter of real-world environments. This limits visual realism and geometric accuracy.
2. **Constrained Data Diversity:** Data generated often suffers from heuristic scene configurations, such as fixed object orientations, and predominantly consists of homogeneous, successful-only trajectories. This restricts the coverage of the state-action manifold, leading to policies that may struggle with real-world variability.
3. **Persistent Sim-to-Real Gap:** Significant visual and dynamic mismatches between simulation and reality continue to degrade the performance of policies trained in simulation when deployed in physical environments. Traditional sim-to-real transfer methods often rely on domain randomization or system identification, which can require extensive domain expertise and parameter tuning.

This work, HyperSim, positions itself within this landscape by proposing a holistic framework designed to systematically address these limitations across data generation, policy training, and real-world deployment. It seeks to bridge the sim-to-real gap by enhancing visual fidelity, expanding data coverage, and promoting domain-invariant representations, thereby supporting the development of more generalizable and robust robotic manipulation policies, particularly for foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of HyperSim is to develop a comprehensive framework that enables robust sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation policies, mitigating the limitations of existing synthetic data generation and transfer methodologies. The motivation for this objective stems from the challenges in scaling data for embodied intelligence, specifically:

- **Addressing the Data Bottleneck:** The prohibitive cost and complexity of collecting real-world robotic interaction data necessitate scalable synthetic data generation. HyperSim aims to provide an efficient and effective alternative to physical data acquisition.
- **Enhancing Environmental Realism:** Existing simulation environments are often too simplistic. A key motivation is to synthesize photorealistic and geometrically accurate environments that better mimic the complexity and unstructured nature of real-world settings, thereby reducing the visual sim-to-real gap.
- **Expanding Data Diversity and Coverage:** Current synthetic data often lacks diversity, leading to policies that overfit to specific scenarios. HyperSim aims to generate a wider range of physically plausible scenes and trajectories, including those that involve recovery from perturbations, to improve policy robustness and generalization across a broader state-action manifold.
- **Bridging Visual and Dynamic Discrepancies:** The inherent visual and dynamic mismatches between simulation and reality are critical barriers to effective sim-to-real transfer. The framework is motivated to systematically close this gap through a multi-faceted approach rather than relying on isolated solutions.
- **Supporting End-to-End Policy Learning:** With the rise of data-driven, end-to-end policy learning and robot foundation models, there is a need for high-quality, diverse data that facilitates generalization. HyperSim aims to provide such data and training strategies to empower these advanced models.
- **Improving Policy Robustness:** Policies trained on homogenous data often perform poorly under unexpected real-world dynamic uncertainties. A specific motivation is to make policies more robust to such perturbations by incorporating adversarial elements into the training data generation.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
1. Introduce a holistic framework (HyperSim) that systematically bridges the sim-to-real gap.
2. Propose a hybrid scene synthesis strategy for photorealistic and geometrically accurate environments.
3. Develop an adversarial trajectory generation mechanism to expand state-action coverage and enforce policy recovery behaviors.
4. Implement a sim-and-real co-training strategy to learn domain-invariant representations.
5. Rigorously validate the framework's real-world transferability and policy robustness through extensive physical experiments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HyperSim is designed as a two-layer framework that separates scalable data generation from advanced domain transfer techniques. It integrates three core functional modules to systematically bridge the sim-to-real gap: high-fidelity environment synthesis, adversarial trajectory generation, and sim-and-real co-training.

#### 3.1. High-Fidelity Simulation Environment

The framework employs a hybrid scene synthesis strategy that decouples the environment into a foreground manipulation area and a surrounding background.

- **Interactive Foreground:** For the manipulation area, HyperSim utilizes a constraint-aware generator to create diverse, physically plausible workspaces. This involves a library of 18 spatial relation solvers categorized into unary geometric priors, explicit pairwise relations, and implicit multi-object formations. Task-specific foreground requirements are translated into a subset of these constraints to generate valid layouts. These layouts are then populated with high-fidelity 3D assets from databases or text-to-3D models, ensuring physical interactivity.
- **Photorealistic Background:** To minimize the visual sim-to-real gap, the background is reconstructed using Geometry-aware Gaussian Splatting (GPGS). This involves collecting synchronized multimodal data (RGB, LiDAR, IMU) and leveraging geometric priors from fused LiDAR scans to reconstruct the environment into Gaussian primitives. These primitives are spatially aligned with LiDAR-defined surfaces, allowing for high-fidelity novel view synthesis of both color and depth maps. The parameters of Gaussian primitives (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients) are optimized. During rendering, 3D Gaussians are projected and alpha-blended to generate pixel color and depth values. A colorized mesh structure, structurally aligned with the Gaussian representation, is then generated using the TSDF algorithm from the rendered color and depth maps. This hybrid Gaussian-mesh representation allows 3D Gaussian splats for photorealistic rendering and underlying meshes for precise collision and contact dynamics in standard physics simulators.

#### 3.2. Adversarial Trajectory Generation

This module focuses on expanding the state-action distribution and inducing recovery behaviors by generating adversarial trajectories.

- **Piecewise Trajectory Generation:** Manipulation tasks are decomposed into a sequence of object-centric subtasks. For each subtask, a "bottleneck pose" is defined where the Tool Center Point (TCP) enters a small hemisphere around the target object's center of mass. This partitions the subtask into an "approaching primitive" (initial state to bottleneck pose) and an "interaction primitive" (object contact and manipulation). These primitives are synthesized piecewise using motion planners, inverse kinematics solvers, and gripper controllers.
- **Adversarial Perturbation and Recovery:** When the gripper reaches the bottleneck pose, abrupt perturbations (translation and rotation) are injected into the target object's state. This forces the motion planner to compute a dynamic recovery trajectory towards a newly updated bottleneck pose, requiring continuous repositioning and reorientation of the end-effector. This perturbation-recovery cycle serves to broaden the spatial coverage of the target's state distribution and expose exteroceptive sensors to diverse viewpoints during recovery. The maximum number of perturbations per trajectory is empirically limited to three to balance data diversity with motion stability.

#### 3.3. Sim-and-Real Co-Training

Co-training aims to learn domain-invariant representations by jointly optimizing robot policies over a mixture of simulated and real-world data.

- **Formulation:** The problem is formulated as minimizing a behavioral cloning loss, $L_D_α = αL_D_s + (1 − α)L_D_r$, where $D_s$ is the simulation data and $D_r$ is the real-world data. The quantity of simulation data ($|D_s|$) is significantly larger than real data ($|D_r|$).
- **Co-training Ratio (α):** The parameter $\alpha \in$ represents the probability of drawing a training sample from the simulation dataset. A value of $\alpha=1$ corresponds to zero-shot deployment (training solely on simulation data). For co-training (few-shot strategy), $\alpha$ is set marginally less than 1, incorporating a small amount of real-world data to encourage the policy to extract features that generalize across domains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation involved a large-scale empirical study with over 400 real-world task executions on a Galaxea R1 humanoid robot. The task involved transferring a target object from a deep bin to an adjacent bin, a challenging scenario due to kinematic constraints and collision risks. Two representative policies, ACT and π₀, were evaluated using three fine-grained metrics:
- **Target Alignment Rate (TAR):** Success rate of the end-effector navigating to the target's bottleneck pose.
- **First-Attempt Success Rate (SR₁):** Percentage of trials completed successfully without retries.
- **Overall Success Rate (SR₃):** Percentage of trials completed within three attempts.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Transfer Capabilities

Experiments assessed policies trained exclusively on simulated data (α=1).
- **Impact of Visual Fidelity and Adversarial Diversity (Table I):**
 - Progressive integration of enhancement modules (BaseSim → ADSim → 3DGS-ADSim) consistently improved performance.
 - **ADSim** (Adversarial Data) substantially elevated the Target Alignment Rate (TAR) compared to **BaseSim**. This is attributed to the adversarial mechanism exposing policies to sudden target displacements, leading to a broader and more uniform target state distribution (Fig. 7) and fostering closed-loop visuo-motor alignment.
 - **3DGS-ADSim** (ADSim with 3DGS rendering) yielded an additional performance gain of up to 10% across all metrics. This indicates that high-fidelity rendering reduces the visual domain gap, grounding synthetic observations in physical reality (Fig. 8).
 - Policies showed recovery capabilities; SR₃ was up to 15% higher than SR₁ for 3DGS-ADSim, indicating successful recovery from initial execution errors.
- **Synergy Between Foundation Models and Synthetic Data (Table I):**
 - The **π₀** policy consistently and significantly outperformed the **ACT** policy, with a 25–55% margin across all metrics under identical training conditions. For example, 3DGS-ADSim with π₀ achieved an SR₃ of 75%. This suggests that the generalization priors inherent in large-scale pre-trained models (like π₀) synergize effectively with high-quality synthetic data, lowering the zero-shot sim-to-real transfer barrier.

#### 4.2. Few-Shot Co-Training Efficacy

This evaluation incorporated a small set of 35 human demonstrations ($Real_{35}$) for co-training ($\alpha < 1$).
- **Performance Gains (Table II):** Co-training consistently improved performance for both ACT and π₀ policies compared to simulation-only baselines.
 - For the **ACT** policy, which exhibited limited zero-shot transfer, co-training with real data (e.g., $Real_{35}$ &ADSim vs. ADSim) provided significant physical grounding, leading to a substantial increase of over 35% in both SR₁ and SR₃.
 - For the **π₀** policy, which already demonstrated strong zero-shot capabilities, co-training (e.g., $Real_{35}$ &3DGS-ADSim) further narrowed the sim-to-real gap, elevating all metrics by over 15% and achieving an SR₃ of 95%.
- **Comparison with Real-Only Baselines (Table III & Table VI):** Policies trained with co-training outperformed their real-only counterparts (e.g., $Real_{35}$ &ADSim vs. $Real_{35}$ alone). This indicates that the high-quality synthetic data effectively augments limited real-world demonstrations, preventing overfitting and boosting overall performance. The positive correlation between performance and real-world data quantity was observed, but co-trained policies consistently surpassed real-only policies.

#### 4.3. Dynamic Robustness

A dynamic perturbation test was conducted where a human operator altered the target object's state during online policy inference.
- **Enhanced Robustness (Table IV):** Policies trained with adversarial trajectories (both $Real_{35}$ &ADSim and $Real_{35}$ &3DGS-ADSim) exhibited superior robustness compared to the non-adversarial counterpart ($Real_{35}$ &BaseSim). The SR₁ for adversarial-trained policies was 60%, a 35% improvement over the 25% achieved by the non-adversarial baseline. This is attributed to the adversarial mechanism, which integrates dynamic uncertainties into the training data, fostering a stronger capability to recover from unexpected perturbations during real-world inference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HyperSim demonstrates a systematic and effective approach to bridging the sim-to-real gap for robotic manipulation, which is a critical challenge in scaling embodied intelligence. The significance of this work lies in its multi-faceted strategy and the empirical validation of its components:

- **Holistic Framework:** By integrating high-fidelity environment construction, adversarial trajectory generation, and sim-and-real co-training, HyperSim provides a comprehensive solution addressing visual fidelity, state-space distribution, and cross-domain data representation discrepancies. This integrated approach offers a more robust path to sim-to-real transfer than isolated methods.
- **Enhanced Data Fidelity and Coverage:** The hybrid scene synthesis strategy combining constraint-aware foreground generation with geometry-aware 3D Gaussian Splatting for photorealistic backgrounds significantly improves the realism and geometric accuracy of simulated environments. The adversarial trajectory generation expands the state-action manifold, exposing policies to diverse scenarios and promoting learning of recovery behaviors, which is crucial for real-world deployment.
- **Improved Policy Robustness:** The explicit incorporation of dynamic uncertainties through adversarial perturbations during training leads to policies that are demonstrably more robust to unexpected environmental changes and physical disturbances. This is a crucial step towards deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic real-world settings.
- **Synergy with Foundation Models:** The findings highlight a strong synergy between high-quality synthetic data and large-scale pre-trained foundation models (like π₀). This suggests that advanced models, when combined with well-designed synthetic data pipelines, can achieve high zero-shot transferability and benefit further from minimal real-world data through co-training. This can dramatically lower the data requirements for deploying complex robotic skills.
- **Scalability for Embodied AI:** By providing a framework for generating scalable, high-fidelity, and diverse synthetic data, HyperSim contributes to overcoming the data bottleneck in embodied AI research. This can accelerate the development and generalization of robot foundation models and complex manipulation tasks.
- **Practical Implications:** The achieved high success rates (up to 95% SR₃ with π₀ in few-shot co-training) indicate the practical applicability of HyperSim for real-world robotic tasks, even challenging ones like deep-bin picking.

Despite the advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, primarily the current empirical validation being constrained to a specific task suite and a single humanoid embodiment due to hardware safety and resource intensity. Future work aims to scale HyperSim across a broader spectrum of complex manipulation tasks and diverse robotic morphologies. The intention to open-source the pipeline and dataset further underscores its potential impact by fostering broader research and development in generalized Embodied AI.
