# Tactile-WAM: Touch-Aware World Action Model with Tactile Asymmetric Attention

> [!summary] Summary
> Tactile-WAM integrates tactile sensing into World Action Models via an asymmetric attention mechanism, allowing robots to better reason about physical interactions in contact-rich tasks. The approach yields a 38.9 percentage point increase in mean success rate over RGB-only baselines in simulation and a 33 percentage point improvement in real-world contact-intensive manipulations.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) primarily rely on visual information, which is insufficient for precise contact-rich manipulation tasks due as critical physical cues are often occluded or weakly visible.
- Naively injecting sparse, local, and event-driven tactile tokens into visually pretrained WAMs can cause "tactile pollution," degrading the model's established visual and action prediction capabilities.
- Current methods often lack 'complete touch awareness,' failing to adequately leverage dynamic changes in contact state (e.g., contact onset, slip beginning) that are crucial for guiding corrective actions.

## Method

- **Tactile-WAM**, an extended World Action Model, is developed to jointly predict future visual latents, future tactile contact states, and action chunks, operating on a unified token sequence.
- A ==Tactile Asymmetric Attention Mechanism (TAAM)== is introduced, featuring a VIDEOCLEAN mask to prevent visual-query tokens from attending to tactile tokens, thereby protecting the visual prediction pathway.
- The TAAM also incorporates touch-state and touch-change proxies, along with a touch-aware bias, to selectively guide action denoising towards tactile anchors predicting significant contact transitions.

## Results

- Tactile-WAM achieved a mean success rate of **44.7%** in simulation, representing a **38.9** percentage point improvement over the RGB-only DreamZero WAM baseline (**5.8%**).
- In real-world contact-rich tasks, Tactile-WAM attained a **51%** overall success rate, an improvement of 33 percentage points compared to the RGB-only DreamZero WAM baseline (**18%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the VIDEOCLEAN mask improved RGB prediction quality (e.g., **4.42%** reduction in RGB MSE) and that the full Tactile-WAM architecture was essential for significant performance gains, as naive tactile integration initially harmed performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unconstrained integration of sparse tactile information can degrade the robust visual dynamics learned by WAMs, necessitating an asymmetric attention mechanism to protect visual prediction (VIDEOCLEAN mask).
> - Effective tactile awareness for action generation requires focusing on dynamic *changes* in contact state (e.g., initial contact, slip onset) rather than merely static touch, which can be achieved via touch-aware biases.
> - Strategically routing multi-modal information allows a WAM to leverage tactile input for precise action guidance in contact-rich tasks without compromising the model's established visual prediction capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is positioned within the rapidly evolving field of generalist robot policies and World Action Models (WAMs). WAMs represent a significant advancement, coupling action generation with predictions of future world states, thereby enabling policies to reason about the consequences of their actions. This approach leverages large-scale robot datasets, language conditioning, action chunking, and generative action decoders.

Existing WAMs, such as DreamZero, often rely primarily on visual information (RGB) to represent the future world. While effective for many tasks, this visual-centric approach presents limitations in contact-rich manipulation scenarios. Tasks like insertion, assembly, search, and reorientation frequently depend on subtle physical cues such as contact onset, slip, jamming, contact normals, or millimeter-scale alignment errors. These cues are often weakly visible, occluded, or entirely absent in RGB camera feeds, leading to visually plausible but physically incomplete predictions.

The integration of tactile sensing has emerged as a natural solution to this limitation. Vision-based tactile sensors provide complementary evidence regarding local deformation, slip, and pressure patterns. Prior research has demonstrated the benefits of tactile representation learning and tactile-conditioned policies for contact-rich manipulation. More recently, visuo-tactile world models and tactile WAMs have begun to model tactile dynamics and future touch states alongside visual futures and actions (e.g., VT-WM, OmniVTA, Dream-Tac).

This paper identifies two specific, attention-level issues within the context of visually pretrained WAMs that integrate tactile information:
1. **Tactile pollution:** Unconstrained injection of tactile tokens can degrade video and action prediction. Tactile signals are sparse, local, and event-driven, and forcing a visual dynamics model to absorb these signals can interfere with its established predictive capabilities for video.
2. **Incomplete touch awareness:** While tactile information is valuable, its most relevant contribution often comes from *changes* in contact state (e.g., initial contact, slip onset, changes in compression) rather than merely the static presence of touch. Current approaches may not adequately differentiate these critical temporal events.

Tactile-WAM does not claim to be the first tactile WAM, acknowledging prior work in visuo-tactile world modeling. Instead, its contribution lies in an architectural approach that strategically integrates tactile information into a *pretrained video-generation WAM backbone*. It differentiates itself by introducing future tactile contact-state registers as explicit physical state variables, supervising them with contact dynamics, and routing this tactile information through an "asymmetric action path" via its Tactile Asymmetric Attention Mechanism (TAAM). This careful routing aims to selectively use touch where it is most impactful for action, while preventing it from interfering with the visual prediction pathway.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance of World Action Models (WAMs) in contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks by effectively integrating tactile sensing. The authors identify a critical gap: while existing WAMs can generate visually plausible future states and corresponding actions, these visual predictions often lack the physical completeness necessary for precise contact-dependent tasks. Manipulations such as insertion, assembly, and reorientation are highly sensitive to subtle contact dynamics (e.g., slip, jamming, contact normals, small alignment errors) that are frequently invisible or difficult to infer from visual data alone.

This overarching motivation is driven by two specific, identified challenges in combining visual and tactile information within WAMs:

1. **Addressing Tactile Pollution:** A key motivation is to prevent the degradation of visual and action prediction that can occur when tactile tokens are naively integrated into WAMs. The authors hypothesize that because tactile signals are sparse, highly localized, and event-driven, their unconstrained injection can force a visually pretrained WAM to process information that is weakly predictive of future video. This "tactile pollution" can perturb the model's robust visual dynamics path, leading to diminished performance in both visual prediction and action generation. The objective is to design an attention mechanism that selectively routes tactile information to avoid this detrimental effect on visual processing.

2. **Achieving Complete Touch Awareness for Action:** Another core motivation is to ensure that tactile information is leveraged in the most relevant way for action generation. The authors argue that merely including tactile tokens is insufficient; the model needs to be "touch-aware," particularly concerning *changes* in contact state. Critical physical events like the onset of contact, the beginning of slip, changes in compression, or jamming are highly informative for guiding corrective actions. The objective is to incorporate a mechanism that highlights these dynamic tactile events and uses them to modulate action attention, thereby improving the robot's ability to execute precise contact-rich maneuvers.

To achieve these objectives, the research aims to:
- Develop **Tactile-WAM**, a WAM variant capable of jointly predicting future visual latents, future tactile contact states, and action chunks.
- Introduce the **Tactile Asymmetric Attention Mechanism (TAAM)**, specifically featuring a **VIDEOCLEAN mask** to protect visual prediction from tactile pollution and a **touch-aware bias** to guide action denoising using predicted touch changes.
- Validate the effectiveness of Tactile-WAM through comprehensive evaluation on challenging contact-rich manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world settings.
- Conduct ablation studies to demonstrate the individual contributions of the VIDEOCLEAN mask and the touch-aware bias components.

In essence, the motivation is to build a more physically intelligent WAM by strategically and selectively integrating tactile perception, ensuring that the benefits of touch are realized without compromising the strengths of visual prediction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Tactile-WAM builds upon existing World Action Models (WAMs) by extending their predictive capabilities to include future tactile contact states alongside visual latents and action chunks. The core of its methodology is the **Tactile Asymmetric Attention Mechanism (TAAM)**, designed to address the challenges of tactile pollution and incomplete touch awareness.

**A. Model Formulation and Architecture:**
- **Extended WAM Formulation:** Standard visual WAMs predict future visual latents ($z_v$) and action chunks ($a$). Tactile-WAM extends this by incorporating tactile observations ($o_τ$) and predicting future tactile contact-state latents ($z_τ$) as well.
 $p_θ(z_{v_{t+1:t+T}}, z_{τ_{t+1:t+K}}, a_{t:t+H-1} | o_{v_{\le t}}, o_{τ_{\le t}}, s_{\le t}, l)$
- **Joint Denoising Backbone:** Tactile-WAM employs a single Wan/WAM-compatible denoising backbone (likely a Diffusion Transformer or similar architecture) that operates over a joint token sequence: $X = [X_v | X_τ | X_a | X_s]$.
 - $X_v$: Visual tokens representing observed and future visual states.
 - $X_τ$: Future tactile registers, which are specifically designed to be aligned with the action horizon, enabling action tokens to access predicted contact states at relevant time steps.
 - $X_a$: Action tokens for the action chunk.
 - $X_s$: Context tokens, likely including proprioceptive states and language instructions.
- **Inference Process:** During inference, future tactile states and actions are initialized from noise and jointly refined through an iterative denoising process. Ground-truth future tactile states are exclusively used as training targets.

**B. Tactile Asymmetric Attention Mechanism (TAAM):**
TAAM integrates tactile information into the WAM with two distinct attention-level components:

1. **VIDEOCLEAN Attention Mask:**
 - **Purpose:** To prevent "tactile pollution," where sparse tactile signals could degrade the visual dynamics path of a pretrained WAM.
 - **Mechanism:** An attention mask ($M_{vc}^{q,k}$) is introduced that explicitly blocks video-query tokens ($G(q) = V$) from attending to tactile key/value tokens ($G(k) = τ$). This is achieved by setting the attention bias to $-\infty$ for these specific query-key pairs, effectively zeroing out their contribution.
 - **Effect:** This ensures that the visual prediction pathway remains largely unperturbed by tactile information, preserving the strong visual dynamics priors learned during pretraining. Crucially, the mask *does not block action-query access to tactile tokens*, allowing tactile information to remain available for action generation. The overall attention bias then combines this mask with the backbone's original causal, blockwise, or local attention rules.

2. **Touch-State Proxies and Touch-Aware Bias:**
 - **Purpose:** To make tactile hidden states more action-relevant by highlighting significant contact transitions.
 - **Touch-State Proxy ($c_τ$):** A motion-derived proxy that captures local contact loading from tactile image motion.
 - **Touch-Change Proxy ($\Delta c_τ$):** Derived as the temporal difference of the touch-state proxy ($\Delta c_τ_i = c_τ_i - c_τ_{i-1}$). This proxy is designed to emphasize step-like contact transitions crucial for corrective actions, such as first contact, slip onset, compression changes, or jamming. These proxies are auxiliary targets used for supervision during training, not additional observation tokens.
 - **Tactile Proxy Head:** A dedicated head predicts tactile velocity, $c_τ$, and $\Delta c_τ$ from the tactile hidden states. These predictions are supervised with SmoothL1 losses against the derived target proxies.
 - **Touch-Aware Bias:** This bias is computed from the predicted touch-change proxy and specifically applied *only* to action-query–tactile-key attention logits.
 - A touch-change signal magnitude ($d_i$) is calculated from $\Delta c_τ$.
 - This magnitude is then transformed into a scalar bias ($b_τ_i$) using a thresholded saturating function. This function ensures that only significant contact changes contribute to the bias and that the bias remains within a bounded range.
 - The bias is added to the attention logits for action queries attending to tactile keys. This selectively increases the attention of action denoising towards tactile anchors where significant contact changes are predicted.
 - **Training and Inference for Bias:** During training, the touch-aware bias is computed from the *target* touch-change proxies (teacher forcing). During inference, the initial denoising step uses no bias. Subsequent denoising steps use the *previously predicted* touch-change proxy to construct the bias, ensuring that the mechanism does not rely on unavailable future ground truth.

**C. Tactile Register Implementation (Appendix B.A):**
- Tactile image frames from vision-based tactile sensors are processed by a frozen tactile representation model (e.g., UniT-style continuous latents).
- A cross-attention token adapter compresses patch-level tactile features for each anchor-sensor pair into a fixed number of compact tokens.
- These tactile registers incorporate time, anchor, sensor, and slot embeddings to allow action tokens to read future contact states relative to the action prediction horizon.

**D. Training:**
- Future tactile tokens and action chunks are denoised by interpolating clean targets with noise.
- The total loss function combines losses for video prediction, action prediction, tactile prediction, and the two proxy supervisions ($L_{state}$, $L_{change}$).

This comprehensive methodology ensures that tactile information is integrated in a highly targeted and asymmetric manner, allowing it to guide action generation precisely where contact dynamics are critical, without compromising the overall model's predictive integrity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Tactile-WAM was conducted across both simulated and real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, with comparisons against RGB-only World Action Models (WAMs) and $\pi_{0.5}$-based generalist action-policy baselines.

**A. Simulation Results (ManiFeel Benchmark):**
- **Overall Success Rate:** Tactile-WAM achieved a mean success rate of **44.7%** (201/450 trials), demonstrating a substantial improvement of **38.9 percentage points** over the RGB-only DreamZero WAM baseline (5.8%, 26/450 trials) and significantly outperforming the $\pi_{0.5}$ action-policy baseline (1.3%, 6/450 trials).
- **Contact-Rich Task Performance:** The performance gains were particularly pronounced on tasks requiring intricate contact interactions. For the designated "contact-centric subset" (bolt-nut assembly, gear insertion, power insertion, and USB insertion), Tactile-WAM achieved an **87.5%** success rate (175/200 trials), contrasting with 1.5% for the DreamZero WAM baseline (3/200 trials).
 - Specifically, Tactile-WAM achieved 100% success on gear insertion and USB insertion, 78% on power insertion, and 72% on bolt-nut assembly.
- **Identified Limitations:** While effective for contact-sensitive corrections, Tactile-WAM did not show significant improvements in tasks requiring extensive visual search (e.g., object search) or long-horizon manipulation (e.g., peg reorientation), suggesting its primary contribution is in local contact-dependent behaviors.

**B. Analysis Experiments:**
Two analysis experiments were conducted to dissect the contributions of Tactile-WAM's components:

1. **VIDEOCLEAN Prediction Quality (Table I):**
 - This analysis compared a naive visuo-tactile WAM (without VIDEOCLEAN) against its VIDEOCLEAN counterpart regarding decoded RGB prediction quality.
 - **Results:** VIDEOCLEAN consistently improved RGB prediction metrics. It reduced RGB Mean Squared Error (MSE) by 4.42% and RGB Mean Absolute Error (MAE) by 1.39%. Additionally, it increased Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) by 0.14 dB and Global Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) by 0.0017.
 - **Conclusion:** These quantitative improvements, supported by qualitative observations of reduced visual artifacts (Figure 9), validate the tactile pollution hypothesis. The VIDEOCLEAN mask effectively protects the visual prediction pathway from degradation by tactile tokens.

2. **Component Analysis (Table II):**
 - This ablation study evaluated the incremental contributions of future tactile prediction, VIDEOCLEAN, and the touch-state/touch-change proxies with touch-aware bias.
 - **RGB-only WAM:** Established a baseline success rate of 5.8%.
 - **Naive VT-WAM (future tactile tokens, no VIDEOCLEAN, no proxies/bias):** Resulted in a success rate of 1.3%. This demonstrated that merely adding tactile inputs without proper routing can *harm* performance, reinforcing the need to address tactile pollution.
 - **VT-WAM + VIDEOCLEAN (future tactile tokens, VIDEOCLEAN, no proxies/bias):** Improved success to 4.9%. This indicates that protecting video prediction is necessary to recover performance, but not sufficient for substantial gains beyond the RGB-only baseline.
 - **Full Tactile-WAM (future tactile tokens, VIDEOCLEAN, proxies & bias):** Achieved the highest success rate of 44.7%. The most significant performance increase was observed when future tactile prediction was combined with the touch-state/touch-change proxies and the touch-aware bias for action denoising.

**C. Real-Robot Results:**
- **Tasks:** Evaluation on five real-world contact-rich tasks: bolt-nut assembly, bulb insertion, gear insertion, peg insertion, and power insertion.
- **Overall Success Rate:** Tactile-WAM achieved **51%** overall success (51/100 trials). This represents a **33 percentage point improvement** over the RGB-only DreamZero WAM baseline (18% success) and a **41 percentage point improvement** over the $\pi_{0.5}$ action-policy baseline (10% success).
- **Qualitative Consistency:** The real-robot results generally aligned with the simulation findings, demonstrating that Tactile-WAM provides the largest benefits on tasks demanding contact-sensitive alignment, detection of contact transitions, and corrective insertion behaviors.
- **Conclusion:** The successful transfer of performance from simulation to the real world suggests that predicted tactile contact states and touch-aware action denoising are robust and practical.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that Tactile-WAM significantly improves performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks by effectively integrating tactile information. The analysis confirms that both the VIDEOCLEAN mask for mitigating tactile pollution and the touch-aware bias for guiding actions with predicted contact changes are critical components contributing to the overall success.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on Tactile-WAM presents several significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation and multi-modal learning, with potential implications across various applications.

**A. Enhanced Robot Dexterity and Robustness in Contact-Rich Tasks:**
The primary significance lies in addressing a fundamental limitation of visually-driven robot policies in contact-rich environments. By effectively integrating future tactile state prediction, Tactile-WAM enables robots to reason about the physical consequences of their actions in a more complete manner. This leads to substantial improvements in task success rates for operations requiring precise contact, such as assembly, insertion, and manipulation of delicate objects. The demonstrated gains, particularly on challenging contact-centric tasks in both simulation and real-world settings, indicate a step towards more dexterous and robust robotic systems capable of operating in complex, constrained physical environments.

**B. Principled Multi-Modal Data Fusion:**
The work offers a significant architectural insight into integrating diverse sensory modalities. The concept of "tactile pollution" and its mitigation through the **VIDEOCLEAN mask** provides a principled approach for combining sparse, local, and event-driven data (tactile) with dense, global data (visual) within a unified learning framework. This asymmetric attention mechanism prevents the potentially detrimental interference of one modality on another's specialized processing pathway, preserving strong visual dynamics priors while still leveraging tactile information. This method of selective information routing could have broader applicability in other multi-modal learning scenarios where different sensor streams exhibit varying predictive powers for different aspects of a task or environment.

**C. Advancing Touch-Aware Action Generation:**
The introduction of **touch-state and touch-change proxies**, coupled with a **touch-aware bias** for action denoising, represents an advancement in how robots leverage tactile information. Instead of merely registering the presence of touch, the system focuses on the dynamics of contact—specifically, when and where significant changes in contact occur. Events like initial contact, slip onset, or changes in compression are critical for executing fine-grained corrective actions. By modulating action attention based on these predicted contact changes, Tactile-WAM enables more intelligent and responsive manipulation behaviors. This shifts the paradigm towards more "physically intelligent" robots that understand the *evolution* of physical interaction.

**D. Impact on Robotic Applications:**
- **Industrial Automation:** Improved performance in assembly, insertion, and part handling could enhance automation in manufacturing, reducing errors, increasing efficiency, and enabling the robotic handling of more complex or delicate components.
- **Service and Assistive Robotics:** Robots with better tactile awareness could perform tasks requiring sensitive physical interaction, such as handling fragile household items, assisting in healthcare settings, or manipulating objects with varying material properties.
- **Research and Development:** The identified limitations (e.g., in visual search, recovery, and long-horizon reasoning) provide clear directions for future research. The architectural solutions presented also serve as a foundation for developing more sophisticated multi-modal world models.

**E. Considerations and Future Directions:**
While significant, the paper acknowledges limitations. The reliance on motion-derived tactile proxies, rather than calibrated force measurements, suggests avenues for exploring richer and more precise tactile feedback. The conservative routing of the VIDEOCLEAN mask, while effective, might evolve into learned or adaptive cross-modal routing in future systems, particularly with larger paired visuo-tactile datasets. Addressing challenges in robust visual search, long-horizon planning, and recovery behaviors would further enhance the capabilities of such physically aware WAMs.

In conclusion, Tactile-WAM contributes a robust and architecturally sound method for integrating tactile perception into generative world models, pushing the boundaries of what robots can achieve in contact-rich manipulation tasks and setting a precedent for principled multi-modal learning in robotics.
