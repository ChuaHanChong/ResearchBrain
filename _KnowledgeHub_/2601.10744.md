# Explore with Long-term Memory: A Benchmark and Multimodal LLM-based Reinforcement Learning Framework for Embodied Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce LMEE-Bench, a new benchmark for embodied exploration, and MemoryExplorer, a multimodal LLM-based reinforcement learning framework. This approach enables embodied agents to actively leverage long-term memory for improved multi-goal navigation and memory-based question answering capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI tasks primarily focus on single-shot outcomes, neglecting the critical process of exploration and active utilization of long-term episodic memory.
- Current multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) approaches for embodied exploration often treat memory as a passive component, limiting proactive decision-making and generalization.
- There is a lack of comprehensive benchmarks that holistically evaluate an agent's success in multi-goal navigation alongside its ability to reason using accumulated long-term memory.

## Method

- A new paradigm, ==Long-term Memory== Embodied Exploration (**LMEE**), unifies an agent's exploratory cognition and decision-making by integrating multi-goal navigation with memory-based question answering.
- LMEE-Bench, a comprehensive dataset built on HM3DSem, was constructed to evaluate agents across multi-goal navigation efficiency and accuracy in retrieving/reasoning with episodic memory.
- MemoryExplorer, an MLLM-based (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) reinforcement learning framework, facilitates active memory recall and proactive exploration through a multi-task reward function.

## Results

- MemoryExplorer achieved higher overall success rates (SR **23.53**, SPL **14.99**) and memory-based QA scores (MLLM-Score **43.62**, Acc **65.52**) on LMEE-Bench, outperforming established baselines.
- The framework demonstrated superior performance on GOAT-Bench, with an SR of **46.40** and SPL of **28.03**, surpassing prior modular and MLLM-based exploration methods.
- Qualitative experiments on a physical ROSMASTER X3 robot showed successful sim-to-real generalization for multi-goal navigation and memory-based question answering in real-world office environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active, rather than passive, utilization of long-term episodic memory is crucial for embodied agents to achieve lifelong learning capabilities and adapt to complex, dynamic environments.
> - Integrating MLLMs with reinforcement fine-tuning allows agents to proactively query and leverage multimodal memory, enhancing autonomous decision-making in exploration.
> - A multi-task reward function, combining action prediction, frontier selection, question answering, and output format consistency, effectively unifies scene understanding, memory utilization, and planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied intelligence aims to develop agents capable of interacting with and learning within physical environments. A significant objective within this field is the achievement of lifelong learning capabilities, which would enable agents to perform complex, long-horizon tasks continuously in dynamic and often unfamiliar environments. This necessitates not only task completion but also the ability to leverage past experiences and memory to inform future decision-making.

Current research paradigms in embodied AI primarily focus on single-shot tasks such as goal navigation and embodied question answering. While these tasks contribute to agent development, they often emphasize the final outcome (e.g., whether a target object was found or a question was answered accurately) rather than the underlying process of exploration and environmental understanding. Multi-goal navigation tasks represent a step towards longer-horizon challenges but still tend to overlook how the cumulative exploration process contributes to an agent's scene understanding and strategic decision-making. This focus on outcomes rather than the exploratory process can hinder the development of agents capable of continuous learning and adaptation.

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated potential in embodied exploration, particularly in complex scenes. However, existing approaches frequently treat memory as a passive component. For instance, imitation learning-based methods train agents to replicate expert trajectories, which can restrict generalization to novel scenarios and may not foster proactive exploration. Other vision-language exploration techniques often rely on memory snapshots and employ filtering strategies to manage context window limitations but do not fully exploit the potential for MLLMs to actively query and utilize stored information. Models with long-term spatiotemporal memory typically engage in post-exploration reasoning, missing opportunities to guide exploration proactively.

The current work addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that integrates active memory utilization and comprehensive evaluation of both the exploration process and outcome, aiming to advance embodied agents toward lifelong learning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance embodied agents towards possessing lifelong learning capabilities, enabling them to handle complex, long-horizon tasks and operate continuously in general environments. The authors identify a gap in existing embodied AI research, specifically that mainstream one-shot tasks predominantly focus on task completion results, often neglecting the critical process of exploration and the active utilization of long-term episodic memory.

The motivation behind this work stems from the observation that an ideal embodied agent should not only accurately accomplish given tasks but also leverage accumulated long-term episodic memory to optimize decision-making and develop a comprehensive understanding of its environment. This capability is considered essential for agents to continuously learn, adapt, and evolve in complex real-world settings.

To address these challenges, the research outlines several key objectives:

1. **Unify Exploratory Cognition and Decision-Making**: The work aims to integrate the agent's understanding of the environment (cognition) with its actions and planning (decision-making) within the exploration process. This unification is posited as crucial for developing general embodied intelligence.
2. **Propose a New Embodied Exploration Paradigm**: The introduction of Long-term Memory Embodied Exploration (LMEE) emphasizes both the outcome (task goals) and the process (memory accumulation and utilization) of embodied tasks. This paradigm is designed to encourage agents to dynamically construct "on-the-fly" memories during navigation and use them for future interactions.
3. **Construct a Comprehensive Benchmark (LMEE-Bench)**: A specific objective is to create a dataset and benchmark that holistically evaluates agents. LMEE-Bench incorporates multi-goal navigation and memory-based question answering to assess an agent's success rate and efficiency in navigation, alongside its accuracy in retrieving and reasoning with episodic memory. This aims to provide a more thorough assessment of an agent’s cognitive understanding and decision-making abilities.
4. **Develop a Method for Active Memory Recall and Proactive Exploration**: The researchers aim to propose a novel method, MemoryExplorer, that enhances an agent's ability to actively query and retrieve multimodal memory information. This method, based on fine-tuning a multimodal large language model (MLLM) through reinforcement learning, is intended to encourage autonomous exploration strategies.
5. **Design a Multi-Task Reward Function**: A specific objective within MemoryExplorer's development is to design a reward function that integrates various aspects of the task, including action prediction, frontier selection, and question answering. This is intended to unify scene understanding, memory utilization, and planning-based decision-making within the reinforcement learning framework, thereby enabling the model to tackle complex tasks.

In summary, the motivation is to move beyond outcome-centric embodied tasks to a more process-aware paradigm that fosters lifelong learning through active memory management and a unified approach to cognition and decision-making.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the introduction of a new framework, Long-term Memory Embodied Exploration (LMEE), and a corresponding MLLM-based reinforcement learning agent, MemoryExplorer.

**LMEE Framework**
LMEE is designed to unify an agent's exploratory cognition and decision-making. It comprises two core components:
1. **Multi-goal Navigation**: Agents are tasked with navigating unknown environments to reach multiple specified goals. During this process, they dynamically collect and store observations, forming an episodic memory bank.
2. **Memory-based Question Answering (QA)**: After or during navigation, agents are required to answer questions related to the navigation targets or the general environment, leveraging their stored memories. This component evaluates the agent's ability to utilize its episodic memory for cognitive understanding and reasoning.

**LMEE-Bench Dataset Construction**
To facilitate LMEE, a comprehensive dataset and benchmark, LMEE-Bench, was constructed using the real-world HM3DSem dataset, which provides semantic labels for indoor scenes. The construction process involves three main parts:
1. **Task Instruction Generation**: A Large Language Model (LLM) (Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct) is utilized to generate multi-goal task instructions by combining object categories and region information from 145 training scenes and 36 test scenes. Tasks are categorized into easy, medium, and difficult based on the number of regions, goals, and distances to targets.
2. **Exploration Trajectory Generation**: The Habitat-Sim platform is used to plan optimal exploration paths from initial agent positions to target objects, generating step-by-step sequences of actions, observations, positions, and rotations. Concurrently, an image tagging model labels object information in each observation image, constructing a multimodal memory bank.
 - **Memory Bank**: Each entry `(p_i, f_i, o_i)` in the memory bank `M` stores position, text features (from image tagging), and image features. CLIP is used to extract visual and text features. Similarity `s_i` between a current state `(p_c, f_c, o_c)` and a memory entry is calculated as a weighted sum of dot products of features and an exponential function of Euclidean distance for position. Temporal consistency is maintained by aggregating recent similarity scores and using an adaptive threshold for filtering. Goal-related memories are explicitly inserted to ensure correct retrieval.
 - **Continuous Actions**: To address simulator limitations, a continuous action window samples consecutive identical actions to simplify action prediction training.
3. **Question Answering Generation**: A Vision Language Model (VLM) (Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct) generates goal-oriented question-answer pairs from observation images associated with navigation targets. This ensures questions relate to observed objects and aids the model in understanding task progress. Questions are classified into five types (attribute, counting, location, relationship, state) with open-ended or multiple-choice answer formats.

The dataset totals 1,982 tasks, with 377,311 entries, including 8,880 goals and 9,286 questions (406 unique questions after sampling) covering 246 object categories.

**MemoryExplorer Method**
MemoryExplorer is an MLLM-based (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) framework for active exploration and memory retrieval, trained via Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT).
1. **Task Definition**: The model policy `πθ(I, O, Q; M)` takes task instructions (I), current multi-view observations (O), a memory-based goal-oriented question (Q), and an external long-range memory (M). It outputs a single-step action (S), a frontier (F), and an answer (A).
2. **Memory Retrieval**: The MLLM is guided to generate code that invokes an external memory retrieval tool `R`. This tool processes a query text to find the top-k most similar memories from the long-term memory bank using cosine similarity of CLIP-encoded features. The retrieved multimodal memories are then fed back into the MLLM as additional input to support reasoning. The focus is on single-round tool invocation.
3. **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)**: MemoryExplorer's training objective maximizes the expected reward `rϕ(I, O, Q, y)` while minimizing the KL divergence from a reference policy `πref`, using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This approach encourages autonomous decision-making and evaluates tool call effectiveness through final reward feedback.
4. **Reward Modeling**: A Multi-Task Reward function `r_total` integrates four components to optimize multi-dimensional responses:
 - `r_action`: Action accuracy.
 - `r_frontier`: Frontier correctness (selecting the best next exploration region).
 - `r_answer`: Answer precision for memory-based QA.
 - `r_format`: Output format completeness (ensuring structured ACTION, FRONTIER, ANSWER segments).
 The total reward is `r_total = w_act · r_action · c + w_front · r_frontier · c + w_ans · r_answer + w_fmt · r_format`. A consistency coefficient `c` penalizes logically inconsistent action-frontier pairs. A scaling factor `α` is applied to sub-rewards, amplifying them when external tool assistance is involved and reducing them when tool invocation fails, thereby encouraging efficient tool utilization. The final `r_total` is clipped to.

**Experimental Details**:
The model is trained on 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs for 160 steps with a global batch size of 128. Hyperparameters include a learning rate of 1e-6, a KL penalty coefficient of 0.1, `topk` for memory retrieval set to 3, consistency coefficient `c` at 0.5, and a scaling factor `α` of 1.2 for tool-assisted scenarios and adjusted values for tool invocation failures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the performance of MemoryExplorer on both the newly introduced LMEE-Bench and the existing GOAT-Bench.

**LMEE-Bench Evaluation (Table 2)**
MemoryExplorer was compared against established embodied exploration models (Explore-EQA, 3D-Mem) and a Retrieval-Augmented Memory (RA-Mem) variant based on 3D-Mem. The benchmark evaluates both multi-goal navigation (Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL)) and memory-based question answering (MLLM-Score for open-ended, Accuracy (Acc) for multiple-choice).
- **Navigation**: MemoryExplorer achieved SRs of 31.91 (Easy), 21.35 (Medium), and 23.40 (Hard), with overall SR 23.53 and SPL 14.99. These figures represent an improvement over baselines, particularly compared to 3D-Mem (overall SR 16.91, SPL 6.86) and RA-Mem (overall SR 20.96, SPL 12.18). Explore-EQA, lacking memory capabilities, showed lower performance in navigation across all difficulty levels.
- **Question Answering**: MemoryExplorer demonstrated MLLM-Scores of 35.71 (Easy), 48.14 (Medium), and 34.38 (Hard), with an overall MLLM-Score of 43.62. For accuracy on multiple-choice questions, it achieved 68.57 (Easy), 63.83 (Medium), and 68.75 (Hard), with an overall Acc of 65.52. These results were generally higher than those of MLLM baselines evaluated for retrieval-augmented QA, such as Qwen2.5-VL-7B (overall MLLM-Score 41.72, Acc 55.86) and GPT-4o (overall MLLM-Score 39.31, Acc 55.17). The findings also indicated that different MLLMs exhibit varying strengths, with Qwen2.5-VL-7B performing better on open-ended questions and Qwen3-VL-8B/LLAVA-OV-7B on multiple-choice, suggesting potential misalignments between cognitive understanding and action decision-making.

**GOAT-Bench Evaluation (Table 3)**
MemoryExplorer was also evaluated on a subset of the "Val Unseen" split of GOAT-Bench, a multimodal lifelong navigation benchmark.
- MemoryExplorer achieved a Success Rate of 46.40 and an SPL of 28.03. This performance surpassed prior modular baselines (e.g., Modular GOAT SR 24.9, SPL 17.2) and MLLM exploration methods. Notably, MemoryExplorer outperformed RA-Mem (SR 42.81, SPL 21.95), indicating the performance benefits of reinforcement fine-tuning for improved utilization of exploratory memories. RA-Mem itself showed better performance than 3D-Mem (SR 37.05, SPL 20.26), attributed to its active query generation for memory retrieval.

**Qualitative Results (Figure 4)**
A qualitative example on LMEE-Bench illustrates MemoryExplorer's behavior: an agent successfully navigates to a refrigerator, oven, and dresser. When subsequently asked about the oven door's state, it retrieves relevant memory and correctly answers, "The oven door is closed." This demonstrates the integrated multi-goal navigation and memory-based QA capabilities.

**Ablation Studies (Tables 4, 6, 7, 8)**
- **Training Task Setting (Table 6)**: Introducing the memory retrieval tool significantly improved performance (overall SR 23.53, SPL 14.99, MLLM-Score 43.62, Acc 65.52) compared to a navigation-only setup (SR 20.59, SPL 12.34, MLLM-Score 39.14, Acc 58.62), highlighting the importance of active retrieval learning.
- **Question Type (Table 4)**: Training with a richer variety of question types ("All" setting) led to stronger performance (overall SR 22.06, SPL 15.13, MLLM-Score 43.28) than using only simple or multiple-choice questions in isolation.
- **Reward Design (Table 7)**: The inclusion of consistency penalties (for action-frontier pairs) and tool-usage penalties (scaling factor `α`) in the multi-task reward function contributed to improved model performance, indicating their effectiveness in guiding learning.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity (Table 8)**: Experiments on reward weighting coefficients (`w_act`, `w_front`, `w_ans`, `w_fmt`) showed that the chosen default configuration (0.2, 0.2, 0.4, 0.2 respectively) yielded the highest performance, demonstrating the influence of reward balancing.

**Visualization (Figure 5)**
Training visualization showed that the model progressively learned to invoke the memory-retrieval tool more accurately over training steps, which correlated with an increase in overall reward and improved answer accuracy.

**Real-World Testing (Supplementary Material, Figure 8)**
Qualitative experiments on a ROSMASTER X3 robot in office environments demonstrated the sim-to-real generalization of MemoryExplorer. The agent successfully performed multi-goal navigation (e.g., finding a water bottle and rubbish bin) and memory-based question answering (e.g., "The bottle of water is on the chair," or locating a tripod not initially targeted). This indicated the robustness and practical applicability of the approach, particularly the strong coupling between cognition and decision-making.

**Failure Case Analysis (Supplementary Material, Figures 10, 11, 12)**
Identified failure modes included:
- Ambiguities in data generation leading to correct memory retrieval but incorrect answers.
- Limitations in MLLM spatial understanding, resulting in retrieval of incorrect memories or inaccurate descriptions even with correct memory.
- Common navigation failures such as selecting wrong objects or exceeding exploration steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Long-term Memory Embodied Exploration (LMEE) and the MemoryExplorer framework, contributing to the field of embodied AI in several ways.

Firstly, the introduction of LMEE as a new paradigm is significant. By explicitly unifying exploration with memory-based reasoning, it moves beyond the conventional focus on single-shot task outcomes. This shift encourages the development of agents that not only complete tasks but also actively understand and adapt to their environment through continuous memory accumulation and utilization. This approach aligns with the long-term goal of achieving lifelong learning capabilities in embodied agents.

Secondly, the creation of LMEE-Bench provides a comprehensive and standardized benchmark. This dataset and evaluation framework are designed to holistically assess critical agent abilities, including memory utilization, cognitive understanding, and decision-making, across multi-goal navigation and memory-based question answering. By evaluating both the process and outcome of embodied exploration, LMEE-Bench offers a more nuanced understanding of agent performance compared to benchmarks that primarily focus on task completion. This can facilitate more targeted research and development in embodied intelligence.

Thirdly, the proposed MemoryExplorer framework addresses the challenge of passive memory usage in MLLM-based embodied agents. By leveraging reinforcement learning for fine-tuning, MemoryExplorer enables active exploration and proactive memory retrieval. The multi-task reward function, integrating action prediction, frontier selection, and question answering, is designed to optimize an agent’s policy for effective reasoning in complex scenes. This active memory mechanism allows agents to dynamically query and utilize past experiences, fostering more autonomous and adaptable behaviors.

The demonstrated robust performance of MemoryExplorer on both LMEE-Bench and GOAT-Bench, coupled with successful real-world testing, indicates the practical potential and generalization capabilities of the approach. The ability to transfer learned behaviors from simulation to a physical robotic platform suggests that the core principles of active memory utilization and integrated cognition-decision-making are transferable and effective in unstructured environments.

The findings have potential impact on the development of more intelligent and autonomous embodied agents that can operate effectively over extended periods in dynamic environments. By emphasizing the integration of cognitive processes with decision-making through active memory, this work lays groundwork for agents that can continuously learn, refine their understanding, and make informed decisions based on accumulated experience, thereby advancing towards truly intelligent and self-evolving embodied systems. The identified limitations, such as inference speed and challenges with complex long-term memory scenarios, also point to important future research directions for the community.
