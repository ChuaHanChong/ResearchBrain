# MiCA Learns More Knowledge Than LoRA and Full Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the RAIR Lab and an independent researcher introduced Minor Component Adaptation (MiCA), a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for large language models that adapts underutilized minor singular directions of pre-trained weights. MiCA achieved up to 5.9x better knowledge acquisition on novel domain data while using 6-60% fewer trainable parameters than LoRA, without substantially degrading general capabilities.

## Problem

- Adapting large language models to specialized tasks requires substantial computational resources for full fine-tuning.
- Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PeFT) methods, like LoRA, do not explicitly target specific adaptation subspaces, potentially leading to updates overlapping with dominant components.
- This implicit subspace learning may result in inefficient use of representational capacity or shifts in previously acquired knowledge.

## Method

- **MiCA** extends LoRA by explicitly incorporating the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of pre-trained weight matrices to identify and adapt minor singular directions.
- It initializes the adapter matrix 'B' using the least significant singular vectors (minor components) from the pre-trained weight matrix 'W', while 'A' is initialized to zero and solely optimized.
- A two-stage fine-tuning strategy combines the task-specific fine-tuned base model with the instruction-tuning delta to retain general instruction-following capabilities.

## Results

- MiCA achieved **61.33%** accuracy on the BLOGS-MC dataset with Llama-2-7B, outperforming LoRA (**58.28%**) by **3.05** percentage points and utilizing approximately **6%** of LoRA's trainable parameters (rank 16 vs 128).
- On the German HISTORY-MC dataset, MiCA attained **39.2%** accuracy, surpassing LoRA (**29.4%**) and full fine-tuning (**30.4%**) for knowledge uptake.
- An ablation study demonstrated that minor-r adaptation (MiCA) consistently outperformed major-r adaptation and random component adaptation on the BLOGS-MC benchmark, validating its hypothesis for effective domain adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minor singular directions of pre-trained weight matrices offer a plastic and effective subspace for integrating new, task-specific knowledge, as opposed to dominant components saturated with generic knowledge.
> - Constraining adaptation to these spectrally identified minor components enhances knowledge acquisition and significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters.
> - Explicitly selecting adaptation subspaces based on spectral properties of pre-trained weights, rather than implicitly learning them, can lead to more stable and efficient model specialization.
