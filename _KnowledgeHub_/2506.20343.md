# PIMBS: Efficient Body Schema Learning for Musculoskeletal Humanoids with Physics-Informed Neural Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Physics-Informed Musculoskeletal Body Schema (PIMBS), leverages physics-informed neural networks to efficiently learn the body schema of musculoskeletal humanoids from limited data. This approach integrates physical laws and initial conditions into the learning process, demonstrating up to 70% reduction in evaluation error in simulations with sparse data and effective application on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Accurately modeling musculoskeletal humanoids is challenging due to complex muscle paths, wrapping effects, and nonlinear elastic elements, causing discrepancies from simplified geometric models.
- Learning the intricate 'body schema' (relationships among joint angles, muscle tension, and muscle length) empirically requires extensive data collection, which is labor-intensive and often incomplete.
- Existing data-driven body schema learning methods struggle to achieve robust and accurate performance with limited data, leading to poor generalization and control for these complex robots.

## Method

- ==Proposes Physics-Informed Musculoskeletal Body== Schema (**PIMBS**), a neural network framework that learns the body schema by mapping joint angles and muscle tensions to muscle lengths.
- Integrates physical laws, specifically the relationship between joint torque and muscle tension (τ=−GT(θ)f\tau = -G^T(\theta)fτ=−GT(θ)f), as a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) loss component.
- Includes an additional constraint loss that enforces the initial condition where muscle length is zero at zero joint angle and muscle tension, combining all losses into a weighted sum for training.

## Results

- In simulation, PIMBS reduced the evaluation error (LbestevalL_{best_eval}Lbeste​val​) by approximately 60-**70%** for AL-Map learning with very limited training data (Ntrain=3,5N_{train}=3, 5Ntrain​=3,5) compared to purely data-driven methods.
- For ATL-Map learning in simulation, PIMBS consistently achieved the highest performance across various data amounts, leading to a 20-**35%** reduction in evaluation error.
- On an actual 5-DOF musculoskeletal humanoid arm, PIMBS improved performance after optimizing the physical law weight (e.g., α=10−7\alpha=10^{-7}α=10−7 for Ntrain=10N_{train}=10Ntrain​=10), demonstrating the method's applicability to real-world systems despite unmodeled complexities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating physics-informed constraints and initial condition constraints significantly improves body schema learning accuracy and data efficiency, particularly when empirical data is scarce.
> - The relative contribution of the initial condition constraint loss and the physics-informed loss can vary, with both providing benefits depending on the specific body schema formulation (e.g., AL-Map vs. ATL-Map).
> - Successful application on a physical robot necessitates careful tuning of the weighting factor for physical laws to accommodate real-world discrepancies like friction that are not captured in idealized physical models.
