# PIMBS: Efficient Body Schema Learning for Musculoskeletal Humanoids with Physics-Informed Neural Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Physics-Informed Musculoskeletal Body Schema (PIMBS), leverages physics-informed neural networks to efficiently learn the body schema of musculoskeletal humanoids from limited data. This approach integrates physical laws and initial conditions into the learning process, demonstrating up to 70% reduction in evaluation error in simulations with sparse data and effective application on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Accurately modeling musculoskeletal humanoids is challenging due to complex muscle paths, wrapping effects, and nonlinear elastic elements, causing discrepancies from simplified geometric models.
- Learning the intricate 'body schema' (relationships among joint angles, muscle tension, and muscle length) empirically requires extensive data collection, which is labor-intensive and often incomplete.
- Existing data-driven body schema learning methods struggle to achieve robust and accurate performance with limited data, leading to poor generalization and control for these complex robots.

## Method

- ==Proposes Physics-Informed Musculoskeletal Body== Schema (**PIMBS**), a neural network framework that learns the body schema by mapping joint angles and muscle tensions to muscle lengths.
- Integrates physical laws, specifically the relationship between joint torque and muscle tension (τ=−GT(θ)fτ = -G^T(θ)fτ=−GT(θ)f), as a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) loss component.
- Includes an additional constraint loss that enforces the initial condition where muscle length is zero at zero joint angle and muscle tension, combining all losses into a weighted sum for training.

## Results

- In simulation, PIMBS reduced the evaluation error (LbestevalL_{best_eval}Lbesteval) by approximately 60-**70%** for AL-Map learning with very limited training data (Ntrain=3,5N_{train}=3, 5Ntrain=3,5) compared to purely data-driven methods.
- For ATL-Map learning in simulation, PIMBS consistently achieved the highest performance across various data amounts, leading to a 20-**35%** reduction in evaluation error.
- On an actual 5-DOF musculoskeletal humanoid arm, PIMBS improved performance after optimizing the physical law weight (e.g., α=10−7α=10^{-7}α=10−7 for Ntrain=10N_{train}=10Ntrain=10), demonstrating the method's applicability to real-world systems despite unmodeled complexities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating physics-informed constraints and initial condition constraints significantly improves body schema learning accuracy and data efficiency, particularly when empirical data is scarce.
> - The relative contribution of the initial condition constraint loss and the physics-informed loss can vary, with both providing benefits depending on the specific body schema formulation (e.g., AL-Map vs. ATL-Map).
> - Successful application on a physical robot necessitates careful tuning of the weighting factor for physical laws to accommodate real-world discrepancies like friction that are not captured in idealized physical models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Musculoskeletal humanoids are robotic systems engineered to replicate the intricate anatomical and biomechanical features of the human musculoskeletal system. These robots offer distinct advantages, including the ability to achieve variable stiffness control through nonlinear elastic elements, inherent redundancy that allows continued operation even with muscle damage, and a human-like structure conducive to implementing and verifying human reflex control and learning mechanisms.

A fundamental challenge in the development and control of these complex robots is the accurate modeling of their body structure. Geometric models often exhibit significant discrepancies with the actual robot, primarily due to variations in muscle paths and the difficulty in precisely representing muscles as they wrap around joints. This necessitates the development of methods to learn the robot's "body schema"—the intricate relationships among joint angles, muscle tension, and muscle length—directly from empirical data.

Prior research in body schema learning for musculoskeletal humanoids has explored several approaches. Some studies have focused on constructing data tables or employing polynomial approximations to map joint angles to muscle lengths, which can then be differentiated to obtain muscle Jacobians for tasks like joint angle estimation. More recently, neural networks have been utilized to learn these mappings, offering an alternative to tabular or polynomial methods and enabling applications in state estimation and control. However, these data-driven methods typically rely heavily on large quantities of data collected from the physical robot. The process of acquiring such extensive datasets is labor-intensive and can be a significant practical barrier to the widespread adoption and development of these robots.

This work addresses the limitation of data scarcity by integrating the concept of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) into the body schema learning framework. PINNs, a deep learning paradigm, leverage knowledge of physical laws, often expressed as differential equations, to augment data-driven learning. By embedding physical constraints directly into the neural network's loss function, PINNs aim to achieve higher accuracy and generalization from less data compared to purely data-driven methods. This approach represents a shift from solely relying on empirical data to a hybrid model that combines data with foundational physical principles, thereby making body schema learning more efficient and robust for musculoskeletal humanoids.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent complexity of musculoskeletal humanoid robots and the practical challenges associated with their control and calibration. While these robots offer advantages in mimicking biological systems, their intricate structure, involving numerous tendons, pulleys, and nonlinear elastic elements, leads to significant deviations from simplified geometric models. This makes accurate forward and inverse kinematics challenging.

Existing methods for learning the body schema of musculoskeletal humanoids primarily rely on collecting large amounts of data (joint angles, muscle tensions, muscle lengths) from the robot as it moves. This data collection process is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and can be difficult to perform exhaustively across the robot's full operational range, especially when considering the many degrees of freedom and muscle combinations. Consequently, learning robust and accurate body schema becomes problematic when data is limited, leading to poor generalization and control performance. There is a clear need for learning methods that can achieve high accuracy even with sparse data.

Against this background, the key objectives of this study are:

1. **Develop an efficient body schema learning method:** To propose a novel approach, termed Physics-Informed Musculoskeletal Body Schema (PIMBS), that can learn the complex relationships between joint angles, muscle tensions, and muscle lengths with high accuracy using a minimal amount of data.
2. **Integrate physical laws into the learning process:** To apply the principles of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) by incorporating physical constraints—specifically, the relationship between joint torque and muscle tension, assuming an accurate joint geometric model—into the neural network's loss function. This allows the learning process to leverage not only data points but also their differential information derived from known physical laws.
3. **Validate the proposed method in diverse environments:** To empirically evaluate the effectiveness and characteristics of PIMBS through experiments conducted in both a simulated musculoskeletal environment and on an actual physical musculoskeletal humanoid robot. This validation aims to demonstrate the method's robustness and applicability in realistic scenarios.
4. **Analyze the impact of different loss components:** To investigate the individual and combined contributions of basic data-driven loss, physical constraint loss (PINN), and an initial condition constraint loss, in improving learning performance, especially under data-limited conditions.

Ultimately, the research aims to make the acquisition of accurate body schema more practical and less resource-intensive, thereby facilitating the development and control of advanced musculoskeletal humanoids.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Physics-Informed Musculoskeletal Body Schema (PIMBS), integrates physical laws into a neural network framework for learning the body schema of musculoskeletal humanoids. The body schema describes the relationship between joint angles ($\theta$), muscle tension ($f$), and muscle length ($l$).

**A. Basic Structure of Musculoskeletal Humanoids**
Musculoskeletal robots typically comprise a skeleton, joints, and muscle wires. Muscle wires are wound around motor pulleys, pass through tension measurement units (loadcells), and connect to the skeleton via nonlinear elastic elements. Muscle length ($l$) is derived from motor encoders, and muscle tension ($f$) is measured by loadcells. Joint angles ($\theta$) may be directly measured or estimated.
The relationship between geometric wire length ($l_{geom}$) and joint angle is given by $l_{geom} = h_{geom}(\theta)$. The actual muscle length ($l$) also accounts for wire stretch and nonlinear elasticity, expressed as $l = h(\theta, f) = h_{geom}(\theta) + \Delta n(\theta, f)$, where $\Delta n$ is the stretch. The muscle Jacobian ($G$) is defined as $G(\theta) = \partial h_{geom}(\theta) / \partial \theta$, often approximated as $\partial h(\theta, f) / \partial \theta$ due to $\Delta n$'s relatively small variation with $\theta$.

**B. Basic Body Schema Learning (Baseline)**
The conventional approach involves training a neural network to learn the function $h$. Two forms of body schema are considered:
1. **AL-Map (Angle-Length Map):** $l = h(\theta)$, where muscle length is solely a function of joint angle, implying inextensible wires or unique determination of $l$ from $\theta$.
2. **ATL-Map (Angle-Tension-Length Map):** $l = h(\theta, f)$, where muscle length depends on both joint angle and muscle tension, accounting for wire stretch and nonlinear elastic elements.

Data $D_i = (\theta_{data,i}, f_{data,i}, l_{data,i})$ is collected from the robot. The neural network's output $l_{pred}$ is compared to $l_{data}$ using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss function:
$L_{basic} = \frac{1}{N_{train}} \sum (l_{pred} - l_{data})^2$.

**C. Physics-Informed Body Schema Learning (PIMBS)**
PIMBS augments the basic learning by incorporating physical laws into the loss function. It assumes the joint structure (link positions, weights) is accurate, allowing for calculation of gravity compensation torque ($\tau$).

1. **Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) Loss:**
 The core physical law leveraged is the relationship between joint torque and muscle tension: $\tau = -G^T(\theta)f$. In this context, $G(\theta)$ is the muscle Jacobian, obtained by differentiating the neural network's learned function $h$.
 The PINN loss, $L_{pinn}$, penalizes discrepancies between the gravity compensation torque calculated from the network's Jacobian and the known gravity compensation torque:
 $G_{pred} = \frac{\partial h(\theta)}{\partial \theta}$ or $\frac{\partial h(\theta, f)}{\partial \theta}$
 $L_{pinn} = \frac{1}{N_{train}} \sum (G_{pred,T} f_{data} + \tau_{data})^2$.

2. **Constraint Loss (Const):**
 An additional constraint enforces the initial condition: when joint angle $\theta=0$ and muscle tension $f=0$, the relative muscle length $l$ should be $0$.
 $L_{const} = (h(0) \text{ or } h(0,0) - 0)^2$.

The total training loss for PIMBS is a weighted sum of these components:
$L_{train} = L_{basic} + L_{const} + \alpha L_{pinn}$, where $\alpha$ is a weighting factor for the physical laws.

**D. Experimental Setup**
Experiments were conducted on two platforms:
1. **2-DOF 4-muscle Musculoskeletal Simulation:**
 - **Data Generation:** Joint angles were varied randomly within $[-0.5, 0.5]$ rad. Geometric muscle lengths ($l_{geom}$) and gravity compensation torques ($\tau$) were calculated. Muscle tension ($f$) was generated by solving a quadratic programming problem to satisfy $\tau = -G^T(\theta)f$ with $f \ge f_{min}$, where $f_{min}$ was either 0 N (for AL-Map) or varied randomly in $[10, 300]$ N (for ATL-Map). Wire stretch ($\Delta n$) was derived from $f = e^{K\Delta n} - 1$.
 - **Map Types:** Both AL-Map and ATL-Map learning were evaluated.
 - **Training Data:** $N_{train}$ varied as $\{3, 5, 10\}$ for AL-Map and $\{5, 10, 30\}$ for ATL-Map.
2. **Actual Musculoskeletal Humanoid (5-DOF 10-muscle left arm):**
 - **Robot:** The left arm of a musculoskeletal humanoid was used.
 - **Data Collection:** Joint angles were randomly controlled and held stationary. During stationary phases, muscle tension control was applied to eliminate slack, and data was collected.
 - **Map Type:** ATL-Map learning was performed due to nonlinear elastic elements.
 - **Training Data:** $N_{train}$ varied as $\{10, 30\}$. A total of 496 data points were collected, with $N_{train}$ points used for training and the remainder for evaluation.

**E. Neural Network and Training Details**
- **Architecture:** A neural network with three fully connected layers, 1000 hidden units, and hyperbolic tangent activation functions after the first two layers.
- **Optimizer:** Adam optimizer.
- **Epochs:** 20000.
- **Batch Size:** Equal to $N_{train}$.
- **Weight $\alpha$ for $L_{pinn}$:** Fixed at $1.0 \times 10^{-5}$ in simulation. For the actual robot, it was varied among $1.0 \times 10^{\{-5,-6,-7,-8\}}$.
- **Evaluation:** Performance was assessed using $L_{eval}$, the MSE on a separate evaluation dataset $D_{eval}$. The best model (lowest $L_{basic}$ on training data across epochs) was selected, and its $L_{eval}$ value was denoted as $L_{best\_eval}$. Each experiment was repeated five times with different random seeds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the performance of body schema learning under different loss function configurations (Basic, Basic+Const, Basic+PINN, Basic+Const+PINN) and varying amounts of training data ($N_{train}$), in both simulation and on a physical robot.

**A. Body Schema Learning of AL-Map for 2-DOF 4-muscle Musculoskeletal Simulation**
- **Low Data (`N_{train} = 3, 5`):** For extremely limited data, Basic+Const+PINN demonstrated the most substantial performance improvement. For $N_{train}=3$, it reduced $L_{best\_eval}$ by approximately 60% compared to the Basic method. At $N_{train}=5$, this reduction was around 70%. In these scenarios, PINN generally showed a greater individual impact on performance than Const.
- **Moderate Data (`N_{train} = 10`):** As the amount of training data increased, the performance differences among the methods significantly diminished, with all methods achieving comparable, low error rates.
- **Qualitative Observations (`N_{train} = 5`):** The inclusion of $L_{const}$ led to muscle length curves that accurately intersected at the origin (zero joint angle, zero muscle length). The inclusion of $L_{pinn}$ improved the model's ability to extrapolate at the extremes of the joint angle range, where data points were sparse, by leveraging gradient information.

**B. Body Schema Learning of ATL-Map for 2-DOF 4-muscle Musculoskeletal Simulation**
- **All Data Regimes (`N_{train} = 5, 10, 30`):** Basic+Const+PINN consistently achieved the highest performance across all tested data amounts. It reduced $L_{best\_eval}$ by approximately 20-35% compared to the Basic method, with the performance gap decreasing as $N_{train}$ increased.
- **Contribution of Components:** In the ATL-Map setting, the Const loss contributed more significantly to performance improvement than the PINN loss, unlike the AL-Map case. Both components, however, had a positive effect on learning.

**C. Body Schema Learning of ATL-Map for the Actual Musculoskeletal Humanoid**
- **Initial $\alpha$ (1.0 x 10^-5):** When the same $\alpha$ value used in simulation ($1.0 \times 10^{-5}$) was applied to the actual robot, adding the PINN loss (Basic+PINN or Basic+Const+PINN) led to a degradation in performance over training epochs compared to Basic or Basic+Const.
- **Adjusted $\alpha$:** Reducing the weight $\alpha$ for the physical laws ($1.0 \times 10^{\{-6,-7,-8\}}$) progressively mitigated this degradation. For $N_{train}=10$, Basic+Const+PINN with $\alpha=10^{-7}$ yielded the best performance. For $N_{train}=30$, Basic+Const+PINN with $\alpha=10^{-8}$ was most effective.
- **Overall Performance:** While optimal $\alpha$ values improved performance, the overall accuracy achieved on the actual robot did not reach the levels observed in simulation.
- **Impact of $N_{train}$:** Similar to simulations, increasing $N_{train}$ reduced the performance gap between different learning methods, suggesting that more data can compensate for the lack of physical constraints or optimal weighting.

**Summary of Key Findings:**
- **Data Efficiency:** PIMBS (Basic+Const+PINN) consistently demonstrated superior performance, particularly when training data was scarce, in both simulation environments.
- **Contribution of Components:** Both the initial condition constraint ($L_{const}$) and the physics-informed constraint ($L_{pinn}$) contributed to improved learning, with their relative importance varying with the specific problem (AL-Map vs. ATL-Map).
- **Real-World Discrepancies:** In actual robot experiments, significant discrepancies between the assumed physical model and real-world phenomena (e.g., friction) necessitated careful tuning of the physical law weight ($\alpha$). A high $\alpha$ could lead to performance degradation, while a reduced $\alpha$ allowed the model to benefit from the regularization without being overly constrained by potentially inaccurate physical assumptions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces PIMBS, a novel approach for learning the body schema of musculoskeletal humanoids that leverages Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs). The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

**A. Addressing Data Scarcity in Robotics:**
A core challenge in deploying complex robotic systems, especially those mimicking biological structures, is the extensive and often labor-intensive process of data collection for calibration and modeling. PIMBS demonstrates a method to achieve high-accuracy body schema learning even with a limited amount of empirical data. This significantly reduces the practical overhead associated with initial robot setup, calibration, and adaptation to new tasks or environments, making these advanced robots more accessible and easier to operate.

**B. Enhanced Learning Efficiency and Robustness:**
By incorporating physical laws directly into the neural network's loss function, PIMBS injects fundamental knowledge about the system's mechanics. This approach acts as a strong regularizer, allowing the model to generalize better from sparse data, particularly at the boundaries or less-explored regions of the joint space. The qualitative results, showing improved learning at joint angle extremes due to gradient information and accurate behavior at the origin due to the constraint loss, highlight this enhanced efficiency and robustness. This is crucial for musculoskeletal robots where full coverage of the operational space with data can be infeasible.

**C. Bridging the Reality Gap:**
The study rigorously evaluates PIMBS in both simulation and on an actual musculoskeletal humanoid. While simulation results demonstrate ideal performance gains, the challenges encountered on the real robot (e.g., the need to adjust the physical law weight due to unmodeled friction) provide valuable insights into the "reality gap." The finding that judiciously weighting physical laws can still provide benefits even when perfect physical models are unavailable is an important step towards developing more robust learning methods for physical systems. It suggests a pathway for integrating imperfect domain knowledge effectively.

**D. Implications for Robot Control and State Estimation:**
An accurate body schema is fundamental for precise control and reliable state estimation in musculoskeletal humanoids. PIMBS's ability to learn these relationships more efficiently directly translates to:
- **Improved Control:** More accurate muscle Jacobians derived from the learned body schema can lead to better torque control and trajectory tracking.
- **Robust State Estimation:** A precise mapping of joint angles to muscle lengths/tensions enhances the robot's proprioception and self-awareness, which is vital for autonomous operation and interaction with the environment.

**E. Foundation for Future Research:**
The work lays a foundation for several promising future research directions identified by the authors:
- **Dynamic Modeling:** Extending the current static relationship learning to incorporate dynamic effects like wire friction, viscosity, and hysteresis is critical for achieving even higher fidelity models, especially for robots operating at speed or under varying loads.
- **Handling Model Inaccuracies:** Addressing scenarios where the assumed joint geometric model itself is inaccurate would significantly broaden the applicability of physics-informed methods, allowing for even greater self-calibration capabilities in robots with unknown or changing physical parameters.
- **Optimizing Knowledge Integration:** Further research into adaptively weighting physical constraints (e.g., based on data uncertainty or confidence in the physical model) could lead to more robust and generalized learning algorithms.

In conclusion, PIMBS represents a significant step towards more autonomous and efficient musculoskeletal humanoid robots. By integrating physical knowledge with data-driven learning, it addresses a key practical bottleneck, paving the way for more sophisticated control, greater adaptability, and reduced deployment costs for these complex biomimetic machines.
