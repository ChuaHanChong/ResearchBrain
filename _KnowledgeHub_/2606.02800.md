# Cosmos 3: Omnimodal World Models for Physical AI

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA introduces Cosmos 3, a family of omnimodal world models that jointly process and generate language, image, video, audio, and action sequences within a unified Mixture-of-Transformers architecture for Physical AI. This framework achieves competitive to state-of-the-art performance across 48 understanding benchmarks and leads open-source models in various image, video, and robot policy generation tasks, including a 39.7% success rate on RoboLab.

## Problem

- Traditional Physical AI approaches rely on compartmentalized models for perception, simulation, and action, leading to fragmented and inefficient pipelines.
- Training Physical AI agents directly in the real world is slow, expensive, and potentially hazardous, hindering scalability.
- Scaling training data and environments for Physical AI agents remains a persistent bottleneck.

## Method

- **Cosmos 3** employs a unified ==Mixture-of-Transformers== (MoT) architecture to jointly process and generate language, image, video, audio, and action sequences.
- The model integrates modality-specific encoders and decoders with distinct Reasoner (autoregressive for understanding) and Generator (==diffusion== for generation) pathways, co-initialized from pre-trained VLMs.
- A multi-stage training curriculum with extensive curated real and synthetic data is used, alongside a comprehensive infrastructure stack for data, training, and serving.

## Results

- Cosmos 3 Reasoner models demonstrated strong performance across 48 benchmarks, outperforming several open-source and closed-source models in domain-specific Physical AI tasks (robotics, autonomous driving, smart infrastructure).
- Cosmos3-Super achieved state-of-the-art results among open-source models in video generation (Text-to-Video, Image-to-Video, physics adherence, human motion) and ranked highly in image generation leaderboards.
- Cosmos3-Nano-Policy-DROID achieved state-of-the-art robot policy results, with a **39.7%** success rate on RoboLab and ranking #1 on the RoboArena real-world benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying multimodal understanding and generation within a single, scalable framework is crucial for developing general-purpose Physical AI agents.
> - A Mixture-of-Transformers architecture with specialized Reasoner and Generator pathways can effectively integrate diverse modalities (vision, language, audio, action) while maintaining coherence.
> - High-fidelity synthetic data generation and scalable training environments are essential for overcoming real-world data limitations and fostering robust Physical AI development.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Physical AI agents, capable of perceiving, reasoning, and interacting with the physical world, represents a significant area of research. Traditionally, this domain has relied on a fragmented approach, employing separate models for distinct capabilities. For instance, discriminative models such such as Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have been used for perception and reasoning, while generative models like Video Generation Models and Forward Dynamics Models have been applied for world simulation. Additionally, action-prediction models, including Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) and World-Action Models (WAMs), have addressed execution.

The authors argue that this compartmentalization limits the efficacy and scalability of Physical AI systems. Integrating these capabilities into a single framework is presented as essential, as understanding often involves predicting future world states and action consequences, while generation benefits from structured representations of the world and agent behaviors. Existing approaches have largely treated these pillars of understanding and generation in isolation.

Cosmos 3 is introduced as a step towards unifying these disparate functionalities within a single omnimodal world model. This work extends the concept of world models, which describe environmental evolution and agent actions, beyond predictive latent models (focused on compact internal dynamics for planning) to generative models that produce inspectable multimodal simulations. It also builds upon advancements in multimodal understanding, video generation, action modeling across diverse modalities, and audio-visual generation, aiming to merge them into a single, cohesive architecture. The paper positions Cosmos 3 as an omnimodal, omni-functional model, designed not only for multimodal inputs and outputs but also to integrate scene interpretation, future simulation, action inference, and multimodal rollouts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified, scalable framework for Physical AI that integrates understanding and generation capabilities across multiple modalities. This framework, named Cosmos 3, aims to jointly process and generate language, image, video, audio, and action sequences within a single mixture-of-transformers architecture.

The motivation for this unification stems from several challenges inherent in training and deploying Physical AI agents:
- **Fragmentation of Current AI Paradigms:** Existing Physical AI systems often consist of disjointed models for perception (e.g., VLMs), world simulation (e.g., video generators), and action prediction (e.g., VLAs). This fragmentation leads to complex, computationally inefficient pipelines that require significant effort to stitch together for an agent to perform multi-faceted tasks, such as a home robot cleaning a table.
- **Scalability and Data Bottlenecks:** Training Physical AI agents in the real world is resource-intensive, slow, and potentially hazardous. The construction of simulated environments offers a safer and more scalable alternative. However, effectively training agents in these simulations necessitates models capable of both understanding observed dynamics and generating plausible future states or actions.
- **Interdependence of Understanding and Generation:** The authors contend that understanding and generation are inherently coupled. Accurate understanding of a scene or event often requires implicit reasoning about its future evolution and the consequences of potential actions. Conversely, effective generation relies on a coherent, structured representation of the world and agent behaviors. Separating these capabilities can lead to suboptimal performance and a lack of holistic world representation.
- **Need for a General-Purpose Backbone:** The goal is to create a foundational model that can serve as a versatile backbone for various Physical AI applications, eliminating the need for architectural modifications when transitioning between tasks like vision-language understanding, video generation, or robotic policy learning. This design is expected to enable more scalable learning through shared representations and joint multi-task supervision.
- **Addressing Specific Physical AI Gaps:** The research is motivated by the need to improve synthetic data generation for training, facilitate task-specific specialization (e.g., for robotics or autonomous driving), and build better training environments for Physical AI agents by enabling high-fidelity, diverse visual data synthesis.

By addressing these motivations, Cosmos 3 seeks to provide a more integrated and efficient pathway toward advanced Physical AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Cosmos 3 is designed as a family of omnimodal world models that integrate understanding and generation across language, image, video, audio, and action modalities. The core architecture is a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT).

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The architecture comprises modality-specific encoders, a token arrangement strategy, the MoT backbone, and a multimodal position embedding scheme.

#### 3.1.1. Encoders

Input sequences from different modalities are projected into a unified representation space. A learnable, modality-specific embedding vector is added to each non-language modality to distinguish it within the shared transformer.
- **Image and Video:** Two separate encoders are used. A Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder, pre-trained for vision-language alignment, is used for understanding tasks. For generation tasks, a video Variational Autoencoder (VAE) encoder, from Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, compresses video temporally by 4x and spatially by 32x32. The ViT is jointly trained with the backbone, while the VAE is frozen.
- **Audio:** An audio VAE architecture, based on Lee et al. (2025b), encodes raw stereo audio (48 kHz) into 25 tokens per second. This encoder is also frozen during training.
- **Action:** Action modeling supports diverse embodiments (autonomous vehicles, camera motion, robots, egocentric human motion). These heterogeneous control spaces are mapped into a unified action interface. Action tokens ($a_t$) represent transitions between video states ($v_{t-1}$ to $v_t$). Actions are composed of ego poses (3D translation + 6D rotation), effector poses, and grasp states. Ego and effector poses are pseudo-actions derived from relative transforms. Grasp states directly encode the current manipulation state. Domain-aware input and output projection layers convert action vectors to and from the transformer's hidden dimension, while the MoT backbone is shared.

#### 3.1.2. Token Arrangement and Generation Mode

The input token sequence is divided into an autoregressive (AR) subsequence and a diffusion (DM) subsequence.
- **AR Subsequence:** Contains language tokens and ViT-encoded vision tokens. It is responsible for reasoning and understanding and is routed to a dedicated set of transformer parameters (reasoner tower).
- **DM Subsequence:** Contains VAE-encoded video/image tokens, audio tokens, and action tokens. It is responsible for generation, iteratively denoising noisy tokens to produce clean ones, and is routed to a separate parameter set (generator tower).
Tokens are arranged with AR tokens before DM tokens, and within the DM subsequence, clean conditioning tokens precede noisy diffusion tokens, ordered by vision, audio, and action modality.

The model supports various generation modes:
- **Language:** Operates as a VLM. AR subsequence only.
- **Text-to-Image (T2I):** AR contains language, DM contains noisy target image tokens.
- **Text-to-Video (+Audio):** AR contains language, DM contains noisy target video and optional audio tokens.
- **Image-to-Video/Video-to-Video (+Audio):** AR contains language, DM contains clean conditioning image/video tokens followed by noisy target video and optional audio tokens.
- **Video Transfer:** AR contains language, DM contains clean control-video tokens (e.g., edge, depth) followed by noisy RGB video tokens.
- **Action:** Supports Forward Dynamics (predicts future visual states conditioned on observed context and clean actions), Inverse Dynamics (infers actions explaining a visual transition), and Policy (jointly predicts actions and video tokens).

#### 3.1.3. Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Architecture

The MoT architecture uses two pathways within each transformer decoder layer, initialized from a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM).
- **Dual-Tower Layer Structure:** Each pathway has its own parameters (LayerNorms, attention projections, FFNs). The AR subsequence is routed to the reasoner tower, and the DM subsequence to the generator tower.
- **Dual-Stream Joint Attention:** AR tokens use causal self-attention only within the AR subsequence. DM tokens use full bidirectional attention, attending to both AR and DM tokens. AR tokens are not updated by DM tokens.

#### 3.1.4. Multimodal Position Embedding

A 3D Multimodal RoPE (MRoPE) with absolute temporal indexing aligns tokens across modalities.
- **Position Index Allocation:** Language tokens use a 1D RoPE behavior ($t=h=w$). ViT-encoded tokens share $t$ per frame, with $h, w$ for spatial location. Diffusion tokens (video, image, audio, action) have modality-specific index assignments. A fixed temporal gap is inserted between AR and DM subsequences to mitigate over-saturation and checkerboard artifacts.
- **Absolute Temporal Modulation:** FPS modulation aligns tokens with different temporal resolutions onto a shared physical temporal axis by scaling temporal increments based on "Temporal Steps Per Second" (TPS), relative to a base TPS (6, for 24 FPS video).

#### 3.1.5. Model Variants

Cosmos 3 includes three model scales: Edge (4B parameters, dense 2B transformer), Nano (16B parameters, dense 8B transformer), and Super (64B parameters, dense 32B transformer). Nano and Super are initialized from Qwen3-VL models, while Edge uses a custom Nemotron backbone.

#### 3.2. Data

The training data curriculum is multi-stage for both the Reasoner and Generator.

#### 3.2.1. Reasoner Data

- **Pre-training (22.0M samples):** Dominated by image-text and text-only data (e.g., OCR, 2D Grounding, Visual QA) to establish broad general visual understanding. Data is semantically deduplicated and filtered by an AI-judge for faithfulness, completeness, and correctness.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) (2.2M samples):** Shifts towards Physical AI specialization, with 50% video-text samples. Focuses on general spatial and temporal understanding (2D/3D grounding, temporal event understanding, physical plausibility judgment), autonomous vehicle tasks (Action CoT, temporal event localization, 3D vehicle grounding), robotics and embodied AI (Robot action CoT, embodied reasoning, robotic surgery understanding), and smart infrastructure (warehouse spatial intelligence, pedestrian localization, traffic/anomaly reasoning). SFT uses stricter AI-judge filtering.

#### 3.2.2. Generator Data

Follows a progressive multi-stage curriculum, introducing modalities incrementally.
- **Pre-training:** Utilizes 767M images and 347.7M video clips, along with 138.9M audio tracks. Data is collected, embedded for deduplication, categorized, and filtered for quality. Multi-resolution training (256p, 480p, 720p) is employed across five aspect ratios and variable frame counts. Four generation modes are trained: T2I, T2V, I2V, V2V. FPS modulation is used for temporal consistency.
- **Mid-training:** Continues from pre-trained checkpoints, introducing action and video transfer data. Focuses on domain specialization (high-quality images, robotics, autonomous driving, human activity, synthetic data like SDG-PhyxSim, SDG-RobotSim, SDG-DriveSim, SDG-SynHuman, SDG-Warehouse) and multimodal integration (action-conditioned FD, ID, policy, and control-conditioned video transfer like edge, depth, segmentation maps). Multi-resolution training continues with increased rectified-flow shift values.
- **Post-training:** Creates domain-specialized models (e.g., Cosmos3-Super-Text2Image, Cosmos3-Super-Image2Video, Cosmos3-Nano-Policy-DROID) using compact, high-quality, targeted datasets. Structured JSON captions, with fine-tuned in-house captioning models, are used across all stages to ensure detailed and consistent scene descriptions.

#### 3.3. Training

The Cosmos 3 generator is optimized using a rectified flow matching objective, predicting constant velocity to denoise noisy latents.

#### 3.3.1. Reasoner Training

- **Pre-training:** All components are trained jointly from the start, using a next-token prediction objective. Square-root normalized per-token loss weighting is applied. Optimization uses AdamW with a cosine decay learning rate schedule.
- **SFT:** Uses an importance-aware sampling strategy, mixing in a filtered subset of pre-training data to preserve general capabilities. AdamW optimizer with a linear warm-up and cosine decay.

#### 3.3.2. Generator Training

- **Pre-training:** Only generation-specific parameters are updated; the reasoner tower remains frozen. Uses FusedAdamW with a linear decay learning rate schedule.
- **Mid-training:** Continues from the pre-trained checkpoint with domain-specialized and multimodal data. The total loss is a sum of per-modality velocity MSEs, with action losses scaled.
- **Post-training (T2I, I2V, Robot Policy):** Specialized SFT stages further refine specific generation capabilities, often using a two-stage SFT for T2I. For robot policy, proprioceptive signals are incorporated, and inference latency is reduced.

#### 3.4. Infrastructure

The project leverages a custom infrastructure platform for data engineering (SILA), large-scale distributed GPU training, model serving (TensorRT-LLM, vLLM, vLLM-Omni), and benchmarking & validation. Key features include token-budgeted packed sequences, joint data loaders, rank-synchronous stream selection, look-ahead packing, two-way flat attention, Hybrid Sharded Data Parallelism (HSDP) with Context Parallelism (CP), selective activation checkpointing, Torch Compile, chunked video tokenization, and asynchronous checkpointing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Cosmos 3 was evaluated across a range of understanding and generation tasks, demonstrating performance across its Reasoner and Generator components.

#### 4.1. Reasoner Evaluation

The Reasoner was evaluated on 48 benchmarks grouped into general, robotics, smart infrastructure, and driving categories.
- **General Benchmarks:** Cosmos 3 variants showed competitive performance with open-source models, outperforming Cosmos-Reason2, while generally trailing closed models like Gemini 3.1 Pro on some metrics (e.g., MMBench-Dev, RealWorldQA). Cosmos3-Super achieved a General Avg. score of 73.7, compared to Gemini 3.1 Pro's 77.5.
- **Robotics, Smart Infrastructure, and Driving Domains:** Cosmos 3 demonstrated stronger domain-specific reasoning. In Robotics, Cosmos3-Super achieved an average of 57.8, outperforming Qwen3-VL-32B (52.6) and Gemma-4-31B (51.0), and was competitive with Gemini 3.1 Pro (58.2). In Smart Infrastructure, Cosmos3-Super scored 62.6, exceeding Qwen3-VL-32B (56.1) and Gemma-4-31B (51.3). For Driving, Cosmos3-Super achieved 79.3, surpassing Qwen3-VL-32B (40.7) and Gemma-4-31B (36.6). This performance indicates that Cosmos 3 exhibits domain-specific reasoning capabilities in these Physical AI areas.

#### 4.2. Generator Evaluation: Image Generation

- **UniGenBench:** Cosmos3-Super-Text2Image achieved a score of 91.36 on the full benchmark, including a Physical-AI subset, which was the highest among evaluated models on the aggregated "All" score, outperforming other open-source models.
- **CVTG (Complex Visual Text Generation):** Cosmos3-Super-Text2Image achieved GNED/PNED scores of 80.88/89.08 for English text and 32.02/41.22 for Chinese text. These scores indicate performance in rendering legible and correctly spelled text in complex scenes.
- **Human Preference Scores:** Cosmos3-Super-Text2Image achieved HPSv3 scores of 11.60 and Aesthetic V2 scores of 5.91, which were competitive with or surpassed other open-source models and some closed-source models.
- **Artificial Analysis Text-to-Image Leaderboard:** Cosmos3-Super-Text2Image, with an agentic workflow, was ranked as the top open-weight model and among the top models overall at the time of the technical report.

#### 4.2.2. Video Generation

- **PAIBench-G:** Cosmos3-Super achieved the highest overall scores in both Text-to-Video (T2V) (80.0) and Image-to-Video (I2V) (82.8) settings among open-source models, and was competitive with closed-source models like Veo-3.1 (79.1 for T2V, 82.6 for I2V).
- **RBench:** Cosmos3-Nano achieved a score of 58.4% in Image-to-Video, which was competitive with the best closed-source model (Seedance-1.5-Pro, 58.4%) and higher than other open-source models.
- **Physics-IQ:** Cosmos3-Super demonstrated competitive results in I2V (43.8 directly, 48.9 with WMReward+BoN) and V2V (59.7 directly, 63.4 with WMReward+BoN) settings, surpassing leading open-source and some closed-source baselines in physics adherence.
- **Cosmos HUE (Human Evaluation):** Cosmos3-Super achieved 89.3 for T2V and 89.6 for I2V, positioning it as the top open-source generator, and performing comparably to leading closed-source models (Veo-3.1: 91.3 T2V, 89.7 I2V).
- **Human World Bench (HWB):** Cosmos3-Super achieved 71.9, which was the highest score among all evaluated models for egocentric image-to-video generation for human manipulation tasks.
- **Artificial Analysis Image-to-Video Leaderboard:** Cosmos3-Super-Image2Video was ranked as the top open-weight model and competitive with proprietary offerings.

#### 4.2.3. Audio Generation

- **Cosmos-SoundBench:** Cosmos3-Nano achieved the highest Semantic Audiovisual Quality (SAV) score (8.35), particularly in Semantic Audio (SA) (8.33) and Audiovisual Alignment (AVAlign) (8.16), among all evaluated models. However, it trailed closed-source models in overall Audiovisual Quality (AVQ) due to lower perceptual audio quality (PQ).

#### 4.2.4. Transfer Generation

- **PAIBench-C (Single-Control):** Cosmos 3 variants (Nano and Super) achieved competitive results in blur SSIM, and surpassed the Cosmos-Transfer2.5 baseline in segmentation (Cosmos3-Nano, 0.72), edge F1 (Cosmos3-Super, 0.50), and depth si-RMSE (Cosmos3-Super, 0.58), demonstrating effective control-conditioned video generation within a unified architecture.
- **AVBench-C (Autonomous Driving):** Cosmos 3 variants matched or surpassed Cosmos-Transfer2.5 on automated metrics (e.g., ego drift 0.003 for both Nano and Super vs. 0.008 for baseline), and human evaluation (e.g., video quality 2.86 for Cosmos3-Super vs. 2.59 for baseline), indicating enhanced photorealism and control fidelity in driving scenes.

#### 4.2.5. Action Generation

- **Forward and Inverse Dynamics:** Mid-training initialization (MT-init) consistently improved performance compared to pre-training initialization (PT-init) across autonomous vehicle inverse dynamics, camera motion forward dynamics, egocentric motion forward dynamics, and robotics forward dynamics. For example, Cosmos3-Super with MT-init achieved 0.142° RRE in camera motion, outperforming Lingbot-World (0.299° RRE).
- **Robot Manipulation Policy:** Cosmos3-Nano-Policy-DROID demonstrated strong performance, ranking first on the RoboLab simulation benchmark with a 39.7% success rate (specific task instructions) and on the RoboArena real-world benchmark, and the MolmoSpaces simulation benchmark.
- **Adaptation to New Embodiments:** MT-init enabled faster adaptation to new environments, as seen in the LIBERO-10 environment, where Cosmos3-Nano (MT-init) reached 24.6% success at 500 iterations, while Cosmos3-Nano (PT-init) was at 0.0%.
- **Action Data Synergy:** Co-training across ego-motion and robot manipulation domains showed positive transfer, where training on certain domains (e.g., AV data for camera motion) improved performance in other domains, suggesting shared underlying structures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Cosmos 3 represents a step toward a unified foundation for Physical AI, offering a single model that integrates multimodal understanding and generation across language, image, video, audio, and action. This unification aims to reduce the complexity and computational overhead associated with traditional fragmented approaches that combine separate models for vision-language tasks, video generation, world modeling, and action.

The significance of this work and its potential impact include:
- **Streamlined AI Agent Development:** By providing a general-purpose backbone, Cosmos 3 could simplify the development of embodied agents. This approach may eliminate the need for extensive architectural modifications or complex pipeline orchestration when deploying agents across diverse tasks and environments, potentially accelerating research and deployment in areas like robotics, autonomous driving, and smart infrastructure.
- **Enhanced Physical AI Capabilities:** The model's performance across a range of benchmarks, including those emphasizing physical plausibility, temporal consistency, and action accuracy, suggests that a unified world model can learn and apply robust physical priors. This could lead to AI agents that understand and interact with the real world in more coherent and physically realistic ways.
- **Improved Synthetic Data Generation:** Cosmos 3's generative capabilities, particularly its specialized post-trained variants, offer a mechanism for synthesizing high-fidelity, diverse multimodal data. This could alleviate data scarcity bottlenecks in Physical AI, enabling more scalable and safe training of agents in simulated environments before real-world deployment.
- **Accelerated Task-Specific Specialization:** The ability to effectively post-train the general Cosmos 3 model into domain-specific experts (e.g., for text-to-image, image-to-video, or robot policy) demonstrates its adaptability. This could provide a more efficient starting point for developing highly specialized AI solutions without starting from scratch, leveraging the broad world knowledge acquired during earlier training stages.
- **Foundation for Closed-Loop Training Environments:** The model's capacity for forward and inverse dynamics, coupled with policy generation, positions it as a component for creating more sophisticated closed-loop training environments. This could allow AI agents to not only simulate future states but also to infer necessary actions and observe their expected consequences within the same integrated framework, leading to more effective policy learning.
- **Open Research and Deployment:** The release of code, model checkpoints, curated synthetic datasets, and an evaluation benchmark under an open-source license aims to foster broader community engagement and accelerate further advancements in general-purpose Physical AI.

Overall, Cosmos 3 contributes to the trajectory of developing more integrated, comprehensive, and scalable AI systems for physical interaction, bridging the gap between abstract reasoning and concrete action within multimodal environments.
