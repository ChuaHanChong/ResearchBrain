# Path-conditioned Reinforcement Learning-based Local Planning for Long-Range Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich developed a reinforcement learning-based local planning policy for long-range navigation that opportunistically leverages global path information. This policy improves navigation efficiency by 7.02% with optimal paths and maintains robustness when path guidance is degraded, demonstrating successful real-world deployment on a quadruped robot.

## Problem

- Hierarchical navigation systems often suffer from local planners' sensitivity to the quality of global paths, which can be noisy or contain locally infeasible waypoints.
- Local planners frequently operate with limited global context, leading to inefficient trajectories, redundant exploration of dead ends, and suboptimal long-range performance.
- Existing plan-conditioned reinforcement learning methods often enforce strict adherence to a given path, limiting the agent's ability to opportunistically deviate for more efficient or safer alternatives.

## Method

- An ==RL-based local navigation policy== was developed that is conditioned on an ==encoded representation of the entire global reference path==, rather than just immediate waypoints.
- A training strategy was implemented using a ==controlled distribution of optimal, suboptimal, and perturbed reference paths== to teach the policy robustness to imperfect guidance.
- The reward function was designed to be purely goal-driven, augmented with a ==shortcut reward==, encouraging opportunistic deviation from suboptimal paths without explicit path-following penalties.

## Results

- The path-aware model increased the Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) by **7.02%** with optimal global paths, achieving an SPL of **0.82** compared to a baseline policy.
- The proposed model maintained performance comparable to the baseline (SR: **0.83**, SPL: **0.74**) even when evaluated with significantly degraded (noisy and suboptimal) reference paths.
- Successful sim-to-real transfer was demonstrated on a Unitree B2W quadruped robot in a novel real-world indoor environment, validating the policy's ability to integrate global path information for adaptive long-range navigation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A reinforcement learning policy can effectively utilize global path information as flexible guidance, rather than a strict mandate, to achieve more efficient long-range navigation.
> - Training with a diverse set of suboptimal and noisy paths is crucial for a policy to learn to discern and opportunistically exploit or disregard path guidance, enhancing robustness.
> - A goal-oriented reward structure combined with a specific 'shortcut reward' encourages agents to bypass inefficiencies in a given reference path, leading to improved overall efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation is a foundational capability for robotic systems, particularly as robots are increasingly deployed in complex, real-world environments with higher degrees of autonomy. The field has experienced advancements due to deep learning and reinforcement learning (RL), which offer improved robustness to uncertainty, partial observability, and sensor noise, along with enhanced generalization capabilities in dynamic or novel environments. These properties position RL as a relevant approach for long-range navigation, especially where explicit maps may be unavailable or inaccurate.

A common approach to long-range navigation involves hierarchical pipelines. In these systems, a high-level global planner generates a path, often decomposed into a sequence of waypoints, which is then followed by a low-level local planner. Current challenges within this hierarchical structure include the sensitivity of local planners to the quality of the global path. Inaccurate or incomplete remote sensing data can lead to the generation of locally infeasible waypoints, which can degrade the performance of local execution. Additionally, local planners often operate with limited global context, which can hinder overall long-range efficiency. Some existing methods either assume an ideal, fully feasible global path and encourage strict adherence or provide only minimal global context, such as a single waypoint. Both scenarios can result in the local planner lacking a comprehensive understanding of the intended trajectory, leading to inefficiencies, repeated traversal of dead ends, and suboptimal long-range performance.

In contrast to hierarchical methods, end-to-end Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) approaches have been explored for navigation in unknown environments. These methods bypass explicit planning and mapping by learning reactive policies that directly map sensory inputs to control actions. While exhibiting strong autonomy and adaptability, such approaches have been reported to reduce navigation efficiency, often producing longer or more circuitous trajectories due to the absence of global map information.

Another line of research involves plan- or path-conditioned reinforcement learning policies, which provide agents with a reference trajectory and incorporate reward structures that explicitly encourage adherence to the given path. While such formulations, like plan-based shaping rewards, have been applied successfully in domains requiring precise path tracking, they inherently constrain the agent's behavior, potentially limiting its ability to explore or deviate from the reference path even when more efficient or safer alternatives exist. Previous work, such as L2E, has introduced plan-conditioned policies that allow deviations when a plan becomes infeasible, but their reward structures still prioritize proximity to a valid plan.

This research addresses these limitations by introducing a path-aware RL framework that incorporates global path information as heuristic guidance without enforcing strict adherence. It seeks to combine the flexibility of learned policies with global guidance, allowing for opportunistic deviation, thereby bridging the gap between purely goal-oriented DRL navigation and rigidly path-conditioned control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the limitations observed in existing long-range navigation systems, particularly those employing hierarchical planning. These systems often struggle with two main issues:
1. **Sensitivity to Global Path Quality:** Global planners, relying on coarse or incomplete map information, can generate paths containing locally infeasible waypoints. When local planners are designed to strictly follow these waypoints, their execution can be severely degraded.
2. **Limited Global Context for Local Planners:** Many local planners are provided with only immediate waypoints or limited contextual information. This necessitates heavy reliance on reactive behaviors and exploration, often leading to inefficient trajectories, redundant exploration of dead ends, and suboptimal performance over long distances.

To address these challenges, the key objective of this work is to develop a reinforcement learning-based local navigation policy that effectively leverages global path information as contextual guidance. This policy is designed to overcome the limitations of both rigid path-following and purely goal-oriented exploration.

The specific objectives and contributions of the paper are:
- **Develop a path-conditioned navigation architecture:** The research introduces a policy architecture capable of learning a structured representation of the global path and fusing it with perceptual observations. This architecture aims to enable anticipatory behavior over long horizons by encoding the entire reference path.
- **Propose a path-conditioned training strategy:** A training procedure is designed to allow the policy to remain effective even when the path guidance is noisy or suboptimal. This involves generating reference paths that include suboptimal and perturbed solutions, preventing the policy from collapsing into strict path-following behavior.
- **Validate through real-world deployment:** The proposed approach is validated through a series of simulation experiments and real-world deployment. These experiments assess the impact of architectural choices, the effectiveness of training procedures, and the robustness of the system to noisy and suboptimal path observations. The ultimate goal is to enable a local planner that can be integrated into hierarchical navigation systems and intelligently exploit global planner path information, while remaining adaptive to uncertainty.

The core idea is to enable the policy to opportunistically exploit path information when it is informative, and to disregard it when it is misleading or degraded, without explicit path-following rewards. This approach aims to enhance navigation efficiency for long-range tasks where high-level plans may be approximate, and local execution requires adaptability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on an RL-based local navigation policy conditioned on reference path observations, without explicit path-following rewards, allowing for opportunistic deviation.

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The navigation task is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The agent operates in an unknown 3D environment and must reach a goal specified as a relative position within a maximum time horizon. Observations ($o_t$) include a front-facing depth camera image, relative goal position, proprioceptive observations, and a relative reference path representation. Since the environment state ($s_t$) is not fully observable, a state estimate ($\hat{s}_t$) is generated by fusing current observations with observation history. Both the state estimation function and the policy ($\pi$) are learned end-to-end via reinforcement learning.

**B. Path Representation and Encoding:**
To provide contextual guidance, the entire reference path is encoded rather than a truncated segment. This fixed-length sequence of $N=15$ waypoints ($P_t = \{p_i\}_{i=1}^N$) is expressed in the robot’s egocentric frame. Each waypoint $p_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is augmented to $\tilde{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ to include normalized direction and log-distance information, ensuring bounded inputs and generalization across varying path lengths. The final path representation $\tilde{P}_t = \{\tilde{p}_i\}_{i=1}^N$ provides a relative encoding. The relative goal position is explicitly included and not constrained to align with the final waypoint of the reference path.

The path encoding module utilizes a combination of self-attention and cross-attention mechanisms. Self-attention enables interaction among waypoints to capture global path structure. Cross-attention, implemented with a learned embedding of fixed dimension as its query, allows the policy to selectively focus on relevant path segments (e.g., sharp turns). The resulting path embedding is concatenated with the output of a spatially-enhanced recurrent unit (SRU), which processes visual features. Proprioceptive features are integrated upstream of the SRU, contributing to its hidden state, reflecting the assumption that the reference path remains fixed during an episode and does not require temporal integration within the recurrent memory.

**C. Training Path Generation:**
The training procedure involves sampling reference paths from a controlled distribution that includes both optimal and suboptimal solutions, some of which are perturbed with noise to become locally infeasible.
- **Optimal paths:** Generated using A* search on a Probabilistic Roadmap (PRM).
- **Suboptimal paths:** Generated using Greedy Best-First Search (GBFS) with a biased heuristic. This heuristic incorporates an attraction term toward a randomly sampled detour point, controlled by a parameter $\beta \in$. Lower $\beta$ values increase sub-optimality.
During training, the reference path for each episode is randomly selected between an A*-generated path and a GBFS-generated path. All paths undergo a line-of-sight smoothing procedure and are then subjected to independent random perturbations on each waypoint to increase variability and robustness to imperfect guidance.

**D. Reward Design for Opportunistic Deviation:**
The reward function is purely goal-driven, with no explicit path-following term, to encourage opportunistic deviation rather than strict adherence. The total reward $r_t$ at each timestep includes four components:
- **Task Reward ($r_{task_t}$):** Promotes reaching the goal region efficiently, inversely proportional to the relative goal position.
- **Regularization ($r_{reg_t}$):** Enforces motion smoothness by penalizing large action changes and joint accelerations.
- **Penalties ($r_{pen_t}$):** Penalizes unsafe behaviors such as collisions and excessive body inclination.
- **Shortcut Reward ($r_{shortcut_t}$):** A positive reward granted when the agent's progress along the reference path significantly exceeds a threshold within a single timestep. This explicitly reinforces behaviors that bypass unnecessary detours suggested by the reference path.

The policy is trained using an Asymmetric Actor-Critic Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) pipeline, augmented with regularization techniques from previous work, namely Temporally Consistent Dropout and Deep Mutual Learning, to enhance training stability and generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research involved extensive simulation experiments and a real-world deployment to validate the proposed path-aware local navigation policy.

**A. Training and Simulation Details:**
The RL training was conducted in Isaac Sim using Isaac Lab and the rsl-rl framework. The policy was trained for 39.5 hours on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090, involving 1046 parallel agents across 180 procedurally generated environments. Domain randomization was applied to policy inputs to enhance sim-to-real generalization. The depth camera had a 105° HFOV, 78° VFOV, and 10 m range. The navigation policy operated at 5 Hz, outputting actions for a 50 Hz low-level locomotion policy.

**B. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Metrics:**
Evaluation was performed on larger, unseen environments (50x50m compared to 30x30m training environments) with an increased maximum episode duration (120s vs. 60s), ensuring generalization assessment. Performance was quantified using Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL). Two reference path scenarios were used: (1) optimal paths from a global planner and (2) degraded paths, which were both noisy (waypoint displacement up to 2m, compared to 1m in training) and suboptimal (1.25 to 3.33 times the optimal length).

**C. Comparison with Baseline Navigation:**
- **Optimal Path Scenario:** The path-aware model achieved a 7.02% increase in SPL compared to the baseline policy (from), demonstrating its ability to effectively utilize high-quality path information (Baseline SR: 0.83, SPL: 0.75; Our model (optimal path) SR: 0.87, SPL: 0.82).
- **Degraded Path Scenario:** The proposed model maintained performance comparable to the baseline, indicating robustness to misleading or unreliable path information (Our model (non-optimal & noisy) SR: 0.83, SPL: 0.74).
Qualitative results (Fig. 3) showed that the path-aware model consistently used path information to guide exploration, leading to trajectories with lower variability and greater consistency. It could also reach goals when paths were suboptimal or blocked, demonstrating a balance between guidance and exploration.

**D. Ablation Study:**
1. **Model Architecture Ablation:** Different path encoding module variants were compared in an idealized setting with optimal reference paths. The variant employing self- and cross-attention with a *learned query* exhibited the steepest initial learning curve and consistently higher rewards over the first 2000 training iterations (Fig. 4). This architecture was adopted for the final model.
2. **Training Process Ablation:** Robustness was evaluated under poor path quality. Models trained without waypoint noise showed significantly reduced robustness. The "Full Model" (trained with suboptimal path, path noise, and shortcut reward) achieved the highest average SR and exhibited less degradation on longer trajectories compared to other ablated variants, confirming the benefits of the complete training strategy (Table III).

**E. Robustness to Missing Inputs:**
- **Reference Path Ablated:** When the reference path observation was removed (replaced with zero tensors), the agent's behavior was similar to the baseline, relying solely on perceptual inputs for unguided exploration and successfully reaching goals.
- **Depth Image Ablated:** When the depth input was removed, the agent exhibited path-following behavior, navigating primarily based on the waypoint sequence. This demonstrated the model's capacity to interpret structured path information even without visual input, though successful execution was mainly observed for shorter, less perturbed paths (Fig. 5).

**F. Real-world Test:**
The trained policy was successfully deployed on a Unitree B2W quadruped robot in a previously unseen indoor university environment (Fig. 6). The robot utilized a ZEDX depth camera and NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin for onboard computation, with proprioceptive observations from a LiDAR-based state estimation system.
The real-world results aligned with simulation observations, demonstrating consistent long-range navigation. The policy leveraged intentionally suboptimal reference paths (e.g., to avoid stairs) to bias exploration towards safer or more desirable routes, even if similar-length alternatives existed. This indicated successful sim-to-real transfer and the model's ability to integrate global path information with local perception to achieve goal-reaching performance while favoring safer trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a reinforcement learning-based local navigation policy that distinguishes itself by leveraging global path information opportunistically, rather than enforcing strict adherence. This approach addresses several limitations inherent in conventional hierarchical navigation systems and purely goal-oriented DRL methods.

The primary significance lies in its demonstrated ability to improve navigation efficiency when high-quality global plans are available, without sacrificing robustness when these plans are imperfect, noisy, or even locally infeasible. The implicit conditioning of the RL policy on path observations, coupled with a goal-driven reward function (without explicit path-following rewards), enables the agent to selectively exploit beneficial guidance and disregard misleading information. This "opportunistic use" mechanism prevents the rigid behavior often seen in path-following approaches, where agents may struggle when forced to adhere to unachievable or suboptimal trajectories.

Potential impacts of this work include:
- **Enhanced Long-Range Navigation:** The method is particularly well-suited for long-range navigation scenarios in complex, real-world environments where high-level plans are often approximate due to coarse mapping or dynamic changes. Robots can benefit from global context to reduce inefficient exploration (e.g., dead ends, unnecessary detours) while retaining the adaptability to local uncertainties and unexpected obstacles.
- **Improved Robustness and Adaptability:** The policy's robustness to degraded path information means that global planners do not need to generate perfectly feasible or optimal paths for the local planner to operate effectively. This can simplify the requirements for global planning modules and improve overall system resilience in diverse operational conditions.
- **Flexible Integration in Hierarchical Systems:** The proposed local planner can be integrated into existing hierarchical navigation architectures, providing an intelligent interface between high-level strategic planning and low-level reactive control. It offers a means to inject global intent without over-constraining local execution.
- **Reduced Training Data Constraints:** By training with a distribution of suboptimal and noisy paths, the approach implicitly learns to gauge path reliability. This could potentially reduce the reliance on exclusively optimal, costly-to-obtain expert demonstrations or highly precise environmental models during training.

While the current fixed path representation limits scalability to extremely long-range scenarios, and the implicit modeling of path reliability relies on the training distribution, the fundamental concept of opportunistic path conditioning offers a promising direction. Future work could explore variable waypoint counts and explicit reasoning about path quality to further enhance the capabilities and applicability of this approach. The successful sim-to-real transfer further underscores its practical relevance for deploying autonomous robots in real-world settings.
