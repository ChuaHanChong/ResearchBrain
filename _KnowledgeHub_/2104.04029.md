# TRiPOD: Human Trajectory and Pose Dynamics Forecasting in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> TRiPOD jointly forecasts multi-person global trajectory and body pose from in-the-wild video. Attention graphs encode skeleton joints, human-object and human-human interactions, fused by iterative message passing, with social attention re-applied at every decoding step and a per-joint visibility indicator predicted alongside coordinates. Trained with curriculum learning, it substantially lowers error against the best joint baseline on 3DPW and PoseTrack.

## Problem

- Pose forecasting methods predict joints relative to a body center, discarding global translation, so outputs cannot supply the trajectory navigation and collision reasoning need.
- Trajectory predictors model social interaction but treat each person as a single point, ignoring fine-grained joint dynamics and scene objects that shape imminent body motion.
- Prior joint pose-and-trajectory work models social context crudely and assumes every track and joint stays observable, which occlusion and field-of-view exits break in the wild.

## Method

- Attentional pose history: a ==graph attention network== over skeleton joints feeds an **LSTM** encoder, replacing flat coordinate vectors so unequal joint-to-joint influence is learned rather than assumed.
- Sparse human-object attention graph over ==Mask R-CNN== detections plus **I3D** scene context, and a dense human-human social graph, fused by iterative ==message passing== that makes fusion order-invariant.
- Decoder re-applies the shared social graph at every future step and predicts per-joint binary visibility with cross-entropy alongside visibility-masked MSE, trained by ==curriculum learning== over growing frame blocks.

## Results

- On 3DPW at 900 ms, visibility-ignored error reaches **150.41 cm** versus **208.31** for the joint baseline SC-MPF and **249.91** for the best disjoint pairing of specialist models.
- On PoseTrack at 560 ms, visibility-aware error drops to **96.98** pixels from **133.78**, and visibility-ignored error to **71.78** from **93.41**, with higher visibility IoU and F1.
- Ablation on 3DPW at 900 ms: **181.32** baseline, **167.32** adding the pose attention graph, **161.72** with message passing, **153.12** with future social attention, **150.41** with curriculum learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint pose-plus-trajectory modelling beats combining specialist models: even the naive joint baseline outperforms the strongest disjoint pairing, so the two tasks share exploitable structure.
> - Interaction should be re-estimated while decoding, not only encoded from history; refreshing the social graph at each predicted step still helps after message passing.
> - Predicting visibility as an output converts occlusion from silent failure into a reliability score, and metrics must score it, else benchmarks reward confidently hallucinated joints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The prediction of human movement, encompassing both global trajectory and localized body pose dynamics, is a foundational challenge in computer vision and artificial intelligence. This field is critical for applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, healthcare, and surveillance systems, where anticipating human actions enhances safety, efficiency, and human-machine interaction.

Prior research in human movement forecasting has largely addressed trajectory prediction and pose dynamics forecasting as separate problems. Pose dynamics methods typically focus on changes in joint positions relative to a central point, often neglecting global motion and the intricate environmental and social interactions that influence overall movement. Similarly, trajectory forecasting models often focus on the global path of individuals, frequently overlooking the fine-grained dynamics of individual body joints.

A common limitation in both areas has been the assumption that all human tracks and body joints are consistently visible throughout observation and prediction periods. This assumption is often unrealistic in real-world "in-the-wild" scenarios, where occlusions, partial visibility, or individuals moving out of view are common occurrences. Furthermore, existing models have often struggled to effectively capture the complex, dynamic, and multi-layered interactions between multiple humans, as well as between humans and objects within a scene. While some recent efforts have attempted to unify pose and trajectory forecasting, they often model social interactions in a simplistic manner or do not account for joint invisibility.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a unified framework that simultaneously forecasts human trajectory and pose dynamics. It explicitly models various levels of interactions—skeleton dynamics, human-human social dynamics, and human-object interactions—and introduces mechanisms to handle the practical challenge of joint invisibility. By integrating these previously disparate or overlooked factors, this work aims to advance the accuracy and robustness of human movement prediction in complex, unconstrained environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust method for the joint forecasting of human trajectory and pose dynamics in unconstrained, real-world environments. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges and requirements identified in current research and practical applications:

- **Holistic Human Movement Understanding:** The paper asserts that global trajectory and local pose dynamics are inherently coupled. Existing approaches that treat them as separate problems or combine them naively often yield suboptimal predictions. The motivation is to develop a model that captures this intricate relationship to provide a more comprehensive and accurate understanding of future human movement.
- **Modeling Complex Interactions:** Humans are social agents whose movements are influenced by others and by their environment. The research aims to explicitly model:
 - **Skeleton Dynamics:** The internal dependencies and connections between joints within a human body.
 - **Human-Human Interactions:** The social influences between multiple individuals in a shared space, recognizing that these interactions can vary in intensity and evolve over time.
 - **Human-Object Interactions:** The influence of static and dynamic objects in a scene on human actions and poses, as humans interact with or navigate around their environment.
 The motivation is to move beyond simplistic interaction models to capture nuanced and dynamically changing relationships.
- **Addressing Invisibility and Occlusion:** In real-world scenarios, body joints or entire individuals may become occluded or move out of the sensor's field of view. Current models typically assume full visibility, leading to unreliable predictions when these conditions are met. A key motivation is to incorporate an explicit mechanism to predict the visibility of each body joint, thereby providing an indicator of prediction reliability, which is crucial for safety-critical applications like autonomous navigation.
- **Mitigating Accumulative Error:** Sequential forecasting models, particularly recurrent neural networks, can suffer from the accumulation of prediction errors over long prediction horizons. The paper aims to develop training strategies that address this issue to ensure stable and accurate long-term forecasts.
- **Establishing a Standardized Benchmark:** A significant barrier to progress in this joint forecasting task is the lack of a suitable benchmark dataset and standardized evaluation metrics that encompass all the identified real-world challenges (multi-person scenes, diverse interactions, occlusions, and global/local movement). The motivation is to introduce a new benchmark, repurposing existing datasets, and defining novel metrics to facilitate fair comparison and advancement in the field.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to produce a more practical, accurate, and reliable human movement forecasting system that is directly applicable to complex real-world systems like robotics and autonomous agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed TRajectory and POse Dynamics (TRiPOD) method is a multi-component neural network architecture designed to address the challenges of joint human trajectory and pose dynamics forecasting.

**Problem Definition:**
The task is defined as predicting the set of future poses, Y^p_{t_o+1, t_o+t_f}, for each person `p` given their past global history, X^p_{1:t_o}. Each pose `x^p_t` or `y^p_t` for a person `p` at time `t` is represented by three indicators for each joint `k`: offset (temporal location velocities `Δℓ`), absolute location (`ℓ`), and a binary visibility score (`s`). Notably, the model operates on poses in their original, uncentered space, meaning they inherently include global trajectory information.

**TRiPOD Model Architecture:**

1. **Attentional Human Pose History:**
 - The model begins by representing the human skeleton pose as a graph, explicitly incorporating the natural connectivities between joints.
 - An attentive graph encoder (ATT_In) processes the state information (offsets, absolute locations, visibility scores) of each body joint at each input frame. This generates `G^p_In,t`, a body pose representation that attends to different joint interactions.
 - An encoder RNN (e.g., LSTM) then encodes the temporal sequence of these pose graphs `G^p_In,1:t_o` for each person, producing `Z^p`, the encoded past global pose history for person `p`.

2. **Object and Global Scene Features:**
 - To capture human-object interactions and scene context, an object detector (Mask R-CNN) extracts objects from the last observed frame. These objects are represented by visual features, geometrical information, and class labels, which are then passed through embedding layers to obtain object features (`O`).
 - For holistic scene features (`C`), a spatio-temporal model (I3D) processes the sequence of observation frames. Its feature vectors are also passed through embedding layers.

3. **Human to Object Attention Module (ATT_H2O):**
 - A graph attention module (ATT_H2O) models the interactions between each person's encoded past representation (`Z^p`) and the extracted object features (`O`). This module identifies and encodes varying levels of interrelation between humans and objects, producing `H^p_H2O`.

4. **Social Attention Module (ATT_H2H):**
 - To incorporate human social interactions, another graph attention module (ATT_H2H) processes the human-object interaction representations (`H^p_H2O`) for all individuals. This module models the social relationships between different persons, outputting `H^p_H2H`, which contains weighted social interactions.

5. **Message Passing:**
 - An iterative message passing mechanism is employed to efficiently fuse the distinct information from the human-human (ATT_H2H) and human-object (ATT_H2O) graphs. This process involves nodes exchanging information with their neighbors across `N` steps. Specifically, a person's node hidden state is updated based on messages from its neighbors in both H2O and H2H graphs, while object states remain static. This ensures the framework is invariant to the order of these interactions and allows for refined representations.

6. **Future Social Interactions (Interactive Decoding):**
 - For future prediction, the model dynamically reconsiders social interactions. After encoding all historical individual and social information, the refined features (`f^p_N`) serve as the initial hidden state for a decoder RNN.
 - The decoder recursively generates future poses. At each time step `t`, the predicted pose `y^p_{t+1}` is refined by passing the current hidden states of all persons through the H2H attention graph (ATT_H2H), forming `h^p_{t+1}`, which becomes the hidden state for the next prediction step. This allows future predictions to be socially inter-correlated.

**Training Strategies:**

- **Curriculum Learning:** To address the accumulation of errors in long-term sequence forecasting, a curriculum learning approach is adopted. The model is initially trained on shorter prediction horizons, gradually increasing the difficulty by adding more future frames. This strategy is reported to expedite convergence and improve performance for longer sequences.
- **Skip Connections:** Skip connections are incorporated in the decoder to retain continuity of output and allow the model to recover from accumulating errors by considering both input and output of the RNN decoder at each time step.
- **Joint Loss Function:** A unified loss function is defined in the global data coordinates, combining:
 - L2 loss (MSE) for the offset `Δℓ` and absolute location `ℓ` of joints.
 - Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) loss for the visibility score `s`.
 - During training, if a joint is invisible in the ground truth, its MSE loss is masked (set to zero), while the BCE loss for visibility prediction is still calculated.

**Benchmarking:**

- **Datasets:** The research introduces a new benchmark by repurposing two multi-person datasets:
 - **3D Poses in the Wild (3DPW):** Provides 3D pose and trajectory data in unconstrained outdoor settings.
 - **PoseTrack:** Offers 2D pose annotations in dynamic, crowded scenarios, notably including cases with invisible (occluded or out-of-frame) joints.
- **Metrics:** New evaluation metrics are proposed to account for joint invisibility:
 - **Visibility-Ignored Metric (VIM):** A standard Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) that discards invisible joints during calculation.
 - **Visibility-Aware Metric (VAM):** A novel metric that penalizes both positional error and visibility prediction errors. It uses a "miss-distance" concept, similar to the OSPA metric, to calculate distances between predicted and ground-truth singleton sets (which can be empty if a joint is invisible), incorporating a cutoff value `β`.
 - **Visibility Score Metric:** Evaluates the accuracy of the binary visibility score predictions using Intersection over Union (IoU) and F1-score.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TRiPOD, conducted on the proposed benchmark datasets (3DPW and PoseTrack) and using the new metrics, demonstrated its performance relative to existing methods.

**Quantitative Results:**

- **Joint Evaluation (Global Space):**
 - **3DPW (VIM):** TRiPOD significantly outperformed all baselines in the joint trajectory and pose forecasting task. For example, at a 900ms prediction time, TRiPOD achieved an error rate of 150.41 cm, compared to the best combined baseline (Mo-Att + ST-GAT) at 249.91 cm and the previous joint learning method (SC-MPF) at 208.31 cm.
 - **PoseTrack (VIM and VAM):** TRiPOD consistently exhibited lower error rates across all prediction times and both visibility-ignored (VIM) and visibility-aware (VAM) metrics. For instance, at 560ms prediction time, TRiPOD achieved a VIM of 71.78 pixels and a VAM of 96.98 pixels, substantially outperforming the best combined baseline (Mo-Att + ST-GAT, VIM: 140.83, VAM: 149.78) and SC-MPF (VIM: 93.41, VAM: 133.78).
 - **Visibility Score Metrics (PoseTrack):** TRiPOD demonstrated the ability to estimate joint invisibility effectively. When considering all data, TRiPOD achieved an IoU of approximately 0.8 and an F1-Score of around 0.9. When evaluating only joints with at least one future case of invisibility, the performance gap between TRiPOD and baselines (which assume constant visibility) became more pronounced, confirming TRiPOD's capability in predicting occlusions and disappearances.

- **Separate Evaluation (Center Pose and Trajectory):**
 - TRiPOD also achieved the lowest error rates (VIM) for center pose prediction on both 3DPW and PoseTrack, demonstrating its efficacy in local pose dynamics.
 - For trajectory forecasting, TRiPOD performed comparably to ST-GAT on 3DPW and surpassed other methods on the more challenging PoseTrack dataset.

**Ablation Study (on 3DPW):**
The ablation study systematically evaluated the contribution of each TRiPOD component:
- **Scene Context (C):** Including scene context (extracted by I3D) reduced error rates (e.g., SC-MPF with C reduced 900ms error from 249.18 to 208.31 cm), indicating its importance.
- **Input Pose Representation (P):** Transitioning from tensor-based input (T) to graph attention networks (G) for initial pose encoding yielded notable improvements (e.g., Baseline 1 (3 T G) to Baseline 2 (3 G G) reduced 900ms error from 181.32 to 167.32 cm).
- **Social Module (H):** Using graph attention (G) for social interactions instead of max-pooling (M) also improved results.
- **Human-Object Graph (O):** Explicitly modeling human-object interactions further decreased error (Baseline 2 to Baseline 3, 900ms error from 167.32 to 166.68 cm).
- **Message Passing (M):** Incorporating the iterative message passing mechanism for fusing interaction types contributed to performance gains (Baseline 3 to Baseline 4, 900ms error from 166.68 to 161.72 cm).
- **Future Human Interactions (FH):** Dynamically modeling social interactions during the decoding phase showed a positive effect.
- **Curriculum Learning (CL):** The final integration of curriculum learning provided the best overall performance, demonstrating its effectiveness in handling accumulative errors (TRiPOD to TRiPOD(CL) reduced 900ms error from 153.12 to 150.41 cm).
- **Message Passing Iterations:** An additional ablation study showed that 3 iterations of message passing yielded the optimal performance, with diminishing or negative returns beyond this point.
- **Input Skeleton Representation (Sparse vs. Dense):** A dense (fully connected) graph for input skeleton representation performed better than a sparse (natural connections) graph, suggesting the model benefits from learning inter-joint relationships without predefined constraints.

**Qualitative Results:**
Visualizations demonstrated TRiPOD's ability to produce more accurate and contextually appropriate predictions compared to baselines. Specific examples highlighted:
- The model's capacity to interpret human-object and human-human interactions, leading to more realistic future poses.
- Effective handling of occlusion cases by predicting appropriate poses or indicating invisibility rather than generating erroneous outlier joint predictions.
- Accurate forecasting even when agents partially or completely leave the scene.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of human movement forecasting by proposing TRiPOD, a comprehensive model that unifies trajectory and pose dynamics prediction. The core contributions and their potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Holistic Human Movement Understanding:** By jointly modeling global trajectory and fine-grained pose dynamics, TRiPOD provides a more integrated and accurate representation of future human actions than approaches treating these problems in isolation. This holistic understanding is crucial for applications that require a detailed grasp of human intent and motion.
- **Enhanced Real-World Applicability:** The explicit incorporation of human-human and human-object interactions, coupled with dynamic modeling of these interactions over time, makes TRiPOD more robust for complex "in-the-wild" scenarios. This capability is vital for autonomous systems (e.g., robots, self-driving cars) operating in human-populated environments, where understanding social cues and environmental context is paramount for safe and efficient navigation.
- **Reliability through Invisibility Prediction:** The ability to forecast joint visibility addresses a critical limitation of previous models. By indicating which joints are likely occluded or out of view, TRiPOD provides a measure of prediction reliability. This is particularly impactful for safety-critical applications, enabling systems to make informed decisions by accounting for uncertainty and missing information, such as in collision risk assessment or human-robot collaboration.
- **Robustness to Long-Term Forecasting and Error Accumulation:** The implementation of curriculum learning and skip connections effectively mitigates the problem of accumulating errors in recurrent models over extended prediction horizons. This improves the stability and accuracy of long-term forecasts, which is often a challenge in sequence-to-sequence models.
- **Standardized Evaluation Platform:** The introduction of a new benchmark based on repurposing existing challenging datasets (3DPW and PoseTrack) and the proposal of novel visibility-aware metrics (VIM, VAM, Visibility Score Metric) provide a much-needed standardized platform. This fosters fair comparisons, encourages further research, and facilitates accelerated progress in the joint human trajectory and pose dynamics forecasting domain.
- **Foundational for Advanced AI Systems:** The advancements presented in TRiPOD contribute a fundamental building block for future generations of intelligent systems that need to anticipate and react to human behavior. This includes developing more intuitive and safe human-robot interfaces, proactive surveillance systems for anomaly detection, and sophisticated healthcare applications for movement analysis.

In summary, TRiPOD's integrated approach to modeling complex interactions, handling invisibility, and addressing long-term prediction challenges, combined with its new benchmark, signifies a substantial step towards more practical and reliable human movement forecasting in diverse real-world settings.
