# ReVSI: Rebuilding Visual Spatial Intelligence Evaluation for Accurate Assessment of VLM 3D Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Simon Fraser University and collaborators developed ReVSI, a meticulously rebuilt benchmark for evaluating Visual Spatial Intelligence in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), addressing critical validity flaws in previous evaluation datasets. ReVSI revealed that proprietary VLMs generally outperform open-source models on numerical tasks, while specialized fine-tuned 3D models often exhibit reduced or even negative performance gains, frequently hallucinating due to noisy annotations and data biases in prior benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing VLM benchmarks for visual spatial intelligence suffer from "Annotation-to-Video Ground-Truth Drift," where QA pairs derived from 3D annotations are inconsistent with visual evidence in video frames.
- Current evaluations often assume full-scene observability by VLMs, despite models typically processing sparsely sampled video inputs, leading to a "Scene-Observability Mismatch."
- These systemic flaws yield unreliable conclusions about VLM spatial reasoning and mask crucial model behaviors like hallucination or over-reliance on priors.

## Method

- **ReVSI** was constructed through precise ==video-aligned object and scene geometry re-annotation== across five datasets, using a custom 3D web interface and human experts for high-quality ground truth.
- QA pairs were entirely re-generated with rigorous ==human verification and bias control==, ensuring strict consistency with raw input videos and modifying tasks like object counting and distance estimation for improved validity.
- Frame-budgeted evaluation protocols (16, 32, 64, all frames) were established, along with ==visibility-guided diagnostic tests using "dummy videos"== to assess models' sensitivity to visual input and tendencies for hallucination.

## Results

- ReVSI evaluations **reversed trends observed in VSI-Bench**, demonstrating that proprietary models consistently outperformed open-source counterparts on numerical tasks, while specialized fine-tuned models often showed **reduced or negative performance gains**.
- Dummy video stress tests revealed that proprietary VLMs had **significantly lower hallucination rates** than open-source and fine-tuned models, with fine-tuned models catastrophically failing object counting when objects were absent.
- Leading open-source models exhibited divergent behaviors: **Qwen3-VL** showed minimal hallucination, whereas **InternVL3.5** frequently relied on scene context or memorized priors, even predicting objects in entirely black videos.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prior VLM benchmarks, like VSI-Bench, can provide misleading evaluations due to biased data and ground-truth inconsistencies, specifically affecting the assessment of numerical reasoning and model hallucination.
> - Fine-tuning VLMs on existing 3D spatial intelligence benchmarks can lead models to learn incorrect answers or rely on category-level priors, often resulting in diminished or even detrimental performance compared to base models on more rigorously validated evaluations.
> - Controlled diagnostic tests are essential for distinguishing between genuine visual reasoning and predictions driven by memorized priors or hallucination, uncovering fundamental behavioral differences among VLMs that standard benchmarks alone cannot reveal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by Zhang et al. is positioned within the field of artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the evaluation of Visual-Language Models (VLMs) and their capabilities in 3D spatial reasoning. As VLMs demonstrate increasing proficiency across various tasks, the need for robust and reliable benchmarks to assess their understanding of complex 3D environments has grown.

Prior work in spatial reasoning evaluation primarily focused on models operating on 3D geometric data, utilizing benchmarks derived from datasets like ScanNet. With the advent of more capable VLMs, evaluations have adapted to video-based and embodied settings, exemplified by benchmarks such as VSI-Bench (Yang et al., 2025a), SPAR-Bench (Zhang et al., 2025a), and VSI-SUPER (Yang et al., 2025c). These benchmarks typically repurpose existing 3D annotations for video-based evaluation.

This paper identifies a critical gap in this existing landscape: the validity of current VLM evaluation methods for 3D spatial reasoning. It argues that relying on 3D annotations, originally curated for traditional 3D perception, for video-based VLM evaluation introduces systematic issues, including annotation inaccuracies and a mismatch between assumed scene observability and actual model inputs (sparse frame sampling). ReVSI aims to address these identified pitfalls by constructing a benchmark and associated protocols that align evaluation criteria more precisely with the visual evidence available to VLMs from video inputs. The work contributes to the development of more reliable assessment tools, which in turn supports the accurate development and specialization of VLMs for spatial understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the validity and reliability of benchmarks for evaluating the 3D spatial intelligence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The authors argue that existing evaluation protocols can systematically invalidate assessments under modern VLM settings, leading to potentially misleading conclusions about model capabilities.

Two critical pitfalls are identified as the main motivations for this work:

1. **Annotation-to-Video Ground-Truth Drift:** Existing benchmarks, such as VSI-Bench, frequently derive Question-Answer (QA) pairs from point-cloud-based 3D annotations. These annotations are often generated from imperfect 3D reconstructions (e.g., of indoor scenes) and designed for traditional 3D perception tasks. When repurposed as ground truth for video-based VLM evaluation, this approach introduces systematic discrepancies. Specific issues include:
 - **Missing or Spurious Objects:** Objects clearly visible in raw video frames may be absent in the 3D annotations due to reconstruction artifacts, or vice-versa.
 - **Incorrect Object Labels:** Poor mesh quality can lead to mislabeled object identities.
 - **Corrupted Geometry-Dependent Answers:** Information such as object size, room size, or relative positions, derived from noisy 3D geometry, may be inaccurate when compared to the visual evidence in the video.
 - **Ambiguous QA Pairs:** These inaccuracies result in QA pairs that are either incorrect or ill-defined when evaluated against the raw video content, distorting the true performance signal of VLMs.

2. **Scene-Observability Mismatch under Frame Budgets:** Many current VLM evaluations implicitly assume full-scene access, meaning that all queried objects or scene elements are consistently observable. However, practical VLMs often operate under strict input budgets, processing only sparsely sampled video frames (e.g., 16-64 frames). This mismatch between the full-scene assumption and the model's actual, limited input can render many questions effectively unanswerable or alter their ground truth under the specific frames provided to the model. Questions about object appearance order, for example, are particularly sensitive to frame sampling.

To address these issues, the authors propose ReVSI (Rebuilding Visual Spatial Intelligence Evaluation) with the guiding principle of ensuring strict consistency between what the model "sees" (its input frames) and what the benchmark "asks." The objective is to provide a more accurate and diagnostic assessment of VLM spatial reasoning by:
- Improving the accuracy and consistency of annotations with raw video data.
- Ensuring that QA pairs are valid and answerable given the actual visual input to the model under various frame budgets.
- Enabling controlled diagnostic analyses that can differentiate between failures due to missing visual evidence and failures due to deficient spatial reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ReVSI benchmark and protocols are designed to address the identified validity pitfalls in VLM spatial reasoning evaluation. The methodology involves comprehensive data re-annotation, rigorous QA pair generation with bias mitigation, and the establishment of frame-aware evaluation protocols.

#### 3.1. Video-Aligned Object and Geometry Reannotation

In contrast to prior benchmarks that rely on original, potentially low-quality 3D scene annotations, ReVSI constructs a new set of high-quality manual annotations.
- **Source Datasets:** Annotations are re-curated across 381 scenes from five datasets: ScanNetv2, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes, 3RScan, and MultiScan.
- **Annotation Process:** A custom 3D web interface was developed, enabling annotators to view and refine 3D bounding boxes and object labels while cross-referencing raw video frames and rendered 3D geometry.
- **Object Labels:** An open-vocabulary setting is adopted for object labeling, with labels manually assigned by human annotators. GPT-5.2 is used solely as an auxiliary tool for verification when annotators are uncertain, ensuring fine-grained and precise descriptions.
- **Bounding Boxes:** All objects are assigned tight and accurate 3D bounding boxes. In cases of missing or fragmented geometry, boxes are extrapolated based on raw video frames and spatial context. Bounding boxes are initialized using a gravity-aligned oriented bounding box (OBB) algorithm and then manually refined.
- **Quality Assurance:** All annotation tasks were performed by authors with expertise in 3D datasets, leading to an expansion in scale and diversity (e.g., 381 scenes, 5365 objects, 504 open-vocabulary labels compared to VSI-Bench's 288 scenes, 3185 objects, 65 labels).

#### 3.2. QA Regeneration with Verification and Bias Control

The QA pairs from VSI-Bench are entirely rebuilt to address observed error rates and biases. While task definitions largely align, generation strategies are refined, and comprehensive human verification is enforced for every sample. The "Object Appearance Order" task is excluded due to its focus on temporal reasoning and ambiguity with partially visible objects.
- **Object Counting:**
 - Bias Mitigation: Single-count queries are reintroduced for common categories to reduce the "count of 2" bias in VSI-Bench. Cumulative counting questions across two categories are added.
 - Query Refinement: The query template is changed from "How many objects are in this room?" to "How many objects are in the scene?" to align with ground truth, which often counts objects globally across multi-room or open-plan areas. Ambiguous categories (e.g., shoes) are excluded.
- **Object Size Estimation:**
 - Bias Mitigation: Object categories with near-fixed dimensions (e.g., toilet, bed) are excluded. Instances of other categories are selectively sampled to ensure out-of-distribution examples.
 - Ground Truth Improvement: Manual 3D bounding box annotations provide accurate object dimensions, improving over VSI-Bench's reliance on noisy 3D segmentation masks.
- **Object Absolute Distance:** Questions with ground-truth distances below 1m (often solvable with 2D cues) are removed. More long-range object pairs are added, and questions with 1-3m distances are subsampled to create a broader distance distribution.
- **Object Relative Direction:** Positioning objects with footprints exceeding 1 m² are excluded, and a minimum separation of 1m between referenced objects is required to reduce ambiguity. Templates where the agent faces away from the orienting object are added.
- **Object Relative Distance:** Questions with relative distance differences less than 0.3m or query-anchor distances below 1m are excluded to avoid ambiguity and trivial cases. Farthest object queries are introduced.
- **Room Size Estimation:**
 - Ground Truth Improvement: Manual annotation of room boundary polygons from orthogonal top-down views addresses inaccuracies from VSI-Bench's Alpha Shape algorithm on noisy 3D reconstructions. Scenes with ill-defined boundaries are excluded.
 - Query Refinement: An additional template is added to ask for the area of the main single room, complementing the combined area query.

#### 3.3. Frame-Aware Evaluation Protocols

- **Frame-Budgeted Evaluation:** QA pairs are constructed for 16, 32, 64, and all-frame sampling settings. Object visibility is determined by rasterizing sampled frames and computing 2D projections. Objects with less than 5% pixel coverage are manually annotated for visibility. Room Size Estimation and Route Planning are excluded from the 16-frame setting due to insufficient context. The protocol ensures that questions are answerable and ground truth is correct under each specific frame budget, unlike VSI-Bench, which uses full-scene ground truth for subsampled inputs.
- **Visibility-Guided Controlled Diagnostics (Dummy Videos):**
 - **Construction:** Under the 16-frame setting, dummy videos are created by modifying original videos. For a given question, all frames containing the queried object are removed (Query-Drop). Other variants include repeating the first frame of a Query-Drop video (First-Frame) or using entirely black frames (Black).
 - **Purpose:** These dummy videos serve as stress tests to assess whether model predictions are grounded in visual input or driven by memorized indoor-scene priors or hallucination. The ground truth for object counting in these settings is deterministically zero.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VLMs on ReVSI revealed several insights into model spatial reasoning capabilities, often diverging from conclusions drawn from prior benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Discrepancies with VSI-Bench Evaluation

- **Numerical Reasoning:** ReVSI proved to be more challenging due to its higher-fidelity annotations and reduced bias. On numerical tasks, particularly object counting, VSI-Bench systematically underestimated proprietary models' performance while making open-source models appear stronger. On ReVSI, proprietary models (e.g., GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro) consistently outperformed open-source counterparts (e.g., Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5). This indicates that the apparent advantage of open-source models on VSI-Bench was linked to hallucination behaviors not adequately penalized by its biased data.
- **Absolute Distance Estimation:** Despite ReVSI introducing more challenging long-range queries and removing short-range (<1m) questions, many models achieved higher scores on ReVSI for absolute distance estimation. This was attributed to the interaction between the Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) metric, which is more tolerant of absolute error at larger ground-truth distances, and models' strong performance on longer ranges. VSI-Bench's skew towards smaller distances resulted in stricter penalties.

#### 4.2. Performance of Specialized VLMs

- **Limited Gains:** Models explicitly fine-tuned for 3D spatial intelligence (e.g., Cambrian-S, SpaceR, Spatial-MLLM, VLM3R) showed substantially smaller performance improvements on ReVSI compared to their base models, often contrasting with large gains reported on VSI-Bench. In some cases (e.g., SpaceR), fine-tuning led to performance degradation across multiple tasks.
- **Causes for Discrepancy:** This was attributed to two main factors: (1) specialized models learning incorrect answers from VSI-Bench's noisy 3D annotations, interfering with existing capabilities, and (2) biases in training data encouraging reliance on category-level priors or hallucinated cues rather than genuine visual evidence.
- **Data Quantity vs. Quality:** Increasing training data size for fine-tuned models (e.g., Spatial-MLLM from 135k to 820k samples) yielded only marginal performance gains (around 3%), suggesting that data quality and supervision fidelity are more significant bottlenecks than quantity alone.

#### 4.3. Visibility-Based Stress Tests and Hallucination

- **Object Counting on Dummy Videos:** Human performance on dummy videos (Query-Drop, First-Frame, Black), where queried objects were absent and ground truth was 0, exhibited zero hallucination. Proprietary models generally showed lower hallucination rates than open-source models, indicating stronger grounding in visual evidence.
 - **Catastrophic Failure of Fine-tuned Models:** All fine-tuned models failed catastrophically on object counting in dummy video settings, frequently predicting non-zero counts despite object absence. This revealed severe overfitting to noisy training supervision and a disregard for visual input.
 - **Divergence between Zero-Shot Models:** A notable divergence was observed between InternVL3.5 and Qwen3-VL. While both performed comparably on standard object counting, Qwen3-VL correctly predicted 0 when objects were absent and showed near-zero hallucination on black videos. InternVL3.5, however, was easily misled by Query-Drop videos (retaining scene context but lacking objects) and consistently predicted a count of 2 even on black videos, aligning with observed dataset bias. This suggested InternVL3.5 memorized scene priors rather than reasoning from visual evidence.
- **Object Size Estimation on Dummy Videos:** Similar findings emerged for object size estimation. InternVL3.5 relied heavily on priors, achieving high task scores even on black videos, despite explicit prompts to base answers on visual evidence. Qwen3-VL exhibited almost zero hallucination in this setting. This reinforced that high benchmark performance can mask fundamentally different reasoning behaviors, effectively exposed by ReVSI's diagnostic tools.

#### 4.4. Fine-grained Performance Analysis

- **Task Variants:** Performance asymmetries were found across different question formulations. Models achieved higher accuracy when counting single objects compared to multiple objects, and performed better at identifying the closest object rather than the farthest. Forward-facing directional queries were also easier to handle than backward-facing ones.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReVSI benchmark and its associated evaluation protocols offer several significant contributions to the field of VLM research, with potential impacts on model development and assessment.

**Improved Evaluation Validity and Reliability:**
ReVSI addresses fundamental validity issues in existing spatial reasoning benchmarks, such as inconsistent ground truth, poor annotation quality, and scene-observability mismatches. By enforcing frame-budget-aware evaluation, visibility-consistent question generation, and systematic human verification, ReVSI provides a more accurate and reliable foundation for assessing VLMs' 3D spatial intelligence. This improved reliability can help researchers avoid misleading conclusions about model capabilities, which is crucial for guiding research and development efforts.

**Enhanced Diagnostic Capabilities:**
A key impact of ReVSI is its ability to facilitate fine-grained diagnostic analyses. The introduction of frame-budgeted evaluation protocols allows for a systematic study of model performance under varying visual evidence availability. More notably, the visibility-guided controlled diagnostics, particularly the dummy video settings, enable researchers to differentiate between genuine spatial reasoning and predictions driven by memorized scene priors, statistical biases, or hallucination. This diagnostic capability reveals systematic behavioral differences in VLMs that are obscured by conventional evaluation methods, providing deeper insights into model grounding and robustness.

**Guidance for VLM Development:**
By exposing limitations such as hallucination, reliance on non-visual priors, and the diminishing returns of data scaling under current data construction paradigms, ReVSI can inform and redirect VLM development strategies. Researchers can use ReVSI to:
- Develop models that are better grounded in visual evidence rather than susceptible to dataset biases.
- Focus on improving the quality and fidelity of supervision data rather than merely increasing data quantity.
- Design architectures and training methodologies that explicitly account for varying levels of visual observability and avoid overfitting to noisy or biased annotations.

**Implications for Real-World Applications:**
Accurate assessment of 3D spatial reasoning is important for applications where VLMs interact with and interpret physical 3D environments. This includes robotics, augmented reality, scene understanding, and potentially even medical imaging analysis. Misleading evaluations can lead to overconfidence in VLM capabilities, which could have safety or functional implications in sensitive applications. ReVSI contributes to ensuring that VLMs deployed in such contexts have a genuinely robust understanding of space.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that the high-quality, expert-level human annotation process for ReVSI is costly, limiting its scalability to significantly larger datasets or training-scale supervision. This indicates a future research direction towards developing automated or semi-automated pipelines for generating high-quality spatial supervision more efficiently. This work highlights a broader need for robust data curation in multimodal AI research.
