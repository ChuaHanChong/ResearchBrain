# Taming Diffusion Probabilistic Models for Character Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, the University of Hong Kong, and Tencent AI Lab developed a framework leveraging diffusion probabilistic models for real-time character control. Their Conditional Autoregressive Motion Diffusion Model (CAMDM) produces high-quality, diverse, and controllable character animations that respond seamlessly to user input at over 60 frames per second.

## Problem

- Traditional methods for real-time character animation struggled to produce diverse and natural-looking motions while maintaining precise control and computational efficiency.
- Adapting generative diffusion models for interactive character control faced challenges including high computational cost (requiring many denoising steps), difficulty in maintaining precise control, lack of motion diversity, and issues with smooth style transitions.
- Previous deep learning approaches for character control often lacked motion diversity and sometimes produced artifacts, while probabilistic models struggled with trajectory tracking and real-time performance.

## Method

- The authors introduce a ==Conditional Autoregressive Motion Diffusion Model (CAMDM)== that generates each animation frame sequentially based on past motion, user-specified style, future trajectory guidance, and facing direction.
- The model employs a transformer-based architecture with Separate Condition Tokenization (SCT) to process distinct conditioning information and a technique called Classifier-Free Guidance on Past Motion (CFG-PM) for smooth style transitions.
- A Heuristic Future Trajectory Extension (HFTE) technique is incorporated to improve motion diversity and ensure smooth trajectory following by intelligently extending predicted future paths.

## Results

- The system generates natural animations with fewer artifacts, superior to previous methods in motion quality, control precision, and diversity.
- Characters accurately follow user-specified trajectories and maintain proper orientation while smoothly transitioning between animation styles, even without explicit transition data.
- The approach achieves real-time performance at over 60 frames per second on consumer-grade hardware by using only 8 denoising diffusion steps during inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separate Condition Tokenization (SCT) effectively balances the influence of diverse control signals, preventing any single feature from dominating the generation process.
> - Classifier-Free Guidance on Past Motion (CFG-PM) enables smooth and natural transitions between animation styles by dynamically reducing the influence of past motion during style changes.
> - Heuristic Future Trajectory Extension (HFTE) enhances motion diversity and smoothness by intelligently extending predicted trajectories, mitigating jitter and promoting natural movements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Character animation in real-time interactive applications like video games and virtual reality experiences demands a delicate balance between realism, responsiveness, and computational efficiency. Traditional methods have struggled to produce diverse and natural-looking character motions while maintaining precise control and real-time performance.

This paper, authored by researchers from the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, the University of Hong Kong, and Tencent AI Lab, introduces a novel framework that leverages diffusion probabilistic models for real-time character control. The approach successfully addresses the longstanding challenges of generating high-quality, diverse, and controllable character animations that respond seamlessly to user input in real-time applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

Character animation has evolved significantly over the years, from simple state machines and motion graphs to sophisticated deep learning approaches. Recent advancements in generative models, particularly diffusion-based approaches, have shown remarkable capabilities in producing high-quality motion sequences. However, adapting these models for interactive character control presents several challenges:

1. **Real-time performance** - Diffusion models typically require hundreds or thousands of denoising steps, making them too computationally expensive for interactive applications.
2. **Control precision** - Maintaining accurate control over character movement while preserving motion naturalness is difficult.
3. **Motion diversity** - Generating varied animations that don't appear repetitive or mechanical is essential for realism.
4. **Style transitions** - Smoothly switching between different animation styles (walking, running, jumping) remains challenging.

Previous methods have addressed these challenges separately but often make trade-offs between quality, diversity, and computational efficiency. Deep learning approaches like Phase-Functioned Neural Networks (PFNN) improved character control but still struggled with motion diversity and artifacts. Probabilistic models such as VAEs and normalizing flows enhanced diversity but faced issues with trajectory tracking and real-time performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Technical Approach

The paper introduces a Conditional Autoregressive Motion Diffusion Model (CAMDM) that effectively "tames" diffusion models for real-time character control. The approach operates within an autoregressive framework where each frame of animation is generated sequentially based on:

1. Past character motion history
2. User-specified style labels
3. Future trajectory guidance
4. Facing direction control

The system takes these inputs and generates the next animation frame in real-time, allowing for responsive character control while maintaining motion quality and diversity.

The authors present three key algorithmic designs that address the specific challenges of adapting diffusion models for real-time character control:

#### 3.2. Separate Condition Tokenization (SCT)

Rather than representing all conditioning information as a single feature vector, the model learns separate tokens for each condition type: past motion, style label, future root positions, and orientations. This prevents certain features from dominating the regression process and ensures more stable control over the character.

```
Condition types:
- Past motion token (p)
- Style label token (c_l)
- Future root positions token (c_rv)
- Future root orientations token (c_ro)
- Diffusion timestep token (t)
```

This approach allows the model to properly balance the influence of different control signals and prevents overemphasis on any particular aspect.

#### 3.3. Classifier-Free Guidance on Past Motion (CFG-PM)

To facilitate smooth transitions between animation styles (e.g., from walking to running), the model applies classifier-free guidance specifically to the past motion token. During style transitions, the influence of past motion is dynamically reduced, allowing the model to focus more on the new style label.

The mathematical formulation for this approach is:

$$\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, p, c) = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \emptyset, c) + s \cdot (\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, p, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \emptyset, c))$$

Where $s$ is the guidance scale that can be adjusted during transitions, $p$ represents past motion, and $c$ contains other conditioning information. This differs from standard classifier-free guidance which typically applies to style labels.

#### 3.4. Heuristic Future Trajectory Extension (HFTE)

To improve motion diversity and ensure smooth transitions, the model incorporates a technique that extends the predicted future trajectory using a heuristic approach. This involves:

1. Taking the model-predicted future trajectory
2. Flipping part of this trajectory by 180 degrees
3. Recycling this modified trajectory to create extended future guidance

This approach prevents abrupt jittering that can occur when blending model-predicted and user-supplied trajectories, resulting in more natural character movements.

#### 3.5. Model Architecture

The CAMDM employs a transformer-based architecture that processes the various conditioning tokens and generates the next animation frame.

The motion is represented using local root displacements and 6D joint rotations, which are converted to global positions during training using differentiable forward kinematics. This representation allows for precise control while maintaining the natural expressiveness of character movements.

A critical aspect of the implementation is the use of only 8 denoising diffusion steps during inference, dramatically reduced from the 1000 steps typically used in other diffusion models. This optimization enables real-time performance without significantly compromising motion quality.

#### 3.6. Real-time Character Animation

The real-time animation system operates by:

1. Collecting user input (trajectory, facing direction, style selections)
2. Blending the user-supplied trajectory with model-predicted and heuristically extended trajectories
3. Feeding conditioning information into the CAMDM
4. Running 8 denoising steps to generate the next animation frame
5. Applying the generated pose to the character
6. Repeating this process for each frame of animation

This pipeline runs at over 60 frames per second on consumer-grade hardware (RTX 3060 GPU), making it suitable for interactive applications like games and VR experiences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Results and Performance

The authors evaluate their system against several baseline approaches, demonstrating superior performance in terms of:

1. **Motion quality** - The generated animations appear more natural and contain fewer artifacts compared to previous methods.
2. **Control precision** - Characters accurately follow user-specified trajectories and maintain proper orientation.
3. **Motion diversity** - The system produces varied animations even when following similar paths, avoiding the repetitive movements common in deterministic approaches.
4. **Style transitions** - Characters smoothly transition between different animation styles, even when transition data is absent from the training dataset.

Ablation studies confirm the importance of each proposed component. Removing Separate Condition Tokenization led to overly smooth motions and control alignment issues. Disabling the Heuristic Future Trajectory Extension resulted in jitters during runtime. Substituting the transformer backbone with an MLP decreased network performance. The studies also showed that applying classifier-free guidance to past motion produced better results than applying it to style labels.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Applications and Impact

The proposed framework has significant implications for several domains:

1. **Video games** - Enabling more realistic and diverse character animations that respond naturally to player input.
2. **Virtual reality** - Creating immersive experiences with believable virtual characters.
3. **Animation production** - Providing animators with tools to rapidly generate high-quality character movements.
4. **Robotics** - Potentially informing more natural movement patterns for humanoid robots.

The ability to generate diverse animations in real-time while maintaining precise control addresses a fundamental challenge in interactive character animation. This advancement could lead to more engaging and realistic virtual experiences across multiple platforms.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its achievements, the system has several limitations that suggest directions for future research:

1. **Environmental interaction** - The current implementation focuses on locomotion but does not handle complex interactions with the environment or objects.
2. **Character variations** - The model is trained on a specific character skeleton and does not automatically adapt to characters with different body proportions.
3. **Computational requirements** - While achieving real-time performance, the system still requires dedicated GPU hardware to run efficiently.

Future work could extend the approach to handle more complex scenarios such as character interactions with objects, terrain adaptation, and physical constraints. Additionally, optimizing the model for deployment on less powerful devices like mobile phones or standalone VR headsets would broaden its applicability.

#### 5.3. Conclusion

The paper presents a significant advancement in real-time character control by effectively adapting diffusion probabilistic models for interactive applications. The proposed Conditional Autoregressive Motion Diffusion Model (CAMDM) with its three key innovations—Separate Condition Tokenization, Classifier-Free Guidance on Past Motion, and Heuristic Future Trajectory Extension—successfully addresses the challenges of generating high-quality, diverse, and controllable character animations in real-time.

By reducing the computational requirements of diffusion models while maintaining their generative power, this work bridges the gap between offline motion generation and interactive character control. The results demonstrate that diffusion models can indeed be "tamed" for real-time applications, opening new possibilities for creating more engaging and realistic interactive experiences.

This research represents an important step toward the next generation of character animation systems that combine the quality and diversity of sophisticated generative models with the responsiveness required for interactive applications.
