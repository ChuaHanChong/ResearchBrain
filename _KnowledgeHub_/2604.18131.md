# Training LLM Agents for Spontaneous, Reward-Free Self-Evolution via World Knowledge Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> A method trains Large Language Model agents to spontaneously explore unseen environments and generate structured "World Knowledge" without external human supervision or rewards during inference. This "Native Evolution" approach, optimized via an outcome-based reward during training, yielded up to a 19% absolute increase in task success rates and a 17% efficiency gain, allowing smaller models with self-generated knowledge to surpass larger, unassisted models in performance.

## Problem

- Existing "self-evolving" LLM agents rely heavily on external human supervision, such as predefined tasks, workflows, or verified reward signals.
- Agents lack the ability for true autonomy and spontaneous learning in novel environments without explicit instructions or rewards, unlike human curiosity-driven exploration.
- Current paradigms often shift the engineering burden to complex agent orchestration rather than enabling genuine workflow-free and reward-free self-evolution.

## Method

- Introduces **Native Evolution**, a ==decoupled agent lifecycle== where agents autonomously explore and summarize an environment into structured ==World Knowledge (K)== without tasks or rewards at inference time.
- Develops an ==outcome-based reward function== that measures the utility of generated K by its performance on potential downstream tasks, used exclusively during training.
- Employs a ==two-stage training framework==: **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** to internalize meta-evolution behaviors, followed by **Reinforcement-based Rejection Sampling (RFT)** to optimize exploration and information management.

## Results

- Trained models achieved up to a **+19% absolute increase** in task success rates (e.g., **40.91% vs. 22.04%** baseline on WebWalker) and an average **17% efficiency improvement** by reducing execution steps.
- Self-evolved World Knowledge demonstrated universal portability, boosting compact models (e.g., **Qwen3-14B accuracy by +18.3%**) and enabling them to surpass larger, unassisted models.
- Analysis showed **diminishing returns** for excessively long World Knowledge, indicating an optimal balance between informative content and redundant noise.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The utility of self-generated World Knowledge can be quantitatively defined by its impact on an agent's downstream task performance, providing a learning signal for truly reward-free self-evolution at inference.
> - High-quality, spontaneously generated environmental knowledge is a more critical performance bottleneck for agents than raw model scale, enabling compact models to outperform larger, unassisted ones.
> - Training through SFT and RFT enables agents to internalize meta-evolution capabilities, allowing them to spontaneously construct effective "cognitive maps" of unknown environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Current research into "self-evolving" agents typically relies on external human supervision, such as predefined tasks, meticulously engineered workflows, or verified reward signals, to guide their improvement. The paper identifies two main existing paradigms:

- **Experience-Driven Evolution:** This approach involves agents solving human-crafted tasks and receiving feedback via predefined, environment-specific reward functions. The agent learns by optimizing its performance based on accumulated experience (trajectory-score pairs). While it allows for refinement of system prompts, memory, tool libraries, or internal model parameters, it is limited by the extensive human effort required to design tasks and rewards.
- **Adversarial Evolution:** This paradigm attempts to reduce manual design by employing a challenger-solver dynamic. A challenger agent synthesizes environment-specific tasks for a solver to execute, pushing the solver to improve. While it reduces human labor for task and reward design, the authors argue it merely shifts the engineering burden to orchestrating complex agent workflows, and the agent remains confined to solving "synthesized exercise books" rather than engaging in unguided exploration.

This work addresses the fundamental limitation of these paradigms: the lack of true autonomy and spontaneous learning in novel, unseen environments without external guidance or rewards. It proposes a **Meta-Learning-Driven Evolution** paradigm, aiming to move beyond task-specific optimization towards a "Workflow-free and Reward-free self-evolution." The paper also differentiates its approach from "Test-Time Training (TTT)," which requires gradient-based weight updates or parameter modifications during inference, making it incompatible with mainstream high-throughput inference frameworks. In contrast, this work distills observations into structured world knowledge that is fed into the agent's prompt as an external context module, avoiding runtime training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Large Language Model (LLM) agents to possess an intrinsic "meta-evolution" capability, allowing them to spontaneously learn about unseen environments *prior* to task execution, without human-defined rewards or instructions at inference time. The core motivations are:

- **Overcoming Dependency on External Supervision:** The authors argue that most existing "self-evolving" agents are not truly autonomous. Their evolution is fundamentally dependent on human-defined workflows, tasks, and reward signals. Without this external guidance, the evolution ceases, indicating a lack of native intelligence.
- **Mimicking Human Intuition:** Human intelligence is characterized by curiosity and proactive learning. When encountering a new environment, humans spontaneously explore and build an internal understanding or "map" without explicit tasks or rewards. Current agents lack this ability, remaining passive and awaiting instructions.
- **Achieving Workflow-free and Reward-free Self-Evolution:** The goal is to bridge this gap by granting agents "Native Agency" – the ability to proactively understand a new environment before a specific task is assigned. This entails developing an intrinsic meta-evolution capability: exploring a novel environment and distilling observations into structured "World Knowledge" entirely autonomously.
- **Defining a Training Signal for Task-Free Exploration:** A significant challenge is how to train an agent to explore and summarize effectively when the evolution phase itself is task-free and thus lacks immediate ground-truth labels or traditional rewards. The motivation is to devise an outcome-based reward mechanism that measures the *utility* of self-generated knowledge, thereby providing a learning signal for effective meta-evolution.
- **Enhancing Agent Performance and Efficiency:** By providing agents with a "mental map" of the environment, the expectation is that they will perform better and more efficiently on subsequent downstream tasks, even with compact models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces "Native Evolution" by allowing agents to generate "World Knowledge (K)"—a compact, structured representation of an environment's landscape. This knowledge is implemented as a Markdown document, an external module compatible with existing agent architectures. The agent's life cycle is decoupled into two phases:

1. **Native Evolution Phase:** Upon entering a new environment (E), the agent autonomously explores and summarizes to generate `K` (i.e., `K ← π_evolve(K|E)`). This phase is entirely task-free and reward-free at inference time.
2. **Knowledge-Enhanced Execution Phase:** When a downstream task is assigned, the agent uses `K` to guide its actions (i.e., `a_t ∼ π_task(a_t|o_t, K, Task)`).

The central technical challenge is training an agent to explore and summarize effectively without human-provided rewards. This is addressed by an outcome-based reward mechanism and a two-stage training framework.

#### 3.1. Outcome-Based Reward Design

The quality of generated World Knowledge (K) is defined by its utility in improving the agent's performance on potential downstream tasks.
- **Reward Function:** `R_evolve(K) = Success(T_E|K) − Success(T_E|∅)`, where `T_E` is a set of downstream tasks for environment `E`. `Success(T_E|K)` is the agent’s performance aided by `K`, and `Success(T_E|∅)` is the baseline without prior knowledge.
- **Practical Implementation:** A training set of 600 deep search questions across 20 websites is used. For each environment with `M` labeled tasks, `Success(T_E|K)` is calculated as the average accuracy `(1/M) * Σ [f(Q_j, K) = A_j]`, where `f(Q_j, K)` is the agent's predicted answer with knowledge `K`.
- **Crucial Point:** This reward signal is used *exclusively during training* to teach the model how to identify and compress high-value information. At inference time, the agent performs native evolution without any external rewards or predefined tasks.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training Framework

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Objective:** To warm up the base policy model (`π_θ_0`) and enable it to internalize meta-evolution behaviors (planning, exploring, refining, summarizing).
 - **Teacher Model:** Gemini-2.5-Pro (`π_T`) is used to generate expert trajectories.
 - **Data Generation Pipeline:** `π_T` interacts with diverse web environments and autonomously constructs structured world knowledge.
 - **Data Selection:** For each environment, `π_T` generates three candidate `K` representations. These are evaluated by their performance gain on downstream tasks using a baseline agent (Qwen3-30B-A3B). The best-performing `K*` and its full exploration trajectory (`T*`) are selected as step-level training data.
 - **Outcome:** The fine-tuned model (`π_θ_1`) develops a foundational instinct for autonomous evolution. The teacher-generated `K*` showed an average absolute accuracy improvement of 10.72% for the baseline model.

2. **Reinforcement-based Rejection Sampling (RFT):**
 - **Objective:** To further optimize exploration and information management strategies via trial-and-error, addressing the limitations of standard online RL (extremely long horizons, heavy reward evaluation).
 - **Process:** The `π_θ_1` policy autonomously explores environments to produce candidate world knowledge representations. These candidates are evaluated using the `R_evolve` function. The highest-scoring trajectories (demonstrating strongest meta-evolution utility) are selected to construct the training set for the next iteration.
 - **Iterations:** This rejection sampling process is performed for two iterations.
 - **Outcome:** The final optimized policy (`π_θ*`) internalizes the ability to adapt to unknown environments, spontaneously constructing high-utility world knowledge at inference time without external guidance.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Agent Framework:** Cognitive Kernel-Pro is used, with Playwright for web interaction.
- **Action Space:** Predefined webpage operations (click, scroll, goto, goback, stop).
- **Observation:** Accessibility tree of visible webpage components.
- **Input Processing for Native Evolution:** Websites are pre-processed into a more navigable format (`G(U)`) using:
 - **Importance Scoring:** Websites are modeled as directed graphs, and each webpage's importance is scored based on in-degree and out-degree.
 - **Clustering:** Webpages are grouped into clusters based on shared URL prefixes. This reduces cognitive load for the agent.
- **Instruction Construction (SFT Stage):** A specific prompt guides the teacher agent to generate world knowledge within token budget constraints, involving token allocation planning, exploration, summarization, and refinement.
- **Backbone Models:** Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 and Seed-OSS-36B-Instruct were used for training and evaluation.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Subsets of WebWalker (conference, game, organization, education domains) and WebVoyager (Wolfram, Apple, Dictionary, Coursera websites) were used, filtering out questions answerable by pretrained knowledge.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Accuracy.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** LLM judges (Qwen-2.5-32B for WebWalker, Gemini-2.5-Flash for WebVoyager) were used to verify answers against ground truth.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Effectiveness and Efficiency (RQ1)

- **Effectiveness:** The trained models demonstrated significant improvements in task success rates. On WebWalker, `Ours (RFT)` achieved a 40.91% success rate (with Qwen3-30B-A3B), an almost 19% absolute increase over the `Without` baseline (22.04%), and surpassed the `Prompt-only (Gemini)` teacher (29.85%). Untrained models (`Prompt-only (Base)`) performed worse than the baseline, indicating that without training, self-generated knowledge can be detrimental ("noise bottleneck").
- **Efficiency:** Integrating world knowledge resulted in an average efficiency improvement of 17% across various domain websites (Table 2), as measured by a reduction in execution steps. This suggests that the generated knowledge acts as a "cognitive map," guiding the agent to relevant pages more swiftly.

#### 4.2. Cross-Model World Knowledge Transfer (RQ2)

- **Universal Portability:** The self-evolved world knowledge (K) proved highly transferable across different parameter scales and model families (Qwen3-14B, GPT-OSS-120B, Kimi-K2-Turbo, Gemini-2.5-Flash). For example, knowledge generated by Seed-36B increased Qwen3-14B's average accuracy by 18.3% across two domains and boosted Kimi-K2-Turbo by 21.0%.
- **Exploration over Parameters:** The results indicated that high-quality environment exploration via generated K can be more effective than brute-force parameter scaling. A compact Qwen3-14B model, when equipped with K, outperformed the unassisted Gemini-2.5-Flash (e.g., 35.6% vs. 31.3% on the Conference domain). Lighter models with transferred K could even surpass the performance of their unassisted, larger counterparts (e.g., Kimi-K2-Turbo vs. Kimi-K2.5, Gemini-2.5-Flash vs. Gemini-2.5-Pro). This suggests that accurate world knowledge of an environment is a more critical bottleneck for agent performance than model scale.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (RQ3)

- Performance consistently improved across training stages (base, SFT, RFT1, RFT2).
- Both the initial SFT stage and the first round of RFT (RFT1) yielded substantial performance boosts.
- The subsequent RFT round (RFT2) provided marginal gains or slight fluctuations, indicating that SFT and RFT1 establish the crucial foundation for meta-evolution capabilities.

#### 4.4. Sensitivity Analysis (RQ4)

- Increasing the length of generated world knowledge demonstrated diminishing returns.
- Expanding from a short context (e.g., 4k-8k tokens to 8k-16k tokens) led to substantial gains (e.g., success rate on game websites jumped from 30.74% to 39.71%).
- Further lengthening an already extensive context provided marginal benefits or even a slight decline (e.g., from 16k-32k to 32k-64k tokens on game websites, accuracy declined from 41.56% to 40.72%).
- This non-linear trend is attributed to information loss in overly short contexts and the introduction of redundant noise in excessively long contexts.

#### 4.5. Case Study

A case study on the ACL 2024 website demonstrated that with world knowledge, the agent could retrieve key information and identify required dates within two steps, leading to a correct answer. Without world knowledge, the agent required more steps, found partial information, and ultimately failed to locate crucial details, resulting in an incorrect answer. This illustrates how world knowledge provides contextual guidance for more effective problem-solving.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes to the field of AI agents by presenting a paradigm for truly autonomous self-evolution, moving beyond reliance on human-defined tasks and rewards during inference.

- **Autonomous Agent Development:** By enabling agents to spontaneously explore and distill environment knowledge, the research moves closer to realizing agents with "Native Agency" that can adapt to entirely unknown environments without human intervention.
- **Re-evaluation of Scaling Laws:** The finding that compact models equipped with self-generated world knowledge can outperform larger, unassisted models suggests that effective exploration and knowledge acquisition might be a more critical factor for agent performance than raw parameter count. This could inform future agent design, shifting focus towards "scaling capacity for proactive exploration and learning" rather than solely parameter scaling.
- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** The demonstrated 20% absolute performance improvement and 17% efficiency gain highlight the practical benefits of this approach for web-based tasks and potentially other domains requiring environmental understanding.
- **Foundation for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** The ability to spontaneously understand and adapt to novel environments, analogous to human curiosity-driven learning, is considered a fundamental step towards achieving AGI. This research contributes to that long-term goal by providing a framework for agents to build internal representations of the world autonomously.
- **Robustness in Dynamic Environments:** Agents capable of spontaneous knowledge generation could be more robust in dynamic or rapidly changing environments, where pre-programmed knowledge or human-defined tasks quickly become outdated.
