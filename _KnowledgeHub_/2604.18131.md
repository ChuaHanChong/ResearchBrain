# Training LLM Agents for Spontaneous, Reward-Free Self-Evolution via World Knowledge Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> A method trains Large Language Model agents to spontaneously explore unseen environments and generate structured "World Knowledge" without external human supervision or rewards during inference. This "Native Evolution" approach, optimized via an outcome-based reward during training, yielded up to a 19% absolute increase in task success rates and a 17% efficiency gain, allowing smaller models with self-generated knowledge to surpass larger, unassisted models in performance.

## Problem

- Existing "self-evolving" LLM agents rely heavily on external human supervision, such as predefined tasks, workflows, or verified reward signals.
- Agents lack the ability for true autonomy and spontaneous learning in novel environments without explicit instructions or rewards, unlike human curiosity-driven exploration.
- Current paradigms often shift the engineering burden to complex agent orchestration rather than enabling genuine workflow-free and reward-free self-evolution.

## Method

- Introduces **Native Evolution**, a ==decoupled agent lifecycle== where agents autonomously explore and summarize an environment into structured ==World Knowledge (K)== without tasks or rewards at inference time.
- Develops an ==outcome-based reward function== that measures the utility of generated K by its performance on potential downstream tasks, used exclusively during training.
- Employs a ==two-stage training framework==: **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** to internalize meta-evolution behaviors, followed by **Reinforcement-based Rejection Sampling (RFT)** to optimize exploration and information management.

## Results

- Trained models achieved up to a **+19% absolute increase** in task success rates (e.g., **40.91% vs. 22.04%** baseline on WebWalker) and an average **17% efficiency improvement** by reducing execution steps.
- Self-evolved World Knowledge demonstrated universal portability, boosting compact models (e.g., **Qwen3-14B accuracy by +18.3%**) and enabling them to surpass larger, unassisted models.
- Analysis showed **diminishing returns** for excessively long World Knowledge, indicating an optimal balance between informative content and redundant noise.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The utility of self-generated World Knowledge can be quantitatively defined by its impact on an agent's downstream task performance, providing a learning signal for truly reward-free self-evolution at inference.
> - High-quality, spontaneously generated environmental knowledge is a more critical performance bottleneck for agents than raw model scale, enabling compact models to outperform larger, unassisted ones.
> - Training through SFT and RFT enables agents to internalize meta-evolution capabilities, allowing them to spontaneously construct effective "cognitive maps" of unknown environments.
