# EgoAVFlow: Robot Policy Learning with Active Vision from Human Egocentric Videos via 3D Flow

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Tech and KAIST developed EgoAVFlow, a framework that enables robots to learn manipulation policies and active vision strategies from human egocentric videos using a shared 3D flow representation. This approach resulted in a 1.8x to 2.5x improvement in task success rates compared to existing methods, demonstrating superior visibility maintenance and robust manipulation under actively varying camera viewpoints.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation frequently requires active viewpoint adjustment for critical visibility, a capability often missing when learning from passive egocentric human videos.
- Directly imitating human viewpoints is often suboptimal for robots due to human-specific biological priors that do not translate effectively to robotic perception.
- Existing representation gaps make it challenging to develop view-invariant policies that bridge the human-robot embodiment difference and support geometric visibility reasoning for active perception.

## Method

- Introduced a shared 3D flow representation, derived from human egocentric videos, to bridge the human-robot embodiment gap and serve as an embodiment-agnostic interface for both manipulation and viewpoint control.
- Developed a modular framework using three diffusion models to predict future robot actions, future 3D flow, and camera viewpoints, conditioned on historical sensory data and predicted actions.
- Implemented a visibility-aware reward function with a soft value-based denoising strategy to refine predicted viewpoints at test time, actively maximizing the visibility of task-critical elements without needing differentiable rewards.

## Results

- EgoAVFlow achieved significantly higher success rates across four manipulation tasks, outperforming Human Viewpoint Imitation (HVI) by up to 4x (e.g., 20/25 vs. 5/25 for 'Spray') and other baselines like EgoZero by 1.8x to 2.5x.
- The visibility-aware view policy consistently yielded higher average visibility rewards compared to the HVI baseline, ensuring task-critical elements remained in view.
- Failure analysis showed EgoAVFlow drastically reduced manipulation-related failures (grasping and object pose) compared to baselines, demonstrating its robustness under active vision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous, active viewpoint adjustment is essential for robust manipulation tasks, as fixed camera perspectives inevitably lead to occlusions and failures.
> - A robot's independent, visibility-optimized viewpoint strategy significantly outperforms direct imitation of human viewpoints, validating the need to decouple robotic perception from human biological priors.
> - The 3D flow representation enables a manipulation policy that is robust to actively changing viewpoints, addressing the challenges of view-invariance and embodiment transfer simultaneously.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work resides at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: robot learning from demonstration, egocentric computer vision, active perception, and embodiment transfer. The overarching goal of enabling robots to learn complex manipulation skills directly from human videos has garnered significant attention due to the scalability and richness of human data compared to costly robot-specific data collection.

**Learning from Human Demonstration:** Prior research has explored various avenues for transferring human skills to robots. Some approaches focus on translating human videos into robot-centric observations using generative editing techniques (e.g.,), while others infer manipulation affordances. Another line of work involves collecting paired human-robot data through co-training pipelines or specialized hardware setups like smart glasses. Additionally, flow-based representations have emerged as a promising method to mitigate the human-robot embodiment gap, providing an abstract representation of motion that is less tied to specific physical forms. However, a critical limitation of many existing methods is their assumption of passively set camera viewpoints, neglecting the robot's need for dynamic visual information. EgoAVFlow directly challenges this assumption by integrating active vision.

**Active Vision:** In robotics, active vision traditionally involves dynamically controlling camera viewpoints to achieve specific perceptual goals, such as scene reconstruction, object pose estimation, or uncertainty reduction. For manipulation tasks, some works leverage novel-view synthesis to expand training data, but they do not typically involve online viewpoint planning during execution. More recently, efforts to imitate human viewpoints have emerged, often using hardware-based teleoperation interfaces that track operator head motion and gaze. However, EgoAVFlow critically observes that direct imitation of human viewpoints is often suboptimal for robots. Human vision involves unique biological priors, such as rapid saccadic eye movements and the vestibulo-ocular reflex, which may not translate effectively to robotic perception. Robot learning approaches using reinforcement learning for viewpoint control exist but typically suffer from high sample complexity, making real-world training impractical.

**EgoAVFlow's Position:** EgoAVFlow distinguishes itself by addressing the fundamental gap between *passive observation* in human egocentric videos and the *active perception* required for robust robot manipulation. Its key innovation lies in proposing a shared 3D flow representation that not only bridges the human-robot embodiment gap but also explicitly supports geometric visibility reasoning for active viewpoint control. Unlike prior flow-based methods that might focus on 2D flow or assume static scenes, EgoAVFlow leverages 3D flow as an embodiment-agnostic interface that directly informs both manipulation and viewpoint planning. Furthermore, its use of diffusion models with a novel reward-maximizing denoising strategy for viewpoint adjustment offers a practical solution to combine the learning of a natural camera motion prior (from human data) with real-time optimization for task-critical visibility, without needing extensive robot interaction or fully differentiable reward functions. This approach advances the field by enabling robust robot skill transfer from human videos, even when active and flexible perception is essential.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable robots to acquire manipulation policies and an independent active vision strategy directly from scalable egocentric human video demonstrations, thereby circumventing the need for expensive robot-specific data collection.

The motivation for EgoAVFlow stems from several critical challenges identified when attempting to leverage egocentric human videos for robot learning:

1. **Demand for Active Perception:** While egocentric human videos provide rich information about manipulation tasks, real-world robotic manipulation frequently necessitates adjusting the camera viewpoint to maintain visibility of task-critical objects, the end-effector, or specific target locations. Failure to do so can lead to execution failures.
2. **Suboptimality of Human Viewpoint Imitation:** A straightforward approach might be to imitate human camera movements. However, the authors argue that human viewpoints are governed by human-specific biological priors (e.g., saccades, vestibulo-ocular reflex) that do not optimally translate to robotic perception. Simply mimicking human head or eye movements can provide unreliable visual signals for a robot policy, making it crucial for the robot to learn its *own* independent viewpoint adjustment strategy.
3. **Representation Gap for Visibility Reasoning:** Learning an independent view policy requires a predictive representation that can effectively reason about future 3D configurations of objects, the end-effector, and the scene geometry. This representation must possess several key properties:
 - **3D Motion Capture:** It must accurately capture task-relevant motion in 3D space.
 - **Geometric Visibility Compatibility:** It needs to be compatible with geometric computations for assessing visibility from various candidate viewpoints.
 - **View-Invariant Robustness:** It should be robust to changes in viewpoint and appearance.
 - **Embodiment Agnosticism:** Crucially, it must allow policies trained on human videos to transfer effectively to robots with different embodiments.
 Standard 2D visual features often fail to meet these criteria, as they conflate appearance with viewpoint and offer no direct means to forecast 3D object positions for visibility scoring.

To address these motivations, EgoAVFlow proposes to:
- Introduce a **shared 3D flow representation** that serves as a common interface, bridging the human-robot embodiment gap and unifying the learning of manipulation and viewpoint control. This representation is designed to be robust to viewpoint changes and suitable for zero-shot transfer without robot demonstrations.
- Develop a **robot policy learning framework** (EgoAVFlow) that leverages this 3D flow to predict robot actions, future 3D flow, and camera trajectories.
- Implement a **visibility-aware reward function** that allows for test-time refinement of viewpoints using reward-maximizing denoising. This mechanism ensures that the robot actively adjusts its camera to maximize visibility, even if it means deviating from the human demonstrator's original viewpoint, thereby yielding two independently functioning capabilities: visibility-aware viewpoint adjustment and 3D-aware manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoAVFlow's methodology centers on a modular, diffusion model-based framework that integrates manipulation policy learning with active vision, all facilitated by a shared 3D flow representation derived from egocentric human videos.

**A. Data Pre-processing (Section III.A)**
The initial step involves processing raw egocentric human videos to extract robot-relevant information and a consistent 3D scene description:
- **Robot Data Extraction:** Human 3D hand keypoints and 6DoF wrist poses are estimated using HaMeR. These are then mapped to a gripper-equivalent 6DoF pose and a binary gripper command (open/close) to form robot proprioception `p_t` and actions `a_t` (defined as `p_t+1`).
- **3D Flow for Scene Description:** 2D pixel trajectories are tracked across frames using CoTracker3. With per-pixel depth information, these 2D trajectories are unprojected into 3D flow `F_t`. Camera poses `v_t` are estimated using DROID-SLAM to interpret 3D tracks and hand poses within a consistent coordinate frame.
- **Marker Coordinate System:** To ensure consistency across diverse demonstrations, all 3D quantities (robot actions, proprioception, 3D tracks, camera poses) are transformed into a marker coordinate system defined by a ChArUco board.

**B. Soft Value-Based Denoising (Section III.B)**
This algorithmic primitive is critical for EgoAVFlow's active vision component, enabling the refinement of diffusion model outputs (viewpoints) to maximize a non-differentiable reward function at test time. It works by:
- **Reward-Tilted Distribution:** Aiming to generate samples `x_0` from an entropy-regularized, reward-tilted distribution `p^(α)(x) ∝ exp(r(x)/α)p_pre(x)`, where `r(x)` is the reward and `p_pre(x)` is the pre-trained diffusion model's distribution.
- **Soft Value Function:** Introducing `v_k-1(x_k-1)` as a "soft value" that estimates the expected future reward for an intermediate noisy state `x_k-1` under the pre-trained denoising dynamics.
- **Value-Weighted Denoising:** Modifying the denoising kernel `p_pre_k-1` to `p*^,α_k-1` such that it preferentially selects candidates that are predicted to lead to higher final rewards, weighted by the soft value.
- **Per-Step Importance Resampling:** In practice, this is approximated by sampling multiple candidates from `p_pre_k-1`, assigning weights based on an estimated soft value (using `r(x_0(x_k))` as an estimate for `v_k(x_k)`), and then resampling one candidate. This biases the generation process toward higher-reward samples without requiring differentiable reward functions or additional training.

**C. EgoAVFlow Framework (Section IV.A & Fig. 2)**
The core framework consists of three interdependent diffusion models, all built upon a diffusion transformer backbone and trained using DDPM with sampling via DDIM. These models operate with a prediction horizon `T=24` and iteratively sample action chunks of `H=12` steps.
1. **Robot Policy (π_r):** This model learns to predict future robot action sequences `a_t+1:t+T`. It is conditioned on the history of 3D flow tracks `F_t-h:t` and robot proprioception `p_t-h:t`.
2. **Future Flow Generation Model (f):** This model predicts future 3D flows `F_t+1:t+T` (representing future object motion). It takes as input the same context as `π_r` but also includes viewpoint history `v_t-h:t` and, critically, the *future robot action sequence* `a_t+1:t+T` predicted by `π_r`. This allows the model to anticipate how the robot's planned actions will affect the scene's 3D motion, which is crucial for visibility reasoning.
3. **View Policy (π_v):** This model is trained to predict future camera viewpoints `v_t+1:t+T`. It is conditioned on the same context as `f` (excluding proprioception history from the input to `f` for clarity in the method description, but effectively relying on `π_r`'s outputs and `f`'s predictions). At inference time, instead of simple sampling, `π_v` leverages the **soft value-based denoising** algorithm to refine its predictions, biasing them towards viewpoints that maximize a specifically designed visibility-aware reward.

**D. View Selection via Visibility-Aware Reward (Section IV.B)**
The reward function for viewpoint selection is designed to ensure task-critical elements remain observable. Since this reward is non-differentiable (involving mesh raycasting), it is used within the reward-guided denoising process:
- **Query Points:** Task-relevant query points `q_t,i` are derived from the *predicted* future 3D flows `F_t+1:t+T` generated by model `f`.
- **Visibility Reward (R_vis):** This core reward term assesses whether query points are both (a) unobstructed by obstacles and (b) within the camera's field-of-view (FoV).
 - **Unobstructed LOS:** Computed by raycasting from candidate camera poses to query points against a combined mesh of the environment `M_e` (reconstructed using Nvblox) and a time-varying robot mesh `M_r_t` (constructed from `a_t+1:t+T`).
 - **In-FoV:** Checked by projecting query points onto the image plane of the candidate camera pose.
 `r_vis_t,i` is 1 if both conditions are met, 0 otherwise, and `R_vis` is the average across time and query points.
- **Auxiliary Reward Terms:** To provide a more continuous signal and ensure stability/safety, additional terms are included:
 - `R_close`: Encourages camera positions to be reasonably close to query points.
 - `R_marg`: Promotes views that are robust to small camera pose perturbations by checking visibility under multiple perturbed viewpoints.
 - `R_safe`: Penalizes camera viewpoints that are too close to the robot end-effector.
The final reward `R` is a weighted sum of `R_vis` and these auxiliary terms, guiding the `RewMaxDiff` function (Algorithm 1) to iteratively refine viewpoints.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluate EgoAVFlow's ability to perform robust manipulation with active vision, comparing it against both fixed viewpoint settings and various baselines. The evaluation benchmark comprises four manipulation tasks (Spray, Doll, Toilet Paper, Towel) that explicitly require active viewpoint adjustment. The hardware setup includes a Trossen WidowX robot for manipulation and a Unitree Z1 robot with a RealSense D435 camera for viewpoint adjustment.

**A. Continuous Viewpoint Adjustment is Necessary for Reliable Visibility:**
The initial finding, demonstrated with a "spray" task, clearly illustrates the inadequacy of fixed camera viewpoints. Even when selecting the "best" fixed viewpoint, it was unable to maintain full visibility of task-critical query points throughout the entire task execution. The optimal viewpoint for visibility constantly shifted, highlighting that continuous, active viewpoint adjustment is indispensable for successful and robust manipulation in dynamic environments where objects move and occlusions occur.

**B. Visibility-Aware Viewpoint Planning Outperforms Human Viewpoint Imitation:**
EgoAVFlow was compared against a Human Viewpoint Imitation (HVI) baseline, where the view policy (`π_v`) directly imitated human camera motion without the visibility-maximizing denoising process. Both methods used the same manipulation policy.
- **Success Rates (Table I):** EgoAVFlow significantly outperformed HVI across all four tasks, achieving success rates of 20/25 (Spray), 18/25 (Doll), 17/25 (Toilet Paper), and 18/25 (Towel), compared to HVI's 5/25, 10/25, 7/25, and 5/25 respectively.
- **Visibility Reward (Fig. 5):** EgoAVFlow consistently achieved higher average visibility rewards (`R_vis`) across all tasks, quantitatively demonstrating its superior capability in maintaining visibility.
- **Qualitative Observations (Fig. 6):** Qualitatively, EgoAVFlow was observed to maintain reliable visibility of query points throughout rollouts, while HVI frequently failed to keep objects in view due to occlusions or points moving out of the FoV.
This finding strongly validates the core hypothesis that human viewpoint imitation is often suboptimal for robots, and explicit, visibility-aware viewpoint optimization is crucial for robust task completion.

**C. 3D Flow Policy Outperforms Under Actively Varying Viewpoints:**
To assess the effectiveness of EgoAVFlow's 3D flow-based manipulation policy, it was compared against several representative robot policy learning methods: AMPLIFY (2D flow-based), EgoZero (3D flow-based but assumes static objects), and Phantom (generative synthesis for robot observations). All baselines were equipped with EgoAVFlow's visibility-maximizing view policy to isolate the effect of the policy representation.
- **Success Rates (Table I):** EgoAVFlow demonstrated superior manipulation capability, outperforming all baselines with improvements of 1.8x to 2.5x over the second-best baseline across tasks. For example, in the "Spray" task, EgoAVFlow achieved 20/25 successes, while EgoZero, the best baseline for that task, only managed 10/25.
This result confirms that the proposed 3D flow-based policy is highly view-invariant and robust to actively changing viewpoints, benefiting from its inherent 3D representation.

**D. Failure Analysis:**
A detailed breakdown of failure categories (Grasping failure, Out-of-viewpoint, Object pose failure) provided insights into the strengths and weaknesses of each method (Fig. 7):
- **Manipulation-Related Failures:** AMPLIFY, EgoZero, and Phantom accounted for the majority of grasping (91.6%) and object pose (81.3%) failures. This indicates that their learned representations or underlying assumptions (e.g., EgoZero's static object assumption, or the distribution shift faced by 2D image-based methods like AMPLIFY and Phantom under novel viewpoints) hinder robust manipulation when the camera viewpoint is actively changing. EgoAVFlow, conversely, showed stronger robustness in these manipulation aspects.
- **Out-of-Viewpoint Failures:** HVI accounted for over 50% of "out-of-viewpoint" failures, directly supporting the claim that without explicit visibility optimization, human viewpoint imitation is insufficient. EgoAVFlow also had some "out-of-viewpoint" failures, but its overall success rate was significantly higher because its robust manipulation meant tasks progressed further, reaching stages where viewpoint issues could arise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoAVFlow makes several significant contributions to the fields of robot learning, active vision, and human-robot interaction, with substantial potential impact:

**A. Bridging the Human-Robot Embodiment Gap and Scalable Learning:**
The development of a shared 3D flow representation is a crucial step towards effectively bridging the embodiment gap between humans and robots. By representing task-relevant motion in a 3D, view-agnostic manner, EgoAVFlow enables robots to learn complex manipulation skills directly from readily available egocentric human videos, significantly reducing the reliance on costly, time-consuming, and difficult robot-specific data collection or specialized teleoperation setups. This dramatically scales up the potential for robot skill acquisition, making advanced robot capabilities more accessible.

**B. Enhanced Robot Autonomy through Active Vision:**
EgoAVFlow empowers robots with an independent, visibility-maximizing viewpoint adjustment strategy, decoupling it from potentially suboptimal human-specific visual priors. This capability is vital for real-world deployment, where robots must operate robustly in dynamic, cluttered, and partially occluded environments. By actively managing their perception to maintain visibility of critical objects and the end-effector, robots can achieve higher task success rates and greater reliability, moving closer to truly autonomous manipulation.

**C. Novel Integration of Diffusion Models for Test-Time Optimization:**
The innovative use of diffusion models combined with a soft value-based, reward-maximizing denoising process represents a methodological advancement in active perception. This approach effectively leverages the generative power of diffusion models to provide a strong prior over plausible camera motions, while simultaneously allowing for online, test-time optimization against a non-differentiable geometric visibility reward. This avoids the high sample complexity of traditional reinforcement learning for active vision and the limitations of purely imitation-based methods.

**D. Robustness to Viewpoint Variations:**
The experimental results unequivocally demonstrate that EgoAVFlow's 3D flow-based manipulation policy, coupled with its active vision system, yields superior and more robust performance under actively varying viewpoints compared to prior works. This robustness is critical for generalizing learned skills to diverse environments and operational conditions where camera perspectives are rarely static or perfectly aligned with training data.

**E. Potential Impact and Future Directions:**
The technology developed in EgoAVFlow has the potential to revolutionize various applications:
- **Industrial Automation:** Improving the robustness of robotic assembly, inspection, and pick-and-place tasks in factories.
- **Service Robotics:** Enabling more capable and flexible robots for household chores, elderly care, and assistance in dynamic human environments.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Allowing robots to navigate and interact more effectively in unknown or hazardous environments by adaptively acquiring visual information.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** Lowering the barrier to entry for developing new robotic applications by making human demonstrations a more direct and effective source of training data.

The authors acknowledge a promising direction for future work: incorporating reasoning about which "points of interest" should be tracked, rather than assuming initial observability. This would further enhance the autonomy of robots by enabling active visual search and identification of task-relevant features in novel scenarios, pushing the boundaries towards more intelligent and adaptable robotic manipulation systems.
