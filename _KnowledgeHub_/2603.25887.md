# World Reasoning Arena

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the PAN Team at MBZUAI introduced WR-Arena, a new benchmark designed to assess advanced capabilities of world models beyond short-term prediction, including action simulation fidelity, long-horizon forecast, and simulative reasoning for planning. Evaluations revealed that current models struggle with error accumulation in long-horizon simulations and consistently performing environment-level interventions, with action-state aligned models showing improved planning performance.

## Problem

- Existing world model (WM) benchmarks primarily focus on low-level metrics like next-state prediction or pixel-level fidelity, often in single-turn or short-term settings.
- Current evaluation frameworks inadequately assess WMs' ability to maintain long-horizon dynamics, interpret multi-step instructions, or support goal-directed simulative reasoning for planning.
- There is a gap in determining if WMs can function as robust internal 'simulation sandboxes' for complex decision-making and purposeful action, rather than just passive predictors.

## Method

- The **WR-Arena** benchmark was introduced to evaluate WMs across three dimensions: ==Action Simulation Fidelity==, ==Long-horizon Forecast==, and ==Simulative Reasoning and Planning==.
- A structured ==taxonomy of evaluation tasks== and curated datasets were developed, leveraging LLMs to propose actions and VLMs as judges for multi-step scenarios.
- The methodology involves assessing a WM's capacity for interpreting ==high-level instructions==, maintaining coherent simulations over extended interactions, and supporting ==iterative planning== through VLM-WM collaboration.

## Results

- PAN, a world model incorporating action-state aligned fine-tuning, achieved the best performance on Long-horizon Forecast metrics, demonstrating a flatter degradation curve and mitigating error accumulation more effectively.
- MiniMax showed strong overall performance in Action Simulation Fidelity (**72.33%** on Agent Simulation, **51.67%** on Environment Simulation), while PAN improved significantly over general video generators due to targeted training.
- Integrated with a VLM planner, PAN yielded substantial gains in simulative reasoning, improving trajectory-level success by **26.33%** in open-ended settings and **23.40%** in structured environments compared to a VLM-only baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current world models face significant challenges in sustaining coherent simulations over long horizons, with none exceeding 65% on consistency metrics due to error accumulation.
> - Performing environment-level interventions is substantially harder for WMs than agent-centric manipulations, with models averaging 11.5% lower accuracy and none surpassing 60% on environment simulation tasks.
> - Effective planning requires WMs to produce 'semantically actionable rollouts' rather than just perceptually plausible ones, indicating the need for explicit alignment between action representations and state transitions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models (WMs) are foundational components for intelligent agents, designed to serve as internal simulators that enable understanding, anticipation, and action within complex environments. The contemporary research landscape for WMs has seen the development of various benchmarks (e.g., Li et al., 2025; Duan et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2025; NVIDIA Deep Imagination Research, 2024). However, the authors identify a prevalent limitation within these existing evaluation frameworks. Current benchmarks primarily concentrate on low-level metrics such as next-state prediction, pixel-level fidelity, and short-term action or physics simulation. They often assess models in single-turn settings or focus on perceptual realism, such as video fidelity.

Previous analyses (Xing et al., 2025) have indicated that models capable of producing visually plausible outputs may still exhibit limitations in maintaining physical consistency or long-term scene structure. This suggests a gap in the underlying world understanding of these models. The broader research goal for WMs is to enable capabilities beyond mere observation prediction, encompassing hypothetical simulation, the evaluation of action outcomes under diverse conditions, and the performance of "thought experiments" to ground reasoning, planning, and decision-making.

This work, by introducing WR-Arena, seeks to address the identified limitations by shifting the focus of WM evaluation towards richer simulation capabilities essential for intelligent behavior. It aims to move beyond single-turn and purely perceptual assessments to evaluate high-level action simulation, long-horizon dynamics coherence, and simulative reasoning and planning. By doing so, it positions itself as a development that aims to provide a more comprehensive and ecologically valid assessment of WMs, aligning their evaluation with the demands of real-world applications in areas such as embodied AI and autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the recognized gap in existing world model (WM) evaluation benchmarks. The authors contend that while WMs are intended to function as internal hypothetical simulators capable of representing diverse future possibilities, current benchmarks inadequately assess these capabilities. Existing evaluations predominantly focus on low-level metrics, such as immediate next-state prediction or visual fidelity (e.g., pixel-level accuracy and action reconstruction quality), often in short-term or single-turn settings.

This narrow focus overlooks critical aspects required for intelligent behavior in complex, real-world environments. For instance, intelligent agents need to maintain coherent and physically plausible environments over extended interactions, respect causal dynamics, follow semantically meaningful multi-step instructions, and support goal-directed reasoning and planning by evaluating alternative future scenarios. Without assessing these advanced capabilities, current benchmarks cannot reliably determine if a WM functions as a robust "simulation sandbox" for long-horizon decision-making and purposeful action. The authors liken a truly capable WM to the human ability to perform mental simulations, project multiple possible futures, and adapt knowledge to novel situations.

To address this deficiency, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Introduce a Comprehensive Benchmark**: To establish WR-Arena as a new standard for evaluating WMs, designed to probe beyond short-term prediction and perceptual quality.
2. **Evaluate Advanced Simulation Capabilities**: To systematically assess WMs along three fundamental dimensions of "next world simulation":
 - **Action Simulation Fidelity**: To evaluate a WM's ability to interpret and follow semantically meaningful, multi-step instructions (targeting agents or environments) and generate diverse counterfactual rollouts. This probes whether a WM can translate abstract commands into coherent observation trajectories.
 - **Long-horizon Forecast**: To measure a WM's capacity to sustain accurate, coherent, and physically plausible simulations over extended interactions, minimizing error accumulation and preserving consistency across time. This assesses the stability of WMs in multi-round scenarios.
 - **Simulative Reasoning and Planning**: To determine if a WM can support goal-directed reasoning by simulating, comparing, and selecting among alternative futures in both structured and open-ended environments. This evaluates a WM's function as an active planner rather than a passive predictor.
3. **Develop a Task Taxonomy and Curate Datasets**: To build a structured taxonomy of evaluation tasks and curate diverse datasets specifically designed to probe these advanced capabilities, moving beyond traditional single-turn and purely perceptual evaluations.
4. **Diagnose Current Model Limitations**: To conduct extensive experiments with state-of-the-art WMs to reveal specific performance gaps in these advanced simulation capabilities, thereby exposing the distance between current models and human-level hypothetical reasoning.
5. **Provide a Diagnostic and Guideline Tool**: To establish WR-Arena as a diagnostic tool for identifying weaknesses in WMs and a guideline for the development of next-generation world models capable of robust understanding, forecasting, and purposeful action in complex environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces WR-Arena, a benchmark designed to evaluate advanced capabilities of world models across three core dimensions: Action Simulation Fidelity, Long-horizon Forecast, and Simulative Reasoning and Planning. The framework moves beyond short-term prediction or visual quality to assess a model’s ability to simulate, reason, and act in realistic, long-horizon scenarios.

#### 3.1. Action Simulation Fidelity

This dimension assesses a world model's ability to accurately follow semantically specified, multi-step natural language instructions and generate reasonable state sequences.
- **Process**: Given an initial world state ($s_0$) and high-level control instructions (A), a large language model (LLM), such as GPT-4o, proposes multiple multi-step, feasible action sequences. The world model then simulates a rollout $R(s_0, A)$ conditioned on these sequences.
- **Evaluation**: Simulations are scored using vision-language models (VLMs) as judges, following established protocols, with a focus on action faithfulness and action precision.
- **Settings**:
 - **Agent Simulation**: Evaluates if the model can drive a controllable entity through intended high-level behaviors while maintaining stable background dynamics. It assesses the model's capacity to generate multiple coherent futures from a single starting state, crucial for comparing different action strategies.
 - **Environment Simulation**: Evaluates the model's ability to apply high-level scene interventions and simulate their causal consequences, assuming a neutral agent policy. It considers both accurate simulation and multi-future diversity of scene-level changes.

#### 3.2. Long-horizon Forecast

This dimension assesses a world model's ability to maintain coherent, high-quality simulations over extended sequences of interactions, beyond short-term accuracy, and its capacity to avoid error accumulation.
- **Process**: Given an initial world state ($s_0$) and a sequence of actions (A) spanning multiple rounds, the model simulates a rollout $R(s_0, A)$ that must remain visually faithful, dynamically plausible, and consistent throughout the extended horizon.
- **Metrics**:
 - **Transition Smoothness (Multi-round Smoothness - MRS)**: Probes multi-step dynamics by extending each initial state with a sequence of actions over *k* rounds. The model is expected to produce smooth, physically plausible trajectories without sudden jumps or artifacts. Optical flow continuity across frames is used to quantify motion ($v_t$) and its finite-difference acceleration ($a_t$). A high MRS score is awarded for visible motion with gradual changes, penalizing static segments and abrupt transitions using an exponential penalty for jerks.
 - **Generation Consistency (APλ)**: Measures the cumulative robustness of long-horizon rollouts. It tracks content alignment and style consistency across *k*-round action sequences, building on the WorldScore metric (Duan et al., 2025). Additive penalties are applied to amplify small inaccuracies as rounds progress, resulting in a weighted average score that reflects both early-step accuracy and long-term stability. The score exponentially decays as the average deviation from the initial performance increases.

#### 3.3. Simulative Reasoning and Planning

This dimension highlights the role of world models as active simulators for goal-directed behavior, supporting iterative decision-making in complex planning and reasoning contexts.
- **Process**: Given an initial world state ($s_0$) and a goal (g), the model must generate a rollout $R(s_0, g)$ where each intermediate state reflects progress toward the goal. The evaluation assesses whether the WM can collaborate with a VLM to plan in natural language space. The VLM acts as a planner, iteratively proposing candidate actions, which the WM simulates. The VLM then selects the action whose predicted next observation is closest to the goal.
- **Settings**:
 - **Step-Wise Simulation**: Evaluates the WM's predictive capability for immediate consequences from a given action. Using robot arm manipulation tasks from WM-ABench (Gao et al., 2025), models select the correct next observation from one ground-truth and three distractors. Generative models are human-assessed for physical effects; embedding-based models compute latent state similarity. All models are finetuned on robotic manipulation datasets (e.g., Agibot). V-JEPA 2 is extended with a UMT5 encoder for language-based actions.
 - **Open-Ended Simulation and Planning**: Evaluates reasoning and action in complex, naturalistic household settings. Requires multi-step reasoning and long-horizon foresight. An iterative VLM agent (e.g., OpenAI-o3) proposes actions, the WM simulates, and the VLM selects the best action. 15 scenarios from the Agibot dataset are curated, with human assessment on trajectory-level completion and simulation quality.
 - **Structured Simulation and Planning**: Focuses on controlled, structured settings, such as tabletop manipulation with objects from the Language Table dataset (Lynch et al., 2022). It uses the same iterative agent-WM planning loop. 47 cases are curated for various spatial arrangements (e.g., grouping, aligning). Blinded human assessments are conducted on goal achievement and trajectory quality.

#### 3.4. Baselines

The study includes a comprehensive evaluation of various state-of-the-art world models and video generation models.
- **World Models**: Cosmos (NVIDIA et al., 2025), V-JEPA2 (Assran et al., 2025), and PAN (PAN et al., 2025). PAN is described as a general, interactable, long-horizon WM using a Generative Latent Prediction (GLP) architecture.
- **Video Generation Models**: Open-source models like WAN-2.1 & 2.2 (Wan et al., 2025a) and closed-source commercial APIs including KLING (Kuaishou Technology, 2025), MiniMax (MiniMax, 2025), and Gen-3 (Runway Research, 2024). These models typically operate in a prompt-to-video manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation results from WR-Arena reveal several insights regarding the capabilities and limitations of current world models (WMs) and video generation models. A general observation is a substantial gap between current models and human-level hypothetical reasoning, particularly in maintaining long-horizon transition smoothness and consistency, and applying fine-grained environment changes.

#### 4.1. Action Simulation Fidelity

- **Agent vs. Environment Simulation**: A consistent performance disparity was observed, with agent-centric manipulations outperforming environment-level manipulations by an average of 11.5%. No model exceeded 60% accuracy on environment simulation, indicating that faithfully modeling scene-level interventions remains a challenge.
- **Model Performance**: MiniMax demonstrated the strongest overall performance (72.33% on Agent Simulation, 51.67% on Environment Simulation).
- **Impact of Training**: WAN2.1, a general video generator, exhibited weaker simulation fidelity compared to PAN, which incorporates targeted fine-tuning on action–state aligned sequences. PAN showed gains of +16.66% on Agent Simulation and +10.0% on Environment Simulation over WAN2.1.
- **Conclusion**: Sequence-level action grounding is a difficult capability for current WMs. Explicit alignment between action representations and state transitions is essential for high-fidelity simulation, especially for environment-centric manipulations where models show the greatest difficulty.

#### 4.2. Long-horizon Forecast

- **Error Accumulation**: No model exceeded 65% on either Transition Smoothness or Generation Consistency metrics, indicating that error accumulation is a fundamental challenge for long-horizon simulation. Models showed measurable degradation per-round during multi-turn generation.
- **Model Performance**: PAN achieved the best performance on both Transition Smoothness (53.6%) and Generation Consistency (64.1%). Its long-context conditioning is suggested to provide effective regularization for next-state generation.
- **Degradation Analysis**: A fine-grained analysis of generation consistency over nine consecutive action steps showed monotonic degradation for all models as sequence length increased. WAN 2.1 exhibited the most severe decline, dropping from approximately 90% to 30% over nine rounds. MiniMax, Cosmos-1, and Gen3 also fell below 60% after round 7. PAN, while not having the highest consistency in initial rounds, maintained the highest consistency after round 3, exhibiting a flatter degradation curve and mitigating error accumulation more effectively, attributed to its self-forcing training strategy.
- **Comparison**: PAN surpassed commercial video generation models like KLING (59.15%) and MiniMax (57.17%) in Generation Consistency. WAN2.1 performed poorly, tending to exaggerate motion and produce jittery, non-smooth trajectories with pronounced visual drift.

#### 4.3. Simulative Reasoning and Planning

- **Actionability for Planning**: Only WMs that produce *semantically actionable rollouts* significantly improved planning performance, indicating that perceptual quality alone is insufficient for decision-making.
- **Model Performance**: PAN demonstrated the largest improvements when integrated with a VLM planner, achieving a 26.33% gain in trajectory-level success for open-ended settings and 23.40% in structured environments over a VLM-only baseline. This suggests PAN’s capacity for reliable counterfactual thought experiments benefits multi-step planning.
- **Inconsistent Performance**: Cosmos 1 & 2 and V-JEPA 2 exhibited inconsistent effects, sometimes providing modest improvements but occasionally failing to guide planning effectively. This suggests their simulations may lack the semantic grounding or causal informativeness required for effective downstream planning.

#### 4.4. Overall Model Comparison

- **Commercial Video Generators (KLING, MiniMax, Gen-3)**: Showed strong perceptual quality and reasonable short-horizon control, but their closed training and limited domain adaptation constrained their utility for task-specific planning.
- **Embedding-centric or Generative Open-Source Models (V-JEPA 2, Cosmos)**: Supported internal rollouts for planning better, but often lagged in faithful control and temporal stability, leading to inconsistent gains in planning. Their simulations appeared to lack the semantic grounding needed for effective decision-making.
- **PAN**: Achieved the most balanced performance profile across all evaluated dimensions. Its grounding in VLM priors and action–state aligned fine-tuning enabled robust semantic understanding, coherent multi-turn predictions, and reliable instruction following, which collectively facilitated reliable simulations in the planning process.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

- **Structured Simulation and Planning (Figure 4)**: In arranging blocks into a line, PAN produced goal-directed and diverse action sequences, demonstrating state-aware refinement and iterative replanning. Cosmos 1 showed repetitive patterns, often centering objects, which was less aligned with the goal. Cosmos 2 produced more heterogeneous actions but with loose coupling to the final objective. This highlights the need for goal-conditioned action policies in planning.
- **Simulation Fidelity (Figure 5)**: In a driving scenario with environmental interventions (dry to rain), PAN demonstrated strong intervention-following fidelity and scene continuity. It maintained stable scene elements while introducing physically grounded weather cues, resulting in smooth and realistic temporal evolution. Cosmos 1 and 2 exhibited weaker fidelity, less structured transitions, and less tightly coupled changes to the intended progression, sometimes lacking key physical cues or showing inconsistent rendering. This underscores the importance of identity preservation and faithful intervention realization for simulation fidelity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces WR-Arena, a benchmark designed to address critical evaluation gaps in the field of world models (WMs). Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several points:

- **Comprehensive Evaluation Framework**: WR-Arena moves beyond existing benchmarks' emphasis on short-term prediction and perceptual fidelity. By systematically probing Action Simulation Fidelity, Long-horizon Forecast, and Simulative Reasoning and Planning, it provides a more comprehensive and functionally relevant assessment of WMs as "next world simulators." This shift in evaluation criteria is significant because it aligns the assessment of WMs with the capabilities required for real-world intelligent agents.
- **Diagnosis of Current Limitations**: Through extensive experiments with state-of-the-art WMs, the benchmark reveals substantial limitations. It identifies that current models struggle with faithfully following high-level instructions, particularly for environment-centric commands, and face significant challenges in maintaining long-horizon simulation consistency due to error accumulation. These diagnostic insights are crucial for directing future research efforts towards specific areas of weakness.
- **Guideline for Next-Generation WM Development**: The findings suggest that only WMs capable of producing semantically actionable rollouts meaningfully improve planning performance, indicating that perceptual quality alone is insufficient for decision-making. Furthermore, the balanced performance of models like PAN suggests that architectures jointly optimizing understanding, prediction, and control as coupled objectives are more promising. WR-Arena thus serves as a roadmap, guiding the development of unified architectures that can better integrate these crucial capabilities.
- **Advancing Real-World Intelligence**: By fostering the development of WMs capable of robust understanding, long-range forecasting, and purposeful action, this work has the potential to significantly impact the field of artificial intelligence. Such advanced WMs are fundamental for progress in domains like embodied AI, autonomous driving, and complex robotic control, where agents must navigate and interact with dynamic, unpredictable environments over extended periods.
- **Bridging the Gap to Human-Level Reasoning**: The benchmark explicitly aims to highlight the gap between current WMs and human-level hypothetical reasoning. By establishing clear metrics for advanced simulation capabilities, WR-Arena contributes to the long-term goal of endowing machines with the ability to perform complex thought experiments, compare alternative futures, and make informed decisions, mirroring aspects of human cognitive processes.

In summary, WR-Arena functions as both a diagnostic tool that identifies the shortcomings of current WM technologies and a prescriptive guideline for future research, poised to accelerate the development of more capable and intelligent world models.
