# OmniRoam: World Wandering via Long-Horizon Panoramic Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> OmniRoam introduces a two-stage global-to-local framework for generating long-horizon panoramic videos that enable "world wandering" through synthesized environments. The method achieves improved visual quality, more precise trajectory control, and enhanced long-term scene consistency on established benchmarks compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Perspective video models struggle with complete scene representation and global consistency due to their limited field-of-view, hindering comprehensive environmental modeling.
- Existing long-horizon video generation methods often suffer from accumulated errors and temporal drift, especially during extensive camera movements, degrading quality over time.
- Prior panoramic generation approaches frequently adapt perspective architectures without fully leveraging panoramic representations' inherent advantages for long-term spatial and temporal coherence.

## Method

- A two-stage ==camera-controlled framework== (preview-refine) for panoramic video synthesis is developed, starting with coarse panoramic video generation and followed by ==segment-wise refinement== for temporal and spatial enhancement.
- A ==canonical panoramic coordinate system== is established to define trajectories strictly via translation, eliminating camera self-rotation for simplified, robust generation.
- Camera motion control is decomposed into orthogonal ==scale== (playback speed) and ==flow== (directional displacement) components for fine-grained and intuitive trajectory conditioning.

## Results

- **OmniRoam** quantitatively outperforms baseline methods in visual quality (e.g., FAED of **5.27** compared to Matrix-3D's 8.64 for 81-frame 480p videos) and trajectory controllability (e.g., PSNR'75 of **19.87** versus Matrix-3D's 17.04).
- Achieves superior long-term spatial coherence, demonstrated by a higher `C_loop` score (e.g., **2.34** compared to Matrix-3D's 1.38 for 81-frame 480p videos), indicating better visual consistency upon loop closure.
- The framework enables practical extensions, including a real-time preview mode generating 81-frame panoramic videos in approximately **7 seconds**, and successfully reconstructs coherent 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes from its generated videos.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Panoramic representations intrinsically offer holistic scene coverage and maintain global spatial memory, proving crucial for robust long-term consistency in large-scale scene generation.
> - A global-to-local generation strategy effectively mitigates the accumulated errors and temporal drift typically observed in direct autoregressive generation, particularly for extended video sequences.
> - The explicit decomposition of camera motion into scale and flow components provides an intuitive and precise control mechanism for synthesizing specific camera trajectories in generated scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the broader landscape of generative models for visual content creation, specifically focusing on video generation and scene modeling. Recent advancements in diffusion models have enabled the synthesis of high-fidelity images and short videos. However, a significant challenge remains in generating coherent visual content at scale, particularly for modeling comprehensive scenes over extended durations and large camera movements.

Most existing scene generation methods primarily utilize perspective video models, which inherently capture a human-centric viewpoint with a limited field-of-view (FoV). This limitation often leads to issues with completeness and global consistency when attempting to model the entire physical environment. While autoregressive world models show promise for longer video synthesis, they frequently encounter accumulated errors and temporal drift, particularly during extensive camera movements. These perspective-based approaches struggle to maintain global scene consistency across both space and time due to their narrow viewpoint.

The present research addresses these limitations by leveraging panoramic vision. Panoramic representations intrinsically offer holistic scene coverage and maintain global spatial memory, providing a more robust foundation for long-term consistency in scene generation. While some prior work has adapted generative architectures to panoramic data, these efforts often treat panoramas as direct extensions of perspective imagery without fully exploiting their unique representational advantages for maintaining long-term spatial and temporal coherence in large-scale scene exploration. Camera-controlled video diffusion models have emerged to decouple dynamics from content, with some applications in the panoramic domain, but they typically focus on short clips and also suffer from error accumulation over long horizons. OmniRoam seeks to bridge this gap by developing a framework specifically designed to leverage panoramic properties for controllable, long-horizon video generation, thereby enabling scalable and globally consistent scene wandering.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a controllable panoramic video generation framework that enables "long-horizon scene wandering" with high fidelity and global coherence. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

**Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
- **Perspective Video Models:** The majority of existing video generation models rely on perspective views. These models have a restricted field-of-view, making it challenging to model an entire physical environment comprehensively. This leads to issues such as incomplete scene representations and difficulties in maintaining global consistency across different viewpoints or over extended periods.
- **Accumulated Errors in Long-Horizon Generation:** Even advanced autoregressive-based world models, designed for longer video synthesis, often suffer from accumulated errors and temporal drift when generating videos involving significant camera movements. This degradation in quality and consistency over time limits their utility for extensive scene exploration.
- **Underutilization of Panoramic Advantages:** While panoramic data offers inherent advantages like holistic scene coverage and global spatial memory, existing panoramic generation methods often adapt perspective architectures without fully capitalizing on these representational benefits. They may not explicitly address the challenges of long-term consistency and user-controlled camera trajectories in a globally consistent manner.

**Desired Capabilities for Scene Modeling:**
- **Comprehensive Scene Coverage:** A demand exists for models that can generate a complete representation of an environment, rather than fragmented views. Panoramic representations naturally provide this holistic coverage.
- **Long-Term Spatial and Temporal Consistency:** For immersive "world wandering," it is crucial for generated content to remain consistent across extended trajectories, ensuring that objects and structures retain their identity and spatial relationships over time.
- **Controllability:** Users require explicit control over camera movement and trajectory to explore synthesized scenes effectively and interactively.
- **High Fidelity:** The generated videos should exhibit high visual quality, with clear details and stable geometric structures, to provide an immersive experience.

**Proposed Solution Strategy:**
To address these motivations, the authors propose a novel **global-to-local design** leveraging panoramic vision. The central idea is that establishing a global scene structure first using panoramic representation and then progressively refining local details will result in a scene representation that is both high-fidelity and globally coherent. This design aims to:
1. **Exploit Panoramic Representation:** Utilize panoramas' rich per-frame scene coverage and inherent long-term spatial and temporal consistency.
2. **Enable Scalable Scene Wandering:** Provide a mechanism for users to traverse extended trajectories within a generated scene.
3. **Offer User Control:** Allow users to specify camera trajectories, providing a controllable experience.

The key objectives are thus to design a framework that can generate long-horizon panoramic videos that are visually high-quality, adhere accurately to specified camera trajectories, and maintain strong long-term global consistency, thereby enabling immersive exploration of synthesized environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OmniRoam is structured as a two-stage camera-controlled framework for long-horizon panoramic video synthesis, based on a global-to-local generation strategy. It also includes a dedicated data generation pipeline and a novel evaluation metric.

#### 3.1. Trajectory-Controlled Video Preview Stage

The preview stage is designed to generate a rapid overview of a scene. It takes a user-provided input image or video and a specified camera trajectory to produce a coarse panoramic video at an accelerated playback speed.

- **Foundation Model:** The framework builds upon a pretrained video generation model (Wan2.1-1.3B) which is fine-tuned to operate as a panoramic video generative model.
- **Frame-Dimension Video Conditioning:** To ensure visual continuity, the method adopts a frame-dimension conditioning strategy similar to ReCamMaster. Input and target videos are encoded into latent representations using a 3D variational autoencoder. These latents are then patchified and concatenated along the frame dimension, with the source latent serving as a conditioning signal.
- **Decomposed Trajectory Conditioning:** Camera motion control is decomposed into two orthogonal components for finer-grained and intuitive control:
 - **Scale (𝑠):** A global scalar representing the magnitude of camera displacement per timestep, quantifying playback speed. A log-space representation is used, and its embedding is injected globally into transformer blocks to uniformly modulate temporal tokens.
 - **Flow (D = {d_k}):** A sequence of normalized 3D unit vectors representing the camera's directional displacement at each timestep. An encoder, a zero-initialized fully connected layer, injects this frame-wise directional information into each transformer block. This decomposition relies on assumptions of uniform velocity and fixed camera orientation, enforced during data curation.
- **Training Objective:** The preview model is trained using a rectified flow framework. Noise is added to the target latent while the source latent remains clean. The loss is computed solely on the target portion, aiming to predict the velocity field that maps from a noisy latent to the data.

#### 3.2. Long-horizon Video Refinement Stage

Following the preview, the refine stage enhances the generated video in terms of temporal length and spatial quality, addressing the computational infeasibility of directly generating long, high-resolution videos in a single pass.

- **Segment-wise Refinement:** The refinement is performed segment-wise through scale alignment and visibility mask conditioning. The refined segments are then concatenated to form the final long-horizon video.
- **Scale Alignment:** Given a preview video generated at an accelerated scale `s` (e.g., 1.1 to 8.0), and a target scale `s'` (e.g., 1.0 for normal playback), the temporal resolution is expanded by a factor of `s/s'`. The number of segments required (`n`) is calculated based on this factor.
- **Visibility Mask Conditioning:** For each segment, a binary visibility mask is constructed to select specific frames from the preview video to serve as sparse conditioning. This mask selects a window of corresponding preview frames for each segment. During training, the starting frame of this window is randomly sampled for generalization. The preview frames are encoded, and the mask is downsampled and broadcast across latent dimensions for masked latent conditioning.
- **Training Objective:** The refine model also uses a rectified flow objective. The target latent at scale `s'` is concatenated with the masked preview latent along the frame dimension. Noise is added only to the target portion, and the loss is computed on the target video segment. Both models are fine-tuned from a pretrained Diffusion Transformer (Wan2.1-1.3B).

#### 3.3. New Dataset and Benchmark

The authors highlight the importance of scalable and precise trajectory-aligned training data.

- **Canonical Panoramic Coordinate System:** A rotation-invariant coordinate system is established for panoramas. Unlike 6-DoF approaches, camera self-rotation (roll, pitch, yaw) is eliminated, defining trajectories strictly via translation (x, y, z) relative to the equirectangular projection (ERP) center. This simplifies the generation space, ensuring visual dynamics are driven solely by spatial translation.
- **Hybrid Dataset Construction:**
 - **Scene-Reality Real Data:** Approximately 2,000 handheld panoramic videos (5 million frames) are collected from diverse real-world environments. COLMAP is used to estimate camera trajectories, and footage is gravity-aligned. Videos with abnormal scale are filtered to maintain consistency.
 - **Trajectory-Precise Synthetic Data:** 1,000 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes from InteriorGS are used to render synthetic panoramic sequences. An automated pipeline generates physically feasible, constant-speed trajectories.
- **Metric for Long-term Scene Consistency (Loop Consistency, C_loop):** To quantitatively evaluate long-horizon spatial consistency, a novel metric is proposed. `C_loop` measures how well a generated scene returns to its initial visual state after traversing a closed-loop camera trajectory. It is defined as `(1 - S2) / (1 - S1)`, where `S1` (Loop Closure Score) measures CLIP embedding cosine similarity between the first and last `P` frames (P=5 for robustness), and `S2` (Intermediate Score) measures similarity to intermediate frames. A higher score indicates strong loop closure while maintaining sufficient variation during exploration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate OmniRoam focused on visual quality, trajectory controllability, and long-term scene consistency, comparing the proposed method against state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Visual Quality:** OmniRoam consistently outperformed baseline methods (Matrix-3D, Imagine360) across all evaluated metrics: Fréchet Auto-Encoder Distance (FAED) (lower is better), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) (higher is better), and LPIPS (lower is better). For 81-frame videos at 480p, OmniRoam achieved FAED 5.27, SSIM 0.70, LPIPS 0.18, compared to Matrix-3D's FAED 8.64, SSIM 0.63, LPIPS 0.37. Similar improvements were observed at 720p resolution.
- **Trajectory Controllability:** The method demonstrated superior adherence to specified camera trajectories, indicated by higher PSNR values across multiple temporal windows (PSNR’25, PSNR’55, PSNR’75, and for long videos, PSNR’615, PSNR’635). For instance, at 480p (81 frames), OmniRoam achieved PSNR’25 23.06, PSNR’55 20.69, PSNR’75 19.87, which were notably higher than Matrix-3D's 19.05, 17.35, and 17.04, respectively.
- **Loop Consistency:** OmniRoam achieved higher `C_loop` scores than baselines, indicating stronger long-term spatial coherence. For 81-frame videos at 480p, OmniRoam scored 2.34, compared to Matrix-3D's 1.38. For 641-frame videos at 720p, OmniRoam achieved a score of 1.96. This suggests that generated videos return to a visual state closer to the initial view after completing a full loop.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Scene Coherence and Detail:** Qualitative comparisons showed that OmniRoam consistently produced semantically coherent content with clearer object boundaries and more stable geometric structures, effectively following specified trajectories. In contrast, Imagine360 often failed to generate meaningful panoramic content, while Matrix-3D, despite following trajectories, exhibited blurry appearance and noticeable geometric distortions, especially around object boundaries.
- **Long-Video Generation:** Visualizations of 641-frame videos with loop trajectories demonstrated OmniRoam's ability to maintain consistency over long distances. The final frames closely resembled the initial frame of the sequence, confirming stable long-term behavior and effective temporal refinement.

#### 4.3. Design Analysis

- **Effect of Representation Choice:** A comparative analysis revealed that panoramic representations are crucial. Replacing panoramic videos with perspective videos, even under identical settings, consistently degraded visual quality, trajectory controllability, and loop consistency for both short (81 frames) and long (641 frames) sequences. This indicates that the limited FoV of perspective videos hinders the preservation of global scene structure during large camera motions.
- **Effect of Generation Strategy:** The global-to-local preview–refine strategy was shown to be superior to a direct autoregressive panoramic video generator. The autoregressive variant performed worse across all metrics, particularly in loop consistency, achieving nearly half the score of OmniRoam (0.89 vs 1.96 for 641 frames). This suggests that the two-stage approach effectively mitigates error accumulation inherent in direct autoregressive generation.
- **Loop Consistency Temporal Analysis:** Temporal analysis using CLIP similarity for loop trajectories reinforced these findings. OmniRoam exhibited the expected behavior: similarity to the first frame decreased as the camera moved away and then recovered as the loop closed. The autoregressive variant, however, showed a monotonic decline, indicating significant drift from the initial view. The perspective-video variant also showed structural degradation, with limited recovery and significant differences in final frames compared to the initial view.

#### 4.4. Extensions and Applications

- **Real-time Preview via Self-forcing:** The framework was extended to include a real-time preview mode using self-forcing distillation. This enabled the generation of 81-frame panoramic videos in approximately 7 seconds, a substantial acceleration compared to the original preview stage model (approximately 5 minutes) and Matrix-3D (approximately 11 minutes), while preserving overall scene structure.
- **3D Scene Generation:** OmniRoam's consistent long-horizon panoramic videos were successfully used for 3D scene generation and reconstruction. By extracting perspective views from generated panoramic frames, coherent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes could be reconstructed. This demonstrates that the generated panoramic videos provide sufficient view coverage and multi-view consistency to serve as a reasonable input for 3D representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OmniRoam framework represents a significant advancement in controllable video generation, particularly for large-scale and immersive scene modeling. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations in Scene Generation:** By moving beyond perspective-centric models, OmniRoam effectively addresses the long-standing challenges of completeness, global consistency, and error accumulation in long-horizon video synthesis. This shifts the paradigm for generating complex, explorable virtual environments.
- **Enabling Immersive World Wandering:** The ability to generate coherent, high-fidelity panoramic videos along user-specified, extended trajectories facilitates "world wandering." This could have a substantial impact on applications requiring virtual exploration, such as architectural visualization, virtual tourism, gaming environments, and immersive storytelling, providing a more intuitive and consistent user experience.
- **Advancing Controllable Generative Models:** The explicit decomposition of camera trajectory into orthogonal flow and scale components offers a more granular and intuitive control mechanism for panoramic video generation. This contributes to the field of controllable generative AI, making these models more practical for creative and industrial applications.
- **Introduction of Novel Benchmarking Tools:** The proposal of a "loop consistency" metric provides a quantitative method to evaluate long-term spatial coherence, a crucial aspect previously difficult to measure effectively in video generation. This metric can serve as a valuable tool for future research in scene-level video generation, fostering development of models that maintain consistency over extended periods.
- **Contribution of New Datasets:** The construction of a scalable hybrid panoramic video dataset, incorporating both scene-reality real data and trajectory-precise synthetic data, with an established canonical coordinate system, is a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset can accelerate further advancements in panoramic video generation and 3D scene modeling.
- **Practical Applications and Extensions:** The demonstrated extensions, such as real-time preview and the ability to drive 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction, highlight the practical utility of OmniRoam. Real-time generation capabilities can empower interactive design workflows, while robust 3D reconstruction from generated video opens avenues for creating realistic 3D assets and environments from synthetic imagery.
- **Validation of Global-to-Local Design:** The empirical validation of the global-to-local preview-refine strategy and the inherent advantages of panoramic representations over perspective views provide critical insights for the design of future generative architectures for complex scene synthesis. This design principle may inform other areas of large-scale content generation.

In summary, OmniRoam's methodological contributions and demonstrated capabilities position it as a foundational step towards more scalable, consistent, and controllable generation of immersive virtual worlds, impacting various domains from content creation to scientific visualization.
