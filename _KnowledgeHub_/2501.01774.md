# A Unifying View of Linear Function Approximation in Off-Policy RL Through Matrix Splitting and Preconditioning

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified mathematical framework uses matrix splitting and preconditioning to connect Temporal Difference (TD), Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI), and Partial Fitted Q-Iteration (PFQI) algorithms, clarifying their distinct convergence properties and relationships in off-policy linear function approximation. This work establishes necessary and sufficient convergence conditions for each method, introduces a novel 'rank invariance' condition for solution consistency, and corrects inaccuracies in prior literature.

## Problem

- The traditional conceptualization of TD, FQI, and PFQI based on the number of target updates is insufficient for fully understanding their convergence behaviors.
- A lack of a unified mathematical framework to rigorously connect these algorithms and precisely explain their intricate convergence relationships.
- Existing convergence proofs often rely on restrictive assumptions, such as linear independence of features, limiting the generality of theoretical results.

## Method

- Developed a unifying framework that views TD, FQI, and PFQI as iterative methods solving the same LSTD fixed-point equation, differentiated by their specific ==matrix splitting== schemes and preconditioners.
- Introduced 'Rank Invariance' as a necessary and sufficient condition for the consistency and uniqueness of solutions to the target linear system.
- Provided a detailed analysis of algorithm-specific convergence conditions, relaxing common assumptions and offering an 'encoder-decoder' perspective for TD stability.

## Results

- Formally established necessary and sufficient convergence conditions for TD, FQI, and PFQI, detailing their respective fixed points and iteration matrices.
- Precisely characterized the valid learning rate interval for stable TD convergence and demonstrated that on-policy stochastic TD can converge without assuming linearly independent features.
- Identified and corrected several inaccuracies in the convergence conditions presented in previously published reinforcement learning literature.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - TD, FQI, and PFQI are fundamentally iterative solvers for the same LSTD fixed-point equation, differing primarily in their choice of preconditioner (constant for TD, pseudo-inverse for FQI, and an incrementally adapting one for PFQI).
> - The 'Rank Invariance' condition is critical for ensuring the target linear system has consistent solutions, especially important as it was often overlooked previously.
> - The convergence of TD does not necessarily imply the convergence of FQI, and vice versa, even under strong feature assumptions, challenging earlier assumptions about their mutual stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of off-policy policy evaluation (OPE) in reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on methods employing linear value function approximation. The paper addresses a foundational challenge in understanding the convergence and stability properties of three prominent algorithms: Temporal Difference Learning (TD), Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI), and Partial Fitted Q-Iteration (PFQI).

Traditionally, these algorithms have been conceptually differentiated by the number of updates made towards a target value function: TD performs one update, FQI makes an infinite number, and PFQI (akin to target networks in Deep Q-Networks or DQN) executes a finite number. This conventional perspective, as highlighted by the authors, is considered insufficient for fully capturing the intricate convergence relationships between these methods and can lead to inaccurate conclusions, such as the erroneous assumption that TD convergence implies FQI convergence.

Previous research has explored the convergence and instability of TD (e.g., Baird 1995, Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis 1996), with specific results for stochastic TD often relying on assumptions like decaying learning rates and linearly independent features (e.g., Tsitsiklis and Van Roy 1996, Borkar and Meyn 2000). FQI has been empirically noted for its stability (Voloshin et al. 2019), with some theoretical analyses focusing on finite-sample behavior (Perdomo et al. 2022). PFQI, popularized by DQN, has seen studies suggesting stabilization through increased target updates under certain conditions (Fellows et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2021, Che et al. 2024). The LSTD (Least-Squares Temporal Difference) framework (Bradtke and Barto 1996, Boyan 1999) has served as a reference for the underlying linear system.

The present paper identifies and aims to address several gaps in this existing landscape: the absence of a unified mathematical framework that rigorously connects these algorithms, the lack of clarity regarding their precise convergence relationships, and the frequent reliance on restrictive assumptions (e.g., linear independence of features) that may limit the generality of prior theoretical results. By introducing matrix splitting and preconditioning into the analysis, the authors seek to provide a more comprehensive and accurate theoretical foundation, which also leads to the correction of several specific claims in previous literature (e.g., by Ghosh and Bellemare 2020, Asadi et al. 2023, Xiao et al. 2022, and Che et al. 2024).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unifying mathematical perspective for the Temporal Difference (TD), Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI), and Partial Fitted Q-Iteration (PFQI) algorithms, particularly in the context of off-policy policy evaluation (OPE) with linear value function approximation.

The motivation for this unification stems from the observation that the traditional conceptualization of these algorithms, based on the number of updates to a target value function, is considered inaccurate and insufficient for explaining their distinct and interconnected convergence behaviors. The authors contend that this traditional view hinders a complete understanding of their relationships and can lead to incorrect inferences about their stability.

More specific objectives include:
- To precisely identify the fundamental convergence conditions for TD, FQI, and PFQI without relying on restrictive assumptions, such as linear independence of features.
- To mathematically demonstrate that these three algorithms are, in essence, different iterative methods designed to solve the *same* underlying linear system, specifically the LSTD fixed-point equation, and that they primarily differ in their choice of matrix splitting schemes and preconditioners.
- To provide a formal mathematical explanation for the role of increasing the number of updates (the "target network" technique) in PFQI as a gradual transition from a constant preconditioner (TD) to a data-feature adaptive one (FQI).
- To clarify and establish the convergence connections and divergences among TD, PFQI, and FQI, thereby explaining phenomena such as why TD convergence does not necessarily guarantee FQI convergence, and vice versa.
- To introduce and analyze a novel condition termed "rank invariance," demonstrating its necessity and sufficiency for ensuring the consistency and uniqueness of solutions for the target linear system, a condition previously overlooked in much of the literature.
- To offer an "encoder-decoder" perspective that aids in understanding the intrinsic convergence conditions of TD, particularly regarding how feature representations interact with system dynamics.
- To formally prove that, for TD, when a larger learning rate fails to ensure convergence, a sufficiently smaller one may succeed, providing a sharper characterization of the learning rate's influence.
- To relax certain common assumptions in convergence proofs, such as demonstrating that the assumption of linearly independent features can be omitted for stochastic TD in the on-policy setting without compromising convergence guarantees.
- To formally introduce matrix splitting as a analytical tool for the convergence analysis of these reinforcement learning algorithms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodological approach of this paper involves re-framing Temporal Difference (TD), Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI), and Partial Fitted Q-Iteration (PFQI) within a unified mathematical framework of iterative methods for solving linear systems. This framework primarily utilizes the concepts of matrix splitting and preconditioning.

1. **Unified Iterative Method for a Target Linear System:** The authors demonstrate that all three algorithms aim to solve the same underlying linear system, identified as the LSTD fixed-point equation: $(Σ_{cov} - \gamma Σ_{cr})\theta = \theta_{\phi,r}$. They show that the distinction among TD, FQI, and PFQI lies in their specific choice of preconditioner ($M$) within the general iterative scheme $x_{k+1} = (I - MA)x_k + Mb$.
 - **TD:** Interpreted as using a constant preconditioner, $M_{TD} = \alpha I$.
 - **FQI:** Interpreted as employing the inverse of the feature covariance matrix (or its Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse when the matrix is singular), $M_{FQI} = Σ_{cov}^{-1}$ (or $Σ_{cov}^\dagger$). The formulation for FQI without assuming invertibility of $Σ_{cov}$ is also explicitly derived, establishing its fixed-point equation and showing its relation to the target linear system.
 - **PFQI:** Its preconditioner, $M_{PFQI} = \alpha \sum_{i=0}^{t-1} (I - \alpha Σ_{cov})^i$, is shown to naturally transition from the TD preconditioner when $t=1$ to the FQI preconditioner as $t \to \infty$.

2. **Analysis of the Target Linear System:**
 - **Consistency and Nonsingularity:** The paper rigorously investigates the existence and uniqueness of solutions for the target linear system. It introduces "Rank Invariance" (Condition 4.1), defined as $Rank(\Phi) = Rank(\Phi^T D(I - \gamma P_\pi)\Phi)$, as a necessary and sufficient condition for the linear system to be consistent for any reward function (universal consistency). For a unique solution (nonsingularity), both Rank Invariance and "Linearly Independent Features" (Condition 4.3, i.e., $\Phi$ being full column rank) are identified as necessary and sufficient conditions.
 - **On-Policy Setting:** The paper proves that Rank Invariance holds automatically in the on-policy setting, thus guaranteeing the consistency of the target linear system in this context.
 - **Linear Realizability:** The authors establish conditions under which solutions to the target linear system correspond to parameters that perfectly realize the true Q-function.

3. **Algorithm-Specific Convergence Analysis:** For each algorithm (FQI, TD, PFQI), the authors derive necessary and sufficient conditions for convergence. This involves applying established theorems for iterative methods, which typically require the consistency of the underlying linear system and the "semiconvergence" of the iteration matrix ($H = I - MA$).
 - **Fixed Points:** The characteristics of the fixed points to which the algorithms converge are detailed, generally showing them to be the sum of the Drazin (or group) inverse solution of the respective linear system and a vector from the null space of the iteration matrix.
 - **Learning Rate for TD:** A precise characterization of the valid learning rate interval $(\alpha \in (0, \epsilon))$ for stable TD convergence is provided, based on the eigenvalues of the system matrix.
 - **Encoder-Decoder Perspective for TD:** An "encoder-decoder" view is introduced to analyze $A_{LSTD} = Φ^T D(I - \gamma P_\pi)Φ$, where $Φ$ acts as an encoder and $Φ^T$ as a decoder. This perspective is used to understand how properties of the system's dynamics, specifically positive semi-stability, are preserved through the feature mapping, and how this impacts TD convergence.
 - **Impact of Assumptions:** The paper systematically evaluates how assumptions like "linearly independent features" and "rank invariance" affect the convergence conditions of each algorithm, often demonstrating that certain conditions can be relaxed compared to prior work.

4. **Analysis of Inter-Algorithm Relationships:** The framework is used to systematically investigate the convergence relationships between TD, FQI, and PFQI, including presenting specific counterexamples where the convergence of one algorithm does not imply the convergence of another. Conditions for mutual implication (e.g., under a "Z-matrix System" assumption) are also explored.

5. **Corrections and Extensions:** The paper explicitly details corrections to previously published convergence conditions for related work. It also discusses how the derived results for expected forms of the algorithms can be adapted to stochastic and batch settings by replacing expected quantities with their empirical counterparts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several key findings and results that unify and clarify the theoretical understanding of TD, FQI, and PFQI in off-policy linear function approximation:

1. **Unified Iterative Framework:** The core finding is that TD, FQI, and PFQI can be viewed as distinct iterative methods for solving the *same* target linear system, the LSTD fixed-point equation $(Σ_{cov} - \gamma Σ_{cr})\theta = \theta_{\phi,r}$. Their differences are mathematically formalized by their choice of **preconditioner** ($M$).
 - TD uses a constant preconditioner ($M_{TD} = \alpha I$).
 - FQI uses the pseudo-inverse of the covariance matrix ($M_{FQI} = Σ_{cov}^\dagger$).
 - PFQI uses a preconditioner $M_{PFQI} = \alpha \sum_{i=0}^{t-1} (I - \alpha Σ_{cov})^i$, which formally transitions from TD-like ($t=1$) to FQI-like ($t \to \infty$) behavior.

2. **Crucial Conditions for Target Linear System:**
 - **Rank Invariance (Condition 4.1):** Defined as $Rank(\Phi) = Rank(\Phi^T D(I - \gamma P_\pi)\Phi)$, this is established as a necessary and sufficient condition for the target linear system to be universally consistent (i.e., possess a solution for any reward function). This condition was previously overlooked in much of the literature.
 - **Nonsingularity:** The target linear system has a unique solution if and only if both Rank Invariance and Linearly Independent Features (Condition 4.3, i.e., $\Phi$ is full column rank) hold.
 - In the **on-policy setting**, Rank Invariance is shown to hold automatically, guaranteeing the consistency of the target linear system.

3. **FQI Convergence Conditions (Theorem 5.1 & Corollaries):**
 - FQI converges if and only if its associated linear system is consistent and its iteration matrix ($H_{FQI} = \gamma Σ_{cov}^\dagger Σ_{cr}$) is semiconvergent.
 - When Rank Invariance holds, FQI's associated linear system is nonsingular, and convergence is simplified to $\rho(H_{FQI}) < 1$. This provides a theoretical explanation for FQI's empirical robustness.
 - The assumption of linearly independent features is not central to FQI's convergence properties but rather determines the specific form of the linear system FQI solves.

4. **TD Convergence Conditions (Theorem 6.1 & Corollaries):**
 - TD converges if and only if the target linear system is consistent and its iteration matrix ($H_{TD} = I - \alpha A_{LSTD}$) is semiconvergent.
 - **TD Stability (Corollary 6.2):** TD is stable (i.e., converges for some positive learning rate) if and only if the target linear system is consistent, $A_{LSTD}$ is positive semi-stable, and $Index(A_{LSTD}) \le 1$.
 - **Learning Rate Interval (Corollary 6.3):** When stable, TD converges if and only if the learning rate $\alpha$ falls within a specific interval $(0, \epsilon)$, with $\epsilon$ precisely characterized by the eigenvalues of $A_{LSTD}$. This formally supports the intuition that "a smaller learning rate may help."
 - **Encoder-Decoder Perspective:** The analysis highlights that TD convergence requires the feature mapping (acting as an "encoder-decoder") to preserve the positive semi-stability of the underlying system dynamics. For example, orthogonal features guarantee TD stability in over-parameterized scenarios (Proposition E.10).
 - **On-Policy TD (Theorem 6.4):** The paper proves that in the on-policy setting, TD is stable even without assuming linearly independent features, a relaxation of conditions in prior work.

5. **PFQI Convergence Conditions (Theorem 7.1 & Corollaries):**
 - PFQI converges if and only if the target linear system is consistent and its iteration matrix ($H_{PFQI} = I - M_{PFQI} A_{LSTD}$) is semiconvergent.
 - The effect of increasing the number of updates ($t$) in PFQI can be detrimental if features are not linearly independent, as the preconditioner $M_{PFQI}$ may diverge, leading to overall algorithm instability. This provides a more nuanced understanding of target network behavior.

6. **Inter-Algorithm Relationships and Counterexamples (Section 8):**
 - The convergence relationships between TD, PFQI, and FQI are clarified. While TD stability can imply PFQI convergence for finite $t$, and PFQI convergence for large $t$ can imply FQI convergence, the implications are not always unconditional.
 - The paper provides concrete counterexamples (Appendix G.3) demonstrating that TD stability does not necessarily imply FQI convergence, and vice versa, even under conditions of linearly independent features and a nonsingular target system. This challenges previous assumptions about their mutual stability.
 - Under specific "Z-matrix System" (Assumption H.1) and Rank Invariance conditions, the stability of TD and the convergence of FQI are shown to be equivalent (Theorem H.2).

7. **Correction of Previous Results:** The paper identifies and corrects several inaccuracies in convergence conditions presented in prior reinforcement learning literature, including works by Ghosh and Bellemare (2020), Asadi et al. (2023), Xiao et al. (2022), and Che et al. (2024).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the theoretical foundation of reinforcement learning, particularly in off-policy policy evaluation with linear function approximation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Fundamental Understanding:** By providing a unified mathematical framework grounded in matrix splitting and preconditioning, the paper offers a more coherent and precise understanding of TD, FQI, and PFQI. This deeper insight transcends earlier, often intuitive, categorizations of these algorithms, clarifying their underlying mechanics and relationships.

2. **Improved Theoretical Precision:** The establishment of necessary and sufficient convergence conditions for each algorithm, often with fewer or relaxed assumptions compared to previous work, leads to sharper theoretical results. This includes the explicit characterization of the learning rate interval for stable TD and the demonstration that on-policy stochastic TD can converge even without assuming linearly independent features. Such precision strengthens the theoretical bedrock of reinforcement learning.

3. **Discovery of Novel Crucial Conditions:** The introduction of "rank invariance" as a fundamental condition for the consistency and uniqueness of solutions for the target linear system is a notable contribution. This previously overlooked factor is critical for understanding when these algorithms can even find a meaningful solution, guiding future work in feature design and problem formulation.

4. **Refined Algorithmic Insights and Challenges to Intuition:**
 - The "encoder-decoder" perspective offers a new lens through which to analyze how feature representations influence TD's stability, providing guidance for designing or learning features that preserve crucial system properties.
 - The nuanced analysis of PFQI (target networks) clarifies that simply increasing the number of updates ($t$) does not universally guarantee stability, particularly when feature representations are poor. This offers important practical considerations for deep reinforcement learning.
 - The analytical and empirical demonstration of scenarios where TD and FQI exhibit divergent stability behaviors (one converges while the other diverges), even under strong feature assumptions, directly challenges conventional wisdom and highlights the unique complexities of each algorithm.

5. **Foundation for Future Algorithmic Development:** The insights derived from this unifying framework—especially regarding preconditioner design and the impact of feature properties—could inform the development of new, more robust, and theoretically grounded algorithms. This framework can potentially be extended to other TD variants (e.g., Emphatic TD), and potentially inspire analyses for non-linear function approximation methods by focusing on their linear components.

6. **Correction and Refinement of Literature:** The explicit identification and correction of inaccuracies in previously published convergence conditions for related algorithms serve to rectify the theoretical landscape, preventing the propagation of potentially misleading assumptions or conclusions in future research.

In summary, this paper provides a robust theoretical framework that not only unifies the understanding of several core reinforcement learning algorithms but also yields precise convergence conditions and novel insights that can guide both theoretical advancements and practical algorithm design.
