# A Unifying View of Linear Function Approximation in Off-Policy RL Through Matrix Splitting and Preconditioning

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified mathematical framework uses matrix splitting and preconditioning to connect Temporal Difference (TD), Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI), and Partial Fitted Q-Iteration (PFQI) algorithms, clarifying their distinct convergence properties and relationships in off-policy linear function approximation. This work establishes necessary and sufficient convergence conditions for each method, introduces a novel 'rank invariance' condition for solution consistency, and corrects inaccuracies in prior literature.

## Problem

- The traditional conceptualization of TD, FQI, and PFQI based on the number of target updates is insufficient for fully understanding their convergence behaviors.
- A lack of a unified mathematical framework to rigorously connect these algorithms and precisely explain their intricate convergence relationships.
- Existing convergence proofs often rely on restrictive assumptions, such as linear independence of features, limiting the generality of theoretical results.

## Method

- Developed a unifying framework that views TD, FQI, and PFQI as iterative methods solving the same LSTD fixed-point equation, differentiated by their specific ==matrix splitting== schemes and preconditioners.
- Introduced 'Rank Invariance' as a necessary and sufficient condition for the consistency and uniqueness of solutions to the target linear system.
- Provided a detailed analysis of algorithm-specific convergence conditions, relaxing common assumptions and offering an 'encoder-decoder' perspective for TD stability.

## Results

- Formally established necessary and sufficient convergence conditions for TD, FQI, and PFQI, detailing their respective fixed points and iteration matrices.
- Precisely characterized the valid learning rate interval for stable TD convergence and demonstrated that on-policy stochastic TD can converge without assuming linearly independent features.
- Identified and corrected several inaccuracies in the convergence conditions presented in previously published reinforcement learning literature.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - TD, FQI, and PFQI are fundamentally iterative solvers for the same LSTD fixed-point equation, differing primarily in their choice of preconditioner (constant for TD, pseudo-inverse for FQI, and an incrementally adapting one for PFQI).
> - The 'Rank Invariance' condition is critical for ensuring the target linear system has consistent solutions, especially important as it was often overlooked previously.
> - The convergence of TD does not necessarily imply the convergence of FQI, and vice versa, even under strong feature assumptions, challenging earlier assumptions about their mutual stability.
