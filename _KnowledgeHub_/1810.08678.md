# Optimization of Molecules via Deep Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> MolDQN, a deep reinforcement learning framework, optimizes molecular properties by directly incorporating chemical domain knowledge, achieving 100% chemical validity for all generated molecules without requiring pre-training. The system reached a top penalized logP score of 11.84 and a QED score of 0.948, outperforming several existing methods in various single and multi-objective optimization tasks.

## Problem

- The traditional process of designing new molecules with specific desired properties is often time-consuming, resource-intensive, and costly.
- Many computational generative models for molecules struggle to ensure the chemical validity of their outputs, frequently necessitating complex grammar rules or post-processing steps.
- Existing reinforcement learning approaches for molecular design often rely on pre-training on large datasets, which can introduce biases and limit the exploration of novel chemical spaces.

## Method

- Formulated molecule modification as a ==Markov Decision Process (MDP)== where actions are intrinsically defined to be chemically valid, ensuring 100% validity of generated molecules.
- Employed **MolDQN**: ==Deep Q-Networks (DQN)== with ==double Q-learning== and ==bootstrapped DQN== to approximate the optimal action-value function, learning from scratch without pre-training on existing molecular datasets.
- Extended the framework to ==multi-objective reinforcement learning== by using a ==scalarized reward function== with user-defined weights, allowing for the simultaneous optimization of multiple molecular properties.

## Results

- Achieved a top penalized logP score of **11.84** and a top QED score of **0.948**, outperforming state-of-the-art methods like GCPN, JT-VAE, and ORGAN in single-objective optimization.
- Demonstrated **100%** success rates in constrained optimization tasks, maintaining Tanimoto similarity above thresholds while achieving statistically significant improvements in penalized logP compared to GCPN.
- Successfully performed multi-objective optimization, balancing properties like QED and similarity to a starting molecule based on user-defined weights, and consistently hit specific property targets with **100%** success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating chemistry domain knowledge directly into the MDP's action space ensures chemical validity by design, simplifying the molecular generation process compared to models requiring post-hoc validation.
> - Learning molecular optimization from scratch through reinforcement learning enables the exploration of a broader and potentially more novel chemical space by avoiding biases introduced by pre-training datasets.
> - Utilizing bootstrapped DQN proves beneficial for deep exploration and robust performance in more complex optimization tasks, contributing to improved molecule property scores and success rates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The design of new molecules with specific desired properties is a fundamental objective in chemistry, with significant applications in fields such as material science and drug discovery. This process is often resource-intensive, requiring substantial time and financial investment. Machine learning, particularly deep learning, has emerged as a strategy to partially automate this design process.

Previous computational approaches to molecule design predominantly fall into two categories:

1. **Generative Models:** These models typically map points in a high-dimensional latent space to molecular structures. Examples include models using SMILES strings as representations, like those by G´omez-Bombarelli et al., Blaschke et al., Segler et al., Lim et al., and Putin et al. A notable challenge with these string-based generators is ensuring the chemical validity of the output molecules, though some approaches, such as Kusner et al. and Dai et al., incorporated grammar constraints to improve validity. Graph-based generative models, such as those by Li et al., De Cao and Kipf (MolGAN), and Jin et al. (Junction Tree VAE), aim to generate molecules by constructing graphs in a step-wise manner. While these often achieve high validity, they frequently require a separate optimization step, typically involving Gaussian process models, on the high-dimensional, non-convex latent space, which can be computationally challenging.

2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) based Methods:** A distinct set of approaches applies reinforcement learning to molecule generation. Early examples, including Olivecrona et al., Guimaraes et al., Putin et al., and Popova et al., applied RL on top of string generators to achieve specific properties but encountered difficulties with chemical validity. More recent work, such as the Graph Convolutional Policy Network (GCPN) by You et al., successfully generated graph representations of molecules with 100% validity using deep RL. However, a common characteristic of most existing RL methods is their reliance on pre-training on large datasets, which can introduce biases from the training data and limit the exploration of novel chemical space.

The presented work, MolDQN, positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of prior methods. It integrates chemistry domain knowledge directly into the reinforcement learning framework to ensure 100% chemical validity by design, distinguishing itself from generative models that struggle with validity or require post-processing. Unlike many existing RL approaches, MolDQN operates without pre-training on external datasets, aiming to reduce bias and facilitate broader exploration. Furthermore, it utilizes value function learning, which is presented as more stable and sample-efficient in applicable contexts compared to policy gradient methods often employed by other RL-based molecular design algorithms. This dual focus on intrinsic chemical validity and learning from scratch represents an advancement in the automation of molecular optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The fundamental motivation for this research is to partially automate the process of designing new molecules with specific desired properties, particularly in fields such as material design and drug discovery. The current molecular design process is characterized by significant time and cost expenditures, often spanning years and incurring millions of dollars for the development of a single new drug. The authors seek to mitigate these challenges through the application of advanced machine learning techniques.

The key objectives of this study are:

1. **Develop a Robust Framework for Molecule Optimization:** The primary objective is to create a framework, termed Molecule Deep Q-Networks (MolDQN), that effectively combines chemistry domain knowledge with state-of-the-art reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, specifically double Q-learning and randomized value functions, for the purpose of optimizing molecular properties.
2. **Ensure Chemical Validity:** A crucial objective is to guarantee 100% chemical validity of all generated molecules. This is addressed by directly defining modification actions on molecules in a manner that intrinsically adheres to chemical rules, thereby avoiding the need for validation checks after generation or complex grammar constraints.
3. **Learn from Scratch to Avoid Bias:** The authors aim to develop a model that operates without pre-training on any existing molecular dataset. This objective is motivated by the desire to circumvent potential biases introduced by specific training sets, thereby enabling the model to explore a wider and potentially more novel chemical space.
4. **Implement Multi-Objective Optimization:** Recognizing that real-world applications, such as medicinal chemistry lead optimization, often require balancing multiple properties simultaneously, a key objective is to extend the MolDQN model to perform multi-objective reinforcement learning. This allows for the optimization of properties like drug-likeness while maintaining similarity to an original molecule, according to user-defined priorities.
5. **Enhance Model Interpretability:** The research aims to provide insights into the model's decision-making process by demonstrating the path through chemical space taken to achieve optimization for a given molecule. This objective seeks to improve understanding of how the model functions, which can be valuable for chemists.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MolDQN framework for molecule optimization integrates principles from chemistry with deep reinforcement learning. The core approach involves formulating molecule modification as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and solving it using Deep Q-Networks (DQN).

#### 3.1. Molecule Modification as a Markov Decision Process (MDP)

Molecule optimization is conceptualized as a step-wise process, where each step involves a chemically valid modification. The MDP is defined by the following components:

- **State Space (S):** Each state *s* is a tuple *(m, t)*, where *m* is a chemically valid molecule and *t* is the number of steps taken. An explicit limit *T* is placed on the maximum number of steps, controlling the divergence from an initial molecule. Terminal states are reached when *t = T*.
- **Action Space (A):** Actions are defined as valid modifications to a molecule *m*, belonging to three categories:
 1. **Atom Addition:** Involves adding an atom from a predefined set of elements (e.g., {C, O}) and forming new bonds (with allowed bond orders) between the added atom and the existing molecule. Implicit hydrogens are considered for valence.
 2. **Bond Addition:** Actions involve increasing the bond order between two atoms with free valence or forming a new bond (single, double, or triple) if no bond exists.
 - **Heuristics for Chemical Reasonableness:** To prevent high-strain molecules, bond formation between atoms within rings is disallowed. An option to restrict ring formation to specific common sizes (e.g., 3-6 atoms) is also included.
 3. **Bond Removal:** Actions decrease the bond order of an existing bond or completely remove it. Complete bond removal is permitted only if the resulting molecule remains connected or generates a single disconnected atom which is also removed. Aromatic bonds are not directly broken, though aromaticity can be lost.
 - An additional "no modification" action allows the molecule to remain unchanged until the step limit *T* is reached. Critically, only chemically valid actions are considered, ensuring 100% validity of generated molecules.
- **State Transition Probability ({P_sa}):** The state transitions are deterministic; applying an action *a* to state *s* leads to a specific next state *s'* with a probability of 1.
- **Reward Function (R):** Rewards are provided at each step *t* based on the properties of the molecule *m*. This differs from approaches that only provide terminal rewards, and is empirically shown to improve learning performance. Rewards are discounted by a factor of γ^(T-t) to emphasize final states.
- **Implementation:** The RDKit software framework is used for implementing molecule state transitions and calculating molecular properties.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning (Deep Q-Networks - DQN)

The objective is to find a policy π that maximizes cumulative future rewards. The authors utilize a Deep Q-learning algorithm to approximate the optimal action-value function, Q(s, a).

- **Q-function Approximation:** A neural network, parameterized by θ, approximates the Q-value function, Q(s, a; θ). This network is trained by minimizing the Huber loss function between the predicted Q-value and a target value *y_t = r_t + max_a Q(s_{t+1}, a; θ)*.
- **Time-Dependent Policy:** Given a limited number of steps, the MDP is time-dependent, resulting in a time-dependent optimal policy.
- **Double Q-Learning:** The implementation incorporates double Q-learning, an improvement over standard DQN, to mitigate overestimation of Q-values.

#### 3.3. Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning

For scenarios requiring optimization of multiple properties, the framework extends to multi-objective reinforcement learning.

- **Scalarized Reward:** The environment returns a vector of rewards for *k* objectives, *~r_t*. A user-defined weight vector *w* is used to scalarize these rewards into a single value: *r_{s,t} = w^T * ~r_t*. The MDP then aims to maximize this cumulative scalarized reward.

#### 3.4. Exploitation vs. Exploration

To balance the trade-off between exploiting known high-reward actions and exploring potentially better, unknown actions, the model employs a combination of strategies:

- **ε-greedy:** Selects the predicted best action with probability (1 - ε) and a random action with probability ε.
- **Bootstrapped-DQN:** To enhance deep exploration, *H* independent Q-functions are maintained (implemented as a multi-task neural network with shared layers and separate heads). In each episode, one Q-function is uniformly chosen for decision-making. During training, ε is annealed from 1 to 0.01 in a piecewise linear fashion.

#### 3.5. Deep Q-Learning Implementation Details

- **State Representation:** Input molecules are converted to Morgan fingerprints (radius 3, length 2048). The number of remaining steps in the episode is concatenated to this vector.
- **Network Architecture:** A four-layer fully-connected neural network with hidden sizes of and ReLU activations is used. The output dimension corresponds to the number *H* of independent Q-functions.
- **Training Parameters:** Models are trained for 5,000 episodes using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.0001. The discount factor γ is set to 0.9. The maximum number of steps per episode is typically 40 (or 38 for logP optimization, 20 for constrained optimization).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The MolDQN framework was evaluated across various molecule optimization tasks, comparing its performance against baseline models and published state-of-the-art algorithms.

#### 4.1. Single Property Optimization

The model was tested on maximizing penalized logP and Quantitative Estimate of Druglikeness (QED).

- **Penalized logP Optimization:**
 - MolDQN-bootstrap achieved a top penalized logP score of 11.84, outperforming GCPN (7.98), JT-VAE (5.30), and ORGAN (3.63), as well as simpler baselines (random walk, greedy, ε-greedy).
 - All MolDQN variants maintained 100% chemical validity.
 - The authors note that logP maximization without constraints can lead to non-drug-like molecules and that penalized logP values tend to increase linearly with the number of atoms, suggesting it may not be an ideal metric for general model evaluation without specific constraints.
 - Bootstrapped DQN did not provide a significantly better result for logP maximization compared to naive DQN, implying the task's simplicity did not necessitate extensive exploration.
- **QED Optimization:**
 - MolDQN-bootstrap and MolDQN-twosteps both achieved a top QED score of 0.948, matching GCPN, and outperforming JT-VAE (0.925) and ORGAN (0.896).
 - Bootstrapped DQN demonstrated an improvement over naive DQN for QED, suggesting its exploration efficiency was beneficial for this task.
 - The model exhibited 100% chemical validity.

#### 4.2. Constrained Optimization

This task focused on maximizing the penalized logP improvement while maintaining a Tanimoto similarity (SIM) to the original molecule (*m_0*) above a threshold *δ*.

- MolDQN-naive and MolDQN-bootstrap consistently achieved 100% success rates in satisfying the similarity constraint across various *δ* values (0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6). In contrast, JT-VAE had significantly lower success rates for higher *δ* values (e.g., 46.4% for *δ* = 0.6).
- Both MolDQN variants demonstrated statistically significant improvements in mean penalized logP compared to GCPN for all *δ* values. For example, at *δ* = 0.6, MolDQN-bootstrap achieved an improvement of 1.86 ± 1.21, compared to GCPN's 0.79 ± 0.63.
- The bootstrap variant generally outperformed the naive model in terms of logP improvement, except for *δ* = 0.2.

#### 4.3. Multi-Objective Optimization

The objective was to maximize QED while maintaining similarity to a starting molecule, using a scalarized reward function with a user-defined weight *w* balancing QED and similarity.

- As the weight *w* on similarity increased, the optimized molecules exhibited higher similarity to the starting molecule, but with generally lower relative QED improvements. This demonstrated a successful trade-off between the two objectives.
- Visual examination of generated molecules (Figure 3c) indicated that for *w* >= 0.4, the model preserved the original molecular scaffold, highlighting its ability to maintain structural integrity under higher similarity constraints.

#### 4.4. Property Targeting

MolDQN was applied to find molecules with properties within specific ranges (molecular weight and logP).

- The model achieved 100% success rates in finding molecules within all specified ranges, outperforming baseline models (random walk, JT-VAE, ORGAN, GCPN) which had lower success rates (Table S1). This suggests MolDQN's ability to accurately target specific property values.
- For multi-objective property targeting (Synthetic Accessibility (SA) and QED), the generated molecules exhibited narrow distributions around the target values, demonstrating precise control over multiple properties simultaneously (Table S2).

#### 4.5. Visualization and Interpretation

The authors provided a visualization of the decision-making process, showing the Q-values associated with various actions and a step-by-step trajectory of QED maximization for a specific molecule.

- This visualization indicated that the Q-network predicts Q-values for actions, demonstrating that adding a hydroxyl group was favored, while breaking a five-member ring was disfavored.
- The trajectory illustrated that the algorithm can take actions that temporarily decrease a property value (e.g., QED in step 6) if it leads to higher cumulative future rewards, highlighting the long-term planning capability of the RL agent.

Overall, the findings indicate that MolDQN is a robust framework that achieves high performance in various molecular optimization tasks, ensuring chemical validity without reliance on pre-training, and offers capabilities for multi-objective and constrained optimization, alongside interpretability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MolDQN framework represents a methodological advancement in computational molecular design, combining deep reinforcement learning with an explicit integration of chemical domain knowledge. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key features:

1. **Guaranteed Chemical Validity:** By design, MolDQN ensures that all generated molecules are chemically valid through the precise definition of allowable actions within the Markov decision process. This addresses a persistent challenge in many generative models, where ensuring validity often requires complex grammar rules or post-hoc filtering, which can be computationally intensive or result in inefficient generation.
2. **Reduced Bias and Enhanced Exploration:** The model's ability to learn from scratch, without pre-training on existing molecular datasets, is a notable contribution. This approach helps mitigate the biases inherent in training data, allowing for a broader and potentially more creative exploration of chemical space. This could lead to the discovery of novel molecular structures that might be outside the scope of molecules represented in common databases.
3. **Improved Optimization Performance:** The comparative results demonstrate that MolDQN achieves equivalent or superior performance compared to established algorithms (such as JT-VAE, ORGAN, and GCPN) in optimizing specific molecular properties (e.g., penalized logP, QED) and in constrained optimization tasks. The statistically significant improvements in constrained logP optimization are particularly relevant for practical applications.
4. **Flexible Multi-Objective Optimization:** The implementation of multi-objective reinforcement learning, allowing users to define the relative importance of different objectives (e.g., drug-likeness and structural similarity), is crucial for real-world drug discovery and material science applications. This flexibility enables the fine-tuning of molecular properties according to complex design criteria.
5. **Interpretability of Decision-Making:** The capacity to visualize the Q-values of actions and trace the step-by-step optimization path enhances the model's interpretability. This feature is valuable for chemists, enabling them to understand *how* the model arrives at a particular design, fostering trust, and potentially guiding their intuition in future experimental work.
6. **Addressing Limitations in Evaluation Metrics:** The authors provide a critical commentary on the limitations of commonly used evaluation metrics in the field (e.g., logP, QED) and advocate for the prioritization of therapeutically relevant properties verifiable by experiment. This self-awareness contributes to the ongoing discourse on improving the rigor and applicability of generative model evaluation, suggesting future directions for the field.

The MolDQN model has the potential to significantly assist medicinal and material chemists by partially automating molecular design. By streamlining the search for molecules with desired properties, reducing the need for extensive experimental screening, and offering interpretable design pathways, it could contribute to accelerating the discovery and development process for new drugs and advanced materials. Future work, as suggested by the authors, may involve exploring alternative Q-function approximators (e.g., MPNN) and more sophisticated hyperparameter optimization, further enhancing its capabilities.
