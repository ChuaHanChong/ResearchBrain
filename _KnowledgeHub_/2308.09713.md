# Dynamic 3D Gaussians: Tracking by Persistent Dynamic View Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> A method extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to model dynamic scenes, achieving simultaneous photorealistic novel-view synthesis and dense 6-DOF tracking through a persistent particle-based representation. This approach demonstrates 2.21 cm median 3D trajectory error and 1.57 pixel median 2D trajectory error on complex human motions, coupled with real-time rendering capabilities.

## Problem

- Reconciling high-quality, real-time novel-view synthesis with accurate, consistent long-term 3D tracking in dynamic scenes.
- Addressing the computational cost and slow performance of many existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods, particularly for real-time applications.
- Inferring dense 6-DOF motion of scene elements without relying on explicit, potentially noisy, correspondence inputs like optical flow.

## Method

- Extending 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== to dynamic scenes by representing them as persistent 3D Gaussians, each maintaining its identity and fixed appearance attributes while allowing its position and rotation to vary over time.
- Employing an analysis-by-synthesis framework that optimizes the dynamic Gaussian parameters by minimizing the difference between rendered and observed multi-view images across timesteps using differentiable rendering.
- Integrating physically-based regularization losses, such as short-term local rigidity and long-term local isometry, to ensure stable and plausible 6-DOF motion tracking of dense scene elements.

## Results

- Achieved a 3D median trajectory error of 2.21 cm and a 100% survival rate for long-term tracking on the PanopticSports dataset, demonstrating high metric accuracy.
- Outperformed state-of-the-art 2D long-term trackers, yielding an average median trajectory error of 1.57 pixels and a 100% survival rate on the same dataset.
- Demonstrated fast training (2 hours for 150 timesteps) and real-time rendering at **850 FPS**, maintaining the efficiency benefits of 3D Gaussian Splatting, and enabled scene editing capabilities like object manipulation and consistent propagation of image edits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense 6-DOF tracking can emerge as an inherent property from optimizing a persistent 3D scene representation for novel-view synthesis, without requiring explicit correspondence inputs.
> - The efficiency of 3D Gaussian Splatting can be effectively extended to dynamic scenes, enabling both fast training and real-time rendering capabilities for complex motions.
> - Integrating physically-based regularization, particularly local rigidity and long-term isometry constraints, is crucial for achieving stable, accurate, and plausible 6-DOF motion for dense points over extended durations.
