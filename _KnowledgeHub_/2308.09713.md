# Dynamic 3D Gaussians: Tracking by Persistent Dynamic View Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> A method extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to model dynamic scenes, achieving simultaneous photorealistic novel-view synthesis and dense 6-DOF tracking through a persistent particle-based representation. This approach demonstrates 2.21 cm median 3D trajectory error and 1.57 pixel median 2D trajectory error on complex human motions, coupled with real-time rendering capabilities.

## Problem

- Reconciling high-quality, real-time novel-view synthesis with accurate, consistent long-term 3D tracking in dynamic scenes.
- Addressing the computational cost and slow performance of many existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods, particularly for real-time applications.
- Inferring dense 6-DOF motion of scene elements without relying on explicit, potentially noisy, correspondence inputs like optical flow.

## Method

- Extending 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== to dynamic scenes by representing them as persistent 3D Gaussians, each maintaining its identity and fixed appearance attributes while allowing its position and rotation to vary over time.
- Employing an analysis-by-synthesis framework that optimizes the dynamic Gaussian parameters by minimizing the difference between rendered and observed multi-view images across timesteps using differentiable rendering.
- Integrating physically-based regularization losses, such as short-term local rigidity and long-term local isometry, to ensure stable and plausible 6-DOF motion tracking of dense scene elements.

## Results

- Achieved a 3D median trajectory error of 2.21 cm and a 100% survival rate for long-term tracking on the PanopticSports dataset, demonstrating high metric accuracy.
- Outperformed state-of-the-art 2D long-term trackers, yielding an average median trajectory error of 1.57 pixels and a 100% survival rate on the same dataset.
- Demonstrated fast training (2 hours for 150 timesteps) and real-time rendering at **850 FPS**, maintaining the efficiency benefits of 3D Gaussian Splatting, and enabled scene editing capabilities like object manipulation and consistent propagation of image edits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense 6-DOF tracking can emerge as an inherent property from optimizing a persistent 3D scene representation for novel-view synthesis, without requiring explicit correspondence inputs.
> - The efficiency of 3D Gaussian Splatting can be effectively extended to dynamic scenes, enabling both fast training and real-time rendering capabilities for complex motions.
> - Integrating physically-based regularization, particularly local rigidity and long-term isometry constraints, is crucial for achieving stable, accurate, and plausible 6-DOF motion for dense points over extended durations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within computer vision and graphics: dynamic scene reconstruction, novel-view synthesis, and dense long-term tracking.

**Novel-View Synthesis and Radiance Fields:** The field of novel-view synthesis experienced a paradigm shift with the introduction of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) in 2020. NeRF revolutionized photorealistic 3D scene representation from multi-view images. Following NeRF, numerous "dynamic NeRF" papers emerged to extend this capability to dynamic scenes. These typically fall into categories such as per-timestep representations, Eulerian 4D space-time grids, canonical space deformation fields, or template-guided methods. While successful in view synthesis, many of these approaches struggle with explicit, accurate, and consistent long-term 3D correspondences or suffer from slow training/rendering times.
More recently, **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** by Kerbl et al. (2023) emerged as a groundbreaking alternative to NeRF for *static* scenes. 3DGS represents scenes as a collection of 3D Gaussians and achieves unparalleled rendering speed (real-time, high FPS) and quality through efficient splatting-based rasterization and differentiable rendering.

**Dynamic Scene Reconstruction and Tracking:** Traditional dynamic reconstruction often relies on depth cameras or pre-computed correspondences like optical flow, which can be computationally intensive and scale poorly. Dense long-term tracking has also seen advancements, typically through deep learning models trained on large datasets of ground-truth tracks. Methods like OmniMotion (Wang et al., 2023) also fit dynamic radiance fields for tracking but often require dense optical flow as input, making them dependent on other potentially noisy and inconsistent correspondence estimates.

**Contribution to the Landscape:** "Dynamic 3D Gaussians" directly builds upon the success of 3DGS, being the *first* to extend this highly efficient representation to *dynamic* scenes. Its core innovation is to enable accurate and persistent 6-DOF tracking *as an emergent property* of novel-view synthesis, without requiring any explicit correspondence (like optical flow) as input. This addresses a critical gap: combining the speed and quality of 3DGS for rendering with robust, physically-plausible dense 6-DOF tracking. By adopting a Lagrangian (particle-based) representation, it directly contrasts with most dynamic NeRFs that use Eulerian or canonical space approaches, which inherently make long-term correspondence more challenging to extract. This work aims to provide a unified framework that overcomes the limitations of previous methods regarding accuracy, speed, and the ability to infer fine-grained 6-DOF motion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind "Dynamic 3D Gaussians" are multifaceted:

1. **Simultaneous Dynamic Scene Novel-View Synthesis and Dense 6-DOF Tracking**: The overarching goal is to create a unified system that can both generate high-quality, photorealistic novel views of dynamic scenes and accurately track the full 6-DOF motion (position and orientation) of all dense scene elements over time.
2. **Achieve Photo-Realistic Reconstruction with Accurate Tracking**: Current methods often compromise on one aspect or the other. The authors aim to produce reconstructions that are visually appealing (photo-realistic) while simultaneously yielding highly accurate 3D and 2D tracks for every part of the scene.
3. **Real-time Performance for Rendering and Fast Training**: A significant motivation is to develop a scalable approach that can handle high-resolution imagery and operate in real-time. Many existing dynamic scene reconstruction techniques are computationally expensive, hindering their practical application in areas like AR/VR or interactive content creation. Building on 3D Gaussian Splatting, the authors aim to inherit and extend its speed advantages.
4. **Tracking as an Emergent Property (Analysis-by-Synthesis)**: A core conceptual motivation is to demonstrate that dense 6-DOF tracking can naturally emerge from fitting a persistent 3D scene representation to observed imagery. Unlike methods that rely on pre-computed optical flow or other explicit correspondences, this work aims to infer motion solely from appearance consistency over time, regularized by physical priors.
5. **Enable Downstream Applications**: The authors are driven by the potential for transformative applications. A persistent, metric-space reconstruction with accurate 6-DOF tracking is crucial for robotics (understanding environment dynamics), augmented reality (seamless integration of virtual objects), and self-driving cars (predicting object motion). On the generative side, it could enable novel content creation, such as easily editable and controllable dynamic 3D assets for movies, video games, or the metaverse.

In essence, the paper seeks to fill the void of a single, efficient, and accurate solution for holistic dynamic 3D world modeling, which integrates both discriminative (tracking, reconstruction) and generative (novel-view synthesis, content creation) capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of "Dynamic 3D Gaussians" lies in extending the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework to model dynamic scenes, employing an analysis-by-synthesis optimization strategy.

**Analysis-by-Synthesis Framework**: The method formulates dynamic scene reconstruction and tracking as an optimization problem. Given a set of synchronized multi-view images from different timesteps and cameras, along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, the goal is to optimize a persistent dynamic 3D representation (S) of the scene. This representation is used to render images (`^I_t,c`) which are then compared to the input images (`I_t,c`). The errors drive gradient-based updates to the parameters of S via differentiable rendering.

**Dynamic 3D Gaussians Representation**:
The scene `S` is parameterized by a collection of Dynamic 3D Gaussians, each representing a small, soft area of physical space. Crucially, the *identity* of each Gaussian is preserved over time, acting as a persistent particle.
- **Persistent Attributes**: For each Gaussian `i`, its 3D size (`s_x, s_y, s_z`), color (`r, g, b`), opacity logit (`o`), and background logit (`bg`) are fixed and *consistent over all timesteps*. This persistence is key to enabling long-term tracking, as each Gaussian is expected to represent the same physical element throughout its trajectory.
- **Dynamic Attributes**: Only the 3D center (`x_t, y_t, z_t`) and 3D rotation (`q_w, q_x, q_y, q_z`) vary for each Gaussian over time `t`. This allows the Gaussians to track the 6-DOF motion of scene elements.
- **Covariance Matrix**: The 3D Gaussian’s shape and orientation at any time `t` are defined by its covariance matrix `Σ_i,t`, which combines its fixed scaling `S_i` and time-varying rotation `R_i,t`. The "softness" of the Gaussian (infinite extent) ensures that gradients can flow effectively during optimization, even if Gaussians are initially misplaced.

**Differentiable Rendering via Gaussian 3D Splatting**:
The method adopts and extends the differentiable 3D Gaussian renderer from Kerbl et al. (2023). This involves:
1. **Projection**: Each 3D Gaussian's center (`μ_i,t`) is projected into 2D image coordinates using standard camera intrinsics (`K`) and extrinsics (`E_t,c`).
2. **Covariance Projection**: The 3D covariance `Σ_i,t` is projected into a 2D covariance `Σ_2D` using a formula that accounts for the Jacobian of the point projection.
3. **Splatting and Volume Rendering**: The 2D Gaussians are splatted onto the image plane. For each pixel, the influence of all overlapping Gaussians is combined using front-to-back volume rendering (similar to NeRF's Max formula), weighted by their opacity and color. This process is fully differentiable, allowing for gradient-based optimization. The highly optimized CUDA implementation of 3DGS ensures very fast rendering speeds (e.g., 850 FPS), which translates directly to fast training.

**Physically-Based Priors (Regularization Losses)**:
To ensure stable and physically plausible long-term tracking, especially in texture-less regions, the authors introduce three regularization losses:
1. **Short-term Local Rigidity (`L_rigid`)**: This is the most crucial prior. For a Gaussian `i` and its k-nearest neighbors `j`, this loss enforces that the relative position of `j` with respect to `i` in timestep `t` follows the rigid-body transformation (translation and rotation) of `i` between `t-1` and `t`. This ensures that local neighborhoods of Gaussians move as approximately rigid bodies, preventing unnatural deformation or drifting. It is weighted by the initial distance between Gaussians, making it strictly local.
2. **Short-term Local Rotational Similarity (`L_rot`)**: This loss explicitly encourages neighboring Gaussians to have similar rotational changes between `t-1` and `t`. While implicitly covered by `L_rigid`, explicit enforcement improves convergence and accuracy of 6-DOF tracking.
3. **Long-term Local Isometry (`L_iso`)**: Applied over a longer time horizon, this weaker constraint ensures that the *distances* between neighboring Gaussians remain approximately constant over time, even if `L_rigid` might be slightly relaxed. This helps prevent large-scale drifting or collapse of structure over many timesteps.

**Optimization Details**:
- **Online Optimization**: The scene is reconstructed timestep by timestep.
- **Initialization**: The first timestep's Gaussians are initialized using a coarse point cloud (e.g., from depth cameras or COLMAP), and all parameters are optimized.
- **Subsequent Timesteps**: For all subsequent timesteps, the size, color, opacity, and background logits of Gaussians are *fixed*. Only their positions and rotations are optimized. This enforces the persistence.
- **Forward Propagation**: Gaussians for a new timestep are initialized by linearly extrapolating their motion from the previous two timesteps (velocity-based forward estimate), which is crucial for good results. Adam optimizer momentum parameters are reset.
- **Densification**: The Gaussian densification strategy from 3DGS (adding/splitting/cloning Gaussians) is only applied in the first timestep to achieve high-quality initial reconstruction. For subsequent dynamic frames, the number of Gaussians is fixed.
- **Background Segmentation Loss (`L_Bg`)**: To prevent foreground objects from being confused with the background, a pseudo-ground-truth background mask is used to apply a loss that discourages background Gaussians from moving or rotating, and restricts rigidity/rotation/isometry losses to foreground elements.
- **Camera Calibration**: Per-camera scale and offset parameters for color channels are optimized in the first timestep to account for variations in camera properties, then fixed.

**Tracking Extraction**: Once the Dynamic 3D Gaussian scene representation is trained, dense 6-DOF tracking becomes straightforward. Any 3D point's motion is determined by the Gaussian with the most influence over it. This provides a well-defined, invertible mapping of any 3D point (and its associated rotation) across all timesteps. 2D pixel tracking is then achieved by unprojecting a pixel to 3D, tracking the 3D point, and re-projecting it into the target camera frame.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling quantitative and qualitative results, demonstrating significant advancements in dynamic novel-view synthesis and, particularly, dense 6-DOF tracking.

**Quantitative Results (PanopticSports Dataset)**:
The authors introduce and evaluate their method on the "PanopticSports" dataset, derived from the CMU Panoptic Studio, featuring complex human motions across six sequences. They use 27 training and 4 testing cameras for 150 frames.

- **Novel-View Synthesis**:
 - The method achieves a respectable PSNR of 28.7, SSIM of 0.91, and LPIPS of 0.17 on hold-out test views.
 - Compared to a baseline of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3GS-O) run in an online mode, their dynamic approach consistently shows better PSNR and SSIM, indicating that correctly modeling temporal consistency improves rendering quality for dynamic scenes.
- **3D Long-Term Point Tracking**:
 - **Outstanding Performance**: The method achieves a remarkably low median trajectory error (MTE) of **2.21 cm** over 150 timesteps (5 seconds of video), demonstrating highly accurate metric 3D tracking.
 - **Robustness**: It boasts a **100% survival rate**, meaning it never loses the point it's tracking, and a high position accuracy (δ) of 71.4 (percentage of trajectories within specific error thresholds).
 - In stark contrast, 3GS-O (without dynamic modeling) performs poorly, with an MTE of 55.9 cm, highlighting the critical role of the proposed dynamic modeling and priors.
- **2D Long-Term Point Tracking**:
 - **Superior Accuracy**: The method achieves an average median trajectory error (MTE) of only **1.57 pixels**, which is an order of magnitude (approx. 10x) lower than the state-of-the-art 2D long-term tracker, PIPs (15.7 pixels MTE), even though PIPs is specifically trained for this task on large datasets while "Dynamic 3D Gaussians" is not.
 - It also achieves a 100% survival rate (compared to 79% for PIPs) and higher accuracy (78.4 vs. 39.6 for PIPs). This is a remarkable finding, as tracking emerges without any explicit correspondence input or pre-training on tracking datasets.

**Quantitative Results (Particle-NeRF Dataset)**:
On the simpler, synthetic Particle-NeRF dataset, the method achieves near-perfect scores (PSNR 39.49, SSIM 0.99, LPIPS 0.02), significantly outperforming other dynamic NeRF-based methods like Particle-NeRF, InstantNGP, and TiNeuVox, demonstrating its efficiency and accuracy even for simpler scenes.

**Efficiency and Speed**:
- **Fast Training**: The method trains on 150 timesteps with 27 training cameras in just 2 hours on a single RTX 3090 GPU (4 minutes for the first frame, 50 seconds for subsequent frames). This is significantly faster than many dynamic NeRF approaches.
- **Real-time Rendering**: It renders novel views at 850 FPS, matching the exceptional speed of static 3D Gaussian Splatting, making it highly suitable for real-time applications.

**Ablation Study**:
The ablation study on the Juggle scene confirmed the importance of key components:
- `L_rigid` (local rigidity loss), `L_Bg` (background segmentation loss), parameter fixing (color/opacity/size), and forward propagation for initialization are crucial for both good tracking and improved view synthesis. Removing any of these leads to significant degradation.
- `L_rot` (local rotation similarity) and `L_iso` (long-term local isometry) provide smaller quantitative gains but contribute to visually more coherent and correct reconstructions.

**Qualitative Results and Applications**:
Beyond quantitative metrics, the paper demonstrates several compelling qualitative results and applications (Figure 1, 7):
- **Persistent Dynamic View Synthesis**: Visualizations show accurate novel-view renders of color images and depth maps across dynamic scenes, with Gaussians moving and rotating consistently.
- **Relative Rotation Tracking (6-DOF)**: Vectors attached to Gaussians clearly show they correctly model the 6-DOF motion, including rotation, of dense scene points (Figure 3).
- **Scene Editing**: The Gaussian representation enables intuitive scene editing, such as:
 - Removing, duplicating, or combining dynamic objects from different scenes.
 - Propagating image edits (e.g., adding a logo) across all timesteps consistently.
 - Attaching cameras to dynamic Gaussians for "first-person" views (e.g., following a juggling ball).
 - Scanning and integrating new objects into dynamic scenes, allowing them to follow scene elements with correct 6-DOF motion (e.g., a hat staying on a person during a handstand).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Dynamic 3D Gaussians" marks a significant advancement in the field of 4D scene understanding and representation, with far-reaching implications across various domains.

**Unified Framework for Reconstruction and Tracking**: The most significant contribution is the successful integration of high-fidelity novel-view synthesis with accurate, dense, and long-term 6-DOF tracking within a single, efficient analysis-by-synthesis framework. Crucially, tracking emerges *naturally* from the persistent dynamic view synthesis, eliminating the need for separate, potentially noisy, correspondence inputs (like optical flow) or extensive pre-training on tracking datasets. This "tracking-by-synthesis" paradigm is a powerful conceptual leap.

**Practical Efficiency and Scalability**: Inheriting the speed of 3D Gaussian Splatting, this method offers real-time rendering and fast training times. This performance makes it highly practical for applications that demand interactivity and rapid processing, which has been a major bottleneck for many radiance-field-based dynamic scene methods.

**High-Fidelity 6-DOF Tracking**: Achieving sub-centimeter accuracy in 3D tracking and sub-pixel accuracy in 2D tracking for complex non-rigid motions over extended periods is a groundbreaking result. The ability to track both position and rotation (6-DOF) for every dense point in space provides a level of detail and physical understanding previously unattainable at this scale and speed.

**Transformative Applications**:
- **Robotics**: Provides a precise metric-space understanding of dynamic environments, crucial for robot navigation, manipulation, and interaction with moving objects.
- **Augmented Reality (AR) / Virtual Reality (VR)**: Enables seamless integration of virtual content into real-world dynamic scenes, with virtual objects correctly interacting with and occluding real ones, and cameras able to follow scene elements.
- **Self-Driving Cars**: Offers a richer understanding of moving pedestrians, vehicles, and other dynamic scene elements for improved prediction and decision-making.
- **Content Creation and Digital Twins**: Opens new avenues for creating, editing, and manipulating dynamic 3D assets for movies, video games, visual effects, and the metaverse. The demonstrated editing capabilities (removing/adding objects, propagating edits, dynamic camera movements) are highly desirable for creative industries.
- **Human-Computer Interaction**: Accurate tracking of human motion (including fine-grained hand/facial movements) can lead to more immersive and natural interactions.

**Limitations and Future Directions**:
The paper openly acknowledges limitations that also serve as exciting avenues for future research:
- **Dependence on Initial Frame Visibility**: The method can only track parts of the scene visible in the initial frame, unable to reconstruct or track objects that enter the scene later. This points towards future work on online learning or dynamic topology changes.
- **Multi-camera Requirement**: The current approach relies on a multi-camera setup. Adapting it to monocular video input, while significantly more challenging due to inherent ambiguities, would greatly expand its applicability.
- **Sensitivity to Background Segmentation**: The background segmentation loss is crucial for accurate tracking, particularly in challenging scenarios like similar foreground/background colors. Improving robustness to less precise background masks could be a future area.

In conclusion, "Dynamic 3D Gaussians" represents a significant leap forward in dynamic 3D scene understanding. By elegantly extending 3D Gaussian Splatting and incorporating physically-based priors, it delivers an unprecedented combination of photorealistic rendering, accurate 6-DOF dense tracking, and real-time performance. Its implications are broad, promising to accelerate advancements in robotics, AR/VR, and content creation, while also laying a strong foundation for future research into more robust and generalizable dynamic scene representations.
