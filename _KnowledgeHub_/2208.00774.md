# Interaction Mix and Match: Synthesizing Close Interaction using Conditional Hierarchical GAN with Multi-Hot Class Embedding

> [!summary] Summary
> Interaction Mix and Match synthesizes a follower's reactive motion from a leader's input motion using a conditional hierarchical GAN: a body-part-wise attentive Bi-LSTM seq2seq generator conditioned on a multi-hot class embedding, adversarially trained by a multi-class discriminator with a fake class. Setting labels positive, neutral or negative blends or suppresses interaction types, cutting AFD and FID below prior state of the art.

## Problem

- Prior GAN-based reactive synthesis produced a single fixed reactive pattern per input, since no label guided generation, so resulting interactions lacked variety and could not mix reactions.
- Algorithmic close-interaction methods like interaction meshes or motion graphs need hand-designed representations and parameter tuning, making large-scale generation of varied two-character interactions inefficient.
- Action-conditioned generative models synthesize single-character or multi-person motion from one-hot labels, but do not react to a given partner motion nor support multi-class label control.

## Method

- **Multi-Hot Class Embedding**: interaction labels take +1, 0 or -1 (extendable to floats), concatenated only with the whole-body slice, letting inference blend or negate reaction classes unseen in training.
- Conditional hierarchical generator: five body-part encoders plus a label-concatenated whole-body encoder, each a ==Bi-LSTM==, feeding a Bi-LSTM decoder through frame-level seq2seq ==attention== that weights informative leader frames.
- Two-layer Bi-LSTM multi-class ==discriminator== sees only the follower motion and adds an (N+1)th fidelity class, replacing the separate binary discriminator; training combines ==adversarial==, L1, bone and continuity losses (weight 1).

## Results

- On SBU, lowest Average Frame Distance in all six classes (**0.32-0.50**) versus **0.44-0.72** for the prior GAN baseline, with a tighter spread across classes.
- FID drops in every class: SBU hug **29.5** to **19.3**, shake hands **23.8** to **15.1**; 2C kick **194.2** to **164.2**, punch **148.1** to **122.4**.
- Ablations show removing the discriminator, continuity loss, fully-connected layer or multi-hot embedding all worsen 2C FID (**164.2** to **174-180**); augmenting SBU raised accuracy in five of six classes, exchange-object by **21.4** points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training on one-hot labels but querying multi-hot at inference gives an extrapolative latent space: unseen hybrid behaviours emerge without ever collecting mixed-interaction data.
> - Dropping the binary real/fake discriminator and judging only the generated partner's motion reduces mode collapse; a fidelity class inside the multi-class head recovers the lost realism signal.
> - Loss terms should match capture quality: bone-length loss hurts reconstruction metrics on noisy depth data but matters for rigid-body animation; neither loss handles interpenetration or foot sliding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Character animation plays a role in various applications, including computer games, CGI/3D anime movies, and virtual reality. Traditionally, character animation has been a labor-intensive process requiring significant manual intervention from animators. Advances in motion acquisition and deep learning techniques have semi-automated parts of the animation pipeline, particularly for single-character or character-object interactions. However, the synthesis of multi-character interactions, especially close interactions such such as dancing, hugging, or fighting, presents greater challenges. These challenges include the requirement for precise spatiotemporal alignment to avoid interpenetration of body parts, while simultaneously preserving the contextual meaning of the interaction.

Existing approaches to multi-character interaction synthesis have explored several avenues. Algorithmic methods combine individually captured motions and edit interactions based on additional constraints, often requiring careful design of algorithms and parameter tuning. Data-driven methods have leveraged datasets of interactions to synthesize reactive motions, for instance, in virtual reality applications like dancing or sword fighting. While these methods can generate reactive motions, they frequently produce motions highly similar to pre-recorded data, resulting in a limited variety of synthesized interactions. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been used to model correlations between character motions for prediction and recognition tasks, but they typically offer low controllability for animation synthesis. Other works, such as music-driven approaches, are often domain-specific (e.g., for dancing) and not universally applicable to different leader-follower interaction types.

A directly relevant work is the GAN-based interaction synthesis framework proposed by Men et al., which generates reactive motion for a follower character based on a leader's input motion. This framework employs a sequence-to-sequence reactive motion generator and utilizes binary and multi-class discriminators. However, this model lacks label-guided control, meaning it can only produce fixed reactive patterns learned from the input data, thereby limiting the variety and mixture of reactions it can create.

This paper addresses the limitations in variety and controllability by introducing a novel method for generating diverse and customizable close interactions. It aims to overcome the fixed reactive patterns of previous GAN-based approaches by integrating a multi-hot class embedding, allowing for the synthesis of interaction types not explicitly present in the training data and providing a higher degree of control over the reactive styles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent complexities and limitations in existing methods for synthesizing multi-character interactions, particularly close interactions. These interactions, such as dancing or fighting, demand precise spatiotemporal alignment and coordination between characters. Current approaches face challenges in generating diverse and varied reactive motions, often producing results that closely mimic pre-recorded data without offering sufficient creative control to animators.

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

1. **To develop a novel method for generating realistic human reactive motions that are not directly presented in existing datasets.** This is achieved by enabling the "mixing and matching" of different types of close interactions. The goal is to move beyond mere reproduction of observed motions to synthesize new and combined reactive styles.

2. **To propose a Conditional Hierarchical Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) with Multi-Hot Class Embedding (CHGAN-MHE).** This architectural choice is intended to facilitate the generation of reactive motions for a follower character given the motion sequence of a leader, while incorporating explicit control over the interaction type.

3. **To enable "Interaction Mix" through the multi-hot class embedding.** This objective aims to allow users to synthesize reactive motions that combine characteristics from multiple interaction types. By modifying the multi-hot class embedding, the model can generate hybrid interaction behaviors that might not exist as distinct categories in the training data, thereby increasing the diversity of synthesized animations. For example, a reaction might simultaneously exhibit elements of "shaking hands" and "hugging."

4. **To enable "Interaction Match" by adjusting the numeric scale of the label indicator.** This objective focuses on generating a single, specific type of reactive motion but with variations in its intensity or style. By manipulating the scale of the label, the trained generator can produce different levels of reactive variations within a given interaction type, enhancing the controllability for animators.

5. **To enhance the quality and controllability of synthesized reactive motions.** The research aims to achieve higher quality and more flexible reactive patterns compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly those that do not incorporate explicit label information for diverse reactions. This includes producing motions with better resemblance to original data and clearer classification boundaries in the latent space.

6. **To provide an augmented dataset with realistic reactive motions.** By making the synthesized data publicly available, the authors aim to stimulate further research and development in the challenging domain of multi-character interaction synthesis, potentially by improving the robustness of interaction recognition models.

In summary, the core motivation is to provide animators and researchers with a powerful and flexible tool for generating a wider spectrum of realistic and controllable multi-character interactions, moving beyond the constraints of strictly data-driven reproduction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves an end-to-end framework named Conditional Hierarchical Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) with Multi-Hot Class Embedding (CHGAN-MHE). This framework is designed to synthesize stylized reactive motion, informed by a multi-hot action labeling, given an input action sequence from a leader character.

#### 3.1. Class Embedding

The system introduces a multi-hot class indicator to control the pattern of generated reactive motions. Unlike traditional one-hot encoding, which specifies a single class, multi-hot encoding allows for softer constraints and the blending of multiple interaction styles. During inference, a label can be a positive indicator (1) for a desired reaction, a neutral indicator (0), or a negative indicator (-1) to avoid a specific reaction. This allows for both "Interaction Mix" (combining characteristics of different styles by using multiple positive labels) and "Interaction Match" (generating a specific reaction with varying degrees by adjusting a single label's magnitude, potentially using floating-point numbers). During training, standard one-hot encoding is utilized. The label embedding is concatenated with the whole body skeleton representation, rather than individual body parts, to prevent overfitting.

#### 3.2. Generator Backbone

The generator (G) employs an attentive Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)-based sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) architecture. This design is chosen to model temporal dynamics and generate realistic reactive motions.
- **Hierarchical Encoder:** The input motion of character A is first processed by a hierarchical encoder that models the spatial features of the skeleton. It segments the body into five distinct parts (e.g., arms, legs) and also considers the whole body, resulting in six body structures. Each of these structures, including the label-concatenated whole body skeleton, is initially encoded by a fully-connected layer to capture structural features.
- **Bi-LSTM Encoder:** A one-layer bidirectional LSTM (Bi-LSTM) processes each of the six body structure embeddings to capture temporal dynamics in both forward and backward directions. The hidden states from these six Bi-LSTMs are then concatenated.
- **Attentive Bi-LSTM Decoder:** The concatenated hidden state from the encoder is fed into a single-layer bidirectional LSTM decoder. A frame-level attention mechanism is integrated to enhance the connection between the encoder and decoder. This mechanism allows the model to identify the most informative frames from the input motion and assign corresponding importance to the generated reactive motion frames, ensuring prompt and contextually relevant reactions. The decoder includes a fully-connected layer at its output to reconstruct the reactive pose for each frame.

#### 3.3. Multi-class Discriminator

A multi-class discriminator (D) is designed to evaluate the quality and class membership of the synthesized reactive motion (`x_B_hat`). Its purpose is to increase the diversity of reactive patterns and improve the quality of the generated output.
- **Architecture:** The discriminator consists of two-layer bidirectional LSTMs, which are effective in extracting high-level semantic features from sequential data for classification.
- **Classification:** Instead of classifying both input and reactive motions, the discriminator solely focuses on the synthesized reactive motion of character B. This approach reduces dependency on features extracted from character A's motion, aiming to mitigate mode collapse by encouraging a wider variety of generated reactive patterns.
The discriminator classifies the input reactive motion into `n` real interaction classes (e.g., "kick," "punch") and an additional `n+1`-th "fidelity" class, which acts as a "fake" label. This `n+1`-th class compensates for the functionality typically provided by a binary discriminator, where it determines if a sample is real or fake. The output is a probability distribution over these `n+1` classes via a softmax activation function after a fully-connected layer.

#### 3.4. Loss Function

The model is optimized using a min-max adversarial training scheme. The overall loss function (`L_total`) comprises several components:
- **`L_CGAN` (Conditional GAN Loss):** This adversarial loss encourages the generator to produce realistic motions that are classified correctly by the discriminator into their intended interaction class (real classes `y < N+1`) while also fooling the discriminator into not classifying them as fake (`y = N+1`). Simultaneously, the discriminator is trained to correctly classify real motions as their true class (`y < N+1`) and synthesized motions as either their specified class or fake.
- **`L_1` (Reconstruction Loss):** An L1 norm is used to minimize the geometric difference between the synthesized reactive motion (`x_B_hat`) and the ground truth reactive motion (`x_B`), aiming for intensity similarity.
- **`L_c` (Continuity Loss):** This loss term, derived from prior work, is designed to preserve motion consistency and smoothness in the generated trajectories. A higher weight (λc = 1) is assigned to this loss compared to some previous methods (e.g., used 0.01) to prioritize smoother joint trajectories.
- **`L_b` (Bone Loss):** Also adopted from previous work, this loss helps maintain the natural bone lengths throughout the animation, ensuring anatomical plausibility. A weight of `λb = 0.01` is applied.

#### 3.5. Datasets and Experimental Settings

- **SBU Two-person Interaction Dataset:** This dataset contains 3D skeletal motions captured using Microsoft Kinect (depth-based), featuring 7 subjects and 197 sequences across 8 interaction classes. Six classes (pushing, kicking, punching, exchanging objects, hugging, shaking hands) were used, with approaching and departing interactions excluded due to minimal reactive motion. Each character is represented by 15 joints. This dataset is noted for its potential noise.
- **Character-Character (2C) Dataset:** This dataset provides high-quality kickboxing motions captured with an optical MoCap system. It includes 44 sequences for two classes: kicking and punching, with diverse reactive patterns. Each character is represented by 20 joints using 3D angular values, which were converted to joint positions for this work.
- **Data Processing:** For both datasets, 3D joint positions were made relative to character A's root joint, and global root translation and rotation (about the y-axis) were removed for standardization. Leave-one-subject-out cross-validation was used for the SBU dataset, while a 3:1 train:test ratio was applied to the 2C dataset.
- **Implementation:** The framework was built on Keras, utilizing RMSprop optimizer with a learning rate of 0.01. Specific LSTM neuron configurations were used for SBU and 2C datasets, with a batch size of 16. Training involved 1600 epochs for SBU and 2000 epochs for 2C.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments to evaluate the proposed CHGAN-MHE framework, comparing it against existing methods and analyzing its components.

#### 4.1. Motion Synthesis Quality

##### 4.1.1. Quantitative Analysis

- **Average Frame Distance (AFD):** This metric measures the geometric similarity between synthesized and ground truth skeletal motions. The proposed method consistently achieved the lowest AFD across all six interaction classes in the SBU dataset (Kick, Push, Shake Hand, Hug, Ex. Obj., Punch) compared to traditional machine learning methods (NN, HMM, MDP, kernel-RL, ME-IOC) and the deep learning approach by Men et al.. The AFD values for the proposed method ranged from 0.32 to 0.50, indicating a consistent performance across different classes, whereas the state-of-the-art method exhibited a larger range (0.44 to 0.72).
- **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID):** FID assesses the difference in distribution between synthesized and original motions. The proposed method yielded lower FID values than Men et al. for all SBU interaction classes (Table 2) and both Kick and Punch classes in the 2C dataset (Table 3). This indicates that the motions synthesized by the proposed method more closely resemble the quality and distribution of the original motion data across different datasets.

##### 4.1.2. Qualitative Analysis

- **Interaction Match:** Visual evaluation (as demonstrated in accompanying video demos) confirmed that the synthesized reactive motions closely resembled the ground truth interactions while introducing natural variations. This capability was demonstrated across both the noisy SBU dataset (Figure 3) and the high-quality 2C dataset (Figure 4), showcasing the method's generality. The contextual meaning of interactions was preserved in the synthesized outputs.
- **Interaction Mix:** The multi-hot embedding enabled the generation of hybrid reactive patterns. For example, the model could synthesize a character reacting with elements of "shaking hands" and "hugging" simultaneously, or "shaking hands" while "avoiding being pushed" (Figure 5). This demonstrated the method's ability to create novel interaction types not explicitly present in the training data by blending reactive styles.

#### 4.2. Analysis of the Latent Space

The t-SNE visualization of the learned embedding space highlighted a structured and interpretable latent representation. For the proposed method (Figure 6), most interaction classes formed distinct clusters with low inter-class similarity. Four out of six classes (Exchange Objects, Shake Hands, Hug, Kick) showed no overlap. While Punch and Push classes exhibited some overlap, reflecting their similar skeletal motions, the Push samples largely remained separate. This structure indicates that the latent space effectively captures both the distinctiveness and semantic similarities between different interaction types. In contrast, the t-SNE visualization for the method by Men et al. (Figure 7) showed samples from different interaction types mixed together, suggesting a less coherent latent space. The clear separation and meaningful relationships in the proposed method's latent space are considered beneficial for informed "Interaction Mix" and extrapolation of interactions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted to evaluate the contribution of individual components of the proposed framework:
- **SBU Dataset (AFD, Table 4):** On this dataset, the model without the bone loss (`L_b`) achieved the lowest AFD across five of the six interaction classes. The full proposed framework was lowest for the Punch class and second lowest for the rest. This finding suggests that for noisy datasets like SBU (captured with Kinect), where bone lengths may vary due to sensor inaccuracies, enforcing strict bone length consistency (`L_b`) may not always be beneficial for geometric similarity (AFD).
- **2C Dataset (FID, Table 5):** On the high-quality 2C dataset, the full proposed model with all loss terms (including `L_b` and `L_c`) demonstrated the best motion quality with the lowest FID. This indicates that for accurate MoCap data with stable bone lengths, the bone loss effectively contributes to overall motion quality. The continuity loss (`L_c`) and the Multi-Hot Embedding were also shown to be significant contributors, particularly for distinguishing similar interaction types.

#### 4.4. Data Augmentation for Interaction Recognition

The synthesized motions were also evaluated for their utility in data augmentation. A new dataset of 300 synthesized interactions (50 per class) was generated from the SBU data. When an interaction classifier (structured like the multi-class discriminator) was trained and tested on an augmented SBU dataset (original + synthesized data), it showed an improvement in classification accuracy. Specifically, 5 out of 6 classes experienced an increase in accuracy, ranging from 1.33% to 21.39% compared to training only on the original dataset (Table 6). This result suggests that the synthesized interactions are realistic and diverse enough to positively impact the robustness of interaction recognition models, providing a valuable resource for training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for synthesizing two-character interactions that addresses existing limitations in diversity and controllability, holding several implications for character animation and related fields.

The **primary significance** lies in the introduction of a Conditional Hierarchical GAN with Multi-Hot Class Embedding, which enables a greater degree of control over synthesized multi-character interactions. By using multi-hot class embeddings, the framework facilitates two key capabilities:
1. **Interaction Mix:** This allows for the generation of novel interaction types that combine characteristics of different styles, moving beyond the direct reproduction of observed data. This extrapolative capability can enrich the variety of animations available.
2. **Interaction Match:** The method also allows for the synthesis of diverse variations within a single interaction type, offering animators flexibility in fine-tuning reactive motions.

The **improved performance** of the proposed method over state-of-the-art approaches, demonstrated through quantitative metrics like Average Frame Distance (AFD) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), indicates that it generates motions that more closely resemble original data distributions and exhibit higher geometric fidelity. Qualitatively, the synthesized motions are described as more natural and adaptable across different reactive styles.

The **structured latent space** learned by the model is another notable aspect. The t-SNE visualizations illustrate that the latent space organizes interaction types into distinct clusters, reflecting their semantic relationships. This clear representation can assist animators in understanding similarities between interaction types and in intelligently combining them for Interaction Mix scenarios.

Furthermore, the research demonstrates a **practical application in data augmentation**. The synthesized interactions were shown to improve the robustness of interaction recognition models by increasing classification accuracy across most interaction classes. This suggests that the generated data is realistic and diverse enough to serve as a valuable resource for training machine learning models, particularly in domains where real-world interaction data might be sparse or costly to acquire.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Computer Animation and Entertainment:** The framework offers animators a more efficient and controllable method for creating diverse multi-character scenes in computer games, CGI/3D anime movies, and virtual reality environments, potentially reducing the manual effort involved.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability to generate varied and realistic human-like reactive motions could inform the development of more natural and responsive robot behaviors in interactive tasks.
- **Human Behavior Modeling:** The framework contributes to a deeper understanding of human interaction dynamics, which could be relevant for social science simulations or training applications.
- **Future Research Stimulation:** By releasing the augmented dataset, the authors aim to foster further research in multi-character interaction synthesis, providing a benchmark and a foundation for subsequent investigations.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper also acknowledges several limitations. The current method does not explicitly handle collisions, which can lead to artefacts such as interpenetration of body parts when mixing very dissimilar interaction types (e.g., kicking and hugging). Future work could integrate collision avoidance techniques (e.g., Interaction Mesh, Aura Mesh, or post-processing methods). Additionally, foot sliding artefacts were observed in the synthesized motions due to the absence of an explicit loss term for stepping patterns, a common issue in GAN-based motion synthesis. Incorporating contact consistency losses or additional post-processing steps could address this.

Future research directions include further exploring the learned latent space, particularly for interpolation and extrapolation, and developing a topology-aware latent space to inherently avoid interpenetration. Investigating classifier-generated multi-hot class embeddings and extending the multi-hot concept to single-body motion synthesis are also proposed.
