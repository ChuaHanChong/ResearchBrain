# CogCoM: A Visual Language Model with Chain-of-Manipulations Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> CogCoM, a visual language model developed by Tsinghua University and Zhipu AI, introduces Chain of Manipulations (CoM) to enable intrinsic visual operations for multi-step reasoning, leading to improved performance on detailed visual question answering and visual grounding benchmarks while enhancing interpretability.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often provide "conclusive responses" without revealing essential intermediate visual reasoning steps.
- This limitation causes VLMs to struggle with tasks requiring subtle visual details or multi-step analysis, leading to unfaithful responses and hallucinations.
- The black-box nature of current VLM outputs hinders interpretability and error diagnosis.

## Method

- Introduces ==Chain of Manipulations (CoM)==, a sequence of intrinsic visual operations (e.g., OCR, Grounding, ==CropZoomIn==) designed to mimic human cognitive problem-solving.
- Develops an automated data generation pipeline that leverages large language models and visual foundational models to produce **70K** CoM reasoning chains for training.
- Extends the ==CogVLM architecture== with a ==memory-based system== to handle multi-turn interactions and integrate sequentially processed original and manipulated images.

## Results

- CogCoM achieves state-of-the-art performance on detailed Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmarks, including up to a **9.0 percentage point** improvement on TextVQA over its CogVLM baseline.
- It demonstrates superior visual grounding capabilities, achieving top performance across RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg datasets, comparable to specialist models.
- The model improves general multimodal abilities on MM-Vet and shows enhanced performance in reducing hallucinations on POPE, while ablation studies confirm the benefits of Chain of Manipulations training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling visual manipulations allows Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to perform step-by-step, evidential reasoning, mimicking human cognitive processes.
> - Training an end-to-end VLM to intrinsically perform visual manipulations, rather than relying on external tools, enables deeper integration of visual reasoning.
> - An automated data generation pipeline, combining large language models for linguistic annotation and visual foundational models for visual annotation, can efficiently create high-quality, complex multimodal reasoning data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, largely driven by extensive training on vast image-text datasets, enabling capabilities ranging from visual question answering (VQA) to visual grounding and image captioning. However, a significant limitation of many existing VLMs is their tendency to produce "conclusive responses" without revealing the underlying "essential intermediate visual reasoning." This often leads to unfaithful responses, hallucinations, and failures in handling tasks that demand subtle visual details or multi-step analysis.

This paper addresses this critical gap by introducing **Chain of Manipulations (CoM)**, drawing inspiration from human cognitive processes. Just as humans might mark, zoom in, or make auxiliary drawings to solve complex visual problems, CoM enables VLMs to perform a series of intrinsic visual operations (manipulations) to arrive at a faithful and explainable answer. This approach is conceptually similar to "Chain of Thought" (CoT) reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs), but adapted for the visual domain. While CoT helps LLMs break down complex linguistic problems into intermediate steps, CoM extends this idea to incorporate explicit visual interactions, generating evidential steps that can be traced.

The work builds upon a lineage of multimodal research, especially models like CogVLM, which established strong foundational visual understanding and grounded generation capabilities. Unlike approaches that rely on external tools (e.g., ViperGPT, VisProg) or simply list visual objects (V*), CogCoM focuses on training an end-to-end VLM to *intrinsically* perform these manipulations. This distinction is crucial: instead of calling external APIs, CogCoM *learns* to elicit and apply visual manipulations as part of its internal reasoning process.

The paper also positions itself among other efforts to enhance VLM reasoning, such as:
- **Grounded reasoning:** KOSMOS-2 and Shikra aim to improve VLMs' ability to localize and ground entities. CogCoM integrates grounding as a core manipulation within a multi-step reasoning process.
- **Understanding artificial visual scenes:** CogAgent and CHARTVE focus on specialized domains like GUI interfaces and charts. CogCoM’s human-annotated graphical mathematical problems dataset (MathVista, ChartQA) aligns with this, extending VLM capabilities to complex data visualizations.
- **Hallucination mitigation:** Works like Hallucidoctor and Woodpecker explicitly address VLM hallucination. CogCoM contributes to this by providing transparent, evidence-based reasoning, making errors traceable and responses more faithful.
- **Detailed recognition:** V* and DualFocus also seek to enhance detail recognition. CogCoM's multi-turn, multi-image architecture, combined with operations like `CropZoomIn`, directly addresses the need for fine-grained analysis.

By explicitly introducing and training VLMs with a "Chain of Manipulations," CogCoM represents a significant step towards more robust, interpretable, and human-like visual reasoning in generalist multimodal models, moving beyond mere "conclusive alignments" to "evidential problem-solving."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing CogCoM stems from a recognized limitation in existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs). While these models excel at aligning visual inputs with language responses through extensive training, this "conclusive alignment" often bypasses essential intermediate visual reasoning steps. This deficiency leads to several critical failures:
- **Inability to handle detailed visual tasks:** VLMs struggle with questions requiring subtle observations, fine-grained recognition (e.g., small text, specific patterns), or multi-step visual analysis.
- **Unfaithful responses and hallucinations:** Without explicit reasoning steps, models may rely on statistical priors or linguistic biases, leading to incorrect or fabricated answers (hallucinations), as exemplified by CogVLM incorrectly identifying text on a pillar due to prior knowledge rather than direct observation.
- **Lack of interpretability:** The black-box nature of current VLM outputs makes it difficult for users to understand *why* a particular answer was given, hindering error diagnosis and trust.

Inspired by human cognitive problem-solving, where individuals often interact with visual information (e.g., marking, zooming in, drawing auxiliary lines) to simplify and precisely solve problems, the authors propose to imbue VLMs with similar "manipulation" capabilities. Many VLMs already possess intrinsic visual understanding abilities (like grounding and text recognition) from their pre-training. The core idea is to further train them to *actively and intrinsically* use these capabilities in a step-by-step, evidential manner.

The key objectives of this research are multi-faceted:
1. **Develop a flexible design of manipulations:** To comprehensively cover a wide array of visual problems, ranging from detailed recognition and counting to geometric reasoning. This involves identifying atomic manipulations that can be combined for complex tasks.
2. **Create an efficient automated data generation pipeline:** To overcome the bottleneck of manual annotation, the pipeline must leverage existing large language models (LLMs) and visual foundational models (VFMs) to automatically generate abundant, high-quality training data that incorporates Chain of Manipulations reasoning.
3. **Design a compatible VLM architecture:** The model needs to support multi-turn interactions and multi-image inputs, allowing for sequential processing of original and manipulated images, and integrating seamlessly with existing VLM frameworks.
4. **Establish a robust training process:** To effectively endow the VLM with versatile capabilities (chat, captioning, grounding, and CoM reasoning) by incorporating the newly generated data alongside existing multimodal corpora.
5. **Annotate high-quality challenging samples:** Specifically for graphical mathematical problems, to push the boundaries of VLM reasoning in a domain requiring precise visual and logical steps.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to advance VLMs beyond superficial understanding, enabling them to engage in deeper, more reliable, and interpretable visual reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology behind CogCoM is comprehensive, encompassing manipulation design, data generation, model architecture, and training protocols.

#### 3.1. Manipulation Design

The authors first conducted pilot experiments using GPT-4 on TextVQA to understand common human-like actions for solving visual problems. This analysis led to the formal definition of 6 atomic manipulations:
- `OCR(tgt) -> txt`: Optical Character Recognition, to extract text from a target region.
- `Grounding(tgt) -> bbx`: To locate objects described by a target phrase, returning bounding boxes.
- `Counting(tgt) -> num`: To count objects specified by a target, returning a number.
- `Calculate(tgt) -> num`: To perform calculations based on visual information, returning a number.
- `CropZoomIn(bbx, x) -> img`: To crop a region defined by bounding box `bbx` and zoom in by `x` times, returning a new image.
- `Line(pts) -> img`: To draw lines on the image based on specified points, returning a modified image.
These manipulations are designed to be intrinsic, meaning the VLM learns to perform them internally rather than calling external tools during inference. The framework also allows for the creation of new manipulations if needed.

The concept of a "Chain of Manipulations (CoM)" is defined as a sequence of steps, `ς = (step_1, step_2...)`, where each `step_i = (f_i, c_i)` consists of a manipulation `f_i` (or `None` if no manipulation is needed) and a free-form textual description `c_i` incorporating the manipulation and its results. This structure provides explicit, evidential reasoning paths.

#### 3.2. Data Collection

The paper leverages two main approaches for data collection:

- **Automated Data Generation (70K samples):**
 - **Linguistic Annotation:** Given an image-question-answer (I, Q, A) triplet from existing VQA datasets (TextVQA, ST-VQA, TDIUC), a large language model (GPT-4) acts as a "linguistic annotator." It is prompted with guidelines and few-shot demonstrations to generate step-by-step solving procedures in the CoM format. Crucially, the manipulation results (e.g., bounding box coordinates, recognized text) are left as symbolic placeholders.
 - **Visual Annotation:** Visual Foundational Models (VFMs)—specifically, GroundingDINO for grounding and PaddleOCR for OCR—serve as "visual annotators." They execute the manipulations requested by the linguistic annotator, filling in the actual results (e.g., `[[x0,y0,x1,y1]]` for `bbx`).
 - **Path Traversal:** Since a manipulation might yield multiple results, leading to a tree-like structure of reasoning paths, a Depth First Search (DFS) is performed. This traversal identifies all "positive paths" that correctly terminate at the ground truth answer `A`, ensuring the quality and correctness of the generated CoM samples. This pipeline is used to generate 70K CoM samples requiring detailed recognition and counting.

- **Human Annotation (7K samples):**
 - To address more challenging tasks like graphical mathematical problems (from MathVista and ChartQA), 10 human experts manually annotate high-quality CoM samples. These experts provide both linguistic solving steps and the precise results of manipulations on the images, including drawing auxiliary lines.

- **Data Transformation:** All CoM samples are converted into a multi-turn VQA format, `[(I0, Q, C0), (I1, Q_bar, C1)..., (In, Q_bar, A)]`. This means each step involving a new image or intermediate result becomes a new turn in the conversation, where `Q_bar` is a generic prompt asking the model to continue reasoning based on the history.

#### 3.3. Model Architecture

CogCoM adopts and extends the architecture of CogVLM, a general VLM that uses:
- **Visual Encoder:** EVA2-CLIP-E (4B parameters) for initial visual feature extraction.
- **MLP Adapter:** A two-layer SwiGLU MLP to bridge the visual features to the linguistic space.
- **LLM Backbone:** Vicuna-7B-v1.5 for linguistic understanding and generation.
- **Visual Expert Module:** 6.5B additional parameters integrated into the LLM backbone's attention and feed-forward layers for deep multimodal fusion.

To support multi-turn and multi-image scenarios, CogCoM implements a memory-based architecture. It accumulates KV memories from each layer of the LLM backbone across turns. In subsequent turns, the attention mechanism incorporates these accumulated memories, and new images (often cropped and zoomed-in versions from previous steps) are processed and integrated.

#### 3.4. Training Process

The training is performed in two main stages:
- **Stage 1: Pre-training:**
 - **Sub-phase 1 (Foundational Visual Understanding):** Trained on 1.5 billion image-text pairs (from LAION-2B, COYO-700M) for broad visual understanding.
 - **Sub-phase 2 (Grounded Generation):** Trained on 40 million grounded image-question-answer triples (from LAION-115M), where answers include grounded object coordinates, to develop precise position understanding.
 - Both sub-phases use a next-token prediction objective, primarily training the 6.5B visual expert parameters.

- **Stage 2: Alignment (Fine-tuning):**
 - This stage integrates the newly produced 70K CoM data (including math samples) with other diverse instruction-following, text-recognizing, and detailed-captioning corpora (MultiInstruct, LLaVAR, ShareGPT4V), totaling 570K samples.
 - A "launching prompt" (`P_M`) is randomly prepended to questions in the CoM data to explicitly encourage the model to utilize manipulations.
 - The model (specifically the visual experts) is trained with next-token prediction to align its responses with human preferences and the explicit reasoning steps provided in the CoM data.

This meticulously designed methodology allows CogCoM to learn not just to answer, but to *reason* and *show its work* through interpretable visual manipulations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CogCoM, with its 17B parameters, demonstrates significant advancements across various multimodal tasks, achieving State-of-the-Art (SOTA) performance among generalist models and competitive results against specialist models.

#### 4.1. Detailed Visual Question Answering (VQA)

- **Benchmarks:** Evaluated on GQA (compositional reasoning), TallyVQA (object counting), TextVQA (textual cues), and ST-VQA (scene text VQA).
- **Results:** CogCoM achieves SOTA performance compared to all generalist models (e.g., Flamingo, InstructBLIP, CogVLM).
 - It shows substantial improvements over its baseline, CogVLM: up to 5.97 percentage points on complex reasoning benchmarks (like GQA) and 9.0 percentage points on detailed recognition benchmarks (like TextVQA).
 - On GQA and TextVQA, CogCoM's performance is comparable to large-scale specialist SOTAs, highlighting its effectiveness in solving detailed recognition problems.

#### 4.2. Reasoning Accuracy and Time Complexity

- **CoM-test Benchmark:** A newly constructed testbed based on TextVQA, featuring CoM reasoning chains.
- **Keypoints-aware Metric:** A novel metric was introduced to evaluate the correctness of reasoning paths, considering the actions, targets, and visual contents of manipulations, along with their execution order (using normalized Levenshtein Distance and BLEU score).
- **Reasoning Accuracy Results:** Training with just 70K automated CoM data, CogCoM rapidly achieves satisfactory performance (48.41 accuracy at 2K training steps) and peaks at 55.59 accuracy with 8K steps. The gradual improvement of "explanation scores" alongside model performance indicates that successful reasoning steps directly contribute to reaching the correct final answer.
- **Time Complexity Results (MM-Vet):** CogCoM produces information-intensive reasoning content (e.g., detection boxes, auxiliary lines) without incurring excessive time overhead. For example, it generates 262.9 informative tokens in approximately 9 seconds, demonstrating efficiency despite the added complexity of multi-step reasoning.

#### 4.3. Visual Grounding (VG)

- **Benchmarks:** RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg.
- **Results:** CogCoM achieves the best performance in 6 out of 8 subsets across these benchmarks. It exhibits superior grounding ability, on par with specialist SOTAs (e.g., UNINEXT, ONE-PEACE), and even surpasses ONE-PEACE on RefCOCO+ test-A. This indicates that integrating CoM training enhances foundational grounding skills within a generalized multimodal framework.

#### 4.4. General Multimodal Capabilities & Hallucination Examination

- **Benchmarks:** MM-Vet (general multimodal capabilities) and POPE (hallucination detection).
- **Results:** CogCoM improves performance on MM-Vet by 0.6 points compared to its CogVLM baseline (46.1 vs. 45.5). On POPE, it achieves superior performance (87.8%), consistent with or slightly better than its baseline, and outperforming most other generalist VLMs. This suggests that the CoM mechanism helps maintain strong general multimodal abilities while simultaneously reducing hallucinatory outputs, likely due to its emphasis on grounding and evidential reasoning.

#### 4.5. Ablation Experiment (Training with/without CoM Data)

- **Setup:** CogCoM was trained with and without the 70K CoM training data, keeping other settings constant.
- **Results:** Training *with* CoM data leads to substantial improvements: +6.6 points on TextVQA, +0.2 points on MMVet, and +0.9 points on MathVista. This unequivocally demonstrates the significant benefit of the proposed CoM training data for tasks requiring detailed recognition, multimodal reasoning, and mathematical capabilities.

In summary, the results strongly validate the effectiveness of the Chain of Manipulations mechanism. CogCoM not only achieves impressive quantitative gains on a wide array of benchmarks but also demonstrates an ability to produce more interpretable and faithful reasoning outputs, addressing a core challenge in VLM development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Chain of Manipulations (CoM) in CogCoM marks a significant stride in the development of Vision-Language Models, addressing a fundamental limitation of current approaches that often provide unfaithful or uninterpretable "conclusive responses." The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced Visual Reasoning and Interpretability:** By enabling VLMs to actively manipulate visual inputs (e.g., zoom, ground, draw) and generate step-by-step evidential reasoning, CogCoM greatly improves the model's ability to tackle complex, detail-oriented visual problems. This shift from black-box answers to transparent, traceable reasoning paths is crucial for building trust in AI systems and facilitating error diagnosis. It mimics human cognitive processes, making AI reasoning more understandable.

2. **Mitigation of Hallucination:** The explicit, grounded nature of CoM reasoning inherently reduces the propensity for hallucination. When a model is forced to explicitly locate an object or read text from a specific region, it is less likely to fabricate information based on general priors or irrelevant visual cues, leading to more faithful responses.

3. **Novel Data Generation Paradigm:** The proposed cascading data generation pipeline, leveraging the strengths of both Large Language Models (for linguistic annotation) and Visual Foundational Models (for visual annotation and execution), is highly innovative. This automated framework efficiently generates high-quality, complex reasoning data, which is a major bottleneck in multimodal AI research. This paradigm can be adapted and applied to various other data-intensive machine learning scenarios, democratizing the creation of sophisticated training datasets.

4. **Advancement in Challenging Domains:** The manual annotation of 7K high-quality graphical mathematical samples with CoM reasoning paths specifically addresses a very challenging domain for VLMs. This dataset and the model's improved performance on such tasks can significantly advance VLM capabilities in scientific, engineering, and educational applications where understanding complex figures and charts is paramount.

5. **Robust and Versatile Generalist Model:** CogCoM demonstrates superior performance across a broad spectrum of tasks, from detailed VQA and visual grounding to general multimodal evaluation. This indicates that CoM training not only enhances specific reasoning skills but also contributes to a more robust and versatile generalist VLM, capable of handling diverse real-world scenarios.

6. **Paving the Way for More Human-like AI:** The core idea of "Chain of Manipulations" brings VLMs closer to human-like problem-solving, where explicit interaction with the visual environment is often necessary. This conceptual alignment could inspire future research in more intuitive and interactive AI systems.

**Potential Future Impacts:**
- **Improved Human-AI Collaboration:** With interpretable reasoning paths, users can better understand and correct VLM outputs, fostering more effective collaboration in tasks like image analysis, medical diagnosis, or technical support.
- **Enhanced Educational Tools:** VLMs capable of step-by-step visual reasoning could power next-generation educational tools, helping students understand complex diagrams, charts, and spatial problems.
- **Safer AI Systems:** By making reasoning transparent, CoM could contribute to the development of safer AI systems, particularly in critical applications where unfaithful responses could have severe consequences.

In conclusion, CogCoM represents a significant methodological contribution that pushes the boundaries of VLM capabilities, offering a compelling path towards more intelligent, interpretable, and reliable multimodal AI systems.
