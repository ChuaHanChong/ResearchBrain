# Bridging Perception and Reasoning: Token Reweighting for RLVR in Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Science and Technology of China introduce a Token-Reweighting (ToR) strategy for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which explicitly models and optimizes the interplay between perception and reasoning tokens. By dynamically reweighting critical tokens identified via predictive entropy and visual sensitivity, ToR consistently improved performance on diverse multimodal reasoning and perception benchmarks, achieving state-of-the-art results for the evaluated configurations.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) generate responses that interleave tokens for visual perception (grounding) and symbolic reasoning (logical chains), which represent distinct yet interdependent capacities.
- Existing RLVR approaches for MLLMs often optimize these perception and reasoning capabilities in isolation, leading to suboptimal performance and an imbalance where improving one capacity can inadvertently impair the other.
- The granular interaction and interdependence between perception-related and reasoning-related tokens within MLLMs during RLVR training remained underexplored.

## Method

- A plug-and-play ==Token-Reweighting== (**ToR**) strategy is introduced, applicable to existing ==RLVR== optimization algorithms such as **GRPO** and **DAPO**.
- Critical reasoning-related tokens are identified based on high ==predictive entropy==, indicating decision uncertainty, while perception-related tokens are identified by high ==visual sensitivity==, measured by the visual input's influence on token log-probability.
- During ==policy optimization==, identified critical tokens are dynamically reweighted with specific factors (γr, γp) to simultaneously reduce reasoning uncertainty and strengthen ==visual grounding==, rather than masking gradients.

## Results

- The ToR strategy consistently improved performance across various multimodal reasoning and perception benchmarks, showing gains when applied to both GRPO and DAPO RLVR baselines.
- ToR-DAPO, trained on ViRL-39K with Qwen-2.5-VL-7B, achieved the highest reported performance across all evaluated benchmarks, including MathVerse (**54.3**), MathVision (**31.6**), MathVista (**74.2**), WeMath (**73.0**), and HalluBench (**73.6**).
- ToR exhibited strong generalizability, yielding performance improvements across different training data scales (Geometry3K to ViRL-39K) and MLLM model sizes (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B to Qwen-2.5-VL-3B).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing MLLM perception or reasoning capabilities in isolation is empirically suboptimal, demonstrating a tight coupling between these two token types that requires joint consideration.
> - Intrinsic model signals, such as token-level predictive entropy and visual sensitivity (log-probability difference due to image presence), can effectively identify critical reasoning and perception tokens.
> - Balanced joint optimization via dynamic token reweighting is crucial; imbalanced emphasis on either perception or reasoning tokens leads to degraded overall multimodal reasoning performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen significant advancements in complex reasoning tasks, particularly with the integration of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLVR enables LLMs to generate outputs that can be automatically verified against ground truth, thereby improving their reasoning capabilities. The extension of RLVR to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) is a current area of active research. MLLMs face a distinct challenge compared to unimodal LLMs: their generated responses inherently interleave tokens related to visual perception (grounding visual content) and tokens related to symbolic reasoning (constructing logical chains).

Existing RLVR approaches for MLLMs have largely focused on optimizing these capabilities in isolation. Some methods target reasoning skills through chain-of-thought objectives (e.g., Huang et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2025), while others address visual perception through augmentations or perception-oriented rewards (e.g., Wang et al., 2025e; Xiao et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025a). The present work identifies a gap in this landscape: the interaction and interdependence between perception-related and reasoning-related tokens at a granular level remain underexplored. The paper posits that isolated optimization of these distinct yet interdependent capacities is suboptimal.

This research contributes to the broader effort of enhancing MLLM performance by proposing a mechanism that explicitly models and optimizes the coupling between perception and reasoning. It seeks to move beyond segregated training objectives towards a more integrated approach, aiming to improve both visual grounding and coherent reasoning concurrently within the RLVR framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a perceived fundamental challenge in extending Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). MLLM responses contain an interweaving of two distinct types of tokens:
1. **Perception-related tokens:** These tokens are responsible for grounding the textual output in the visual content of an image.
2. **Reasoning-related tokens:** These tokens contribute to the construction of logical reasoning chains within the response.

The authors hypothesize that these two token types represent distinct yet interdependent capacities—visual grounding and symbolic reasoning—and that current RLVR strategies for MLLMs, which often optimize these capacities in isolation, are suboptimal. This isolated optimization may lead to an imbalance where improvements in one capacity inadvertently impair the other.

To empirically validate this hypothesis, the authors conducted a "selective optimization" study. Using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), they identified reasoning-related tokens based on high next-token entropy (indicating decision uncertainty) and perception-related tokens based on visual sensitivity (measured by changes in token log-probability when an image is present versus absent). They then trained models by masking gradients on non-selected tokens, comparing optimization solely on reasoning tokens, solely on perception tokens, and on all tokens (vanilla GRPO). The results indicated that optimizing either perception-only or reasoning-only tokens consistently underperformed full optimization. Qualitatively, reasoning-only models produced coherent reasoning chains but misinterpreted visual content, while perception-only models maintained low-level grounding but failed to integrate it into coherent reasoning. This empirical evidence served as the core motivation, demonstrating that perception and reasoning are tightly coupled at the token level and require joint optimization for robust multimodal reasoning.

The key objective of this work is to address this identified challenge by proposing a strategy that explicitly models the interdependence between perception and reasoning tokens. The goal is to facilitate a balanced and simultaneous enhancement of both visual grounding and logical reasoning abilities within MLLMs during RLVR training. This is intended to lead to improved performance on multimodal reasoning benchmarks by ensuring that critical tokens for both capacities are appropriately emphasized.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research focuses on proposing and evaluating a plug-and-play Token-Reweighting (ToR) strategy for RLVR in MLLMs. The approach comprises three main components: (1) defining the RLVR procedure and relevant optimization algorithms, (2) developing criteria for identifying critical reasoning-related and perception-related tokens, and (3) integrating these identified tokens into the RLVR objective through dynamic reweighting.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: RLVR and Optimization Strategies

The paper first reviews the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) framework for MLLMs. Given a multimodal input (image $I_b$, question $q_b$) and a ground truth $y_b$, the model $\pi_\theta$ generates an output $o_b$ comprising a reasoning process and a prediction. A binary reward function $R(\cdot)$ assesses correctness by comparing the extracted prediction with the ground truth. The objective is to maximize this reward: $J_{RLVR}(\theta) = \max_\theta E[R(o_b, y_b)]$.
Two existing RLVR optimization algorithms, on which ToR is applied, are described:
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** This strategy stabilizes training by computing advantages within response groups. Its objective function incorporates an importance ratio clipping mechanism and a KL regularization term to maintain policy proximity.
- **Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO):** DAPO refines GRPO by removing KL regularization and introducing decoupled clipping thresholds for positive and negative advantages, along with dynamic sampling and a token-level loss.

#### 3.2. Token Identification

ToR identifies two types of critical tokens using intrinsic model signals:
- **Reasoning-Related Tokens:** These are identified based on token-level predictive entropy. High entropy values are associated with pivotal "forking" points in reasoning chains, indicating decision uncertainty. For each generated token $o_{b,i,t}$ in a rollout, its entropy $H_{b,i,t}$ is calculated over the top-$p$ vocabulary (where $p=0.95$). The reasoning-token set $T_r$ comprises the top-$\alpha_r$ fraction of tokens with the highest entropy across a batch.
- **Perception-Related Tokens:** These tokens indicate positions whose predictions are strongly influenced by visual inputs. Visual sensitivity $S_{b,i,t}$ is quantified by comparing the token's log-probability when conditioned on the image and question, versus when conditioned on an empty placeholder for the image and the question. A large $S_{b,i,t}$ implies strong visual dependence. The perception-token set $T_p$ consists of the top-$\alpha_p$ fraction of tokens with the highest visual sensitivity across a batch.
 - *Investigation of Perception Proxy (Appendix C):* The authors conducted an ablation study on various proxies for perception (probability difference, log-probability difference, entropy difference, and attention scores). Log-probability difference was empirically found to offer the best balance between capturing visual grounding fidelity and information-theoretic significance.

#### 3.3. Token Reweighting (ToR)

ToR addresses the interdependence of perception and reasoning by assigning token-specific weights during policy optimization, rather than masking gradients. This approach aims for simultaneous reduction of reasoning uncertainty and strengthening of visual grounding.
The modified GRPO objective (ToR-GRPO) and DAPO objective (ToR-DAPO) incorporate weighting factors $\gamma_r$ for reasoning-related tokens and $\gamma_p$ for perception-related tokens. Tokens identified as belonging to $T_r$ or $T_p$ receive these weights, while tokens outside these sets are effectively excluded from the advantage calculation (assigned a weight of zero). For overlapping tokens (those in both $T_r$ and $T_p$), they are assigned the same weight as reasoning-related tokens for training stability.

#### 3.4. Experimental Settings

- **Training Data:** Geometry3K (2,100 training, 300 validation samples).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Five benchmarks are used: MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, WeMath (for visual reasoning), and HalluBench (for visual perception).
- **Base MLLM:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B (and Qwen2.5-VL-3B for generalization tests).
- **Framework:** EasyR1 for RL training.
- **Hyperparameters:** Standard settings including a learning rate of 1e-6, global batch size of 128, rollout batch size of 512, and rollout 'n' of 12.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings that support the core hypothesis and demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed Token-Reweighting (ToR) strategy.

#### 4.1. Empirical Validation of Perception-Reasoning Coupling

The initial "selective optimization" study provided empirical evidence for the interdependence of perception and reasoning tokens.
- **Reasoning-only optimization:** When only reasoning-related tokens (identified by high entropy) were optimized, performance consistently underperformed vanilla GRPO across various benchmarks (e.g., approximately a 2% absolute drop on WeMath). Qualitative analysis showed coherent reasoning chains but misinterpretation of visual content.
- **Perception-only optimization:** Optimizing only perception-related tokens (identified by visual sensitivity) fared worse than reasoning-only optimization (e.g., approximately a 3% drop on WeMath). While low-level visual grounding was preserved, models failed to integrate it into coherent reasoning.
These results, supported by Figure 2 and Tables 1 and 2, confirmed the hypothesis that optimizing either capability in isolation is suboptimal and that perception and reasoning are coupled, requiring joint optimization.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Joint Reweighting (Ablation Studies)

Ablation studies investigated the impact of jointly reweighting reasoning and perception tokens.
- Table 3 demonstrates that jointly reweighting both token types consistently improved performance over isolated optimization across all benchmarks.
- A perception token weight ($\gamma_p$) of 0.5 (with $\gamma_r$ fixed at 1.0) was identified as providing a balanced trade-off, leading to improved reasoning while maintaining perception accuracy. Extremely low or high $\gamma_p$ values degraded performance, indicating the importance of balanced emphasis.
- A token selection ratio ($\alpha_r, \alpha_p$) of 30% was adopted as a robust operating point, balancing sufficient learning signal with the benefits of selective reweighting.

#### 4.3. Performance Comparison with State-of-the-Art Approaches

Table 4 presents a comprehensive comparison of ToR with various state-of-the-art MLLMs and RLVR approaches.
- **Improvements over RLVR baselines:** ToR demonstrated consistent performance gains when applied to existing RLVR algorithms. ToR-GRPO improved MathVerse from 50.8 to 53.0 and HalluBench from 69.8 to 72.4. Similarly, ToR-DAPO outperformed DAPO across all benchmarks, with notable gains on WeMath and MathVista.
- **Generalization:** ToR exhibited generalizability across different data scales and model sizes. When scaling training data from Geometry3K (2.1K samples) to ViRL-39K (39K samples), ToR-DAPO continued to yield performance improvements, achieving strong overall results on Qwen-2.5-VL-7B. ToR also generalized to a smaller Qwen-2.5-VL-3B backbone, consistently improving over the DAPO baseline despite reduced model capacity.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The ToR-DAPO model, trained on ViRL-39K with Qwen-2.5-VL-7B, achieved the highest reported performance across all evaluated benchmarks (MathVerse: 54.3, MathVision: 31.6, MathVista: 74.2, WeMath: 73.0, HalluBench: 73.6), indicating a new state-of-the-art for this experimental setup.

#### 4.4. Token Distribution and Interdependence Analysis (Appendices)

- **Token Distributions (Appendix A):** Analysis of token distributions on HalluBench (perception-related) and WeMath (reasoning-related) showed distinct characteristics. For perception tasks, a small set of visually sensitive tokens remained stable and critical. For reasoning tasks, high-entropy tokens were more robust and crucial for capturing key reasoning processes. This validated the distinct nature of the identified token types.
- **Perception-Reasoning Interdependence (Appendix B):** Visualizations demonstrated a "strong push-pull dynamic relationship" between reasoning uncertainty (entropy) and perception strength (log-probability difference) across the training and validation sets of Geo3K, further confirming their intricate coupling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and advancement of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) in several ways, with implications for future development and application.

**Fundamental Insight:** The work provides an empirical demonstration and conceptual framework for the intrinsic interdependence between perception-related and reasoning-related tokens within MLLMs during Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) training. This addresses a previously underexplored aspect of MLLM behavior, moving beyond the isolated optimization of these capabilities. The finding that optimizing either perception or reasoning in isolation is suboptimal suggests a limitation in current MLLM training paradigms.

**Methodological Contribution:** The proposed Token-Reweighting (ToR) strategy offers a simple yet effective approach to address this interdependence. Its "plug-and-play" nature allows seamless integration into existing RLVR objectives (e.g., GRPO, DAPO) without requiring extensive pipeline modifications. By identifying critical tokens based on intrinsic model signals (predictive entropy and visual sensitivity) and dynamically reweighting them, ToR provides a self-contained mechanism for joint optimization. This balances the model's focus, enabling simultaneous enhancement of visual grounding and logical reasoning.

**Performance Enhancement:** The consistent and robust performance gains demonstrated across multiple MLLM architectures (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B, Qwen-2.5-VL-3B), training data scales (Geometry3K, ViRL-39K), and diverse multimodal reasoning and perception benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, WeMath, HalluBench) highlight the practical efficacy of ToR. Achieving state-of-the-art results in the evaluated configurations indicates a significant improvement in the ability of MLLMs to perform complex multimodal tasks.

**Efficiency and Stability:** By strategically focusing gradient updates on tokens crucial for both perception and reasoning, ToR can lead to more efficient and stable training. The balanced optimization regime helps to mitigate the negative trade-offs observed when these capacities are optimized in isolation.

**Future Research Directions:** The work lays a foundation for future exploration. The discussion section outlines promising avenues, including:
- **Fine-grained token identification:** Utilizing advanced vision models (like SAM) to pinpoint perception-critical regions with greater precision.
- **Dynamic token reweighting:** Developing more sophisticated, adaptive weighting schemes based on gradient contributions or relationships to final outcomes.
- **Contextual interdependence:** Extending the framework beyond individual tokens to broader semantic contexts, acknowledging that token meanings are derived from their surroundings.
- **Broader applications:** Applying the interdependence framework to more complex multimodal generation and understanding tasks.

The potential impact lies in improving the reliability and capability of MLLMs for real-world applications requiring nuanced visual understanding and sophisticated reasoning, such as advanced human-computer interaction, complex problem-solving with visual aids, and robust content generation. By fostering a more integrated learning process, this research could lead to MLLMs that are not only more accurate but also more robust in their multimodal reasoning abilities.
