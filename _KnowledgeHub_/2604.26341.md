# SpatialFusion: Endowing Unified Image Generation with Intrinsic 3D Geometric Awareness

> [!summary] Summary
> SpatialFusion introduces a framework that integrates a Spatial Transformer with Multimodal Large Language Models and Diffusion Transformers to imbue unified image generation models with intrinsic 3D geometric awareness. This approach achieved over 70% accuracy in object and camera pose tasks on the GenSpace benchmark and showed substantial improvements in spatial relation reasoning compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Unified image generation models lack intrinsic 3D geometric awareness, leading to difficulties in accurately representing object poses, spatial relationships, and metric measurements.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle to form robust 3D geometric representations during image understanding, providing only high-level semantic embeddings.
- Current diffusion backbones operate without explicit 3D structural priors, resulting in spatially incoherent images despite semantic correctness.

## Method

- **SpatialFusion** integrates a parallel ==Spatial Transformer== with an MLLM via a ==Mixture-of-Transformers== architecture to derive 3D geometric representations from semantic context.
- ==Semantic-guided attention== lets the Spatial Transformer learn intrinsic 3D geometric states, decoded into dense ==metric-depth maps==.
- A ==Depth Adapter== fuses depth maps with the diffusion model's latent space, providing explicit 3D structural priors for geometry-constrained synthesis.

## Results

- **SpatialFusion** achieved highest overall average scores on GenSpace for spatially-aware T2I generation and instruction-based editing.
- Over **70% accuracy** in Camera and Object Pose tasks; **23%** relative gain for allocentric spatial relations vs. GPT-4o.
- Modest **18.5%** parameter increase and **<3%** inference latency increase; competitive or improved performance on standard benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - While MLLMs excel at semantic understanding, their internal representations weakly encode 3D geometry; explicit integration of a specialized Spatial Transformer is necessary to derive robust 3D structural scaffolds.
> - Incorporating derived 3D geometric cues directly into the diffusion model's latent space, before patchification, effectively guides the denoising process to achieve spatially coherent and accurate image synthesis.
> - A progressive two-stage training strategy, including geometric-aware pre-training and geometry-guided joint training, enables the model to effectively learn and leverage intrinsic 3D geometric awareness without sacrificing generative quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of unified image generation has experienced notable advancements, primarily through the integration of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for semantic interpretation and diffusion backbones for image synthesis. These models, operating on an "understanding-reinforced generation" principle, have demonstrated proficiency in producing prompt-consistent and visually realistic images. However, a recurring limitation in existing models, including advanced ones like GPT-4o, is their struggle with tasks requiring precise spatial understanding. These tasks include accurately representing object poses, reasoning about spatial relationships, and adhering to metric measurements. This limitation is attributed to a lack of intrinsic 3D geometric awareness and the absence of explicit geometric guidance during the generation process.

Previous attempts to incorporate geometric guidance have involved layout-to-image methods utilizing 2D bounding boxes or segmentation maps, which are inherently limited to planar arrangements and cannot address 3D geometric tasks. Other approaches have introduced external 3D cues, but their dependency on auxiliary information not available in text-only scenarios restricts their applicability and fails to achieve inherent geometric awareness.

Recent developments in 3D foundation models, such as Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT), have demonstrated the capacity to learn and represent rich 3D geometric information within a unified Transformer architecture. This suggests a potential for integrating 2D visual semantics and 3D geometry within a shared representational framework. SpatialFusion positions itself within this evolving landscape by addressing the fundamental gap in spatial awareness of unified image generation models. It aims to internalize 3D geometric understanding as a native capability, moving beyond superficial 2D conditioning or reliance on external 3D data. By building upon the architectural homogeneity of Transformer-based models, SpatialFusion seeks to extend this unified framework to generative models, enabling them to derive 3D structural scaffolds from 2D semantics and subsequently guide 2D image synthesis with these structures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to endow unified image generation models with intrinsic 3D geometric awareness. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations and challenges within the current state of the art:

1. **Addressing Deficiencies in Spatially-Aware Tasks:** Current unified image generation models exhibit significant limitations when faced with tasks that demand precise spatial understanding. These include accurately understanding and rendering object poses, correctly reasoning about spatial relationships between multiple objects, and adhering to specific metric measurements (ee.g., size, distance). The paper illustrates these shortcomings with examples where leading models fail to correctly interpret spatial instructions.

2. **Rectifying Geometry-Deficient Representations in Understanding:** The authors hypothesize that the root cause of these limitations lies in the MLLM's inability to form robust 3D geometric representations during the understanding phase. A probing experiment conducted by the researchers showed that fine-tuning a depth prediction head on a frozen MLLM's hidden states yielded no semantically-aligned depth maps. This suggests that while MLLM features excel at capturing categorical concepts, they weakly encode geometric attributes, preventing them from providing a reliable internal 3D scaffold for subsequent generation.

3. **Enhancing Geometry-Unconstrained Synthesis in Generation:** Building on the previous point, when the MLLM provides only high-level semantic embeddings without geometric awareness, the diffusion backbone operates without explicit structural priors. The paper observes that the iterative denoising process of diffusion models, in the absence of dense geometric signals (like depth maps), struggles to anchor precise spatial arrangements. This leads to synthesized images that may be semantically correct but spatially incoherent. The motivation is to provide these explicit geometric signals to guide the diffusion process, ensuring structural integrity.

4. **Moving Beyond External and 2D-Limited Guidance:** Existing methods for geometric guidance, such as 2D layout control or external 3D cues, are either inherently 2D-limited or depend on auxiliary information that may not always be available in text-only scenarios. The motivation for SpatialFusion is to transcend these limitations by developing a framework that *inherently* learns and derives 3D structures from semantic input, integrating this awareness directly into the generative process rather than relying on external, conditional execution.

5. **Leveraging Advances in 3D Foundation Models:** The emergence of 3D foundation models, which can learn rich 3D geometric information within unified Transformer architectures, inspired the central question of this work: Can this unified framework be extended to generative models, enabling 2D semantics to drive the derivation of 3D structural scaffolds, which then guide 2D image synthesis? The motivation is to build upon this insight to create a system where 3D geometric awareness is an intrinsic capability rather than an add-on.

In summary, the research aims to overcome the fundamental spatial reasoning deficits in current unified image generation models by introducing a mechanism for internalizing 3D geometric awareness, enabling more controllable, coherent, and spatially accurate image synthesis and editing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpatialFusion is a novel framework designed to integrate intrinsic 3D geometric awareness into unified image generation models. It comprises four core components: a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) for semantic context parsing, a parallel Spatial Transformer for 3D geometric modeling, a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for latent space compression, and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) for image synthesis. The methodology is structured around two synergistic mechanisms and a progressive two-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture and Synergistic Mechanisms

1. **Semantics-Guided Geometric Derivation:**
 - **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Architecture:** A parallel Spatial Transformer is introduced to operate alongside the MLLM. This MoT paradigm allows the Spatial Transformer to specialize in 3D geometric modeling while sharing self-attention layers with the MLLM to interact with semantic contexts.
 - **Geometric State Initialization:** Learnable query tokens are initialized and dynamically augmented with DINOv2 features from the input image to form the initial geometric states.
 - **Interactive Attention:** At selected interactive MoT layers, geometric queries, keys, and values are generated from the geometric states, and semantic queries, keys, and values are generated from the MLLM's hidden states. Semantics-guided attention is enabled by concatenating the keys and values from both pathways, allowing geometric queries to learn 3D representations grounded in the MLLM's semantic context.
 - **Metric-Depth Map Decoding:** The final geometric-aware hidden states from the Spatial Transformer are fed into a DPT head, which decodes a dense metric-depth map (D) representing the 3D structure of the target image.

2. **Geometry-Constrained Image Synthesis:**
 - **Depth Adapter:** The derived metric-depth map (D) is processed by a dedicated Depth Adapter (E_phi) to align it with the diffusion model's latent space, producing depth features (F_depth).
 - **Latent Fusion:** These depth features (F_depth) are fused with the VAE-encoded noisy latents (z_t) via element-wise addition *before* the latent patchification step. This embedding of 3D constraints occurs at a foundational spatial level.
 - **Spatially-Aware Denoising:** The DiT backbone then processes these geometry-augmented noisy latents along with the MLLM's last hidden states as a unified sequence. This process predicts noise at each denoising step, enabling spatially-aware denoising that respects both semantics and geometry, leading to images that adhere to 3D poses, spatial relations, and metric scales.

#### 3.2. Training Tasks

To facilitate rigorous geometric awareness, a large-scale geometry-augmented dataset is curated, encompassing web-sourced and synthetic samples. Training tasks are categorized into three primary competencies:

1. **Foundational Image Generation Tasks:**
 - Standard text-to-image generation for prompt-visual alignment.
 - Instruction-guided image-to-image editing for local and global modifications.

2. **Spatially-Aware Generation Tasks:**
 - **Spatial Pose Synthesis:** Generating images with specific 3D object/camera positions and orientations.
 - **Spatial Relation Reasoning:** Accurately rendering multi-object layouts and relative positioning.
 - **Spatial Measurement Adherence:** Controlling quantitative spatial details (e.g., object sizes, intervals).

3. **Geometry-Consistent Editing Tasks:**
 - **Geometry-grounded object addition:** Placing new elements at plausible depths.
 - **Structural-aware object removal:** Inpainting background while adhering to original geometry.
 - **Subject replacement:** Ensuring new entities conform to the spatial footprint of originals.
 - Other tasks involving spatial constraints.

#### 3.3. Progressive Two-Stage Training Strategy

1. **Stage 1: Geometric-Aware Pre-training:**
 - **Objective:** To train the Spatial Transformer and its learnable query tokens to derive accurate geometric representations.
 - **Setup:** MLLM and diffusion backbone are frozen.
 - **Supervision:** A frozen 3D foundation model (VGGT) acts as a teacher, extracting pseudo-ground-truth metric-depth maps (D_gt) from target images.
 - **Loss Function:** The Spatial Transformer is optimized using a standard regression objective (L_depth) computed as the pixel-wise L2 distance between predicted (D_pred) and pseudo-ground-truth depth maps over valid image regions.

2. **Stage 2: Geometry-Guided Joint Training:**
 - **Objective:** To integrate the pre-trained Spatial Transformer with the diffusion backbone for end-to-end optimization, steering image synthesis with derived geometric scaffolds.
 - **Setup:** All training tasks from Sec. 3.2 are incorporated; the diffusion backbone is unfrozen. The Spatial Transformer produces on-the-fly metric-depth predictions.
 - **Loss Function:** A joint loss function is used: L_total = L_diff + lambda * L_depth, where L_diff is the primary diffusion training objective and L_depth is the geometric regression loss from Stage 1. The hyperparameter lambda balances generative quality and geometric constraints.
 - **Curriculum Learning:** Within both stages, a coarse-to-fine curriculum is applied: initial training on a large-scale data mixture for broad alignment, followed by fine-tuning on a curated high-quality subset for precision and fidelity.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

SpatialFusion is built upon the OmniGen2 backbone, utilizing Qwen2.5-VL-3B as the MLLM and a custom diffusion decoder. A lightweight 10-layer Spatial Transformer is introduced, initialized from scratch, sharing self-attention with the MLLM. Training uses 16 NVIDIA H200 GPUs with a global batch size of 256.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SpatialFusion demonstrated enhanced performance across various benchmarks, particularly in spatially-aware tasks, while also showing generalization capabilities in standard image generation and editing scenarios.

#### 4.1. Main Results on GenSpace (Spatially-Aware Benchmarks)

On the GenSpace benchmark, designed to evaluate spatial awareness, SpatialFusion achieved the highest overall average scores in both text-to-image generation and instruction-based image editing.

- **Text-to-Image Generation (Table 1):**
 - **Spatial Pose:** SpatialFusion exceeded 70% accuracy in Camera and Object Pose tasks, significantly outperforming competitive unified models like GPT-4o (which plateaued around 60%). This indicates a superior ability to ground objects according to directional instructions.
 - **Spatial Relation:** The model showed substantial improvement in allocentric (approx. 23% relative gain) and intrinsic relationship tasks (approx. 31% relative gain) over GPT-4o, indicating better translation of complex relational prompts into coherent 2D scene layouts.
 - **Spatial Measurement:** SpatialFusion achieved the highest overall average, demonstrating effectiveness in generating images that adhere to precise quantitative constraints guided by internally generated metric-depth maps, an area where most models struggle.

- **Instruction-based Image Editing (Table 2):**
 - **Spatial Pose:** The method exhibited strong structural versatility in pose manipulation, surpassing top unified baselines like GPT-4o by over 45% in object pose manipulation. This capability includes successfully rotating or reorienting objects while preserving original textures and identities.
 - **Spatial Relation:** SpatialFusion improved the ability to add new objects into scenes based on complex spatial descriptions, achieving the leading average score. It seamlessly inserts objects at precise egocentric or allocentric locations without structural degradation.
 - **Spatial Measurement:** While precise quantitative editing remains challenging, SpatialFusion demonstrated advancements in executing metric-aware scale transformations directly from textual instructions, pushing the boundaries of the baseline model.

#### 4.2. Main Results on Standard Benchmarks (Generalization)

- **Text-to-Image Generation (GenEval, T2I-CompBench++, DPG-Bench - Table 3):**
 - **Generalization:** Performance on T2I dimensions (e.g., Color, Texture, Shape) remained competitive or improved, suggesting that 3D geometric awareness enhances overall generative quality, not just spatial constraints.
 - **Robustness:** Consistent improvements across benchmarks with varying prompt complexities (short, structured, descriptive) indicate the MoT architecture maintains linguistic flexibility.
 - **Compositional and Spatial Reasoning:** Significant gains were observed on GenEval's Position metric and T2I-CompBench++'s 2D-spatial and 3D-spatial metrics, demonstrating improved generalization in composition-intensive scenarios requiring accurate spatial relationships.

- **Instruction-based Image Editing (ImgEdit-Bench, GEdit-Bench-EN - Table 4):**
 - **Semantic and Perceptual Fidelity:** Consistent gains in Semantic Consistency (SC) and Perceptual Quality (PQ) on GEdit-Bench and substantial improvements across diverse editing tasks on ImgEdit-Bench were observed. This indicates that spatial guidance aligns with high-level semantic intent without degrading synthesis quality.
 - **Structure-Sensitive Editing:** The model significantly outperformed the baseline in challenging editing scenarios on ImgEdit-Bench, with relative improvements of 17% for object addition, 24% for replacement, and 38% for removal. This indicates stronger adaptability in object placement and coherent background completion.
 - **Consistent Editing:** Consistent performance gains across all benchmarks demonstrated improved robustness and stability for diverse editing operations.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Comparisons (Figure 3, 5)

Qualitative examples illustrated that internally derived metric-depth maps guided the generation and editing processes, leading to consistent adherence to object poses, spatial layouts, and metric constraints, where baseline models often exhibited spatial errors.

#### 4.4. In-Depth Analysis

- **Effect of Shared Attention on Geometry Prediction (Figure 4a):** All shared-attention variants consistently outperformed the "No Sharing" baseline for geometry prediction, highlighting the importance of semantic priors. Uniform Sampling across MLLM layers performed best, suggesting that interacting with a balanced spectrum of semantic features (low-level to high-level) enhances geometric reasoning.
- **Effect of Geometric Guidance on Generation Performance (Table 5):** Incorporating 3D guidance (Concatenation and Addition) significantly improved performance over the "w/o Injection" baseline, confirming that performance gains stem from intrinsic 3D geometric awareness. Element-wise Addition consistently outperformed Concatenation, likely due to less disruption of pretrained priors by acting as a residual.
- **Analysis of Geometric Supervision Weight on Joint Training (Figure 4b):** Varying the weighting factor (lambda) for the geometric auxiliary loss (L_depth) in Stage 2 revealed a trade-off. Increasing lambda strengthened metric-depth derivation, but an overly large lambda negatively impacted primary image synthesis capabilities. An optimal balance was found at lambda = 0.5.
- **Computational Overhead (Table 5):** The introduction of the lightweight spatial transformer, depth adapter, and VGGT DPT head resulted in a modest 18.5% increase in total parameters. However, the impact on inference latency was minimal, adding less than a 3% delay per image for both text-to-image generation and image editing tasks, which is considered acceptable given the performance gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SpatialFusion represents a notable advancement in the field of unified image generation by addressing a fundamental limitation of current models: the absence of intrinsic 3D geometric awareness. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning Capabilities:** The framework directly tackles the challenges of understanding object poses, reasoning about spatial relations, and adhering to metric measurements, which have traditionally been difficult for generative models. By internalizing 3D geometric awareness, SpatialFusion significantly improves the accuracy and coherence of generated and edited images in spatially complex scenarios.

2. **Intrinsic Geometric Understanding:** Unlike prior methods that rely on external 2D conditioning (e.g., bounding boxes) or auxiliary 3D cues, SpatialFusion enables the model to derive 3D structural scaffolds directly from multimodal semantics. This represents a paradigm shift towards truly inherent geometric understanding within the generative process, making it more robust and broadly applicable to text-only prompts without requiring explicit geometric inputs.

3. **Broadened Applicability and Generalization:** The research demonstrates that enhancing 3D geometric awareness not only improves performance on specialized spatial tasks but also generalizes to standard text-to-image generation and image editing scenarios. This suggests that a deeper understanding of underlying geometry serves as a foundational prior that elevates the model's overall generative and reasoning capabilities, leading to improved semantic and perceptual fidelity across diverse tasks.

4. **Architectural Innovation:** The adoption of a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture, allowing a parallel spatial transformer to interact with and learn from the MLLM's semantic contexts, provides a pathway for specialized modules to enhance core capabilities without compromising the flexibility of multimodal large language models. The integration of a depth adapter and the two-stage progressive training strategy offer a practical and effective method for incorporating this geometric intelligence.

5. **Efficiency and Practicality:** Despite the significant gains in spatial accuracy and editing consistency, SpatialFusion maintains negligible inference overhead. The marginal increase in latency makes this sophisticated geometric reasoning accessible for practical applications, potentially without requiring substantial additional computational resources.

The potential impact of SpatialFusion extends to various applications requiring precise visual control and scene understanding. This includes creating more realistic and controllable virtual environments, assisting in content generation for design and entertainment, improving robotic vision by providing more accurate scene interpretations, and advancing the development of more intelligent and spatially aware generative AI systems. By bridging the gap between semantic understanding and 3D geometric synthesis, SpatialFusion contributes to the ongoing evolution of generative models towards capabilities that more closely mirror human-level visual reasoning.
