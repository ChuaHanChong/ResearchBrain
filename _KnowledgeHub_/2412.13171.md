# Compressed Chain of Thought: Efficient Reasoning Through Dense Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> The Compressed Chain of Thought (CCoT) framework enables large language models to perform complex reasoning efficiently by learning to generate compact, continuous representations of reasoning steps. This method achieves up to a 9 percentage point increase in accuracy on the GSM8K dataset while incurring only a 0.45-second latency increase compared to a direct answer baseline.

## Problem

- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning significantly boosts Large Language Model (LLM) performance on complex tasks but incurs high generation latency due to explicitly decoding verbose reasoning steps.
- Existing methods that provide LLMs with extra computation during inference often rely on non-contentful or discrete 'contemplation tokens', which may not be effective for all types of reasoning.
- There is a need for an efficient alternative to explicit CoT that can perform 'additional reasoning over dense contentful representations' without the full generation cost.

## Method

- Introduces ==Compressed Chain of Thought (CCoT)==, a two-phase LoRA finetuning framework for decoder-only LLMs (LLAMA2-7B-CHAT).
- Trains models to generate =='contentful' and 'continuous' contemplation tokens== autoregressively by matching their intermediate hidden states (e.g., at layer 15) to gold hidden states from a full CoT trace using scaled Mean Squared Error (MSE).
- A separate module is finetuned to decode the final answer conditioned on the initial query and the ==generated contemplation tokens==, with an additional classifier to predict the optimal number of contemplation tokens at inference.

## Results

- CCoT with a **10x** compression ratio achieved **17.9%** accuracy on GSM8K with a **0.78-second** decode time, representing a **9 percentage point** accuracy improvement over the **8.9%** baseline (**0.33s**).
- CCoT significantly outperformed non-contentful PAUSE tokens, which showed only marginal accuracy improvements (**0.3-1.0** percentage points) at comparable decode times.
- The framework demonstrated an adaptive trade-off between reasoning accuracy and generation speed, allowing for dynamic control over computational effort by adjusting the compression ratio.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex reasoning tasks, such as those in GSM8K, benefit more from sequential, 'depth-bottlenecked' computation provided by autoregressive continuous contemplation tokens rather than purely parallel methods.
> - Intermediate hidden states (e.g., at layer 15 for LLAMA2-7B) are optimal for encoding and passing information across autoregressive contemplation steps.
> - An adaptive control over reasoning depth, achieved by varying the compression ratio of contemplation tokens, allows for a flexible trade-off between accuracy and inference efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving areas within large language model research: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, efficiency in LLMs, and the exploration of "contemplation tokens" or internal computation mechanisms.

**Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** CoT, introduced by Wei et al. (2023), revolutionized LLM reasoning by enabling models to simulate "thinking out loud," decomposing complex problems into sequential steps. While highly effective, CoT's main drawback is its high generation latency due to the explicit decoding of verbose reasoning steps. This paper directly addresses this efficiency bottleneck, seeking to retain CoT's reasoning benefits without its computational cost. It builds upon the idea that the *information* in the reasoning chain is crucial, not necessarily its explicit textual form.

**Contemplation Tokens / Filler Tokens / Pause Tokens:** The authors introduce "contemplation tokens" as a unifying term for special tokens used during inference to allow for extra computation. Prior work (e.g., Pfau et al., 2024; Goyal et al., 2024; Herel & Mikolov, 2024) has explored various forms of these tokens, often referred to as filler, pause, or thinking tokens. Critically, most of these prior approaches utilize *non-contentful*, *discrete* tokens that are often decoded *in parallel*. This paper differentiates itself by proposing *contentful* and *continuous* contemplation tokens that are decoded *autoregressively*. The contentful nature implies they represent actual reasoning, while the continuous and autoregressive aspects aim to leverage the sequential processing power of Transformers, which the authors argue is crucial for "depth-bottlenecked" reasoning tasks.

**Knowledge Distillation and Implicit Reasoning:** The paper relates to research on distilling knowledge chains (e.g., Deng et al., 2023; 2024) or training LLMs to reason in a continuous latent space (Hao et al., 2024, contemporaneous work). These methods aim to internalize or compress the explicit reasoning process. CCoT contributes to this by explicitly training the model to generate compressed, *text-grounded* representations of reasoning, a key distinction that allows for potential post-hoc inspection of the LLM's "thoughts," which is not easily achievable with purely latent or non-contentful approaches. The paper also highlights CCoT's data efficiency compared to some distillation methods.

**Contextual Compression:** While distinct, the work touches upon the broader theme of contextual compression (e.g., Ge et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024), which focuses on efficiently handling long input contexts. CCoT, however, is unique in that its compressed representations are *generated* during inference (not just read from input) and represent *unknown* future reasoning, rather than known context.

In essence, CCoT bridges a crucial gap: it offers a method to gain the reasoning advantages of explicit CoT while significantly reducing its computational overhead, by learning to generate compact, meaningful internal representations of reasoning steps, which is a novel approach compared to existing discrete or non-contentful contemplation tokens.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Compressed Chain-of-Thought (CCoT) framework are:

1. **Address the High Latency of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** The core motivation stems from the observation that while CoT significantly boosts LLM reasoning capabilities on complex tasks, it comes at a substantial cost in terms of generation latency. Explicitly decoding every step of a reasoning chain (which can be many times longer than the initial query) dramatically increases computation time due to the autoregressive nature of LLMs and the quadratic complexity of self-attention. The authors cite a practical example of GPT-4o being nearly 10x slower with CoT.

2. **Develop an Efficient Alternative to Explicit CoT:** The aim is to create a method that provides "additional reasoning over dense contentful representations" without incurring the full generation cost of explicit CoT. This means enabling the model to perform more computation and internal "thought" without verbose text generation.

3. **Introduce Contentful and Continuous Contemplation Tokens:** Existing approaches to provide LLMs with extra computation during inference (termed "contemplation tokens" by the authors) often rely on *non-contentful* (e.g., period characters, special placeholder tokens) and *discrete* tokens. The authors hypothesize that *contentful* (semantically meaningful, grounded in text) and *continuous* representations of reasoning chains would be more broadly applicable and effective for complex reasoning tasks. This "continuous space" reasoning is a key conceptual contribution.

4. **Enable Adaptive Control over Performance-Efficiency Trade-off:** A practical goal is to allow for dynamic adjustment of the computational effort. CCoT aims to provide a mechanism where the number of generated contemplation tokens (and thus computational effort) can be adaptively modified on demand, allowing users to fine-tune the balance between accuracy and generation speed.

5. **Maintain Inspectability (Potential):** While not fully demonstrated, a significant motivation for using *contentful* contemplation tokens (even in compressed form) is the future potential for decoding these compressed representations back into human-readable reasoning chains. This could offer a level of interpretability of the LLM's "thinking process" that is often lost in purely latent or non-contentful distillation methods.

In essence, CCoT seeks to achieve the best of both worlds: the robust reasoning of CoT with the efficiency benefits of compressed, internal representations, providing a more practical and adaptable reasoning paradigm for LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Compressed Chain of Thought (CCoT) framework proposes a novel method for training a decoder-only LLM to generate compressed, contentful, and continuous contemplation tokens. The approach involves two distinct but sequential finetuning phases on a pretrained LLM, leveraging LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) for parameter efficiency.

**Core Idea:**
Instead of explicitly generating a long, text-based reasoning chain (CoT), CCoT learns to generate a shorter sequence of *dense vector representations* (contemplation tokens) that encapsulate the essence of the reasoning. These tokens are trained to approximate the hidden states produced by a large language model performing a full CoT trace.

**Two-Phase Finetuning:**

1. **Finetuning CCoT_φ (Contemplation Token Generation Module):**
 - **Goal:** Train a module (`CCoT_φ`) to generate `k` contemplation tokens that are compressed representations of a full `m`-token reasoning chain, where `k` is determined by a fixed compression ratio `r` (i.e., `k = ceil(r * m)`).
 - **Gold Label Generation:** For each training instance (query `w`, full reasoning chain `t`, answer `a`), the pretrained base LLM (`LM_θ`) first computes the hidden states of the concatenated input `[w; t; a]`. From the hidden states corresponding to the reasoning chain `t` (`t_hat_1:m`), a *subset selection* is performed to identify `k` "gold" hidden states (`z_1:k`). This subset selection uses a pre-trained scorer (from Qin et al., 2024), which is a linear layer operating on a specific intermediate layer (`T=3`) of the hidden states. The selected `k` hidden states `z_1:k` serve as the target for `CCoT_φ`.
 - **Autoregressive Input for Contemplation Tokens:** A crucial design choice is how `CCoT_φ` generates these `k` tokens autoregressively. Instead of predicting discrete tokens that are then embedded, CCoT uses the *intermediate hidden state* at a specific layer (`l`, chosen as `l=15` in experiments) of the *previous contemplation token* as the *continuous input* for generating the next one. For the first contemplation token, the input is the hidden state of the last query token. This approach leverages the internal continuous representations of the model directly.
 - **Loss Function:** `CCoT_φ` is trained using a **scaled Mean Squared Error (MSE)** loss between the generated contemplation token hidden states (`z_hat_i^l`) and the gold hidden states (`z_i^l`). The scaling by `1/variance(z_i^l)` normalizes for varying L1 norms across different layers, allowing for consistent learning rates.
 - **Layer-by-Layer Training:** The parameters of `φ` are finetuned layer by layer. When training the *i*-th layer, only the parameters corresponding to that layer are updated, while preceding layers are frozen. This progressive training improved the quality of generated tokens.

2. **Finetuning DECODE_ψ (Answer Decoding Module):**
 - **Goal:** Train a separate module (`DECODE_ψ`) to decode the final answer, conditioned on the initial query and the *generated* contemplation tokens from the now-trained `CCoT_φ`.
 - **Contemplation Token Generation During Finetuning:** Unlike the `CCoT_φ` training that uses precomputed gold hidden states, `DECODE_ψ` is trained with contemplation tokens *generated autoregressively* by `CCoT_φ`. This ensures the decoding module learns to leverage the *actual* output of `CCoT_φ`.
 - **Loss Function:** The standard **cross-entropy loss** is used for decoding the answer tokens (`a_1:o`) given the query and the generated contemplation tokens.
 - **Parameter Unfreezing Strategy:** Initially, `ψ` is finetuned with `φ` frozen. Then, some parameters of `φ` (specifically, those corresponding to layers *after* the autoregressive layer `l`) are unfrozen, allowing `φ` to receive a downstream signal from the answer decoding task, which empirically improved performance.
 - **Inference Length Prediction:** Since the number of contemplation tokens (`k`) is unknown at inference time, a binary classifier `END_ψ` is additionally trained. This classifier takes the final layer hidden state of a generated contemplation token and predicts whether more tokens should be generated, effectively controlling the variable sequence length.

**Inference (Algorithm 2):**
During inference, given a query:
1. Embed the query and compute its hidden states.
2. Autoregressively generate contemplation tokens: Starting with the hidden state of the last query token (at layer `l`), `CCoT_φ` iteratively generates subsequent contemplation tokens. Each new token's hidden state (at layer `l`) serves as the input for the next. This process continues until `END_ψ` predicts termination or a maximum length is reached.
3. Decode the answer: Once the contemplation tokens are generated, `DECODE_ψ` produces the final answer, conditioned on both the initial query and the generated contemplation tokens.

**Implementation Details:**
- Uses LoRA for efficient finetuning.
- Base model: LLAMA2-7B-CHAT.
- Dataset: GSM8K (with calculator annotations removed from reasoning chains).
- Hyperparameters: Compression ratio `r` (0.05, 0.10), subset selection layer `T=3`, autoregressive input layer `l=15`.

This multi-stage, fine-grained training approach allows CCoT to effectively compress reasoning traces into dense, continuous representations while maintaining the ability to perform complex reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors evaluate CCoT primarily on the GSM8K math word problem dataset, focusing on two key metrics: **Accuracy (Exact Match)** and **Decode Time**. They compare CCoT against several baselines, including:
- **No Contemplation Tokens (r=0.0):** Model directly outputs the answer.
- **Full Explicit CoT (r=1.0):** Model generates the full reasoning chain as its contemplation tokens.
- **PAUSE (Goyal et al., 2024):** A method using non-contentful, discrete pause tokens generated in parallel, evaluated at similar compression ratios.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Significant Accuracy Improvement with Modest Latency Increase:**
 - The baseline with no contemplation tokens achieves an accuracy of 8.9% with a decode time of 0.33 seconds.
 - CCoT with a compression ratio of `r=0.10` (meaning contemplation tokens are 10% the length of full CoT, denoted as "CCoT 10x") achieves **17.9% accuracy** with a decode time of 0.78 seconds. This represents a **9 percentage point improvement** in accuracy for only a **0.45 second increase** in generation time.
 - CCoT with `r=0.05` ("CCoT 20x") achieves **15.1% accuracy** with a decode time of 0.49 seconds, a **6.2 percentage point improvement** with just a **0.16 second increase**.
 - In contrast, full explicit CoT ("CCoT 1x") achieves 31.5% accuracy but at a much higher decode time of 8.10 seconds. CCoT offers a favorable trade-off for scenarios where sub-second latency is critical.

2. **Superiority over Non-Contentful Pause Tokens:**
 - The PAUSE method, evaluated at `r=0.05` ("PAUSE 20x") and `r=0.10` ("PAUSE 10x"), achieved accuracies of 9.2% and 9.9% respectively. While slightly better than the "no contemplation tokens" baseline, these gains are nominal (0.3-1.0 percentage points) and significantly lower than CCoT's improvements.
 - PAUSE tokens also incurred a small decode time increase (0.35s and 0.37s), but their limited impact on reasoning suggests that simply adding more parallel computation (as PAUSE tokens allow) is not sufficient for "depth-bottlenecked" reasoning tasks like GSM8K. The authors hypothesize that GSM8K requires more *sequential* computation, which CCoT's autoregressive nature provides.

3. **Adaptive Performance-Efficiency Trade-off:**
 - The results clearly demonstrate that CCoT allows for an adaptive trade-off between reasoning accuracy and generation speed by controlling the compression ratio `r`. Increasing `r` generally improves accuracy but increases decode time. The authors note that accuracy plateaus after `r=0.2`, possibly due to noise propagation from approximation errors in successive autoregressive steps.

4. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:**
 - **Autoregressive Layer (l):** The choice of the intermediate layer `l` (whose hidden state is used as input for the next contemplation token) is crucial. `l=15` (roughly `L/2` for LLAMA2-7B, which has 32 layers) yielded the best performance. Layers too early (e.g., `l=3`) or too late (e.g., `l=31`) resulted in poor performance, suggesting that intermediate layers encode optimal global information suitable for this autoregressive decoding scheme.
 - **Subset Selection:** While a learned scorer was used, the authors found that simply selecting `k` evenly spaced tokens from the gold reasoning chain yielded similar performance, implying that the specific choice of *which* `k` tokens are selected might be less critical than the *fact* that a compressed representation is used.

In summary, CCoT effectively demonstrates that compressed, contentful, and autoregressively generated contemplation tokens can significantly enhance LLM reasoning on challenging tasks like GSM8K while maintaining much higher efficiency compared to traditional verbose Chain-of-Thought, and outperforming non-contentful contemplation token approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Compressed Chain of Thought (CCoT) framework introduces several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact on the field of large language models:

1. **Bridging the Efficiency-Reasoning Gap:** CCoT offers a practical solution to the long-standing trade-off between LLM reasoning capabilities (enhanced by CoT) and inference efficiency. By enabling models to perform internal "thought" through dense, compressed representations rather than explicit text, CCoT makes sophisticated reasoning more feasible for real-time applications where latency is critical. This is a crucial step towards deploying highly capable LLMs in low-latency environments.

2. **Introducing a New Paradigm for Reasoning (Continuous Space):** The concept of "reasoning in continuous space" by generating and operating on continuous hidden states as contemplation tokens is a novel and powerful idea. Unlike discrete tokens, continuous representations can potentially encode richer, more nuanced information. This work suggests that LLMs can internally "think" in a more abstract, compressed manner, which could lead to more efficient and robust reasoning processes. It opens up new avenues for research into how LLMs can internalize and manipulate complex information without relying on human-interpretable linguistic constructs.

3. **Unified Understanding of "Contemplation Tokens":** The authors' introduction of "contemplation tokens" as an umbrella term provides a valuable conceptual framework for discussing various techniques that infuse additional computation into LLMs during inference. This standardization can help organize and direct future research in this burgeoning area, distinguishing between contentful/non-contentful, discrete/continuous, and parallel/autoregressive modes of operation.

4. **Adaptive Computational Control:** The ability to adaptively control the number of contemplation tokens generated (via the compression ratio `r`) offers a flexible knob for managing the performance-efficiency trade-off. This is highly beneficial for real-world deployments where computational resources and latency requirements can vary. Developers can dynamically tune the "thinking time" of an LLM based on task complexity or available compute.

5. **Potential for Post-hoc Inspectability (Future Work):** While CCoT's tokens are compressed, their "contentful" nature implies a grounding in semantically meaningful text. This opens up the intriguing possibility of inverse-mapping these compressed representations back to human-readable reasoning chains for inspection or debugging. If successful, this could provide much-needed transparency into the internal workings of LLMs, addressing a critical challenge in AI interpretability.

6. **Theoretical Advancements:** The paper's theoretical discussion (in Appendix B) on the computational capacity of autoregressively decoded contemplation tokens is significant. By formalizing how such tokens can enhance the "depth" of computation beyond a model's fixed number of layers, it provides a deeper understanding of why CCoT is effective for "depth-bottlenecked" reasoning tasks. This theoretical underpinning reinforces the value of autoregressive generation over purely parallel approaches for certain problem classes.

In essence, CCoT represents a notable advancement in making LLM reasoning more efficient and flexible. It shifts the paradigm from verbose, explicit reasoning to compact, internal "thought," paving the way for more practical, adaptable, and potentially more interpretable intelligent systems.
