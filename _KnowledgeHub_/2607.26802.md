# Risk-Aware Motion Planning with Learned Trajectory Primitives and Probabilistic Safety Assessment

> [!summary] Summary
> A hybrid motion planning framework for autonomous vehicles integrates learning-based trajectory generation with an analytic probabilistic safety assessment and an optimal control refinement step. This approach resulted in a 24% collision rate, the lowest among benchmark methods, while maintaining a 96% kinematic feasibility rate in dense urban driving scenarios.

## Problem

- Autonomous vehicle motion planning needs to balance safety, efficiency, dynamic feasibility, and real-time computational tractability in complex urban environments.
- Existing optimization-based methods struggle with computational complexity and non-convex collision constraints, while sampling-based methods often compromise dynamic feasibility.
- Learning-based motion primitives lack kinematic feasibility guarantees and interpretability, and deterministic safety approaches can lead to overly conservative driving.

## Method

- A hybrid framework generates trajectories by integrating learning-based motion primitive generation, ==analytic probabilistic safety assessment==, and optimization-based refinement.
- Candidate trajectories are efficiently generated by an **MP-RBFN** network trained on optimal control solutions, followed by risk-aware goal state selection.
- An analytic method precisely quantifies collision probability by computing spatial occupancy overlap between polygons under Gaussian uncertainties, and a lightweight ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== problem refines the selected low-risk trajectory for kinematic feasibility.

## Results

- The hybrid planner achieved the lowest collision rate (**24%**) across 1000 CommonRoad scenarios, significantly outperforming Frenetix (**33%**), MP-RBFN_only (**35%**), and MPC_only (**69%**).
- It demonstrated a high kinematic feasibility rate of **96%** due to the MPC refinement, substantially improving upon MP-RBFN_only (**45%**) and Frenetix (**83%**).
- The planner exhibited improved trajectory smoothness, indicated by a compact steering-rate distribution and low longitudinal jerk, balancing safety with comfortable driving, sometimes leading to waiting when safe trajectories are unavailable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the motion planning problem into distinct stages of exploration, risk assessment, and refinement allows the system to leverage the strengths of diverse methodologies.
> - Learning-based motion primitives (MP-RBFN) efficiently generate a diverse and dynamically consistent set of candidate maneuvers, expanding the solution space for subsequent optimization.
> - An analytic probabilistic safety assessment provides a precise and computationally efficient quantification of collision risk under uncertainty, enabling robust and interpretable safety-critical decision-making without excessive conservatism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The successful deployment of autonomous vehicles (AVs) in complex and dynamic urban environments necessitates motion planning systems that can generate trajectories that are simultaneously safe, efficient, dynamically feasible, and computationally tractable. This research is situated within the field of autonomous driving motion planning, which faces challenges in balancing these often conflicting requirements, particularly concerning collision risk and real-time performance under uncertainty.

Existing motion planning strategies generally fall into a few broad categories. **Optimization-based methods** can produce smooth, dynamically feasible trajectories but often struggle with the non-convex nature of collision avoidance constraints, leading to high computational complexity and potential convergence to local optima. **Sampling- and graph-based approaches** efficiently explore the solution space by generating and evaluating multiple candidate trajectories. However, these methods frequently rely on simplifying assumptions regarding trajectory geometry or vehicle dynamics to achieve computational efficiency, which can compromise dynamic feasibility.

Recent advancements have introduced **learning-based motion primitives**, where neural networks approximate optimal control solutions for rapid trajectory inference. While offering speed and potentially high accuracy in approximating optimal solutions, these learned primitives often lack guarantees for kinematic feasibility and can be difficult to interpret, posing challenges for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving.

Risk mitigation is a critical aspect, addressed by **deterministic worst-case approaches** that aim to exclude any safety violation. These methods, often using bounding volumes and reachability analysis, provide strong robustness and formal safety guarantees but can result in overly conservative and restrictive driving behavior. In contrast, **risk-aware planning methods** define continuous risk measures, such as collision probabilities, to balance safety with performance, often allowing for a predefined risk threshold. These methods range from one-dimensional criticality metrics (e.g., time-to-collision) suitable for structured environments to risk fields and probabilistic formulations for more granular risk assessment.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a hybrid framework that seeks to combine the strengths of these diverse approaches while mitigating their individual limitations. It integrates learning-based methods for efficient candidate generation, an analytic probabilistic safety assessment for robust risk evaluation, and an optimization-based refinement for ensuring kinematic feasibility and interpretability. This aims to bridge the gap between fast, data-driven trajectory generation and the safety, dynamic consistency, and interpretability required for practical AV deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a motion planning framework for autonomous vehicles that can generate safe, efficient, and dynamically feasible trajectories in dense and unpredictable urban environments, while remaining computationally efficient for real-time operation. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and challenges inherent in current autonomous driving systems:

1. **Ensuring Safety and Dynamic Feasibility under Uncertainty:** Autonomous vehicles must navigate complex traffic scenarios with inherent uncertainties, such as the unpredictable behavior of other road users and inaccuracies from perception and prediction modules. Motion planning must explicitly account for collision risk arising from these uncertainties while generating trajectories that adhere to the vehicle's dynamic limits.
2. **Addressing Computational Complexity of Optimization-Based Methods:** Traditional optimization-based planners struggle with the non-convex nature of collision avoidance constraints when interacting with dynamic obstacles. This significantly increases computational load and can lead to suboptimal solutions or failure to converge in real-time. The motivation is to reduce this complexity without sacrificing solution quality or safety.
3. **Improving Dynamic Consistency and Interpretability of Learning-Based Methods:** While learning-based motion primitives offer computational efficiency and can approximate optimal control solutions, they often lack explicit guarantees for kinematic feasibility and their decision-making processes can be opaque. A key motivation is to leverage the speed of learned primitives while ensuring that the resulting trajectories are dynamically valid and that safety-critical decisions are interpretable.
4. **Overcoming Conservatism of Deterministic Risk Approaches:** Deterministic worst-case planning provides strong safety guarantees but frequently leads to overly cautious and inefficient driving behavior by completely ruling out any potential safety violation. The objective is to adopt a more nuanced, probabilistic approach to risk assessment that allows for a balance between safety and performance, enabling more natural and efficient driving.
5. **Achieving a Balanced Trade-off between Exploration and Refinement:** Sampling-based methods excel at exploring a wide range of maneuvers but may use simplified dynamics. Optimization methods can refine trajectories for precision but are sensitive to initialization. The motivation is to combine efficient exploration (generating diverse, low-risk candidates) with a robust refinement step to ensure both global optimality and local feasibility.

Based on these motivations, the paper proposes the following specific contributions and objectives:

- **To develop a hybrid motion planning framework:** This involves integrating learning-based motion primitives for efficient candidate generation, an analytic collision risk evaluation for robust safety assessment, and an optimization-based trajectory refinement for ensuring dynamic feasibility and interpretability.
- **To create an efficient and interpretable method for evaluating collision probabilities:** The aim is to precisely quantify collision risk under real-world uncertainties, enabling a thorough and reliable safety assessment of candidate trajectories.
- **To implement a post-refinement approach using Model Predictive Control (MPC):** This objective focuses on guaranteeing kinematic feasibility and smooth control actuation for the selected trajectory, while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for urban driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed hybrid motion planning framework is designed to generate dynamically feasible and low-risk trajectories with computational efficiency by systematically separating exploration and risk assessment from optimization-based refinement. The overall architecture involves several sequential steps in each planning cycle:

**A. Learning-Based Motion Primitive Generation**
The initial phase involves generating candidate trajectories using a Radial Basis Function Network (RBFN) called MP-RBFN, which was previously introduced by Kaufeld et al. (2025).

1. **Input and Output Representation:** MP-RBFN takes an initial vehicle state and a target pose within a fixed planning horizon (T) as input. The input vector `q = [x₀, y₀, θ₀, v₀, δ₀, x_f, y_f, θ_f]ᵀ` includes initial and terminal position, yaw, initial velocity, and steering angle. The network outputs a time-discretized, jerk-minimal trajectory `ζ = {x_τ}τ∈T`, where each state `x_τ = [x_τ, y_τ, v_τ, δ_τ, θ_τ]ᵀ` comprises position, velocity, steering angle, and yaw at time step `τ`.
2. **Network Architecture:** MP-RBFN projects the input vector `q` into a latent feature space `z = Zq`. It then applies Gaussian radial basis activations, centered at `c_k` with shape factor `ϵ_k`, for `k = 1, ..., K`. Finally, a linear output layer maps these activations to the trajectory vector `ζ`. The parameters (Z, W, c, ϵ) are learned through backpropagation.
3. **Network Training:** The MP-RBFN is trained offline on a dataset of optimal control solutions. These solutions are generated by solving a minimum-jerk optimal control problem (OCP) for various initial and terminal states. The OCP minimizes longitudinal and lateral jerk, subject to a nonlinear kinematic single-track vehicle model (Eq. 3), and enforces vehicle feasibility through minimal and maximal bounds on states and controls. An elliptic combined acceleration constraint (Eq. 4) is also applied, where maximum longitudinal acceleration varies with velocity.
4. **Risk-aware Goal State Selection:** Within the planning framework, the MP-RBFN queries goal states sampled from a structured feature space. Longitudinal bounds are determined by forward-simulating vehicle dynamics to ensure physical attainability. For each longitudinal position, lateral bounds are adaptively mapped from road geometry to exclude environmental constraint violations. Subsequently, the collision probability of these candidate goal states is calculated using the predicted trajectories of surrounding vehicles (as detailed in Section B). Goal states exceeding a predefined collision risk threshold are discarded. If all states exceed the threshold, the 100 lowest-risk trajectories are retained to ensure continued planning.

**B. Analytic Probabilistic Collision Evaluation**
The collision risk for each candidate goal state and each state along the generated motion primitives is assessed using an analytic measure based on the probability of spatial occupancy overlap.

1. **Formulation:** This method computes the exact probability of spatial overlap between two arbitrarily shaped polygons (ego vehicle and obstacle). Given uncertain ego and obstacle poses (`x_e`, `x_o`) with probability densities `p_x_e` and `p_x_o`, the collision probability `P_c` is expressed as an integral over their spatial overlap (Eq. 6).
2. **Relative Distribution and Whitening Transform:** By introducing the relative pose `˜x = x_o - x_e`, the integral is reformulated over a collision region `Ω` defined by the Minkowski sum of the vehicle footprints (Eq. 7). For Gaussian uncertainties, the relative distribution `p_˜x` remains Gaussian, with `˜μ = μ_o - μ_e` and `˜Σ = Σ_o + Σ_e` (Eq. 8). To improve numerical conditioning, a whitening transform `ˆx = W(˜x - ˜μ)` is applied, converting the distribution into an isotropic form.
3. **Efficient Integration:** The integral over the collision region is efficiently computed using sequential one-dimensional integrations along the boundary of the collision region (Eq. 9).
4. **Trajectory Risk Aggregation:** The overall collision risk for a trajectory `ζ` is obtained by aggregating the per-step probabilities, typically using the maximum probability along the planning horizon (`P_c,ζ = max_τ∈T {P_c^τ}`).

**C. Trajectory Refinement via Optimal Control**
After generation and risk evaluation, candidate trajectories are ranked using a multi-objective cost function (Eq. 10), which considers comfort (velocity, acceleration, jerk), progress (reference path and orientation offset), and collision probability. The partial cost terms and their weights are detailed in Table I. The lowest-cost candidate `˜ζ` is then selected for refinement.

1. **Optimal Control Problem (OCP):** The selected motion primitive `˜ζ` is refined using a lightweight optimal control problem (OCP) (Eq. 11). This OCP minimizes a combination of deviation from the reference trajectory `˜ζ` and control effort `u`, over the planning horizon `T`.
2. **Model and Constraints Consistency:** The same nonlinear kinematic single-track model (Eq. 3) and vehicle constraints (Eq. 4) used for MP-RBFN training are applied during refinement to ensure consistency.
3. **Computational Efficiency:** Since the collision-aware exploration has already restricted the candidate space to safe regions, the refinement stage does not require computationally expensive additional collision constraints. Its focus is on enforcing strict dynamic feasibility and smoothing controls, contributing to overall computational tractability.

**D. Implementation Details**
The MP-RBFN, with 1024 radial basis function centers, is implemented in PyTorch and trained on a dataset of over 2 million optimal control solutions. The analytic collision risk calculation uses a vectorized implementation of Eq. (9). The trajectory refinement OCP is solved using `acados`, a framework optimized for nonlinear MPC, with `HPIPM` as the internal quadratic subprogram solver. Vehicle parameters align with those derived from the CommonRoad benchmark. A planning horizon of 3 seconds, a resampling step size of 0.1 seconds, and specific sampling densities for position and orientation are used. A collision probability threshold of 0.01 is set for discarding risky goal states.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed hybrid planner against three benchmark methods (Frenetix, MP-RBFN_only, and MPC_only) across 1000 CommonRoad benchmark scenarios yielded the following main findings:

**A. Quantitative Comparison**

1. **Collision Rate and Success Rate:** The proposed hybrid planner achieved the lowest collision rate among all evaluated methods, colliding in 24% of scenarios. This compares to 33% for Frenetix, 35% for MP-RBFN_only, and 69% for MPC_only. Simultaneously, the hybrid planner maintained a success rate of 52%, which is comparable to Frenetix (52%) and MP-RBFN_only (52%). This indicates that the reduction in collisions was not achieved by significantly sacrificing task completion.
2. **Safety-Efficiency Trade-off:** The hybrid planner exhibited a higher "time limit" failure rate (14%) compared to Frenetix (13%) and MP-RBFN_only (5%), suggesting that it prioritizes safety by waiting when no sufficiently low-risk trajectory is available, thereby avoiding collisions.
3. **Cost Comparison (Safety and Comfort):** Analysis of normalized costs showed that the obstacle distance costs were similar across sampling-based methods. The hybrid approach, however, displayed a higher maximum obstacle distance cost, implying its ability to navigate closer to obstacles when the probabilistic collision risk remains acceptable. Furthermore, the hybrid planner demonstrated a compact steering-rate distribution and low longitudinal jerk, indicating improved trajectory smoothness attributable to the refinement step, while preserving the jerk-minimal structure of the learned primitives.
4. **Kinematic Feasibility:** The CommonRoad drivability checker assessed the feasibility of the final trajectories. MP-RBFN_only had a low feasibility rate of 45% (violating constraints in over 55% of scenarios), while Frenetix achieved 83%. In contrast, the proposed hybrid planner demonstrated a feasibility rate of 96%, confirming the necessity and effectiveness of the MPC-based refinement stage in ensuring dynamically valid trajectories.

**B. Qualitative Comparison**

Representative scenarios further illustrate the planner's performance and the limitations of benchmark methods:

1. **Intersection with Oncoming Traffic (Fig. 7):** In a left-turn scenario with oncoming traffic, the proposed hybrid planner successfully executed the turn while staying within the drivable area and managing the oncoming vehicle. Frenetix, conversely, continued almost straight, colliding with the road boundary. This suggests that Frenetix's Mahalanobis-distance-based risk approximation may not adequately capture complex collision probabilities, leading to a suboptimal trade-off between avoiding obstacles and adhering to road boundaries. MPC_only converged to a locally poor evasive trajectory due to its lack of prior maneuver exploration.
2. **Dense Traffic Scenario (Fig. 8):** In a dense traffic situation with both leading and oncoming vehicles, the hybrid planner made a slight shift toward the lane center to increase clearance to the leading vehicle while maintaining sufficient distance from oncoming traffic. Frenetix prioritized separation from surrounding vehicles and drove off the drivable area. MPC_only steered too far into the opposite lane, leading to a collision with oncoming traffic.

These qualitative examples support the quantitative findings, demonstrating that the combination of maneuver-level exploration, precise probabilistic risk evaluation, and local trajectory refinement in the hybrid planner results in a more effective balance among progress, road compliance, collision avoidance, and dynamic feasibility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a significant contribution to the field of autonomous vehicle motion planning by introducing a hybrid framework that addresses several critical challenges simultaneously.

The **primary significance** lies in the demonstration that combining learning-based trajectory generation with an analytic probabilistic safety assessment and optimization-based refinement can yield superior performance in urban driving scenarios compared to purely sampling-based or optimization-based methods. This architectural decomposition allows the system to leverage the strengths of each component:
- **Efficiency of Learned Primitives:** Learning-based motion primitives (MP-RBFN) efficiently generate a diverse set of dynamically consistent candidate trajectories, effectively expanding the maneuver solution space beyond what simplified analytic methods offer. This reduces the search space for the subsequent optimization problem to near-optimal regions, minimizing sensitivity to initialization and the risk of converging to poor local optima.
- **Robustness of Probabilistic Safety Assessment:** The analytic collision probability evaluation provides a precise and computationally efficient risk measure, which is crucial for safety-critical decision-making. Unlike approximate risk estimations, this method accurately quantifies spatial overlap under uncertainty, leading to more reliable collision avoidance, particularly in dense and complex scenarios. The results show a clear reduction in collision rates.
- **Guaranteed Feasibility and Smoothness through Optimization:** The MPC-based refinement step is critical for ensuring strict kinematic feasibility and smooth control behavior. This addresses a common limitation of purely learned or analytically simplified trajectories, which often violate vehicle dynamic limits. The high feasibility rate (96%) achieved by the hybrid planner confirms the effectiveness of this approach.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Enhanced Safety and Reliability:** By reducing collision rates and improving kinematic feasibility, the proposed framework contributes to the development of safer and more reliable autonomous driving systems, which is a prerequisite for widespread public acceptance and deployment.
2. **Improved Driving Comfort and Efficiency:** The generation of jerk-minimal and smooth trajectories, as evidenced by low steering rate and longitudinal jerk costs, implies a more comfortable driving experience for passengers. The ability to balance safety with progress suggests efficient navigation, avoiding the excessive conservatism of deterministic approaches.
3. **Increased Interpretability and Verifiability:** Unlike purely black-box learning approaches, the hybrid nature of this framework maintains interpretability. Safety decisions are grounded in an analytic collision probability metric, and dynamic consistency is guaranteed by an optimal control formulation, making the system's behavior more explainable and verifiable for regulatory and safety assurance purposes.
4. **Practical Applicability in Complex Environments:** The evaluation in dense urban CommonRoad scenarios suggests that this framework is well-suited for challenging real-world conditions where dynamic obstacles and uncertainties are prevalent.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies avenues for future work, such as integrating more sophisticated probabilistic prediction models for multimodal traffic and adaptive online updating of sampling densities. These directions highlight the framework's potential as a flexible and extensible base for further advancements in autonomous motion planning.

While the work shows promise, the performance in rare or out-of-distribution scenarios, which might not be sufficiently covered by learned primitives, remains an area for further investigation. Nevertheless, the integration of learning-based exploration, analytic safety assessment, and optimization-based refinement offers a robust and balanced approach to autonomous motion planning.
