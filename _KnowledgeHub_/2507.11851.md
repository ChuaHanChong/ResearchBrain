# Your LLM Knows the Future: Uncovering Its Multi-Token Prediction Potential

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework developed by researchers at Apple enables pretrained autoregressive large language models to perform multi-token prediction, yielding inference speedups of up to 5.35x for code and math tasks and approximately 2.5x for general tasks while preserving generation quality.

## Problem

- High latency and computational cost of autoregressive LLM inference due to its inherent sequential, token-by-token generation process.
- Difficulty in efficiently adapting existing powerful pretrained autoregressive LLMs for multi-token generation without degrading their original next-token prediction capabilities.
- The need for practical, efficient methods to unlock and utilize the implicit future token knowledge already encoded within pretrained autoregressive models.

## Method

- A ==masked-input formulation== using special mask tokens appended to the input, enabling the LLM to predict multiple future tokens in parallel within a single inference step.
- ==Gated LoRA==, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning technique that applies ==low-rank adaptations only when processing mask tokens==, preserving the original model's next-token prediction capabilities.
- A lightweight, learnable sampler module and ==Latent Consistency Matching (LCM) loss== used during training to improve the coherence and alignment of multi-token predictions with ground-truth autoregressive outputs, enhancing speculative decoding acceptance rates.

## Results

- The framework achieved significant inference speedups, with acceptance rates translating to nearly **5.0x** to **5.35x** for code and math tasks, and approximately **2.5x** for general chat and knowledge tasks, averaging **3.17x** across benchmarks.
- Gated LoRA successfully preserved the original model's next-token prediction (NTP) accuracy and cross-entropy loss, demonstrating no degradation of baseline performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Quadratic Decoding, the lightweight sampler head, and Latent Consistency Matching (LCM) loss each contribute to improved speedup and acceptance rates, with their combined use showing the strongest performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing autoregressive LLMs implicitly contain knowledge about future tokens, which can be explicitly leveraged for multi-token prediction.
> - It is possible to adapt pretrained LLMs for new capabilities like multi-token prediction without degrading their original performance by using gated adaptation mechanisms such as Gated LoRA.
> - Carefully designed decoding strategies, such as Quadratic Decoding, combined with coherence-enhancing components like a sampler head and Latent Consistency Matching loss, are crucial for maximizing the efficiency and reliability of multi-token generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) is dominated by the autoregressive paradigm, where models predict one token at a time based on all preceding tokens. This paradigm, while highly effective for training due to its simplicity and ability to leverage vast text data, suffers from inherent sequentiality during inference. Each token generation step requires a full forward pass through the model, leading to high latency and computational expense, particularly for long sequences. This paper directly addresses this fundamental limitation.

The broader research landscape for accelerating LLM inference can be categorized into several key areas:

- **Model Compression and Quantization:** Techniques to reduce model size and computational footprint (e.g., pruning, distillation, quantization) without significant performance loss. This work complements, rather than replaces, these methods.
- **Architectural Innovations:** Exploring new model architectures that are inherently more efficient (e.g., recurrent models, state-space models).
- **Non-Autoregressive (NAR) Generation:** A more radical departure, where models generate entire sequences or chunks in parallel. Examples include diffusion-based language models, which are still nascent and often require building entirely new modeling and training pipelines. The authors explicitly acknowledge these (e.g., Nie et al., 2025; Gong et al., 2025) but choose to focus on adapting the *existing* autoregressive setup.
- **Speculative Decoding (SD):** This is the most directly related line of work. SD methods (e.g., Leviathan et al., 2022; Cai et al., 2024; Bhendawade et al., 2024; Cheng et al., 2024) typically involve a smaller, faster "draft model" that proposes several future tokens, which are then quickly verified by the larger "verifier model." If the draft is correct, multiple tokens are accepted in one go. While effective, SD still fundamentally relies on the main model performing an autoregressive verification step. This paper takes a step further by investigating if the *main* model itself can be trained for multi-token prediction (MTP) and how this can be seamlessly integrated with speculative decoding.
- **Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) within Autoregressive Models:** This specific sub-area aims to enable a single autoregressive model to produce multiple tokens concurrently. The authors cite Gloeckle et al. (2024) as prior work in MTP. More specifically, methods leveraging "mask tokens" for MTP or speculative decoding are highly relevant (e.g., Gerontopoulos et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024; Liu and Zhu, 2024; Xiao et al., 2024). The key differentiation of this work from these prior mask-based methods is its ability to utilize the *full depth and capacity* of the LLM for future token inference, along with the novel "gated LoRA" mechanism to guarantee quality preservation.
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT):** LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) is a prominent PEFT technique. This paper innovates by introducing "gated LoRA," which directly addresses the challenge of adding new capabilities (MTP) without degrading existing ones (NTP), a common problem in fine-tuning.
- **Knowledge Distillation and Consistency Training:** Techniques like knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015; Kim and Rush, 2016) are used in many MTP and speculative decoding approaches (Zhou et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024a) to align a smaller model with a larger one. This paper introduces "Latent Consistency Matching (LCM) loss" as a self-distillation mechanism to align MTP predictions with the original model's autoregressive outputs, enhancing the acceptance rate in speculative decoding.

This work positions itself as an innovative adaptation of the autoregressive paradigm, leveraging the inherent predictive capabilities of LLMs to achieve significant speedups with minimal modifications and guaranteed quality preservation, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in practical LLM inference optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to significantly accelerate the inference speed of large language models while ensuring that there is no degradation in the quality of the generated output. The authors are driven by a fundamental question: Can an existing autoregressive language model generate *multiple* tokens in a single inference step?

The primary motivations underpinning this objective are:

1. **Addressing Inherent Sequentiality:** Autoregressive generation is inherently sequential, requiring a full model pass for each token. This leads to high latency and computational costs, making real-time, high-throughput LLM deployment challenging. The authors are motivated to find ways to parallelize this process.
2. **Unlocking Implicit Knowledge:** The authors observe that pretrained autoregressive models, even though trained for next-token prediction, implicitly encode information about future tokens. Their initial experiments (Figure 1, left panel) show that correct future tokens appear within the top 200 logits when placeholder tokens are appended to the prompt. This insight suggests that instead of building entirely new non-autoregressive models, it might be possible to "unlock" and structure this latent knowledge within existing powerful LLMs.
3. **Efficiency with Minimal Changes:** While diffusion-based language models offer true non-autoregressive generation, they require entirely new modeling and training pipelines. The authors are motivated to adapt the current, well-established autoregressive training and inference setup with minimal modifications, thereby reducing development effort and leveraging existing model investments.
4. **Preserving Quality and Functionality:** A key challenge in adapting pretrained models is the risk of performance degradation, especially for their original next-token prediction (NTP) capabilities. The authors are specifically motivated to develop a method that guarantees no loss in generation quality for existing tasks, ensuring practical applicability.
5. **Improving Practicality and Deployment:** Faster inference directly translates to lower operational costs (less GPU time), reduced latency for user-facing applications, and the potential to deploy larger, more capable models in real-time scenarios or on edge devices.

In essence, the paper aims to transform standard autoregressive LLMs into efficient multi-token predictors by strategically fine-tuning them with mask tokens, a specialized LoRA adaptation, a lightweight sampler, and coherence-enhancing losses, all while maintaining their core performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, "Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) Potential," introduces several interconnected innovations to enable simultaneous prediction of multiple subsequent tokens from a vanilla autoregressive language model. The core idea is to transform the next-token prediction task into a multi-token fill-in-the-blank problem.

#### 3.1. Masked-Input Formulation

The foundation of the approach is the use of special `k` unique mask tokens (`m_1..., m_k`). During inference, these masks are appended to the end of a given input sequence `X = [x_1..., x_n]`, forming `X_m = [x_1..., x_n, m_1..., m_k]`. The model is then trained to predict the `k` future tokens (which would normally be generated sequentially) into these mask positions.
During training, the input modification is more complex and crucial for efficiency. For an original sequence of length `n`, it simulates `n` separate prompts in parallel within a single model invocation. Specifically, for each `i` from `1` to `n`, the `i`-th prompt consists of `[x_1..., x_i]` followed by `k` masks. This parallel processing significantly speeds up training. Crucially, the input modifications (position IDs, attention biases) are carefully designed to preserve the model's original Next Token Prediction (NTP) behavior.

#### 3.2. Gated LoRA Formulation

To enable fine-tuning for MTP without degrading the original pretrained model's NTP functionality, the authors introduce **Gated LoRA**. Low-Rank Adapters (LoRA) are typically added in parallel to the existing linear layers of the model, with only the LoRA parameters being updated during fine-tuning. In Gated LoRA, an indicator function `I(t) ∈ {0, 1}` is introduced, which is `1` only for MTP tokens (mask tokens) and `0` for NTP tokens (original sequence tokens). This means the LoRA adaptation `(A · B · x_t)` is only applied when processing mask tokens, effectively creating distinct functional paths for NTP and MTP tokens. This mechanism ensures that the original model's weights and their outputs for NTP tokens remain untouched and uninfluenced by the MTP fine-tuning, thereby preserving baseline quality.

#### 3.3. Lightweight, Learnable Sampler Module

While the masked-input formulation allows the LLM to predict distributions over future tokens, generating a coherent sequence from these individual predictions is challenging. To address this, a lightweight sampler head is introduced. This is a two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) that generates a token's logits by conditioning on both the model's latent representation of the current masked token (`z_n`) and the embedding of the *previously sampled token* (`E_y_n-1`). This sequential conditioning within the sampler head improves the coherence of the multi-token output sequence.

#### 3.4. Auxiliary Training Losses

Beyond standard cross-entropy, two additional loss components are used:
- **Cross-entropy loss:** Applied to both the base unembedding layer's predictions (`L^b_t`) and the sampler head's predictions (`L^s_t`).
- **Latent Consistency Matching (LCM) loss:** This is a crucial innovation to enhance the alignment between MTP predictions and the ground truth autoregressive outputs. For each NTP token `z_t`, there are corresponding MTP tokens (`z` from `S(z_t)`) that should ideally predict the same future token. LCM loss minimizes the squared Euclidean distance `(z_t - z)^2` between these MTP representations and the corresponding NTP latent representation (`z_t`), which is treated as an anchor and detached from the computation graph. This ensures that MTP predictions are pulled towards the 'correct' autoregressive representations, effectively acting as a form of self-distillation and improving the acceptance rate during speculative decoding.

#### 3.5. Speculative Generation Strategy

To leverage the multi-token predictions and verify their correctness, the authors employ a speculative decoding scheme.
- **Linear Decoding:** In this strategy, the model generates one verified NTP token and `k` speculative MTP tokens. In the subsequent step, a new input is formed by appending these `k` speculative tokens and `k` new mask tokens. The newly generated NTP token (which is guaranteed to be correct) is compared against the first speculative token from the previous step. This verification continues sequentially. If a mismatch occurs, only the already verified tokens are accepted, limiting the speedup.
- **Quadratic Decoding:** This novel strategy aims to guarantee `k` new speculative tokens for verification in *every* step, irrespective of previous mismatches. It does this by interleaving `k` new mask tokens within the `k` speculative tokens from the previous step when forming the input for the next generation. For example, `[x_n, x^t_{n+1}, m_1..., m_k, x^t_{n+2}, m_1..., m_k..., x^t_{n+k}, m_1..., m_k]`. This requires a carefully constructed attention bias (Tree Attention) to prevent future mask tokens from attending to speculative extensions of earlier ones. Despite involving `k^2` masks, the overhead is negligible as `k` is small. This method guarantees a higher acceptance rate by providing consistent speculative tokens.

#### 3.6. Training Configuration

The fine-tuning is performed on the Tulu3-8B SFT model, freezing the main model weights. Only the Gated-LoRA parameters (rank 128) and the 2-layer MLP sampler module parameters are updated. Training involves 50,000 iterations using the AdamW optimizer with a flat learning rate and warmup.

This comprehensive methodology allows the pretrained LLM to be efficiently adapted for multi-token prediction, preserving its original capabilities while significantly enhancing inference speed through novel architectural and training innovations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation provides strong evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed framework in accelerating LLM inference without compromising quality.

1. **Quality Preservation through Gated LoRA:**
 - A critical finding is that **Gated LoRA successfully preserves the original model's next-token prediction (NTP) accuracy**. Figure 6a clearly shows that zero-shot accuracy on the ARC-Challenge benchmark (a measure of general language understanding) remains stable with Gated LoRA, whereas it sharply drops when standard LoRA is used.
 - Further analysis (Figure 6c) reveals that the NTP cross-entropy loss stays nearly constant with Gated LoRA, confirming that the model's core autoregressive behavior is unaffected. In contrast, standard LoRA leads to an increasing NTP loss, indicating degradation. This validates the design choice of gated LoRA, which prevents gradients from propagating through NTP tokens.

2. **Significant Speedups across Diverse Domains:**
 - The self-speculative decoding framework demonstrates substantial speedups, measured by the acceptance rate (`G/T`, tokens accepted per generation step).
 - **Table 1** presents acceptance rates for various benchmarks and domains, using `k=8` mask tokens.
 - **Code and Math tasks** (e.g., HumanEval, GSM8k) show the highest speedups, reaching nearly **5.0x to 5.35x**. This is attributed to the higher predictability of future tokens in these structured domains.
 - **General Chat and Knowledge tasks** (e.g., MMLU, AlpacaEval) achieve significant speedups of almost **2.5x**.
 - **Safety benchmarks** also show strong gains, ranging from approximately **2.8x to 3.7x**.
 - The **average speedup across all benchmarks is 3.17x** for `k=8` masks.
 - The results show a trend of **diminishing returns** as the number of masks increases, meaning adding too many masks beyond a certain point yields only marginal additional speedup.

3. **Impact of Individual Components (Ablation Studies - Figure 7):**
 - **Quadratic Decoding:** This strategy significantly improves speedup compared to linear decoding, particularly when more masks are used. This is because quadratic decoding ensures that `k` new speculative tokens are always available for verification, leading to a more consistent and higher acceptance rate.
 - **Sampler Head:** Incorporating the lightweight MLP sampler head leads to improved speedup, especially for larger numbers of masked tokens. This highlights its role in generating coherent sequences from the predicted future token distributions, which becomes more challenging as the sequence length increases.
 - **Latent Consistency Matching (LCM) Loss:** The inclusion of LCM loss further boosts speedup by improving the alignment between the multi-token predictions and the model's underlying autoregressive outputs, leading to a higher acceptance rate in speculative decoding.
 - The "advanced" configuration (Quadratic Decoding + Sampler Head + LCM Loss) consistently outperforms simpler configurations.

4. **Effect of LoRA Rank (Figure 8):**
 - The MTP model, even without the sampler head, achieves reasonable speedup with **very low LoRA ranks (e.g., 1, 4, 16)**. This supports the authors' hypothesis that the pretrained autoregressive model already possesses substantial implicit knowledge about future tokens, and even a small-rank LoRA adaptation can effectively organize and leverage this knowledge.
 - The memory overhead associated with these low LoRA ranks is negligible.
 - Adding the sampler head has a **more significant positive impact on the acceptance rate than simply increasing the LoRA rank.**
 - Interestingly, increasing the LoRA rank beyond 128 (the optimal found) can *negatively impact* speedup, possibly due to overfitting on the relatively small SFT dataset used for fine-tuning.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that the proposed framework is highly effective in accelerating LLM inference by enabling multi-token prediction. It achieves this by carefully designing a training and inference pipeline that leverages the inherent capabilities of autoregressive models, guarantees quality preservation, and benefits from novel decoding strategies and auxiliary losses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Your LLM Knows the Future: Uncovering Its Multi-Token Prediction Potential" holds significant implications for the practical deployment and future development of large language models.

1. **Revolutionizing LLM Inference Efficiency:** The most immediate and profound impact is the ability to achieve **substantial speedups (up to 5x for code/math, 2.5x for general tasks)** without any loss in generation quality. This directly addresses one of the biggest bottlenecks in LLM deployment: the high computational cost and latency of autoregressive inference. For companies like Apple, this translates to:
 - **Reduced Operational Costs:** Lower GPU utilization and energy consumption for serving LLM requests.
 - **Improved User Experience:** Faster response times for AI-powered features in products (e.g., Siri, on-device assistants, intelligent applications).
 - **Scalability:** Enabling more users or more complex LLM applications to be supported simultaneously.
 - **Feasibility of Larger Models:** Making it more practical to deploy larger, more capable LLMs in real-world scenarios.

2. **Pragmatic Adaptation of Existing Models:** The work demonstrates that it's not always necessary to build entirely new non-autoregressive architectures from scratch to achieve multi-token generation. Instead, existing, powerful autoregressive models can be efficiently adapted. This approach minimizes development effort, leverages established training procedures, and maximizes return on investment in current model training. This is a highly attractive proposition for organizations with significant investments in their current LLM infrastructure.

3. **Guaranteed Quality Preservation via Gated LoRA:** The introduction of **Gated LoRA** is a critical innovation. A common challenge in fine-tuning models for new capabilities is the risk of "catastrophic forgetting" or performance degradation on original tasks. By actively decoupling the MTP fine-tuning from the original NTP path, Gated LoRA ensures that the base model's performance remains untouched. This guarantee of quality is paramount for production systems, making the proposed method a much safer and more reliable choice for adoption.

4. **Deeper Understanding of LLM Internal Representations:** The initial observation that "autoregressive models already encode some information about future tokens" is a significant insight. It suggests that LLMs develop richer internal representations than just the immediate "next token" during pretraining. This research provides a concrete methodology to "materialize" and leverage this inherent potential, offering new avenues for understanding and exploiting the capabilities of pretrained models.

5. **Novel and Robust Speculative Decoding:** The **Quadratic Decoding** strategy is a clever improvement over standard linear speculative decoding. By guaranteeing that a fixed number of speculative tokens are always available for verification, it ensures consistent progress and higher acceptance rates, making the speedup more reliable and predictable in practical scenarios.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper opens up several promising research avenues. Applying this multi-token prediction approach during the *pretraining* stage, rather than just supervised fine-tuning, could yield even greater efficiencies and potentially lead to models that are inherently better at multi-token generation. Furthermore, exploring the interplay between this method and diffusion-based generation approaches positions multi-token prediction as a valuable bridge between fully autoregressive and fully non-autoregressive paradigms.

In conclusion, this research offers a highly practical, robust, and impactful solution for accelerating LLM inference, directly addressing a critical bottleneck in the widespread deployment of these powerful AI systems. Its innovations in training methodology, architectural adaptation, and decoding strategies contribute significantly to the field of efficient large language model serving.
