# Embrace Collisions: Humanoid Shadowing for Deployable Contact-Agnostics Motions

> [!summary] Summary
> This research empowers humanoid robots to perform diverse, contact-rich motions—such as getting up from the ground or engaging in breaking dance moves—by learning a whole-body control policy from a specialized dataset in simulation and deploying it zero-shot to a physical Unitree G1 robot. The framework introduces a transformer-based motion command interface and a multi-critic reinforcement learning approach for robust execution.

## Problem

- Humanoid robotics typically focuses on bipedal locomotion and dexterous manipulation, neglecting whole-body contact with the environment, limiting robot versatility.
- Existing model-predictive control (MPC) methods struggle with unpredictable, multi-contact sequences, and reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for humanoids often use simplified collision models and restrictive reward/termination designs that hinder extreme motions.
- There is a lack of a robust, unified motion command interface that can effectively guide humanoids through a wide range of postures, including those involving large body rotations and ground contact.

## Method

- A reinforcement learning framework is developed to train a whole-body control policy in simulation, utilizing a custom-curated "extreme-action dataset" of diverse, contact-rich human motions.
- A transformer-based encoder processes sequences of future key-frame motion targets, referenced relative to the robot's current base frame, to provide consistent command interpretation across various body orientations.
- A multi-critic (advantage mixing) technique balances sparse task rewards with dense regularization rewards for stable learning, while adaptive termination conditions allow the robot to explore and learn extreme contact-rich poses without premature failure.

## Results

- The multi-critic technique achieved substantially higher success rates in simulation, reaching **94.30%** for "Get up from ground" compared to **65.13%** with a single critic, alongside faster training convergence.
- The learned control policy successfully demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, enabling it to execute complex contact-agnostic motions like getting up from prone and breaking dance moves.
- Policies trained exclusively on the custom "extreme-action dataset" were crucial for enabling ground interaction capabilities, demonstrating a **100%** success rate on standing dance tasks despite specialized training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning contact-agnostic motions requires a specialized dataset of diverse, extreme human actions, as general human motion datasets are often biased towards standing poses and lack essential contact-rich examples.
> - The multi-critic reinforcement learning approach effectively resolves the challenge of conflicting sparse task rewards and dense regularization/safety rewards, leading to faster training, better convergence, and smoother robot behaviors.
> - Referencing motion targets relative to the robot's base frame and providing sequences of future key-frames significantly improves the success rate of complex motions by enabling the policy to learn anticipatory and preparatory behaviors.
