# Embrace Collisions: Humanoid Shadowing for Deployable Contact-Agnostics Motions

> [!summary] Summary
> This research empowers humanoid robots to perform diverse, contact-rich motions—such as getting up from the ground or engaging in breaking dance moves—by learning a whole-body control policy from a specialized dataset in simulation and deploying it zero-shot to a physical Unitree G1 robot. The framework introduces a transformer-based motion command interface and a multi-critic reinforcement learning approach for robust execution.

## Problem

- Humanoid robotics typically focuses on bipedal locomotion and dexterous manipulation, neglecting whole-body contact with the environment, limiting robot versatility.
- Existing model-predictive control (MPC) methods struggle with unpredictable, multi-contact sequences, and reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for humanoids often use simplified collision models and restrictive reward/termination designs that hinder extreme motions.
- There is a lack of a robust, unified motion command interface that can effectively guide humanoids through a wide range of postures, including those involving large body rotations and ground contact.

## Method

- A reinforcement learning framework is developed to train a whole-body control policy in simulation, utilizing a custom-curated "extreme-action dataset" of diverse, contact-rich human motions.
- A transformer-based encoder processes sequences of future key-frame motion targets, referenced relative to the robot's current base frame, to provide consistent command interpretation across various body orientations.
- A multi-critic (advantage mixing) technique balances sparse task rewards with dense regularization rewards for stable learning, while adaptive termination conditions allow the robot to explore and learn extreme contact-rich poses without premature failure.

## Results

- The multi-critic technique achieved substantially higher success rates in simulation, reaching **94.30%** for "Get up from ground" compared to **65.13%** with a single critic, alongside faster training convergence.
- The learned control policy successfully demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, enabling it to execute complex contact-agnostic motions like getting up from prone and breaking dance moves.
- Policies trained exclusively on the custom "extreme-action dataset" were crucial for enabling ground interaction capabilities, demonstrating a **100%** success rate on standing dance tasks despite specialized training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning contact-agnostic motions requires a specialized dataset of diverse, extreme human actions, as general human motion datasets are often biased towards standing poses and lack essential contact-rich examples.
> - The multi-critic reinforcement learning approach effectively resolves the challenge of conflicting sparse task rewards and dense regularization/safety rewards, leading to faster training, better convergence, and smoother robot behaviors.
> - Referencing motion targets relative to the robot's base frame and providing sequences of future key-frames significantly improves the success rate of complex motions by enabling the policy to learn anticipatory and preparatory behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has traditionally focused on robots as "bipedal mobile manipulation platforms," primarily emphasizing walking, running, and dexterous manipulation using only feet and hands. This narrow scope has led to significant advancements in bipedal stability and object interaction, but it overlooks a vast array of human motions that involve contact with the environment using other body parts, such as sitting, lying down, getting up from the ground, crawling, or rolling.

This paper directly challenges this conventional paradigm by *embracing* collisions and whole-body contact, positioning itself at the forefront of research aiming to unlock the full potential of humanoid morphology. It addresses a critical gap: enabling humanoids to perform "contact-agnostic motions" and endure "extreme torso movements"—scenarios where existing model-predictive control (MPC) methods falter due due to unpredictable contact sequences, and reinforcement learning (RL) methods struggle with simplified collision models and restrictive reward/termination designs.

**Compared to existing methods:**

- **Model-Based Control (MPC):** Traditional MPC methods often simplify the robot model (e.g., inverted pendulum) and assume only feet contact, making them computationally intractable for planning unpredictable, multi-contact sequences. This work sidesteps these computational hurdles by using a learning-based approach.
- **Learning-Based Locomotion (RL):** While RL has seen remarkable success in bipedal and quadrupedal locomotion (e.g., agile walking, rough terrain traversal, parkour), these efforts typically assume "feet-only" contact and often ignore upper-body interactions. Papers like Radosavovic et al. (2024) or Zhuang et al. (2024) focus on sophisticated bipedal gaits. This work explicitly tackles *any* body part contact, moving beyond foot-centric or hand-centric control paradigms.
- **Motion Generation and Imitation:** Prior works generating humanoid motions often either define simple interfaces (e.g., velocity commands for bipedal motion) that become ambiguous with large body rotations, or rely on dense imitation targets that demand real-time computational power and are sensitive to physical infeasibility. Datasets like AMASS, while extensive, are biased towards standing poses. This paper proposes a more robust, key-frame-based, and robot-centric motion command interface that tolerates physical deviations and operates in real-time.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Successful sim-to-real for legged robots heavily relies on GPU-accelerated simulations and simplified collision models. However, these simplifications are usually applied under the "feet-only" assumption. This paper pushes the boundary by demonstrating zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for *contact-rich* motions, proving that simplified collision shapes combined with domain randomization can still lead to robust real-world performance even when unexpected contacts occur.

In essence, this research represents a significant leap towards more versatile and human-like humanoid robots. It transitions from viewing humanoids as specialized mobile manipulators to general-purpose embodied agents capable of a much broader spectrum of physical interaction, laying groundwork for future applications in assistance, rescue, and unstructured environments where full-body contact is inevitable and beneficial.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the disparity between human and robotic interaction with the environment. Humans naturally utilize all body parts—not just feet and hands—to sit, lie down, get up, roll, and brace themselves. Conventional humanoid robot research, however, largely constrains robots to bipedal locomotion and dexterous manipulation, neglecting the potential of their full morphology. This limitation restricts humanoids from performing many crucial tasks, especially in scenarios requiring recovery from falls, navigating confined spaces, or engaging in diverse physical interactions.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets out to achieve the following key objectives:

1. **Enable Extreme Contact-Agnostic Motions:** The overarching goal is to empower humanoid robots to execute a broad spectrum of motions that involve significant and often unpredictable contact with the environment beyond just feet and hands. This includes tasks like getting up from a lying position, recovering from prone, or transitioning through kneeling postures.
2. **Develop a Unified and Effective Motion Command Interface:** To facilitate these diverse motions, a robust interface is needed that can command the robot across a wide range of postures (standing, sitting, lying). This interface must overcome the ambiguities of traditional velocity commands when the robot's base undergoes large rotations and must manage high-dimensional motion targets effectively without real-time high-level planning.
3. **Produce a Deployable Humanoid Control Policy Trainable in Simulation:** The objective is to develop a whole-body control policy robust enough to be trained entirely in simulation (leveraging GPU acceleration) and then transferred zero-shot to real-world hardware, even in the presence of stochastic contacts and physically challenging movements. This requires addressing the challenges of collision simplification and sim-to-real fidelity for multi-contact scenarios.
4. **Overcome Reinforcement Learning Challenges Specific to Extreme Motions:** This includes tackling the problem of sparse task rewards (where the robot receives feedback only at specific target frames) conflicting with dense regularization rewards (for hardware safety), and redefining termination conditions that would typically classify extreme postures as "failures" in conventional humanoid control.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to push the boundaries of humanoid capabilities, enabling robots to interact with their environment in a far more versatile and human-like manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a general humanoid motion framework that leverages zero-shot sim-to-real reinforcement learning to enable contact-agnostic motions. Their approach is characterized by several novel technical designs:

**A. Addressing the Data Challenge:**
- **Problem Identification:** Existing human motion datasets like AMASS primarily contain standing postures, lacking the contact-rich motions (sitting, lying, crawling) essential for this work. Furthermore, kinematic differences between human subjects and humanoid robots make direct retargeting of human motions often physically infeasible for robots.
- **Extreme-Action Dataset Construction:** To overcome this, the authors curate a specialized "extreme-action dataset." This dataset is constructed by selecting specific contact-rich motions from AMASS (e.g., CMU mocap 140 for getting up) and by extracting additional extreme human motions from Internet videos using the 4D-Human pose estimation system. These motions are then retargeted to the joint-level targets of the Unitree G1 robot.
- **Handling Physical Infeasibility:** Recognizing that retargeted human motions might not be perfectly feasible for the robot, the policy is designed to tolerate some deviation. Instead of strict adherence, the termination conditions are loosened, allowing the policy sufficient space to explore and generate physically viable solutions within the general spirit of the motion command.

**B. Motion Command Representation and Interface:**
- **Robot Base Frame Referencing:** All motion targets (base position, base orientation, joint positions, link positions) are expressed *relative to the robot's current base frame*. This is crucial for maintaining consistent command interpretation regardless of the robot's global orientation (standing, lying, tilted), overcoming the ambiguity of global or velocity-based commands in extreme poses.
- **Key-Frame-Based Motion Command:** The robot receives a *sequence of future motion targets* (key-frames) rather than just an immediate target. This allows the low-level policy to "plan ahead" and exhibit preparatory behaviors, significantly increasing the success rate. Each target frame includes concatenated information: `[˜pb, ˜αb, ˆθi, ˆljb, (ˆθi − θi), (ˆljb − ljb), t_passed, t_left]`, representing target base pose, joint positions, link positions (in base frame), and the current errors to these targets, along with temporal information.

**C. Network and Training Pipeline:**
- **Transformer-based Motion Command Encoder:** To handle the variable number and sequence nature of future motion targets, a multi-headed self-attention (Transformer-based) encoder is employed. This encoder processes the sequence of motion commands and their associated errors. The output latent embedding corresponding to the closest future target (smallest positive `time-left-to-target`) is then concatenated with a stack of historical proprioceptive observations (without linear velocity) and fed into an MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) to produce the real-time joint motor actions.
- **Advantage Mixing for Sparse Task Rewards:** A key challenge in training these complex, contact-rich motions is the reward design. The primary "task" reward (following the motion command) is inherently sparse, only provided when a specific key-frame is expected to be reached. This contrasts with dense regularization rewards (e.g., minimizing energy, torque, joint acceleration) necessary for hardware safety.
 - **Solution:** The authors adopt a multi-critic technique called "advantage mixing." Instead of a single critic, they use one actor network and three separate critic networks, each supervised independently by different groups of rewards (Task, Regularization, Safety). The advantages estimated by these individual critics are then combined via a weighted average for the policy gradient update, preventing sparse task signals from being overshadowed by dense regularization signals. This ensures smoother learning and better capture of the primary task.
- **Newly Designed Termination Condition:** Conventional termination conditions for humanoids (e.g., base orientation exceeding a threshold, base height below a threshold) are inappropriate for motions involving sitting, lying, or crawling, as these states would be prematurely classified as failures.
 - **Solution:** New termination conditions are defined based on the deviation from the *target motion* only at the expected reach time. A rollout terminates if the robot's base position error exceeds 0.5m, base orientation error exceeds 0.8 (imaginary quaternion part magnitude), or any joint position error exceeds 1.0 rad *relative to the current target*. This allows the robot to explore extreme postures without premature termination.

**D. Sim-to-Real Deployment:**
- **Hardware:** The trained policy is deployed on a **Unitree G1 humanoid robot**, with the policy running on an onboard **Nvidia Jetson Orin NX** at 50Hz.
- **Communication & Software:** A separate laptop sends high-level motion commands via ROS2 over Ethernet, testing communication latency. ONNX is used to accelerate the policy network and forward kinematics computations on the Jetson board.
- **Kinematics & Control:** Pytorch Kinematics is used for computing target link positions, and the forward kinematics graph is exported to ONNX for real-time onboard execution. The policy outputs target joint positions, which are then fed to the robot's built-in PD controllers running at 1000Hz.
- **Odometry and Heading Alignment:** The paper hypothesizes that odometry is not a dominant factor for these motions and uses base position targets relative to the robot's base frame. For base orientation, IMU data provides real-time world-frame orientation. A crucial step involves correcting for the initial heading misalignment between the motion command and the robot's IMU by applying a yaw correction at the start of the motion.

This comprehensive methodology allows the robot to learn and execute complex, multi-contact motions by carefully integrating advanced RL techniques, tailored data handling, and robust sim-to-real transfer strategies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted both in simulation and on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework and its core technical designs. The findings can be categorized as follows:

**A. Effectiveness of Multi-Critic (Advantage Mixing):**
- **Improved Training Efficiency and Convergence:** Quantitative results in simulation show that using the multi-critic technique leads to significantly faster training speed and a quicker convergence rate compared to a single-critic approach. This is attributed to the ability to effectively balance sparse task rewards with dense regularization/safety rewards without one overshadowing the other.
- **Higher Success Rates:** For contact-agnostic tasks, the multi-critic method achieved substantially higher success rates. For "Get up from ground," it reached 94.30% compared to 65.13% with a single critic. For "Ground Interaction," it achieved 98.25% versus 84.12%. Both methods achieved 100% for "Standing Dance," indicating that for simpler, less contact-rich motions, the difference is less pronounced.
- **Smoother Robot Behavior:** A detailed behavioral analysis (e.g., for "get-up-from-laying" motion) revealed that policies trained with multi-critic exhibit much smoother actions, characterized by the absence of extreme spikes in maximum joint acceleration. This indicates that the policy is learning more stable and hardware-safe motions while still achieving the task objective, a crucial aspect for real-world deployment.

**B. Importance of Using an Extreme-Action Dataset:**
- **Overcoming Data Bias:** The study highlighted a significant limitation of using general human motion datasets like AMASS. When trained on the entire AMASS dataset, which is predominantly composed of standing postures, the policy learned "safer" behaviors that actively avoided ground contact, often only performing squats rather than true multi-contact maneuvers.
- **Enabling Ground Interaction:** Training exclusively on the custom-built "extreme-action dataset" (comprising selected contact-rich motions from AMASS and internet videos) was crucial for the robot to successfully learn and perform motions involving ground contact with body parts other than feet. This demonstrates the critical role of specialized data in achieving novel robotic capabilities.
- **Generalization Ability:** Despite being trained on a specialized dataset for extreme actions, the policy still exhibited strong generalization to standing motions, achieving 100% success rate on "Standing Dance" tasks.

**C. Behavior Analysis and Successful Deployment of Extreme Contact-Agnostic Motions:**
- **Real-World Feasibility:** The core finding is the successful zero-shot deployment of these complex, contact-agnostic motions on the real Unitree G1 humanoid robot. This includes challenging tasks like getting up from the ground and performing breaking dance moves, which involve intricate coordination and contact with hips, knees, thighs, elbows, and hands.
- **Adaptive Behavior:** The real robot demonstrated adaptive behaviors. For instance, in a breaking dance motion where the simulator policy might rely solely on legs and knees to get up, the real robot might spontaneously use its hands for additional balance, reflecting the nuanced differences between simulation (e.g., rigid hands) and the real world (e.g., deformable rubber hands).
- **Value of Future Motion Targets:** The inclusion of a sequence of future motion targets significantly improved the success rate of complex motions. For tasks like "getting-up-from-ground," the robot developed "preparing behavior" by shifting its center of mass forward, which was crucial for successful execution, a behavior absent when only immediate motion commands were provided.

In summary, the research successfully demonstrates that by combining a novel motion command interface, a specialized dataset, and advanced RL techniques (multi-critic and refined termination conditions), humanoid robots can learn and execute a broad spectrum of complex, contact-rich motions in the real world, previously considered intractable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of humanoid robotics, fundamentally challenging the conventional understanding of humanoid capabilities. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

1. **Expanded Humanoid Capabilities:** The most immediate impact is the profound expansion of humanoid robots' functional repertoire. By "embracing collisions" and enabling contact-agnostic motions, humanoids can move beyond walking/running and dexterous manipulation to perform more diverse and human-like interactions with their environment. This includes sitting, lying down, getting up, rolling, crawling, and recovering from falls—skills crucial for operating in unstructured environments, providing assistance, or navigating confined spaces where typical bipedal locomotion is insufficient.
2. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Multi-Contact Motions:** The work successfully demonstrates zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex, contact-rich motions, which has been a persistent challenge. By showing that simplified collision shapes and domain randomization can suffice even for stochastic, full-body contacts, it validates a scalable paradigm for training advanced robot skills without extensive real-world data collection. This provides a clear pathway for developing even more sophisticated contact-rich behaviors.
3. **Novel Technical Contributions to Reinforcement Learning:** The paper introduces and validates several key technical innovations in the RL domain:
 - **Transformer-based Motion Command Encoder:** This architecture offers a robust solution for handling variable-length, sequence-based motion commands, making the control interface more flexible and expressive for complex, multi-stage motions.
 - **Advantage Mixing for Sparse Rewards:** This multi-critic approach effectively addresses the long-standing problem of balancing sparse task rewards with dense regularization rewards, leading to more stable learning, faster convergence, and safer policies for hardware deployment. This technique could be broadly applicable to other complex, sparse-reward robotic tasks.
 - **Adaptive Termination Conditions:** Redefining termination conditions to be task-specific and context-aware (i.e., based on deviation from target at expected reach time rather than fixed orientation/height thresholds) is crucial for enabling robots to explore and learn extreme poses that were previously considered "failures."
4. **Foundation for General Humanoid Intelligence:** By proposing a unified motion command interface that integrates locomotion, manipulation, and whole-body control, this work lays a critical foundation for future hierarchical control systems. It enables high-level planners or large action models to command robots with greater generality, abstracting away the low-level complexities of contact and dynamics. This is a crucial step towards developing truly intelligent and versatile humanoid agents.
5. **Accelerated Research and Development:** The ability to train complex, contact-rich skills in simulation and deploy them directly to hardware significantly accelerates the research and development cycle. It reduces the need for costly and time-consuming real-world experimentation, making advanced humanoid capabilities more accessible to a wider research community.

Despite some limitations, such as the need for higher-quality, diverse contact-rich datasets and improved human-to-robot motion retargeting, this research fundamentally alters the perception of what humanoid robots can achieve. It paves the way for a new generation of humanoids capable of seamless and versatile interaction with the physical world, unlocking potential applications in diverse fields from human assistance and disaster response to exploration and entertainment.
