# CoinRobot: Generalized End-to-end Robotic Learning for Physical Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ZhiCheng AI, Peking University, and Stanford University developed CoinRobot, a generalized end-to-end robotic learning framework that leverages diffusion-based policies and a modular software system to achieve cross-platform adaptability. The framework demonstrated superior generalizability and robustness across diverse physical manipulation tasks and environmental conditions, with the complete dataset and trained models released to the community.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic learning systems often require specialized, expensive hardware setups, limiting accessibility and requiring significant, repetitive effort for adaptation.
- Existing modular training infrastructures do not always prioritize adaptability across diverse hardware or seamless real-world deployment, leading to engineering overhead.
- Prior imitation learning models, such as explicit policies like Action Chunking Transformers (ACT), can struggle with multimodal behaviors and generalization across varied scenarios.

## Method

- **CoinRobot** introduces a unified, end-to-end architecture featuring a modular software system (Collection, Translation, Inference) designed to decouple hardware, sensors, and model platforms.
- ==The Translation Module== converts diverse robot and sensor messages into a unified JSON dictionary format, enabling hardware agnosticism and simplified integration of new robot types via ROS2 templates.
- The policy system employs Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) as its core action prediction architecture, integrating multimodal perception (FPN-based ResNet34) for robust and generalizable control.

## Results

- Diffusion policies trained with CoinRobot consistently outperformed both LeRobot-trained Diffusion Policies and ACT models, achieving up to **64%** success on the 'PickPlace' task while other methods yielded 0-**30%** success.
- A model pre-trained on 'SlamDunk' could be efficiently fine-tuned on a combined 'SlamDunk' and 'Sorting' dataset with only 50 additional epochs to successfully perform the 'Sorting' task.
- The framework demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen camera configurations in the 'Sorting' task, achieving a **20%** success rate, whereas the fixed-view baseline model completely failed (**0%** success).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion-based policies within the CoinRobot framework consistently offer superior performance and generalizability, particularly for tasks requiring diverse actions and varied item locations, compared to explicit policies like ACT.
> - A robust perception encoder, specifically an FPN-based ResNet34, is crucial for effectively capturing multi-resolution visual features and significantly enhancing overall model performance.
> - Multi-task learning strategies and fine-tuning on combined datasets enable a single model checkpoint to generalize efficiently across distinct tasks and demonstrate robustness to unseen camera configurations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic learning, particularly end-to-end and imitation-based techniques, is experiencing rapid growth, largely influenced by advancements in generative artificial intelligence. "CoinRobot" directly addresses several critical limitations and emerging needs within this dynamic landscape.

Traditionally, robotic learning systems have often required specialized, expensive, or bespoke hardware setups. Projects like ALOHA and UMI, while demonstrating success in learning from human demonstrations, typically rely on professional-grade equipment, which limits accessibility for many researchers and practitioners. Furthermore, existing modular training infrastructures, such as LeRobot, do not always prioritize adaptability across diverse hardware or seamless real-world deployment. This often leads to researchers spending significant, repetitive effort adapting their models and systems to new robot types or different training platforms.

CoinRobot enters this landscape as a generalized framework explicitly designed to overcome these barriers. Its core contribution is to "bridge this gap" by introducing a unified architecture that emphasizes cross-platform adaptability. This allows for seamless deployment across industrial-grade robots, collaborative arms, and novel embodiments "without task-specific modifications." This directly contrasts with the hardware-specific or platform-locked nature of many prior approaches.

In terms of learning paradigms, the paper acknowledges the shift towards imitation learning (IL), with behavioral cloning (BC) as a widely used technique. However, it also points out the limitations of explicit policies, such as Action Chunking Transformers (ACT), which can struggle with multimodal behaviors and generalization across varied scenarios. CoinRobot integrates and champions Diffusion-based policies (specifically Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models, DDIM) within its framework. These diffusion models are noted for addressing the instability of implicit policies and improving training stability, offering superior performance and generalizability, particularly for tasks requiring diverse actions. This positions CoinRobot at the forefront of leveraging advanced generative models for robust robotic control.

Moreover, the research landscape is moving towards models that can generalize across multiple tasks and environments. Single-task models, while effective, are not scalable for real-world applications. CoinRobot's multi-task learning approach, demonstrated by fine-tuning a pre-trained model on combined datasets to adapt to new tasks, aligns with efforts to create more versatile and "generalist" robot policies, akin to large-scale language models in NLP. The paper's mention of X-Embodiment in future work signifies an awareness and intention to integrate with large-scale, open-source robotic trajectory datasets, a crucial direction for reducing data dependency and enhancing generalization.

In summary, CoinRobot distinguishes itself by prioritizing practical utility, hardware agnosticism, and cutting-edge learning algorithms (diffusion models) to accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robotic systems. It aims to democratize access to advanced robotic learning by reducing complexity and cost, pushing the boundaries of what embodied intelligence can achieve in diverse, real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the "CoinRobot" research is to develop and validate a **generalized end-to-end robotic learning framework capable of achieving physical intelligence across diverse platforms and environments.** This primary goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from current challenges in the field:

1. **Addressing the Burden of Adaptation:** A significant motivation is to alleviate the "repetitive and time-consuming task of adapting to new robots or modifying robot systems for different model training platforms." Researchers often face substantial engineering overhead when integrating new hardware or switching between software frameworks. CoinRobot aims to provide a "general platform that can seamlessly integrate new types of robots and switch to new model training platforms as needed," thereby drastically reducing this development burden and allowing researchers to focus more on core AI challenges.

2. **Enabling Cross-Platform Adaptability and Scalability:** Existing systems frequently "struggle with scalability, adaptability to heterogeneous hardware, and objective evaluation in real-world settings." The authors are motivated to create a "unified architecture that supports cross-platform adaptability," enabling deployment across a variety of robotic arms (industrial, collaborative, novel embodiments) without requiring task-specific modifications. This objective aims to broaden the applicability and practical reach of robot learning solutions.

3. **Enhancing Generalization and Robustness through Multi-task Learning:** The paper highlights the limitations of single-task models prevalent in earlier imitation learning work. A key objective is to "successfully enable a single checkpoint to perform multiple tasks by combining datasets and applying minor adjustments to the training strategy." This motivation is to demonstrate improved generalization beyond specific tasks or environments, moving towards more robust and versatile robot behaviors.

4. **Improving Data Collection and Inference Efficiency:** The authors recognize that "code adaptation for data collection and inference can often be the most tedious part of the research process." They aim to streamline this by introducing CoinRobot's modular software system (Collection, Inference, and Translation modules) designed to decouple robots, sensors, and model platforms, thereby simplifying the integration of new hardware and sensor configurations.

5. **Promoting Open Science and Reproducibility:** A significant underlying motivation is to support the broader research community. By "releasing the complete dataset and trained models using our framework," along with "complete setup details, training insights, and lessons learned," the authors aim to facilitate replication, encourage further exploration, and contribute to the collective advancement of robotic learning. This fosters a more collaborative and transparent research ecosystem.

In essence, the "CoinRobot" project is motivated by a desire to democratize advanced robotic learning, making it more accessible, efficient, generalizable, and robust for real-world deployment, ultimately pushing the boundaries of physical intelligence in embodied systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "CoinRobot" framework adopts a comprehensive, end-to-end approach to generalized robotic learning, encompassing hardware setup, data collection, model design, training, and evaluation. Its core philosophy revolves around decoupling components to achieve maximum flexibility and adaptability.

**A. Hardware Preparation:**
The framework emphasizes support for a diverse range of hardware, moving away from expensive, specialized setups.
- **Robotic Arms:** Experiments utilized both dual-arm (FAIRINO FR5, an industrial-grade robot) and single-arm (Seed Robot, industrial-grade) configurations. The framework also explicitly states support for other cobots like AgileX Piper and Realman Gen72, indicating broad compatibility.
- **Cameras:** Various cameras are supported, including Intel RealSense D415 RGB-D cameras and Hikvision web RGB cameras. Multiple camera perspectives are used: wrist-mounted (first-person) and fixed external (global/third-person) views, often with depth information.
- **Controllers:** For data collection, an in-house designed "leader bot" with joint-to-joint length matching the follower bot is used for dual-arm tasks, providing intuitive control. For single-arm tasks, an Oculus Quest 2 headset and controller are employed. This diverse controller support highlights flexibility in human demonstration input.

**B. Software System - The CoinRobot Platform:**
The heart of the methodology is the CoinRobot software platform, structured into three key, decoupled components:
- **Collection Module:** Gathers data during remote control operations. It leverages **ROS2** (Robot Operating System 2) for communication due to its advantages in managing multi-process communication, coordination, and cost reduction. Robot manufacturers often provide ROS2 packages, simplifying integration.
- **Translation Module:** This is crucial for hardware agnosticism. It subscribes to diverse ROS2 topics from various robots and sensors, converts these messages into a **unified JSON dictionary format**, and republishes them. This means new robots only require a simple ROS2 package based on provided templates to convert their native messages into the unified format, significantly reducing adaptation efforts.
- **Inference Module:** Reuses the data-fetching capabilities of the collection module, streamlining the integration of new sensor configurations for deployment. It also offers a "model bridge template" to connect seamlessly with "any model platform," further enhancing software stack flexibility.
- **Data Alignment:** Data from multiple sensors, captured at their native frequencies, are timestamped and subsequently aligned to a common, lower frequency (e.g., 10 Hz) post-collection, ensuring temporal consistency.

**C. Data Collection:**
- A single operator performs teleoperation using the master bot/controller to complete tasks, while also arranging objects.
- Data includes robot state (60 Hz), control instructions (160 Hz), and camera images (30 Hz).
- Over 3,000 episodes across 7 real-world tasks were collected, tagged with operator names to track variations.
- Tasks are designed with "easily accessible objects" and "a degree of randomness" in initial object positions to promote generalization.

**D. Robot Policy System (End-to-End Learning):**
The policy system is modular and supports various architectures. It is decomposed into two main parts:
- **Perception Module:**
 - Integrates multimodal data: visual inputs from multiple cameras (wrist-mounted, fixed global/third-person) and low-dimensional state information (e.g., end-effector pose).
 - Processes this data using deep learning networks to generate compact embeddings.
 - Evaluated several architectures, with **FPN-based ResNet34** demonstrating superior performance in capturing multi-resolution visual features.
- **Action Prediction Module:**
 - Maps the encoded perception data into executable trajectories.
 - Employs **Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM)** as the core architecture, chosen for its ability to decouple training and inference iterations, allowing faster inference.
 - Explored both Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Transformers within this module.
 - The framework also supports other imitation learning models like Action Chunking Transformers (ACT) for comparison, highlighting its architectural flexibility.

**E. Task Design and Evaluation:**
- **Seven real-world manipulation tasks** are defined, covering both dual-arm (CleanDish, Gathering, PickPlace, CollectDish) and single-arm (SlamDunk, Sorting, Hoopla) scenarios. Each task targets specific model capabilities and incorporates variability.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Acknowledging the weak correlation between training loss and practical performance, the authors propose **Mean Squared Error (MSE) in action prediction** and **visualization of action trajectories** for more intuitive and practical assessment of policy performance, consistent with established approaches.

This methodology emphasizes modularity, hardware abstraction, and the use of state-of-the-art diffusion models, all aimed at fostering a more generalized, adaptable, and reproducible approach to real-world robot learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using the CoinRobot framework yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the framework's efficacy and the advantages of its proposed approaches:

1. **Superior Performance and Generalizability of Diffusion Policies:**
 - Diffusion-based models (DP) trained within the CoinRobot framework consistently **outperformed both DP models trained with the LeRobot framework and Action Chunking Transformers (ACT)** across diverse manipulation tasks. For instance, in the 'PickPlace' task, DP/CoinRobot achieved a 64% success rate compared to 0% for DP/LeRobot and 30% for ACT/LeRobot.
 - While ACT showed high success rates on tasks with fixed item locations, DP models, particularly within CoinRobot, demonstrated **superior generalizability to varied item locations**, which is crucial for real-world robustness.

2. **Impact of Perception Encoder Architecture:**
 - An ablation study on model architectures revealed that the **FPN-based ResNet34 perception encoder consistently demonstrated superior performance** across various tasks, effectively capturing multi-resolution visual features and enhancing embedding quality. This highlights the importance of robust visual processing for effective robot learning.

3. **Correlation Between Task Characteristics and Success Rates:**
 - **Action Types:** Tasks sharing fundamental action characteristics, even if visually different (e.g., various pick-and-place operations), exhibit latent features that are transferable. Models trained on one such task could be **efficiently fine-tuned for another related task with minimal additional training** (often just a few epochs). This suggests significant potential for leveraging pre-trained models and transfer learning.
 - **Visual Distinguishability:** Tasks requiring differentiation based on color achieved higher success rates when utilizing a ResNet-based perception encoder, emphasizing the need to tailor model architectures to task-specific visual cues.

4. **Demonstrated Model Generalization Capabilities:**
 - **Multi-task Generalization:** The framework successfully enabled a single model checkpoint to generalize across distinct tasks. A model pre-trained on 'SlamDunk' could be **fine-tuned on a combined 'SlamDunk' and 'Sorting' dataset for only 50 additional epochs** to successfully complete the 'Sorting' task. This was particularly notable given the significant differences in objects, setups, and end-effector motion profiles between these tasks, underscoring the efficiency and effectiveness of the multi-task learning strategy.
 - **Environmental Scene Generalization (Camera Views):** The model demonstrated robustness to variations in camera placement. By fine-tuning with multi-angle camera data (a fixed wrist camera plus two distinct external views), the model was able to **generalize to zero-shot, unseen camera configurations** (positions and angles) in the 'Sorting' task, achieving a 20% success rate, whereas the fixed-view baseline model completely failed (0% success rate). This indicates a significant step towards models that are less sensitive to environmental visual conditions during deployment.

5. **Reproducibility and Open-Source Contribution:**
 - The authors committed to releasing the complete dataset (over 3,000 episodes across 7 tasks) and trained models, along with comprehensive setup details, training insights, and lessons learned. This commitment is a significant practical contribution to the research community, facilitating replication and further exploration.

In essence, the findings validate CoinRobot as a robust and flexible framework for generalized robotic learning, showcasing the power of diffusion models, modular architecture design, and strategic multi-task/transfer learning for achieving real-world robotic intelligence and adaptability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "CoinRobot" framework presents several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact across the fields of robotics and artificial intelligence:

1. **Democratization of Robotic Learning:** By prioritizing cost-effectiveness, cross-platform adaptability, and ease of integration for diverse hardware, CoinRobot significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers, educators, and even hobbyists in robotics. This democratization can accelerate innovation by allowing a broader community to engage with and contribute to advanced robotic learning, moving beyond the reliance on expensive, specialized lab setups.

2. **Accelerated Research and Development Cycle:** The framework's ability to decouple robot hardware, sensors, and model training platforms drastically reduces the tedious and time-consuming efforts typically required for adaptation. This means researchers can spend less time on system integration and more time on developing novel algorithms and pushing the boundaries of physical intelligence, thereby accelerating the pace of research and development in the field.

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness for Real-World Deployment:** The demonstrated success in multi-task learning and generalization to novel environmental conditions (e.g., zero-shot camera views) is crucial for deploying robots in complex, unstructured real-world settings. Robots trained with CoinRobot's principles are less likely to fail when encountering minor variations, making them more practical and reliable for industrial automation, service robotics, and collaborative tasks. The superior performance of Diffusion policies for multimodal behaviors further enhances this robustness.

4. **Foundation for More Capable Embodied Intelligence:** By providing an end-to-end pipeline from data collection to deployment, and by focusing on learning complex behaviors from demonstrations, CoinRobot directly contributes to the advancement of "physical intelligence." This is a step towards robots that can not only execute pre-programmed tasks but also learn new, intricate manipulations and adapt to unforeseen circumstances, moving closer to truly intelligent embodied agents.

5. **Promotion of Open Science and Reproducibility:** The commitment to releasing datasets, trained models, and detailed methodologies is a major impact. This fosters transparency, allows for direct comparison of new methods, and ensures that research findings can be verified and built upon by others. This open-source ethos is vital for the collective progress of the robotics community, akin to the impact of large public datasets in other AI domains.

6. **Guidance for Future Research Directions:** The paper not only presents a solution but also offers "systematic guidelines for task design, model evaluation metrics," and insights into the correlation between task characteristics and model performance. Furthermore, it explicitly outlines future work, such as leveraging large-scale pre-trained datasets (e.g., X-Embodiment) and integrating human language instructions, aligning with cutting-edge trends in the broader AI landscape. This proactive guidance can inspire and direct future investigations, particularly in areas like reducing data requirements and enhancing logical reasoning in robots.

In conclusion, CoinRobot is a significant advancement that addresses core bottlenecks in robotic learning. Its generalized, adaptable, and open-source nature positions it as a foundational framework with the potential to significantly accelerate the development of more intelligent, versatile, and accessible robotic systems for a wide array of real-world applications.
