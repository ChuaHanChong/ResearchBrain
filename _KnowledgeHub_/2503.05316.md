# CoinRobot: Generalized End-to-end Robotic Learning for Physical Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ZhiCheng AI, Peking University, and Stanford University developed CoinRobot, a generalized end-to-end robotic learning framework that leverages diffusion-based policies and a modular software system to achieve cross-platform adaptability. The framework demonstrated superior generalizability and robustness across diverse physical manipulation tasks and environmental conditions, with the complete dataset and trained models released to the community.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic learning systems often require specialized, expensive hardware setups, limiting accessibility and requiring significant, repetitive effort for adaptation.
- Existing modular training infrastructures do not always prioritize adaptability across diverse hardware or seamless real-world deployment, leading to engineering overhead.
- Prior imitation learning models, such as explicit policies like Action Chunking Transformers (ACT), can struggle with multimodal behaviors and generalization across varied scenarios.

## Method

- **CoinRobot** introduces a unified, end-to-end architecture featuring a modular software system (Collection, Translation, Inference) designed to decouple hardware, sensors, and model platforms.
- ==The Translation Module== converts diverse robot and sensor messages into a unified JSON dictionary format, enabling hardware agnosticism and simplified integration of new robot types via ROS2 templates.
- The policy system employs Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) as its core action prediction architecture, integrating multimodal perception (FPN-based ResNet34) for robust and generalizable control.

## Results

- Diffusion policies trained with CoinRobot consistently outperformed both LeRobot-trained Diffusion Policies and ACT models, achieving up to **64%** success on the 'PickPlace' task while other methods yielded 0-**30%** success.
- A model pre-trained on 'SlamDunk' could be efficiently fine-tuned on a combined 'SlamDunk' and 'Sorting' dataset with only 50 additional epochs to successfully perform the 'Sorting' task.
- The framework demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen camera configurations in the 'Sorting' task, achieving a **20%** success rate, whereas the fixed-view baseline model completely failed (**0%** success).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion-based policies within the CoinRobot framework consistently offer superior performance and generalizability, particularly for tasks requiring diverse actions and varied item locations, compared to explicit policies like ACT.
> - A robust perception encoder, specifically an FPN-based ResNet34, is crucial for effectively capturing multi-resolution visual features and significantly enhancing overall model performance.
> - Multi-task learning strategies and fine-tuning on combined datasets enable a single model checkpoint to generalize efficiently across distinct tasks and demonstrate robustness to unseen camera configurations.
