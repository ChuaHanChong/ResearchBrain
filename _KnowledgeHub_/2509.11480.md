# Cross-Platform Scaling of Vision-Language-Action Models from Edge to Cloud GPUs

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Northeastern University systematically characterizes Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model performance and scaling behavior across a continuum of computing environments, from power-constrained edge devices to high-performance datacenter GPUs. It identifies specific architectural choices that yield improved accuracy and efficiency, demonstrating that an optimized VLA model on a modern edge device can achieve higher throughput than on an older datacenter GPU.

## Problem

- A significant gap exists in understanding VLA model performance and scaling across heterogeneous hardware platforms and varying power budgets.
- Prior studies focused predominantly on algorithmic improvements or single hardware platform evaluations, neglecting practical trade-offs in accuracy, latency, throughput, and memory utilization for diverse robotic deployments.
- Lack of comprehensive performance data impedes informed decision-making regarding VLA model selection, architectural optimization, and hardware provisioning for real-world robotic systems.

## Method

- Evaluated ==five representative VLA models==, including established baselines (**OpenVLA**, **SpatialVLA**, **OpenVLA-OFT**) and two newly developed architectures (**VOTE** variants, **QwenVLA**), with varying ==LLM backbones== and ==action heads==.
- Characterized performance across diverse hardware platforms: ==NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin== (edge, with multiple power modes) and four datacenter GPUs (NVIDIA H100, A100, A6000, V100).
- Measured key metrics including ==success rate on the LIBERO benchmark==, peak memory usage, ==inference latency==, and ==throughput== for consistent performance comparison.

## Results

- VOTE-1T achieved the highest average success rate of **96.9%** on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT (**95.3%**), while the smaller QwenVLA (1.5B backbone) surpassed the larger OpenVLA (7B backbone) with **78.8% vs. 76.5%** SR.
- QwenVLA and SpatialVLA exhibited the lowest peak memory footprints (approximately **7.4 GB** and **7.8 GB** respectively), showcasing the impact of smaller backbones on memory efficiency.
- The Jetson AGX Orin in MAX power mode achieved **55.57 Hz** throughput with VOTE-MLP4, which exceeded the NVIDIA V100 datacenter GPU's **32.28 Hz** for the same model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA architectural choices, such as action tokenization strategies and language model backbone size, critically influence inference throughput and memory footprint.
> - Modern high-end edge devices can, under certain configurations and with optimized VLA models, achieve higher throughput than older datacenter GPUs, challenging conventional hardware superiority assumptions.
> - Optimized chunked decoding strategies offer a pathway to high throughput with minimal accuracy trade-offs, providing guidance for designing efficient VLA models.
