# VLANeXt: Recipes for Building Strong VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper addresses the fragmented VLA landscape by systematically dissecting design choices and distilling 12 key "recipes" for building effective models. Applying these recipes, the researchers developed VLANeXt, a 2.5B parameter model that achieves state-of-the-art performance on the LIBERO and LIBERO-plus benchmarks, outperforming larger models like OpenVLA-OFT (7B parameters) while demonstrating strong real-world generalization.

## Problem

- The VLA (Vision-Language-Action) research landscape is fragmented, characterized by inconsistent training protocols, diverse architectural designs, and varied evaluation setups.
- This fragmentation makes it difficult to systematically understand and identify which specific design choices truly impact VLA model performance.
- A lack of a unified framework hinders clear comparisons and structured progress within the rapidly evolving VLA field.

## Method

- Systematically re-examined the VLA design space through comprehensive ablation studies across foundational components, perception essentials, and action modeling perspectives.
- Distilled 12 empirically validated "recipes" for constructing strong VLA models, focusing on efficiency and performance.
- Developed VLANeXt, a simple yet powerful VLA model, by integrating these identified optimal design choices under a unified evaluation framework.

## Results

- VLANeXt (2.5B parameters) achieved state-of-the-art success rates on both the standard LIBERO and challenging LIBERO-plus benchmarks.
- On LIBERO-plus, VLANeXt demonstrated an average success rate of 80.1%, surpassing OpenVLA-OFT (7B parameters) by 10%.
- The model exhibited strong generalization capabilities in real-world single-arm and bimanual tasks, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT and π0.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dedicated, expressive policy modules (e.g., transformer-based with meta queries), action chunking, and continuous action objectives (Flow Matching) improve action generation and coherence.
> - Stronger VLM backbones, soft VLM-policy connections, multi-view inputs, and VLM-integrated proprioception are crucial for multimodal information fusion and performance.
> - An auxiliary frequency-domain loss effectively boosts action prediction with minimal overhead, whereas temporal observation history is often not beneficial, and world modeling is effective but computationally expensive.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a developing area within artificial intelligence and robotics: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The broader research landscape has witnessed a significant shift with the advent of large foundation models, which have influenced the development of general-purpose robot control. Traditional approaches often involve training task-specific policies, whereas VLA models leverage the robust visual and language understanding capabilities of pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to directly map visual observations and natural language instructions to robot actions. This paradigm, notably introduced by RT-2, aims to enable scalable, general-purpose, language-conditioned robot policies.

Currently, the VLA research space is characterized by rapid exploration and a diverse array of proposed models. However, this rapid development has also led to a fragmented landscape with inconsistencies in training protocols and evaluation settings. This makes it challenging for researchers to discern which specific design choices are genuinely effective or contribute most significantly to performance. Prior works have explored specific aspects of VLA design, but often without a unified framework or comprehensive scope, limiting the generalizability of their findings. This paper positions itself as a systematic effort to bring structure and clarity to this evolving field by re-examining the VLA design space under a standardized framework and evaluation methodology. It aims to move beyond ad-hoc model variations towards a more principled understanding of VLA model construction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to provide a systematic and comprehensive understanding of the Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model design space. The authors aim to achieve this by re-examining various VLA design choices under a unified framework and a consistent evaluation protocol.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in the current VLA landscape:
- **Fragmentation and Inconsistency**: The rapid emergence of numerous VLA models from academia and industry has resulted in a "primordial soup" of ideas. These models often employ differing training protocols, architectural configurations, and evaluation setups. This inconsistency makes direct comparisons difficult and obscures the true impact of individual design decisions. Consequently, it is challenging to identify which specific components or strategies are most effective for building robust and generalizable VLA models.
- **Lack of Structural Understanding**: While VLA models generally follow a similar paradigm—integrating pre-trained LLMs or VLMs to process multimodal inputs for policy learning—the pipeline involves numerous design choices. These include how VLMs interface with policy modules, how policies are trained, how perceptual inputs are selected, and how actions are represented and modeled. A structured investigation into these dimensions is needed to guide future research and development.
- **Need for Practical Guidance**: Despite strong performance demonstrations, the lack of clear, distilled principles makes it difficult for researchers to build strong VLA models efficiently. The authors seek to distill practical "recipes" or key findings that can serve as a guide for model development, reducing reliance on aggressive model scaling or task-specific engineering.

The overall goal is to identify and validate a set of effective design principles that can lead to strong and efficient VLA models. The culmination of this exploration is the development of a specific model, VLANeXt, which serves as an instantiation of these principles. A secondary objective is to release a unified and user-friendly codebase to facilitate reproducibility, enable further exploration of the VLA design space, and support the development of new VLA variants on a shared, transparent foundation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic, incremental ablation study approach to explore the VLA design space. The methodology begins with a defined baseline and progressively incorporates or modifies design choices across three main dimensions, evaluating their impact on performance.

**Baseline Setup:**
The study commences with a simple VLA baseline analogous to RT-2 and OpenVLA. This baseline utilizes LLaMA 3.2 as the language backbone and SigLIP2 as the vision encoder. Since LLaMA does not inherently handle visual inputs, the SigLIP2 vision encoder is paired with it. Actions, which are continuous, are discretized using a binning strategy (256 bins after normalization to [-1, 1]) and modeled as a classification problem over these bin indices. A subset of rarely used text tokens is repurposed as action tokens within the autoregressive framework. This minimal setup provides a controlled starting point for evaluating subsequent design modifications.

**Evaluation Protocol:**
All model variants are evaluated on two benchmarks:
1. **LIBERO**: This standard benchmark (Liu et al., 2023a) assesses task learning ability across four categories (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), each with 500 expert demonstrations over 10 tasks. It measures policy generalization to different spatial layouts, objects, goals, and long-horizon tasks.
2. **LIBERO-plus**: This extension (Fei et al., 2025b) introduces systematic variations and unseen perturbations (e.g., in visual conditions, physical layouts, language instructions) to evaluate robustness and generalization more rigorously. It includes 10,030 demonstrations across the four LIBERO suites.
Experiments primarily focus on the spatial suite for detailed roadmap exploration, with insights generalized across other suites. Models are fine-tuned on the modified LIBERO dataset for 10,000 steps with a batch size of 256. Learning rates are adjusted based on model size (1e-4 for models < 3B, 5e-5 otherwise).

**Systematic Dissection of Design Space (Three Dimensions):**

1. **Foundational Components:** This dimension investigates core architectural choices and learning objectives.
 - **Policy Module Design:** Explored options include reusing text tokens (baseline), appending a class token for a separate two-layer policy head, and a larger, multi-token policy network (conceptually similar to MetaQuery).
 - **Action Chunking:** Evaluated predicting actions one step at a time versus predicting multiple future actions jointly (chunk sizes 4 and 8).
 - **Action Learning Objective:** Compared binning-based classification (baseline) with direct regression, diffusion-based losses (DDIM, flow matching), and VQ-VAE-based codebook classification.
 - **VLM Backbone Capacity:** Assessed the impact of different VLM backbones, including PaliGemma-3B, LLaMA-3.2-3B + SigLIP, Qwen3-VL-2B, and Qwen3-VL-4B.
 - **VLM-Policy Connection:** Investigated how the VLM and policy module interact, including loose (fully decoupled, MetaQuery-style), tight (layer-by-layer connection), and soft (layer-by-layer with learnable queries as a latent buffer) strategies, all using cross-attention and adaLN for timestep conditioning.

2. **Perception Essentials:** This dimension focuses on the role and integration of various input modalities.
 - **Temporal Observation History:** Explored using only the current frame (baseline) versus incorporating multiple past frames, leveraging the video capabilities of the VLM backbone.
 - **Camera View Horizon:** Compared a single third-person view (baseline) with multi-view inputs (combining third-person and wrist camera views).
 - **Proprioception Conditioning:** Examined approaches to integrate proprioceptive inputs (robot's internal state) by either omitting them (baseline), conditioning the VLM, conditioning the policy module, or conditioning both. Different integration mechanisms (linear projector, transformer-based projector, transformer projector with masked reconstruction pre-training) were also compared.

3. **Action Modelling Perspectives:** This dimension explores auxiliary objectives and designs to facilitate action generation.
 - **World Modelling:** Augmented action prediction with an auxiliary objective to predict future image tokens using Emu3.5 image tokenizer and a next-token objective, inserted between the VLM and policy module.
 - **Time Series Forecasting:** Introduced a simple auxiliary loss that minimizes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted and ground-truth actions in the frequency domain, using discrete cosine transform and weighted components.

Over 500 distinct experiments were conducted to systematically gather empirical evidence for each design choice. The findings from these ablations are then synthesized to form the "recipes" for building strong VLA models, culminating in the VLANeXt architecture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic exploration across the VLA design space yielded 12 key findings, which form a recipe for building strong VLA models and led to the development of VLANeXt.

**Findings from Foundational Components:**
- **Policy Module Design:** A separate policy head performs better than reusing text tokens directly. Furthermore, a larger, more expressive policy module (similar to MetaQuery with multiple tokens and more layers) yields a significant performance improvement.
- **Action Chunking:** Predicting multiple future actions jointly (action chunking) consistently improves performance, with a chunk size of 8 being adopted. This suggests that modeling a longer temporal window enhances action coherence.
- **Action Learning Objective:** Continuous action objectives generally outperform classification-based approaches. Regression showed strong performance, while flow matching, adopted for its ability to represent precise control signals, eventually surpassed regression at higher performance levels. VQ-VAE based classification underperformed due to the low-rank nature of robot action spaces, where simple binning is sufficient.
- **VLM Backbone Capacity:** Stronger VLM backbones consistently lead to better VLA performance. Qwen3-VL-2B was identified as an effective and efficient choice, with Qwen3-VL-4B showing further gains.
- **VLM-Policy Connection:** A "soft" connection strategy, which involves layer-by-layer interactions with learnable query buffers, slightly outperforms both loose (fully decoupled) and tight (direct layer-by-layer) connections. This suggests that a latent buffer can facilitate better transfer of representations from the VLM to the policy module.

**Findings from Perception Essentials:**
- **Temporal Observation History:** Incorporating temporal observation history (multiple past frames) did not improve action generation and, in some cases, slightly degraded performance, indicating potential noise or distraction.
- **Camera View Horizon:** Combining multiple camera views (third-person and wrist camera) significantly improves performance, providing complementary geometric cues and resolving spatial ambiguities.
- **Proprioception Conditioning:** Conditioning proprioceptive input directly within the VLM yields the best performance. Injecting it into the policy module or omitting it entirely was less effective. A linear projector for proprioception was found to be sufficient, despite transformer-based projectors showing marginal improvements at higher computational costs.

**Findings from Action Modelling Perspectives:**
- **World Modelling:** Augmenting action prediction with an auxiliary world modeling objective (predicting future image tokens) improves action generation performance. However, this benefit comes at a substantial computational cost, nearly tripling training time.
- **Time Series Forecasting:** Introducing a simple auxiliary loss that minimizes MSE in the frequency domain for action prediction improves performance. This strategy offers benefits comparable to world modeling but with negligible training overhead, acting as an effective regularization term to enhance generalization.

**VLANeXt Architecture:**
The integrated design choices, culminating in VLANeXt (2.5B parameters), include: a deeper, dedicated policy module (MetaQuery-like); action chunking (chunk size 8); flow matching as the action learning objective; Qwen3-VL-2B as the VLM backbone; soft VLM-policy coupling; multi-view inputs; VLM-side proprioception conditioning; and a frequency-domain auxiliary loss.

**Quantitative Results:**
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** VLANeXt achieved a state-of-the-art average success rate of 97.4%, outperforming prior methods such as OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%), π0.5 (96.9%), FLOWER (96.9%), and UniVLA (95.2%).
- **LIBERO-plus Benchmark:** On this benchmark, designed to test robustness and generalization, VLANeXt demonstrated a significant improvement, achieving an average success rate of 83.9%. This represents a substantial lead (13% higher) over the previous state-of-the-art, OpenVLA-OFT (69.6%).
- **Real-World Evaluations:** VLANeXt successfully adapted to real-world single-arm (table cleaning, drawer manipulation) and bimanual (basket lifting, bimanual table cleaning) tasks. It consistently showed higher success rates compared to OpenVLA-OFT and π0, demonstrating its practical deployability and adaptability across different embodiments even with limited bimanual training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes to the field of robotic learning by providing a structured and systematic analysis of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, moving beyond the current ad-hoc development paradigm. The primary significance lies in distilling a practical "recipe" comprising 12 key design choices that demonstrably lead to strong VLA model performance. This systematic approach clarifies which architectural and methodological components are genuinely impactful.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

- **Guidance for VLA Model Design:** The identified recipes offer concrete guidance for researchers and practitioners, potentially streamlining the development process for future VLA models. Specific insights, such as the effectiveness of a soft VLM-policy connection, the benefits of VLM-side proprioception conditioning, and the utility of frequency-domain objectives, can inform architectural decisions and training strategies.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** The resulting VLANeXt model demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on both standard (LIBERO) and generalization-focused (LIBERO-plus) benchmarks. Its substantial improvement on LIBERO-plus (13% higher average success rate than prior state-of-the-art) highlights the efficacy of the proposed design principles in fostering robustness to unseen perturbations. This can lead to more reliable and adaptable robotic systems.
- **Efficiency-Aware Design:** The study explicitly considers computational costs, noting that while some techniques like world modeling improve performance, their significant training overhead may render them less practical. Conversely, the frequency-domain loss provides comparable benefits with negligible additional cost, promoting the development of efficient yet high-performing models.
- **Reproducibility and Community Support:** The release of a unified and easy-to-use codebase is a significant contribution. It standardizes training and evaluation, enabling researchers to reproduce the findings, explore alternative design choices, and build new VLA variants from a transparent and shared foundation. This fosters collaborative progress and accelerates research in the VLA domain.
- **Cross-Embodiment Adaptability:** The real-world experiments demonstrate VLANeXt's ability to perform well on diverse manipulation tasks, including bimanual scenarios, even with limited bimanual training data. This indicates promising cross-embodiment adaptability, which is crucial for general-purpose robotic agents.

In summary, this research provides a foundational understanding of critical design factors in VLA models, leading to empirically validated best practices and a high-performing model. Its impact lies in bringing structure to a rapidly evolving field, fostering more principled research, and accelerating the development of capable and generalizable embodied AI agents. Future work directions, such as extending the perspective to diverse embodiments, longer-horizon reasoning, and richer world-interaction objectives, are also indicated.
