# Hybrid Rigid-Soft Robotic Gripper with Shape Adaptation, Uniform Force Distribution, and Self-Locking Capabilities

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a hybrid rigid-soft robotic gripper featuring shape adaptation, uniform force distribution, and energy-free self-locking capabilities. The gripper demonstrated a 50.05% reduction in energy consumption and a 4200g load capacity, while consistently grasping delicate and irregularly shaped objects without damage.

## Problem

- Rigid robotic grippers often damage delicate, irregularly shaped objects due to concentrated forces and lack of adaptability.
- Soft robotic grippers, while compliant, typically lack the structural rigidity for high-load tasks and precise control, leading to instability.
- Existing hybrid rigid-soft gripper designs often exhibit limitations such as restricted compliance, insufficient force control, and poor energy efficiency.

## Method

- A **hybrid rigid-soft gripper** architecture was developed, integrating three fingers, each with four joints consisting of ==rigid links== and ==compliant pneumatic actuators==.
- A ==dual ratchet-pawl self-locking mechanism== was incorporated into each joint to enable ==energy-free grasping== and enhance load capacity and locking resolution.
- Low-cost ==pneumatic chambers== were fabricated from commercially available ==nylon-polyethylene composite membranes==, simplifying manufacturing and maintenance.

## Results

- The gripper achieved a **1.75–35.29%** Difference Ratio for contact pressure distribution, indicating more uniform force compared to a rigid gripper (**56.77–66.44%**).
- It demonstrated a maximum load capacity of **4200g** with the self-locking mechanism engaged, surpassing typical soft grippers which reached **210.14g** under continuous pressure.
- Energy consumption was reduced by **50.05%** per grasp cycle compared to conventional soft grippers, thanks to energy-free holding by the self-locking mechanism.
- The gripper successfully performed non-destructive grasping of **six** types of fruits and other objects, including a field harvesting demonstration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of compliant pneumatic actuation with rigid links effectively balances adaptive grasping for delicate objects and structural stability for high loads.
> - A dual ratchet-pawl self-locking mechanism significantly reduces energy consumption by maintaining grasp without continuous power and enhances load capacity.
> - The use of low-cost, membrane-based pneumatic chambers and additive manufacturing for rigid components allows for economical fabrication and maintenance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic grippers are integral components in various automated systems, including manufacturing, healthcare, and particularly agricultural automation, where tasks involve handling delicate and irregular objects like fruits. The design of grippers typically involves a trade-off between the precision and force output of rigid structures and the adaptability and safety of soft, compliant mechanisms.

Traditional rigid robotic grippers are proficient in high-force and precise manipulation but often lack the adaptability to conform to irregularly shaped, fragile, or geometrically complex objects. This limitation is particularly pronounced in unstructured agricultural environments where targets vary in shape, size, and fragility, and can be easily damaged by concentrated forces.

Conversely, soft robotic grippers offer inherent mechanical compliance, enabling adaptive grasping with reduced control complexity and enhanced safety for delicate objects. They can passively deform to conform to object shapes, distributing contact forces and mitigating impact. However, soft grippers commonly face challenges with high-load tasks and precise control due to insufficient structural rigidity, which can lead to instability and reduced accuracy when grasping heavy or hard objects.

To address these limitations, hybrid rigid-soft grippers have emerged, aiming to combine the benefits of both approaches. Existing hybrid designs have demonstrated improvements in areas such as payload capacity, dexterous manipulation, and variable stiffness. However, these systems often present their own set of limitations, including restricted lateral compliance, insufficient force control precision, limited structural robustness under dynamic loads, and unvalidated performance under prolonged loading or energy efficiency. For example, a gripper by Zhu et al. showed high payload but limited lateral compliance, while an origami-based design by Zhang et al. achieved variable stiffness but lacked robustness for heavy loads. Keely et al.'s RISOs gripper offered versatility but lacked validation in long-term loading and energy efficiency.

This work contributes to the landscape of hybrid grippers by proposing a design that specifically targets the simultaneous achievement of shape adaptation, uniform force distribution, high load capacity, and energy-free self-locking, directly addressing the identified trade-offs and limitations of both conventional soft grippers and existing hybrid designs. The integration of a dual ratchet-pawl mechanism with segmented pneumatic chambers represents an effort to bridge the gap between compliance and load-bearing capability while enhancing energy efficiency and reducing manufacturing costs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research was to develop a robotic gripper that concurrently achieves shape adaptation, uniform force distribution across grasped objects, and energy-free self-locking capabilities. This overarching goal was motivated by the significant challenges faced in agricultural automation, specifically the persistent trade-off between compliant, adaptive grasping, balanced pressure distribution, and high load capacity, often accompanied by high energy consumption.

The specific motivations for developing this novel hybrid rigid-soft gripper included:

- **Addressing limitations of rigid grippers:** Traditional rigid grippers struggle with irregular, fragile, or geometrically complex objects common in agriculture, leading to potential damage and limited operational flexibility. The goal was to enhance adaptability for such scenarios.
- **Overcoming deficiencies of soft grippers:** While soft grippers excel in compliance, they typically lack the structural rigidity required for high-load tasks, precise control, and stability when handling heavy objects, which can compromise reliability in varied agricultural environments. The research aimed to integrate the benefits of soft compliance with improved load capacity and stability.
- **Improving upon existing hybrid designs:** Current hybrid rigid-soft grippers have demonstrated progress but still present limitations such as restricted compliance in certain directions, insufficient force control, or unaddressed energy efficiency and long-term performance. This work aimed to introduce a solution that comprehensively tackles these issues.
- **Achieving energy efficiency:** Continuous pneumatic actuation, common in soft grippers, leads to high energy consumption. A key motivation was to significantly reduce energy use by implementing a self-locking mechanism that maintains grasp without continuous power input.
- **Ensuring uniform force distribution:** Minimizing localized stress concentrations is crucial for handling delicate agricultural produce. The objective was to design a gripper that distributes contact forces more evenly, thereby preventing damage.
- **Enhancing load capacity:** Agricultural tasks can involve objects with considerable weight or requiring significant detachment forces. A critical motivation was to increase the gripper's load-bearing capability beyond that of conventional soft grippers.
- **Facilitating low-cost fabrication and maintenance:** Practical deployment in agricultural settings necessitates designs that are affordable to manufacture and easy to repair. Utilizing low-cost materials and additive manufacturing was a direct motivation to meet these economic requirements.

To achieve these objectives, the study focused on three main contributions:
1. Developing a hybrid rigid-compliant gripper architecture integrating a ratchet-pawl locking mechanism with segmented pneumatic chambers for stability and adaptive grasping.
2. Designing a low-cost, membrane-based pneumatic chamber using nylon-polyethylene composite materials for simplified fabrication and maintenance.
3. Implementing a dual ratchet-pawl self-locking mechanism to enhance joint locking resolution, increase load capacity, and reduce energy consumption.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed robotic gripper features a hybrid rigid-soft design, structured with three fingers. Each finger incorporates four joints, comprising four rigid links, three compliant pneumatic actuators, and three dual ratchet-based self-locking mechanisms. Two servo actuators are employed for disengaging the ratchet-pawl mechanisms. Each joint module is compactly designed with dimensions of 23 mm length, 25 mm width, and 23 mm height.

**A. Finger Design:**
The finger design integrates compliant pneumatic actuation units with rigid structural components. The pneumatic chambers are fabricated from thin plastic membranes, designed to deform significantly upon pressurization, thereby driving joint bending and allowing the fingers to conform to object geometries. The rigid components provide structural support and maintain positional accuracy, especially under higher loads. The four joints of each finger bend under pneumatic actuation, facilitating adaptive grasping. Each joint exerts a relatively low force during actuation, which helps in mitigating local stress concentration and reducing the risk of surface damage to delicate objects. The joints are connected in series, offering independent rotational degrees of freedom to ensure compliance and adaptability. The mechanical design of the joint axes aims to minimize friction and wear, contributing to long-term reliability.

**B. Dual-Ratchet Pawl Mechanism Design:**
A dual ratchet-pawl self-locking mechanism is incorporated into each joint to reduce energy consumption and increase the grasping load capacity. This mechanism allows the gripper to maintain a stable grasp without continuous pneumatic power once the desired bending position is achieved. Unlike single-ratchet mechanisms that may have limited angular resolution, the dual-ratchet structure, assembled in an offset configuration, doubles the rotational resolution (e.g., from 15° to 7.5°). Both ratchets in a system possess identical tooth count and pitch. Spring-loaded pawls ensure consistent engagement with the ratchet teeth during motion, enabling rapid and robust locking. For resetting, a servo motor tightens a 12-strand cable, disengaging the pawl and allowing the joint to return to its initial position. The cable is selected for its flexibility, tensile strength, and toughness. The ratchet components are fabricated using additive manufacturing, which supports cost reduction and rapid prototyping.

**C. Low-Cost Pneumatic Chambers:**
The pneumatic actuation system utilizes low-cost, commercially available check-valve membranes made from a nylon-polyethylene composite material. This membrane-based design reduces manufacturing complexity and cost compared to chambers requiring dedicated molds or precise machining. The material is inexpensive, and the design simplifies assembly and replacement, improving maintainability. The segmented configuration of the chambers provides compliance, allowing the gripper to conform to diverse object geometries, suitable for handling fragile agricultural products.

The fabrication process for the air chambers involves:
1. Purchasing strip-shaped check-valve membranes.
2. Measuring and marking the required lengths for each finger segment (e.g., 45 mm).
3. Performing partial heat sealing along the marked positions, leaving areas for perforation.
4. Punching a hole in the center of each sealed section, trimming excess material, and sealing the tail end to a PU air tube with hot-melt adhesive.

**D. Experimental Setup and Evaluation:**
The gripper was mounted on an aluminum profile test bench. An air pump provided pneumatic power, and a digital DC power supply recorded power consumption. Resistive thin-film pressure sensors (8 mm diameter, RP-C18.3-LT) embedded in a silicone layer on the contact surfaces measured joint contact pressures, with data collected via an Arduino UNO. Spherical models of 50 mm, 60 mm, and 70 mm diameters, 3D-printed from PLA (15% infill), were used as test objects.

Five main experiments were conducted:
- **Grasping Force Test:** Evaluated joint contact pressures at 5 kPa input pressure on spherical models (50, 60, 70 mm), comparing the proposed gripper with a rigid parallel-link gripper. A "Difference Ratio" quantified load distribution uniformity.
- **Grasping Force Tests under External Loads:** Assessed joint pressure responses under a longitudinal external pulling force (2N increments up to 10N) while the gripper maintained a grasp solely via the self-locking mechanism.
- **Load Capacity Test:** Determined the maximum load-bearing capacity by suspending weights, comparing the proposed gripper (5 kPa actuation, then self-locking) with a conventional soft gripper (under 5-30 kPa continuous pressure). A destructive test using a dynamometer measured the ultimate load limit.
- **Energy Consumption Test:** Measured power consumption during the pneumatic actuation, self-locking holding, and reset stages. Total energy consumption was calculated and compared with a conventional soft gripper requiring continuous pneumatic actuation.
- **Fruit Grasping and Harvesting Tests:** Evaluated adaptability and versatility by grasping various real objects (six types of fruits, tennis ball, square box, cylindrical coil) and performing a field harvesting demonstration of a yellow peach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation demonstrated the performance of the proposed hybrid rigid-soft gripper across several key metrics:

**A. Grasping Force Test (Experiment I):**
The proposed gripper consistently exhibited lower contact pressures and more uniform load distribution compared to a rigid parallel-link gripper when grasping 50 mm, 60 mm, and 70 mm spherical models.
- **Difference Ratio:** The proposed gripper achieved significantly lower Difference Ratios between distal and Middle II joint pressures (1.75–35.29%) across all object sizes. For instance, with a 60 mm sphere, the proposed gripper showed a 1.75% Difference Ratio, indicating nearly uniform load sharing (0.56 N vs. 0.57 N).
- **Comparison with Rigid Gripper:** The rigid gripper consistently showed higher Difference Ratios (56.77–66.44%) and more pronounced distal force concentration (e.g., 1.49 N vs. 0.50 N for 60 mm sphere), along with greater pressure variability.
- **Contact Force Levels:** The maximum contact forces remained below surface damage thresholds (e.g., 1.8 N for peaches), indicating suitability for delicate objects. The three-finger configuration of the proposed gripper contributed to improved contact distribution and grasp stability.

**B. Grasping Force Tests under External Loads (Experiment II):**
During the self-locking stage, when an external longitudinal pulling force was applied to grasped spherical models (50 mm and 60 mm), the gripper maintained a relatively balanced force distribution among its joints.
- As the external pulling force increased (from 2 N to 10 N), the pressure at the distal joint generally rose, while pressures at the other joints gradually decreased. This indicated an adaptive response to external disturbances, with the distal joint bearing an increasing share of the load as the pulling force intensified, while the overall grasp remained stable due to the self-locking mechanism.

**C. Load Capacity Test (Experiment III):**
The proposed gripper demonstrated a significantly higher load-bearing capacity compared to a conventional soft gripper.
- **Conventional Soft Gripper:** Achieved maximum load capacities ranging from 45.14 g (at 5 kPa) to 210.14 g (at 30 kPa) under continuous pneumatic pressure.
- **Proposed Gripper:** In a destructive test, the proposed gripper sustained an approximate maximum load capacity of 4200 g (42 N) after pneumatics were turned off and the self-locking mechanism engaged. Failure occurred at the joint connections, indicating the robustness of the self-locking mechanism itself.

**D. Energy Consumption Test (Experiment IV):**
The self-locking mechanism resulted in a substantial reduction in energy consumption.
- **Proposed Gripper:** Total energy consumption per grasp cycle was approximately 42.6 J. This included 41.0 W during the 0.8 s pneumatic actuation stage and 4.9 W during the 2 s reset stage. Crucially, no additional energy was required during the self-locking holding stage.
- **Conventional Soft Gripper:** Required continuous energy input, consuming an average of 41.0 W, leading to a total energy consumption of 85.28 J over a 2.08 s duration for maintaining the grasp.
- **Energy Reduction:** The proposed gripper achieved a 50.05% reduction in total energy consumption compared to the conventional soft gripper, attributed to the elimination of continuous pneumatic pressure during grasp maintenance.

**E. Fruit Grasping and Harvesting Tests (Experiment V):**
The gripper demonstrated broad adaptability and versatility in handling diverse objects.
- **Object Variety:** Stable grasps were achieved across six types of fruits (yellow peach, pear, July red pear, apple, kiwifruit, plum) and three daily objects (tennis ball, square box, cylindrical coil) in all five trials per object.
- **Non-destructive Grasping:** No noticeable surface damage was observed on the fruits, highlighting the gripper's compliance and distributed contact for deformable objects.
- **Field Application:** A field harvesting experiment with a yellow peach successfully demonstrated the gripper's ability to grasp and detach fruit without dropping or damage under real-world conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The presented hybrid rigid-soft robotic gripper represents a notable development in addressing critical challenges within robotic manipulation, particularly for agricultural automation. Its significance lies in successfully integrating conflicting design requirements—compliant adaptation, high load capacity, and energy efficiency—within a single, cost-effective system.

**Significance:**
- **Bridging the Gap:** The design effectively bridges the gap between the adaptability and gentleness of soft grippers and the stability and high force output typically associated with rigid grippers. This combination enables the gripper to handle delicate, irregularly shaped objects without damage while also securely grasping heavier items or resisting external forces.
- **Enhanced Performance Metrics:** The experimental results demonstrate a substantial improvement in key performance metrics:
 - **Uniform Force Distribution:** The ability to distribute contact forces more evenly across an object's surface minimizes localized stress, which is crucial for handling fragile items like fruits and vegetables, thereby reducing post-harvest damage.
 - **Superior Load Capacity:** The integration of the dual ratchet-pawl self-locking mechanism allows the gripper to sustain loads of up to 42 N, significantly exceeding the capabilities of conventional soft grippers (typically below 0.21 N). This robust load-bearing capability is vital for tasks involving heavier produce or fruits requiring significant force for detachment.
 - **Reduced Energy Consumption:** The energy-free self-locking mechanism leads to an approximate 50% reduction in energy consumption per grasp cycle. This is a critical factor for robotic systems deployed in environments with limited power supply or requiring extended operational periods, such as remote agricultural fields.
 - **High-Resolution Locking:** The dual-ratchet design enhances the angular resolution of the locking mechanism, contributing to more precise and stable grasps.
- **Low-Cost and Manufacturability:** The use of low-cost, commercially available nylon-polyethylene composite membranes for pneumatic chambers and additive manufacturing for ratchet components contributes to a design that is economical to fabricate and maintain, promoting scalability and broader adoption.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Agricultural Automation:** The gripper offers a robust and efficient solution for scalable agricultural harvesting and manipulation tasks. Its ability to non-destructively grasp a variety of fruits, coupled with high load capacity and energy efficiency, makes it a suitable candidate for tasks ranging from delicate fruit picking to sorting and packaging.
- **Broader Robotic Applications:** Beyond agriculture, the principles demonstrated by this gripper could be applied to other fields requiring compliant yet strong manipulation, such as:
 - **Logistics and Warehousing:** Handling diverse items with varying fragility and weight.
 - **Healthcare:** Assisting in tasks requiring gentle interaction with human tissues or medical devices.
 - **Service Robotics:** Enabling robots to interact more safely and effectively with unstructured environments and diverse objects.
- **Future Development of Hybrid Systems:** This work provides a foundation for the design of future hybrid rigid-soft robotic systems, demonstrating a successful integration strategy for achieving multiple, often conflicting, performance objectives.

While the current design shows limitations in independent joint control and optimal grasping for non-rounded or concave objects, and operates within a finite object size range, its modularity offers avenues for future design adjustments. The demonstrated capabilities, particularly in balancing compliance, load, and energy, position this gripper as a practical and impactful development in robotic manipulation. Further research focusing on actuator durability and sensory feedback integration could enhance its robustness and autonomy for long-term, diverse applications.
