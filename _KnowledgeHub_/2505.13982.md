# Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion with Predictive Force Attention for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed AdapTac, a visuo-tactile fusion method that uses force-guided attention and self-supervised future force prediction to dynamically prioritize visual and tactile inputs for dexterous manipulation. This approach achieved a 93% average success rate on complex real-world tasks and demonstrated 75% generalization capability on unseen objects.

## Problem

- Effectively integrating heterogeneous visual and tactile sensor data for dexterous manipulation, given their distinct characteristics (global vs. local, contextual vs. precise).
- Existing visuo-tactile fusion methods struggle with dynamic adaptation, often employing fixed strategies or relying on manually defined thresholds.
- Ensuring appropriate utilization of tactile feedback, which can be noisy or overshadowed by visual data, especially during critical contact-rich phases.

## Method

- Implemented a ==Force-Guided Attention Fusion (FGAF)== module that uses a cross-attention mechanism, querying force embeddings against visual and tactile features to dynamically weigh modality importance.
- Introduced a self-supervised Future Force Prediction (FFP) auxiliary task, where a diffusion head predicts future forces to provide temporal context and reinforce tactile input.
- Integrated these components into a 3D diffusion model-based imitation learning framework (AdapTac) to predict dexterous robot actions.

## Results

- Achieved a **93%** average success rate on three complex real-world dexterous manipulation tasks (Open Box, Reorientation, Flipping), outperforming all baselines.
- The future force prediction and guidance mechanism improved task success (e.g., from **50%** to **90%** on Flip task) and significantly reduced average episode length (AEL) by providing crucial temporal context.
- Demonstrated robust generalization with a **75%** average success rate on five unseen objects with varying physical properties.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The relative importance of visual and tactile modalities dynamically shifts during manipulation, requiring an adaptive fusion mechanism.
> - Contact-induced force provides a natural and effective self-supervisory signal to guide attention between visual and tactile inputs.
> - Incorporating future force prediction offers critical temporal context, enhancing tactile utilization and contributing to more efficient and robust manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a crucial challenge in robotic manipulation: effectively integrating and utilizing multi-sensory data, specifically vision and touch, to enable robots to perform dexterous tasks with generalization capabilities akin to humans. The broader research landscape is characterized by increasing efforts to move beyond single-modality perception for robotics, recognizing that real-world interaction demands a richer, more nuanced understanding of the environment.

**Challenges in Visuo-Tactile Fusion:**
The authors highlight the inherent **heterogeneity** of visual and tactile modalities. Vision provides global, contextual information, while touch offers local, precise feedback on physical contact. Bridging these distinct characteristics is a significant hurdle. Prior works have largely explored two main strategies:
1. **Data-level fusion:** Directly combining raw or preprocessed inputs (e.g., concatenating point clouds and tactile data into a unified 3D representation). While preserving fine details, this struggles with differing spatial resolutions, sampling rates, and noise distributions across modalities.
2. **Feature-level fusion:** Encoding each modality separately into latent representations before combining them, often through simple concatenation or more complex self-supervised pretraining (e.g., cross-modal prediction, contrastive learning, transformer-based reconstruction). These methods aim for comprehensive representations but frequently overlook a critical aspect: the **dynamic importance of each modality at different manipulation stages**.

**Limitations of Prior Adaptive Approaches:**
Some prior works have attempted to adapt modality weighting. For example, FoAR predicts contact probability to adjust tactile feature weights, preventing noise interference. However, such methods often rely on **manually defined thresholds** for contact labeling and frequently **assume visual dominance**, limiting their scalability and generalization across diverse tasks and contact dynamics. The paper explicitly states that these approaches "overlook that different modalities require varying levels of attention at different manipulation stages" and that, in some cases, "additional modalities can even be a distraction."

**This Work's Position:**
Li et al.'s work, Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion with Predictive Force Attention (AdapTac), directly tackles these limitations. It positions itself as an advancement in **adaptive, context-aware visuo-tactile fusion** for dexterous manipulation. By leveraging contact-induced force as a natural signal for attention guidance and incorporating self-supervised future force prediction, AdapTac moves beyond fixed assumptions and manual tuning. It aims to achieve a more human-like adaptive sensory processing, where the robot can autonomously determine "when, where, and how contact occurs" and appropriately integrate visual and tactile information.

The paper draws upon advancements in several sub-fields:
- **3D Imitation Learning:** Leveraging modern techniques like Diffusion Policy, Action Chunk Transformer (ACT), and 3D point cloud-based policies (e.g., RISE) for robust action generation.
- **Tactile Sensing for Dexterous Manipulation:** Building on the development of compact and robust tactile sensors (e.g., magnetic sensors like PaXini, which provide tri-axial force feedback), and recent learning-based approaches for pretraining and diverse tactile representations.
- **Multimodal Representation Learning:** Improving upon existing visuo-tactile fusion methods that either combine raw data or feature embeddings, by introducing a dynamic attention mechanism.

In essence, AdapTac aims to create a more intelligent and flexible visuo-tactile policy that can autonomously arbitrate between visual and tactile inputs based on the immediate task context and interaction dynamics, thereby enhancing robustness and generalization in complex, contact-rich robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from observing human dexterity. Humans seamlessly switch their focus between global visual information and local tactile feedback depending on the task at hand and the current stage of manipulation (e.g., using vision to locate an object, then touch to finely adjust grip and force). Current robotic systems often struggle to replicate this adaptive sensory processing, leading to limited generalization and robustness in complex, contact-rich tasks.

The authors identify several specific gaps in existing visuo-tactile fusion methods:
1. **Lack of adaptive attention:** Many methods apply a fixed fusion strategy throughout a task, failing to recognize that the relative importance of visual and tactile information changes dynamically.
2. **Reliance on manual tuning:** Adaptive approaches often depend on predefined thresholds or human labeling to determine when to prioritize one modality over another, which limits scalability and generalization.
3. **Data imbalance and underutilization of tactile data:** Visual data, being richer and more global, can sometimes overshadow crucial tactile feedback, or tactile data might be noisy and distracting if not integrated carefully.

Based on these motivations, the paper sets out the following key objectives:

- **Objective 1: Develop an adaptive visuo-tactile fusion mechanism.** The primary goal is to create a module that can dynamically adjust the attention weights assigned to visual and tactile features. This mechanism should enable the robotic policy to prioritize the most relevant sensory input at any given moment during manipulation.
- **Objective 2: Eliminate the need for human labeling.** The adaptive fusion should be guided by inherent signals from the interaction, specifically contact-induced force, rather than requiring manual annotations or task-specific thresholds. This aims to improve the method's generalization and ease of deployment.
- **Objective 3: Reinforce the tactile modality and improve data balance.** To ensure that tactile feedback is appropriately leveraged, especially in contact-rich phases, the policy needs a mechanism to explicitly encourage its use. This also addresses potential data imbalance where visual information might otherwise dominate.
- **Objective 4: Provide temporal context for attention guidance.** The fusion mechanism should not only consider the current state but also anticipate future interactions to guide attention more effectively, making the weighting context-aware and efficient.
- **Objective 5: Demonstrate effectiveness and robustness in real-world dexterous manipulation.** The proposed approach must be validated through extensive real-world experiments on complex, fine-grained tasks, showcasing superior performance, adaptability, and generalization compared to existing baselines, including on unseen objects.

In essence, the paper aims to answer the fundamental question: "how can we design multisensory robotic policy frameworks that effectively bridge the heterogeneity between vision and touch, leveraging their complementary strengths?" by proposing a novel, self-supervised, and force-guided adaptive fusion strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion with Predictive Force Attention (AdapTac)**, integrates a force-guided attention mechanism with a self-supervised future force prediction task within an imitation learning framework.

**Robot System Setup:**
The hardware consists of:
- A 7-DoF Flexiv Rizon 4 robot arm for general manipulation.
- A customized 16-DoF Leap Hand dexterous hand with four fingers.
- **PaXini tactile sensors** distributed on each fingertip and fingerpad. These magnetic sensors provide continuous tri-axial force feedback (F ∈ R^3) from a 3x5 array of taxels, making them suitable for fine-grained contact sensing.
- An Intel RealSense L515 camera mounted diagonally for visual input (point clouds).
For expert demonstration collection, an Intel RealSense D415 camera with HaMeR tracks human hand poses, which are then retargeted to the robot via Dexpilot. Both demonstration and inference run at 5 Hz.

**Problem Formulation:**
The objective is to learn a policy π that predicts the next `n` robot actions (A_t ∈ R^25, comprising 3D translation, 6D rotation, and 16-DoF hand joint positions) given a history of observations {O_t-h+1..., O_t}. Each observation O_t includes point cloud data (O_pc ∈ R^(N×3)) and tactile inputs (O_tac ∈ R^(120×12), representing data from 120 taxels, each with 12 features from).

**Core Components of AdapTac:**

1. **Force-Guided Attention Fusion Module:**
 - **Feature Extraction:**
 - A sparse convolutional encoder extracts **point cloud features (Z_pc)** from O_pc.
 - A pretrained tactile encoder extracts **tactile features (Z_tac)** from O_tac.
 - **Force Embedding:** The observed net force (F_nO), calculated as the sum of tri-axial forces from all taxels (transformed to the camera coordinate frame), is projected into an embedding (e_F) using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) g_F.
 - **Attention Mechanism:** The core of the adaptive fusion lies in a cross-attention module:
 - **Query (Q_F):** Generated from the force embedding e_F (or later, the guide force F_nG).
 - **Keys (K) and Values (V):** Formed by concatenating the dimension-aligned point cloud features (e_pc = g_pc(Z_pc)) and tactile features (e_tac = g_tac(Z_tac)).
 - **Weight Calculation:** Attention weights (α_pc, α_tac) are computed between Q_F and K using a scaled dot-product attention followed by a softmax function.
 - **Fused Representation:** The weights are then applied to the corresponding values to produce the fused feature representation (Z_fuse = α_pc V_pc + α_tac V_tac). This allows the policy to dynamically adjust the importance of visual and tactile features based on the current force signals.

2. **Self-Supervised Future Force Prediction and Guidance:**
 - **Motivation:** To overcome the bias towards vision and ensure proper utilization of tactile feedback, especially when contact is critical, an auxiliary self-supervised task is introduced.
 - **Future Force Predictor:** A transformer-based diffusion head takes the current visual and tactile features as input and predicts the **future net force (F_nP)** for the next `n` steps.
 - **Guide Force (F_nG):** The observed net force (F_nO) and the predicted future forces (F_nP) are concatenated to form a comprehensive "guide force."
 - **Guided Query:** This F_nG is then used to generate the query (Q_F) for the force-guided attention module, effectively providing temporal context about contact dynamics. This explicit prediction encourages the network to better integrate tactile information and anticipate future interactions.

3. **Visuo-Tactile Policy Learning:**
 - **Base Policy Architecture:** AdapTac integrates these components into a 3D diffusion model-based imitation learning framework, specifically using RISE as the base policy architecture.
 - **Action Prediction:** The final fused features (Z_fuse) are passed to a diffusion action head to predict the future action chunk.
 - **Loss Function:** The total loss (L) during training combines the standard policy loss (L_π) with an additional self-supervised future force prediction loss (L_ffp): L = L_π + αL_ffp, where α is a hyperparameter balancing the two losses.

The overall approach is designed to create a unified framework where force signals not only indicate current contact but also predict future interactions, enabling an intelligent and adaptive weighting of visual and tactile modalities without explicit human supervision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on three real-world, contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks: **Open Box**, **Reorientation**, and **Flipping**. Each task involved complex finger coordination and force application.

1. **Manipulation Policy Comparison (Table I):**
 - **AdapTac (Ours) achieved an average success rate of 93%**, significantly outperforming all baselines. It achieved 100% on Open Box, 90% on Reorientation, and 90% on Flipping.
 - **RISE (vision-only)** performed reasonably well on Open Box (90%) and Reorientation (90%), where visual cues are dominant for reaching and overall pose estimation. However, it struggled significantly on the Flip task (40%), which heavily relies on precise tactile feedback under visual occlusion. This highlights the limitations of vision-only policies for fine-grained contact manipulation.
 - **3DTacDex-P (visuo-tactile concatenation)** showed poor overall performance (40% average), even underperforming RISE. The authors attribute this to potential overfitting to tactile patterns in the expert demonstrations, while struggling with visual variations in the larger workspace, suggesting that simple concatenation is insufficient for robust fusion.
 - **FoAR (force-aware reactive policy with contact prediction)** was unstable, achieving only 50% average success. While it matched RISE on Reorientation (90%), it failed on Open Box (20%) and Flip (40%). The main reason identified was its reliance on manually set thresholds for contact labeling, which proved unreliable for tasks with frequent and dynamic contact changes.

2. **Effectiveness of Force-Guided Attention Fusion (Table II):**
 - When only the force-guided attention fusion (FGAF) module was included (Ours w/o FFPG), the average success rate increased from 40% (no fusion or guidance) to 67%. This demonstrated that the attention module alone is effective in allowing the policy to learn adaptive weighting.
 - However, analysis of attention weights revealed that visual features were still typically prioritized over tactile features, likely due to data imbalance. This explains why its performance was similar to RISE in some tasks, underscoring the need for further tactile reinforcement.

3. **Importance of Future Force Prediction and Guidance (Table III):**
 - An ablation study on the Flip task revealed the critical role of the future force prediction and guidance mechanism.
 - Without both FFP and FFG, the success rate was 50%, with a high average episode length (AEL) of 221.
 - Introducing **Observed Force Prediction (OFP) and Guidance (OFG)** improved the success rate to 70% and reduced AEL to 182, showing that explicit force prediction and guidance are beneficial.
 - Replacing OFP with **Future Force Prediction (FFP)** further boosted the success rate to 90% (AEL 166), matching the full AdapTac's success rate but with longer episode lengths. This confirmed the superior importance of predicting *future* forces.
 - The full AdapTac, which includes both FFP and FFG (using both observed and future forces as guide force), achieved a 90% success rate with the lowest AEL of 113. This indicates that while FFP helps achieve high success, FFG is crucial for efficiency, robust execution, and avoiding risky behaviors (e.g., continuously squeezing the object), as it provides critical temporal context.
 - Both OFP and FFP conditions showed that more attention was given to the tactile modality upon contact, confirming that force prediction effectively reinforces tactile input and helps balance attention.

4. **Analysis of Attention (Fig. 3):**
 - Visualizations confirmed the adaptive nature of AdapTac's attention. During **reaching stages**, visual attention dominated.
 - As the robot's hand made **contact and began manipulation**, attention shifted significantly towards the tactile modality.
 - For tasks like Reorientation, while tactile attention increased during contact, visual attention remained relatively high. This was attributed to the continued need for visual feedback to assess global task progress, such as reaching the target orientation.

5. **Generalization on Unseen Objects (Table IV & Fig. 4):**
 - AdapTac demonstrated strong generalization, achieving a 75% average success rate on five unseen objects with varying colors and geometries (e.g., a small paper cup, whiteboard eraser). This highlights the robustness of the adaptive fusion approach to novel environmental conditions and object properties.

In summary, the main findings robustly demonstrate that AdapTac's novel force-guided attention and self-supervised future force prediction effectively address the challenges of adaptive visuo-tactile fusion, leading to significantly improved performance, adaptability, and generalization in complex dexterous manipulation tasks in the real world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion with Predictive Force Attention for Dexterous Manipulation" holds significant implications for the field of robotics and artificial intelligence, offering several advancements and potential impacts:

1. **Novelty in Adaptive Multimodal Fusion:** The paper introduces a highly novel approach to visuo-tactile fusion by proposing a **force-guided cross-attention module** paired with a **self-supervised future force prediction auxiliary task**. This is a significant departure from prior methods that often rely on fixed fusion strategies or manual labeling. The idea of using force signals as a dynamic query for attention is intuitive and effective, mimicking how biological systems might prioritize sensory information.

2. **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation Capabilities:** By enabling robots to adaptively weigh visual and tactile inputs, AdapTac empowers them to perform complex, fine-grained, and contact-rich manipulation tasks with unprecedented success rates in real-world scenarios. The demonstrated performance on tasks like "Flipping" (where vision-only policies fail) showcases the critical role of this adaptive fusion for achieving high dexterity. This directly contributes to bridging the gap between current robotic capabilities and human-level manipulation.

3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** A key limitation of many robotic policies is their fragility to variations in objects or environments. AdapTac's strong performance on unseen objects with diverse geometries and colors demonstrates its robust generalization capabilities. This is vital for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic real-world settings where pre-programmed solutions or highly specific training data are impractical.

4. **Elimination of Manual Labeling and Fixed Assumptions:** The self-supervised nature of the future force prediction task and the inherent guidance from force signals eliminate the need for laborious human labeling of contact states or predefined thresholds. This significantly reduces the engineering effort required for system setup and improves scalability, making the approach more practical for broader applications. It also moves away from limiting assumptions like "vision dominance," allowing the policy to genuinely adapt.

5. **Addressing Data Imbalance and Tactile Underutilization:** The explicit reinforcement of the tactile modality through future force prediction directly addresses the common problem of visual data overpowering tactile signals. This ensures that crucial contact information is leveraged effectively, especially during phases of manipulation where touch is paramount, leading to more balanced and efficient sensory processing.

6. **Inspiration for Future Research:** The success of AdapTac opens new avenues for research in several areas:
 - **Multimodal Learning:** The approach can inspire similar adaptive fusion strategies for other sensory modalities (e.g., proprioception, audio) in robotics or other AI domains.
 - **Self-Supervised Learning for Robotics:** The effective use of an auxiliary self-supervised task for force prediction highlights the potential for creating richer supervisory signals from robot-environment interactions without human intervention.
 - **Embodied AI and Human-Robot Interaction:** More dexterous and adaptable robots can lead to more seamless human-robot collaboration and more capable embodied AI systems.

7. **Potential for Industrial and Service Applications:** The ability to handle diverse objects and perform delicate, contact-rich tasks could have a transformative impact on industries requiring fine manipulation, such as electronics assembly, pharmaceutical handling, quality control, and even domestic service robotics.

While the authors acknowledge limitations, such as not guaranteeing complete success and suggesting combination with reinforcement learning for further robustness, the current work represents a substantial leap forward. Its innovative use of force as an adaptive attention mechanism, coupled with predictive capabilities, sets a new benchmark for visuo-tactile fusion in dexterous manipulation, paving the way for more intelligent, flexible, and human-like robotic interaction with the physical world.
