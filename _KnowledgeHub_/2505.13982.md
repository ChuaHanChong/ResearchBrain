# Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion with Predictive Force Attention for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed AdapTac, a visuo-tactile fusion method that uses force-guided attention and self-supervised future force prediction to dynamically prioritize visual and tactile inputs for dexterous manipulation. This approach achieved a 93% average success rate on complex real-world tasks and demonstrated 75% generalization capability on unseen objects.

## Problem

- Effectively integrating heterogeneous visual and tactile sensor data for dexterous manipulation, given their distinct characteristics (global vs. local, contextual vs. precise).
- Existing visuo-tactile fusion methods struggle with dynamic adaptation, often employing fixed strategies or relying on manually defined thresholds.
- Ensuring appropriate utilization of tactile feedback, which can be noisy or overshadowed by visual data, especially during critical contact-rich phases.

## Method

- Implemented a ==Force-Guided Attention Fusion (FGAF)== module that uses a cross-attention mechanism, querying force embeddings against visual and tactile features to dynamically weigh modality importance.
- Introduced a self-supervised Future Force Prediction (FFP) auxiliary task, where a diffusion head predicts future forces to provide temporal context and reinforce tactile input.
- Integrated these components into a 3D diffusion model-based imitation learning framework (AdapTac) to predict dexterous robot actions.

## Results

- Achieved a **93%** average success rate on three complex real-world dexterous manipulation tasks (Open Box, Reorientation, Flipping), outperforming all baselines.
- The future force prediction and guidance mechanism improved task success (e.g., from **50%** to **90%** on Flip task) and significantly reduced average episode length (AEL) by providing crucial temporal context.
- Demonstrated robust generalization with a **75%** average success rate on five unseen objects with varying physical properties.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The relative importance of visual and tactile modalities dynamically shifts during manipulation, requiring an adaptive fusion mechanism.
> - Contact-induced force provides a natural and effective self-supervisory signal to guide attention between visual and tactile inputs.
> - Incorporating future force prediction offers critical temporal context, enhancing tactile utilization and contributing to more efficient and robust manipulation.
