# V-RAE: Rethinking Video Latent Spaces for Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> V-RAE is a video autoencoder that uses a frozen vision foundation model as its latent space instead of learning one from pixel reconstruction. A lightweight temporal-attention pooler compresses redundant frames 4x, and a spatiotemporal Transformer decoder with 3D RoPE and multi-frame unpatchify restores motion. It reaches 2.13 rFVD and 19.16 gFVD on K600, converging up to 6x faster than VAE latents.

## Problem

- Video autoencoders optimize latents for pixel reconstruction, forcing generators to rediscover dynamics from low-level codes; prior semantic work used representation features as auxiliary supervision, never as the generative latent space.
- Image representation autoencoders do not transfer to video: encoders emit temporally dense features, and compression must keep both temporal coherence and inherited semantic structure.
- Reconstruction fidelity is the standard selection metric for tokenizers, but it does not indicate whether a generator can model the surrounding latent distribution.

## Method

- **V-RAE**'s ==Temporal Attention Pooling (TAP)==, a 3M-param module with a shared query per head, zero-initialized to start as mean pooling then become content-adaptive, feeds a spatiotemporal ==ViT decoder== with ==3D RoPE== and ==multi-frame unpatchify==.
- Latent ==DiT== trained by ==rectified flow== with a dimension-and-temporal-length-dependent noise-schedule shift, clean-latent prediction, plus an auxiliary head at block 8 used for internal guidance.
- Reconstruction objective combines L1, LPIPS, GAN and Gram losses under latent noise augmentation with ==non-affine LayerNorm==; a ==tFVD== diagnostic decodes each interior latent replaced by its temporal-neighbor midpoint to probe latent temporal smoothness.

## Results

- Reconstruction: **2.13** rFVD (K600, **40.5%** better than best VAE's 3.58) and **6.12** rFVD/**89.13%** probing on UCF101 (second to Wan2.1's 6.05), where TAP's 3M-param design beats mean (94.25/91.82), conv (5.91/78.26), 67M Q-Former (6.87/84.37).
- Semantics and generation: latents probe at **89-91%** top-1 on UCF101 versus **30.83%** for the best VAE; gFVD **117.86** (UCF101) and **19.16** (K600), **5-6x** faster convergence.
- Metric analysis: rFVD correlates weakly with gFVD (r=**0.200** UCF101, **0.473** K600) while tFVD reaches **0.621** and **0.919**; Cityscapes prediction improves gFVD **144.47** to **111.36**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A latent space good for reconstruction is not automatically good for generation; evaluate off-trajectory decodability and temporal smoothness, not just encode-decode fidelity.
> - Compression capacity is not the lever: content-adaptive aggregation at mean pooling preserves semantics better than heavier resamplers; freezing a pretrained semantic encoder and learning only compression/decoding gives a space where only dynamics must be learned.
> - Evidence comes from moderate-scale class-conditional benchmarks; open-domain text-to-video at higher resolution and longer horizons is untested.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern video generation models predominantly rely on learned latent spaces derived from video autoencoders. These autoencoders compress high-dimensional raw pixel data into more compact representations, which significantly reduces the computational complexity for subsequent generative models, such as diffusion or autoregressive transformers. However, a prevalent challenge in this domain is that most existing video autoencoders, whether continuous Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), discrete tokenizers, or query-based designs, are primarily optimized for pixel-level reconstruction fidelity. This optimization often prioritizes local textures, colors, and fine-grained appearance details, leading to latent spaces that may lack high-level semantic organization. Such reconstruction-optimal latent spaces are not necessarily ideal for generative modeling, where the goal is to produce novel, diverse, and semantically coherent content, encompassing appearance, motion, and long-range temporal dependencies.

Recent efforts in image generation have started to address this limitation by incorporating semantic information into latent space design. This includes using semantic alignment objectives during autoencoder training or directly adopting the feature space of frozen image representation encoders (e.g., RAE and RAEv2) as the generative latent space. These approaches have shown that semantically rich and structurally coherent latents can be highly suitable for diffusion modeling, fostering a shared representation for both visual understanding and generation.

The extension of this paradigm to video generation is non-trivial due to inherent challenges introduced by the temporal dimension. These include the temporal density of features produced by general representation encoders, the necessity to preserve temporal coherence (to avoid flicker and jitter), and the need to ensure that temporal compression maintains the semantic structure inherited from the original encoder. This paper positions itself at the intersection of these challenges, aiming to explore whether frozen visual foundation model (VFM) representations can be effectively adapted to form generative latent spaces for video, thereby bridging the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level video generation. The work seeks to move beyond pixel-centric optimization towards semantic-centric latent space design for video.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop V-RAE, a video representation autoencoder that constructs compact, generative latent spaces by leveraging frozen representations from vision foundation models. The motivation stems from the observed mismatch between reconstruction-optimal latent spaces and the requirements for effective generative modeling in video.

The detailed motivations and objectives are as follows:

- **Addressing Limitations of Reconstruction-Optimal Latent Spaces:** Current video autoencoders primarily optimize for pixel-level reconstruction (e.g., L1, LPIPS, GAN losses). While effective for pixel fidelity, this approach often yields latent spaces that are not well-suited for generative models. Such spaces may lack high-level semantic organization, making it challenging for generative models to learn meaningful content, appearance, motion, and temporal dependencies. V-RAE aims to create a latent space that is intrinsically more semantically meaningful, by grounding it in features from pre-trained VFMs.

- **Leveraging Frozen Vision Foundation Models (VFMs):** Inspired by successes in image generation, the authors propose to directly utilize the semantically rich and structurally coherent feature spaces of frozen VFMs. This is motivated by the hypothesis that pre-trained VFMs already capture robust high-level visual semantics, which could simplify the task of generative modeling if these semantics are preserved in the latent space. The goal is for V-RAE to act as an interface between these powerful frozen encoders and a video decoder, rather than training an encoder from scratch for pixel reconstruction.

- **Overcoming Video-Specific Challenges in Leveraging Frozen VFMs:** Extending the frozen VFM paradigm to video introduces specific technical challenges that V-RAE aims to solve:
 - **Temporal Redundancy and Compression:** Vision representation encoders, especially those pre-trained on images, produce temporally dense features. Video generation requires significantly higher temporal compression ratios to manage computational costs. V-RAE must incorporate a mechanism to reduce this temporal redundancy effectively.
 - **Preserving Temporal Coherence:** Naive temporal compression or decoding can lead to artifacts like flicker and jitter in reconstructed or generated videos. The V-RAE design must ensure that both the compressed latents and the decoded motion maintain temporal consistency.
 - **Maintaining Semantic Structure:** The core advantage of using frozen VFM features is their semantic richness. Any temporal compression or decoding process must be designed to preserve this semantic structure to retain the benefits of the pre-trained encoder.

- **Developing a Generation-Oriented Diagnostic Metric:** The paper identifies that traditional reconstruction metrics (like rFVD) do not reliably correlate with downstream generation quality (gFVD). A secondary objective is to introduce and validate a new diagnostic metric, tFVD (Temporal Fréchet Video Distance), which assesses the temporal coherence and smoothness of the latent space under local interpolation. This aims to provide a more informative measure of a latent space's suitability for generative tasks.

- **Demonstrating Broader Applicability:** Beyond class-conditional video generation, the work seeks to evaluate V-RAE's utility in related tasks like future video prediction, demonstrating that its semantic latent space can support complex visual world modeling.

In summary, the motivation is to create more effective and efficient video generation systems by designing latent spaces that prioritize semantic meaningfulness and temporal coherence through the integration of frozen foundation models, rather than solely focusing on pixel-level reconstruction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

V-RAE (Video Representation Autoencoder) is designed to operate by building a generative latent space on top of frozen vision foundation model (VFM) representations. It consists of three main components: a frozen visual representation encoder, a learnable temporal pooling module, and a learnable spatiotemporal Transformer decoder.

#### 3.1. V-RAE Architecture

- **Visual Representation Encoder:**
 - **Frozen Nature:** The visual representation encoder is kept frozen throughout the V-RAE training process. This preserves the pre-trained semantic organization of its feature space.
 - **Encoder Types:** V-RAE supports various frozen encoders:
 - **Image-pretrained encoders:** DINOv3-L, SigLIP2-L, and EUPE-B are used. For these, each video frame is encoded independently, and the resulting spatial features are arranged along the temporal dimension. These encoders provide strong spatial and semantic representations but lack native temporal modeling.
 - **Video-native encoders:** V-JEPA 2.1-L is utilized. This encoder jointly processes multiple frames, producing spatiotemporal features that already incorporate some level of temporal dependency.
 - **Output:** The encoder produces dense feature maps $F \in \mathbb{R}^{T_E \times C \times h \times w}$, where $T_E$ is the feature sequence length (equal to input frames $T$ for image encoders, or $T/2$ for V-JEPA 2.1-L due to a tubelet size of two).

- **Temporal Pooling Module (P):**
 - **Purpose:** To compress the temporally dense features from the frozen encoder, remove temporal redundancy, and generate a compact video latent sequence $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{T_Z \times C \times h \times w}$. This compression is crucial for making the latent space computationally tractable for generative models.
 - **Mechanism:** It divides the encoder features $F$ into non-overlapping temporal windows of length $r_P$ and maps each window to a single latent feature map. The total temporal compression ratio is $r_{all} = r_E \cdot r_P$. For image encoders ($r_E=1$), $r_P=4$, yielding $r_{all}=4$. For V-JEPA 2.1-L ($r_E=2$), $r_P=2$, also yielding $r_{all}=4$.
 - **Temporal Attention Pooling (TAP) (Default):**
 - The chosen pooling mechanism involves a learnable query vector, key and value projections derived from encoder tokens within a temporal window, and learnable temporal biases.
 - Attention weights are calculated based on the query, keys, and biases, allowing adaptive weighting of features across the temporal window. The pooled features are then a weighted sum of the values.
 - A non-affine LayerNormalization (LayerNorm without learned scale/bias) is applied to the output. This prevents the optimizer from artificially increasing the signal-to-noise ratio during noise-augmented training and maintains latent scale calibration.
 - Initialization: Queries and biases are initialized to zero, and projection layers preserve input features, making the initial behavior approximate temporal mean pooling. This conservative starting point allows learning content-adaptive weights only when beneficial for reconstruction.
 - **Ablation Study on Pooling Designs:** The paper compares TAP with Mean Pooling (parameter-free, uniform averaging), 3D Convolutional Pooling (learnable local spatiotemporal filter), and Q-Former Pooling (high-capacity, transformer-based). TAP was selected due to its balance of reconstruction fidelity and semantic preservation, offering content-adaptive aggregation with modest parameter count (around 3M).

- **V-RAE Decoder (D):**
 - **Architecture:** A spatiotemporal Transformer decoder, inspired by the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) architecture. It consists of a stack of Vision Transformer (ViT) blocks.
 - **Positional Encoding:** Extends Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) to three dimensions (temporal and two spatial) to inject explicit spatiotemporal positional information into each latent token.
 - **Attention Pattern:**
 - For image-pretrained encoders (DINOv3-L, SigLIP2-L, EUPE-B), a "chunk-wise causal self-attention" is used. Tokens within a temporal latent chunk interact bidirectionally, but each chunk can only attend to itself and preceding chunks.
 - For video-native V-JEPA 2.1-L, "full self-attention" is employed, allowing every latent chunk to attend to both preceding and subsequent chunks, consistent with the non-causal temporal context provided by its frozen encoder.
 - **Multi-frame Unpatchify Layer:** Modifies the standard unpatchify layer. Instead of reconstructing a single frame per latent time step, each latent time step is mapped to $r_{all}$ consecutive frames. This accommodates the overall temporal compression ratio and enables continuous motion reconstruction.

#### 3.2. V-RAE Learning Objective

The training optimizes only the temporal pooling module and the video decoder. The objective function is a combination of several terms:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{recon} = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda_{lpips} \mathcal{L}_{LPIPS} + \lambda_{gan} \mathcal{L}_{GAN} + \lambda_{gram} \mathcal{L}_{Gram} $$
- **$\mathcal{L}_1$:** L1 reconstruction loss, computed over all reconstructed frames.
- **$\mathcal{L}_{LPIPS}$:** Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity loss, which encourages perceptual similarity.
- **$\mathcal{L}_{GAN}$:** Adversarial loss (Goodfellow et al., 2014) to enhance visual realism, using a VideoMAE-based discriminator. This loss is applied from epoch 30 onwards.
- **$\mathcal{L}_{Gram}$:** Gram-matrix loss, which encourages the reconstructed features to match the statistical properties of the target features.
- **Noise Augmentation:** During reconstruction training, clean latents ($z_{clean}$) are perturbed by adding Gaussian noise ($z_{train} = z_{clean} + \sigma \epsilon$, where $\sigma \sim U(0, 0.8)$). This augmentation forces the decoder to be robust to variations in the latent space, mimicking conditions during generation where latents might not be perfectly "on-manifold."

#### 3.3. V-RAE for Generation

After the V-RAE is trained and its parameters are frozen, it serves as the latent space for a downstream generative model.
- **Generative Model:** A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) (Peebles and Xie, 2023) is trained using rectified flow (Liu et al., 2023) in the V-RAE's latent space.
- **Noise Schedule Adjustment:** To handle the high dimensionality of V-RAE latents, a dimension-dependent shift ($s$) is applied to the noise schedule.
- **Prediction Parameterization:** The DiT is trained to directly predict the clean latent ($ \hat{Z}_\theta $) from a noisy latent ($Z_{\hat{\tau}}$) and the noise schedule parameter ($\hat{\tau}$).
- **Auxiliary Loss and Guidance:** An auxiliary clean-latent prediction head is attached to the output of the 8th transformer block of the DiT. This provides an additional loss term during training ($\mathcal{L}_{base_{RF}}$) and internal guidance during inference.
- **Inference:** A 100-step Euler sampler is used to solve the rectified flow trajectory, generating latent sequences. These sequences are then decoded into video frames by the frozen V-RAE decoder.

#### 3.4. Experimental Settings

- **Datasets:** UCF101 and Kinetics-600 (K600) for reconstruction and class-conditional generation. Something-Something V2 (SSv2) and Kinetics-400 (K400) for semantic probing. Cityscapes for future video prediction.
- **Metrics:** LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM, rFVD (reconstruction FVD), gFVD (generation FVD), gFID (generation FID).
- **tFVD (Temporal Fréchet Video Distance):** A novel diagnostic metric. It involves taking a temporal latent sequence, replacing interior latent codes with the midpoint of their temporal neighbors (e.g., $z'_t = \frac{1}{2}(z_{t-1} + z_{t+1})$), decoding this perturbed sequence, and computing FVD between the reconstructed clips and the corresponding real clips. A lower tFVD implies smoother temporal latent trajectories and better decoder robustness to off-manifold latent states.
- **Baselines:** Comparison with various large-scale pretrained video VAEs and tokenizers (e.g., Wan2.1 VAE, Wan2.2 VAE, HunyuanVideo VAE, CogVideoX VAE, AToken, Open-MAGVIT2, OmniTokenizer, LARP), and frame-wise RAE/RAEv2 to isolate temporal modeling effects.
- **Controlled Comparisons:** For generation, all tokenizer latent spaces use the same DiT backbone and a matched latent budget (1,280 tokens) to ensure a fair comparison of the latent representations themselves.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings across reconstruction, semantic preservation, generation, and the utility of the proposed diagnostic metric.

#### 4.1. Reconstructing Videos from Semantic Latents

- **Competitive Reconstruction Fidelity (rFVD):** V-RAE demonstrates competitive performance in video reconstruction, particularly as measured by rFVD. On K600, V-RAE (V-JEPA 2.1-L) achieves an rFVD of 2.13, outperforming the strongest evaluated VAE baseline (3.58) by 40.5%. On UCF101, V-RAE (DINOv3-L) achieves 6.12 rFVD, ranking closely behind Wan2.1 VAE (6.05). All V-RAE variants consistently outperform benchmark-scale video tokenizers like Open-MAGVIT2, OmniTokenizer, and LARP.
- **Encoder Generalizability:** The reconstruction capability of V-RAE is not tied to a single encoder or pretraining paradigm. Both image-pretrained (DINOv3, EUPE, SigLIP2) and video-native (V-JEPA 2.1) encoders yield effective latent spaces, with optimal performance depending on the dataset.
- **Distinction from Pixel-Level Metrics:** While V-RAE sometimes shows higher LPIPS, lower PSNR, and SSIM compared to some large-scale VAEs, its strong rFVD performance indicates a distinction between pixel-wise fidelity and video-level distributional similarity. This suggests that high-quality video reconstruction can be achieved without optimizing for low-level pixel recovery using a re-optimized encoder.
- **Improved Temporal Stability:** The Temporal Reconstruction Error Difference (TRED) metric shows that V-RAE variants exhibit lower frame-to-frame fluctuations in reconstruction error compared to frame-wise RAEv2, suggesting explicit temporal modeling reduces flicker and jitter in reconstructed videos.
- **Effectiveness of Temporal Pooling:** Comparison with frame-wise RAEv2 demonstrates that temporal pooling and cross-frame self-attention significantly improve temporal consistency. Among various pooling designs, temporal attention pooling provides the most balanced trade-off between semantic preservation and reconstruction fidelity, achieving content-adaptive aggregation with minimal semantic degradation.

#### 4.2. Semantic Preservation

- **Richer Semantic Information:** V-RAE latents retain substantially more semantic information compared to conventional VAE latents. For instance, V-RAE (DINOv3-L) achieves 89.13% top-1 accuracy on UCF101, significantly higher than the 30.83% of the strongest VAE baseline. Similar gains are observed on SSv2 and K400, indicating that the compressed latents preserve both appearance and motion semantics.
- **Modest Semantic Degradation:** Temporal compression via attention pooling results in only a modest decrease in semantic accuracy (typically within 3-4 percentage points) compared to the original frozen encoder features, demonstrating that temporal redundancy can be reduced without erasing semantics.

#### 4.3. Generation from Semantic Latents

- **Superior Generation Quality (gFVD):** All V-RAE variants consistently outperform conventional video tokenizers on both UCF101 and K600 in terms of gFVD. V-RAE (V-JEPA 2.1-L) achieves the best performance with 117.86 gFVD on UCF101 and 19.16 on K600, representing substantial improvements over the strongest non-V-RAE baselines.
- **Enhanced Optimization Efficiency:** V-RAE-based DiTs converge significantly faster than VAE-based counterparts. On K600, V-RAE (EUPE-B) matches the gFVD of Wan2.2 VAE at 180K updates after only 30K updates, demonstrating up to 6x faster convergence. This suggests that semantically organized latents are not only more effective but also easier for generative models to learn.

#### 4.4. Good Reconstruction Does Not Guarantee Good Generation

- **Discrepancy between rFVD and gFVD:** The study explicitly shows that high reconstruction quality (low rFVD) does not necessarily translate into high generation quality (low gFVD). For example, HunyuanVideo VAE has a lower rFVD than V-RAE (EUPE-B) on UCF101 but a substantially worse gFVD. This indicates that fidelity to encoded data points does not fully characterize the generative suitability of the latent space.
- **Utility of tFVD:** The proposed tFVD metric, which probes local temporal geometry and decoder robustness to off-manifold latent states, shows a substantially stronger correlation with gFVD. On K600, the Pearson correlation increases from 0.473 (rFVD vs. gFVD) to 0.919 (tFVD vs. gFVD), and on UCF101, from 0.200 to 0.621. This finding suggests that tFVD is a more reliable diagnostic for assessing the generative utility of video latent spaces.

#### 4.5. Future Video Prediction in Semantic Latent Space

- **Improved Predictive Modeling:** V-RAE significantly outperforms the Wan2.2 VAE baseline on the Cityscapes dataset for future video prediction. V-RAE (EUPE-B) reduces gFID from 15.02 to 11.52 and gFVD from 144.47 to 111.36 under matched settings, despite having worse rFVD.
- **tFVD as Predictor for Prediction:** The superior future prediction performance of V-RAE correlates with its lower tFVD (224.6040 vs. 319.0233), reinforcing the idea that temporal smoothness and decoder robustness to latent deviations are critical for tasks involving latent space exploration.
- **Qualitative Stability:** Qualitatively, V-RAE maintains better object contours, lane structure, and instance separation at longer prediction horizons compared to the baseline, which exhibits more blurring and distortion. This suggests that a semantically organized and temporally smooth latent space reduces structural and identity drift during future-state prediction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "V-RAE: Rethinking Video Latent Spaces for Generation" has several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of video generation and broader visual AI research:

- **Paradigm Shift in Latent Space Design:** V-RAE introduces a shift from purely pixel-reconstruction-optimized latent spaces to semantically rich, foundation-model-grounded latent spaces for video generation. This approach demonstrates that leveraging the high-level semantic understanding capabilities of frozen VFMs can lead to more effective and efficient generative models, rather than relying on autoencoders trained solely for low-level pixel recovery.

- **Enhanced Video Generation Quality and Efficiency:** The consistent outperformance of V-RAE variants in gFVD metrics and their significantly faster convergence rates (up to 6x faster) indicate that semantically organized latent spaces simplify the task of learning video distributions for generative models. This could lead to more robust, coherent, and realistic video generation with reduced training time and computational resources.

- **Improved Understanding of Generative Latent Spaces:** The clear dissociation between reconstruction fidelity (rFVD) and generation quality (gFVD) highlights that the criteria for evaluating autoencoders for generative tasks need to extend beyond pixel-level reconstruction. This finding challenges conventional evaluation practices and encourages a more nuanced understanding of what constitutes a "good" generative latent space.

- **Introduction of a Novel Diagnostic Metric (tFVD):** The proposed tFVD metric offers a valuable tool for diagnosing the generative utility of video latent spaces. Its strong correlation with downstream generation performance means it can guide the design and selection of video autoencoders more effectively than traditional reconstruction metrics. This could accelerate research and development in video latent space optimization.

- **Broadened Applicability to Visual World Modeling:** V-RAE's success in future video prediction, beyond class-conditional generation, suggests its potential as a general-purpose latent state space for visual world modeling. This opens avenues for more advanced AI systems that can not only generate videos but also understand and predict complex visual dynamics based on high-level semantic representations.

- **Efficient Transfer Learning:** By leveraging frozen, pre-trained VFMs, V-RAE promotes an efficient form of transfer learning. Instead of training monolithic video encoders from scratch for every generative task, V-RAE allows researchers to build upon powerful existing vision models, focusing development efforts on temporal compression and coherent decoding. This modularity could lead to more scalable and adaptable video generation frameworks.

In conclusion, V-RAE represents a step towards aligning video generative modeling with advancements in semantic representation learning, providing a robust framework that could underpin future progress in video synthesis, prediction, and understanding.
