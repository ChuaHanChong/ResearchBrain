# Learning Context-Aware Motion Priors for Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology developed Context-Aware Motion Priors (CMP), a framework that adaptively reweights reference motions based on current task context to provide more relevant guidance for humanoid control policies. This approach consistently improved task performance and sample efficiency across multiple control tasks and demonstrated robustness to imbalanced reference datasets, showing up to an 11.5% performance advantage.

## Problem

- Existing motion prior methods (e.g., AMP, SMP) learn a general, task-agnostic prior from human demonstrations, applying it uniformly regardless of the specific task context.
- This uniform application can lead to irrelevant or conflicting guidance, slowing down policy optimization and hindering sample efficiency in reinforcement learning.
- General motion priors are also susceptible to biases from imbalanced reference datasets, potentially leading to undesirable behaviors in the learned policy.

## Method

- The framework introduces **Context-Aware Motion Priors (CMP)** which learn a ==relevance function== that quantifies the compatibility between a given task context and reference motion sequences.
- A ==contrastive learning== approach is used to learn this relevance function, leveraging both online task-relevance signals from policy rollouts and a demonstration-positive branch grounded in the reference motion distribution.
- CMP integrates modularly with existing motion prior frameworks (like ==AMP== and ==SMP==) by using relevance-weighted losses to train a lightweight, context-conditioned adapter that provides context-dependent guidance.

## Results

- CMP consistently improved task performance and sample efficiency across **five** humanoid control tasks, showing higher task returns and requiring fewer environment interactions with both AMP and SMP instantiations.
- Analysis of learned relevance weights demonstrated dynamic association of task demands with suitable reference motions (e.g., running for far targets, walking for near targets) as training progressed.
- CMP-AMP exhibited strong robustness to imbalanced reference datasets, maintaining stable performance (max **2.8%** decrease) even when a dominant motion type was **100 times** more frequent, whereas base AMP performance dropped up to **11.5%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptively weighting motion prior guidance based on the current task context significantly improves the efficiency and performance of humanoid policy learning.
> - Contrastive learning can effectively learn context-motion compatibility from policy experience and demonstrations without requiring manual skill labels or explicit dataset partitioning.
> - Including a 'demonstration-positive' loss branch is crucial for anchoring learned context representations to the available reference motion space, preventing drift and ensuring the retrieval of appropriate motions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of humanoid control, particularly in the domain of reinforcement learning guided by motion priors. Traditional reinforcement learning for complex robotic control often struggles to produce naturalistic and physically plausible behaviors. Motion priors, such as Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) (Peng et al. 2021) and Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP) (Mu et al. 2025), address this by incorporating supervision from human demonstrations, encouraging learned policies to generate motions resembling human movement.

However, existing motion prior methods typically learn a general, task-agnostic prior from an entire reference dataset. This general prior is applied uniformly throughout policy training, regardless of the specific task context (e.g., current goal, command, or environment state). The paper identifies a limitation in this approach: while a motion might be generally plausible, it may not be relevant or optimal for a given task context. This can lead to conflicting guidance, slower policy optimization, or undesirable biases towards frequently occurring motions in an imbalanced reference dataset.

Prior work has attempted to address motion heterogeneity by learning latent skills or behavior modes, allowing a downstream controller to select appropriate skills (Peng et al. 2022; Dou et al. 2023; Tessler et al. 2023; Luo et al. 2024). These methods generally involve learning an intermediate skill space, which is typically fixed before downstream control. Another line of research focuses on adaptive reference selection based on imitation difficulty or hand-designed matching costs (Klipfel et al. 2023; Yu et al. 2025; Pan et al. 2025; Liao et al. 2025; Chen et al. 2025; Clavet 2016; Bergamin et al. 2019; Holden et al. 2020; Ponton et al. 2025).

This research differentiates itself by proposing a framework, Context-Aware Motion Priors (CMP), that adapts a general motion prior *online* to the current task context *directly in the original reference space*. Unlike skill-based methods, it does not require manual skill labels, dataset partitioning, or a separate skill discovery stage. Instead, it leverages contrastive representation learning, a technique often used for visual representation learning or goal-conditioned value functions (van den Oord, Li, and Vinyals 2018; Radford et al. 2021; Laskin, Srinivas, and Abbeel 2020; Eysenbach et al. 2021, 2022; Zheng et al. 2024), to measure the compatibility between a task context and a reference motion, thereby addressing the mismatch between general motion plausibility and context-dependent task relevance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables motion priors to provide context-aware guidance for humanoid policy learning. The authors aim to overcome the limitations of existing motion prior methods that treat all reference motions as equally relevant regardless of the immediate task context.

The key motivations are:
1. **Addressing Irrelevant Guidance**: Current motion priors, when trained on diverse reference datasets, may provide supervision from motions that are physically plausible but unsuitable for the current task context. For instance, in a "Target Location" task, a distant target might require running, while a nearby target might require walking. A context-agnostic prior would treat both as equally valid, potentially leading to inefficient or conflicting learning signals.
2. **Improving Optimization Efficiency**: Irrelevant or conflicting guidance from a general motion prior can slow down policy optimization and hinder sample efficiency in reinforcement learning. By adapting the prior to emphasize context-relevant motions, the learning process is expected to become more efficient.
3. **Enhancing Behavioral Versatility**: As humanoid controllers are developed for increasingly versatile tasks, they often rely on heterogeneous collections of human demonstrations. The challenge is to leverage this diversity effectively without diluting the learning signal with contextually inappropriate motions. The goal is to allow the policy to leverage the full behavioral coverage of a diverse dataset, but in a context-sensitive manner.
4. **Avoiding Manual Annotation and Fixed Skill Spaces**: Prior approaches often involve manual labeling of skills, partitioning datasets, or learning fixed intermediate skill representations. The authors are motivated to develop an approach that adapts the motion prior directly and online, without these requirements, making the system more autonomous and flexible.
5. **Robustness to Imbalanced Datasets**: When reference datasets are imbalanced (e.g., containing many more walking motions than running motions), a context-agnostic prior might bias the learned behavior towards the dominant modes, even when other motions are more appropriate for a specific context. A context-aware approach is expected to mitigate this sensitivity.

In summary, the core objective is to learn and apply context-dependent relevance to motion supervision, thereby guiding policies to generate more naturalistic, task-appropriate, and efficiently learned behaviors for humanoid control, directly within the existing motion prior framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Context-Aware Motion Priors (CMP) framework introduces a modular approach to augment task-agnostic motion priors with context-dependent reference motion relevance. This enables adaptive utilization of motion references based on the current task.

#### 3.1. A Reweighting View of Context-Aware Motion Priors

CMP conceptualizes its operation as learning a relevance function, $R_\phi(c, x)$, which quantifies the compatibility between a given task context $c$ and a reference motion sequence $x$. This relevance function generates an importance weight $w_\phi(c, x)$:
$w_\phi(c, x) = \frac{\exp(\alpha R_\phi(c, x))}{E_{x' \sim p_E}[\exp(\alpha R_\phi(c, x'))]}$, where $\alpha > 0$ controls the sharpness of the reweighting, and $p_E(x)$ is the empirical distribution of the reference motion dataset $D_E$. This weight modifies the reference distribution to form an effective context-conditioned distribution $q_\phi(x|c) = w_\phi(c, x)p_E(x)$, which assigns greater importance to context-compatible motions.

In practice, a minibatch approximation is used for efficiency. For a minibatch of paired contexts and reference motions, $\mathcal{B} = \{(c_i, x_i)\}_{i=1}^B$, the normalized and clipped weights are computed as:
$\bar{w}_\phi(c_i, x_i) = \text{clip}_{[w_{min}, w_{max}]} \left( \frac{B \exp(\alpha R_\phi(c_i, x_i))}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(\alpha R_\phi(c_i, x_j))} \right)$. These detached (stop-gradient) relevance weights are then used to train a lightweight context-conditioned adapter with parameters $\psi$. The adapter objective is given by:
$L_{adapt}(\psi | \theta, \phi) = E_{c \sim d_\pi, x \sim p_E} [ \text{sg}[\bar{w}_\phi(c, x)] \ell_{adapt\psi}(c, x; \theta) ]$, where $d_\pi$ is the context distribution induced by the current policy, and $\ell_{adapt\psi}$ is the prior-specific adapter loss. This formulation allows the base motion prior's training procedure to remain unchanged.

#### 3.2. Contrastive Relevance Learning

The relevance function $R_\phi(c, x)$ is learned using contrastive learning. It is defined as the cosine similarity between context and motion embeddings: $R_\phi(c, x) = \frac{f_{\phi_c}(c)^\top g_{\phi_x}(x)}{\|f_{\phi_c}(c)\|_2 \|g_{\phi_x}(x)\|_2}$, where $f_{\phi_c}$ and $g_{\phi_x}$ are context and motion encoders, respectively. The similarity score is then $s_\phi(c, x) = \exp(R_\phi(c, x)/\tau)$, with $\tau$ as the contrastive temperature.

The relevance function is learned through two branches:
1. **Online Task-Relevance Branch**: This branch extracts task-relevance signals from online policy rollouts. Given a minibatch of rollouts $\mathcal{B}_{on} = \{(c_i, x_i, \hat{A}_i)\}_{i=1}^B$, where $\hat{A}_i$ is the standardized Generalized Advantage Estimate (GAE), samples with high advantage ($\hat{A}_i > 0$) are identified as positive context-motion anchors. The online loss $L_{on}(\phi)$ is a contrastive objective that pulls positive context-motion pairs closer while pushing apart other motions in the batch:
 $L_{on}(\phi) = - \frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{on}|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{on}} \rho_i \log \frac{s_\phi(c_i, x_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^B s_\phi(c_i, x_j)}$, where $\mathcal{I}_{on} = \{i | \hat{A}_i > 0\}$ and $\rho_i = \sigma(\hat{A}_i/\beta_{adv})$ weights pairs by advantage.
2. **Demonstration-Positive Branch**: To ensure the learned relevance is grounded in the reference motion distribution, a demonstration-positive branch is introduced. For the same context batch $\{c_i\}_{i=1}^B$, reference motions $\mathcal{B}_E$ are sampled. The loss $L_{demo}(\phi)$ encourages compatibility between contexts and *all* reference motions, while implicitly contrasting against online rollout motions. This prevents context representations from drifting away from the space of available demonstrations.
 $L_{demo}(\phi) = - \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \log \frac{\sum_{x_E \in \mathcal{B}_E} s_\phi(c_i, x_E)}{\sum_{x \in \mathcal{B}_E \cup \mathcal{X}_{on}} s_\phi(c_i, x)}$.
The final relevance objective is $L_{rel}(\phi) = L_{on}(\phi) + \lambda_{demo}L_{demo}(\phi)$.

#### 3.3. Integration with Motion Priors

CMP is designed for modular integration with existing motion prior frameworks. The paper instantiates it with two distinct prior formulations:

1. **Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP)**: For AMP, the base discriminator outputs a scalar logit $l_\theta(x)$. CMP defines a context-conditioned logit as $l_{\theta, \psi}(c, x) = \text{sg}[l_\theta(x)] + \lambda_{res}\Delta l_\psi(c, x)$, where $\Delta l_\psi$ is a context-conditioned residual adapter. The adapter is trained using a relevance-weighted binary cross-entropy loss for reference motions ($L^+_{AMP}(\psi)$) and an unweighted negative term for policy-generated motions ($L^-_{AMP}(\psi)$). The resulting adapted motion-prior reward is $r_{AMP}(c, x) = - \log(1 - \sigma(l_{\theta, \psi}(c, x)))$.

2. **Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP)**: For SMP, CMP works with a pretrained denoiser $\epsilon_\theta(\tilde{x}_t, t)$. The adapted prediction is $\hat{\epsilon}_{\theta, \psi}(c, \tilde{x}_t, t) = \text{sg}[\epsilon_\theta(\tilde{x}_t, t)] + \lambda_{res}\Delta \epsilon_\psi(c, \tilde{x}_t, t)$, where $\Delta \epsilon_\psi$ is the context-conditioned residual denoising adapter. The adapter is trained with a relevance-weighted mean-squared error objective ($L_{CMP-SMP}(\psi)$), which also includes regularization for the residual. The adapted $\hat{\epsilon}_{\theta, \psi}$ then replaces $\epsilon_\theta$ in SMP's reward calculation, yielding context-conditioned motion-prior guidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CMP focused on task performance, sample efficiency, context–motion compatibility, component contributions through ablations, and robustness to reference dataset imbalance.

#### 4.1. Performance and Efficiency

Across five humanoid control tasks (Target Location, Steering, Trajectory Following, Dodgeball, Dribbling) and both AMP and SMP formulations, CMP consistently improved task performance and sample efficiency:
- **AMP-based instantiation (CMP-AMP)**: Qualitatively, CMP-AMP showed more efficient task execution, such as reaching targets earlier or initiating turns/acceleration sooner. Quantitatively, CMP-AMP achieved higher task returns on all five tasks compared to base AMP. It also reached performance thresholds with fewer environment interactions on four of five tasks, matching AMP on the Steering task. Similar improvements were observed on a simulated G1 humanoid model.
- **SMP-based extension (CMP-SMP)**: The framework delivered consistent improvements in both final return and sample efficiency across all five tasks when integrated with SMP. While the gains in final performance were generally smaller than with AMP, they demonstrated the broad applicability of the context-aware adaptation mechanism.

#### 4.2. Analyzing Context–Motion Compatibility

Analysis of the learned relevance weights and retrieved motions on the Target Location task (using seven representative contexts) indicated that CMP captures meaningful context–motion compatibility:
- **Evolution of Relevance Weights**: Early in training, walking motions received high weights across contexts. As training progressed, the weights became increasingly context-dependent. For instance, "front-far" contexts favored fast running, while "back-medium" contexts emphasized directional running. This indicated that the relevance model dynamically associates task demands with suitable reference motions.
- **Motion Retrieval with Demonstration Support**: Comparisons with a variant lacking the demonstration-positive loss ($L_{demo}$) showed that this component is crucial. Without $L_{demo}$, context embeddings sometimes drifted, leading to less context-appropriate retrieved motions (e.g., dynamic motions for nearby targets). With $L_{demo}$, the system retrieved more relevant motions (e.g., walking for front-near, running for front-far, braking for back-far targets), suggesting that $L_{demo}$ anchors context queries to the available demonstrations.

#### 4.3. Ablations of Contrastive Relevance

Ablation studies on CMP-AMP investigated the contributions of the adapter, context–motion alignment, and demonstration-positive branch:
- **Uniform Adapter**: A variant using the context-conditioned adapter but uniform relevance weights showed that adapter capacity alone did not consistently explain CMP's gains, outperforming AMP on one task but showing little benefit on another.
- **Shuffled Relevance**: Randomly permuting relevance weights across reference motions within batches reduced sample efficiency and increased variance, particularly on the Dodgeball task. This indicated that meaningful context–motion correspondence, beyond just a varied weight distribution, is important for efficient and reliable adaptation.
- **Online Branch Only**: Removing the demonstration-positive branch still achieved competitive final returns but required more samples. This highlighted the role of demonstration support in stabilizing relevance learning and accelerating policy optimization.

#### 4.4. Robustness to Reference Dataset Imbalance

Tests on the Target Location task with increasingly imbalanced reference distributions (by multiplying walking motions) demonstrated CMP-AMP's robustness:
- As the proportion of walking motions increased (up to 100x), AMP's return dropped significantly (up to 11.5%). In contrast, CMP-AMP's performance remained stable, decreasing by a maximum of 2.8%. This finding suggests that CMP effectively reduces the sensitivity of task-agnostic priors to dominant motion frequencies by adaptively emphasizing context-compatible references.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Context-Aware Motion Priors (CMP) offers a advancement in humanoid control by addressing the fundamental mismatch between general motion plausibility and context-dependent task relevance in motion priors. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Improved Learning Efficiency and Performance**: CMP consistently demonstrates improved task performance and sample efficiency across various humanoid control tasks and two distinct motion prior formulations (AMP and SMP). This indicates that providing context-relevant guidance during policy learning can make reinforcement learning for humanoid control more effective and practical.
- **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness**: The framework enables motion priors to adapt online to diverse task contexts without requiring explicit skill labels or dataset partitioning. Its robustness to highly imbalanced reference datasets is particularly impactful, suggesting that policies can learn to utilize appropriate motions even when those motions are underrepresented in the demonstration data.
- **Reduced Manual Effort**: By learning context-motion compatibility from policy experience and demonstrations, CMP bypasses the need for manual skill labeling or pre-defining skill spaces, which can be laborious and may not generalize well to novel tasks or datasets.
- **Generalizability Across Prior Formulations**: The successful integration and performance gains with both adversarial (AMP) and score-matching (SMP) priors suggest that the CMP framework is broadly applicable and can enhance different types of motion prior techniques.

Despite these contributions, the paper acknowledges several limitations that point to areas for future research:
- **Reliance on Reference Data Support**: CMP cannot generate task-relevant behaviors that are entirely absent from the reference dataset. Its capabilities are bounded by the diversity and coverage of the provided demonstrations.
- **Sensitivity to Learning Components**: The relevance learning process relies on policy advantages, making it potentially sensitive to critic errors or insufficient exploration in the reinforcement learning environment.
- **Scope of Evaluation**: The current evaluation is limited to structured contexts and simulated humanoids. Future work would involve high-dimensional perceptual inputs, real-world robotic deployment, and direct assessment of subjective motion quality.
- **Dependence on Underlying Prior**: The observed differences in performance gains between AMP and SMP indicate that CMP's effectiveness is also influenced by the inherent representational and guidance translation mechanisms of the base motion prior.

Overall, CMP represents a step towards more intelligent and adaptive humanoid control systems that can leverage large, diverse motion datasets effectively by selectively applying relevant guidance based on the current operational context.
