# IndEgo: A Dataset of Industrial Scenarios and Collaborative Work for Egocentric Assistants

> [!summary] Summary
> The IndEgo dataset provides 197.1 hours of egocentric and 96.8 hours of exocentric multimodal data from authentic industrial settings, featuring collaborative work and long-horizon tasks, collected using Meta Project Aria devices. Baseline evaluations reveal that state-of-the-art multimodal models achieve limited performance on benchmarks like mistake detection (23.3-40.9% F1) and reasoning-based QA (57.6-64.1% accuracy), highlighting the complexity of industrial egocentric AI challenges.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric vision datasets primarily focus on household or general activities, lacking the specificity and complexity of real industrial environments.
- Industrial tasks involve dynamic, physically demanding, and collaborative workflows with diverse tools and cluttered spaces, posing unique challenges for AI systems.
- There is a need for rich multimodal data to develop AI assistants that can understand, assist, and collaborate with humans in these complex, long-horizon industrial scenarios.

## Method

- Developed IndEgo, a large-scale multimodal dataset featuring 197.1 hours of egocentric and 96.8 hours of exocentric video from authentic industrial settings.
- Utilized Meta's Project Aria devices to capture comprehensive multimodal data, including RGB, eye gaze, hand pose, semi-dense point clouds, and audio, along with exocentric video.
- Implemented a rigorous data collection protocol covering unguided, guided, collaborative, and mistake detection tasks performed by 20 participants, with extensive fine-grained annotations and processed sensor data.

## Results

- IndEgo comprises 3,460 egocentric recordings (197.1 hours) and 1,092 exocentric recordings (96.8 hours), capturing approximately 34,000 fine-grained actions across five industrial task categories.
- Baseline evaluations show state-of-the-art multimodal models achieve zero-shot F1 scores between 23.3% and 40.9% for mistake detection, indicating substantial room for improvement.
- For reasoning-based video question answering, baseline models reached 57.6% to 64.1% accuracy, falling considerably short of human performance (90%) and struggling with situated and temporal reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art multimodal language models (VLMs) demonstrate limited performance on industrial egocentric tasks, indicating the significant challenges of these environments.
> - Exocentric camera views and multimodal cues like audio and eye gaze provide valuable, context-dependent information, with audio proving essential for understanding collaborative tasks.
> - The dataset's complexity, particularly in differentiating egocentric and co-worker actions in collaborative settings, reveals a major hurdle for current AI in analyzing human-human dynamics.
