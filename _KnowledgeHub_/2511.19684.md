# IndEgo: A Dataset of Industrial Scenarios and Collaborative Work for Egocentric Assistants

> [!summary] Summary
> The IndEgo dataset provides 197.1 hours of egocentric and 96.8 hours of exocentric multimodal data from authentic industrial settings, featuring collaborative work and long-horizon tasks, collected using Meta Project Aria devices. Baseline evaluations reveal that state-of-the-art multimodal models achieve limited performance on benchmarks like mistake detection (23.3-40.9% F1) and reasoning-based QA (57.6-64.1% accuracy), highlighting the complexity of industrial egocentric AI challenges.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric vision datasets primarily focus on household or general activities, lacking the specificity and complexity of real industrial environments.
- Industrial tasks involve dynamic, physically demanding, and collaborative workflows with diverse tools and cluttered spaces, posing unique challenges for AI systems.
- There is a need for rich multimodal data to develop AI assistants that can understand, assist, and collaborate with humans in these complex, long-horizon industrial scenarios.

## Method

- Developed IndEgo, a large-scale multimodal dataset featuring 197.1 hours of egocentric and 96.8 hours of exocentric video from authentic industrial settings.
- Utilized Meta's Project Aria devices to capture comprehensive multimodal data, including RGB, eye gaze, hand pose, semi-dense point clouds, and audio, along with exocentric video.
- Implemented a rigorous data collection protocol covering unguided, guided, collaborative, and mistake detection tasks performed by 20 participants, with extensive fine-grained annotations and processed sensor data.

## Results

- IndEgo comprises 3,460 egocentric recordings (197.1 hours) and 1,092 exocentric recordings (96.8 hours), capturing approximately 34,000 fine-grained actions across five industrial task categories.
- Baseline evaluations show state-of-the-art multimodal models achieve zero-shot F1 scores between 23.3% and 40.9% for mistake detection, indicating substantial room for improvement.
- For reasoning-based video question answering, baseline models reached 57.6% to 64.1% accuracy, falling considerably short of human performance (90%) and struggling with situated and temporal reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art multimodal language models (VLMs) demonstrate limited performance on industrial egocentric tasks, indicating the significant challenges of these environments.
> - Exocentric camera views and multimodal cues like audio and eye gaze provide valuable, context-dependent information, with audio proving essential for understanding collaborative tasks.
> - The dataset's complexity, particularly in differentiating egocentric and co-worker actions in collaborative settings, reveals a major hurdle for current AI in analyzing human-human dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of IndEgo is strategically positioned to address critical gaps in the rapidly evolving field of egocentric computer vision and embodied AI. Egocentric vision, which focuses on understanding the world from a human's first-person perspective, has gained significant traction due to its potential for developing helpful AI assistants and embodied agents. Existing foundational datasets like EPIC-KITCHENS and Ego4D have largely focused on everyday activities in household or diverse general settings. While these datasets have propelled research in areas like action anticipation, episodic memory, and human-object interaction, they often lack the specificity and complexity of industrial environments.

Some recent efforts, such as Meccano, Assembly101, and HoloAssist, have begun to explore industry-like or assistive contexts. However, the authors argue that these datasets often involve participants working at workstations or desks, omitting the dynamic, physically demanding, and collaborative nature of real industrial tasks. Workers in industrial settings frequently move around, manipulate diverse tools, operate in cluttered spaces, and collaborate with colleagues, presenting unique challenges for egocentric vision systems, including real-time action recognition, object localization amidst occlusions, and adaptation to varying conditions.

IndEgo distinguishes itself by focusing explicitly on "true industrial settings" and leveraging the advanced multimodal sensor capabilities of Meta's Project Aria device. The Aria device’s lightweight, untethered form factor allows for natural movement and comprehensive data capture (RGB, eye gaze, hand pose, semi-dense point cloud, audio, motion), which is vital for simulating realistic industrial workflows. The dataset's emphasis on collaborative work, where two workers jointly perform cognitively and physically intensive tasks, also fills a significant void, as future AI assistants are expected to operate alongside humans. Furthermore, IndEgo addresses the underrepresentation of "long-horizon activities" (20+ minutes), providing data for more complex and sustained tasks typical in industry. By offering diverse industrial scenarios, multimodal data, and dedicated benchmarks for mistake detection and collaborative understanding, IndEgo aims to accelerate research toward robust, deployable egocentric AI assistants in industrial applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the IndEgo dataset is to enable and accelerate research in egocentric AI assistants and embodied agents, specifically tailored for complex industrial environments. The authors are motivated by several key goals:

- **Bridging the Gap in Industrial Scenarios:** To address the significant underrepresentation of authentic industrial tasks in existing egocentric vision datasets. These scenarios are characterized by their complexity, diversity of tasks, and dynamic nature, which pose unique challenges for AI systems.
- **Enabling Egocentric AI Assistants:** To provide a rich multimodal resource for developing AI assistants capable of understanding users' actions, intentions, and needs in industrial contexts, and offering valuable guidance. Such assistants could be instrumental in skill acquisition, navigation, and improving user experience.
- **Fostering Research in Embodied Agents:** To facilitate the development of embodied agents that can learn from human demonstrations, assist, and collaborate with humans in physically and cognitively demanding industrial tasks.
- **Supporting Collaborative Work Understanding:** A key focus is on collaborative tasks, where two workers interact and cooperate. The dataset aims to provide the necessary data to train AI systems to understand inter-human dynamics, differentiate between worker actions, and infer roles (e.g., partner, teacher-student, leader-assistant).
- **Benchmarking Key AI Capabilities:** To establish challenging benchmarks for critical AI capabilities in industrial settings, including procedural and non-procedural task understanding, mistake detection, and reasoning-based question answering. This aims to push the boundaries of state-of-the-art multimodal models.
- **Improving Industrial Productivity, Safety, and Efficiency:** Ultimately, the long-term motivation is to investigate the practical application potential of wearable egocentric vision-based assistants to enhance operational performance, safety protocols, and overall efficiency in real-world industrial operations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The creation of the IndEgo dataset involved a comprehensive data collection and annotation methodology designed to capture the complexity and diversity of industrial tasks.

**Data Collection Process:**
- **Participants:** Data was collected from 20 participants (15 male, 5 female) with varying levels of industrial skilled work experience (beginner to expert) and diverse nationalities. All participants used English for narration.
- **Hardware Setup:** Each participant wore a **Project Aria device** for egocentric recordings, utilizing a custom profile to record data from all available sensors: 8MP RGB camera (2880x2880) at 10FPS, SLAM cameras, eye-tracking cameras, microphones, IMUs, magnetometer, barometer, and GPS. For longer recordings, a portable power bank extended sessions up to 68 minutes. Additionally, **exocentric cameras** (Sony A6400, Samsung Galaxy A51, iPhone 16) were used for 1,092 recordings, placed from a third-person observer's perspective, capturing video at 1920x1080 resolution at 30FPS.
- **Privacy and Ethics:** Strict protocols were followed, including informed written consent from all participants and individuals appearing in the background. The project objectives and data collection procedures were approved by reviewing authorities. Recordings were anonymized.
- **Protocol:** Activities took place in an industrial research facility. Participants underwent eye gaze calibration. Tasks were carried out under different conditions:
 - **Unguided Tasks:** Participants were given a goal (e.g., disassemble a mechanical frame) without specific instructions, encouraged to narrate their inner monologue, including uncertainties and mistakes.
 - **Guided Tasks:** Participants received thorough, step-by-step instructions and guidelines for correct task execution.
 - **Collaborative Tasks:** Two participants, both wearing Aria devices, jointly performed challenging activities, engaging in dialogue. Scenarios included coworking (equal partners) and teacher-student dynamics (experienced guiding less experienced).
 - **Mistake Detection (MD) Tasks:** Participants repeated specific sequences with planned and unplanned mistakes, including safety violations, incorrect order, and mishandling tools.
 - **Singular Actions, Tools/Objects in Context, and Tools/Objects Demo:** Additional shorter recordings focused on specific actions, tool interactions, and tool explanations to add variations and support different research avenues.

**Annotation, Labelling, and Data Modalities:**
- **Self-Annotation and Review:** Participants annotated their own recordings, followed by a review by a second person. For collaborative work, each user annotated their individual actions. This approach aimed to capture intentions and actions accurately from the first-person perspective.
- **Annotation Types:**
 - **Fine-grained Actions:** Annotated as logical segments (verb + noun + adjective pairings), e.g., "connect metal bar," "attach drill bit."
 - **Keystep Annotations:** High-level procedural steps.
 - **Mistake Annotations:** Labels and descriptions for planned and unplanned errors, with categories like "Severe," "Process Failure," "Impact Future," and "Harm."
 - **Narration Transcripts:** Extracted audio transcripts of participants' inner monologues and dialogues.
 - **Task Graphs and Instruction Guides:** Provided for procedural activities.
- **Processed Data:** Raw sensor data from Aria devices (VRS files) is shared, along with processed outputs from Meta's Machine Perception Service (MPS): eye gaze estimation, hand pose estimation, semi-dense point clouds, and user trajectory data.
- **Inter-Annotator Agreement:** A study revealed excellent agreement for keystep annotations (Krippendorff’s Alpha = 0.97) and moderate agreement for fine-grained annotations (α = 0.54 when grouping similar verbs/nouns), reflecting natural variations in human interpretation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The IndEgo dataset presents a rich, challenging resource for egocentric AI research, as demonstrated by its scale, diversity, and baseline evaluations.

**Dataset Statistics and Distribution:**
- **Scale:** IndEgo comprises 3,460 unique egocentric video recordings (197.1 hours) and 1,092 accompanying exocentric video recordings (96.8 hours). This translates to 7.1 million egocentric RGB frames, 13.9 million SLAM frames, 13.9 million eye-tracking frames, and 10.5 million exocentric RGB frames.
- **Categories:** The dataset covers five main industrial task categories: Assembly/Disassembly (avg. 15.2m), Inspection and Repair (avg. 7.8m), Logistics and Organisation (avg. 4.5m), Woodworking (avg. 7.5m), and Miscellaneous (avg. 1.5m, including MD tasks). Additional categories include Tools/Objects in Context, Tools/Objects Demo, and Singular Actions.
- **Annotations:** Approximately 34,000 fine-grained actions were annotated. Top nouns include "bolt," "tool," "screw"; top verbs include "pick," "put," "remove"; top adjectives include "wooden," "clean," "hard." The median annotation duration is 5.7 seconds, with a range from 0.19s to 214s.
- **Collaborative Work:** A significant portion of the longer tasks involves two-person collaborative activities, highlighted in the duration histograms.

**Baseline Evaluations and Challenges:**
The paper evaluates state-of-the-art (SOTA) multimodal models, including VideoLLaMA3 (VL3), Intern-VL-2.5 (IVL2.5), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (QVL2.5), and Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking Experimental (GFT*), using zero-shot and fine-tuning approaches.

- **Mistake Detection (MD):**
 - **Performance:** Zero-shot F1 scores ranged from 23.3 (VL3) to 40.9 (GFT*). Fine-tuning with MLP or Transformer classifiers showed slight improvements (F1 up to 39.5 for MLP, 36.7 for Transformer). Early Mistake Detection (EM) performance dropped to around 30-32%.
 - **Perspective Impact:** Joint Ego + Exo views slightly outperformed single views (e.g., GFT* F1: Ego-only 0.43, Exo-only 0.39, Joint 0.44), indicating that exocentric views provide valuable context for occluded or out-of-focus mistakes.
 - **Modality Ablation:** The utility of audio and gaze modalities was context-dependent. Audio was crucial for collaborative tasks, while gaze was beneficial for in-field-of-view mistakes but could worsen performance if the mistake occurred outside the gaze region.
 - **Conclusion:** Current VLMs struggle significantly with accurately detecting various types of industrial mistakes, especially distinguishing between subtle errors and correct actions in cluttered, dynamic environments.
- **Reasoning-Based Video Question Answering (QA):**
 - **Performance:** SOTA VLMs achieved overall accuracy ranging from 57.6% (IVL2.5) to 64.1% (GFT*) on singular action QA. This is significantly lower than human performance (90.0%).
 - **Challenges:** VLMs particularly struggled with situated reasoning and temporal understanding, often misinterpreting actions or their context, even for questions easily answered by humans. Giving the model action labels (e.g., Mistral-Large2 + Label) did improve performance for some question types but not others.
- **Task Understanding in a Collaborative Setting:**
 - **Performance:** Zero-shot evaluation yielded a low accuracy of 35.2% (GFT*). Fine-tuning showed some improvement (42.1% for VL3 + Tr).
 - **Challenges:** VLMs struggled to differentiate between the actions of the egocentric viewer and the coworker, especially when users worked in close proximity. Removing the audio modality significantly impacted performance, highlighting the importance of spoken communication in collaborative tasks.

Overall, the baseline evaluations consistently demonstrate that IndEgo presents a substantial challenge for current SOTA multimodal models across all benchmark tasks, indicating that there is ample room for future research and model development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The IndEgo dataset holds significant promise for advancing the field of egocentric vision, embodied AI, and industrial automation, offering several key contributions:

- **Fills a Critical Research Gap:** By focusing on diverse, cognitively, and physically intensive industrial tasks, IndEgo addresses a crucial void in existing egocentric datasets which predominantly cover daily activities. This specificity allows for the development of AI solutions directly applicable to real-world industrial challenges.
- **Enables Robust Industrial AI Assistants:** The dataset provides the necessary foundation for training and evaluating AI assistants capable of advanced functions in industrial settings, such as:
 - **Skill Transfer and Training:** Assisting new workers in acquiring complex skills by providing step-by-step guidance and immediate feedback on mistakes.
 - **Mistake Detection and Prevention:** Proactively identifying procedural errors, safety violations, or incorrect tool usage, thereby enhancing workplace safety and preventing costly production delays.
 - **Contextual Assistance:** Providing on-demand information, answering reasoning-based questions, and offering adaptive guidance based on the worker's gaze, actions, and surrounding environment.
 - **Process Optimization:** Analyzing workflows, identifying inefficiencies, and supporting better organization and logistics.
- **Pioneers Collaborative AI Research:** The inclusion of multimodal, synchronized egocentric data from two collaborating workers is a pioneering feature. This enables research into complex human-human and future human-AI collaboration, including understanding shared intentions, role differentiation, and coordinated actions, which is vital for the next generation of embodied AI agents.
- **Leverages Advanced Multimodal Data:** The comprehensive sensor data from Project Aria (RGB, eye gaze, hand pose, 3D point clouds, audio, motion) provides an unparalleled multimodal view of industrial interactions, opening avenues for advanced research in cross-modal learning, situated reasoning, and fine-grained action understanding.
- **Establishes Challenging Benchmarks:** The proposed benchmarks for mistake detection, reasoning-based QA, and collaborative task understanding demonstrate that current SOTA models are far from solving these industrial challenges. This will drive innovation in developing more robust and intelligent vision-language models capable of handling complex, real-world scenarios.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** IndEgo is designed as a catalyst for increased attention and further exploration of egocentric AI in industrial domains. Its open availability (via Hugging Face and GitHub) encourages community engagement, fostering new algorithms, models, and applications that can significantly enhance productivity, safety, and efficiency in industrial operations.

In conclusion, IndEgo stands as a vital resource for bridging the gap between general egocentric vision research and the specific demands of industrial environments, paving the way for a new generation of intelligent, collaborative AI assistants.
