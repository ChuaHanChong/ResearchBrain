# CodeGraphVLP: Code-as-Planner Meets Semantic-Graph State for Non-Markovian Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CodeGraphVLP tackles non-Markovian long-horizon manipulation by pairing a persistent semantic-graph scene state with a one-time LLM-synthesized code planner that queries it for progress checks, then masks observations to only subtask-relevant objects before VLA execution. Improves real-world task success to 81.7% average, beating memory-augmented and VLM-in-the-loop baselines while cutting planning latency roughly tenfold.

## Problem

- Short-horizon VLA policies condition only on the latest observation, breaking when task-relevant evidence is occluded or only visible earlier in the trajectory.
- Memory-augmented VLAs trade capacity for efficiency: scaling history or retrieval raises compute and latency, yet bounded memory can still miss sparse past evidence.
- Hierarchical VLM-VLA systems keep a VLM in the loop for repeated replanning, adding heavy inference latency and relying on language-only subtask cues brittle to clutter.

## Method

- ==Persistent semantic-graph state== built via segmentation, VLM-based relevance filtering, multi-view ==CLIP== and geometric association, and rule-based relation induction, updated online with a ==Cutie tracker==.
- **Code-as-Planner**: an ==LLM== synthesizes a task-specific Python policy once at initialization that queries the graph with helper routines and boolean predicates to track task memory and emit subtasks.
- ==Clutter-free visual-language prompting== masks multi-view RGB observations to only subtask-relevant object masks and pairs them with short planner-issued subtask instructions for ==VLA== fine-tuning and inference. Together this is **CodeGraphVLP**.

## Results

- On three real-world non-Markovian tasks, CodeGraphVLP reaches **81.7%** average success versus **56.7%** for the best history-enabled Gr00T N1.5 baseline and **0%** for pi-0 FAST.
- Ablation on Swap Cups shows Code-as-Planner with the semantic graph hits **85%** success at **0.328** sec/step, versus **55%** at **3.142** sec/step for VLM-based planning with the same graph.
- Removing the clutter-free visual cue while keeping the subtask language cue drops Swap Cups success from **85%** to **40%**, isolating the visual masking contribution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Externalizing task progress into a queryable structured state, rather than raw history tokens, decouples long-horizon reasoning cost from context length.
> - Synthesizing a planner once via LLM and executing it cheaply afterward avoids the latency penalty of keeping a large model in the closed control loop.
> - Language-only subtask instructions are insufficient in clutter; pairing them with object-level visual masking is what drives grounding robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a notable advancement in generalist robot control, typically by fine-tuning pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on large datasets of robotic manipulation. These models predict actions based on sensory input and instructions. However, a common architectural characteristic of many VLAs is their deployment as short-horizon policies. This approach operates under the implicit assumption that the latest observation provides sufficient information for action reasoning. This assumption becomes a limiting factor in real-world, long-horizon tasks, which are frequently non-Markovian. In such scenarios, critical task-relevant information may be partially observed, occluded, or present only in past observations, and the presence of clutter and distractors can compromise fine-grained visual grounding.

To address the limitations of short-horizon VLAs in non-Markovian contexts, two primary strategies have emerged:

- **Memory-augmented VLAs:** These systems incorporate temporal context by integrating step-wise histories, condensed memory tokens, or retrieving relevant past experiences. While aiming to support temporally referential grounding, these methods often face a trade-off between memory capacity and computational efficiency. Scaling history or retrieval mechanisms can increase computational demands and latency, while bounded memory might fail to retain sparsely occurring but critical past evidence.
- **Hierarchical VLM–VLA systems:** This paradigm positions a VLM as a high-level planner that determines subtasks, which are then executed by a VLA policy. This structure leverages VLMs for long-horizon reasoning and progress estimation while utilizing VLAs as generalist controllers. However, these systems typically do not maintain a persistent, grounded task state. Consequently, decision-making can still rely heavily on current observations and language prompts, making the VLA susceptible to clutter and distractors. Furthermore, the iterative invocation of a VLM in the loop introduces substantial inference latency, which can hinder real-time robotic manipulation.

This research introduces CodeGraphVLP as a hierarchical framework that aims to overcome the identified limitations of existing approaches. It seeks to integrate a persistent, structured representation of the task state, an efficient planning mechanism, and targeted visual-language prompting. By doing so, it attempts to enable more reliable long-horizon control in cluttered, non-Markovian environments while maintaining practical inference efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable reliable long-horizon robot manipulation using Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in non-Markovian and cluttered environments. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of current VLA architectures:

1. **Addressing Non-Markovian Dependencies:** Standard VLA models often assume that the next action depends only on the current observation, a Markovian property. This assumption breaks down in complex, long-horizon tasks where critical information for decision-making might be from earlier in the task (e.g., an object's initial position before it was moved or became occluded) or unavailable in the immediate visual input due to partial observability. The research aims to develop a system that explicitly accounts for these temporal dependencies, allowing decisions to be based on an accumulated understanding of the task state over time.

2. **Robustness to Clutter and Distractors:** Real-world environments are frequently cluttered, with numerous objects present that are irrelevant to the immediate task. Current VLA models, when provided with raw visual observations, can struggle with fine-grained visual grounding in such cluttered scenes. This can lead to mis-grounded actions, reduced reliability, and increased error rates. A key motivation is to devise a mechanism that filters out irrelevant visual information, allowing the VLA executor to focus on task-critical entities.

3. **Efficient Progress Estimation and Planning:** Hierarchical VLM-VLA systems attempt to tackle long-horizon tasks by employing a VLM as a high-level planner. While effective for reasoning, these systems often incur high computational overhead due to repeated VLM inference calls for planning and progress checks. This latency can make real-time, closed-loop robot control impractical. A core motivation is to achieve efficient progress estimation and subtask planning without the constant invocation of computationally expensive large models, thereby enabling practical online deployment.

4. **Maintaining a Compact, Explicit Task State:** Existing memory-augmented VLAs often rely on unstructured histories or bounded memory tokens, which can be insufficient for explicitly tracking complex task progress or reasoning about persistent object relationships. The research is motivated by the need for a compact, explicit, and queryable task state that can persistently encode task-relevant entities, their attributes, and their relationships across time, even under partial observability. This structured state should facilitate robust reasoning about task configuration and progress.

In summary, the key objective is to design a hierarchical VLA framework that integrates a persistent, queryable semantic graph for scene context, an efficient, code-based planner for progress-guided subtask generation, and clutter-suppressing visual-language prompting for robust VLA execution. This aims to provide a solution that is both reliable for long-horizon, non-Markovian tasks in cluttered environments and computationally efficient for real-time robotic deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CodeGraphVLP is a hierarchical framework designed to address non-Markovian long-horizon manipulation in cluttered environments. Its methodology integrates three core components: a persistent semantic-graph state, an executable code-based planner, and progress-guided visual-language prompting.

**Problem Formulation:**
The paper first frames the limitation of typical VLA models, which are often formulated under a Markovian assumption, conditioning actions solely on the latest observation and instruction. This is contrasted with non-Markovian long-horizon tasks, where the optimal policy depends on the entire interaction history. Direct modeling of this history is often impractical due to computational costs and the sparse, temporally distant nature of relevant evidence. CodeGraphVLP aims to overcome this by introducing a structured, compact representation of the task state.

**A. Persistent Progress State via Semantic-Graph**
At the foundation of CodeGraphVLP is a dynamic, persistent semantic-graph state, $G_t = (V_t, E_t)$, which explicitly represents task-relevant objects ($V_t$) and their relationships ($E_t$) over time. This graph is designed to be compact by filtering out irrelevant clutter and robust under partial observability.

- **Semantic-Graph State Initialization:**
 1. **Object Segmentation:** For an initial multi-view RGB observation ($o_0$), an off-the-shelf YOLOE instance segmenter is used to extract binary masks for all objects in each view. This segmenter was fine-tuned with robot arm masks to enhance its performance.
 2. **Identify Task-Relevant Objects and Attributes:** The segmented objects are presented to a VLM (using Set-of-Mark prompting) alongside the natural language instruction ($l$). The VLM identifies task-relevant objects and their attributes, discarding irrelevant entities.
 3. **Multi-view Association:** To create view-consistent graph nodes, masks across different views are linked. This involves two steps: first, matching objects by their CLIP visual feature cosine distance (below a threshold $\tau_{vis}$), and then using a geometric distance (normalized center-of-mass distance to anchor objects) to resolve remaining associations (below a threshold $\tau_{geo}$).
 4. **Relation Induction:** Once view-consistent nodes are established, relationships (e.g., 'in', 'on', 'near') between objects are induced using lightweight geometric rules based on segmentation masks (e.g., proximity, containment, vertical ordering, and boundary contact).

- **Online Semantic-Graph State Update:**
 For subsequent timesteps ($t > 0$), the semantic graph is continuously updated. Cutie is employed to track existing task-relevant objects and update their per-view mask groundings in $G_{t-1}$ to reflect the new observation $o_t$. To address potential tracker drift and discover new objects, the instance segmenter and the relevance filtering/multi-view association procedures are periodically re-run. If a candidate region matches an existing node, it corrects the node; otherwise, it is added as a new node only if deemed task-relevant, followed by a refresh of relation edges.

**B. Code-as-Planner**
The planning component is an executable Python program, $P$, synthesized by a Large Language Model (LLM) once at the task's initiation. This program operates over the semantic graph $G_t$ to output the next subtask instruction ($l_{sub_t}$) and the subtask-relevant objects ($O_{rel_t}$).

- **Constructing Programmatic Planner:**
 Given the long-horizon instruction ($l$), the graph schema, and the initial graph ($G_0$), an LLM (specifically, GPT-5) generates a task-specific Python planner. This planner includes:
 - **Helper Routines:** Functions for common graph queries (e.g., filtering objects by class/attributes, locating containers, checking robot's grip, finding empty containers).
 - **Boolean Predicates:** Lightweight functions encoding task constraints and progress conditions (e.g., single-object holding, exclusivity, relation checks).
 - **`policy(graph)` Function:** The main function that uses these primitives to select the next subtask.
 The LLM is prompted to perform one-time role binding (identifying key objects and their initial states from $G_0$) and to generate a reactive program. Crucially, this program maintains a lightweight persistent `graph.task_memory` to record completed subtasks, preventing redundant execution. This one-time synthesis amortizes the LLM/VLM inference cost, allowing for cheap, repeated execution of the planner.

- **Planner Structure and Task Memory Maintenance:**
 During deployment, $P$ is invoked after each executed action chunk. It queries $G_t$ using its predicates to check if the current subtask is satisfied. If so, it updates `graph.task_memory` and advances to the next unfinished subtask; otherwise, it re-issues the current subtask. It returns $l_{sub_t}$ and $O_{rel_t}$, where $O_{rel_t}$ contains only objects necessary for the immediate decision. This approach decouples graph updates, planning, and VLA execution, facilitating reactive planning without continuous VLM involvement.

**C. Clutter-Free Visual-Language Prompting**
The outputs of the code-as-planner ($l_{sub_t}$, $O_{rel_t}$) and the semantic-graph state ($G_t$) are used to construct a progress-guided visual-language prompt for the VLA executor, aiming to mitigate issues arising from clutter.

- **Subtask Language Cue:** The VLA executor is conditioned on the planner-produced short, imperative subtask instruction ($l_{sub_t}$) rather than the long-horizon task description ($l$). This focuses the language context on the immediate decision.
- **Clutter-Free Visual Cue:** A clutter-free observation ($\tilde{o}_t$) is created by retaining only the subtask-relevant objects ($O_{rel_t}$). For each view, a view-specific retention mask ($M_{v_t}$) is generated by taking the union of binary segmentation masks of all objects in $O_{rel_t}$. All pixels outside $M_{v_t}$ are blacked out in the raw RGB image, producing $\tilde{I}_{v_t}$.
- **VLA Execution:** The VLA executor predicts the next action chunk based on this clutter-free multi-view observation ($\tilde{o}_t$), proprioception ($s_t$), and the subtask language cue ($l_{sub_t}$).

**Training Objective:**
The VLA is fine-tuned via imitation learning. For each teleoperated demonstration, the semantic-graph construction and online update pipeline are applied, and the synthesized code planner $P(G_t)$ is executed at each timestep to generate $l_{sub_t}$ and $O_{rel_t}$. This allows for the creation of clutter-free observations $\tilde{o}_t$. The VLA parameters are then optimized using maximum-likelihood estimation on these subtask-conditioned, object-focused training tuples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CodeGraphVLP was conducted on three real-world tabletop manipulation tasks characterized by non-Markovian dependencies and clutter, designed to answer questions regarding task completion reliability, planning efficiency, and clutter-robustness.

**A. Experimental Setups**

- **Tasks:**
 1. **Pick-and-Place Twice:** Involves moving a cube between two plates and returning it to its original plate, requiring memory of the initial state.
 2. **Place-and-Stack:** Requires placing a cube into the nearest cup, then stacking another cup on top of the cup containing the now-occluded cube, necessitating reliance on past observations.
 3. **Swap Cups:** Involves swapping the positions of two cups using a temporary buffer plate, with a constraint on which cup to move first, demanding memorization of initial assignments and multi-step planning.
- **Demonstrations:** 100 demonstrations for Pick-and-Place Twice, 100 for Place-and-Stack, and 200 for Swap Cups (split between starting cup variants) were collected via teleoperation for VLA fine-tuning.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against open VLA baselines: $\pi_0$, $\pi_0$ FAST, $\pi_{0.5}$, and Gr00T N1.5. An additional history-enabled Gr00T N1.5 variant, receiving four past frames at 1-second intervals, was also evaluated to assess the benefit of raw temporal context. All models were fine-tuned under the same protocol.
- **Implementation Details:** The VLA executor within CodeGraphVLP utilized $\pi_0$. All VLA models were fine-tuned for 50K iterations using low-rank adaptation on NVIDIA A6000 GPUs. GPT-5 was used for one-time synthesis of the code planner. Inference occurred on a single GPU, executing 10 actions per forward pass before replanning.
- **Hardware Setup:** Experiments were conducted on a UR10e robotic arm with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, using multi-view RGB observations from a fixed over-shoulder camera and a wrist-mounted camera.

**B. Main Results**

Table I (provided in the original text) indicates the success rates across the three tasks for CodeGraphVLP and the baselines.

- **Overall Performance:** CodeGraphVLP achieved the highest task completion rates across all three non-Markovian tasks. Its average success rate was 81.7%, significantly outperforming the next best baseline, Gr00T N1.5 + Multi-frame, which achieved 56.7%.
- **Task-Specific Success:**
 - **Pick-and-Place Twice:** CodeGraphVLP achieved 100% full task success (with 80% for the 'PnP Once' subtask), while the best baseline, Gr00T N1.5 + Multi-frame, reached 100% full task success but only 75% for 'PnP Once' subtask.
 - **Place-and-Stack:** CodeGraphVLP achieved 95% full task success (with 80% for 'Drop Cube' subtask and 100% for 'Stage Cup' subtask). The best baseline, Gr00T N1.5 + Multi-frame, achieved 50% full task success (with 50% for 'Drop Cube' and 90% for 'Stage Cup').
 - **Swap Cups:** CodeGraphVLP achieved 100% full task success (with 85% for 'Success Rate' subtask). The best baseline, Gr00T N1.5 + Multi-frame, achieved 45% full task success.
- **Baseline Performance:**
 - $\pi_0$ FAST exhibited 0% success on all tasks.
 - VLAs with diffusion or flow-based decoders ($\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, Gr00T N1.5) showed more stability but still had limited success rates for full task completion, particularly in non-Markovian scenarios.
 - The history-augmented Gr00T N1.5 variant improved performance over its non-augmented counterpart, but its success rates remained substantially below CodeGraphVLP, suggesting that raw temporal context alone is insufficient without structured progress reasoning and visual cues.

**C. Ablation Analysis**

- **Efficiency Benefits of Code-as-Planner (Table II):** Ablations on the "Swap Cups" task demonstrated the efficiency of the Code-as-Planner.
 - A VLM-based planner without a semantic graph achieved 25% success with a latency of 2.967 seconds per step.
 - A VLM-based planner augmented with the semantic graph improved success to 55% but still had a high latency of 3.142 seconds per step.
 - CodeGraphVLP, leveraging the Code-as-Planner over the semantic graph, achieved 85% success with a significantly lower latency of 0.328 seconds per step. This indicates that one-time synthesis and lightweight graph queries provide substantial speed and reliability advantages over repeated VLM inference.
- **Effectiveness of Clutter-Free Visual-Language Prompting (Table III):** Ablations on the "Swap Cups" task highlighted the contribution of clutter-free visual cues.
 - CodeGraphVLP without the clutter-free visual cue (i.e., using raw RGB observations but retaining subtask language cue) achieved a success rate of 40%.
 - CodeGraphVLP with the clutter-free visual cue achieved a success rate of 85%. This indicates that progress-guided perceptual inputs, which suppress distractors, are important for improving clutter-robust action reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CodeGraphVLP framework represents an advancement in the field of generalist robot manipulation, particularly for tasks exhibiting non-Markovian dependencies and operating in cluttered environments.

The primary significance of this work lies in its integrated approach, which combines a persistent, structured semantic-graph state, an efficient code-based planner, and progress-guided visual-language prompting. This combination directly addresses several critical limitations of prior Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:

1. **Enhanced Reliability in Non-Markovian Tasks:** By maintaining an explicit semantic graph that captures task-relevant entities and their relationships over time, CodeGraphVLP enables robust reasoning about task progress even when critical information is partially observed, occluded, or only available from past observations. This allows for reliable execution of complex, multi-step tasks where traditional short-horizon VLAs might fail due to a lack of historical context.
2. **Improved Robustness to Clutter:** The use of clutter-free visual cues, generated by focusing the VLA executor solely on subtask-relevant objects identified by the planner, significantly improves visual grounding. This mitigates the problem of distractors in cluttered scenes, which can lead to mis-grounded actions in unprompted VLA policies.
3. **Increased Planning Efficiency:** The synthesis of an executable code-based planner once at the task's initiation, which then queries the semantic graph for progress checks, substantially reduces planning latency compared to hierarchical systems that repeatedly invoke large Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This efficiency is critical for real-time, closed-loop robotic control, making long-horizon reasoning more practical for online deployment.
4. **Structured Task State Representation:** The semantic graph provides a compact, explicit, and queryable representation of the scene and task context. This structured state is more effective than unstructured temporal histories or bounded memory tokens in tracking complex task progress and understanding persistent object relationships.

The potential impact of CodeGraphVLP includes enabling robots to perform more complex and longer-horizon manipulation tasks in unstructured, real-world settings with greater autonomy and reliability. This could extend to applications in manufacturing, logistics, domestic assistance, and hazardous environments, where robots need to understand evolving contexts and execute multi-stage operations without constant human supervision. The framework's ability to maintain high performance while reducing computational overhead for planning contributes to the feasibility of deploying advanced robotic systems.

However, the authors also acknowledge certain limitations: the pipeline's reliance on foundational models (for semantic-graph construction and program synthesis) means its performance is contingent on their capabilities and potential failure modes. Specifically, VLM-inferred attributes and relations can be sensitive to visual quality and viewpoints, and the generated code-as-planner requires careful prompt engineering to ensure robust and executable logic. Future work aims to streamline semantic-graph generation, potentially through open-world modules, and develop automated verification and self-check mechanisms for synthesized code-based planners, which would further enhance the framework's robustness and autonomy.
