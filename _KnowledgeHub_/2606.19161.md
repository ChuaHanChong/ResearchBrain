# HT-Bench: Benchmarking and Learning Dexterous Full-Hand Tactile Representations with Egocentric Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> A new benchmark, HT-Bench, offers a standardized evaluation for dexterous full-hand tactile representations integrated with egocentric vision, complemented by the HandTouch encoder. HandTouch achieves enhanced capabilities in tactile structural modeling and vision-touch alignment, improving Hit@1 to 99.27% in similarity retrieval and achieving 0.010 RMSE in tactile inpainting compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Developing robust multimodal foundation models for robotic manipulation is hindered by the lack of standardized data formats and evaluation benchmarks for tactile sensing.
- Tactile sensing systems suffer from intrinsic heterogeneity due to diverse sensor designs and robotic embodiments, making it difficult to create generalizable tactile encoders.
- Existing tactile datasets and evaluation protocols are often sensor-specific or task-dependent, preventing comprehensive assessment and comparison of tactile representation learning methods.

## Method

- Introduced **HT-Bench**, a large-scale, multi-task benchmark comprising approximately 10 million RGB video frames and 7.8 million full-hand tactile frames with synchronized egocentric vision.
- Developed ==HandTouch==, a vector-quantized vision–tactile encoder, trained progressively through three stages to learn spatial topology, vision-tactile alignment, and temporal dynamics.
- Defined four complementary evaluation tasks (fine-grained similarity retrieval, masked inpainting, vision-to-tactile synthesis, multimodal frame prediction) within HT-Bench, including provisions for out-of-distribution generalization.

## Results

- HandTouch improved Hit@1 to **99.27%** and Recall@5 to **85.23%** on fine-grained tactile similarity retrieval, outperforming baseline encoders by a substantial margin.
- Achieved **0.010** RMSE and **0.911** cIoU for full-map masked tactile inpainting on the standard test split, demonstrating strong spatial reasoning and reconstruction capabilities.
- Reduced full-map RMSE to **0.031** and improved cIoU to **0.705** for vision-to-tactile synthesis, indicating effective learning of cross-modal correspondences and demonstrating superior generalization in OOD settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Focusing on a specific, scalable data regime like egocentric vision combined with full-hand tactile data is a practical approach to establishing standardized benchmarks for tactile representation learning.
> - A progressive, multi-stage training methodology that explicitly targets spatial topology, cross-modal alignment, and temporal dynamics is effective for learning generalizable dexterous tactile representations.
> - Vector quantization, enhanced by a curriculum-based dual masking strategy, can effectively capture discriminative tactile features and improve spatial reasoning under incomplete observations.
