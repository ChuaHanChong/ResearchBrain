# HT-Bench: Benchmarking and Learning Dexterous Full-Hand Tactile Representations with Egocentric Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> A new benchmark, HT-Bench, offers a standardized evaluation for dexterous full-hand tactile representations integrated with egocentric vision, complemented by the HandTouch encoder. HandTouch achieves enhanced capabilities in tactile structural modeling and vision-touch alignment, improving Hit@1 to 99.27% in similarity retrieval and achieving 0.010 RMSE in tactile inpainting compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Developing robust multimodal foundation models for robotic manipulation is hindered by the lack of standardized data formats and evaluation benchmarks for tactile sensing.
- Tactile sensing systems suffer from intrinsic heterogeneity due to diverse sensor designs and robotic embodiments, making it difficult to create generalizable tactile encoders.
- Existing tactile datasets and evaluation protocols are often sensor-specific or task-dependent, preventing comprehensive assessment and comparison of tactile representation learning methods.

## Method

- Introduced **HT-Bench**, a large-scale, multi-task benchmark comprising approximately 10 million RGB video frames and 7.8 million full-hand tactile frames with synchronized egocentric vision.
- Developed ==HandTouch==, a vector-quantized vision–tactile encoder, trained progressively through three stages to learn spatial topology, vision-tactile alignment, and temporal dynamics.
- Defined four complementary evaluation tasks (fine-grained similarity retrieval, masked inpainting, vision-to-tactile synthesis, multimodal frame prediction) within HT-Bench, including provisions for out-of-distribution generalization.

## Results

- HandTouch improved Hit@1 to **99.27%** and Recall@5 to **85.23%** on fine-grained tactile similarity retrieval, outperforming baseline encoders by a substantial margin.
- Achieved **0.010** RMSE and **0.911** cIoU for full-map masked tactile inpainting on the standard test split, demonstrating strong spatial reasoning and reconstruction capabilities.
- Reduced full-map RMSE to **0.031** and improved cIoU to **0.705** for vision-to-tactile synthesis, indicating effective learning of cross-modal correspondences and demonstrating superior generalization in OOD settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Focusing on a specific, scalable data regime like egocentric vision combined with full-hand tactile data is a practical approach to establishing standardized benchmarks for tactile representation learning.
> - A progressive, multi-stage training methodology that explicitly targets spatial topology, cross-modal alignment, and temporal dynamics is effective for learning generalizable dexterous tactile representations.
> - Vector quantization, enhanced by a curriculum-based dual masking strategy, can effectively capture discriminative tactile features and improve spatial reasoning under incomplete observations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Tactile sensing has emerged as a crucial modality for developing robust multimodal foundation models in robotic manipulation. It provides direct physical interaction data, such as contact forces, pressure distributions, and slip detection, which visual perception alone cannot reliably acquire. This capability is particularly significant for robotic systems that require contact-rich reasoning and precise dexterous manipulation.

The field of visual perception has benefited from standardized data formats, scalable architectures, and mature evaluation benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet, COCO). In contrast, tactile representation learning faces significant challenges due to the intrinsic heterogeneity of tactile sensing systems. This heterogeneity stems from diverse sensor designs (including hardware principles, spatial layouts, signal modalities, and mounting configurations) and varied robotic embodiments (introducing different end-effector morphologies and contact patterns). Consequently, existing tactile datasets are often heterogeneous, processing pipelines are sensor-specific, and evaluation protocols are task-dependent. This makes it difficult to establish a universal benchmark or to develop generalizable tactile encoder backbones.

The current research positions itself by acknowledging the impracticality of creating a single universal benchmark that resolves all sensor and embodiment discrepancies. Instead, it explores a specific, scalable, and promising direction: the integration of egocentric vision with full-hand tactile data. This combination is considered attractive because egocentric observations naturally capture interaction-centric visual context, while full-hand tactile sensing provides distributed contact information during dexterous manipulation. The paper aims to address the gap in standardized evaluation and generalizable representation learning within this particular, high-potential setting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of tactile representation learning for dexterous full-hand manipulation by providing a standardized evaluation framework and a corresponding robust learning model.

The key motivations are:
- **Addressing Tactile Heterogeneity**: The diverse nature of tactile sensors and robotic embodiments makes it challenging to develop universal tactile representations. The authors argue that seeking a fully universal benchmark is currently impractical. Instead, they motivate a focus on a scalable and promising data regime: egocentric vision paired with full-hand tactile data. This specific pairing offers a practical foundation for developing and evaluating tactile representations.
- **Need for a Standardized Benchmark**: Existing evaluations for tactile sensing are often limited to narrow tasks or specific sensor configurations. This makes it difficult to assess whether learned features are transferable or merely task-specific adaptations. There is a need for a large-scale, multi-task benchmark that can evaluate tactile encoders beyond a single downstream objective and assess their general capabilities.
- **Evaluating Core Capabilities**: A robust tactile representation should demonstrate several core capabilities. The authors motivate evaluating these capabilities from three perspectives:
 1. **Encoding Contact Geometry**: The ability of a representation to capture the meaningful structural characteristics of contact interactions.
 2. **Aligning with Visual Information**: The capacity to integrate and relate tactile observations with corresponding visual context, reflecting cross-modal understanding.
 3. **Generalization to Unseen Tasks**: The capability to perform effectively on tasks or scenarios not encountered during training, indicating transferability.

To address these motivations, the research introduces two main contributions:
- **HT-Bench**: A large-scale, multi-task benchmark specifically designed for dexterous full-hand tactile sensing, incorporating egocentric vision. It aggregates a substantial volume of synchronized data and defines four complementary evaluation tasks to assess the core capabilities outlined above, including specific provisions for out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization.
- **HandTouch**: A vector-quantized vision–tactile encoder proposed to learn general dexterous tactile representations. HandTouch is motivated by the need for an encoder that can effectively leverage the data and meet the evaluation criteria set by HT-Bench, specifically by learning representations that are structurally discriminative, visually grounded, and temporally aware.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around two main components: HT-Bench, a multi-task tactile evaluation benchmark, and HandTouch, a vector-quantized vision–tactile encoder.

#### 3.1. HT-Bench: Multi-Task Tactile Evaluation Benchmark

HT-Bench is constructed from existing open-source tactile and visuo-tactile datasets, augmented with newly collected real-world full-hand tactile sequences.
- **Data Scale**: It comprises approximately 10 million RGB video frames and 7.8 million tactile frames.
- **Data Modalities**: The dataset features synchronized egocentric visual observations and full-hand tactile sequences.
- **Diversity**: HT-Bench covers a variety of environments and scene categories, including home, electronics workbench, chemistry lab, retail, general workbench, and outdoor scenarios.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Evaluation**: To assess generalization, the dataset employs a task-level partitioning strategy. One entire interaction task is held out as the OOD evaluation split, while the remaining tasks are divided into training and in-distribution test sets with a 9:1 ratio.
- **Evaluation Tasks**: Four distinct tasks are defined to examine different aspects of tactile representation learning:
 - **Fine-Grained Tactile Similarity Retrieval**: Evaluates whether tactile embeddings preserve fine-grained contact characteristics. For a given query tactile map, candidate tactile maps are ranked based on their Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) to establish a ground-truth ranking. The encoder's performance is then measured by comparing its cosine-similarity ranking of learned embeddings against this reference.
 - **Masked Tactile Inpainting**: Assesses spatial reasoning under incomplete tactile observations. The model is required to reconstruct masked-out regions of a tactile map using the remaining tactile data and synchronized visual cues. This task simulates scenarios of partial sensor information loss.
 - **Vision-to-Tactile Synthesis (RGB-to-Tactile)**: Evaluates cross-modal alignment between vision and touch. Given a single RGB observation, the model predicts the corresponding tactile pressure distribution. This task explores the ability to infer tactile properties from visual appearance.
 - **Multimodal Tactile Frame Prediction**: Evaluates temporal contact modeling capabilities. Given recent visual observations (v_{T-2}) and past tactile trajectories (t_{T-2}), the model predicts the tactile distribution (t_T) at a future time step T. This assesses the integration of visual context and tactile history for anticipating future contact states.

#### 3.2. HandTouch: Vector-Quantized Vision–Tactile Representation Learning

HandTouch is a vector-quantized vision–tactile encoder trained progressively through three stages, each targeting a core capability evaluated by HT-Bench.

- **Stage 1: Vector-Quantized Tactile Reconstruction (Spatial Topology Learning)**
 - **Objective**: Capture the spatial topology of full-hand tactile maps through unimodal reconstruction.
 - **Process**: A normalized tactile map (t) is tokenized into non-overlapping patches by a convolutional projection layer. These patch tokens, with added positional embeddings, are processed by an 8-layer Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder, yielding continuous latent features (Z_e). A factorized vector quantizer converts these continuous features into a compact, discrete token space using a shared codebook (C). A stop-gradient operator is used during quantization. To prevent codebook collapse (where few entries are used), a mechanism tracks usage frequency and reinitializes underutilized codebook entries. A decoder reconstructs the tactile map from the quantized tokens.
 - **Loss Function**: Combines an L2 reconstruction loss for the tactile map and a commitment loss to optimize the codebook.

- **Stage 2: Cross-Modal Masked Tactile Inpainting (Vision–Tactile Alignment)**
 - **Objective**: Integrate visual cues to learn cross-modal vision–tactile alignment by reconstructing corrupted tactile maps.
 - **Process**: A curriculum-based dual masking strategy is applied to the tactile input:
 - *Regional Random Masking*: Masks specific anatomical hand parts (e.g., thumb, palm) to simulate partial information loss.
 - *Complete Masking*: Masks the entire tactile map, requiring inference primarily from visual context. The probability of complete masking increases gradually over training (P_full(γ)) to increase task difficulty.
 - Synchronized visual frames (v) are encoded by a frozen pre-trained ViT to provide visual context (F_v). A cross-attention layer and MLP integrate these visual features, allowing tactile tokens to query visual information for reconstruction. A learnable hand-specific token (t_left or t_right) is introduced to mitigate geometric ambiguity.
 - **Loss Function**: Weighted L2 reconstruction loss, emphasizing masked regions, combined with the vector quantization losses from Stage 1. Non-frozen modules are jointly optimized, and the codebook restart mechanism remains active.

- **Stage 3: Multimodal Tactile Frame Prediction (Temporal Dynamics Modeling)**
 - **Objective**: Model temporal contact dynamics by predicting future tactile frames.
 - **Process**: The model takes recent visual observations (v_{T-2}) and past tactile trajectories (t_{T-2}) as input. These multimodal sequences are temporally aggregated and projected into the discrete latent space learned in previous stages. The decoder then predicts the tactile distribution (ˆt_T) at time step T.
 - **Loss Function**: An L2 loss between the predicted and ground-truth tactile frame (t_T). Prediction modules, the shared codebook, and the decoder are fine-tuned in this stage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate HandTouch against several representative tactile encoder baselines: a CNN-based encoder, ResNet-18, a VQ-VAE-based encoder, and a ViT-based encoder. All baselines are pretrained and evaluated under the same HT-Bench protocols.

- **Overall Performance**: HandTouch consistently achieved the highest performance on most evaluation metrics across fine-grained tactile similarity retrieval, masked tactile inpainting, and vision-to-tactile synthesis. This indicates stronger tactile structural modeling, cross-modal alignment, and generalization capabilities in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution (OOD) settings.

- **Fine-Grained Tactile Similarity Retrieval**:
 - HandTouch improved **Hit@1 from 94.27% to 99.27%** and **Recall@5 from 74.65% to 85.23%** compared to the strongest baseline (ViT-based encoder).
 - This suggests that HandTouch's learned embedding space more effectively preserves fine-grained structural similarity in tactile observations. CNN- and ResNet-based models were less effective at organizing samples based on global structural correspondence, while VQ-VAE showed notably worse performance, indicating potential loss of subtle structural cues in its discrete latent representations.

- **Spatially Masked Tactile Inpainting**:
 - On the standard test split, HandTouch achieved a full-map **RMSE of 0.010** and a full-map **cIoU of 0.911**, outperforming all baselines. For masked hole regions, it achieved 0.024 RMSE and 0.758 cIoU, demonstrating its ability to infer missing information.
 - Under the OOD setting, HandTouch maintained strong full-map reconstruction performance, with 0.039 RMSE and 0.768 cIoU. However, ResNet-18 performed comparably or slightly better on OOD hole-region metrics (0.056 RMSE, 0.620 cIoU for ResNet-18 vs. 0.066 RMSE, 0.565 cIoU for HandTouch). This suggests that while HandTouch generalizes well at the global tactile-map level, precise reconstruction of severely corrupted local regions in unseen tasks remains a challenge.

- **Vision-to-Tactile Synthesis**:
 - On the standard test split, HandTouch reduced the full-map **RMSE to 0.031** and improved **cIoU to 0.705**, surpassing all baselines. This indicates effective learning of vision–tactile correspondence.
 - Under the OOD setting, HandTouch achieved the highest **cIoU of 0.459**, demonstrating better cross-modal generalization to unseen tasks. VQ-VAE showed a slightly lower OOD RMSE (0.081 vs. HandTouch's 0.082) but a substantially lower cIoU (0.408), suggesting that while VQ-VAE might produce smoother predictions, it may not capture the localized contact patterns as effectively as HandTouch.

- **Multimodal Tactile Frame Prediction**:
 - HandTouch demonstrated accurate prediction, achieving an **RMSE of 0.031 and cIoU of 0.677** on the test split. It also maintained reasonable performance under OOD tasks.
 - This indicates HandTouch's capability to model temporal contact dynamics by integrating visual context and tactile history to anticipate future contact states.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation and embodied AI by addressing critical challenges in tactile perception.

The **significance** of this work lies in:
- **Establishing a Scalable Benchmark**: HT-Bench provides a large-scale, multi-task benchmark for dexterous full-hand tactile sensing paired with egocentric vision. By focusing on this specific, scalable data regime, the authors offer a practical alternative to the challenging goal of a truly universal tactile benchmark. This standardized evaluation protocol allows for consistent assessment of tactile encoders across key capabilities: contact geometry understanding, vision-touch alignment, and generalization to unseen tasks.
- **Advancing Tactile Representation Learning**: HandTouch demonstrates an effective approach for learning transferable tactile representations. Its progressive training methodology, incorporating vector quantization and cross-modal reasoning, enables it to model spatial topology, vision-tactile alignment, and temporal dynamics. The consistent outperformance of HandTouch over representative baselines across diverse evaluation tasks and OOD settings indicates the efficacy of its design principles.

The **potential impact** of this research includes:
- **Accelerating Research in Tactile AI**: HT-Bench has the potential to serve as a foundational resource for the development and evaluation of future tactile representation learning methods, much like prominent benchmarks have driven progress in visual perception. By offering a standardized platform, it can foster innovation and comparison within the community.
- **Enhancing Dexterous Robotic Manipulation**: More robust and generalizable tactile representations, such as those learned by HandTouch, are crucial for advancing dexterous manipulation capabilities in robots. These representations can contribute to more precise and adaptive control in contact-rich tasks, potentially improving robot performance in areas like grasp adjustment, slip detection and mitigation, and complex object interaction.
- **Towards Robust Multimodal Foundation Models**: This work contributes to the broader objective of building robust multimodal foundation models for robotics. By effectively integrating tactile sensing with egocentric vision, it moves towards a more comprehensive understanding of physical interactions, which is essential for more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.

**Limitations** acknowledged by the authors provide directions for future research:
- **Benchmark Scope**: HT-Bench is not universal; it focuses specifically on egocentric vision and full-hand tactile sensing, excluding other tactile sensing paradigms (e.g., fingertip optical sensors, force/torque arrays, skin-like taxel arrays) or non-hand robot embodiments. Expanding the benchmark to cover broader hardware platforms is an important future direction.
- **Experimental Analysis Depth**: Due to the computational cost of large-scale pretraining and multi-task evaluation, the current ablation studies are preliminary. More comprehensive analyses of HandTouch's individual components (e.g., vector-quantized codebook, masking curriculum, cross-attention fusion, hand-specific token, temporal prediction module) and sensitivity studies to training scale and OOD settings are needed.
- **Evaluation on Downstream Robotic Tasks**: The current evaluations focus on representation-level capabilities (retrieval, inpainting, synthesis, prediction) rather than direct metrics of downstream robotic performance (e.g., success rates in grasp adjustment or slip-aware manipulation). Future work aims to evaluate HandTouch in real-world dexterous manipulation scenarios.
- **Data Collection Efficiency**: The reliance on large-scale paired and synchronized visuo-tactile data, while scalable, requires careful calibration and maintenance during collection. Future efforts will explore more efficient data collection methods, stronger self-supervised objectives, and cross-dataset adaptation to reduce dependence on tightly synchronized datasets.
