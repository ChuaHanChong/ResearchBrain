# EvoTrainer: Co-Evolving LLM Policies and Training Harnesses for Autonomous Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoTrainer is an autonomous agentic-RL training framework that co-evolves the LLM policy and the training-side diagnostic harness. An LLM trainer agent builds versioned training runs in isolated git worktrees, diagnoses rollout-level score, signal and behavior evidence, revises its own metrics and analyzers, and banks reusable skills in persistent memory. It matches or exceeds human-engineered RL on Math, Coding and SWE, gaining 4.39 BC% on SWE-9B.

## Problem

- Autonomous experimentation systems frame model improvement as recipe search, leaving diagnostic views, memory and intervention logic fixed even as the dominant training bottleneck shifts.
- Scalar validation rewards in agentic RL mask distinct failure modes: reward leakage, behavior collapse, evaluation artifacts and low-information rollout groups all look alike under one score.
- Prior harness-optimization work targets inference-time scaffolding such as context, tools and memory, not the training-side signals like reward variance and dead-group ratios that steer RL runs.

## Method

- ==Policy self-evolution==: each version spawns single-factor intervention branches in isolated git worktrees, trained in parallel, then kept, pruned, reverted or merged into the next baseline.
- ==Trainer self-reflection== upgrades the harness along four axes, metric expansion, analyzer specialization, procedure revision from score-first to behavior-first, and external retrieval, over ==score/signal/behavior/version== diagnostic layers.
- ==Persistent memory== holds a version ledger, case memory, skill library and search trace; the SWE core adds a composed reward (CR, IF, SBE, ETT) plus **StdGroupFilter** dead-group removal.

## Results

- SWE-9B reaches **38.16 BC%** versus **30.19** no-RL and **33.77** human-engineered RL (**+4.39**, p<0.001); Math gains **+2.88**, while SWE-4B and Coding match the human reference within CI.
- A score-dominant subpath saturated at **33.33 BC%** by v3; richer diagnostics and backtesting then carried v4 to **36.30** and v8 to **38.16**, a **+4.83** gain over v3.
- Harness audit caught a Git-history leak inflating v1 to **48.80 BC%** (true **31.04**); reused StdGroupFilter lifted Coding v9 by **+1.17** and Math by **+0.96**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treat the diagnostic harness as a first-class evolving object: what you measure must change as bottlenecks shift, not only the training recipe.
> - Score-only iteration promotes invalid branches; a leaked reference patch looked like a breakthrough until behavior-level auditing of tool calls exposed it.
> - Validated mechanisms transfer across domains, a SWE variance filter fixed dead groups in coding, but autonomy costs roughly 400 million trainer-agent tokens per run.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of AI systems capable of participating in their own model development, including code editing, experiment launching, outcome inspection, and proposing new training versions, represents a growing area of research. This field suggests a future where model improvement is driven by agents iteratively revising training recipes through empirical feedback.

Existing autonomous experimentation systems primarily focus on optimizing candidate training recipes, such as in AutoResearch and Bilevel Autoresearch, which target GPT pretraining benchmarks (Karpathy, 2026; Qu and Lu, 2026). Other works, like GEAR, introduce population-style search for agentic code (Jeddi et al., 2026). A related line of research focuses on optimizing inference-side harnesses for Large Language Model (LLM) applications, as seen in Meta-Harness and AHE, which improve scaffolding around the model at inference time (Lee et al., 2026; Lin et al., 2026). These systems address aspects like context management, tool integration, and memory.

However, a recurring limitation in much of this work is the tendency to keep the decision infrastructure around training largely fixed. This means that while candidate recipes are explored, the diagnostic views, memory, and intervention logic used to interpret results often remain static. This limitation becomes particularly salient in complex reinforcement learning (RL) settings, especially in agentic RL, where bottlenecks can shift from reward sparsity to behavior collapse, evaluation artifacts, or the need for diverse diagnostic tools. Scalar validation scores may not adequately capture the complexities of diverse failure modes and the evolving nature of training challenges.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing an autonomous training framework that treats the training system itself as an object of improvement. While previous work has explored evolving code, algorithms, or training curricula, EvoTrainer distinguishes itself by extending this approach to agentic LLM RL training, where the "trainer" (the decision-making system overseeing training) must additionally evolve its training-side diagnostic harness. This distinguishes it from systems that optimize inference-time scaffolding by focusing on the diagnostic infrastructure that interprets training-time outcomes, such as reward distributions, group variance, and behavioral trajectories. The work positions itself as the first autonomous training framework in agentic LLM RL to consider the training-side diagnostic harness as an evolving component.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an autonomous training framework, termed EvoTrainer, that can co-evolve both LLM policies and their associated training-side diagnostic harnesses. This framework aims to move beyond conventional autonomous experimentation, which often frames LLM training as a "recipe search" with largely static diagnostic tools.

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations observed in current autonomous training paradigms, particularly within agentic reinforcement learning:
1. **Static Training Harnesses:** Most autonomous experimentation systems keep the diagnostic infrastructure fixed, relying on the same metrics, analyzers, and intervention logic across successive training versions. The paper argues this is insufficient for complex RL scenarios where the dominant training bottleneck can change dynamically.
2. **Shifting Bottlenecks in Agentic RL:** In agentic RL, where models interact with environments, use tools, and perform multi-turn actions (e.g., editing code, executing tests), the nature of training challenges can evolve. Early stages might struggle with reward sparsity, while later stages might face issues like behavior collapse, evaluation artifacts, or the need for specific diagnostic tools. A fixed diagnostic template struggles to identify and address these shifting issues.
3. **Masking of Failure Modes by Scalar Rewards:** Scalar validation scores, while useful, can mask diverse failure modes. A high score might conceal unhealthy behaviors or reward leakage, while a failed branch might contain valuable negative evidence. The evidence and procedures needed to guide training often need to evolve alongside the policy itself.
4. **Need for Evolving Diagnostics:** The diagnostics required to interpret training outcomes are not static; they often change across policy versions and cannot be fully specified in advance. For example, early versions might be interpretable via coarse validation trends, but later versions may require new behavioral metrics, reward audits, group-variance statistics, or specialized failure taxonomies.

To address these motivations, the paper defines a "trainer" as a decision-making system that observes completed policy versions, analyzes rollout evidence, proposes interventions, updates its diagnostic infrastructure, and determines the next steps in training. The core motivation is to enable this trainer to improve across training runs by accumulating evidence, revising its diagnostic harness, and reusing operational skills, in addition to the policy improving within a single training run.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EvoTrainer is an autonomous training framework designed to co-evolve LLM policies and their training-side diagnostic harnesses through empirical feedback. It operates through a dual-evolution process, supported by persistent memory and a reusable skill library.

#### 3.1. Versioned Autonomous Training

The framework formulates autonomous training as a sequence of evidence-conditioned version transitions. Each policy version (vᵢ) generates artifacts (metrics, rollouts, configurations, logs, diffs), which are interpreted by the current training harness (hᵢ). A complete training state (Tᵢ) includes the policy, harness, artifacts, a diagnosis (dᵢ), a proposed intervention (∆ᵢ), and an observed outcome (ωᵢ). This allows for nuanced outcomes beyond simple pass/fail, such as improvement, regression, mixed evidence, or insufficient evidence. The version transition is the unit of autonomous improvement, allowing the system to track and learn from both successes and failures.

#### 3.2. Policy Self-Evolution through Version-Controlled Exploration

The trainer agent iteratively constructs and refines runnable training versions.
- **Candidate Generation:** For a given version vᵢ, EvoTrainer creates a set of candidate branches (Bᵢ), each representing an intervention (∆⁽ᵏ⁾ᵢ) applied to the current baseline (vᵢ ⊕ ∆⁽ᵏ⁾ᵢ). These interventions can target various aspects like rewards, filtering, data selection, rollout settings, optimization choices, or tool-use behavior.
- **Controlled Interventions:** By default, EvoTrainer prioritizes single-factor interventions to enable clearer attribution of outcomes. Bundled changes are only introduced if components have prior independent support or if the interaction itself is the subject of testing, with rationales recorded in a version ledger.
- **Parallel Exploration and Selection:** Branches are materialized as isolated worktrees, allowing for parallel training. After training, the trainer compares evidence from all branches and recommends whether to keep, prune, revert, or merge each. A successful branch becomes the next baseline (vᵢ₊₁), while failed or ambiguous branches are retained as negative evidence. This version-control discipline ensures an auditable lineage of explored interventions.

#### 3.3. Trainer Reflection and Harness Evolution

Recognizing the limitations of fixed diagnostics, EvoTrainer treats the training harness itself as an evolving object. The harness is organized into four diagnostic layers:
- **Score:** Validation metrics and benchmark-level improvements.
- **Signal:** Reward variance, dead-group ratio, component-level reward adoption.
- **Behavior:** Tool-use patterns, search/edit/test structure, trajectory length, degeneration modes, environment anomalies.
- **Version:** Cross-branch promotion, rejection, and retention decisions.
These layers are complementary, providing different perspectives on training outcomes.
- **Harness Upgrades:** A "diagnostic gap" (when existing evidence is insufficient to explain an outcome, distinguish hypotheses, or select an intervention) triggers a harness upgrade along four axes:
 - **Metric expansion:** Adding new metrics (e.g., dead-group ratio, rollout diversity, first-edit turn).
 - **Analyzer specialization:** Developing domain-specific analyzer routines.
 - **Procedure revision:** Adjusting analysis priorities (e.g., shifting from score-first to behavior-first).
 - **External evidence retrieval:** Querying external sources (papers, repositories, past traces) to synthesize candidate interventions or harness updates.

#### 3.4. Persistent Memory and Reusable Skill Library

EvoTrainer incorporates a persistent memory layer to facilitate cumulative improvement:
- **Version Ledger (L_ver):** Stores model lineage, configuration diffs, Git diffs, and decisions (keep/prune/merge).
- **Case Memory (C_case):** Records recurring patterns, such as score-and-behavior divergence or interventions that consistently fail under specific domain structures.
- **Skill Library (S_skill):** Stores validated analyzer skills, repair strategies, and procedure templates that can be retrieved, extended, or adapted by later versions.
- **Search Trace (S_search):** Logs retrieval queries, external sources, distilled insights, and adopted patches or procedures.
This memory layer enables EvoTrainer to learn from past experiences and reuse validated operational mechanisms across domains.

#### 3.5. Trainer-Agent Implementation and Autonomy Scope

In the experiments, the trainer agent is implemented using Claude Sonnet 4.6, with access to repository files, shell execution, experiment artifacts, and retrieval utilities.
- **Human Role:** Humans bootstrap the initial task, benchmark, data, compute environment, and initial playbooks. They also approve costly or consequential execution steps.
- **Trainer Agent Role:** The agent autonomously executes the diagnostic loop, recommending interventions, performing artifact analysis and diagnosis, updating the harness, creating skills, and conducting external search and backtesting. This division of labor maintains human oversight for critical actions while automating the iterative diagnostic and planning processes.

#### 3.6. SWE Training-Core Instantiation

The framework was instantiated for Software Engineering (SWE) tasks using a GRPO-style training core. This core incorporates asymmetric Clip-Higher bounds and weak KL regularization to a fixed reference policy. It calculates group-relative advantage based on scalar rewards, with group variance being crucial for learning signals.
The SWE reward design is composite, combining task correctness (CR) with behavior-sensitive components: instruction-following (IF), search-before-edit (SBE), and edit-then-test (ETT). These components emerged through the evolutionary process rather than being pre-defined. Variance-aware group filtering is applied before advantage normalization, removing "dead groups" with zero reward variance to address sparse hidden-test outcomes common in SWE.

#### 3.7. Experimental Setup

EvoTrainer was evaluated across three domains:
- **Mathematical Reasoning (Math):** Trained on BigMath-Hard, evaluated on AIME and CNMO problems.
- **Competitive-Programming Code Generation (Coding):** Trained on TACO-verified, evaluated on LiveCodeBench-v6 problems.
- **Repo-Level Software Engineering (SWE):** Trained on swe-rebench-v6, evaluated on held-out Python instances in Docker environments with hidden fail-to-pass tests.
Evaluation used Avg@8 (mean over 8 independent rollouts) with a single random seed (42). Correctness was judged by a Qwen3.5-4B LLM for Math, stdin/stdout execution for Coding, and BC% (proportion of valid executions passing hidden tests) for SWE. Baselines included a no-RL base model, a human-engineered RL configuration, AutoResearch (score-only iteration), and several algorithmic baselines (GSPO, GRPO, RAGEN variants).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EvoTrainer across mathematical reasoning, competitive programming, and software engineering tasks yielded several findings.

#### 4.1. Cross-Domain Performance Improvement

EvoTrainer consistently demonstrated performance improvements across all evaluated domains.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** EvoTrainer matched or exceeded prior baselines in every setting. Against the no-RL base model, statistically significant gains were observed across all domains (paired Wilcoxon p < 0.001).
- **Human-Engineered RL Reference:** EvoTrainer delivered statistically significant improvements over the human-engineered RL reference on SWE-9B (gain of +4.39 BC%, 95% CI [+2.61, +6.34], p < 0.001) and Math (gain of +2.88, p < 0.001). On SWE-4B and Coding, EvoTrainer’s performance matched the human reference within the bootstrap confidence interval (p > 0.1).
- **AutoResearch Comparison:** EvoTrainer exceeded AutoResearch's score-only iteration in every domain, including Coding, where AutoResearch performed below the no-RL base.
- **SWE-9B Result:** EvoTrainer achieved 38.16 BC% on SWE-9B, compared to 30.19 BC% for no-RL and 33.77 BC% for the human-engineered RL baseline, representing a +7.97 and +4.39 absolute gain, respectively.
- **Domain-Specific Recipes:** The retained EvoTrainer recipes varied substantially across domains, indicating that the framework adapts interventions to specific bottlenecks rather than applying a universal template. For instance, Math evolved towards computation-aware tool augmentation, Coding towards execution-aligned reward shaping with variance-aware filtering, and SWE towards a richer behavior-sensitive training pathway.

#### 4.2. Adaptation Across Math and Coding

The framework demonstrated adaptive behavior in different domains:
- **Math Evolution:** Early diagnosis identified a response-length bottleneck and subsequently computational-heavy errors. The process involved length-budget correction, reward-side refinement, transfer of a variance-aware filter (StdGroupFilter), and integration of a Code Interpreter to address computational limitations.
- **Coding Evolution:** The trajectory for Coding started with repairing a measurement-side artifact (format-gate/truncation issues). This was followed by replacing binary correctness with shaped continuous reward (based on passed-test ratio) to recover partial execution progress. Residual zero-variance groups then motivated the reuse of StdGroupFilter, adapted from SWE, leading to a Dual-Level Filter in the final configuration.

#### 4.3. Component-Level Counterfactual Evidence

Analysis of EvoTrainer's trajectory provided evidence for the importance of its components:
- **Richer Diagnostics Break Saturation (SWE-9B):** An early subpath for SWE-9B that relied solely on scalar score comparison and published-style recipe adaptation saturated at 33.33 BC%. Once richer diagnostics, backtesting, and harness-guided intervention planning were engaged, the trajectory advanced to 38.16 BC%, demonstrating a +4.83 gain that score-only inspection did not achieve.
- **Harness Audit Blocks False Promotion (SWE-9B):** In an uncleaned repository state, an early SWE-9B version achieved an apparent 48.80 BC% due to accessing reference patches via Git history commands. Harness-level inspection, through behavior-level tool usage analysis, identified this anomaly. After sanitization, the legitimate score was 31.04 BC%. This demonstrated the harness's ability to prevent the promotion of invalid, high-scoring branches.
- **Skill Reuse Alters Candidate Set (Coding v9):** In Coding, after shaped continuous reward, approximately 31% of rollout groups still had near-zero reward variance, similar to a prior SWE regime where StdGroupFilter was validated. The trainer retrieved and adapted this filter, leading to a gain of +1.17 Avg@8 for v9 and seeding the Dual-Level Filter for v10 (another +1.08 gain). This illustrates how the skill library enabled the selection of a mechanism-matched candidate that might otherwise have been absent, impacting the search trajectory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EvoTrainer introduces a framework for autonomous model training that formulates the improvement process as a cross-version evolution of both LLM policies and their accompanying training-side diagnostic harnesses. This approach deviates from conventional autonomous experimentation that primarily focuses on iterating through fixed training recipes based on scalar score comparisons.

The empirical results indicate that EvoTrainer matches or exceeds human-engineered RL references across diverse domains, including mathematical reasoning, competitive-programming code generation, and repository-level software engineering. The most substantial gains were observed in long-horizon agentic software engineering tasks (SWE-9B), where the system achieved 38.16 BC% compared to the human-engineered RL reference of 33.77 BC%.

The significance of this work extends beyond score-based performance metrics:
- **Adaptive Training Process:** The research demonstrates that the retained training strategies and recipes diverge across different domains, indicating that EvoTrainer adapts its interventions to specific bottlenecks and characteristics of each task rather than applying a static, general-purpose approach.
- **Robustness through Diagnostic Harness:** The component-level analyses provide evidence that the evolving diagnostic harness is instrumental in preventing the promotion of invalid, high-scoring branches (e.g., through detecting data leakage) and for guiding the training process beyond local optima that simple score-driven iteration might encounter.
- **Cumulative Learning:** The implementation of a persistent memory and a reusable skill library enables the system to accumulate and deploy validated operational mechanisms (like the StdGroupFilter) across different domains, fostering a cumulative rather than isolated experimental process. This suggests that the trainer learns how to *interpret* and *improve* the training process itself.

The potential impact of EvoTrainer lies in its contribution to more robust, efficient, and intelligent autonomous model development. By enabling the joint evolution of policies and the diagnostic infrastructure that interprets their outcomes, the framework suggests a pathway for AI systems to autonomously navigate the complexities of RL training, identify subtle failure modes, and adapt intervention strategies, potentially reducing the reliance on extensive human expertise for hyperparameter tuning and training recipe design. This marks a conceptual shift from merely searching for optimal training recipes to actively evolving the very mechanisms that guide and diagnose the training process.

The paper acknowledges limitations, including the computational cost of trainer-agent tokens (approximately 4.0 × 10⁸ tokens) and the current exploration depth (7 to 10 versions per domain), which suggests that scalability for hundreds of versions with dynamic memory management remains an area for future work. However, the reported total SWE GPU-hours being below the human-engineered RL reference suggests that trainer reasoning can substitute for, rather than merely add to, training-side search efforts.
