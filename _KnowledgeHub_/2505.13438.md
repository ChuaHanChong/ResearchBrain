# Optimizing Anytime Reasoning via Budget Relative Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sea AI Lab and the National University of Singapore developed AnytimeReasoner, a framework that optimizes Large Language Models for reasoning under varying computational budgets. This approach, using verifiable dense rewards and Budget Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO), consistently improves "anytime" accuracy and final performance across mathematical reasoning benchmarks while enhancing token efficiency.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) typically optimize reasoning for a single, fixed, and often large token budget, leading to high computational costs and limited serving capacity for online services.
- Existing LLM reasoning methods lack inherent "anytime" capability, meaning they cannot gracefully provide improving solutions or optimal performance under variable or interrupted computational budgets.
- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in LLMs suffers from sparse, end-of-trace rewards, which hinder effective credit assignment for long reasoning chains and often lead to unstable training.

## Method

- The AnytimeReasoner framework ==decouples the thinking process== (Chain-of-Thought) from the ==answer summarization==, allowing for a valid summary even when the thinking process is incomplete or truncated.
- It optimizes for anytime reasoning by sampling varying thinking budgets and generating ==verifiable dense rewards== at multiple truncation points within a reasoning chain, providing richer training signals.
- A novel variance reduction technique, ==Budget Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO)==, is introduced, combining intra-trajectory progress and group average baselines to stabilize and improve the efficiency of RL training for the thinking policy.

## Results

- AnytimeReasoner variants consistently outperformed the GRPO baseline across all evaluated mathematical reasoning benchmarks and thinking budget prior distributions, showing superior "anytime" and final accuracy.
- Models trained with the framework achieved significantly shorter "Average Thinking Length" under maximum budgets, indicating improved token efficiency and more concise reasoning without compromising accuracy.
- Ablation studies confirmed that verifiable dense rewards, decoupled policy optimization, and Budget Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO) each independently contribute to improved reasoning performance and training stability.
- The framework maintained its clear performance advantages, showing up to **5 absolute percentage points** improvement in "anytime" reasoning accuracy, when scaled to larger **7B** parameter LLMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense, verifiable rewards generated at various budget points throughout a reasoning trace significantly improve credit assignment and learning stability for LLMs compared to traditional sparse end-of-trace rewards.
> - Decoupling the thinking and summary policies, and specifically training the summary policy with a uniform budget distribution, is crucial for robustly extracting optimal answers from incomplete reasoning processes.
> - Budget Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO) effectively reduces variance in RL training for LLM generation, enhancing stability and performance, especially for optimizing long reasoning sequences.
> - Optimizing LLMs for "anytime" reasoning, which maximizes performance across all computational budgets, also inherently leads to improvements in final accuracy under maximum budget conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the deployment and scaling of Large Language Models (LLMs): **optimizing their reasoning capabilities under varying computational constraints, particularly token budgets.**

The broader research landscape for LLMs has seen significant advancements in reasoning abilities through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting [Wei et al., 2022] and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) [Lambert et al., 2024]. Projects like OpenAI's o1 [OpenAI, 2024] and DeepSeek-R1 [Guo et al., 2025] have showcased the power of scaling "test-time compute" – essentially allowing LLMs to "think" more extensively before providing an answer. However, the prevailing paradigm in RLVR-based reasoning has been to optimize for a single, often large, fixed token budget, typically focusing on maximizing final accuracy.

This approach presents several limitations that "Optimizing Anytime Reasoning" aims to resolve:

- **Efficiency in Deployment:** Fixed, large token budgets are costly for online services, limiting serving capacity. There's a need for models that can gracefully handle interruptions or variable resource allocations.
- **Flexibility and Adaptability:** Users or systems might desire different levels of reasoning depth depending on the task's criticality or available resources. Current models often don't natively support this "anytime" capability, where solution quality improves with more compute time.
- **Credit Assignment in RL:** Traditional RLVR uses sparse, end-of-trace rewards. This makes credit assignment challenging for long reasoning chains, as it's hard to pinpoint which intermediate steps contributed to the final outcome.

This work specifically positions itself as an advancement in **anytime algorithms** [Dean and Boddy, 1988; Zilberstein and Russell, 1995], adapting this concept to LLM reasoning. Unlike previous methods that focus on static budget constraints or merely penalize length, AnytimeReasoner actively trains LLMs to be proficient at providing the best possible answer *at any given point* in their reasoning process.

The paper differentiates itself from closely related work, such as MRT [Qu et al., 2025], which also explores optimizing test-time compute. While sharing a high-level goal, AnytimeReasoner adopts a principled RL approach with dense rewards and an emphasis on long-term returns, contrasting with MRT's meta-RL and greedy optimization of immediate progress.

By introducing **verifiable dense rewards** through budget-constrained summarization and a novel variance reduction technique (BRPO), this research contributes significantly to making LLM reasoning more:
1. **Economical:** By allowing for variable compute usage and reusing computation, supporting incremental thinking.
2. **Robust:** By training models to perform well even when interrupted.
3. **Trainable:** By providing richer learning signals for RL, addressing the sparse reward problem.

This research marks a significant step towards more practical, efficient, and adaptable LLMs for complex reasoning tasks, moving beyond the "one-size-fits-all" large budget approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **optimize the reasoning performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) under varying "thinking budget" constraints**, a paradigm they term "anytime reasoning." This contrasts with prevailing methods that typically optimize for a single, fixed, and often large token budget, primarily focusing on final performance.

The key motivations driving this objective are:

- **Enhancing Efficiency and Flexibility in LLM Deployment:** Current LLM reasoning approaches, which often involve extensive "Chain of Thought" (CoT) generation, are computationally expensive. For online services, maintaining long CoT processes for every request can severely limit serving capacity and increase operational costs. The authors highlight the need for models that can adapt to available computing resources, providing the "best possible solution so far" if interrupted, and improving solution quality with more resources.
- **Addressing the Limitations of Sparse Rewards in RL:** Most existing RL-based LLM reasoning methods, like those inspired by R1-Zero, use sparse rewards (0/1 correctness) obtained only at the very end of a reasoning trace. This makes effective credit assignment difficult, especially for long and complex reasoning processes. It's challenging for the RL agent to understand which specific steps contributed to a successful outcome or where it went wrong. This often leads to unstable and inefficient RL training.
- **Improving Token Efficiency and "Anytime" Performance:** The goal is not just to reduce overall token usage but to make the reasoning process inherently "anytime" – meaning the model should be able to produce a high-quality summary at any given point during its thought process. This allows for a flexible trade-off between computation and accuracy, enabling strategies like incrementally increasing budgets based on user needs or system load. The paper explicitly mentions use cases like Gemini 2.5, where users might want to control thinking budgets, and online services prioritizing throughput.
- **Developing Robust and Stable RL Training for LLMs:** Recognizing the instability and inefficiency often associated with sparse rewards in RL for LLMs, the authors aim to develop more robust training techniques. This includes introducing dense rewards and an improved variance reduction method to make the learning process more stable and sample-efficient.

In essence, the paper seeks to move LLM reasoning from a fixed-computation, final-answer-focused paradigm to a dynamic, resource-aware, and continuously improving "anytime" paradigm, while simultaneously addressing core RL training challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AnytimeReasoner framework introduces several key methodological innovations to achieve its objectives:

#### 3.1. Decoupled Thinking and Summary Policies

The standard RL objective for LLM reasoning typically combines the generation of the thinking process ($z$) and the summarized answer ($y$) into a single policy ($\pi_\theta$). The authors argue that in real-world scenarios, a valid summary should always be provided, even if thinking is incomplete.
- **Decoupling:** They propose separating the thinking policy ($\pi_\theta$) from the summary policy ($\pi_\phi$). The thinking policy generates the Chain-of-Thought ($z$), and the summary policy then generates the answer ($y$) based on $z$.
- **Forced Summarization:** When the thinking process is truncated due to budget constraints, ellipses are inserted, followed by a special `</think>` token, to prompt the model to produce a summary.
- **Objective Redefinition:** The overall objective becomes $J(\theta, \phi) = E_{x \sim p_X, z \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)} [r_\phi(x, z)]$, where $r_\phi(x, z) = E_{y \sim \pi_\phi(\cdot|x, z)} [r(x, y)]$. This means the reward for a thinking process $z$ is the expected reward of summaries generated from it.

#### 3.2. Optimizing Anytime Reasoning with Sampled Budgets and Dense Rewards

This is the core of the anytime reasoning objective.
- **Budget Sampling:** Instead of a fixed, large token budget, the thinking budget $b$ is sampled from a prior distribution $p_B$ over a set of increasing budgets $\{b_1, \dots, b_m\}$.
- **Truncated Thinking and Dense Rewards:** For each sampled budget $b$, the complete thinking process $z$ is truncated to $z_{\le b}$. The model is then forced to summarize an answer from this truncated thinking, and a verifiable reward $r(x, y)$ is calculated. This process generates **verifiable dense rewards** at multiple points in the reasoning chain, providing richer training signals than sparse end-of-trace rewards.
- **Anytime Objective:** The objective is to maximize the expected reward across all sampled budgets: $J_{anytime}(\theta, \phi) = E_{b \sim p_B, x \sim p_X, z \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)} [r_\phi(x, z_{\le b})]$. This effectively maximizes the area under the accuracy-vs-budget curve.
- **Credit Assignment:** The dense rewards enable better credit assignment, localizing the contribution of tokens to achieving correctness at various stages of reasoning.

#### 3.3. Budget Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO) for Variance Reduction

To improve the stability and efficiency of RL training with these dense rewards, BRPO is introduced as a novel variance reduction technique for the thinking policy.
- **Advantage Function:** The gradient for the thinking policy is calculated using an advantage function $(R(x, z, j_t) - V(x, z_{<t}))$, where $R(x, z, j_t)$ is the cumulative reward from the current token $t$ up to the nearest budget $b_{j_t}$ and beyond, weighted by the prior probabilities.
- **Hybrid Baseline:** BRPO proposes a baseline $V(x, z_{<t})$ that combines two components:
 - **$V_1$ (Current Progress Baseline):** This component leverages the scores (rewards) evaluated at *previous* thinking budgets within the *current* reasoning trajectory, weighted by a discount factor $\lambda$. This capitalizes on the monotonic improvement assumption of optimal summary policies (i.e., $r_\phi(x, z_{<t}) \le r_\phi(x, [z_{<t}, z_{\ge t}])$). It's particularly effective for longer reasoning chains.
 - **$V_2$ (Group Average Baseline):** This component is a variant of GRPO, using the average return of a group of reasoning trajectories for the same question. It serves as a complement when $t$ is small and $V_1$ might be less informative.
- **Interpolated Baseline:** The final baseline $V(x, z_{<t})$ is an interpolation of $V_1$ and $V_2$, dynamically weighting them based on the current thinking token step $t$ relative to the maximum budget $b_m$. This allows BRPO to leverage the strengths of both baselines across different stages of thinking.

#### 3.4. Decoupled Optimization for Thinking and Summary Policies (with different budget distributions)

- **Optimal Summary Policy:** An optimal summary policy is crucial, especially for incomplete thinking. Its effectiveness is highly sensitive to the budget distribution it's trained on.
- **Uniform Summary Budget:** To ensure a robust summary policy across all possible interruption points, the summary policy $\pi_\phi$ is optimized using a uniform budget distribution $p'_B$ (typically over all supported budgets), irrespective of the $p_B$ used for the thinking policy $\pi_\theta$. This ensures the summary policy learns to extract the best answer even from short, unfinished thoughts.
- **Shared Model:** When a single LLM acts as both thinking and summary policies (i.e., $\phi = \theta$), the overall gradient is a sum of gradients from both objectives.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Tree-like Generation and Training:** To efficiently handle multiple truncations and summaries, a tree-like generation structure is used with vLLM's prefix caching.
- **Tree Structure Attention Mask:** For training, a custom attention mask based on FlexAttention is implemented to avoid redundant computations when multiple summaries are appended to a shared thinking prefix.
- **PPO:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used to optimize both policies.
- **Dataset and Base Model:** Fine-tuning is performed on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B models using 40,315 math problems from DeepScaleR.

This multifaceted approach allows AnytimeReasoner to learn efficient and adaptable reasoning strategies by providing dense, verifiable feedback throughout the thinking process, stabilized by an advanced variance reduction technique, and supported by a robust summary generation mechanism.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling empirical results demonstrating the effectiveness of AnytimeReasoner across various mathematical reasoning benchmarks and model sizes.

#### 4.1. Superior Anytime and Final Performance

- **Consistent Outperformance:** Across all evaluated mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AMC 2022, AIME 2024, MATH, Minerva Math, Olympiad Bench) and various thinking budget prior distributions (base, uniform, linear), AnytimeReasoner variants consistently outperform the GRPO baseline.
- **Anytime Accuracy Improvement:** AnytimeReasoner-uniform, which explicitly optimizes for performance across all budgets, shows superior performance at smaller budgets, highlighting its ability to adapt to interruptions.
- **Final Accuracy Improvement:** Even at the maximum token budget (8000 tokens), which reflects standard reasoning task performance, AnytimeReasoner significantly outperforms GRPO. For instance, on AMC 2022, AR-base achieves 68.4% vs GRPO's 65.0%, and on AIME 2024, AR-base achieves 32.7% vs GRPO's 28.9%. This indicates that optimizing for anytime reasoning also benefits final, full-budget performance.
- **Robustness to Prior Distributions:** While different prior distributions show slight variations, the overall AnytimeReasoner framework demonstrates robustness, converging to similar strong performance at larger budgets irrespective of the specific prior.

#### 4.2. Impact of Verifiable Dense Rewards (Ablation Study)

- **Improved Performance:** Introducing dense rewards (using a linear prior for the thinking policy) leads to improvements in both anytime and final accuracy compared to GRPO. This confirms the hypothesis that richer, verifiable signals enhance credit assignment and learning.
- **Enhanced Token Efficiency:** Models trained with dense rewards exhibit a significantly shorter "Average Thinking Length" under the maximum budget. This is because the objective naturally incentivizes the model to reach correct answers as quickly as possible, favoring more concise reasoning.
- **Superior to Reward Shaping:** Unlike reward shaping techniques (e.g., length penalties) which can reduce thinking length but often sacrifice performance or stability, dense rewards achieve brevity without compromising accuracy and with greater training stability.

#### 4.3. Impact of Decoupled Optimization (Ablation Study)

- **Better Summary Policy:** Decoupling the optimization of the thinking and summary policies (by training the summary policy with a uniform budget distribution) leads to noticeable improvements in both anytime and final accuracy, particularly on benchmarks like AMC.
- **Criticality of Summary Policy:** This finding underscores that a well-trained, robust summary policy capable of extracting optimal answers from incomplete thoughts is crucial for effective anytime reasoning performance.

#### 4.4. Impact of Budget Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO) (Ablation Study)

- **Effective Variance Reduction:** BRPO significantly reduces variance during RL training, especially for longer thinking sequences, as evidenced by its correlation with the Monte Carlo return and normalized variance curves.
- **Performance Gains:** Incorporating BRPO's variance reduction techniques into GRPO (even when optimizing only the maximum budget) results in improved performance, particularly on the AIME benchmark.
- **Synergy with Decoupled Optimization:** The benefits of BRPO are further amplified when combined with decoupled optimization, suggesting that a better summary policy allows the advanced variance reduction technique to realize its full potential.

#### 4.5. Scaling to Larger Models (7B Model Evaluation)

- **Consistent Superiority:** The AnytimeReasoner framework maintains its clear performance advantages even when scaled to a larger 7B parameter model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B).
- **Significant Improvement:** Up to 5 absolute percentage points improvement in anytime reasoning accuracy is observed compared to the GRPO baseline.
- **Training Stability:** Despite higher variance in final accuracy for larger models, AnytimeReasoner generally outperforms GRPO during training.

In summary, the main findings strongly support the effectiveness of AnytimeReasoner's core components: budget-sampled training for dense rewards, decoupled policy optimization for robust summarization, and BRPO for stable and efficient RL training. The framework consistently improves LLM reasoning performance across varying budgets, enhances token efficiency, and scales effectively to larger models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Optimizing Anytime Reasoning via Budget Relative Policy Optimization" paper introduces a highly significant framework with broad potential impact across several dimensions of LLM research and application:

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in LLM Reasoning Optimization

The most significant contribution is shifting the LLM reasoning optimization paradigm from a "fixed-budget, final-answer" approach to an "anytime, resource-adaptive" one. This aligns LLM capabilities with real-world computational constraints and user expectations for flexible performance.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Practicality and Scalability for Online Services

- **Cost-Efficiency:** Online services can dynamically adjust the thinking budget based on demand, reducing computational costs during peak loads while maintaining acceptable accuracy. This directly translates to higher serving capacity and lower operational expenses for LLM providers.
- **Improved User Experience:** Users can specify desired latency or accuracy levels, allowing the model to provide quick, good-enough answers or thorough, highly accurate ones, catering to diverse application scenarios (e.g., real-time chatbots vs. complex problem-solving tools).
- **Incremental Reasoning:** The ability to provide continuous, improving solutions allows for "thinking on demand," where computation can be paused and resumed, reusing previous work.

#### 5.3. More Effective Reinforcement Learning for LLMs

- **Solving Sparse Reward Problem:** The introduction of verifiable dense rewards is a crucial advancement for RL in LLMs. It provides rich, continuous learning signals, greatly improving credit assignment compared to traditional sparse outcome rewards. This can lead to faster convergence and more stable training for complex tasks.
- **Advanced Variance Reduction:** BRPO offers a novel and effective variance reduction technique tailored for sequential generation in LLMs. Its hybrid approach, combining intra-trajectory progress with group averages, is a methodological improvement that can be adopted by other RL applications in LLM generation beyond reasoning.

#### 5.4. Token Efficiency and Concise Reasoning

The framework naturally encourages LLMs to arrive at correct answers more efficiently, leading to shorter reasoning chains for the same or even better accuracy. This is a highly desired property for LLMs, as it reduces latency, computational cost, and the environmental footprint of large models.

#### 5.5. Broader Applicability and Future Research

- **Beyond Math Reasoning:** While demonstrated on mathematical reasoning, the principles of anytime reasoning, dense verifiable rewards, decoupled policies, and BRPO are generalizable to other complex reasoning domains (e.g., code generation, scientific discovery, logical deduction) where iterative thinking processes are involved and intermediate verifiers can be designed.
- **New Evaluation Metrics:** This work necessitates and encourages the development of new evaluation metrics and benchmarks that assess "anytime performance" effectively, rather than just final accuracy at a fixed budget.
- **Foundation for Adaptive Agents:** AnytimeReasoner lays groundwork for more sophisticated adaptive LLM agents that can dynamically manage their cognitive resources, self-monitor their progress, and decide when to stop or continue thinking based on a dynamic utility function.

In conclusion, "Optimizing Anytime Reasoning" moves LLM reasoning a significant step forward from a static, brute-force approach to a dynamic, intelligent, and resource-aware one. Its innovations in dense reward structures and variance reduction have methodological implications for the broader field of RL applied to generative models, promising more efficient, flexible, and robust LLM systems.
