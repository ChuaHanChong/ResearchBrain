# Extending Embodied Question Answering from Perception to Decision

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed EQA-Decision, a dataset with over four million multimodal question-answer pairs, and RoboDecision, a baseline model, to extend embodied question answering beyond static perception to dynamic decision-making in interactive environments. RoboDecision-8B achieved an overall score of 68.06 on the new EQA-Decision Benchmark, outperforming leading proprietary and open-source models, and demonstrated consistent improvements on external embodied benchmarks like RoboVQA.

## Problem

- Existing Embodied Question Answering (EQA) datasets were fragmented and limited in scope, focusing primarily on static perception or isolated reasoning skills.
- Prior work neglected dynamic decision-making processes, failing to model the 'instant decisions' crucial for real-world agent interaction with changing environments.
- There was a lack of a unified, large-scale framework to systematically evaluate embodied reasoning across scene understanding, spatial interpretation, task dynamics, and action-level decisions.

## Method

- Constructed **EQA-Decision**, a large-scale dataset with over four million multimodal Q&A pairs covering static scene construction, spatial understanding, task dynamics reasoning, and instant decision-making, using diverse simulated and real-world data sources.
- Introduced new task formulations within **EQA-Decision** for progress analysis and context-aware instant decision, enabling evaluation of agents' ability to reason over temporal dynamics and adapt actions in real time.
- Developed RoboDecision, a baseline model based on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, trained with a three-stage pipeline (SFT, CoT-SFT, ==GRPO==) and a hybrid reward function to connect perception, reasoning, and action-level decision-making.

## Results

- RoboDecision-8B achieved the highest overall score of 68.06 on the EQA-Decision Benchmark, outperforming leading proprietary models like Gemini-2.5-Pro (48.68) and GPT-5 (51.03), and open-source MLLMs like Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (48.84).
- It demonstrated substantial improvements across all EQA-Decision modules, with notable gains in temporal reasoning (from 23.98 to 52.95) and instant decision tasks, highlighting its enhanced ability to integrate spatial and temporal cues for action-level decisions.
- RoboDecision-8B consistently outperformed general-purpose VLMs and other embodied baselines on external benchmarks such as RoboVQA (BLEU-4 43.55 vs. 18.64 for Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct), ERQA, and Where2Place, validating its generalizability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematically integrating reasoning across static scene construction, spatial understanding, task dynamics, and instant decision-making is essential for developing agents capable of robust perception-to-decision behavior.
> - A multi-stage training approach combining Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervised fine-tuning and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is effective in enhancing multi-step reasoning, interpretability, and grounding decisions in visual input.
> - Hybrid reward functions that align reasoning coherence, factual correctness, and visual evidence are crucial for guiding vision-language models towards perception-guided decision-making in embodied tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence has experienced advancements driven by multimodal large language models (MLLMs), which integrate visual perception with language reasoning. These models, such as GPT-4V, Gemini-2.5, and Qwen2.5-VL, have demonstrated capabilities in tasks like visual question answering, spatial reasoning, and instruction following, representing a step towards unifying perception, cognition, and action within a single framework.

Building on these developments, research has increasingly focused on adapting MLLMs to robotic and embodied environments. This has led to the creation of various datasets and benchmarks for evaluating perception and reasoning in realistic settings. Prior efforts have concentrated on spatial understanding and task planning (e.g.,), with large-scale egocentric collections (e.g.,) providing multimodal observations of human-object interactions. Benchmarks (e.g.,) have aimed to assess embodied reasoning and grounding capabilities through standardized evaluation protocols.

However, the existing landscape of Embodied Question Answering (EQA) datasets and benchmarks has been characterized by fragmentation and limited scope. Many previous works primarily focused on static perception or isolated reasoning skills, such as spatial grounding or procedural planning. They often did not explicitly model the dynamic decision-making processes that unfold as an agent interacts with a changing environment. This limitation meant that current benchmarks generally did not capture the "instant decisions" crucial for real-world embodied intelligence, where perception, reasoning, and action must evolve over time.

This paper addresses these limitations by extending the scope of EQA. Previous EQA datasets mainly target perception-oriented tasks, where models primarily describe visible elements. This work introduces a new paradigm that moves beyond static perception to dynamic interaction and decision-making. It aims to bridge the gap between perception, reasoning, and action by providing a framework that systematically covers a broader range of embodied reasoning dimensions, including task dynamics and instant decision-making, which were not comprehensively addressed by prior datasets like Robo2VLM, RoboVQA, or ShareRobot. By focusing on temporally grounded and decision-oriented paradigms, this research seeks to provide a more unified and comprehensive approach to evaluating embodied intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to extend embodied reasoning from static perception to dynamic interaction and real-time decision-making within embodied environments. This overarching goal is pursued through two main contributions: the development of a new dataset and a corresponding baseline model.

The motivation behind this work stems from several limitations identified in the existing Embodied Question Answering (EQA) landscape:

1. **Fragmented Reasoning Skill Coverage:** Existing EQA datasets and benchmarks typically focus on a limited subset of reasoning skills, such as spatial understanding or procedural reasoning. This fragmentation prevents a unified and comprehensive evaluation of an agent's capabilities across the diverse demands of embodied intelligence. The lack of a single, large-scale framework makes it difficult to assess how different reasoning skills integrate.
2. **Neglect of Dynamic Decision-Making:** A significant gap identified is the emphasis of prior work on static perception or isolated reasoning skills. While tasks like spatial grounding and planning reasoning are important, they often do not account for the real-time decision-making process that is essential for an agent interacting with a dynamic environment. Without explicit modeling of how perception, reasoning, and action evolve over time, current benchmarks are unable to capture the "instant decisions" required for practical embodied intelligence. This limits the ability to evaluate an agent's adaptability and responsiveness to changing circumstances.
3. **Lack of Unified Evaluation Framework:** There was no unified, large-scale dataset or benchmark that systematically covers the complementary dimensions of embodied reasoning from scene understanding to action-level decisions. This hindered consistent and reproducible evaluation of MLLMs in complex embodied tasks.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of this research are:

- **To construct EQA-Decision:** A large-scale embodied QA dataset that systematically covers four complementary dimensions of embodied reasoning: static scene construction, spatial understanding, task dynamics reasoning, and instant decision. This dataset is designed to contain over four million question-answer pairs with hierarchical annotations across diverse embodied scenarios, moving beyond perception-oriented tasks to decision-centric ones.
- **To introduce new task formulations:** Specifically for progress analysis and context-aware instant decision, enabling the evaluation of agents' ability to reason over temporal dynamics and adapt their actions in real time.
- **To establish the EQA-Decision Benchmark:** A unified evaluation suite with standardized task protocols and scoring metrics to consistently assess perception, reasoning, and decision-making capabilities of both open-source and closed-source multimodal models.
- **To develop RoboDecision:** A strong baseline model aligned with the EQA-Decision Benchmark. This model is intended to provide a unified framework that jointly evaluates perception, reasoning, and action-level decision-making. Its development aims to demonstrate that decision-aware optimization can enhance spatial grounding, temporal understanding, and action-level reasoning, serving as a robust baseline for future research.

Ultimately, the motivation is to provide a comprehensive foundation for advancing embodied intelligence research by enabling the development and benchmarking of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that can effectively perform spatial and interaction reasoning leading to informed decisions in dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves the construction of a large-scale dataset, EQA-Decision, and the development of a baseline model, RoboDecision, along with an evaluation benchmark.

#### 3.1. EQA-Decision Dataset Construction

The EQA-Decision dataset is a large-scale, hierarchically organized resource comprising over four million multimodal question-answer pairs. It is designed to extend embodied QA from perception to decision, covering both simulated and real environments across four major reasoning modules and nine fine-grained sub-tasks.

##### 3.1.1. Data Sources

The dataset integrates diverse sources to ensure comprehensive coverage:
- **Simulated Environments:**
 - HM3D: Used for generating navigation trajectories with diverse spatial coverage.
 - ScanNet: Provides realistic human-guided paths.
 - AI2-THOR (via ALFRED and CAPEAM agent): Records robotic trajectories in household scenes with synchronized visual and semantic information.
 - Approximately 20,000 trajectories were collected from these simulated environments, spanning kitchens, offices, and living rooms.
- **Image-QA Datasets:**
 - Bunny-695K: Refined with spatial and depth annotations.
 - RoboVQA: Re-annotated and processed to align with the embodied reasoning framework.
 - RoboPoint: Sampled for affordance-oriented QAs with keypoint-level spatial cues.
- **Egocentric Video Datasets:**
 - Ego4D: Provides diverse first-person recordings for temporal reasoning.
 - EPIC-KITCHENS-100: Focuses on kitchen tasks with detailed action and object annotations for procedural and action understanding.
- **Embodied Datasets:**
 - Open X-Embodiment corpus: Curated subsets covering various robot types and manipulation skills (e.g., pick-and-place, insertion). Each trajectory includes RGB observations, proprioceptive states, and action annotations.
 - AgiBot World: Includes long-horizon teleoperated data across realistic household, industrial, and office scenes.

##### 3.1.2. Annotation and Processing Pipeline

A combination of Gemini-2.5-pro for initial generation and human verification was used for annotation.
- **Static Scene Construction:** Focuses on object existence, state, counting, and location. Gemini-2.5-pro summarized visible objects and attributes from HM3D and ScanNet. ALFRED execution metadata was transformed into structured QA. Filtered Bunny-695K samples were included.
- **Spatial Understanding:** Explores depth/direction, grounding/referring, and affordance. RGB frames from Bunny-695K and Open X-Embodiment were used, with depth maps estimated by ZoeDepth. Gemini-2.5-pro generated factual Q&A pairs. RoboPoint samples were adopted for grounding/referring, and affordance areas were annotated in Open X-Embodiment frames.
- **Task Dynamics Reasoning:** Captures temporal and causal relationships through sub-task planning, state tracking, and progress estimation.
 - **Sub-task Planning:** Temporally annotated datasets (Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS-100, AgiBot, Open X-Embodiment) were used to construct Q&A pairs related to contextual planning and future predictions. Gemini-2.5-pro expanded question formulations.
 - **State Tracking & Causality:** Processed to determine current sub-task execution, completion status, and underlying reasoning using Gemini-2.5-pro around sub-task boundaries.
 - **Progress Estimation:** Trajectories from AgiBot and Open X-Embodiment were segmented, and normalized progress ratios were computed.
- **Instant Decision:** Models real-time decision-making using densely annotated video datasets (Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS-100, AgiBot, Open X-Embodiment). Intermediate frames between consecutive action annotations (within 5 seconds) were sampled. Gemini-2.5-pro summarized contextual details (current step, completion, spatial relations) to construct context-aware decision and next-action prediction Q&A pairs. Random human verification was conducted to ensure quality.

##### 3.1.3. Data Statistics and Distribution

The dataset contains over four million multimodal Q&A pairs. Depth and Direction (1.1M), State Tracking & Causality (0.93M), and Instant Decision (1.0M) constitute the largest portions, emphasizing spatial, temporal, and decision-oriented reasoning. Data sources are balanced across simulated, image-QA, egocentric video, and embodied robot trajectories.

#### 3.2. RoboDecision Model Development

RoboDecision is a baseline model built upon Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, designed to connect perception with decision-making through a multi-stage training pipeline.

##### 3.2.1. Model Base

- Qwen3-VL-8B: Employs a vision encoder and a language decoder architecture.
- DeepStack fusion mechanism: Multilevel ViT features are repeatedly injected into transformer layers of the language model for fine-grained multimodal alignment.
- Vision encoder: Processes native-resolution images/video frames using multi-resolution rotary positional encoding (MRoPE) to capture spatio-temporal cues.
- Decoder-only transformer: Supports long-context reasoning.

##### 3.2.2. Training Strategies (Three-stage pipeline)

1. **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - Initialization: Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct.
 - Method: LoRA training on the EQA-Decision dataset.
 - Vision encoder: Frozen for stability. Language and fusion layers are optimized.
 - Data: Uniformly sampled from all four reasoning modules for balanced coverage.
 - Objective: Inject embodied-domain knowledge and enhance basic spatial, temporal, and decision reasoning.
2. **Stage 2: Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-Tuning (CoT-SFT):**
 - Based on the SFT foundation.
 - Data: Approximately 10% of training data across all modules, augmented with CoT annotations generated by Gemini-2.5-pro (rationale + answer fields).
 - Method: Finetuning on this CoT-augmented subset using LoRA.
 - Objective: Enhance reasoning depth and interpretability, learning to construct multi-step reasoning chains and explicit causal dependencies, and providing a warm initialization for GRPO.
3. **Stage 3: Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (GRPO):**
 - Based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
 - Objective: Strengthen the model's ability to connect perceptual understanding with decision-making, mitigating reliance on textual priors.
 - **Hybrid Reward Function:** `R_total = αR_reason + βR_answer + γR_visual`, where α, β, γ are adaptive weights.
 - `R_reason`: Evaluates coherence and causal soundness of generated rationale. Computed as cosine similarity between predicted reasoning embedding and reference CoT embedding (E5-large). Encourages structured reasoning reflecting spatial, causal, and temporal dependencies.
 - `R_answer`: Measures factual correctness. For free-form responses, E5-large embeddings similarity. For structured outputs (coordinates, bounding boxes), task-specific rule-based scoring.
 - `R_visual`: Aligns reasoning with input image. Computed as similarity between OpenCLIP embedding of visual observation and embedding of generated rationale. Ensures reasoning is grounded in perceptual evidence.
 - Through joint optimization of these signals, the model is guided toward perception-guided decision-making, learning to reason directly from images and adapt decisions to spatial layout, scene dynamics, and task demands.

#### 3.3. EQA-Decision Benchmark

- **Purpose:** A unified evaluation suite to systematically assess perception, reasoning, and decision-making in dynamic embodied settings.
- **Structure:** Covers six task categories: static scene understanding, spatial-depth reasoning, grounding and referring, temporal reasoning, planning reasoning, and instant decision-making.
- **Samples:** 2,118 carefully curated evaluation samples, strictly disjoint from the training set, following a unified multimodal QA format with balanced category distribution.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Grounding and Referring:** Pixel space evaluation. Model outputs (2D points or regions) are converted to dense pixel sets, and accuracy is computed as the average mask value over valid predicted points, indicating spatial overlap with the ground-truth region.
 - **Other QA types:** LLM-Match, where GPT-5 assigns a correctness score (1-5 scale) based on agreement with ground truth, linearly mapped to. This provides a unified metric.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluate RoboDecision-8B against a variety of multimodal systems on the EQA-Decision Benchmark and other established embodied benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on EQA-Decision Benchmark

- **Overall Performance:** RoboDecision-8B achieved the highest overall score of 68.06, establishing the top performance across all six task modules of the EQA-Decision Benchmark (Table 2). This indicates its ability to integrate spatial cues, temporal progression, and action-level reasoning essential for decision-oriented embodied intelligence.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **General Baselines:** RoboDecision-8B significantly outperformed leading proprietary VLMs (Gemini-2.5-Pro: 48.68, GPT-5: 51.03) and open-source MLLMs (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct: 48.84).
 - **Embodied Baselines:** It also surpassed specialized embodied-robotics systems like RoboBrain-7B-2.0 (37.32).
- **Specific Task Module Gains:** Compared to Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, RoboDecision-8B showed substantial improvements across all modules, with the most notable gains in grounding–referring, temporal reasoning, planning reasoning, and instant decision tasks. For example, its score improved from 23.98 to 52.95 in Temporal Reasoning and from 48.84 to 68.06 overall. These improvements are attributed to the structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision and visual-semantic alignment rewards employed during training.

#### 4.2. Performance on Other Embodied Benchmarks

- **Generality Validation:** RoboDecision-8B was evaluated on three representative external embodied benchmarks: RoboVQA (for long-horizon robot VQA), ERQA (for fine-grained embodied reasoning), and Where2Place (for free-space placement understanding).
- **Consistent Outperformance:** RoboDecision-8B consistently outperformed both general-purpose VLMs and other embodied baselines across these benchmarks (Table 3), demonstrating that the learned capabilities effectively transfer beyond the EQA-Decision dataset. For instance, on RoboVQA, its BLEU-4 score was 43.55, compared to 18.64 for Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

A controlled set of ablations (Table 4) was conducted to quantify the contribution of each training component and data module.

- **Training Stages:**
 - **CoT-SFT:** Removing CoT-SFT resulted in a significant performance drop, particularly in grounding–referring and temporal reasoning, indicating its crucial role in multi-step reasoning and interpretability. The overall score dropped from 68.06 to 54.52 without CoT.
 - **GRPO:** Removing GRPO primarily weakened spatial understanding, grounding quality, and instant decision-making, confirming its role in optimizing decisions by grounding reasoning in visual input. The overall score dropped to 59.85 without GRPO.
- **Data Modules:**
 - **Spatial Data:** Ablating spatial data notably weakened spatial-depth and grounding–referring skills, indicating its direct impact.
 - **Task Dynamics Data:** Excluding task dynamics data significantly reduced temporal reasoning performance.
 - **Instant Decision Data:** Removing decision data most strongly harmed instant decision-making capabilities.
 - **Static Scene Data:** Removing static scene data mainly affected static scene construction tasks and had a modest impact on instant decision and temporal reasoning, suggesting its foundational but more localized benefit.
 - **Cross-module Sensitivity:** Instant Decision performance showed sensitivity to the removal of spatial or task data, indicating that effective decision-making requires combining spatial layout, temporal cues, and task progression knowledge. This suggests that the four modules provide complementary supervision, and robust perception-to-decision behavior emerges from their joint utilization.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative examples (Figure 4) supported the quantitative findings. The full RoboDecision model produced consistent decisions by jointly utilizing spatial cues, temporal progress, and task intent. In contrast, ablated variants exhibited errors: without spatial data, the model misjudged object positions and issued premature actions; without task-dynamics data, it misinterpreted the stage of an ongoing task and selected inappropriate next steps. These observations underscore the dependency of reliable perception-to-decision behavior on the combined learning of spatial understanding, temporal reasoning, and task-aware interpretation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces EQA-Decision and RoboDecision, which collectively offer a methodological contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence.

**EQA-Decision Dataset:**
The creation of EQA-Decision provides a large-scale, comprehensive dataset that extends Embodied Question Answering (EQA) beyond static perception to encompass decision-centric reasoning. By systematically covering static scene construction, spatial understanding, task dynamics reasoning, and instant decision, the dataset addresses the fragmentation and limited scope of prior EQA resources. Its hierarchical annotations and millions of question-answer pairs across diverse simulated and real-world scenarios offer a robust foundation for training and evaluating embodied agents. This resource is positioned to facilitate research into more dynamic, temporally grounded, and context-aware reasoning abilities, moving towards agents capable of real-time adaptive decision-making.

**RoboDecision Framework:**
The development of RoboDecision, a baseline model trained with a multi-stage approach (SFT, CoT-SFT, GRPO) and a hybrid reward function, demonstrates an effective methodology for aligning vision-language models with decision-oriented embodied tasks. Its performance on the EQA-Decision Benchmark and other established benchmarks indicates that this training strategy can enhance visual grounding, multi-step reasoning, and action-level decision-making. RoboDecision provides a concrete example of how to build agents that integrate perception, reasoning, and action, setting a new benchmark for performance in complex embodied QA tasks.

**Broader Impact on Embodied Intelligence Research:**
The work contributes a unified framework for the evaluation and development of embodied intelligence. By providing both a comprehensive benchmark (EQA-Decision Benchmark) and an effective training methodology (RoboDecision), this research can support future advancements in several key areas:
- **Bridging Perception-Action Gap:** It offers tools to develop agents that can transition from perceiving environments to making actionable decisions within them.
- **Advancing VLM Capabilities:** The dataset and benchmark specifically challenge and enhance VLM capabilities in spatial and interaction reasoning.
- **Development of Generalizable Agents:** The diverse data sources and reasoning dimensions could encourage the development of more robust and generalizable embodied agents capable of operating in complex, dynamic real-world environments.
- **Interpretability:** The Chain-of-Thought (CoT) component in RoboDecision promotes more interpretable reasoning processes, which can be crucial for understanding and debugging agent behavior.

In summary, this work provides a detailed framework and a robust baseline that are designed to advance the understanding and capabilities of embodied agents in tasks requiring nuanced perception, complex reasoning, and real-time decision-making.
