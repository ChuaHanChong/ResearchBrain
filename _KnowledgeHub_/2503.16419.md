# Stop Overthinking: A Survey on Efficient Reasoning for Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Rice University and the University of Houston present the first structured survey on efficient reasoning for Large Language Models, classifying methodologies that address the "overthinking phenomenon" by optimizing the length and necessity of generated reasoning sequences. This survey provides a comprehensive taxonomy and critical insights to guide the development of more practical and cost-effective LLM applications.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often exhibit an "overthinking phenomenon," generating verbose and redundant reasoning sequences for complex tasks.
- This verbosity leads to substantial computational overhead, increased inference costs, and higher latency, hindering practical deployment in real-world, computation-sensitive applications.
- The inefficiency limits the application of powerful reasoning models in latency-sensitive or resource-constrained environments.

## Method

- The survey systematically categorizes current research on efficient reasoning into three primary mechanisms: ==model-based==, ==reasoning output-based==, and ==input prompts-based== approaches.
- It analyzes techniques for intrinsic model modifications, such as ==RL with length rewards== and supervised fine-tuning with variable-length Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data, to produce concise reasoning.
- It explores methods that dynamically compress or guide reasoning during inference (e.g., ==latent representations==, ==confidence-based pruning==) and strategies leveraging prompt attributes (e.g., token budgeting, complexity-driven routing).

## Results

- The survey identifies three main categories of efficient reasoning: model-based (e.g., RL with length rewards, SFT with variable-length CoT data), reasoning output-based (e.g., latent representation compression, dynamic inference paradigms), and input prompt-based (e.g., explicit token budgeting, complexity-driven routing).
- It details how techniques like integrating length penalties in RL or constructing concise CoT datasets in SFT can fine-tune models for intrinsic conciseness.
- The analysis shows that approaches leveraging latent representations or dynamic inference paradigms, guided by confidence or rewards, can significantly reduce explicit reasoning steps during inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient reasoning is emerging as a crucial subfield for the widespread and sustainable adoption of advanced LLMs, moving beyond marginal optimization.
> - Current LLM training paradigms often implicitly encourage verbosity, necessitating dedicated research to achieve efficiency in reasoning.
> - A fundamental trade-off exists between reasoning efficiency and model safety, which requires careful consideration in future research endeavors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities across a wide array of complex tasks, with recent advancements in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) like OpenAI o1 and DeepSeek-R1 pushing the boundaries of System-2 reasoning in domains such as mathematics and programming. These LRMs typically achieve their enhanced performance through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL), which encourage explicit, step-by-step problem-solving.

However, this increased reasoning capability comes at a significant cost. The paper identifies a critical challenge: the "overthinking phenomenon." While longer CoT sequences can improve accuracy, they often lead to verbose and redundant outputs, incurring substantial computational overhead, increased latency, and higher inference costs. This phenomenon limits the practical deployment of powerful reasoning models in real-world, computation-sensitive applications like autonomous driving, interactive assistants, and large-scale search engines.

This survey emerges precisely at the intersection of these two trends: the impressive power of LRMs and the burgeoning need for their practical efficiency. It addresses a gap by providing the "first structured survey" to systematically categorize and analyze the nascent field of "efficient reasoning." Unlike model compression techniques (e.g., quantization, KV cache compression) that reduce model size, efficient reasoning focuses on optimizing the *length and necessity* of generated reasoning sequences. This work positions efficient reasoning not as a marginal optimization but as a crucial, emerging subfield necessary for the widespread and sustainable adoption of advanced LLMs. It highlights that the current training paradigms often implicitly encourage verbosity, making the task of achieving efficiency a non-trivial challenge that requires a dedicated research focus.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to provide the **first structured and comprehensive survey** of the current progress in achieving efficient reasoning in Large Language Models. The authors aim to systematically investigate and explore existing methodologies, thereby offering a foundational taxonomy and critical insights to guide future research and development in this promising area.

The motivation behind this survey stems directly from the "overthinking phenomenon" observed in state-of-the-art Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). While these models excel in complex reasoning tasks by generating detailed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) sequences, their verbose outputs lead to several practical drawbacks:
- **Significant Computational Overhead:** Longer sequences translate to increased processing time and higher energy consumption, making inference expensive. For instance, the paper cites OpenAI o1 costing $60 per 1M generated tokens.
- **Increased Latency:** Real-time applications demand quick responses, which lengthy reasoning chains impede.
- **Limited Practical Application:** The high costs and slow speeds restrict the deployment of powerful LLMs in resource-constrained or latency-sensitive environments.
- **Potential for Error Accumulation:** In some cases, excessive reasoning steps can introduce errors or obscure logical clarity, potentially leading to incorrect answers.

By categorizing and analyzing diverse approaches to "efficient reasoning," the authors seek to highlight solutions that optimize reasoning length while preserving, or even enhancing, reasoning capabilities. This effort is motivated by the desire to make advanced LLMs more practical, cost-effective, and deployable for a broader range of real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The survey adopts a systematic and structured approach to categorize and analyze existing research on efficient reasoning in LLMs. The authors begin by providing essential background on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, the mechanisms behind Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), and a detailed explanation of the "overthinking phenomenon" that necessitates efficient reasoning.

The core of their methodology involves classifying current research into three main "key directions," based on the inherent mechanism of LLMs they optimize:

1. **Model-based Efficient Reasoning:** This category focuses on intrinsic modifications to the LLM itself, either by optimizing existing full-length reasoning models into more concise variants or by training efficient reasoning models from the ground up.
2. **Reasoning Output-based Efficient Reasoning:** This direction explores methods that dynamically reduce the number of reasoning steps and the overall length of the output during the inference process, often by compressing explicit thoughts into latent representations or adopting dynamic inference paradigms.
3. **Input Prompts-based Efficient Reasoning:** This approach leverages properties of input prompts, such as difficulty or explicit length control instructions, to enhance reasoning efficiency.

Beyond these three primary categories, the survey also delves into several other critical, albeit related, topics to provide a holistic view of the field:

- **Training Reasoning Models with Efficient Data:** Investigating how the quantity and quality of training data can influence reasoning efficiency.
- **Reasoning Abilities of Small Language Models (SLMs) and Model Compression:** Examining the performance of smaller models and the impact of compression techniques (like distillation, pruning, and quantization) on their reasoning capabilities.
- **Evaluation and Benchmarking of Efficient Reasoning Models:** Discussing the metrics and frameworks used to assess the effectiveness of efficient reasoning approaches.

For each category, the authors present a comprehensive overview of specific techniques, often detailing their underlying principles, comparing their methodologies (e.g., different length reward formulations in RL), and citing numerous relevant studies. They pose "key questions" for each major section to highlight the central research challenges addressed by the methods discussed. The paper concludes by exploring applications and broader discussions, including the trade-offs between safety and efficiency, and future research directions. A continuously updated public repository further underscores their commitment to tracking progress in this evolving field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey's main findings are presented as a comprehensive taxonomy of existing approaches to efficient reasoning, accompanied by detailed descriptions of the techniques within each category.

#### 4.1. Model-based Efficient Reasoning

This category focuses on fine-tuning LLMs for intrinsic conciseness.
- **RL with Length Reward Design:** Many reasoning models are trained with RL (e.g., PPO, SimPO, GRPO). To mitigate overthinking, studies integrate length-based rewards that penalize verbose or incorrect outputs while rewarding short, correct answers. Techniques include:
 - Explicit length penalties or ratios (e.g., O1-Pruner).
 - Cosine-based rewards or exceed length penalties (e.g., Demystifying).
 - Modifying training data with length constraints (e.g., L1).
 - SimPO with constructed length-preference datasets (e.g., DAST).
- **SFT with Variable-Length CoT Data:** This involves constructing datasets with varying reasoning lengths (long and short CoT) and using SFT to train models to generate compact reasoning chains.
 - **Constructing Datasets:**
 - **Post-reasoning CoT Compression:** Generating full CoT and then reducing redundancy via heuristic criteria or other LLMs (e.g., C3oT using GPT-4 for compression, TokenSkip using semantic importance).
 - **During-reasoning CoT Compression:** Prompting LLMs to generate shorter reasoning steps directly during inference (e.g., LearnSkip with human-like step-skipping, Token-Budget with optimal token budgets, Self-Training with Best-of-N sampling, CoT-Valve with parameter mix-up).
 - **Fine-Tuning Approaches:** Standard fine-tuning (LoRA or full fine-tuning) or progressive fine-tuning (gradually reducing reasoning steps or mixing parameters of long/non-reasoning models).
 - **Model Merging:** Investigating strategies to merge models to produce shorter, more concise reasoning (e.g., Kimi k1.5, Unlocking).

#### 4.2. Reasoning Output-based Efficient Reasoning

This category modifies the output paradigm during inference.
- **Compressing Reasoning Steps into Fewer Latent Representations:** Rather than explicit text, reasoning occurs in compressed, non-textual forms.
 - **Training LLMs for Latent Representations:**
 - **Coconut (Chain of Continuous Thought):** Reuses final-layer hidden states as "continuous thought" tokens, gradually adding them.
 - **CODI:** Self-distillation aligns hidden activations for implicit CoT.
 - **CCOT:** Condenses long CoT into "contemplation tokens" via LoRA modules.
 - **Heima:** Extends latent reasoning to MLLMs using single "thinking tokens."
 - **Token Assorted:** Uses discrete latent tokens learned via VQ-VAE alongside text tokens.
 - **Looping Transformers:** Iteratively processes within latent space, simulating CoT.
 - **Training Auxiliary Modules:** SoftCoT uses a lightweight auxiliary model to generate "soft thought tokens" for a frozen LLM.
- **Dynamic Reasoning Paradigm during Inference:** Using explicit criteria to guide reasoning strategy at test-time.
 - **Reward-guided:** Speculative Rejection optimizes Best-of-N decoding by discarding low-quality outputs based on a reward model. Reward-Guided Speculative Decoding (RSD) uses a Process Reward Model (PRM) to accept high-quality draft model outputs.
 - **Confidence/Certainty-based:** Dynamic Parallel Tree Search (DPTS) optimizes tree-based reasoning by pruning uncertain paths. FastMCTS prioritizes high-confidence traces. Certaindex quantifies LLM confidence to allow early termination. Length-filtered Vote identifies optimal CoT length based on prediction uncertainty. Self-Calib and CISC also leverage confidence for adaptive scaling and weighted voting.
 - **Consistency-based:** Self-Truncation Best-of-N (ST-BoN) uses consistency of latent embeddings to estimate response quality and enable early termination.
 - **Summarization-based:** LightThinker trains LLMs to compress intermediate thoughts into "gist tokens." InftyThink iteratively summarizes and discards previous thoughts, enabling unbounded reasoning depth.

#### 4.3. Input Prompts-based Efficient Reasoning

This category leverages prompt properties.
- **Prompt-guided Efficient Reasoning:** Explicitly instructing LLMs to generate concise reasoning.
 - **Token Budgeting:** TALE-EP estimates and specifies optimal token budgets in prompts (e.g., "use less than <Token-Budget> tokens").
 - **Chain-of-Draft (CoD):** Encourages step-by-step reasoning but with strict verbosity limits (e.g., "5 words at most").
 - **Concise Chain-of-Thought (CCoT):** Simple instruction "Be concise."
 - **Token Complexity:** Systematic study of various compression instructions (e.g., bullet points, word/char/token limits, no proper grammar).
- **Prompts Attribute-Driven Reasoning Routing:** Dynamically assigning queries to appropriate LLMs based on complexity.
 - **Unknown Criteria:** Claude 3.7 Sonnet introduces hybrid reasoning with quick/step-by-step modes, implying an internal routing mechanism.
 - **Training a Classifier:** RouteLLM trains a query router (e.g., DistilBERT-based in Sketch-of-Thought) to dispatch queries to faster/slower LLMs based on complexity.
 - **Uncertainty-based:** Self-Ref fine-tunes "uncertainty-specialized tokens" to align predictions with correctness, triggering routing for uncertain outputs. Confident or Seek Stronger uses calibrated data for routing decisions.

#### 4.4. Reasoning Abilities via Efficient Training Data and Model Compression

- **Training Reasoning Models with Less Data:** Focusing on constructing minimal but high-quality training data.
 - **Minimal but High-Impact Data Selection:** LIMO uses carefully curated examples (817 samples) based on difficulty, generality, knowledge diversity, and optimal structural organization to achieve high performance. s1 curates compact, high-quality datasets (e.g., s1K with 1,000 questions) and combines them with "budget forcing" for efficient test-time scaling.
 - **Self-Verification as Data-Efficient Training Signal:** S2R infuses LLMs with self-verification and self-correction through RL on minimal initialization samples.
- **Reasoning Capabilities of Small Language Models via Distillation and Model Compression:** Addressing the challenge of deploying powerful LLMs in resource-constrained environments using SLMs.
 - **Distillation:** Techniques to transfer reasoning capabilities from LLMs to SLMs. Methods include mixed distillation (blending long/short CoT or CoT/PoT), counterfactual distillation (generating multi-view CoT data), feedback-driven distillation (iteratively refining datasets), and incorporating probing/retrieval mechanisms (PRR, SKIntern).
 - **Pruning and Quantization:** Compression techniques. Quantization (reducing precision) generally preserves reasoning performance well, reducing memory/computation. Pruning (removing weights/neurons) often severely degrades reasoning quality. This suggests compression methods are more effective than training SLMs from scratch, but SLMs still struggle with instruction following.

#### 4.5. Evaluation and Benchmark

The paper highlights benchmarks crucial for assessing efficient reasoning:
- **Inference-time Computation:** Sys2Bench evaluates reasoning across arithmetic, logical, commonsense, algorithmic, and planning tasks. Bag of Tricks examines various inference-time strategies under a budget. Studies (e.g.,) show that compute-optimal test-time scaling can enable smaller models to outperform larger ones.
- **Evaluating Overthinking:** Frameworks to analyze "overthinking" patterns (e.g., Analysis Paralysis, Rogue Actions, Premature Disengagement) and measure an "overthinking score" (e.g.,). Benchmarks like S1-Bench focus on System 1 (intuitive) thinking.
- **Effect of Long CoT Reasoning:** Research shows a strong relationship between CoT length and effectiveness, with optimal lengths existing for tasks, and models tending to "overthink" when information is missing (MiP-Overthinking).
- **Effect of Compression on Reasoning Models:** CompressionReasoning benchmarks quantization, distillation, and pruning, finding that parameter count impacts knowledge retention more than reasoning, and shorter outputs often improve performance. QuantRM evaluates various quantization techniques.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the future of Large Language Models and their real-world deployment. By being the first structured review of efficient reasoning, it formalizes a nascent research area, providing a much-needed taxonomy and consolidating diverse approaches under a unified framework. This organization will undoubtedly accelerate research by clarifying different avenues of investigation and identifying open challenges.

The potential impact of achieving efficient reasoning is substantial and far-reaching:

- **Cost Reduction:** Minimizing computational overhead directly translates to lower operational costs for deploying LLM-powered applications, making advanced AI more accessible and economically viable for businesses and research.
- **Improved Responsiveness and Latency:** Faster inference times are critical for real-time applications, enabling more seamless and interactive user experiences in conversational AI, autonomous systems, and real-time decision-making.
- **Enhanced Scalability:** Efficient models can handle a larger volume of queries with fewer resources, facilitating broader deployment and integration into large-scale systems.
- **Broader Applicability:** Reduced resource demands open up possibilities for deploying powerful reasoning LLMs in edge devices, mobile applications, and other resource-constrained environments, expanding their utility across various sectors.

The paper explicitly highlights several application domains that stand to benefit immensely:
- **Autonomous Driving:** Efficient reasoning LLMs can process vast sensor data quickly, enabling better decision-making, route planning, risk assessment, and safe interaction with smart road systems.
- **Embodied AI:** Robots and smart devices can become more intelligent, making faster decisions, adapting to dynamic environments, and interacting safely and explainably with humans.
- **Healthcare:** LLMs can analyze medical data more rapidly and accurately, assisting with diagnoses, treatment recommendations, and making complex information understandable for professionals and patients.
- **Recommender Systems:** Efficient models can generate more accurate, personalized, and context-aware recommendations in real-time, improving user satisfaction and engagement (e.g., ReaRec's latent reasoning framework).

Beyond these applications, the discussion section delves into crucial future research directions:
- **Improving Intrinsic Reasoning Ability:** Beyond efficiency, continuous efforts are needed to enhance the core reasoning capabilities of LLMs (e.g., Meta-Reasoner for strategy selection, ITT for dynamic depth scaling, SyzygyoT for task decomposition).
- **Safety of Efficient Reasoning:** A critical trade-off exists between efficiency and safety. Optimizing one can degrade the other. Research is needed to develop methods that maintain safety checks (e.g., filtering harmful content, mitigating attacks) without excessive computational overhead (e.g., H-CoT attacks, dynamic output length control, safeMLRM).
- **Efficient LLMs for Agentic AI:** Efficient reasoning is paramount for advanced AI agents, influencing their decision-making speed, resource utilization, and effectiveness in complex tasks (e.g., merging planning trees, structured memory, CoA for verification, adaptive multi-agent debate).
- **RL vs. SFT Paradigms:** The paper acknowledges the ongoing debate on whether RL or SFT is superior for efficient reasoning, suggesting that a hybrid approach combining the strengths of both might be optimal.

In conclusion, this survey is a timely and invaluable contribution that frames "efficient reasoning" as a vital new frontier in LLM research. By systematically mapping the current landscape, it not only clarifies the progress made but also illuminates the significant challenges and opportunities that lie ahead in making these powerful AI systems more practical, sustainable, and broadly beneficial.
