# Deep Long-Tailed Learning: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from National University of Singapore and ByteDance AI Lab/SEA AI Lab provide the first comprehensive survey of deep long-tailed learning, systematically categorizing over 50 methods into a novel taxonomy and empirically analyzing over 20 state-of-the-art approaches using a new "relative accuracy" metric to better evaluate their effectiveness in addressing class imbalance.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks exhibit an inherent bias towards dominant "head" classes and perform poorly on under-represented "tail" classes when trained on real-world long-tailed data distributions.
- This class imbalance severely limits the practical adoption and reliability of deep learning models in critical applications such as medical image diagnosis and species identification.
- The rapidly growing field of deep long-tailed learning lacked a comprehensive, systematic overview and empirical comparison of existing methods.

## Method

- A novel taxonomy is proposed, categorizing deep long-tailed learning methods into three main paradigms: ==Class Re-balancing==, ==Information Augmentation==, and ==Module Improvement==.
- An extensive empirical study evaluates over 20 state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets (==ImageNet-LT==, ==iNaturalist 2018==) under a unified setup.
- A **"Relative Accuracy"** (Ar=At/AuA_r = A_t / A_uAr=At/Au) metric is introduced to isolate a method's direct effectiveness in addressing class imbalance by normalizing against an ideal balanced performance.

## Results

- The majority of specialized long-tailed learning methods significantly outperform the standard Softmax baseline, confirming their effectiveness in mitigating class imbalance.
- Ensemble-based methods (e.g., SADE, RIDE) emerge as the current state-of-the-art, achieving strong overall and relative accuracy while better managing the head-tail trade-off.
- The proposed "Relative Accuracy" metric proved crucial for a nuanced evaluation, highlighting whether performance gains stemmed from direct imbalance alleviation or general model improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Most methods improve performance on middle and tail classes, but often at the cost of degrading performance on head classes, indicating a pervasive "performance seesaw" trade-off.
> - Information augmentation (e.g., self-supervised pre-training) and standard data augmentation techniques are complementary and consistently boost other long-tailed learning methods.
> - Different class re-balancing subtypes can be exclusive, and naive combinations might reduce performance; however, they can learn similarly high-quality representations when applied within a decoupled training scheme.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual recognition has undergone a revolution with the advent of deep learning, leading to remarkable breakthroughs in tasks like image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. However, this success is heavily reliant on the availability of large, balanced datasets. In the real world, data often naturally follows a "long-tailed" distribution, where a few "head" classes have an abundance of samples, while a vast majority of "tail" classes are severely under-represented. This inherent class imbalance poses a significant challenge for deep neural networks, causing them to be biased towards the dominant head classes and perform poorly on crucial, but rare, tail classes. This limitation severely hampers the practicality of deep learning models in numerous real-world applications, including fine-grained species classification, robust face recognition, accurate medical image diagnosis, and comprehensive urban scene understanding.

Despite the rapid proliferation of studies addressing deep long-tailed learning in recent years, the field has lacked a cohesive and systematic overview. This survey paper precisely fills that critical gap. It consolidates and categorizes the fragmented advancements, providing a much-needed structured understanding of the various approaches. By offering a comprehensive review of methods published up to mid-2021, it serves as a foundational resource for researchers entering or already working in this dynamic area.

The paper carefully distinguishes deep long-tailed learning from related but distinct problems, clarifying its unique challenges:

- **Non-deep long-tailed learning:** Pre-deep learning methods largely relied on handcrafted features (e.g., SIFT, HOG) and classical machine learning algorithms, often augmented with prior knowledge. Deep learning, with its unparalleled ability to learn high-quality, discriminative features, has drastically surpassed the performance of these traditional methods and opened up new solution paradigms like transfer learning and ensemble methods.
- **Class-imbalanced learning:** While long-tailed learning is a subset of class-imbalanced learning, it presents amplified challenges. In long-tailed scenarios, the number of classes is typically very large, and tail-class samples are extremely scarce in an absolute sense, unlike general class imbalance where minority classes might still have a reasonable number of samples. This survey highlights that while high-level ideas like re-balancing are shared, the severity and scale of the imbalance in long-tailed data necessitate more sophisticated solutions.
- **Few-shot learning:** This is effectively a sub-problem within long-tailed learning, specifically focusing on the extreme end of the tail where classes might have only one or a handful of samples. Long-tailed learning encompasses the entire spectrum of class frequencies, from abundant to extremely rare.
- **Out-of-domain generalization:** This broad field deals with discrepancies between training and test data distributions. Long-tailed learning is identified as a specific instance of out-of-domain generalization where the *class distribution* is inconsistent between training and testing.

By meticulously mapping out the landscape and drawing clear boundaries with related areas, this survey provides context for past work, a framework for understanding current research, and a roadmap for future exploration. It underscores the critical importance of deep long-tailed learning for deploying robust and fair AI systems in practical, data-imbalanced environments, thereby pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with deep learning in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from a significant practical limitation of deep learning in real-world visual recognition tasks: its inherent bias towards dominant classes when confronted with long-tailed data distributions. While deep learning has demonstrated remarkable success in generic visual recognition with balanced datasets, this success falters when a small fraction of classes (head classes) possess abundant training data, and the majority (tail classes) have only a few samples. This imbalance causes deep models to learn representations and decision boundaries that are heavily skewed, leading to excellent performance on head classes but severely degraded performance on crucial tail classes. This fundamental problem limits the widespread adoption and reliability of deep network-based recognition models in critical applications such as medical image diagnosis, species identification, and face recognition.

Given the rapid proliferation of research efforts aimed at mitigating this long-tailed problem in recent years, the field has become somewhat fragmented, lacking a unified understanding and systematic categorization of existing approaches. To address this, the authors set forth several key objectives for their comprehensive survey:

1. **Systematic Classification and Review:** To provide a structured overview of existing deep long-tailed learning studies. They propose a novel taxonomy, grouping methods into three main categories: Class Re-balancing, Information Augmentation, and Module Improvement, with further sub-categories. The goal is to review these methods in detail, offering a coherent framework for understanding their underlying principles and technical contributions.
2. **Empirical Analysis and Evaluation:** To go beyond theoretical review by empirically analyzing the effectiveness of state-of-the-art methods. This includes evaluating their performance on widely used benchmark datasets (ImageNet-LT, iNaturalist 2018) under a unified setup.
3. **Introduction of a Novel Evaluation Metric:** To propose and utilize a "relative accuracy" metric ($A_r = A_t / A_u$). This metric's objective is to provide a more nuanced understanding of *to what extent* a method truly addresses class imbalance, distinguishing performance gains due to imbalance alleviation from those arising from general model improvements (e.g., better architectures or more data). This aims to offer a more accurate reflection of a method's direct contribution to solving the long-tailed problem.
4. **Identification of Applications:** To highlight the diverse and important real-world application scenarios where deep long-tailed learning is crucial, reinforcing the practical significance of the research area.
5. **Future Research Directions:** To identify and articulate promising avenues for future research, encompassing both methodological innovations and new task settings. This serves as a forward-looking guide, stimulating further advancements in the field.

In essence, the core motivation is to bring order and clarity to a burgeoning research domain, provide a standardized empirical basis for comparison, and chart a clear path for future innovation to accelerate the development of robust and practical deep learning solutions for real-world imbalanced data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a multi-faceted methodology to achieve their objectives, combining a structured literature review with a rigorous empirical analysis.

**A. Structured Literature Review and Taxonomy Development:**
The primary approach for organizing existing knowledge is the development of a novel, comprehensive taxonomy. This taxonomy categorizes deep long-tailed learning methods into three main paradigms, each further divided into sub-categories:

1. **Class Re-balancing:** These methods aim to counteract the negative influence of class imbalance at the data or objective level.
 - **Re-sampling:** Adjusting the number of samples per class in training batches (e.g., over-sampling tail classes, under-sampling head classes, or dynamic/adaptive sampling).
 - **Class-sensitive Learning:** Modifying loss functions to weigh classes differently based on their frequency or learning difficulty. This includes re-weighting (e.g., Focal Loss, Class-balanced loss) and re-margining (e.g., LDAM loss).
 - **Logit Adjustment:** Post-processing the output logits of a biased model to re-calibrate predictions, often based on class frequencies.

2. **Information Augmentation:** These methods introduce additional information to improve model training, particularly for tail classes.
 - **Transfer Learning:** Leveraging knowledge from other domains or tasks. Sub-schemes include model pre-training (supervised or self-supervised), knowledge distillation (from multiple experts or a balanced teacher), head-to-tail model transfer (adapting head-class knowledge to tail classes), and self-training (generating pseudo-labels for unlabeled data).
 - **Data Augmentation:** Expanding the dataset by creating synthetic samples or features. This includes head-to-tail transfer augmentation (e.g., translating head-class features to augment tail classes) and non-transfer augmentation (e.g., mixup, generating features from learned priors).

3. **Module Improvement:** These approaches focus on enhancing specific components of the deep neural network architecture.
 - **Representation Learning:** Designing methods to learn more discriminative and balanced feature spaces. This encompasses metric learning (e.g., triplet loss variants, contrastive learning), prototype learning (e.g., maintaining class-specific feature prototypes), and sequential training (e.g., hierarchical feature learning).
 - **Classifier Design:** Developing new classifier architectures or prediction schemes that are robust to class imbalance (e.g., cosine classifiers, learnable weight scaling, causal classifiers, hierarchical classifiers).
 - **Decoupled Training:** Separating the training process into distinct stages, typically focusing first on learning robust feature representations and then on training an unbiased classifier.
 - **Ensemble Learning:** Strategically combining multiple network modules or "experts," often trained on different subsets or with varying focuses, to leverage diverse expertise.

The authors systematically review over 50 prominent works published before mid-2021, detailing their core mechanisms, advantages, and limitations within this structured framework.

**B. Empirical Analysis and Novel Metric:**
To provide objective insights, the survey conducts an extensive empirical study of over 20 state-of-the-art methods.

1. **Datasets and Baselines:** They utilize two widely-used long-tailed benchmark datasets: ImageNet-LT (a large-scale, artificially generated long-tailed version of ImageNet) and iNaturalist 2018 (a real-world long-tailed species classification dataset). Experiments are conducted with standard deep learning backbones (ResNeXt-50 for ImageNet-LT, ResNet-50 for iNaturalist) under consistent training settings (optimizer, batch size, learning rate schedule, basic data augmentation).
2. **Evaluation Metrics:** Besides standard Top-1 accuracy (overall, head, middle, and tail classes) and mean Average Precision (mAP), the authors introduce a novel metric: **Relative Accuracy ($A_r$)**.
 - $A_r = A_t / A_u$, where $A_t$ is the test accuracy of the long-tailed method being evaluated.
 - $A_u$ (upper reference accuracy) = $\max(A_v, A_b)$.
 - $A_v$ is the vanilla accuracy of the backbone trained on a *balanced version* of the training set with a standard cross-entropy loss.
 - $A_b$ is the accuracy of the model trained on the *balanced training set* using the *same long-tailed method* being evaluated.
 - The purpose of $A_r$ is to normalize the long-tailed performance by an "ideal" performance ceiling (obtained on balanced data) and thereby isolate the method's effectiveness in *specifically addressing the class imbalance issue*, rather than general architectural improvements or better data utilization that might also boost performance on balanced data.
3. **Comparative Analysis:** The empirical study investigates:
 - Overall performance trends (accuracy vs. relative accuracy).
 - Impact of training epochs (90 vs. 200).
 - Performance distribution across head, middle, and tail classes to assess trade-offs.
 - Interactions between different method types (e.g., combining class re-balancing subtypes, or integrating transfer learning/data augmentation with other paradigms) to determine complementarity or exclusivity.
 - Performance of class-sensitive losses within the decoupled training scheme.

This dual approach of comprehensive categorization and unified empirical validation provides a robust and insightful analysis of the deep long-tailed learning landscape, offering both theoretical understanding and practical guidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical studies and comprehensive review reveal several key findings regarding the current state of deep long-tailed learning:

1. **Effectiveness of Long-Tailed Methods:** The vast majority of specialized long-tailed learning methods significantly outperform the standard Softmax baseline. This confirms the effectiveness of these approaches in mitigating the class imbalance problem. However, a few methods (e.g., Decouple-CB-CRT, BBN) surprisingly showed slightly worse overall accuracy than the baseline, attributed to factors like poor representation learning or excessive focus on tail classes at the expense of heads.

2. **The Value of Relative Accuracy:** The newly proposed Relative Accuracy ($A_r$) metric proves crucial for a nuanced evaluation. It highlights that while some methods achieve high overall accuracy, a portion of this gain might come from general model improvements (reflected in higher Upper Reference Accuracy, $A_u$) rather than solely from resolving class imbalance. For instance, MiSLAS initially showed higher accuracy than Balanced Softmax, but Balanced Softmax demonstrated a higher relative accuracy, indicating its superior direct alleviation of class imbalance at that specific training stage. The overall trend, as seen in $A_r$ improving over time, confirms that the field is indeed making genuine progress in tackling the core imbalance issue.

3. **Head-Tail Trade-off:** A pervasive challenge is that most methods improve performance on middle and tail classes at the cost of degrading performance on head classes. This "performance seesaw" indicates a significant area for future research. Notably, information augmentation methods (e.g., SSP) and ensemble learning (e.g., SADE) show promise in mitigating this trade-off, achieving improvements across all class subsets.

4. **Performance by Method Type:**
 - **Class Re-balancing (CR):** Generally effective, with methods like LADE, Balanced Softmax, and LDAM achieving strong results. However, their upper reference accuracies are often similar to the baseline, meaning their accuracy gains largely reflect direct imbalance alleviation. Focal Loss, while effective in object detection, struggles with the large number of classes in ImageNet-LT.
 - **Information Augmentation (IA):** Methods like Self-supervised Pre-training (SSP) and Rare-class Sample Generator (RSG) are beneficial, improving both overall and relative accuracy, demonstrating their ability to genuinely tackle imbalance.
 - **Module Improvement (MI):** All methods in this category contribute positively. Crucially, **ensemble-based methods (e.g., SADE, RIDE) emerge as the current state-of-the-art** in terms of both overall accuracy and relative accuracy. They effectively combine multiple "experts" to achieve robust performance across various class frequencies.

5. **Interactions Between Method Types:**
 - **Class Re-balancing Subtypes:** Different subtypes (re-sampling, class-sensitive learning, logit adjustment) are often **exclusive** to some degree. Combining them naively without careful design can lead to diminished performance, as a method that already re-balances effectively might be perturbed by further re-balancing adjustments.
 - **Transfer Learning (Pre-training) & Other Paradigms:** Transfer-based methods, particularly self-supervised pre-training (e.g., SSP), are **complementary** to other long-tailed paradigms (class re-balancing, module improvement). This means pre-training can consistently boost the performance of other long-tailed techniques.
 - **Data Augmentation & Other Paradigms:** Standard data augmentation techniques (e.g., RandAugment) are also **complementary** and lead to consistent performance gains when combined with various long-tailed methods from different categories.
 - **Class-sensitive Losses in Decoupled Training:** The empirical analysis suggests that different class-sensitive losses, despite varying performance in joint (end-to-end) training, tend to learn **similarly high-quality feature representations** when applied within a decoupled training scheme. This indicates an **interchangeable role** or reinforcement between class re-balancing and decoupled training, emphasizing that classifier re-training can significantly compensate for representational biases.

6. **Overall Best Practice:** The current empirical evidence suggests that **ensemble learning combined with class re-balancing** strategies offers the most promising approach for deep long-tailed recognition, achieving a better trade-off across all class types without necessarily sacrificing head-class performance.

These findings provide a clear empirical landscape, guiding researchers on effective strategies and highlighting the complexities and interdependencies between different long-tailed learning methodologies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper on "Deep Long-Tailed Learning" is a significant contribution to the fields of computer vision and machine learning, offering both a much-needed organizational framework and critical empirical insights. Its potential impact is multi-faceted, benefiting researchers, practitioners, and the broader development of robust AI systems.

**1. Unifying and Structuring a Rapidly Evolving Field:**
The most immediate significance lies in its role as the **first comprehensive survey** of deep long-tailed learning. By proposing a clear, three-category taxonomy (Class Re-balancing, Information Augmentation, Module Improvement) and detailing their sub-categories, the authors bring much-needed order to a fragmented and rapidly evolving research area. This systematic organization helps researchers understand the diverse approaches, identify commonalities, and recognize distinct paradigms, preventing redundant efforts and fostering more targeted innovation. It provides a foundational reference for anyone delving into this domain.

**2. Providing Rigorous Empirical Analysis and Practical Guidance:**
Beyond a theoretical review, the paper conducts extensive empirical studies on over 20 state-of-the-art methods under a unified experimental setup. This standardized comparison is invaluable, offering concrete evidence of method effectiveness, performance trade-offs (e.g., head-tail accuracy balance), and computational considerations. The introduction of the **"relative accuracy" ($A_r$) metric** is particularly impactful. By attempting to disentangle a method's direct ability to resolve class imbalance from general performance improvements, $A_r$ provides a more precise and insightful evaluation tool. This empirical validation helps researchers assess true progress in addressing the core problem and offers practitioners a data-driven basis for selecting and combining methods in real-world scenarios. The findings on method complementarity (e.g., information augmentation with other paradigms) and exclusivity (e.g., certain class re-balancing subtypes) are crucial practical insights.

**3. Catalyzing Future Research and Identifying Open Problems:**
A critical contribution of the survey is its forward-looking perspective, dedicating a section to **identifying promising future research directions**. These include novel task settings like test-agnostic long-tailed learning, open-set long-tailed learning, federated long-tailed learning, class-incremental long-tailed learning, multi-domain long-tailed learning, robust long-tailed learning, long-tailed regression, and long-tailed video learning. By articulating these challenges and potential innovation avenues, the survey acts as a roadmap, inspiring and guiding the next generation of research efforts. This foresight is vital for sustained progress in the field, ensuring that future work addresses critical, unsolved problems and expands the applicability of long-tailed learning to increasingly complex and realistic scenarios.

**4. Enabling Robust and Fair AI Systems:**
The core problem of long-tailed data is ubiquitous in real-world applications. Models biased towards head classes can lead to poor performance on underrepresented groups or critical rare events, impacting safety (e.g., medical diagnosis of rare diseases), fairness (e.g., face recognition for minority demographics), and efficiency (e.g., detection of rare defects). By advancing the understanding and solutions for deep long-tailed learning, this survey directly contributes to the development of more robust, reliable, and equitable AI systems. Its insights are crucial for building models that perform well across the entire spectrum of data, not just the majority classes, thus enhancing the practical utility and trustworthiness of AI in diverse domains.

In conclusion, "Deep Long-Tailed Learning: A Survey" is more than a compilation of existing works; it is a meticulously structured analysis that synthesizes knowledge, validates empirical observations, and charts a course for future innovation. It provides an indispensable foundation for the community, ensuring more informed research and enabling the deployment of high-performing deep learning models in the complex, imbalanced data environments of the real world.
