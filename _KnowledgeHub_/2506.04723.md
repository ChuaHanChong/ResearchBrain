# Beyond Accuracy: Dissecting Mathematical Reasoning for LLMs Under Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Wisconsin-Madison and Salesforce AI Research developed the SPARKLE framework to diagnose how reinforcement learning impacts large language model mathematical reasoning across planning, knowledge use, and subproblem decomposition. Their SparkleRL-PSS multi-stage training pipeline, which uses partial step scaffolding on hard problems, improved average AIME24 performance by 2.02% over initial RL tuning, reaching 50.42%.

## Problem

- Existing research lacked granular understanding of how RL training specifically improves LLM reasoning, focusing mainly on overall accuracy.
- The field struggled to evaluate particular cognitive components, such as planning or knowledge application, that RL might enhance in LLMs.
- A common practice was to exclude extremely difficult problems from RL training sets due to sparse positive reward signals.

## Method

- Introduced **SPARKLE**, a ==three-axis analytical== framework (planning, knowledge utilization, subproblem chains) to systematically diagnose RL's effects on LLM reasoning.
- Created the SPARKLE benchmark by augmenting existing math datasets with rich annotations to enable fine-grained evaluation.
- Proposed SparkleRL-PSS, a multi-stage RL pipeline that reuses challenging problems with "partial step scaffolding" to guide model exploration effectively.

## Results

- The SparkleRL-PSS pipeline achieved a **2.02%** average improvement on AIME24 over baseline RL-tuned models, reaching an overall **50.42%** accuracy.
- RL-tuned models showed average performance gains of **4.3%** to **4.2%** when provided with external knowledge, while base models experienced an average **5.4%** decline.
- RL-tuned models on AIME24 achieved **50.4%** accuracy on full problems but only **17.5%** on correctly solving all constituent subproblems, indicating a gap in compositional reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning enhances an LLM's ability to integrate external knowledge and develop its own planning strategies, but detailed human-provided plans can sometimes hinder performance.
> - Challenging problems can be effectively incorporated into RL training using partial step scaffolding, contrary to the conventional belief of their limited utility.
> - Current RL methods primarily improve high-level problem-solving, with LLMs still exhibiting fragility in consistently resolving all constituent subproblems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) have positioned them at the forefront of complex reasoning tasks, particularly in mathematics. Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a dominant paradigm for enhancing these capabilities, with methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and its variants (e.g., GRPO) demonstrating substantial empirical gains on various reasoning benchmarks. This has led to a proliferation of papers proposing new RL-based training techniques, models, and evaluation datasets.

However, a critical gap exists in the current research landscape: a comprehensive, granular understanding of *why* and *how* RL training actually improves LLM reasoning performance. Most existing works primarily focus on "beyond accuracy" metrics, such as the final answer correctness on standardized benchmarks like AIME or MATH. While these metrics indicate overall capability, they offer limited insight into the underlying behavioral shifts or specific cognitive components enhanced by RL. The field currently lacks tools to dissect whether RL improves strategic planning, knowledge application, or problem decomposition.

This paper directly addresses this gap by introducing a framework for fine-grained analysis. It connects to the established principles of cognitive science, which have long recognized planning, knowledge integration, and problem decomposition as fundamental to human problem-solving. By drawing this parallel, the authors propose a more nuanced approach to evaluating LLMs, moving beyond simple input-output fidelity.

Furthermore, the work challenges a common belief in the RL community: that extremely difficult problems are not useful for training because they rarely yield positive reward signals, leading to their exclusion from training sets. This paper argues against this "filtering" strategy, proposing a novel curriculum learning approach (SparkleRL-PSS) to effectively leverage hard problems, aligning with the broader trend of making RL training more data-efficient and robust. The investigation into *what* capabilities RL imparts also relates to ongoing debates about whether RL primarily reweights existing reasoning paths or enables entirely new capabilities in LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research is to gain insights beyond superficial accuracy metrics to understand the specific cognitive capabilities that reinforcement learning imbues in large language models for complex mathematical reasoning. The authors argue that merely tracking accuracy gains on benchmarks provides an insufficient signal for advancing LLM reasoning.

The key objectives are:

- **Develop a Fine-Grained Analytical Framework (SPARKLE)**: The primary objective is to introduce SPARKLE, a novel framework designed to systematically dissect the effects of RL across three crucial dimensions of reasoning: (1) plan following and execution, (2) knowledge integration, and (3) chain of subproblems. This framework aims to provide a diagnostic tool for understanding *which* aspects of reasoning are enhanced by RL and *where* its limitations lie.
- **Construct an Augmented Reasoning Benchmark**: To instantiate the SPARKLE framework for mathematical reasoning, the authors aim to augment existing math datasets with rich annotations, including planning skeletons, requisite knowledge, and candidate problem decompositions. This augmented benchmark (SPARKLE benchmark) is essential for enabling the proposed fine-grained analysis.
- **Investigate the Role of Problem Difficulty in RL**: The paper seeks to challenge the conventional wisdom that hard problems, often yielding sparse positive rewards, are not useful for RL training. The objective is to study how problem difficulty impacts RL and to devise methods for effectively utilizing these challenging examples.
- **Propose a Multi-Stage RL Pipeline for Hard Problems**: Building on the investigation of problem difficulty, a key objective is to introduce SparkleRL-PSS, a multi-stage RL pipeline. This pipeline aims to effectively guide model exploration on hard problems by reusing existing data with partial step scaffolding, thereby avoiding the need for additional data generation.
- **Derive Practical Insights for RL Pipeline Design**: Ultimately, the research aims to provide a principled foundation and actionable insights for developing more adaptive, data-efficient, and interpretable RL pipelines for reasoning tasks in LLMs. This includes understanding when external guidance (like plans or knowledge) is beneficial and how models autonomously formulate reasoning strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-pronged methodology: the **SPARKLE analytical framework** for fine-grained evaluation and **SparkleRL-PSS**, a multi-stage RL training pipeline, to address how RL shapes reasoning and leverages problem difficulty.

#### 3.1. SPARKLE: A Three-Axis Framework for LLM Reasoning Evaluation

SPARKLE decomposes reasoning along three axes, inspired by cognitive science research on human problem-solving:

- **Axis 1: Planning and Execution**: To differentiate between a model's ability to devise a strategy ("what to do") and its ability to carry out steps ("how to do it"), models are evaluated with and without explicit, high-level planning skeletons. These plans outline major steps but omit specific computational details, allowing for isolation of execution capabilities.
- **Axis 2: Knowledge Utilization**: To disentangle knowledge retrieval from logical reasoning, models are tested under two conditions: (1) relying solely on their internal knowledge and (2) being explicitly provided with relevant external knowledge (definitions, theorems, formulas). This helps identify if performance bottlenecks are due to insufficient knowledge recall or impaired deductive abilities.
- **Axis 3: Chain of Subproblems**: To pinpoint exact reasoning breakdown points, problems are decomposed into a sequence of self-contained subproblems. The model is incrementally assessed, given the original problem, previously solved subproblems, and the current subproblem. This provides a fine-grained error analysis, revealing where compositional reasoning fails.

#### 3.2. SPARKLE Benchmark Construction and Validation

To support the SPARKLE framework, existing mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AMC23, MATH500, GSM8K, OlympiadBench) are augmented. The augmentation pipeline involves:
1. **High-Capacity Agent Generation**: Using models like GPT-4.1, initial annotations (planning skeletons, subproblem decompositions with answers, relevant knowledge components) are generated from ground-truth solutions. Knowledge retrieval from reliable internet sources is integrated.
2. **Verification Agent**: A second GPT-4.1 agent verifies the correctness, coherence, completeness, and pedagogical soundness of the generated annotations.
3. **Expert Human Validation**: Graduate students with advanced mathematics backgrounds perform final validation to ensure annotation fidelity.
The resulting SPARKLE benchmark comprises 2,564 open-ended questions spanning 10 difficulty levels and 9 mathematical domains.

#### 3.3. SparkleRL-PSS: Multi-Stage Reinforcement Learning with Partial Step Scaffolding

This component explores the effective utilization of problems with varied difficulties through a two-stage curriculum-style RL training:

- **Stage 1: General Reasoning Boost**: The base LLM (Qwen-2.5-Math-7B) undergoes RL fine-tuning using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on a broad dataset of 40K diverse math problems (DeepScaleR-Preview training set). This establishes a strong general reasoning capability.
- **Stage 2: Targeted Hard Problem Learning**: From the 40K problems, 5.7K most challenging problems that the Stage 1 model consistently failed are identified and curated. The model is then further fine-tuned from its Stage 1 checkpoint on this subset of hard problems. Three variants are explored:
 - **Mixed Difficulty**: Training on a random mixture of easy and hard problems.
 - **Hard-Only**: Training exclusively on hard problems.
 - **Hard-Augmented (SparkleRL-PSS)**: Training on hard problems augmented with "partial solution scaffolding." This involves dividing reasoning traces into semantic chunks and providing 0 to 4 chunks as additional input context, guiding the model without generating new data or performing external distillation.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Base Models**: Qwen-2.5-Math-7B is the primary model. Ablation studies also use Qwen2.5-32B and Qwen2.5-32B-SimpleRL-Zoo.
- **Reward Design**: A rule-based reward function is used: +2 for correct answer and format, +1 for correct answer but incorrect format, and -1 otherwise. This aims to mitigate reward hacking.
- **Training Details**: Stage 1 uses a learning rate of 1e-6 and a KL loss coefficient of 0.001. Stage 2 maintains similar settings but increases the KL loss coefficient to 0.01 to penalize divergence from the Stage 1 policy more strongly. Sampling temperature is 0.6, generating 32 samples per problem during training.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Avg@8, Pass@k, and specific performance comparisons tailored to each SPARKLE axis.
- **Hardware/Software**: Training on NVIDIA H200 and A100 GPUs, using Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.4.0, and Transformers 4.47.1, built upon the verl framework for RL and lm-evaluation-harness for evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several significant findings, providing fine-grained insights into how RL shapes LLM reasoning.

- **Multi-Stage RL Effectively Utilizes Hard Problems**: Initial Stage 1 RL training on diverse math problems dramatically improves performance (average 29.78% gain). Crucially, the **SparkleRL-PSS** approach, which reuses hard problems with *partial step scaffolding*, significantly outperforms training on mixed-difficulty or hard-only problems in Stage 2. It yields an average 2.02% improvement over Stage 1, achieving a new peak of 50.42% on AIME24, comparable to larger (32B) state-of-the-art RL-tuned models. This finding refutes the notion that RL cannot benefit from the hardest problems due to sparse rewards, demonstrating that structured guidance can unlock their value.
- **RL Improves Sample Efficiency**: Multi-stage RL (SparkleRL-Stage 2-pss) consistently achieves higher performance at lower 'k' values (Pass@k) compared to single-stage or base models. This suggests that RL not only improves overall accuracy but also makes models more efficient in finding correct solutions with fewer attempts, indicating a more fundamental shift in reasoning capability.
- **RL Enhances Plan-Following Flexibility, but Human Plans Can Hinder**: RL-tuned models exhibit improved flexibility in plan following. However, a surprising finding is that explicitly providing human-crafted, step-by-step plans can *degrade* performance, especially on highly challenging benchmarks like AIME24. RL-tuned models often perform best when allowed to develop their *own internal planning strategies* or when provided with *high-level* guidance. Base models, in contrast, consistently show performance drops when given external plans, except for very simple tasks where basic decomposition is beneficial. This suggests RL fosters internal strategies that might conflict with externally imposed, detailed human plans.
- **RL Significantly Improves Knowledge Integration**: Base models consistently *decline* in performance when provided with supplementary external knowledge (average 5.4% drop). This indicates a fundamental limitation in their ability to effectively incorporate new information. In stark contrast, both Stage 1 and Stage 2 RL-tuned models show *substantial performance gains* (average 4.3% and 4.2%, respectively) when given the same knowledge. This highlights a critical distinction: RL enhances models' mechanisms for integrating new information during inference, making targeted external knowledge injection a potent strategy for knowledge-intensive tasks.
- **Subproblem Resolution Remains a Challenge**: Despite overall performance improvements, both base and RL-tuned models still *struggle considerably* with detailed subproblem resolution. A significant gap exists between a model's ability to solve a complete problem and its ability to correctly solve *all* constituent subproblems (e.g., on AIME24, SparkleRL-Stage 2-pss achieves 50.4% on full problems but only 17.5% on all subproblems). This suggests that current RL methods favor autonomous, high-level reasoning strategies rather than ensuring consistent, step-by-step compositional problem-solving.
- **Knowledge Gains Scale with Difficulty**: Analysis across different difficulty levels reveals that the benefits of knowledge integration *increase dramatically* as problem difficulty rises. For RL-tuned models, supplementary knowledge becomes a major driver of performance on complex problems. Conversely, planning guidance provides smaller and less consistent gains, sometimes even reducing accuracy on harder tasks. Base models show the opposite trend, largely failing to leverage auxiliary information effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in understanding the internal mechanisms through which reinforcement learning enhances the reasoning capabilities of large language models, moving beyond the traditional reliance on overall accuracy metrics. The introduction of the **SPARKLE framework** provides a novel diagnostic tool for dissecting LLM reasoning into actionable components: planning, knowledge integration, and subproblem solving. This framework's ability to reveal *how* and *why* RL affects these specific dimensions is a critical contribution to the transparency and interpretability of LLM behavior.

The findings have several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Principled Foundation for RL Research**: By providing a fine-grained understanding of RL's effects, this work offers a principled foundation for future research in LLM reasoning. Researchers can now design more targeted RL algorithms and curricula, knowing which specific capabilities (e.g., knowledge integration, flexible planning) are most amenable to improvement through RL.
- **Enhanced Data Efficiency**: The **SparkleRL-PSS** multi-stage pipeline demonstrates how to effectively re-utilize challenging problems by providing partial step scaffolding. This is a crucial step towards more data-efficient RL training, reducing the need for extensive new data generation, which is often costly and time-consuming. This approach can make advanced RL accessible to a broader range of researchers and applications.
- **Adaptive and Robust LLM Development**: The observation that RL-tuned models exhibit greater flexibility in plan following and significantly enhanced knowledge integration capabilities can guide the development of more adaptive and robust LLMs. Instead of rigid instruction following, future models could be trained to dynamically formulate their own reasoning strategies and intelligently incorporate external knowledge, leading to more resilient performance in diverse and complex scenarios.
- **Improved Human-AI Collaboration**: The nuanced understanding of how models interact with human-crafted plans and external knowledge is vital for designing better human-AI collaboration interfaces. Instead of prescriptive plans, focusing on high-level guidance or targeted knowledge injection could yield superior results, aligning with the model's intrinsic reasoning dynamics.
- **Directions for Overcoming Limitations**: The identified fragility in compositional subproblem solving across all models highlights a critical area for future research. This finding suggests a need for novel RL methods specifically tailored to ensure consistency and robustness in detailed, multi-step problem decomposition, potentially drawing from fields like formal verification or program synthesis.
- **Ethical AI Development**: By fostering more transparent and interpretable reasoning models, this work contributes to responsible AI development. Understanding *how* models arrive at their conclusions, rather than just *what* they conclude, is essential for building trustworthy systems, especially in high-stakes applications such as education, scientific discovery, or complex decision-making. The discussion of limitations (e.g., structured domains, annotation scalability) and societal impacts (e.g., fairness, alignment) further reinforces this commitment.

In conclusion, "Beyond Accuracy" provides not just a set of improved models, but a comprehensive lens through which to examine and understand the intricate relationship between reinforcement learning and LLM reasoning. Its analytical framework and algorithmic contributions are poised to influence the design of next-generation, more intelligent, and transparent reasoning agents.
